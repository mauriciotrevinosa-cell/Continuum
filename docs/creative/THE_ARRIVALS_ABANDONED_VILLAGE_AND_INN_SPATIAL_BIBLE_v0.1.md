# The Arrivals — Abandoned Village + Inn Spatial Bible v0.1

**Status:** APPROVED SPATIAL BASE / CANON PROVISIONAL  
**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Project:** `The Arrivals`  
**Branch:** `creative/s1-season-board-v0.1`

This document defines the current spatial logic of the abandoned settlement and the inn used by G1+G2. It is intentionally practical rather than decorative: the layout exists to support repeatable manga staging, character movement, domestic continuity and future reconstruction.

The architecture may be visually refined later, but core spatial relationships should remain stable unless an explicit story need requires revision.

---

# 1. Settlement scale and setting

The settlement is small and partially reclaimed by forest.

- The inn is the largest surviving building and becomes the household center.
- Roughly five small abandoned houses remain around it.
- Most nearby roofs are partially or fully collapsed.
- Old lanes exist but are overgrown enough that the settlement does not read as connected to an active road network.
- Forest surrounds the settlement on most sides.
- A low hill with one prominent shade tree sits behind the inn.
- A modest cultivation plot lies beside the inn.
- A well sits close enough to the inn to be part of daily life.
- A narrower path leads to the lake.
- The former road toward the mercantile settlement is almost entirely reclaimed by vegetation. It should not initially look like an obvious usable road.
- After the cast discovers the mercantile town, they recognize that one road/path from town points back toward the abandoned settlement and can retrace it home.

The village should feel abandoned for a long time, but not ancient or impossibly ruined. There are enough usable remnants for salvage and repair.

---

# 2. Lake orientation and future route

The lake lies on the sunset side of the settlement.

This matters for visual continuity:
- lake scenes naturally support sunset / late-afternoon light;
- E14's emotional lake scene is oriented toward that light;
- the settlement and inn can visually imply where the lake is even when it is off-panel.

The lake has:
- a small weathered waterside hut;
- a short dock / pier extension;
- no usable boat at the start;
- signs that the location once supported local water access.

The lake also connects to a river/waterway that eventually leads toward the island region. This future connection is not yet functionally exploited in early S1, but it should exist geographically from the start so later exploration feels discovered rather than invented.

---

# 3. Settlement resources and salvage logic

The surrounding houses serve as a finite material bank.

Possible salvage includes:
- bent or mismatched cooking utensils;
- crockery;
- nails and brackets;
- boards;
- doors;
- shutters;
- roofing material;
- glass fragments / intact panes where lucky;
- shelving;
- tables and stools;
- cloth;
- storage jars;
- hand tools;
- buckets;
- rope;
- hardware.

Not every house should yield the same thing.

Over time some nearby structures visibly diminish because the household dismantles unsafe or unusable buildings for materials. This creates a visual record of the inn expanding while the dead village around it slowly becomes raw material for a living home.

The five small houses can therefore have different future utility:
1. one richer in cooking / domestic remnants;
2. one with recoverable structural timber;
3. one with shelving / furniture;
4. one mostly collapsed but useful for stone/hardware;
5. one left more intact for later repurposing rather than immediate dismantling.

---

# 4. Cultivation plot

Beside the inn is a small but meaningful agricultural area.

It is not a commercial farm.

It is large enough that, once restored, it can meaningfully supplement food for roughly ten residents without making the household self-sufficient in everything.

It supports:
- vegetables;
- herbs;
- simple staple crops appropriate to the world;
- Maomao's useful plants where sensible;
- household work scenes;
- gradual visual improvement across the season.

The plot begins neglected and partially overgrown. Fence lines can be incomplete or broken and later repaired.

---

# 5. Well

The well sits beside the inn, close to the cultivation/service side.

Early state:
- structurally usable enough to inspect;
- water is initially not trusted / not clean enough for ordinary drinking;
- it requires cleaning and maintenance before becoming a dependable household resource.

Approved future micro-beat:
- Rimuru investigates the well directly;
- determines the water is not yet potable / adequately clean;
- goes inside / down to inspect and clean it using his unusual abilities;
- once finished, he launches himself rapidly out of the well in an unexpectedly comedic way;
- Yuta or another household member witnesses the exit;
- Rimuru is briefly embarrassed by how ridiculous it looked despite the useful result.

The comedy should also establish that the well is a real domestic system the household had to reclaim, not background decoration.

---

# 6. Light / solar orientation of the inn

The inn is positioned so that:
- bedrooms receive stronger morning light;
- common areas receive warmer late-afternoon / evening light;
- the lake side corresponds broadly to the sunset direction.

This orientation should influence recurring visual language:
- morning bedroom scenes feel cooler/brighter and directional;
- late common-room scenes become warm and communal;
- lake scenes naturally inherit sunset symbolism.

---

# 7. Inn — architectural concept

The inn is the largest building in the settlement and was built as a rural communal lodging house rather than a private residence.

It should feel:
- sturdy enough to survive;
- visibly neglected at first;
- repairable rather than miraculous;
- spacious enough for the growing cast;
- full of awkward corners, reused rooms and later modifications.

The inn should evolve visibly through the season.

---

# 8. Ground floor — clean layout v1

## 8.1 Covered exterior entry

The inn has a double-entry transition.

First stage:
- an exterior covered entry / porch;
- covered by the projection of the second floor above;
- protects arrivals from rain and snow;
- gives characters a place to pause before entering.

Second stage:
- a small shoe-removal / transition space inspired by a simple genkan function;
- not literally a modern Japanese genkan, but serving the same spatial logic;
- shoes, muddy boots and wet outerwear can be left here.

Then:
- one raised step leads into the lived common-room floor level.

This threshold should become a recurring visual indicator of `outside → home`.

## 8.2 Common room

The common room is the social heart of the inn.

It includes:
- a large shared table;
- generous open circulation around it;
- room for people to eat, read, argue, work and drift in/out of conversations;
- direct visual relationship with the hearth/chimney;
- enough space for overlapping ensemble staging.

The common room should not look empty once inhabited. It gradually accumulates:
- chairs that do not match;
- lamps;
- baskets;
- repaired shelves;
- maps;
- guild papers;
- personal objects;
- food storage overflow;
- tools waiting to be put away.

## 8.3 Hearth / chimney

A permanent hearth/chimney anchors the common area.

It functions as:
- heat;
- cooking support in the earliest period if needed;
- night gathering point;
- visual center for quiet scenes;
- key S1 motif for `people inhabiting what was empty`.

## 8.4 Kitchen

The kitchen is attached/open or semi-open to the common area rather than isolated behind multiple rooms.

It contains:
- work surfaces;
- salvaged shelving;
- hanging utensils;
- pots/pans recovered from neighboring houses;
- basic food-prep space;
- access close to cellar/storage.

This arrangement keeps cooking socially integrated with the common room.

## 8.5 Underground storage / cellar

Primary bulk storage is subterranean.

Access:
- via small stairs or a trapdoor/stair combination near the kitchen/service side.

Uses:
- grain;
- root vegetables;
- preserves;
- jars;
- spare cookware;
- salvage inventory;
- household supplies;
- later purchased goods.

The cellar should feel practical, cool and somewhat rough rather than like a fully finished basement.

## 8.6 Bathroom / shower / wash area

A functional wash/bath zone is located on the ground floor, where water movement and repair are easier to justify.

It includes:
- toilet/latrine solution appropriate to the setting;
- washing area;
- shower/bathing capability once repairs allow it;
- drainage/water access kept spatially sensible.

Early on it can be partially damaged / inconvenient and later improved.

## 8.7 Main stairs

The stairs to the upper floor are visible / easy to access from the common-area circulation.

This supports:
- characters hearing activity below;
- pauses on landings;
- people meeting halfway;
- late-night arrivals;
- scenes where someone considers going upstairs, then decides not to.

## 8.8 Rear/service exit

A rear or side service door connects the inn to:
- well;
- cultivation plot;
- hill/tree;
- salvage houses;
- path toward lake.

This reduces the need for every exterior movement to pass through the formal front entrance.

# 9. Upper floor — room logic v1

The upper floor is the private/resting zone.

A central or bent corridor/landing distributes the rooms. It must contain enough hall space for characters to:
- stop outside a door;
- hear voices inside;
- change their mind and walk away;
- cross paths without immediately entering another room.

This hallway is narratively important.

## 9.1 Frieren's room — largest room

The largest bedroom is technically **Frieren's room**.

Features:
- larger floor area than the other bedrooms;
- strong morning light;
- likely access to a small private or semi-private terrace/balcony;
- enough room for two people's belongings without initially being designated as a shared room.

Critical spatial/emotional canon:
- Mau has his own bedroom elsewhere;
- nevertheless, Mau gradually spends most nights / personal time in Frieren's room;
- his books, cup, clothing and everyday objects accumulate there;
- the household comes to treat him as effectively living there even though architecture still gives him a separate room;
- this is why the later separation arc can happen through objects moving back into Mau's nominal room.

In other words:
> `Frieren's room` architecturally → `their room` behaviorally.

E14's `Our room? / ...Yes.` confirms the emotional reality rather than creating a new architectural arrangement.

## 9.2 Bocchi's room

Bocchi's bedroom sits directly beside or very near Frieren's room.

It should be slightly larger/more usable than the rough-sketch `technical room` originally occupying that position.

Reasons:
- Bocchi benefits from a protected, identifiable room;
- later Fern can sleep with Bocchi naturally;
- proximity to Frieren's room supports E14's sleeping-arrangement maneuver;
- soundproofing improvements can become visibly tied to this room.

## 9.3 Mau's nominal room

Mau has a real room of his own.

It should sit near Yuta's room and within the same general upstairs wing.

Function over S1:
- initially it can be sparse / underused;
- gradually becomes study/books/overflow;
- during E11–E13 Mau moves belongings from Frieren's room into this room and begins sleeping there more consistently;
- after E14 it remains his nominal/study room even though his lived domestic rhythm returns to Frieren's room.

This allows the story to distinguish:
- `having a room` from
- `where he actually chooses to live/sleep`.

## 9.4 Yuta's room

Yuta's room is close to Mau's nominal room.

This supports:
- brother-like conversations;
- spontaneous visits;
- Mau spending time with Yuta during the separation arc;
- Frieren approaching Mau's door, hearing Mau and Yuta speaking inside, hesitating, and choosing to leave instead of interrupting.

Approved micro-scene seed:
- Frieren wants to speak with Mau;
- approaches his nominal room;
- raises a hand / reaches for door;
- hears Mau talking with Yuta;
- decides he is occupied / perhaps better without her interruption;
- quietly leaves;
- Mau never knows she came.

This is a spatially grounded extension of the E13 misunderstanding.

## 9.5 Fern's room / flexible occupation

Fern has a designated bedroom on the upper floor, but her actual sleeping pattern is flexible during G2 integration.

Progression:
- on arrival, Fern stays with Frieren initially;
- later she moves into / shares Bocchi's room as their sister-like bond develops;
- her assigned room can then become flexible guest/storage/temporary sleeping space until needed.

This should be treated as a change in **occupancy**, not architecture.

## 9.6 Momo's room

Momo receives a separate room.

Recommended placement:
- closer to the socially active side of the upper corridor / stairs;
- easy access to common room and kitchen below;
- not directly inside the most emotionally loaded Frieren/Mau/Yuta cluster.

This suits her tendency to remain connected to household activity.

## 9.7 Maomao's room

Maomao receives a separate room.

Recommended placement:
- somewhat quieter than Momo's;
- close enough to stairs/service circulation that she can move easily between room, kitchen, cultivation area and eventual work/lab spaces;
- shelves and storage can accumulate over time.

Her primary serious medicine/workspace may eventually extend beyond the bedroom rather than turning the bedroom itself into a full laboratory.

## 9.8 Anko's room

Anko receives a separate room.

Recommended placement:
- quieter edge of the upper floor;
- room for a desk/notebook habit;
- enough separation for solitary observation/writing beats without isolating her from the household.

## 9.9 Rimuru's room

Rimuru receives a nominal room despite not needing human sleeping arrangements in the same way.

This is useful because:
- it gives him storage/private space;
- reinforces that the household assigns everyone a place regardless of physical need;
- can become comedic when Rimuru is found elsewhere anyway.

## 9.10 Extra / damaged room

Keep at least one additional room partially damaged, under repair or multipurpose during early S1.

Possible uses:
- temporary storage;
- guest room;
- future arrival overflow;
- materials staging;
- room conversion later.

Do not make every available room perfectly finished from the start.

# 10. Upper-floor secondary spaces

## Landing / hallway

Mandatory.

It should be wide enough for visual storytelling rather than merely functional architecture.

Key uses:
- overheard conversations;
- hesitation at doors;
- nighttime encounters;
- characters carrying objects between rooms;
- E11–E14 spatial storytelling.

## Linen / small utility closet

A small upper-floor utility/linen closet is recommended for:
- bedding;
- towels;
- simple cleaning supplies;
- household clutter.

Primary bulk storage remains in the cellar.

## Terrace(s)

At minimum:
- a small terrace tied to Frieren's large room or immediately adjacent to it.

Optional:
- one secondary/shared balcony/landing elsewhere.

Terraces support:
- morning light;
- quiet reflection;
- conversations that remain physically inside `home` but outside bedroom privacy;
- weather / seasonal shots.

---

# 11. Room occupancy — important distinction

The inn's architecture and sleeping arrangements are not the same thing.

By late G1 / early G2 integration:

- Frieren technically owns the largest room.
- Mau technically owns his own separate room.
- Mau nevertheless spends most nights and keeps many belongings in Frieren's room.
- Yuta has his own room.
- Bocchi has her own room.
- Fern initially stays with Frieren, then increasingly stays with Bocchi.
- Momo, Maomao, Anko and Rimuru have nominal rooms.

This flexibility is intentional.

It allows the house to feel like an actual household rather than a hotel assignment chart.

---

# 12. E11–E14 spatial misunderstanding support

The layout should strengthen, not merely host, the separation arc.

Important approved logic:

1. Before G2 arrives, Mau has a nominal room but habitually lives in Frieren's larger room.
2. On Fern's return, Fern initially stays with Frieren.
3. Mau begins moving his belongings back into his nominal room because he believes Frieren and Fern should have their old closeness back.
4. Frieren interprets Mau's movement as a sign that he wants independence / no longer needs their shared domestic rhythm.
5. Mau interprets Frieren adapting to that movement as proof that his prior place was temporary.
6. Physical doorways, hallway pauses and belongings carry much of this misunderstanding visually.
7. Fern later sleeping with Bocchi creates the practical opening that allows Frieren to return to Mau without a large emotional speech.
8. E14 ends by affirming that Frieren's large room is behaviorally `our room`, while Mau's nominal room still exists and remains useful.

# 13. Future reconstruction / expansion rule

The inn is not static.

Across S1 and beyond it can visibly evolve through:
- repaired roof sections;
- replacement beams;
- reclaimed doors;
- repaired windows;
- improved bedding;
- proper cookware;
- repaired bath/shower;
- cleaned well;
- restored cultivation plot;
- better lamps;
- soundproofing for Bocchi;
- shelves/work surfaces for Maomao;
- extra storage;
- reinforced terraces;
- possible annex or expanded workshop later.

Nearby abandoned houses may shrink/disappear over time as salvage is consumed.

The visual rule is:
> the dead village slowly contracts while the inn becomes more alive.

- subterranean cellar;
