## 1. Coordinate convention

Inn geometric center = **(0,0)**.

- +X = east / southeast / mercantile approach.
- -X = west / southwest / lake side.
- +Y = north / northwest / service-hill side.
- -Y = south / southeast.

All coordinates are approximate centers in meters.

## 4. Inn silhouette anchors visible in CAL-01

CAL-01 must preserve:

- long two-storey 22 × 13 m body;
- steep simple gable roof, ~42°;
- ridge along long north–south axis;
- upper floor projecting ~0.7–0.8 m over part of main facade;
- long ~11 × 2.4 m covered porch;
- heavy off-center stone chimney in northwestern quadrant;
- south-roof repair/damage scar;
- practical timber/stone rural construction;
- no tower, no grand fantasy-hotel roof massing;
- no smoke;
- visibly abandoned but structurally repairable.

The inn must remain the strongest built landmark in the frame. The hill/tree may support the silhouette but must not visually replace the inn as the primary architectural anchor.

---

## 5. Worker-cottage identity

The five surrounding buildings are former **inn staff quarters / worker studios**, not full family farmhouses.

They should read as small, simple, practical dwellings:
- approximately 18–32 m² each;
- timber/stone language related to the inn;
- little independent domestic infrastructure;
- believable dependency on the inn for food, bathing, work, and storage;
- varied damage states;
- finite salvage.

Do not make them equal-sized copies.

---

## 6. CAL-01 camera family

Primary benchmark camera:

- entering-lane viewpoint from east / southeast;
- slightly elevated but still human-observer believable;
- wide establishing composition;
- inn dominant in midground;
- some cottages partly occluded by vegetation or terrain;
- hill/tree readable behind;
- service-side well/cultivation readable without becoming diagrammatic;
- forest enclosure visible;
- lake itself does not need to be visible;
- old route should feel almost lost to vegetation.

The composition should feel **observed rather than arranged**.

## 7. Environmental discretion

The renderer may decide the exact placement of:
- patches of mud;
- grass;
- moss;
- loose stones;
- weeds;
- puddles;
- fallen branches;
- small wood piles;
- discarded boards;
- broken fencing;
- barrels;
- tools;
- minor salvage;
- leaf litter;
- incidental bushes.

These details are not fixed coordinates.

They must support:
- abandonment;
- practical former inn operations;
- cold-season rural weathering;
- natural reclamation;
- future repairability.

They must not:
- create new major buildings;
- block canonical routes;
- move the well/cultivation;
- turn open ground into dense urban clutter;
- make the place look actively maintained;
- invent current residents.

---

## 8. Ground readability rule

CAL-01 should preserve open breathing room between structures.

The ground should naturally form three readable movement systems:

1. **Main arrival route**
   - old mercantile approach → inn porch.

2. **Service circulation**
   - inn rear/service side → well → cultivation → worker cottages / salvage.

3. **Private/natural route**
   - inn / settlement edge → narrow lake path → forest → lake.

These routes may be visually broken by vegetation in E2, but the terrain must make them believable.

---

## 9. CAL-01 pass/fail spatial checklist

PASS requires all of:
- inn clearly largest surviving structure;
- five small worker cottages, not a dense village;
- irregular distribution and open ground;
- well + cultivation together on service side;
- low hill + one prominent tree behind inn;
- entering lane readable;
- lake path separate and narrow;
- old mercantile road heavily reclaimed;
- forest encloses settlement;
- no people;
- no smoke;
- no text;
- no modern architecture;
- future pages could reuse the same geography.

A beautiful image that violates these anchors is a spatial FAIL.

---

## 10. Relationship to later growth

This map is the E2/CAL-01 baseline only.

Later phases may add repair, bathhouse, workshops, specialized rooms, private quarters, internal paths, and fortification without moving the original anchors arbitrarily.

The original settlement should remain legible underneath later growth.
