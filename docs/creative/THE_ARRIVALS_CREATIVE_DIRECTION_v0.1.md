# The Arrivals — Creative Direction v0.1

**Status:** creative/product direction; not final canon.  
**Project/app:** `Continuum`  
**Working title of the primary series:** `The Arrivals`

This document preserves creative decisions and high-confidence brainstorm direction added after the initial Phase 0 foundation work. It is intentionally separate from Phase 0 implementation scope.

## 1. Naming hierarchy

- **Continuum** is the app/platform/project umbrella.
- **The Arrivals** is the working title for the main series/story created inside Continuum.
- Continuum may later host side stories, alternate branches, What If projects, or entirely different stories.

## 2. Opening Cohort is user-sized

The earlier `10 characters + Avatar` idea was only an example.

The actual rule is:

```text
OPENING_COHORT_SIZE = USER_DECIDED
```

The opening group may be smaller or larger. The design goal is not a fixed number; it is a group small enough that the audience can learn each person and the early relationships can breathe.

The user should choose the final Opening Cohort visually before the first season is locked. Continuum can recommend balance and narrative load, but the user makes the decision.

## 3. Arrivals remain a long-running arc

Characters do not all appear at once.

- Some arrive together.
- Some arrive alone.
- Some can remain undiscovered in another region for weeks or months.
- Arrival order does not have to match source-canon chronology.
- New franchises can be added later without restarting the world.
- A new source volume/season can create a delta review rather than reopening every prior decision.

The world keeps developing while new people continue to arrive.

## 4. Frieren / Fern emotional core

High-confidence developing premise:

- Frieren and Fern do not arrive together.
- Circumstances eventually lead Frieren to genuinely believe Fern died.
- This reopens the wound left by Himmel, but in a different form: Frieren had already learned to value Fern while Fern was alive.
- Frieren comes to understand that losing Fern feels less like losing a traveling companion and more like losing family / a daughter.
- While alone, Frieren remains recognizably Frieren but behaves subtly outside her usual emotional pattern.
- Visual storytelling should show Fern's absence through routines, empty space, objects, habits and pauses rather than explaining everything with dialogue.
- When Fern is found alive, Frieren's reaction can be unusually physical and emotional for her: freezing, verifying that Fern is real, embracing her, not immediately letting go.
- The reunion does not instantly reset Frieren. Temporary overprotection and emotional aftereffects can continue afterward.

Exact timing, dialogue and episode placement remain open.

## 5. Character review philosophy

The current selector is not a closed census of every character who can ever exist in Continuum.

A narratively identifiable character who was missed should default conceptually to:

```text
UNREVIEWED_RESERVE
```

not `EXCLUDED`.

Background extras with no meaningful identity do not need individual records until promoted by a narrative reason.

Future character review should keep these concepts separate:

```text
REVIEW_STATE
SOURCE_PROMINENCE
CONTINUUM_ROLE
ARRIVAL_PRIORITY
```

A minor source character may become important in Continuum; a source protagonist may remain low-focus for long stretches.

## 6. Visual cast closure

Before a franchise receives a real cast lock, Continuum should eventually:

1. ingest the user's available manga/anime/LN/source material;
2. identify narratively distinguishable characters;
3. reconcile aliases/duplicates;
4. ignore anonymous background population;
5. present pending characters visually, one by one or in a browsable gallery;
6. show image, name, brief description, source prominence and evidence/source coverage;
7. let the user decide `SÍ / AIRE / NO`;
8. preserve unresolved cases as `UNREVIEWED_RESERVE`.

A closed review can later reopen only for newly detected characters:

```text
CAST REVIEW v1 — CLOSED
NEW SOURCE MATERIAL ARRIVES
CAST REVIEW v2 — 6 NEW CHARACTERS TO REVIEW
```

## 7. Avatar — identity and power direction

The Avatar remains a developing concept, not locked canon.

Core direction preserved so far:

- arrives with amnesia and no usable memory of their origin;
- may be a new/independent existence derived from a divine being rather than secretly being that original being;
- identity is earned through lived choices, not inherited from the source entity;
- possible internal analysis companion inspired by the type of relationship we like in Raphael/Ciel, but it must be original and develop its own identity;
- the companion should not have a convenient complete answer to the Avatar's origin;
- self-analysis may return unknown/restricted results, preserving the mystery;
- potential can be effectively unbounded without current power being infinite.

The conceptual ability loop remains:

```text
OBSERVE → UNDERSTAND → EVOLVE / CONSTRUCT
```

Understanding a power is not the same as being able to execute it, and copying a mechanism does not copy years of experience.

## 8. Separate energy systems and construction costs

The Avatar can begin at zero in systems they do not naturally possess.

Examples:

```text
Cursed Energy = 0
Mana          = 0
```

Different systems remain independent unless a later, earned conversion/unification mechanism is built.

Growth should track at least:

```text
Capacity
Control / Efficiency
Tolerance
Recovery
```

Construction and operation are different costs.

A major body/system upgrade can consume maximum capacity as a structural investment. Example only:

```text
CE max before ocular construction = 100
structural investment             = 35
CE max immediately after          = 65
```

The lost maximum does not simply return after sleeping; the reservoir must be trained upward again. Separately, using an extreme technique may drain current energy or put a subsystem into temporary burnout even when some energy remains.

## 9. Limitless / perceptual-system example

The Avatar observing Gojo's Limitless should not produce instant access.

A plausible progression:

```text
observe Limitless
→ understand part of the mechanism
→ execution fails
→ missing perceptual/computational infrastructure identified
→ train/build own equivalent architecture
→ pay construction cost
→ learn to sustain it
→ eventually evolve beyond the initial inspiration
```

The Avatar's eventual eyes/perception system should become their own design rather than a literal duplicate of Six Eyes.

## 10. Normal characters: everybody can grow, not everybody needs powers

**Important new direction:** progression is multidimensional.

The Arrivals should not use a single hierarchy where destructive combat power equals character level.

Possible mastery dimensions include:

```text
Combat
Magic
Physical Ability
Art
Craft
Science
Medicine
Engineering
Music
Social Skill
Leadership
Teaching
Logistics
Cultural Impact
Experience
```

A character from a rom-com or slice-of-life can become extraordinary at an ordinary discipline without ever becoming a fighter.

Example: an artist can improve visual memory, color judgment, hand efficiency, composition, speed, stamina and precision until work that once required a day can be completed much faster at equal or better quality.

This can feel almost superhuman through mastery without turning the brush into a weapon.

Three paths can coexist:

```text
A) human/professional growth
B) extraordinary mastery of an existing discipline
C) optional later learning of a supernatural system
```

Path C is never automatic. It requires the rules of that system to permit it, plus compatibility, training, time, costs and narrative justification.

Some originally normal characters may eventually become extremely powerful. Others may never want or need to. Neither path is treated as failure.

A person can be crucial to the future nation with:

```text
COMBAT POWER       2
MAGIC              0
ARTISTIC MASTERY  97
CULTURAL IMPACT   91
```

The story should repeatedly demonstrate that destructive power and social value are different variables.

## 11. Romance and optional harem-comedy gag

The Avatar's final romance remains deliberately undecided.

A light or recurring accidental-harem joke is allowed if it emerges naturally, but:

- nobody automatically falls in love with the Avatar;
- no franchise's original romantic mechanic is transferred by default;
- the Avatar does not receive a passive seduction power;
- multiple crushes do not force a particular end-state;
- strong friendship can remain friendship;
- major relationships never auto-canon.

The fun version is social misunderstanding, gradual chemistry, the Avatar missing obvious signals, or the internal companion trying to model romance too clinically.

## 12. City and civilization direction

The future city should not become a theme park with one district per franchise.

Its identity emerges from:

- local geography and materials;
- shared survival needs;
- technologies actually developed;
- magic/supernatural systems that can be safely integrated;
- migration;
- trades;
- institutions;
- decades of lived history.

Broad visual progression remains:

```text
camp → settlement → town → port → city → autonomous territory / nation
```

Useful future concepts include `Arrival House`, preservation of the original camp as a historic `Old Camp`, civic institutions, healthcare, markets, workshops, port logistics, education and culture.

## 13. Visual direction — multistyle

**Developing visual goal:** characters may retain meaningful elements of their original visual grammar while still occupying one physically coherent world.

Do not homogenize every character into one generic anime house style, but also do not make them look like unrelated flat assets pasted on top of a background.

Character/franchise-specific traits that may survive include:

- line weight;
- facial simplification;
- expression timing;
- smear/deformation language;
- texture;
- shading treatment;
- impact FX;
- manga hatching;
- comic onomatopoeia where appropriate;
- apparent animation cadence;
- POV-specific composition.

Shared scene layers should unify:

- perspective;
- contact with the ground;
- key light direction;
- cast shadows;
- occlusion/depth;
- weather;
- environmental particles;
- physical interaction;
- camera and spatial sound.

A useful experiment is Bocchi + Frieren + Okarun in one scene:

- Bocchi retains softer/flat readability and extreme comedic deformation when her POV dominates;
- Frieren retains calm, clean fantasy composition and restrained motion;
- Okarun can bring aggressive manga hatching, speed and contrast during danger/action;
- the same sunset, floor plane, environment and physical lighting still bind all three together.

The reference is the *philosophy* of mixed visual languages, not imitation of any one existing film's pipeline.
