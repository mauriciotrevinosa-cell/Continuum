## 1. E2 pacing candidate — give Frieren + Mau more time alone before Bocchi

Current E2 rough has Frieren and Mau find the inn and hear Bocchi's scream the same night.

Final-audit candidate:

- extend the interval between finding the inn and Bocchi's arrival;
- favored working duration: roughly **10–14 days** of in-world time;
- the extra time should not become repetitive survival filler;
- use a compact montage plus a few emotionally important scenes;
- allow E2 to become longer if needed rather than forcing the material into the current page/runtime expectation.

The purpose is to let the audience experience a real `Frieren + Mau` phase before the household begins expanding.

### Survival / first-home montage material

Possible material:
- gather firewood;
- salvage usable objects from nearby ruined houses;
- clear broken glass and debris;
- secure water;
- collect food / seeds / supplies;
- patch drafts / damaged openings;
- make the hearth usable;
- test the damaged kitchen;
- organize a small amount of storage;
- establish safe walking paths;
- learn which parts of the settlement are still useful;
- move the shared table enough to create a temporary sleeping area closer to the hearth;
- use improvised bedding while the inn is still too cold / damaged for normal room use.

This progression should visually support:

```text
CAL-02 ruined discovery
→ CAL-03 first usable state
→ pre-Bocchi two-person routine
```

The building is becoming usable, not restored.

## 2. Emotional purpose of the added time

The added days are primarily relationship time, not repair exposition.

Fundamental direction:

> **Frieren is the one who gradually chooses to open herself to Mau.**

Do not flatten this into Mau merely learning to give her space.

Mau already knows who Fern is and understands the source of Frieren's grief.

Across these early days:
- Frieren begins mentioning Fern without being prompted;
- she shares small memories / thoughts;
- she stays with Mau longer during quiet moments;
- she increasingly allows him to see emotional states she normally contains;
- Mau becomes her emotional rock because Frieren repeatedly chooses to trust him with that vulnerability.

This intimacy is emotional, not sexual.

The wider future household may understand that Frieren is still suffering, but Mau can remain the person with whom she most fully lowers her defenses.

## 3. First major night-breakdown candidate

A favored early scene during the extended E2 period:

- Mau returns somewhat late from a practical task;
- the inn is quiet and the hearth is low;
- Frieren is alone and trying to keep herself composed;
- she is crying / close to breaking because she is still processing Fern;
- she initially tries to hold herself together;
- Mau knows enough about Fern to understand what is happening;
- Mau does not solve the grief with exposition;
- unsure what else to do, he embraces Frieren.

Favored dialogue:

> **Mau:** "Everything will be alright. I'm here to help you."

Frieren holds herself together for another moment.

> **Frieren:** "Okay."

Then she finally breaks down and allows herself to cry against him.

After `Okay`, favor silence / visual acting over additional dialogue.

Possible visual details:
- Frieren's posture losing its rigid control;
- her hand gripping Mau's clothing;
- Mau simply remaining present;
- no speech explaining what the images already communicate.

Narrative meaning:

> This is the first major audience-visible moment in which Frieren voluntarily lets her defenses fully fall with Mau.

## 4. Evolution of the recurring night dynamic

Do not simply repeat the exact same crying scene.

Show progression.

Possible stages:
- first time: Mau is uncertain and the embrace is an instinctive attempt to help;
- later: Frieren initiates more of the conversation herself;
- later: she sits beside / leans against him without needing explanation;
- later: she may initiate physical comfort herself;
- Mau increasingly understands how to remain steady without turning grief into a problem to "fix."

The important progression is:

```text
Frieren chooses to open
→ Mau proves safe
→ Frieren opens more
→ Mau becomes her rock
```

This strengthens the already-existing S1 principle that E1–E3 establish an unusually deep survival/grief bond.

## 5. Bocchi-arrival integration candidate

Bocchi should still discover the Frieren/Mau night dynamic shortly after arriving, but the scene now gains history.

Favored version:

- Bocchi approaches because she is cold / frightened / unable to sleep alone;
- before walking into the room, she hears part of Frieren and Mau's private conversation;
- she realizes Frieren is crying and that Mau is supporting her;
- Bocchi does **not** interrupt;
- she waits quietly until Frieren has fallen asleep;
- only then does Mau notice Bocchi / Bocchi approach;
- Mau gives Bocchi warmth / a blanket / permission to remain nearby.

Key effect:

> For Bocchi, this is not the creation of the Frieren/Mau dynamic. She is accidentally witnessing a pattern that already exists.

This preserves the planned safe-space expansion:

```text
Frieren + Mau
→ Bocchi quietly joins the safe space
```

It also makes Bocchi's realization stronger: the two people helping her are themselves carrying pain.

## 6A. Frieren / Mau confession scene — exact working sequence to preserve for final audit

**Status:** PROVISIONAL CREATOR DIRECTION — NOT YET FULL CANON

The current CAL-18 calibration page is only an intimacy / acting stress test. Its five-panel calibration order must **not** be mistaken for the intended story blocking.

The exact working story sequence to preserve for the final audit is:

1. After the preceding crisis, Fern and Maomao give Frieren something that makes her sleep because she is physically / emotionally exhausted and will not rest normally.
2. Frieren later wakes.
3. Mau is there, alive, gently stroking / caressing her.
4. Frieren initially does not fully process what she is seeing.
5. The realization hits: **Mau is alive.**
6. Frieren acts before she can intellectualize the moment and **kisses Mau**.
7. Only **after the kiss**, Frieren tells Mau that she loves him.
8. Mau embraces Frieren.
9. Mau reassures her with the working line:
   > **"Everything will be alright."**

Important emotional rule:
- the realization that Mau survived drives the kiss;
- the confession follows the kiss rather than causing it;
- Frieren is the one who crosses the emotional threshold;
- Mau receives, reciprocates, embraces, and reassures;
- the scene should preserve shock, relief, delayed processing and emotional release rather than behaving like a conventional planned confession scene.

This sequence remains subject to the full continuity / pacing audit. If surrounding canon needs adjustment to support it, the audit may revise nearby material, but this intended emotional order should not be silently replaced by the generic CAL-18 stress-test sequence.

## 7. Final-audit pacing rule — do not let CAL page counts dictate manga rhythm

Current CALs exist to validate:
- character consistency;
- environment consistency;
- action readability;
- rendering language;
- composition stress;
- provider / pipeline behavior.

They are **not** automatic final manga layouts.

During final panelization, scenes may expand significantly when a dramatic beat needs time.

This should be explicitly checked in the final pacing audit.

Questions to ask:
- Does a major reveal need a page turn?
- Does an emotional reaction need silence before dialogue?
- Does an action transformation need a pre-impact hold?
- Is a CAL one-page composition actually stronger as 2–4 manga pages?
- Are any episodes compressed only because their rough roadmap was written before page rhythm was tested?
- Should some material move to another episode / seasonal block to preserve breathing room?

Story-driven length remains preferable to quota-driven length.
