# The Arrivals — Long-Term Timeline and Arrival Pacing v0.1

**Status:** STRONG DIRECTION + PROVISIONAL LATE-ARC RESERVOIR  
**Date:** 2026-09-26

This document establishes a planning clock, not an immutable chapter calendar.

---

## 1. Core pacing principle

The Arrivals should not compress every major convergence, relationship, city milestone and crisis into a single in-world year.

It also should not stretch the arrival phase across a decade.

Current strong target:

> **By roughly in-world Year 4, essentially all major intended Otherworlders should already have arrived and/or become part of the wider city story.**

This leaves enough time for:
- relationships to breathe;
- birthdays and anniversaries;
- festivals;
- seasons/weather;
- construction;
- institutions;
- travel;
- recovery;
- births of traditions;
- repeated holidays;
- multiple crises between arrival waves.

After the main convergence era, the story can increasingly shift away from:
- finding new people;
- sheltering arrivals;
- basic survival;

toward:
- civilization growth;
- politics/diplomacy;
- exploration;
- science/magic;
- economy;
- culture;
- world adaptation;
- large external conflicts;
- long-term character arcs.

---

## 2. Provisional season clock

Current useful planning target:

```text
Mau arrival                         = Month 0
S1 end                              = ~Month 4
S2 end / future-home site chosen    = ~Month 13–14
early S3 settlement phase           = ~Month 14–18
Year 2–3                            = rapid convergence / institutions / expansion
by Year 4                           = essentially all major intended Otherworlders present
```

These are planning ranges, not hard timestamps.

The story may compress or expand individual arcs as long as lived time remains visible.

## 3. Calendar life must be visible

If ~1.5 years have passed by early/mid S3, the audience should have felt that time.

Required recurring life texture can include:
- Mau's birthdays;
- other characters' birthdays where meaningful;
- seasonal festivals;
- harvest/food celebrations;
- settlement anniversaries;
- relationship anniversaries or private callbacks;
- winter/summer/weather changes;
- changing clothes;
- recurring traditions;
- improvements to the same places over time.

### Mau birthdays

S1 already contains Mau's first birthday celebration in Continuum.

Strong direction:
- S2 should contain enough calendar/slice-of-life material that another year passing does not feel invisible;
- S3 can include Mau's **second birthday after the S1 birthday**, depending on final month accounting;
- do not force exact birthday numbering until the final chronology pass.

The important rule is that birthdays become recurring continuity markers rather than one-off episodes.

---

## 4. S2 slice-of-life should carry calendar weight

S2 already needs extensive ordinary-life runway.

Use some of that space for:
- festivals;
- birthdays;
- dates;
- seasonal activities;
- local customs;
- household traditions;
- changes in clothing/weather/food;
- small annual callbacks.

This helps the audience believe the group has lived together for many months before Sukuna/post-Sukuna/Ori/new-home events conclude.

---

## 5. S3 onward: arrival phase gradually becomes civilization phase

The narrative identity can evolve roughly as:

```text
S1
survive + become family

S2
family expands + threats converge + choose a future home

S3
build institutions + G5 + memory crisis + widening world systems

Year 2–3
rapid city growth + more arrivals + diplomacy + ecology/world adaptation

by Year 4
major intended cast substantially converged

afterward
less "collect everyone"
more "what does this civilization become?"
```

This does not mean arrivals stop forever after Year 4.

It means they stop being the dominant structural engine of the story.

## 6. Rimuru's strategic caution — long-term political value

As the city becomes more visible, Rimuru has a strong reason not to rush into presenting it as an unstoppable world power.

His prior experience can make him wary of:
- expanding political visibility faster than defenses;
- creating dependencies without logistics;
- drawing hostile attention before evacuation/food/medicine/perimeter systems exist;
- assuming good intentions will protect the settlement from fear, opportunism or hostile states.

This should not make the city isolationist.

It supports:
- controlled diplomacy;
- gradual recognition;
- trade;
- reputation repair;
- defensive preparation;
- learning before declaring major geopolitical ambitions.

Later hostile states/factions can still decide the Otherworlder city is dangerous and attempt:
- containment;
- destabilization;
- espionage;
- alliances against it;
- proxy attacks;
- direct war.

Sukuna may become useful to such enemies because of his own desire for revenge, but **the exact coalition/arc is long-term reservoir, not locked**.

## 7. Jin-Woo / Solo Leveling gate theory — PROVISIONAL RESERVOIR

Jin-Woo's exact arrival season is **not decided**.

Strong theory worth preserving:

### 7.1 Localized gate emergence

Before or around Jin-Woo's major convergence, a ruined/ancient zone near the growing Otherworlder city begins producing **Gates**.

Initially:
- they appear concentrated in one broader region rather than instantly everywhere;
- each Gate contains hostile spaces/enemies/resources;
- successful clears yield unfamiliar materials/gems;
- the city gradually learns how to survey, clear and exploit them safely.

This can create:
- a new scout/combat profession;
- resource extraction;
- trade goods;
- economic leverage for the Otherworlder city;
- new political interest from neighboring powers.

### 7.2 Gate resources become outward-facing trade

New gems/materials can become some of the first resources strongly associated with the Otherworlder settlement.

That creates both opportunity and danger:
- trade;
- research;
- wealth;
- dependence;
- theft/smuggling;
- foreign interest;
- pressure to control Gates.

Do not lock exact Solo Leveling item/drop mechanics until source verification.

### 7.3 The frightening discovery: Gates can contain people

After the cast has learned a stable rule:

> `clear the Gate → recover resources → return`

one clear breaks that expectation.

A hidden/minor room is discovered after or near the clear.

Inside is an **unconscious person**.

This changes Gates from:
- monster/resource anomalies

into:
- **possible Arrival conduits / containment spaces / something connected to how people enter Continuum.**

This should feel important and frightening because it destroys confidence that Gates are understood.

Possible later variations:
- a person found after a clear;
- an inhabited side-room;
- someone whose arrival timing does not match the Gate's appearance;
- a Gate whose internal rules resemble a source system more than Continuum wilderness.

Exact first person found and exact relation to Jin-Woo remain OPEN.

### 7.4 Jin-Woo timing

Jin-Woo can arrive before, during or after the Gate phenomenon becomes institutionalized.

Do not lock yet.

Potential value:
- if Gates precede him, Jin-Woo arrives into a phenomenon that resembles something he understands but is not necessarily identical;
- if he precedes them, his knowledge becomes strategically important when they begin;
- if he emerges from one, his introduction becomes directly tied to the mystery.

All three remain viable.

## 8. Gate/world-adaptation interaction

The Gate theory can combine with the emerging Continuum world-adaptation rule.

Possible model:

```text
new source pressure / Otherworlders
→ world creates compatible phenomena/ecology/resources
→ Gates cluster near a region with unusually high cross-world density
→ city exploits them
→ later discovers that the same phenomenon may also move people
```

This is not locked cosmology.

Do not decide yet whether:
- Gates cause arrivals;
- arrivals cause Gates;
- both are symptoms of a third Continuum process;
- or the relationship changes over time.

The uncertainty is useful.

---

## 9. Long-term phase change after convergence

Once the major intended cast is substantially present by ~Year 4, the story should be free to accelerate outward.

The core question changes from:

> **Who else is out there and how do we keep them alive?**

toward:

> **What are we building, what is this world becoming, and what happens when other powers react to us?**

This opens:
- international conflicts;
- hostile coalitions;
- trade networks;
- scientific/magical revolutions;
- culture;
- education;
- military doctrine;
- exploration;
- large monster/ecology crises;
- internal governance stress;
- Sukuna revenge schemes;
- long-term Mau/system/origin story.

Year 4 is therefore a **convergence target**, not an ending.
