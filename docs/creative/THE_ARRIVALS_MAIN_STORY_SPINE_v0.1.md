# The Arrivals — Main Story Spine v0.1

**Status:** high-confidence main-story direction; expandable, not scene-locked  
**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Project:** `The Arrivals`  
**Scope:** broad Season 1–3 narrative spine and escalation logic  

This document promotes the currently favored season-level structure into the main story direction for `The Arrivals` while preserving flexibility for exact episode counts, group membership details, scene order, and later refinement.

It does not replace the more specific opening-group or character-state documents. Where those documents are more specific, they continue to govern those details.

---

## 1. Core seasonal progression

The current favored macro-structure is:

```text
SEASON 1
loss / disorientation
→ survival
→ first groups
→ convergence
→ reunion
→ hope that loved ones may still arrive
→ proof that later arrivals are possible
→ growing temporary settlement
→ false sense that the story may remain mostly hopeful
→ final reveal: dangerous Arrivals can come too
→ Sukuna / catastrophic cliffhanger

SEASON 2
fragile routine
→ news of Sukuna catastrophe reaches the protagonists later
→ Otherworlder fear / collective blame grows
→ trade and social relations deteriorate
→ internal disagreement over stay / leave / fight / prepare
→ controlled trust pact and power-system disclosure
→ rising monster pressure and reconnaissance need
→ social breaking point
→ relocation / secure territory
→ city-building begins as necessity
→ preparation for long-term conflict and Sukuna

SEASON 3
continuation of Season 2 consequences, not a reset
→ city growth
→ institutions mature
→ exploration / reconnaissance becomes essential
→ more distant Otherworlder groups become discoverable
→ rumors of the new city spread
→ some hidden or isolated Arrivals migrate toward it
→ others remain hidden or distrust the rumors
→ civilization, technology, defense, trade, culture, and migration accelerate
→ later arrivals such as scientific / infrastructure-oriented characters can meaningfully change development
```

## 2. Season 1 — emotional deception and widening hope

Season 1 should spend enough time with the first arrival groups that the audience begins to believe the story may primarily become a found-family / survival / rebuilding series.

The season should establish:

- loss of source-world family and friends;
- uncertainty about whether anyone else will ever arrive;
- multiple small groups rather than one giant cast dump;
- different arrival states, power compatibility, memory endpoints, and subjective timelines;
- the gradual formation of a shared temporary home;
- the first strong reunions and proof that known people can appear later.

The emotional lesson should evolve from:

> "We may have lost everyone."

into:

> "We do not know who will arrive or when — but now we know someone can."

That hope should be real.

The season-ending counterpoint is that the same phenomenon that may return loved ones can also import monsters, villains, disasters, and unresolved source-world threats.

A favored final tonal turn is Sukuna appearing far from the protagonists, sensing familiar presences, and using `Domain Expansion: Malevolent Shrine` against a major native city before the protagonists even know he exists in the new world.

The season should end with the audience understanding the danger before the main cast does.

---

## 3. Season 2 — from fragile coexistence to choosing a safer home

Season 2 should begin with apparent stability rather than immediate catastrophe.

The combined groups may be living in or restoring a small abandoned / partially abandoned settlement near a functioning mercantile city.

Current favored timing: Season 2 should carry the **decision, fortification, search and first move** toward a safer long-term home, while most of the actual settlement / city construction continues into Season 3. This avoids overloading Season 2 with a complete city-building arc on top of Sukuna, Otherworlder fear, internal politics and relationship development.

This creates room for:

- trade;
- income;
- everyday roles;
- relationships with locals;
- first economic integration;
- medicine, supplies, tools, and cultural exchange;
- the sense that ordinary life might actually be possible.

The protagonists initially do not know what happened in Sukuna's city.

News arrives gradually through trade routes, travelers, refugees, rumor, and eyewitness reports.

The sequence may escalate from:

```text
"a city was attacked"
→ "an Otherworlder did it"
→ recognizable details
→ Yuta identifies Sukuna
```

This transforms Sukuna from a private Jujutsu Kaisen threat into a world-level political and social crisis.

---

## 4. Otherworlder fear / social rupture

The Otherworlder conflict should remain morally and politically complex.

The public fear is not completely invented because genuinely catastrophic Arrivals exist.

At the same time, collective punishment and blanket hatred remain unjust.

Sukuna may become the first major event that collapses public distinctions between very different Arrivals.

Consequences may include:

- suspicion;
- travel restrictions;
- surveillance;
- price discrimination;
- refusal of service;
- political factions demanding expulsion or containment;
- harassment of harmless Arrivals;
- attacks on vulnerable people;
- deteriorating alliances with nearby native cities.

A strong emotional breaking point is an attack on Bocchi during a normal trip to the mercantile city. The injury need not be severe; its significance comes from the fact that Bocchi is an obviously nonresponsible, noncombatant Arrival being punished for Sukuna's actions.

That incident can become the final push from:

> "Can we repair this relationship?"

into:

> "We need somewhere we can protect our people ourselves."

## 5. City-building is necessity, not empire-building

The future city should not begin because Rimuru, Mau, or anyone else suddenly wants a kingdom.

It should emerge from pressure, but its **timing should be earned**. The Bocchi incident can trigger agreement that a safer home is necessary without forcing the cast to abandon the current base overnight.

The late Season 2 progression can therefore be:

temporary base
→ social rupture / Bocchi incident
→ agreement to find a safer long-term home
→ fortify the current base while it is still needed
→ reconnaissance and comparison of candidate sites
→ choose the new location
→ first departure / first camp / first construction near the end of S2
→ major settlement and city-building continues through S3

The long-term city still emerges from pressure:

- lack of safe housing;
- unstable trade access;
- repeated monster attacks;
- need for civilian protection;
- future Arrivals requiring shelter;
- defense against dangerous Arrivals;
- need for food, medicine, water, tools, logistics, and training;
- need for independence without total isolation;
- growing population and institutions.

The settlement progression remains:

```text
camp
→ temporary settlement / abandoned town
→ secure territory
→ permanent settlement
→ town
→ port / trade hub where geography allows
→ city
→ later autonomous territory / nation only if the story earns it
```

The city must feel collectively built rather than the project of one character.

## 6. Sukuna as long-form threat and information war

Sukuna should not necessarily attack the protagonists immediately after his first catastrophe.

A favored direction is that he withdraws, studies the new world, and assesses familiar opponents before committing.

The information asymmetry is central:

```text
Sukuna may arrive near / at full power.
Yuta may arrive heavily nerfed / compatibility-limited.

Yuta does not know Sukuna retained full power.
Sukuna does not know Yuta arrived nerfed.
```

A second asymmetry matters even more:

```text
Sukuna perceives / recognizes ~17-year-old Yuta.
That Yuta may actually carry decades of later memories and experience.
```

Sukuna's mental model of Yuta can therefore be badly outdated even if Yuta's current raw output is temporarily lower.

As Yuta progressively regains access, he may reveal tactical habits, refinements, techniques, counters, or judgment Sukuna never encountered in the younger version he remembers.

This should create an information war rather than a simple power-level contest.

---

## 7. Trust pact and power disclosure

The Sukuna crisis can force the combined groups to deliberately become a team.

A high-value turning point is a controlled trust pact:

> If threats from our worlds can arrive here, then trusted allies need to know what we can do, what we cannot do, and what can kill us.

This disclosure is selective, not absolute.

It can include:

- power-system type;
- current access;
- costs;
- limits;
- failure conditions;
- counters;
- compatibility problems in Continuum;
- known dangerous people / entities from source worlds.

The heroes gain strength by sharing weaknesses with trusted allies.

Sukuna gains strength by hiding information and extracting it from others.

That contrast should remain thematically important.

## 8. Mau's growth inside the seasonal spine

Mau's early growth should emerge alongside the group's growing willingness to expose their systems.

A favored early example is Frieren's flower-field magic.

Mau loves the spell, but Frieren cannot initially teach it because he effectively has no usable mana.

His fragmented internal companion may eventually produce something like:

```text
pattern recognized: flower-field magic
minimum requirement: 10 mana
current mana: 1
```

Mau trains in secret, reaches the minimum threshold, reconstructs his own version of the spell, succeeds, and immediately collapses from the cost.

Frieren sees it happen and the event remains private between them for a time.

This should establish that Mau's core progression is:

```text
OBSERVE
→ UNDERSTAND
→ CONSTRUCT
```

and that his first major constructed success is an act of creation rather than violence.

## 9. Exploration / reconnaissance becomes central

The increasing monster pressure and the possibility of isolated Arrivals make a formal exploration / reconnaissance body essential.

Its mission may include:

- map territory;
- identify monster zones;
- establish safe routes;
- scout threats;
- locate resources;
- escort civilians;
- investigate anomalies;
- search for lost or newly arrived Otherworlders;
- make first contact with isolated groups;
- report on distant settlements and changing conditions.

This institution may draw thematic inspiration from Attack on Titan's exploration mentality without being a literal duplicate.

The emotional motive matters as much as the military one:

> Someone we care about may be out there and may never find us without help.

## 10. Season 3 — consequences, migration, and acceleration

Season 3 should continue Season 2's growth rather than begin a disconnected new storyline.

Season 3 should begin close enough to the Season 2 move that the audience can experience the build rather than skip over it.

By then or progressively during the opening stretch:

- the chosen site is becoming a real settlement rather than already being a completed city;
- exploration routes exist or are being formalized;
- trade rumors travel farther;
- dangerous monster regions are increasingly mapped;
- stories about an Otherworlder-founded city spread across the world.

This creates a new phenomenon: **migration by informed choice**.

Some Otherworlders who arrived far away may:

- travel toward the city;
- hide as common folk;
- distrust the rumor;
- fear being identified after Sukuna;
- create their own smaller communities;
- wait until the city proves safe;
- arrive with completely different experiences of the world.

This allows the map and cast to expand without pretending everyone conveniently appeared near Mau.

Season 3 can therefore deepen:

- migration;
- diplomacy;
- infrastructure;
- technology;
- exploration;
- defense;
- culture;
- education;
- medicine;
- industry;
- governance;
- conflict between integration and separation.

Later arrivals such as Senku can matter more here because a functioning settlement now exists that can actually apply scientific and engineering progress at scale.

## 11. Relationship to future groups

This main spine does not require every later character to arrive in a numbered group.

Important future arrivals may happen:

- alone;
- in small clusters;
- separated by weeks or months;
- far from the main settlement;
- after the city already exists;
- after hearing rumors and deliberately traveling toward it.

Current strong timing direction:

- Group 3 is still being finalized.
- Okarun and Mikasa are current strong Group 3 anchors.
- Stark is favored for a later arrival, potentially early Season 2 rather than Group 3.
- Eren is favored for a later separate arrival, potentially around mid-Season 2 rather than arriving with Mikasa.
- Senku belongs to a later wave / Group 4-type phase and is not part of Group 3.

Exact timing remains expandable.

## 12. What is fixed vs open

### High-confidence main-story direction

- S1 = loss, survival, convergence, reunion, hope, then dangerous-arrival cliffhanger.
- S2 = routine shattered by Sukuna consequences, Otherworlder fear, internal debate, Bocchi as a social breaking point, fortification of the current base, search for safer territory, and the first move toward it near the end of the season.
- S3 = direct continuation of that move through settlement construction, city growth, exploration, migration, and broader civilization progress.
- The city emerges from necessity, not imperial ambition.
- Exploration / reconnaissance becomes essential because of monsters and isolated Arrivals.
- Sukuna should function as both combat threat and political/social catalyst.
- Later arrivals can occur very far away and migrate toward the city after rumors spread.

### Still open for later expansion

- exact episode counts;
- exact runtime of individual episodes;
- exact Group 3 membership;
- Gojo arrival timing;
- Stark arrival timing;
- Eren arrival timing;
- Senku's exact entry point;
- exact location / geography of the first city;
- exact order of some Season 2 debates and incidents;
- exact Sukuna confrontation timing;
- exact final structure of Season 3.

The story should now be developed **outward from this spine**, not rebuilt from zero each time a new character or source is introduced.
