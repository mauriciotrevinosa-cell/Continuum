## 1. Group 2 — locked current composition

**LOCKED CURRENT GROUP:**

1. **Fern**
2. **Maomao**
3. **Momo Ayase**
4. **Anko Uguisu**

Group 2 is intentionally smaller and less combat-heavy than Group 1.

Its dramatic function is not to prove that Arrivals can be powerful. Group 1 already does that. Group 2 demonstrates the problem from the perspective of people who must investigate, improvise, survive, and protect vulnerable companions with far fewer immediately reliable tools.

### Fern

Fern is the primary defensive anchor and the emotional bridge back to Frieren.

From Fern's subjective point of view, only a short time — potentially around two hours — may pass between the source-world explosion and waking in Continuum.

From Frieren's point of view, roughly two weeks may have passed by the eventual reunion:

```text
~1 week grieving/searching in the source world
+
~1 week surviving with Group 1 in Continuum
```

Fern initially has no reason to believe she changed worlds. She may assume she was displaced somewhere in her own world and prioritize finding Frieren.

A distant high-power event caused by Group 1 can be mistaken for Frieren's magic. Fern follows the wrong clue for the right emotional reason, and that false inference can still lead Group 2 toward the settlement where Frieren actually is.

### Maomao

Maomao provides a grounded survival intelligence rather than combat power.

Useful early functions include:

- testing food, water, herbs, poisons, and medicines;
- noticing symptoms and environmental hazards;
- improvising treatment from unfamiliar materials;
- questioning assumptions other characters ignore;
- helping turn survival from 'fight the monster' into 'understand the environment'.

Her curiosity can create dry comedy without making her careless or incompetent.

### Momo Ayase

Momo arrives from a source-state where Okarun's condition is emotionally urgent.

Strong direction:

- immediately before arrival, Momo witnesses Okarun in or approaching a dangerous Oni-like transformation/state;
- crossing into Continuum causes a discontinuity in her current source-state;
- memories that were inaccessible or lost return strongly enough that she remembers Okarun and their relationship;
- she can still sense that her psychic power exists, but access to it is impaired, blocked, unstable, or otherwise incompatible with the new world at first.

This makes her case distinct from Fern, whose magic may remain usable, and from Raphael, who remains functional but lacks world knowledge.

Momo therefore carries two urgent questions:

```text
What happened to Okarun?
Why can I feel my power but not use it correctly?
```

### Anko Uguisu

Anko adds an adult investigative perspective that Group 2 otherwise lacks.

She is useful because she is likely to distrust easy explanations and compare evidence:

- geography;
- sky/stars;
- ecology;
- architecture;
- language behavior;
- the different power systems represented by the group;
- inconsistencies in everyone's memory of where they should be.

A strong early role is for Anko to become one of the first people in Group 2 to seriously propose that none of them are where they think they are.

## 2. Series opening hook — before Continuum

A strong current opening direction is that `The Arrivals` does **not** begin in Continuum.

It begins in Frieren's source world during the event that separates Frieren and Fern.

Working sequence:

```text
Frieren + Fern in active conflict / battle
→ major explosion / destructive event
→ Fern disappears
→ no reliable body or confirmation
→ Frieren searches / waits / processes the loss
→ compressed passage of roughly one week
→ grief is shown through routine, silence, empty space, and exhaustion
→ Frieren eventually falls asleep
→ hard transition
→ Frieren wakes in an unknown place beside Mau
→ The Arrivals proper begins
```

The opening should make clear that the story begins with loss and uncertainty, not with a celebratory crossover reveal.

Mau's presence then gives Frieren an immediate vulnerable person to protect while she is still carrying unresolved grief over Fern.

This direction pairs naturally with a long-form premiere rather than forcing the material into a fixed 22-minute structure.

## 3. Momo / Okarun memory-inversion mini-arc

**HIGH-CONFIDENCE STORY DIRECTION.**

When Okarun eventually arrives, the reunion should not simply restore the source relationship immediately.

Preferred twist:

```text
Momo arrives
→ her memories of Okarun return
→ she fears he may have been lost / transformed / harmed
→ time passes
→ Okarun arrives
→ Okarun does not remember Momo
```

This creates an intentional inversion of the source-state dynamic.

Momo may initially react with overwhelming relief:

> Thank God. You're alive.

She may finally say things she could not say while memory was broken — including admitting how deeply she loves him.

Then Okarun reacts as though she is a stranger.

The emotional beat can become a one- or two-episode romantic/comedic mini-arc in which Momo is hurt, furious, and convinced for a while that he may be retaliating for the period when she did not remember him.

Possible comic energy:

```text
Momo: You are doing this because I forgot you, aren't you?!
Okarun: I genuinely do not know who you are.
Momo: THAT IS WORSE.
```

Okarun may spend part of the episode actively avoiding an increasingly emotional and frustrated Momo while the rest of the settlement realizes this is not a normal lovers' quarrel.

The comedy must sit on top of a real emotional problem: one of them regained an entire relationship while the other lost it.

The exact source snapshot, Oni-state details, and memory endpoint should be locked only after the relevant source material is ingested/reviewed so project canon can distinguish source facts from The Arrivals overrides.

A flashback can revisit selected source moments from Momo's perspective when her memories return, emphasizing that for her the relationship becomes emotionally real again all at once.

## 4. Cross-world language anomaly

Characters should notice early that communication is **too easy**.

This is not treated as coincidence and not explained as every franchise secretly speaking the same language.

Preferred rule:

> Arrivals do not necessarily speak the same native/source language. The Arrival phenomenon provides semantic comprehension of living speech in Continuum.

In-world behavior:

```text
speaker produces their own language / speech patterns
→ listener perceives the actual voice and cadence
→ meaning is understood across the language boundary
```

This should initially be mysterious rather than fully explained.

Important limits:

- written language is **not automatically translated** by default;
- scripts, books, signs, codes, and literacy therefore remain meaningful barriers;
- proper nouns may remain phonetic rather than translated;
- highly technical terms, poetry, puns, dialect jokes, wordplay, or concepts with no clean equivalent may translate imperfectly;
- characters can still notice accents, cadence, speech habits, and unusual terminology;
- the system should not erase cultural identity.

The effect may also allow basic spoken communication with native Continuum inhabitants, which prevents every first-contact scene from becoming a months-long language-learning arc while preserving the usefulness of translators, scholars, writing systems, and linguistic study.

Raphael can recognize the phenomenon as abnormal without knowing its cause:

```text
communication is occurring
known source languages are not identical
mechanism = unknown
```

This creates an early shared question for both groups:

> Why can we understand each other?

The answer should be discovered later rather than handed out immediately.

## 5. Group 1 / Group 2 contrast

The current contrast is intentionally strong:

```text
GROUP 1
Frieren / Mau / Bocchi / Yuta / Rimuru
→ establish
→ protect
→ organize
→ begin building

GROUP 2
Fern / Maomao / Momo / Anko
→ investigate
→ survive
→ search
→ follow clues
```

Fern is the only consistently reliable high-level supernatural defense in Group 2 at first.

Momo has potential but impaired access.
Maomao is extraordinarily useful but physically vulnerable.
Anko is capable and investigative but human.

This puts real pressure on Fern to keep people alive while also searching for Frieren.

The symmetry with Frieren is deliberate: both Frieren and Fern are protecting vulnerable people while believing the other is absent for completely different reasons.

## 6. Group proximity

Groups do not need to materialize as preassembled parties.

A useful pattern is that members of Group 2 appear within the same broad geographic region over a short span, then encounter one another quickly enough to become a practical traveling group.

That raises another mystery:

> Why did these particular people appear close enough to find each other?

The story does not need to answer whether proximity is random, probabilistic, intentional, or caused by another factor during Season 1.

## 7. Future payoff notes

Several future beats are deliberately preserved rather than solved now:

- Stark's eventual arrival can produce strong comedy precisely because Fern/Frieren's reunion and the early Arrival chaos already changed their emotional baseline.
- Okarun's arrival can become the first major romance-centered memory inversion arc.
- Group 3 is free to be significantly larger than Groups 1 and 2.
- Later groups may contain villains, civilians, families, dangerous clusters, or people with almost no combat capability.

The Arrival phenomenon should continue to feel irregular rather than mechanically balanced.
