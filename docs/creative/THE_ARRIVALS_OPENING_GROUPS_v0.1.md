## 1. Opening-season structure

Season 1 should not open as one permanently unified cohort.

The opening is built from multiple small arrival groups / mini-arcs that initially experience the new world separately and later converge.

High-confidence structure:

```text
Group 1 arrives
→ establishes a temporary base / early settlement
→ time passes
→ another group is followed separately
→ audience learns that arrivals are not isolated incidents
→ groups converge
→ characters compare where/when/how they arrived
→ the mystery of arrival ordering deepens
```

The first half of Season 1 may follow roughly 2–4 groups rather than one large cast dump.

Season 2 may expand to groups that appeared farther away and spent longer isolated, eventually hearing rumors of a city / settlement associated with Otherworlders.

Groups do **not** need to obey the same composition rule. A group may be mostly civilians, mostly dangerous people, mostly villains, mixed-power, family-like, accidental, or otherwise structurally different from another group.

## 2. Arrival-state rule

The following are separate variables and must not be collapsed into one timeline:

```text
source exit time
source status at exit
arrival time in Continuum
arrival body state
apparent age
memory endpoint
memory integrity
```

Therefore:

```text
source chronology != arrival chronology
```

Possible arrival cases include:

- alive -> transported alive;
- alive -> reconstructed body;
- dead -> reconstituted;
- older life memories -> younger / prime body;
- earlier source snapshot -> earlier memories;
- earlier body -> later memories;
- mid-event extraction -> apparent death to those left behind;
- uncertain death -> uncertain arrival state.

No character should assume that death guarantees arrival, resurrection, or eventual reunion.

The emotional consequences of death and apparent death remain real.

## 3. Group 1 — The First Arrivals

**LOCKED CURRENT GROUP:**

1. **Frieren**
2. **Mau** — the MC / Avatar
3. **Hitori Gotoh (Bocchi)**
4. **Yuta Okkotsu**
5. **Rimuru Tempest**

The group intentionally remains small and varied enough to establish the scale of the phenomenon without immediately turning the opening into a giant crossover cast.

### Frieren

Narrative functions:

- emotional anchor;
- experienced but not omniscient powerhouse;
- long-term source of insight, patience, memory, and unusual behavior;
- potential mentor/protector for Mau;
- carries active grief over Fern's presumed death.

Frieren's bond with Mau should be allowed to develop before being labeled. It may become family-like, mentor/student, deeply intimate, romantic, or something else depending on how the story evolves. The story should earn the relationship rather than preselect its final category.

### Mau

Mau is the amnesiac MC.

He begins without a usable past and without automatic authority over the group.

His contrast with Frieren is important:

```text
Frieren = enormous past / memory
Mau     = missing past / identity still being built
```

Frieren may initially protect and guide him in part because she has just experienced the apparent loss of Fern and refuses to casually lose another vulnerable person.

Mau's final romantic structure remains intentionally open. Single-partner, no-partner, multi-partner, or other relationship outcomes must emerge from story and character agency rather than being pre-forced.

### Hitori Gotoh / Bocchi

Bocchi establishes immediately that Arrivals are not limited to warriors, chosen ones, or supernatural powerhouses.

She provides:

- civilian vulnerability;
- grounded fear and homesickness;
- comedy without trivializing the situation;
- music and future cultural contribution;
- a reason for stronger members to confront the responsibility of protecting people who cannot protect themselves.

Do not automatically bring another Kessoku Band member into Group 1. Bocchi's isolation is narratively valuable because it forces her to form relationships with people she never would have chosen to meet.

### Yuta Okkotsu

Yuta is preferred over Gojo for Group 1.

He provides protection without immediately dominating the group socially or narratively.

A high-confidence future-state direction is:

```text
body ~= young / prime Yuta
memory endpoint = much later in life
```

Exact age, death state, and memory cutoff remain to be locked later, but the important creative possibility is that the group sees a young man whose lived experience may be far older than his body suggests.

This also preserves Gojo's later arrival as a major emotional event rather than spending it inside the initial cast reveal.

### Rimuru Tempest

Rimuru contributes:

- organization;
- survival planning;
- early settlement logic;
- diplomacy;
- resource thinking;
- social-system thinking;
- a second major analytical perspective on the Arrival phenomenon.

Rimuru must not trivialize early survival merely because of source-scale power.

The new world may create compatibility limitations, access restrictions, abnormal costs, or unavailable environmental assumptions while preserving Rimuru's intelligence, personality, leadership, and problem-solving strengths.

## 4. Raphael — Continuum behavior

Raphael remains fundamentally the Raphael produced and refined inside Tensura's history.

Her extraordinary refinement is not erased.

However, arrival in Continuum creates an epistemic boundary:

> Raphael can be fully functional while still knowing essentially nothing about this world's underlying rules because this is not her source world.

Desired behavior:

```text
Raphael state: functional
internal reasoning: extremely capable
source-world knowledge: retained
Continuum-world knowledge: initially absent / unverified
arrival mechanism: unknown
new-world systems: must be observed and learned
```

Raphael should be able to say, in effect:

> I am functioning correctly. The problem is that this world is outside my knowledge base.

This is preferred over artificially damaging or dumbing down Raphael.

Her strength becomes useful for inference, comparison, testing, and hypothesis generation — not free exposition.

Raphael may identify that another person exhibits the same structural anomaly as previously observed Arrivals without knowing who transported them, why, or whether another missing person will arrive later.

## 5. Mau's internal companion vs Raphael

Mau's internal analytical companion must not become a renamed Raphael.

The contrast should remain explicit.

Possible high-level distinction:

```text
Raphael
- mature
- highly structured
- precise
- refined through enormous prior experience
- source-world knowledge intact
- new-world knowledge initially unknown

Mau's companion
- original entity
- incomplete / developing
- identity-linked
- may return UNKNOWN / RESTRICTED
- may begin fragmented or only partially communicative
- grows alongside Mau
```

Raphael attempting to analyze Mau or Mau's internal companion may itself produce uncertainty rather than an immediate answer.

## 6. Frieren / Fern — opening emotional spine

This is a central early-Season-1 storyline.

Working chronology:

```text
FRIEREN SOURCE WORLD

Day 0:
Fern is caught in / disappears during a major explosion while fighting.
Fern is actually transported rather than killed.

Frieren's perspective:
Fern disappears.
No reliable confirmation of survival is available.
The evidence leads Frieren to believe Fern died.

Approximately one week later:
Frieren herself is pulled from her world.
```

Continuum arrival order does not need to preserve source exit order.

Therefore Fern can leave the source world before Frieren but appear in Continuum after Frieren.

That creates the question:

> If Fern disappeared from our world before I did, why did I arrive here first?

Frieren should experience the loss as real. She should not assume that Fern will eventually reappear simply because Arrivals exist.

This reopens the wound left by Himmel in a different way: Frieren had already learned to value Fern while Fern was still alive, and now believes she lost someone she understood as family.

### Fern's side

Fern may wake in Continuum with little or no elapsed subjective time after the explosion and initially believe she is still somewhere in her own world.

She does not immediately know that Frieren has also been transported.

A strong direction for Group 2 is that Fern is traveling with more vulnerable people and looking for Frieren / guidance.

She eventually observes a distant high-power event — potentially caused by Yuta or another member of Group 1 — and mistakes it for evidence that Frieren is nearby.

The false inference can still lead her toward the settlement where Frieren actually is.

The audience may know both sides before they reunite:

```text
Fern:    "I need to find Frieren."
Frieren: "Fern is dead."
```

The reunion should not erase the emotional aftermath immediately.

## 7. Gojo — project-continuity death interpretation

**LOCKED THE ARRIVALS PROJECT CANON.**

For The Arrivals, Gojo's fatal lapse in the Sukuna fight is **not** explained as simple overconfidence.

The project-continuity interpretation is:

- Gojo has pushed Sukuna to the point where he believes he has created the decisive opening;
- in that moment, he sees Megumi inside the situation rather than only seeing Sukuna as an opponent;
- emotionally, he sees the child he took responsibility for after killing Toji;
- that recognition disrupts his combat focus at the critical instant;
- the lapse creates the opening that leads to his death.

This is the authoritative explanation for The Arrivals continuity even if source commentary offers a different interpretation.

If Gojo later appears as an Arrival, he **knows that he died**.

His own account may include the emotional truth that, at the decisive instant, he stopped seeing only the enemy and saw Megumi.

This does not require every other character to know or agree with his internal interpretation immediately.

Gojo is intentionally **not** part of Group 1 so that his later arrival can carry larger emotional weight, especially for characters such as Yuta who may already remember and have processed his death.
