# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 1 — The Other End of the Blanket

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S10E01
**Continuity state:** B+153 morning. Mau home and functional. Prior visual and hand sequelae persist; new scars and mixed brown-white hair. Daily review remains, no training clearance. Yuka has door work with Mai.

*When: Mau wakes, the first thing he has to do for himself is get his end of the blanket back.*

He takes hold of a corner. Yuka takes hold of the other corner without opening her eyes.

“I was here first,” she says. ʸ

“I live here.” ᴹ

Her eyes open.

It was a stupid thing to say. He can see it reach her anyway. For a moment she looks at him with the same concentration she gave the floor the first time Shoko let him take a step. Then she pushes half the blanket back over him and tucks the end under his chin.

“You do.” ʸ

He waits for her to put her head down again. She doesn't. Her hand stays where she put the blanket. She draws one finger along the part of his neck that is still pink and stops when she gets to the place that feels odd to him.

“That one feels like you're touching it through my shirt,” he says. ᴹ

She moves her finger down to the collar.

“Here?” ʸ

“That feels like my shirt.” ᴹ

She laughs into his shoulder. His side pulls when he laughs with her, but the pain makes sense. He waits for it to pass and gets his arm around her.

For a while the whole room is her breathing. The other room makes a sound like Rimuru searching for a shoe without turning a lamp on. Neither of them calls through the wall to help him.

* * *

There is a sheet of paper lying under the portrait. It has three doors drawn on it, each one with a different thing wrong with it. Mau has been home for one night and already knows that one of the drawings is upside down. Yuka drew the hinges before she decided which side people would use.

He asks her about it when she sits up.

She pulls the paper over by a corner.

“It's for the practice room. Mai made a latch you can open with your elbow. I wanted to see if it would work when you're carrying something, so I brought her a bucket.” ʸ

“Was there anything in it?” ᴹ

“That was the point of the bucket.” ʸ

There is a drawing at the bottom he thought was a hinge seen from above. It is a very small picture of somebody pouring water into a boot.

“Her boot?” ᴹ

“Mine. She shut the door on me.” ʸ

He gets one hand under the paper so it doesn't slip when she unfolds the last part. There's a calculation beside a handle, crossed out twice. She takes a pencil from the sill and shows him what happens if the return spring sits on the other side. She's already tried this on a board. The idea she wants him to understand is the one she hasn't tried yet.

He starts to say that he can hold the bucket.

“That's the easy part,” she says. “Tell me what you would do if you came to it in the dark.” ʸ

He looks again.

“Put my hand out too high. There ought to be something I can follow down from here.” ᴹ

She makes a mark where his finger stops. Then she makes a second one lower down.

“Ori's shorter than that.” ʸ

He had been thinking of the door as his first useful thing to do after the clinic. She has been thinking of who else will have to use it. He takes his hand off the picture so she can keep drawing.

* * *

He can dress himself. The right cuff takes him one try. The left takes him a little longer, as it has for years. Yuka is sitting on the bed by then, tying her boots with more force than either of them needs.

“I keep thinking I have to get you down to breakfast while there's still someone to watch you eat,” she says. ʸ

He gets the button through the hole.

“You could watch me eat.” ᴹ

She gives him a look.

“I meant because you want some too,” he says. ᴹ

She looks down at the last knot. She puts the foot on the floor and gets up without using her hands.

“I do want some.” ʸ

He moves toward the door. When he gets there she catches the good cuff and draws him back a step.

She kisses him once, slow enough that it stops feeling like something either of them needs to make time for. Then she puts her forehead against his. He can feel her thinking about something. He doesn't ask her to explain it before she's ready.

“Let's have breakfast somewhere quiet,” she says. “I want you to finish telling me what you were going to say about the door.” ʸ

He reaches for her hand. She lets him take it, then gives him the paper to carry too.

“Don't fold it the other way. Mai will know.” ʸ

He looks down at the paper. For the life of him he cannot see how anybody could know.

He carries it flat all the way down the stairs.
