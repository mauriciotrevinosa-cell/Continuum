## Chapter 4 — The One He Hates

*When: B+153, late afternoon. Yuta sees who has come out to the practice yard with her, and he starts to take off his shirt so that Mau can look at the bruise.*

“Put that back,” Maki says. ᴹᵃ

Yuta puts it back.

They give Mau the upturned bucket. He is starting to think of it as his. Maki stands next to him with her arms crossed and a practice spear on her shoulder, and she doesn't look at him at all. She has spent many years not looking at people she is glad to see.

“She's been at this for a week,” she says. “It's not pretty.” ᴹᵃ

Out in the middle of the yard, Yuka rolls her neck. She has a wooden blade in each hand. Her shadow lies on the packed dirt in front of her, and it looks like nothing, like anybody's.

“The Garden loses,” she calls across. “That's the setup. Say you broke it. You got me. I've got no floor, I burned it, I'm out. What do I do?” ʸ

Mau considers the question. He has some experience in this field.

“Fall down?” ᴹ

“Wrong.” ʸ

She looks at Yuta and nods.

He comes at her, and he isn't going easy. She gives ground with the Ox out in front of her and Nue above, the way she does. Then the Ox is gone, her hands have met, and there's a word Mau can't quite hear.

The boundary closes over both of them.

From the bucket, it's only a black shape in the middle of the yard, small and round, about the size of a kitchen. It's the same one she has always had, the part she can actually hold. Nothing comes through it. Inside there will be that dark floor that takes your feet, and things coming up out of it, and a duplicate of her where you were sure she wasn't. He could open the eyes and look. He promised Shoko he wouldn't.

Maki is counting under her breath.

It doesn't break. Nothing pushes it, and no line runs across it. It simply opens, all at once, while it could still have held. The yard comes back, and there's Yuta in the middle of it, half turned toward somewhere she isn't, with his blade coming round. There she is, four paces off, with her hands empty and her breath going.

That has cost what it always costs. Her technique has burned out, and there will be no dogs or anything else from her until it comes back. Yuta knows it. Anyone who has been in that position knows what happens next, and what you do is run.

He steps in to finish it.

She was already moving. She closed it herself so that she would be, and that's all she did. She comes in under his arm with her whole small body and plain reinforcement, closer than anyone with any sense would come. Her elbow goes in below the last rib with everything she weighs.

Yuta sits down.

He doesn't fall. He just stops being upright. He stays where he is on the dirt with his mouth open, trying to work out how breathing goes.

The yard is quiet for a moment. Somewhere a pigeon is having a difficult time.

“I've got nothing left,” Yuka says. She's panting, and she's lit up from the inside like a lamp. “See? I closed it, I'm burned out. Everyone knows I'm done. So they stop looking at my hands. And I've still got elbows.” ʸ

She turns around to look at him.

“Well?” ʸ

Mau has a number of things going through his mind. He is thinking of a quarry. It isn't a technique. There's nothing to it that anybody would write down. She has simply worked out what to do with the worst second of a fight, which is to walk toward it. She's so happy. He would like very much to go and lie down in a dark room.

“I hate it,” he says. ᴹ

She flings both arms in the air.

“*I told you!*” ʸ

“Who taught you that?” ᴹ

“I did,” says Maki, beside him, not looking at him. Her voice has no expression in it at all. “She asked all of us what to do when the Garden loses. Yuta's teaching her the barrier work, and that's going to take a long time. I told her that in the meantime she's still got a body. She came back the day after you got your hand back and asked me to show her how close.” ᴹᵃ

He turns his head.

Maki shifts the spear on her shoulder.

“It's a family thing,” she says. ᴹᵃ

* * *

She gets that one try. Her technique won't answer again until tomorrow at the soonest, and nobody here pretends otherwise.

So they spend the rest of the afternoon on the step. There's no Garden in it, only Yuta coming at her with a practice blade, and Maki saying “now” when it would have broken. It works about half the time. Twice Yuta is ready for it and puts her on her back. She lies there looking at the sky and swearing at him in a clear, carrying voice, and she gets up with dirt in her teeth and goes straight back to her mark.

Mau stays on his bucket. No one has told him he has to. He's got a bad knee and a sore side, and he's not allowed to lift anything heavier than a chair. He watches every one. After a while, without really meaning to, he has started to look at her feet, and at the half step before “now”, where she gives it away.

“Your left heel,” he says. ᴹ

She stops, breathing hard, and looks over.

“Right before you go. It comes up. Just a bit. I'd see that.” ᴹ

Yuka looks down at her heel. She looks at him. For a moment he thinks that he may have made a mistake, and that he's about to hear a very clear opinion on people who were recently asked to sit quietly and hate things.

She walks over. She picks up one of her dropped blades and draws a line in the dirt with the end of it, about where a heel ought to go. She looks at that.

“Show me again what you saw,” she says. ʸ

He gets up.

It's a very small thing. He shows her with his own foot, with one hand on the bucket, and it hurts. She watches him do it, with her head on one side, frowning at his boot. Nobody's looking after anybody. There's a good solid hour of afternoon left, and Yuta hasn't got his breath back. Maki has turned away to look at something, and if you didn't know her, you wouldn't see that she's smiling.
