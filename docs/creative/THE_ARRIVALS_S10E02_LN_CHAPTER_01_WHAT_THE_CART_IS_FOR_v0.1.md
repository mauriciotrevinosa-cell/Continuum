# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 6 — What the Cart Is For

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S10E02
**Continuity state:** B+156 late morning. First contact outside the south marker. A village offers surrender and prisoners based on the quarry rumor. No keys or people accepted as property. Cart still at marker. Mau has seen the fear at Ashby the day before.

*When: B+156, late morning. Mau is on his way up to look at the new door when Jinshi crosses the hall below him, with his register folded open over one arm.*

Mau has one foot on the stairs when he hears two taps at the window.

A shadow in a guard's coat stands on the other side. It's wearing a strip of pale cloth on its wrist so the people at the guest desk can tell it's come in from a post. It lays the little slate from the south turn flat on the sill. Jinshi puts one finger over the corner so it won't slide.

“A cart and five on the road,” he reads. “They asked for a meeting but wouldn't give the post all their names. Has anyone passed the marker?” ᴶⁱ

The soldier shakes its head.

Mau comes back down the step. He used to check that turn through the soldier's eyes when a signal came in. He can feel the other sentry still in place if he reaches for it. Last week he'd needed help to get to the window himself. The slate is already under Jinshi's hand.

“Same thing as when I was upstairs,” Mau tells the soldier. “If someone asks to come in, bring it to the desk. One of us will come out to meet them.” ᴹ

Jinshi turns the slate so he can see the bottom. Mau sees the last line and wishes he had let Jinshi read it first.

“Keep the wicket shut for now,” Jinshi says. “I'll go down.” ᴶⁱ

The soldier takes the slate and leaves by the yard. Jinshi looks up at Mau.

“They asked for the man from the quarry.” ᴶⁱ

Mau looks up the stairs. Yuka is up there with Mai and a door that has decided to stick. There is time yet, if this is something that can be done in the time people usually take to do things.

“Who else is going?” ᴹ

“Fern. Rimuru. Me.” ᴶⁱ

He waits.

“You can come with us,” Jinshi adds. ᴶⁱ

That helps more than it ought to.

* * *

At the south marker, the man beside the cart takes a key from a pouch and lays it in the road.

He doesn't throw it. He sets it down with the teeth pointing back the way he came, then gets up and brushes both knees. His coat has a patch that was put on with exceptional care. Mau can see it from here because it is the first thing the man turns away from him.

Jinshi stays on the home side of the marker.

“Whose key is it?” ᴶⁱ

“It's ours,” the man says. “I have the other three if he wants them.” ᴱᵐ

Jinshi looks at the people behind the man. Two have come on foot. One is holding the horse at its head. A fourth sits on the axle with both hands under her thighs. None looks dressed for a ceremony, except that all four are trying very hard to hold still.

“I asked whose key it is.” ᴶⁱ

“To the grainhouse. I'm the keeper. It's the largest building we lock.” ᴱᵐ

Fern takes out her book. She has asked for the name of the village before she has asked what anybody means to do with it. The keeper gives it to her and starts to give her the names of the farms attached to it. She writes down the first and asks whether the rest of the people have agreed to be included.

He looks at the key.

“They agreed I should come.” ᴱᵐ

* * *

Rimuru takes a few steps sideways. From there he can see the rear of the cart. Mau sees his expression change before he hears anything himself. Then a board moves against a board and there is a sound from underneath the canvas. A heel scraping for purchase.

The person at the horse's head looks sharply over her shoulder.

“How many people are in there?” Rimuru asks. ᴿⁱ

“Three.” ᴱᵐ

“Are they hurt?” ᴿⁱ

“They can walk.” ᴱᵐ

“Then let them down, please.” ᴿⁱ

Nobody moves. The person on the axle pulls her hands free. She has a length of cord around one wrist, wound twice, to keep the end from dragging.

After yesterday, Mau had been ready for somebody frightened: a farmer with a stone he wanted moved and no idea how to ask. For most of the walk he has been thinking of things it would be possible to do about a stone without having to lift it.

“What did you bring them for?” ᴹ

The keeper has not looked directly at him yet. He does now, and for a moment there is a rather ordinary man looking back at Mau, hoping that it will turn out that he has understood the instructions.

“We heard how it ended,” he says. ᴱᵐ

Mau waits.

“At the quarry. We thought if we came to you before you came to us, there wouldn't be any need for you to come.” ᴱᵐ

Mau looks for something on the cart that can explain the key in the road. A sack of grain from the building the man just offered. Something for a table. He sees a blanket pinned under a boot on the axle and the end of the cord in the woman's hand. She tightens it when somebody inside moves.

“Did you bring them to work here?” he asks. “Is that what somebody told you we'd take?” ᴹ

The keeper looks at the cart so sharply that the woman takes her boot off the blanket.

“We brought food for the road,” she says. “There's still bread in the front. I gave them some when he let me.” ˣ

“He isn't asking about the bread,” the keeper tells her. ᴱᵐ

Then nobody helps him say it.

“We heard you'd eaten the one you killed,” he tells Mau. “We thought if you had enough here, you'd leave the rest of us alone.” ᴱᵐ

Fern closes the book over one finger.

* * *

The horse blows into the hand at its nose. The woman holding it doesn't flinch. Mau has seen her kind of attention before. She will keep the horse calm because she knows how to do that and she has no idea how to do anything else here.

He takes his own hands out of his pockets. It seems better to have them where everyone can see them.

“I didn't eat him.” ᴹ

The keeper nods so fast that Mau almost tries to say it a different way. He had expected the man to be relieved. He looks more frightened than he did a moment ago.

Mau looks down at the key. The people who use it have been persuaded to send somebody here to give away their food, and that somebody has spent the whole road trying to guess what a man would eat if he could have anything he wanted.

He wants to tell them to go home. He looks at the axle and thinks about what the woman with the cord would do before they got there.

“Rimuru,” he says. ᴹ

Rimuru has gone a little farther down the marker. He is looking at the opening at the back of the cart, at the width of a gap where a person could put a hand. He looks up when Mau says his name.

“Can we talk to the three of them first?” ᴹ

“Yes,” Rimuru says. “We should.” ᴿⁱ

Fern opens her book again. She leaves the names of the farms where they are and starts a new line. Jinshi walks along the marker to where he can speak to the person sitting on the axle.

Mau steps aside to give the back of the cart some room.

For the first time since they arrived, somebody inside asks a question.

“Is there water?” ˣ

Fern turns to Rimuru.

“Four cups from the desk,” she says. “Bring the jug too.” ᶠᵉ
