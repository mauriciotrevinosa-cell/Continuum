# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 4 — Their Own Names

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S10E02
**Continuity state:** B+153 midday. House obtains the travelers' separate accounts. Captives released from the cart with their consent and admitted to guest yard. No territorial transfer, automatic pardon, or supernatural interrogation. Mau returns the grainhouse key and asks the keeper to report what happened.

*When: the runner brings the jug, the keeper holds out both hands for it.*

Fern asks him to step back. Then she takes a cup herself and goes around the side of the cart. The runner stays beside her with the jug. There is just enough room behind it for a person to come down if the woman on the axle gets off.

“You can bring the stool from the marker,” Fern tells her. ᶠᵉ

The woman has been expecting a worse thing than work. She gets the stool. When she puts it down one of the feet is in a rut, so she kneels and puts a flat stone under it. Fern waits until she's satisfied with it.

Rimuru asks if somebody inside can move the canvas. A face appears. The man has the marks of sleep on one side of it and a string tied around two fingers where the nail has split. He looks at Rimuru, then at Fern's cup.

“Hands first, please,” he says. ˣ

She sets the cup on the board by his foot.

“May I cut the cord?” ᶠᵉ

He holds his wrists out.

* * *

The three of them have three names. Jinshi puts them on three different pages because he can't ask how they came to be there until he has stopped calling them the same thing.

The man with the split nail says his name is Perin. He stole a goat. He would like that written down before anybody else tells them what he did. The goat was thin and he sold it to a man going east, and it hadn't been his, and he has thought about the goat for eleven days.

The second is a woman named Nell. She has a bit of straw in her hair that she keeps reaching up to remove and missing. She has not been told what she did. The keeper says her name was on an order from upriver. Jinshi asks him to show it to her. He hasn't got it with him.

The third is a boy who calls himself West. He is old enough to have finished growing, though not by much, and would like to know if there's a place he can use the privy without one of the others having to go with him.

Rimuru tells him there is. He asks West if he would rather wait here for someone to show him where it is, or come as far as the guest yard on his own feet. West asks how far away the yard is.

“You'll be able to see it before you have to decide.” ᴿⁱ

West drinks his water. He puts the cup down beside his boot, the way somebody puts a thing down if he expects to come back for it.

* * *

“We don't have a gaol,” the keeper tells Mau. “The storehouse isn't one. We put the bolt on it from the outside. We didn't have any place else.” ᴱᵐ

He is looking at the key as he says it. Mau thinks, for the first time, that the man might have been glad to give it away for reasons that had nothing to do with him.

“What happened when you told them what the cart was for?” ᴹ

“They got in.” ᴱᵐ

Mau waits.

The keeper moves his foot half a step back. He takes it back again.

“We told them it was to take them upriver.” ᴱᵐ

On the other side of the cart, Perin asks Fern whether she wants the name of the man who bought the goat. She tells him he can give it to her when he has finished his water. The keeper hears this too. For the first time since they arrived he takes his eyes off the key.

* * *

Nobody signs a surrender. Fern asks the three separately whether they want to stay overnight in the guest yard while Jinshi verifies the orders. Perin asks about the price of the bed. Nell says yes before she can hear an answer. West looks as far as the open wicket, sees where the yard begins and says he can get that far.

Rimuru walks with them. At the marker he tells the guard who is coming through and why. The cart stays where it is.

Mau bends for the key. It takes less out of him than he expected and a little more than he wants anyone to see. He stands back up with it on the flat of his hand. The keeper looks at the hand, then down at his own coat, as if he is searching for the place he was supposed to put the other keys.

“Take it,” Mau says. “People still need to eat when you get home.” ᴹ

The keeper takes the key by the bow. He holds it away from his coat for a moment before he can put it back in the pouch.

“What do I tell them?” ᴱᵐ

Mau has an answer about himself. He has been carrying it since he heard what was under the canvas. Now he looks past the man at the horse, at the person who held it still, and at the woman who took so much care to put a stone under a stool.

“Tell them what you brought. And tell them the three of them walked out.” ᴹ

The keeper seems to be waiting for a third thing.

“Then tell them you have your key.” ᴹ
