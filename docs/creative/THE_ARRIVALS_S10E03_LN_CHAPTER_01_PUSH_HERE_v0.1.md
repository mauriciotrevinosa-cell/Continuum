# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 8 — Push Here

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S10E03
**Continuity state:** B+156 afternoon. Mau and Yuka talk after the delegation. Latch adjusted and tested after installation. Yuka takes a request to the guest yard and returns to her workshop. The captives begin deciding what they want recorded. Jinshi checks orders and the path back; no public correction yet.

*When: Mau gets back to the practice room, somebody has got the door to shut without a fight.*

He can tell by how little of the room he can see. There's a strip of light under it and a voice on the other side which belongs to Ori.

“This one says push.” ᴼ

“Can you read it from where you are?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“I can read it because you told me what it says.” ᴼ

“Can you find it with your hand?” ʸ

There's a noise that might be a hand finding it. Mau steps back. The door opens, and there is Ori, carrying a basket with both arms. A towel over the top has gone almost all the way in. It is very clear that the basket weighs hardly anything. It is also very clear that she has been told to act as if it does.

She walks past him with a look of great effort.

“Heavy?” he asks. ᴹ

“Full of towels.” ᴼ

“Ah.” ᴹ

She gets it down onto a bench and goes back to do the other side. Mai crosses out a little arrow. Yuka's holding a square of cloth against the hinge to see if it catches. She looks at Mau over Ori's head and makes room beside her on the wall.

He goes there. Nobody asks him for the whole story while the door is still in the air. He is grateful that the two things can take turns.

* * *

Ori asks to be paid in one of the towels. Mai tells her to take two if she'll leave the basket.

When Ori has gone, Mai tries the handle from the far side. There is a little spring to it that was not there before. She opens and closes it three times, and on the third she puts her head against the wood and smiles. It is small enough that Mau could have missed it if he had been busy telling her she did a good job.

“That'll hold,” she says. ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka takes the cloth out of the hinge and sets it down with the rest of the scraps. She asks if Mai wants to write both their names on the drawing before it goes up.

“Under the door?” ᴹᵃⁱ

“In the corner.” ʸ

Mai thinks about it, takes the pencil and writes two names in the corner. Her own goes first. She puts the paper where they can see it through the open door.

* * *

They go outside with the scraps. There is a bench by the practice room which is too short to sit on unless you intend to be very close to somebody. Yuka sits on it.

“Did you eat?” she asks. ʸ

Mau looks at her.

“Lunch,” she says. “I meant lunch.” ʸ

He knows. She had meant lunch before he turned and looked at her. Now there is a thing in the middle of a perfectly ordinary question, and he doesn't want them to spend the rest of the day walking around it.

“Three people,” he says. “They brought three people.” ᴹ

She puts the scraps down beside her boot.

He tells her which bit happened first. He gets the number of keys wrong and says it again. When he gets to what the keeper said, he has to look at the place between the boards where a thread of dry grass has grown. The words sound exactly as silly as they did down there. That was the part he had been hoping would change when he said them out loud.

Yuka takes a breath in through her nose.

“I was going to ask you if anybody had been rude,” she says. ʸ

He almost laughs. She sees it and bumps her shoulder against his, lightly.

“I know what you did on that step,” she says. “I saw it. They can come ask me if they want to know.” ʸ

He has a sudden picture of her standing up from the bench and walking straight down the road to give an account to anybody who comes within reach. Part of him would like to go with her. He sits still.

“Nell was thirsty,” he says. “She hadn't been told why she was there.” ᴹ

Yuka looks out at the path to the guest yard. It takes her a little while.

“Do they know anybody here?” ʸ

“I don't think so.” ᴹ

She considers that. She had been planning to spend the rest of the afternoon on another hinge. He can see her decide what she wants to do about that without looking at him for permission.

“I can ask Fern if they need anything. Then I'm coming back for my hinge.” ʸ

“I can carry the scraps in.” ᴹ

“You could do that now.” ʸ

He puts his hand down on the bench beside hers.

“In a minute.” ᴹ

She looks at the short distance between them, shifts over enough to close it and stays until the minute is up.

* * *

When she gets to the guest yard, Fern is in the middle of carrying a washbasin to the rooms. There's a smaller one still on the step. Yuka picks it up and asks where it goes.

“West asked for it,” Fern says. “His is the one with the door open.” ᶠᵉ

He has put his cup on the ledge outside the room. It has the lopsided handle Mau told her about. He'd seen West set it by his boot when Rimuru brought him water. She sets the basin beside it.

“I can bring water too,” she says. ʸ

West comes to the door. He's taken his shoes off. There is a little pile of road dust at the edge of the mat where he knocked them together.

“Is that the only door?” he asks. ˣ

“There's a way into the yard from the other end. You can go out either way.” ʸ

He looks past her as Fern takes the larger basin into Nell's room.

“I meant this room.” ˣ

Yuka looks at the door. The latch has a thumb piece on both sides. She can see the little brass knob of the bolt on the inside, just past his elbow.

“That's the only door,” she says. “Have you tried the bolt?” ʸ

He steps away to let her do it. She shows him which way it slides, then moves her hand so he can try it. He closes the door and she hears the bolt go across. It takes him a while to open it again.

“Can I have the cup back inside?” ˣ

She gives it to him. He takes the basin too, and she hears him set both down before the bolt moves again.

At the other end of the yard, Nell asks Fern to leave her door open. Yuka goes back to the practice room, where Mai has already laid out two different pins. The shorter one is the one Yuka told her wouldn't work.

“Have you put it in yet?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“I was waiting for you so you'd see it work.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka pulls up her stool.
