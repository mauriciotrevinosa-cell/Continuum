# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 11 — Nothing to Carry Back

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S10E04
**Continuity state:** B+158 late morning. Mau and Yuka's short outing inside the protected perimeter after daily review. No new power, travel restriction change, sex or pregnancy.

*When: Yuka asks what he put in the basket, Mau takes it off his arm so she can see for herself.*

There's bread. There's cheese wrapped in paper. There are two apples, a knife and the small board he likes to cut apples on because he can hold one end down with the heel of his hand.

Yuka takes out the second knife.

“One for the cheese,” he says. ᴹ

She takes out the third knife.

“I thought you might want to slice something.” ᴹ

She puts all three back. She has brought a blanket rolled up so tightly it takes two hands to get the strap loose. He offers to do it. She gives him the blanket and takes the basket for herself. It turns out there are ways to help that don't require anybody to ask what his limit is.

They go down the path together, as far as the little dip where the roofs stop being visible. It's inside the protection. They can hear somebody at work beyond the rise, tapping a piece of metal with a hammer. The tapping is far enough away that it has nothing to do with either of them.

Yuka picks a place in the grass. The blanket will not lie down the way she puts it, so he sits on the end that keeps lifting. She comes around and sits on the other.

“Do you remember how fast you said yes the first time I asked you on a date?” Yuka says. ʸ

“You said I didn't know where.” ᴹ

“You said you knew who.” ʸ

He remembers the noise Mai made down the corridor. Judging by Yuka's face, she remembers it too.

“I'd had time to think of a better answer,” he says. ᴹ

“You hadn't even let me finish asking.” ʸ

He looks at her in the light. There's a little red where the wind has got to the tip of her nose. She sees him looking and lifts her chin, as if she's going to make him wait this time.

“Would you go on a date with me?” he asks. ᴹ

She waits just long enough for him to begin smiling.

“Yes,” she says. ʸ

His right lace has worked itself loose on the way here. He leaves it like that. They aren't going anywhere for a while.

* * *

Yuka makes a bad sandwich on purpose. She puts the cheese in crosswise so she can eat the corners first. He has seen her do it enough times to know that what he is meant to do is tell her she is making it wrong. He does. She takes a bite and looks immensely pleased.

He cuts the apples. He gives her the slices as he finishes them. They never get as far as the plate.

When there's only the core left, Yuka wipes her fingers on the cloth and puts both hands out on the blanket. She can open them all the way. There's a place on the inside of her wrist where the small burn still goes pink when she stretches.

“You can ask,” she says. ʸ

“The hinge?” ᴹ

“The first one. I reached over it before Mai said it was cool.” ʸ

He touches a place next to it. She turns her hand over and fits it into his.

“That's the kind of thing I want you to tell me,” he says. ᴹ

“That I burned myself?” ʸ

“That you did something and you were going to tell me about it at dinner.” ᴹ

She squeezes his hand once. It seems to him that what he said reached a place he couldn't have found by saying more. He holds still and lets it stay there.

* * *

When she kisses him, the blanket slips loose on her side. He catches it by one corner and she laughs against his mouth. The two of them lose the corner anyway.

She draws back enough to look at him. Her eyes go to his hands and then straight back to his face.

“We can stay like this a while,” she says. ʸ

He moves the basket behind them. There's a place where she can put her head without his shoulder getting in the way. He gets his arm around her and she settles into it. It takes them a couple of tries. They've had more practice than this. He thinks it would be fine to get some more.

The person with the hammer stops. A bird farther down the hill starts up in the space where the tapping was.

For a long time neither of them thinks about whether they ought to be back by now.

* * *

On the way home Yuka lets him carry the basket. It is lighter, and he's about to say so when she puts her hand through the handle alongside his. He looks down at the two of them holding it.

“You know it's empty,” he says. ᴹ

“I like this side.” ʸ

They carry it between them all the way home.
