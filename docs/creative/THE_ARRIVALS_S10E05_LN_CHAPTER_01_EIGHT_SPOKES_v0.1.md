# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 12 — Eight Spokes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S10E05
**Continuity state:** B+158 afternoon. Hollis brings direct testimony about the first chalk wheel and a request from its maker. Other drawings have different meanings. No supernatural effect asserted, no access or audience granted yet.

*When: Hollis comes in for his second cup, he lays a piece of paper beside the first.*

It has a circle on it. There's a dot in the middle, where someone put down a point, and eight lines going out to the edge. The lines are in pencil. There are marks where somebody tried to rub out the fourth one and drew it over again.

Jinshi looks at the paper. Hollis drinks his tea. He has been waiting to see what someone from the house would do with an actual drawing, instead of the news that there are drawings.

“Where'd you get this?” Jinshi asks. ᴶⁱ

“A woman brought it to the bridge. Says she drew the first one there.” ᴴᵒ

“Did she say what it was for?” ᴶⁱ

“She wanted to mark where her son should wait if he got through before she did. They used to use the tollhouse, but the tollhouse was empty. She put it where she could see it from the road.” ᴴᵒ

Jinshi looks at the dot in the middle.

“Why this?” ᴶⁱ

Hollis puts down his cup. He knows the answer. He has spent the whole return trip wondering which part of it the house would hear first.

“She saw him stand up,” he says. ᴴᵒ

* * *

On the other side of the hall Mau hands Yuka the basket. She tucks it under the bench where they can find it tomorrow. He hears the end of what Hollis says, which is that some people have started leaving food under the drawings. Then he hears Jinshi ask what sort.

“Salt. A bit of bread. Fruit when they have it. I saw one with a plate put out under it.” ᴴᵒ

Mau has seen the salt. It was in a very small dish.

Yuka straightens. Hollis sees her and gets up, but she asks him to finish what he was saying.

“I asked a man why he left the plate. He said it was so you wouldn't have to look farther for a house that fed its guests.” ᴴᵒ

Mau waits for him to say what the man thought the guest would be eating. Hollis doesn't. He might not know. It occurs to Mau that the man who put the plate out might not know either.

He comes over to the table. Hollis pushes the drawing to the side so he can look at it from where he's standing.

“Do they all have plates?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“No. A fellow scratched one off a wall while I watched. He told his daughter they'd put it there so she'd know the road was open. She wanted to keep it. He gave her the chalk instead.” ᴴᵒ

Mau sets his hand beside the drawing. There isn't anything to feel from it. There wouldn't be. Somebody sat down with a pencil and made it while they had the time.

* * *

Jinshi gets another sheet. He puts the woman's request on it exactly as Hollis gives it to him. She would like to come herself. She has heard somebody is drawing wheels for money and telling people it is because Mau asked for them. She wants to know what to tell her son if somebody comes to collect.

“Did she say she wanted to talk to me?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“She asked for whoever looks after the road.” ᴴᵒ

Mau draws his hand back. He had already begun to work out how he would ask her what she saw. It hadn't occurred to him that she might have finished thinking about the quarry long before he did.

Jinshi asks Hollis how far behind him she is. She set out from the bridge when he did, on foot, and she'll be at the inn by dark. He writes that down too. They can send word before she walks the rest of it.

Hollis looks down into his cup. There's about one mouthful left. He drinks it and asks if they still want the onions he brought.

Yuka says they do.

“Good,” he says. “I didn't come all this way just to bring you a circle.” ᴴᵒ

He gets up to go and bring in the onions. Mau holds the door for him. By the time he comes back, there are two things on the table, the drawing and a note for the person who takes care of the road. He reads the note first.
