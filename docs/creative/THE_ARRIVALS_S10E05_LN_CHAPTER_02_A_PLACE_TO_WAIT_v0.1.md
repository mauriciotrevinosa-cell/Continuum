# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 13 — A Place to Wait

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S10E05
**Continuity state:** B+159 morning. The Ashby wheel maker visits the marker after a written reply. No organization or shared supernatural effect is established. Fern agrees to send a plain trade notice with Hollis.

*When: the woman from Ashby gets to the marker, she asks if she's allowed to leave her things on the bench.*

Fern tells her she can. The woman puts down a bag that has about a day's bread in it and a half-finished piece of knitting. She straightens up. Then she goes back for the knitting and brings it with her.

“I'm Esther,” she says. “I've come from the bridge.” ᴱˢ

“Fern. Hollis told me what you asked.” ᶠᵉ

Esther is looking at a man by the bench who has one hand on the back of it and his coat folded over his other arm. It takes her a moment to look away from his neck. Fern watches her work out whether she should lower her head.

“This is Mau,” she says. “He asked if he could sit in. You can tell him no.” ᶠᵉ

Esther looks at Fern, apparently to see if she has said something by accident. Then she looks back at Mau.

“You can sit,” she says. “I came to ask about a road.” ᴱˢ

So he sits. He gets his coat out of the way of her knitting.

* * *

Esther has a boy who likes to walk fast. He always gets to the bridge first. Before the quarry there was a man with a table at the tollhouse who would keep an eye on him. When the road opened the man went home, and nobody told her where a child was supposed to wait while his mother fetched a cart.

“There's a notch where you can sit on the wall. I wanted him there. He'd seen the wheel at the quarry and he knew it when I drew it. He saw it from up on the ridge.” ᴱˢ

Her needles click. Mau thinks about a boy looking at him through a panel. He had been looking at his own hands then. He hadn't known to look up at the benches until much later.

“I thought you were dead,” Esther says. “I let him look away. Then I had to tell him you weren't.” ᴱˢ

She has not told him that so he can answer it. She has got to the end of a row and needs to turn her work over.

“Somebody came with a stamp last week. Said anybody who used the bridge ought to pay for a mark on their lintel. He told my boy if he paid they'd leave our house alone.” ᴱˢ

Fern puts the date next to Ashby in her book.

“Was the money for crossing?” ᶠᵉ

“He didn't have a table. I asked where he'd put it. He said I wasn't old enough to remember what roads were like before the tolls.” ᴱˢ

Esther says this with sufficient dignity that Mau looks down at his boots until he's sure he's not smiling.

“My boy's been saving three coppers for a new hook,” she says. “He took them out of his drawer.” ᴱˢ

There is a pause. Fern asks whether he gave them to the man.

“No. He wanted me to show him what a real stamp looked like first.” ᴱˢ

* * *

Fern has Hollis's blank freight slips in the back of the book. She puts one on the bench. She writes that nobody has to buy a mark to take the road, and the settlement has asked for neither payment nor tribute. She reads it aloud before she puts it down.

Esther slips her needles through the wool. She holds the paper up where the light comes through it.

“Can I leave my drawing where it is?” she asks. ᴱˢ

Mau had been waiting for a chance to tell her what it was. For the first time he considers that she already has a good answer.

“Will your boy still know where to wait if you take it down?” ᴹ

She thinks about that.

“He will. The next one might not.” ᴱˢ

Fern asks Hollis to take the notice with him on his next run. Esther folds her own copy so she can get it into the bag with the bread. She gets as far as the road before she comes back for the knitting.

Mau holds it out to her. It's the toe of a sock, too small for her.

She takes it, and tells him how far she has to get before the light goes.

He wishes her a good walk. Then he goes to help Fern make more copies.
