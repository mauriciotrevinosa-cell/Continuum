# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 20 — What He Brought

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.2
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S10E09
**Continuity state:** B+161 late morning. Bocchi uses Kita's gift for her father's willing arrival. Three coins used, whole family together. They elect to see the rooms together before deciding where to sleep. Father recognizes Kessoku and Seika after the shared source-world search. DIO and Mau remain present without taking over the reunion; the brother introduction remains V61#21.

*When: Bocchi calls for her father, she keeps her eyes open.*

She has her mother's hand in hers. Her mother can feel the coin through the backs of her fingers, or thinks she can. Then there is only the hand.

Her father is wearing a coat he never does up properly. He is holding a bag. It has stopped raining on him, though the tops of both shoulders are wet. He goes to put the bag down and keeps holding it instead.

“You found her,” he says. ˣ

Futari gets down from the chair. She tells him who found whom. He looks as far as the chair, where the dog is, and then looks at Bocchi.

Behind her he sees Kita. Then Nijika and Ryo. He gets one hand out of the bag strap and holds it open without seeming to notice.

“All of you,” he says. ˣ

Seika catches the strap before it slips off his fingers. He turns toward her and lets out the breath he has been holding since Futari spoke.

“You found them.” ˣ

“They're here,” Seika says. “Look at her.” ˢᵉ

He says her name. There isn't a question on the end of it.

Her mother takes the bag. Then he can put both arms around Bocchi. She remembers a time he picked her up from the floor with a guitar still strapped to her and walked her to bed because she was too tired to remember how she got there. Now he lets her stand. He holds her until she has her feet where she wants them.

* * *

Beside the folded notices in her father's bag are two things from home. The first is a spare lead, in case Futari went out with the one that had a broken clasp. The other is a metronome.

He takes it out of the bag. He looks at it and laughs, once, under his breath. He'd had it in his hand when he went out because he'd been trying to find the right place to put it. He'd moved it three times that morning.

“I took it down while I was looking for a picture,” he says. “For the notices. Then I couldn't put it back.” ˣ

Seika looks toward the bag. She knows which pictures he means. None of them asks him to take those out.

Bocchi takes it. The little screen is empty. She looks at the old tape on the back, which she put there when the lid first came loose.

“I have a place for it,” she says. ᴮ

“Where?” ˣ

She nearly says that it's beside the amp. That would be enough of an answer at home. Here she has to tell him where the amp is, and where the room is, and who will be there if he goes in without knocking. The explanation comes out in the wrong order and once he gets the idea he asks her to take him up there instead.

She says she would like to. She says it without a plan for what to play him when they get there.

* * *

Fern tells them the rooms they are in are theirs to use for the day. She asks Futari if she would like somebody to find a bowl for the dog. Futari says they already did, pointing at Kita.

Her father looks at Kita. Bocchi knows the expression he is trying to find. He used to use it when the girls came by with their instruments, before there were photographs to carry from place to place asking if anybody had seen them.

“Still looking after the equipment?” he asks her. ˣ

Kita has to laugh before she can say she has carried a considerable amount of it.

He puts his hand out. She takes it with both of hers.

“Dad,” Bocchi says. ᴮ

He turns.

“She's my girlfriend.” ᴮ

Kita's hand is still in his. Her father lets it go only when she does.

“Oh,” he says. ˣ

He looks back at Bocchi. She makes herself keep looking at him.

Then he turns to Kita again.

“I'm glad it was you here with her.” ˣ

Bocchi has to look at the metronome for a little while. Her father doesn't ask her to look up. He asks Kita whether she would show him where to put the wet coat, and when she comes back she puts her hand out to Bocchi without saying anything about how that went.

* * *

Before they leave, Bocchi tells her father who the people by the desk are. Fern with the book. DIO, who helped make the calling possible and answered the question about the day. Mau, who gives his name and the empty chair nearest the door when her father realizes he is still standing.

Her father sits. For a little while he does nothing except look from one daughter to the other. DIO accepts his thank-you and leaves the rest of the quiet to him.

Mau takes the towel her mother nearly leaves behind and puts it with her things. He catches Bocchi looking at him.

“I'll be around,” he says. ᴹ

She nods. There is something else she wants to tell her parents about him. She wants to do it when she can say it without trying to keep hold of five other sentences.

They decide to go and see the other rooms together. Fern has got the key in a place where no one needs to go back for it. She sets it on the desk and lets Bocchi pick it up.

Futari opens the yard door. She tries to go ahead and the dog goes the other way. Bocchi remembers that the long path goes by the music room and the short one goes straight to the stairs. She asks which way her parents want to go.

“The way you go,” her mother says. ˣ

Bocchi puts the metronome in her pocket. She gets the door for the people behind her. For the first time since the rooms were put on the plan there is somebody to ask her to hold it a little longer.
