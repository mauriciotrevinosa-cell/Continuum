# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 62
## Chapter 12 — Until the Lamp Goes Out

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 62
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S10E15
**Continuity state:** B+164 night. Ko accepts a guest room. Nazuna and Ko discuss feeding risk and ask Anko to help them seek advice. No sample, test, feeding or adaptation.

*When: Nazuna brings Ko a pair of dry socks, he asks what he owes for the room.*

“Nothing tonight,” she says. “Tomorrow you can ask the person who actually knows.” ᴺᵃ

He puts the socks on the end of the bed. They have given him two towels and somewhere to put his wet shoes. The shoes are under the window. The towels are folded on the chair because he can't think of where else they ought to go.

Nazuna pulls one of the towels out and sits on the chair. He has a little seat by the window. He takes it and turns one ankle so he can get the wet sock off without setting his foot on the floor. She hands him the towel. He says thanks without looking up.

She watches him do the other one. He has a scratch across the back of his heel which is almost gone. The part of her that notices these things has not got any worse at it while he's been away.

“I can move,” he says. ᴷᵒ

“You're fine there.” ᴺᵃ

He pulls the dry sock all the way up.

“I wasn't sure if you were looking at me because of that, or because you wanted me closer.” ᴷᵒ

Nazuna folds the wet edge of the towel into the middle. She leaves it on the chair and comes as far as the end of the bed. From there she can see both his feet. They're both dry now.

“I wanted you closer,” she says. ᴺᵃ

* * *

There's a low step under the window. Nazuna sits on it and Ko slides down from his seat to join her. He tells her he'd been practicing what to say if he found her, and everything he practiced came after something else he'd already said. Now that he's here, he can't remember the first part.

“I remember,” she says. “You asked if I was coming over.” ᴺᵃ

He laughs. He leans forward while he does it, and she takes his hand because it comes down close to hers.

He tells her what has happened with his body since he got here. A cut closed wrong and then closed right. His fingers had changed on the road and it took him a while to get them back. He hasn't tried to find out what else has changed by getting hurt on purpose. She listens all the way through.

“I still want to be around you,” he says. ᴷᵒ

She puts her head against the side of the seat behind him. He has said it plainly enough that she can tell him the next part plainly too.

“I love you. That was true before you came here. So was the part where I was afraid to find out what would happen if I bit you.” ᴺᵃ

He looks down at her fingers. She hasn't taken her hand away.

“I love you too,” he says. ᴷᵒ

For a while that's enough to say.

Outside, a cart rolls over the loose board near the guest yard. Ko turns toward the sound. Nazuna has a hand on his arm before she realizes he's only looking. She takes it off again.

“Sometimes I think it's going to be that simple,” she says. “I'll forget for a second, and I'll do what I used to do.” ᴺᵃ

“Would you want to?” he asks. ᴷᵒ

She knows he means the biting. He's got his hand around his heel. The cut is gone. She can still see exactly where it was.

“Sometimes,” she says. “I can want it and leave you alone.” ᴺᵃ

“I don't want you to leave me alone.” ᴷᵒ

She looks at him. He moves his hand from his heel to the step between them.

“I want you to tell me when you're thinking about it,” he says. “I keep thinking maybe you'd rather have the version of me that knew what he wanted to turn into.” ᴷᵒ

Nazuna puts her head back against the wood. She'd liked how sure he'd been. There were nights when she'd leaned on it more than he knew. There were nights when she'd got him all the way across town by saying he could come along while she found a drink, when what she'd wanted was to have him on the next roof with her.

“Do you remember the drinks machine that didn't give change?” she asks. ᴺᵃ

“You told me it was broken after I put the money in.” ᴷᵒ

“You used to come back and buy me one anyway.” ᴺᵃ

“I could do that without turning into anything.” ᴷᵒ

“Yeah.” She turns her hand so he can take it again. “That was the part I wanted you to keep doing.” ᴺᵃ

He puts his thumb on the back of her hand. She watches it go there, and for a little while she lets herself think about the next time she asks him to come out, when it'll just mean coming out.

“We don't have to find out tonight,” she says. ᴺᵃ

Ko nods. He leans far enough toward her that their shoulders touch. He asks if she'd be there when he tells somebody about his hand. She says she would, and asks if he wants to see whether Anko is still downstairs.

* * *

Anko is still in the yard, writing down which side of the river she found his tracks on. Ko waits while she finishes. Nazuna asks her if she knows who they could talk to about what's happening to him. The way she says it makes Anko ask whether both of them want to know.

Ko says yes. He would like someone who can tell him what they actually know before they tell him what he ought to do.

“I can get you a conversation,” Anko says. “You can ask the questions yourselves.” ᴬⁿ

Nazuna looks at Ko. He looks back at her and nods.

Anko goes back to her notebook long enough to write down their names. She doesn't ask either of them to leave anything else with her.

At the door, Ko asks if Nazuna can sit with him until the lamp goes out. She asks if he'd like it to go out soon.

“No,” he says. “It looks like there's quite a lot left.” ᴷᵒ

She looks at the little window, with the light from the room on the sill. Then she goes in and sits down with him.
