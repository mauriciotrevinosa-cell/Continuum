# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 62
## Chapter 14 — The Last Thing He Said

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 62
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S10E15
**Continuity state:** B+165 late afternoon. Uraume asks a single question at the marker, receives Mau's firsthand account of Sukuna's last words, and departs. No alliance, threat or oath.

*When: word comes from the marker that Uraume has asked for Mau, he takes his tea with him because he has forgotten he's holding it.*

They wait outside the marker, in the last of the sun. There are two benches and Uraume has chosen neither. A soldier from the shadow watch turns toward Mau. He asks it to keep its place by the marker, where it can see them.

Uraume looks at the cup in his hand. Mau looks at it too. He sets it on the bench.

“What did he say at the end?” Uraume asks. ᵁʳ

Mau had thought they might ask about the step. He has gone all the way down the path trying to decide how to tell them what he can remember of it. There are parts he can say in order and parts that have come back as a noise. He understands now that neither is what they came to hear.

“After I brought him up?” ᴹ

Uraume inclines their head. They have not stepped closer.

“He said it was a worthy death.” ᴹ

The light reaches Uraume's sleeve, all the way to the hem. Mau waits for them to do something with that. They stand very still. It seems possible to him that he has used the wrong tense, or put the wrong emphasis on the word, and he tries to remember how Sukuna said it. It was the second time, he thinks, that he waited until Mau knew he'd heard.

“He meant it,” Mau says. “He said it to me twice.” ᴹ

Uraume looks up at that. They can see the scar at the top of his collar from where they stand. It takes a while before Mau understands they're waiting to find out if there was anything after.

“He stood up on his own. Before he stepped off, he said, ‘I've eaten.’” ᴹ

At last Uraume moves. They draw one hand inside their sleeve, with the little inward turn of the wrist a person makes to protect a thing they'd rather not drop.

“I can tell you how he said it,” Mau offers. ᴹ

“I know how he said it.” ᵁʳ

They bow. It is shallow and very exact. Then they turn down the road and go.

* * *

The soldier comes as far as the bench and holds out a hand for the cup.

He takes it up and has a swallow first. It is cooler than it was, but not by much. That surprises him. He could have sworn he stood there for a long time.

“I'll finish it like this,” he says. ᴹ

He puts both hands around the cup. From the bench he can see a little of the road. He stays until the bend has taken the last of the light off it and he can see the mark on the ground where he stopped.
