# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 62
## Chapter 14 — The Other Column

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 62
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S10E16
**Continuity state:** B+165 evening. Mai asks Mau for help with a personal matter. Yuka present with Mai's consent. Existing Construction agreement is separate. No romantic match or disclosure to Maki yet.

*When: Mai slides the paper across the table, Mau looks down it for a number.*

There isn't one. There are two scratches near the corner where she set the pencil point and then took it away. On the other side is a drawing of a latch from the back.

“If it's the door again, I did exactly what it said,” he says. ᴹ

“It's a different kind of job.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He turns the paper the right way up. There is still nothing on it. He sees how tightly she's holding the pencil and stops looking for the joke that goes there.

Yuka is beside him with an elbow on the table. She straightens to go. Mai taps the seat with the end of the pencil.

“You can stay. I'd rather you heard it from me.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka sits back down.

* * *

Mai tells them that Maki has been stopping by her room when she passes. She stops by in the mornings when she knows Mai is already up. Sometimes she only has a little time. She stands there long enough to have a look at what Mai's making, and then somebody comes to get her or she remembers where she was going.

“It's what I wanted,” Mai says. “She just comes to see me. I get to have that now.” ᴹᵃⁱ

There's a good deal more to it than she put into those words. Mau can hear where she might add the next part. He waits. Mai puts the pencil down.

“I keep wanting to ask her what she did in the years she was waiting for me. And then I realize those were her years. She wasn't waiting the whole time.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka is looking at the blank side of the paper. Mau asks Mai what she is afraid Maki will say if she asks.

“You could ask something easier.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“I can.” ᴹ

He doesn't ask the easier thing right away. In the time he leaves her she picks the pencil up again, puts it against the other edge of the paper and draws a short line.

“I think I'm afraid she'll leave out the part where I stopped being there.” ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Yuka rubs her thumb across the side of her own cup.

“She leaves out things when she thinks I don't need them,” she says. “Sometimes I tell her I want them anyway.” ʸ

Mai looks at her. She wants to ask how she says it. There is no reason she can't. When she does, Yuka thinks for a while and says that it comes out differently every time, but she makes sure Maki understands she can answer later.

“Do you want to know when it happened?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Mai puts the pencil in the little groove in the table.

“I know it happened,” she says. “I'm not stupid. I could see the two of them looking at me when I got here.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka doesn't take her eyes off her. Mai had been expecting that. It helps, that she can keep talking while Yuka looks at her.

“I want her to tell me. I want to hear it from her, and I want her to know she can tell me.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau nods. He waits to see if there is anything else she wants.

“I'd like you to be in the kitchen tomorrow morning,” Mai says. “If I end up standing outside her door for an hour, I'll need somewhere to go after.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“I'll be there.” ᴹ

Mai reaches for the paper. This time she writes a number, the hour she'd like him to come. Then she draws a line through the number and puts one a little later, because if he comes too early he will try to help Rem with breakfast.

“I'll be there too,” Yuka says. “If you want.” ʸ

Mai writes her name next to the time. She puts the pencil down where they can both see what she's written. After a moment she turns the paper over, shows them what she'd done with the latch and asks if either of them would like to hear how it actually works.
