# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 62
## Chapter 15 — What We Do Tomorrow

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 62
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S10E16
**Continuity state:** B+166 morning. Private Mai and Maki conversation. Maki discloses the source death on Mai's terms. Mai chooses the life they are making here. No memories transferred, partner selected or Momo arrival.

*When: Mai knocks, Maki tells her to come in before she finishes the second knock.*

Maki is at the table with a mug and a boot. The boot has something wrong with the heel. She's been pushing at it with her thumb, with increasing force and very little result.

Mai closes the door. She pulls out a chair. It makes such a noise that she nearly puts it back where it was.

“Can you tell me when I died?” she asks. ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki looks at her for the space of a breath. Then she turns the boot over.

“Do you want me to fix the chair?” ᴹᵃ

Mai sits down in the chair.

“Tell me when.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki sets the boot on its sole. For a little while the only part of it that's touching the floor is the toe.

“I went back for the cursed tools. You were already hurt when I got to the storehouse. Father was there.” ᴹᵃ

Mai folds her hands on the table. She had a very good guess about that last part. Hearing Maki say it is a different thing.

“Did I know you were there?” ᴹᵃⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹᵃ

Maki looks at her hands. There is a nick on the knuckle from the buckle of the boot. Mai waits until her sister looks up again.

“I can tell you the rest,” Maki says. “It'll take me a minute.” ᴹᵃ

“Take the minute.” ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Maki tells her where their father left them and how little strength she had left when Mai spoke. She tells her about the sword. She gets as far as the part where she got out and has to stop and drink from the mug. It's empty. Mai picks it up, goes to the pitcher and fills it for her.

When she puts it back down, she asks Maki whether she would like to go on.

Maki takes the mug. She gives Mai the part she'd been trying to get past. She tells her what Mai did with the strength they were born sharing, and what she had asked in return. The words come more quickly by then. They've had years to get ready.

“I kept wondering what I'd say to you if I ever had to tell you,” she says at the end. “I still don't think that was it.” ᴹᵃ

“It sounded like you were there.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki looks up.

“Sometimes I hear you say something here,” she says, “and for a second I can't remember which of us woke up.” ᴹᵃ

Mai has her hand on the pitcher. She puts it down. It takes her a second to let go of the handle.

“Does it happen when I'm mad at you?” ᴹᵃⁱ

“You asked me if I'd been eating.” ᴹᵃ

“I ask you that because you won't put a plate down and sit for five minutes unless somebody tells you to.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“I know why you ask.” ᴹᵃ

Maki gets it out easily enough that Mai understands she's known it for a long time. Mai looks at the door. She had come here with one question and hoped it would take up all the room she had. She has a dozen now and only one that she wants to ask this morning.

“Will you tell me next time it happens?” ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki rubs her knuckle over the buckle. “What are you going to do?” ᴹᵃ

“Put a plate down.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Her sister looks away. Mai can hear the chair move as Maki puts her feet flat on the floor. When she looks back, she has almost got it together.

“All right,” she says. ᴹᵃ

Mai has been thinking about a room she remembers. She can see the door to it and the place on the wall where she'd once scraped the paint. The rest of what Maki has told her has nowhere to go yet. She can see that it goes somewhere very particular for Maki.

“Did you think I'd ask you to be who you were before?” Mai asks. ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki takes a breath. The thumb on the mug goes over a chip in the rim, back and forth.

“I thought you'd think I'd forgotten you.” ᴹᵃ

“I thought maybe you weren't going to let me see the part that remembered me.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Her sister moves her hand. She leaves it on the table, a little way from Mai's. Mai puts one finger against it. After a while she puts down the rest of the hand.

“I don't remember dying,” Mai says. “So as far as I'm concerned, that doesn't count.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki's mouth opens.

“You get to remember,” Mai says. “I get to ask you for breakfast. And then tomorrow I'm going to ask you again. We're making a life here. I want to be in it while it happens.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki takes hold of her hand. She presses a little too hard. Mai leaves it there until her sister feels it and loosens her grip.

* * *

In the kitchen Yuka has managed to keep Mau away from the stove. They have two plates already set out and a third in front of a chair that will be easy to move if nobody takes it.

Mai comes in with Maki beside her. She stops when she sees the plates.

“Could you get one more?” she asks Mau. ᴹᵃⁱ

He gets it. He sets it down wherever there is room and goes to get the bread.

Maki sits beside her sister. There are two ways Mai could cut the bread when it comes. She asks Maki which way she likes it now.
