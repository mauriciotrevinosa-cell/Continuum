# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 62
## Chapter 17 — The Size of a Marble

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 62
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S10E17
**Continuity state:** B+166 afternoon. Shoko clears a bounded low-output technique exercise after a check. Mai and Mau talk by the practice yard. No combat clearance. No commitment by Kamish.

*When: Gojo measures the spot where Mau is supposed to stop, Mai asks why the measurement is in teacups.*

“That's what Shoko had to hand.” ᴳ

“Why not use one?” ᴹᵃⁱ

Gojo holds up a little ball of paper. He puts it in the middle of the mark, three cups from the wall. Mau is going to move it just far enough to put it on a pencilled cross. He's going to do it with a Blue no bigger than the ball itself. When Shoko agreed to it she had said that if he did one more than he was asked to do, Gojo could explain why he did it. It has had a remarkable effect on the precision of the exercise.

The paper moves a little to the left before Mau gets the pull straight. He lets the technique go. The paper drops within a finger's width of the cross. Gojo takes out a pencil and writes down how far it went.

“Can I try again in a minute?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Gojo looks at the time. He tells him he can ask again in a minute.

* * *

Mai has brought the paper with the latch on it. She rolls it up from the wrong end and looks down through the little opening. She can see Mau through it as he comes across the yard to the bench. She lowers it when he gets close enough to touch.

“Maki came back after breakfast,” she says. “She asked me where she should bring the boot.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“Where should she bring it?” ᴹ

“My place. There are things there to fix boots. Apparently it's easier if she tells me she wants to bring a boot.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau has got a piece of paper stuck to his sleeve. Mai picks it off. Neither of them asks what Maki said in the room.

“What are you going to do after you fix it?” he asks. ᴹ

She thinks about it. She is so used to there being something else that needs doing that the question takes a minute to make sense.

“I want somebody to take me up in a dragon sometime,” she says. “I mean somebody who thought of it themselves. Like you did for Yuka.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau puts both hands on his knees. Mai sees him about to offer something and lifts the rolled paper just enough to warn him.

“I have to find the person,” she says. ᴹᵃⁱ

“I can ask Kamish if he'd want to help when you do.” ᴹ

She lowers the paper. She hadn't thought of Kamish. He's going to have opinions about the whole arrangement. That's suddenly one of the things she likes about it.

“Don't ask him until I tell you.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“I won't.” ᴹ

Across the yard Gojo turns his wrist over and taps it with his pencil. Mau gets up. This time Mai follows him as far as the cups. She wants to see if he can actually hit the cross.
