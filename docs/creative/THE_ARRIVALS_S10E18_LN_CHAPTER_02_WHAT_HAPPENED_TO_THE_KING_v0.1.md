# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 62
## Chapter 20 — What Happened to the King

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 62
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S10E18
**Continuity state:** B+166 evening. Mai makes a palm-sized heel plate for Maki’s boot through her established Construction agreement with Mau. Momo witnesses Construction, learns how the support works, and meets Mau as Yuka’s husband. Mai tells Momo what happened to Sukuna. Mau answers briefly. Momo begins to understand Mai has a life here and accepts company. No archive shared.

*When: Momo asks if she ought to keep the broom by the door, Mai says there's room for it beside the chair.*

Momo puts it where she was told. It sticks up past the top of the chair by rather more than she'd expected. She turns it around. There's the same amount sticking up, but it looks like less.

Mai takes the boot Maki brought and sets it on the bench by her tools.

“You really fix those?” Momo asks. ᴺⁱ

“Mostly I tell her which part she wore out.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Momo watches her pick up the boot and press the heel with the same thumb Maki used. The plate has worn thin enough to curl where it used to sit flush. Mai eases it off and lays it over a tracing she made of the heel.

Mau comes to the open door with the tea Gojo promised. Mai looks up at him.

“Steel heel plate. About this wide.” She shows him the distance with her fingers. ᴹᵃⁱ

He puts the tray down. “All right.” ᴹ

Mai opens her palm. Momo recognizes the gathering of cursed energy a moment before she sees the steel.

“Mai, no. Put it down.” ᴺⁱ

She gets both hands under Mai’s wrist as if she can take some of the weight. The plate is almost as broad as Mai’s palm, curved at the back with holes where the old one was fastened. It makes a solid sound when Mai sets it on the bench.

Momo keeps holding her wrist.

“What hurts? Your hand? Sit down a second. I can get Maki.” ᴺⁱ

Mai turns her hand over in Momo’s. The fingers open without a tremor. Momo looks from them to the plate and back again.

“You couldn’t make that much before,” she says. ᴺⁱ

“I couldn’t pay for that much before.” Mai tips her chin toward the door. “The one who touched your broom made a deal with me. I make the piece. His cursed energy supplies it, and his body takes the strain.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau flexes his right hand once. Mai sees it.

“Warm for a second,” he tells her. “It’s passing.” ᴹ

Only then does she bring the new plate over to the heel to check the fit. Momo lets go of her wrist, one hand at a time.

“I’m Mau,” he says. “Sorry about the broom.” ᴹ

“Yuka’s husband,” Mai adds. “She’s Maki’s granddaughter.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Momo looks at him. Then she looks at the boot, as though it might belong to a different Maki after all.

“Maki has a granddaughter who’s old enough to have a husband?” ᴺⁱ

“Yes,” says Mai. ᴹᵃⁱ

“And he’s standing right there?” ᴺⁱ

Mau starts to raise a hand. Momo points at him before he gets it all the way up.

“I know you’re there. I just needed her to say it again.” ᴺⁱ

Mai laughs. The plate shifts under her thumb and she has to line it up a second time. Momo watches her do it. She knew the size of the things Mai used to make and the look on her face afterward. She doesn’t know what to do with the relief of seeing her laugh with a piece that size in her hand.

“What else do you make here?” she asks. ᴺⁱ

Mai shows her the latch from the inside. Momo tries it with her elbow, too high the first time and just right the second. Then she asks how much of this place was here before the fighting.

“Which fighting?” Mai says. ᴹᵃⁱ

Momo looks at her. That hadn't been a question anybody needed to ask where she came from.

“Did you find out where Sukuna is?” ᴺⁱ

Mai lets the latch spring back under her hand.

“He died.” ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

It takes three attempts to explain it. The first time Momo thinks they mean the vessel died. The second time she thinks somebody has sealed him again. The third time Mai looks toward the doorway, where Mau is still standing. She asks if he has a minute.

“You're the one who did it?” Momo says. ᴺⁱ

Mau draws the nearest chair a little closer. There is a bit of the old training paper still caught in his sleeve. It comes off when he brushes it this time.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“What did you do to him?” ᴺⁱ

He looks at Mai. Mai doesn't look away, but she moves the tray nearer so he has a place to put his hand if he wants it.

“I stayed up longer than he did,” he says. “Then we talked for a minute. He chose to go.” ᴹ

Momo keeps watching him. The person she had come down to see was someone she thought might know exactly what to do about Sukuna. This one has a scar going into the neck of his shirt and paper on his sleeve and has brought them tea. She looks down at the tray. There are three cups.

“Did you want one?” she asks him. ᴺⁱ

“That one's for Maki if she comes back for her boot.” ᴹ

Mai tells him the plate fits, but she still has to fasten it. He tells her he’ll warn Maki. Then he leaves so Momo can ask Mai anything she didn't want to ask with him there.

* * *

Momo sits down at the table.

“I think I want to know what you did yesterday,” she says. ᴺⁱ

Mai looks at the boot. Yesterday she waited until she could tell somebody what she wanted in the right words. She thinks about saying that. Then she looks up at Momo, who has given her the whole afternoon to begin anywhere she likes.

“I helped fix a door,” she says. “Do you want to hear why the first hinge was rubbish?” ᴹᵃⁱ

Momo puts both hands around her cup. She says she does.
