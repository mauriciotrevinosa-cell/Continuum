# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 1 — One Square

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S10E20
**Continuity state:** B+168 afternoon. Maomao makes two separate chocolate preparations; Mau knowingly tries one square of the mild preparation, which is stronger than intended. Only Mau affected. Group comedy, no intimate escalation or legal effect.

*When: Maomao comes back for the bowl, Jinshi is holding the wrong spoon over it.*

“That's for stirring,” she says. ᴹᵐ

“I was stirring.” ᴶⁱ

“With the spoon for the other bowl.” ᴹᵐ

He puts the spoon down. It is a very small spoon, about as hard to recognize as its fellow. Maomao examines the handle. She had drawn a dot on the cloth where the stronger test portion belonged. Somebody has put the cup containing the unused spoon directly on the dot. She moves it back and makes a second mark, farther from the cups.

On the windowsill there is a folded note from Kusuri. It has three questions about the consultation. The third is underlined twice and Maomao wants to know why. She starts to open it, thinks better of doing that over the chocolate and puts it down beside the cloth.

“Would you find the tray for the plain ones?” she asks. ᴹᵐ

Jinshi finds the tray. By the time he's back she's reading the note, holding it away from the bowl with the hand she uses to count. She puts the note back before she pours.

Kusuri has drawn a line beside the last question, then another one after it. *If the amount goes down, which part goes away first?*

Maomao takes a clean slip of paper. She's had enough reports about what happens when the amount goes up. People are still telling those stories with no particular agreement about how many days they lasted. She would rather see what happens when a person can decide what to do with the rest of an ordinary afternoon.

Jinshi comes to look over her shoulder. She moves the pencil to the other side of the paper so he can't use it to add his name.

“I was going to ask if you wanted me to keep time,” he says. ᴶⁱ

“I do. And I want you to take the plain tray out before somebody finds a reason to make the test larger.” ᴹᵐ

He takes the plain tray out. He comes back with three people who want to know what the test is.

* * *

Mau comes in for the cups. He has two in his hand already. One is Yuka's and the other one he washed because he liked the way the handle felt, which means it probably belongs to somebody he knows.

“Do you remember the chocolate?” Maomao asks. ᴹᵐ

He looks at her.

“I remember people remembering the chocolate.” ᴹ

“One square. It's the small dose. You're welcome to try it if you like. Or the plain ones are on the long tray.” ᴹᵐ

He asks what he has to do afterward. Maomao says she wants him to tell her when he notices a difference, and that he may go on with his afternoon after that. On his way in he'd left Beru at the table with the end of a story he'd been telling Ori. Beru asked if he could stay there after she went to get the map. Mau had said yes, and warned him that if he finished the story without her he would have to tell it twice.

He asks if Maomao would like him to take the cups first. She says yes, please, because all three people at the other end of the room are about to try to use the same one.

He takes them. He comes back and eats a square of chocolate from the plate she showed him.

* * *

The first thing Mau notices is that Jinshi has rolled his sleeves back to help with the bowls.

He tells him how much he likes that.

Jinshi looks at his sleeves. Then he looks at Maomao.

“Is that what you meant?” he asks. ᴶⁱ

Maomao starts to answer. By then Mau has found Rem beside the door and told her that he has been thinking all week about the first good bread she made him after the clinic. Then he asks Taihei if he knows how to fold the warm towel small enough to fit in the teapot basket. When Taihei says he does, Mau puts a hand on his shoulder and says that's a wonderful thing to know.

Maomao looks down at the cloth. The paper from Kusuri is lying across the first dot. She moves it.

“A little stronger than intended,” she says. ᴹᵐ

* * *

Gojo has one foot on the practice bench. Maki tells him to get it off. He puts the other one up too.

Mau comes out with Taihei's folded towel in his hands and catches the end of this. He stops to watch. Maki sets her practice sword down across both of Gojo's ankles. Gojo takes both feet off the bench.

“That's the best thing I've seen all afternoon,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

Maki picks up her sword. “It was a bench.” ᴹᵏ

“You'd have thought of it if it was anything else.” ᴹ

Mau sets the towel down and takes the place Gojo had been saving with his feet. Gojo starts to tell him that he was there first. Mau looks up at him with an expression of sincere delight.

“I know. You made room.” ᴹ

Maki laughs so hard she has to put the sword down again. Gojo looks from her to Mau, sees he's lost the bench and sits on the other end of it before anybody can lose that for him.

“Can you tell me one thing I'm good at?” he asks. ᴳᵒ

“You always know when the toast's going to burn.” ᴹ

“That's it?” ᴳᵒ

“Sometimes you even take it out.” ᴹ

Shoko walks past while Gojo is trying to come up with an answer. She takes the towel. She can use it. Mau tells her that's what he brought it for and means it with such feeling that Taihei, passing behind her, has to stop and ask how many times the towel has been given away.

“Twice,” Mau says. “You want it back?” ᴹ

“No. I want to know if you've had any lunch.” ᵀ

* * *

Lunch makes it as far as the next table. There is a piece of bread on Mau's plate. Beru has saved him the chair on the left. He has also heard, from Maki at one end of the table and Gojo at the other, that Mau is telling people what they're good at. He looks down at the bread with his arms hanging rather sadly at his sides.

“My king,” says Beru. “I heard you had compliments.” ᴮᵉ

Mau sets the bread down. He tells Beru he's very good at sitting with somebody who's fallen asleep before the end of a story. He always remembers which part they missed.

Beru straightens. His claws come all the way off the table.

“Would my king like me to tell him the end of one now?” ᴮᵉ

“Are there any where somebody brings me a cup?” ᴹ

Beru disappears around the door so fast that the towel on Shoko's shoulder lifts in the draft. He comes back with three cups and puts all of them where Mau can reach. Mau fills the first one and gives it to him.

Beru stands there with the cup. It's too small to hold the way everybody else does. He brings the points of two claws together on either side of the handle and moves his chair a little closer. Mau pushes the second cup toward Maki before Gojo can get it. Gojo takes the third one and tells him they could have avoided all this if he'd said he was good at sharing.

“You're right,” Mau says. “I'll remember for next time.” ᴹ

He breaks the bread in half and eats the first piece himself.

* * *

Yuka comes in just as Mau finishes carrying the second chair to where Ori is standing. He puts the chair down and makes a small whistling noise when he sees Ori has a map. Ori looks up.

“Do you need something carried?” he asks her. ᴹ

“I might.” ᴼ

Yuka looks toward Maomao.

“One square from the small tray,” Maomao says. “I know how much to cut off next time.” ᴹᵐ

Mau turns around. He smiles as though he's been waiting all afternoon for someone to ask him a question he knows the answer to.

“You made a very good door,” he tells Yuka. ᴹ

She takes the chair nearest him.

“I know,” she says. “Sit down a minute and tell me about the hinges.” ʸ

“That's what I was going to do. You turn your pencil around before you erase something so you can keep looking at it. I like watching you do that.” ᴹ

Yuka takes a second to work out what face she'd like to make in front of everybody.

“Have you said anything this good to him?” she asks Maomao. ʸ

“I've written down the time,” Maomao says. ᴹᵐ

Yuka laughs. She puts a hand on the seat beside her and leaves it there until he reaches the chair.

He sits. Ori climbs into the other chair and unfolds the map across all three of their knees. Within a minute she's put him in charge of holding the corners down. He takes the job exactly as seriously as she wanted him to.
