# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 8 — Stay With Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S10E23
**Continuity state:** B+169 after the Fort returns. Yesterday’s chocolate cleared before the outing. Yuka offers Mau a second, smaller, measured piece after he is home. He chooses to take it. They spend the afternoon together inside the ward.

*When: Ori finally lets go of the map, Mau reaches for the empty lunch bag and Yuka catches his hand.*

Mau looks down at their hands. He leaves the bag on the bench.

“What are you doing now?” she asks. ʸ

He looks toward Ori. Chloe is beside her, arguing that Gojo’s arms need to be longer still. Coco has already lent her a pencil. Ori has a great deal to say about the scale.

“I think I’ve been dismissed.” ᴹ

“Good. I want you with me.” ʸ

He turns to her. There is dust on her left knee from where she knelt to help with Suika’s boot. He can see a bright line of color at the cuff where she’s rolled it back. He’s been walking behind her for most of the morning, close enough to see that line whenever she put a hand out to help somebody. Now she’s asking for him.

“Where are we going?” ᴹ

“To eat something. After that I might make you sit in a chair and talk to me.” ʸ

She’s halfway through the door when he doubles back for the bag. He catches up to her with it over one shoulder.

“You forgot it,” he says. ᴹ

“I left it there on purpose. You can put it down.” ʸ

He goes back. She can still see him through the door when he comes out again. This time his hands are empty.

* * *

There’s a piece of chocolate on each of the little plates. One is very small. Maomao has written down how much it weighs beside her notes on the square from yesterday. Yuka asks which plate to bring over.

“The one on the left,” Maomao says. “I cut it myself. Have your lunch first.” ᴹᵐ

Yuka eats hers while she tells Mau what she heard from the kitchen yesterday. Rem’s bread. The way Taihei folded the towel. Gojo having such a good eye for burnt toast. She makes it as far as Beru’s cup before Mau asks whether she was listening to the whole thing.

“I was trying to get through the door to find you,” she says. “People kept stopping me to tell me what you’d said to them. Then I got there and you told me I’d made a very good door.” ʸ

“You did.” ᴹ

“I know. I wanted to hear the rest.” ʸ

She waits until he’s finished the bread. Then she picks up the small piece and holds it between two fingers where he can see it.

“Would you like another? This one’s from me.” ʸ

“Am I still invited to sit with you if I don’t take it?” ᴹ

“You’re sitting with me either way.” ʸ

He smiles and takes it from her. Maomao notes the time. Yuka takes the piece from the other plate and turns it until the label marked *plain* is facing him. She eats it while he watches.

* * *

It takes a while. By the time he comes round to her side of the table, Yuka has forgotten to look for a change. She has one boot off and is trying to prise a pebble out of the seam of the other. Mau takes it when she offers it to him. He sits down with it on his knee and works it loose with a blunt spoon.

“You always tuck the laces in when you take them off,” he says. ᴹ

She looks down at the loose boot. She did it without thinking.

“You trip over them,” she says. ʸ

“I know. And you always do it.” ᴹ

She looks at his face. He is still looking at the laces with an attention they have never in their lives deserved. She takes the boot from him and puts it on the ground.

“Come up here,” she says. ʸ

He puts his arm around her when she leans against him. She gets one leg across his and lets her head find the place she likes on his shoulder. Outside, somebody asks Ori whether she’s coming back to finish the map. Ori says she can finish it herself. Mau hears her. Yuka can tell because he smiles right against her hair.

“What?” she asks. ʸ

“You let them try.” ᴹ

“The scouts?” ʸ

“And me. You always make room for me to do something I can actually do.” ᴹ

She tucks her foot a little farther under his knee. He puts the spoon on the table, where he can forget why he brought it.

“I meant it when I said I wanted you with me,” she tells him. “You don’t have to find something to do.” ʸ

He stays exactly where he is for a while. Then his thumb finds the bent clip holding her hair back. He leaves it in place, but she can feel the little consideration beginning in him.

“What have you thought of?” she asks. ʸ

“Something I’d like to make you.” ᴹ

She sits up enough to see him. He still has his arm around her.

“One thing?” ʸ

He takes rather too long to answer.
