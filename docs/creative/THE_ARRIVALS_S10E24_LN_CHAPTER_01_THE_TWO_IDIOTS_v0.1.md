# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 11 — The Two Idiots

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S10E24
**Continuity state:** B+170 morning. The Fort’s delegates negotiate with Mau and Rimuru. Yuka sits with the Fort. Agreement in principle: local independence over cupboard, outings and order list, household safeguards and accounting. Final text/notice to follow.

*When: the two representatives of the rest of the house sit down, the Fort asks them to move one chair to the left.*

They move both. It takes longer than it should because Rimuru goes first and leaves Mau with the chair that has to go all the way around the other one.

Umaru waits until they're seated. She has the written notice flat in front of her. Ori has the list of people who counted yesterday. There is a place for Rem beside the cupboard drawing. Yuka is on the Fort side with her sleeves rolled up. Milim occupies the chair marked *Guardian*, where she sits with quite extraordinary stillness.

“Are you authorized to speak for the Fort?” Rimuru asks Umaru. ᴿⁱ

“Are you authorized to speak for him?” Umaru asks, pointing at Mau. ᵁ

Rimuru looks down the table.

“On some things.” ᴿⁱ

Mau pushes his cup a little closer to Rimuru's. They have agreed to signal each other if something sounds expensive.

* * *

The Fort wants to manage the cupboard, plan its own expeditions and be able to ask for supplies without asking three adults to carry the same message. That is what Yuka says after she reads the first line aloud and everybody has a chance to object. The first thing Ori says is that people should still be able to ask Frieren directly to come somewhere with them.

“That goes in,” Yuka says. ʸ

Rimuru asks whether the cupboard being theirs means they can keep somebody out of the common room.

“No,” says Umaru. “It means you have to ask before you eat the biscuits.” ᵁ

Rimuru touches his cup.

“How many biscuits?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“We would like a number large enough that we don't have to count them every time.” ᵁ

Rem tells her that if they want to know what to order they will still have to count them sometimes. Umaru takes this under consideration. She asks Ori if the count can happen when they already have the book open. Ori says yes if the person counting has washed their hands. Mau looks over at Rimuru, who lifts his cup and drinks from it. They have already lost whatever advantage the signal was supposed to give them.

Seiko opens the door to get her scissors. Milim turns her whole body in the Guardian's chair to ask whether she comes as an observer.

“I come as the person who owns the scissors,” says Seiko. ˢᵉ

Rimuru is holding them. He took them from the drawer so he could cut their side a piece of paper big enough to write on. He puts them on the table.

“We could ask you to settle the question of who gets to borrow what,” he says. ᴿⁱ

Seiko looks at Mau. “Did he ask to borrow these?” ˢᵉ

Mau looks at the cup. Rimuru already has a finger on it.

“I'd like to change sides,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuka looks down at the folded charter. “We have a place for a witness.” ʸ

“Perfect.” ᴹ

“It's on the side you're already sitting on.” ʸ

Seiko takes the scissors. The two of them are still arguing over which one was supposed to bring the paper when she shuts the door.

* * *

Jinshi has left a blank receipt book on the corner of the table. Yuka pulls it nearer. Hollis can take a Fort order with one named person to check it and a limit the house agrees to in advance. Someone at the Fort gets to choose what goes on the list. The same supplies go into the same kitchen if they're needed there, and a person who doesn't have a sash can still eat dinner.

“Can we still declare independence?” asks Umaru. ᵁ

Rimuru thinks about it. Ori has a little mark beside her list where somebody wrote that she was going to be in charge yesterday. Everybody came back, and everybody knows who got them there. He thinks about the rooms people share here and the things they've learned to do without asking him.

“Write down what you want to be independent about,” he says. “I'll agree to what's on that page.” ᴿⁱ

Milim raises one hand. Rimuru stops talking.

“If it's dangerous, I can stop it,” she says. “Frieren can too. Anybody can decide to come back. We did that yesterday.” ᴹⁱ

Ori puts her finger on the same mark. She says that was part of being in charge too.

Yuka writes it down. She turns the book so both sides can read it before she reaches for the paper with Mau's name on it.

“Do you want to tell them what you thought you were signing?” she asks him. ʸ

Mau looks at the cupboard again.

“I thought they should have something of their own.” ᴹ

Umaru takes the paper by the corner and puts it beside the new page.

“Good,” she says. “We can start with that.” ᵁ
