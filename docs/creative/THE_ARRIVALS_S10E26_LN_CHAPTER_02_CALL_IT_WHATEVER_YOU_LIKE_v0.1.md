# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 16 — Call It Whatever You Like

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S10E26
**Continuity state:** B+171 late morning. Mau rejects title, sets personal red line, departs by choice while Rimuru stays.

*When: Luminous says openly, Mau looks at the turn on the map where the road becomes the road home.*

“What do you mean by sends people?” he asks. ᴹ

“An actual envoy. A delegation that starts asking about somebody your house has taken in. At what point does a refusal from them become a threat from you?” ᴸᵘ

“If somebody wants to talk to the house, they can knock. If they want to ask about a person, that person can decide whether to come to the door.” ᴹ

“And if the envoy has a writ?” Leon asks. ᴸᵉ

“Then they can show it to whoever comes to the door.” ᴹ

It would be easy to say it more pleasantly. Mau can still think of the words. There is a part of him that is waiting to find out whether being pleasant will turn the question back into one about lamp oil.

“Suppose the person who sent them believes your door doesn't have the right to refuse,” Guy says. ᴳᵘ

Mau doesn't answer him right away. He looks at his own hands. The two fingers that don't close all the way are resting on the edge of the map. Years ago he had looked down at his hands and there had been somebody else's blood on them. He can still see where the cut went across Frieren. He remembers how little it mattered what anybody had called him the second before he saw it.

“You want to know what happens if you raise a hand against my family.” ᴹ

Somebody farther down the table moves a cup. Mau hears it knock against a saucer.

“I want to know if you hear an attack in that question,” Luminous says. ᴸᵘ

“I hear that it's the third way I've been asked to say you get to decide who comes out of my house.” ᴹ

Rimuru doesn't reach for the papers. He watches Mau.

“I can't give you anybody in there. Call me whatever you like. Leave my family alone and I'll leave you alone.” ᴹ

Guy's hand has gone still on the table. Mau looks at him, then at Leon, then at Luminous. He takes the time to look the rest of the way down the table.

“Raise a finger against them and I'll make sure there's no trace of you or what you did to them. You don't have to put my name on that paper to understand me.” ᴹ

The next sound is his chair going back.

He hasn't touched the road note. He pushes the other paper back into the space in front of Leon. He thinks about adding that he meant what he said about the wagons, and decides the name he took down will do more for the wagons than another sentence at this table.

Rimuru looks up when Mau draws level with him.

“Do you want me outside?” he asks. ᴿⁱ

“Do you want to finish?” ᴹ

Rimuru glances at his open bag. He brought things he still needs to ask. Mau can see it in the way he puts his palm down on the top page.

“Yes.” ᴿⁱ

“I'll be at the bench.” ᴹ

Rimuru nods. Mau walks out past the man who was at the door when they came in. The man takes a step to make room. The door is heavy enough that Mau can let it go while it's still open and hear it shut behind him several paces down the road.

* * *

Inside, Rimuru pulls his chair a little closer to the table.

Nobody has taken Mau's paper away. Leon looks at the place for his seal.

“If he'd signed it, what would you have brought back to the house?” he asks Rimuru. ᴸᵉ

“A piece of paper the house had never seen, with his name on it,” Rimuru says. ᴿⁱ

Guy lifts his glass. “You'd have been happy to tell them that.” ᴳᵘ

Rimuru looks up at him.

“I would've had to tell them that.” ᴿⁱ

Leon picks up the paper, folds it along the space where the seal would go and sets it beside his own things. The note about the wagons stays on the table.

Luminous has the road note in her hand. She looks from it to the paper in front of Leon.

“I'd still like to tell that woman where she can send her wagons,” she says. ᴸᵘ

“So would I,” Rimuru says. “Let's start there.” ᴿⁱ
