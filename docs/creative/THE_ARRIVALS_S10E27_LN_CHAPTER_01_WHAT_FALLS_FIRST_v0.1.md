# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 17 — What Falls First

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S10E27
**Continuity state:** B+171 late morning. Outside Walpurgis, Falling Devil seizes on Mau's fear. She destabilizes gravity. Contact with the prior life at the overwrite seam begins.

*When: Mau rounds the low wall, he can still see the bench they chose from the last place the hall is visible.*

He sends Rimuru a short word through the connection they usually use for this kind of thing. Outside. Rimuru acknowledges it. Mau takes the map out of his pocket again. He hadn't meant to make it sound as if he would sit there without complaint until somebody was done for the day. He folds it open enough to find the turn toward home.

There is a woman standing on the other side of the low wall. She wasn't there when they walked in. The top of the wall is high enough to hide her hands. He starts to go around it so he can see whether she's trying to come through the same gap he is.

His heel lifts before he means to move it.

The map comes out of his hand. He sees it catch once on the edge of the wall and open all the way. It rises past the woman's shoulder. The loose stones on the roadside rattle against each other. One passes his face.

He catches the top of the wall with both hands. The mortar moves under his stiff fingers. For a second he thinks the wall is falling on him. Then he sees a strip of grass tear free from the root and disappear into the sky.

“Try to keep your eyes open,” the woman says. ᶠᵈ

She is looking at his face instead of the grass. She tips her head toward the top of the wall and his coat pulls tight across the back.

He lets go with his right hand. He knows how to put himself down when he's been in the air a little too long. He tries it now, the same small correction he uses coming down beside the bed. His right boot goes back until the sole touches stone. For an instant he can put weight on it. Then the drop comes back, harder, and the boot scrapes up the wall like he's forgotten how to stand.

He reaches across for the road with the other grip, the one that doesn't need his fingers. Gravel stops moving in the ditch. He can feel exactly how much of it he's holding. His body keeps turning until one shoulder is higher than the wall. He tries to take hold of the stone itself. There's a second where he has to look at it to remember what it feels like.

He gets his fingers far enough along the wall to find the line where the top stone ends. If he can hold that, he can get his good foot against the earth and take the rest of his weight down in stages. He reaches the line. The top stone comes away in his hand.

He lets it go. It goes up past his face so quickly that it takes the edge of his sleeve with it.

*Rimuru. Outside the wall.* ᴹ

There had been a little change in the connection when Rimuru answered him before. He waits for it. He can't hear anything above the gravel on the road, and the gravel has stopped being on the road.

He reaches down with the grip and pulls hard enough that for a moment he has both feet on the earth. The woman turns to see him put one foot in front of the other.

“I can get the rest of the way from here,” he tells her. ᴹ

His second step goes sideways. The third is back toward the wall. He puts out a hand and finds the top again. He is on the same side of it and his shoulder is touching the place where the stone came off. It had taken three steps to go nowhere. He tries again with his eyes on the bench this time.

*Ciel, can you see where I'm standing?* ᴹ

He gets the beginning of an answer and a rush of cold in his stomach that has nothing to do with where his feet are.

He knows this drop. It is the half second when he can still reach somebody and knows he is already too late. He looks down, trying to find the road by its stones. The stones are there. He can see where he chipped the mortar with his hand. He can also see the edge of a bed that has never stood anywhere near this road.

He tries to shut his eyes. The bed is still there.

“You could go back and look,” the woman says. ᶠᵈ

The hand he has on the wall closes a little harder. He knows the bed. He knows who is about to fall asleep in it. He can hear what he promised her with so much clarity that for a second he can't remember whether he ever said it out loud again.

He tells himself to listen to the road.

A buckle slips on the strap of his coat. Somewhere over his head the map makes a sound like something small being torn in half. He doesn't hear a voice coming from the woman anymore. She has turned her head as if something has surprised her.

Mau can feel the moment the road stops being the thing he is trying to hold on to. He tries to say Yuka's name before he loses the air to do it. His fingers slip. The wall goes past his face and everything goes black.
