# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 20 — Let Us Hear It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.2
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S10E28
**Continuity state:** Day475 old night. The house chooses to see selected memories through Mau's present viewing work with Ciel and Rimuru. Temporary chosen images only. No memory rewriting, cure or shared combat archive. The gray and Yuka's color are visible as his remembered perception.

*When: Gojo asks whether he has to get another chair, nobody has remembered to make room for him.*

He has been standing behind Yuta with his arms folded and the blindfold pushed up. Mau hadn't heard him come down.

“There are chairs upstairs,” Gojo says. “I'm asking before I bring one which turns out to be an important part of the conversation.” ᴳᵒ

Shoko puts her foot under the empty chair beside Maki and slides it his way. Gojo takes it. Maki pulls another out for herself.

There are still people on the stairs. Rem has come down with a pitcher and forgotten to put it anywhere. Kaneki takes it from her and sets it on the table within reach of Shoko. He stays at the edge of the light.

“Do you want us to go?” he asks Mau. ᴷᵃ

Mau starts to tell him he doesn't have to be here for this. Kaneki looks at him, then at Rimuru.

“That wasn't what I asked.” ᴷᵃ

“I don't want to keep telling you things after everybody's gone back upstairs,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Then start where you can,” Kaneki says. ᴷᵃ

Mau looks around the room. He had spent so long remembering the people who would not know. He doesn't know how to explain what it is to find them here, angry and tired and listening.

Yuka has asked to know where she got to.

He wants her to see.

* * *

*Can I show them?* he asks Ciel. ᴹ

There is a space before she answers. He remembers that she was the one he kept saying he would spare while taking the choice out of her hands.

*I can help you recall something,* she says. *You need to tell them what it is before we send it.* ᶜ

*The house. A day at the house.* ᴹ

*Whose day?* ᶜ

He looks at Yuka.

*Mine.* ᴹ

He tells Rimuru what he wants to do. The view-sharing he knows how to hold now grew out of work Rimuru hasn't done yet. Mau has brought that work with him. He can offer a piece he is remembering through the thought-link between them and let Rimuru carry it to the people who want to watch.

Rimuru makes him try with the view of the table first.

For a second it is their table, seen from Mau's end. The pitcher is turned the other way. Gojo's cup is missing from the image because it was never there in the moment Mau looked at it. The image falls away when he turns to find Gojo's face.

“Keep looking at the thing you're remembering,” Rimuru says. “I can do the rest.” ᴿⁱ

Mau puts his hands flat on his knees.

“Some of it is private,” he tells the room. “I'll show you the house, if you want. I can stop.” ᴹ

Yuta asks what happens if somebody wants to stop seeing it. Rimuru says he can close that person's part while the rest stay. Rem sets her cup down and asks to see the kitchen. Maki says she wants to see it from the beginning of the day.

Frieren comes farther into the room. She takes the chair next to the wall, where she can still hear if Ori calls from upstairs.

* * *

The light changes.

There is a window above a bed and a blanket pulled crookedly over the person lying beside Mau. The room is gray. The blanket is gray. Yuka's hand, when it closes over the corner he wants, has color.

“I was here first,” she says in the memory.

The Yuka at the table puts her own hand down on her knee.

“I live here,” Mau answers.

He had said it without thinking. In the memory, his wife opens her eyes and gives half of the blanket back. She puts it under his chin.

Maki draws in a breath when she hears Yuka say *you do*.

Mau feels the room begin to slip. He can remember the whole morning. He could follow it all the way to the kiss before they left, could let it run because he wants them to understand and the people watching would have no way to decide where to look.

He stops at the blanket.

The kitchen comes next. Yuka is beside Mai, showing him which way somebody can reach for a latch when their hands are full. Mai has a shaving of wood on her sleeve. He can see the place where Yuka puts her hand on the drawing so he can follow the change she made.

Maki leans forward.

“That's Mai,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“She's still making things?” ᴹᵏ

“All sorts. Some of them for the house. She tells me what they cost, even when I tell her she doesn't have to.” ᴹ

Maki looks down. Her fingers close on the side of the chair, and for a second the wood makes a small sound.

In the image Mai says, *You do the shouting. I already did the door.*

Gojo starts to laugh. Shoko gets a hand over her mouth. Maki keeps looking at her sister, then laughs once, painfully, without taking her eyes off her.

“Keep that part,” she says. “A little longer.” ᴹᵏ

Mau keeps it.

* * *

When he reaches the yard, there are too many paths.

Fern is the first one to ask which is the way to dinner. She has come down while the image was on the kitchen. Nobody had seen her find a place beside Rem.

Mau follows the path he took with the empty plates. There is a little rise and a roof that doesn't belong to the old house. He can hear somebody practising guitar far enough away that the sound comes through an open window.

Kaneki asks him to go slower at the turn. He wants to see the faces at the far table. Mau remembers enough of them to give him the moment. He tells him how some people arrived and how others were found.

Rem sees herself pass through the edge of the view with Umaru talking beside her. The two of them are arguing over which cup belongs on the little tray. Rem's hand is at Umaru's elbow. She takes it away to pick up the cup, and Umaru reaches back for it without interrupting what she's saying.

At the table, Rem touches her own wrist. She doesn't ask a question. Mau gives her time to look.

The next sound is Ori calling from the other side of the yard.

Frieren sits very straight.

* * *

It hurts differently to hear Ori in the image.

She is the same girl and has grown into mornings this house has never had. Mau remembers turning to look for her because she'd said his name. In the view he gives the others, the whole world goes gray around her and his wife passes behind her shoulder in color.

“Why can we see her like that?” Shoko asks. ˢʰ

“That's how I see now,” Mau says. “Everything else is gray.” ᴹ

Yuka turns to him.

“Since when?” ʸ

“When I came back with both lives. I thought it would change again. It didn't.” ᴹ

She looks at the image of herself crossing a yard she has never stood in.

Mau wants to explain that he knows how it sounds. He wants to put a reason in front of it and stop her thinking he chose the world she can see in his eyes.

He doesn't know the reason.

“I don't know why,” he says. ᴹ

Frieren asks him to leave the image where Ori is standing. She has something silver in both hands. The compass catches the light, gray and familiar.

“It's hers?” Frieren asks. ᶠ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Frieren looks at him for a second, and then back.

“She can still find me,” she says. ᶠ

Mau tries to answer. His throat closes before he gets the first word out.

Ori in the image turns the compass and tells him where her mother is. She is delighted to be right. Frieren can hear that even before she sees the girl smile.
