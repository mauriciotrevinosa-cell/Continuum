# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 20 — The First Place She Would Show Him

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S10E29
**Continuity state:** Day475 late night. Original Ori asks Mau for a ride and leaves a message for her later self. No claim of fatherhood in present life.

*When: Ori unfolds the paper, there is a thumbprint in the middle of the part she wants him to read.*

She had wanted to go up with Kaisel. Not a long flight. She shows him how short with her fingers, and then opens them a little farther because what she means is that she doesn't want to go anywhere she can't still point to the house.

“Did Kaisel tell you all this?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“I asked Beru to ask him. Then I asked him myself because Beru wanted to make it a procession.” ᴼ

“What did Kaisel say?” ᴹ

“He let me put my hand on his face.” ᴼ

Ori closes the paper over the thumbprint. On the other side she has drawn part of a roof, with an arrow that starts at the door and goes up to a spot above the tree.

“I wanted to show you the place you can see Mum's window and mine at the same time.” ᴼ

Mau looks at the drawing. In the town there is a path below his window that Ori could follow to find both of them. She knows the way without having to look up. He has never asked what it looks like from above.

“We can't go up tonight,” Ori says. “I know. I was only going to ask when you came back.” ᴼ

He slides down the wall until he can sit without having to look so far down at her. She looks at Rimuru and he takes a few steps toward the stairs, where he can watch the passage without being in it.

“Do you know what time you were going to ask me?” Mau says. ᴹ

“Tomorrow. After Mum had breakfast. I didn't want to ask about leaving if she hadn't come down yet.” ᴼ

Mau remembers thinking she would have tomorrow. He remembers thinking it so clearly that he doesn't know how he ever moved past it to the thought that it didn't matter what tomorrow was like.

“I'm sorry I wasn't there for that,” he says. ᴹ

Ori looks at him with the crease between her eyebrows that means she thinks there is a mistake she could correct if he would only let her finish.

“You're here now.” ᴼ

He gives her the paper back and tells her what he can. He tells her there is a place where they go up on the dragon together and he has to let her decide when they turn. He tells her that she used that privilege to make him fly in a circle twice. She asks if she can still find her mother without getting on anything. Mau says she's much better at it than he is.

“Do I come to your house if I want you?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“Yes. Sometimes you bring everybody.” ᴹ

“Do I have to?” ᴼ

“No. You can come by yourself.” ᴹ

She thinks about that. Mau keeps his hands on his knees. She lays one of hers against the paper, flat.

“Can you ask me to show you that place?” she says. “I know I went up already. Maybe I didn't show you.” ᴼ

“I can ask.” ᴹ

She turns the paper over. There's a little blank space underneath the arrow. She takes a short pencil out of her pocket and writes in it with her hand hiding the words. Then she folds the drawing all the way into itself.

“You can tell me it's mine,” she says, handing it to him. “I can decide when to look.” ᴼ

He puts the paper in his inside pocket. Ori watches him until she sees he's got it all the way in.

“Dad?” she says. ᴼ

He can answer without speaking. He doesn't have to reach for her. She has already come in close enough to put the side of her face against his shoulder.

After a little while she says her mother was awake when she went to see whether there was a light under the door.

“I wanted to tell her about the dragon too,” she says. ᴼ

“Would you like me to come with you?” ᴹ

She thinks about it, then puts her hand in his so he can stand.
