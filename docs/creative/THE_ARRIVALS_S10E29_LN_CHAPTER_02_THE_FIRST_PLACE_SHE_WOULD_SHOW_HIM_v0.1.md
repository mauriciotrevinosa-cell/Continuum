# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 22 — The First Place She Would Show Him

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.2
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S10E29
**Continuity state:** Day475 old night. Original Ori sees a selected memory of the later ride, asks how she knows Mau there, says goodbye as his daughter here and gives her unopened folded message for her later self. Current Ori retains her own bond and words.

*When: Ori puts the paper in Mau's hand, he sees that she has drawn the house from the wrong side of the tree.*

She points to the roof before he can say anything.

“I know you can't see both those windows from the ground,” she says. “That's why I asked him.” ᴼ

“Who?” ᴹ

“Kaisel. Beru first, but he wanted to make it a procession.” ᴼ

She pulls the paper back far enough to show him the little shape at the corner. The first dragon has a great many people standing in front of it. She has crossed them all out. The second has room for the people she actually wants.

Mau sits on the landing. He starts to bring his knee up, remembers how little space that leaves for her to sit and moves his feet down a step instead.

Ori sits beside him.

Rimuru goes far enough down the stairs to give them the landing. He stays where the light from the common room will show Mau where to put his foot if he stands up.

“What did Kaisel say?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“He let me put my hand on his face.” ᴼ

“That sounds like him.” ᴹ

“He was being very still so I could finish asking.” ᴼ

Mau looks at the two small people on the second dragon.

One of them has long hair. One has a mark at his throat. Ori puts a finger on the other side of the page.

“Mum can watch if she doesn't want to go up. I wanted to show you where you can see my window and hers together.” ᴼ

He remembers the town from above. The turn where the later Ori will point at her own window and then find his.

“Were you going to ask me tomorrow?” he says. ᴹ

“After breakfast. Mum was going to try coming down.” ᴼ

She says it plainly, as another thing she'd thought about when she made the plan. Mau looks at the thumbprint on the drawing and has to put his free hand over his mouth.

* * *

Ori waits for him to look at the paper again.

“Mum said you came from a long way after now,” she says. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Do I get bigger?” ᴼ

“A little.” ᴹ

“How much?” ᴼ

He holds his hand above her head. She stretches up to reach it.

“Like that?” ᴼ

“Something like that.” ᴹ

“Can I see?” ᴼ

He tells her he can show her a small thing he remembers. She chooses the flight. He doesn't give her the whole view that was on the table downstairs. He gives her the piece where she points out the paths from above and Kaisel turns so she can finish counting the windows.

Ori watches herself.

Her mouth opens a little. She had thought of the size of the tree, the little people at the bottom and the place her mother would stand. She hadn't thought of the sound she'd make the first time she got high enough to see all of them.

“That was me,” she says. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Was I scared?” ᴼ

“For a second. Then you wanted to go around again.” ᴹ

Ori watches herself ask for the turn. She puts her fingers flat against the drawing. The image ends with Kaisel banking gently past the place she wants to see.

“Why didn't I show you Mum's window?” she asks. ᴼ

“We were doing something else that day.” ᴹ

“Then you can ask me.” ᴼ

“I can.” ᴹ

She looks at him. She's been working out the next question while he answers the easier ones.

“Mum said you're going home to Yuka,” she says. “Where do I go?” ᴼ

Mau has held dying people without needing to know what to do with his hands afterward. He cannot work out where to put them now.

“You live with your mum,” he says. “You come over when you want me.” ᴹ

“Do I know you're my dad?” ᴼ

The floor seems to tip.

Mau looks straight at her. She is waiting for an answer she can use. He can give her one, even if it takes him a moment to get it out.

“You know me a different way there. You know I love you.” ᴹ

Ori looks at the two windows on the paper.

“I call you Mau?” she asks. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She says his name once, quietly, as if she's trying out how it would feel to use it when she wants him to look around.

He puts his head down.

* * *

Ori moves closer until her knee presses against his.

“I'll know where to find you,” she says. ᴼ

Mau takes a breath. It shakes so badly that she turns toward the stairs to see whether Rimuru has heard.

He has. He stays where he is.

“Does she come by herself?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“Sometimes. Sometimes she comes with everybody. She found my window before I thought to ask if she knew which one it was.” ᴹ

Ori looks satisfied for the first time since she asked about him being her father.

“Then she'll show you,” she says. “Ask her about this bit.” ᴼ

She draws a line from the place above the tree to the side of the roof where she thinks the two windows will be clear. Mau knows he will remember that line even if the paper doesn't come with him.

“I will,” he says. ᴹ

He tries to ask whether there is anything else she wants him to tell the girl who lives with Frieren in the town. The question gets caught halfway through. Ori hands him the drawing so he has something to hold.

Then she takes it back.

“Wait. I have to write it.” ᴼ

He gets the little pencil from the stair where it rolled when she sat down. He puts it into her hand.

She writes with her forearm covering the words. He can hear the scratch and the harder press when she goes back over one letter to make it clear. He looks at the other wall until she is done.

She folds it three times.

“It's for me,” she says. “You can tell me that. I can decide when I want to look.” ᴼ

Mau puts it in his inside pocket. He makes sure she sees it go all the way in.

“Safe,” he says. ᴹ

Ori touches the pocket once with the tips of her fingers.

* * *

“Dad?” ᴼ

He looks up.

She stands so she can put her arms around his neck without having to climb over his knees. He catches her with both arms and makes a sound he was trying to keep inside.

Ori holds tighter.

He has been careful for years with the place between what he remembers and what the girl in front of him knows. Here she knows. She comes into his arms because it is where she has come a hundred times, tired or angry or pleased with something she wants him to see.

He can feel the little pressure of her hands through his shirt.

He had forgotten how small she was when she did this.

“I love you,” he says into her hair. ᴹ

“I know,” she says. ᴼ

He laughs once, with his face still pressed against the top of her head. It hurts all the way down. She laughs a little too, because she recognizes something in it, then goes quiet and lets him hold her.

When she pulls back, she puts both hands on his shoulders so he has to look at her.

“Tell her about us,” she says. “The nice things too.” ᴼ

“I will.” ᴹ

“And ask her to show you.” ᴼ

“I will.” ᴹ

Ori wipes a wet place on his cheek with her sleeve. She inspects the result, then does the other side. He sits still for both.

“Mum's waiting,” she says. ᴼ

She puts her hand out for him. Mau takes it and stands slowly enough that she can stay beside him.

At the end of the landing, Frieren has left her door open.
