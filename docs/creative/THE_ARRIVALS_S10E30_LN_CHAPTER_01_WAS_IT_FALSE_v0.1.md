# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 23 — Was It False

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.2
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S10E30
**Continuity state:** Day475 old night. Frieren sees her later self with Ori at the wedding meal, takes responsibility for the harm here and asks whether their original love was real. Mau distinguishes this lived past from the current household, no transferred blame or automatic forgiveness.

*When: Frieren lets them into the room, Ori goes straight to her and puts both hands around one of hers.*

“I gave it to him,” she says. ᴼ

Frieren looks at Mau's pocket. He puts his hand over the fold so she can see where it is.

“Did you tell him what he has to do with it?” Frieren asks. ᶠ

“Yes. Ask me.” ᴼ

“Then he knows.” ᶠ

Ori stays beside her for a while, leaning on the arm she is holding. Frieren runs her other hand over the top of her head. She isn't looking down at her. She's looking at the man in the doorway, at the red around his eyes and the place where his shirt is damp from his daughter's face.

Rimuru has come to the landing with a cup of warm water. Ori asks if she can have the first sip. Frieren lets go of her hand slowly enough that Ori can decide when to leave.

At the door Ori looks back once.

Mau raises his hand.

She raises hers and goes with Rimuru.

* * *

There is a book on the chair.

Frieren takes it off and sets it on the bed. She has been holding her place with a strip of cloth. It falls out when she moves it. She watches it land and then sits down beside it instead of picking it up.

Mau takes the chair.

“You were looking for your house,” she says. ᶠ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Out there.” ᶠ

He looks toward the window. From here he can see the top of the tree and a piece of the old roof.

“I kept thinking it might be behind something. If I found the right turn, I'd see the window.” ᴹ

Frieren folds her hands. The skin at the knuckles has gone pale.

“What does my window look like?” she asks. ᶠ

He starts with the shape. He can do that part without losing his voice. The room, the little path and the way Ori goes from one door to the other. Then the books, some in places the woman in front of him would have chosen and some where her daughter has made a convincing argument for keeping them.

Frieren asks which books. He can remember one grimoire by its damaged binding and the ordinary story Ori has been using to test whether something counts as a lesson.

“Does it?” Frieren asks. ᶠ

“Depends on what she says she learned.” ᴹ

Frieren's mouth moves. The little smile costs her something. She keeps it long enough that he can see it.

“Sometimes I come in and you're smiling at a page,” he says. “I ask if you want to show me and you say no. Then you go back to it. You look like you have all the time in the world to read that book.” ᴹ

She looks toward the one she has left on the bed.

* * *

“Can I see the part after your wedding?” she asks. ᶠ

Mau puts both hands on his knees.

He finds the moment from the meal afterward. Ori is telling Frieren something with such seriousness that she keeps going back to the start when Mau looks over. His wife is beside him. The view settles on Frieren while she listens.

There she is, beyond everything this room has held. Her daughter is pleased with something she wants to explain. Frieren smiles when she turns to check a detail. She has a plate in front of her and hasn't remembered to eat.

The woman on the bed leans toward the image.

“That's me,” she says. ᶠ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She watches the little turn of her head. A thousand things could have meant that turn to a woman who had lived as long as she had. It means her daughter has called her and she has heard.

When the image ends, Frieren takes the strip of cloth off the bed and holds it between her fingers.

“I thought I'd have to know exactly what I lost before I could stop losing it,” she says. ᶠ

Mau looks at her.

“When you showed them the wedding, I kept looking for myself. I thought I would be able to tell from my face.” ᶠ

“What?” ᴹ

“Whether I was pretending.” ᶠ

She looks down at the cloth.

“I wasn't. I could see it.” ᶠ

* * *

Mau remembers the garden.

He has remembered it from so many directions that there are places in it he can see even when his own eyes never stood there. The space between Frieren and Yuka. The moment in which someone could still have stopped. The thing she chose to do.

Frieren looks up as if she can see where his memory has gone.

“I wanted to hurt her,” she says. “I had time to stop and I didn't.” ᶠ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Is that in her life now?” ᶠ

“No. It happened here.” ᴹ

“Did she have to forgive me before she could be happy?” ᶠ

He makes himself answer the question she asked.

“She didn't have that to forgive there.” ᴹ

Frieren nods once. She keeps looking at him.

“You did.” ᶠ

Mau lets his breath out. He has been carrying an answer that belongs to a woman who doesn't remember asking it. Here he can put it in front of the one who does.

“I remember what you did. And I remember what I did tonight. I wanted to make it so nobody could tell me how much it hurt.” ᴹ

“I hurt you before you chose that.” ᶠ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She nods again. The cloth has twisted tight enough between her fingers to look like cord.

“I want you to be able to keep the good things too,” he says. “I don't want the last thing we were to each other to eat all the years before it.” ᴹ

Frieren's eyes fill. She takes one breath and then another, as if there is a thought she cannot say until she finds enough room.

* * *

“Was it false?” she asks. ᶠ

Mau had heard the question coming in every other one she asked. He still needs her to ask it plainly.

“What we felt,” she says. “If a life like that could grow out of one where it never happened—” ᶠ

She stops. Her eyes have gone to the chair, to the place where his hand used to rest when he'd sit beside her and try to look as if he hadn't come up just to be there.

“Was I loving something that wasn't there?” ᶠ

He shakes his head. He gets up because if he stays across the room she will have time to think he is looking for a careful answer.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

He takes the place on the floor beside the bed where she can see his face without turning away from the window.

“I was there.” ᴹ

Frieren looks at him. The cloth loosens between her hands.

“Tell me,” she says. ᶠ
