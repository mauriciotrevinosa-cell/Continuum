# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 63
## Chapter 22 — There Is a Place to Put Your Foot

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 63
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S10E30
**Continuity state:** Day475 late night to return threshold. Original Frieren hears Mau's goodbye and freely helps him leave for his current life.

*When: Mau thinks of an answer, it comes with the sound of rain against a window nobody had remembered to close.*

“No,” he says. “I loved you when you gave me that compass. I loved you the morning you complained I'd left it pointing at you on the table. I left it there because I liked to see it do that.” ᴹ

“You said you'd forgotten it.” ᶠ

“I was embarrassed.” ᴹ

She looks at him. Her mouth changes first. He hadn't planned on making her laugh. She puts her hand on the bed beside her and looks at the gap where the door is still open.

“It was beautiful,” he says. “Somebody found out I'd do anything to keep it. After a while it felt like the world only had to say your name to get me to walk into whatever it wanted. And I kept doing it.” ᴹ

Frieren holds her hand very still.

“I thought if you were happy somewhere else, it meant I hadn't given you enough to stay,” she says. ᶠ

“You gave me years I still love. I'm sorry I kept taking what happened to those years out on us. When I couldn't fix it I tried to take everybody's choice away. You should have had a chance to tell me what you wanted before I did that.” ᴹ

Frieren wipes the side of her face with her sleeve. Mau remembers the first time he noticed she did it that way. He had offered her a handkerchief and she'd kept it for weeks without using it.

“Do you love me now?” she asks. ᶠ

He looks at her. He knows what it would cost both of them to make her guess which part of the truth he means.

“I love you for the life we had. I'm in love with my wife. I can say both of those to you and mean them.” ᴹ

Frieren lowers her hand. There is a damp line at the edge of her sleeve.

“Tell me one good thing about her,” she says. “The other me.” ᶠ

Mau remembers Frieren sitting across from Ori and letting her draw a lesson plan with enough room for a nap. It would be easy to tell her about her daughter again. He thinks for another minute.

“Sometimes I come in and you're smiling at something you're reading. I ask if you want to show me and you say no. Then you go back to it. You look like you have all the time in the world to read that page.” ᴹ

Frieren takes that in quietly. Then she asks if he can help her keep enough time to do it. He says yes. She asks if he could do the same thing for himself when he gets there.

* * *

Something small hits the other side of the window.

Mau looks up. For a second he sees a piece of torn paper held against a low wall. When he looks straight at it, he can see the dark square of glass again.

He had been holding on so hard when he fell. It hadn't occurred to him that the road might still be beneath him.

“There it is again,” he says. ᴹ

Frieren stands. She comes as far as the door, but doesn't move to hold it shut.

“What is?” ᶠ

“The place I was standing when I fell.” ᴹ

He sees a line in the mortar that wasn't there before he caught the wall. He puts one hand over his pocket. He can feel Ori's folded paper. Behind him there is still a bed where Frieren was sitting. He wants more time with the people on this side of it. He has had enough time to ask what they want, and they have told him.

Frieren reaches out and puts her palm flat against the back of his shoulder. He can tell from where she puts her feet that she's making sure he can take a step without her coming with him.

“Thank you for telling me,” she says. “Go to your life, Mau.” ᶠ

He feels the pressure of her hand once. The small push is enough to remind him that he can move his foot before he can see the ground all the way to where it lands.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

He takes the step. The floor leaves his sight. This time he has somewhere to put his weight.
