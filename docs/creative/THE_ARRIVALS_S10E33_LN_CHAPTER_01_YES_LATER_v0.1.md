# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 64
## Chapter 4 — Yes, Later

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 64
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S10E33
**Continuity state:** B+171 evening. Mau tells Frieren about the old visit and asks before offering Ori the paper. Current Ori elects to receive the message later and leaves it folded.

*When: Mau tells Frieren that Ori found both windows, Frieren asks whether it was before or after she put a light in hers.*

“Before,” he says. ᴹ

“Then she really does know which one it is.” ᶠ

Frieren is keeping her finger between two pages. She lifts her other hand to turn the book face down. Mau can still see the finger in it.

He tells her why he asked Ori about the flight. He tells her that the first Ori wanted to show him the same thing when there was only the old roof to look at. At the part about the paper, he takes it out and puts it where Frieren could touch it if she wanted to.

“Has anybody read it?” she asks. ᶠ

“No. I watched her write a bit of it with her hand over the paper. She told me Ori could decide when she looked.” ᴹ

Frieren keeps looking at the fold. She doesn't move her hand off the book.

“What did I say?” she asks after a while. ᶠ

Mau hadn't been planning to ask her to listen to that part. He tells her what he can say without asking her to answer for the woman he spoke to. He tells her about the book on the chair and the thing he told her about having time to read a page.

Frieren looks down. She loosens the hand holding her place in the book. The pages come apart on their own and she finds the place again.

“I think I'd have wanted to hear that too,” she says. ᶠ

Mau lets her look for as long as she likes.

“Is it all right if I offer Ori the paper?” ᴹ

“I think you can tell her you have it. Whether she reads it is her decision.” ᶠ

He folds his hand around the paper and puts it back in his pocket.

“Will you be there if she asks for you?” ᴹ

“Of course.” ᶠ

* * *

He finds Ori on the steps with a pencil across both knees. She has been trying to draw the turn they made around the house. Mau tells her she can ask him to hold the corner of the page down.

She lets him. She redraws the turn a little wider.

When he tells her about the folded paper, she puts the pencil down where it can't roll off. She asks if there's something inside it that he thinks she has to hear.

“No. You can ask me for it whenever you want. You can leave it alone too.” ᴹ

Ori looks at his pocket, then at the drawing between them.

“Yes,” she says. “I want it. But not now.” ᴼ

Mau nods. She makes sure he has understood that she means she'll ask for it later. He promises to keep it folded until then. He doesn't reach for it.

Ori picks her pencil back up and puts a small mark beside the line she's drawn around the house.

“Which window's that?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Yours. I want to remember where we were when I saw it.” ᴼ

He holds the corner until she's done.
