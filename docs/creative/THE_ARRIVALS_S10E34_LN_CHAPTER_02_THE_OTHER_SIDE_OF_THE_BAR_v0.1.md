# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 64
## Chapter 7 — The Other Side of the Bar

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 64
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S10E34
**Continuity state:** B+172 afternoon. Hollis is detained on the unprotected side of the crossing under the king's order and injured. A witness gets the facts back to the house.

*When: Hollis steps round the end of the counter, the man waiting outside lowers the bar in front of him.*

Hollis steps back before the bar finishes coming down. The man who lowered it says the king's name. Hollis has heard it on enough receipts to recognise it. This is the first time somebody has said it to him with a hand on a sword.

He asks the man if he's come with a letter. The man has one. He wants Hollis to read it without holding it. Hollis reads what he can from where he stands. There is an order to sign for responsibility for people trading with a false mark. He can make out his own name, added close enough to the rest of the words that the letters nearly touch.

“I can give you the names of the people I've sent this week,” Hollis says. “I'll have somebody come and copy them so we both know exactly what you have.” ᴴᵒ

“You can tell the king you're refusing when you see him.” ᴳʳ

“May I go back and tell the woman inside who's taking me?” ᴴᵒ

The man looks past him. The woman who keeps the tally has stood up behind the counter. She puts the heel of her hand against the little door so it won't swing shut.

“Don't bother her,” Hollis says. “She hasn't come outside.” ᴴᵒ

The other guard takes his wrist. Hollis tries to turn so he can still face the woman at the counter. The guard twists it harder than he has to and Hollis stops turning. The guard who spoke watches him do it. Then he folds the order and tells the other man to get Hollis on the wagon.

Hollis had come out to give the driver the key to a crate. It is still in his other hand. He puts it on the counter before the guard pulls him out of reach.

“Check the other side of the tally before you close,” he tells the woman. ᴴᵒ

She keeps her hand against the door until the wagon leaves.

* * *

There is only one sentence on the other side of the tally. Hollis wrote it the morning they opened, after the woman asked what she was supposed to do if somebody tried to make a purchase without using the counter. It tells her where to send for somebody who can take over.

She does that first. Then she writes which man read the order and which took Hollis by the wrist. She has to stop and read the last line twice before it says what she actually saw. He tried to turn around. The guard hurt him until he stopped.

The driver whose crate is still on the road gives her a clean piece of wrapping paper to go around the note. He says he'll take it if she tells him which door to go to. Then he asks if she would rather go there herself.

She looks at the little door she's been holding all this time. She puts the key in the driver's hand and tells him which crate it opens. She takes the note herself.
