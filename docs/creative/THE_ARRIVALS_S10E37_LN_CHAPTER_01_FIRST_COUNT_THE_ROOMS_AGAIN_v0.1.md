# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 64
## Chapter 12 — First, Count the Rooms Again

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 64
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S10E37
**Continuity state:** B+173 night. House and shadows dismantle the empty castle and store it in pieces. Independent civilian houses, well, and routes stay intact.

*When: Fern sends the signal, Mau tells the shadows to count the rooms again.*

Igris puts one group on the stairs and one on the wall above the well. From below, the people who came out can see the shadows through the windows. Someone asks if they're searching for anything. Meret says they have already sent back a man's work boots and somebody's supper wrapped in a cloth, so she thinks they are.

The room under the stairs is the last one Igris reports. Nothing there but a broken shelf. Mau asks him to take the shelf before they start. The person who comes to get it can decide if it is still worth fixing.

The castle has to come apart in an order the people living below it can survive. Mau begins where the roof comes clear of the adjoining house. Shadows take the tiles down in stacks and send them along the wall to him. He takes each stack into inventory, keeping the tiles from each slope apart so Ciel can tell him which ones belong with the next load of timber. The work goes quicker once the braces are in. It still takes the time it takes to pick up every piece.

Halfway along the roof Mau stops being able to stand with his weight even. He could keep standing. He looks down and realizes his right foot has been doing all the work for the last three stacks. Igris brings him the chair from the gatehouse. Mau sits in it while the next load is brought over. He asks for the stack to be set down on his good side. There's no point making Igris pick it up twice.

From the low street there is one dreadful moment when a wall goes away and the people below can see the sky where the windows used to be. Nobody talks through it. The line of shadows goes on passing the stones along. The room next to the wall is Meret's neighbor's. His own wall is still there. He can see it only after the dust settles.

The neighbor goes partway up his steps. Then he stops. He calls down to Meret to ask whether there was always a line in the plaster above his door. She looks up from where she's standing with the basket. She remembers he put a nail through it last winter. She can't see the nail from here.

Mau signals Igris to hold the next course. One of the shadows sets his stone down so carefully that the neighbor can hear it through the open window. Mau asks the man if he can go up beside him. The man says yes, and for a while they both look at the mark above the door.

The old nail is still in it. Mau shines a little light where the man points so he can see how much of the plaster it took out. Then he moves the light along the wall toward the brace. At the end he asks if the man wants to go down while they finish.

“I'll stand here,” the man says.

Mau waits beside him until Igris gets to the brace. He watches the man watching it. When they are sure the wall is holding, he leaves the light over the man's doorstep and goes back to take the stone.

* * *

Yuka waits by the well until the last part of the gatehouse is gone. She has put one hand on the coping where she can feel if something beneath her shifts. There's been a little grit on the water since the first roof came down. When the work stops she draws a bucket and sets it aside for the dust to settle, then draws another for the people still using the steps.

Mau comes down when he has made sure the last group is out of the ditch where the castle drain used to be. He has mortar on his sleeve. Yuka brushes it off while he tells her they left the small stone steps on the outside because there are two doors nobody can reach without them.

“Are they steady?” she asks. ʸ

“Igris checked both.” ᴹ

Mau looks back up. From here he can see across the ground where the castle stood to a light in one of the houses on the far side. The person who put it there goes past the window and back again, and the light stays on.

He has brought down a board taken from the empty entry hall. Hollis helped him write what is on it. Fern checks that there is still a route to the schoolhouse and chooses a place for the board that doesn't block the steps.

The writing is large enough for Edran to read from the road.

*You gave the order to take Hollis. You renewed it after he came home. You started this. I kept my word. When you apologize for what you did, I will return what I took.*

Mau sets the board where Fern points. Then he goes back down to help Yuka carry the water.
