# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 64
## Chapter 13 — Bread Before the Story

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 64
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S10E37
**Continuity state:** B+174 morning. Vey residents assess aftermath. No deaths in the dismantling, civic services working and restitution open. Hollis reopens the crossing on his terms.

*When: the first man comes down the hill to tell Meret what happened to the castle, she has a loaf in the new oven and asks him to wait until she has turned it.*

He waits. The door is heavier than the old one. She has to use her left hand to hold it open far enough to get the peel in. On the way back out she can let go. The door closes without her having to put a weight against it.

The man tells her what he saw from the road. The stones were there when he came up and gone when he got to the bend. He says there are people saying the king is in the same place the stones went.

“The king walked down past me with his clerk,” Meret says. “They asked me which way to the lower road.” ᴹᵉ

“Do you know where they went?” he asks.

“I know which way they went when they left me.” ᴹᵉ

She gives him a piece from a loaf she'd cut earlier. He holds it while he asks if he can go to the well himself. He has been carrying two empty jugs since he came down the hill. He puts the bread in his mouth and picks them up before Meret can answer.

* * *

There's a child waiting at the well. She has a shirt tucked under her arm and wants to know if the grain shed still opens at the same hour. The woman who has the key tells her yes. The child looks up the hill as if she's going to ask something else, then brings the shirt out from under her arm and folds it a different way.

The man draws enough water to fill both jugs. He stays for a moment after he has the second one corked. He can see the sky all the way down to the low wall where there used to be a tower, even with his feet still on the first step. He's lived below that tower all his life. It's going to take him more than one morning to stop looking for it.

He carries one jug to the schoolroom first. The woman on the mat by the door tells him she wants it where she can reach it herself. He puts it where she points. She asks if anybody has seen her blue bowl. He says he'll ask at the storehouse on his way down.

By midmorning two letters leave Vey. One says there is a hill with nothing on it and that nobody knows how many people were killed. The other has a list of the rooms the people from the castle are sleeping in, and an account of how many of them asked for an extra blanket. Meret sends a loaf along with the second one.

* * *

At Ashby the pear seller hears that the man from the quarry ate the king.

He puts the basket down harder than he meant to. Three pears roll toward the edge and his grandson catches two. The other one lands underneath the barrel. Neither of them goes after it.

“Was it the one with the white in his hair?” the boy asks. ˣ

The man doesn't know. The customer who brought the story is already halfway down the row, telling it to the woman who sells onions. She starts to turn her slate around. The man remembers what the end of the row sounded like the morning the flute stopped. He could hear the girl set down each pear.

“I know what she paid,” he says. “Go get the one that fell.” ᴹⁿ

The boy gets down on his knees just as a driver comes up from the bridge with a note for the market. The driver reads the names of the people he saw at the schoolroom. There's a woman from the laundry and a girl who baked bread in a new oven this morning. The pear seller knows the woman's sister. He asks if the driver actually saw her. The driver says she gave him the note.

“Did you see the king?” the seller asks. ᴹⁿ

“No. I was delivering bread.” ˣ

The seller can make use of that answer. He puts his own slate back where it was, with the price showing. Across the row somebody has drawn a fresh wheel above the old one. He leaves it alone. When his grandson brings the bruised pear up from under the barrel, the seller gives him the knife to cut out the bad part.

* * *

Hollis reads the second one at the crossing. The tally keeper comes over to see if he wants a hand with the shutter. He says yes. She puts the key down where both of them can find it later. For today all deliveries will be taken at the inner window. She writes it above the place for his name and he signs with his good hand braced against the bottom.

Mau has come as far as the crossing with him. He is still standing behind the line Hollis usually asks customers to stand behind.

“Do you want me to stay?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Hollis looks down the road. There's a wagon coming with two small barrels tied down close to the front. He doesn't know the driver yet.

“Come back at lunch,” Hollis says. “I want you to try the bread from her new oven.” ᴴᵒ

Mau stays long enough to see him lift the shutter. Then he goes home to see if Yuka wants to come back with him.
