# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 64
## Chapter 26 — A Third Way to End It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S10 v0.1
**Volume:** 64
**Reading order:** 26
**Source episode:** S10E44
**Continuity state:** B+176 night. Each Beru remains with his king. The two compare and combine notes over a drama. Season ends at the house before any proposal.

*When: Jin-Woo comes back to the common room, Beru is standing by his chair with the look of somebody who has saved up a very important question.*

Mau's Beru is standing by Mau's chair with exactly the same look.

“My king,” they say together. ᴮ

Both kings look up. Both Beru wait for the other one to finish.

Yuka sets her cup down. “Well, that settles which one is which.” ʸ

Mau looks at Jin-Woo. Jin-Woo has his hand over his face already.

“Is yours always that dramatic?” Mau asks him. ᴹ

“Depends what he's watching,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

Mau is about to ask what he means when Jin-Woo's Beru explains that there are stories where you get to see what people do after they've said a thing, and then what the other people in the house do when they hear it, and sometimes it takes an entire evening to find out who heard it wrong. He calls them telenovelas. He says this with the patience of somebody who has had to explain why an evening is worth spending on one before.

Mau's Beru thinks for a moment.

“Do they go back and say it correctly?” he asks. ᴮᵐ

“Eventually,” says Jin-Woo's Beru. ᴮʲ

Jin-Woo takes his hand off his face so he can drink something. He asks if they'd like to let everybody finish supper before discussing how long it takes to say a sentence.

“My king. Of course.” His Beru draws back at once. Then he leans across the table, very discreetly, toward the other Beru. “Take notes. It's important.” ᴮʲ

* * *

They find paper. Yuka gives them a pencil each. It takes about as long as it would take to ask one question for both Beru to use up an entire side.

Jin-Woo's Beru wants to know what Mau's Beru would have done with a letter that arrives after everybody has gone out to look for the person who wrote it. Mau's Beru thinks somebody should open the letter, see what it says and find the person who needs it. Jin-Woo's Beru puts a line through something he'd written about rain.

“Let them find her before it rains,” he says. “I'll give you that.” ᴮʲ

Mau's Beru looks pleased. Then he looks down at the other paper, which has an arrow leading from the letter to the aunt who has been waiting outside for most of the story.

“I would keep the aunt,” he says. “She has waited a very long time.” ᴮᵐ

Jin-Woo's Beru straightens up. “You see it.” ᴮʲ

“I do.” ᴮᵐ

They put a mark on each other's page. Mau thinks this might mean they're finished. He reaches for the jam.

“Or,” says his Beru. ᴮᵐ

Jin-Woo stops with his cup in his hand. Across the table, both Beru turn their pages over at the same time.

“What if the aunt wrote it?” Mau's Beru asks. ᴮᵐ

The other one looks at him. Then he starts making notes so quickly that the table moves a little.

* * *

Jin-Woo turns to Mau. Neither of them has to say anything. Mau can see the exact moment Jin-Woo works out that there are now two of them to come up with another ending.

“I'm sorry,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

Mau laughs. He tries to put the jam down before he does it again. Yuka takes the jar out of his hand and leaves her shoulder where he can lean against it.

At the far end of the table somebody asks if there is room for the aunt to have her own story. Both Beru look up. Their kings look at each other again.

Mau moves his chair over and makes room.
