# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 1 — The Morning Guest

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S11E01
**Continuity state:** B+177 morning, following V64#26. Jin-Woo and his own Beru are overnight guests. No family arrival, cross-world reopening or new acquisition. Mau's old left-hand/vision sequelae and gray except Yuka persist. The household's established shadow watch continues.

*When: Yuka asks whether their guest has had breakfast, Mau has been awake for almost three minutes.*

“You could start with good morning.” ᴹ

“Good morning. Has he?” ʸ

“I've been here.” ᴹ

She lifts her head from his shoulder to verify this. Her hair has caught under his arm. He frees it carefully, then lets her put her head back where it was.

“That is your strongest argument.” ʸ

“I have several.” ᴹ

“Tell me another.” ʸ

He kisses her forehead. She accepts this as an argument worth considering and gives him another minute.

He spends most of it looking at her. The light has reached the edge of the bed, making a pale shape across his sleeve. Her fingers, curled against it, have color. When she moves them to his face, he catches her hand and kisses those too.

“You're doing it again.” ʸ

“What?” ᴹ

“Trying to make me forget we were getting up.” ʸ

“Is it working?” ᴹ

She considers lying. He can see her consider it.

“A little.” ʸ

They stay until a knock comes from the next room. Rimuru asks whether either of them has seen his other boot. Yuka tells him where he left it. Mau closes his eyes.

“You knew?” ᴹ

“He put it there yesterday.” ʸ

“You could have told him before.” ᴹ

“I was busy being persuaded.” ʸ

* * *

They find Jin-Woo outside.

He has gone no farther than the path between the guest rooms and the common building. One of Mau's ordinary soldiers stands at the turn. The soldier has just finished pointing toward the outer road; it lowers its hand when Mau approaches.

“Did something happen?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“I wanted to look at the place I came through. I asked which way I should go.” ᴶʷ

“He can show you. He can't answer out loud.” ᴹ

“I noticed.” ᴶʷ

Jin-Woo thanks the soldier anyway. The helmet dips, a short acknowledgment of the kind Mau has taught the watch to give when someone needs to know they have been heard.

Yuka comes up beside Mau carrying the coat he forgot to pick up. He takes it with a quiet thank-you.

“Breakfast first?” she asks Jin-Woo. ʸ

He looks toward the road again.

Mau knows that look. There is something to check, and until it has been checked, every ordinary offer becomes a delay.

“I'll send him to look at the bends,” Mau says. “He'll tell me if there's anything there. You can still go after you've eaten.” ᴹ

“I'd rather see it myself.” ᴶʷ

“Okay.” ᴹ

Mau turns toward the path. Yuka catches his coat before it slides off his arm a second time.

“Bring him back while it's warm.” ʸ

“Which one of us?” ᴹ

She looks at Jin-Woo, then at Mau.

“I'll take either improvement.” ʸ

Jin-Woo almost smiles.

* * *

They walk to the bends. Mau leaves his soldier where the morning wagons will need him and points out the turn with the old wheel rut. Jin-Woo recognizes it. He steps off the road without asking Mau to follow.

There is nothing between the two trees.

He examines the ground, then raises a hand toward the space where the opening should have been. Something gathers around his fingers. Mau feels Noise's attention sharpen; he asks it to watch without trying to open anything.

Jin-Woo lowers his hand.

“Gone?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“From this side.” ᴶʷ

“Could someone be waiting on the other?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴶʷ

Mau lets the answer have the room it needs. He asks whether Jin-Woo wants him to leave a watcher nearby. Jin-Woo says yes and describes what he would consider a sign, including the things which would be too small for an ordinary traveler to notice. Mau repeats the instructions through the link, then tells Jin-Woo exactly what he has asked the soldier to do.

“It won't open a way for me,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“I know. But you won't have to keep coming back to check if something happens.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks once more between the trees.

On the walk home he asks how many soldiers Mau has on the house watch. Mau tells him about Muster and the forty ordinary forms assigned to the home rotation, then explains that the road positions change with the work. A figure standing beside a wagon is not necessarily a new unit. It may be the same one that helped unload the last wagon, given another job.

“And the people?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶʷ

“They tell me when the system I've invented is annoying.” ᴹ

“Often?” ᴶʷ

“You met my wife.” ᴹ

This time Jin-Woo does smile.

* * *

The last pan is still on the heat when they return.

Rem has saved them two plates without saving them an entire table. She tells them where the bread is and takes her own breakfast to the seat Umaru has been protecting with an elbow. Ram calls after her to take the butter. Rem comes back for it, with the resigned expression of someone who has already forgotten it once.

Jin-Woo stands with his plate until Yuka moves a cup out of his way.

“Here. Unless you want somewhere quieter.” ʸ

“Here is fine.” ᴶʷ

Across the room, both Beru are discussing something over a page. Mau recognizes his own by the angle at which it has turned to keep him in view. Recognition lasts until the other one does the same thing for Jin-Woo.

“We are going to need a system,” he says. ᴹ

“You already have one,” Yuka says. “Ask which king is wrong.” ʸ

Both Beru look up.

“Don't,” Mau says immediately. ᴹ

Mai, passing with a cup, slows down just enough to hear the answer. There isn't one. She looks disappointed and goes to sit beside Momo Nishimiya.

Jin-Woo eats half an egg before anybody asks him what he can do.

It is Gojo who asks, arriving with Shoko and an entirely unjustified claim that he has been awake longer than either of them. Shoko sits down. Gojo stays standing until she removes her bag from the chair with her foot.

“We were wondering how much of it works here,” he says. ᴳ

“You were wondering,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

“I include you in my interests.” ᴳ

“Eat.” ˢʰ

Jin-Woo glances at Mau. Mau is buttering a piece of bread with the concentration of a man who refuses to rescue his teacher.

“It isn't all answering,” Jin-Woo says at last. ᴶʷ

Gojo's face changes. He pulls the chair in and sits properly.

“Since you arrived?” ᴳ

“Since I crossed. I still have the connections. Reaching through them is different.” ᴶʷ

“We should check what that means before anyone starts measuring,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

“You say that like measuring isn't checking.” ᴳ

“You know which kind you mean.” ˢʰ

Yuka offers Jin-Woo the bread. He takes it. The discussion of what might be done with a yard stays a discussion because Shoko asks him to finish eating before letting Gojo put him in one.

Mau watches his guest reach for the jam, discover that the Beru have carried it away to weight the corner of their paper, and decide whether interrupting them is worth it.

“Beru,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“My king.” ᴮʲ

“The jar.” ᴶʷ

It returns so quickly that Yuka has to catch her cup.

* * *

By the time the dishes are going back, Ori has arrived with Frieren and a question about whether the two Beru count as two citizens or one extremely good idea. Umaru says this requires consideration. Frieren asks who left a book near the washing water. Fern appears in the doorway holding it at arm's length.

“That would be the one you were looking for,” Mau tells Frieren. ᴹ

“I found it.” ᶠ

“Fern found it.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᶠ

Jin-Woo sets down his cup. He has been following the exchanges without trying to join all of them. When Ori asks him which Beru is his, he points. She greets that one by name too.

For a moment nobody needs anything from Jin-Woo. He looks toward the door as if checking what he has forgotten.

Mau nudges the bread basket toward him.

“There's more.” ᴹ

“I should tell you why I followed the trace.” ᴶʷ

“You said you recognized it.” ᴹ

“I hoped it meant the person I heard was still there.” ᴶʷ

Yuka stops folding the cloth beside her plate. Jin-Woo notices, then looks directly at Mau.

“I came to ask whether you would help me fight.” ᴶʷ

Mau keeps his hand on the basket a moment longer. Behind them, Ori has persuaded Umaru that an excellent idea should receive its own biscuit.

“Okay,” he says. “Tell us what you're fighting.” ᴹ
