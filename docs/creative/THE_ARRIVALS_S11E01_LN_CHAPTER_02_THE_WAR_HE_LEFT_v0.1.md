# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 2 — The War He Left

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S11E01
**Continuity state:** B+177 later morning. Jin-Woo's account of his own ongoing war is newly shared with Mau, Yuka and Rimuru. Local output restriction observed only through arrival report; no numerical tier assigned in dialogue, no restored source maximum. No agreement to leave, no new world route or family extraction.

*When: Jin-Woo asks how much Mau knows about the beings who made his world, Mau looks toward the room where they keep the spare maps.*

“Enough to know I should sit down for the rest.” ᴹ

They use the room because it is free and because the door can stay open without every person on the way to breakfast having to pass the table. Yuka comes with him. Rimuru brings the paper Jin-Woo asked for, then goes back for a pencil that has not been sharpened into something suitable for sewing.

Jin-Woo waits until they have settled before beginning again.

He draws one circle, then another, leaving a wide space between them. He puts a mark inside the first.

“This is where the war is reaching the people I went to protect.” ᴶʷ

“Your world?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴶʷ

He draws several marks outside it.

Mau recognizes part of the history when Jin-Woo explains it. He has heard a version of the first maker and the war that followed from people who stood inside it. Jin-Woo lets him say what he knows, corrects the point at which Mau has joined two things that happened far apart, then moves the pencil past that history.

“The ones moving now belong to other worlds. Beings who made them, and the forces they send ahead.” ᴶʷ

Rimuru leans forward.

“Are the makers coming themselves?” ᴿ

“Not all of them. Not in the same way.” ᴶʷ

“Then let's separate those things.” ᴿ

Jin-Woo does. He uses the edge of the page for the places he has held and draws shorter lines for the routes by which attacks have reached them. He stops calling everything an army once Yuka asks whether all of it can be made to retreat. Some forces can. Others keep coming until the passage feeding them is closed.

Mau asks what happens to the ordinary people behind the line when Jin-Woo has to move.

“That depends on whether I can leave enough behind.” ᴶʷ

The answer makes the rest of the drawing easier to understand.

It is not a picture of Jin-Woo losing every fight. It is a picture of a man winning in one place while another place becomes the one that needs him.

“You want someone who can hold one of these,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Or help me close one without opening another.” ᴶʷ

“How long?” ʸ

Jin-Woo looks at Yuka.

“I can't give you an honest number.” ᴶʷ

“Thank you for saying that.” ʸ

Mau puts his hand over hers beneath the table. She turns her palm up and holds it, still looking at their guest.

* * *

Jin-Woo explains what he thought he had heard through the broken connection: another user of the power, a voice which answered and then vanished before they could establish how either of them existed. He had wanted to find Mau well before the opening appeared.

“I didn't come here expecting you to already owe me something,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Good,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Mau looks at him.

“What? We're getting the useful parts said early.” ᴿ

Jin-Woo nods as though this is a perfectly reasonable way to conduct a meeting. Yuka likes him a little more for it.

Mau tells him about the tower in the life he remembers, the copies encountered there, the people at home who helped him survive it. He gives Jin-Woo the explanation of the two Beru without opening the archive of the war which came much later. When he reaches the point at which the old life and the present one meet, he asks Jin-Woo whether he needs the whole account now.

“Only if it changes what you're offering,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“I'm not offering yet.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴶʷ

The answer is so untroubled that Mau catches himself preparing an argument he doesn't have to give.

He turns the page so he can read the routes from Jin-Woo's side.

“What would happen if you stopped coming back?” ᴹ

Jin-Woo's fingers still on the pencil.

Yuka looks at Mau. Rimuru does too, but neither interrupts.

“They would keep moving,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“After the people you left?” ᴹ

“After what they want. The people I left would be in the way.” ᴶʷ

“Your family.” ᴹ

“Among others.” ᴶʷ

Mau looks at the marks again. There are no names in the circles. He understands why Jin-Woo has drawn them that way, and why that makes it possible to keep explaining.

“Who's waiting for you?” he asks. ᴹ

Jin-Woo takes longer to answer this than he took to describe the war.

“My wife. My son.” ᴶʷ

He doesn't reach for the pencil while saying it.

“How long have you been away?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Long enough that there are things about him I know because someone told me.” ᴶʷ

Mau feels Yuka's hand tighten around his.

* * *

There is a sound at the door. Fern has come to ask whether they need the table after lunch. Rimuru says they might and asks what else was planned. She explains where the accounts can go instead; it requires moving two books and asking Senku not to put something wet on them.

“I'll ask,” he says. ᴿ

“Ask before he starts.” ᶠᵉ

She leaves them the room.

Jin-Woo looks at the place where she stood. Mau can't tell whether he is thinking about the ordinary interruption or the fact that it was allowed to interrupt.

“I can't promise to go through an opening with no way home,” Mau says. “And I don't know how to make another one to begin with.” ᴹ

“Neither do I. Not to this world.” ᴶʷ

“But you want to ask the people here who might.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴶʷ

“DIO gets to answer for herself. Ashborn too.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo puts down the pencil.

“I heard him last night. I'm not asking him to take his war back.” ᴶʷ

“Okay.” ᴹ

Rimuru watches Mau examining the diagram again. He recognizes the expression before Yuka does. When she turns, Rimuru has already put a clean sheet of paper beside her husband.

“Explain it before you decide you've solved it,” he says. ᴿ

“I haven't solved it.” ᴹ

“That has never stopped the expression.” ᴿ

Mau ignores him with dignity. Yuka pushes the paper closer.

“Explain.” ʸ

He doesn't begin by drawing a weapon.

He turns Jin-Woo's picture toward him and puts his finger on the circle with the names that have not been written inside it.

“Would they come here if they could?” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

“I would have to ask them.” ᴶʷ

“That's what I'm asking. If they wanted to, would you consider it?” ᴹ

For the first time that morning, Jin-Woo looks as if Mau has asked a question he never allowed to remain open long enough to answer.

* * *

Yuka lets go of Mau's hand so he can turn the papers without dragging her arm across the table. Then she asks what bringing the family would do about the people who remain.

“It doesn't solve the war,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“And we don't know whether the same opening could be followed.” ᴿ

“I know that too.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks from one to the other. They are still talking to Mau, not arguing over whether Jin-Woo deserves to sit there. It takes him a little time to find his place in the discussion again.

“Moving the people I love won't make the others safe,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Then we need to talk about what would make the attackers stop,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Defeating what they send.” ᴶʷ

“You've been doing that.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo says nothing.

Mau picks up the clean page and holds it beside the first. One is crowded with positions, routes and the work of staying ahead. The other still has nothing on it.

“Would they stop if they thought something had changed?” he asks. ᴹ

Rimuru shuts his eyes briefly.

“Please tell me the next word isn't god.” ᴿ

Mau looks at him.

Yuka begins to laugh before she can stop herself. It is a small, disbelieving sound, and Mau has the courtesy to look a little embarrassed by how completely he has been read.

Jin-Woo looks at the blank page.

“What kind of change?” ᴶʷ

Mau lowers the paper.

“I need to ask permission before I can even suggest part of this,” he says. “But if you're willing to hear something stupid, I think we should ask.” ᴹ
