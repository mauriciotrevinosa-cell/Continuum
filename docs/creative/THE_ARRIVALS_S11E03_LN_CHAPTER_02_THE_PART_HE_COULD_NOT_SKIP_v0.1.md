# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 6 — The Part He Could Not Skip

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S11E03
**Continuity state:** B+177 evening. Movement Learner is proposed and a narrow annotated-review prototype authorized; no new execution/mastery or autonomous body control. Mini Ciel silent filter distinct from speaking Ciel. Mau/Yuka ordinary married evening and her own training priorities continue. No blanket permission to construct new abilities.

*When: Mau demonstrates his afternoon's lesson with a spoon, Yuka takes the bowl out of his other hand.*

“I had it.” ᴹ

“You had the spoon.” ʸ

She puts the bowl on the table before he can attempt to prove that both statements were true. The stew is still hot enough that he can see the steam. His eyes tell him much more about it than the shape it makes in the gray light; none of that will help him if he puts it in his lap.

He demonstrates without the bowl. Yuka watches the shoulder.

“You did it again.” ʸ

“You could pretend it was impressive.” ᴹ

“It was impressive. Now do the useful one.” ʸ

He laughs and puts the spoon down.

They have carried supper to the quieter end of the common room because Yuka wants to explain what she and Maki tried while Mau was being politely defeated. It involved making a fold in the wrong place deliberately, then using the mistake as the beginning of the next motion.

“I keep wanting to fix it,” she says. “Grandma said she wanted to see what I did after.” ʸ

“What did you do?” ᴹ

“Got hit.” ʸ

“A family tradition.” ᴹ

Yuka kicks his boot under the table.

Then she shows him with the napkin. Mau follows her hands while she folds a corner inward and changes the place at which the next pull would meet it. He suggests an angle; she explains why she already tried that and why she thinks another one will work better.

He asks whether he can be the target when she tests it.

“After the slow version,” she says. ʸ

“Nobody trusts me.” ᴹ

“I would like to keep the wall.” ʸ

* * *

The idea for the learner begins with a correction Mau cannot remember in the right order.

He remembers Jin-Woo moving. He remembers every point of pressure on his arm and the exact instant the practice knife reached him. When he tries to hold all of that while doing the movement himself, the useful part gets lost among things he already knows how to see.

*You're asking for a lesson to be presented in a different form,* Ciel says. ᶜ

*Something I can review without sorting through everything that happened around it.* ᴹ

Noise offers the motion as it observed it. For a moment Mau has Jin-Woo's shoulder, wrist and balance placed against the space in front of him. He is still sitting with Yuka, his hand resting beside his bowl. Nothing moves the hand for him.

*That's the part I mean,* he says. *Can you show where I got it wrong?* ᴹ

The image changes. Too many differences arrive together. Weight, timing, height, the old limit in his fingers, the angle at which he tried to reach past the first knife.

Mau closes the presentation before he starts to rub his eyes.

Yuka leans forward.

“Too much?” ʸ

“It showed me all of my talent at once.” ᴹ

She gives him time to explain the actual problem.

“Pick one,” she says when he finishes. ʸ

* * *

They ask Ciel and Noise to begin with the shoulder.

The existing observation is enough to mark the turn Mau made. Ciel separates the useful correction from the rest of the record; Noise holds it as a comparison. Mau can look at his own attempt and the alternative without having the other person's balance mistaken for something his body already knows.

Yuka listens while he describes it aloud. When she asks whether it can judge an attempt they haven't recorded yet, Ciel says that will need its own work. When Mau suggests comparing every fighter he knows, Yuka raises one eyebrow.

“One,” she reminds him. ʸ

“I have accepted your cruel conditions.” ᴹ

*No motor execution,* Ciel says. *No correction imposed while you are moving. The first version is a review of a chosen exercise.* ᶜ

*That was what I wanted.* ᴹ

*You also wanted everything at once.* ᶜ

Yuka sees his face.

“What did she say?” ʸ

He tells her.

“I like her.” ʸ

*I am aware,* Ciel says, pleased. ᶜ

Mau conveys that too. Yuka looks delighted.

* * *

The name arrives before the work is finished.

Mau calls it a movement learner because he needs something to call it. Ciel repeats the phrase with an interest which should have warned him.

*Mini Ciel's little sister,* she says. ᶜ

He stops with his cup halfway to his mouth.

*You have decided that already?* ᴹ

*A related support process with a narrower task. It is appropriate.* ᶜ

*Does Mini Ciel get an opinion?* ᴹ

*Mini Ciel is performing her work perfectly. I see no reason to interrupt her.* ᶜ

Noise considers the new designation.

*Little sister,* it says. ᴺ

*Yes,* Ciel says immediately. ᶜ

Yuka waits until Mau has finished laughing before asking what happened. He explains the family arrangement that has apparently been settled without him.

“Congratulations,” she says. ʸ

“To whom?” ᴹ

“I'm not sure yet.” ʸ

The new review remains unfinished. Naming it does not supply a tested correction, and its older sister continues filtering the incoming information without joining the conversation. Mau asks Noise to keep the first sample small and tells it when he will be free to try the result.

There is a pause before Noise agrees.

Mau notices it.

*What?* ᴹ

*Other work,* Noise says. ᴺ

*I know. We'll look at it.* ᴹ

*You said.* ᴺ

He lowers his cup.

* * *

Yuka sees the shift and asks whether something is wrong. Mau tells her about the other projects, the observations kept ready, the work he has repeatedly promised to revisit once the next urgent thing was over.

“Did you tell him when?” she asks. ʸ

“Not properly.” ᴹ

“Then tell him when.” ʸ

He does. After breakfast tomorrow, he will look at the work with Noise and Ciel. If it needs more time, they will arrange the next part before leaving the room. It is a small promise, much easier to make than a promise to save someone.

Noise repeats the time.

*After breakfast.* ᴺ

*Yes.* ᴹ

It returns to the narrow comparison with a concentration Mau can feel. He stays aware of that contentment while Yuka takes the bowls to the tray and tells him they should go upstairs before Rimuru discovers the new naming system and volunteers himself as an uncle.

“Too late,” Rimuru says from behind them. ᴿ

Mau turns.

Rimuru has come for a cup. He has heard only the last sentence. Yuka tells him that is enough to be concerned about.

“I live beside you,” he says. “Concern is included.” ᴿ

* * *

In their room, Mau tries the foot placement once more while Yuka brushes her hair. He does it slowly enough to finish with his balance under him. Then she holds out the brush.

“You promised.” ʸ

He remembers. Earlier, before Jin-Woo's question changed the morning, she asked him to do the back because the knot had defeated her patience. He takes the brush and sits behind her.

“If I pull, tell me.” ᴹ

“I will absolutely tell you.” ʸ

She leans back into him after he has worked the knot loose. He rests his chin against her shoulder and asks whether she minds that tomorrow morning has acquired another conversation.

“We still have the afternoon,” she says. ʸ

“What are we doing?” ᴹ

“I'm deciding.” ʸ

“Dangerous.” ᴹ

She turns enough to kiss him.

“You married me.” ʸ

“I did.” ᴹ

He says it with such uncomplicated pleasure that she kisses him again before putting the brush away.
