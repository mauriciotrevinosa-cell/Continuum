# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 12 — A God for Three Breaths

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S11E06
**Continuity state:** B+179 midday. DIO/Ashborn give narrowly scoped permission for a local, isolated signal-expression test using retained functional derivatives. Guests remain protected; no live energy donated, private memories opened or new contact inferred. Mana from Mau powers a brief test, shut by Ciel. Jin-Woo/Frieren detect specific defects; no false-deity combat capability, cross-world broadcast or real deity created.

*When: Ashborn asks how long the first god is meant to live, Ciel says three breaths.*

“A short career,” he says. ᴬˢ

“We can make it less impressive if you'd prefer,” Mau says. ᴹ

“You have already drawn it.” ᴬˢ

DIO turns away to hide a smile. Rimuru doesn't bother.

They have brought the test back to the shared guest space because the people whose old models it uses have asked to see the entire operation. Jin-Woo is present by invitation. Frieren stands beside Mau at the threshold; Yuka has chosen a place from which she can see both the small test surface and her husband's face.

There is no stage and no proposed corpse. Senku's reduced outline occupies a part of the table no larger than a plate. The only thing they want the first model to do is make its pattern recognizable under observation.

“No new reading of either guest,” Ciel says. “The retained pieces only, for this expression. No continuing operation after the test closes.” ᶜ

Ashborn asks Noise what it is going to use.

Noise identifies the stored reference, the part of the expression selected for the test and the boundary which keeps the rest apart. DIO asks the same question about hers. She waits until the answer is clear before giving permission.

“For this test,” she says. ᴰ

Ashborn nods after her.

Mau looks toward Noise.

“Ready?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴺ

* * *

The first attempt makes nothing anybody could call a god.

Something gathers above the test surface, dark at one edge and pale at another. Jin-Woo's attention changes before his posture does. Frieren tilts her head. The edge lifts, catches and begins repeating the same movement instead of completing it.

Ciel closes the test before the third breath.

Mau feels the small draw stop. His hand remains steady where Yuka can see it.

“I assume that was terrible,” he says. ᴹ

“It was unfinished,” Frieren says. ᶠ

“I appreciate the distinction.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo doesn't answer yet. He looks toward Ashborn, then back to the empty test surface.

“There was something recognizable in it,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Mine?” Ashborn asks. ᴬˢ

“Yes. But it arrived before the rest.” ᴶʷ

DIO folds her hands.

“And mine sounded borrowed.” ᴰ

Mau looks at her.

“How can a presence sound borrowed?” ᴹ

“Like someone answering to a name a moment before remembering it is meant to be theirs.” ᴰ

The answer is strange enough to make him understand what the technical explanation did not. He asks Ciel to mark the order in which the parts appeared. Noise does it before she has finished agreeing.

* * *

They examine the failed attempt while the guests keep their own rooms' richer contact closed. The stored model has enough relations to express something recognizable. Combining two such expressions does not give the result a believable reason to exist as one thing.

Frieren draws two marks on the page.

“You joined these because you have both of them,” she says. “The observer doesn't know your reason.” ᶠ

“The observer also doesn't know what a new god has to look like,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“It knows when an edge is pretending to meet another edge.” ᶠ

She points to the interruption Ciel marked. The shape did not fail because it lacked enough power to be large. It failed while trying to be small.

Mau asks Noise whether there is a way to let the second response grow out of the first instead of placing both side by side. Noise offers a possibility. Ciel asks it to show the change in the model before attempting another expression.

Jin-Woo follows the explanation until he can point to the part a scout from his war would test.

“There,” he says. “Something would try that before reporting the presence as real.” ᴶʷ

Mau moves the mark onto Senku's reduced outline. It changes the first test more than making the figure taller would have done.

Ashborn watches him erase the extra shape.

“Better,” he says. ᴬˢ

Mau looks up.

“I haven't done it yet.” ᴹ

“You stopped adding things.” ᴬˢ

Yuka hides her smile against her hand.

* * *

The second attempt lasts the full three breaths.

The pale edge meets the dark one without catching. There is still no face. There are no arms with which to conquer a world. For the moment the construction is a brief expression held together by Mau's supplied mana and the model they have agreed to examine.

It gives him the unpleasant sensation of seeing something familiar stand in the wrong place.

He knows who is still seated behind it. He looks toward DIO anyway.

She is watching the same thing with an expression that has lost its amusement.

Ciel closes the test at the promised interval.

Nobody asks for an extra breath.

“I understand why he noticed,” Jin-Woo says quietly. ᴶʷ

Mau turns toward Ashborn. The old discovery is in the room with them, present enough that no one needs to retell it.

“Yes,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ashborn looks at the empty surface. Then at the actual people around it.

“And now I have seen what was done.” ᴬˢ

“Would you like us to stop?” ᴹ

Ashborn takes the question seriously.

“For today.” ᴬˢ

Mau nods to Ciel. Noise lets the selected path close, leaves the work apart and returns its attention to the shared room.

* * *

They keep the observations instead of trying to keep the expression alive.

Jin-Woo says the second version might attract an investigating presence. He does not believe it would end a war merely by appearing. They still need the act which would change the observer's conclusion, and they need a safe means of showing it where it matters.

“So we have something that can get us into trouble,” Mau says. ᴹ

“You already had that,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Yuka touches Mau's wrist before he can reply.

“You also stopped when you said you would.” ʸ

He looks at her, then at Noise.

“We did.” ᴹ

Noise seems pleased to be included in the answer. DIO asks to keep the page with the two attempts next to the terrible drawing. When Mau suggests throwing the drawing away now that they have something useful, she refuses so promptly that Ashborn starts smiling again.

* * *

Jin-Woo stays after the others begin leaving.

He asks Ashborn whether seeing the imitation changed his mind about letting them continue. Mau pauses at the threshold, prepared to take the papers away if that is what his guest wants.

Ashborn looks toward the open window beyond his private room.

“I want the quiet I asked for,” he says. “If this might help you find some of your own, you may bring me the next question.” ᴬˢ

“I will,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“Bring a better drawing.” ᴬˢ

Mau raises a hand.

“That was entirely unnecessary.” ᴹ

“It was the part I wanted,” Ashborn says. ᴬˢ

Mau leaves him with that small victory. Outside, Yuka catches his hand and asks whether he remembers what they planned for the rest of the afternoon.

He does.

For once, the answer requires no map.
