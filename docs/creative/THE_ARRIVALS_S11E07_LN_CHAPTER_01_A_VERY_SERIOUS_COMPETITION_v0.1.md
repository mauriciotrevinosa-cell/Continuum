# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 13 — A Very Serious Competition

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S11E07
**Continuity state:** B+179 afternoon. Mau/Yuka keep a chosen outing within familiar protected grounds. Existing inventory used openly. Shared ordinary time, no CT/level gain, pregnancy or wedding-state change. Old color and hand conditions persist; no forced opening of memories.

*When: Yuka asks Mau whether he is going to win by throwing the whole riverbank, he puts the large stone down.*

“I was selecting.” ᴹ

“Select smaller.” ʸ

She has taken him to the water with bread, a little fruit and no particular intention of finding anything worth selling. He carried their things in the inventory until she picked a place. Now the food is on the cloth between them and her boots are lined up beside it.

Mau has one boot off. He removes the other before she can accuse him of delaying the competition.

“What are the rules?” he asks. ᴹ

“Most skips.” ʸ

“That's the entire system?” ᴹ

“It has served humanity for some time.” ʸ

“You invited the wrong person to trust a system.” ᴹ

She holds out a flat stone. He takes it and sends it across the surface with the care of a man trying very hard to appear ordinary about his hands.

It skips twice, strikes at a bad angle and disappears.

Yuka looks delighted.

“Two.” ʸ

“I was demonstrating the lower range.” ᴹ

She throws hers. Three skips, then one that is mostly a splash but which she insists counts.

Mau examines the water.

“That last one was swimming.” ᴹ

“Four.” ʸ

* * *

The next few minutes become very serious.

Yuka catches him checking the shape of a stone with more attention than the activity deserves and informs him that having excellent eyes does not permit him to interrogate the entire river. He asks whether being married gives her the authority to regulate his hobbies.

“Yes.” ʸ

“I missed that paragraph.” ᴹ

“You were distracted.” ʸ

He looks at her standing barefoot at the edge of the water, holding a stone between two fingers and watching for his answer.

“I remember why.” ᴹ

She tries not to smile. He enjoys making the attempt difficult.

His next throw gives him four honest skips. Hers gives her five, one so close to the far bank that he has to turn to keep following it. She raises both hands before he has found a reason to dispute the result.

“Victory.” ʸ

“We'll need witnesses.” ᴹ

“You were here.” ʸ

“I am deeply compromised.” ᴹ

She comes back and puts her hands on his shoulders, still laughing.

“You'll survive.” ʸ

He catches her around the waist when she leans in to kiss him. She lets him keep her close for a moment, then looks at his hands as though considering whether to make another rule.

“That isn't how you overturn the result.” ʸ

“I wasn't trying to.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸ

She kisses him again and goes to collect the fruit.

* * *

They eat sitting close enough that her knee rests against his.

Yuka describes the kind of expedition she would like when they have an ordinary free day: a route they can finish before supper, something she can plan, a reason to bring back an account that isn't mostly what went wrong. Mau asks whether she wants it to involve a gate or a place outside one.

“Maybe both, eventually. I like going somewhere with you.” ʸ

“I have an excellent record as an assistant.” ᴹ

“Ori has been telling me.” ʸ

“She is generous.” ᴹ

“She said you followed almost all the directions.” ʸ

“A glowing reference.” ᴹ

Yuka breaks off some bread for him.

“What would you choose?” ʸ

He thinks about the question instead of finding something she has already said she likes.

“A game I can finish without somebody calling me away in the middle.” ᴹ

“With whom?” ʸ

“You. Umaru if she'll accept defeat. Maybe Rem, if she'd enjoy watching Umaru refuse it.” ᴹ

Yuka smiles.

“And something for just us?” ʸ

He turns his palm up beneath hers.

“I liked not deciding in advance what this afternoon had to accomplish.” ᴹ

She looks at the water, then down at the food they are slowly failing to finish.

“I wanted that too.” ʸ

* * *

When she asks about Noise, Mau tells her how completely he has lost control of the committee.

“DIO makes a face. He copies it. Ciel explains why the face is justified.” ᴹ

“Usually is it?” ʸ

“I was seeking support.” ᴹ

“You have mine.” ʸ

“You sound just like her.” ᴹ

Yuka rests her head against his arm. He tells her about Noise reviewing the broom after this morning's demonstration, the satisfaction of finding a question it could actually ask someone. She says she would like to hear what it finds out.

“He'll like being asked.” ᴹ

“Then tell him I asked.” ʸ

Noise, listening within the ordinary link, notices before Mau can repeat the request.

*Wife friend,* it says. ᴺ

Mau laughs softly.

Yuka lifts her head.

“What?” ʸ

He tells her.

“Yes,” she says, looking toward him with enough seriousness that he knows the answer is meant for both of them. “Wife friend.” ʸ

* * *

They lie back after putting the food away.

Mau stores the things they no longer need, leaves the cloth where it is and puts his arm where she can use it as a pillow. The light moves through the leaves above them. He watches it for a while, then closes his eyes because he likes the feel of her hand drawing idle shapes against his wrist.

“You can sleep,” she says. ʸ

“I might miss something.” ᴹ

“I'll wake you if I become more impressive.” ʸ

“Impossible standard.” ᴹ

Her fingers stop for a moment. Then she turns toward him and kisses the corner of his mouth.

He opens his eyes.

“What was that for?” ᴹ

“I can have reasons that aren't an assignment.” ʸ

He brings her closer. She settles with her cheek against his chest, and for a little while they hear the water without discussing it.

There are plans waiting at home. A man deciding whether there might be another way to live, a construction they are trying to make convincing, a list of questions Noise finally has time to ask. Mau knows where they left all of them.

He doesn't go and pick them up before she is ready to move.

* * *

Yuka asks for one last throw on the way back.

Mau finds a flat stone, gives it to her and selects one of his own without consulting every visible property of the bank. She watches him choose with a suspicious expression.

“What?” ᴹ

“You look pleased.” ʸ

“I am.” ᴹ

“You're going to claim you let me win.” ʸ

“I would never.” ᴹ

She throws first. Four skips. He throws after her and gets three, the last close enough to the surface that he thinks he might argue it into a fourth.

Yuka turns toward him.

“Say it.” ʸ

He looks at her, then at the water where his stone has disappeared.

“My wife is extraordinarily talented.” ᴹ

She takes his hand with the satisfaction of a champion being properly received.

On the walk home, he tells her he wants a rematch. She says she will put it in her very busy calendar. He asks what else is in it.

“You, mostly.” ʸ

He stops her long enough to kiss her before they reach the common-room door.
