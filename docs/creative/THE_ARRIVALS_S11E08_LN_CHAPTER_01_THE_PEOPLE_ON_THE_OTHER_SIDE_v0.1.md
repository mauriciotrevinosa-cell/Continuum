## Chapter 15 — The People on the Other Side

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65

**Source episode:** S11E08
**Continuity state:** B+180 morning. Creator-selected Jin-Woo source has Hae-In/Suho located together and sister/father/mother alive, potentially reachable through same intended family contact. DIO studies recorded arrival and voluntary Jin-Woo reference through existing room support; no general world portal, contact or extraction yet. Source-powered wider access versus locally supported body distinguished; no family price paid.

*When: DIO asks Jin-Woo whom he intends to speak to, he starts with his wife's name and then stops himself.*

“All of them, if we can.” ᴶʷ

Mau looks up from the paper.

“Who is all?” ᴹ

“My wife and my son. My sister. My parents.” ᴶʷ

DIO makes five marks, leaving space beside each.

“They are alive?” she asks. ᴰ

“Yes.” ᴶʷ

“And you know where to begin looking?” ᴰ

“Hae-In and Suho are together. My family knows how to reach them.” ᴶʷ

Mau is glad the names have arrived before they have begun talking about a capacity. He doesn't want to find himself explaining to a man which of the people he loves would fit through a plan they designed without hearing who they were.

Rimuru leans over the paper.

“Then we start with getting a message to them. One that can tell them where you are.” ᴿ

“And let them answer,” Yuka adds. ʸ

DIO nods and turns the page toward Jin-Woo.

* * *

There are three things they can examine without inventing another opening.

Jin-Woo remembers the crossing. Mau's watcher has observed the place where it ended, and the old communication between the two men left a recognizable trace in each of them. None is a road on its own. DIO asks to compare the parts they can describe and show voluntarily.

She stays in the room where it is safe for her to work. The mark on the outer road is brought to Mau through the watcher already there; when she needs an angle the soldier cannot supply from its position, Mau sends it around the tree rather than telling her she ought to be able to see through one.

“Farther,” DIO says. ᴰ

The view changes.

“Too far.” ᴰ

Mau moves the soldier back.

“I am an excellent tripod.” ᴹ

“You have been very helpful,” she says, with suspicious emphasis. ᴰ

Yuka smiles at him from beside the threshold. He continues being useful.

Jin-Woo offers the part of his power which answered when he recognized the opening. Ciel keeps that reference separate from the private memories he is not offering. Noise follows the relation to the old interrupted voice, then returns to a gap none of them can fill by wanting it to be smaller.

“What is missing?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶʷ

“A way to keep an answer on the same path as the question,” DIO says. ᴰ

She points to the place where the first communication broke.

* * *

The difference between the power Jin-Woo remembers and what he can use here becomes useful before it becomes comfortable.

His body has crossed. His present connections answer him at the scale he described in the yard. Reaching through them toward the much larger structure he carried in his own world meets the same interruption as the path back.

“You think the part I can't reach could help hold the route?” he asks. ᴶʷ

“I think its relationship to the place you left gives us something to examine,” DIO says. “I am not asking you to open it at full force.” ᴰ

Jin-Woo looks toward Mau.

“Would it be dangerous to try?” ᴶʷ

Mau remembers the pressure over the table during yesterday's little test. He remembers what the actual people behind that impression have been protecting from casual use.

“We don't know enough to use that as the test,” he says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo nods.

There is no disappointment in being told to begin smaller. He has spent enough of his life holding a dangerous thing closed to recognize the value of somebody who will help him think before opening it.

“What can we do safely?” he asks. ᴶʷ

Ciel proposes a call with no physical crossing attached. They would attempt to pass a chosen voice along a narrow continuation of the old trace, holding the return long enough to hear whether anyone is there. The family would not be pulled through by answering. Nothing they say would count as agreeing to leave.

Mau looks at DIO.

“Can we close it ourselves?” ᴹ

“That is the first thing we test.” ᴰ

* * *

They test it without a voice.

The first short pulse reaches the break and comes back with no useful change. The second does something different because Jin-Woo recognizes the direction in which the response should continue and holds the offered reference steadier.

Mau feels Noise find a relation it had only inferred before.

*There,* it says. ᴺ

*Keep it as an observation,* Ciel answers. *We finish this operation first.* ᶜ

The attempt closes when they ask it to. DIO puts her hand flat on the table afterward, resting before anyone tries to call the first useful answer a reason to keep working through her fatigue.

Rimuru takes the page away from the edge of her sleeve.

“Enough for the morning?” ᴿ

She considers the question, then says yes.

Jin-Woo doesn't try to persuade her.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴶʷ

She looks up at him.

“I would like you to be able to tell them.” ᴰ

* * *

Outside, Yuka asks whether Jin-Woo wants the house to be present when the call happens.

“I don't know what they'll ask,” he says. ᴶʷ

“That wasn't the question.” ʸ

He thinks about it.

“I'd like them to meet you. But I'd like to hear them first.” ᴶʷ

“Then we can do that.” ʸ

Mau asks whether he wants to write anything down before the next attempt. Jin-Woo looks at the five names on the page DIO has given him, then folds it without hiding it from them.

“I'd rather not prepare the whole conversation before they get to speak.” ᴶʷ

Yuka nods.

“Good.” ʸ

* * *

They find Mai in the passage, returning the two notebooks she borrowed for the critics. She asks whether the god has acquired a face. Mau tells her it has acquired several technical problems instead.

“Those are easier to draw,” she says. ᴹᵃ

Jin-Woo holds the folded paper while the others exchange their plans for lunch. He has a wife, a son, a sister and two parents on the other side of a path they have not yet managed to carry a sentence through.

Mau can see how much of him has already gone there.

“We'll tell you when she's ready,” he says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks back at him.

“I know.” ᴶʷ

He follows them toward the common room. When Yuka asks whether he wants something warm or something he can carry outside, he has to think before answering.

“Warm,” he says. ᴶʷ
