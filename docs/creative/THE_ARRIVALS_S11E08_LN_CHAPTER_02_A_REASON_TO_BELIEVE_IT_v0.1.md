# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 16 — A Reason to Believe It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S11E08
**Continuity state:** B+180 afternoon. Local physical rehearsal for false-deity staging; no cross-world audience or claimed enemy fooled. Jin-Woo substitutes an offered ordinary shadow form to test withdrawal timing, no person injured and no new extraction. Mau does not copy Jin-Woo's full kit. Retained guest-derived signal not activated this chapter.

*When: Jin-Woo tells Beru that he would like to fall over without a tribute, both Beru begin taking notes.*

“Just mine,” he says. ᴶʷ

Mau's Beru pauses, then carries on because it apparently has thoughts about that instruction too.

They have chosen the empty practice yard for the physical part of the performance. Senku put a mark where an observer might stand. Frieren moved it. Senku asked why, listened to the answer and put a second mark farther to the side.

“They may not watch from where you want,” she says. ᶠ

“That's why I invited you.” ˢᵉⁿ

“You invited me because you wanted to know whether you could hide the mistake.” ᶠ

“Two excellent reasons.” ˢᵉⁿ

Yuka comes with Maki and stays near the boundary so she can see the whole approach. Shoko examines the proposed contact and says she will allow no actual blade near anybody's neck just because the angle might be convincing.

Mau lifts the wooden trainer.

“This one?” ᴹ

“You know what I mean.” ˢʰ

He does. He puts it down while Jin-Woo explains the substitution he wants to try.

* * *

The first version looks completely false.

Jin-Woo crosses the marked space, places a shadow form where he stood and withdraws himself along the short path he has already checked. Mau knows when to look away because Jin-Woo told him. Everybody else sees exactly where the real man went.

Mai, watching from beside Momo, raises a finger.

“I have a note.” ᴹᵃ

“I think we all do,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

The shadow stands alone with admirable loyalty and no resemblance to a convincing death.

Jin-Woo returns.

“Now we know,” he says. ᴶʷ

Mau asks whether he wants to try moving faster.

“That might conceal the movement. It won't explain why they should believe the thing left behind is me.” ᴶʷ

Frieren nods.

“And something would notice that you left.” ᶠ

They leave the shadow in place while they walk around the marks. From one angle, the form's position hides the retreat. From another, it makes the absence more obvious. Senku draws the two sight lines without turning either result into a success.

Mau watches the empty place where Jin-Woo had been.

“We keep trying to hide your departure,” he says. “Could the departure be the thing they think happened?” ᴹ

* * *

The second version begins with a different question.

If the apparent god acts, what would the observer think it did to the person in front of it? A body falling is only one possible answer. Jin-Woo could disappear in a way the observer attributes to the attack, provided the presence carrying his identity ends with the motion and the route he actually uses is concealed within it.

“You mean make them see what they expect to explain the loss,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Something like that.” ᴹ

“Then your timing needs to belong to the thing attacking him.” ʸ

She steps forward far enough to show them with the trainer. She doesn't touch Jin-Woo. She begins a slow motion, stops at the point she wants Mau to notice and asks him when he would expect the result.

Mau points too early.

“That's when you know,” she says. “It's not necessarily when they do.” ʸ

Jin-Woo looks toward Frieren.

“Would you notice the difference?” ᴶʷ

“Yes.” ᶠ

“Then show us.” ᴶʷ

The next part of the rehearsal becomes less theatrical and more exact. Frieren follows the trace she can perceive. Senku follows the visible motion. Maki follows Jin-Woo's commitment to the exchange instead of the place everybody is telling her to watch.

Each finds a different defect.

* * *

By the fifth attempt, Mau is tired of being wrong in such a cooperative environment.

“We should invite a less observant audience.” ᴹ

“That might be difficult to arrange with the actual enemy,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“You could let me have the complaint.” ᴹ

“It was very good.” ᴿ

Jin-Woo waits until Mau has finished glaring at his brother, then asks to change the approach.

He moves as though he intends to fight through the supposed attack rather than stop in front of it. The real effort gives the withdrawal a cause: the observer sees him commit, sees the response swallow the place he reaches and loses him within an event that appeared to have something to overcome.

It is still a slow local practice. There is no divine signal wrapped around it and no enemy present to be deceived.

For the first time, however, the action tells a story before either king has to explain it.

Mai leans forward.

“That.” ᴹᵃ

Mau looks toward her.

“He was trying to do something. You can see what got interrupted.” ᴹᵃ

Jin-Woo's Beru writes with furious concentration.

Mau's Beru, after a moment, underlines something twice.

* * *

They end with a working shape and several honest failures.

Jin-Woo can withdraw safely to the checked mark behind the temporary screen. Shoko has seen every participant come through without an injury. The optical part improves when Mai changes the edge of the screen. The magical part remains incomplete; Frieren can still follow the actual retreat because the place has not stopped carrying the answer.

“The signal and this movement have to be tested together,” she says. ᶠ

“Another day,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

Mau agrees before anybody can blame him for the objection he was considering.

Jin-Woo calls the ordinary shadow back. It disappears into his own connection, belonging to him as it did before the rehearsal began.

“Thank you,” he tells it. ᴶʷ

Mau watches the empty mark for a moment, then looks toward Yuka.

“Your note helped.” ᴹ

“I have many talents.” ʸ

“An increasingly public problem.” ᴹ

She hands him the trainer and makes him carry it back to the rack.

* * *

At the gate of the yard, both critics ask permission to discuss the king's performance.

“Which king?” Mau asks. ᴹ

They exchange a glance.

“Oh, terrible question,” Yuka says. ʸ

Jin-Woo puts a hand over his face. Mai settles herself on the wall because she has no intention of missing the answer. Momo stays beside her.

The criticism begins with a respectful acknowledgment of greatness, proceeds to several unnecessary pauses, and becomes so precise about where Mau stood that he has to ask for the notebook.

Beru gives it to him with obvious pride.

Among the elaborate headings there is a simple little drawing of the place Mau was supposed to leave open for Jin-Woo. He had stepped into it during the third attempt because he was watching the result too intently.

“Fair,” Mau says. ᴹ

His Beru looks delighted.

Jin-Woo's Beru turns another page.
