# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 19 — Five Voices

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S11E10
**Continuity state:** B+181 late afternoon, after DIO's chosen rest. Second bounded voice call. Source-side family assembles using their own ordinary communications: Hae-In, Suho, Jin-Ah, mother/father alive by creator-selected source point. Proposal and dangers disclosed, each speaks; no compelled movement, transfer, new coin or claim that hesitation is refusal of love. Source point is Continuum choice, not an assertion about every Ragnarok chapter.

*When: Jin-Woo's mother hears his voice, she tells him to say something else.*

He has said her name. He says it again because nothing more useful comes to him.

“Yes. More than that,” she says. ᴶᵐ

Jin-Ah makes a small sound in the background. Suho tells her to come closer to the place carrying the call. There is a brief discussion of who is standing where, followed by Hae-In asking everyone to let him answer.

Jin-Woo looks around the room which has become the other end of his family.

“I'm well. They gave me breakfast. I've been sleeping indoors.” ᴶʷ

Rimuru lowers his face into his hand.

“That will have to do for now,” his mother says. ᴶᵐ

Her voice catches on the final word. Jin-Woo stops trying to think of something impressive to tell her.

“I wanted to hear you,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Then keep talking.” ᴶᵐ

* * *

Mau stays near the threshold with Yuka. This time they have brought a second cup for DIO and a folded cloth she can put beneath the hand which tires first. Ciel has repeated what the call can carry and what ends when she closes it. Noise keeps the return to the same narrow path instead of treating a successful connection as permission to explore everything attached to it.

Jin-Woo's father asks about the crossing after his mother has heard enough ordinary words to believe she is hearing her son.

Jin-Woo explains the road, the trace he recognized and the closed opening. His father listens to the part about the larger power which does not answer here and asks how much remains available.

“Enough to protect myself,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“That wasn't what I asked.” ᴶᶠ

Jin-Ah laughs once, softly. Jin-Woo looks as though he has walked into a familiar room and found all the furniture where it always was.

He gives the clearer answer. The body answers at an earlier scale he recognizes. He can call the connections available to him here. The much larger reach remains on the other side of the interruption, something they have studied carefully instead of trying to force open beside the people hosting him.

“And you are still well?” his father asks. ᴶᶠ

“Yes.” ᴶʷ

“Then we can speak about what comes next.” ᴶᶠ

* * *

Jin-Ah asks to speak to the person who found a way to make her brother describe meals.

“There were several of us,” Yuka says before Mau can nominate somebody. ʸ

“Then thank all of them.” ᴶᵃ

Mau introduces himself again. This time he explains who the room belongs to, how Jin-Woo arrived and what the house has actually agreed to attempt. He leaves the successful prototype at its real size: three breaths, a recognizable impression, several defects.

“It's a performance you're trying to make convincing,” Jin-Ah says. ᴶᵃ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And my brother is involved in acting?” ᴶᵃ

Jin-Woo closes his eyes.

“We have experts,” Mau says. ᴹ

Both Beru, waiting farther from the table than they would prefer, straighten with such simultaneous pride that Yuka has to look away.

“I would like reports,” Jin-Ah says. ᴶᵃ

“I'll see what I can do.” ᴹ

“You are enjoying this,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“I'm listening carefully to the people helping you.” ᴶᵃ

Hae-In tells her that can be done without making him regret introducing them.

Jin-Ah makes no promise.

* * *

The question of coming here changes the room again.

Jin-Woo explains that it would mean living in another world. They have not built a way for the family to commute between the two. He refuses to let the existence of a voice call become a promise that every ordinary goodbye could be postponed by opening the same path whenever someone missed home.

His mother asks whether they would live near him.

“That is what I'd want,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Then say that before explaining the power again.” ᴶᵐ

He looks down at his hands.

“I'd want you near me.” ᴶʷ

It is much easier to describe where an army should stand.

“All of us?” Jin-Ah asks. ᴶᵃ

“All of you who want to come.” ᴶʷ

“And if somebody needs time?” ᴶᵃ

“Then we give them time.” ᴶʷ

Mau hears DIO take a quiet breath beside the table. He knows how much that answer matters to her too.

* * *

Hae-In asks Yuka what the house is actually like.

Yuka starts with where they eat, the homes around the common spaces and the people who prefer to come and go rather than sit at the same table every night. She explains that the family would have a place of their own, not five beds added to a visitor's floor because somebody promised hospitality before looking for room.

“There are people who teach here. People who make things. There are children and people who insist they're too old to be called that.” ʸ

“Which are you?” Jin-Ah asks. ᴶᵃ

“Married to the man currently trying not to answer for me.” ʸ

Mau spreads his hands.

Hae-In laughs. Jin-Woo looks at Yuka with a gratitude too private to interrupt her explanation.

Yuka tells them about the Fort and its complicated citizenship. She tells them the ordinary part before describing the title people have given Mau, because otherwise the vending citizen will sound like a job requiring a uniform.

“Does it?” Suho asks. ˢᵘ

“Not yet,” Mau says. ᴹ

He sees Yuka's face and immediately regrets saying it where she could hear.

* * *

Jin-Woo's father asks about danger.

This time Mau answers.

He tells them that the protected area has held against attacks and that the protection belongs to a person with her own limits. He says there are people outside it who want things from him, from others in the house or from the place itself. He will not offer them a world in which nothing can ever happen.

“You are inviting them into something you protect,” Hae-In says. ᴴᶦ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“What do you expect from them?” ᴴᶦ

“To tell us what they need to live here. To help us keep the place livable in whatever ways they choose. Being his family isn't a duty to stand in front of an attack.” ᴹ

Suho asks whether they would be allowed to train.

“Absolutely.” ᴹ

“That answer sounded prepared,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“I have a teacher who would be delighted to meet you,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuka gives his sleeve a small tug. He tells Suho they can discuss that properly if he comes and asks what he actually wants to keep doing.

The answer starts with training. It grows into several other things once nobody interrupts the first one.

* * *

Jin-Woo's mother asks what the move would cost him.

The room settles around the question.

“We are still finding out how a safe path could work,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Then find out before telling us we needn't worry.” ᴶᵐ

He nods, though she cannot see it.

“I will.” ᴶʷ

DIO speaks after him.

“You will hear the conditions before anyone asks you to cross. From me as well, if you wish.” ᴰ

“I do,” his mother says. ᴶᵐ

“Then you will.” ᴰ

Nobody at the table treats the request as an insult to the work they have done. Mau feels more comfortable with her asking it than he would with a family agreeing to whatever their most exhausted member says will be fine.

Jin-Woo reaches for the paper, finds he has no reason to write and leaves his hand beside it.

“I want this,” he says. “If we can make it work without leaving something worse behind, I want to be somewhere we can have an ordinary day.” ᴶʷ

Hae-In answers first.

“Then let's find out what that would take.” ᴴᶦ

* * *

They do not settle five lives before the connection ends.

Jin-Ah wants to know what she can bring and whom she would no longer be able to call. His father wants to hear the plan for the people left on the other side. Suho wants to understand how the defenders will be supported if his father stops trying to arrive everywhere himself. His mother wants the conditions in words that do not require her to pretend she has followed every diagram.

Hae-In wants to hear her husband again after they have talked among themselves.

Each answer becomes something Jin-Woo can help find rather than another reason to decide for them in advance.

When Ciel gives the closing signal, his mother asks whether he has somebody to eat with tonight.

He looks around the table. Yuka is leaning against Mau's shoulder. Rimuru has kept the water within DIO's reach without asking her to interrupt. Both Beru are trying to appear as though the words ordinary day have not affected them at all.

“Yes,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“Good. Tell them I said thank you.” ᴶᵐ

This time he doesn't have to repeat it. They have all heard.

* * *

After the close, Mau offers to help him keep the questions in order.

Jin-Woo accepts. They begin with the things his family actually asked, using a clean page rather than finding space beside the positions of an army.

His sister's luggage. His mother's request for the conditions. His father's questions about those who remain. Suho's wish to keep helping. Hae-In's next conversation.

Yuka asks whether he wants the paper left in the meeting room or taken to his own.

“Mine,” he says. ᴶʷ

He takes it himself when he stands.
