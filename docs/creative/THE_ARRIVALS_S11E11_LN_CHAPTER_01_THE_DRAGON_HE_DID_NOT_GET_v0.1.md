# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 21 — The Dragon He Did Not Get

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S11E11
**Continuity state:** B+182 midday. Mau explicitly calls Kaisel/Kamish to exercise on established clear route; both already belong to Mau, no recruitment. Jin-Woo first personally sees Mau's sustained Kamish and expresses friendly envy. Explains original rune integration/Dragon's Fear through voluntary bounded reference, no public roar, old rune re-created, crystal consumed or new Mau skill granted. Ordinary mana crystals versus skill-bearing runes distinguished.

*When: Kamish raises his head above the trees, Jin-Woo stops in the middle of a sentence.*

Mau has called both dragons to the clear ground beyond the practice yard. Kaisel has been waiting in his shadow through several days of meetings, and the afternoon is good enough to let him take the route he knows. Kamish has accepted the same invitation with the dignity of someone who would like it understood that exercise is not a command to perform for an audience.

Jin-Woo has become an audience anyway.

“Is that Kamish?” ᴶʷ

Mau looks up.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

“You said the tower had copies.” ᴶʷ

“It did.” ᴹ

“You didn't mention that one.” ᴶʷ

“I was trying to finish the explanation before breakfast.” ᴹ

Kamish turns his great head toward the two voices. Jin-Woo's Beru has emerged beside its king and gone completely still. Mau's Beru appears on the other side, recognizes what they are looking at and stands a little straighter.

“You kept him,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“He chose to stay.” ᴹ

There is a pause.

“That makes it worse.” ᴶʷ

Mau starts laughing.

* * *

Jin-Woo walks closer after Mau tells Kamish who wants to meet him.

The dragon lowers its head enough that they can speak without either human shouting. It examines the stranger with an attention Mau recognizes, following a familiar kind of presence to an unfamiliar person.

“Another king,” Kamish says. ᴷ

“Sung Jin-Woo,” Mau tells him. ᴹ

“I heard.” ᴷ

Jin-Woo stops beyond the reach of the lowered jaw, not because Mau thinks the dragon will bite him but because it is a courteous place to stand.

“I met another version of you,” he says. ᴶʷ

Kamish waits.

“Too late to keep him.” ᴶʷ

The amusement leaves Mau's face. He knows the outline of that history. Hearing it from the person who actually stood there gives it a different weight.

Kamish's eye turns toward him.

“This one was not late,” the dragon says. ᴷ

Mau rests a hand against the scale nearest him.

“He nearly wasn't anything at all afterward,” Beru says very quietly. ᴮᵐ

Kamish lifts its head a little.

“He came back.” ᴷ

Jin-Woo looks at Mau's hand, then at the old marks visible above the collar. He doesn't ask for the price to be recited in front of an audience.

“I'm glad he did,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

Kaisel is considerably less interested in the conversation than in being allowed to leave the ground.

Mau gives him the route, confirms the turn where the watch will be looking for wagons and tells both dragons when he would like them back. Kamish chooses to accompany the flight rather than remain for further introductions. His wings open with enough sound to bring Yuka and Rimuru to the edge of the yard.

Jin-Woo watches the two rise.

“Now you're doing it on purpose,” he says. ᴶʷ

“I made these arrangements before your very reasonable jealousy arrived.” ᴹ

“Reasonable,” Jin-Woo agrees. ᴶʷ

Yuka asks what she missed. Mau points at their guest.

“He wanted my dragon.” ᴹ

“I said I was jealous.” ᴶʷ

“An important distinction,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

Rimuru spreads his hands, delighted to be helping.

* * *

The dragon leads them to a lesson Mau had not expected.

Jin-Woo asks what became of the materials the tower left behind. Mau explains what he remembers carrying out, what the house used and what he would have to check rather than claim is still waiting in storage. He describes the kinds of crystals they have been mounting, using for power and sorting for sale.

Jin-Woo asks whether he distinguishes all of them from stones which carry a skill.

“The appraisals don't always give me the same kind of answer,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Then that is worth learning to recognize.” ᴶʷ

They sit at the bench. Yuka stays because she knows how many useful objects can become mysterious when somebody assumes the person beside him already knows what they are for.

Jin-Woo explains what he learned from runes: some materials hold energy to be spent, others carry an operation which can be received and made part of the user. The crystal which feeds a lamp and a stone that teaches a skill may look similar until someone checks what is actually being held.

“So the effect can stay after the stone is used,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Yes. The stone is how it arrives.” ᴶʷ

Mau thinks about the work Noise has collected from watching people move.

“And some might fit into something I already have?” ᴹ

“If you understand what it is offering and what it would be joining. A new way to use a familiar operation can matter as much as another name in a list.” ᴶʷ

Noise attends to that distinction so intensely that Mau asks Jin-Woo whether he can show them an example.

“Kamish,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

Mau looks toward the empty sky.

“You are not getting him by changing the subject.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs.

“The rune,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

* * *

He does not release a roar across a home full of people.

He tells Mau what he is offering to show, then lets a bounded piece of the integrated operation become visible to the inspection they agree to make. Ciel keeps the offered reference distinct from the rest of the man. Noise follows the relation without opening his memories for a more convenient account.

Dragon's Fear is a name for something made possible inside him. The demonstration concerns how that learned operation remains available after the original stone has ceased to be an object he can hand somebody.

Mau can follow the route far more clearly than he could have from hearing the result shouted at him.

*Inside,* Noise says. ᴺ

*Yes,* Ciel answers. *A reference he is choosing to offer. We aren't activating it in Mau.* ᶜ

Jin-Woo closes the demonstration when they have enough to ask a useful next question.

“You don't have to wait until an object is a weapon to learn what else it might be,” he says. ᴶʷ

“I should bring you the things I've kept apart,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Bring the records first. Then we choose something that is actually still there.” ᴶʷ

Yuka nods very firmly.

“I like this lesson,” she says. ʸ

“You're collecting people who tell me to read,” Mau says. ᴹ

“An ambitious project.” ʸ

* * *

Mau asks why observing the operation would not make the rune unnecessary.

Jin-Woo considers the question, then asks him to remember the knife exercise.

“You can see what I did. Your hand still has to learn to answer.” ᴶʷ

“The stone supplies a different part of that?” ᴹ

“It can. What your way of building would do with it is something you'll have to examine. I wouldn't promise that every stone from every gate follows the same rules.” ᴶʷ

Ciel approves of the answer. Mau can feel it before she says anything.

He agrees to sort the possibilities rather than consuming the first impressive crystal he finds. Noise asks to keep the offered reference with the other work Jin-Woo has knowingly shown them. Jin-Woo says yes to that part, not to an inspection of everything he carries.

Mau thanks him.

“You let me meet Kamish,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“That wasn't payment.” ᴹ

“I know. I'm still jealous.” ᴶʷ

* * *

When the dragons return, Ori has come out with Frieren to watch them land.

Mau keeps the landing space clear. Kaisel lowers his head toward the small figure waving at him and waits until she is brought close enough to touch. Ori tells Frieren that he remembers the last outing. Frieren says she thinks that is likely.

Jin-Woo watches Ori pat the dragon with the careful authority of someone who has been allowed to have a job.

“My son would have liked that,” he says. Then, after a moment: “He might still.” ᴶʷ

Mau follows his gaze.

“We'll ask.” ᴹ

Kamish settles a little farther off and informs them that the route was clear. Mau thanks him and asks whether he wants to remain outside while they finish. The dragon chooses the sunlit ground.

Jin-Woo looks at him again.

Mau raises a hand before he can speak.

“I know.” ᴹ

“You have no idea.” ᴶʷ
