# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 24 — The Choice Was Theirs

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 24
**Source episode:** S11E12
**Continuity state:** B+183 afternoon. Third bounded family voice call explains voluntary source-access sacrifice and maximum one hundred selected shadows. Five adults choose proposed move after individual questions; transfer remains conditional on successful safe plan and final door consent. No actual crossing or sacrifice performed this chapter. Source-side farewell/preparation time agreed, no forced arrival or new coin spent.

*When: Jin-Woo explains the hundred, his sister asks whether he wants someone to tell him not to do it.*

He looks at the number on the page, then toward the place her voice is coming from.

“No,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Then tell us what you do want.” ᴶᵃ

He has brought the terms DIO gave him. He has spoken to Ashborn, looked over what he can still call and spent the morning teaching a young man who keeps discovering reasons to laugh while doing something difficult. He could give them a careful account of all of that.

“I want to live with you,” he says instead. ᴶʷ

For a moment nobody on the other side speaks.

Mau looks down so Jin-Woo doesn't have to find a reply on the face of everyone in the room. Yuka sits beside him without touching the call's place on the table. DIO has asked to explain the conditions herself after the family has heard why the choice is being offered.

“Then we should hear the rest,” his father says. ᴶᶠ

* * *

DIO begins with what would remain.

Jin-Woo would arrive at a settled relation to this world, with a body and power above the ordinary National Hunter scale he has used to describe it. His acquired knowledge and the self who learned it would remain his. He could train. He could change. The much greater deployment tied to the other side would no longer be available simply because he reached back for it.

One hundred chosen shadows would remain his to call here. Each would count as one. The number includes the companion who crossed with him. It does not mean one hundred officers each carrying an uncounted army into a loophole.

His father asks whether the loss would weaken the family after they crossed.

“It would change what he can do,” DIO says. “He would still be capable of protecting people. He would also have people willing to help him.” ᴰ

His mother asks whether he would become ill from the change.

“The passage is being designed around what the world already supports,” DIO says. “If that cannot be held safely, it does not happen.” ᴰ

“And if I say stop?” Jin-Woo asks, making sure his family hears it too. ᴶʷ

“We stop before the final commitment. I will tell you where that is.” ᴰ

* * *

Suho asks about the people left behind.

Jin-Woo explains the work needed before the end of the connection: duties handed back to people who can actually carry them, positions accounted for and a chance to make the invading forces reconsider the world itself. He won't treat leaving his family in safety as proof that every other person has become safe.

“And if the performance doesn't work?” Suho asks. ˢᵘ

“Then we don't pretend it did.” ᴶʷ

“You could still have the option to come here.” ᴹ

Mau speaks only after Jin-Woo turns toward him.

“But we'd have to decide what comes next with the failure in front of us. I won't tell you that one successful rehearsal is enough to settle all of it.” ᴹ

Suho takes the answer seriously.

“I want to keep helping,” he says. ˢᵘ

“I know,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“Here too. If we come.” ˢᵘ

“Then you will decide what that means when you've seen it.” ᴶʷ

Suho is quiet for a moment.

“I'd like to come,” he says. ˢᵘ

It is the first clear answer. Jin-Woo's hand moves toward the page and stops before he turns it into a mark too quickly.

* * *

Jin-Ah has practical questions.

What may be brought? Do the clothes need to be suited to a different winter? Does the house have a place where she can close a door? Would she need to arrive already knowing what work she would do?

Yuka answers the questions about the home. Mau explains that they will establish the allowed luggage before anybody packs more than the passage can safely carry. Jin-Woo says he can help with the things he knows she will miss and refuses to volunteer her to become useful on the first morning.

“I didn't ask to be useless,” she says. ᴶᵃ

“I know.” ᴶʷ

“Good. I would also like a first morning.” ᴶᵃ

Yuka smiles.

“We can arrange breakfast.” ʸ

“Has he told you what happens when we all try to decide where he should sit?” ᴶᵃ

“We have some experience.” ʸ

Jin-Ah laughs, then says she wants to come, provided there is time to speak to the people she will be leaving. She names that as part of her decision rather than a test the hosts must pass to prove themselves kind.

“Of course,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

* * *

His mother doesn't ask how many soldiers each of her children is worth.

She asks whether they can eat together after the difficult part. Jin-Woo says yes before looking toward anybody else.

“Then I want to be there,” she says. ᴶᵐ

His father speaks after her.

“I want you to make the arrangements you said you would make. Then I would like to see the place where you can sit down.” ᴶᶠ

“You don't have to come because she is,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

There is a brief silence.

“I know,” his father says. ᴶᶠ

Jin-Woo looks away from the table. Mau stays very still beside the threshold, suddenly aware of how easily people who love one another can miss the chance to hear an answer plainly given.

DIO lets the silence last until Jin-Woo is ready to speak again.

* * *

Hae-In has waited.

She asks the others on her side to give her a little room, then asks whether Jin-Woo wants the people with him to hear the next part. He says they can, unless she would prefer otherwise.

“You asked me to follow you into another world,” she says. ᴴᶦ

“I asked whether you wanted to.” ᴶʷ

“I know. I want you to hear that the answer is mine.” ᴴᶦ

He stops looking at the paper.

“I want to be with you,” she says. “I want to see what ordinary days we can have now. I also want you to tell me when being here is difficult, before you decide you should make the choice for me next time.” ᴴᶦ

“I will try.” ᴶʷ

“Good. So will I.” ᴴᶦ

There is a sound on the other side as she shifts closer to the call.

“And I'd like to meet the wife who tells you the question you actually need to answer.” ᴴᶦ

Yuka looks startled, then delighted.

“I'd like that too.” ʸ

Mau leans close enough to whisper that the alliance is becoming alarming. She presses his hand without taking her attention from the woman speaking to them.

* * *

They agree on the preparation, not an instant disappearance.

The family needs time for goodbyes and the list of things to bring. The people holding the old duties need real instructions. The performance has to be tested with the parts that still fail. DIO needs rest between the work, and the family now knows why a silence from this end will not mean Jin-Woo has vanished again.

Before the call closes, he tells them each good night by name or by the name he has used all his life.

This time they answer him one after another.

* * *

Mau begins stacking the papers after Jin-Woo stands, then notices the man isn't moving toward the door.

“Do you need a minute?” he asks. ᴹ

Jin-Woo shakes his head.

“I think I need to do something ordinary.” ᴶʷ

Yuka looks at Mau. He understands the suggestion before she says it.

“We're going to help carry the supper things,” he says. “You could come.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at the five answers they have written down, each with the questions still belonging to it.

Then he folds the page and comes with them.
