# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 25 — The Hundred He Keeps

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 25
**Source episode:** S11E13
**Continuity state:** B+184 morning. Jin-Woo selects exactly one hundred individual bonds for settled local life, including his Beru and named companions; no units donated to Mau, no containers bypass cap. Existing ordinary shadow supply distinct. Limited source call used to settle standing duties/release surplus deployment as part of intended final departure, not an active local million-unit army. Final physical passage/surrender not executed yet.

*When: Mau asks whether Jin-Woo wants help choosing, Jin-Woo says no before realizing what kind of help he means.*

Mau is holding two cups.

“I could also leave,” he says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at the cups, then at his guest-room table.

“Stay a little.” ᴶʷ

His Beru has taken the place beside the door. There is no scarf this morning. No hat, no notebook filled with suggestions about the quality of an apparent death. It stands so quietly that Mau would have looked for it if he hadn't known where it was.

Jin-Woo has names in front of him.

He does not need the page to remember them. Mau recognizes that too. The page makes it possible to decide where to begin and to stop at a place he can return to without pretending the rest has become less important.

“One hundred,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Including him.” ᴶʷ

Beru lowers its head.

Mau sets the cup within Jin-Woo's reach.

* * *

Some choices do not take long.

Beru. Igris. Bellion. Tank. The great shaman Jin-Woo calls Tusk, whose counterpart Mau has known as Fang. Kaisel, whose familiar shape makes Mau look toward the door as though his own dragon might have heard its name from somewhere else.

“Two of them will be interesting,” he says. ᴹ

“You already have two Beru.” ᴶʷ

“I was trying to sound prepared.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo smiles faintly.

The rest are not simply the ninety-four largest things he can fit behind those names. He chooses soldiers who know how to keep a position, bodies suited to carrying someone out rather than entering first, companions whose usefulness has never been the loudest thing about them. Every selection has to count as itself. No one comes hidden within another's place.

Mau does not offer to improve the arithmetic.

He asks whether Jin-Woo wants the drink warmer.

“It's fine,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

He drinks before it stops being true.

* * *

The harder part belongs to the world on the other side.

They have arranged a short use of the returning call before the final preparation. Jin-Woo can send the instructions which settle the standing positions and hear the report he needs without bringing the great deployment through the room. The passage is still being held as an address for voices and chosen relations, not an invitation to summon everything attached to them.

Mau stays outside that conversation.

He has offered to be available if Jin-Woo needs him. He has not been asked to witness every farewell merely because the structure supporting the call passes through work he helped make.

When Jin-Woo comes back, the list is complete.

“They understand,” he says. ᴶʷ

Mau waits.

“The part I leave will be released from the deployment after the last duties are settled. I won't keep calling for more from here because I decide my choice should have been easier.” ᴶʷ

Beru turns toward its king.

Jin-Woo rests a hand against the ant's arm before it can speak.

“You are staying because I want you here.” ᴶʷ

For a moment Mau thinks Beru will manage an answer.

It bows instead.

* * *

Hae-In has asked to hear the final choice before the physical operation begins.

Jin-Woo tells her he has made it. She asks whether he would like to talk before hearing the packing questions. He says the packing questions would help.

Jin-Ah has apparently taken charge of those.

The questions arrive in an order so organized that Yuka, listening by invitation, looks toward Fern. Fern has come with the allowed list for temporary guest rooms, not to decide which parts of another family's home deserve to exist here.

“I like her,” Fern says quietly. ᶠᵉ

Jin-Ah hears enough to ask who said it. Yuka introduces them. Within two minutes, they have established the difference between what the family needs for the first few nights and what might be considered after they choose a permanent arrangement.

Mau looks at Jin-Woo.

“You'll be outnumbered here too.” ᴹ

“I am beginning to suspect that's the attraction.” ᴶʷ

* * *

The rehearsal after lunch is the last one they will attempt without a real observer.

DIO and Ashborn have agreed to the duration and purpose. The signal will remain an imitation built from the retained models, supplied by Mau, separated from the guests' living authority. Jin-Woo will make the withdrawal which causes his presence on the source-side relation to end. Ciel will keep the family path distinct from the part used to display the event.

No actual attack is sent at Jin-Woo's body. The apparent contact happens where he is no longer standing.

Shoko watches that part twice.

“Again,” she says after the first. ˢʰ

“It worked,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Yes. I would like it to work when you know I am watching.” ˢʰ

He accepts the correction.

On the second attempt, Frieren loses the false presence at the right moment but can still distinguish the retreat because she knows exactly where to look. She explains the remaining weakness. They change the angle rather than increasing the power.

The next version gives her a reason to follow the wrong edge for the breath they need.

“That could work,” she says. ᶠ

Rimuru makes everyone hear the could.

* * *

Before they finish, both Beru put their hats back on.

Mau has been waiting for this with an apprehension he refuses to explain aloud. Jin-Woo doesn't even try to stop his own. They have earned their place in the rehearsal, and the fact that their comments arrive dressed like this has become a condition everyone accepts.

“My king,” Mau's Beru begins. ᴮᵐ

“Yes?” ᴹ

“The second gesture is unnecessary.” ᴮᵐ

Mau looks at the construction's recorded motion.

“It was Gojo's suggestion.” ᴹ

“An understandable difficulty,” Jin-Woo's Beru says. ᴮʲ

Gojo, who has come to see how the entrance turned out, puts a hand against his chest.

“I am being persecuted by the arts.” ᴳ

Mai laughs so openly that Momo has to hold the cloth before she drops it.

Jin-Woo looks at his hundred selected connections, then at the ridiculous hat his first choice has put on voluntarily.

For the first time all morning, the number looks like the beginning of something as well as its limit.
