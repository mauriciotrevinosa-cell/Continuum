# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 26 — The Audience They Did Not Choose

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 26
**Source episode:** S11E13
**Continuity state:** B+185 morning, source-side preparation/farewells completed by family between calls. Actual limited false-deity performance reaches a source-war observing force along original specific trace; no new named god killed, universal victory or uncontrolled portal. Actor/model is a mana-powered imitation, not live DIO/Ashborn. Jin-Woo voluntarily withdraws larger source signature as stage event and commits settling above National reference with 100 selected bonds. Family passage not yet used; remaining scoped support held for named crossings, no combat army access restored.

*When: the audience looks back, Mau understands why Jin-Woo wanted them to practise from the wrong angle.*

The preparation has taken most of the morning. Jin-Woo has heard his family say they are ready. He has accounted for the old duties he could settle, given the remaining instructions to people who can carry them and completed the choices he will not be allowed to undo by reaching for an army he agreed to release.

The house has cleared the work area. Only the people needed for the operation are close to it. The ordinary watch keeps its ordinary positions; there is no theatrical emergency sent through the household to make the performance feel more important.

DIO remains protected. Ashborn has chosen to watch. Neither is standing in the imitation made from what they permitted the others to use.

Jin-Woo looks toward Mau.

“If we stop before this part?” ᴶʷ

“We return to the work we've tested,” Ciel says. “After your source withdrawal is committed, we keep the smaller path for the family. The wider access is given up. It isn't a pause you can remove after they cross.” ᶜ

He nods.

Before they begin, he asks Ciel to keep the reference he is about to show separate from the staged signal. The final change will expose the relation between his inherited power, the learned operations he has fitted to it and the paths he is choosing to close.

“You may keep that structure,” he tells Mau. “The work. My family's conversation stays theirs.” ᴶʷ

Mau looks toward Noise before answering.

“The structure only. We study it afterward.” ᴹ

Noise agrees. It has observed fragments since Jin-Woo arrived; the offered reference gives those fragments a much fuller arrangement. A map of the inheritance can be retained without installing all its answers into Mau's body during somebody else's departure.

Mau offers him the last chance to ask the room to empty. He says no.

Then he tells them to begin.

* * *

They do not open the common room onto the battlefield.

The first thing sent along the old relation is the trace Jin-Woo's enemies already know. A familiar answer at a familiar contested edge. It belongs to the part of the conflict he described, a place where a new presence would be examined before anything expensive was committed to it.

Jin-Woo guides that beginning. He knows what the observers have been following. Noise keeps the prepared structure together, and Ciel holds the offered response apart from the return which belongs to the family.

The view they receive is narrow. A torn span of stone, something moving beyond it, light caught against an opening that does not belong to any sky Mau has seen. There is no army arriving in the room with them. There is a place listening for the man who has been in its way.

Something notices.

The pressure following that notice is enough to make Mau's attention try to spread toward every detail at once. Mini Ciel keeps the flood divided. He closes the part he doesn't need and holds the specific work in front of him.

“Different angle,” Frieren says. ᶠ

They change it before the imitation appears.

* * *

The false god has acquired no crown.

Mai removed the extra shape when it became clear that a silhouette trying too hard to announce itself gave the observers more places to find a mistake. What remains is a form around a presence, large enough to alter the relation of the space it occupies and simple enough to finish the brief action they have actually built.

Mau supplies the mana. Noise expresses the prepared model. The signal carries the two familiar, borrowed relations as though they have become one reason for the place to answer.

On the other side, the movement beyond the stone stops.

Jin-Woo makes his part of the exchange.

The apparent defender turns toward the new presence and commits to the opening they have rehearsed. The watcher receives the beginning of a real intention through a familiar trace, then the response which seems to swallow it.

The first thing Mau wants to do is look at Jin-Woo's actual body.

He doesn't need to. Yuka is beside the checked withdrawal point, watching for exactly that. Her small nod reaches him before he can lose the timing by verifying it twice.

Jin-Woo is safely there.

The familiar source signature is not.

* * *

Something strikes at the false presence.

The model has no army with which to win a second war. It has one prepared answer: the expression holds while the observer's test reaches the place in which a body would have stood. The physical substance expected there is not what the attacker finds. Beyond the imposed view, part of the stone tears away.

The loss makes the watcher change its attention.

“Don't follow it,” Ciel says. ᶜ

Mau has already begun to. He brings his attention back to the fixed work, lets the model complete its gesture and closes the visible edge at the moment agreed upon.

The apparent god gives the intruder no second useful examination.

The actual source access Jin-Woo has offered collapses into the narrow support reserved for the family. Noise leaves that path alone. Ciel checks the separation before allowing Mau to hear the report coming back from the observer's side.

The thing beyond the torn stone has moved away from the edge.

It is not fleeing in mindless terror. It is looking for the defender it can no longer find and refusing to spend the next attack without accounting for the presence which apparently removed him.

That is the hesitation they asked for.

* * *

Mau releases the expression.

The work closes exactly as it did in the local test. His body remains his own; the guests remain in their rooms. He can feel the mana he spent and the effort of keeping to the operation when every unexpected detail offered a reason to add something else.

Jin-Woo takes one step, then another.

For a moment his hand reaches toward a distance which no longer answers it.

Beru appears beside him. His Beru. The connection is present at the settled scale they agreed to keep.

Jin-Woo puts a hand against its arm.

“I'm here,” he says. ᴶʷ

Mau looks toward the hundred selected relations. No new soldier has become his. No unselected king has been harvested to make the choice seem less expensive.

“The family path?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶʷ

Ciel answers after checking it.

“Held.” ᶜ

* * *

Nobody declares the war finished.

They have an actual observer withdrawing from the contested edge, the loss of Jin-Woo's larger trace and the limited evidence his source-side arrangements can still provide before the last support ends. They have a new reason for the attacking forces to investigate rather than advance along the same assumption.

They do not have the private thoughts of every maker of worlds.

Jin-Woo hears the report, asks the questions needed to distinguish what moved from what someone hopes it means, and accepts the answer as the opening they have won.

“Then bring them,” he says. ᴶʷ

DIO raises her hand before the words can turn into a hurried operation.

“We ask each person at the threshold. As agreed.” ᴰ

Jin-Woo turns toward her.

“As agreed.” ᴶʷ

* * *

Mai is the first to notice the two critics have not spoken.

Both stand with their notebooks held against their chests. Jin-Woo's Beru is watching the king who has come through the performance alive. Mau's Beru is looking between its own king and the friend beside him, as though it has finally found a story for which the ending matters more than the notes.

“Well?” Mai asks softly. ᴹᵃ

Jin-Woo's Beru opens its mouth.

“My king was magnificent.” ᴮʲ

Jin-Woo shuts his eyes. Mau begins laughing, and the sound takes some of the last tightness out of the room.

“You asked for no tribute,” Mau reminds him. ᴹ

“I remember.” ᴶʷ

Yuka takes Mau's hand before he can find a way to keep working without stopping for water. He drinks while Ciel and DIO prepare the next, smaller operation.

On the table, the page with five names is still exactly where Jin-Woo can reach it.
