# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 65
## Chapter 27 — The Last Step Home

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S11E14
**Continuity state:** B+185 morning, continuous from #26. Named, bounded physical passage for five consenting relatives only, powered by already committed Jin-Woo source-access surrender/remaining scoped support. Hae-In, Suho, mother, father, Jin-Ah arrive; path closes, no free world travel or extra coin. Bodies at selected source state, no unasked rejuvenation. Jin-Woo100 individual bonds, Mau6.615 unchanged.

*When: Hae-In sees the room, she looks past the opening before looking at her husband.*

There is a table. A woman seated behind it with one hand resting flat while she holds the last part of the operation. An open way into an ordinary room beyond, people staying far enough back to leave the arrival clear.

Then Jin-Woo steps where she can see him properly.

She stops examining the room.

“There you are,” she says. ᴴᶦ

His hand lifts. For an instant he looks as though he expects the relation between them to behave like another signal, something he must hold correctly before it breaks.

“Here,” he says. ᴶʷ

DIO asks Hae-In whether she still wants to cross. The question is quiet and plain. Hae-In looks toward her, says yes and takes the step herself.

Jin-Woo reaches her before the second foot has quite settled.

Mau looks down at the edge of the work Ciel is holding, giving them the few seconds he can offer without ceasing to help. He hears Hae-In's breath leave her when the distance becomes a body she can hold.

Yuka keeps her hand against Mau's arm.

“She's through,” Ciel says privately. ᶜ

They keep the path steady for the next person.

* * *

Suho has a bag over one shoulder and another in his hand.

He looks at his parents together, shifts the second bag out of the way and tells DIO he wants to come before she has finished asking. She lets him hear the entire question anyway. He answers it again, more carefully.

Then he crosses.

Jin-Woo turns toward him. There is a moment in which both men try to decide how much of a greeting a grown son wants in front of strangers.

Suho puts the bag down.

That settles it.

Jin-Woo gets an arm around him and holds on. Hae-In keeps a hand against their backs. The three stand together while the people helping the operation continue to do the practical things which allow that moment to last.

Mau can feel Noise attending to it without turning the family into something to inspect. He asks it to keep the path steady and leave the rest to them.

It does.

* * *

Jin-Woo's mother has been ready with her answer since before the opening showed her where it led.

She repeats it when DIO asks, takes her bag in both hands and steps through without letting anybody take her balance away by trying to help too soon.

Her son comes toward her.

She sets the bag down before he reaches her. She has spent enough of her life knowing what she intends to do when one of her children finally stands within reach.

Jin-Woo bends to meet her arms.

“You're thin,” she says against him. ᴶᵐ

He laughs once, helplessly.

“Mother.” ᴶʷ

“I have eyes.” ᴶᵐ

She holds him harder. The next words don't come out clearly enough for anyone else to make a conversation of them. Jin-Woo answers into her shoulder, a few quiet syllables that belong there.

His father waits until DIO has acknowledged the next choice. He crosses with the things his wife refused to put in her own bag and stops just inside, watching his son being held.

Hae-In takes the extra bundle from him.

He thanks her before putting a hand on Jin-Woo's shoulder.

When his son turns, the hand becomes an embrace.

* * *

Jin-Ah is last.

She checks the place behind her, the bags they agreed could come and the people already standing on the new side. She has said the goodbyes she asked time for. There are things she will miss, and she has not mistaken agreeing to come for deciding that none of them matter.

“Still yes?” DIO asks. ᴰ

“Yes.” ᴶᵃ

She takes the step.

Her brother is waiting with one hand free. She gives him a bag, watches him automatically take it, and then gets both arms around him before he can decide what to do with the luggage.

“You can put that down,” she says. ᴶᵃ

He does.

“I noticed.” ᴶʷ

She laughs against his shoulder. His mother reaches to keep one of the bags from falling over. Suho catches it first, looking absurdly pleased to be useful at precisely that scale.

* * *

Ciel checks every arrival before closing the remaining support.

The family is here. The source side is clear. There is no sixth person waiting with an unasked question and no unnamed passenger hidden in the things they brought. Jin-Woo confirms the final release he agreed to make.

The path ends.

Mau feels it close where the morning's work had required so much attention. The absence is plain, smaller and more final than any display could have made it. What remains in Jin-Woo is the life and the hundred chosen bonds he has decided to keep here.

DIO leans back.

Rimuru puts the water in her hand. She takes it without pretending she doesn't need the rest.

Jin-Woo looks toward the place which is no longer a way back.

His wife touches his arm.

He turns toward her.

* * *

The introductions happen badly, which is to say they happen while people are still holding one another.

Mau gives his name. Yuka gives hers. Rimuru begins to explain where the family can put the bags and is interrupted by Jin-Woo's mother thanking him for the chair her son has apparently learned to use.

“We worked very hard,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“I heard.” ᴶᵐ

Jin-Woo looks at the two of them with the expression of a man recognizing a threat he has voluntarily brought home.

Shoko comes forward after he invites her. She asks the new arrivals how they feel, watches each find their footing and says they can sit before anyone tries to turn a welcome into a tour. Nobody's first useful act in the new world has to be carrying every bag themselves.

Mau offers the inventory for the larger pieces. Jin-Ah asks whether she will be able to get her things back without him. He says yes, he will put them where the family chooses as soon as they reach the rooms.

She lets him take the heavy bag and keeps the smaller one.

“Important?” he asks. ᴹ

“Mine,” she says. ᴶᵃ

He nods and leaves it in her hand.

* * *

Before they leave the guest area, Jin-Woo asks whether his family may thank DIO properly.

She tells him she would like that after she has sat quietly for a little while. His mother immediately says they will come when she wants company and asks whether there is anything else she would like with the water.

DIO looks surprised.

Then pleased.

Mau watches her accept the offer of something ordinary instead of having to supply another answer about the structure of a world.

“Later,” she says. “I would like that.” ᴰ

Noise remains near the shared table while the family moves toward the outer room. Hae-In looks back once, noticing the shadow shape beside the woman who helped bring them through.

Mau tells her they can introduce everyone when the work is finished and the people doing it have had their rest.

“Of course,” she says. ᴴᶦ

Jin-Woo hears her say it and smiles.
