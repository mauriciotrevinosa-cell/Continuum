## Chapter 28 — Your Turn to Sit

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 65
**Reading order:** 28
**Source episode:** S11E14
**Continuity state:** B+185 late morning. Five arrivals settle temporarily in actual guest accommodation; permanent home decisions deferred to family. First private family time/parental reunion receives space, no makeover/extra summons/new staff. DIO rests. Household introductions are by residents, shadow ordinary forms do not gain speech.

*When: Jin-Woo's mother tells him to sit, three people move a chair.*

Rimuru stops first. Yuka stops second. Jin-Woo looks at the remaining chair in Mau's hands and gives him an expression that should have been discouraging.

Mau puts it down behind him.

“I'm helping.” ᴹ

“I can see that.” ᴶʷ

His mother waits until he sits before accepting the place beside him. His father settles on her other side. Hae-In remains standing for a moment to put the smaller things where they will be able to find them again. Suho gets the last bundle out of the way without being assigned the task.

The guest rooms have enough space for the first nights. Nobody has drawn permanent lines around the family before they have had a chance to see the place. Fern explains which rooms are ready, where the meals happen and whom to ask if something doesn't work.

Jin-Ah asks about the nearest window she can open herself.

Yuka shows her.

Mau places the stored bags where the family points. He waits while Jin-Ah opens the larger one to check she has the thing she wanted, then leaves it with her instead of helpfully hiding it in storage again.

Jin-Woo's mother is still looking at her son.

* * *

Mau offers to fetch food and discovers the others have already arranged some.

Rem brings the tray to the door with Taihei, who was there when the request reached the kitchen and volunteered to carry the heavier side. They introduce themselves. Rem tells the family the food can stay here if they would rather have a quiet first meal, and that coming to the common room later is an invitation, not a requirement to meet everybody while still holding luggage.

Jin-Woo's father thanks her.

His mother asks whether there is enough for the people helping too.

“Yes,” Rem says. “And they'll remember if there isn't.” ᴿᵉ

Mau looks wounded.

“I haven't said anything.” ᴹ

“Yet.” ʸ

Jin-Ah studies them with growing interest.

“Is this how he gets fed?” she asks, pointing at her brother. ᴶᵃ

“It has been working,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Keep doing it.” ᴶᵃ

Jin-Woo reaches for his cup and decides there are too many people to answer at once.

* * *

They are given the room to themselves after the practical questions are finished.

Jin-Woo watches the last of the hosts leave, then turns toward his family as though the absence of an audience has made him less certain how to begin.

His mother doesn't have that difficulty.

She takes his hand, turns it over and holds it between hers. He has used it for work she cannot measure and distances she has had to let him describe without pretending she has seen them. It is still the hand of her son.

“Let me look at you,” she says. ᴶᵐ

He stays still.

His father asks whether he has pain from the crossing. He says no. The answer is checked again by the way his mother watches him lift the cup, because she has not decided that a man becomes a reliable reporter of every discomfort simply by getting very powerful.

“I slept here,” he tells her. ᴶʷ

“I heard.” ᴶᵐ

“Properly.” ᴶʷ

“Good. You can do it again.” ᴶᵐ

Jin-Ah laughs from beside the bag she has finally opened.

Hae-In takes the place across from them and watches her husband discover there is no urgent task with which to interrupt his own welcome.

* * *

His father waits until they have eaten a little before asking about the hundred.

“You chose them?” ᴶᶠ

“Yes.” ᴶʷ

“And you said goodbye to what you needed to leave?” ᴶᶠ

Jin-Woo looks down at the cup.

“As well as I could.” ᴶʷ

His father nods. He doesn't tell him it must be nothing beside having the family here. He has heard enough to know the choice carried things a person can be glad about and still grieve.

“You can tell us about them another day,” he says. ᴶᶠ

Jin-Woo looks up.

“I would like that.” ᴶʷ

“Then another day.” ᴶᶠ

It is the first promise made in the new room that does not concern whether they can arrive at all.

* * *

Suho asks whether he can look outside after they finish.

His father starts to rise, then catches himself because his mother is still holding his hand. Suho notices. He glances toward Hae-In, who gives him a smile small enough not to embarrass either of them.

“I can go with the people showing us around,” he says. ˢᵘ

“Take your sister's bag first,” Jin-Woo's mother says, forgetting for a second which generation she is directing. ᴶᵐ

There is a silence.

Jin-Ah looks at Suho.

“I have always wanted someone else to receive that instruction.” ᴶᵃ

Suho laughs and picks up the bag she points to. His grandmother apologizes while laughing herself. The mistake has made the room feel familiar faster than an explanation could have done.

* * *

Mau is waiting in the passage when they are ready for the short walk.

He asks Suho and Jin-Ah what they want to see first and gets two different answers. Suho wants the open space where he heard people training. Jin-Ah wants to know where she can buy the things she decided not to carry.

“Both are achievable,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuka takes the second question, explaining the difference between what they keep in the house, what Hollis can arrange and what depends on the next trip. Mau notices her making room for Jin-Ah to decide which needs are immediate.

They keep the first walk short. They show the common building, the way to the guest rooms and the place where the paths return to one another. A helmet turns as they pass the ordinary watch. Mau explains who it is and how to get a person's attention if they need more than a gesture.

Suho looks toward Mau's shadow, then back at the sentry.

“You use them for this?” ˢᵘ

“He is good at it,” Mau says. ᴹ

The helmet dips.

Jin-Ah thanks the soldier and asks Yuka which door leads back to her mother before the walk becomes longer than the older woman has been promised.

* * *

Jin-Woo stays sitting after they leave.

His mother has let go of his hand so he can eat. His father has moved the water closer without saying anything about it. Hae-In is opening the smaller bundle they kept with them: a few familiar things, carefully chosen, neither treasures won from a war nor evidence that anybody has left their whole life behind without looking back.

He watches them make room on the table.

For a moment he reaches toward the old distance.

It does not answer as it once did.

His mother asks whether he wants more tea.

He turns toward her.

“Yes,” he says. ᴶʷ
