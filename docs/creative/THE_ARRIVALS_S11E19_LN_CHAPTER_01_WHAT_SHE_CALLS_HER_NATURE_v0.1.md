# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 66
## Chapter 7 — What She Calls Her Nature

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 66
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S11E19
**Continuity state:** B+189 noon. Day-of inspection and actual mutual meeting vow B before speech. Mau/Yuka/Rimuru/Frieren attend; Gojo/Yuta return-side, Igris coordinating, Shoko/Jin-Woo house-side. Makima alone. Prospective wording exactly scoped to initiations/orders/authorizations/activations during interval; no retroactive historical fact-verdict. Ciel linked, no compulsion/attack yet. Makima's account is her own framing, not omniscient canon/exoneration.

*When: Makima says good afternoon, Yuka puts the final copy on the table.*

“First,” she says. ʸ

Makima looks at the page, then at the ring on the hand holding it in place.

“Of course.” ᴹᵏ

They have checked the approaches again. The return line is clear. Igris has acknowledged his position; Gojo and Yuta have taken theirs on the other side of the agreed space. Nothing about the fixed protection has moved with the people sitting down beyond it.

Mau keeps his attention broad enough to know where his wife and friends are without letting every movement in the woods become the center of the room. Ciel is with him. The offered emergency route is available in the form Yuka chose; her own pattern remains hers to reach for.

Rimuru reads the undertaking aloud.

From acceptance until the conversation and separate departures are complete, none of them will initiate an attack, issue or authorize a hostile instruction, activate a delayed attack or exert compulsion against the other participants or their named return escorts. No marks or followers will be placed during the visit. Defense against an independent attack remains permitted. Ending the conversation requires no permission from the person who would like it to continue.

Each accepts their own restraint.

Makima is last.

The pledge takes.

Mau feels the same clear relation he has recognized in agreements which held before. There is no strain in her acceptance, no second voice bargaining for a different clause. Ciel confirms the undertaking is active without claiming it can tell him which sentence the woman will lie in first.

Rimuru sets the copy where all of them can see it.

“We agreed on half an hour,” he says. ᴿ

Makima nods.

Then Yuka takes the chair beside Mau.

* * *

“What did you want to explain?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Makima folds her hands on the table.

“Why I thought we could help one another.” ᴹᵏ

“You put me in a gate.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

She doesn't soften the answer. Mau had prepared for a different opening, something which would ask him to contest a fact before reaching her reason for it. The simple admission makes him listen more carefully, not less.

“You wanted to see what it made of me.” ᴹ

“I wanted to see what you could become.” ᴹᵏ

“I was already someone,” he says. ᴹ

Her gaze rests on him a moment longer.

“I know that matters to you.” ᴹᵏ

Frieren speaks before Mau can.

“It matters to the explanation.” ᶠ

Makima turns toward her. There is no dislike in the movement that Mau can point to. There is an adjustment, as though the speaker has placed another piece where the woman must acknowledge it before going on.

“Then let me begin earlier,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

She tells them what she calls herself.

Control. A devil shaped by fear, capable of turning an unequal relation into something more direct than persuasion when the conditions belong to her. She doesn't pretend this world's limits have disappeared because she has named the thing. She speaks about the distinction between the power answering and the arrangements she can make while waiting for it to answer more completely.

Rimuru asks which parts require the person on the other end to agree.

She smiles faintly.

“You have seen me sign an agreement.” ᴹᵏ

“I asked about the other parts.” ᴿ

“Not all of them.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's hand stays flat on the table.

“Then asking us to hear you doesn't make us free afterward unless you choose to let us remain that way.” ʸ

“That is why you asked for the terms.” ᴹᵏ

Mau watches the place where her hands meet. The pledge remains undisturbed. Saying what she might want is not an attempt to do it to them through the words.

He makes himself keep those things separate.

“What does protecting my family mean to you?” he asks. ᴹ

* * *

Makima gives him an answer with attractive edges.

A place which cannot be easily threatened. People who can sleep without deciding who will bleed for the road tomorrow. Enough power, properly directed, that enemies stop treating the people he loves as a useful handle.

Mau lets her finish.

“Who decides what properly directed means?” ᴹ

“Someone has to.” ᴹᵏ

“That wasn't the question.” ᴹ

Her eyes move briefly to Yuka, then back.

“You already make decisions for a great many people.” ᴹᵏ

“And they tell me when they don't want one.” ᴹ

“Sometimes.” ᴹᵏ

The word is quiet enough that he could let it pass. He feels Yuka shift beside him and doesn't mistake her staying close for needing him to answer every part on her behalf.

“I tell him,” she says. ʸ

Makima inclines her head.

“I have noticed.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

The next part is the reason Makima says she came.

She describes a wish which sounds almost ordinary when she leaves out the methods. Closeness. A world with fewer things worth fearing. People kept within reach of a power which can prevent the particular injuries that make a life difficult to love.

Then the method returns, quietly, inside the wish.

“My nature makes equal arrangements difficult,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Difficult for you,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“And for the people you make smaller to solve it.” ᴿ

She looks at him with what could almost be appreciation for a precise objection.

“Would you rather I pretend it is kindness because I enjoy the result?” ᴹᵏ

“I'd rather you answer whether you intend to stop,” Mau says. ᴹ

Makima regards him for several seconds.

“I would like you to reconsider which side of me you stand on.” ᴹᵏ

There is the request. Not hidden behind the explanation anymore.

Mau sits back a little, keeping his hand where Yuka can reach it if she wants to.

“Then you'll have to explain why that would be a choice I'd still own.” ᴹ

* * *

Frieren asks whether Makima has ever wanted somebody whose answer she could not direct.

For the first time since the pledge took, the woman's expression changes without looking like a deliberate adjustment for the audience.

“Yes,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Mau notices Ciel becoming very attentive to the answer without taking it as evidence of honesty.

“Who?” he asks. ᴹ

“Pochita.” ᴹᵏ

The name means nothing useful to him yet.

Makima can see that. She turns one hand over on the table as though offering them an object they have been trying to describe without seeing its shape.

“I should tell you about Denji first.” ᴹᵏ
