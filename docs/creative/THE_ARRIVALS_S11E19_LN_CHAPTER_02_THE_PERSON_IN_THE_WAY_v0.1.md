*When: Makima explains the boy, Mau waits to hear which part of him she wanted.*

The answer is not slow in arriving.

Pochita was with him. Pochita had chosen something in that life which Makima could observe, interrupt and resent, but could not make into the choice she wanted simply by deserving it more.

She talks about Denji with enough detail to make clear that she paid attention to what could move him. Food. Shelter. Affection offered at exactly the distance from which it could be withdrawn. A person can be described very closely without ever being allowed to become the center of his own story.

Yuka looks at Mau once, then back at the woman speaking.

“You learned what he needed,” she says. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“And used it.” ʸ

Makima doesn't deny that either.

Mau feels his fingers begin to close against his palm. He opens them before the movement becomes the only thing he is doing with the account.

“What did you hate about him?” he asks. ᴹ

* * *

Makima's answer places Denji between herself and something she believes she understood better than he could.

She wanted Pochita. The boy's ordinary wants kept occupying the life which held him. He received a closeness she had made into an ideal, and he used that life for things which looked small beside what she thought the power should be allowed to accomplish.

“Because he was a person,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“Because he stood where I wanted something else to stand,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Mau looks directly at her.

“That sounds familiar.” ᴹ

She returns the look.

“You have more say in what you are becoming than he did.” ᴹᵏ

“You tried to change that.” ᴹ

“And you came back.” ᴹᵏ

The sentence is spoken almost kindly. It contains none of the cost, none of the days which did not happen at the same speed for anyone outside the gate. Mau feels the old bodily answer begin and keeps his attention on the table in front of him.

Yuka puts her hand over his.

He turns his palm beneath it.

“Coming back didn't make what you did a gift,” he says. ᴹ

“I did not call it one.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Pochita's part of the explanation is worse in a different way.

Makima describes a power which, in the world she knows, could make more than a body disappear. A devil eaten by him could take the thing its name represented out of the world's remembrance. She gives examples only as claims from the history she carries, not proof laid on the table for the others to examine.

Mau asks whether she has seen the same effect here.

“No.” ᴹᵏ

“Then we don't know what it does under this world's rules.” ᴹ

“We know what it could mean.” ᴹᵏ

Frieren speaks quietly.

“We know what you want it to mean.” ᶠ

Makima looks toward her with renewed interest.

“A world without certain kinds of suffering would not be a small thing.” ᴹᵏ

“And the people who no longer get to decide what they remember?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Mau's hand goes very still beneath hers.

She is not asking because she has forgotten the life he told them. She is asking because she remembers what it cost him to carry it alone.

* * *

Makima returns the conversation to the present.

She speaks of Mau's capacity to build, the way different powers have begun to make sense around him and the number of people whose safety depends on whether he continues to become more than the world expected. She presents the possibility of cooperation as though refusing it would require him to prefer a smaller chance of protecting them.

“You could help me do something useful with what I am,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Useful to whom?” he asks. ᴹ

“To the people you brought with you. To the ones waiting at home.” ᴹᵏ

Mau listens to the answer without lending her the right to choose which of those people can be spent on the way to it.

Inside, Noise has been following the account.

*Maybe,* it says. ᴺ

Mau keeps his face toward Makima and asks inwardly what it means.

The answer is a possibility, not a construction already underway: a relation between the thing Makima describes as her nature and the ways a power can be held apart from the choices made with it. Something to examine. Something that might help, if they knew what part was true.

He feels how easily wanting to help could outrun knowing what he is offering.

*At home,* he tells Noise. *We discuss it there. We need to know everything that other power can actually do before we give her something enormous.* ᴹ

Noise accepts the limit.

Ciel keeps the idea as an unanswered question.

* * *

Makima has noticed the pause.

“Did someone have a suggestion?” ᴹᵏ

“A question,” Mau says. “It comes home with us before we do anything about it.” ᴹ

“I thought you might say that.” ᴹᵏ

“Then you know where the answer is today.” ᴹ

Rimuru asks which names, places or observable facts she is willing to leave them for checking. She supplies a few particulars about herself and separates the ones which would require someone from her world from the ones the house might examine here.

She is not giving them the means to verify every claim. She is giving them enough work that refusing to look would leave a question she can use later.

Mau lets Rimuru write the names without calling the act trust.

* * *

Near the end, Yuka asks the question Makima has repeatedly approached from other sides.

“What would you do differently?” ʸ

Makima looks at her.

“I am asking.” ᴹᵏ

“You're asking for a different position. I asked about what happens to us in it.” ʸ

The woman's gaze rests on Yuka's hand over Mau's.

“You would be protected.” ᴹᵏ

Mau answers this time.

“She gets to say what that means for her.” ᴹ

“So you have told me.” ᴹᵏ

He hears the impatience hidden inside the mild sentence. It is small, but it is hers.

* * *

Rimuru closes the notes when the agreed time is almost over.

They have an account to compare with what they know. A request they have not accepted. A dangerous name whose powers they will need to investigate before even deciding which parts of Makima's description could apply in this world.

Mau checks the return through the watch and receives the ordinary acknowledgment from Igris. Yuka shifts in her chair, ready to stand beside him when the conversation ends.

Makima leaves her hands folded on the table.

She has not touched either of them. She has issued no instruction to anyone else. The pledge remains as clear as it was when she accepted it.

Outside the place they have been watching, the devil looks at the time.
