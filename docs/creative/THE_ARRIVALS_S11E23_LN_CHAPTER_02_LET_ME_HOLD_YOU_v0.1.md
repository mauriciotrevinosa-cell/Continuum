# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 66
## Chapter 16 — Let Me Hold You

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 66
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S11E23
**Continuity state:** B+192 evening/night. Yuka home after checked repair, faint new mark remains, no enforced loss of autonomy. They discuss fear/duty and give reciprocal comfort, not guilt punishment or instant trauma cure. Mau finally sleeps after >three days awake. Small shared flotation with happiness uses established sleep/Authority behavior, no new technique or color recovery. Yuka settles/blankets both without telling him to stop.

*When: Yuka reaches for the cup, Mau has already picked it up.*

She looks at his hand.

He looks at hers.

“Water?” he asks. ᴹ

“That was the plan.” ʸ

He gives it to her. She drinks while he checks the pillow, the cloth near her collar and the place where she has put her feet. None needs changing. He notices that after beginning to change the pillow anyway.

Yuka catches his wrist gently.

“Sit.” ʸ

He sits beside her at once.

She puts the cup down herself.

* * *

The room is familiar enough to make the missing days strange in another way.

Her things are where she left them. The catch she asked him to undo before the meeting is still on the chair. One of the pages Ori corrected has been left flat because she wanted to show Yuka the new mark when she came back.

Mau follows her gaze and starts to explain who brought it.

“You can tell me slowly,” she says. ʸ

“Okay.” ᴹ

He does, and for a little while they talk about the map. Frieren bringing Ori. The folded cloth beside the clinic bed. The new family learning which way the common room is and the exact moment Umaru discovered Jin-Woo's mother was good at cards.

Yuka laughs once, quietly.

Mau stops halfway through the next sentence to look at her.

“What?” she asks. ʸ

He shakes his head.

She reaches for his face.

* * *

They are close enough that she can feel how long he has been holding himself still.

She draws his head down until his forehead rests against hers. His eyes close. He lets out a breath that doesn't finish cleanly.

“I'm here,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You keep looking like you need to check.” ʸ

He opens his eyes and finds her watching him.

“I do.” ᴹ

She nods. It isn't an accusation he has to escape. She has the first small fragments of the injury and the account of a room which stayed awake around her. He has all the hours between the two.

“You can hold me,” she says. ʸ

He puts his arms around her. At first it is so careful that she presses closer to show him where she wants to be.

Then he holds on.

* * *

The apology comes against her shoulder.

“I'm sorry.” ᴹ

She rubs a hand over the back of his neck.

“Mau.” ʸ

“I checked. We checked everything we knew to check, and I thought—” ᴹ

The words break apart.

She lets him finish the part he can. The line he recognized. The response reaching her after something had already happened. The hand which stayed warm without squeezing his back. Shoko telling him why more power would hurt instead of help.

He keeps returning to the moment before the cut as though saying it accurately enough might put a different action there.

“I said I wouldn't let it happen again.” ᴹ

Yuka holds him while he cries.

She doesn't answer with a promise that he will never be frightened this way again. They have just learned what an assurance can leave outside its words.

“You reached me,” she says. “I'm here with you.” ʸ

He draws a rough breath.

She stays close enough for him to feel hers.

* * *

When they can look at one another again, Yuka touches the familiar old mark near his collar.

“Now you know a little of what it's like over here,” she says, trying for the gentle joke she couldn't quite make in the clinic. ʸ

His face crumples again.

“I'm sorry. I'm sorry.” ᴹ

“I wasn't trying to hurt you.” ʸ

“I know. I just—if I'm not there, who is supposed to be? How do I look at somebody else and let it be them?” ᴹ

She listens to the question as the real thing it is. This is the man who has kept coming home to her, carrying what he could prevent and what he couldn't, trying to make the next person safer than the last.

“I know why you go,” she says. “I love that you care. I hate what people do with it.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“I can be proud of you and frightened for you at the same time. You don't have to decide one makes the other wrong.” ʸ

His hand comes over hers against his chest.

“I didn't understand the chair,” he says. ᴹ

She knows which chair he means.

“You do now.” ʸ

* * *

They take their time putting the evening back into ordinary things.

She washes her face with him passing the cloth instead of trying to do the whole thing for her. He gets the blanket she asks for, then leaves the other one where it is when she says one is enough. She lets him help with the sleeve which catches at her wrist and does the next one herself.

There is no great victory in any of it. He still watches too closely when she bends. She still has to tell him what she wants before he can stop guessing from the fear in his own body.

They can practise that tomorrow.

Tonight she wants him beside her.

* * *

Mau tells Ciel he is going to sleep before he lies down.

The statement is so simple that he has to say it again inwardly for himself.

*She is steady,* Ciel tells him. *We know what to watch. You can rest.* ᶜ

*Thank you.* ᴹ

Noise remains close enough for him to feel the answer without a new piece of work arriving with it.

He thanks it too.

Then Yuka lifts his arm and settles beneath it in the same small claim she made on the evening before they left.

He looks down at her.

“I love you,” he says. ᴹ

“I love you.” ʸ

She puts her hand against his cheek until his eyes close.

* * *

Yuka wakes a little later because the blanket has slipped away from her shoulder.

The bed is just below them.

Only a little. Enough that the edge of the blanket trails across it while the two bodies beneath the rest have risen together. Mau's arm stays around her. His face is quiet in a way she hasn't seen since opening her eyes in the clinic.

She remembers what Rimuru has told her about good dreams.

For a moment Mau shifts as if trying, even in sleep, to keep them from rising any farther. They settle a fraction lower and remain there.

Yuka doesn't wake him to say anything about it.

She turns carefully until she is lying against his chest, secure in the arm he has kept around her, and pulls the blanket over them both.

He makes a small sound in his sleep. Holds her a little closer.

She puts her hand over his.

“Sleep,” she whispers. ʸ

The room stays quiet around them.
