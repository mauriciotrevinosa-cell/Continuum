*When: Ori asks whether she may borrow Mau, Frieren looks up from the book she has been reading.*

“For what?” ᶠ

“An expedition.” ᴼ

Frieren closes the book with a finger keeping the place.

“Tell me.” ᶠ

Ori has brought the map. Not because she intends to use the old dungeon's route in another place, but because she wants to show which parts she did herself and which parts Mau helped her understand when she was leading.

“He can help again,” she says. ᴼ

“Have you asked him?” ᶠ

Ori looks toward the common-room table.

Mau is there with Yuka. She has almost finished the thing she and Mai have been working on; he is holding a corner and answering slowly when she asks about the next one. Every so often he looks at the place beneath her collar instead of the cloth.

“I want to ask properly,” Ori says. ᴼ

Frieren watches him for a moment too.

Then she asks Ori what she would like to do with him.

* * *

The idea is small enough to be considered as an outing rather than a new undertaking for the whole house.

One of the current E routes has a short, surveyed approach. Frieren has seen the ordinary inspection and will check the place again before anyone leaves. Mau would be capable of dealing with what the appraisal and scouting have actually found without pushing his body into anything like the recent battles.

Ori would like to lead. She wants a job, supplies and somebody who can answer a question without doing the whole thing before she gets to finish asking it.

Frieren looks at the map.

“That is a fair thing to want,” she says. ᶠ

“And he likes being the assistant.” ᴼ

Frieren's mouth moves a little.

“He likes you telling people about it.” ᶠ

Ori considers that, pleased by the possibility.

* * *

They ask the adults who need to know before asking the assistant to report for duty.

Shoko wants to know what is expected of Mau. She has seen him sleep, eat and return to ordinary activity since Yuka woke. A calm outing with help available is different from signing him up for a contest because he looks less exhausted.

Frieren tells her exactly what Ori has proposed.

“That sounds possible,” Shoko says. “Check the frame and keep the first plan small.” ˢʰ

Yuka hears next.

She listens with the seriousness Ori wants the proposal to receive, asks what she intends to take and whether she wants help putting it together.

Ori says she has another person to ask about that part.

“Then I won't interfere,” Yuka says. ʸ

She looks toward Mau without calling him over. He has gone back to asking Rimuru one of the questions they agreed the others could help investigate. There is nothing wrong with the question. There is also a little girl beside her who would like to have him for an afternoon.

Yuka gives Ori's hand a small squeeze.

“Ask him,” she says. ʸ

* * *

Mai hears a knock at the workshop later.

Ori stands on the other side holding something in both hands. Mai looks past her, sees Frieren far enough away to leave the request hers and crouches so they can speak without the whole passage joining in.

“An important commission?” Mai asks. ᴹᵃ

Ori nods.

She explains what an expedition leader needs to look like. Pockets. Somewhere for the map. A place to keep a light which will not fall out when she bends down to examine something. She has opinions about the boots too, though Mai tells her those will have to begin with the boots that actually fit.

Ori accepts that practical amendment.

Then she opens her hands.

There are sweets in them.

* * *

For a moment Mai doesn't say anything.

“For the work,” Ori explains. ᴼ

Mai looks at the little payment, then at the face waiting to find out whether it is enough.

“That is a very serious offer,” she says. ᴹᵃ

Ori nods again.

Mai takes one sweet and leaves the others in the small hands.

“A deposit. The rest after you approve the equipment.” ᴹᵃ

Ori looks relieved, then remembers the other condition.

“Mau can't know yet.” ᴼ

Mai glances toward the room beyond the passage, where the man in question is being asked to leave his papers alone long enough to help Yuka carry something ordinary.

“Don't worry,” she says. “I'll handle him.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

At supper, Mau sees Ori and Mai exchange a look.

He asks Yuka whether he ought to be concerned.

“About those two?” she says. ʸ

“I recognize coordination.” ᴹ

She takes his hand beneath the table.

“You'll survive.” ʸ

He looks at her, hearing the phrase differently for a moment. She sees it happen and holds his hand a little more firmly.

Then Ori asks him to pass the bread, and he does.
