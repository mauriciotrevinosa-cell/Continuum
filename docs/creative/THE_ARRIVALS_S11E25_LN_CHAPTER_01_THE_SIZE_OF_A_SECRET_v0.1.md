# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 1 — The Size of a Secret

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S11E25
**Continuity state:** B+195 morning. Mai fulfills Ori's confidential explorer-kit commission through her existing support contract with Mau. Ordinary sewn garments and fitted boots remain ordinary; limited Construction supplies useful fittings, not a new power or an armored child. Yuka remains physically well. Departure not yet begun.

*When: Mai asks whether he has time for something important, Mau puts his pen down before asking how important.*

“Extremely.” ᴹᵃ

“Then yes.” ᴹ

“Good. I'll warn you before I start.” ᴹᵃ

She turns to leave.

He watches her reach the door, waits for the rest of the explanation and finally has to call her back.

“What am I helping with?” ᴹ

Mai considers him over her shoulder.

“Confidential.” ᴹᵃ

“I see.” ᴹ

“You don't.” ᴹᵃ

“No. That's becoming clear.” ᴹ

She holds her hands apart, then adjusts the distance a little.

“About this big.” ᴹᵃ

Mau looks at the space between them.

“That doesn't tell me whether it's a bowl or an assassination.” ᴹ

“I'll put you down for a bowl.” ᴹᵃ

From the other end of the table, Yuka makes a sound which could have been a cough if she had put any effort into it.

Mau turns.

She is folding the cloth she and Mai finished yesterday. She has already been outside, already carried her own cup back and already endured him asking whether she would like him to carry it for her. Her sleeves are pushed up. When she sees him looking, she folds the last corner deliberately and waits.

“You know.” ᴹ

“I know several things.” ʸ

“About the bowl.” ᴹ

“I'll tell you if I hear anything about an assassination.” ʸ

Mai leaves before he can recruit a second witness.

For a while Mau manages to return to the page in front of him. He has drawn a line between the time the agreement began and the time the attack happened. Rimuru has the other copy. There are questions beside the line, some underlined more than once.

Yuka finishes folding.

“What were you going to do after this?” she asks. ʸ

He almost says that he needs to work something out first. He hears the shape of the answer before he gives it.

“Help with a bowl, apparently.” ᴹ

“Then put that somewhere it won't get tea on it.” ʸ

She doesn't ask him to throw it away. She touches his shoulder as she goes past, and he puts the paper inside his notebook.

* * *

Mai has no intention of constructing a whole outfit when there is perfectly good fabric in the workshop.

Ori discovers this while holding up a design with more pockets than the front of the jacket has room for.

“If I make every pocket you drew, you won't be able to bend,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Ori studies the drawing.

“What if these two are inside?” ᴼ

“Better. What goes in them?” ᴹᵃ

“Supplies.” ᴼ

“Which supplies?” ᴹᵃ

Ori puts a little packet of sweets on the table.

Mai nods as if she has just been shown a necessary instrument.

“One on the inside. Not both. You need somewhere for the map.” ᴹᵃ

Momo is sitting beside the sewing, drawing a short line where a strap needs to lie flatter. She asks Ori to lift her arm. When the jacket pulls at the shoulder, she unpins it rather than telling Ori to keep her arm lower.

Frieren sits near the open door with the boots. She has inspected the soles and returned the little stones caught in them to a dish. Ori sits down for the fitting and promptly tries to stand again when Mai mentions the hat.

“Boot first,” Frieren says. ᶠ

Ori sits.

The hat is what finally makes her quiet.

It isn't grand. The brim can fold back so she can look up without tipping the whole thing off, and the band holds a small light securely. Mai has left a place for the emblem Ori wants to add herself.

Ori holds it in both hands.

“Can I keep it after?” ᴼ

Mai looks up from the fastening she is measuring.

“It's yours.” ᴹᵃ

“Even if we don't use everything?” ᴼ

“Especially then. Equipment doesn't get confiscated because the expedition went well.” ᴹᵃ

Ori puts it on before anyone has finished checking the band.

Momo laughs and reaches to straighten it.

“Now we have to work around the expedition leader.” ᴹᵒ

“We were already doing that,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

The pieces Mai does construct are small enough for her to explain before beginning. A secure clasp for the map case. A fitting which lets Ori release the light with one hand. A smooth keeper for the strap, with no edge to catch a finger.

She calls Mau through their existing arrangement, tells him the scale and waits for his answer. The support comes as agreed: his reserves carry what her own would struggle to supply, his body takes the burden they arranged for him to take. Mai still watches her work and her breathing. A larger source does not excuse a careless design.

At the table across the house, Mau feels the first change and stops turning a page.

*That is a very small bowl.* ᴹ

Mai's answer arrives with the composure of somebody who has expected him to be annoying.

*Confidential.* ᴹᵃ

He laughs once, unexpectedly.

Yuka looks over.

“Am I allowed to know?” she asks. ʸ

“Apparently not. I'm financing organized secrecy.” ᴹ

“That sounds like your family.” ʸ

He turns the page without reading the first line again.

* * *

By the time the last fitting is finished, Ori has tested the pockets by crouching, standing and walking around the table. Nothing spills. The light stays where it belongs. The map slides out without pulling the strap over her head.

Mai asks her to do it once more with the gloves she actually intends to wear.

Ori discovers that one loop is too small.

“Good thing we tried it,” Mai says, and changes it. ᴹᵃ

There is no disappointment in the correction. That seems to matter to Ori. She points out another place she would like moved, and Mai listens.

When they finish, Ori lays out the remaining sweets from yesterday's offer.

Mai takes a second one.

“Balance settled.” ᴹᵃ

“There are more.” ᴼ

“Those are expedition supplies.” ᴹᵃ

Ori looks down at them, then places one in Momo's hand and one beside Frieren's book.

“Consultants,” she explains. ᴼ

Frieren accepts hers without disputing the accounting.

Mai helps Ori put the jacket on properly. She smooths the fabric at the shoulder and checks the clasp one last time. Her hand rests there for a moment after the work is finished.

“If something's uncomfortable, you say so. Even if you like how it looks.” ᴹᵃ

Ori nods.

“And you don't have to bring everything back full.” ᴹᵃ

“The pockets?” ᴼ

“Your assistant will try.” ᴹᵃ

Ori considers Mau's tendencies and accepts the warning as useful professional advice.

At the door, she turns to Frieren.

“Do I look ready?” ᴼ

Frieren gets up, bends the folded brim back into place and looks at her for long enough that Ori begins to smile before she answers.

“Yes.” ᶠ

Ori takes her hand for the walk down the passage. When they reach the common room, she lets go so she can enter by herself.

Mau is still at the table.

This time, when she comes in, he looks up immediately.
