# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 2 — The Leader Has a Request

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S11E25
**Continuity state:** B+195 late morning. Ori invites Mau; Yuka/Frieren/Shoko informed. Existing E survey checked again before crossing. Ordinary shop/inventory retained. Departure authorized for two, home contact arranged through Ciel/Rimuru and an ordinary shadow relay outside the frame. Purple transformation begins only after both cross.

*When: Ori stops in front of the table, Mau closes his notebook.*

She has a hat.

That is the first thing he notices. Then the jacket, the little light, the case at her side, and finally the effort she is making to let him notice all of it before she speaks.

He gets out of his chair.

“I should have worn something official.” ᴹ

Ori looks him over. His clothes will do. The lack of a hat is more serious.

“You can still come.” ᴼ

“That's generous.” ᴹ

She brings her hands behind her back.

“The Fort requires the vending citizen for an expedition.” ᴼ

Mau looks from her to Mai, who has stopped beside the doorway. Mai raises her hands, showing him approximately the same distance as before.

“About this big,” she says. ᴹᵃ

He looks back at Ori.

“I've been an accomplice.” ᴹ

“Can you come?” ᴼ

The official voice slips on the question. He hears the small uncertainty underneath it and crouches so she doesn't have to look so far up.

“Where are we going?” ᴹ

She takes out her map. Not the old route this time: a fresh sheet with the frame, the surveyed entry area and the things they have actually been told about the E dungeon. There is an empty space beyond the inspection notes.

Ori points to it.

“I want to do this part.” ᴼ

He studies it with her. He asks who has checked the route, what they have decided to take and whether she would like to be the one to carry the map.

By the third question she has stopped worrying that he might say no.

“You can carry the things that don't fit,” she tells him. ᴼ

“I've trained my whole life for this.” ᴹ

She smiles. He finds himself smiling back without having to remember to do it.

* * *

Yuka has put his jacket on the back of his chair.

He sees it when he stands, and the reflex is immediate: find her, check her face, check the place the collar covers, ask whether she needs something before he leaves.

She catches his hand before the question can become a whole list.

“I have plans,” she says. ʸ

“Good plans?” ᴹ

“Excellent. You're going to miss several opinions about you.” ʸ

“That's most of the conversation here.” ᴹ

She pulls him close enough to kiss him. He puts his free hand at her waist, gently enough that she notices the care and leans against it instead of pretending she hasn't.

“Go,” she says against his mouth. “Let her show you something.” ʸ

He looks past her at Ori, who is checking the clasp Mai made. Frieren is helping with the strap without taking the case away.

“If you need me—” ᴹ

“I'll say.” ʸ

He nods. It takes a second.

From behind them, Gojo raises his voice with terrible timing.

“Don't worry. I'll look after your wife.” ᴳ

Mau turns his head.

He opens the familiar shop window, makes a very small purchase and catches the biscuit that appears over his palm. Yuka sees him weigh it, sees Gojo see him weigh it, and steps out of the line between them.

“You wouldn't,” Gojo says. ᴳ

Mau throws it.

Gojo could stop it. They all know that he could stop it. Instead he turns just enough for it to hit his cheek rather than his nose and lets out a delighted, offended noise.

“In front of the expedition leader?” ᴳ

Ori is too busy laughing at the biscuit on the floor to defend him.

Yuka holds out her hand.

Mau looks at it, then at her.

“Evidence?” he asks. ᴹ

“A second opinion.” ʸ

He buys another.

Gojo catches this one. It has been coming much more slowly, and Yuka is already laughing before it reaches him.

“I'm telling Shoko this is why the recovery is taking so long,” he says. ᴳ

Shoko, passing the doorway with a cup, glances at the biscuit in his hand.

“Yours?” ˢʰ

Mai has to turn away.

* * *

They don't leave the safety conversation at the breakfast table.

Frieren goes with them to the frame. Mau reads the current inspection with her, then looks through the opening himself. The entry remains what the scouts described: a broad landing, low growth along one wall and enough unobstructed floor to make a proper first assessment. No sudden increase in pressure, no new interference in the outside measurements.

Ori waits while they finish. When she asks whether that means they can enter, Frieren explains which things they know and which they will have to check as they go.

“And if something changes?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“You stop and look again,” Frieren says. ᶠ

Mau nods beside her.

That part, at least, he would like to be very good at.

An ordinary soldier from the standing watch remains outside the frame. Mau gives it a simple task: remain visible to the people waiting, carry his reports through the existing command link and alert them if the opening changes. It does not answer aloud. It inclines its head and takes its position where Frieren can see it.

Ciel checks the contact to Rimuru while Mau is still outside.

*I hear you,* Rimuru answers. *I would also like it recorded that I was not given a biscuit.* ᴿ

*You can lodge a complaint with the expedition leader.* ᴹ

Ori asks what he is smiling at. Mau tells her, and she says they will consider the complaint after the expedition's supplies have been reviewed.

Rimuru hears the answer through Mau and makes a resigned sound.

The first plan is to explore, make their map and come back. If they want to change that plan, they will contact home before treating it as agreed. Frieren says this directly to Ori. Mau listens too.

He has brought water and food, ordinary things inside the inventory, with the amounts written on the sheet Ori has chosen to carry. A small bag at her side holds the things she wants within her own reach. They test the light once more.

Then Frieren kneels in front of her.

“Have a good expedition.” ᶠ

Ori leans forward to hug her. Frieren's hand settles between the little shoulder blades, careful of the new case.

When Ori steps back, Mau is waiting.

“Ready?” he asks. ᴹ

She takes his hand, looks through the opening and nods.

They cross together.

For three steps, it is the same landing Mau saw from outside.

Ori lets go to reach for the map. He turns to look back at Frieren through the frame, already intending to tell her they are through.

The light at its edge folds inward.

It doesn't flare red. The change is darker, a depth his eyes cannot name by color. Ciel names it for him as the flat view of the path outside disappears.

*Purple. The frame is changing configuration.* ᶜ

Mau's hand is back around Ori's before the last line closes.

He doesn't pull her off her feet. He brings her beside him and looks at the space where the exit was.

For one heartbeat he cannot hear anything else.

Then Ciel speaks again.

*Mau. I am here. Rimuru can hear us.* ᶜ

Ori's fingers tighten in his.

He looks down at her.

“We stop,” he says. “And look again.” ᴹ
