# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 3 — A Different Kind of Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S11E26
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / first internal day. Purple is an altered instance with a guardian-controlled exit and a permanent personal-shop expansion opportunity. No Architect attribution, new governor or lost Ciel. Ciel/Rimuru and Mau's existing soldier link remain functional, but internal time is faster; message delivery does not make the clocks equal. Outside inspection was accurate for the E configuration before alteration. Initial assessment shows ordinary localized threats, far below Mau's stable capacity.

*When: Mau asks Ciel to repeat herself, Ori looks up at him instead of at the vanished exit.*

*I am here,* Ciel says. *Rimuru is listening. The command link to the soldier outside is intact.* ᶜ

Mau swallows.

“All right.” ᴹ

He says it aloud because Ori is still beside him and has heard only his half of the conversation.

“Ciel can talk to home. We're going to tell them what changed.” ᴹ

“Can they open it?” ᴼ

“We're checking.” ᴹ

He doesn't tell her yes to make the next few moments easier.

The landing has widened. A stairway has appeared where the low growth met the far wall, descending toward a square of daylight. Behind them, the frame is solid and dark within its edges. Mau puts out his free hand and touches it with a measured extension of Authority before bringing his fingers near. It behaves like a closed surface. Nothing catches at him or tries to pull him in.

He moves Ori to the middle of the clear floor, where she can sit on a broad, low step with her back to a wall.

“Stay here while I look. Right here beside me.” ᴹ

She nods. Her map case has slipped a little, and he helps her move it out from under her leg.

Then he sends a plain soldier as far as the bottom of the stairs. Another remains at the turn where he can see it. Neither has to fight. The first reports a courtyard, doorways, moving growth and a few small shapes that retreat from its approach.

Mau takes a slow breath and asks Ciel for the outside.

Rimuru's first question is whether both of them are unhurt.

Mau answers that before he tries to explain anything else. He gives their positions, the change to the frame and what the soldiers have seen. Ori listens, her hands wrapped around the map case.

*Frieren's here. Same frame on our side. We saw it change after you entered,* Rimuru says. *It isn't letting us through.* ᴿ

Mau looks at the wall again.

*Don't force it yet. Let us see what it says.* ᴹ

The message has been waiting beside his view. He hadn't looked at it because the first thing he needed to know was whether the voice would keep answering.

Now he opens it.

> INSTANCE ALTERATION: PURPLE
>
> PERSONAL STORE — EXPANSION OPPORTUNITY
>
> Catalogue access: exchange counter.
>
> Return access: guardian defeat.
>
> Earned catalogue additions remain available after departure.

Mau reads it twice, looking for another line. A time limit. An instruction about which parts of himself he is allowed to use. Something that will make him understand too quickly where he is.

Nothing else appears.

*That is the whole notice,* Ciel confirms. *It has not applied a suppression effect. I can still compare your condition with the reading before entry.* ᶜ

“Mau?” Ori asks. ᴼ

He realizes he has been silent for longer than she likes.

“It's showing me a way out,” he says. “A guardian. And something for my shop.” ᴹ

“The sweet shop?” ᴼ

He looks at her.

“Among other things.” ᴹ

That gets a small smile from her, though she still looks at the door afterwards.

Mau reports the whole notice to Rimuru. He has Ciel pass the words as they appear rather than letting his memory shorten them. Frieren asks for the pressure reading; he gives it, then the comparison between the little creatures below and things he already knows he can stop without exerting himself.

It is not the route they approved. That fact doesn't make the original inspection false. The landing was what they saw. The configuration changed around the people who entered it.

*Can Ori speak to me?* Frieren asks. ᶠ

Mau holds out his hand and explains how he will relay the conversation, then lets Ori choose her words.

“I'm all right,” she says first. ᴼ

He passes it on without adding anything.

Frieren asks whether she is hurt, whether she wants to sit a little longer and what she can see. Ori answers each question. After the third one she points toward the steps.

“There's daylight. We haven't gone down yet.” ᴼ

“Good,” Mau says when Frieren's reply reaches him. “She says looking before going is good.” ᴹ

Ori pulls her hat back into position.

* * *

They stay on the landing long enough to do the checks properly.

Mau uses the soldier outside for a second confirmation separate from Ciel's conversation. Rimuru touches its shoulder at an agreed moment; Mau receives the command report. Ciel also asks him to compare elapsed minutes.

The answer takes longer than Mau expects.

*Less than one,* Rimuru says. ᴿ

Mau has been on the landing for considerably longer than that. He looks down the stairs at the square of daylight, then has Ciel repeat the comparison after another interval.

The difference grows.

*We are moving faster inside,* she tells him. *I can pass the words as messages. That doesn't make the two sides share a clock.* ᶜ

He reports the finding before doing anything else. It means a pause before an outside answer can last longer for them. Ciel promises to tell him when she is waiting for a response, so a silence does not have to become something he guesses about.

The people outside know they are connected. The rate is something they will keep measuring.

The scouts below find a closed gate across the far end of the courtyard. Beyond it, a larger presence remains still. There are tracks along the nearer path and a working-looking counter under a shallow roof. Mau does not send a soldier past the gate to start anything.

Ori watches him finish one check and begin another.

“Are we still doing the expedition?” ᴼ

He turns to her. Her voice is careful. She is not asking him to pretend the opening hasn't closed.

“I want to get you home safely.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴼ

He hears the part he hasn't answered.

The easy thing would be to lift her, have the soldiers draw a straight line to the guardian and finish before she has time to wonder whether she has done something wrong. He can already feel the beginning of that plan: child held securely, neutral barrier maintained, every uncertain thing removed before it gets near her.

Ori looks down at the empty space on her map.

“I don't know this route,” she says. ᴼ

“Neither do I.” ᴹ

“So we have to make a new one.” ᴼ

He crouches in front of the step.

“Some of the jobs need to change,” he says. “I have to decide what's safe to approach. If something moves at us, you listen to me straight away. You don't need to work that out while it's happening.” ᴹ

She nods with her eyes on his face.

“But you can still choose what we're looking for. Which things you want to examine. You can ask me to check a path before you choose it.” ᴹ

“And you can suggest things.” ᴼ

“I was hoping you'd allow it.” ᴹ

She thinks about it, then lifts the map case between them.

“Emergency changes,” she says. ᴼ

“Exactly.” ᴹ

“I'm still the leader?” ᴼ

Mau reaches to straighten the clasp that has caught on her sleeve. His hand is steady now.

“I'm still applying for the assistant job.” ᴹ

Ori makes him wait while she considers the application.

“All right.” ᴼ

He tells home the amended plan. Frieren asks him to report before they leave the first courtyard and whenever they change what they intend to do. He agrees.

Then he looks back at Ori and holds up one finger.

“There's something I'd like to offer before we go down.” ᴹ

She leans forward to hear it.
