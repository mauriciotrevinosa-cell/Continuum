# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 4 — Three Steps Away

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S11E26
**Continuity state:** B+195 near noon. Ori accepts a revocable, gate-duration protective contract after explanation and demonstrations. Mau sustains an extension of his active neutral Limitless within three of his paces; Mini Ciel/eyes assist filtering of observable local hazards. No separate installed CT, spatial-cut immunity, omniscience or permanent grant. Supply/cost lies with Mau; one active CT rule remains. Ori can touch harmless objects and people, end the contract, and retains expedition choices within adult safety decisions.

*When: Mau says he would like to put Infinity around her, Ori looks down at her hands.*

“Like yours?” ᴼ

“A little like mine. There's something important about that.” ᴹ

He sits on the step beside her so he can show her without standing over the explanation.

“Mine runs from me. This one would too. I wouldn't be putting a technique into your head. I'd be keeping part of the protection close to you while you're close to me.” ᴹ

Ori moves her hands apart, examining the empty space.

“Would I be able to pick things up?” ᴼ

“Yes. We can test that before you agree to keep it.” ᴹ

“What if I wanted to hold your hand?” ᴼ

He holds his out.

She takes it, plainly not satisfied with a promise that this part would still be possible later.

“That too,” he says. ᴹ

The contract is narrower than the help he and Yuka have built together. Ori is not being given a share of the whole bag, permission to call whatever she wants or a new demand on her body. Mau will hold the active technique and pay its cost. The link will let him maintain a small protected space around her instead of asking her to operate it herself.

Three of his ordinary walking paces is the distance they settle on for this outing. They measure it on the clear landing. He asks her to look at where his boots are, then at where hers are, and tells her he will warn her before she gets near the edge.

“If I go farther?” she asks. ᴼ

“You come back when I ask. I won't pretend it can reach everywhere.” ᴹ

“Can I stop it?” ᴼ

“Whenever you want. Say so. Or end the agreement from your side. We'll practice that part too.” ᴹ

She looks at the place the doorway used to be.

“What if you don't want me to?” ᴼ

He takes a moment with the question.

“Then I'll tell you why. I can ask you to stand somewhere safe while we talk. But the agreement is still yours to stop.” ᴹ

Ciel listens while he says it. He has asked her to check his terms for the things he might make too broad without meaning to. When she answers, she points out a duration he has left vague.

*Until you leave this gate together,* she suggests. *Any continuation afterwards would need another conversation.* ᶜ

He repeats that to Ori.

“We can talk at home if you like it,” he adds. “You don't have to decide about home from here.” ᴹ

“I want to try it.” ᴼ

* * *

He starts with the things she wants to be able to do.

Ori picks a harmless stone from the floor. The protection lets her bend, close her fingers around it and bring it up to show him. She opens the map case. She touches the front of his jacket. The layer around her doesn't push him away.

“Can I eat?” she asks. ᴼ

He takes a wrapped sweet from their supplies and gives it to her.

“An important test.” ᴹ

She eats it very seriously.

Mau has Ciel help him check the filter while Ori chews. His eyes and the silent work of Mini Ciel distinguish the ordinary contact they have just allowed from a small object arriving with a speed that would hurt. He keeps the test local, watched and simple. Nothing here is meant to claim he can understand every dangerous substance in every possible world by looking once.

He shows Ori the little pebble he intends to move. It is smaller than the stone she has kept. First, he brings it toward her slowly with Authority and lets it settle on her open palm.

Then he asks her to set it down again.

“This time it will move quickly. It won't get to you.” ᴹ

Ori draws her shoulders up.

“Do it to you first.” ᴼ

“Good idea.” ᴹ

He brings it toward his own hand with just enough speed to make the difference clear. It slows before contact and hangs where her eyes can follow it. He lets the movement go, retrieves it and shows her his unmarked palm.

Only when she nods does he perform the smaller demonstration beside her sleeve. The pebble stops in the protected approach, well short of reaching the cloth.

Ori stares at it.

Then she reaches around to pick it up, and it comes into her hand as easily as the first one did.

“You have to keep doing that?” ᴼ

“The technique, yes. The thinking has help. That's part of what the eyes and Mini Ciel do.” ᴹ

She knows the name. It makes her look pleased rather than frightened.

“Does it hurt you?” ᴼ

“Not like this. It uses something I have to keep supplying, so we keep checking. If I need a rest, I'll tell you.” ᴹ

He does not promise that tiredness is impossible because he wants her to feel better.

The last test is the one she makes him repeat.

Ori says she wants to stop.

Mau lets the link fall quiet. Ciel confirms its closure. He brings the pebble slowly into Ori's palm again, without any protective slowing on the way.

“And now?” she asks. ᴼ

“Now it's off.” ᴹ

She touches her sleeve, then his hand.

“I can ask for it again?” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Then again.” ᴼ

They renew it with the same terms. This time Ori watches for the moment it begins, although there is nothing her ordinary eyes need to see.

Mau gives home the result, including the distance and the fact that she has already ended and renewed it once. Frieren asks to hear that Ori has understood. He relays the question.

“It stays near Mau. He does the work. I can pick things up. And if I don't like it, I tell him.” ᴼ

He passes on the answer exactly.

* * *

At the top of the stairs, Ori pauses.

Mau is at her shoulder. A soldier stands at the turn below, waiting. Beyond it, daylight lies across a courtyard neither of them has mapped.

“Does it stop everything?” she asks. ᴼ

His eyes go to her face.

He could give her the small answer and keep all the exceptions in his own head. She is young. They are somewhere unfamiliar. He wants, with an ache he can feel under his ribs, for her to have no reason to wonder whether the next step is safe.

“No,” he says. “It stops the kinds of things we're checking for here. It doesn't make every danger impossible.” ᴹ

She looks down the stairs.

“That's why you look first.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Ori takes out the map.

She draws the landing, the place where the exit closed and the first stretch of stairs. He waits while she decides how much room to leave for the courtyard.

Then she points at the soldier below.

“Can he stay there until we've finished this part?” ᴼ

Mau sends the instruction. The soldier turns to face the lower approach.

“He can.” ᴹ

Ori takes his hand for the steps. Her other hand holds the map, and after the first turn she asks him to slow down because she wants to count them.

He slows down.

It is such an ordinary thing to be asked for that he almost laughs.
