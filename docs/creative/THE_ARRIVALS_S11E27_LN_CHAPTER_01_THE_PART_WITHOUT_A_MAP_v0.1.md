# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 5 — The Part Without a Map

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S11E27
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal day1 afternoon. First courtyard explored under temporary Infinity. Mechanical scavengers are constructs, no new shadow extractions. Mau uses existing Authority/physical control while maintaining neutral CT. Ori chooses the route after scouting, discovers site plan showing an extensive terraced trade station. Guardian approach available directly; slower exploration is a choice, not seven mandatory keys.

*When: the first thing they meet tries to steal a buckle, Ori stops drawing.*

It has six legs, a flat back and the purposeful manner of something which has found the exact object it came for. It emerges from beneath the counter's roof, crosses the courtyard and heads straight for the fitting on Ori's map case.

Mau sees the change in its little forelimbs before it jumps.

His fingers close, and Authority catches it sideways above the paving. Its legs keep moving. The jaws at the front snap twice around nothing.

Ori leans forward.

“Stay beside me,” he says. ᴹ

She stops. The creature turns in the air until its underside faces them. There are fitted plates there, a spring and a central piece turning between two strips of metal.

“It's made,” she says. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Does it want the clasp?” ᴼ

Mau moves a spare metal fastener from their supplies to the other side of its head. The jaws immediately turn toward that instead.

“Seems likely.” ᴹ

Ori looks at the counter. Several things have been pulled off its front. A shallow trail runs from it to the crack the little construct came out of.

She points.

“Maybe it took those too.” ᴼ

“Let's check before we give it any more.” ᴹ

He has one of the soldiers approach the crack. Three more scavengers come out, then two larger ones. They go for the metal of the soldier's legs and find themselves lifted, one after another, into a crooked row above the ground.

Ori puts a hand over her mouth.

Mau looks at her.

“What?” ᴹ

“They're walking.” ᴼ

They are. All six are working very hard at getting somewhere while he holds them motionless in the air.

He lets one rotate a little. Its feet paddle in a new direction, equally uselessly.

Ori laughs properly this time.

There are no living bodies to extract. Mau disables the mechanisms, catches the loose pieces before they scatter and lays them on a flat stone for inspection. The largest unit contains a box of stolen fittings, none of which it seems to have found a use for beyond taking them.

“A bad assistant,” he says. ᴹ

Ori looks from the heap of buckles to the little metal legs.

“It didn't ask what anyone wanted.” ᴼ

“Terrible professional standards.” ᴹ

She adds a mark to the map: a small creature with far too many legs and a cross over the clasp beside it.

Mau asks whether he may keep the mechanisms for the people at home who would like to understand them. Ori says yes, provided he writes down where they came from before putting them away.

He produces a tag from their supplies.

“I'm being managed.” ᴹ

“You're doing well.” ᴼ

He has to look away from her for a moment, smiling.

* * *

The counter has been closed for a long time, but it is not entirely dead.

When Mau gets the stolen panel back into position, a narrow display wakes beneath it. Ciel helps him distinguish the mechanism's current response from the grime and damage around it. Noise follows the moving parts with quiet interest. He does not start rebuilding them inside Mau.

*For Mai?* he asks when Mau sets the repaired casing down. ᴺ

*Some of the parts, if they're useful. Let's keep the broken one together so she can see it.* ᴹ

Ori is looking at a relief set into the wall behind the counter.

At first she thinks it is decoration. Then she finds the stairs they used to come down and brings her own map close enough to compare them.

“Mau.” ᴼ

He joins her.

The courtyard is a small part of it.

The relief shows a terraced station built around a valley: water channels crossing between retaining walls, roofed walks, a garden, storerooms, a market and an upper approach leading toward the place marked for the guardian. A direct route runs along one edge, steep and plain. Other paths wander through the terraces, meeting at sheltered stops.

Ori puts one finger on the direct route.

“Is that where we have to go?” ᴼ

“Eventually.” ᴹ

She follows the other paths instead.

There is room on the relief for things Mau could pass in seconds without ever knowing what they were. A long channel ends at a wheelhouse. Beside the garden is a roof drawn with open sides. The market has the same emblem as the counter behind them.

“Can we see these?” Ori asks. ᴼ

He looks at the direct route again before answering.

The larger presence has not moved. His forward scout has found a clear boundary beyond which the guardian waits. No pursuit. No wave of monsters building behind it. The little scavengers were defending nothing except their appetite for fittings.

He asks Ciel to keep recording changes rather than treating one quiet reading as a guarantee.

Then he turns back to the relief.

“Which one first?” ᴹ

Ori moves her finger to the water channel.

“Because if we go there, we can look at the market from above before choosing where to enter.” ᴼ

“Good reason.” ᴹ

“And the wheel.” ᴼ

“Also a good reason.” ᴹ

She looks at him to see whether he means it. He takes her map and holds it flat while she copies the first turn.

* * *

They eat on the first sheltered walk.

Mau takes the food out of the inventory one container at a time so Ori can tell him where she wants it. He has no difficulty producing the water. Finding the spoon she insists is the correct expedition spoon takes longer because he put it in with the wrong bundle.

She gives him a patient look when he finds it.

“Don't tell Mai,” he says. ᴹ

“She told me you would fill everything.” ᴼ

“I haven't even started.” ᴹ

Ori laughs into her cup.

While they eat, he sends home the new layout and the fact that there is a direct approach to the guardian. The detours are theirs to choose. He reports the construct scavengers, the safe stop and the continued measurements. A reply is still travelling back through the difference between their clocks when he finishes his own food.

Ciel tells him she is waiting.

He thanks her instead of asking again ten seconds later.

Ori rests her head against his arm while she looks over the map. The touch is light. He still feels himself check her position, the active extension and the path in front of them. Then he remembers there is nothing to do with the spoon except wash it.

He gives it a little water and dries it with the cloth they brought.

“There are a lot of places,” Ori says. ᴼ

“There are.” ᴹ

“We shouldn't do all of them before eating again.” ᴼ

“I support that policy.” ᴹ

She draws a small square beside the sheltered walk.

“Lunch.” ᴼ

He looks at the mark for a while after she has moved on to the next part.
