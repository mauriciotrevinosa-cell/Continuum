# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 6 — Seven Days If We Do It Properly

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S11E27
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside, roughly one hour after entry / internal day1 evening. Mau estimates about seven internal days at Ori's chosen pace. Home explicitly hears time difference, available faster guardian route, safety assessment and abort priority. Frieren agrees to expanded outing. Contact intact, no disappearance. First campsite established; ordinary personal-shop blanket purchase does not yet constitute the expansion reward.

*When: Mau tells Rimuru that it is getting dark, the first reply is a question about whether he means a room.*

*Outside. The sky,* Mau says. ᴹ

He stands beneath the edge of the sheltered walk, looking at the light fading across the far terrace. Ori is nearby drawing the place where they stopped for water. One of the soldiers stands on the approach; the other has finished checking the back of the shelter.

There is a pause before Rimuru's answer.

*It's been about an hour here.* ᴿ

Mau looks toward Ori.

She has taken her hat off because the band left a line on her forehead. Her hair sticks up a little where she rubbed at it. He has watched her eat twice, counted steps with her, helped her identify a safe place to sit and listened to an increasingly ambitious route through the terraces.

*It has been rather more than that for us.* ᴹ

*You sound normal.* ᴿ

*I am normal. Extremely normal.* ᴹ

*That's not what I meant.* ᴿ

For the first time since the frame changed, Mau laughs without needing Ori to make it happen.

Ciel passes the next report in order. The words are messages carried through their connection, not voices forced to cross the air at the rate of the two skies. She can deliver an intelligible sentence on either side while recording how much internal waiting falls between the exchanges. The clocks have continued separating. The connection has not weakened with the evening.

Frieren asks what they have found and how long he thinks the route would take.

Mau looks at the map Ori has spread on the stone.

*If we follow her plan? About seven days. Ours,* he adds. *With meals and sleep and time to actually look at things.* ᴹ

The silence that follows is longer.

Ori notices him looking at the map and comes over.

“Is it Mum?” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Can you tell her about the walking ones?” ᴼ

“I did. She wants to know about the rest too.” ᴹ

Ori settles beside him while he gives it.

He describes the courtyard, the scavengers and the path they have not yet taken. He reports the guardian's boundary, the ordinary scale of what the scouts have observed and the fact that none of the side paths is required to open the return. They have enough supplies; the shop he already possesses can replace ordinary camp necessities. He is keeping Ori close under the agreement she accepted. He has not needed to push his body or exhaust his reserves.

The next question comes from Rimuru.

*Could you hurry it up?* ᴿ

Mau looks down at Ori's hat in her lap.

*I could go straight to the guardian,* he says. *I could carry her there and finish the route before she got to draw much of it.* ᴹ

He pauses, sorting what he means before he makes it sound grander than it is.

*I promised to respect her plan. She asked me to come with her, not take her somewhere and bring her back. If something feels wrong, I'll act quickly. I'm not leaving a dangerous decision to her because we agreed she's the leader.* ᴹ

Ori has been watching his face.

“What did they ask?” ᴼ

“Whether we could get home sooner.” ᴹ

Her hands close around the brim.

“Do we have to?” ᴼ

“Your mum is thinking about it.” ᴹ

He doesn't answer for Frieren. They wait together.

When her reply arrives, it is plain enough that he can repeat it without improving anything.

“She says it's all right. Keep telling her what we're doing. And if the place changes again, we look at the plan again.” ᴹ

Ori's shoulders loosen.

“Tell her yes.” ᴼ

He does.

* * *

Yuka adds a message before they stop for the night.

She wants to know whether he has eaten the food instead of just handing it out. Mau looks at the empty containers and gives a truthful answer. She tells him to sleep too.

He finds himself wanting to explain all the things he can keep watch over while he does it. Then he hears how that would sound to the woman who has just watched him refuse several days of sleep.

*I will,* he says. *I love you.* ᴹ

*Love you. Tell Ori I want a proper report.* ʸ

Ori asks whether she should put that in the map case.

“Yes,” Mau says. “Very important audience.” ᴹ

She begins a second page.

They choose the back of the shelter for their bed. The stone is dry; there is no broken edge overhead. Mau has already checked the surrounding approaches, but Ori watches him walk the last few steps and asks why he chooses one place rather than another. He shows her where water would run if it rained and where he can keep the open side in view.

She chooses which end will be hers.

He gets the bedding from their supplies, discovers the second blanket is thinner than he intended and opens the familiar shop for another. It is an ordinary item already available in his catalogue. The new opportunity hasn't given them anything yet.

Ori touches the folded blanket when it appears.

“You can do that here too.” ᴼ

“Fortunately for my reputation as a vending citizen.” ᴹ

“Does the Fort know about blankets?” ᴼ

“If it doesn't, I'd like to keep one secret.” ᴹ

She gives him a look which promises nothing.

* * *

After supper he sits where he can see the approach.

It isn't a declaration that he will remain there all night. He wants one last quiet interval with the scouts before handing the routine watch to them and Ciel. Ori comes to sit beside him, insisting she isn't sleepy yet.

He asks her what the best part of the day was.

She considers the six-legged machines, then the map, then the fact that the emergency changes did not mean she had lost her job.

“You waited while I counted,” she says. ᴼ

Mau looks at her.

“The stairs?” ᴹ

She nods.

He could have told her the number. He had known it by the time she reached the turn. He remembers looking down while she counted, hearing her start a section again because she had lost her place.

“You were doing something,” he says. ᴹ

Ori leans against his side.

She asks whether tomorrow they can see the wheelhouse. He says that is still the first suggestion on the map. Then she asks a question about where the water comes from, and he tells her they can find out together.

By the time he thinks to ask whether she would like to move to the bedding, she is asleep across his legs.

He stays still for a little while.

Then he draws the new blanket around her, waits for her to settle and lifts her with both hands so he can put her down properly without waking her all the way. She catches his sleeve in her sleep.

“Still here,” he says softly. ᴹ

Ciel confirms the watch positions. Nothing has changed at the guardian's boundary. Rimuru has received the last report. Frieren knows they are settling down.

Mau lies down close enough for the little fist to keep its hold.

For a few minutes he listens to Ori breathe and notices how often he wants to check one more thing.

Then Ciel tells him she has it.

He lets his eyes close.
