# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 7 — The Water Finds Another Way

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S11E28
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal day2. Waking report to home; temporary protection checked and maintained with rest and supply monitoring. Aqueduct route explored, bridge hazard addressed before crossing. Existing Authority and soldiers used for repair/support; no new movement skill installed. Ori chooses a longer sound route and studies a working water system. No new units or skill rune consumed.

*When: Ori wakes, Mau is sitting beside the bedding with her hat balanced on one knee.*

“Did you sleep?” she asks. ᴼ

He looks down at her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“All night?” ᴼ

“Ciel says I asked whether something had moved once. Apparently it was my foot.” ᴹ

Ori laughs before she has fully opened her eyes.

He hands her the hat, and she sits up to put it on. Her hair has other plans. He tries to smooth one part down, discovers that he has made a second part stand up and admits they may need to ask the expedition leader for an extension on getting ready.

She presses both hands over her head and puts the hat on top of them.

“Done.” ᴼ

“Excellent management.” ᴹ

They eat before setting off. Mau sends the waking report, checks the cost of the protection and asks Ori whether anything felt uncomfortable yesterday. She says the strap did after lunch. He asks to see where it rubbed and adjusts the length rather than telling her to bear with it until they get home.

The change makes the case sit better.

She puts a small mark beside the supplies list: fix before walking.

“That's a good rule,” he says. ᴹ

“Mai said.” ᴼ

“She usually is.” ᴹ

* * *

The water channel is larger than it looked from below.

It runs through the terrace in a raised trough, following a curve toward the wheelhouse. The sound reaches them before they can see the wheel. Ori walks quickly for several steps, remembers the distance they agreed and looks back. Mau has kept beside her without making her feel chased.

At the bridge, he asks her to wait.

Nothing in the span looks dramatic enough to announce that it is rotten. That is why he tests it. He sends a soldier over first and watches the way one of the boards bows beneath the weight.

“That one?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“That one, and probably the pieces holding it.” ᴹ

She looks at the drop. It is not a bottomless ravine. The water is still moving quickly enough that Mau has no intention of letting her find out what falling into it feels like.

“Can we go around?” ᴼ

He points out the longer walkway marked on the relief. It is visible beyond the bend, a little farther uphill.

“Or I can carry you. Or we can repair this before we use it.” ᴹ

Ori considers all three.

“Would the repair be for other people?” ᴼ

“I don't know if anyone will use this place after we leave.” ᴹ

She looks back at their map.

“We can mark it. And go around.” ᴼ

He nods and waits while she draws the warning.

The longer route takes them past the back of the wheelhouse. It turns out to be the better place to enter. The front has a broken stair; the back has a broad door and a view of the water before it reaches the wheel.

Ori stops just inside.

“We would have missed this.” ᴼ

Mau follows her gaze.

One channel has clogged with debris. The water has found another way around it, spilling over a low division and driving a smaller wheel beside the main one. The smaller wheel is turning, but the machinery above it is only catching every few rotations.

“It keeps trying,” Ori says. ᴼ

“Something's not lining up.” ᴹ

She looks at him, brightening.

“Can we see?” ᴼ

He checks the structure overhead before agreeing.

* * *

Mau could pull the obstruction out in one movement.

He starts to lift his hand, then looks at the wheel again and lowers it. Ori is following the motion from the paddles to the shaft. She has just found where the force stops travelling properly.

“That part's moving, but that one isn't.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Can it get stuck if we move this?” ᴼ

“It could. Let's stop the water first.” ᴹ

They do it carefully. Mau holds the gate with Authority while a soldier clears the lodged debris from the dry side. Ori stays beside him, pointing out which piece she means instead of reaching toward the mechanism. When the flow has slowed, he lets her look at the coupling from a place where nothing can catch her fingers.

The repair isn't beautiful. A piece has slipped and worn against the part beside it. They have the right kind of replacement among the scavengers' stolen fittings, but the first one Mau chooses is a little too short.

Ori waits while he tries the next.

“Don't tell Mai,” he says again. ᴹ

“She might know a better way.” ᴼ

“That's what I'm afraid of.” ᴹ

The second fitting holds. He eases the water back slowly, ready to stop it if the coupling catches. The little wheel turns, the repaired shaft follows and a row of shutters opens along the far wall.

Light falls across shelves they couldn't see before.

Ori makes a surprised sound.

There are jars there, most empty, some holding plain metal pieces, and a board covered in old diagrams. Mau finds no active spellwork in the drawings. They are instructions for maintaining the machinery.

“For Senku?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“He'd enjoy telling us what we did wrong.” ᴹ

“We fixed it.” ᴼ

“That won't stop him.” ᴹ

She decides they should take a copy of the diagram rather than pull the board off the wall. Mau helps her hold a sheet flat while she sketches the arrangement, then adds his own notes at the bottom, clearly separated from hers.

* * *

They finish the day at the sound end of the raised walk.

Ori's feet are tired. She admits it before Mau has to ask a second time. He looks at the map, sees the distance to the next shelter and offers to carry her for that stretch.

She thinks about whether accepting means she has stopped leading.

“You can tell me where to go from up here,” he says. ᴹ

That resolves it.

He lifts her onto his back with the case settled where it won't press into either of them. Her arms go around his shoulders. She gives him a direction almost immediately, and he obeys it even though he can see the turn himself.

At the shelter she makes a mark where the bridge was unsafe and another where the wheel began working again.

Then she adds a small shape beside the longer path.

“What's that?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“A good way.” ᴼ

He leaves the map open while he gets supper out.

Later, Ciel offers him the movement review he saved from a practice with Jin-Woo. He watches one correction, tries it slowly in the clear space and stops after he has understood what he still needs to work on. There is no prize for turning their shelter into another training ground.

Ori asks what he was doing.

“Learning where to put my foot.” ᴹ

She glances at the diagram they copied.

“So it lines up?” ᴼ

Mau looks at her, then laughs.

“Exactly.” ᴹ
