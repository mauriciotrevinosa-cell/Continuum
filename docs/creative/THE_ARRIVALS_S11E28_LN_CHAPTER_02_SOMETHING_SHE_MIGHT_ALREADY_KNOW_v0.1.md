# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 8 — Something She Might Already Know

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S11E28
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal day3. Archive reached by ordinary exploration, scouted before entry. Ori chooses a non-hostile grimoire for Frieren; content may duplicate known magic and is not automatically copied or performed. Gift remains in Ori's own satchel by choice. Mau recognizes value of giving without technical novelty. Home contact maintained; no archive of Sukuna opened.

*When: Ori asks whether Frieren might already know the spell, Mau realizes he has answered the wrong question.*

They have been in the archive for most of the afternoon.

It is not the sort of place where every book is waiting to bite somebody. Mau checked before letting Ori touch the lower shelves. The damaged cases near the entrance were the real problem: one door had come free of its hinge, and a top shelf had sagged far enough that it needed support before anyone walked beneath it.

Once those were made safe, the room became a place full of things Ori wanted to show him.

A rolled map with a harbour drawn on it. A book of plant shapes whose pages had stuck together. Several narrow volumes with diagrams that looked like instructions until Mau followed one through and realized it was a catalogue illustration.

Ori found that funny. She had thought the little person in the picture was performing a difficult spell.

“He's trying to sell a lamp,” Mau told her. ᴹ

She looked at the raised arms again.

“He's very excited.” ᴼ

“Good lamp, apparently.” ᴹ

Now she has found a book which really does contain magic.

Mau studies the open page without feeding power into it. The inscription is inert as it lies there; reading does not activate it. He can follow enough of its structure to recognize a small effect concerned with directing light along a prepared surface. It isn't a weapon. He says that first.

Then, because he is Mau, he starts explaining the part Frieren would probably find familiar.

Ori's smile gets smaller.

“Does that mean she won't want it?” ᴼ

He stops.

The book rests in her two hands. She has wiped the dust off the cover with the cloth Mai packed for her. There is a little place inside the case where it would fit if she moves the map into the other compartment.

“No,” he says. “That's not what it means.” ᴹ

She looks up at him.

“I don't know whether she already has this one. I was thinking about what it does.” ᴹ

“I wanted to bring her something.” ᴼ

“Then bring her this.” ᴹ

Ori looks down at it again.

“Even if she knows?” ᴼ

He sits on the floor beside her rather than reaching for another book which might be technically better.

“She likes looking at magic. And she likes hearing where you found things. You can tell her about the lamp man.” ᴹ

Ori smiles a little.

“He's in the other book.” ᴼ

“Then this present has a story with references.” ᴹ

She turns the grimoire over in her hands.

“We should tell her it's been checked.” ᴼ

“Yes. And let her check it herself before trying anything.” ᴹ

That sounds like a proper job. Ori gets out the tag sheet.

* * *

They make a record before packing it.

Ori writes where the book was found and dictates the part Mau needs to add about the shelf and the inactive inscription. She wants to write that it is a present, so nobody mistakes it for ordinary stock.

“I think she'll know when you hand it to her,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ori writes it anyway.

He helps her wrap the corners. When he offers to put it in the inventory, she holds it a little closer.

“I'd like to carry this one.” ᴼ

“All right.” ᴹ

He checks that the weight sits comfortably and makes room for the map in the other pocket. The mechanical parts, the copied diagram and the ordinary materials they collected go into the inventory with their own labels. She does not need to carry everything to prove she has found it.

The distinction seems to please her. She has something of her own at her side, and an assistant who can produce lunch without adding another bag to her shoulders.

They eat by the archive's open doors.

Mau reads the harbour map while Ori studies the lamp illustration again. She has asked to take that page's information too; he is copying it rather than tearing it out of the bound volume.

The drawing isn't difficult. Remembering to draw only what the page actually shows is harder. His eyes can follow the joints of the mechanism, and Noise is interested in three separate ways it might be constructed.

*Later,* Mau tells him. *We can keep the observation. We're finishing what we came to look at first.* ᴹ

*Good lamp,* Noise answers. ᴺ

Mau laughs into his cup.

Ori asks what he said. Mau tells her, and she nods with the satisfaction of finding an expert who agrees.

* * *

The reply from home arrives while they are putting the lunch things away.

Frieren has heard about the archive and wants to know whether the paper is dry. Ori answers that it is, except for the plant book, which they left where it could be examined without peeling the pages apart.

Then she starts to mention the present and stops herself so suddenly that Mau nearly repeats the first word.

He catches the look she gives him.

“No confidential information?” he asks. ᴹ

Ori presses her lips together and nods.

He reports a successful day in the archive, a safe camp within the cleared approach and no need to revise the faster route to the guardian. He also tells Frieren that Ori is keeping an excellent record.

Ori leans against him while he waits for the answer.

It comes back in two parts. First, Frieren asks him to make sure the paper isn't pressed against anything wet. Then she says she is looking forward to hearing the report herself.

Ori looks down at the case where the book now rests.

“She didn't guess?” ᴼ

“I think we're very mysterious.” ᴹ

That evening she asks him to read part of an ordinary travel account they brought from the archive. They sit close enough that she can follow his finger along the line. He explains a word, then has to admit that he doesn't know exactly what one of the objects in the next picture is.

“We can ask Mum,” Ori says. ᴼ

He nods.

It is not a small thing, the ease with which she says it. Something to bring back. Something to ask together. He has spent so much of the last several days trying to draw a line around the next bad thing that he almost forgot how many reasons there can be to return.

Ori turns the page before he is ready.

“You were looking,” she says. ᴼ

“I was.” ᴹ

“At the picture?” ᴼ

He looks down at her face beside the book.

“At you.” ᴹ

She smiles, then points to the next line because the story is not going to read itself.
