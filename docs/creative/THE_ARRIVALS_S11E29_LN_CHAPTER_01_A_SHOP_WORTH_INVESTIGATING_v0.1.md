# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 9 — A Shop Worth Investigating

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S11E29
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal day4. Purple opportunity realized at the market's intact exchange counter: existing personal shop gains permanent provision/confectionery catalogue listings. No new shop, inventory, infinite credit or distribution of CT. Purchase confirmed and balance charged before acceptance of further supplies. Ori selects a mundane hairpin for Yuka; bulk stock stored in inventory with distribution labels. Guardian still required to open return.

*When: Ori finds the second counter, she makes Mau wait while she checks the ground around it for little metal thieves.*

“Clear,” she announces. ᴼ

He looks down at her.

“Excellent. Can I approach?” ᴹ

She nods, then remembers that he is the one checking for danger rather than just stolen buttons.

“After you've looked.” ᴼ

“Also excellent.” ᴹ

The market has a roof along one side and open stalls along the other. Most of the things left behind are ordinary materials: cord, empty containers, folded cloth and small tools whose handles need more work than their blades. Mau checks the unstable shelves first. Ori makes a little pile of things they can inspect together after he has finished.

The counter at the far end has the same emblem as the broken one below. This one still has its front panel. When Mau comes close, the interface he saw on the landing appears beside it.

> PERSONAL STORE DETECTED.
>
> FIELD CATALOGUE EXTENSION AVAILABLE.
>
> Accept permanent listings?

He doesn't touch the confirmation immediately.

“Is it the shop thing?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“I think so. I'm reading the rest.” ᴹ

Ciel checks the link the counter is offering. It points to the shop Mau already uses, with its existing balance and purchases. It is not asking him to replace that shop, surrender its contents or install a new governor over his body. The terms concern what the shop will let him buy after this visit.

Some of the new listings are travel provisions. Some are ordinary household goods. A considerable number, to Ori's immediate interest, are food.

Mau opens the preview.

She gets closer to his arm.

“Does it have sweets?” ᴼ

“This appears to be the Fort branch of the investigation.” ᴹ

The first page gives them enough information to see the scope without turning it into a promise that everything in the universe is now for sale. New preserved meals. More sizes of bedding. Snacks whose packaging he doesn't recognize. Ordinary drinks in combinations he is fairly sure Umaru will have an opinion about.

He asks Ciel whether the additions will stay once they leave the gate.

*Those are the terms offered. No purchase allowance is being added. Items still cost your existing credit.* ᶜ

“Not free,” he tells Ori. ᴹ

She looks disappointed for precisely long enough to be reasonable.

“But you can still buy them?” ᴼ

“Within the balance.” ᴹ

“We should test one.” ᴼ

“You sound like Senku.” ᴹ

She takes that as permission to choose the test.

* * *

Mau accepts the extension.

The new page attaches to the familiar shop interface. He closes the counter's view, steps away and opens his own shop again. The listings are still there.

> CATALOGUE EXTENSION REGISTERED.
>
> Availability retained after departure.

Ori asks for the packet with the little animal on the front. Mau checks the contents as far as their ordinary descriptions allow, sees nothing that makes it something other than a snack and buys one.

The balance decreases.

A sealed packet lands in his hand.

For a moment they both look at it as if it might deliver a speech.

Then Ori asks him to open it.

He breaks the seal and offers her one of the crisps. She tastes it, thinks about it and takes a second before giving an opinion.

“Umaru should try these.” ᴼ

“Is that a good opinion or a request for help?” ᴹ

Ori laughs.

“They're good.” ᴼ

Mau tries one. There is something sharp and sweet in the seasoning which he cannot put a name to. He turns the packet to read the label, then decides that the people who conduct the Fort's snack reviews will enjoy the argument more than he will enjoy solving it for them.

He buys more than one packet, but not so many that the trip becomes a purchase order.

Ori appoints herself in charge of identifying which ones are for the Fort and which ones are for the rest of the house. Mau shows her how he is labelling the bundles before putting them into the inventory.

“So we don't lose the important ones,” she says. ᴼ

“They're all apparently the important ones.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴼ

He cannot find a fault in the argument.

* * *

The present for Yuka is not in the shop.

Ori finds it among the ordinary things in one of the stalls: a small hairpin with a smooth bead suspended from its end. The bead catches the light in a way Ori likes. She holds it up and asks Mau whether Yuka wears things like it.

“Sometimes.” ᴹ

“Do you think she'd like this?” ᴼ

He turns it carefully, checking the fastening and the edge which would rest against her hair. There is no active working in it, no hidden moving part. He could make something more elaborate. That has nothing to do with what Ori is asking.

“I think she'd like you choosing it for her.” ᴹ

Ori gives him a doubtful look.

“And the pin?” ᴼ

He smiles.

“The pin too.” ᴹ

She has him put it in a small cloth wrap separate from the materials. The grimoire remains in her case. These are different presents and need to arrive as different presents.

Mau agrees to the organization.

He finds himself enjoying being told where to put something when the reason is this clear.

Before they leave, he reports the permanent catalogue addition to home. He includes the purchase test and the fact that it used his existing credit. Rimuru sends back a request that he not accidentally buy the whole place because somebody said the word supplies.

Mau looks at Ori.

“We have received foreign interference.” ᴹ

She asks what it means.

He explains, and she says Agent Bird will still be allowed to try the snacks if he behaves.

Mau passes that on too.

* * *

They have supper near the upper edge of the market.

Ori wants to try one of the new meals. Mau buys a single portion for them to inspect, then gets the rest of supper from what they already brought. He doesn't need to make the new catalogue responsible for feeding them before he knows what is in it.

The meal is good enough that Ori wants to add it to the report.

“You should tell Rem,” she says. ᴼ

“Why?” ᴹ

“So she can have it when she doesn't want to cook.” ᴼ

He looks at her across the food.

“Yes,” he says. “We should.” ᴹ

She goes back to eating, having solved a practical problem.

Mau leaves the shop window closed for the rest of the meal.
