# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 10 — What She Is Allowed to Call It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S11E29
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal day5. Rain leads to a rest and quieter garden-route exploration, not a crisis. Ori voices paternal feelings toward Mau and uncertainty about parents not being a couple. Mau affirms connection without imposing a label, replacing Frieren or reviving romance. No adoption decree. Ciel/home contact continues; no new ability.

*When: the rain begins, Ori is halfway through drawing a leaf.*

The first drop lands beside the outline. She looks up, checks that it came from the sky rather than a branch and moves the paper beneath her jacket before Mau has to suggest it.

He holds a hand over the case while she puts it away.

“Shelter?” he asks. ᴹ

She points to the open-sided roof they saw from the upper walk.

“That one.” ᴼ

He has already checked the approach. They make it under the roof before the rain gets heavy, with only the ends of Ori's sleeves damp. Mau brings out a dry cloth, a place to sit and the ordinary cups they have used all week.

Ori takes her hat off.

There is a wet mark on the brim. She examines it, then looks at the rain beyond the roof with something close to pride.

“It worked.” ᴼ

“Mai will want a report.” ᴹ

Ori puts the hat carefully beside her.

They have done less walking today. She was tired when they started, though she tried to describe it as a problem with the path. Mau asked whether it would help to sit for a while. Once she had sat, she admitted that it might help to sit for a little longer.

He let them.

The garden route has things to look at without needing to reach another building. They watched water gather along a leaf and run to the stem. Ori found a seedpod she wanted to draw, then decided to leave it on the plant rather than take it home without knowing what it needed.

Mau sent the observation to Ciel for the record instead. Nothing was planted inside him. Noise accepted the picture with a thoughtful sound and no proposal to turn the afternoon into a construction test.

Now the rain makes a steady noise beyond their shelter.

Ori wraps her hands around her cup and looks at Mau.

“Can I ask you something?” ᴼ

He turns fully toward her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“You don't have to know.” ᴼ

“Good. I was about to make a very bad promise.” ᴹ

She smiles, then looks back into the cup.

It takes her a little time to find the beginning.

* * *

“When I stay with you, it feels…” ᴼ

She stops. Mau waits.

“I like it,” she says instead. ᴼ

“I like it too.” ᴹ

“Not just the expedition.” ᴼ

“I know.” ᴹ

Her thumb moves around the rim.

He knows the danger of helping too quickly here. There are answers he could supply from memories she has not lived, explanations which would make the feeling fit a story he already understands. She has come to him with her own question. He keeps his hands around his cup and lets her ask it.

“Sometimes it feels like when other people have their dad there,” she says. ᴼ

He goes very still.

Ori notices and looks up quickly.

“I don't mean Mum isn't—” ᴼ

“You don't have to take anything away from her to tell me.” ᴹ

She searches his face.

“But a dad is with your mum.” ᴼ

There it is. A rule small enough to have seemed obvious until it starts hurting somebody she loves.

Mau sets his cup down where it cannot spill and moves a little nearer, leaving the choice of the last distance to her.

“Some parents are together,” he says. “Some aren't. That doesn't tell you everything about who can love you or be there for you.” ᴹ

Ori's eyes move toward the rain.

“But you and Mum aren't.” ᴼ

“No.” ᴹ

He lets the answer stand. He doesn't soften it into something which would make Yuka disappear or promise Frieren a life she hasn't asked for.

“Does that make it wrong?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“Feeling close to me?” ᴹ

She nods.

“No.” ᴹ

The word comes easily. The rest takes more care.

“You can love your mum. You can want time with me. You can like being with Yuka too. None of those has to mean you've stopped choosing one of the others.” ᴹ

Ori lets out a breath so small he might have missed it if he weren't watching her.

“Do I have to call it something?” ᴼ

Mau shakes his head.

“Not now. We don't have to decide a name for every feeling this afternoon.” ᴹ

“What if later?” ᴼ

“Then you can tell me later.” ᴹ

She looks at the empty space beside his leg.

He opens his hand on the mat between them.

Ori moves into his side.

* * *

For a while neither of them says anything.

Mau rests an arm around her shoulders. The roof is sound. The water is running away from their bedding. The soldier on the approach has nothing to report. He knows all of that without turning his head again.

Ori puts her cheek against his sleeve.

“Would you still come if I didn't need help?” ᴼ

His throat tightens before he can answer.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Even if I had a different assistant?” ᴼ

“I'd be terribly offended by the recruitment process.” ᴹ

She laughs against him.

“But yes,” he says. “I want time with you. That's not a job you have to keep finding for me.” ᴹ

Her fingers close around a fold of his sleeve.

He looks out at the rain until he trusts his voice again.

There have been so many times when he answered love with another useful thing. A guard. A way out. Food, a room, a hand offered before anybody had to ask. He knows those things matter. He is sitting beneath a roof with a little girl who has asked whether he would stay if somebody else could do all of them.

He presses his cheek briefly to the top of her head.

“I love you,” he says. “Even when there's nothing to fix.” ᴹ

Ori stays very close.

* * *

When the rain eases, she wants to walk to the end of the garden path.

Mau checks the wet stones. A shorter section has become slippery enough that he suggests another way around it. Ori looks, considers and agrees. She does not make him carry her, and he does not lift her before she has answered.

At the end of the path they can see the upper terraces through the last of the rain.

She points to the roof they think belongs to the workshops.

“Tomorrow?” ᴼ

“If the approach is sound.” ᴹ

“We need something for Mai.” ᴼ

“We do.” ᴹ

They return to the shelter to find the cloth where Ori left it. She hangs the hat where it can dry, adds the rain mark to the map and asks to tell Frieren about the seedpod.

Mau relays the story. He doesn't add the conversation under the roof to the report. It belongs to Ori until she wants to share it.

Before bed she asks whether she can sit with him again.

He pats the space beside him.

She comes close without having to invent a question about supplies first.
