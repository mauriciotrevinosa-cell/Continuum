# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 11 — A Commission for the Commissioner

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S11E30
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal day6. Workshop explored and supplies appraised. Gift for Mai: sound small tools, stock/fittings and ordinary material samples with provenance, no new Construction unlock. Igris deliberately summoned to supervise existing ordinary carriers; he is not assumed to have been physically present all week. Mau retains Infinity, uses compatible Authority for a runaway cart. No new extraction or crystal integration.

*When: Ori decides what to take for Mai, she rejects the first three things Mau shows her.*

“She has those.” ᴼ

“Probably,” he admits. ᴹ

“And she has those.” ᴼ

“Also probably.” ᴹ

The third thing is a tool with a cracked handle. Ori points at the split.

“That one needs a present.” ᴼ

Mau puts it down.

“You're a difficult customer.” ᴹ

She gives him a look remarkably like Mai's.

“It's an important commission.” ᴼ

He bows his head in acknowledgment.

The workshop has more things than either of them intends to carry out by hand. They have already sorted the ones worth bringing home from the ones best left undisturbed. Mau has checked the benches and the overhead racks before letting Ori approach them. He keeps the broken machines separate from the sound tools, so nobody reaches into a bundle later expecting a safe handle and finds an exposed spring instead.

Ori has made labels for both categories.

The ordinary store is where they finally find what she wants: small fittings, smooth wire in sealed rolls, a set of narrow tools with handles that fit her own hand but are clearly meant for finer work. Mau looks at their edges. They have survived better than the equipment on the open benches.

“Would she use them?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“I think so. She can decide what they're best for.” ᴹ

Ori likes that answer. It leaves Mai something to do with the present besides thank her.

She chooses a roll of the wire, then has Mau help her wrap the tools. He adds a separate note about where they were found and what he has checked. The material samples go in another packet. None has acquired a magical property just because the room called itself a workshop.

“Should we pay her again?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“For what?” ᴹ

“If she shows us how to use them.” ᴼ

“You can ask. I think she might accept another sweet.” ᴹ

Ori considers the remaining packet of supplies with professional concern.

* * *

They are moving the sound stock toward the clear part of the floor when the cart gets away from them.

It isn't a trap. Mau can see exactly what happened: the catch on a sloped rail let go when a soldier shifted the load. The cart begins rolling toward a gap in the platform, taking two heavy crates with it.

“Wait here,” he tells Ori. ᴹ

His hand closes.

Authority catches the cart, but the crates keep moving. He turns his wrist to lower the front rather than wrench the whole weight backwards. One crate lands hard on the boards. The other tips, spills a shower of fittings and strikes the edge of the platform.

Ori makes a startled sound.

Mau stops the crate before it falls and sets it beside the first. The protection around Ori has not changed. He hasn't had to choose between holding it and using another active cursed technique to catch a box.

For a moment he stands with his hand still raised.

Then he looks at the little scattered pieces all over the floor.

“That was badly packed.” ᴹ

Ori looks up at him.

“Are you hurt?” ᴼ

“No.” ᴹ

“Then we need another label.” ᴼ

He lowers his hand.

“What does this one say?” ᴹ

“Don't put these on the moving thing.” ᴼ

Mau laughs, a little breathlessly, and gets the paper.

* * *

For the larger sorting job, he calls Igris.

The knight rises from Mau's shadow into a space he has cleared beside the entrance. Ori looks pleased to see someone she knows rather than merely another pair of arms. Igris bows to her, then to Mau.

“We need help getting the sound stock into groups,” Mau says. “Nothing moves onto a cart until we've checked it.” ᴹ

Igris looks at the scattered fittings.

“Understood, my liege.” ᴵ

Mau looks back at him.

“Nobody saw anything.” ᴹ

“Of course.” ᴵ

Ori laughs hard enough that she has to lean against Mau's leg.

The ordinary carriers come next, drawn from the army he already has. Igris gives them places to work. Ori tells him which piles belong to the house, which ones need somebody to inspect them and which small bundle is not to go into any common stack because it is a present.

Igris listens without performing surprise at being given instructions by a child.

When she points to the label on the little wrapped tools, he kneels enough to read it and says he will make sure the bundle reaches its intended owner.

Ori stands a little taller.

Mau watches the exchange, then puts the bulk materials into the inventory by group. The shadows help with sorting and checking the floor; he doesn't make them carry miles of stock which he can store safely himself.

The final group is less certain. An old rack of mechanisms. Some ruined, some possibly useful, all needing a person who knows more about this particular work than Mau does.

He asks Noise whether a record will be enough for now.

*Record first. Build later,* Noise answers. ᴺ

*You make that sound very accusing.* ᴹ

*Practice.* ᴺ

Ciel makes a small sound which suggests she is not helping him.

Mau accepts the loss and keeps the useful pieces together for the people at home.

* * *

On the walk to their last campsite, Ori asks whether Igris may stay with them for supper.

Mau looks at the knight, then explains that he does not need supper the same way they do, but company can still be offered.

Ori turns to ask him herself.

Igris accepts.

He takes a place near the approach while they sit together. Ori tells him about the scavengers, the wheel and the book she has found for Frieren. When she reaches the part about the lamp, Mau realizes she has been saving the description for a new audience.

He lets her tell it without correcting the order.

Later, she puts the wrapped present for Mai beside the map case and checks it hasn't been crushed.

“Almost everything,” she says. ᴼ

“One guardian left.” ᴹ

She looks up at the upper terrace, hidden now beyond the bend.

“You'll tell me where to stand?” ᴼ

Mau puts his hand over hers.

“Yes.” ᴹ
