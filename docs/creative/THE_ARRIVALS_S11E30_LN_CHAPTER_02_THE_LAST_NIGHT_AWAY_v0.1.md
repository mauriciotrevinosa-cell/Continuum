# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 12 — The Last Night Away

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S11E30
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal night6. Full guardian plan and escape priorities reviewed; Ori's task concerns records/supplies, never responsibility for combat safety. Igris and ordinary soldiers provide existing watch. Contact with Yuka/Frieren maintained; elapsed-time asymmetry acknowledged without claiming vanished week outside. No new power, no full Sukuna archive.

*When: Yuka asks how long the trip feels now, Mau has to count the nights.*

He has been reporting them separately so the people at home don't have to guess which day he means. Ciel keeps the record. Ori has a little row of marks in the corner of her map, each beside the place where they slept.

*Sixth night,* he says. ᴹ

*I've had a rather shorter afternoon.* ʸ

He leans back against the support behind their bedding. Ori is sitting with Igris a short distance away, showing him where she wants the final route drawn. Mau can see her, and she is within the distance they agreed.

*How was it?* he asks. ᴹ

Yuka tells him.

There is no crisis in the story. She has eaten with Mai, helped with something Maki asked for and discovered that Gojo would like his biscuit incident officially recorded as an act of unprovoked aggression. Shoko has declined to take a witness statement.

Mau laughs with his eyes closed.

*I miss you,* he says when she finishes. ᴹ

The answer takes time to reach him. He doesn't fill it with another report.

*I miss you too. Come home with your expedition leader. Then you can tell me all the parts you keep leaving out because somebody has classified the presents.* ʸ

He opens his eyes to find Ori looking at him.

“No confidential information,” she reminds him. ᴼ

*I am being supervised,* he tells Yuka. ᴹ

Her laugh arrives a little later, warm enough to make the waiting worthwhile.

* * *

The guardian plan is shorter than the route plan.

Mau has been looking at it each day through his scouts, keeping the boundary under observation. The larger construct has not come out to hunt them. Its court is separate from the paths they have used, and its movement remains confined to the upper space.

Tomorrow they will take the direct final approach. Ori can make the last map marks from the protected doorway before he begins. Igris will stay with her at the rear of that doorway, with Mau close enough to sustain the neutral extension.

If Mau asks her to move, she moves. The destination will be a specific place they have checked, not a frightened instruction to run somewhere on her own. If the plan no longer fits what the guardian actually does, he can call more of the army and finish differently. There is no rule asking him to entertain the construct at Ori's expense.

Ori listens to all of it.

“What do I do while you fight?” ᴼ

“Stay where Igris and I ask. You can watch if you want. You don't have to.” ᴹ

“And after?” ᴼ

“That's your logistics department.” ᴹ

Her expression changes.

“The things?” ᴼ

“The things. We've kept the groups separate. We can bring them out in the order you want when we're home.” ᴹ

She thinks about the wrapped book, the tools, the copied diagrams and all the packets whose labels she has been checking each evening.

“Can there be enough soldiers?” ᴼ

Mau glances at Igris.

The knight waits with admirable composure.

“I think we can find enough,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ori begins making a list.

* * *

After the list is finished, she asks to hear the end of the travel story.

Mau takes out the book they have been reading and finds the page she marked. Igris remains nearby. The ordinary soldiers take the watch positions they have used for this shelter; Ciel confirms the outer approach and tells Mau that the next home report is waiting to be delivered, not lost.

Ori leans against his chest so she can see the page.

By now he knows which words she will ask about and which ones she likes to try herself. He lets her read a short line, helps when she gets stuck and waits while she starts it again without him.

The traveller in the book reaches a harbour after making a very poor decision about a mountain path. Ori has already expressed opinions on that part. Now he meets the person who has been waiting for him, and the story gives the reunion less space than Ori thinks it deserves.

“Did they go home?” she asks. ᴼ

“It says they went through the gate together.” ᴹ

“But after?” ᴼ

Mau turns the page. There is no more story, only a diagram and several blank lines where someone might once have written a note.

“It doesn't say.” ᴹ

Ori looks dissatisfied.

“They should have written that.” ᴼ

He closes the book over one finger.

“What would you put?” ᴹ

She considers.

“They ate something. And he showed her what he brought back.” ᴼ

“That sounds likely.” ᴹ

“And they slept.” ᴼ

“An important part.” ᴹ

Ori nods, satisfied that the missing ending can be repaired with sensible decisions.

Mau writes her version on a separate sheet and puts it with their own notes. He does not add it to the old account as if the traveller had written it himself.

“For our report,” he says. ᴹ

She rests her cheek against him.

* * *

Frieren's message arrives before Ori falls asleep.

It is for her. Mau lets her sit up and hear each part, then relays the answer in the same order. They talk about the hat, the rain and whether the grimoire's wrapping stayed dry, though Ori only calls it the important paper.

Frieren asks whether she is ready to come home after tomorrow's route.

Ori nods before remembering that her mother cannot see it through Mau's voice.

“Yes. I want to show you.” ᴼ

Mau passes it on.

Then Ori looks up at him.

“Can you tell her I love her?” ᴼ

He does, and waits with her for the answer.

It is a small thing, doing nothing while a message crosses the difference between the two places. He doesn't try to shorten it by giving Ori the reply he knows Frieren will want to make.

When the real answer comes, Ori smiles against his shirt.

He puts the book away, adjusts the blanket and tells Ciel he is ready to sleep.

Igris inclines his head from the edge of the shelter.

For once, Mau doesn't give him another instruction before lying down.
