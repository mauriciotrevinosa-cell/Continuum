# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 13 — The Assistant Takes His Turn

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S11E31
**Continuity state:** B+195 outside / internal day7 morning. Mechanical guardian defeated; no living soul or new extraction. Mau maintains neutral Limitless/Ori extension throughout and combines practiced dagger guidance with independent Authority, precision and observation, not a second concurrent CT. Igris protects from the agreed rear position. Guardian opens the return frame. No injury, strain peak or emergency evolution; Ori never responsible for winning.

*When: they reach the upper doorway, Ori asks whether the guardian has seen them yet.*

“Yes,” Mau says. ᴹ

The construct stands on the other side of the court with its head turned toward the entrance. It is taller than the things they met below, built around a broad central housing and four jointed limbs. Two are planted on the floor. The others end in a shield and a heavy, short-edged striking tool.

It has not crossed the line in the stone.

Mau looks at the doorway, the two sound supports on either side and the clear stretch behind them. His scouts have checked the approach again this morning. There is a place for Ori where she can see without being asked to stand in the court.

He points it out.

Igris takes the rear position they agreed. Ori stands close enough to Mau that their sleeves almost touch, then moves half a step back when he asks. The protective extension remains within its tested distance. He asks whether she would like to watch from there or turn toward Igris.

“Here,” she says. ᴼ

“If it's too loud, tell me.” ᴹ

She nods.

He crouches for one last check of the strap which has kept the grimoire safe all week.

“You don't have to do anything to help me win. All right?” ᴹ

Ori looks at his face.

“All right.” ᴼ

He stands, and the daggers appear in his hands.

* * *

The guardian responds to the weapons.

Its planted limbs separate. The central housing drops, revealing a narrower joint beneath the shield, and the head turns once toward the doorway behind Mau before returning to him.

He notes the turn.

It is looking for an approach. That doesn't mean it is entitled to find one.

Mau advances just enough to bring himself ahead of the support. Ori remains behind him, within three of his ordinary paces. He doesn't change the active technique. The neutral space around her is still there when the construct sends its first stone chip skimming toward the entrance.

The chip slows short of reaching her.

She sees it hang, exactly as the little pebble did on the landing.

Igris is beside her without blocking her view.

Mau lets the chip fall.

The guardian comes forward.

He could cut the central housing with enough force to take the wall behind it too. The thought is there, ready in the part of him accustomed to finishing before the next thing can hurt someone. He looks at the load-bearing seam in the court, at the opening which will have to bring them home and at the little case Ori is holding.

He chooses the joint.

The first dagger leaves his hand, turning low along the court. The shield shifts to meet it. Mau lets the blade keep its line until the instant the guardian commits its weight, then gives it a measured pull with Authority.

The dagger changes direction beneath the shield.

It strikes the exposed hinge. Metal parts with a sharp crack.

The guardian's shield drops far enough for Mau to see the mechanism behind it. He calls the blade back past its outside edge instead of across Ori's line. His left hand catches it in the grip Jin-Woo made him practice, adjusted around the fingers that no longer close quite the way they once did.

The return lands harder in his palm than he wanted.

He corrects his hold.

Not perfect. Good enough to do the next thing properly.

The striking limb comes down at him.

Infinity arrests its approach. The force still drives the guardian's planted limbs against the floor. Cracks spread out beneath its feet, and Mau sees the beginnings of a second problem before the construct has finished its first attack.

He doesn't stand there letting it break the court around them.

Authority takes the weight under the damaged side. He draws it off balance, only a little, just enough that the planted foot loses its purchase. The striking limb rises as the central housing twists.

The second dagger goes through the opening that movement creates.

This time he doesn't have to correct it in flight.

It severs the narrow linkage between the housing and the rear limb. The guardian drops onto one side. Its remaining arm swings outward, sweeping through the place he would have been if he had followed his first blade all the way into the court.

He stays where he chose to stand.

Ori makes a little sound behind him. Not pain. Surprise.

“Still all right?” he asks without looking away. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴼ

The construct attempts to recover. Its shield scrapes the floor. A shutter across the housing opens and closes twice, revealing the core which drives the limbs.

Mau waits for the third opening.

He uses Authority to keep the fallen shield from closing over it, sends the recovered blade along the cleared line and cuts the drive free of its support.

The movement stops.

He catches the housing before its full weight can hit the paving.

For a few seconds the court contains nothing but the sound of small metal pieces settling.

Then the far wall begins to open.

* * *

Mau doesn't lower his hand until the guardian is resting safely on its side.

He checks the core, the disconnected limbs and the mechanism which is opening the return. Nothing is gathering itself for another strike. The presence he has watched all week has fallen quiet with the machine.

The daggers disappear into the inventory.

He turns.

Ori is looking from the broken joint to his empty hands.

“You made it turn,” she says. ᴼ

“The shield was in the way.” ᴹ

“So you sent it there.” ᴼ

“So it would put the shield there.” ᴹ

She thinks about that.

Mau looks at Igris. The knight has stayed where they agreed, ready without needing to take the fight out of Mau's hands. Ori is unharmed. The grimoire hasn't so much as touched the floor.

His chest feels strangely light.

He crouches in front of her.

“How was that?” ᴹ

“Loud.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“But I saw.” ᴼ

He nods. She reaches to touch the place on his palm where the returning dagger pressed against him.

“Does that hurt?” ᴼ

“A little less clever than I meant it to be,” he says. “I'll practice.” ᴹ

Ori accepts the answer. Then she turns to the last empty space on her map.

* * *

He reports before they approach the return.

Guardian down. Both unhurt. The frame has opened. Ciel confirms the connection and asks Rimuru for the outside view.

They wait in the cleared doorway until the answer arrives. No new prohibition appears. The shop still carries the added catalogue. The exit shows the familiar approach on the other side.

Frieren is waiting there.

Ori steps closer to Mau, eager enough that he can feel it through the hand she has put in his.

“Wait for me to check the threshold,” he says. ᴹ

She stops.

He sends one ordinary soldier through first. It reaches the outside path and reports the people whose names Mau already knows are waiting. Frieren looks at it, then toward the opening.

Mau follows with Ori beside him, not carried, her map case at her hip and the presents still where she chose to put them.

The light changes around them.

The sounds from home arrive without Ciel having to relay a single word.

Ori takes a breath and calls for her mum.
