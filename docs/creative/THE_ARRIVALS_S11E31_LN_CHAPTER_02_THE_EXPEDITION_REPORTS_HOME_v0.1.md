# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 67
## Chapter 14 — The Expedition Reports Home

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 67
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S11E31
**Continuity state:** B+195 early evening outside / internal day7 completed. Both home unharmed, temporary gate-duration Ori protection ends on departure. Existing soldiers/Igris return; twenty existing carriers briefly summoned for sorting/delivery, no new units. Bulk crossed in Mau's inventory; carriers unload by Ori's distribution plan. Gifts handed to Frieren/Yuka/Mai. No permanent father label or new romance.

*When: Frieren hears Ori call, she is already coming toward the frame.*

Ori lets go of Mau's hand to run the last few steps.

Frieren bends before they meet. The case bumps her knee, the brim of the hat catches at her sleeve and Ori's arms go around her neck with enough enthusiasm that she has to shift one foot behind her to take the hug.

Mau stops on the path.

For a little while he doesn't need to check anything.

“We finished,” Ori says into Frieren's shoulder. ᴼ

“I heard.” ᶠ

“But not everything.” ᴼ

Frieren draws back enough to see her face.

“Then you'll have to tell me.” ᶠ

Ori nods, very seriously, and starts with the part about the six-legged machines.

Mau looks past them.

Yuka is waiting beside Rimuru. She has a hand raised halfway to him, as though she started to wave and decided to let the mother and daughter have their first moment without turning it into a crowd.

He walks to her.

She takes his face between her hands, looks him over and kisses him before he can offer a medical report.

“Hi,” she says. ʸ

“Hi.” ᴹ

“Seven days?” ʸ

“About that.” ᴹ

“You did eat?” ʸ

“Under strict supervision.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸ

She puts her arms around him. He holds her close, feeling the familiar weight settle against him, and breathes in slowly enough that she notices.

“Missed you,” he says into her hair. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

She doesn't laugh at him because the day was shorter for her.

* * *

The first practical question is whether anybody is still inside.

Mau answers it with Ciel and Igris rather than assuming the return took care of it. The scouts come through. Igris follows the last one, checks the clear approach and gives his acknowledgment. The bulk of the useful stock crossed with Mau in the inventory; there is no queue of loaded soldiers trapped beyond the frame.

The little agreement with Ori has ended with their departure. Ciel confirms it. Mau tells Ori when she comes back to show Yuka the map.

She touches her sleeve, then his hand.

“Can we talk about it later?” she asks. ᴼ

“Whenever you want.” ᴹ

She accepts that and turns the map around so everyone can see it properly.

Rimuru studies the marks for their camps.

“You really did the long way.” ᴿ

“We fixed a wheel,” Ori says. ᴼ

“That seems to have added an entire terrace.” ᴿ

“It was a good wheel.” ᴹ

Ori nods beside him.

Rimuru looks between them and gives up with a smile.

* * *

The procession begins after they reach the clear unloading space near home.

Mau brings the labelled groups out of the inventory. Twenty ordinary carriers rise from his shadow at his instruction, drawn from the army which was already there. Igris has them wait while Ori checks the order she wrote last night.

Frieren turns from one of the small parcels to find her daughter standing in front of a row of soldiers with the map case open.

“These are the workshop things,” Ori says, pointing. “Those are for checking before anybody uses them. The paper stays dry.” ᴼ

The soldiers move when the instructions pass through Mau and Igris. None answers with a suddenly acquired voice. They lift the correct bundles, bring them to the places Ori chooses and stop when she asks to look at a tag.

Mau stands at her shoulder, holding a package while she decides where it belongs.

“Your assistant?” Frieren asks her. ᶠ

Ori looks up from the list.

“He's good.” ᴼ

Mau straightens.

“I'd like that in writing.” ᴹ

“It is,” Ori says, and points to the corner of the report where she has drawn him carrying the lunch. ᴼ

Frieren's mouth moves before she can hide it.

* * *

Ori gives her the book herself.

She explains the archive, the damaged shelf and how Mau checked that the writing wasn't doing anything before she touched it. Then she points to the tag which says present and waits while Frieren unwraps the corners.

Frieren turns the book over slowly.

“It might be something you know,” Ori says. ᴼ

“It might.” ᶠ

She opens it to the first diagram. Her eyes follow a line, return to the note beside it and remain there for a few moments.

Ori looks at Mau, uncertain again.

He doesn't answer for Frieren this time either.

“Where did you find it?” Frieren asks. ᶠ

Ori begins explaining, and the uncertainty vanishes into the exact part of the expedition she wanted to share. Frieren listens all the way through the lamp man, although the lamp man is in another book and is not strictly necessary to explain the gift.

When Ori finishes, Frieren rests a hand against her cheek.

“Thank you.” ᶠ

Ori leans into the touch.

Mau looks away long enough to give them the moment without making it something he has to watch perfectly.

* * *

Yuka opens the little hairpin next.

Ori tells her that the fastening has been checked and that it isn't a dangerous magical object. Yuka thanks her for both pieces of information with such seriousness that Mau has to look down at the wrapping to keep from laughing.

“Would you help me put it in?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Ori looks delighted.

Mau holds the loose hair out of the way while Yuka bends. Ori slides the pin into the place Yuka shows her, frowns at the angle and adjusts it once.

Then Yuka turns to her husband.

“Well?” ʸ

The bead catches the light beside the one person in front of him whose face still carries color. He doesn't need to decide what the bead itself is allowed to be for the sight to stop him a little.

“Very good choice,” he tells Ori. ᴹ

Yuka smiles at both of them.

Mai is less prepared for her present.

She came over to inspect the jacket, to ask whether the strap behaved and whether anything rubbed after the first day. Ori answers her questions, then holds out the wrapped tools and the small roll beside them.

“For your work,” she says. ᴼ

Mai starts to tell her she already paid. The words stop when she sees how carefully the package has been labelled.

She crouches to open it.

“These are useful,” she says after looking at the first tool. ᴹᵃ

Ori looks relieved.

“Mau said you could decide what to do with them.” ᴼ

Mai glances up at him, then back at the little face waiting for her answer.

“I can. You can come see when I do.” ᴹᵃ

Ori agrees immediately.

The expedition leader has another appointment to put on the report.
