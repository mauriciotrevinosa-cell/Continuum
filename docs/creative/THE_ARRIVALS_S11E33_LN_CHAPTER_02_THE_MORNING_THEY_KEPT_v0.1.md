# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 1 — The Morning They Kept

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S11E33
**Continuity state:** B+196 morning, directly after the common-room sleepover. Temporary Ori Infinity agreement remains ended. Mau/Yuka remain married and warm, Frieren remains Ori's mother. Ori chooses to ask about her origin with Frieren/Yuka; no new memories implanted, new construction authorized or secret meeting yet. Jin-Woo's five family members remain actual residents/guests with choices.

*When: Ori wakes, the common room is still pretending to be a bedroom.*

Someone has carried a tray halfway across it and stopped because there is no obvious place to put it. A blanket lies over one of the chairs. Near the window, a folded cushion has acquired a sleeping person who was not there when Ori closed her eyes.

Her mother's hand is beside hers.

Ori touches the fingers before sitting up. Frieren opens one eye, finds her and moves the edge of the blanket back over her knee.

“Morning.” ᶠ

“Are we staying?” ᴼ

“For a little while.” ᶠ

That is a good answer. Ori sits against the cushions instead of getting up at once.

Mau is asleep on her other side. Yuka has moved close enough to him that one of his arms is underneath her pillow. Her hand rests over his, holding it where it belongs. Ori watches them for a moment and thinks about the rain.

She has been trying to decide which part to tell her mother first.

The question under the roof is not written on the map.

She looks down at Frieren's hand again.

“Did you sleep well?” she asks. ᴼ

Frieren opens the other eye.

“Yes.” ᶠ

Ori smiles and leans into her side.

* * *

The tray belongs to Rem.

She has brought cups and hot water rather than breakfast, which has not yet become available just because Umaru asked about it with considerable urgency. She finds a clear corner, puts everything down and has to lift a pillow out of the way before she can sit.

Umaru moves it for her.

“A successful arrangement,” Umaru says. ᵁ

“Sleeping here?” ᴿᵉ

“Tea brought to the Fort's temporary embassy.” ᵁ

Rem looks at the ordinary table.

“You brought your own cup.” ᴿᵉ

“We believe in preparedness.” ᵁ

Ori hears Mau laugh before she sees his eyes open.

Yuka lifts her head at the sound. For a moment he looks confused by the number of people in front of him; then he sees Ori and Frieren, the blankets and the book beside the pillow. His face settles into the morning.

“Did I miss a constitutional change?” he asks. ᴹ

“Several,” Umaru says. ᵁ

“How expensive?” ᴹ

“We're still negotiating.” ᵁ

He nods as if this is only fair and looks down at Yuka.

“Hi.” ᴹ

She smiles before answering him.

“Hi.” ʸ

His free hand moves to her hair. The pin Ori gave her is resting safely with the wrapped things, and he leaves the loose strands as they are instead of trying to put the whole morning in order.

“Tea?” Rem asks. ᴿᵉ

Yuka sits up. Mau follows because she is still holding his hand.

* * *

Putting the room back takes longer than making a declaration that everybody should help.

Some of the bedding belongs in nearby rooms. Some needs airing. Mau brings out the empty containers they used for it and asks which ones people want carried; the inventory takes the bulk that is easier to move that way. Nothing needs to become a heroic journey down a passage because he has forgotten what he can store.

Ori folds her blanket with Frieren. The first attempt puts the corners in the wrong places. She looks at it, unfolds one side and starts that part again.

Mau kneels beside them to retrieve his missing pillow.

“Professional expedition folding?” he asks. ᴹ

“At home it's different.” ᴼ

“More regulations.” ᴹ

Frieren hands him the pillow which has been behind her back.

“You can put this one away.” ᶠ

“Evidence of a successful assistant.” ᴹ

Ori grins. He touches the brim of the hat resting beside her case without putting it on her head.

“Any complaints about the equipment?” ᴹ

“I have to tell Mai.” ᴼ

“I see. I'm not authorized.” ᴹ

She thinks about the proper order.

“You can come.” ᴼ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

He stands when Yuka calls his name from the table. She has found the sheet with the ending they wrote for the travel story and wants to know where it belongs. Ori goes over to show her.

By the time they finish, the room has almost become a common room again.

* * *

Breakfast contains the places people choose when nobody has assigned a procession.

Jin-Woo comes with Hae-In and Suho. His sister arrives a little later with their mother, carrying something she wants to ask about rather than waiting for Jin-Woo to introduce her to every useful person. Their father has already found a seat and is talking to Kaneki about the path outside.

Mau stops to greet them before he sits down.

“How did your expedition go?” Hae-In asks. ᴴᶦ

Ori, still close enough to hear, turns immediately.

Mau points toward her.

“You'll want the official report.” ᴹ

She fetches the map.

Suho makes room on the table, and Jin-Woo moves his cup before Ori has to ask. Mau sees her notice both things. She begins with the entrance, the change and the rules they agreed before continuing.

When she reaches the six-legged machines, Suho asks whether the constructs were fast.

“They were very determined,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ori demonstrates their feet with both hands.

Hae-In laughs. Jin-Woo looks at Mau across the table with the small, tired amusement of someone finding out what another strong man's afternoon can contain.

“Sounds difficult.” ᴶ

“Personnel management.” ᴹ

Ori agrees so readily that Yuka nearly loses her tea.

For a while the table follows the map. At another, someone is arguing about whether a folded blanket counts as a reserved seat. The ordinary noise of the house moves around them without becoming another task Mau needs to finish.

Yuka puts a piece of food on his plate while he is answering Suho.

He catches her wrist gently before she draws it back and kisses the inside of it.

“I'm eating.” ᴹ

“You were talking.” ʸ

“A developing skill.” ᴹ

She waits until he takes a bite.

* * *

Ori finds her mother after breakfast.

Frieren has gone back for the grimoire. She is sitting where the light falls over the page, checking the note Mau added about its condition. Ori stands beside the chair until Frieren turns toward her.

“Did you find something?” she asks. ᴼ

“A way of arranging the line I haven't seen written quite like this.” ᶠ

Ori looks relieved.

Frieren sees it and holds the book where she can look too.

“You can show me the shelf on your map later.” ᶠ

Ori nods. She has been offered another good place to begin.

The question she actually brought waits a little longer.

Frieren leaves the page open without continuing to read.

“What is it?” ᶠ

Ori looks toward the other end of the room. Mau is helping Yuta demonstrate something with an ordinary spoon to Suho, and Yuka is watching with the expression she uses when she intends to remember the result for later.

“Could I talk to DIO?” ᴼ

“Of course.” ᶠ

“About before.” ᴼ

Frieren's fingers stop beside the page.

She closes the book carefully.

“Which before?” ᶠ

Ori touches the edge of the map case.

“Me.” ᴼ

Frieren stays quiet long enough to hear what comes next.

“I know Mau remembers things. And she was there. I want to know what she did.” ᴼ

“Would you like me to come?” ᶠ

Ori takes her hand.

“Yes.” ᴼ

She looks toward Yuka again. This time Frieren follows the look.

“Her too?” she asks. ᶠ

Ori nods.

* * *

Yuka doesn't ask Mau to explain the invitation for Ori.

She comes over when Ori calls, listens to the request and crouches beside the chair. Frieren remains close enough for Ori to keep holding her hand.

“You want me there?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“If you can.” ᴼ

“I can.” ʸ

“Mau doesn't have to come yet.” ᴼ

Yuka glances toward him, then back.

“Then we'll tell him you're with us. You can decide what you'd like to tell him afterwards.” ʸ

Ori nods, visibly relieved by the distinction.

Across the room, Mau looks up when Yuka reaches him. She tells him they are going to see DIO, that Ori wants to ask something about herself and has chosen company.

He looks toward the chair.

Ori meets his eyes. He smiles and gives her a little wave instead of coming over to find out whether she needs him more than she has asked for.

She waves back.

Then Frieren stands, the book left safely on the table, and the three of them go together.
