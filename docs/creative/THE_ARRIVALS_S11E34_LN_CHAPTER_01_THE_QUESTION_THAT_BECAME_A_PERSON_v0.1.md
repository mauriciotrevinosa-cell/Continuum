# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 2 — The Question That Became a Person

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S11E34
**Continuity state:** B+196 late morning. Ori asks DIO about her origin with Frieren/Yuka present in the protected guest accommodation. DIO describes her own intervention and the original line, not a memory download or a new claim to control Ori. This line's mother remains Frieren. No automatic import of earlier relationships or private archives.

*When: DIO sees who has come with Ori, she puts the paper in her hand down without folding it away.*

“Come in.” ᴰ

Ori steps inside first. Frieren follows her, and Yuka closes the door after checking that DIO wants it closed.

The room is ordinary in ways Ori has grown used to. A cup left near a book. Something on the chair which has to be moved before a visitor can sit. DIO picks it up, looks briefly for somewhere to put it and finally sets it on the bed.

Usually Ori would have asked what she was doing.

Today she sits beside her mother and keeps her hands together.

DIO takes the chair opposite them.

“Did you bring the expedition?” she asks. ᴰ

“I can show you later.” ᴼ

“Then I would like that.” ᴰ

Ori nods. The answer has left her a later, which is useful. She looks at Yuka, finds her waiting without trying to begin for her and turns back.

“I want to ask about me.” ᴼ

DIO's smile changes.

She doesn't glance at Frieren for permission to understand the question.

“What would you like to know?” ᴰ

“How I happened.” ᴼ

* * *

There is a quiet interval in which DIO seems to consider several easier answers.

Ori can almost see them. A story about finding her. Something about how much everybody loves her now. Both might be true enough to let an adult finish talking without having answered.

She keeps looking at DIO.

“I know there was before,” she says. “Mau remembers. And you were there.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴰ

“Did you make me?” ᴼ

DIO rests her hands on her knees.

“The beginning of you came from something I did.” ᴰ

Frieren's hand is near Ori's. She hasn't closed it around the little fingers to make them stop moving.

“Why?” Ori asks. ᴼ

DIO looks at her for long enough that the word becomes harder to answer honestly.

“Because I wanted to understand Mau.” ᴰ

Yuka stays very still beside them.

* * *

DIO begins with what she watched before Ori existed.

She doesn't describe it as a game with charming rules. She tells Ori that she had given Mau choices which were supposed to leave him with only two futures. In the choices she was offering, staying alive had a price somebody else would have to pay.

“He wouldn't accept that,” she says. ᴰ

Ori looks down at her hands.

“So you gave him another way?” ᴼ

“I gave him more reasons to choose one of the ways I already understood.” ᴰ

The answer draws Frieren's eyes to her face.

DIO holds the look for a moment, then returns to Ori.

“He kept choosing to put himself where I thought nobody would stay. There were things he could have done to live. He would not make the people he loved the cost of it.” ᴰ

“And he died?” ᴼ

“In that life, yes.” ᴰ

Ori looks toward her mother quickly.

Frieren's expression is quiet, not empty. She brings her hand a little closer, and this time Ori takes it.

DIO waits before continuing.

“I wanted to know why. Not the answer he could say when somebody asked. I wanted to be close enough to see what he did when the question wasn't being put in front of him.” ᴰ

Ori looks back.

“So you made me.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴰ

There is no soft word between them to make that purpose smaller.

* * *

For a little while Ori doesn't ask anything else.

DIO's room remains the same around her. The cup is still beside the book. Yuka is still sitting near enough to reach. Somewhere outside, a person laughs and the sound goes past the window without coming into the conversation.

Ori thinks of the things she intended to show DIO. The broken wheel. Mai's fittings. The place where Mau waited while she counted.

“Was that why?” she asks. ᴼ

DIO leans forward slightly.

“Which part?” ᴰ

Ori opens one hand, then closes it again.

“All of it.” ᴼ

Nobody answers before she is ready to find the words.

“If you wanted to know him, is that why I want to be with him? Is that why I love Mum? Was that already what I was supposed to do?” ᴼ

Frieren's fingers tighten once around hers.

Yuka puts her own hands flat in her lap instead of reaching across to interrupt the question with a hug.

DIO's face changes in a way Ori hasn't seen during their ordinary visits.

“Ori,” she says. ᴰ

Ori looks at her.

“I am telling you why I acted. I am not telling you why every choice you make belongs to me.” ᴰ

“But you made—” ᴼ

“The beginning. And then there was someone who could choose something I had not chosen for her.” ᴰ

She pauses, taking care with what she can actually promise.

“I can't undo my reason by saying it was kinder than it was. I wanted something. I made a life part of how I tried to get it. But you did not remain only the answer to my question.” ᴰ

Ori looks at Frieren's hand holding hers.

DIO follows the look.

“Did you ask me whether you could choose that hand?” she asks. ᴰ

Ori shakes her head.

“Did I choose the book you brought her?” ᴰ

Another small shake.

“Those are yours.” ᴰ

The certainty doesn't make Ori stop hurting immediately. DIO seems to understand that. She stays where she is rather than asking whether the answer helped.

* * *

“What was I like?” Ori asks eventually. ᴼ

DIO looks at Frieren and Yuka then, not for permission to answer but to acknowledge the other life sitting in the room with them.

“You took time to understand what being close to him meant to you. Nobody could decide it into something simple for you.” ᴰ

“Did I decide?” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴰ

Ori waits.

“You chose him as your father.” ᴰ

Frieren lets out a breath so quietly that Ori feels it more than hears it.

“And Mum?” ᴼ

“You loved her too. You built things with her which were yours together. They didn't stop mattering when he wasn't in the room.” ᴰ

Ori moves nearer to Frieren's side.

DIO tells her what she can from her own part in that history. How the people around Mau became people whose choices he would put ahead of his own escape. How Ori's safety became something he would bargain for when DIO expected him to bargain for himself. How a door he would refuse for his own sake became another question when somebody he loved was placed on the other side of the price.

Frieren looks up at that part.

“You don't have to tell it as something she owes him for,” she says. ᶠ

“She doesn't,” DIO answers at once. ᴰ

She faces Ori again.

“You were not a debt. He wasn't waiting for you to pay him back by loving him correctly.” ᴰ

Ori wipes at one eye with the back of her hand, annoyed by how little warning it gave her.

“Did he know?” ᴼ

“That you loved him?” ᴰ

She nods.

“You found ways to tell him.” ᴰ

* * *

Ori looks at Yuka.

“Were you there?” ᴼ

“In the life he remembers, yes,” Yuka says. “I don't have her memories. I know what Mau has chosen to tell me.” ʸ

“Did I like you?” ᴼ

Yuka's smile trembles a little before it settles.

“From what he's told me, yes.” ʸ

DIO nods.

“You didn't keep love in separate rooms so that choosing one person required leaving someone else outside.” ᴰ

Ori's eyes return to Frieren, then to Yuka.

They are here. This room, these hands, the pin in Yuka's hair which she picked herself.

She presses her lips together.

“Mau said something,” she begins. ᴼ

Frieren turns toward her properly.

“In the gate?” ᶠ

Ori nods.

DIO leans back a little, leaving space for a conversation which is no longer hers to begin.
