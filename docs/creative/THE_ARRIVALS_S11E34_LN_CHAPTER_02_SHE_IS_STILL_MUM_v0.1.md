# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 3 — She Is Still Mum

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S11E34
**Continuity state:** B+196 late morning, same four-person conversation. Ori shares her own Purple conversation voluntarily. Frieren affirms mother bond and Ori's freedom to want Mau as father; Yuka identifies herself without demanding maternal title. No imposed adoption decree or current Mau/Frieren romance. Himmel's relationship remains his own, not a substitute father assigned to Ori. No one has told Mau this conversation yet.

*When: Ori starts telling them about the rain, Frieren doesn't ask her to get to the important part.*

She describes the shelter first.

The hat worked. The paper stayed dry. Mau had shown her where water would run if the rain became heavier, and she had moved the bedding because it would have been in the wrong place otherwise.

Yuka nods at the part about the hat. DIO asks whether the map case held up. Ori answers both before remembering what she meant to say.

“And I asked him something.” ᴼ

Frieren keeps her hand where it has been all through the story.

“You can tell us.” ᶠ

Ori looks at her, then down at their hands.

“I said it feels like having a dad there.” ᴼ

Nobody makes a sound large enough to turn the words into an announcement.

“Being with Mau?” Frieren asks. ᶠ

Ori nods.

“But I thought…” ᴼ

She stops and tries again.

“When people have a mum and a dad, they're together. But you and Mau aren't. And I didn't know if saying it would mean I wanted you to be.” ᴼ

Frieren's fingers become very still around hers.

“Do you?” she asks quietly. ᶠ

Ori's face tightens.

“I don't want anybody to go away.” ᴼ

“Nobody is being asked to go away,” Yuka says. ʸ

Ori looks at her quickly.

Yuka stays close without reaching across Frieren's place.

“You can keep telling us.” ʸ

* * *

It takes a little longer with both of them listening.

Under the shelter it had seemed difficult enough to say it to Mau. Here her mother can hear every place where Ori is afraid a word will make something change.

“He said I could love you,” she tells Frieren. “And want time with him. And like being with Yuka. It didn't have to mean I stopped choosing somebody.” ᴼ

Frieren lowers her eyes to their joined hands.

“He said you were my mum.” ᴼ

“I am.” ᶠ

“And that wasn't changing.” ᴼ

Frieren looks up again.

“No.” ᶠ

The answer comes without the uncertainty Ori has been trying to solve for her.

Her mother's shoulders ease. Ori sees it happen and understands, belatedly, that there might have been something Frieren was afraid of too.

“I wasn't trying to get a different mum.” ᴼ

“I know.” ᶠ

“Or make you sad.” ᴼ

Frieren moves her free hand to Ori's cheek.

“You can tell me something important even if I need a moment with it.” ᶠ

Ori leans into her palm.

“I love you.” ᴼ

“I love you too.” ᶠ

For a while that is enough for both of them to say.

* * *

When Frieren asks the next question, she asks it as though there is room for the answer.

“Would you like Mau to be your papa?” ᶠ

Ori looks at her mother's face.

She has imagined the word often enough to know how it feels before she says it.

“Yes.” ᴼ

Frieren nods slowly.

“Why?” ᶠ

Ori considers the things she has heard today: another life, another choice, a door and a promise. They matter. She knows they matter.

But the answer she finds belongs to last week inside the gate.

“He's there,” she says. ᴼ

Yuka's eyes lower for a moment.

“Even when I don't know what to ask. He waits. And he tells me if something's dangerous, but he lets me do things.” ᴼ

She brushes her sleeve where the case has left a small crease.

“I don't only want him when we go out. Sometimes I want him when nothing's happening.” ᴼ

Frieren's hand moves once over her hair.

“Have you told him that you love him like a papa?” ᶠ

Ori shakes her head.

“I said some of it.” ᴼ

“Would you like to tell him the rest?” ᶠ

Ori begins to nod, then looks toward Yuka.

The small movement brings everything she has not yet asked into the space between them.

“But Mum is Mum. And if he's…” ᴼ

She makes a helpless motion with one hand.

Yuka moves off the chair and crouches beside them.

“I'm Yuka,” she says. ʸ

Ori watches her.

“I'm Mau's wife. And I'm your friend.” ʸ

“But do I have to—” ᴼ

“You don't have to find a new name for me before we're allowed to keep loving each other.” ʸ

Ori swallows.

Yuka holds out her hand, palm up.

“You can call me Yuka. You can ask me to come sit with you. You can tell me you want your mum, or you want Mau, and I'll help you find them. I'm still going to love you.” ʸ

Ori puts her hand into hers.

“Even if I don't know what it is?” ᴼ

“Even then.” ʸ

“Or change it?” ᴼ

Yuka smiles.

“Then you can tell me what you like better.” ʸ

Ori makes a small sound and reaches for her before she has quite decided whether to laugh or cry.

Yuka comes close enough for the hug without pulling her out of Frieren's lap.

* * *

DIO reaches for the cloth beside her cup.

She offers it rather than wiping Ori's face herself. Ori takes it, presses it under her eyes and hands it back because it has already become rather wet.

“It will survive,” DIO says. ᴰ

Ori laughs through the last uncertain breath.

Then she turns fully toward Frieren.

“You don't mind?” ᴼ

Frieren looks at her for a moment.

There are several kinds of honesty she could choose. She could describe the small ache of wanting Ori close while also wanting her to have the happiness she has just described. She could explain how much a life can change while someone is still trying to understand which part she thought would remain still.

Ori is asking whether she is allowed to love somebody.

Frieren gives her the answer to that.

“I don't mind you wanting him as your papa.” ᶠ

Ori's arms go around her neck.

Frieren holds her properly, one hand between her shoulders and the other beneath the back of her head.

“You're my mum,” Ori says into her shoulder. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᶠ

“I still love you.” ᴼ

Frieren closes her eyes.

“I know.” ᶠ

She lets the little body settle against her before adding anything else.

“You can love me and love other people. You don't have to make one smaller to keep the other.” ᶠ

Ori stays there until the words have stopped feeling like something she needs to hold very carefully.

* * *

They don't leave as soon as the crying stops.

DIO gets another cup. Yuka asks whether Ori wants water before anything warm. Frieren remains where she is while Ori leans against her and starts looking around the room as if it has become an ordinary place again.

After a while Ori asks DIO whether she would like to hear about the wheelhouse now.

DIO looks at her.

“Yes.” ᴰ

“We fixed something.” ᴼ

“I would very much like to hear that.” ᴰ

Ori goes to get the map from the case. Yuka helps keep the grimoire's wrapping from getting caught in the strap, then leaves her to open it herself.

Frieren watches them.

Himmel would enjoy the story, she thinks. He would ask about the bridge and the path Ori chose. He would probably find something worth praising in the fact that she decided to go around.

The thought belongs to him. It doesn't require him to become a place someone else has left empty.

She looks down when Ori spreads the map across her knees.

“Here,” Ori says, pointing. “This was the bad way.” ᴼ

Frieren bends close to see.
