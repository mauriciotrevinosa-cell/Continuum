# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 4 — When She Calls Him

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S11E35
**Continuity state:** B+196 afternoon. Ori chooses to tell Mau how she wants their bond named, after the conversation of four. Frieren remains her mother and her home/bedroom unchanged; Yuka keeps her own name and bond. Mau's memories of the original line do not compel current Ori. No legal decree, marriage change or claim on Himmel. No secret construction yet.

*When: Ori comes to find him, Mau has the wheelhouse diagram upside down.*

Stark points to it.

“Unless the water goes up.” ˢᵗ

Mau turns the paper around.

“An advanced feature.” ᴹ

“Did it?” ˢᵗ

“Not while we were there.” ᴹ

Ori laughs from the doorway.

Both of them look up. Stark moves his hand off the corner he has been holding, and Mau leaves the diagram where they can return to it without having to remember what they were doing.

“Did we get something else wrong?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Ori shakes her head.

She has put her hat away. The map case is gone too. For the first time since they set off, there is nothing in her hands to explain why she has come.

“Can you come with me?” ᴼ

“Of course.” ᴹ

He tells Stark they will finish later. Stark nods and finds an actual clear place for the diagram, apparently unwilling to trust either of them to recognize a safe table edge.

Ori waits until Mau reaches her before taking his hand.

* * *

She leads him to one of the quieter seats facing the courtyard.

It is near enough to hear somebody call from the common room and far enough that they do not have to become the next conversation at the table. The tree is visible beyond the open walk. A little wind moves the edge of a cloth hanging out to dry.

Mau sits when she asks him to.

Ori gets onto the seat beside him, folds her hands and looks toward the courtyard.

He follows her gaze, waiting.

“I talked to DIO,” she says. ᴼ

“Yuka told me you wanted to.” ᴹ

“About before.” ᴼ

He turns toward her a little more.

“Did she answer?” ᴹ

Ori nods, then rubs one hand over the other.

“Some of it was sad.” ᴼ

Mau feels Ciel's attention settle beside his own. She says nothing. He is grateful for the quiet.

“Do you want to tell me?” he asks. ᴹ

“She said she wanted to know you. And that's why I was there.” ᴼ

He looks at the little hands in her lap.

There are old answers he has carried for a long time. None of them is large enough to make the beginning gentle simply because he wishes she could hear it that way.

“How did that feel?” he asks instead. ᴹ

Ori looks down.

“Like maybe I was only doing things she wanted.” ᴼ

He stays very still.

“And now?” ᴹ

“She said I chose things. That I wasn't just…” ᴼ

She searches for the word, then gives up and opens her hands.

Mau puts one of his beside them on the seat.

“You can take your time.” ᴹ

She rests her fingers over his.

* * *

“She said I chose you,” Ori says. ᴼ

Mau's eyes close for a moment.

He opens them again before she has to wonder whether he heard.

“In the other life,” she adds. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“As my papa.” ᴼ

The word changes the afternoon around him without making a sound larger than her voice.

He looks at her face. This face. The girl who counted the steps, whose hat left a mark on her forehead, who asked whether he would come even if she didn't need an assistant.

“She did,” he says. ᴹ

Ori thinks about that for a while.

“Do you miss her?” ᴼ

He takes a careful breath.

“Sometimes I remember things which nobody else here remembers with me. That can hurt.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

He turns his hand beneath hers.

“But when I'm with you, I'm with you. I don't want you to do something because another Ori did it first.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴼ

The certainty surprises him enough that she notices.

“You waited,” she says. ᴼ

He looks down at their hands.

“I wanted you to have the time.” ᴹ

“I talked to Mum too.” ᴼ

He nods.

“She said I could love you like that. And she's still my mum.” ᴼ

“Always.” ᴹ

“Yuka said she's Yuka.” ᴼ

Despite everything gathering in his chest, Mau laughs softly.

“She is.” ᴹ

“And she loves me even if I don't have a name for it.” ᴼ

He nods again, unable for a moment to improve on what his wife has said.

Ori looks toward the common room, then back at him.

“I think I have a name for this one.” ᴼ

* * *

He doesn't make her ask three different ways before believing her.

“Can I call you Papa?” she says. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

The answer comes before the breath he has been preparing to take.

Ori searches his face.

“You want me to?” ᴼ

Mau bends closer, his free hand resting on the edge of the seat because he needs something ordinary to do with it.

“If that's what you want, I'd love that.” ᴹ

“It is.” ᴼ

Her eyes are wet. He notices his own are too late to pretend otherwise.

Ori shifts toward him, and he lifts her onto his lap when she reaches.

She puts her arms around his neck.

For a little while there are no words for either of them to get right.

He holds her with one hand at her back and the other supporting her where she has climbed close. The courtyard remains around them. Somebody walks past the far end of the covered path, sees them and keeps going without calling out.

Ori presses her face against his shoulder.

“I love you,” she says. ᴼ

Mau makes a small, uneven sound before he can answer.

“I love you too.” ᴹ

He says it again because the first time barely made it through.

* * *

Eventually Ori draws back enough to look at him.

“Are you sad?” ᴼ

He shakes his head, then thinks about the answer.

“There's a lot happening at once. Mostly I'm very happy.” ᴹ

She considers that and accepts it more readily than she would have accepted him saying there was nothing at all.

He wipes one cheek with his sleeve. Ori does the other with hers, then looks displeased by the result.

Mau produces a clean cloth from the inventory.

“Should have started with the equipment.” ᴹ

She laughs, leaning against him again.

He lets the afternoon settle. There is no need to explain where she will sleep tonight or to move the things in her room before this can count. Frieren's place in her life has not narrowed. Yuka has not been asked to become a replacement for anyone.

Ori has chosen a word for what she wants with him.

He can hold that without trying to turn it into the next arrangement everybody must make.

* * *

Yuka finds them when it is time to bring the cups back.

She stops at the edge of the seat, sees their faces and sets the little tray down before coming closer.

“Room?” she asks. ʸ

Mau moves one knee enough for her to sit beside them.

Ori reaches for her hand without leaving his lap.

“I told him.” ᴼ

Yuka smiles.

“How did it go?” ʸ

Ori looks at Mau.

“He cried.” ᴼ

“A little,” he says. ᴹ

Ori looks at his damp sleeve.

Yuka laughs softly and leans her forehead against his temple. He closes his eyes for the small contact, his arm still secure around the girl against his chest.

“Good afternoon?” she asks him. ʸ

“Very.” ᴹ

Ori shifts so she can see both of them, then begins explaining that she still needs to tell her mother what happened. Mau nods, and Yuka says she knows where Frieren went with the grimoire.

When they stand, Ori reaches for Mau's hand.

“Papa?” ᴼ

He turns immediately.

“Yes?” ᴹ

She smiles at how easy the answer was.

“Can we go show Mum the map again?” ᴼ

Mau looks toward the covered path, where the sounds from home are waiting.

“Yes,” he says. “We can.” ᴹ
