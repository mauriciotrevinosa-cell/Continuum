# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 5 — No Room Has to Move

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S11E35
**Continuity state:** B+196 afternoon/night. Lived consequences of Ori's chosen paternal name. Frieren hears it from Ori, family privacy preserved. Fort receives chosen news, not DIO's private account. Household/rooms remain in place. Married Mau/Yuka affectionate, future-family question opens without pregnancy, fertility guarantee or calendar claim. No forbidden construction order yet.

*When: they find Frieren, she is showing Himmel the grimoire.*

Ori slows at the end of the path.

Himmel looks up first. His smile reaches her before he stands, and she brings Mau along the last few steps rather than waiting for the adults to arrange an introduction they no longer need.

“Did you bring another report?” Himmel asks. ᴴ

“A little one.” ᴼ

Frieren closes the book over a loose sheet.

Ori looks at Mau, then at her mother.

“I asked him.” ᴼ

Frieren's expression softens.

“And?” ᶠ

“He said yes.” ᴼ

Mau stands beside Yuka with the strange feeling that he has arrived to be reported on by somebody whose account matters much more than his own.

Frieren looks at him.

“Good,” she says. ᶠ

He nods, still not entirely trusting his voice with a larger answer.

Ori steps forward to hug her. Frieren bends to meet her, and Himmel takes the book before its loose sheet can slide out.

It is an ordinary, useful movement. He puts it safely on the seat and waits while mother and daughter finish.

“Would you like to hear about the wheel?” Ori asks him afterwards. ᴼ

“Very much.” ᴴ

Mau glances at Yuka.

“Another authorized audience.” ᴹ

“You could learn something,” she says. ʸ

“I was there.” ᴹ

“And the diagram was upside down.” ʸ

Ori turns, appalled by the speed of the information.

Himmel starts laughing before she can explain.

* * *

The next audience is less patient about waiting for the wheel.

Umaru finds them while Ori is demonstrating where the cart almost fell. Chloe is beside her, comparing a new mark with the map. Mau has remained within reach, but he is no longer being asked to supply every missing word.

“Papa caught it,” Ori says. ᴼ

Umaru stops.

She looks at Mau, then Ori, then at Yuka sitting near enough to have heard.

For a moment Mau expects the usual machinery of a Fort announcement to begin.

Umaru crouches instead.

“Did you decide?” she asks Ori. ᵁ

Ori nods.

“With him?” ᵁ

Another nod.

Umaru smiles and opens her arms. Ori goes into them without being asked to justify what kind of family it makes.

Mau looks down at the map until the sudden sting in his eyes has become manageable.

When Umaru releases her, she looks up at him with considerably more of her usual authority.

“There will be administrative consequences.” ᵁ

“For me?” ᴹ

“Obviously.” ᵁ

He considers arguing, sees Ori laughing and decides he can afford this government.

“Submit the paperwork.” ᴹ

Chloe asks whether the paperwork has to go in the expedition case. Ori immediately objects: the case is organized, and the Fort can use its own folder.

Umaru accepts the jurisdictional distinction.

Rem arrives in time to hear the last part. She touches Umaru's shoulder as she passes, listens when Ori tells her and gives Mau a look which makes no demand of him at all.

“That's lovely,” she says. ᴿᵉ

He manages to answer her this time.

“It is.” ᴹ

* * *

Not everybody needs the whole story.

Ori tells the people she wants to tell. If someone asks where the idea came from, she can say they talked during the expedition. DIO's account remains a conversation she had with people she chose. It does not become an explanation Mau offers over supper to make the name seem legitimate.

He sits with her while she puts the little tools and the map case back in order. One of Mai's fittings has acquired a scratch. Ori finds it, touches the mark and looks at him.

“It still works,” he says. ᴹ

“Should we tell her?” ᴼ

“You can show her. Equipment gets used.” ᴹ

She considers that, then leaves the scratch where it is.

Later she goes home with Frieren carrying the book herself.

Mau watches them take the covered path. Ori turns once to wave. He waves back and stays where he is instead of finding a reason to follow them all the way to the door.

Yuka's fingers move between his.

“You can be happy,” she says softly. ʸ

He turns toward her.

“I am.” ᴹ

“I know. You look like you're trying to work out what you're supposed to do with it.” ʸ

He looks at the path again.

“A little.” ᴹ

Yuka leans against his arm.

“Come have supper.” ʸ

* * *

That night their own room is quiet.

There is no extra bedding on the floor, no expedition bag between the bed and the chair. Yuka places the hairpin beside the things she actually uses, then catches Mau watching and holds it up.

“You think she was right?” ʸ

“About you liking it?” ᴹ

“About the choice.” ʸ

He comes closer.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Me too.” ʸ

She sets it down and turns toward him. He puts his arms around her, rests his face near her hair and stays there long enough for the posture to become an embrace rather than a pause on the way to something else.

Yuka moves her hand up his back.

“A lot today?” ʸ

He nods against her.

She waits.

“I remembered wanting to hear it,” he says. “And trying not to want it so much that she could feel what I was waiting for.” ᴹ

Yuka holds him a little closer.

“She chose.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

He draws back enough to see her face.

“Thank you. For what you said to her.” ᴹ

“I meant it.” ʸ

“I know that too.” ᴹ

They sit on the bed together. Yuka leans against him while he takes the tie out of her hair, and he has to stop halfway through because she turns to kiss him.

When she settles again, her cheek rests over his heart.

“Someday,” she says, “I'd like us to have that too.” ʸ

His hand stops beside her hair.

“A child?” ᴹ

She nods, then lifts her head so she can see him answer.

“I still want that,” he says. ᴹ

“I wasn't asking for tomorrow.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

He touches her cheek.

“I want to be able to talk about it properly. What we can find out. What we'd need.” ᴹ

Yuka studies him, then smiles a little.

“We can find things out together.” ʸ

“Together.” ᴹ

She puts her forehead against his.

For tonight they leave it there: a thing they want, still allowed to be wanted without becoming a promise he has to make possible before morning.

He lies down when she does.

Her hand finds his in the dark.
