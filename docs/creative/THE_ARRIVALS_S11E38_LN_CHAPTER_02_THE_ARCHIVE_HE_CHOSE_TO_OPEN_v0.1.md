# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 11 — The Archive He Chose to Open

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S11E38
**Continuity state:** B+198 afternoon. Mau deliberately opens the entire acquired Sukuna archive to himself/Noise for study; previous closure was voluntary and real. Full access to acquired technical record is different from watching every lifetime minute or mastering all operations. Selected exchanges from their actual duel and an older unnamed fight are examined. No living Sukuna, automatic possession, CT of every opponent, new domain or physiological upgrade. No Makima visit yet.

*When: Noise asks whether he wants only the fight, Mau looks at the closed archive for a long time.*

He has chosen an afternoon when there is space to sit without making somebody wait for him at a door.

Yuka is with Mai. Ori has gone with Frieren. His next promised task is later, and the ordinary watch does not need him to stand beside every soldier in order to work. He has put water within reach because sitting still is not the same thing as remembering to drink.

There is no emergency making the choice for him.

*All of it,* he tells Noise. ᴹ

The archive remains closed for another moment.

*Memories?* ᴺ

*The record we took. What he knew. How he did it. I want to be able to look at the whole of what we actually have.* ᴹ

Noise draws the boundary of the retained material where he can examine it. Not an invitation to a person waiting on the other side. Not a new bargain with the man he released. The knowledge was already his to face or leave alone.

He has left it alone for a reason.

He remembers the last exchange, the short time in which Sukuna had accepted an answer and then gone where Mau could no longer ask him anything. He does not need to make that departure false to study what remains.

*Open it.* ᴹ

* * *

The first thing he chooses is familiar enough to hurt.

Their own fight.

A line he remembers from the other side. His body had been turning toward what seemed the next useful opening. The archive gives him the decisions which had made the opening appear: a pressure offered, a movement held back, the calculation that Mau would see enough to commit and not enough to know what was being kept for the next breath.

He stops the sequence before the wound.

*There,* he says. ᴹ

Noise holds it.

Mau looks at the difference between what Sukuna could do and what Sukuna wanted him to believe he would do.

They are not always the same shape.

He studies it once with his own memory beside it. Then again without the part he remembers thinking was important.

The movement becomes smaller.

That is the first surprise. He had expected more force in the answer. There is force, more than enough, but the advantage begins before the release. Sukuna has already made the next position inconvenient to refuse.

Mau feels his hand begin to imitate the preparation.

He stops it before supplying anything through the pattern.

Reading is not a reason to let every useful operation run through him while he is seated indoors.

Noise keeps the action separate from its construction.

* * *

They go back farther.

The older exchange has no names Mau can use. A fighter in worn clothing, the ground broken near one foot, the particular forward lean of someone who has learned that being brave can sometimes get him inside an opponent's timing.

Mau sees the approach and thinks of an answer.

The memory gives Sukuna's instead.

He lets the first contact happen.

There is a cost to it. Pain, received without making pain the only useful fact in the moment. The contact fixes the other fighter in a place the man believes he has chosen. The next movement ends the exchange.

Mau closes the image afterwards and reaches for the water.

The person is dead in a record of a time he cannot change. He has already learned the movement before he has decided how he feels about having learned it.

*Useful,* Noise says quietly. ᴺ

Mau puts the cup down.

*Yes.* ᴹ

He doesn't like how readily the answer arrives.

*Not the whole thing,* he adds. *The way he took the position. Keep the cost with it. I don't want it becoming a free answer because the record ends before I have to carry the wound.* ᴹ

Noise keeps both.

* * *

With the acquired archive open, the relationships in it become easier to follow.

A change in the supply which makes an old operation possible under worse conditions. A way of preparing a release before the body appears committed to it. The difference between an incantation used because it is needed and one used to persuade an observer that there is time left to act.

Mau recognizes things he has fought, things he has attempted and things he has only ever held as possible.

He has Noise separate them.

What he already executes. What his body can attempt with practice. What requires construction. What cannot be responsibly joined to the next thing merely because both are now visible to him.

The resulting work does not fit into a single neat answer.

Neither does the archive.

Having its acquired contents available does not mean he has lived through every minute again this afternoon. He can follow a question to its source now instead of stopping at the place he had chosen to leave shut. He can return to the older fights, compare decisions and examine a technique without pretending the name alone supplies its execution.

He can also see the boundary of what the record contains.

An opponent's attack, even one Sukuna understood well enough to defeat, is not necessarily a complete account of the opponent's own technique. Noise marks missing relationships instead of filling them with confidence.

Mau leaves those questions open.

There is already enough here to keep them working for a long time.

* * *

He takes the first movement outside.

An empty stretch of practice ground. No domain. No released cut. He has a wooden trainer in his hand because his own grip should be part of the exercise before a weapon's advantage becomes part of the result.

The remembered action feels easy until he tries to do it.

His shoulder commits too soon.

He stops, resets his feet and begins again.

On the second attempt he delays the shoulder and loses the position he wanted. On the third he understands why changing only one part of the movement cannot give him the whole of the answer.

Noise brings the two points close enough to compare.

Mau looks, tries and misses again.

He laughs once under his breath.

*Of course.* ᴹ

Knowing what the man had done has not made his own body agree to it.

He works more slowly. Finds the preparation, the place where he would invite the contact and the choice he would have to make if the contact became real. The movement has consequences even when nobody is standing there to supply them.

By the time he gets one attempt he wants to keep, the light has moved across the far wall.

He stops with it still imperfect.

* * *

Yuka finds him putting the trainer away.

“Busy afternoon?” she asks. ʸ

He turns toward her.

She has a small thing Mai has been helping her repair. He asks to see it, and she shows him the change with enough pleasure that he can tell the work has finally done what she wanted.

“That's better,” he says. ᴹ

“Much.” ʸ

She touches his arm.

“What were you doing?” ʸ

“Looking at a movement I wanted to understand.” ᴹ

“Did you?” ʸ

He glances at the trainer in its place.

“Some of it.” ᴹ

She nods and takes his hand for the walk back.

He tells himself that answer is true.

Inside, the archive remains open for their return.
