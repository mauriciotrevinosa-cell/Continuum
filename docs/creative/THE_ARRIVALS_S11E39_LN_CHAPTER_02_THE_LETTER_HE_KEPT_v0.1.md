# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 13 — The Letter He Kept

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S11E39
**Continuity state:** B+199 morning/day. A fresh Makima letter is left at the exterior exchange point and reported through Mau's existing forty-soldier/Muster watch. Mau takes custody during his own routine check, privately replies for B+200 afternoon and does not forward the offer to the house. Letter delivery does not renew a vow. No promise of perfect secrecy, no Ciel shutdown, no relocation of the fixed ward. Jin-Woo/family and Yuka have their own day.

*When: the soldier shows him the folded paper, Mau knows who left it before he reads it.*

The watch has no opinion to offer about the handwriting. It gives him the remembered approach, the woman stopping beyond its assigned limit, the paper put down where messages can be collected without admitting the person carrying them.

Then the woman leaving.

No attack. No request for entry.

Mau reviews the return path before taking the letter. This is part of the morning check he told the house he would make. Muster has the ordinary positions covered; the soldier in front of him is one of the same forty, not a new person hired to know when he walks outside.

He unfolds the paper.

*You thanked me for the devil.*

*I have been thinking about your answer.*

There is a place farther down the road, away from the protected boundary, and two afternoons on which she says she will be there. No claim that they have an agreement because she has offered one. No account of what happened to Yuka.

The last sentence is shorter.

*There is a question you have not asked me directly.*

Mau reads it again.

He can hear his wife saying the question in the room where they examined the attack. He remembers Rimuru separating the action from the visible order. A clock, an empty approach, an undertaking which held through the moment that almost killed her.

They have asked one another.

They have not received an answer from the person who could give it.

* * *

He could bring the letter in.

Fern would put it where everyone who needs to read it could find it. Rimuru would ask which new term Makima expects them to trust. Frieren would sit through the sentences without letting their ordinary sound make them less ugly.

Yuka would want to choose whether she came.

Mau folds the paper along the crease Makima made.

It is the image of that last choice which stops him.

He sees the chair beside his, her hand over his hand, the terrible interval between understanding that an attack has happened and understanding what is left to save.

He knows that taking the letter inside does not require another meeting. He knows it even while the next thought arrives: if he goes without her, she cannot be the person beside him when the answer turns cruel.

That thought is easier to hold than what she would say about being excluded from the decision.

He puts the letter in his inventory.

The place he keeps it has room beside the harmless things they brought home together.

* * *

Jin-Woo finds him later with a trainer in his hand.

“Again?” he asks. ᴶ

Mau looks down as though the piece of wood might provide an innocent explanation for being in a practice yard.

“It was here.” ᴹ

“So were you.” ᴶ

“A suspicious coincidence.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo takes the second trainer.

The first exchange is slow. Mau is working on the movement from yesterday, trying to keep the preparation small enough that it doesn't announce the answer before he has chosen to use it. Jin-Woo touches his shoulder with the flat of the wood.

“You were waiting for the movement you remembered,” he says. ᴶ

Mau lets out a breath.

“I saw it.” ᴹ

“You saw one answer.” ᴶ

They reset.

This time Jin-Woo doesn't give him the approach at all. He leaves a space Mau wants to occupy, waits until Mau decides it is an invitation and takes his wrist instead.

Mau looks at the captured hand.

“I liked you better when your family was distracting you.” ᴹ

“They're very distracting.” ᴶ

“Apparently not enough.” ᴹ

Hae-In comes to the edge of the yard before they can begin a third time. She waits until both trainers are lowered.

“Your mother wants to know whether you're coming with us,” she tells Jin-Woo. ᴴᶦ

He looks toward Mau.

Mau holds both wooden weapons out to take them back.

“Go. I need to lose against something less judgmental.” ᴹ

“The post won't tell you what you did wrong.” ᴶ

“Exactly.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo gives him the trainer and goes to his wife. She asks about the wrist before they are out of hearing. He demonstrates with an empty hand, and she moves his fingers into a different position while telling him something which makes him smile.

Mau watches them go.

He wants that to remain possible. A man being called away from a fight because his mother would like to have him on an ordinary walk.

He can make almost any private decision sound reasonable if he starts there.

* * *

His reply is brief.

Tomorrow. The later hour. No return to the place beside the protected boundary. He will examine the offered ground before approaching, and no meeting happens without terms he accepts then.

He does not put his family's names on the paper.

The soldier is told where to leave it and when to return to its assigned position. It will not follow her, offer another undertaking or admit anyone through the ward.

When it comes back, he checks the rest of the watch as carefully as he did before reading the letter.

The care doesn't undo what he has decided to keep.

* * *

Yuka asks him to help with her hair before lunch.

One side has caught where she fastened it. She is trying to free it without pulling, and her patience is nearly finished by the time he reaches her.

“Don't laugh,” she says. ʸ

“I haven't.” ᴹ

“You're considering it.” ʸ

He sits behind her and works the caught strands loose. She holds still for him, then tips her head back against his shoulder when the fastening finally comes away.

“Better?” he asks. ᴹ

“Much.” ʸ

He kisses her temple.

She stays there, one hand resting over his knee, and tells him what Mai said about Marin's dinner. Something about giving Gojo a task with no knife, no fire and no opportunity to describe himself as essential.

“So he can watch,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Shoko said he can wash.” ʸ

“An inspiring use of whatever those two are doing.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs and reaches back to touch his cheek.

He turns into her hand.

Nothing about how much he loves this moment has to be false for the letter to remain hidden.
