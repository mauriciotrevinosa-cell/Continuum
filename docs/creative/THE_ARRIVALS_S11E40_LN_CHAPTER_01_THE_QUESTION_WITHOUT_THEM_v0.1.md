## Chapter 14 — The Question Without Them

*When: Makima sees that he has come alone, she doesn't pretend not to notice.*

“Good afternoon, Mau.” ᴹᵏ

He stops beyond the fallen stone where the road divides.

The place matches the letter. He has examined it from two approaches, checked what lies behind the empty building and left an ordinary soldier at a return position she has not been invited to use. He is carrying his weapons. His own shadow answers him.

None of those preparations makes this a decision the people at home have agreed to.

Makima stands beside the remains of a low wall. No table this time. No chair waiting for his wife.

“You wanted a question,” he says. ᴹ

“I thought you might have one.” ᴹᵏ

“Terms first.” ᴹ

She inclines her head.

He has brought a more careful undertaking than the one he watched hold while Yuka bled. No violence, compulsion, marking or pursuit between them or against the named people whose safety could be used to reach them, from acceptance through his confirmed return. No new instructions through a third party. Any danger already arranged for this visit must be disclosed before acceptance, and any ongoing hostile operation she can halt must be halted. His return soldier is included.

He asks her directly whether there is such an operation.

“No,” she says. ᴹᵏ

The answer is hers. He cannot make his ability to recognize an undertaking into an instrument which certifies every fact spoken before it.

He knows that now.

She accepts. He accepts. The restraint settles between them.

Only then does he cross to the wall.

* * *

“Did you send it?” he asks. ᴹ

Makima looks at him.

“The devil.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

He had rehearsed what he might say if she denied it.

The simple answer leaves all that preparation unused.

For a moment he hears the clock again. Not a memory of its actual sound so much as the fact of its continuing while everybody else in the world should have stopped.

“Before the agreement,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

His fingers close.

The edge of the wall under his hand breaks into grit. Makima watches the fragments fall without moving toward him.

He looks down at what he has done. Makes himself open the hand.

“You knew she'd be there.” ᴹ

“I expected her to be.” ᴹᵏ

“You sat across from her.” ᴹ

“I did.” ᴹᵏ

“And listened to her ask you whether she would still get to choose.” ᴹ

Makima's expression changes very slightly.

He wants to make the next question an accusation loud enough that she cannot answer it in that ordinary voice. He wants to put the room back around them, put his family in it, let somebody else hear her say this before he decides what to do with the answer.

Instead he sees Yuka on the ground again.

“Why?” he asks. ᴹ

* * *

Makima takes enough time to choose that he knows she could have supplied something more flattering.

“I thought that, without her, you would need somewhere else to put what you keep trying to give,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Mau stares at her.

“Somewhere else.” ᴹ

“Someone else.” ᴹᵏ

He laughs once. There is no pleasure in it.

“You thought I'd come to you.” ᴹ

“I thought you might come to understand why an arrangement with me would prevent the next loss.” ᴹᵏ

“The loss you made.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

His attention narrows so suddenly that he has to choose to widen it again. A damaged wall. Open road. His own feet, still where he placed them. His wife alive at home, not here to become the thing which happens next.

*Here,* Noise says. ᴺ

Mau lets the single word reach him.

He is here. He has not moved. Nothing in the undertaking is asking him to stand closer to her.

“Was it the gate again?” he asks. “Make something hurt enough and wait until I become useful?” ᴹ

“You were already useful.” ᴹᵏ

“I didn't ask you to compliment the plan.” ᴹ

For the first time, she looks away from his face.

Not far. Only to the fragments under his hand.

“You thanked me,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I know what I said.” ᴹ

“I expected you to try to destroy me.” ᴹᵏ

“I was keeping her alive.” ᴹ

She looks back up.

“I saw.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

He sits on the unbroken part of the wall because his legs have become too ready to act on what the rest of him has not decided.

Makima remains standing.

“She was unconscious for days,” he says. ᴹ

The woman's attention doesn't drift.

“I could put power into her and still make it worse. There was a moment when I had to be told to stop doing too much, because I was so frightened of doing too little.” ᴹ

He didn't plan to tell her that.

The admission makes him angrier than the broken stone did.

“You don't get to hear that and decide it means you found the right place to press.” ᴹ

“Would you prefer I pretend I haven't heard it?” ᴹᵏ

“I'd prefer you understand what you're talking about taking.” ᴹ

Makima considers him.

“You didn't leave her,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“No.” ᴹ

“Even to punish me.” ᴹᵏ

“No.” ᴹ

“And now you've come.” ᴹᵏ

He lifts his eyes.

“For the answer.” ᴹ

She accepts the correction without agreeing to stop thinking beyond it.

He can almost see her fitting the pieces into a new shape. The man who had not chased her. The woman he had stayed beside. The fact that gratitude had been possible without obedience.

He hates that she is interested.

He also needs her interested in something other than finding a better way to kill his wife.

* * *

“Listen carefully,” he says. ᴹ

Makima does.

“If you hurt Yuka to make a place for yourself, there will never be a place for you with me.” ᴹ

“Never is a long time.” ᴹᵏ

“Then you'll have time to remember it.” ᴹ

The quiet after the answer feels different.

Mau doesn't fill it.

“Not because I haven't understood what you want,” he continues. “Because I have. You want to be near somebody without being the person they can leave. So you take the leaving away. Or you take away everyone they might leave you for.” ᴹ

“Would you like me less if I called that love?” ᴹᵏ

“I'd like you to stop using the word as a reason they don't get a choice.” ᴹ

Her mouth moves at one corner, then stills.

He can feel his pulse in his injured hand. He flexes the fingers which do not quite follow the others and rests them on his knee.

“What happened is not something I can forgive for her,” he says. “Or for the people who sat outside that room. I don't get to offer them to you because you gave me an explanation.” ᴹ

“But yourself?” ᴹᵏ

He looks at the road home.

The answer he should bring back is simple: she did it. They were right to suspect her. The opening in the agreement was enough.

There is another answer growing beside it, less simple and much more attractive to the part of him which has not stopped trying to get ahead of the next attack.

“I'm willing to see whether you can stop,” he says. ᴹ

Makima's eyes remain on him.

“That isn't permission to do it differently,” he adds. “No replacing Yuka. No hurting somebody else to teach me that I need you. No punishment for a choice you didn't like.” ᴹ

“And if I can?” ᴹᵏ

“Then we can talk about something other than how to keep you away from us.” ᴹ

* * *

She sits at last, leaving space between them.

It is such an ordinary movement that it makes him suspicious.

“You think you can change me,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I think I don't know enough about you to promise anything.” ᴹ

“That doesn't usually stop people from promising.” ᴹᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

For a while she tells him what she believes makes an unequal relationship comfortable. How much uncertainty can be removed when one person no longer has to ask. The relief she thinks obedience should provide.

He makes her stop at the word relief.

“Whose?” ᴹ

Makima regards him with the same attention she gave the broken wall.

“Mine,” she says eventually. ᴹᵏ

It is the first answer that afternoon which feels smaller after he hears it.

He doesn't mistake that for safety.

He asks what happens when somebody does not obey, and she answers with enough examples that he has to stop twice to make sure she means what she says. Some she offers readily. Others she returns to after asking why a distinction matters to him.

Noise keeps the language and the observed relationships separate. There is no demonstration of a person losing their will. Mau has not asked for one.

Understanding her account is already unpleasant enough.

* * *

At the end of the agreed time, Makima asks whether he will come again.

He stands.

“If we talk again, the conditions don't become less important because we've done it once.” ᴹ

“You haven't said no.” ᴹᵏ

“I haven't said when.” ᴹ

She rises too.

He checks his return position before moving away. The soldier is still where he ordered it to remain. No one has approached. The road gives him the ordinary facts he can verify, none of the certainty he would like to take home.

“Mau,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

He stops without turning fully back.

“You asked me the question yourself.” ᴹᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Will you tell them the answer?” ᴹᵏ

His jaw tightens.

He can hear what she has found before she asks anything more of him: an answer he has taken responsibility for carrying, and a house which doesn't know he went to get it.

“That's my decision,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes,” she replies. “It is.” ᴹᵏ

He leaves before she can make the agreement sound like generosity.

The soldier returns with him. The undertaking ends only after he is back where he said he would be.

Yuka is alive. The road is empty. He has the truth he went for.

For a little while, he lets those facts stand in for the question of what he has done with it.
