# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 68
## Chapter 15 — The Place Beside Her

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 68
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S11E40
**Continuity state:** B+200 late afternoon/night, after the private visit. Mau returns through his own anchor and joins Yuka's ordinary day. He does not disclose Makima yet; nobody asks him a direct question about her and receives a lie. Yuka retains perceptions/initiative, not ignorance of all changes. Ciel speaks through their existing connection at supper, no suppression or false memory. Married tenderness and discomfort coexist; no separation, pregnancy or recovered colors.

*When: Mau gets back, Yuka is losing an argument with a piece of paper.*

She is holding one end. Mai is holding the other. Neither is pulling particularly hard, but both appear prepared to continue until the paper admits which of them is right.

“It's too narrow,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“For you.” ʸ

“I don't have unusually large shoulders.” ᴹᵃ

Mau stops in the doorway.

“I could make this much worse,” he offers. ᴹ

Yuka looks up.

The pleasure in her face reaches him before he has decided where to put his hands.

“Come here.” ʸ

He does.

She gives him the paper. It is a rough plan for the cooking tables, with places marked for bowls, ingredients and enough room between people that Marin's proposed dinner will not become a contest over who can keep an elbow out of somebody else's food.

“Mai thinks we need more space.” ʸ

“Mai is standing right here,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Mau looks at the measurements.

“Mai is also right.” ᴹ

Yuka narrows her eyes.

“Welcome home.” ʸ

“Thank you. I missed you.” ᴹ

Mai takes her end of the paper back before Yuka can ruin the dignity of the argument by smiling.

* * *

The extra room costs them one proposed place along each side.

Marin arrives to see the change and points out that they can add another table rather than arrange the participants like vegetables being packed for travel. Wakana agrees so quickly that Mai asks how long he has been waiting for someone else to say it.

“Since I saw the drawing,” he admits. ᵂ

Yuka folds her arms.

“So nobody was going to support me.” ʸ

“I can stand behind you while you're wrong,” Mau says. ᴹ

She looks over her shoulder at him.

“A loving husband would help.” ʸ

“That's why I'm offering moral support instead of pretending your elbow fits.” ᴹ

She reaches back and catches his shirt.

He lets himself be pulled closer. Mai makes an impatient sound which has no effect on either of them.

Momo Nishimiya takes the drawing from her before the paper can become a casualty.

“We can measure the tables that actually exist,” she suggests. ᴹᵒ

Everybody looks toward the yard.

It is such a reasonable idea that Mau waits for someone to take credit for having thought of it first.

Mai gets up instead.

“Come on. Before he calls this consulting.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

They measure. They move one bench. They discover that the narrow space looks much worse when two people have to stand in it.

Yuka accepts this without admitting defeat in any language Gojo might hear later.

Mau holds the end of the line while Momo reads a mark. When they need another table brought over, he asks whether it should stay there for supper as well, then calls two of his ordinary carriers and gives them the positions.

No strangers arrive to do it for him. The soldiers lift where he indicates, set the table down and withdraw after Mai checks that its feet are steady.

Ori comes to investigate with Chloe.

“Are we cooking too?” she asks. ᴼ

“If you want,” Marin says. ᴹᵃʳ

“Is it only for couples?” Chloe asks. ᶜʰ

Marin looks between the two faces and realizes the scale of the problem she has created.

“It's for teams,” she says. ᴹᵃʳ

“You said dates,” Yuka reminds her. ʸ

“Some teams are dates.” ᴹᵃʳ

Ori considers this.

“We can be a team,” she tells Chloe. ᴼ

Chloe nods with immediate conviction.

Mau looks down at his end of the line. His hand has been holding it too tightly. The last few moments of the meeting are still running somewhere behind the yard: a wall crumbling, the woman explaining why she had thought there would be space for her after Yuka was gone.

“Papa?” Ori says. ᴼ

He looks up.

“Yes?” ᴹ

“You're moving the line.” ᴼ

He loosens his grip.

“Sorry.” ᴹ

Ori takes it from him to show Chloe where it should sit. Her confidence is practical and complete; she needs an end held, not a promise about every dangerous thing in the world.

Mau sits on the bench and holds it where she asks.

* * *

At supper, Ciel wants to know why Rimuru has been nominated to advise on a cooking activity when he has spent part of the planning insisting that his contribution should be eating.

*Experience,* Mau offers. ᴹ

*In eating?* ᶜ

*Extensive.* ᴹ

Across the table, Rimuru looks up.

“I can hear enough of this to object.” ᴿ

Shizue passes him a bowl.

“You could offer to help.” ˢᶻ

He accepts it with a wounded expression.

“I've offered several useful opinions.” ᴿ

“About what you'd like us to make,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Important information.” ᴿ

Mau laughs with the others.

Ciel asks whether Ori and Chloe have chosen something sensible. He tells her about the line and the very serious discovery that teams may contain people who have no interest in a date.

*A necessary correction,* she says. ᶜ

*They made it quickly.* ᴹ

*They had a good reason.* ᶜ

He can answer her. Nothing is stopping him from bringing the afternoon into the conversation. His link has not been cut; she has not been sent somewhere she cannot hear him call.

He asks whether she thinks Rimuru can be trusted with the ingredient list.

Ciel gives the question the consideration it deserves.

*With supervision.* ᶜ

* * *

Later, Yuka finds him sitting at the edge of their bed.

He has taken one shoe off and then forgotten the other.

She looks at the bare foot, then at the one still wearing a shoe.

“Difficult operation?” ʸ

“I got through the first half.” ᴹ

She sits beside him.

He finishes before she can offer help. For a few moments they remain there with their shoulders touching.

“You've been far away today,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks at her hands.

“A little.” ᴹ

“Is it something you want to talk about?” ʸ

His throat tightens.

She has given him a place to put the answer. No accusation to defend against. No demand that he become less frightened before she is willing to listen.

“Not tonight,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka studies him.

“Okay.” ʸ

The word does not mean she has failed to notice. She moves one hand over his and waits until he looks at her.

“Stay here with me, then.” ʸ

“I'm here.” ᴹ

“I know where you're sitting.” ʸ

He lets out a breath he has been holding since she entered.

Then he turns and puts his arms around her.

She settles against him, her hand at the back of his neck. He can feel how little effort it takes for her to trust the arms around her. He holds her carefully, with none of the awkward distance that once made the simple act frightening after he came home.

“I love you,” he says into her hair. ᴹ

“I love you too.” ʸ

“I want you here.” ᴹ

She leans back far enough to see him.

“Good. I live here.” ʸ

The laugh gets out of him before he can stop it.

She smiles, but her hand stays against his face.

“And when you want to talk, I want to hear it,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

For tonight she lets him hold her. The unspoken thing remains between his ribs, where she cannot know its shape merely because she is willing to make room for it.
