*When: Hollis puts a broken strap on the table, the discussion stops being about lines on a map.*

“That used to go round a box,” he says. ᴴᵒ

Rimuru looks at the cut end.

“An attack?” ᴿ

“A wheel went down where the road used to be.” ᴴᵒ

He shows them the place. Not very far from a turn Souei recognizes, and too far from the last reliable stopping place for a driver to treat getting stuck as a short inconvenience.

The ground had changed. The route in the older account hadn't. Somebody had put branches over the gap so the next cart might see it. Then it rained, and the branches became another thing a driver could mistake for a surface that would hold.

“We got the load out,” Hollis says. “Lost time. Lost one crate. Nobody died.” ᴴᵒ

Ori, sitting beside Umaru at the end of the table, looks at the narrow place marked on the paper.

“But somebody could.” ᴼ

“Yes,” Hollis says. ᴴᵒ

She doesn't ask whether that means the road has a boss.

* * *

Fern puts the reports beside one another instead of copying the newest line over the older one.

They need to know which route was seen by the person speaking, when they saw it and what kind of load actually got through. A traveler walking around a hole hasn't proved a loaded cart can follow.

Jinshi asks Hollis how long a warning would remain useful if it had to travel back through the usual buyers before reaching him.

“It depends on whether the buyers know where I'm going,” Hollis says. ᴴᵒ

“And do they?” ᴶⁱ

“The honest ones usually ask.” ᴴᵒ

Luminous studies the route.

“People are already using the gaps between authorities,” she says. ᴸ

“Some because nobody has told them where a road belongs now,” Rimuru answers. ᴿ

“And some because they know precisely which answer will take longest to arrive.” ᴸ

Souei indicates a second stretch.

“We can patrol this. We cannot promise someone at every turn between it and here.” ˢᵒ

Mau has been looking at the broken strap.

“I can give you a hundred,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Nobody misunderstands what he means, but Yuka asks him to say it properly anyway.

“Soldiers,” he says. “Ones I already have. Not a hundred new people you need to feed and find beds for.” ᴹ

“Doing what?” Souei asks. ˢᵒ

Mau looks up.

It is a useful question, said without gratitude arriving early enough to make the answer embarrassing.

“Patrol. Stand where you need an observer. Follow instructions about the routes you actually want covered. Report what they see.” ᴹ

“Through you.” ˢᵒ

“The direct bond comes to me. I can give them tasks which include signaling your people. I can't make the ones who don't speak suddenly give you a detailed account in the middle of a road.” ᴹ

“Then the first task is making their signals unambiguous.” ˢᵒ

Mau nods.

Rimuru leans forward.

“And someone at this end has to know which reports actually need Mau.” ᴿ

“Yes,” Yuka says. ʸ

The word lands firmly enough that Mau turns toward her.

“If a cart breaks an axle, you don't need to appear beside it with both daggers,” she says. “We need to know who can bring another axle.” ʸ

Hollis smiles.

“I'd welcome that distinction.” ᴴᵒ

Mau holds up his hands.

“I can bring an axle.” ᴹ

“You can also tell someone where to bring it,” she says. ʸ

He looks at her for another moment, then back at the map.

“All right. We build the response with the report.” ᴹ

* * *

The hundred will come from ordinary units not assigned to the house watch.

Muster's forty remain where they are. Souei will choose the first useful routes with people who know the ground. They will try the soldiers' instructions on a short, known stretch before sending them toward places where a mistaken signal can cost a life.

Mau asks that no one use a patrol as an excuse to enter a private home or follow a traveler because they dislike a face. Souei asks what happens when a unit is destroyed beyond a place its partner can see.

“I'll know I lost the unit,” Mau says. “That doesn't mean I get to know everything it failed to see before it was hit.” ᴹ

“Then a missing return is a report too.” ˢᵒ

“Yes.” ᴹ

They put that beside the ordinary signals.

Umaru listens without interrupting until Fern pauses to move the broken strap away from the paper.

“Does this mean Hollis won't need to buy as much protection?” she asks. ᵁ

“Not immediately,” Hollis answers. “And a soldier noticing trouble isn't a promise the cart gets past it. I still need people who know what the animals are doing, which places will take a late arrival and when a good sale isn't worth a bad road.” ᴴᵒ

She looks at the map again.

“So the shadows aren't the whole plan.” ᵁ

“They make more of the plan possible,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Mau sits back and lets him answer the next question too.

* * *

Luminous waits until they have agreed to test the first signals.

“I have roads with the same problem,” she says. ᴸ

“We can look at them,” Mau answers. ᴹ

“Another hundred?” ᴸ

He glances at her.

She is watching to see whether he will promise the number before he has seen the work.

“Show me where you want them.” ᴹ

“Sensible.” ᴸ

“I have moments.” ᴹ

Yuka makes a small sound beside him.

He looks at her.

“You can support me.” ᴹ

“I'm enjoying the moments.” ʸ

Hinata hides a smile behind her cup badly enough that Anko notices.

Luminous lays out a narrower first proposal: an approach to be examined, not a whole country assigned protection because Mau has offered help to Tempest. Fern asks for the accounts they will need before deciding where to place anything.

They leave that part open with a real next task.

* * *

The second offer makes Hinata put her cup down.

“An anchor?” she asks. ᴴⁱ

“In your shadow,” Mau says. “If you want one. Something with instructions to alert me if you're in danger, and a place I could exchange to if I can reach it.” ᴹ

“Does it watch everything I do?” ᴴⁱ

“It can observe where it's placed. It doesn't read your mind. We decide what it reports, and you can tell me to take it out. I don't need a report about you having lunch.” ᴹ

Anko looks at the table instead of answering the question she has not been asked.

Hinata notices that too.

“If I call for you, you come,” she says. “If it thinks something is wrong, it warns first when there's time. You don't move me somewhere else without asking unless waiting would mean I cannot answer.” ᴴⁱ

“Agreed. And I can't promise the exchange is available at every instant. We don't make the rest of your protection depend on my being free.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴴⁱ

Souei asks about the range of ordinary reporting, the difference between the anchor existing and Mau attending to it, and what the unit should do if another observer contests its presence. They answer the parts they can and keep the rest as conditions to test.

Nobody puts a shadow into Anko because Hinata has accepted one. Nobody adds a ruler to a list because she happened to hear the offer.

Afterward, in the courtyard, Mau calls one ordinary soldier from his unassigned legion. Hinata sees it before she consents again. It lowers itself into her shadow with the instructions they have agreed, then responds to the small test she chooses: an intentional request for Mau's attention, followed by his acknowledgment from several steps away.

No exchange is needed.

Hinata asks it to emerge once so they can prove removal is real. Mau recalls it immediately, then waits while she decides whether to renew.

She does.

Anko lets out the breath she has kept out of the discussion.

* * *

When the meeting ends, Ori comes to the map again.

“Can the Fort help?” she asks. ᴼ

Mau crouches beside the table.

He could turn the answer into something about being too young to worry. She has spent the afternoon listening to people describe work she had thought happened simply because the things she needed reached the house.

“You can help check that the signals make sense to people who haven't been in this meeting,” he says. “At home, on the practice route. Nobody's sending you down an unknown road to test an alarm.” ᴹ

Ori looks at Umaru.

Umaru nods thoughtfully.

“The Fort can be very difficult to give instructions to.” ᵁ

“An important testing resource,” Mau says. ᴹ

Rem laughs from the doorway.

Hollis picks up the strap before leaving the table. Yuka stops him and asks whether he wants it repaired or whether the torn end needs to remain visible for somebody else to understand what happened.

He considers it.

“Leave it for now,” he says. ᴴᵒ

She nods and gives it back.

Mau watches that small exchange. There are things even a repair can make harder to see if nobody asks first.
