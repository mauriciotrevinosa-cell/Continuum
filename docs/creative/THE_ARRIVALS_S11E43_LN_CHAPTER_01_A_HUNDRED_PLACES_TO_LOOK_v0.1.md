*When: Umaru raises the wrong flag, the soldier does exactly what it has been told.*

It stands in the path.

Mau waits.

Umaru looks at the flag. Then at the empty space where she had expected the soldier to move.

“I need help,” she says. ᵁ

“You told it the road is closed,” Souei answers. ˢᵒ

“Yes. Because I need help.” ᵁ

“Those aren't the same report,” Ori says beside her. ᴼ

Umaru lowers the flag.

The soldier stays where it is. No one has given it the agreed reopening signal.

Rem presses her lips together.

“You may laugh,” Umaru tells her. “This is a deliberate test.” ᵁ

Rem laughs.

* * *

The first version had too many signals.

Souei says so without protecting anybody's feelings about the effort which went into them. A traveler frightened beside a stopped cart is not going to remember an elegant system better because the people who designed it were pleased with the distinctions.

They keep the immediate messages simple. Stop here. Do not use this stretch. Help requested. Someone must come look before the route is declared safe again.

The detailed report goes to people who can give it in words. The soldiers have instructions about where those people can be found, and Mau has a defined warning from his own bond if one of the assigned urgent conditions is met.

They test what happens when no one answers.

That takes longer than the flags.

Chloe asks which observer is supposed to know that the first observer hasn't come back. Souei moves one of the marked positions. Ori draws the new line. It is a small correction, and Mau sees how much less useful the whole exercise would have been if he had arrived determined to demonstrate that his first plan already worked.

Hinata watches from beside Anko.

“She was right,” she says, indicating Chloe. ᴴⁱ

“She usually wants to know where the person is going,” Anko answers. ᴬⁿ

Hinata looks at her.

“You sound as though you know the answer to that question about everyone here.” ᴴⁱ

“Only the interesting people.” ᴬⁿ

“Should I be worried?” ᴴⁱ

Anko smiles without answering.

* * *

The hundred emerge in an open space where nobody has to mistake the rising shadows for an attack.

Mau has warned the people using the yard. Jin-Woo's son comes to watch, standing beside his father rather than wandering into the space the units need to form. Hollis examines the first rank with the practical attention of a man who wants to know whether he can trust a warning from someone who doesn't complain about the weather.

They are mostly ordinary soldiers. No Beru at the front to turn a route assignment into a proclamation. No Kamish large enough to make the map temporarily irrelevant. Igris stands beside Mau to help identify the task groups, then remains when they are ready to go.

Souei walks past the first positions and tests the instructions directly.

The units answer with the responses Mau has authorized. Follow this route. Stop at the marked point. Accept specified task changes from Souei; report anything outside that scope rather than quietly turning one person's request into a new campaign.

They still belong to Mau. The delegation gives someone else a useful way to work with them instead of requiring every ordinary direction to travel through his mouth.

“How many stay here?” Suho asks. ˢᵘʰ

“The forty on the house watch keep their assignment,” Mau says. “These are a different hundred.” ᴹ

“And the one with Hinata?” ˢᵘʰ

“Separate. Existing soldier, different instructions.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at the formation.

“You should write that down before somebody decides you created a hundred and one new ones this morning,” he says. ᴶ

“Fern already did.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴶ

“You sound very pleased to have someone else keeping count.” ᴹ

“I am.” ᴶ

* * *

They don't send every unit down the same path merely because it looks impressive.

The task groups leave with Souei according to the stretches he has agreed to inspect and cover. Mau accompanies the first group far enough to see the change from the protected paths to the ordinary road. Rimuru comes too, with Yuka beside Mau and Hollis bringing the cart whose dimensions matter more to this part of the exercise than anybody's fighting strength.

At the bad stretch, the driver stops.

Mau could lift the cart across.

He sees the whole easy solution before Hollis finishes pointing to the damaged edge. It would take less time than hearing the end of the sentence.

Then the next cart would come.

He crouches to examine the ground with Souei instead.

They mark the part which will not hold. The soldiers take positions where a driver can see them before committing the wheels. Hollis walks the proposed alternative on foot and refuses it for the loaded cart until someone has checked the softer ground on the far side.

Rimuru nods.

“Then that's the first real report.” ᴿ

Not victory. Not a road miraculously repaired because the strong people came out to look.

A place people should stop until the next part of the work is done.

Mau gives the instruction. The unit takes its place.

He leaves it there when they turn for home.

* * *

Luminous asks him about that part afterward.

“They know what they need to do.” ᴹ

“And if the next thing requires you?” ᴸ

“They know how to tell me.” ᴹ

“You make that sound easy.” ᴸ

“I'm practicing.” ᴹ

Her own route accounts will need comparing before an assignment is chosen. She asks whether he would discuss the matter again while she is here, then asks something much less administrative: whether the witches would mind another person at tea.

Mau looks surprised enough to interest her.

“I can ask them,” he says. ᴹ

“I asked whether they would mind, not whether you could order another cup.” ᴸ

“Then you're asking the right question.” ᴹ

* * *

At home, Ori has already begun drawing the first practice route properly.

She wants the signals on the same page as the places, because the flag she remembers best may not be the one Chloe remembers first. Umaru is reviewing what happened during her deliberate test with Rem, whose version becomes less flattering every time Umaru asks her to start again.

Yuka takes Mau's hand before he can volunteer to fix the drawing.

“We said we'd have some time,” she reminds him. ʸ

“I remember.” ᴹ

“Then come on.” ʸ

He follows her.

Behind him, a hundred parts of his army are going somewhere else without needing him to stand in the middle of the road. It is a good thing. He can feel the room it gives him, the space between a useful warning and the assumption that he must already be there.

Later he will use some of that space to work with Noise.

For now, Yuka pulls him toward a patch of afternoon sun and tells him he is sitting down with her whether he thinks that counts as a plan or not.

He tells her it sounds like an excellent plan.

Then he lets her choose where.
