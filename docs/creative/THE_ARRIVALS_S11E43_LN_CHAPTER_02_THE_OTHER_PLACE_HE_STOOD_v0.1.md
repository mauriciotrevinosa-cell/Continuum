# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 1 — The Other Place He Stood

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S11E43
**Continuity state:** B+205 morning, after B204 time with Yuka and ordinary night. First produced limited physical-double prototype from retained observations of Souei, including V46#3/S8E37CH01. Constructed by Noise from incomplete retained record without Souei's new cooperation; not original technique mastered, second soul/guest/army unit or free full kit. Body-pattern extension strains the containment walls as distance increases; abort, no permanent injury. Short-lived local projection is not useful remote surveillance. Mau has not yet requested Souei's demonstration.

*When: the second Mau turns its head, Noise becomes so pleased that the first one almost misses the warning.*

*There.* ᴺ

He sees it.

The outline is the right height. The shoulders settle into a shape he knows from reflections, with the slight difference in his left hand preserved instead of improved away. For a moment it is unsettling to see the ordinary damage he has learned to work around presented as part of what makes the copy accurate.

*Does it see me?* he asks. ᴹ

Noise gives him the answering angle.

It is not a second person becoming aware of the yard. It is a second place from which the same working model can take a limited observation. Mau turns his real head and the other view lurches with the correction.

He stops.

The copy stops too.

*Not useful yet,* he says. ᴹ

Noise offers the first moment again, when the head turned cleanly.

Mau laughs under his breath.

*Yes, that part was very good.* ᴹ

* * *

The reference is older than yesterday's meeting.

Yuka in a ring with an ordinary rope. Souei changing the number of places from which an attack could come. Mau sitting outside the barrier with both hands on his knees because seeing the answer had not entitled him to give it to her.

He remembers that part with unusual clarity now.

Noise has retained what it could observe of the doubles. It has compared later appearances, followed the relationships it could distinguish and left holes where seeing an operation end did not explain how it began.

Mau asked it to try construction anyway.

The first version had been almost entirely appearance. This one can hold its place, take a step and return an angle of the yard while he stands within easy reach of it. It needs much more of his attention than he would ever want to spend looking at the back of an empty shed.

But an empty shed is a tolerable place to discover that a second point of view can arrive sideways.

He chooses a mark on the wall.

*Keep that one. Don't follow my eyes.* ᴹ

Noise steadies the returned view.

Mau looks down at his real hand.

The mark remains where it was.

*Better,* he says. ᴹ

* * *

He moves away from the copy by one ordinary pace.

Its shape holds.

Another pace.

The pressure begins so quietly that he mistakes it for the concentration needed to hold both observations apart. He stops walking, lets his shoulders drop and waits to see whether the feeling follows his attention or the distance.

Noise shows him the difference.

The copied body has not acquired a separate version of everything which keeps his real one from coming apart. It is still extending a pattern which belongs to him. As he moves away, an inner boundary answers to both positions and begins to stretch.

He understands the connection to yesterday's failed Step with a sudden, cold unpleasantness.

One operation had tried to leave a boundary behind.

This one is trying to make the same boundary cover two places.

*Close it,* he says. ᴹ

The copy loses its face first.

Then its hands, shoulders and the rest of the borrowed shape collapse back into the expressed pattern. Noise cuts the supply before the pressure becomes a tear.

Mau is alone in the yard.

He presses a hand against his ribs and waits for his breathing to become the only movement he can feel there.

* * *

They do not try the distance again.

Noise wants to repair the model immediately. Mau asks it to show him which part failed before making a better-looking version of the same assumption.

The answer takes time.

An ordinary double would not need to include the strange interior he has made. It would have its own relationship to the person creating it, its own limits about what can be divided and what must remain at the source. Noise has followed what Souei's technique visibly accomplishes and supplied a way through the structures it knows Mau actually has.

The result works close enough to look promising.

It becomes dangerous precisely where a useful scout would need to begin.

*A separate shell,* Mau suggests. ᴹ

Noise offers him a shell without the observation link.

*A very handsome empty room,* Mau says. *I was hoping it could tell me if somebody had come in.* ᴹ

The outline tilts its head.

He smiles despite the pressure which has only just gone away.

*I know. You're working on it.* ᴹ

* * *

He lets Noise make one more local expression after the warning has faded completely.

No walking apart. No attempt to copy the contents of his guest rooms. The source holds the complicated work; the shell receives only the bounded observation he asks it to return.

It lasts long enough to watch a leaf reach the ground beside the shed.

Then the angle degrades and he closes it.

Useful information. Not useful surveillance yet.

No new soldier rises when he says the exercise is over. Nothing living has been made to stand there waiting for him to decide whether it deserves a name.

Mau writes the distances and the failures where Noise can compare them with the Step model later.

He is beginning to accumulate more real work than he can comfortably describe as a few little exercises.

* * *

Souei is still at the guest Inn when Mau comes back.

The first patrol groups have given him enough practical questions to keep the morning busy. He is making a note about a signal which can be seen from the road and missed completely from a lower bank.

Mau waits until he finishes the line.

“Could I ask you about something else?” ᴹ

Souei sets down the pencil.

“What?” ˢᵒ

Mau looks at the hand which has just left the paper.

“The doubles.” ᴹ

Souei's expression changes by so little that an observer could mistake it for no change at all.

Mau, who has spent the morning trying to make another face look convincing, notices.
