# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 2 — What He Wouldn't Show

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S11E44
**Continuity state:** B+205 later morning. Souei refuses a requested technical demonstration/access to how doubles are sustained. Mau admits the incomplete prototype honestly when asked. Refusal does not grant permission or complete the technique. Souei knows of that experiment, not whole forbidden backlog or Makima visits. Mau does not discard retained observations or promise he has stopped all construction. No new ability or disclosure to entire house.

*When: Souei asks what he wants to know, Mau gives him the difficult part first.*

“How you keep the connection from dragging everything else into it.” ᴹ

Souei regards him.

“What have you made?” ˢᵒ

“A short-lived copy. It holds a view close by. Moving away pulls at things it shouldn't.” ᴹ

“Things in you.” ˢᵒ

“Yes.” ᴹ

He doesn't try to disguise it as a harmless question about a technique he might eventually consider. The copy has stood in a yard. The pressure has been real. It would be absurd to ask for the answer while pretending he has not already found the problem.

Souei moves the road notes aside.

“From the tournament?” ˢᵒ

“Some of the record is from then. Noise kept what it could understand. Later observations helped. Not enough.” ᴹ

Mau remembers sitting in the audience, wanting to tell Yuka where the real opponent had gone. He can remember that without regretting keeping silent.

Remembering it doesn't explain what he owes the man standing in front of him now.

“You want a demonstration,” Souei says. ˢᵒ

“A closer one. If you'd allow it.” ᴹ

“No.” ˢᵒ

* * *

Mau leaves the answer where it lands.

Souei doesn't offer a softer one to reward him for not arguing.

“I am willing to work with you,” he says. “I don't intend to make every part of how I work available to be reconstructed.” ˢᵒ

“All right.” ᴹ

“Do you understand the difference?” ˢᵒ

Mau looks at him.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Souei waits a moment longer. Not to see whether Mau can repeat a more acceptable sentence. To see whether he is already looking for a route around the answer while still standing there.

Mau doesn't ask a narrower version of the same question.

“The prototype isn't suitable for the patrols,” he says instead. “I wasn't going to put it on the road as it is.” ᴹ

“Good.” ˢᵒ

“They keep the units we agreed on. None of this changes your instructions.” ᴹ

Souei nods.

The ordinary work between them remains ordinary. A hundred soldiers have not become a price paid for a demonstration, and a refusal has not made Souei an obstacle to be removed from the plan.

Mau is glad of that distinction.

He is less glad of how easily he can use it to avoid the rest of the discomfort.

* * *

At the door, Souei stops him.

“If you don't know which part of yourself is moving, don't send it somewhere you can't afford to lose it.” ˢᵒ

Mau looks back.

“I stopped when it pulled.” ᴹ

“This time.” ˢᵒ

The words are not an accusation. That makes them harder to shrug off.

“I know,” Mau says. ᴹ

He leaves without the demonstration.

Outside, Noise still has the imperfect model. Souei's refusal has supplied no missing mechanism, no clean blueprint and no permission which becomes valid merely because Mau waited until he was out of hearing.

*Other way?* Noise asks. ᴺ

Mau stops in the patch of shade beside the guest Inn.

There is work he can do with what they already retained. There are guesses he can test away from people. He has not promised to leave every useful possibility untouched because its first source would rather not help him finish it.

That is how he puts the answer together.

*We can look,* he says. ᴹ

Noise brings back the two boundaries which had strained in the yard.

Mau studies them until he hears Yuka calling from the path.

He looks up immediately.

She has come to ask whether he would like to go with her and Hinata before the next conversation, not to accuse him of anything. He says yes, closes the working model and goes to meet them.

The unfinished copy remains where he has chosen to keep it.
