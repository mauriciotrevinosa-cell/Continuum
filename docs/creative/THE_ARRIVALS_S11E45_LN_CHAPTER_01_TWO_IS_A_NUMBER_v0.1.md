# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 4 — Two Is a Number

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S11E45
**Continuity state:** B+205 evening. DIO/Ciel/Noise meet in the existing shared inner guest area by choice; Ciel's established representation is not a new independent physical body. Feelings accumulated over their earlier friendship become explicit. DIO/Ciel agree they would be willing to share a relationship, but cannot grant Noise's consent. No automatic marriage, obligatory romance, pregnancy, new power or discovery/forgiveness of the hidden work. Mau responds to Noise's call; Yuka remains his wife and no substitution occurs.

*When: Noise calls for him, Mau arrives expecting something to have caught fire.*

Nothing is burning.

The shared room is intact. The chairs remain chairs. DIO is on one side of Noise, Ciel on the other, and the shadow outline has gone so still that he initially thinks it is waiting for a construction to finish.

Then it turns toward him.

“Help.” ᴺ

Mau looks at the two women.

“What happened?” ᴹ

Ciel's expression changes.

It is the change which begins to worry him.

“We were having a conversation,” she says. ᶜ

“About?” ᴹ

“Us,” DIO says. ᴰ

Noise points from one woman to the other, then at itself.

Mau looks at the arrangement again.

“Oh.” ᴹ

* * *

The conversation had begun with a chair.

Noise had adjusted the place Ciel uses so she could sit beside the table without moving the cup every time she wanted to examine something DIO was showing her. Ciel had not requested the change. She had mentioned the inconvenience once, while thinking about something much more important.

Noise remembered.

It made the adjustment, waited until she tried it and asked whether it was better.

There have been enough little things like that now. Choices made when no one is assigning it a useful task. The way it turns toward a voice it recognizes. The quiet pride when somebody notices something it has finished, and the much quieter attention when a friend cannot explain immediately what is wrong.

Ciel can account for these moments individually.

That has not made the feeling they add up to any easier to account for.

DIO had noticed her noticing.

They had talked before coming here together. Neither has the excuse of having misunderstood the other's meaning.

Noise, unfortunately, had not attended that earlier discussion.

* * *

“We told it we would like to be closer,” DIO explains. ᴰ

“Friends,” Noise says, with visible relief at finding a word it can use. ᴺ

Ciel sits back a little.

“We are friends.” ᶜ

“Already.” ᴺ

“Yes.” ᶜ

Noise looks at Mau.

The expression is difficult to read in an outline with no ordinary face, but Mau has known it long enough to recognize a very specific appeal: please tell them the answer they are apparently waiting for has already been supplied.

He presses his lips together.

DIO sees.

“Do not laugh.” ᴰ

He fails.

Noise turns fully toward him.

“Traitor.” ᴺ

“I'm sorry.” ᴹ

He has to stop to get the next breath under control.

“I'm sorry. I really thought there was an emergency.” ᴹ

“There is,” Noise says. ᴺ

Ciel closes her eyes for a moment.

That makes it worse.

* * *

Mau comes farther into the room once he can do it without laughing at the first thing somebody says.

He does not take the empty chair. Noise has backed close enough to it that sitting down would leave it between three people who all appear to know something it doesn't.

“All right,” he says. “What did you understand?” ᴹ

Noise gestures at the chairs.

“Closer.” ᴺ

“The chairs are closer,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

“We explained that part,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“I'm starting where it is.” ᴹ

She looks at Noise, then at the small amount of space she and DIO have left it.

Both women move back.

The outline loosens slightly.

Mau is glad they notice.

“They mean something like the difference between the way I love my friends and the way Yuka and I have chosen to be together,” he says. “Not because being friends is small. Because they think they might want something else with you, if you want it too.” ᴹ

Noise remains very still.

Then turns toward the two women.

“Wife?” ᴺ

DIO draws herself up a little.

“Possibly. Eventually. That is not the immediate—” ᴰ

“Two wives,” Ciel says, with the air of someone who has decided a precise statement is preferable to watching the explanation become longer. ᶜ

Mau puts a hand over his mouth.

Noise turns toward him again.

“Two.” ᴺ

“It's a number,” he manages. ᴹ

“Not now,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“I know. I'm helping.” ᴹ

“You are enjoying yourself.” ᶜ

“Several things can be true.” ᴹ

* * *

The laughter fades when Noise asks the next question.

“Must?” ᴺ

Mau lowers his hand.

“No.” ᴹ

Both women answer almost with him.

“No,” Ciel repeats. “You don't have to.” ᶜ

DIO leans forward, then catches herself before using nearness as an explanation again.

“We wanted to tell you,” she says. “That was all we should have expected you to receive today.” ᴰ

Noise studies her.

“Friends?” ᴺ

“Even if you don't want the rest,” Ciel says. ᶜ

The outline turns toward Mau as though asking him to check a construction which matters more than the shape of the room.

“That's what asking means,” he tells it. “The answer gets to be yours.” ᴹ

Noise looks back at the women.

Something about the stillness changes. It is no longer trying to find the word which will make a difficult situation stop. It is considering that a word can change the situation because it has chosen to say it.

“Think,” it says. ᴺ

Ciel nods.

“Take your time.” ᶜ

DIO moves one of the chairs back toward the table.

“And we can still have a pleasant evening while you do.” ᴰ

* * *

Mau stays until the room becomes a room again.

DIO shows Noise the small thing she had come to explain before the confession took over. Ciel corrects one of the words and is corrected in turn. Noise moves its own chair closer this time, to a place it chooses, and both women manage not to make that choice carry more meaning than it has offered.

At the threshold, Mau feels it ask for his attention.

“Good?” Noise says quietly. ᴺ

He looks back.

“You did fine.” ᴹ

“Not answer.” ᴺ

“You answered. You said you wanted time.” ᴹ

Noise considers that.

Behind it, DIO has begun arguing that one of the chairs is now too far from the table for anybody to reach anything. Ciel says the chair has not moved since she last found the distance entirely acceptable.

Mau smiles.

“Let yourself think about what you want,” he tells it. “You don't have to build something just because somebody's excited about the plan.” ᴹ

The words stay with him after he leaves.

He has given excellent advice.

He wishes it were less easy to recognize the parts he has stopped applying to himself.
