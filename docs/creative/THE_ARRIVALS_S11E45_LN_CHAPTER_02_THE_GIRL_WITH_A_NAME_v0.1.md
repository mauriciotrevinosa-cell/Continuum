# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 5 — The Girl with a Name

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S11E45
**Continuity state:** B+206 afternoon. Second private Makima visit, arranged after the first through the exterior message point, new inspection/return anchor and renewed bounded terms. Family still not informed. Makima shows a local holding site outside the ward and introduces Reze, human/devil hybrid, not a pure devil or new house resident. Mau demands a real interval without commands; Reze speaks/chooses for herself and agrees personally to nonviolence for the visit. Temporary release of ongoing direction not guaranteed permanent freedom or immunity to later Control. No promised romance/rescue ownership, new copied ability or general exoneration of Makima.

*When: Makima says she has someone to show him, Mau doesn't move toward the door.*

“Someone?” he asks. ᴹ

“You wanted to understand what I keep.” ᴹᵏ

They are outside another empty building, farther along the route she offered in her reply. He has inspected the approach. His return soldier is in place. The terms have been accepted again, with no attack already prepared for this visit disclosed or permitted, no new compulsion against him or the named people at home, and no pursuit on his way back.

He still feels the temptation to count precautions as permission nobody else needs to give.

“Who is inside?” ᴹ

Makima considers the question.

“Reze.” ᴹᵏ

It is a name he doesn't know.

“Does she know you're bringing me?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“Did she choose to meet me?” ᴹ

Makima says nothing for a moment.

The pause is answer enough that he looks at the door differently.

“Then ask her.” ᴹ

* * *

The girl is sitting on a chair beside a shuttered window.

No chains around her wrists. No cage. Her hands rest neatly on her knees, and her gaze moves from Makima to Mau with a small delay which makes the order of the movement more disturbing than stillness would have been.

Makima stands just inside the threshold.

“Reze. This is Mau.” ᴹᵏ

“Hello,” the girl says. ᴿᶻ

Mau waits.

She waits too.

There is a dark fastening at her throat. He notices it, then looks back at her face rather than allowing the unknown thing to become more important than the person wearing it.

“Do you want to speak to me?” he asks. ᴹ

“You may answer,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

Mau turns toward her.

“That is not the same question.” ᴹ

“It permits the answer.” ᴹᵏ

“It makes her wait for you before giving one.” ᴹ

Reze's eyes remain on his face.

Mau moves out of the doorway, leaving it clear.

“I can wait outside,” he tells her. “You can tell me to leave.” ᴹ

The girl looks at the empty doorway.

For the first time he sees something which doesn't look like a response waiting to be completed.

* * *

Makima calls her a devil when Mau asks what has been done to her.

Reze's mouth moves almost imperceptibly.

He sees that too.

“Is that what you call yourself?” he asks. ᴹ

Makima begins answering.

He lifts a hand, not toward either woman, only between his own chest and the beginning of another sentence he doesn't want supplied for her.

“I'm asking Reze.” ᴹ

Reze looks at the hand.

Then at the ring on it.

“It isn't the whole answer,” she says. ᴿᶻ

Her voice is soft. The distinction is not.

The explanation comes in pieces. Human and devil joined. A dangerous body with more than an ordinary person's ways to hurt someone. Makima supplies names for some of it; Reze corrects the part which makes her sound as though being made into a weapon had been the first thing she chose.

Mau listens to both and believes neither woman has finished saying what the history cost.

“Being dangerous doesn't make her an object,” he says. ᴹ

“You have an army which answers you without argument,” Makima replies. ᴹᵏ

“And if Beru has something to say, I hear it.” ᴹ

“Would you let him leave?” ᴹᵏ

“I have let people go.” ᴹ

She studies his face.

He thinks of a man who had accepted one last conversation before disappearing into a darkness Mau had chosen not to command him back from.

“You could try it,” he adds. ᴹ

* * *

Makima does not become generous immediately.

She explains what Reze could do, what injuries are not enough to end a body like hers and how easily an apparently ordinary conversation can become something which leaves no useful place for an unprepared witness to stand.

Mau does not ask her to pretend the danger is imaginary.

He asks which part of it requires Reze to wait for permission before saying whether she would like him to leave the room.

Reze turns her head toward the window.

He can't tell whether she is hiding a smile or simply looking for another place to put her attention while two people discuss the conditions of her next sentence.

That uncertainty makes him stop arguing over her.

“What do you want?” he asks her. ᴹ

For a moment the room seems to wait with him.

“To know whether I could go through that door if I stood up,” Reze says. ᴿᶻ

Mau looks at Makima.

“Well?” ᴹ

* * *

They make the next part explicit.

Makima withdraws the standing direction which has kept Reze waiting on her next instruction. For the rest of this visit she undertakes not to reassert control, issue another command, pursue her or prevent her leaving. The restraint includes indirect action. Reze is told what is being offered before anyone asks her to agree to a term of her own.

Mau asks for no loyalty in return.

Reze chooses not to attack anyone during the conversation and departure. She says it herself. The undertaking holds in the same world which has let other people make chosen promises without turning them into puppets.

Then she stands.

Mau doesn't offer a hand she hasn't asked for.

She goes to the door, touches its edge and steps outside.

For several breaths neither of the people inside follows her.

Makima watches the empty place where she was.

“You think that proves something,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I think she wanted to know.” ᴹ

* * *

Reze comes back after a while with dust on one shoe.

She looks at Mau as though she is still deciding what kind of dangerous person he has chosen to be.

“You could have come after me,” she says. ᴿᶻ

“You were leaving.” ᴹ

“People leave for reasons.” ᴿᶻ

“You didn't owe me yours.” ᴹ

She considers that answer, then takes the chair near the door rather than the one by the window.

Makima sees the choice and lets it remain a choice.

It is a very small distance for a woman who has explained entire worlds in terms of arrangements. Mau notices the effort anyway, and hates how much he wants a small improvement to be evidence that the rest can be made to follow.

“Are you going to ask me to go with you?” Reze asks. ᴿᶻ

He shakes his head.

“I haven't told the people at home I'm here. I'm not going to promise their house to somebody without asking them.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment.

“That sounds like a good rule.” ᴿᶻ

He hears the second meaning clearly enough that he doesn't answer with a joke.

* * *

Makima explains the rest of the holding site after that.

Not everyone here has a body which can sit on a chair and ask to go outside. There are things she has caught after attacks, things which accepted terms they expected to profit from and things she claims are less harmful within reach of her orders than beyond it.

Mau asks which claim she can show him.

She indicates the far building.

“One of them broke its enclosure this morning.” ᴹᵏ

He looks toward it.

No cry for help conveniently arranged for his arrival. No civilians standing in the path to make his next decision for him. A damaged door, something heavy moving behind it and a woman who wants him interested enough to come back again.

“You kept it for me to see,” he says. ᴹ

“I thought you might prefer something observable.” ᴹᵏ

“I prefer knowing when you're arranging the view.” ᴹ

Makima inclines her head.

“Then you know.” ᴹᵏ

Reze gets up from the chair.

Mau turns toward her.

“You don't have to come.” ᴹ

“I heard you,” she says. “I'd like to see something too.” ᴿᶻ

She goes through the door first.

This time Makima has not told her to move.
