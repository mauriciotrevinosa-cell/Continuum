# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 8 — What They Think He Is

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S11E47
**Continuity state:** B+208 morning. Third private Makima visit, consent/inspection/current bounded terms renewed rather than old pledge presumed active. Original local devil formed around fear of Mau and circulated public images/rumors; distinct being, no identical soul, full kit, copied memories or instant counter to all abilities. Makima reports failed attempt to subordinate it, not objective universal power ranking. Mau chooses not to fight something merely because its appearance is disturbing. No household arrival of Reze; Makima's claims about her whereabouts not independently certified. No trial starts here.

*When: Mau hears his name from the dark, he nearly answers before he understands the voice.*

It isn't a person calling to him.

The sound begins in the wrong part of the throat and ends with something which might have been a laugh if the body making it had ever heard one without being frightened.

Makima stands beside the outer opening.

“You asked what else I had found,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I asked what else you were keeping.” ᴹ

“In this case, both.” ᴹᵏ

The renewed terms are active. Mau has inspected the approach and the ordinary route back. Reze is not here; Makima says she has been choosing where to go and has not sent her to greet him. That is a statement, not a fact he has personally checked.

He moves far enough to see beyond the opening.

The thing inside raises its head.

* * *

The resemblance is a collection of things strangers remember badly.

A wheel above the head. Too large. Teeth where a person describing his grin might have put an expression. The suggestion of a blade in each hand, made out of the same dark material as the arms. Something like a cloak which moves even when the rest of the body is still.

No mark on the throat where his own belongs.

No stiffness in the fingers.

No person behind the shape, standing in a room and wondering why his wife has decided he needs his hair moved out of his eyes before he can answer a question.

The creature has the parts which made the stories travel.

Mau looks at it until he can make himself breathe without following its rhythm.

“What is it?” ᴹ

Makima watches him rather than her captive.

“What some of them are afraid you are.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

He doesn't accept the explanation merely because it hurts.

He asks where she found it, what she saw it do and which part of its origin she can actually establish. Makima separates her account from her inference with enough care that he recognizes she has learned something about how to make him keep listening.

The body had repeated accounts people told in its hearing. A sun. An army beneath a man's feet. Cuts with no visible blade. It had grown more interested when the stories were frightened than when the speaker contradicted them.

The repeated name had been his.

That is what she had observed. Her conclusion is that this world has made room for a fear his public life has helped gather into a shape.

It does not contain the things he has done. It knows the stories which have reached it.

Mau keeps that distinction in front of him while the creature tries his name again.

This time the word is almost recognizable.

* * *

“Can you control it?” he asks. ᴹ

“Not as I expected.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at her.

She has given him too much history about arrangements to make the admission insignificant.

“You tried.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“Because it looked like me.” ᴹ

“Because I thought I knew what I was looking at.” ᴹᵏ

The creature moves closer to the boundary which has kept it in the far part of the building. Its false blades scrape together. The sound is ordinary enough to make Mau aware of how much he had been expecting something worse.

“It isn't me,” he says. ᴹ

“No.” ᴹᵏ

“It can't do everything I can.” ᴹ

“I haven't seen it do the things the stories claim.” ᴹᵏ

“Then say that too.” ᴹ

Makima regards him.

“You don't like a conclusion arriving before the evidence.” ᴹᵏ

He thinks of every private exercise he has justified by the future injury it might prevent.

“Not when the conclusion has teeth,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Noise examines what it can observe without accepting the false wheel as a replica of Mahoraga's work.

There is no learned adaptation in the movement it can see. The blades are parts of the creature's body. Their shape is not proof of Shrine. The dark which trails it is not automatically a shadow waiting for Mau to call it by name.

He feels almost grateful for the plain distinctions.

Then the creature puts one misshapen hand against its own chest and makes a small, tearing sound.

Mau remembers the people who watched his last fight.

The ones who couldn't keep looking. The ones who did and learned something they had never asked to know about the body of the person who came home to sit beside them at dinner.

He doesn't reach for the archive to check whether he looked like that.

He knows enough.

* * *

Makima asks whether he would like it destroyed.

The offer arrives very softly.

Mau looks at her.

“Would that make the fear go away?” ᴹ

“No.” ᴹᵏ

“Would it stop people telling the stories?” ᴹ

“No.” ᴹᵏ

“Then I need a better reason than not liking what it looks like.” ᴹ

She studies him for a long moment.

“It may become dangerous.” ᴹᵏ

“Then we find out what it does and what keeps people safe. We don't make it my guilt wearing a body and call killing it a solution.” ᴹ

The creature looks past him toward a place in the light where there is nothing to attack.

For a moment he can imagine it as another thing which has arrived in a world full of frightened people and been told what it is before it has chosen anything at all.

He does not let that thought make its teeth less real.

He asks Makima what the actual containment requires. Which distance matters. What he should do if the thing gets out. Who else knows where it is.

Questions he can take home.

He notices the last phrase only after he has already decided not to use it aloud.

* * *

They step back from the opening together.

Mau checks the seal she has shown him, then makes her repeat the part of the account which is still guesswork. No adoption. No shadow extraction. No promise to fight the creature later for the privilege of proving it is less than him.

When he looks up, Makima is smiling faintly.

“What?” he asks. ᴹ

“You keep finding a different question.” ᴹᵏ

“You keep offering me terrible answers.” ᴹ

“And yet you came again.” ᴹᵏ

He looks toward the road beyond her shoulder.

He has come again. He has found another thing he does not want loose near his home. Another reason to tell himself that being here is the part of the work someone has to do.

Makima lets him think about it before she asks whether he would like to know what changed her expectation of him.
