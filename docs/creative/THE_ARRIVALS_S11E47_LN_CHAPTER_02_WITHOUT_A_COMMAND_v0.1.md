# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 9 — Without a Command

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S11E47
**Continuity state:** B+208 morning/noon, third visit continued. Makima describes her changed subjective regard for Mau since his thank-you; Pochita account is her testimony. Control/hierarchy not a universal objective power meter. Mau offers to investigate forms of closeness without coercion, no romance, exclusivity, spouse replacement or promise he can alter a devil's nature. Her conduct remains her responsibility. Reze still outside Mau's household, continuing release terms intact. No new ability or knowledge of Ashborn's current private arrangement supplied here.

*When: Makima says he has become difficult to place beneath her, Mau asks whether this is supposed to flatter him.*

“Does it?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

“No.” ᴹ

She smiles a little.

“I didn't think so.” ᴹᵏ

They have moved away from the building. He prefers the open ground after looking at the thing which has learned his name from frightened people. Makima has brought no chair; they stand beside the road, far enough apart that either can leave without making the movement a request to the other.

“You asked what changed,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“You offered to tell me.” ᴹ

“You stayed.” ᴹᵏ

He folds his arms.

“I'm listening.” ᴹ

* * *

At first, she tells him, the question had been what he could be made into.

A body which survived incompatible things. A capacity to grow around injuries which should have ended the useful part of a subject. Power acquired from people who arrived already convinced they understood the limits of their own worlds.

Mau hears the description without supplying the names she does not know.

“That sounds like something you would want to own,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“You said I could be useful.” ᴹ

“I thought I would be able to decide what that meant.” ᴹᵏ

“And the gate was supposed to help.” ᴹ

“Among other things.” ᴹᵏ

He lets the admission remain ugly. There is no reason to make it less so because she has chosen to tell it plainly.

“Then you came back,” she says. “You remained interested in the people you had wanted before you went in. You had become much more dangerous without becoming easier to direct.” ᴹᵏ

“A disappointing result.” ᴹ

“An interesting one.” ᴹᵏ

“Not for the person in it.” ᴹ

Her gaze moves briefly to his throat, then returns to his face.

“I am learning which distinctions you will not let me omit.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

He asks why gratitude mattered.

Makima looks toward the road.

“Because I had made myself the person you should want to hurt,” she says. “And in that moment you chose the person you wanted to keep.” ᴹᵏ

Mau doesn't answer.

She continues without his help.

“I could have taken your attention. You were angry enough. I had given you enough reasons. Instead you thanked me for the part which left her a chance, and then I was no longer the most important thing happening in front of you.” ᴹᵏ

“She was dying.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴹᵏ

“You knew then.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

The anger rises cleanly this time. It doesn't become grit under his fingers. He makes himself keep looking at her.

“You don't get to turn that into a beautiful story about yourself.” ᴹ

“It was an unpleasant story about myself,” she says. “That was part of the difficulty.” ᴹᵏ

For the first time that morning, he has no immediate correction.

* * *

There had been one person she could speak of as above her without first rearranging the description until it became convenient.

Pochita.

Mau has heard the account before. The power to erase more than a body. What that had meant to her imagination of a better world and to the much smaller wish hidden inside it.

This time she does not begin with the world she wanted to make.

“I wanted to be chosen by something I couldn't make choose me,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Mau watches her.

“Then you understood what a choice was.” ᴹ

“I wanted an answer.” ᴹᵏ

“That isn't the same thing.” ᴹ

“So you keep telling me.” ᴹᵏ

“Because you're still doing it.” ᴹ

She turns toward him fully.

“And you are still here.” ᴹᵏ

He nods once.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Why?” ᴹᵏ

It is his turn to look at the road.

* * *

He thinks of her hand moving before the gate opened. The first headless body he had let fall. The laugh which had told him he had not even understood what he had done.

Then her account of wanting closeness and finding control easier to name.

He has spent enough of his life being told what the things inside him must mean about the person he would become. He does not want to answer that kind of certainty by making it the only future he can imagine for someone else.

He also wants her to stop.

Not because he has defeated her at a table. Not because the next clause happens to catch the next attack. Stop wanting the injury which makes those precautions necessary.

“Because if there's a way for you to want something without taking the person apart to get it, I'd like to find it,” he says. ᴹ

Makima regards him for a long time.

“You want to help me.” ᴹᵏ

“I want the people around you to have a choice. Helping you might be part of how that happens.” ᴹ

“You include yourself.” ᴹᵏ

“I'm standing here by choice.” ᴹ

“Without them.” ᴹᵏ

His arms tighten slightly.

“Don't.” ᴹ

She accepts the warning. He can see how much more useful she finds it than an answer.

* * *

“I wanted to control you,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He waits.

“I still think about what it would mean to have you within reach of an instruction. It hasn't stopped being an attractive answer because you told me why you dislike it.” ᴹᵏ

“I didn't expect one conversation to fix you.” ᴹ

“Do you think I need fixing?” ᴹᵏ

“I think the next person you decide to hurt would appreciate a different plan.” ᴹ

She lets out a small breath. Almost amusement, but quieter.

“And now I find myself considering the other side,” she says. “Being near something I can regard as greater than me. Not only something which can end me. Someone who seems able to make another way exist.” ᴹᵏ

He doesn't move closer.

“Control you,” he says. ᴹ

“Perhaps.” ᴹᵏ

“That would still be the same arrangement.” ᴹ

“With a different person making the decisions.” ᴹᵏ

“And no room for the answer you didn't expect.” ᴹ

Makima looks at him in silence.

The road beyond them remains ordinary. Wind moves loose dust against the low wall. Mau can hear something settling inside the damaged building, far enough away that neither mistakes it for a voice.

“I would like to be close to you,” she says at last. ᴹᵏ

He meets her eyes.

“I can hear that.” ᴹ

“It isn't a request you have answered.” ᴹᵏ

“Because you keep offering two versions where one of us doesn't get to remain ourselves.” ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't promise her his companionship as a cure.

He doesn't tell her somebody will surely love her if she behaves well enough. There are too many people in the world already expected to become a reward for someone else's recovery.

He offers a narrower piece of work.

“I can keep trying to understand the difference between what your nature makes difficult and what you've decided is inevitable,” he says. “I can look for ways to make a different choice easier. I can't promise I'll find one. And I won't tell you it means the place beside me is empty.” ᴹ

Her eyes move to his ring.

“I know it isn't empty.” ᴹᵏ

“It doesn't become available because you're honest about wanting it.” ᴹ

She looks back up.

“You have a great many conditions.” ᴹᵏ

“You have caused a great many problems.” ᴹ

That earns a real smile, brief enough that he might have missed it if he had been watching the hand again.

“Will you come back?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

“Send what you can tell me about the fear-devil before another meeting. Observations. Not a story about why it proves I need you.” ᴹ

“And then?” ᴹᵏ

“Then I'll read it.” ᴹ

She lets him make that the end.

* * *

Mau checks his return point before leaving. The soldier is undisturbed. The path is clear. His body carries no new wound home.

Makima stays beside the low wall until he has gone out of sight.

Only then does she look toward the building which holds the thing people fear.

She has made one fewer person obey her this week. The result has brought Mau back twice.

It is not the same as having him.

For now, it is interesting enough that she wants to see what the next choice will buy.
