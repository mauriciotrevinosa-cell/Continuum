Shoko watches both men roll their shoulders, move their hands and answer her questions without treating the examination as an insult.

* * *

The first contact is quiet.

Steel touches steel. Both blades have been prepared for practice, not made harmless by a promise that strong people never make mistakes. Mau feels the weight along the right edge and follows it toward Jin-Woo's outside shoulder.

Jin-Woo is no longer there.

Not vanished. One short step, the front foot taking less ground than Mau expected. The answering blade finds the space beside Mau's ribs and stops close enough that he can feel the end of the motion through his shirt.

He turns with it instead of jumping back.

His left hand comes across.

Jin-Woo meets it with the flat of his blade, gives the stiff fingers precisely the wrong pressure to close comfortably and shifts away before Mau can turn the exchange into a contest of arms.

Mau circles once.

“That was personal.” ᴹ

“That was your grip.” ᴶ

“You've been waiting to say that.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo attacks.

* * *

Mau gives ground on the first two steps and none on the third.

He has seen the invitation before: the half-open line, the suggestion that the next blade will need longer to recover than it actually does. This time he doesn't chase the hand. He puts his foot where the next position must become inconvenient and turns the right blade over Jin-Woo's guard.

Jin-Woo catches it.

But the catch has cost him the easy step.

Mau follows with the left.

For the first time the older fighter has to move farther than he wanted to avoid the touch. The answering scrape throws a spark close to Mau's wrist.

He laughs.

It is entirely involuntary.

Across the field, Gojo leans forward.

“There,” he says. ᴳ

Yuta doesn't look away from the feet.

“I saw.” ʸᵗ

* * *

Jin-Woo changes the rhythm.

The next attack comes with a heavier shoulder and a shorter blade path. Mau could spend more strength against it. Instead he lets the contact travel through his arm into a turn which takes the point past him.

The floor breaks under his heel.

He has controlled the blade and forgotten how much weight his own leg is offering the stone.

Jin-Woo doesn't wait for him to admire the lesson.

He steps into the broken footing, hooks Mau's wrist with his free forearm and uses the small correction Mau has already begun to pull the hand out of the useful line.

Mau releases one weapon.

It is a good decision made almost late enough to be the wrong one. His arm stays free. The dropped blade strikes the floor before either man can kick it back toward the watchers.

He crosses the remaining one in front of him and drives forward.

Jin-Woo goes with the pressure.

The place Mau meant to occupy becomes empty.

* * *

He knows this part too.

That is the problem.

He has been waiting for a sequence his own practice has made familiar. Jin-Woo has given him enough of it to invite the answer, then changed what happens after the second contact.

Mau realizes it while the next move is still forming.

He doesn't stop because the mistake has a name.

He drops lower, takes the impact against his shoulder and turns the blade back across the space Jin-Woo needs to finish. The steel meets nothing. The man has taken the line behind his blurred left edge.

Mau turns his head to recover the view.

Fast enough to see the hand.

Not fast enough to stop it taking his wrist.

* * *

Jin-Woo drives him toward the pillar without striking him into it.

One step. A change of hips. The blunted blade is out of the dangerous line before Mau has a reason to decide whether he can heal an accident.

His right arm is controlled against his own shoulder.

The remaining weapon falls when he opens his hand rather than letting the hold turn the joint farther.

He could try to break the position with strength.

He could also break something which belongs to the person teaching him.

“There,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶ

Mau lets his breath out.

“Yes.” ᴹ

The hold releases immediately.

* * *

For a few moments neither says anything.

Mau rolls the shoulder once, checks the movement and goes to pick up the fallen blades. His breathing is harder than the length of the exchange would suggest. Concentrating on precision has not made the physical work imaginary.

Jin-Woo waits for him to come back.

“You got the first part,” he says. ᴶ

“I noticed the celebration was premature.” ᴹ

“You recognized the opening. Then you trusted the rest of the answer to be the one you'd practiced.” ᴶ

Mau looks at the blades.

“It wasn't.” ᴹ

“No.” ᴶ

He turns one in his hand, slower this time. He remembers the shoulder in the archive, the preparation which had been so much smaller than he expected. He has put days into making his body agree to it.

That doesn't oblige the next opponent to agree to the rest.

“Again?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶ

Mau looks up.

The grin comes back.

“Now it's personal.” ᴹ

They reset farther from the damaged patch of floor.

Yuka watches Mau flex the fingers of the left hand and choose a slightly different grip. She can see the annoyance in him. She can also see the pleasure, the part which wants an answer badly enough to be happy someone has made him need a better one.

Mau doesn't begin with the movement he has practiced this time.

He begins by making Jin-Woo answer one of his own.
