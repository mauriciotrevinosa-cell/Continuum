The second exchange lasts longer.

Mau doesn't win it. He also doesn't give Jin-Woo the same useful correction twice. The wrist which had led him toward the pillar is already moving elsewhere when the hand comes to take it. The left blade stays close enough that his stiff fingers can hold the grip they actually have, rather than the one the remembered movement expects.

Jin-Woo touches his side with the flat.

Mau catches the answering shoulder before the older man can recover it.

They separate, both breathing harder.

“Better,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶ

“I had to work.” ᴶ

Gojo has stopped providing unnecessary encouragement. Yuta is watching with the focused stillness which means he is doing several calculations he hasn't offered to explain.

Shoko checks the time, then asks both fighters whether they still have enough attention left to hear a stop.

They do.

“Then decide what changes,” she says. ˢʰ

The next phase has tools in it.

Jin-Woo places two ordinary shadows where Mau can see them before they settle into their anchor positions. They belong to his own hundred. They have instructions to remain there, not join the fight when their king looks inconveniently occupied.

Mau puts one of his own ordinary units near the far side, within the agreed field.

“Only one?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶ

“I don't want you blaming an unfair advantage.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at the position, then back at him.

“Generous.” ᴶ

“One of my worst qualities.” ᴹ

He looks over.

“Don't become impossible just because you're enjoying yourself.” ʸ

“A narrow distinction.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo is already moving.

* * *

The first blade stops at Infinity.

Mau sees the exact instant Jin-Woo accepts the distance rather than trying to spend more strength against a problem that isn't strength.

The older man turns his wrist, lets the blade lie against the unreachable line and takes the floor away instead.

Authority lifts the slab beneath Mau's feet.

Mau jumps before it tilts far enough to throw him. He could fly; he is already choosing the simpler landing when Jin-Woo's second blade crosses the place his knee will need.

Still outside the neutral.

Not trying to get in.

Telling him where the next useful position is not.

Mau lands short, checks the floor with a fraction of his weight and discovers the next stone has been made uncertain too.

He laughs, almost offended by how much he likes the answer.

“You're renovating.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo doesn't waste breath replying.

He is at the first anchor.

* * *

The exchange takes him out of Mau's prepared view.

Mau turns his head rather than trusting the blurred left edge to supply the whole arrival. The first real warning is a piece of broken floor driven toward his feet, not a blade suddenly declared able to ignore Infinity because its owner has moved quickly.

He lifts the fragment with Authority.

Jin-Woo has followed behind it.

Mau gives up the neutral to take the surface between them.

Sky bends.

The blade meant to set his next position passes across a fold which makes the distance lie. Mau steps under the line, catches Jin-Woo's sleeve with his physical hand and puts the blunted edge toward the exposed ribs.

Jin-Woo lets go of the sleeve by tearing it.

The cloth remains in Mau's fingers.

The man goes lower than the attack, shoulder turning to bring a short, vicious return through the space beneath Mau's arm.

Mau closes Sky and reestablishes the neutral.

The blade stops.

The timing is close enough that both men see the cost of being a little slower.

* * *

“He's making him choose,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

Maki nods.

“Neutral isn't winning the position for him.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka watches Mau release the torn cloth.

There is no panic in the movement. There is delight, then calculation, then the smaller shift which means he has decided to try something inconvenient.

She knows the expression.

Gojo does too.

“All right,” he murmurs. ᴳ

* * *

Mau makes the next invitation obvious.

Too obvious.

Jin-Woo doesn't take it.

He drives another fragment across the floor, changes the angle with Authority and lets Mau spend the first correction before offering the movement which matters.

Mau closes neutral for a bounded cut at the ground between two safe marks they agreed to use. The line parts cleanly. He isn't aiming Shrine at a throat in a practice bout.

Stone shifts.

Jin-Woo has to choose whether to commit his weight or give the position back.

He commits.

Mau chooses Sky again.

The surface turns the apparent approach outward, leaving the true one closer to his right side than the eye expects. He has built a problem from things he already knows rather than asking a new power to solve the fact that Jin-Woo won't obey the first feint.

His blade comes up.

Jin-Woo releases his own.

For an instant Mau thinks he has made him drop it.

Then Authority takes the falling weapon and sends it past his elbow at an angle no wrist needs to supply.

Mau pulls back hard enough to tear the seam beneath his arm.

The blade misses skin.

He lands with both feet outside the place he wanted.

* * *

They break apart with a crash of loosened stone.

Neither has been overwhelmed. That is what makes the next decisions matter.

Mau could bring more power. So could Jin-Woo. Within the local strength the older man has chosen for this life, there is still enough force to make every block an expensive way to say he saw an attack coming.

The chamber doesn't need a bigger explosion.

It needs Mau to understand the last exchange before he supplies the next answer.

He lets the board-cracking model stay closed. Too uncertain for this distance, with another person's body behind the target. Noise keeps the observation waiting instead of placing a new route inside his moving hand.

Mau takes the blade he still holds and changes the grip.

Jin-Woo notices.

“That one again?” ᴶ

“Some of it.” ᴹ

“You know I saw it.” ᴶ

Mau smiles.

“I know.” ᴹ

* * *

The change is in what he lets the older man believe he is protecting.

The bad hand appears to recover too slowly. The right blade offers the obvious answer. The weight goes forward a fraction before the shoulder follows, inviting the pressure which had controlled him at the start.

Jin-Woo sees the trap.

Mau sees him see it.

Neither withdraws.

Gojo's expression loses its amusement.

He recognizes the order of the decisions. Not the exact motion. The readiness to offer a real cost so the opponent has to decide whether refusing it is worse.

“Mau,” he says quietly. ᴳ

It is too low to call a stop from the far side.

The two men meet in the middle of the broken field.
