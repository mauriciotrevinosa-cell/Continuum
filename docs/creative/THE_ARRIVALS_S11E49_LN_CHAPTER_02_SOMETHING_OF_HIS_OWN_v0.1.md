# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 13 — Something of His Own

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S11E49
**Continuity state:** B+209 late morning, expanded training ends. Mau accepts a controlled shoulder impact/bruise, not a lethal wound or new stat increment. Harmonizes own body, bounded psychic grip on one small nearby training weapon, Authority and Sky; no simultaneous twoCT. Jin-Woo's final joint/line position conceded by agreed practice rules, not claim Mau universally stronger by technique alone. No lethal-domain/TrueDragon/BlackFlash, no new extraction, Step or Bang used. Gojo recognizes Sukuna decision style; Mau openly admits reopening acquired archive when asked. All three training anchor units recalled after bout; deployments unchanged. Ciel wants larger conversation, not instant therapy or forced memory access.

*When: Jin-Woo takes the shoulder, Mau lets the cost arrive.*

The flat of the weapon strikes hard enough to turn his breath short.

He has chosen the place, seen the angle and still disliked the actual pain. That part never becomes a diagram merely because he understands why he offered it.

His right arm starts to fold.

Jin-Woo follows it.

The left blade leaves Mau's hand.

For the man in front of him, it is the familiar beginning of a good answer. A hand which doesn't close cleanly under the wrong pressure. A body already turning away from the impact. The second weapon about to become a piece of floor neither of them needs to account for.

This time the blade does not finish falling.

* * *

The shaped grip catches it close to the ground.

Small object. Short distance. One movement, supplied at the rate Mau has been practicing rather than the rate fear once made the old pattern demand.

He does not try to lift Jin-Woo.

He turns his own dropped weapon until its flat occupies the place behind the older man's heel.

Jin-Woo feels the touch and takes his weight off it immediately.

That is the answer Mau needed.

He goes with the shoulder which has just been struck, letting the impact turn him farther instead of fighting to restore the first position. His empty left hand reaches Jin-Woo's elbow. Authority takes the other weapon's loose angle just long enough to stop it lying conveniently across the line Mau wants.

Not a hold against all the strength in the arm.

A correction before the arm has finished choosing which way to spend it.

The older fighter reverses the wrist.

Mau sees it.

This time he doesn't insist that the next part must resemble the one he practiced.

* * *

Sky folds close to his right side.

The answering edge travels along a surface which has ceased to offer the straight path Jin-Woo chose. It doesn't vanish. It doesn't become harmless everywhere. Mau has moved the one line that matters and left himself no reason to assume the rest of the field agrees.

His physical blade comes up under the redirected arm.

Jin-Woo turns his hips and almost gets out.

Mau releases the psychic grip before the two views of his own attack can demand more correction than he has time to give. The dropped weapon hits the ground at last.

Both feet are his again.

He steps into the place Jin-Woo has just given back and catches the wrist against the older man's own shoulder.

The blunted point stops beside the ribs.

For an instant they are motionless, both aware of the strength it would take to keep going and the injury somebody would have to accept to make that a useful choice.

Then Jin-Woo opens his hand.

“There,” he says. ᴶ

* * *

Mau lowers his blade.

His shoulder hurts. His legs are trembling with effort he had not noticed while both men were still offering him something to solve.

He takes two steps back before grinning.

Jin-Woo rolls the controlled wrist once.

“That wasn't the whole movement,” he says. ᴶ

“You complained about the whole movement.” ᴹ

“I did.” ᴶ

“I am extremely responsive to criticism.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo laughs.

Mau laughs too, bends with his hands on his knees and discovers that the shoulder would prefer he stopped making sudden decisions about where his weight goes.

Shoko is already entering the field.

“Sit.” ˢʰ

“I can—” ᴹ

“Yes. Sit.” ˢʰ

He sits.

* * *

She checks both men before either can turn the result into an invitation for another round.

The shoulder will bruise. Mau has not lost movement or sensation. Jin-Woo has a reddening mark at the wrist and enough honesty to say which turn pulled before Shoko has to discover it by moving the joint herself.

No one needs to grow a new organ. No one has supplied a fresh lesson about how long a person can remain standing while missing blood.

Mau lets his breathing slow while Shoko finishes.

“You let that land,” she says. ˢʰ

He nods.

“I knew where it was coming.” ᴹ

“You knew where he intended to put it.” ˢʰ

He looks up at her.

“I know.” ᴹ

“Good. Keep the distinction.” ˢʰ

She goes to check Jin-Woo's grip again. The correction has cost neither man his dignity. Mau can still feel why she wanted it said.

* * *

Yuka brings him water.

He takes it with the uninjured arm and looks up at her with a ridiculous amount of satisfaction.

“Did you see?” he asks. ᴹ

She laughs.

“I was watching.” ʸ

“The last part.” ᴹ

“I saw.” ʸ

He wants to explain it anyway. The pressure behind the heel. Why he let go of the extra grip before the last turn. The part which had finally become his own answer instead of an imitation of a record he had learned to admire.

She sits beside him and lets him say all of it.

When he finishes, she takes the cup before he can set it on a broken piece of stone which will not hold it.

“You looked happy,” she says. ʸ

He looks at the field.

“I was.” ᴹ

* * *

Gojo waits until Shoko has finished the first checks.

Then sits on the other side of Mau without arranging his face into a joke.

“The entry,” he says. ᴳ

Mau glances at him.

“Which part?” ᴹ

“The part where you offered something real, watched him recognize it and still went through the middle.” ᴳ

Yuta has come close enough to hear. He stays standing, the recalled training unit settling back into Mau's shadow after Jin-Woo's two have already returned to their own king.

“Sukuna,” Gojo says. ᴳ

Mau looks at the ground for a moment.

Then back at him.

“I've been studying.” ᴹ

*The archive?* Ciel asks. ᶜ

*Yes.* ᴹ

*You opened it.* ᶜ

*Yes. I chose to.* ᴹ

She is quiet for a few breaths.

Gojo doesn't look frightened of him. That matters. He looks like a man who recognizes a method and would rather not let recognition stand in for understanding why somebody else has chosen to learn it.

“You don't need to fight like him to be difficult,” he says. ᴳ

“I don't intend to fight only like him.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo, coming back with Shoko, hears the answer.

“He didn't,” he says. “That's why the last part worked.” ᴶ

Gojo looks toward him.

The two men hold the distinction between them without needing Mau to choose one account and reject the other.

* * *

Ciel asks to talk once they are back and settled.

Mau can hear more in the request than a question about his shoulder.

He nods before remembering she is not standing in front of him in a body which can see a nod without his attention reaching her.

*Yes,* he says. ᴹ

Yuka takes his hand when he gets up.

“Shall we?” she asks. ʸ

He looks at Jin-Woo.

“Next time,” he says. ᴹ

The older man smiles.

“I'll bring a better answer.” ᴶ

Mau's grin returns immediately.

“You'd better.” ᴹ

He leaves with his wife, sore, pleased and carrying one clear result he can explain without asking anybody to pretend he didn't enjoy earning it.
