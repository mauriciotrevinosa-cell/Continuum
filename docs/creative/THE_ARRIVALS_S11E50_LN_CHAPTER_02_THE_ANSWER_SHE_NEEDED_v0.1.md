# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 69
## Chapter 15 — The Answer She Needed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 69
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S11E50
**Continuity state:** B+209 afternoon, same conversation continued. Mau gives honest account of Makima's prior attack order, continued visits, Reze's freedom, local fear-devil and what he offered/declined. Others distinguish real beneficial choice from secrecy/risk and suspected authorship from newly confirmed admission. No claim Makima already reformed, no romance jealousy, no withholding affection/silent punishment. Mau agrees no further secret Makima meetings and prior review of risky bodily/guest-derived construction; his own archive/autonomy not confiscated, Analyze not magically stopped. Trust work incomplete, larger trauma listening reserved for aftermath. No breaking of MY marriage or household.

*When: Mau says yes, Yuka closes her eyes.*

Only for a moment.

When she opens them, he is still there. Sore from a fight he enjoyed. Holding her hand. Giving her a true answer which has arrived nine days later than it could have.

“You knew,” she says. ʸ

“After the first visit.” ᴹ

“We were still going over what could have happened.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I asked you whether it was something you wanted to talk about.” ʸ

His grip loosens slightly.

“I remember.” ᴹ

She looks down at their hands, then back at him.

“What were you waiting for?” ʸ

* * *

He tells them what he had been trying to do.

Not in a single, flattering sentence. The first letter. The question he wanted answered. His anger when Makima admitted the order. What she had thought would happen if Yuka was gone.

Yuka's face goes very still at that part.

Mau stops before continuing.

“You can be angry,” she says. “I still want the rest.” ʸ

He gives it to her.

The boundary he had stated. No place with him bought by hurting his wife. No promise of closeness replacing the life he already wanted. The possibility that Makima could learn to choose something other than coercion, and his decision to keep looking for a way that might make it possible.

“I thought if I could get her to stop wanting it, we wouldn't have to keep guessing which rule the next attempt would go around,” he says. ᴹ

Rimuru is listening with one hand resting against his own knee.

“And what made you think she had stopped?” ᴿ

“I didn't know she had.” ᴹ

“Then that's part of the answer too.” ᴿ

* * *

Reze changes the conversation.

Mau explains who she is as far as he has actually learned it, what he saw in the room and the decision she made when the door was left open. He gives her own words their place instead of telling it as the afternoon he rescued somebody by being correct.

DIO asks whether the release lasted beyond his departure.

“We made it continue,” he says. “She asked for it herself. Makima accepted terms about not restoring the control or making her come back.” ᴹ

“Have you seen her since?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“No. Makima said she's been choosing where to go. I haven't checked that myself.” ᴹ

“Does she know where you live?” ʸ

“She knows where a message can be left. She knows it isn't permission to enter. I didn't offer the house without asking you.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him for several breaths.

“I'm glad you didn't,” she says. “I'm glad she got to walk through the door. I want those things to be true.” ʸ

He nods.

“They are.” ᴹ

“They don't explain why I had to wait for the answer about the person who tried to kill me.” ʸ

* * *

He wants to say that taking her there again would have been worse.

The thought is so immediate that he almost fails to hear the question it is trying to answer instead of hers.

“If I'd told you, would you have wanted to come?” he asks. ᴹ

“I might have.” ʸ

His breath catches.

“That's what I was afraid of.” ᴹ

“Then you could have told me that too.” ʸ

“And if you still wanted to?” ᴹ

“We would have talked.” ʸ

“She knew where to put the attack last time.” ᴹ

“I was there, Mau.” ʸ

He goes quiet.

Yuka moves closer, not farther away.

“I know you were frightened. I know what my hand felt like when I woke up and you were still holding it. I don't think you went because you stopped caring about me.” ʸ

His eyes lift to hers.

“But you don't get to make loving me mean I only hear about the danger after you've decided what everyone will do.” ʸ

* * *

Rimuru asks what was agreed with Makima beyond the visits themselves.

Mau gives him the actual scope. The bounded meeting restraint. Reze's continuing release. No promise that Makima has become an ally. No access to the protected area. No permission to examine his guests. No commitment that Yuka will accept her nearby if she behaves in a way he likes.

Then he tells them the example he used.

Ashborn. The choice to rest rather than accept the body the Architect had prepared. The room he still has because he wants it, and his freedom to leave it.

Rimuru's expression changes.

“You told her he is still with you.” ᴿ

“I told her he had a choice. That someone that powerful could let another person keep being themselves.” ᴹ

“She can understand the example and use the information,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Mau looks down at his hands.

“I didn't give her a way into his room.” ᴹ

“I heard that. We still need to tell him what she knows now.” ᴿ

DIO nods quietly.

“He should hear it from you,” she says. ᴰ

Mau agrees.

“She wanted me close,” he says. “She talked about controlling me, or letting me be the one above her. I told her that wasn't an answer I was offering.” ᴹ

Rimuru looks at him steadily.

“What did you offer?” ᴿ

“To understand what could change. To look for a way she could want closeness without making the other person unable to refuse it.” ᴹ

“Can you do that?” ᴿ

“I don't know yet.” ᴹ

“Does she know you don't know?” ᴿ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Rimuru lets a breath out.

“I can understand why you wanted to try,” he says. “I also know what it looks like when someone makes you feel as though a dangerous thing can only be handled if you keep going back yourself.” ᴿ

Mau looks away.

The room has not accused him of wanting the wrong person. It has asked him why he made himself the only person who could judge what the meetings were becoming.

He finds that much harder to answer.

* * *

The account of the fear-devil takes longer.

Yuka asks what it looks like. Mau begins with the things he knows it hasn't actually done, then notices that he is avoiding the face.

He describes that too.

Rimuru asks for the observed containment and the name of the place. Mau gives him the route, the boundary Makima showed him and the limits of the woman's account. The room grows quieter as the information becomes concrete enough that someone other than Mau can decide what needs checking.

“I didn't want it near here,” he says. ᴹ

“Neither do I,” Rimuru answers. “Now we can work on that.” ᴿ

There is no triumphant moment in the answer. No promise that knowing the location solves what people have begun fearing.

It is still the first time all afternoon Mau has felt a piece of the task leave his own hands without immediately becoming a failure.

* * *

DIO asks to return to the construction permission before they finish.

Mau listens.

She isn't asking him to become less capable. She isn't demanding an account of every harmless thing he learns while looking at his own world. The models she offered came with an agreement about how they would be expressed. He has told Noise that agreement can be set aside before asking her whether she wants a new one.

“You made me someone who could choose to stay,” she says. “Please don't decide afterward that my choosing is only useful when it gives you the answer you need.” ᴰ

His mouth tightens.

“I wasn't trying to make you smaller.” ᴹ

“I believe you.” ᴰ

That does not make the hurt disappear from her voice.

Noise turns toward her, then toward Ciel.

*Friends?* it asks. ᴺ

Ciel's answer comes immediately.

*Yes.* ᶜ

It waits.

*And we need to talk about what happened,* she adds. *You can stay while we do.* ᶜ

The outline settles back into its chair.

Mau hears the invitation as clearly as if she had said it to him.

* * *

They leave the evening with actual decisions.

No further private Makima meeting kept from the people sharing the risk. Any proposed contact is brought into the open before he chooses the conditions by himself. The fear-devil's account is checked without somebody being sent blindly into the place Makima has prepared.

The projects already in Mau are recorded with their failures. Body tests which could pull on the containment boundaries are reviewed before another release, not supplied to Ciel as a surprise after the fact. Guest-derived expressions return to the scope the guests agreed to until they choose otherwise.

Mau keeps his own archive. He can study. Noise can analyze. Nobody announces that the way to make them trust him is to leave every useful part of himself unfinished.

He has to stop hiding the decisions which ask the people beside him to carry consequences they were not allowed to see.

He says he will.

Yuka watches him while he says it.

“I want you to mean it,” she tells him. ʸ

“I do.” ᴹ

* * *

When they are alone again, he begins to get up to give her room.

She catches his hand.

“Where are you going?” ʸ

“I thought—” ᴹ

“You can sit here while I'm upset.” ʸ

He sits.

For a while neither says anything. The silence has a shape which is unfamiliar only because he keeps expecting closeness to be the thing that has to wait until he has made the rest right.

Yuka leans against his uninjured side.

He doesn't put his arm around her until she moves his hand where she wants it.

“I love you,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

He closes his eyes.

“I don't know how to make another answer fast enough.” ᴹ

She turns toward him.

“You can start by letting me be here while you don't have one.” ʸ

He holds her carefully.

She stays. Still angry. Still the person he wants to find when the door opens. Still willing to hear what he has not worked out how to say.
