*When: Yuka opens the little door, it catches halfway.*

Mau looks up from the bed.

“It does that if you lift the base.” ᴹ

She lowers the small wooden house to the shelf, tries again and gets the door all the way open.

The tree stands beside it. The familiar paths have been cut into the base with patient hands, the work of people who could have lifted a building and chose instead to learn what would make a small one feel like theirs.

Yuka moves one of the little pieces back from the entrance.

“I thought it was broken.” ʸ

“Beru would want an inquiry.” ᴹ

“Beru would want to arrest the hinge.” ʸ

Mau smiles.

She looks back at him and lets herself smile too.

* * *

He comes to stand beside her.

There is room in the gift for things they had only talked about when the shadows made it. Not a prophecy. Not a guarantee built into the wood. A place left because people who loved them had listened to what they hoped they might want.

Yuka touches the edge of that space.

“I still want it,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks at her.

“A family?” ᴹ

She nods.

“Not because we need something to make this week feel better. I just don't want us to stop being able to say it while there are other things to work out.” ʸ

His hand settles beside hers on the shelf.

“I want it too.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

For a while they look at the small open door together.

* * *

He tells her what he wants to find out first.

Not whether they can make a child powerful enough to survive every frightened person outside their home. He can hear that terrible possibility well enough without giving it the dignity of a plan.

Whether the body he actually has can begin that part of a life with her. What has changed since they first asked. Whether there are questions he has been replacing with a promise because saying he wants something is easier than admitting he doesn't know how it would work.

Yuka listens without taking her hand away.

“DIO said she could help with the things built into you,” she says. ʸ

“If there was a child. She didn't say that meant we could definitely have one.” ᴹ

“No.” ʸ

He looks down at her fingers.

“I want to know what I can honestly offer you.” ᴹ

She turns toward him.

“Then we ask together.” ʸ

“I wasn't going to decide for—” ᴹ

He stops.

She waits.

“Together,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

It doesn't become a decision to try tonight.

They still want time. They want a body understood well enough that the question belongs to the two people asking it, not to the next emergency which makes waiting seem dangerous. Yuka wants to hear what Shoko actually knows before an explanation becomes something they repeat because it sounds comforting.

Mau agrees.

“And you don't go to DIO with half the question because you want to bring me a finished answer,” she adds. ʸ

He looks at her.

“I heard that one.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸ

She leans against his side.

He kisses the top of her head. The small house stays on the shelf, its door open, while they stand beside it with no date written into the space that has been left.

* * *

When Yuka goes to wash, Mau takes the paper box out of the drawer.

The drawing lies inside, folded the same three times.

He doesn't open it.

The outside still carries the marks of a child's hands and the journey which should not have been able to bring ordinary paper back with him. It has been safe here while he went into other places. It will remain here when he next needs a clean shirt or something from his inventory before leaving.

It is for Ori.

She asked for later. He has promised later will still be there when she chooses it.

He checks that the corner has not caught beneath the lid, closes the box and puts it back in the drawer.

Yuka returns before he has shut it.

“Her paper?” she asks. ʸ

“Safe.” ᴹ

She comes to look at the closed box, not into it.

“Good.” ʸ

Then nudges the drawer shut with her hip because his hand is still resting on its edge.

* * *

In bed, she gives him the uninjured side without making him ask.

He notices.

“You can be comfortable too,” he says. ᴹ

“I am.” ʸ

She settles closer to prove it. He puts an arm around her and stays awake long enough to hear the last people returning through the common space outside their rooms.

No private work list opens.

Noise asks whether the next bodily model waits for the review. Mau says yes, then tells it good night.

Yuka shifts against him.

“Still here?” she murmurs. ʸ

He touches his mouth to her forehead.

“Here.” ᴹ

She takes his hand under the blanket.

It is the one she wants, even while there are things left for him to explain.
