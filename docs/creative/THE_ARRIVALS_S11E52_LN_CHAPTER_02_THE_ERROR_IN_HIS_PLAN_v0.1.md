*When: Makima tells him Ashborn chose to stay, the Architect asks her to repeat the sentence.*

She does.

Not the explanation she might prefer to give. The words Mau supplied when he wanted her to understand what a powerful person could choose not to take.

“As a guest,” she says. “Free to leave.” ᴹᵏ

The Architect watches her from the other side of the chamber.

“The boy may be repeating what he was permitted to believe.” ᴬʳ

“He corrected me when I said Ashborn had gone to his rest. He said they offered him somewhere to rest.” ᴹᵏ

“An arrangement.” ᴬʳ

“Apparently one he wanted.” ᴹᵏ

The distinction irritates the Architect more than an accusation would have.

* * *

He returns to the observations which have been useful to him.

The signature. The changes in the operator. The continued presence of a boy's habits after enough force had been applied that something greater should have begun deciding which of them mattered.

He had treated the persistence as an unfinished process.

Makima has brought him a different possibility: the greater presence had already chosen, and the choice was not the one he designed the process to produce.

“He spoke of the choice himself?” the Architect asks. ᴬʳ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“No command preceded it?” ᴬʳ

“Not one I could observe. I did not see inside his room. I have not heard Ashborn answer.” ᴹᵏ

“Then you have testimony.” ᴬʳ

“From a person who has been very willing to correct my assumptions.” ᴹᵏ

The Architect looks at her more closely.

She lets him decide how much of that answer concerns the information she has brought and how much concerns her reason for bringing it.

* * *

The old governor is gone.

He cannot reach out and make the operator's earned body become an earlier version because the design would be more convenient that way. He cannot command every route the boy has built merely by insisting that the original system should still be obeyed.

Some of the remaining infrastructure is local.

A threshold which admits an operator. An instance which can accept a definition before it finishes becoming a place. Boundaries that determine what can arrive and which expressions receive an answer inside them.

Makima watches his attention settle.

“You still have rooms,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He looks up.

“The protection around his home isn't yours,” she adds. ᴹᵏ

“I don't need his home.” ᴬʳ

* * *

He asks what she wants from the result.

“Him alive.” ᴹᵏ

“You have acquired a preference.” ᴬʳ

“I've had one for some time.” ᴹᵏ

“And obedient?” ᴬʳ

Makima thinks of the girl walking through an open door. Mau waiting beside the wall instead of following. The repeated question she had expected to become less interesting once she understood why he asked it.

“Reachable,” she says. ᴹᵏ

The Architect's expression does not improve.

“That is not a specification.” ᴬʳ

“It is mine.” ᴹᵏ

He turns back to the incomplete instance.

Whatever she calls the desired result, the work will be his. He is already revising the error: not a vacancy waiting for the correct king, but an operator who has learned to resist the uses his maker intended.

Makima lets him think the revision belongs entirely to him.

* * *

Before she leaves, he asks whether the boy is still looking for things to improve.

She remembers the attention he gave the release of force. The questions about her nature. The insistence that wanting could become a choice rather than an instruction.

“Constantly,” she says. ᴹᵏ

The Architect looks at the field he has not finished building.

“Good.” ᴬʳ

Makima doesn't ask what the word means to him.

She knows enough to understand they are not imagining the same person at the far end of the room.
