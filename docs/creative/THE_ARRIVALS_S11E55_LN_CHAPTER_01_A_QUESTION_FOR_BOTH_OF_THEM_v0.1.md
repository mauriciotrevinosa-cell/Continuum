# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 70
## Chapter 4 — A Question for Both of Them

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 70
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S11E55
**Continuity state:** B+213 afternoon. Chosen MY/Shoko/Ciel/DIO follow-up to S9E31CH01 and V69#18. Fictional present-body review supports possibility rather than guaranteed fertility, healthy child, pregnancy or completed inheritance procedure. DIO reaffirms bounded isolation of acquired constructed routes if later chosen, Yuka native inheritance not erased. Timing remains mutual choice/time together, not new crisis-driven conception. No real-world medical instructions, new power, child or instant all-body stability certificate. Mai talks with Yuka by her invitation afterward, not overhearing confidential exam.

*When: Shoko asks whether they have come to discuss a possibility or announce something, Yuka answers before Mau can decide which expression is least suspicious.*

“A possibility.” ʸ

Shoko looks at him.

“You may breathe.” ˢʰ

He does, with enough exaggeration that Yuka elbows him lightly.

“I was breathing.” ᴹ

“Poorly,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

DIO has agreed to join the review. Ciel is present through the same chosen connection, and the material on the table belongs to this question, not an invitation to open every remembered injury while the couple are trying to think about a future child.

Mau is grateful for that.

He still holds Yuka's hand under the table.

* * *

They begin with what has changed since the first conversation.

The body has survived more change than either parent would call reassuring if the other person's name were attached to it. The acquired structures have become more integrated. Some old limits belonged to a governor which no longer determines how much of him is allowed to exist.

None of that answers the reproductive question merely by sounding impressive.

“A body doing extraordinary things can still fail at an ordinary one,” Shoko says. “We are going to keep those questions separate.” ˢʰ

Mau nods.

“That's what I wanted to ask.” ᴹ

“Then let me finish asking it with you.” ˢʰ

* * *

The review is chosen and limited.

Ciel compares the present structure with the record they actually have. Shoko examines what she can assess through the body and her own work with it. DIO considers the boundary between Mau's underlying life and the acquired constructions which have made the question frightening in the first place.

They don't invent certainty where the available evidence stops.

There is no present finding which makes DIO tell the couple the possibility is closed. The underlying organization has not simply become a vessel for unrelated powers with every ordinary function erased beneath it. That is a useful answer.

It is not proof that conception will happen when they decide to try, nor a promise that an unseen child can be certified by looking hard enough at a father.

Mau listens to the whole distinction.

Then asks DIO to say the first part again.

“You have reason to ask the next questions,” she tells him. “I am not seeing a reason to tell you this future is impossible.” ᴰ

His hand tightens around Yuka's.

She tightens hers back.

* * *

The old offer still has its boundaries.

DIO can help keep the powers constructed into Mau from automatically becoming a possible child's starting structure. It would be work undertaken when they choose, with the right review, not something she performs today because their relief makes silence seem like consent.

Yuka would still be herself. Her own cursed energy, the possibility of a technique in a child, the ordinary uncertainty of a life neither parent has the right to design into a satisfactory answer.

“We aren't asking to choose who they'd be,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I know,” DIO answers. ᴰ

Mau looks toward her.

“I just don't want someone else's beginning to have to survive all the unfinished things in me.” ᴹ

DIO's expression softens.

“Then that is the question we keep working on. Not whether you deserve to want them.” ᴰ

He goes quiet for a moment.

Yuka moves closer until their shoulders touch.

* * *

They leave without a timetable.

Shoko wants continued ordinary monitoring, with changes actually shared instead of discovered from a new symptom. Ciel wants the current work recorded well enough that the next comparison is about a real body rather than a version everyone hopes is still accurate.

Mau agrees.

Yuka asks her own questions about what they would need to decide later. She doesn't let them turn the whole visit into the man who is more difficult to explain while she becomes a hand he holds through the answer.

He is glad when she interrupts him.

Embarrassed too, once, when she points out that she is the one who would have to carry a child and would like to finish speaking before he protects her from the inconvenience of asking something.

Shoko waits for him to realize it.

He shuts his mouth.

Yuka finishes the question.

* * *

Mai comes to sit with her later because Yuka asks.

They are outside the private review, with no record of the examination laid open for someone who wasn't invited. Yuka tells her the part she wants to share: they asked together. The answer leaves them room to want it, and reasons to take their time.

Mai listens.

“And you?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks toward the path where Mau has stopped to speak with Rimuru.

“I want time with him too.” ʸ

“Then have it.” ᴹᵃ

“That sounds very simple.” ʸ

“I didn't say the rest was simple.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka smiles a little.

Mai nudges her shoulder.

“You don't have to turn getting married into the first deadline somebody failed to meet.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka lets the answer settle.

Then calls Mau over before he can turn the conversation with Rimuru into another task.

He comes when she asks.

Mai looks between them and gets up.

“I'm going to find someone less embarrassing to sit with.” ᴹᵃ

“Good luck,” Mau says. ᴹ

She gives him a look, and Yuka begins laughing before either can improve the exchange.

He sits in the place Mai has left, beside the woman who has asked the same difficult question with him and still wants the afternoon which comes afterward.
