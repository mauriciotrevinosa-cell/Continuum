# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 70
## Chapter 6 — What He Hears

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 70

*When: Yuka catches the side of her finger, Mau is already out of his chair before she says his name.*

It is a small cut.

The knife has slipped while she was finishing the piece of fruit. She has enough time to look at the bead of blood and be annoyed with herself before his hand closes gently around her wrist.

“Mau.” ʸ

He looks at her finger.

Then at her face.

The room has not changed. He knows that. A bowl on the counter, the knife lying flat where she put it, the ordinary morning beyond their own rooms. No wound beneath her ribs. No body trying to lose its shape while he begs it to stay.

The little spot on the cloth disappears.

He hasn't stood up a second time or reached for a clean one. A moment ago it was marked; now it is not.

Yuka notices.

“It's my finger,” she says softly. ʸ

His breathing catches on the answer.

“I see.” ᴹ

* * *

He lets go when she asks.

That matters. He hears the request, opens his hand and lets her do the small, practical thing she was already able to do before he reached her.

She presses the cloth against the cut.

It marks again.

He doesn't clean it.

She looks at him over her hand.

“Come sit with me.” ʸ

He brings the chair closer.

For a few breaths they watch the same finger with two very different kinds of attention. The bleeding stops. The mark on the cloth stays. Nothing in the room asks for a rescue.

Mau rubs his palms against his trousers.

“Sorry.” ᴹ

“You don't have to apologize for noticing.” ʸ

“I overdid it.” ᴹ

“You looked frightened.” ʸ

He gives her a little smile.

“For a second. It's fine.” ᴹ

Her expression changes.

* * *

He knows that expression too.

Not anger arriving from nowhere. The tiredness of hearing a familiar answer when she has been trying to ask a different question.

“What happened in that second?” she asks. ʸ

“I saw you were hurt.” ᴹ

“I know what you saw.” ʸ

He looks at the cloth.

The memory is still close enough that naming it feels like asking it to come into the room. The ground beside the meeting place. Her hand which had needed him to keep holding it while her own body could no longer help him understand whether she felt his fingers.

He has already told people what happened there. Everyone knows it was terrible. She was the one injured.

He doesn't want to make the small cut become another thing she has to manage in him.

“It just reminded me,” he says. “I knew where I was. I stopped.” ᴹ

“You did.” ʸ

She says it immediately.

He had been expecting an objection.

“I've seen you trying,” she continues. “The tests. Asking before the next part. Letting us be there. I saw all of that.” ʸ

He looks up.

“Then—” ᴹ

“I still want to hear what happens when you get frightened.” ʸ

* * *

He leans forward, forearms on his knees.

“I don't always know how to make it useful.” ᴹ

“It doesn't have to be useful.” ʸ

“If I tell you every time I remember something, what am I giving you?” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him.

The question has come out more sharply than he intended. He can hear the argument inside it: a body which can take a little more pain ought to take it; a person who can contain a little more fear ought to keep it from becoming anybody else's morning.

He doesn't think she is too weak to hear him.

He thinks hearing him should come with an answer which makes the hearing worthwhile.

“You're letting me know you,” she says. ʸ

His hands stop moving.

“I know you love me,” she continues. “I know you can be careful. I know you were in this room yesterday because you wanted the day we were having. I'm not asking you to prove those things again.” ʸ

“I'm here now.” ᴹ

“I know where you're sitting.” ʸ

The familiar sentence lands harder this time.

* * *

Yuka puts the cloth down.

She is frustrated enough that the next words come before she has chosen the safest way to say them.

“If you won't let me be with you when it hurts, I don't know how to stay in this.” ʸ

Mau looks at her.

The room seems to narrow around the word stay.

She is still talking. Saying she doesn't want every attempt to reach him to end at a smile he has prepared before deciding whether he can tell her what is behind it. Saying she wants him to stop treating his fear as an injury he must finish healing before she gets to touch him.

He hears the words.

One sentence has become much louder than the rest.

He could ask her what she means by this.

The question forms whole in his head. He sees the possible answer, then the other answer she might feel she has to give if he makes her look at him while he asks whether she still wants to be his wife.

His mouth doesn't make the question.

“I understand,” he says instead. ᴹ

* * *

She takes his hand.

He gives it to her with no delay, and the immediate contact steadies her enough that she thinks he has heard the part she most needed to say.

“We don't have to solve it before breakfast,” she says. ʸ

He looks at the bowl, then back at her.

“No.” ᴹ

“But I want us to talk properly. Without you deciding the answer has to make me feel better by the end of the conversation.” ʸ

He nods.

She waits until he looks at her.

“This afternoon?” ʸ

The inspection is already scheduled. The support has already been arranged. He has promised her the time afterward, and for a moment the ordinary shape of that promise gives him something he can still do correctly.

“This afternoon,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka moves closer and rests her forehead against his.

He closes his eyes.

* * *

They eat the fruit.

It is not a reconciliation performed for the benefit of an audience. There is still a difficult conversation waiting, and neither pretends breakfast has replaced it.

Yuka tells him the cut has stopped stinging. He believes her without taking her hand again to verify it. He puts the used cloth where it can be washed normally and leaves the knife lying on the counter until he is ready to clean it with the rest.

When they go downstairs, he carries nothing she has asked to keep in her own hands.

At the common room she stops him before he turns toward Rimuru and the waiting inspection details.

“I love you,” she says. ʸ

His throat tightens.

“I love you too.” ᴹ

She kisses him. He holds her with both arms for a moment, carefully, as though being allowed to do something familiar can tell him what the unfamiliar sentence meant.

Then she draws back.

“Come find me when you're done.” ʸ

He nods.

“I will.” ᴹ

She watches him go to the others, still thinking about how to say the next part more clearly when he comes back.
