*When: he wakes, the water is still too far away.*

Not beyond the reach of Authority.

Beyond the reach of the hand he can move without first deciding which pain deserves to become the center of the room.

He has to think before taking a drink. That is the first thing he hates.

Noise keeps the useful part of the body's record close. The bleeding has stayed closed. The breath still catches at the damaged ribs. The legs remain visible under the cover without feeling like something he can bring toward himself by wanting them.

Mau takes the flask with a small pull and brings it to his good hand.

He drinks one mouthful.

Waits.

Another.

The third makes him cough.

He turns his face toward the cloth before the coughing can leave blood in the place where he needs to breathe. It hurts enough that he loses track of whether the next sound belongs to the ribs or the back or the hand gripping the cover.

When it passes, the flask has rolled away again.

He closes his eyes.

“I know,” he says, though Noise hasn't spoken. ᴹ

* * *

The first day he spends awake is mostly the work of staying in a body.

He can supply repair. He cannot supply enough through the restriction to make every damaged part agree on which one should be first. The nervous structure in the back needs more precision than the torn side did. Something has healed around pressure instead of removing it, and the body keeps trying to defend the position because the position is what has stopped it falling farther apart.

He looks at the comparison until the shapes blur.

Mini Ciel narrows the useless detail.

It does not speak.

He is grateful for the help and misses the person whose silence he has not chosen.

*One,* Noise says. ᴺ

One problem. One correction he can actually keep stable. Then a pause long enough that the next attempt isn't simply the previous failure supplied by a more exhausted brain.

Mau lets the rest wait because the alternative has begun to make his vision disappear.

* * *

He buys a different container for the meal.

One he can keep near his mouth without asking the wrist to do a motion it no longer performs comfortably. Ordinary food, softened by ordinary preparation. No ingredient which tells a broken spine it has been purchased out of the story.

Eating takes time.

He has to stay awake for it. Swallow. Stop. Keep the body from twisting while he reaches for water. Watch the approach over the edge of the cover because an enemy doesn't need to be very large to become a problem beside a person who cannot stand.

A small crawling thing reaches the broken pillar before he has finished.

He kills it with a short, ugly application of Authority.

The body strikes the far wall.

Mau stares after it for several breaths.

Then looks back at the meal.

He is still hungry.

For some reason that is the part which makes his throat close.

* * *

The cleaning comes back while he is trying to change the marked cloth beneath his side.

He has been pulling too hard with one arm. The pain has climbed. There is blood on his shirt again, though less than he feared when he first felt the warmth.

For an instant he wants the cloth clean with the same desperate simplicity with which he had wanted Shoko to let him remain beside Yuka.

The stain is there.

Then isn't.

He goes still.

Noise turns toward the retained route.

*Surface,* it says. ᴺ

“I know.” ᴹ

The wound remains. The body is not repaired because the shirt has become less frightening to look at. He doesn't confuse being clean with being well enough to stop paying attention.

But the cloth is clean.

It is one useful thing he can do without needing to crawl farther.

He laughs once, very quietly.

Then covers his face with the good hand until the laugh becomes something else.

* * *

Noise stays.

No body sitting on the floor beside him. No hand appearing to perform the work he can't do. Its attention is present where his own can still reach it, the one companion the field has not made him stop being able to hear.

*Mau.* ᴺ

He breathes in.

It hurts.

He breathes out.

“I miss her.” ᴹ

The admission feels absurdly small beside the body around it.

Noise doesn't call it small.

*Wife,* it says. ᴺ

Mau nods, with his face still covered.

“Yes.” ᴹ

*Home.* ᴺ

“Yes.” ᴹ

He lowers his hand after a while. The room hasn't become kinder. The water is still there. He takes another drink before the repair can persuade him he has nothing left to do but keep being hurt.

* * *

On the sixth day he tries too much.

Not because he has forgotten the warning. Because part of the leg has begun to feel like pain instead of absence, and hope is quite capable of making a man misjudge what his body has actually offered him.

He tries to bring a knee toward himself.

The movement doesn't finish.

The broken place answers with a white, breathless shock which leaves him shaking against the cover, unable to supply a useful word while Noise keeps the last comparison from disappearing into the panic of wanting everything put back immediately.

He doesn't die.

He doesn't become well because he has survived the attempt.

For a long time he does nothing except get his breathing back.

Then whispers, “Not that way.” ᴹ

Noise keeps the failure.

* * *

The days acquire practical differences.

A meal he can finish. A sleep which lasts longer than the worst edge of the pain. An interval in which the repair doesn't have to choose between the back and a fresh pull in the side. A position in which the wrist can bear a little more before he has to stop pretending the alignment is right.

He marks the days because the work needs a sequence.

He does not know where to put them in the life he wanted to be having when he left the breakfast table.

The afternoon with Yuka remains this afternoon in a part of him which is unwilling to let it become a date he has missed by weeks.

Noise remembers the actual words she said afterward.

*Love,* it reminds him once. ᴺ

“I heard.” ᴹ

*Find me.* ᴺ

He closes his eyes.

“I heard that too.” ᴹ

*Ask,* Noise says. ᴺ

Mau looks toward the opening.

There is no answer he can force across the closed field. No useful way to decide for Yuka which meaning she intended and then call the decision protection.

“When we get back,” he says. ᴹ

Noise stays with him until he sleeps.

* * *

On the twelfth day, a toe moves.

Mau has been watching the wrong part of the repair when it happens. He almost misses it. The movement is so small that he thinks the cover has shifted from a breath or the edge has caught against something.

He looks.

Asks the foot again.

It answers.

He laughs with no air behind the sound.

“Did you see?” ᴹ

*Yes.* ᴺ

He wants to do it again. Wants the whole leg to follow. Wants to stand, walk, find the next room and make the weeks become the unreasonable delay before an otherwise ordinary return home.

He makes the toe move once more.

Then leaves it alone.

* * *

Getting up takes five more days.

Not five days in which he lies still and then discovers the story has become generous. Five days of trying one little part, recovering from it and making the next part an answer rather than an act of faith in how much pain a person ought to tolerate.

When he stands, the body is not straight.

One side catches before the other. The lower back has a tightness he cannot name without using the comparison. The wrist is usable at the cost of an angle which changes how the dagger needs to lie in his hand.

He is standing.

He stays there long enough to believe the feet will continue to be where he put them.

Then sits back down before falling can make the success into another emergency.

“Shoko's going to hate this,” he says. ᴹ

Noise considers the record.

*Help,* it says. ᴺ

Mau looks at his hand.

“She will.” ᴹ

* * *

Before leaving, he returns to the body which had pinned him.

Slowly. With the supplies secured and his own weight arranged around the movement the back can actually perform. He does not mistake the ability to cross a room for permission to throw himself through the next one without examining what changed.

The dead pattern remains.

Mau can reach it.

He has no reason to expect the field to let it stand beside him. Every attempt to express one of his own soldiers has met the same closed permission. But the relation which keeps a pattern is not the act which gives it a body in the room.

He asks Noise to watch the difference.

Then says, “Arise.” ᴹ

* * *

Something catches.

Not a figure climbing from the corpse. Not a new voice greeting its king and offering the healing he has been denied.

A bond takes shape and remains folded inside the local record of his own shadow, held without the outward form the room refuses to admit.

He waits.

No body comes.

He has not found the door by learning another way to keep something with him.

Still, the pattern is there. One more than the thousands he has been unable to call. A quiet, incomplete presence whose proper beginning will have to wait until the world allows him to unfold it.

He doesn't give it a name in the empty hall.

“Later,” he says. ᴹ

Then takes the two daggers, the ordinary pack and the body which has learned to walk badly, and goes to find the next room.
