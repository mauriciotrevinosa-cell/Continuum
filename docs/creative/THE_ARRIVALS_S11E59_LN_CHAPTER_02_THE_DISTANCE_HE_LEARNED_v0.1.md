# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 70
## Chapter 13 — The Distance He Learned

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 70
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S11E59
**Continuity state:** Local days 18–41 (outside B+215 ~1h12m–2h44m at 360x). Mau walks badly after the spinal injury; side, ribs and left wrist imperfectly repaired. Hall of wall-set strikers on a rhythm, modifier x2 then x2.5. Field rule established in scene: each time he adapts, the field changes (day 31 flow restriction makes his responses fractionally later; day 39 modifier rises). Injury from a moved margin, not carelessness: deep left thigh wound, chipped hip edge. Own RCT, finite CE, shop food/water/cloth only. Ciel absent, Noise inside. One stored unmanifestable bond (6,616). No new technique, unit or rescue contact.

*When: Day eighteen. Mau counts the strikes in the long hall while, outside, a little over an hour has passed since he stepped across.*

Seven.

He counts them again from the niche where he slept. The hall is long enough that the far end is a guess, and its walls are set with arms of grey stone, each one folded into a recess like a hinge waiting for a door. They open in turn. They sweep across the floor at the height of a man's knees, or his ribs, or his throat, and fold back. Then the next.

Seven strikes, a pause the length of three breaths, seven more.

“You see it,” he says. ᴹ

*Yes,* Noise says. ᴺ

“Good. Then one of us is being sensible.” ᴹ

He gets up the way he has learned to get up since the floor. Good hand on the wall. Weight onto the right leg first, because the left one still sends a hot wire up into his back if he asks too much of it before it has woken. The lower spine holds. It holds crookedly, as though it has agreed to carry him on the understanding that nobody mentions the shape.

He shoulders the pack. Water, cloth, bread. The two daggers are sheathed where his hands can find them.

The first stretch of floor is twelve paces long.

He watches the arms go past until he can feel the gap with his body and not only count it with his head. Then he goes.

* * *

The first day in the hall he makes two segments and spends the afternoon shaking in the next niche.

The strikes are not fast for something that has fought Sukuna. They are fast for a body that cannot run. Infinity takes the first glancing arm and slows it, and he discovers what the field has done to that answer since the first room: the neutral still holds, but every contact costs him more to keep. Holding it through seven sweeps is a long time to pay.

So he stops relying on it.

He learns the hall instead.

Where the arms reach and where they don't. Which ones fold back early. The tiny rattle in the recess before the third arm opens, a sound he begins to hear before the movement. The places on the floor which are worn smooth and the places that have kept their grit, which matters when his left foot does not quite land where he sends it.

By the fourth day he crosses three segments before resting. By the sixth, four. He eats in the niches and sleeps in them, back to the wall, with the bread wrapped in cloth on his chest so nothing can carry it off without waking him.

The back hurts all the time. He stops reporting it to himself.

“We're getting good at this,” he tells Noise on the ninth day. ᴹ

*Good,* Noise agrees. ᴺ

He laughs, and the laugh pulls at his ribs, and he lets it.

* * *

Twice in those days something tries to share the niches with him: a flat crawling thing with too many joints, and later two of them together. He kills them with the daggers and Authority, carefully, because careful is the only speed his body will lend him. A scratch from the second one opens his forearm far wider than a scratch should. He closes it and looks at it for a long time.

The field has made itself expensive to touch.

He doesn't know the number. He knows that a graze is a cut now and a cut is something he has to stop and sew with cursed energy. He builds the hall around that knowledge the way he built it around the arms.

On the thirteenth day of the hall, which is the thirtieth day of all of it, he crosses five segments without once letting an arm come near enough to need the neutral.

He sits in the niche afterwards with his legs out in front of him and the bread on his knee, very pleased with himself, and makes a mark on the wall with the point of a dagger.

Thirty.

* * *

The next morning he does exactly what worked the day before.

He watches the seven. He waits for the pause. He goes on the first breath of it, along the worn line, past the recess with the rattle.

The third arm opens.

He is already moving to the place where the arm doesn't reach. He has been there forty times. He knows the spot on the floor with the grit on it and the spot he wants his right foot to find.

His foot finds it a little late.

Not much. The width of a fingernail. A slowness so slight that he only understands it afterwards, as a fact his body had already received before his mind was told.

The arm takes him across the left thigh.

There is a sound, wet and heavy. He goes down. The next arm sweeps over the place his head would have been if he had stayed standing, and he hears it pass, and he hears himself making a noise he will not later remember making.

The neutral comes up.

It comes up late, too. He feels that now that he is looking for it. A pause between asking and receiving that was not there yesterday. The fourth arm strikes the space around him and slows, and he drags himself the rest of the way to the niche on his hands and his good leg, through the pause, with the field pressing on his hold like a palm on a bruise.

He gets his back against the wall.

Then he looks.

The cut is deep. The edge of the hip under it has been struck hard enough that something there is not whole any more. The blood is coming fast enough to frighten him.

“Bleeding first,” he says, through his teeth. ᴹ

*Bleeding first,* Noise agrees. ᴺ

* * *

It takes him the rest of the day.

RCT answers the way it has answered since the first room: present, constrained, a river through a narrow pipe. He spends what he has on the vessels first, then the muscle that will let the leg bear weight, and leaves the bone for later because there isn't enough of him left over.

When the bleeding stops he lies on his side and breathes.

He was not careless. That is the thing he keeps turning over. He did not get greedy and try for a sixth segment. He did not grow bored. He did what had kept him whole forty times, and the forty-first time it was not enough.

“I was slower,” he says. ᴹ

*Yes. Small.* ᴺ

“My leg?” ᴹ

*No.* Noise brings the comparison close: his own response yesterday, his own response today. The body is the same body. The difference is in the space between deciding and arriving, where the field takes its share. *Room takes more.* ᴺ

Mau looks up at the ceiling of the niche.

The flight he used on the first day. The neutral. The little lift of Authority. Every one of them has been answering a hair more slowly since this morning, and he didn't notice because he was too good at the hall to be paying attention to himself.

“Since when?” he asks. ᴹ

*Last night.* ᴺ

He closes his eyes.

Last night he made a mark on the wall because he had finally stopped being surprised.

* * *

He relearns the hall.

It is worse the second time because he knows what it costs to be wrong. He gives every arm more room than it needs. He waits for a longer pause before going. He leaves the neutral half-raised through the whole crossing and pays for it at every contact, because a slow answer is better than none. The thigh closes badly and the hip under it grinds when he steps. He crosses two segments a day, then three.

By the thirty-eighth day he has it again. The new lateness lives in his feet now. He sends the step a little early so that it lands where he needs it.

He does not make a mark that night.

On the thirty-ninth morning, an arm he has cleared by a full hand's width brushes his shoulder through the neutral.

It is only a brush. It should be nothing.

It tears the skin from the point of the shoulder to the elbow, and he feels the joint shift.

He stays on his feet this time. He reaches the niche. He sits down, holds the arm against his chest and makes himself look at the hall instead of at the blood.

Nothing in the hall has changed. The arms are the same speed. They reach the same places.

The brush only hurt more.

“They raised it,” he says. ᴹ

*Yes.* ᴺ

“When?” ᴹ

*When you knew it.* ᴺ

Mau sits very still.

He thinks of the thirtieth day, the mark on the wall, and the slowness that came the next morning. He thinks of the thirty-eighth day, when the new timing finally became his, and this.

“It waits,” he says. “It waits until I've learned it. Then it changes the part I learned.” ᴹ

Noise doesn't answer at once. It looks at the same facts from every side it has, the way it does when it wants to be sure.

*Yes,* it says at last. ᴺ

* * *

The worst of it is not the shoulder.

He sews it with what he can spare and binds it with the shop's cloth, and the pain becomes the kind he can carry. The worst of it is understanding what has been built for him.

He has always survived by learning. On the tower floors, inside the Trial, against Sukuna. He watches. He finds the rule. He makes the rule his. It is the thing he trusts most in himself, more than strength, more than any technique he has been given.

Someone has made a place where that is the trap.

The better he gets, the sooner it hurts him.

He looks at the arms going past, seven and a pause, seven and a pause, and he can feel the timing already settling into his body again whether he wants it to or not. He can't stop learning. He doesn't know how to be the kind of person who doesn't.

“Then we learn slower,” he says. ᴹ

*Can't,* Noise says. ᴺ

“I know.” ᴹ

He eats a little of the bread. His shoulder doesn't want to bend to bring it to his mouth, so he leans down to his hand instead, and chews, and swallows.

On the forty-first day he reaches the end of the hall. There is a door there, low and plain. Behind him the arms keep sweeping across an empty floor, seven and a pause, exactly the speed they always were.

He doesn't look back at them.

He goes through, and starts counting whatever comes next.
