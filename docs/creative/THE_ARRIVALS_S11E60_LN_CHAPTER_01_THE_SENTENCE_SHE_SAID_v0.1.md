*When: The counter passes thirty days inside, Yuka realises she has been holding the folded paper for two hours.*

Her fingers have pressed the creases soft.

She puts it back in her pocket and keeps her hand over the place, which is not really putting it away.

The frame stands where it has stood all morning. The words over the empty surface have not changed since the last time she read them, except for the counter, which never stops. It moves the way a clock moves in a fever, too fast to be wrong and too fast to believe.

At some point it began putting days in front of the hours. Thirty, now.

She did the arithmetic once, when Rimuru first said it aloud, and then she stopped doing it, because the numbers kept turning into his face.

* * *

The line beneath the modifier changes a little after the second hour.

Nobody is touching anything when it happens. Gojo is kneeling at the base of the frame with one hand flat on the ground. Yuta is holding the return line. Jin-Woo is beside Ciel's working record, waiting for the next shape of the question. The surface simply rewrites one of its own sentences while they watch.

**OUTBOUND EXPRESSION FLOW: FURTHER RESTRICTED**

Gojo lifts his head.

“That's not us,” he says. ᴳ

“No,” Rimuru says. “Ciel says nothing on our side moved.” ᴿ

Yuka reads the line again. It doesn't tell her what it means. It tells her that something has been taken away from him that he had a moment ago.

She looks at Shoko without meaning to.

Shoko is already looking back.

“We don't know what it does to him,” Shoko says. “Write it down. Don't decide it yet.” ˢʰ

The modifier changes half an hour later.

**HOSTILE DAMAGE MODIFIER: 2.5**

This time Ciel asks Rimuru to read her the time of every change since the start. He does. She has kept them all. The first room. The rise to two. The flow line. This.

Rimuru listens for a while with his eyes half closed.

“She says they're not coming on a schedule,” he tells the others. ᴿ

“Faster as it goes?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶ

“No. Irregular. Long gaps, then a change. She thinks the gaps are when nothing is changing in there.” ᴿ

Yuta frowns at the line in his hands.

“When he's stable,” he says. ʸᵗ

Gojo is very quiet. He has gone still in the particular way that means he is looking at something nobody else has seen yet and does not like it.

“When he's figured it out,” Gojo says. ᴳ

Nobody answers him.

Yuka feels the sentence go through her like cold water.

She knows how Mau gets out of things. She has watched him do it for two years: the way he goes silent and looks, and looks, and then something in his face settles because he has found the rule. She has seen him do it with a door, with a recipe, with Sukuna.

“So when he gets better at it,” she says, “it gets worse.” ʸ

Rimuru doesn't soften it.

“That's what it looks like.” ᴿ

* * *

Maki finds her a little later, sitting on the low wall near the frame where she can see the counter without standing in anybody's way.

Maki doesn't ask whether she's all right. She sits down next to her and puts a cup of something hot into her hands, and waits until Yuka has actually drunk some of it.

“You're doing the thing,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“What thing.” ʸ

“Where you say something in your head forty times to see if it comes out different.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka looks into the cup.

“It doesn't come out different.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹᵃᵏ

They watch Jin-Woo say something to Ciel's record and wait for the surface to answer. It doesn't. He changes one word and asks again.

“Tell me what you said,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

* * *

So Yuka tells her.

The cut finger. The way he was out of his chair before she had even said his name, and the way the spot vanished from the cloth. How he smiled and said it was fine. How she has heard *it's fine* from him so many times now that she can hear the shape of what he isn't saying underneath it, and how this morning she was tired, and frightened, and it came out before she had made it safe.

“I said if he wouldn't let me be with him when it hurts, I didn't know how to stay in this.” ʸ

Maki doesn't say anything.

“I meant *this*,” Yuka says. “The way we keep doing it. Him going away inside where I can't reach and coming back with a smile. I meant I don't know how to keep standing outside that door. I didn't mean—” ʸ

She stops.

“I know what you meant,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Do you?” ʸ

“I know you.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka turns the cup slowly between her hands.

“He said *I understand*.” ʸ

Maki waits.

“He never says that,” Yuka says. “He asks. He asks about everything, he asks what you mean by things you haven't finished saying yet. And he just said *I understand* and held my hand, and I thought—I thought that meant he'd heard the part I needed. I thought we were fine until the afternoon.” ʸ

The counter moves on. Thirty-six days. Thirty-seven.

“He heard *stay*,” she says. ʸ

Her voice does not do anything dramatic. That is almost worse. It says the word as though it has been sitting in her mouth all morning.

Maki puts an arm around her shoulders.

“You don't know that,” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I know him too.” ʸ

* * *

They sit there for a while.

Maki doesn't tell her it isn't her fault. Yuka would not have believed it, and they both know there is a difference between a thing being her fault and a thing being hers to carry. The Architect built the room. She only said the sentence.

But he went into the room carrying it.

“If he's in there thinking I—” Yuka begins. ʸ

“Then he's wrong,” Maki says. “And you get to tell him he's wrong. That's the whole job now. Get him out and tell him.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka laughs once, a short wet sound.

“You make it sound simple.” ʸ

“It's simple. It isn't easy.” ᴹᵃᵏ

* * *

Ori comes across the yard with Frieren a few steps behind her.

She has something in her hands. When she gets close enough Yuka can see it is one of the small flat biscuits from the Fort's cupboard, wrapped in a napkin with the corners folded the way Umaru folds them for important deliveries.

Ori holds it out.

“For later,” she says. “For when he comes out. He'll be hungry.” ᴼ

Yuka takes it carefully.

“He will,” she says. ʸ

Ori doesn't leave. She looks at the frame, then at the counter, then at Yuka's face, with the small crease between her eyebrows that means she is working something out and has decided to be brave about the answer.

“Are you sad because you had a fight?” ᴼ

Frieren's hand settles on Ori's shoulder. She doesn't pull her back.

Yuka thinks about lying. It would be easy to say no, sweetheart, I'm just worried. She thinks about who Ori's father is, and what he does with *it's fine*, and what it has cost all of them this morning.

“Sort of,” she says. “I said something badly. I think he heard it wrong. I want to fix it.” ʸ

Ori considers this.

“You can fix it when he's out,” she says. ᴼ

“That's the plan.” ʸ

“Dad listens,” Ori says, with great certainty. “Even when he's sad. He listened to me in the rain.” ᴼ

Yuka has to look away for a moment.

“Yes,” she says. “He does.” ʸ

Frieren looks at her over Ori's head. She doesn't say anything. She doesn't need to. She knows how long a short time can be, and how long a long one is, and she has never once pretended to Yuka that those are the same thing.

* * *

The work at the frame changes a little before the third hour.

Jin-Woo has kept the source answer and the return line separate for so long that Yuka has stopped hearing the words they use for it. Now Ciel tells Rimuru something, and Rimuru says it aloud, and the people at the frame go still for a different reason.

“Second fold,” Rimuru says. “It's holding.” ᴿ

Gojo lets out a breath.

“How many folds are there?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“She doesn't know yet,” Rimuru says. “But this one stayed open when we stopped pushing.” ᴿ

Yuka stands up.

“Can something go through?” ʸ

They try. Shoko takes a scrap of ordinary paper, a corner torn from Fern's register, and Yuta lays it against the place where the second fold holds. It sits there. Nothing takes it. Nothing refuses it either. It simply doesn't go anywhere.

“Not yet,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶ

Yuka puts her hand back over her pocket.

Not yet. She has heard worse words today.

* * *

When the counter passes forty-five days, she takes the paper out again and unfolds it.

*I love you. I'm staying. Come home so we can finish talking.*

She reads it once. She thinks about adding something under it. She has the pencil in her hand.

Then she folds it back along the same soft creases.

Whatever she needs to say next, she is not going to say it to a piece of paper. She is going to say it to his face, with his hand in hers, and she is going to make him stay in the room until he has heard every word.

She puts the paper away and goes to ask Gojo which part of the frame she can hold.
