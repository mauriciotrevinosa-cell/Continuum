# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 70
## Chapter 16 — Something to Eat

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 70
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S11E61
**Continuity state:** Local days 52–70 (outside ~3h28m–4h40m). Right forearm shattered on day 50, set by hand against a splint of shop camping poles and knit by constrained own RCT; heals shortened and crooked, usable but weak by day 70. Left wrist, thigh/hip, shoulder and back injuries persist. Eating is slow and painful; Authority used once to lift a bowl at real cost. Memory of the hand dragon with Yuka (V64 #2) first comforts him, then begins to turn. First rehearsal of what to tell her. Noise reminds him of her actual words. No new technique, unit, healer or outside contact.

*When: Day fifty-two. Mau has been lying under the same overhang for two days, and outside a little under three and a half hours have gone by.*

He has to set it.

He has known since the first night. He has been putting it off because the arm lies quiet as long as he doesn't touch it, and because the only hand he has left to set it with is the left one, with its stiff fingers and the wrist that has not been right since the first room.

RCT has closed the two breaks in the skin and kept the swelling from cutting off the blood. It cannot put the pieces back where they belong. It doesn't know where they belong. It only knows how to knit what is touching.

If he leaves it, the arm will heal the way it is lying.

“All right,” he says. ᴹ

*Shop,* Noise says. ᴺ

He opens the shop.

He reads the camping goods slowly, looking for anything that is long and straight and stiff. There are tent poles: light ones, in sections. There is cord. There is the plain cloth he has been buying since the first day, which he can tear into strips with his teeth.

He buys two poles, cord and cloth. The balance changes. The things arrive beside him on the ledge.

He looks at them for a while.

Then he puts the end of the cord in his mouth.

* * *

He does it in pieces, because there is no other way to do it.

He wedges his right hand against a knob of stone and leans his weight back, slowly, pulling the arm out to its length. He uses the left hand to press along the forearm, feeling for the places where the bone has stepped out of line. Each time he finds one he pushes, and the pushing goes through him like a nail.

He doesn't scream. He thought he would. Instead he makes small sounds, through his teeth, over and over, the noise of somebody trying to lift something much too heavy.

When he thinks the line is as close to straight as his hand can feel, he lays the poles along it, top and bottom, and binds them with the cloth and the cord. Teeth and left hand. The knots are bad. They hold.

Then he lies back and lets RCT have the arm.

It is slow. He feels each piece take hold of its neighbour, a little at a time, through the narrow pipe the field allows. He gives it everything he can spare, which is not much, and then a little more than he can spare, until Noise tells him to stop.

*Enough. Sleep.* ᴺ

“I'm not tired.” ᴹ

*Lie.* ᴺ

He almost smiles.

He sleeps.

* * *

The hunger comes back on the fifty-fourth day.

He hadn't noticed it go. Pain has a way of taking up all the room in a body. But he wakes on the third morning with his stomach clenched like a fist and understands that he hasn't eaten since before the fall.

The bread in his pack has been crushed under him and is soaked through at one end. He doesn't trust it. He opens the shop instead and buys soup: a plain thick broth in a covered bowl, still warm when it arrives.

It sits on the ledge beside him.

He looks at it.

His right arm is bound to its poles and hurts if he so much as thinks about moving the fingers. His left wrist will hold a weight for a moment, then fold. The bowl is a hand's width away.

He reaches with the left hand, gets it under the rim, and lifts.

The wrist gives.

The bowl tips, and the soup goes over the ledge in a long brown ribbon, and he watches it fall into the rain and vanish.

He doesn't say anything.

After a while he buys another one.

* * *

The second bowl he doesn't try to lift.

He brings his face down to it instead. He turns onto his side, carefully, the bound arm held against his chest, the bad hip grinding, until his mouth is at the rim. He drinks like that, lying on the ledge in the dim grey light, like an animal at a puddle.

It is hot. It burns his lip. He drinks anyway.

Swallowing pulls at the ribs that haven't been right since the hall. Each mouthful goes down past a band of pain, and after the fifth his stomach turns over, all at once, and everything comes back up onto the stone.

He lies with his cheek next to it for a long time.

The rain patters beyond the overhang.

*Again,* Noise says. *Slow.* ᴺ

“I know.” ᴹ

He buys a third bowl. The balance changes. He waits until his stomach has stopped shaking before he tries.

This time he uses Authority.

It is a stupid thing to spend it on. Every use costs, and costs more than it used to, and he has nothing to spare. But he can't go on lying in his own sick drinking off the floor. He lifts the bowl to his mouth with the grip, and holds it there, and feels the field take its share, and drinks one small mouthful at a time, waiting between each one until the ribs have settled.

He finishes it.

When he lowers the bowl his hand is shaking from the cost and he is crying, very quietly, without having noticed when he started. It is not the pain, exactly. It is that he has just been very brave about some soup.

“Don't tell anyone,” he says. ᴹ

*Who?* Noise asks. ᴺ

That makes him laugh, which makes his ribs hurt, which makes him laugh harder.

* * *

On the sixtieth day the arm is knitting, and he has nothing to do but wait for it, and his mind goes where it has been trying to go since the fall.

There is a little light in the shaft. It comes from nowhere he can find, a grey even glow on the walls. Lying under the overhang he can see the shadow of his own left hand on the stone when he lifts it.

He lifts it.

He tries to make the head of the dragon.

Thumb bent for the mouth. Two fingers for the horns. His fingers won't go. Two of them never close the way they should, and the wrist won't hold the angle, and the shadow on the wall is only a hand, a tired hand, shaking.

He remembers the afternoon anyway.

The light across the bench, wide enough for a hand. Yuka leaning across him to see the shape on the wall. *That's not a dragon.* Her first wing too high and the second too close, and the third one right. The ridiculous noise he made for it, and the way she laughed so hard the wing went flying over his head. *Can we do that again?* The second wing she gave it, a little crooked. Her head against him while the two of them let it stay exactly where it was.

He holds onto that for a while.

It helps. For an hour, maybe more, it helps.

Then, without his asking it to, the memory turns a little in his hands, and he sees it from another side.

She was so happy that afternoon.

That was before. Before the clinic, before the secret, before the finger in the kitchen and the look on her face when he smiled at her. Before she had to say what she said.

He lowers his hand.

* * *

He doesn't sleep much that night.

Somewhere in the dark he catches himself practising. He doesn't decide to. It happens the way it used to happen before a hard conversation at home: words lining up in his mouth, testing themselves.

*I heard you.* That's how it starts.

*I heard you, and you were right. I kept going where you couldn't come.*

That one is all right. He goes over it a few times.

Then another one arrives, and he doesn't like it, and it stays anyway.

*You don't have to stay because of what happened.*

He says it aloud, very softly, to see how it sounds.

“You don't have to stay because of what happened.” ᴹ

It sounds kind. That is what frightens him. It sounds like something he could actually say, if he came home looking like this, and she looked at him the way she looked at him over the cut finger.

*Love,* Noise says. ᴺ

He goes still.

*She said love,* Noise says. *After. "I love you." "Come find me."* ᴺ

It has kept the words exactly. It gives them back to him in her voice, which it remembers, the way it remembers everything he has ever let it see. *I love you. Come find me when you're done.*

Mau closes his eyes.

“She'd say that anyway,” he says. ᴹ

*Why?* ᴺ

“Because she's kind. Because I was going somewhere. You don't send someone off with the other thing.” ᴹ

Noise doesn't answer.

He can feel it turn the words over, looking for the place where his reasoning is wrong. It doesn't find one fast enough. He falls asleep before it does.

* * *

On the seventieth day he takes the poles off.

The arm is shorter than the other one. Not by much. He can see it when he holds them side by side: a slight bend below the elbow, a thickening where the pieces met at an angle the bone will remember for the rest of his life. When he closes the fingers, all of them move now. The grip is weak. It is a grip.

He draws the right dagger and holds it.

It sits wrong in his hand. He shifts it until it sits less wrong, and holds it out, and the arm trembles, and holds.

He stands up.

The shaft goes on down below him into the grey. The rain falls on it, gentle and steady. He has two weak hands, one bad leg and a back that tells him every morning exactly how it broke.

He shoulders the pack.

*Home,* Noise says. ᴺ

It's what it always says, when he gets up.

Mau checks the first ledge below him with his eyes, then with his boot. He raises the neutral and steps down into the rain.

He doesn't answer.
