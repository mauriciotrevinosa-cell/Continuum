## Chapter 19 — The Evening Shift

“You'll eat the rest at the next change,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

* * *

The modifier is at four.

It got there in steps, the way it has done everything today. Two and a half in the afternoon, three, then three and a half while Jin-Woo was halfway through a sentence, then four, a little after the lamps were lit. Each time the line rewrote itself, someone wrote the time down. Ciel has the whole list now. Rimuru reads it out when anyone asks, and sometimes when nobody does, because saying it is better than looking at it.

The gaps are still irregular. Long, then short, then long again.

“She says it's slowed,” Rimuru says, at one point. ᴿ

Everybody looks at him.

“The changes. The last gap was longer.” ᴿ

“Is that good?” Mai asks. She has been sitting on the low wall for an hour with Momo beside her, not doing anything useful, because Fern said that sitting there was useful. ᴹᵃⁱ

Rimuru doesn't answer straight away.

“It means he's taking longer to get used to things,” Gojo says from the frame. ᴳ

He doesn't say the rest. Nobody asks him to.

* * *

Four months.

Yuka does the arithmetic despite herself. A hundred and twenty days is four months. Four months is a season. It is the time between the clinic and the wedding, nearly. It is longer than Ori has been calling him Dad.

He has been in there for a season, and she has been standing out here for an afternoon.

She puts her hand over her pocket.

* * *

Yuta is asleep in a chair someone brought out from the common room. He fell asleep holding the return line and Gojo took it out of his hands without waking him and has been holding it since, one-handed, his other hand flat against the frame.

Jin-Woo tries the third fold again.

They have been at it for two hours. The second fold still holds, a little open space in the structure that stays when nobody pushes. The third is behind it, and the third does not want to be found. Every time Jin-Woo and Ciel reach the place where the source answer should open it, the structure turns the question aside, gently, like a hand moving a child's away from a stove.

This time it doesn't only turn aside. It closes.

Jin-Woo takes his hand off the surface.

He doesn't swear. He stands there with his jaw tight, looking at the frame as though it has personally insulted his family. Then Hae-In is beside him with a cup and puts it into the hand that has just failed, and he looks down at her, and something in his shoulders lets go a little.

“Not that way,” he says. ᴶ

“So another way,” Hae-In says. ᴴᶦ

He drinks.

* * *

Ori is supposed to have gone to bed an hour ago.

She is sitting on the step of the common room in her nightclothes, wrapped in a blanket, with Frieren beside her not saying anything about bedtime. They both look at the frame from a distance. Ori has her knees up and her chin on them.

When Yuka comes to the step for a moment, because she can't look at the counter any more, Ori moves over to make room.

“Is it still daytime in there?” Ori asks. ᴼ

Yuka sits.

“I don't know,” she says. “It's been a lot of days.” ʸ

“How many?” ᴼ

Yuka looks at Frieren. Frieren looks back and doesn't make the choice for her.

“About a hundred and twenty.” ʸ

Ori thinks about this. She is good at numbers. Yuka watches her get there.

“That's more than the whole time we were in the purple place,” Ori says. ᴼ

“Yes.” ʸ

“A lot more.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ʸ

Ori pulls the blanket tighter.

“He'll be so tired,” she says. ᴼ

Yuka puts her arm around her, and Ori leans into it, and they sit like that until Frieren says, very gently, that a person who is going to help tomorrow needs to have slept tonight.

Ori goes. At the door she turns back.

“You still have the biscuit?” ᴼ

Yuka takes it out of her pocket, napkin and all, and shows her.

Ori nods, satisfied, and lets her mother take her inside.

* * *

DIO comes out a little after that, with Ashborn.

He has been back in his room for most of the evening, between questions. He has answered three more times when Jin-Woo needed the source to speak, and each time he has gone back to his window afterwards, and nobody has asked him to stay. Now he comes out on his own and stands by the frame, looking at the line that says four.

“May I see the list?” he asks. ᴬˢ

Rimuru reads it to him. Ashborn listens to every time and every number. When it is finished he is quiet for so long that DIO puts a hand on his arm.

“He is not trying to kill him,” Ashborn says. ᴬˢ

Everybody near the frame goes still.

“The maker,” Ashborn says. “The one who built this. I spoke with him once, when he still thought I would take what he had made.” ᴬˢ

Yuka is on her feet. She doesn't remember standing.

“Then what is he doing?” ʸ

Ashborn looks at her. He is very old. Older than the house, older than anything in it except perhaps the woman at his side. He looks at Yuka the way he looked at the window the first day Mau opened it for him, as though he is trying to see something clearly without hurting it.

“He wants a vessel,” Ashborn says. “He made one for me, and I would not take it, and the boy was standing inside it instead. A vessel with someone already living in it is no use to him.” ᴬˢ

“So he wants Mau out.” ʸ

“He wants the boy to stop wanting to be there.” ᴬˢ

Nobody says anything.

“This,” Ashborn says, and he lifts one hand towards the counter, the modifier, the list of changes, “is how you make someone tired. You let him learn and you take it away. You let him rest and you make the rest cost. You do it until he would rather stop. Not die. Stop. Then the place is empty, and something else can stand up in it.” ᴬˢ

* * *

Gojo has turned from the frame.

Yuta is awake now, sitting forward in his chair, his hands on his knees.

“Then he has to want to come home,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

“Yes,” Ashborn says. ᴬˢ

Yuka hears it. She hears exactly what it means, and her whole body goes cold.

She thinks about the kitchen. The cut finger. The sentence. *I don't know how to stay in this.* She thinks about the way he said *I understand*, and held her hand, and went to the inspection.

If he is in there thinking she doesn't want him to come home.

If that is the thing he is carrying through a season of broken bones.

“He'll want to come home,” she says. ʸ

Her voice is perfectly steady. Everyone around the frame hears it, and she sees some of them believe it, and she makes herself keep her chin up until they have looked away.

Then she goes to the far end of the low wall, where the lamplight doesn't reach, and sits down, and takes the folded paper out of her pocket, and holds it in both hands.

She doesn't open it.

She sits there with it while the counter goes past a hundred and forty days, and a hundred and fifty, and night comes down properly over the yard.

Behind her, at the frame, Gojo goes back to holding the line.
