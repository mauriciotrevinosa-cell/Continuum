# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 70
## Chapter 20 — The Balance

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 70
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S11E63
**Continuity state:** Local days 110–150 (outside ~7h20m–10h). Sector of a pack of constructs that learn: each time Mau finds a pattern that wins, the pack begins answering that pattern. Injuries: bitten left calf, torn scalp reopened, a finger on the right hand dislocated and set crooked. Mau notices the shop balance for the first time as a finite number and starts rationing food and cloth. At the end he hears something enormous far below. Volume closes. No new technique or unit; 6,616 bonds.

*When: Day one hundred and twelve. The pack finds Mau two days after the room with the bed; outside, the lamps are being lit.*

There are nine of them.

He counts them by their energy before they come out of the dark: nine small knots of supply moving low along the floor, spread wide, the way dogs spread when they mean to surround something. When they reach the edge of the light his eyes make for him, he sees they are shaped something like dogs too. Long, lean bodies of jointed grey material. Heads that are mostly mouth.

He puts his back to a wall.

That is the first thing he learns: the wall. With stone behind him they can only come from three sides, and three sides he can watch.

The first one comes in low and he meets it with the right dagger, weak arm and all, driving the point up under the jaw. The second comes high and Authority turns it into the wall. The third gets his calf.

Teeth go in and pull. He feels muscle tear.

He kills it with the left dagger and doesn't let himself scream until the other six have backed off to circle again.

* * *

He fights them for four days.

They don't come all at once. They come in threes, testing, and when he hurts one badly the others drag it away into the dark, and he hears it, later, out of reach, being put back together. He sleeps in snatches with his back to whatever wall he has. He eats with one eye on the dark.

By the fifth day he has it.

Wall behind him. Neutral up only in the instant one commits, so the others can't use the cost of holding it to tire him out. Right dagger low for the throat. Authority for the one that jumps. Left dagger for whichever is still coming. Over and over, in that order. He kills three that day and they don't get put back together.

He sits against the wall afterwards, shaking, and feels it settle into him: the order, the timing, the way a thing becomes his.

He knows what comes next. He has known since the hall.

He waits for the change.

* * *

The change is that they learn him.

He doesn't understand it at first. The next three come in exactly as before, low, high, low. He goes right dagger, Authority, left dagger.

The first one isn't where the right dagger goes.

It has stopped short. It has watched the others die at that exact distance, at that exact angle, and it has stopped a hand's width out of reach and waited for the point to pass. The second one doesn't jump. It drops flat under where Authority would have caught it and comes up inside his guard.

He gets the neutral up late.

Not late enough to die. Late enough for its teeth to close on the edge of his right hand, and for one finger to go sideways in a way fingers don't go.

He kills it. He kills the other two. He sits with his back against the wall and holds the finger, which is pointing at the ceiling, and makes a noise like a kettle.

*Learned you,* Noise says. ᴺ

“I noticed,” Mau says, through his teeth. ᴹ

He takes the finger in his left hand and puts it back.

He doesn't look while he does it. It goes back with a sound he will hear in his sleep. It goes back slightly wrong, and stays wrong, and he binds it to the next finger with cloth and teeth and calls it done.

* * *

After that, he can't keep a pattern.

Every time something works, he gets one more day of it, maybe two, before the pack starts answering it. So he has to keep changing. Wall, then not wall. Dagger first, then Authority first. Neutral held, then dropped. He has to fight as though he has never fought them before, every time, with nothing settled underneath him to stand on.

It is exhausting in a way the broken bones were not.

Learning is the thing he has always rested on. In the hall it was a trap, but at least while it lasted it was solid. Now it isn't even that. Every time he finds his feet, they take the floor.

He kills the last of the nine on the hundred and twenty-sixth day. It takes him a long time, and he doesn't do it well.

* * *

On the hundred and thirtieth day, he opens the shop to buy bread, and looks at the number at the top.

He has always known it was there. The balance: the number that goes down when he buys something. He has looked at it a hundred times since the first room and never once really seen it.

He sees it now.

It is not small. But he does the arithmetic the way he used to do it for the Fort's biscuit budget, and he does it again, more slowly, with the prices of bread and water and cloth and the tent poles and the boot, and three bowls of soup because he spilled the first and threw up the second.

He doesn't know how long he is going to be in here.

If it is another hundred days, he is fine. If it is three hundred, he is not.

Nobody outside can put anything into this account. The field won't let them. Every coin in it is a coin he had when he walked through the door, and every coin he spends is gone.

He closes the shop without buying the bread.

* * *

He starts rationing that day.

Half a loaf instead of a whole one. Cloth washed in the flask water and wrung out and used again instead of torn fresh. He doesn't buy another boot when the bound one splits at the side; he binds it again with the cord from the old splint.

He is hungry all the time now. It sits under everything else, a dull ache behind his ribs that the ribs themselves don't need.

“It's fine,” he tells Noise, on the hundred and thirty-fifth day, when it asks about food. ᴹ

*Not fine,* Noise says. ᴺ

“It's enough.” ᴹ

*Not enough.* ᴺ

“Then it's what we've got,” Mau says. ᴹ

He hears how it sounds as he says it. It sounds like him at home, smiling at somebody over a cut finger. *It's fine.*

He doesn't take it back.

* * *

The calf heals into a hard knot that pulls when he walks. The scalp reopens when a crawler drops on his head in a narrow passage and he has to close it again. The finger stays crooked. He stops binding it after a week, and learns to hold the dagger around it.

He is getting thin. He can see it in his wrists when he lifts them in front of the light his eyes make. The bones look closer to the surface than they ought to be.

On the hundred and fiftieth day he comes to the edge of something large.

The passage opens onto a ledge, and beyond the ledge is space, more space than he has seen since the shaft. His eyes show him nothing in it. Far below, much farther than the shaft went, there is a faint warmth on his face, as though somewhere down there something is breathing out.

He stands at the edge and listens.

The sound comes up out of the dark after a long time. Low enough that he feels it in his chest more than hears it. Long enough that he has time to take three breaths before it ends.

Something down there is very large, and it is awake.

Mau stands at the edge of the ledge with his crooked hands and his crooked foot and his half a loaf of bread, and looks down into the dark where it is.

*Big,* Noise says. ᴺ

“Yes,” Mau says. ᴹ

He sits down with his back to the wall and eats the half loaf, all of it, slowly, because whatever is down there, he is going to need it.

Then he gets up and starts looking for the way down.
