# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 1 — Something Breathing Below

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S11E63
**Continuity state:** Local days 150–153 (outside ~10h–10h12m). A dragon-shaped construct of the Architect's field, not a canon named dragon, in a vast lower cavern. Mau, starving and crooked (right forearm short and weak, crooked finger, left wrist, crushed foot, ribs, back), fights with daggers, Authority, sequential Dismantle/Cleave, Zoltraak, flight and neutral. He learns to read the breath by the gathering of energy in the throat; the field hides the gather; he learns heat on the skin; the margin moves and a claw opens his abdomen. He keeps fighting and kills it by cutting the line at the base of the neck. No Purple or domain completed; one attempted Purple collapses under field restriction. No new technique or unit.

*When: Day one hundred and fifty-two. Mau reaches the floor of the great cavern after two days of climbing down; outside it is not yet ten at night.*

He sees it before it sees him.

His eyes find it in the dark: a shape larger than any building in the settlement, coiled on the cavern floor like a cat in front of a fire. The energy inside it is not a knot like the crawlers or the pack. It is a furnace. It runs along the length of the body in great slow channels and gathers at the throat, where it banks and glows and settles again with each breath.

Four legs. A long neck. Wings folded along its back, ragged at the edges, built more for show than for flying. The Architect has made it to look like a dragon, the way he made the first guardian to look like a soldier.

Mau stands at the bottom of the last ledge with his pack on his back and his crooked hands at his sides.

He thinks about Kaisel. Warm in the sun in the yard, one wing stretched out so its shadow reached the path. Ori's hand on his face.

“Not like you,” he says, very quietly. ᴹ

*Big,* Noise says again. ᴺ

“Yes. Thank you.” ᴹ

The head lifts.

* * *

The first breath nearly kills him.

He sees it coming. That's the only reason he lives. The furnace in the throat swells and brightens, the channels along the body pull inward towards it, and he is already moving when the jaws open, sideways and up, Authority on the cavern wall to haul himself along it faster than his foot can run.

Fire fills the place where he was standing.

Not fire. Something hotter than fire, white at the centre, a straight line of heat that turns the floor beneath it to glass. The edge of it passes him at the distance of a long arm. His neutral is up and it stops the heat from touching him and the field leans on the hold so hard that his teeth ache.

The stone where he stood is glowing.

He hangs from the wall by a grip of Authority and his own weak left hand, breathing, looking at it.

“All right,” he says. ᴹ

He drops to the floor and runs.

* * *

He learns it.

It takes most of the first day. The dragon is not fast, for its size, but its size is the problem. A turn of its body sweeps half the cavern. Its tail comes round behind it like a falling tree. When it brings a foreleg down, the floor jumps under him and his crushed foot sends a white line of pain up through his spine.

But the breath has a shape. The furnace swells, the channels draw in, the throat brightens. He can see it every time. He counts it. Three heartbeats from the first swell to the release. Then a long pause while the furnace banks and rebuilds.

Three heartbeats is enough time to get out of a straight line.

By evening, his own evening, the one his body keeps, he has got the dragon to breathe nine times and has not been touched by any of them. He has cut it twice, short bounded Dismantles at the joint of a foreleg, small lines that open the grey hide and let something dark leak out. It is not much. The field takes most of what he puts into the cut before the cut arrives.

He sleeps for an hour in a crack in the wall that is too narrow for anything but the tip of a claw, and the dragon's claw comes looking for him, and scrapes, and goes away.

When he wakes he knows the breath completely.

He lies in the crack and waits for it to change.

* * *

The next breath has no swell.

He is crossing the open floor towards the foreleg he has been working on. His eyes are on the throat. The furnace is banked, low, quiet: the long pause. He has three heartbeats at least, and then three more.

The heat hits him from the side.

There was no gather. The furnace never brightened. The field has done something to it, or to his eyes, so that the line of white comes out of a throat that was dark a moment before, with no warning at all.

The neutral comes up late.

It comes up late the way everything has come up late since the hall, a stretched breath between asking and having, and for that breath the edge of the heat is on his left side.

He feels his skin go.

He doesn't scream. There isn't time. He is on the ground, rolling, the neutral closing over him at last, the floor around him glowing, and his left side from the ribs to the hip is a single sheet of pain so complete that for a moment he can't tell where it ends.

The dragon turns towards him.

He gets up.

* * *

He doesn't know how. Afterwards, he won't remember getting up. He only knows that there is a stretch of time in which he is on his feet, moving, with the left side of his shirt fused to the skin underneath it and the smell of it in his nose, and he is thinking more clearly than he has thought in days.

The breath has no tell now. Fine.

It still has heat.

When the furnace wakes, before the release, the air near the dragon's mouth gets hotter. His burned skin feels it. His good skin feels it. He can't see it but he can feel it on his face, a pressure like standing too close to a stove.

He learns to read the heat on his skin.

It costs him. Twice more the breath catches the edge of him while he is learning, once across the back of his right hand and once along his shin. Each time the neutral answers a little later than it did before, and each time the field takes a little more for holding it.

By the third day, he has it again.

He knows, now, what comes after that.

* * *

The change is small.

It is always small. That's what makes it cruel. He is under the dragon's chest, which is where he has decided he must be. The base of the neck is where the channels of the furnace all come together; he has watched them for three days. If he can cut that line, the furnace goes out.

He goes in under the belly with the neutral half raised and the right dagger in his weak hand. The claws are where they always are when he does this. He has watched them, the way they turn in towards the chest, a sweep he has dodged forty times.

He dodges it.

He dodges it by exactly as much as he has always dodged it, which is a hand's width.

The claw is a hand's width longer than it was yesterday.

* * *

It doesn't hurt at first.

He feels the pressure, a pull across the front of his body from the left hip to the bottom of the ribs, as though someone has dragged a rope across him hard. He keeps moving because his body is still moving. He has the dagger. He is under the neck.

Then he looks down.

His shirt is open. So is he.

He understands it the way you understand a word in a language you don't speak well: slowly, one piece at a time, while the rest of you is still trying to do something else. There is a great deal of red. There are things inside the red that should be inside him and are not.

He puts his left hand over it.

The things move under his hand.

His legs go. He doesn't fall. He has Authority on the dragon's own scales, a grip so hard it costs more than anything he has spent since the first room, and he holds himself up by it, hanging under the chest of the thing that has just opened him, his left arm across his belly holding himself in.

*Mau,* Noise says. *Mau. Mau.* ᴺ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is very calm. It surprises him.

“I know. Not yet.” ᴹ

* * *

He tries Purple first.

It is stupid. He knows it is stupid as he does it. But the line at the base of the neck is right above him, and he has one hand free and no time, and Purple is the biggest thing he owns.

He draws Blue on one side and Red on the other and brings them together.

The field eats it.

He feels it happen. The two halves meet and begin to become the thing he wants, and the restriction on everything leaving him closes around them like a fist. Most of it never arrives. What does arrive is a small, ragged flash that scorches the scales above him and does nothing to the line beneath.

The cost of it goes through him like a knife.

The dragon shifts its weight. Above him, the furnace starts to wake: he can feel the heat on his face.

Three heartbeats.

* * *

He doesn't try anything big again.

He takes the dagger in his right hand, the short crooked arm, the finger that won't close properly, and he holds it the way Jin-Woo taught him, around the hand he actually has.

Dismantle, bounded, along the edge of the blade. A small line. The field takes most of it.

He cuts again.

Small. Exact. The line at the base of the neck where the channels meet. The scales part a little. He cuts again. Again. His left arm is across his belly holding everything in, and the Authority grip on the scales is burning through him, and the heat on his face is growing.

Two heartbeats.

He puts Zoltraak into the opened scales, into the place under them where the channels meet. A thin bright line of mana. The field takes its share of that too, but the line is thin, and it only has to travel the length of a finger.

The channel breaks.

* * *

The furnace goes out.

He feels it go: the heat on his face snuffed like a candle, the great slow river of energy along the body suddenly running nowhere. The dragon makes a sound that shakes the cavern. Its legs fold.

He lets go of the scales.

He falls a short way and lands on his back on the stone, his left arm still across himself, and the dragon comes down. Not on him. Beside him, so close that the side of its jaw rests against his shoulder, warm and heavy, like a dog that has lain down next to its master.

It doesn't move again.

Mau lies on his back in the dark beside it.

He is holding himself together with one hand.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

*Here.* ᴺ

“I need you,” he says. “I need you to tell me where everything goes.” ᴹ

He can hear it, how calm his voice is. He doesn't know where the calm is coming from. He thinks it is probably the part of him that knows exactly how much trouble he is in.

*Yes,* Noise says. ᴺ

Mau closes his eyes for one breath.

Then he opens them, and lifts his hand away, and looks.
