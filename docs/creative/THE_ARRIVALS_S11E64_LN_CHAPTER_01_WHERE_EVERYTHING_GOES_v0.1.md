# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 2 — Where Everything Goes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S11E64
**Continuity state:** Local days 153–165 (outside ~10h12m–11h). Immediately after the dragon. Mau's abdomen is open from left hip to lower ribs with organs exposed; left side burned from ribs to hip, burns on right hand and shin. Noise compares his body against retained records and guides placement; Mau puts his organs back with his own hands and closes the wall in stages with constrained RCT, near losing consciousness repeatedly. Diablo surface cleaning works only on cloth and skin surface, not inside the wound. No healer, no reset. Dragon corpse remains beside him. He calls for Shoko and speaks to Yuka in his head. No new technique; bonds 6,616.

*When: Day one hundred and fifty-three, the first hour after the dragon falls. Outside, it is a little past ten at night.*

He has seen inside a body before.

In the clinic, through Shoko's hands, he has watched Yuka's injury knit under a light he could only help supply. On the quarry floor he saw what Sukuna's cuts did to the soldiers who stood between them. He has watched Beru work on his back without being allowed to look over his own shoulder.

He has never looked inside his own.

His eyes show him the energy first, because that is what they are for. The faint warm lines of his body's supply, running where they ought to run, and then not. Places where the lines go out past the edge of him into the open air and loop there, confused, looking for a way back.

Then he looks with the ordinary part of his eyes.

He makes himself keep looking.

* * *

*Here,* Noise says. *First.* ᴺ

It gives him a picture.

Not a memory of anyone else. His own body, the way Noise has recorded it a thousand times: on the clinic table, in the yard, standing in front of a mirror shaving with his crooked hand. Where everything was. Where everything is supposed to be. It lays the picture over what he can see, very faintly, a pale shape on top of a dark, wet one.

The two don't match.

“Okay,” Mau says. ᴹ

His voice is still calm. He holds onto it.

“Okay. Show me the worst.” ᴹ

Noise shows him.

* * *

There is one vessel that matters more than the others, opened at the edge, bleeding steadily into everything around it. He finds it with the pale picture and his eyes together. He presses two fingers of his left hand against it, the stiff ones, the ones that never close, and they turn out to be good for this, rigid and steady.

He sends RCT down through them.

The trickle goes in. It takes hold of the edges and tries to make them meet. It is so slow. He watches the blood slow, and slow, and he doesn't let himself feel anything about it until it stops.

Then he takes his fingers away and finds the next one.

There are six that matter. He closes them one at a time, and by the fourth one his vision has started to go in from the edges, grey and soft, and he has to stop and breathe and wait for it to come back before he can see where the fifth one is.

*Slow,* Noise says. *Breathe.* ᴺ

“Breathing.” ᴹ

*More.* ᴺ

He breathes more.

* * *

The bleeding stops.

What's left is the part he has been trying not to think about.

Some of what should be inside him is lying on the outside, against his skin and on the stone beside him. Not much. More than he can look at for long. It is warm and it is his and it moves a little on its own, the way it is supposed to move, which is the worst part.

He has to put it back.

He looks at it for a long time, and at his hands, and at the pale picture Noise is holding over everything, showing him where.

“I can't,” he says. ᴹ

It comes out very small.

*Can,* Noise says. ᴺ

“I can't, I don't know how, I'll do it wrong—” ᴹ

*Show you.* ᴺ

“Shoko should be doing this.” ᴹ

He hears himself say it. He hears what it sounds like, the plaintive crack in it, a child asking for a grown-up. He doesn't care.

“Shoko,” he says, louder. “Shoko, please. I need you. I don't know where it goes. Please.” ᴹ

The cavern swallows it. The dragon lies warm and dead against his shoulder. Nobody comes.

He knew nobody would come. He asked anyway.

Then he wipes his face on his good shoulder and puts his hands in.

* * *

He doesn't remember most of it.

That is a mercy. What he remembers is in pieces, the way a fever is remembered afterwards.

His own hands, both of them weak, moving with a care that feels like someone else's. Noise's voice, very close, saying *there* and *no* and *gentle* and *there*. The warmth of it under his fingers. A pain so large it stops being pain and becomes a kind of weather, something that is happening to the cavern rather than to him.

He remembers passing out twice, and waking with his hands still where they were.

He remembers the moment it was all back inside the line of him, and Noise said *yes*, and he started laughing, and had to stop immediately because laughing pulled on everything.

He remembers that the laugh turned into crying, and that the crying hurt too, and that he did it anyway for a long time.

* * *

Then the wall.

The part of him that should hold all of it in is open from his left hip to the bottom of his ribs. He can't stitch it; he has no needle and no thread, and the shop would sell him both, and his hands couldn't use them.

So he closes it the only way he has.

From the bottom up, a finger's width at a time. RCT into the deepest layer first, holding the two sides together with the heel of his left hand while the trickle goes in and makes them remember each other. Then the next finger's width. Then the next. Each one takes as long as boiling water.

He passes out somewhere around the middle. When he wakes, the part he had closed has held, and the part he hadn't is still open, and his hand has fallen away from it.

He puts it back and keeps going.

* * *

It takes two days.

On the second day he burns a little cloth from the shop clean, without meaning to, the stained edge simply not stained any more, and stares at it. He tries it on the burned skin of his side. The surface of the skin comes clean too: the ash, the dried blood, the grit from the cavern floor. Nothing under it changes.

He presses the clean cloth over the closed wound and leaves it there.

*Surface,* Noise says, the way it said it on the floor after the spine. ᴺ

“I know.” ᴹ

He is grateful for it anyway. Something he can do that doesn't hurt.

* * *

When the wall is closed he can't move.

Not won't. Can't. Every part of him has spent everything it had, and his body lies on the stone beside the dragon's jaw and refuses to do anything except breathe. Even his eyes won't stay open for long. He drinks when Noise tells him to, from the flask, a mouthful at a time, without lifting his head.

He doesn't eat. There's no point. He couldn't keep it down.

The dragon is still warm.

He doesn't understand why at first. Then he realizes that something in the great slow channels is still settling, even though the furnace is out: the last of the heat draining out through the body, the way a stove stays warm long after the fire has died.

He moves, a finger's width at a time, until his back is against it.

It's a horrible thing to lie against. He knows that. It tried to kill him and nearly did, and it is the Architect's, and there is a part of him that wants to crawl as far from it as he can.

It's warm.

He lies against it, and closes his eyes.

* * *

He talks to her that night, in his head.

Not calling. He's past calling. He just talks, the way he does sometimes at home when she's asleep beside him and he isn't, telling her things she'll never hear because saying them makes them lighter.

*I fought a dragon,* he tells her. *A big one. You'd hate it. You'd say I should have gone round.* ᴹ

*It opened me up. I put myself back. Noise helped. I did it badly but I did it.* ᴹ

*I'm so tired, Yuka.* ᴹ

He waits, the way you wait for an answer you know isn't coming.

*I keep thinking about the kitchen. About what you said. I keep thinking I should have asked you what you meant. I was scared to. I was scared you'd say it again, properly, and then I'd know.* ᴹ

*If you meant it, it's all right. I want you to know it's all right. I'd understand.* ᴹ

He hears what he has just said.

He doesn't take it back. He lies against the cooling dragon in the dark and lets it stand, the way you let a stone settle in a stream so the water can find its way round.

*I'd understand,* he says again, more quietly, to see how it fits. ᴹ

It fits.

That is the worst thing that happens to him that day, and he has been opened from hip to ribs.

* * *

On the hundred and sixty-fifth day he sits up.

It takes a whole morning. When he gets there, he stays there, propped against the dragon's jaw, looking at his own belly: the long raised line of the closure, angry and ugly and crooked, with a pucker in the middle where he passed out and the two sides met a little wrong.

He puts his hand on it.

It holds.

*Good,* Noise says. ᴺ

“Good,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

He opens the shop. He looks at the balance. He buys the smallest loaf there is, and eats a quarter of it, very slowly, and keeps it down.
