# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 3 — The Months by the Dragon

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S11E64
**Continuity state:** Local days 165–240 (outside ~11h–16h). Recovery beside the dragon construct. Abdominal closure heals puckered and weak, a bulge under it when he coughs; burns on left side heal tight. After he settles into a resting routine in the corpse's warmth, the field lowers the cavern's cold; blankets bought from the dwindling balance. Day ~200 he stores the dragon by Arise as a folded, unmanifestable bond: 6,617. Rehearsals of what to tell Yuka worsen; he stops answering Noise when it reminds him of her real words. He begins describing his injuries to himself as smaller than they are. No healer, reset or outside contact.

*When: Day one hundred and seventy. Mau stands for the first time since the dragon; outside it is about eleven at night.*

He stands for eleven breaths.

He counts them. On the twelfth, something under the closure pulls in a way that makes his vision go white, and he sits down against the dragon's jaw before he falls down.

“Eleven,” he says. ᴹ

*Good,* Noise says. ᴺ

“It's not good. It's eleven.” ᴹ

*Was zero.* ᴺ

He can't argue with that. He doesn't try.

* * *

The days by the dragon go slowly, and then they go fast, and then he can't tell.

He builds a place. That's the only word for it. The space between the dragon's foreleg and its jaw is sheltered on three sides, and the great body still gives off a little of its old heat, and he makes it his. He spreads the blanket from the shop. He sets the flask and the pack where his hand falls. He cuts a shallow notch in a scale with the point of a dagger every morning when he wakes, the way he marked the wall of the hall.

Twelve notches. Twenty. Thirty.

The closure on his belly heals the way everything heals in here: crooked, and slowly, and only as far as the trickle can carry it. The pucker in the middle stays puckered. When he coughs, something underneath it pushes out against the skin, a soft round swelling the size of an egg, and then goes back.

He looks at it the first time it happens for a long, long while.

*Weak,* Noise says. *Inside.* ᴺ

“It's fine,” Mau says. ᴹ

*Not fine.* ᴺ

“It goes back in.” ᴹ

He presses his hand over the place every time he coughs, after that. It becomes a habit before he notices it is one.

* * *

The burns heal tight.

The skin along his left side pulls when he twists, a stiff glossy sheet from his ribs down to his hip. He can't lift his left arm above his shoulder without the whole side complaining. He learns to reach for things with his right arm instead, the short crooked one, and to bring them to him in two movements rather than one.

He walks a little more each day. Eleven breaths. Thirty. A hundred steps, slowly, around the dragon's body and back, one hand on its scales.

By the fortieth notch he can walk the whole length of it.

That night, warm in his place between the foreleg and the jaw, with a quarter of a loaf in his stomach and the blanket over him, he catches himself thinking that this is not so bad.

He knows better. He thinks it anyway.

* * *

The cold comes three days later.

He wakes because he is shivering. Not a little. His teeth are clacking together and his hands won't close. The dragon's scale under his cheek, which has been warm every morning for forty-three days, is cold as a stone in winter.

He sits up and puts his hand flat on the body.

Nothing. The last of the heat is gone from it all at once, as though someone had opened a door somewhere and let it out. And the air in the cavern, which has been cool and still the whole time he has been here, has a bite in it now that goes straight through the blanket and his shirt and into the burned skin of his side.

He understands.

He doesn't say it aloud this time. He doesn't need to. Noise knows. He settled. He made a place. He let himself think it wasn't so bad.

So the field took the warmth away.

* * *

He buys a second blanket.

He looks at the balance for a long time before he does it, and then he does it, because the alternative is shaking himself apart, and a body that is shaking can't heal. The number goes down. He wraps both blankets round himself and sits in his place against the cold dragon and still shakes, but less.

He buys a third, a week later.

He doesn't buy a fourth. He sits with three and his own arms wrapped round himself, and when Noise asks him about it he says he's warm enough.

*Not warm,* Noise says. ᴺ

“Warm enough.” ᴹ

He is getting good at that sentence.

* * *

On the hundredth notch, or what would have been the hundredth if he hadn't missed some mornings, he thinks about Arise.

The dragon's pattern is still there. He can feel it when he lays his hand on the body: the shape of what it was, held in the dead flesh the way a footprint stays in mud after the foot has gone. He could catch it. He knows he could. He did it with the broad one after the spine, and the bond took, and folded away inside him where he can't reach it.

He doesn't know why he wants to.

Not for help. It will never stand up in here. He knows that. Maybe not for anything. Maybe only so that the Architect doesn't get to have made this thing and watched it die and have nothing come of it but a hole in Mau's belly.

Maybe because Kaisel would like the company, someday, when there is a someday.

He puts both hands on the scale.

“Arise,” he says. ᴹ

It catches.

Nothing climbs out of the body. Nothing turns its great head to look at him. A weight settles into the dark below his ribs, beside the others, six thousand six hundred and seventeen now, folded up small where he can feel it and can't call it.

“Later,” he tells it. ᴹ

He doesn't know if that's a promise or a lie.

* * *

He practises what he'll say to her.

He does it every day now. He doesn't decide to. It happens while he walks his slow laps round the cold dragon, the words lining up the way they always do before something hard.

The first version is the one from the shaft. *I heard you. You were right.* He still likes that one. He says it a lot.

Then there is a new one.

*I can sleep in the guest room for a while. If you need space. I won't make it hard.* ᴹ

He tries it out loud, walking. It sounds reasonable. It sounds like something a good husband would offer.

Then, a week later, a newer one.

*Ori can still come to me. You don't have to see me when she does. I'll work it out with Frieren.* ᴹ

He stops walking when he hears himself say that.

He stands with his hand on the dragon's cold side and looks at nothing for a long time.

*Love,* Noise says. *She said love.* ᴺ

He doesn't answer.

*Mau. "I love you." "Come find me."* ᴺ

He starts walking again.

* * *

Noise tries every day after that.

It gives him her voice, the real one, the one it has kept: *I love you. Come find me when you're done.* It gives him the kitchen, not only the sentence but the rest of it, the part where she held his hand, the part where she kissed him. It gives him the evening before, the day he got to have. It gives him things from further back, from the clinic, from the wedding, from a door they built together.

He listens to all of it.

He doesn't say *she'd say that anyway* any more. He doesn't argue. He just doesn't answer, and goes on walking, and after a while Noise stops talking, and they walk together in silence round and round the cold dragon in the dark.

* * *

On a morning he doesn't count, he looks down at his belly while he's dressing and thinks: *it's only a scar.*

He looks at the pucker, and the swelling under it that comes when he coughs, and the stiff glossy burn running down his side, and his crooked arm, and his crooked finger, and the foot that will never bend right.

*It's not that bad,* he thinks. *I've had worse.* ᴹ

He has not had worse. He knows that, somewhere. Somewhere a long way down, under a lot of other things, he knows exactly what his body looks like and what has been done to it.

But it's easier to think it's only a scar.

So he does.

* * *

He leaves on what he thinks is the two hundred and fortieth day.

He has stopped being sure. He has missed notching the scale more often lately, and there are mornings he doesn't remember. He stands at the edge of his place and looks at the dragon: cold, enormous, empty now, the bond folded inside him where its pattern used to be.

He folds the three blankets into the pack. He fills the flask. He looks at the balance and doesn't do the arithmetic.

“Thank you,” he says to the dragon. ᴹ

It's absurd. It tried to kill him. It kept him warm for forty-three days.

He puts his hand on the scale where he cut the first notch, and then he takes it away, and walks into the dark on the far side of the cavern, one hand pressed over his belly, the way he does now without thinking.

*Home,* Noise says, very quietly, as he goes. ᴺ

He doesn't answer.

He hasn't, for a while.
