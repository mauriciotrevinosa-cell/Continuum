# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 4 — After Midnight

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S11E65
**Continuity state:** B+215 late night into B+216 small hours, outside ~13h–17h (local ~195–260 days). A long gap with no changes (while Mau was immobile by the dragon) frightens the house; then the cold change appears. Modifier reaches x5. Beru confirms only that the bond has not released; no news of new bonds reaches the family. Maki makes Yuka lie down; she doesn't sleep. Ori wakes and comes out with Frieren and falls asleep on Yuka's lap. Jin-Woo and Ciel determine the maintenance path will require someone the template recognises to walk it physically, once the third fold opens. No view, vitals, entry or Ruler presence.

*When: The counter passes two hundred days a little before midnight, and nothing on the frame has changed for nearly three hours.*

That is the part that frightens them.

All day the frame has rewritten itself every hour or so. A line here, a number there. Ciel's list has grown long enough that Rimuru reads it from the end now instead of the start. Each change was terrible, and each one meant something was happening in there. Something was being taken away from a man who was still moving.

Since a little after ten, nothing.

The counter runs. The modifier sits where it was. The list does not grow.

“Ciel says it's the longest gap yet,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Nobody asks what it means. Everybody thinks it.

* * *

Yuka asks it in the end, because somebody has to.

“Is he alive?” ʸ

The yard is very quiet. Most of the lamps have burned down. Yuta is asleep in his chair again. Jin-Woo is still at the frame, with Ciel's working record and a cup that went cold an hour ago. Gojo is sitting on the ground with his back against the frame's base and his eyes closed, though Yuka doesn't think he's asleep.

Rimuru listens to Ciel for a long time.

“She can't tell,” he says. “The display doesn't say. The field still names him as the participant. She doesn't know if it would stop if—” ᴿ

He doesn't finish.

Yuka turns.

Beru is standing at the edge of the light, where he has stood most of the night. He has not sat down once. Igris has given up asking him to.

“Beru,” she says. ʸ

The ant looks at her.

“Is he there?” ʸ

Beru's hands close at his sides. For a moment he says nothing, and Yuka understands that he is checking. Not because he doesn't know. Because she asked, and he wants to be sure before he answers her.

“The bond has not released us,” he says. ᴮᵉ

His voice is very low.

“If my king were gone, it would release. All of us would feel it at once. It has not.” ᴮᵉ

Yuka's knees go. She sits down hard on the low wall.

“Thank you,” she says. ʸ

Beru inclines his head, and goes back to standing.

* * *

The frame changes at half past eleven.

**AMBIENT CONDITIONS: ADJUSTED**

It is a line they haven't seen before. Ciel adds it to the list. It sits there, small and plain, meaning nothing they can read.

“So he's moving again,” Gojo says, without opening his eyes. ᴳ

“Or he stopped somewhere,” Yuta says. He is awake after all. ʸᵗ

“And it didn't like that.” ᴳ

Nobody contradicts him. They have all learned the shape of it by now. When he stops being surprised, it hurts him.

Yuka thinks about what it would take for Mau to stop being surprised, after two hundred days. She thinks about a place that would let him stop. She can't picture it. She can only picture him, curled somewhere in the dark, finally resting, and something deciding that rest was a thing it could take away.

* * *

Midnight comes and goes.

It's B+216 now. Fern writes the new date at the top of a fresh page in the register, as though this is an ordinary day that has begun in the ordinary way, and puts the page where everyone can see it. Then she goes to bed, because somebody has to be awake in the morning to run the house, and she has decided it will be her.

The modifier goes to five.

Yuka writes it down. Her handwriting has got worse over the evening.

* * *

Maki makes her lie down at one in the morning.

“I won't sleep,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I didn't say sleep. I said lie down.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Maki has brought out a long chair from the common room and a blanket. She puts the chair where Yuka can see the counter without lifting her head. She doesn't argue. She stands there holding the blanket until Yuka gets into the chair, and then she puts it over her, and then she sits down on the ground beside the chair with her back against it.

“I'll wake you if anything changes,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I won't be asleep.” ʸ

“Then you won't need waking.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka lies there with the blanket up to her chin and watches the counter.

Two hundred and twenty days.

Seven months.

She thinks of seven months ago. He had only just had his birthday. The wedding was still a question he hadn't found the courage to ask. There was no quarry yet, no white in his hair. It was so long ago. It was nothing. For her, it's been this afternoon and this evening and a little of the night.

For him it's been seven months since he kissed her in the kitchen and went to look at a door.

* * *

At some point the step of the common room creaks.

Yuka turns her head.

Ori is in the doorway in her nightclothes, the blanket from earlier wrapped round her twice, her hair flat on one side from the pillow. Frieren is behind her, one hand on her shoulder, looking at Yuka over her head with an expression that says she tried, and lost, and has come to make sure the losing goes gently.

“I woke up,” Ori says. ᴼ

“I can see that,” Yuka says. ʸ

Ori comes across the yard. She doesn't ask permission. She climbs up onto the long chair beside Yuka, under the blanket, the way she climbs onto Mau's lap when he's sitting outside, and fits herself against Yuka's side.

Frieren sits down on the ground next to Maki.

Neither of them says anything about bedtime.

“Is he still in there?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“Yes,” Yuka says. “Beru says so.” ʸ

Ori nods against her shoulder, as though that settles it.

“Beru would know,” she says. ᴼ

Within a few minutes she is asleep.

Yuka lies very still so as not to wake her. Ori's head is heavy against her arm. Her breathing is slow and even. She is holding a fold of Yuka's sleeve in one fist, the same way she holds Frieren's.

Yuka looks at Frieren over the top of Ori's head.

Frieren looks back. She doesn't say anything. She just reaches up and puts one hand on the edge of the chair, near Yuka's, where Yuka can feel it.

Milim comes back across the yard with two cups. Yuka has seen her make that walk twice since midnight, once with fresh water for Jin-Woo and once to find out whether anyone has learned how to get him through the frame. This time she comes straight to the chair.

“Can you take it without moving her?” Milim asks. ᴹⁱˡ

Yuka gets her free hand under the cup. Her shoulder aches as soon as she lifts it. She hadn't realised how hard she'd been holding herself still.

“I'll watch the numbers,” Milim says. “You can look down for a while.” ᴹⁱˡ

Yuka looks at Ori's sleeping face while she drinks. By the time she's finished, Milim has set the other cup on the ground beside Frieren and taken up a place where she can see the frame.

* * *

At the frame, Jin-Woo straightens.

He has been working with Ciel's record for so long that he moves like a man who has forgotten he has a back. He says something to Rimuru. Rimuru listens to Ciel. They talk for a while in low voices that don't carry.

Then Rimuru comes over to the chair.

He crouches beside it so that he doesn't have to raise his voice and wake Ori.

“We know what the third fold wants,” he says. ᴿ

Yuka holds her breath.

“It's not a key. It's a person. The old frame was built to let a monarch walk through it to check on what was built. It wants someone it recognises to actually go down the path. Not a question. Feet.” ᴿ

“Jin-Woo,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Jin-Woo. If we can get the fold open. We're not there yet.” ᴿ

“But you know what it wants.” ʸ

“We know what it wants.” ᴿ

Yuka closes her eyes for a moment.

When she opens them, the counter has gone past two hundred and forty days.

* * *

She doesn't sleep.

She lies on the long chair with Ori asleep against her and Maki and Frieren on the ground beside her, watching the counter run, and somewhere around three in the morning, when the yard is as quiet as it's ever been and the counter has passed two hundred and sixty, she says it.

Very softly. So softly that it barely leaves her mouth.

“Come find me.” ʸ

It's what she said to him in the kitchen. The last thing, before he went to the others.

She doesn't know if he heard that part. She doesn't know if, wherever he is in there, he has kept it, or if he has only kept the other sentence.

She says it again anyway.

“Come find me when you're done.” ʸ

Ori stirs against her, and settles.

The counter runs on.
