# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 5 — A Few Months

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S11E65
**Continuity state:** Local days 240–300 (outside ~16h–20h, B+216 early morning). An open grey plain under a low ceiling where nothing hunts him; wind carries grit priced by the field as hostile contact. He learns to walk the lulls and wrap his face; the field changes the wind when he has learned it. Skin of face, hands and forearms abraded raw; lips cracked. Rationing to a quarter loaf a day; dizziness. He stops marking days without noticing; Noise keeps the exact count. When asked, Mau says "a few months" (it is about ten). First faint awareness of an empty space inside him. No new technique or unit; 6,617 bonds.

*When: Mau thinks it is about the two hundred and fortieth day, and he is right; outside, the house is in the coldest hour before dawn.*

The plain has no walls.

That is the first thing that frightens him about it. For two hundred days there has always been a wall: something at his back, a corner to sleep in, a ledge to stand on. Here the ground runs away flat and grey in every direction until it vanishes, and the ceiling hangs low above it, a lid of dark stone close enough that he feels he could reach up and touch it, and doesn't, in case he can.

Nothing moves on the plain.

His eyes show him nothing. No knots of energy, no channels, no pack spreading wide in the dark. Just flat grey ground and the low grey ceiling and a long way to go.

*Empty,* Noise says. ᴺ

“Good,” Mau says. ᴹ

He doesn't believe it, and he walks out onto it anyway.

* * *

The wind starts after an hour.

It comes from nowhere he can see, low across the ground, carrying a fine grey grit. At first it is nothing: a hiss against his boots, a dust on his trousers. Then it reaches his hands.

It feels like being rubbed with a rasp.

He looks down. The backs of his hands are red where the grit has passed. Not bleeding yet. Raw, the way skin gets when it has been scraped along a stone. Each grain the size of nothing, and each one priced by the field like a thrown pebble.

He pulls his sleeves down over his hands and keeps walking.

By evening, his own evening, his face is raw.

* * *

He learns the wind.

It isn't constant. It comes in long pushes and then drops away into lulls, and in the lulls the grit settles and the plain is silent. He learns to stand with his back to the push and his hands in his armpits and his head down, and to walk in the lulls, fast, as fast as his foot allows.

He tears a strip off one of the blankets and wraps it round his face so that only his eyes show, and then he wraps another strip over his eyes too, loosely, and walks half blind, trusting the faint lines of energy his eyes can still read through the cloth.

His lips crack. He licks them and they crack more. He stops licking them.

By the two hundred and fiftieth day, or thereabouts, he can cross a good stretch of the plain in a single lull.

The wind changes.

It doesn't come in pushes any more. It comes in gusts, short and sudden and out of the lulls, so that the silence he has learned to trust breaks without warning and the grit is in his face before he can turn his back.

He knew it would.

He walks anyway.

* * *

He is so hungry.

He doesn't think about the balance any more. He thinks about bread. He buys a small loaf when the hunger makes the ground tilt, and he cuts it in four with the dagger, and eats one quarter, and wraps the rest. The quarter lasts him most of a day. Sometimes he eats a second quarter before he means to and then doesn't eat at all the next day, to make up for it.

His hands shake. He tells himself it's the cold.

He gets dizzy when he stands up. He tells himself it's the back.

Sometimes, walking in a lull, the grey plain goes soft at the edges and he finds himself sitting down without remembering the decision to sit. He sits until it clears.

*Food,* Noise says, the third time it happens. ᴺ

“I'm fine.” ᴹ

*Not fine. Eat.* ᴺ

“I ate.” ᴹ

*Yesterday.* ᴺ

He doesn't answer that.

He eats a quarter, after a while. Not because Noise asked. Because he notices that his hands won't stop shaking even when he holds them still.

* * *

He stops marking the days.

He doesn't notice when. There's no wall on the plain to scratch, and no dragon's scale, and he tells himself he'll remember, and for a while he does. The two hundred and fiftieth. The two hundred and sixtieth, because the wind changed that morning and he remembered it. After that the days start to run together, grey on grey, walk and stand and walk, and one morning he wakes up on the ground with the cloth over his face and tries to think what day it is and can't.

He lies there for a while trying.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

*Here.* ᴺ

“What day is it?” ᴹ

*Two hundred eighty-one.* ᴺ

Mau lies on his back looking up through the cloth at the low ceiling.

Two hundred and eighty-one.

It sounds like a number somebody else has been counting. He can't feel it. He can feel the plain, and the wind, and the bread, and the quarter left in his pack. He can't feel two hundred and eighty-one days.

“Close enough,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

He talks to himself more, now.

Not to Yuka. He has stopped doing that out loud. To himself, plain practical things, the way a man talks when he lives alone and the silence gets too large. *Lull coming.* *Boot's loose.* *Don't sit, you'll fall asleep.* *Quarter now, quarter tonight.*

On a day that Noise would have been able to name if he'd asked, he hears himself say:

“Been in here a few months now.” ᴹ

He says it casually, the way you'd say it about a job, or a town you'd moved to. A few months.

*Ten,* Noise says. ᴺ

Mau stops walking.

*Ten months,* Noise says. *Almost.* ᴺ

“That's not right.” ᴹ

*Is right. Counted.* ᴺ

He stands in the lull with the cloth over his face and tries to make it true. Ten months. Nearly a year. He thinks back and finds the hall, the rain, the dark, the dragon. Each one feels like a few weeks. Put together, they feel like a long season. They don't feel like a year.

“Felt shorter,” he says. ᴹ

*Wasn't.* ᴺ

He walks on before the next gust.

He doesn't think about it again that day. When he thinks about it the next day, he thinks *a few months*, and it fits better, and he lets it.

* * *

The plain ends without warning.

He is walking in a lull, half blind under the cloth, and his boot comes down on a step that isn't there, and he stumbles forward onto his knees on stone that isn't grey grit but dark and smooth, and the wind is gone.

He pulls the cloth off his eyes.

A wall in front of him. A wall at his back, the edge of the plain, a sheer step down that he has just fallen over. A narrow passage leading into the dark.

He sits with his back against the new wall and breathes. His face stings everywhere the cloth didn't cover. His hands are raw to the wrists. When he touches his lips, his fingers come away with a little blood on them.

He eats a quarter of the bread.

Then, because he has nothing else to do and his body won't let him stand yet, he sits there and listens to himself.

* * *

There is something new.

He doesn't know when it started. He noticed it on the plain, the way you notice a sound you've been hearing for a long time only when it stops. It's not pain. It's not hunger. It's inside him, somewhere under the bonds, under the folded weights of six thousand six hundred and seventeen shadows, under Noise.

A space.

Not empty, exactly. Waiting. Like a room in a house where somebody has moved the furniture out and left the door open, and the air in it is a slightly different temperature from the air in the rest of the house.

He puts his hand on his chest, over the place.

*Feel it?* Noise asks. ᴺ

So it's not only him.

“Yes,” Mau says. ᴹ

*Bigger,* Noise says. *Since the room.* ᴺ

He remembers the warm room with the bed. The lamp that hummed. The voice that said *you are holding something shut*.

He keeps his hand on his chest for a long time.

Then he gets up, very slowly, using the wall, and goes into the passage.
