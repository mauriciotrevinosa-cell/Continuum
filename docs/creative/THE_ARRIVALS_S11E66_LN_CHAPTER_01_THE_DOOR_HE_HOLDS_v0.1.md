# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 6 — The Door He Holds

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S11E66
**Continuity state:** Local day ~300 (outside ~20h, B+216 morning). The Architect speaks a second time, in a narrow passage, and lets Mau feel the empty space inside him as relief: when he leans towards it, the pain recedes. Mau almost lets go. He pulls back because his shadows' bonds would go with the space to whatever stood up in it; the Architect says they would serve better, Mau says they would serve something that doesn't know their names. No naming of the Architect's monarch, no explanation of the Black Heart, no knowledge of private family matters. No new technique; 6,617 bonds.

*When: Mau thinks it is a few months in; Noise knows it is the three hundredth day; outside, the sun is coming up on B+216.*

The passage is too narrow to fall down in.

He discovers this when his knees go. The walls catch his shoulders and hold him up, and he stands there wedged between them with his face against cold stone, waiting for his legs to remember what they're for.

“Rest,” says the Architect. ᴬʳ

Mau closes his eyes.

He's not surprised. He has been feeling the voice coming for days, the way you feel weather.

“I am resting,” he says. ᴹ

“You are leaning on a wall.” ᴬʳ

“It's a good wall.” ᴹ

The voice doesn't laugh. It has never laughed. He doesn't think it knows how.

* * *

“You feel it,” the Architect says. ᴬʳ

He does.

The space under his ribs. The empty room with the door open. It has got bigger since the plain. On the plain it was a draught. Now it is a room he could walk into.

“I feel it,” he says. ᴹ

“Then you know what it is.” ᴬʳ

“You told me. The place you made. With me in it.” ᴹ

“With you in the doorway.” ᴬʳ

Mau opens his eyes. There is nothing to see, only the stone a hand's width from his face.

“You are standing in the doorway of a room built for someone better,” the Architect says. “Holding it shut. It costs you a great deal to hold it. I would like you to see how much.” ᴬʳ

“I don't—” ᴹ

“Lean,” the Architect says. ᴬʳ

* * *

He doesn't mean to.

He doesn't decide anything. He only stops holding, for a moment, the way you stop holding your breath when you didn't know you were holding it. His attention tips, very slightly, towards the open door under his ribs.

The pain goes.

Not all at once. It drains, the way the heat drained out of the dragon. The back first: the hot wire that has run up his spine every morning since the broken floor simply isn't there. Then the foot. The ribs. The tight burned sheet of skin down his side. The pucker on his belly. His hands, raw from the plain.

All of it goes quiet.

He is standing in a narrow passage in the dark, and nothing hurts, and for the first time in three hundred days he isn't hungry, or cold, or afraid.

He makes a sound. He doesn't know what kind. Something between a breath and a sob.

“That,” says the Architect, “is what you are paying.” ᴬʳ

* * *

He stays leaning.

He can't help it. It's so quiet. He can feel the room under his ribs more clearly now that he's leaning into it: large and cool and empty, waiting, with nothing in it but space. Nothing asking him to learn anything. Nothing taking anything away.

He could just go in.

Not die. The Architect was clear about that, in the room with the bed. Not die. Stop. Step back from the doorway and let the door swing open and let the room be what it was built to be. Whatever stood up in it afterwards would be someone else's problem. He wouldn't have to be there to see.

He wouldn't have to go home and watch Yuka's face while she worked out how to tell him.

He leans a little further.

*Mau,* Noise says. ᴺ

Its voice is very far away.

* * *

What stops him is not her.

He will think about that, afterwards, for a long time. That when he was standing in the doorway, with the pain gone and the room open and everything in him wanting to stop, it wasn't Yuka's face that pulled him back. He had already decided, somewhere in the cold by the dragon, that she would be better off.

What stops him is a weight.

Six thousand six hundred and seventeen of them. Folded up below his ribs, beside the open door, where they have always been. He feels them shift as he leans, all together, the way a crowd shifts when the person at the front takes a step.

They are leaning with him.

They are bound to him. To the place. Not to his name, not to his face. To where he is standing. If he steps back out of the doorway and lets the room fill with something else, the bonds won't break. They'll stay right where they are.

And whatever stands up in the room will have them.

Beru. Igris. Kamish, with his deep old voice. Kaisel, who let Ori put her hand on his face. Bellion. The dragon he caught a few months ago, folded small, that has never once stood up. The broad one from after the spine. The forty on the watch at home, and the hundred on the Tempest roads, and every ordinary soldier who has carried a box or stood at a gate or pointed Jin-Woo towards the bends.

They will kneel to it.

Beru will say *my king* to something that has never heard him complain about Gojo.

* * *

He straightens.

It is the hardest thing he has ever done, harder than the floor, harder than putting himself back in by the dragon. He takes his attention out of the doorway one breath at a time, and the pain comes back with each breath.

The back. The foot. The ribs. The burns. The scar. His raw hands.

He is standing in the dark passage again with his face against the stone, shaking, and everything hurts, and he is hungry, and cold, and so afraid that his teeth are chattering.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

The Architect is quiet for a moment.

“You were nearly there,” it says. ᴬʳ

“I know.” ᴹ

“What stopped you?” ᴬʳ

It sounds genuinely interested. That is the worst thing about it. It wants to know, the way it wants to know anything that might improve the design.

“They'd go with it,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Your shadows.” ᴬʳ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“They would serve better,” the Architect says. “They were made for better. You know who held them first. You know what he was.” ᴬʳ

Mau thinks of Beru standing at the edge of the light in the yard. Beru telling him about telenovelas. Beru healing his back with that long cold jealous look at anyone else who tried.

“They'd serve something that doesn't know their names,” he says. ᴹ

“Names are not a specification.” ᴬʳ

“They are to me.” ᴹ

* * *

The voice goes away after that.

He doesn't know how he can tell, since it never seemed to be anywhere. But the passage feels different, emptier, as though somebody who had been standing just out of sight has turned and walked off.

He stays wedged between the walls until his legs stop shaking.

The space under his ribs is still there. He can feel it. It hasn't shrunk because he said no. If anything it feels a little larger, as though the leaning has stretched it, the way a door that has been opened once swings more easily the second time.

He puts his hand over it.

*Mau,* Noise says. ᴺ

It is closer now. Not far away at all.

*You leaned,* it says. ᴺ

“I know.” ᴹ

*I was scared.* ᴺ

Mau stands very still.

In all the time he has known it, Noise has never said that. It has said *here*, and *home*, and *bleeding first*, and *wife*. It has said *love* until he stopped answering. It has never once said what it felt.

“I'm sorry,” he says. ᴹ

His voice cracks.

“I'm sorry. I didn't mean to scare you.” ᴹ

Noise doesn't say anything for a while.

Then:

*Don't lean again.* ᴺ

Mau doesn't promise.

He doesn't lie to it, either. He just stands there in the dark with his hand over the open door, until his legs will carry him, and then he edges sideways along the narrow passage, a step at a time, away from the place where the voice was.
