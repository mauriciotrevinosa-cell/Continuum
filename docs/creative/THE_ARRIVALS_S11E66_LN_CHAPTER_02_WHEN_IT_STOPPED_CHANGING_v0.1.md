# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 7 — When It Stopped Changing

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S11E66
**Continuity state:** Local days ~305–330 (outside ~20h20m–22h, B+216 morning). The bottom. In a quiet round chamber Mau sits down and stops: stops eating, stops learning, stops answering. Because he no longer adapts, the field has nothing to change and goes still. He drifts for about nine days. Noise, for the first time, asks for something for itself: "Stay." It means only staying alive and doesn't resolve Yuka's sentence. Mau eats and gets up out of refusal, not hope. No new technique; 6,617 bonds.

*When: Some days after the narrow passage, Mau comes to a round room with nothing in it; outside it is breakfast time on B+216, and nobody in the yard is eating.*

The room is round, and it is empty, and there is nothing in it that wants to kill him.

He checks anyway. Habit. His eyes show him bare stone walls curving away in the dark, a floor worn smooth in the middle as though something used to turn there, a ceiling too high to see. No energy. No seams. No sound except his own breathing and the scrape of his crooked foot.

There is another doorway on the far side, the way on.

He walks to the middle of the room.

Then he sits down.

* * *

He doesn't decide to.

His legs fold, the way they folded in the narrow passage, only this time there are no walls to catch him, so he goes down to the floor and stays there. Cross-legged at first, then sideways, his cheek against the smooth cool stone.

*Up?* Noise asks. ᴺ

“In a minute.” ᴹ

He lies there.

He is not in pain, especially. No more than usual. The back, the foot, the scar, the burns, the hands. He has stopped noticing which part hurts on which day. It's all just the weather now.

He is not hungry, either, which is strange, because he is always hungry.

He is just tired.

Not tired the way he has been tired for three hundred days, the kind of tired that sleep could touch. Tired the way a stone is tired at the bottom of a river. He lies on the floor of the round room and finds that there is no part of him, anywhere, that wants to get up.

* * *

The first day, Noise asks him to eat.

He doesn't answer. He knows the bread is in the pack. He can see the pack beside him, a hand's width from his face. He doesn't reach for it.

The second day, Noise shows him things.

It does it carefully, the way it did on the walks round the cold dragon. Yuka in the kitchen, the real words, *I love you, come find me*. The day he got to have. Ori calling him Papa on a bench in the courtyard, and then Dad, later, because why be formal. Gojo with chalk all over his trousers. Rem's bread. Beru complaining. The window opening for Ashborn. The wedding. A door they made together.

He watches all of it.

He doesn't feel anything. That's the thing he notices, very distantly, from the bottom of the river. The pictures are clear. He knows they're true. They reach him the way sunlight reaches the bottom of deep water: there, and pale, and no warmth left in it.

*Mau,* Noise says. ᴺ

He closes his eyes.

* * *

On the third day, he notices that nothing has changed.

He has been lying in the round room for three days. In every other place, three days of anything was long enough for the field to notice. Three days of the hall, of the rain, of the dragon's warmth, and something would shift, and he would be hurt.

The round room is exactly as it was.

No sound has gone away. No slab has moved. The air is the same temperature. The floor under his cheek is the same smooth stone.

It takes him a long time to understand, because his thoughts are moving very slowly.

He isn't learning anything.

He's lying on the floor not trying. There's nothing for the field to take away, because he isn't holding anything. It has nothing to change.

He almost laughs.

So this is how you make it stop. You stop.

* * *

He doesn't know how many days he lies there.

Noise knows. He doesn't ask.

He drinks, sometimes, when his mouth gets so dry that it wakes him. He doesn't eat. The pack sits a hand's width from his face. Once, he watches a crawler come in through the far doorway, small and slow, and cross the floor towards him, and stop, and turn round, and go back out, as though even it can see there's no point.

The space under his ribs is very large now.

He can feel it the whole time. The open room with the door swinging. He isn't leaning into it. He promised Noise, sort of. He isn't holding the door shut either. He's just lying beside it, the way he's lying beside the pack, not reaching.

Sometimes he thinks: it would be easy.

Sometimes he thinks: she'd be better off.

Mostly he doesn't think anything.

* * *

Noise talks to him every day.

He hears it from a long way off. It tells him the day. It tells him to drink. It tells him things it remembers. Sometimes it just says his name, over and over, quietly, the way he said hers in the silent dark.

He doesn't answer.

He isn't being cruel. He doesn't have anything to answer with.

On the ninth day, or what Noise says is the ninth day, it stops telling him things.

He notices the silence. It is the first thing he has noticed in days. He lies on the floor of the round room and waits for the next *Mau*, and it doesn't come, and the not-coming goes on for so long that something in him, very small, very far down, begins to be afraid.

“Noise?” he says. ᴹ

His voice is a croak. He hasn't used it in days.

* * *

*Stay,* Noise says. ᴺ

He goes still.

The word goes into him like a needle. He has been carrying it for three hundred days: the kitchen, the cut finger, *I don't know how to stay in this*. He has turned it over so many times that it has worn smooth. He has built every terrible rehearsal around it.

And now Noise has said it, in its own voice, and it means something completely different.

*Stay,* Noise says again. *Please.* ᴺ

“Noise—” ᴹ

*Not for her,* it says. *I know you think. I know. Not for her. For me.* ᴺ

He opens his eyes.

*I want you to stay,* Noise says. *Me. I want. You said I could want things. You said. In the house. You said I could choose a name and it was mine. I chose. I want you to stay.* ᴺ

It is more words than it has ever said to him at once. They come out in pieces, the way its words always do, as though each one has had to be found separately and carried up from somewhere a long way down.

*If you go in the room, I go too,* it says. *I'm in the place. Like them. I don't want to belong to the new one. I want to be Noise. I want you.* ᴺ

* * *

He starts to cry.

He hasn't cried since the dragon. He thought it had gone the way the warmth went out of the pictures. But it comes now, all of it at once, the way the pain came back in the narrow passage, and he lies on the floor of the round room and cries until he can't breathe.

Noise stays with him.

When he can talk again, he says:

“I'm so tired.” ᴹ

*I know.* ᴺ

“I don't want to go on.” ᴹ

*I know.* ᴺ

“I don't think she wants me to come back.” ᴹ

Noise doesn't argue this time. It doesn't give him her words again. Maybe it has learned that he can't hear them. Maybe it has decided that this, right now, isn't the fight.

*I want you to come back,* it says. ᴺ

Mau lies there a long time.

Then he reaches out, a hand's width, and pulls the pack towards him.

* * *

The bread is stale. He has to soak it in water from the flask before he can chew it. He eats a quarter of it, then another quarter, slowly, lying on his side, and his stomach cramps and he waits and keeps it down.

He doesn't feel better. He doesn't feel hope. He doesn't feel anything except the cramp in his stomach and the cold of the floor and the space under his ribs, still open, still large.

But he's eating.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

*Okay?* Noise asks. ᴺ

“For you,” he says. “Okay. For you.” ᴹ

It isn't a promise about anything else. It isn't a reason. It's barely a decision. It's the smallest possible amount of no, said to an empty room and an open door and a voice that once offered him a bed.

It's enough to sit up.

* * *

When he finally stands, the round room changes.

Of course it does. He feels it the instant he's on his feet: the air going colder, a faint grinding somewhere in the walls, something in the floor that wasn't there while he was lying on it. The field has noticed. He has started wanting something again, even if the something is very small, and it has something to take.

He almost smiles.

“There you are,” he says to it. ᴹ

Then he picks up the pack, and puts his hand over the scar on his belly, and limps towards the far doorway before whatever is waking in the walls can finish waking.

*Home?* Noise asks, very carefully. ᴺ

He doesn't answer.

But he doesn't stop walking, either.
