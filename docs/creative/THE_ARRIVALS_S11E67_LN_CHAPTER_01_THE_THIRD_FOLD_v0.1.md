# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 8 — The Third Fold

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S11E67
**Continuity state:** B+216 morning, outside ~20h–23h (local ~300–345 days). Ashborn gives a fresh source answer, Jin-Woo walks the recognised lineage into the third fold, Gojo and Yuta hold it, and it opens onto a path. *When: Fern opens the kitchen at six, and nobody comes in for breakfast; at the frame, the counter is past three hundred days.*

The gap starts a little after four in the morning.

At first nobody notices. They're too tired. The yard has gone grey with the beginning of the light, and the lamps are out, and Rimuru has stopped reading the list aloud because nobody has asked him to in an hour.

Then Ciel says something to him, and he sits up.

“Nothing's changed since four,” he says. ᴿ

Yuka is awake at once. She has been awake all night, but this is a different kind of awake.

“How long is that?” ʸ

“For him?” ᴿ

“For him.” ʸ

Rimuru does the arithmetic, and then doesn't say it, and Gojo says it for him.

“About nine days.” ᴳ

* * *

They have had long gaps before. The one before midnight, when nothing changed for three hours. They know now, from Ashborn, what a gap means. A gap is a stretch of time when Mau isn't getting used to anything. When he isn't learning. When the field has nothing to take.

A gap can mean he's hurt so badly he can't move.

A gap can mean worse.

Yuka is on her feet and across the yard before she knows she is going to do it, and Beru is there, at the edge of the light, where he has been all night, and he turns towards her before she can speak.

“The bond holds,” he says. ᴮᵉ

He doesn't wait for her to ask. He has seen her face.

“Not released,” he says. “Not released. I would know.” ᴮᵉ

Yuka puts both her hands over her mouth and stands there.

Igris, beside him, puts one gauntleted hand on Beru's shoulder. Beru doesn't shake it off.

* * *

Nine days.

What is a man doing for nine days that the field can't touch?

Nobody says it. Gojo is sitting very still with his hand flat on the frame. Yuta has the return line in both hands, so tightly that his knuckles are white. Ashborn has come out of his room again without being asked and is standing beside DIO, watching the counter with his old, patient face, and Yuka thinks she sees something move in it that she has never seen there before.

She thinks she sees him afraid.

At twenty-two minutes past six, the frame changes.

**AMBIENT CONDITIONS: ADJUSTED**

Everybody breathes out at once.

“He's moving,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

His voice cracks on it. He doesn't care.

“He stopped, and he's moving again,” Gojo says. He takes his hand off the frame and covers his eyes with it for a moment. ᴳ

Yuka sits down on the ground where she is standing.

She doesn't know what he was doing for nine days. She will find out, later, and she will wish she hadn't, and she will be glad she did. Right now all she knows is that he stopped, and then he got up.

* * *

The modifier goes to seven at a quarter to eight.

They hardly look at it. They are past looking at numbers now. Jin-Woo is at the frame with Ciel's record and Ashborn beside him, and they have been working at the third fold since before dawn, and something about the way they're standing has changed.

“Again,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶ

Ashborn gives the source answer.

It's the eleventh time. Yuka has counted. Every time, he has given it a little differently, at Ciel's suggestion, at Jin-Woo's. Every time the fold has turned it aside, gently, like a hand moving a child's away from a stove. Every time Ashborn has gone back to his window afterwards and nobody has asked him to stay, and every time he has come back.

This time, Jin-Woo doesn't send a question after it.

He steps forward and puts his hand flat against the surface, and something gathers around his arm, dark at the edges. Not an attack. The shape of what he is. The inheritance, the lineage, the long line of things that made him what the old frame was built to recognise.

He holds it there.

Gojo puts his own hand on the frame beside Jin-Woo's, and Yuka sees the air around it go strange, a narrow, controlled amplification, holding the second fold steady so that it can't close behind the third. Yuta pulls the return line tight.

The surface of the frame goes dark.

* * *

It isn't the dark of the receiving chamber that Mau stepped into yesterday morning, a lifetime ago, a year ago.

It's a path.

Yuka can see it. Everybody can see it. A narrow way going down into the frame, lined with old stone, the kind of stone that has been there longer than anything else in the place. It goes away from them into black, and on the far side of the black there is nothing they can see.

“It's holding,” Rimuru says. “Ciel says it's holding.” ᴿ

Jin-Woo doesn't take his hand away.

“It wants me to walk,” he says. ᴶ

“How many?” Gojo asks. ᴳ

Jin-Woo listens to something nobody else can hear, the frame or the old inheritance or both.

“Me,” he says. “And two.” ᴶ

* * *

Yuka is in front of him before anyone else can move.

“Me,” she says. ʸ

Jin-Woo looks at her.

“I'm going,” Yuka says. “I'm one of the two. I'm going.” ʸ

“Yuka,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“I'm going.” ʸ

She can hear how her voice sounds. She doesn't care. She has stood in this yard for a whole day with a piece of paper in her pocket while he was in there for a year, and now there is a door, and she is going through it.

Gojo stands up.

He doesn't argue with her. He looks at her the way he looked at Mau in the clinic, after Sukuna: the way you look at someone whose pain you can see the exact size of.

“What happens when we find him?” he asks her. ᴳ

Yuka stares at him.

“Think about it,” Gojo says quietly. “Not about getting there. About when we're there.” ᴳ

She thinks about it.

She thinks about Mau, after a year in there, after whatever has been done to him. She thinks about what he will need. Somebody who can fight the thing that built the room. Somebody who can put his body back together while they are still inside it.

Jin-Woo, who the path will open for.

Gojo, who can stand between Mau and anything.

Yuta, who can heal.

She can't heal him. She can't open the path. She could fight beside them, but they would have to spend part of every fight keeping her alive, and the fold only takes two.

“That's not fair,” she says. ʸ

“No,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“That's not *fair*.” ʸ

Her voice breaks. Her eyes are burning. She knows she is shouting and she can't stop.

“I'm his *wife*. I said something to him and he went in there carrying it and I have to *tell* him, I have to tell him I didn't mean—” ʸ

She stops, because she's crying too hard to finish.

Maki is beside her. Maki doesn't hold her back. Maki just stands there, close, so that when Yuka's knees go there is someone for her to go against.

* * *

Yuta comes over.

He doesn't say anything at first. He looks at Yuka, and at the paper she has pulled out of her pocket without noticing she was doing it, still folded along its soft creases.

“Give it to me,” he says. ʸᵗ

She looks up at him.

“I'll carry it,” Yuta says. “When he's out, it's yours to give him. I'm only carrying it.” ʸᵗ

Yuka looks at the paper for a long time.

Then she puts it in his hand, and closes his fingers over it, and holds them closed with both of hers.

“Don't read it,” she says. ʸ

“I won't.” ʸᵗ

“Don't lose it.” ʸ

“I won't.” ʸᵗ

She lets go.

* * *

It's DIO who sees it first.

She has been standing beside Ashborn all morning, quiet, her hands folded. Now she turns her head towards the far edge of the yard, past the frame, past the low wall, towards the place where the ward begins, and goes very still.

Yuka follows her gaze.

There is something standing just outside the ward.

A flat pale shape, like a sheet of light hung in the air with nothing to hold it up. Yuka has seen one like it before, once, the morning before the Rulers' trial: a window they made to watch through. There are three figures beside it. They don't come closer. They don't speak.

They watch the frame.

“Rulers,” Rimuru says, very quietly. ᴿ

Nobody moves.

“Are they here to help?” Yuka asks. ʸ

DIO doesn't answer at once.

“They are here to see,” she says. ᴰ

Ashborn doesn't look at the three figures at all. He looks at the path, at the old stone going down into the dark, and his face is very still.

“Then let them see,” he says. ᴬˢ

* * *

Jin-Woo takes his hand off the frame.

The path stays.

He looks at Gojo. Gojo nods. He looks at Yuta, who has put the paper in an inside pocket and buttoned it shut, and Yuta nods too.

Hae-In is standing beside Shoko. She doesn't come forward. She has watched him walk into wars before, and she knows what is useful and what is not, and what is useful is that he looks at her, once, and she looks back.

“Come home,” she says. ᴴᶦ

“I will,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶ

Yuka stands up. Maki helps her.

“Bring him back,” she says to Gojo. ʸ

She means it as an order. It comes out as begging. She doesn't care about that either.

Gojo puts his hand on her head, once, briefly, the way he does to Ori.

“That's the plan,” he says. ᴳ

Then the three of them step onto the old stone and walk down into the dark, and the counter on the frame goes on running, too fast to be wrong and too fast to believe, and at the edge of the yard, behind their pale window, the three Rulers watch them go.
