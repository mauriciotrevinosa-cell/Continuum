# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 9 — The Last Corridor

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S11E67
**Continuity state:** Local days ~330–368 (outside ~22h–24h30m, B+216 morning). Final approach to the centre. The field now answers each adaptation within hours rather than days. New injuries accumulate: left shoulder dislocated and set, a cut across the right thigh, a tooth lost, more burns from vents. Mau keeps moving out of refusal. Intercut: Jin-Woo, Gojo and Yuta on the maintenance path, which runs beside the operator's route and is also subject to the 360x clock; inside, their legion and shikigami are closed; they walk about nine local days and feel the centre ahead. At the end Mau reaches a great plain door. No new technique; 6,617 bonds.

*When: Mau leaves the round room, and somewhere behind and above him, before he knows it, three people step onto an old stone path; outside, the yard is waiting.*

The field is faster now.

He notices it on the first day out of the round room. In the hall it took him thirteen days to learn the arms, and the change came the morning after. In the dark it took days. Now he learns a thing in the morning and by evening it has been taken away.

A passage where vents in the floor breathe out heat in a rhythm. He learns the rhythm by noon. By night the rhythm has doubled, and the vent he was stepping past opens under his heel, and the skin on the sole of his good foot comes away with the boot.

A stair where every fifth step is false. He learns to count. By the next morning it is every fourth, and then every third, and then the count changes each time he climbs.

He stops trying to learn.

He can't. He doesn't know how to stop learning; he never has. But he stops expecting it to help. He moves the way you move through a burning house: not because you know the way, but because staying is worse.

* * *

The new wounds come quickly, one on top of another.

A slab in a narrow place that he gets most of the way past, and the edge of it catches his left shoulder and puts it out of its socket. He sits with his back against the wall and puts it back himself, with his right hand, crooked as it is, and the sound it makes going back in is the loudest thing he has heard in weeks.

A blade of stone that comes out of the wall at thigh height. He sees it. He dodges it. He dodges it by the margin he has always dodged by, and the margin isn't there any more, and it opens his right thigh from the hip almost to the knee.

He closes it lying on the floor of the passage, with his teeth gritted round a strip of blanket so that he won't bite through his tongue.

When he takes the strip out of his mouth, one of his back teeth comes with it, loose in its socket from a blow he doesn't remember taking.

He looks at it in his palm for a long time.

“That's new,” he says. ᴹ

*Yes,* Noise says. ᴺ

He puts it in his pocket. He doesn't know why. It seems wrong to leave a piece of himself on the floor of this place.

* * *

He eats a quarter of the bread every day now, because Noise asked, and because he said *for you*, and because a man who has said *for you* to somebody ought to keep his word.

He doesn't look at the balance.

He doesn't count the days.

He doesn't say home.

He walks.

* * *

Three people walk down a path of old stone.

The path runs beside the place where Mau is, the way a corridor for servants runs beside the rooms of a grand house, so that people can move through the building without being seen in the rooms. It was built for someone to check on what had been made. It has not been used in a very long time.

Jin-Woo goes first. Every few hundred steps he stops and puts his hand on the wall, and the old stone answers him, and they go on.

Gojo walks behind him. He has his blindfold up, his eyes open, and he is looking at everything.

Yuta brings up the rear. He has a hand on his chest, over the buttoned inside pocket, and he doesn't seem to know he's doing it.

“How long?” Gojo asks, on what his body tells him is the third day. ᴳ

Jin-Woo knows what he means.

“We left a little over ten minutes ago,” he says. ᴶ

Gojo laughs, once, without any humour in it.

“Feels longer.” ᴳ

“It is longer. For us.” ᴶ

* * *

Their shadows won't come.

Jin-Woo found that out on the first day, when he reached for Beru out of habit, his own Beru, and felt the bond answer and the body refuse. The field doesn't care who they are. It has shut the doors in here for everyone.

“So it's us,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“It's us,” Jin-Woo agrees. ᴶ

They have their own bodies. Jin-Woo has his daggers. Gojo has his technique, and it still works on the old path, though Gojo says it feels like running through water. Yuta has his sword and his output, more of it than anyone alive, and the reverse of it, which is the thing Mau is going to need.

They don't talk about what they'll find.

On the sixth day, Yuta says, out of nowhere:

“A year.” ʸᵗ

Neither of the others answers. They don't need to.

* * *

Mau doesn't know any of this.

He only knows that the passages are getting wider and the ceilings higher and the stone older, darker, smoother, as though he is walking towards the part of the place that was built first. The field is everywhere and nowhere. Every step costs.

On a day he doesn't count, he catches himself talking to Yuka again.

Not calling. He stopped calling a long time ago. Just talking, in his head, the way he did by the dragon.

*I think I'm close,* he tells her. *To the middle. I can feel it.* ᴹ

*I don't know what I'll do when I get there.* ᴹ

*I don't know if I'm coming out.* ᴹ

He waits, out of habit, for an answer that isn't coming.

*If I do,* he says, *you don't have to be there. At the door. I'd understand. I just want you to know I tried.* ᴹ

*Mau,* Noise says. ᴺ

He doesn't answer it.

But he notices, distantly, that it's the first time in a long while he has said *if I do* instead of nothing at all.

* * *

On the ninth day of their walk, Jin-Woo stops.

He puts his hand on the wall, and this time he doesn't take it away.

“Close,” he says. ᴶ

Gojo has gone very still. His eyes are fixed on the wall ahead of them, through it, past it, at something none of the others can see.

“I can see the middle,” he says. ᴳ

“Him?” Yuta asks. ʸᵗ

Gojo doesn't answer at once.

“There's someone moving,” he says at last. “Towards it. Slowly. On the other side.” ᴳ

He doesn't say what the someone looks like. He doesn't say how slowly.

Yuta's hand is flat over his pocket.

“Then we go faster,” he says. ʸᵗ

* * *

Mau comes to the door on what Noise would have told him was the three hundred and sixty-eighth day.

It is enormous. Plain dark stone, taller than the dragon, set into a wall that curves away on either side out of sight. There is no handle. There is no lock. There is a seam down the middle, where the two halves meet, a hairline of grey.

He stands in front of it.

He is so thin that his clothes hang off him. His face is scabbed from the plain. One shoulder sits lower than the other, the arm under it short and bent, the fingers of both hands crooked. His foot is bound in a split boot. There is a long puckered line across his belly that he presses his hand over without knowing he's doing it, and a fresh one down his thigh, and a tooth in his pocket.

He can feel the room under his ribs, wide open now, the door swinging.

He can feel, on the other side of this door, somebody waiting.

*Here,* Noise says. *Middle.* ᴺ

“I know,” Mau says. ᴹ

He puts his hand flat against the stone.

It opens.
