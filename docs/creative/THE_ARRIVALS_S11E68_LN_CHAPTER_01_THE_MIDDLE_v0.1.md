# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 10 — The Middle

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S11E68
**Continuity state:** Local day ~368 (outside ~24h30m, B+216 mid-morning). The central chamber. The Architect is present in a built body: tall, plain, exact, a mask-like face that does not move; this story's form, not a claim about any other version. He asks Mau once more to lean. The rescue party arrives at the chamber's edge through the maintenance path; the Architect folds space to keep them apart and delays them with field pressure, which divides his attention. Mau sees Gojo, Jin-Woo and Yuta through the folds and first thinks they are a memory trick. Noise confirms they are real. Mau sees the window. No new technique; 6,617 bonds.

*When: The door opens; outside, the counter is past three hundred and sixty days, and Yuka has stopped being able to read it.*

The middle is a room.

That is all. After everything, after the hall and the rain and the dark and the plain, he had expected something worse. A pit. A throne. A furnace like the dragon's.

It is a room. Round, very large, the ceiling lost in shadow. The floor is smooth dark stone with lines cut into it, fine as hair, running in towards the centre the way spokes run towards a hub. The lines glow faintly, the colour of nothing, the colour of the field itself.

At the hub there is a figure.

* * *

It is tall.

Taller than Gojo, standing very straight, built rather than born. That is the first thing Mau's eyes tell him. The energy in it doesn't knot like a living thing's or furnace like the dragon's. It runs in clean straight channels, exact, like water in pipes, every line meeting every other line at a right angle.

It has a face. The face is smooth and pale and doesn't move, like a mask somebody carved and then forgot to finish.

It has been waiting for him.

“You came to me,” the Architect says. ᴬʳ

The voice is the same voice. Calm and level, reading a measurement aloud. Only now it comes from somewhere he can see.

Mau stands in the doorway with his hand pressed over his belly.

“You built the corridor,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴬʳ

“So I didn't come to you. You brought me.” ᴹ

The mask doesn't change.

“That is a distinction,” the Architect says. ᴬʳ

* * *

He walks into the room.

Not because he has a plan. He doesn't. He has a body that is mostly scar and two weak hands and a pack with half a loaf in it and a tooth in his pocket. He walks in because the door closed behind him as soon as he crossed it, and there is nowhere else to go.

The Architect watches him come.

It doesn't move. It doesn't need to. The lines on the floor glow brighter as he steps over them, and he feels the field lean on him, harder here than anywhere, a weight on every part of him at once.

He stops a long way short of the hub.

“You're very tired,” the Architect says. ᴬʳ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“You have been tired for a long time.” ᴬʳ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“You leaned once. You nearly went in. You stopped yourself because of your shadows.” ᴬʳ

Mau doesn't answer.

“You stopped again in the round room,” the Architect says. “You lay down. I watched the field go quiet around you. You were nearly there.” ᴬʳ

“Something talked me out of it.” ᴹ

“Yes. I would like to understand that, when this is finished.” ᴬʳ

It takes one step towards him.

“Lean,” it says. “Here. Now. It will not hurt. I have made sure of that. It will be the first thing in a year that doesn't.” ᴬʳ

* * *

He can feel the room under his ribs swing wide.

It's so close here. Closer than in the narrow passage, closer than the round room. The door isn't only open. It's pulling. A draught going one way, into the cool empty space where nothing hurts, and every part of his body leaning towards it the way a plant leans towards a window.

He doesn't move.

He stands in the middle of the Architect's room with his teeth clenched and his hand over his belly and his whole body shaking, and doesn't lean, and doesn't step back either, because he isn't sure his legs would carry him.

*Mau,* Noise says. *Don't.* ᴺ

“I'm not,” he whispers. ᴹ

*Don't.* ᴺ

“I'm *not*.” ᴹ

The Architect tilts its head.

Then it stops.

* * *

Mau feels it before he sees it: a change in the weight on him. As though the hand pressing down on everything has had to lift a finger to do something else.

The Architect's mask turns, very slightly, towards the left side of the room.

Mau follows it.

The wall over there is the same smooth dark stone as the rest of the room. Only now it isn't quite. There is a seam in it, a line of grey, the same hairline grey as the door he came through. And the stone on either side of the seam is moving, bending, the way a sheet of paper bends when someone presses on it from behind.

Something is pushing.

The seam opens a hand's width.

Light comes through it. Not the colourless glow of the lines on the floor. Ordinary light, white and flat. And something else with it, a feeling Mau knows in his teeth before he knows it in his head, the way you know a voice in the next room before you can hear the words.

Infinity.

Not his. Somebody else's.

* * *

The Architect lifts one hand.

The space by the seam folds.

Mau sees it happen. The stone doesn't move; the distance does. The place where the seam is opening goes suddenly very far away, as though the left wall has been pulled back a hundred paces in an instant without ever moving. Through the fold, small and distant, he can see figures.

Three.

One of them is tall and white-haired, with his hand up against nothing, his eyes uncovered. One of them is dark and still, a dagger in each hand, standing in front of the others. One is behind them, younger, with a sword half drawn and his other hand flat against his chest.

Mau stops breathing.

* * *

It isn't real.

That's the first thing he thinks. It can't be. The field used Yuka's voice, back in the beginning, at a doorway in the dark. The Architect said, in the room with the bed, that trying is not a door. This is a door, and a picture of the three people he would most want to see coming through it, and the Architect is very good at building rooms.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

His voice comes out broken.

“No. You don't get to—” ᴹ

*Real,* Noise says. ᴺ

He goes still.

*Real,* Noise says again. *I can feel. Him. Gojo. The blue. Same as the house. Same as the clearing. Real.* ᴺ

Mau looks at the fold.

At the tall figure with his hand up against nothing. At the familiar eyes, wide open, looking straight across the impossible distance at him.

Gojo's mouth moves. Mau can't hear it.

He doesn't need to.

He has seen that face make that shape before. In a clearing at dawn, a long time ago, kneeling in front of a man who had asked him to make it quick.

*I'm not going to do that.*

* * *

The Architect lifts its other hand.

The fold deepens. The three figures go farther away, smaller, and Mau sees the field come down on them, the same weight that has been on him for a year, pressing. Jin-Woo staggers. Gojo's hand stays up, and the air around it shivers. Yuta draws his sword the rest of the way and puts his back to the other two.

They don't fall.

They push.

The fold shudders. The Architect's hand stays raised. Its mask is turned towards them now, fully, all of its attention on the place where three people who should not be here are pushing against a wall that should not be pushable.

All of its attention.

Mau feels the weight on him lift a little more. Not gone. Never gone. But less.

Enough.

* * *

He looks at the Architect.

At its back, half turned from him. At the clean straight channels running through its body, every line meeting every other line at a right angle. At its raised hands, holding the fold, holding three people at a distance from him that he could not cross in a year.

At the space between him and it.

Thirty paces. Maybe forty.

He has no plan. He has a body that is mostly scar. He has two crooked hands and a foot that won't bend and an open door under his ribs pulling at him like a tide.

He has a window.

*Mau,* Noise says. ᴺ

It isn't a warning this time.

Mau takes his hand off his belly.

He starts to run.
