# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 11 — Everything He Has Left

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S11E68
**Continuity state:** Local day ~368, continuous (outside ~24h30m). While the Architect holds the fold against Jin-Woo, Gojo and Yuta, Mau reaches him and beats him with what he has: fists, two daggers, Authority, sequential Dismantle, Zoltraak, a brief neutral and Sky pair. His right forearm breaks on the first blow; he half-heals it with a breath of RCT and keeps hitting; ribs and face broken by the Architect's returned blows under the field's multiplier. The Architect cannot both hold the fold and fight him; the fold begins to tear. The Architect's built body cracks; Mau, on top of him, raises his hand for a last blow. No new technique, no domain, no Purple; 6,617 bonds.

*When: Mau starts to run; outside, at the frame, Beru takes a step towards it that nobody tells him to take.*

He can't run.

He does it anyway. It isn't running. It's a lurch, a fall forward caught by the next step, the foot that won't bend slapping the stone, the bad hip grinding, the shoulder that was out of its socket a few days ago sending a white line down his arm with every stride. Thirty paces. It takes him forever.

The Architect hears him at twenty.

The mask turns. One hand stays raised towards the fold. The other comes round towards him, and Mau feels the field come with it, the whole weight of the room swinging his way like a door slammed in the wind.

He puts the neutral up.

It comes late. It always comes late. The weight hits him in the half breath before it closes, and he feels two ribs go on the left side, the burned side, and he doesn't stop.

Ten paces.

The Architect's attention flickers. Behind it, the fold shudders, and through it Mau sees Gojo's hand push forward a finger's width.

The Architect turns back to the fold.

Five paces.

He hits it.

* * *

He hits it with his right hand.

He doesn't decide to. It's what his body does. A year of daggers and Authority and careful bounded cuts, of being clever because he couldn't afford to be anything else, and when he finally reaches the thing that built all of it, his body forgets every clever thing he has ever learned and makes a fist and swings.

His fist hits the Architect in the side of the mask.

The mask cracks.

So does his arm.

He feels it go: the short crooked forearm that a raindrop shattered and he set against tent poles and held together for three hundred days. It breaks where it healed wrong, at the bend below the elbow, and folds, and his fist falls away from the Architect's face hanging from a wrist that isn't connected to anything properly any more.

The Architect staggers.

Only a step. But it staggers.

* * *

He doesn't think.

He sends RCT into the arm. Not a careful trickle. One breath of everything he can push through the narrow pipe, all at once, straight into the break.

It doesn't heal.

It holds.

The ends of the bone grab each other, crooked, the wrong length, and stick. It is a terrible repair. It is the worst thing he has ever done to his own body. He can feel that it will need breaking again by someone who knows what they're doing before it will ever be right.

His fingers close.

He hits the Architect again.

* * *

The Architect hits him back.

It's the first time. A year in this place and the Architect has never once touched him. It has had slabs and claws and drops of water and arms in walls to do that.

It is very strong.

The back of its hand catches him across the face, and the field's price comes with it, everything it has been raising for a year. His cheekbone goes. He feels it go. He feels his head snap sideways and his whole body leave the floor and come down on the stone three paces away, and the room spins, and the room goes grey at the edges.

He gets up.

He doesn't know how. He's on his feet and moving before the grey has gone. The Architect's hand is already coming up towards the fold again, because the fold is shuddering, because Gojo has pushed another finger's width through it while the Architect's attention was on him.

It can't do both.

He understands it all at once, the way he understood the hall.

It can't hold them back and fight him at the same time.

* * *

So he doesn't let it rest.

He hits it with his left hand, the stiff fingers, the wrist that has healed wrong twice. He hits it with his crooked right, the arm held together by one breath of bad repair. He hits it with the daggers, both of them, drawn and driven into the joints of its built body where the clean straight channels meet at right angles.

He puts Dismantle along the edge of the blade, small and bounded, and the field takes most of it, and he does it again.

He puts Zoltraak into the cracks in the mask.

He puts the neutral up when its hand comes for him and Sky beside it for the two heartbeats he has practised, bending the line of the blow away from his throat, and then he drops them both before they can tear something in his head, and hits it again.

The Architect hits him back.

His ribs. His shoulder. His jaw. Each blow carries the whole field behind it, and each one breaks something, and each time he gets up, and each time the Architect has to turn from the fold to meet him, and each time it does, the fold slips.

* * *

He doesn't know how long it goes on.

He loses the edges of it. He knows that he is on the floor sometimes and standing sometimes. He knows that his right arm breaks again, at the same place, and that he holds it together with another breath of RCT, worse than the first. He knows that his mouth is full of blood and that there is another tooth gone and that he can't see out of his left eye any more, the blurred one, because the socket under it is swelling shut.

He knows that the Architect's mask is coming apart.

The cracks spread with each blow. The clean straight channels inside its body are not clean any more, not straight. Where his daggers went in, they leak. Where Zoltraak went in, they spark and fail.

Noise is with him the whole time.

It doesn't say *here*. It doesn't say *home*. It says the only thing that matters, over and over, in the gaps between the blows:

*Left. Up. Now. Down. Now.* ᴺ

He listens. He's always been good at listening to Noise.

* * *

Behind the Architect, the fold tears.

Mau hears it: a sound like cloth ripping, very loud, and the distance by the left wall snaps back to what it was. A hundred paces becomes thirty becomes twenty. Light pours through the seam.

Gojo is through.

Mau sees him for half a second, past the Architect's shoulder: on his knees on the stone just inside the room, one hand still up, blood running from his nose, his eyes wide open and fixed on Mau with an expression Mau has never seen on his face before.

The Architect turns towards him.

Mau puts both daggers into its back.

* * *

It goes down.

Not all at once. It folds the way the dragon folded, one joint at a time, the great built body sinking to its knees on the hub of the room and then forwards onto its hands. The lines on the floor flicker. The weight of the field stutters, comes back, stutters again.

Mau goes down with it.

He doesn't mean to. His legs have stopped. He lands on its back, on the cracked shoulders, and stays there, kneeling on it, his crooked right hand wrapped in the collar of whatever passes for its clothes.

The mask turns its broken face towards him.

“Why?” it says. ᴬʳ

The voice is still calm. Still level. Still reading a measurement aloud. But it is quieter now, as though some of the pipes it comes through have broken.

“You were so tired,” it says. “You were nearly there. Why?” ᴬʳ

Mau looks down at it.

At the cracked mask. At the leaking channels. At the hand that has held three people back from him for a year and a room.

“Because you wanted me not to,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is barely a voice. It comes out through blood and a broken jaw and two missing teeth.

“You wanted me to stop. So I didn't.” ᴹ

* * *

He lets go of the collar.

He raises his right hand, the crooked one, the one held together by two breaths of terrible repair.

He makes a fist.

Somewhere across the room, Gojo is shouting his name. Jin-Woo is through the seam now too, running. Yuta behind him, sword out, his other hand pressed flat against his chest.

Mau doesn't hear them.

He looks at the Architect's broken mask, and at his own fist above it, and at the open door under his ribs that is still there, still wide, still pulling.

He brings the fist down.
