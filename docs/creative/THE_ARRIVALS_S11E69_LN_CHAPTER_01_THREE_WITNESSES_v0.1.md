# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 12 — Three Witnesses

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S11E69
**Continuity state:** Local day ~368, continuous (outside ~24h32m). Mau's last blow is stopped in the air. The three Rulers (ᴸ¹ ᴸ² ᴸ³) enter through a pale window, bind the Architect alive and claim him and the instance's infrastructure as material; they gain no ownership of Noise, Mau's body or the Black Heart. They close the instance and return all present to the outside. Gojo reaches Mau first; Yuta begins stabilising with reverse cursed technique; Jin-Woo shields them. Mau, barely conscious, asks Gojo to tell Yuka she doesn't have to be there; Yuta has her paper in his pocket and says nothing yet. No new technique or unit; 6,617 bonds.

*When: Mau's fist comes down; outside, the pale window at the edge of the yard goes dark.*

It stops.

Not his fist. Everything.

His fist is a hand's width above the Architect's broken mask and it isn't moving. He isn't moving. The blood running from his mouth has stopped halfway down his chin. Across the room, Gojo is caught mid-stride, one hand out. Jin-Woo is a blur that has stopped being a blur. Yuta is a man with a sword who is not quite touching the floor.

Mau can still see.

He can still think. Slowly, as though through deep water, but he can think. He can feel the open door under his ribs. He can feel Noise, very close, very frightened.

He can't move his hand.

* * *

The light comes from the left.

A flat pale shape opens in the air beside the hub, a sheet of light with nothing to hold it up. He has seen one before. At the edge of a yard, on a morning before a trial he didn't understand, while the people who made it told him it was an assessment.

Three figures step through it.

They don't look like anything in particular. They never did. Tall, and pale, and too still, the way the Architect was too still, but differently: the Architect was still because it was built. These are still because they have decided to be.

The first one looks at the Architect.

“The maker is required,” it says. ᴸ¹

The second one looks at the room: the lines on the floor, the hub, the walls.

“The instance is material,” it says. ᴸ²

The third one looks at Mau.

It looks at him for a long time. At his fist, a hand's width from the mask. At his face, at the eye swollen shut, at the arm held together by two breaths of terrible repair.

It doesn't say anything at all.

* * *

“No,” Mau says. ᴹ

He doesn't know how he says it. His mouth isn't moving. Maybe it comes out of him some other way. Maybe the Rulers simply hear what he means.

“No. He's mine. Let me—” ᴹ

“The maker is required,” the first one says again, exactly the same way. ᴸ¹

“He did this. He did *all* of this—” ᴹ

“Yes,” says the second. ᴸ²

That's all. Just yes. An acknowledgement of a fact, like noting the weather.

Mau strains against whatever is holding his hand.

It doesn't give. It doesn't give at all. He has fought a year to get here, through a hall and a rain and a dark and a dragon and a plain and an empty room, and he has got his fist a hand's width from the face of the thing that built it, and three people who did not lift a finger to help him have stepped through a window and told him no.

He starts to cry.

He can't move enough to cry properly. The tears come anyway, sideways, out of the eye that still opens.

* * *

The first Ruler lifts a hand.

Bands of pale light close round the Architect. Not chains. Something tidier than chains: lines, exact as the ones on the floor, wrapping its broken body at every joint where Mau's daggers went in. The Architect's mask turns, a fraction, towards the three figures.

“You were waiting,” it says. ᴬʳ

“Yes,” says the first. ᴸ¹

“For him to do this.” ᴬʳ

“For the instance to be weakened,” says the second. “The operator weakened it.” ᴸ²

The Architect is quiet for a moment.

Then, very quietly, with the voice that comes through broken pipes:

“That is a specification.” ᴬʳ

The bands tighten. It stops talking.

* * *

“The operator,” says the third Ruler. ᴸ³

It is the first time it has spoken. Its voice is the same as the others, clinical and flat. But it says the word slowly, as though it has noticed something it intends to remember.

“The operator's body, the construct within it and the heart are not part of the instance,” it says. “They are not claimed.” ᴸ³

The first one turns its head towards it. Something passes between them that Mau can't read.

“Noted,” says the first. ᴸ¹

* * *

“The instance is closed,” says the second Ruler. ᴸ²

The lines on the floor go dark.

Mau feels it happen. The field, the weight that has been on him every moment for a year, on every breath, every step, every bite of bread, goes out like a lamp. It doesn't lift. It's simply gone. He didn't know how much of him had been spent holding himself up against it until it wasn't there.

His fist drops.

Not onto the mask. Whatever was holding him lets go, and his arm falls, and his whole body falls with it, sideways off the Architect's bound back and onto the stone.

He lands badly. He doesn't feel it.

Somebody is there.

* * *

Gojo gets to him first.

He comes down on his knees on the stone so hard that Mau hears it, and his hands are on Mau before Mau has finished falling: one behind his head, one on his chest, the way they were in a clearing a long time ago. His blindfold is gone. His eyes are wide open and wet.

“Mau,” he says. ᴳ

Mau looks up at him.

Close up, Gojo's face is the realest thing he has seen in a year. Real dirt on his cheek. Real blood dried under his nose. Real eyes, looking at him with an expression Mau doesn't have a name for.

“Is it real?” Mau asks. ᴹ

His voice is almost nothing.

“It's real,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“You're sure?” ᴹ

Gojo makes a sound that might be a laugh.

“I'm sure. I'm extremely real. Ask anyone.” ᴳ

* * *

Yuta is on his other side.

Mau feels him before he sees him: a warmth going in through his chest, much more than his own narrow trickle, sure of itself, knowing exactly what bones are supposed to do. Yuta can still shape his reverse cursed technique steadily where Mau has spent months struggling to hold a thread. It goes into him like water into dry ground.

Mau gasps.

It hurts. Everything Yuta touches hurts as it starts to remember what it should be. The ribs. The face. The arm held together wrong.

“Don't fix the arm,” Mau hears himself say. ᴹ

“Mau—” ʸᵗ

“It's wrong. Shoko has to—it has to be broken again first. Don't make it stick wrong.” ᴹ

Yuta is quiet for a moment.

“Okay,” he says. “Okay. Just the things that'll kill you.” ʸᵗ

“Okay.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo is standing over the three of them with a dagger in each hand, facing the Rulers and the bound Architect, not saying anything. The Rulers don't look at him. They don't need to.

* * *

Mau looks up at Gojo again.

The room is going strange at the edges. Not grey. White. Something is happening to it: the walls getting thinner, the ceiling coming down, the floor not quite as solid as it was.

“How long?” he asks. ᴹ

Gojo understands what he means.

“About a day,” he says. ᴳ

Mau lies there with that.

A day.

“It was a year in here,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ᴳ

“I lost count. Noise didn't.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴳ

“It's fine,” Mau says. ᴹ

He hears it come out of his mouth. *It's fine.* Through a broken jaw, with one eye swollen shut and his arm held together by two breaths of bad repair and a tooth in his pocket.

Gojo's face does something terrible.

“Don't,” he says, very quietly. “Don't do that. Not to me.” ᴳ

Mau doesn't know what he means.

* * *

The white is coming in faster.

The second Ruler says something, flat and clinical. The bound Architect is gone, or going. Jin-Woo turns. Yuta keeps his hands where they are.

Mau grabs Gojo's sleeve.

He doesn't have the strength. His hand closes on it anyway, the crooked fingers.

“Tell Yuka,” he says. ᴹ

Gojo bends closer.

“Tell her she doesn't have to be there.” ᴹ

Gojo goes very still.

“At the door,” Mau says. “When I come out. She doesn't have to. I understand. Tell her I understand.” ᴹ

Across from Gojo, Yuta's hand moves from Mau's chest to his own: flat against the buttoned inside pocket, where a piece of paper is folded along its soft creases.

He doesn't say anything.

He looks at Gojo, and Gojo looks back at him, and neither of them says anything, because there is nothing to say that this is the place for.

“I'll tell her,” Gojo says. ᴳ

His voice is steady. Mau believes him.

“Thank you,” Mau whispers. ᴹ

* * *

*Mau,* Noise says. ᴺ

The white is everywhere now.

*Home,* Noise says. ᴺ

Mau doesn't answer it.

He closes his eye, and lets go of Gojo's sleeve, and the white takes the room, and the Rulers, and the broken stone, and the year.
