# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 16 — What He Asked Me to Tell You

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S11E71
**Continuity state:** B+216, early afternoon. While Mau sleeps after urgent correction, Gojo tells Yuka exactly what Mau asked at the extraction; Yuta returns her unread paper. His brief reassurance in the yard is not allowed to conceal the misunderstanding for another arc. Yuka accepts practical support without being sent away from him. Ori sees him briefly with Frieren after Shoko permits it; she is not asked to comfort the adults. 6,617 bonds; no power changes.

*When: Maki brings a second chair into the clinic because Yuka will not leave the first one.*

“You can move the chairs,” Yuka says. “I'm not moving.” ʸ

Maki puts the second chair beside hers.

“I brought it for me.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka looks at it, then at her grandmother, and the fight goes out of her before it has anywhere to go.

“Oh.” ʸ

Maki sits with a plate balanced on her knees.

It isn't a meal somebody has tried to make beautiful. Small things, easy to pick up. There is enough for two.

Yuka takes a piece because Maki has put it where she can reach without letting go of Mau's fingers. She chews without wanting to. When she swallows, Maki pushes the cup a little closer.

For several minutes that is all they do.

Mau sleeps. Every so often Yuka checks the arm again. The shape of it is right now. She keeps forgetting that it has changed and looking down to see the bend that was there in the yard.

“I wasn't going to leave him,” she says. ʸ

Then, because the words come out too quietly, she says them again.

“I wasn't going to leave him.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“I wanted him to talk to me.” ʸ

Maki takes the plate off her lap and sets it on the spare table.

“I know that, too.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka's mouth trembles. She presses it closed until the feeling passes enough that she can breathe without making a noise.

There is a quiet knock.

Gojo stands in the doorway. Yuta is behind him.

For a moment Yuka sees them as she saw them before they went down the path. Two people she could send where she couldn't go, with a request that was much too large to put into somebody else's hands.

They came back.

She hasn't thanked them.

“Thank you,” she says, before Gojo can speak. ʸ

He looks at Mau, at the supports under his arm, then back at her.

“Can we talk for a minute?” ᴳ

* * *

They go as far as the open doorway.

Maki stays in the chair. From where Yuka stands, she can still see the rise and fall under Mau's blanket.

Gojo doesn't lean on the wall. He looks as though he would like to sit down again. Yuka reaches behind her for a chair and pushes it towards him without thinking.

He takes it.

For once he doesn't say anything about being looked after.

“Before they brought us out,” he says, “he asked me to tell you something.” ᴳ

Yuka's hand closes on the doorframe.

“What?” ʸ

“That you didn't have to be there when he came out. At the door. He wanted you to know he understood.” ᴳ

She looks past him at Yuta.

Yuta nods.

“He…” ʸ

She can't finish the question. It doesn't have a shape she wants answered.

Gojo keeps his voice level.

“When he said he'd known from the first second, in the yard—I don't think that was what he'd been living with.” ᴳ

Yuka looks back into the clinic.

Mau hasn't moved. Maki is adjusting the edge of the blanket where it has slipped beside his shoulder.

“He was trying to make me feel better.” ʸ

“I think so.” ᴳ

“With his face like that.” ʸ

Neither of them answers.

Yuka presses her knuckles to her mouth. It is the only way she can hold it still.

She had wanted to believe him. She hadn't even had time to decide whether she did; his answer had arrived like a little piece of ground beneath her feet. He knew. He hadn't spent that time thinking she wanted him gone.

Now she sees the way his face changed when he saw her.

The surprise in it.

She lowers her hand.

“How long did he think that?” ʸ

“We don't know yet,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

She nods too quickly. Stops. Makes herself look at him.

He doesn't offer her a smaller number.

* * *

Yuta unbuttons his inside pocket.

The paper has been against his chest through something Yuka didn't see. One edge is softened. There is a dark mark on the outside that wasn't there when she folded it.

He holds it out.

“I didn't read it.” ʸᵗ

Yuka takes it with both hands.

“Did he?” ʸ

Yuta shakes his head.

“There wasn't time. When he could look at us, he was already—” ʸᵗ

“I know.” ʸ

She says it before he has to finish. She knows what they were trying to keep inside Mau's body while a room disappeared around them. She doesn't need Yuta to apologise for failing to hand him a letter at the same time.

He watches her smooth a corner without unfolding it.

“You brought him back,” she says. ʸ

“He got himself a long way towards us,” Yuta says. “We reached him.” ʸᵗ

Gojo looks down at his own hands.

“All three of us,” he adds. ᴳ

Yuka nods.

“I'll thank Jin-Woo, too.” ʸ

Then she puts the paper in her pocket, where she can feel it, and sits on the low bench opposite Gojo because she has begun to shake too much to pretend she is only standing here for a moment.

* * *

“I should have said it differently.” ʸ

“Yes,” Gojo says. ᴳ

Yuta glances at him. Gojo doesn't look away from Yuka.

“You should have. You were trying to say something real, and it came out in a way that hurt. You'll talk about that when he can.” ᴳ

She looks at the folded edge showing above her pocket.

“But you didn't build that place,” Gojo says. “You didn't keep him in it. Whatever he thought about those words, that isn't permission for what happened to him.” ᴳ

Yuka's breath catches.

For a little while she cries without trying to talk through it. Yuta sits beside her. He doesn't put an explanation into the space, doesn't ask her to remember that Mau is safe now and stop.

Gojo waits with his elbows on his knees.

When she can speak again, her voice is hoarse.

“I don't want to lose what I was asking for.” ʸ

Yuta looks at her.

“I don't want us to pretend it was all fine before he went in. He was hiding things. He was telling me enough to stop me asking, and I could feel there was more.” ʸ

She wipes her face with the cloth Maki has quietly brought to the doorway.

“I want him here. I want to be with him. I still want him to let me.” ʸ

“Then we'll keep that part,” Maki says. “You don't have to choose between getting him home and telling him the truth.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka nods.

This time it takes a while.

* * *

Ori is sitting with Frieren when Yuka comes out for fresh water.

She has her shoes on. There is dust on one knee from the yard. Somebody offered to change her clothes and she has clearly decided that being ready matters more.

“Can I see him?” ᴼ

Yuka looks towards Shoko.

Shoko has come out to check Gojo and is trying to make him sit straight while he claims to be doing so. She stops long enough to consider the question.

“A little while. He's sleeping, and there are supports you can't climb over. Ask before you touch him.” ˢʰ

Ori nods.

Frieren stands with her.

Inside, Ori stops at the foot of the bed.

The blanket hides much of what she saw in the yard. It doesn't hide his face, or how far his shoulders have sunk into the pillow. His hair has been loosened enough to fall away from his cheek, and it makes him look unfamiliar in a way she doesn't like.

“Does it hurt when he sleeps?” she asks. ᴼ

Yuka looks at Shoko again instead of guessing.

“I'm helping with that,” Shoko says. “He's getting some rest now.” ˢʰ

Ori comes closer.

“Can I touch there?” ᴼ

She points to the fingers resting outside the blanket, the ones Yuka hasn't been holding.

Shoko shows her which part will not move his injured wrist. Ori touches it with one fingertip.

“Hi, Dad.” ᴼ

Mau doesn't wake.

Ori's mouth pulls down. She presses her lips together with a fierceness Yuka recognises from the yard.

“You don't have to say anything,” she tells him. “I'm just saying it.” ᴼ

Frieren puts a hand on Ori's back.

Ori stays until Shoko asks her to give him more time. She doesn't protest. At the door she turns back, making sure Yuka has taken the place by his hand again before she leaves.

* * *

Yuka unfolds her paper after they go.

Three sentences. She had spent so long trying to find the right three sentences, and then put them into a pocket where Mau couldn't hear them.

She reads them aloud now.

“I love you. I'm staying. Come home so we can finish talking.” ʸ

His breathing doesn't change.

She lays the paper on the bedside table, above the dish with his ring. When she reaches for his hand again, she finds herself checking the position Shoko showed her.

“You're home,” she says. “The talking can wait until you can stay awake for it.” ʸ

She leans back in the chair.

Not away from him. Just far enough to rest her spine against something solid.

“I can wait here.” ʸ
