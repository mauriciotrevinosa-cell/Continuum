# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 71
## Chapter 20 — The First Thing She Keeps

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S11 v0.1
**Volume:** 71
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S11E73
**Continuity state:** B+217 afternoon, after further rest and a limited corrective session. Mau is not cleared to walk; back, foot, wrist, nourishment and strength remain under staged care. Ori asks for the original Ori's triple-folded drawing/message, explicitly held since V63#24 and left unread at her request in V64#4. Yuka fetches its paper box from the home drawer. Ori opens it first, chooses to read aloud, adds her own answer and takes custody. Her prior choice of Dad is not produced by the message. Frieren remains Mum; Yuka stays Yuka. Beginning of recovery, not completion of physical repair or S12's full listening.

*When: Ori asks for the paper, Frieren asks whether she wants company.*

“Yes,” Ori says. “But I want to open it.” ᴼ

“Of course.” ᶠ

Frieren puts her book down.

Ori has been thinking about the paper since before breakfast. Not all the time. Sometimes she thinks about Mau's hand, or the way he looked at her this morning when she came with Frieren and he managed to say her name.

He sounded tired.

He sounded like Mau.

Now she wants the thing he brought for her when he came back the other time.

“Does he still have it?” she asks. ᴼ

“We'll ask him.” ᶠ

Ori takes Frieren's hand for the walk.

* * *

He is awake when they reach the clinic.

There is a new arrangement of supports under his left leg. A cloth lies over his eye for a little while, and Shoko is telling Yuka what time she will come back to check him.

Ori waits at the door until Shoko sees her.

“A short visit,” Shoko says. “He's had a tiring morning.” ˢʰ

Ori nods.

Mau lifts the edge of the cloth enough to see who has come.

“Hi,” he says. ᴹ

“Hi.” ᴼ

She comes to the chair Yuka moves into place for her. It is close enough that she doesn't have to stand on tiptoe to be seen.

“I want the paper now,” she says. ᴼ

Mau looks at her.

It takes him a moment. Ori sees the little effort of putting the words beside something he remembers. Then his eye moves to Yuka.

“Box,” he says. “Drawer.” ᴹ

“I know where.” ʸ

Ori looks between them.

“You didn't bring it in the dungeon?” ᴼ

Mau shakes his head a little.

“Home. Safe.” ᴹ

Ori looks relieved in a way that hurts Yuka to recognise. She has seen what came out of the dungeon in his pockets.

“I'll get it,” Yuka says. ʸ

She touches Mau's hand before she stands.

“I'm going to our room. I'll bring the box straight back. Frieren's here.” ʸ

He looks towards Frieren, then back to Yuka.

“Okay.” ᴹ

She goes.

It isn't easy for him to watch her leave. He watches anyway, and then turns his eye back to Ori because she has begun telling him where she wants to sit when the chair is hers to choose.

* * *

The box is ordinary.

Yuka comes back carrying it in both hands, as carefully as if it isn't. She puts it where Mau can see, then lifts the lid without touching the paper inside.

Ori leans forward.

Three folds. A softened corner. A little dark line showing through where the pencil pressed hard on the other side.

“Nobody?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“Nobody,” Mau says. ᴹ

She puts her fingers under the paper and takes it out herself.

For a little while she just holds it.

Frieren sits beside her. Yuka takes the place by Mau's hand again. Neither asks her to hurry.

Ori opens the first fold.

Then the second.

At the third, part of a dragon appears upside down. She turns the page.

“That's Kaisel.” ᴼ

Mau looks away before he can see the words.

Ori notices.

“You can look at the picture,” she tells him. ᴼ

He looks back.

* * *

She has drawn the house from the wrong side of the tree.

Mau remembers saying nothing about it because she had already explained. He remembers the landing, her knee against his, the little pencil rolling where he could reach it. He remembers the question he hadn't known how to answer without hurting both of them.

*Do I know you're my dad?*

The page trembles in the present Ori's hands.

Not because she knows the question. Because she is holding it out towards him and trying to keep all the corners from folding shut.

Yuka reaches to help only when Ori asks her to hold the lower edge.

There are two dragons. One has a great many small figures in front of it, crossed out with determined pencil strokes. The other has room for the people the girl wanted to take.

Ori studies the crossed-out figures.

“Beru?” she asks. ᴼ

Mau lets out a small, broken laugh.

“Procession.” ᴹ

Ori looks at Beru in the chair by the wall.

He straightens.

“It would have been a very respectful one,” he says. ᴮᵉ

Ori considers the drawing, then looks back at him.

“Too many people.” ᴼ

Beru bows his head as if receiving an important revision.

“I see.” ᴮᵉ

* * *

The writing is on the other side.

Ori turns the page towards herself. Mau looks away again, and this time she lets him.

She reads silently at first. Her lips move at one part. Her finger follows a line that has been squeezed between two others when the girl writing it ran out of room.

Frieren watches her face without trying to read over her shoulder.

“Do you want help?” she asks. ᶠ

Ori shakes her head.

Then she reads it again.

The room is quiet enough that Mau can hear the little scrape of her nail against the page. He keeps his eye on the place where Yuka's fingers rest against the blanket.

He hasn't read it. Not on the stairs. Not when he took it out of his pocket after coming home. Not when Ori said *later*, or when he checked that the corner hadn't caught under the lid.

He kept it folded.

It is such a small thing to have managed.

He is crying before Ori says anything.

* * *

“I want to read it out,” Ori says. ᴼ

Frieren nods.

Ori turns the paper a little so Mau can see her face without seeing the writing first.

“It says, 'To me.'” ᴼ

She looks down.

“'This is our house. I drew Mum's window too, but you have to go round the tree to see both. Kaisel said yes. Beru said yes much louder, but I didn't ask him for all those people.'” ᴼ

Beru makes a sound that is mostly air.

Ori keeps reading.

“'Dad says you know him a different way. He showed me you on Kaisel. You wanted another turn, so I think you like him.'” ᴼ

She stops long enough to look at Mau.

“I do.” ᴼ

He can't answer. She doesn't make him.

“'I told him I love him. He knows. You can tell him what you want. Mum is still Mum. She can come too if she wants, or watch from the ground. I want to show him both windows tomorrow. If I can't, ask him to ask you.'” ᴼ

Ori has to bring the paper closer for the last line, tucked into a space at the bottom.

“'He cried on the stairs. I sat next to him. You can still ask him things.'” ᴼ

The page lowers into her lap.

Nobody speaks.

* * *

Yuka doesn't let go of Mau's hand.

She has heard pieces of that goodbye. She knew there was a drawing, knew the child had covered the words while she wrote them, knew Mau had come back with something that had to remain closed until somebody else was ready.

She hadn't known about tomorrow.

The flight that little girl had planned for after breakfast. Her mother coming down. Two windows to show him from the other side of a tree.

Mau has turned his face as far towards Ori as the pillow allows. He is trying to keep the crying quiet, which is making it hurt more.

Frieren reaches for the clean cloth before Yuka can. She places it where Mau can take it if he wants, then rests her hand beside it, waiting.

His fingers touch hers.

Ori looks at the picture again.

“You did ask me,” she says. ᴼ

Mau looks at her.

“We did it. You asked. I showed you.” ᴼ

He remembers the turn around the later house, Ori pointing to her own window and then his. The sound of her asking Kaisel to go round again.

He had brought the request home before he could bear to speak all of it.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

His voice fails on the rest.

Ori moves her chair closer, then stops and looks at Yuka.

“Where can I?” ᴼ

Yuka knows what she is asking.

She checks with Shoko at the door. Shoko comes to show Ori a way to lean beside the pillow without pressing against Mau's shoulder or chest. It isn't the hug she would have given him on the stairs.

Ori gives it carefully anyway.

“I love you,” she says near his ear. ᴼ

He has both hands occupied, one with Yuka's and one with Frieren's. He cannot lift either arm far enough to close it round her.

“Love you,” he says. “So much.” ᴹ

Ori stays until Shoko tells her he needs the space to breathe comfortably again. She sits back at once.

Her eyes are wet now, too.

“I'm not only saying it because she did,” she tells him. ᴼ

He manages a small nod.

“I know.” ᴹ

* * *

Ori wants a pencil.

Frieren finds one in the bag she brought with her book. She asks whether Ori needs another piece of paper, but Ori shakes her head.

There is a little room left beside the drawing, if she writes carefully.

She puts the page on the seat of the chair so she has something solid beneath it. Then she kneels to write, shielding the words with her forearm out of habit.

Mau turns his eye away.

Yuka watches him do it.

When Ori finishes, she holds the page up.

“You can see this bit,” she says. ᴼ

Under the two dragons, in a different hand:

*He asked me. I showed him. I call him Dad too.*

Below it, smaller:

*Mum is Mum. Yuka knows.*

Mau looks at the words for a long time.

“She can't get it back,” Ori says, not quite a question. ᴼ

He takes a careful breath.

“No.” ᴹ

Ori runs her finger along the edge, avoiding the pencil.

“I can keep it, though.” ᴼ

“It's yours.” ᴹ

She folds it along the old creases. Three times. The new writing disappears inside with the old.

Then she puts it in her own pocket.

* * *

Frieren takes Ori's hand when Shoko ends the visit.

Ori doesn't ask to stay longer. She has something she wants to take to her room, somewhere the corner won't bend while she is keeping it.

At the door she looks back.

“I'll show you again when you can go,” she says. ᴼ

Mau looks at Shoko before he looks at Ori. The movement is so small that Yuka nearly misses it.

“When,” he says. ᴹ

“When,” Ori agrees. ᴼ

She goes with Frieren.

Beru rises to open the door, then returns to his chair. He doesn't propose a procession. He sits with his hands together and pretends not to be crying as fiercely as he ever has.

Yuka picks up the empty paper box.

“I'll put this back,” she says. “Later.” ʸ

She sets it on the table. Mau's ring is beside it, and her message, and the small wrapped thing they have kept with the rest of his belongings.

His fingers move against hers.

“Kept it,” he says. ᴹ

“You did.” ʸ

He watches the empty box until his eye begins to close.

There is so much they still have to tell one another. Yuka knows that. His wrist is still supported, his foot still can't take him home, and there are rooms he has not begun to name.

For now she helps him settle against the pillow.

“I'm tired,” he says. ᴹ

“You can be.” ʸ

He looks at her once more before sleep takes him.

This time he doesn't try to make it sound like anything else.
