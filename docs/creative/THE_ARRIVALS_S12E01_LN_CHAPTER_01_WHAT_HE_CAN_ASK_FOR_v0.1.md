# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 1 — What He Can Ask For

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S12E01
**Continuity state:** B+218, morning through afternoon, direct continuation of S11 recovery. The left wrist received limited correction the previous day; the major old finger deficit persists. Shoko explains the next back/leg correction and obtains Mau's participation; Ciel/Noise limit activity to requested medical support. No new construction or statistical upgrade. Yuka remains close and helps with his hair by his choice. A feeding-related response is acknowledged without forced disclosure; full listening remains pending until he can participate. No pregnancy, trial or new enemy move.

*When: Mau asks Shoko whether she has to hurt him again, she answers before asking him to be brave about it.*

“Some of it will hurt,” she says. “I'm going to help with that. I won't begin without telling you what we're doing.” ˢʰ

He lies still.

Yuka is beside him, her hand where he can find it without turning his wrist. The wrist looks more like it used to this morning. Moving it still hurts. The fingers that have been stiff for years remain stiff.

There is no single movement he can make to check whether he is repaired.

Shoko pulls a chair close enough that he doesn't have to lift his head to look at her.

“Your back has been holding you in a position that made walking possible there. It isn't a position I want you learning to live with here.” ˢʰ

“Walked,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ˢʰ

He wants to explain that he walked a very long way like that. Not to argue. It feels relevant. He found the side he could lean towards, the distance he could go before the pain changed, the way to get up without needing both hands.

He had got quite good at it.

Shoko doesn't make him say all of that before she understands the objection.

“It kept you moving,” she says. “I'm not asking you to be ashamed of that. I want you to be able to move without paying for every step.” ˢʰ

He looks at the blanket over his legs.

“And the foot?” ᴹ

“Part of the same problem. We work on it in an order your body can manage.” ˢʰ

Yuka rubs her thumb once along the side of his hand. She is looking at Shoko, too. Listening to the answer he is trying to decide whether he can bear.

“Will I…” ᴹ

He stops. It is a question he has asked himself so many times without words that making them now feels like giving something away.

Shoko waits.

“Walk right?” ᴹ

She glances at the record beside her, then back at him.

“I can see things we can correct. We have help you didn't have in there. I expect improvement, and I'm going to check each part with you instead of promising a result I haven't seen.” ˢʰ

He studies her.

It is not the answer he wanted. It is an answer he can recognise as hers.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Noise is already preparing.

Mau feels it moving around the part of him that has held the same wrong shape for most of a year. It knows where the work will begin. It has had much longer to look at it than Shoko has.

There is a reflex in it that has become a reflex in him: if something is about to fail, hold it together harder.

*Wait,* Mau asks.

Noise stops the preparation.

*It moves.* ᴺ

*I know.*

*Bad if it moves.* ᴺ

Mau looks towards Shoko. She is speaking to Yuta at the other side of the bed, explaining what she wants him to maintain and where he must let her change the shape.

He can hear every word. Nobody has decided he will be easier to handle if they don't explain.

*Ask her,* Mau tells Noise. *Through Ciel. Before we hold anything.*

A hesitation.

*We can follow her plan,* Ciel says. *I will tell you before the support changes. You can ask again at any point.* ᶜ

Noise waits for the first instruction.

Mau does, too.

* * *

Yuka stays until he is asleep.

It takes longer this morning. He watches the door, then the window, then the place where Beru has set his hands, as though leaving out any one thing will make it unsafe to close his eye.

“I'll be in this chair when you wake,” she says. ʸ

He looks at her.

“If I get up, I'll tell Ciel where I'm going. You can ask her.” ʸ

He thinks about it. His eye moves to the cup on the table.

“Water first,” he says. ᴹ

“First?” ʸ

“For you.” ᴹ

She looks at her own cup, untouched beside his.

“All right.” ʸ

She drinks where he can see. He lets out a little breath he might once have hidden in a joke.

Then he looks at Shoko.

“Ready.” ᴹ

She comes closer before doing anything else.

“All right. I'm here.” ˢʰ

* * *

He wakes to the sound of Yuka arguing with Mai about a blanket.

Not an important argument. Something about which end goes where and why the folded edge has to be on that side. Mai thinks Yuka is inventing a problem because she has been made to sit down for too long. Yuka thinks Mai has put the pattern upside down on purpose.

He keeps his eye shut and listens.

His body feels different. Not better in a way he can trust yet. There is pain where he expects numbness and an absence where he has been expecting the same dragging ache every time he breathes in.

He listens to the blankets instead of testing it.

“He'll tell me,” Yuka says. ʸ

“He'll say whichever answer makes you happy.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“Then I'll know you're right and be very upset about it.” ʸ

“Naturally.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau opens his eye.

“Upside down,” he says. ᴹ

They both look at him.

Yuka looks at the blanket. Mai looks at his face, then follows his gaze.

It is upside down.

“You,” Mai tells him, “have been awake for ten seconds.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“Efficient.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs before she can stop herself. Mai folds the blanket properly with a force that suggests she will remember this when he can defend himself.

He watches them with the beginning of a smile, until the effort of staying awake catches up with him.

Yuka sees that, too.

She doesn't ask him to keep performing it.

* * *

Later, Shoko asks him to move his toes.

He has been dreading the question since morning. There is so much more help here. If they ask and it still doesn't happen, he will have to see that in their faces.

He looks at the end of the blanket.

The movement comes.

Small. Not all of what Shoko asked for. Enough to make her ask him to do it again instead of changing the subject.

The second time, Yuka sees it.

She doesn't cheer. She puts her hand against her mouth and nods when Shoko looks at her, because neither of them wants to frighten the little movement away by treating it like a promise.

Mau looks between them.

“That was better?” ᴹ

“Yes,” Shoko says. “That was better.” ˢʰ

He closes his eye after she has finished checking.

Yuka keeps her face turned towards his until he opens it again.

* * *

He asks her to move his hair.

It has been bothering him since the yard. Not every moment; there have been too many other things. But now it drags against his cheek when he turns the little distance he is allowed, and he doesn't know where to put it.

Yuka gets the comb and asks Shoko before she adjusts his pillow.

She works from the ends first, taking small sections, stopping whenever the pull reaches his scalp. Some of it has already been washed. She finds dust where it should not still be possible for dust to hide.

“How short?” she asks. ʸ

He thinks of the shape he had in the mirror before he went to inspect an ordinary gate. He cannot quite put his face inside it now.

“Not all,” he says. ᴹ

She shows him how much she means, holding it between two fingers where he can see.

He chooses a little less.

She follows his choice.

The white is still there when the worst of the length is gone. She doesn't pretend she hasn't seen it. She also doesn't ask what each new piece means.

When she is done, his face is clear of the loose ends. He can turn towards her without the hair touching the raw place near his jaw.

“Better?” ʸ

He looks at her.

“You like it?” ᴹ

She puts the comb down.

“Yes. Do you?” ʸ

He tests the tiny turn again.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Then that's settled.” ʸ

* * *

At supper, his hand stops halfway towards the spoon.

Yuka sees it before he makes himself finish reaching. She has seen the same stillness twice now. There is nothing wrong with the spoon that Shoko has been able to find.

“Do you want me to put it down?” she asks. ʸ

He looks at her hand.

Then, very quietly, “For a second.” ᴹ

She puts it down.

He breathes until his face begins to change back.

“Was there something in there?” she asks. “About eating?” ʸ

Mau watches the place where the spoon lies.

For a moment he can feel the weight of a different thing in his hand. He can't put a date to it. It isn't the dragon, isn't the bread beside it, isn't any part of the year he has just come out of.

There is a woman he knows, and she has let him eat first.

He pushes the memory away so hard it takes a moment to find Yuka again.

“Old,” he says. ᴹ

She nods slowly.

“Do you want to tell me now?” ʸ

He looks at her face. At the question in it, and the effort she is making not to turn it into a demand because she is afraid of what he will put away if she lets him wait.

“Not yet.” ᴹ

“Okay.” ʸ

His fingers start to close, expecting something else.

She keeps her hand where he can see it.

“I will ask again,” she says. “Because I want to know. You can tell me when you can.” ʸ

He looks back at the spoon.

After a little while he asks her to pick it up.
