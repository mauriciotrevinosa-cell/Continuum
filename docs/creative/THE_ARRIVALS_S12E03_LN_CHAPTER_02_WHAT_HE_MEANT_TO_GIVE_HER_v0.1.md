# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 6 — What He Meant to Give Her

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S12E03
**Continuity state:** B+221 night, after the short supper visit. Private Mau/Yuka conversation while medical support remains nearby and Ciel connected. Mau describes the actual rehearsals and beliefs he held inside, beyond admitting he misunderstood: a guest room, avoiding shared space while Ori visited, continuing protection while removing himself from Yuka's life, and the belief she might be better off if he never returned. These are his wounded conclusions, not Yuka's wishes or evidence of manipulation by her. Yuka is hurt by the truth, stays, answers it directly and does not punish the disclosure. No immediate cure of fear or bodily recovery.

*When: Yuka reaches for the lamp, Mau asks her to leave it a little longer.*

“Are you hurting?” she asks. ʸ

“Some. Not why.” ᴹ

She takes her hand away from the lamp.

The visit to supper has tired him. He is back against the pillows with the cardigan folded over the foot of the bed, his hair pushed clear where she brushed it away to kiss him. Shoko has checked him. There is nothing left to do tonight that cannot be done lying still.

Yuka sits on the edge of the other bed, facing him.

“What is it?” ʸ

He looks at their hands. They aren't touching yet.

“What you said. About wanting to know.” ᴹ

She becomes still.

He has said enough today to let himself stop. She told him that. He can remember it, can reach for the permission and find it waiting where she left it.

He doesn't want to use it to put this away again.

“There's something I haven't told you properly.” ᴹ

Yuka moves from the edge of the bed into the chair beside his. Not because the distance is large. Because she knows when he is measuring it.

“Okay.” ʸ

* * *

“I wasn't just thinking you were angry.” ᴹ

Yuka nods slowly.

“I know.” ʸ

“I don't think you do. All of it.” ᴹ

He hears how that sounds and looks up quickly.

“Not because you didn't listen. I keep telling you the bit that sounds…” ᴹ

He stops, searches for a word.

“Smaller?” she asks. ʸ

He nods.

Yuka lays her hand on the mattress where he can take it. He does, settling his fingers against hers.

“I thought about what I'd say when I came out. At first it was just that I'd heard you. That you were right.” ᴹ

His eye moves towards the dark window.

“I said it out loud. I thought if I said it enough, I'd get it right when I saw you.” ᴹ

“How often?” ʸ

He looks back at her.

“Most days. At the beginning.” ᴹ

She takes a breath, lets it out carefully.

He watches her do it. He had prepared for anger, tears, a request to stop. He hasn't prepared for her making space for the next sentence when he can see how much the last one cost her.

“Then it was the guest room,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka's fingers move once beneath his.

“I wasn't offering it because I wanted to sleep somewhere else. I thought that would make it easier for you. I could be nearby if you needed something, and you wouldn't have to…” ᴹ

He looks down.

“Have me there all the time.” ᴹ

“Mau—” ʸ

“I know what you said when I came out. I know. I'm trying to tell you what it was in there.” ᴹ

She closes her mouth.

Then she nods, though her eyes are already full.

“Tell me.” ʸ

* * *

He tells her about walking around the dragon.

Not the wound this time. The laps. The places he stopped to rest, the words he tried while looking at a wall that never answered him.

“I worked out where Ori could come,” he says. “I thought I'd talk to Frieren. So you wouldn't have to see me just because she wanted me.” ᴹ

Yuka's face changes.

He nearly stops.

Her hand closes round his fingers before he can mistake the expression for a request.

“I'm listening,” she says. ʸ

“I thought maybe later I'd find somewhere else in the village. Still close enough. I wasn't going to stop looking after the house. I didn't mean that. I could still do the watch, the gates, whatever…” ᴹ

He hears himself. Stops.

“Whatever meant I wasn't asking you to keep me.” ᴹ

Yuka lowers her eyes to their hands.

He waits for her to lift them again.

When she does, tears have gone down both cheeks.

“You were planning how to leave our home.” ʸ

He swallows.

“I thought I was giving it back to you.” ᴹ

The words hurt her more than anything he has said so far.

He sees it happen. The little movement of her mouth before she presses it closed, the breath that won't go where she wants it, her free hand going to the ring on her finger as though he has asked for it.

He wants to take the sentence back.

He doesn't.

* * *

For a while Yuka cannot speak.

Mau doesn't fill the quiet. He looks at her, frightened enough that she can see the effort it takes him not to begin telling her it wasn't that bad, that it had only been a thought, that he hadn't really meant all of it.

He had meant it. He has finally trusted her with that.

She wipes her face with her free hand.

“I was putting things in the drawer,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't understand.

“In our room. Before the argument. I was annoyed because your things were on my side again. I moved some of mine because I liked them mixed up. I was going to complain about it and leave them there anyway.” ʸ

Her voice breaks.

“And you spent months thinking I'd be happier if you took all of yours out.” ʸ

He closes his eye.

She leans closer, keeps her hand beneath his.

“That hurts to hear. I'm not going to pretend it doesn't.” ʸ

He opens his eye immediately.

“Not because you told me. Because you were there thinking it, and I couldn't get to you to tell you no.” ʸ

He watches her face.

She lets him see it, even though it is the expression he has been trying to spare her from.

“I wanted you in the drawer. On the sofa. In the bed. In the middle of my stupid day asking what I was looking for. I wanted you to stop shutting a door when something hurt.” ʸ

He begins crying without looking away.

“I didn't want you to move out of the room.” ʸ

* * *

Noise is quiet.

Mau can feel it close by, the way he felt it beside the dragon when it kept bringing him the rest of Yuka's words. He has not forgotten that it tried.

“Noise showed me,” he says. “You saying you loved me. Come find me. The whole thing. Not just the sentence.” ᴹ

Yuka nods through her tears.

“But I kept thinking you'd say that anyway. Because you didn't want to hurt me. Because you knew…” ᴹ

He looks at their hands, at her ring.

“You knew how much of everything was you. And I thought that wasn't fair to make you carry. So if you couldn't ask me to go, I should understand without making you say it.” ᴹ

Yuka shakes her head. Not at him for believing it. At the answer she couldn't put into that room a year ago.

“You can ask me what I mean,” she says. “You can be frightened of the answer. You can tell me you don't believe me yet. But I'm the one who gets to tell you what I want.” ʸ

He nods.

“I know.” ᴹ

“Do you want me to say it again?” ʸ

He looks at her. He could save her that much. Say he heard the first time, let her rest, let himself put away the part that still checks the chair every time he wakes.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka brings his hand against her cheek, carefully enough that his wrist stays supported.

“I want to be your wife. I want you in our home. I want to go on being annoyed about how you fold things and steal the blanket and forget you're meant to ask what I want before you try to fix it.” ʸ

His mouth moves at one corner, through the tears.

“I want to help you with the things that hurt. I want you to help me with mine. If I ask for an afternoon by myself one day, I mean an afternoon. I'll tell you. I'm not hiding an ending in it.” ʸ

He presses his fingers a little more firmly into her palm.

She stays close enough that he can feel her breathe against them.

* * *

There is another part.

He has been waiting for the moment when he can leave it unsaid without it feeling like the same old thing. The moment doesn't come.

“Near the end,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka lowers their hands to the mattress again.

He has already told the others he stopped eating. Told them what Noise asked and why he got up. There is a version of it that is honest enough to be true and still doesn't tell her what he was thinking.

He has used that space before.

“Sometimes I thought you'd be better off if I didn't come back at all.” ᴹ

Yuka goes very still.

He looks at her, makes himself finish before the stillness can become an excuse to change what he said.

“Not because you told me to. I know you didn't. It was… everything getting smaller. Nothing left I could imagine doing except getting through the next thing. And I kept thinking coming back would just make you have to work out how to tell me.” ᴹ

He cannot keep looking at her face for the next part.

“I didn't want to make you.” ᴹ

For a moment all Yuka can hear is the sound of the counter that isn't in the yard anymore.

Two hundred days. Three hundred. Standing in front of something that would not let her in, asking an ant whether the man she loved was still alive.

He was there thinking he might spare her by never coming out.

She puts her forehead against the safe part of his hand and cries.

* * *

He doesn't pull his hand away.

He wants to. Not from her touch. From what he has done to her face, to her breathing, to the ordinary night they might have had if he had said he was tired and let her lower the lamp.

*Stay with her,* Ciel says quietly. *You don't need a different answer for what you felt.* ᶜ

He stays.

Yuka lifts her head after a while. Her face is wet, her nose pink to his eye, every tired part of her painfully clear in a world that has almost nothing else in colour.

“I don't want a life that's easier because you aren't in it,” she says. ʸ

He swallows.

“I don't want you dead. I don't want you living in another room so I don't have to look at you. I wanted you out so badly I was angry at everyone who could go where I couldn't.” ʸ

He listens without trying to answer her before she is done.

“I can survive an argument with you. I can be furious and still want you next to me. You don't have to disappear to make my feelings easier.” ʸ

The tears spill out of his eye again.

“I know that now.” ᴹ

She studies his face for a long moment.

“All the way?” she asks. ʸ

He could say yes.

She has given him so much. He could give her that one thing back and make it true later, when his head has caught up, when the chair no longer feels temporary.

“Not every minute,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka closes her eyes.

Then opens them and nods.

“Okay.” ʸ

The word is broken, but it is hers.

* * *

“Do you think that now?” she asks after a little while. ʸ

He looks at her.

“That you'd be better off not coming back.” ʸ

He thinks before answering. She can see him doing it, can see how badly he wants to give her something quick and safe.

“I want to be here,” he says at last. “With you. I don't want to go away.” ᴹ

She lets out a breath she hasn't been able to finish.

“But when I wake up, sometimes I have to find out again.” ᴹ

Her thumb moves against his hand.

“Then we'll tell Shoko and Ciel that part, too,” she says. “So it isn't only something you have to settle while I'm asleep.” ʸ

He nods.

“And if it feels like it did in that room?” ʸ

He looks towards the door.

Shoko is a call away. Ciel is with him. Noise is here. None of them need him to have made the thought reasonable before he says it.

“I'll tell you. One of you. Before I decide it's the truth.” ᴹ

Yuka brings his hand to her mouth and kisses the uninjured part.

She doesn't ask for a promise never to feel it again.

* * *

“I didn't know in the yard,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks up.

“When I said I knew. I heard you crying and I just wanted…” ᴹ

He stops.

“I know,” she says. ʸ

“You asked me not to do that.” ᴹ

“You could barely stay awake.” ʸ

“I'm awake now.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long time.

Then she nods.

“Yes. You are.” ʸ

His fingers shift under hers, trying for a hold that he can keep without hurting.

“I'm sorry I made you ask for the same thing so many times.” ᴹ

She takes a breath. It shakes. She lets him hear it.

“Thank you for giving it to me tonight.” ʸ

* * *

He asks her to come closer.

This time she doesn't need to make him say the rest. She checks the space Shoko has made safe beside his pillow and bends over him, one arm resting on the mattress instead of across his chest.

His cheek touches her shoulder.

It is an awkward embrace. He cannot lift his arm the way he wants. She cannot lie down without moving things that must stay where they are.

They stay in it anyway.

Yuka cries into his shortened hair. Mau stops trying to keep his own crying quiet enough that it won't upset her more.

For a long time neither says anything useful.

Then he whispers, “I wanted this.” ᴹ

Her hand settles against the back of his head.

“Me too.” ʸ

She doesn't move away when he starts crying harder.
