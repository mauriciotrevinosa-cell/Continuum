# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 7 — When She Comes Back

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S12E04
**Continuity state:** B+222, morning and early afternoon. With Mau's agreement, Yuka and he tell Shoko/Ciel the part about his worst belief; it does not become the entire house's unchosen disclosure. He says he wants to remain and is still frightened on waking. Yuka takes a stated break with Mai while he has company, processes her own hurt and returns. No romantic withdrawal, silent punishment or demand that he console her into retracting his truth. An ordinary kiss and invitation replace compulsory reassurance. Physical care continues.

*When: Yuka tells him she is going to wash her face, he starts to apologise and stops.*

“All right,” he says. ᴹ

She watches him for a moment.

It is morning. The curtains are open, Shoko is changing something in the little tray beside his bed, and the room has the unremarkable clutter of people who have spent another night in it.

Yuka's cup. The paper he asked her to keep near. A comb with a strand of white caught in it that she hasn't noticed yet.

“Mai's outside. I might sit with her for a while after.” ʸ

He looks towards the door, then back at Yuka.

“You need to?” ᴹ

She thinks before answering. He did that for her last night. She can do it now.

“Yes. I want to see her. And I want to come back and have lunch with you.” ʸ

His fingers shift on the blanket.

“Okay.” ᴹ

She bends and kisses his forehead. His eye closes at the touch, then opens again before she straightens.

“You don't have to make me feel better before you go,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka has to look down at the little space between them for a moment.

Then she kisses him once more.

“I wanted that one.” ʸ

* * *

They have already told Shoko.

It was harder in daylight. Mau watched Yuka set her cup down and knew she was waiting for him to decide whether he still wanted to say it without the shelter of the dark room and her hand against his face.

He did.

Shoko listened to the whole sentence before she asked anything. Ciel stayed with him through the pauses. Nobody asked him to prove that he loved Yuka by promising he would never think something terrible again.

“I want to be here,” he said. “I mean that. Sometimes I wake up and the other answer gets there first.” ᴹ

Shoko asked what helped him find the room he was in. What frightened him before he could speak. Whether he could tell Ciel when it happened while the others were sleeping.

He could.

“You don't have to wait until you can explain why,” she told him. ˢʰ

He looked at Yuka.

She was listening. She hadn't asked to be excused from the answer she had wanted.

Now Shoko stays while Yuka goes to wash, and Mau lets the door close without trying to hear everything on the other side of it.

* * *

Mai doesn't ask for the whole conversation.

She brings a clean cloth, gives Yuka water, sits with her on the bench near the courtyard. Momo has left a second cup beside them before going to finish something she promised Fern.

For a little while Yuka keeps smoothing the cloth across her knees.

“He told me what he thought,” she says at last. ʸ

Mai waits.

“About leaving our room. How to keep being there for Ori without making me see him.” ʸ

Mai's expression changes. She looks towards the clinic door, then back at Yuka.

Yuka shakes her head.

“I'm not asking you to go in and tell him off.” ʸ

“I wasn't standing up.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka gives a small, helpless laugh.

“Your face was.” ʸ

Mai lets out a breath and shifts back against the bench.

“All right. I'm staying here.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka looks down at the wet place in the cloth.

“I wanted him to tell me. And he did. He looked at me and gave me the thing he'd been trying so hard to make sound different.” ʸ

Her voice starts to go. She waits, gets it back.

“I hated hearing it.” ʸ

Mai nods.

“Of course you did.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“I kept thinking about our things. How I put his book back on the table because he'd want it when he got home. He was taking me out of his mornings while I was doing that.” ʸ

She covers her face.

Mai moves close enough that Yuka can lean against her. She does, crying into the cloth with an anger that has nowhere useful to go.

She is angry at the room that kept him. At herself for a sentence she cannot go back and replace. At every long day he had to make up an answer without being able to ask her what she meant.

She loves him so much she has to put a hand against her chest to make the next breath happen properly.

Mai keeps her arm round her until it does.

* * *

“He saw how much it hurt,” Yuka says after a while. “I could see him wanting to change it.” ʸ

“Did he?” ᴹᵃⁱ

She shakes her head.

“No. He stayed with it. Even the part that was worse.” ʸ

She doesn't tell Mai that part. It belongs to the conversation Mau agreed to have with Shoko, and Mai has not asked to be handed something private simply because she is here.

“I don't want him to think telling me was the mistake.” ʸ

Mai looks at her for a moment.

“Then tell him that. He'll still see that you're hurt. You don't have to make that disappear to mean it.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka rubs at one eye with the heel of her hand.

“What if he believes the crying more?” ʸ

“Then he can ask. And you can answer. You did a lot of that before either of you knew how to be married.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka looks up.

Mai offers her the second cup.

“You don't have to become two different people to get through this,” she says. ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Inside, Mau asks Shoko whether Yuka cried much while he was asleep.

Shoko looks at him over the record she is checking.

“Ask her.” ˢʰ

He looks down.

“I didn't mean…” ᴹ

“I know. But you have something to talk about with her. I won't give you a version of her you can look after without asking what she needs.” ˢʰ

He studies the blanket.

Shoko puts the record aside.

“Are you worried she regrets hearing you?” ˢʰ

After a while, he nods.

“You can ask that, too.” ˢʰ

He looks towards the door again.

Shoko lets him look. Then she asks whether he wants help sitting a little higher before his food comes.

He says yes, and tells her when the movement starts to hurt.

* * *

Yuka comes back carrying both their cups.

Her eyes are sore. Her hair is tied differently. She has washed her face and still looks like someone who has cried.

Mau watches her cross the room.

“Hi,” she says. ʸ

“Hi.” ᴹ

She puts the cups down and moves the chair into the place she wants, a little closer than it was this morning.

He waits until she has sat.

“Do you wish I hadn't told you?” ᴹ

Yuka looks straight at him.

“No.” ʸ

His fingers curl a little in the blanket.

She reaches towards them, stops at the edge of his hand until he turns it for her.

“I wish you hadn't had to think it. I wish I'd said what I meant better. I'm glad you told me.” ʸ

He looks at her eyes.

“You cried.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸ

He waits for the part that will make those two answers agree.

She doesn't hide either one.

“I can cry and still want to know you.” ʸ

He lets out a breath. Looks down at their hands.

“What do you need?” he asks. ᴹ

Yuka thinks.

Then she moves the little tray onto the bed where he can reach it.

“Lunch. With my husband. And I want him to let me be upset without deciding he has to move out because of it.” ʸ

He looks up. There is a little of the old challenge in her face, the one he recognises from before everything hurt.

“I can try that.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸ

She bends towards him, then pauses when his hand moves to her sleeve.

“Here?” he asks. ᴹ

He touches his own mouth with the tip of one finger. The swelling has gone down enough that Shoko no longer has to remind him of the joint every time he speaks.

Yuka smiles, and it makes the tiredness in her face look different.

“Very carefully.” ʸ

She kisses him.

It is brief, gentle, interrupted by both of them smiling before it is quite finished.

When she sits down, he is still holding the edge of her sleeve.

She leaves it there while they eat.
