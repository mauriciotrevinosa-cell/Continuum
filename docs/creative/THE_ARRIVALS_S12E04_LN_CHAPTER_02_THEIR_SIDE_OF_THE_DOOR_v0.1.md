# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 8 — Their Side of the Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S12E04
**Continuity state:** B+222 afternoon. With rest and his chosen scope, Mau speaks with Maki, Yuta, Mai and Frieren; Yuka and Rimuru/Ciel accompany him. The others know his prior actions and injury outline, not every detail from the first listening. He chooses one short, non-graphic memory of trying to reach water during the early back injury; sharing is bounded and stopped before the rest. They describe their own rescue work and reaffirm family without removing accountability for secrecy. Ori's paper remains in her custody. The Architect's later fate is not resolved by this conversation.

*When: Mai sees the cup on the far side of the bed, she moves it before sitting down.*

Mau watches it arrive beside his hand.

“Can reach that now,” he says. ᴹ

“Excellent. Reach it here.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka smiles without looking up from the blanket she is folding. Mai notices, gives Mau a look that suggests she has been outnumbered on purpose, and sits beside Maki.

Yuta has taken the chair nearest the foot of the bed. Frieren is on the other side, where Mau can see her without turning towards the unreliable edge of his vision.

Rimuru sits against the wall with the record they may not need.

Nobody has brought a list of questions.

That makes starting harder.

* * *

“Yuka told you some?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Maki nods.

“The parts you said she could. That you want to tell us more yourself.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks at the cup Mai has moved.

“I thought if I kept working on everything, I could get ahead of the next thing that would hurt one of you. I could see things we hadn't finished. It felt…” ᴹ

He rubs a thumb against the edge of the blanket.

“Wrong to leave them there.” ᴹ

Yuta looks at him.

“Even after you knew how much hiding it was hurting them?” ʸᵗ

Mau nods.

It takes him a moment to do the rest.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Yuta breathes out slowly.

“I knew you'd worry. I knew you'd tell me to wait. I thought it would be easier to explain once I had something that worked.” ᴹ

Mai looks at Yuka, then at Mau.

“So by the time we got to have an opinion, the bit that could hurt you would be over.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looks down.

“That was the idea.” ᴹ

“And we'd be meant to be relieved.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He doesn't answer immediately.

Yuka leaves the blanket folded on her knees. She is listening to him decide whether he can hear the shape of it when someone else says it.

“I didn't think about what came after that,” he admits. ᴹ

* * *

Maki leans forward, forearms on her knees.

“You told us before the trap. You answered questions you didn't enjoy. I want you to remember that part, too.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Mau looks up.

“You made a start. Then the argument happened, and the Architect did what it did. Those things aren't one punishment you earned by being difficult.” ᴹᵃᵏ

His hand goes very still.

“I gave her Ashborn,” he says. ᴹ

“You gave Makima information she could use,” Yuta says. “We've talked about why that matters. She chose what to do with it. The Architect chose what to build.” ʸᵗ

Mau swallows.

“I keep trying to find the bit where I could have stopped it.” ᴹ

“I know,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks at her.

There is no simple answer in her face, no claim that if he had said the right thing there would never have been another room.

“We'll change what we can change,” she says. “You're here for that. You don't have to keep going back through the whole year to earn it.” ᴹᵃᵏ

* * *

It is Frieren who asks about the first days.

Not a demand for the worst thing. She asks what he did when he realised the restrictions would not let him use the help he had brought.

Mau looks at the floor beside the bed.

“At first I thought I could manage. I had enough things left. It was just a different way of using them.” ᴹ

He looks at Rimuru, then back at her.

“Then when I learned it, the field changed.” ᴹ

“We saw the changes,” Frieren says. ᶠ

He nods.

“I didn't know what you could see.” ᴹ

“Not you.” ᶠ

That answer reaches him differently.

He has known, in the general way he knows how much time passed, that the house had no view inside. He hasn't tried to imagine them watching an empty frame and being told only that something had been made harder.

“You didn't know when…” ᴹ

He stops, looks at Yuta.

Yuta shakes his head.

“Not until we got to you.” ʸᵗ

Mau looks at his cup again.

“I can show you a little,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

He chooses it himself.

A short piece, after the first terrible injury to his back. No enemy, no strike, no open wound. The place where he lay, the things he had bought to make staying there possible, the water he put where he thought he would be able to reach it.

Rimuru checks the beginning and end with him. Ciel keeps the boundary they choose. Each person is asked before the memory is shared.

Yuka stays close enough that he can look at her when it ends.

For a few seconds there is cold stone beneath a body that won't move the way it should.

A cloth against his cheek. The smell of damp fabric. Light from a place too far away to be useful.

The water is there. He knows exactly where. He has to keep stopping between the thought of reaching and the hand moving, because moving anything pulls at something he cannot afford to open again.

He tells himself he will do it on the next breath.

Then on the next.

The piece ends before he reaches it.

* * *

Mai is staring at the cup she moved.

Yuta has both hands flat on his knees. Maki's eyes are closed. Frieren is looking at Mau with an expression he remembers from other returns and has never quite known what to do with.

He begins, “I did get—” ᴹ

“I know,” Mai says. ᴹᵃⁱ

Her voice is rough.

She puts her hand on the mattress beside his, not covering it, not making him turn his wrist.

“I know you got it in the end. Let me be upset about how long it took.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looks at her.

She has tears on her face and an anger he cannot repair by saying he has been worse.

He lets her have both.

* * *

“I called you,” he says to Yuta after a while. ᴹ

Yuta looks up.

“Not because I thought it would work. I knew you couldn't hear. Ciel, too. Shoko. Beru.” ᴹ

He stops before Yuka's name because she is sitting so close, then says it anyway.

“Yuka.” ᴹ

She puts her hand beneath his.

“Sometimes just because I wanted to say it to somebody who wasn't the thing telling me what the next room was for.” ᴹ

Yuta rubs his hands together once, then stops doing that.

“I wish I'd heard,” he says. ʸᵗ

Mau begins shaking his head.

“I know. You didn't…” ᴹ

“I know I couldn't,” Yuta says. “I still wish I had.” ʸᵗ

Mau looks at him for a long moment.

Then he nods.

It is harder to let people wish they could have helped than to explain why they shouldn't feel responsible. He doesn't know what he is meant to do with the love when it cannot change what happened.

Yuka's hand stays under his while he works out that he may not have to do anything yet.

* * *

Frieren tells him about Ori in the yard.

How she came out in her nightclothes. How she trusted Beru's answer because he would know. The way she fell asleep against Yuka while all the adults kept watching.

Mau looks at Yuka.

“She was with you?” ᴹ

Yuka nods.

“She held my sleeve. Nearly all night.” ʸ

His face begins to change.

He had worked out a way to keep their lives apart, while those lives were already together outside the door. Ori asleep against his wife. Frieren on the ground beside them. Maki making sure somebody lay down. People who had not been waiting for him to decide who could bear whom.

“I thought…” ᴹ

He stops. Looks at Frieren.

“That you'd have to help me work out when to see her. If Yuka didn't want me there.” ᴹ

Frieren is quiet for a moment.

“Yuka told me that part,” she says. “You said she could.” ᶠ

He nods.

She puts her hand on the edge of the blanket.

“Ori doesn't have to lose any of us to love the others. You've been helping her understand that. You can let it be true for you as well.” ᶠ

He closes his eye.

Yuka leans against the side of his pillow, close enough that her hair brushes his forehead.

He lets her stay there while he cries.

* * *

When they have sat for a little while, Maki asks what he wants them to do differently.

He looks up, startled.

“You already…” ᴹ

“Tell us anyway.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He thinks about the door. The hours he has spent waiting to see whether a chair will still be there when he opens his eye. The way any voice outside can sound like the beginning of a decision he hasn't been allowed to hear.

“Tell me where you're going,” he says. “For a while. If you're with me, and then you need to leave. I know it's stupid—” ᴹ

“No,” Mai says. ᴹᵃⁱ

He stops.

“Just ask the thing,” she says, more gently. ᴹᵃⁱ

He takes a breath.

“And let me say I miss you without deciding it means I can't be left by myself. Sometimes I just want to say it.” ᴹ

Maki nods.

“We can do that.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks at Yuta, then Frieren. They nod, too.

“And you tell us before you put something dangerous in motion,” Yuta says. “When it would still matter for us to answer.” ʸᵗ

Mau looks down at his hands.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Even if you think we'll say no,” Maki adds. ᴹᵃᵏ

He nods.

“Especially then.” ᴹ

* * *

They don't leave feeling better about the year.

They know a little more of it. Enough to make the scars mean time as well as damage. Enough to understand why *I fixed it* sometimes means he had no one to ask how badly he was still hurt.

Mai touches his hair before she gets up. She has to brush it away from her own wet cheek afterwards and looks annoyed with the entire arrangement.

Mau almost smiles.

“Sorry.” ᴹ

She points at him.

He stops, then gives her the smallest, most inconvenient smile he has managed in days.

“Hair,” he says. ᴹ

Mai looks at the strand caught against her sleeve.

“Terrible,” she tells him. ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka laughs into his shoulder without putting her weight there.

The sound follows them all the way to the door.
