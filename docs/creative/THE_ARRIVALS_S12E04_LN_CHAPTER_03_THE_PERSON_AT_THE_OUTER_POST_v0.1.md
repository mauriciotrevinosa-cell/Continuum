# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 9 — The Person at the Outer Post

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S12E04
**Continuity state:** B+222 evening, after the chosen family listening. Makima sends a written request through the existing exterior shadow-watch route; the ordinary soldier delivers paper under prior instructions and admits nobody. She knows the Architect's instance has closed and he no longer answers her, not Mau's private condition or inner conversations. Mau asks to receive her after rest, in the clinic, with Shoko's medical limit and Rimuru/Yuka present by choice. The house objects openly; discussion precedes the decision. DIO's location, guests and private memory archive remain undisclosed. No new arrival, guard species, power or breach of the ward.

*When: Fern reads the name on the outside of the envelope, she doesn't put it with the other visiting requests.*

She takes it to Rimuru.

The soldier from the outer post has already returned to its place. It has done exactly what the watch was told to do with a written request. It has not answered the woman, let her cross the perimeter, or invented a reason to make an exception.

Rimuru reads the name.

Then he reads the short message Fern has opened.

“Does Mau know?” he asks. ᴿ

“Not yet.” ᶠᵉ

He looks towards the clinic door.

Inside, somebody laughs once, quietly. Mai, perhaps. The people who have just heard part of a year are still putting the chairs back where they came from.

Rimuru folds the message along its crease.

“I'll tell him.” ᴿ

* * *

Mau asks him to read it aloud.

He can read it himself. He simply doesn't want to hold anything else for a moment. Yuka has left his hand resting against hers while Shoko checks the support beneath his foot.

Rimuru looks at Shoko before beginning.

She sees the name.

“Absolutely not tonight.” ˢʰ

Mau looks from her to Rimuru.

“What does she want?” ᴹ

Rimuru reads.

The Architect is no longer answering. His instance is closed. Makima wishes to know whether Mau returned, and whether he will speak to her. If he cannot receive a visitor, a written answer will do.

There is no description of his injuries. No claim to know what happened in the room. No affectionate name for him that she has acquired a right to use.

That makes it easier to read and no easier to hear.

“She was waiting for an answer from him,” Yuka says. ʸ

Rimuru nods.

Mau watches the folded paper.

“I want to hear what she knows.” ᴹ

Shoko sets the cloth in her hands down.

“You have been telling us things you couldn't finish without stopping to breathe. You are not doing another meeting tonight.” ˢʰ

“Not tonight.” ᴹ

She studies his face.

“I mean that,” he says. “But I want to answer.” ᴹ

* * *

Maki is still close enough to be called back.

She comes with Yuta. Mai stays at the door for a moment, sees the paper in Rimuru's hand, and comes inside instead of pretending she hasn't heard the name.

Mau looks round the room.

This is the part he used to do without them. Work out how much he could learn, decide it was worth the risk, return with enough information to make the decision sound inevitable.

Now they are here before he has an answer to send.

He is not enjoying it.

“I don't want to keep seeing her behind your backs,” he says. ᴹ

“Then don't,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

He takes the answer without looking away.

“I won't. I'm asking whether she can come here for a little while. With you. Where I don't have to go anywhere.” ᴹ

“Being in the same room doesn't make her safe,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

“I know.” ᴹ

Mai looks at him, then at the bed.

“You can barely get across a room with help. Why does she get any of what you have left today?” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looks at his hands.

There is an answer about information, the Architect, a woman who knows which parts of him she has been trying to turn towards herself.

There is another answer.

“Because I want to know why she asked.” ᴹ

Mai presses her lips together.

“That's the part I don't like.” ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Yuka has been quiet.

Mau turns towards her.

She looks at Rimuru's hand holding the paper. Then at the scar hidden beneath the blanket, the place she now knows he held closed while trying to do everything else.

“I want her to tell us what she knows, too,” she says. ʸ

Mau waits.

“I don't want her beside your bed.” ʸ

He nods slowly.

It isn't an answer he can make easier by saying they want the same thing.

“I don't want to go to her,” he says. “And I don't want to wait until I can, then start thinking I should find a way to do it myself.” ᴹ

“You can tell us you're thinking it,” Yuka says. “That doesn't mean we have to make it happen immediately.” ʸ

He looks down at their hands.

“You're right.” ᴹ

Rimuru puts the message on the table where they can all see it.

“She asked for an answer. We can give her that first. Nothing says she gets to walk in because she sent paper.” ᴿ

Mau takes a breath.

“Tell her I'm alive. And recovering. No details.” ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

“Ask what she expects to discuss. Then we decide whether Shoko thinks I can manage a short visit tomorrow.” ᴹ

He looks at Yuka again.

“I still want to hear her. I'm not pretending I stopped wanting it because I don't like the answers in here.” ᴹ

Yuka's eyes shine. She keeps them on his face.

“Thank you for saying it before you sent the answer.” ʸ

* * *

The reply comes before Shoko ends the evening's visits.

Makima says she believed an agreement with the Architect was being kept. She wishes to discuss what happened to Mau under that agreement. She will accept company, a time limit, and the house's terms for admission.

Maki reads the wording twice.

“An agreement,” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

Mau looks towards Rimuru.

“I want to know what it was.” ᴹ

“So do I,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

That is not the same as liking the visit. Mau can see it in his face.

Shoko comes to stand where he cannot avoid looking at her.

“If I say you've had enough, the conversation ends. Not after one last question.” ˢʰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And you tell me when you're tired. You don't wait for her to decide you've proved something.” ˢʰ

He looks at the paper again.

“Yes.” ᴹ

The decision takes the room a while longer.

Rimuru will speak with Makima at the agreed outer meeting point before she is admitted. The protections for a visit will account for things she has already set in motion as well as what she might begin after arriving. She will bring no companion, no gift to leave behind, no demonstration she has decided would be helpful.

Maki does not turn those arrangements into approval.

“I think she will try to get something from seeing you like this,” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

Mau nods.

“I think you're probably right.” ᴹ

“Then remember you said that.” ᴹᵃᵏ

* * *

Yuka stays after the others have left.

Rimuru has the answer they agreed to send. Mau has not asked for a private addition. The chairs have gone back against the wall.

“Do you want to be there?” he asks her. ᴹ

She thinks about it before answering.

“Yes. I want to hear what she says. I'm not there to make her comfortable.” ʸ

“I won't ask you to.” ᴹ

She looks at his face for a long moment.

“I know you want to understand her,” she says. “I need you to remember that she understands things about you, too.” ʸ

He nods.

She takes the cup he cannot quite reach and puts it into his hand.

Neither of them treats the small kindness as an answer to the thing they still disagree about.
