## Chapter 10 — The One She Wanted Alive

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S12E04
**Continuity state:** B+223 late morning. A short, announced clinic visit follows Rimuru's external meeting and explicit invitation under the already adapted ward conditions. Makima brings no companion or gift; visitor terms address earlier initiated operations, not merely new attacks. Shoko and Rimuru stay, Yuka chooses to be present, Maki remains at the door. Makima admits conveying Ashborn information and discussing the surviving rooms, says she wanted Mau alive/reachable and believed she could direct the outcome; this was not actual automatic Control over the Architect. Distress is visible, not certified as total remorse or reform. Mau offers his hand and reassurance about the Architect's choice, without granting forgiveness for Yuka's attack. Future visits remain requested/announced/accompanied; work meetings postponed until proper walking/recovery.

*When: Makima enters, she sees Yuka before she sees the bed.*

Yuka is standing beside it, one hand on the back of her chair. Shoko is at the small table. Rimuru has come in behind the visitor, and Maki remains where the door will not close on her without somebody choosing to close it.

Makima looks at all four of them.

Then at Mau.

He has been propped high enough to speak without tiring himself just to see her. There is a blanket over his legs. His right arm lies supported beside him. His hair is shorter than it was when he came out, longer than when she last saw him, white in places that used to be brown.

She stops one step inside the room.

The pause is small. Everyone notices it.

“You look different,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Mau glances at the loose ends near his collar.

“Yuka cut my hair.” ᴹ

Makima's eyes return to Yuka.

“I see.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka doesn't answer for him.

Rimuru indicates the chair they have left for the visitor.

Makima sits.

* * *

Nobody asks her whether she would like something to drink.

The terms were settled outside. Her arrival has not turned them into an introduction she can begin again more pleasantly. She has agreed to the visit as offered, to leave when it ends, to keep what she has already set in motion from becoming an attack carried through the door under the cover of having begun earlier.

There is no companion waiting to act for her. Nothing she has brought will be left in the room.

Mau watches her hands.

She notices. Sets them loosely on her knees where he can see.

“What agreement?” he asks. ᴹ

Makima looks up.

“The one you wrote about.” ᴹ

“I wanted you alive.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's fingers tighten on the chair.

“That's an outcome,” Rimuru says. “What did you do to arrange it?” ᴿ

Makima turns towards him, then back to Mau.

“I told him what you told me about Ashborn.” ᴹᵏ

Mau doesn't look surprised. That makes Yuka angrier for a moment than surprise would have.

“All of it?” he asks. ᴹ

“What you gave me. A guest who could leave and had chosen to remain. I had no way to verify his room.” ᴹᵏ

“So you gave him a way to reinterpret what he was seeing.” ᴿ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

Mau looks down at his hand on the blanket.

He knew the risk of what he had said. He had told the house. Hearing that the thing he feared really happened still finds a place beneath the knowing.

“And the rooms?” he asks. ᴹ

Makima is quiet for a moment.

“He still had them. We discussed that.” ᴹᵏ

“What did you think he'd do?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Make a different outcome possible.” ᴹᵏ

“Different for whom?” ʸ

“For me.” ᴹᵏ

Mau lifts his eye to her face.

“You wanted me easier to reach.” ᴹ

Makima watches him.

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“After he did something I didn't choose.” ᴹ

She doesn't answer immediately.

“I believed I could direct the result,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Maki shifts at the door.

“You thought he answered to you.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Makima looks towards her.

“I thought he understood what I required.” ᴹᵏ

“That isn't the same thing,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I am aware.” ᴹᵏ

It is the first answer that loses some of its smoothness.

* * *

Mau moves his supported hand a little. Not reaching for anything. Finding a less painful position.

Makima watches the movement too closely.

“How long?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

Rimuru looks at Mau rather than answering for him.

“About a year,” Mau says. ᴹ

Makima's gaze returns to his face.

He doesn't give her the rooms. The voice he called into the dark. The rehearsals that took his wife out of every possible version of coming home. Those things are not an explanation she has earned by asking what her ally did.

“He kept changing it,” Mau says. “When I found a way to manage, he changed it.” ᴹ

Makima's hands close on her knees.

“That was not what I asked for.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her.

“It was what he did with what you gave him.” ʸ

“I wanted to see whether you had returned,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

Mau waits.

“And if he had done something you would need help understanding.” ᴹᵏ

Rimuru's expression changes.

Makima sees that, too.

“Yes,” she says, before he can ask. “I expected that to bring you to me.” ᴹᵏ

The quiet in the room becomes very exact.

Mau looks at her for a long moment.

“You don't get those parts of me because he hurt them.” ᴹ

Makima lowers her eyes.

“I can see that you have people here.” ᴹᵏ

“I had them before.” ᴹ

Her mouth tightens.

For a moment Yuka thinks the visit is going to become what she was ready for: another argument about power, the value of the people around him, what Makima thinks he could be if they stopped getting in the way.

Instead, Makima looks at his arm again.

And says, “I'm sorry.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Mau doesn't answer immediately.

Makima has said the words without looking at the people who might need convincing. She is watching the place below his elbow where the support ends.

“I believed he would keep the part I had made clear,” she says. “He was useful. He knew what it meant to lose my cooperation.” ᴹᵏ

“He wanted something else,” Mau says. ᴹ

“He was not entitled to take it from you.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's breath catches, very quietly.

The words are so close to something decent. They are coming from the person who once decided how much of her could be removed to change Mau's answer.

Makima lifts one hand, looks at the fingers as though they have failed to hold something she put there.

“This is why,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Nobody asks what she means.

She tells them anyway.

“This is why I control them. An agreement becomes an interpretation. A useful person decides the thing I wanted matters less than his own. If I'd had control of him—” ᴹᵏ

She stops.

Mau is looking at her face.

There is a tear at the edge of one eye. She wipes it away with a quick, irritated movement. Another follows before her hand has returned to her knee.

“I should have known,” she says. “I should have—” ᴹᵏ

“Makima.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“He'd been doing this to me for a long time.” ᴹ

* * *

Mau puts his left hand out.

It isn't an easy movement. The wrist has been corrected; the old fingers still don't straighten the way he wants. He lets the hand rest halfway between the chair and the bed instead of reaching farther than he can manage.

Makima looks at it.

Then at him.

He opens his fingers a little.

She puts her hand beneath his.

Yuka goes completely still beside the pillow.

Mau's hand is cold. Makima feels the weight he cannot quite hold off her palm, the little tremor he has stopped trying to conceal by keeping it under the blanket.

“It wasn't your fault,” he says. ᴹ

“Mau,” Maki says at the door. ᴹᵃᵏ

He hears her. Looks up once, then back at Makima.

“His choice to do that. What he wanted from my body. That started before you knew me.” ᴹ

Makima's fingers move beneath his, unsure for one brief moment what shape to make around an offered hand.

“I helped him see it,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“You did. We're not finished talking about that.” ᴹ

His grip tightens only a little.

“But you didn't invent him. And I'm here. I'm alive.” ᴹ

Makima looks at his face.

The next tear reaches her cheek before she remembers to stop it.

* * *

Nobody in the room helps her hide it.

Mau doesn't tell her to stop crying. He doesn't explain her tears to the people who have to watch him hold her hand. He stays long enough for the words to reach her, then lets his own hand settle back on the bed when it begins to tire.

She releases it at once.

That matters. It doesn't make the room less angry.

“Controlling him wouldn't have made what you wanted fair,” Mau says after a while. ᴹ

Makima looks down at her empty palm.

“No,” she says. ᴹᵏ

It is quiet enough that he nearly misses it.

She wipes her cheek. More slowly this time.

“I don't know what to do with this,” she admits. ᴹᵏ

Mau looks towards Yuka, who is still beside him, then back at the woman in the chair.

“You can start by letting it be something you don't get to arrange.” ᴹ

She sits with the answer.

“I could—” ᴹᵏ

“I'm being helped.” ᴹ

“Not a meeting.” ᴹᵏ

“A visit. We're not starting new work because you've seen me.” ᴹ

“I understand,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

She stands when Shoko asks.

No last request. No attempt to leave a token by his bed. She thanks Mau for seeing her, and Rimuru accompanies her out along the route they agreed before she entered.

Maki goes with them as far as the corridor.

Yuka remains beside the bed.

Mau's hand lies open on the blanket where Makima has left it. He looks at his fingers as though he can still feel the shape of the other palm beneath them.

Then he looks up at his wife.

She is already looking at him.

Neither begins by pretending the room has gone back to how it was before the knock.
