# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 11 — What They Don't Have to Forgive

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S12E04
**Continuity state:** B+223 afternoon, following rest after Makima's visit. The house disputes Mau's reassurance and touch openly. His compassion does not absolve her contribution to the Architect's plan, her attack on Yuka, or other victims' claims; he corrects the scope of his words rather than erasing the gesture. Yuka names the cost of witnessing it without turning the conflict into romantic jealousy or withdrawing the marriage. Future visits are requested, accompanied and hosted by agreement in suitable shared visitor space; never the couple's bedroom. Full work meetings remain postponed. This is a disagreement inside the new honesty, not a new secret visit or another breakup.

*When: Rimuru comes back, Yuka is still sitting beside the bed and Mau has stopped trying to look less tired.*

“She left,” Rimuru says. “At the place we agreed. Nothing left behind.” ᴿ

Mau nods.

Rimuru doesn't sit immediately. He looks at the empty chair, then at the hand resting on Mau's blanket.

“We need to talk about what you said.” ᴿ

Mau closes his eye for a moment.

Yuka puts her cup down.

“After he rests,” Shoko says from the table. ˢʰ

Rimuru looks towards her, then back at Mau.

The answer is fair. That doesn't make waiting easier.

“After,” he agrees. ᴿ

Nobody leaves Mau to wonder whether the postponement is a punishment. Shoko tells him what she is checking. Yuka stays while he closes his eye. Rimuru goes to tell Maki when he will return.

Mau is tired enough to sleep.

He is not tired enough to stop remembering the way Yuka's hand went still when he reached for Makima's.

* * *

When he wakes, Maki is in the room with Rimuru.

Shoko has finished the check. Yuka is drinking the water she ignored before. No one has put the visitor's chair back near the bed.

Maki waits until Mau can look at her properly.

“You told her it wasn't her fault.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He nods.

“She had just told you what she gave him. She wanted something done to you that would make you easier for her to reach. And you told her it wasn't her fault.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Mau looks at the blanket.

“I meant what he chose to do. The year. What he was trying to put in me.” ᴹ

“I heard what you meant,” Maki says. “I also heard what she gets to remember you saying.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks up.

Rimuru sits now, beside Maki.

“She can be wrong about what she controlled and still be responsible for what she encouraged.” ᴿ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then you need to be more careful with the part you let sound like none of it belongs to her.” ᴿ

Mau takes a breath. His fingers begin to fold into the sheet, then stop.

“That's fair,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't tell them he should never have taken her hand.

That would be a different way to make the conversation stop: give them the answer he thinks will repair the room, put the real answer somewhere he can keep it to himself.

He has spent too much of the last few days learning what that does.

“I wanted her to stop thinking the answer was that she should have controlled him harder,” he says. ᴹ

Maki watches him.

“I know what she did. I know she hasn't become safe because she cried. But she was looking at something she couldn't turn back into what she expected. And I…” ᴹ

He stops. Looks at Yuka.

She is listening, though the effort shows.

“I felt sorry for her,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka closes her eyes for a moment.

When she opens them, she doesn't ask him to change the sentence.

“I saw.” ʸ

* * *

Maki leans back in her chair.

“You can choose what you feel about what happened to you,” she says. “You don't get to settle what happened to everyone else while you're doing it.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Mau nods.

“I wasn't trying to forgive her for Yuka.” ᴹ

“I believe you,” Yuka says. ʸ

He turns towards her.

“I still had to sit there and watch it.” ʸ

He waits.

She looks at his hand, the wrist Shoko had to correct, the fingers she has held while he tried to find out whether she wanted him home.

“I was thinking about you saying you couldn't keep everything inside and heal at the same time,” she says. “What you showed them about the water. How you'd called us.” ʸ

Her voice begins to shake. She steadies it without making it gentle for his sake.

“And she got to come in, say she wanted something from what he did, and have you hold her hand while she cried.” ʸ

Mau's face changes.

He reaches towards Yuka, stops before making her take the hand just because he wants to offer it.

“I'm listening,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at his fingers, then his face.

“I don't think you wanted her instead of me. That's not what I'm saying.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I need you to understand how much it cost to be in that room.” ʸ

* * *

Mau looks at her for a long time.

He has been thinking about the moment he recognised something in Makima's face. The strange helplessness, the anger reaching for control because it didn't know what else to do with having failed.

Yuka had been recognising something else.

Her own blood. His. A woman who asked for an outcome and then returned to be comforted about the result.

He cannot make his own recognition cancel hers.

“I should have said it differently,” he says. “I don't think she chose what he made me live through. I think he would have tried to get me eventually. That doesn't mean she didn't help him get there.” ᴹ

Rimuru nods, slowly.

Mau keeps looking at Yuka.

“And I didn't think enough about what I was asking you to watch.” ᴹ

A tear reaches her chin.

She wipes it away herself.

“No,” she says. “You didn't.” ʸ

He lets the answer stay true.

* * *

Shoko puts away the last thing she has been cleaning.

“I don't have an answer about whether she deserves your hand,” she says. “I have an answer about how much of yourself you spent on her this morning.” ˢʰ

Mau looks towards her.

“You were tired before she asked to see you. You told me you would stop when you needed to. You didn't tell me until I was already ending it.” ˢʰ

He opens his mouth, then closes it.

He remembers wanting to ask one more thing. Wanting to get the meaning of her tears right before they took the chair away.

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then that's part of the next decision. Your time matters even when someone else is upset.” ˢʰ

He looks at the cup Yuka has finally drunk from.

“It matters when it's her as well,” Shoko adds, following his gaze. ˢʰ

“Yes,” he says quietly. ᴹ

* * *

They discuss the next visit before anyone has asked for one.

Mau has told Makima she can send a request. He hasn't given her their room, a place beside every treatment, or the right to turn concern into work he has not agreed to do.

Rimuru makes him say that part in his own words.

He does.

Yuka looks towards the door.

“When you're able to leave the clinic, she doesn't come into our bedroom.” ʸ

“No,” Mau says immediately. ᴹ

“And I don't have to sit through every visit to prove I trust you.” ʸ

He nods.

“You don't.” ᴹ

They can choose a visitor room. They can decide who will be there. A request can wait while he is unwell. Makima wanting to know whether he is alive does not make her entitled to every hour he has survived to use.

He is quiet for a moment after that one.

Then asks Rimuru to help him put the terms in the answer they will send if she asks again.

Maki watches him without pretending she has come to like the idea.

“I still think she will try to use what you gave her.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I need you to remember that without deciding you're too clever to be reached.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks at Yuka.

“I don't think that anymore.” ᴹ

* * *

Before Rimuru leaves, Mau asks whether letting her come was another version of doing it anyway.

Rimuru sits back down long enough to answer properly.

“We argued before you sent the reply. You changed what you were going to offer. You heard Shoko and waited. We went with you.” ᴿ

Mau looks at him.

“I don't like every choice you made while she was here. That isn't proof talking was useless.” ᴿ

He puts a hand briefly against the edge of the mattress.

“Let us keep disagreeing while we're still in the room.” ᴿ

Mau nods.

Rimuru stands, takes the empty visitor's chair with him, and tells them where he is going.

* * *

Yuka stays after the next check.

She isn't holding Mau's hand. He doesn't make that mean anything larger before asking her what she wants.

“Would you rather sit somewhere else for a bit?” he says. ᴹ

She looks up.

“No. I'm comfortable here.” ʸ

He nods.

For a few minutes they sit with what neither has made pleasant.

Then Yuka puts her hand beside his, not beneath it.

“I love that you can care,” she says. “I hate that people keep finding ways to ask for it after they've hurt you.” ʸ

He looks at her fingers.

“I don't know how to stop feeling it.” ᴹ

“I'm not asking you to stop being you.” ʸ

He turns his hand slowly, until their fingers rest against one another.

“I don't want to make being with me mean you have to be kind to her.” ᴹ

Yuka lets out a breath.

“Then don't.” ʸ

He nods.

She moves her thumb once against his.

They have not agreed about Makima. They have agreed that Yuka's answer will have a place in what happens next.

Mau asks if she wants him to move the pillow so she can lean closer.

She considers it, then says yes.
