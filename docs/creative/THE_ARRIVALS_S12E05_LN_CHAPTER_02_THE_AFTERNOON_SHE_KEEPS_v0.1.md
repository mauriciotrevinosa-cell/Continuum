# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 10 — The Afternoon She Keeps

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S12E05
**Continuity state:** B+224. Yuka keeps an ordinary afternoon with Mai and Momo Nishimiya in the workshop; Mau voices wanting her near without requiring her to cancel or calling it rejection. His own medically permitted courtyard visit combines a few supported steps with seated rest. A small familiar Authority use moves his cup; it is not a new movement test or physical recovery proof. Return-home preparation uses his established inventory. No outing beyond the ward, training or new enemy move. They discuss tomorrow's possible home transfer with Shoko still responsible for clearance.

*When: Yuka tells him she promised Mai the afternoon, Mau says he wants her to stay.*

Then he looks startled by his own answer.

Yuka pauses with the empty tray in her hands.

He has been speaking more easily since yesterday. Longer sentences, fewer pauses to let his jaw settle. Now he seems to be trying to put a whole second conversation into the breath before she answers.

“I don't mean you have to.” ᴹ

She puts the tray down.

“I know.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“You said you wanted me here. I heard you.” ʸ

“But you want to go.” ᴹ

“Yes. We were going to look at the things Momo brought to the workshop. Mai's been putting her own work off.” ʸ

He nods. Looks at the tray, then at her sleeve where the thread has come loose again.

She has put on the clothes she wants to wear, tied her hair, made a plan that isn't organised round his next check. He likes seeing her look forward to something. He wishes it hadn't arrived while he wanted her hand against his face.

Both feelings are there. He cannot make one of them more decent by pretending the other isn't.

“I'll miss you,” he says. ᴹ

Her face softens.

“I'll be back before supper. If we run late, I'll let you know.” ʸ

He takes a breath.

“Okay. I want to hear what you find.” ᴹ

She leans over to kiss him.

“You will. You might regret asking.” ʸ

His hand catches her sleeve for a moment, then lets it go.

She leaves smiling, and he lies still with the feeling he has managed to say something difficult without turning it into an emergency.

* * *

Gojo arrives with two cups and a complaint about the chair by the window.

“Everyone keeps putting it where the light is.” ᴳ

Mau looks at the window.

“Terrible place.” ᴹ

“No consideration.” ᴳ

Shoko appears behind him.

“You can both consider the courtyard. He can sit there for a while after the walking, if he still wants to.” ˢʰ

Mau looks up at once.

She checks him before he can give her an answer based entirely on the word *outside*.

He answers the questions. Tells her about the ache that has been there since morning. She adjusts the plan. He is irritated for a moment at losing the longer distance he thought he was being offered.

Then he asks whether he can still go to the courtyard afterwards.

“Yes,” she says. “With help.” ˢʰ

He takes the offer.

* * *

The first few steps are awkward.

The support is familiar now. The position his body keeps trying to return to isn't the one Shoko has spent days making possible. He has to think about putting the foot down instead of simply expecting it to appear beneath him.

Gojo doesn't lift him past the difficult part. He gives him the time to find it.

They stop when Mau says to stop.

When they reach the chair waiting for the rest of the way, Mau sits without having to be persuaded that the chair isn't a defeat.

Gojo gives him his cup once they are settled outside.

Mau puts it on the table and looks up.

There is the sky.

No new ceiling. No seam deciding whether it will become rain. No need to look at the length of every shadow before choosing where to put his feet.

He knows that, and his body checks anyway.

Gojo follows his glance without asking him to explain it.

For a while they drink their tea.

* * *

The cup drifts a little towards Mau when he doesn't want to lean across the table.

Authority, small and familiar. It settles without touching the saucer beside it. He has moved cups before. No one congratulates him as though he has just discovered a new way to win a war.

Gojo watches him take a sip.

“You know, when you can do that, stealing the last biscuit gets harder to defend.” ᴳ

Mau looks at him over the rim.

“Evidence?” ᴹ

Gojo points to the crumbs on his own plate.

“Very compelling.” ᴳ

“You ate it.” ᴹ

“And yet I feel wronged.” ᴳ

Mau laughs. A proper laugh, small enough that it doesn't pull at everything. He puts the cup down before it can spill.

It takes him a moment to look up again.

Gojo has left the joke where it belongs. He isn't studying the laugh to find out how much better Mau has become.

He is reaching for another biscuit.

Mau moves the plate out of his reach.

“See?” Gojo says. ᴳ

* * *

They don't have a great conversation.

Gojo tells him something Shoko said that morning. Mau tells him about Ori informing Beru that a procession was too many people. They both sit through a discussion at the far end of the courtyard about where someone left a bucket, without having to become useful to it.

Once, Mau looks towards the workshop path.

Gojo notices, then looks back at the sky.

“You can miss her and drink your tea,” he says. ᴳ

Mau gives him a look.

“I was.” ᴹ

“Excellent. My work here is effortless.” ᴳ

Mau settles farther into the chair, within the position Shoko has said he can use.

He misses Yuka.

It doesn't become anything else while they sit there.

* * *

She comes back with the loose thread fixed and three things she wants his opinion on.

He has returned to the clinic and rested by then. His eyes are clearer when he sees her than they were when she left.

“You went outside,” she says. ʸ

“He stole my biscuit.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at Gojo, who has come as far as the door.

“That's a serious allegation.” ʸ

“Thank you,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“I believe him completely.” ʸ

Gojo puts a hand against his chest and departs in evident disappointment.

Yuka sits beside Mau and begins showing him what she brought.

Not all of it interests him equally. She can tell. She makes him give a real answer about the one he is trying to agree with merely because she likes it.

“That one looks uncomfortable,” he admits. ᴹ

“It is. I still like it.” ʸ

He thinks about that.

“Then that one.” ᴹ

“That's a different answer.” ʸ

He smiles.

She kisses him before putting the pieces away.

* * *

Shoko talks about home after supper.

Not as a reward for having had a good afternoon. She has been checking what he can do, what support he still needs, whether the next part of care has to happen in this room.

“We can try the transfer tomorrow,” she says. “Same help getting up. Same rest. You'll come back for the work we still have to do.” ˢʰ

Mau looks at Yuka.

Yuka is already looking at him.

He doesn't ask which room she means.

“Our room?” he says instead. ᴹ

“Our room.” ʸ

His fingers open against the blanket.

“I want that.” ᴹ

Shoko tells them what will need to be ready. Yuka listens, asks about the bed and the turn by the door. Mau asks about the distance he can try himself and which part he should let someone else do.

The answers make a smaller journey than the one he pictured.

He still wants to take it.

* * *

Before Yuka settles for the night, he asks for the little box of his belongings.

She puts it where he can reach.

His ring. The paper she wrote. The wrapped tooth. Small things that survived being set aside by people who didn't know whether he would want them later.

He chooses the ring first. Yuka helps him when the movement catches, waits until he has it where he wants it.

The fit is loose now.

He looks at it for a while.

“We'll keep it safe,” she says. ʸ

He nods, lets her place it back in the box for the journey rather than pretending it isn't loose enough to lose.

Then he opens his inventory and puts away the things he wants stored. The rest stays in the box because he wants them near him. The familiar space answers without becoming a shop balance, a restriction, a new demand to grow.

Yuka takes the empty water cup.

“You could have put all the clinic things in there.” ʸ

He looks at the chair, the blanket, the things other people will need after he goes home.

“Shoko might object.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs so suddenly that she nearly drops the cup.

He watches her catch it.

Then he asks her to leave his paper out.
