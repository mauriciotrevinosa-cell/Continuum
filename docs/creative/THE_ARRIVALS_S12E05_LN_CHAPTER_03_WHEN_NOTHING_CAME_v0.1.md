# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 14 — When Nothing Came

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S12E05
**Continuity state:** B+224 evening, after The Afternoon She Keeps and before the supported transfer home. Mau tells Shoko and later Yuka about the quiet round room in the Final Trial (V71 #7) and about being worn down by repeated ordeals. Shoko speaks to Gojo about her own fear without sharing Mau's private details. Recovery and tomorrow's transfer plan continue. No new memory access, power, or injury.

*When: Shoko comes back for the cup, Mau tells her that DIO wants to discuss custody.*

“Of who?” ˢʰ

He waits for her to work it out. She waits longer.

“Me,” he says. ᴹ

“Does she know there are weekends?” ˢʰ

Mau laughs and has to put a hand against his side. Shoko sets the cup down until he's done.

“I don't think she wants weekends. I think she wants to be able to say she has custody. It's complicated because she's in love with Noise.” ᴹ

Shoko sits in the chair Yuka left beside the bed. For a moment it looks like she's going to get up again. Then she laughs so hard she puts her forehead against the heel of her hand.

“Does Noise get a say?” ˢʰ

“Noise wants to know if there's food at both houses.” ᴹ

“Smart kid.” ˢʰ

He looks pleased on Noise's behalf. She has seen him make that face before, usually when somebody says something kind about one of the people he has brought home. He settles back against the pillow and she reaches over to pull the corner out from where it's folded under his shoulder. He lifts a little to help. It is a habit they've got between them now.

“You can stay a minute if you want,” he says. ᴹ

“I do.” ˢʰ

He gives her the half of the bedspread he had been holding away from the edge so it wouldn't brush her knee. She lets it fall where it wants. From the corridor they can hear a pair of shoes that have been coming closer and getting farther away for a while. Whoever it is keeps remembering something at one end and then something at the other.

“I don't know how much more I can take.” ᴹ

He says it while looking at the door. Shoko leaves her hand where it is. She was about to ask if he wanted the door shut, and she thinks he might stop if she gives him anything else to answer.

“There was a room,” he says. “Round. The floor was smooth. I'd been trying to find places like that the whole time I was in there. A place I could close my eyes. I found one, and I couldn't even reach my bread.” ᴹ

She remembers the description he'd given them at the table. Months beside a dragon. An interval where he went quiet. He'd given the two things about the same amount of time.

“How long did you stay there?” ˢʰ

“Noise says about nine days.” ᴹ

“What did it feel like to you?” ˢʰ

He draws a breath. She can see him look for an answer she can live with. It's an expression she knows from years of people on beds telling her where they're fine, and from this one in particular who sometimes starts trying to get up before he's finished the sentence.

“You can tell me what it felt like to you,” she says. ˢʰ

“Good, at first.” ᴹ

He sounds almost ashamed.

“I could lie there. There wasn't a sound that meant I had to move. I thought, in a minute. Then I kept thinking that. I knew what would happen when I got up. I'd have to learn how to get through the next thing, and once I learned, it would change. I kept thinking I could have another minute before that.” ᴹ

Shoko looks down at the cover. She has a thumb hooked under the edge of it now. She smooths the place she has gathered up.

“I want to be here,” he says. “I like being here. I was laughing two minutes ago and I meant it. I just keep thinking about how easy it was to stay on that floor, and how many times I've had to get up since all this started.” ᴹ

“Did you want to tell us that when we talked the other day?” ˢʰ

“I wanted to tell somebody. I didn't know how to say it without sounding like I was about to leave again.” ᴹ

She waits until he looks at her.

“Do you want to leave now?” ˢʰ

“No. I want you to stay a bit longer.” ᴹ

“All right.” ˢʰ

He takes hold of her hand. A while later he starts to pull his fingers away and she closes hers around them, lightly enough that he can finish if he wants to. He leaves his hand there.

* * *

Gojo is washing the cups when she finds him.

He has rolled his sleeves above his elbows. There is a wet patch on the front of his shirt that would give her ample material on another night. He looks over at her and dries his hands on the towel.

“Can you sit with me for a minute?” she asks. ˢʰ

He pulls a chair out. She sits down in the one beside it and he takes the other one himself.

“I keep thinking about the quarry,” she says. “About that conversation we had before he went. I knew what I was asking of him. I thought I knew.” ˢʰ

Gojo puts the towel down.

“He talked to me tonight. It's his to tell you if he wants to. I just need to say something about what it felt like to hear him.” ˢʰ

He nods. She had prepared another sentence in case he asked. She lets it go.

“I can see him getting better. I can measure it. He sits up longer and he tells me when his foot goes numb. And I think I've been doing the same thing with how much it costs him to come back. I see him at breakfast and he's making fun of me and I think, there's my boy. He's all right.” ˢʰ

Her voice gives out on the last word. Gojo moves closer. She lets him put an arm around her and cries into the wet place on his shirt. He holds her through it, chin against her hair. When she can see the table again, there's a cup on it. She pushes it towards him with one finger.

“You missed that one.” ˢʰ

“I was saving it. You like that cup.” ᴳ

She looks at it. It is the one she likes. She pushes it a little farther towards him.

“Tea, then. For both of us.” ˢʰ

He takes the cup with him when he gets up. She stays where she is until the water boils.

* * *

Yuka finds Mau awake, the paper still on the bedside table where he'd asked her to leave it. She puts her things down and asks if he wants the light a little lower. He says yes. Then he asks if she can come round to his side.

He tells her what he told Shoko. He gets the order wrong once, putting the bread before the first time he thought of standing. Yuka waits while he goes back for it. She has her hand over his knuckles, and he turns his own until he can hold on.

“Did you want to die?” she asks. ʸ

He can hear how carefully she has said each word. There is a space after it in which he could tell her something easy and she would have to decide whether to believe him.

“No,” he says. “I was cold. Everything hurt. Every time I learned how to survive something, they changed it. And then there was a floor I could lie down on, and for once nothing came after me. I was so tired, Yuka.” ᴹ

She puts her other hand over his. He's looking down at both of them when he feels how close she has moved.

“Did you ever get warm in there?” she asks. ʸ

He thinks about it. He's surprised that's the thing she wants to know.

“A little,” he says. “When I finally ate.” ᴹ

Yuka leans down until he can rest the side of his face against her. She gets the blanket up around his shoulder with one hand. The other one stays where it was.

“I'm going to be here a while,” she tells him. ʸ

The shoes in the corridor go by again. They slow down at the door and keep going. Mau realises he had been listening for them. When he closes his eyes, he lets them go all the way down the hall.
