# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 72
## Chapter 11 — The Things in the Drawer

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 72
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S12E06
**Continuity state:** B+225 afternoon and night. Shoko clears a supported transfer to the couple's existing room with continued checks and rehabilitation, not unrestricted activity. Mau uses assistance for the distance he cannot walk; belongings remain his and his inventory is retained. Rimuru and Shizue remain in the shared apartment arrangement. The miniature house/tree is the ordinary shadow wedding gift, not a mechanism or new present. Future family desire reaffirmed without conception, fertility guarantee or scheduling. They share their own bed within current positioning limits; bodily and emotional healing continue.

*When: The door to their room opens, Mau sees his shirt before he sees the bed.*

It is over the back of the chair.

The one he left there before an inspection he was meant to finish in time to spend the afternoon with Yuka. She has washed it. He can tell from the way the sleeve lies, the crease that isn't where his hand left it.

It is still over the chair.

“I know,” Yuka says behind him. “I've had days to put it away.” ʸ

He can't answer yet.

Shoko has checked the room. Maki and Yuta have helped with the part of the journey he cannot manage on his own. There is a clear way to the bed, supports where he will need them, a place for the things Shoko is leaving with them.

He has heard all the instructions.

He keeps looking at the shirt.

* * *

Getting into bed takes time.

It is easier than it was in the clinic on the first day. That does not make it easy. He has to let Yuta help at the point where his body would like to twist and get the whole thing over with. Maki waits until he says he is ready for the next movement.

Yuka puts the pillow where he asks, then moves it when it doesn't feel the way he thought it would.

The familiar mattress holds him differently from the clinic bed.

For a moment he cannot tell whether that is allowed to be better.

Shoko sees him checking.

“Tell me what feels different.” ˢʰ

He does.

She listens, makes one adjustment, asks again.

He takes a careful breath.

“That's better.” ᴹ

“Good.” ˢʰ

She tells him when she will return. Ciel remains connected; Yuta knows the next part of the plan. Being here does not mean he has to wait until something is unbearable before asking for help.

Mau looks at her.

“I heard.” ᴹ

“I know. I like to say important things twice.” ˢʰ

“I've noticed.” ᴹ

She touches the clear place at his temple and leaves the next check where he can see it.

* * *

Rimuru is in the sitting room when the others leave.

Mau can hear him telling someone that the papers on the table are in an order, and Shizue asking what the order is. There is a pause before Rimuru answers.

Mau looks at Yuka.

“Still working on that?” ᴹ

She smiles.

“Some things survived.” ʸ

Rimuru appears at the doorway.

“I heard that.” ᴿ

“We hoped.” ʸ

He looks between them, then at the bed, the extra pillow, the things Shoko has left on the little table.

“I'll be just there,” he says, nodding towards the sitting room. “You can ask for something before you work out whether it deserves an emergency meeting.” ᴿ

Mau's mouth moves at one corner.

“Tea?” ᴹ

Rimuru looks offended for an appropriately brief moment.

“I was speaking emotionally.” ᴿ

“Emotionally tea.” ᴹ

From the sitting room, Shizue laughs.

Rimuru goes to get it.

* * *

Yuka shuts the door after the cups have come.

She doesn't lock it. The people who need to come back know when. She sits beside Mau, settles the tray, and waits while he looks round the room he has known for months and imagined from another place for a year.

The little house is still on the shelf.

The tree beside it. The paths the shadows carved into the base, the door Beru would have defended from accusations against its hinge. Space left for things he and Yuka hoped might become part of their lives.

He has never wanted to touch it so badly.

Yuka follows his gaze.

“Do you want me to bring it?” ʸ

He nods.

She sets it where he can look without holding its weight. He touches the edge of the path with one finger.

The wood has not changed while he was gone.

He has.

* * *

“I couldn't see this anymore,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka sits close enough that their sleeves touch.

“The house?” ʸ

“After. What I was coming back to. I could remember the things. I knew where everything was. But if I tried to put myself here…” ᴹ

He looks at the bed, at the drawer, at the shirt he has not stopped noticing.

“I always ended up somewhere you wouldn't have to work round me.” ᴹ

Yuka takes a breath.

It hurts. It doesn't send him scrambling to replace the sentence this time.

She touches the little space in the model they have talked about before.

“I still want what we talked about,” she says. “When we're ready to know what we can do. I haven't been finding someone else to put here.” ʸ

He looks at her face.

“I still want it with you,” he says. ᴹ

She nods, her eyes wet.

“But I want this bit, too,” she says. “You and me. Being married. Having time that isn't all waiting for the next awful thing.” ʸ

He follows her finger away from the space in the model, to the little door they already know how to open.

“We can have more of that,” he says. ᴹ

It isn't a prediction about the world. He can hear the difference himself.

“We can make room for it,” Yuka answers. ʸ

* * *

She puts the model back when he is tired of looking.

There are things to unpack, and he wants to choose where they go. The clothes they will not need in the clinic anymore. The things in his box. His ring stays safe while it is too loose; he does not have to risk losing it to prove he wants to wear it.

Yuka opens the drawer.

His book is there, half on top of something of hers. A folded shirt. A small object he has no memory of leaving in quite that place, which she admits she moved because it kept catching on the edge.

“I didn't know which side it was meant to be,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks at the jumble.

He can see the careful empty space he had planned, and what would have had to come out to make it. The cup, the book, his hand reaching for a thing without having to ask whether being here was making her life harder.

Yuka hasn't made that space.

He puts his hand against his mouth.

She sits down before asking him anything.

He cries with the drawer open in front of them.

* * *

“I didn't want you to go,” she says after a while. ʸ

He nods.

He has heard her say it in the clinic, in the yard he cannot quite put back together, beside the bed when he admitted he still didn't know every minute.

Now there is a shirt over the chair, a book in the wrong part of a drawer, a woman who has spent the morning making sure the turn into the room is wide enough for him to come home.

“I believe you,” he says. ᴹ

She goes still.

He looks up at her.

“I'm not saying it so you stop crying.” ᴹ

Her face trembles.

“I know.” ʸ

“I want to be your husband. Here. I want all the little things I was trying to give up before you had to ask.” ᴹ

Yuka comes close enough to hold him the way Shoko has shown them is safe. He lets his face rest against her shoulder.

“Good,” she says into his hair. “They're yours.” ʸ

He keeps crying for a little while.

She doesn't ask him to turn that into another answer.

* * *

They eat upstairs that evening.

Shizue brings part of it in, then makes Rimuru take his own plate before he can begin a new discussion over the tray. Yuka sits where she can help if Mau needs it, and he tells her when he does.

Afterwards, she returns the dishes and comes back complaining that he has arranged the pillow to steal some of her side.

He looks at the pillow.

At her.

“Medical necessity.” ᴹ

“A very convenient condition.” ʸ

She adjusts it, checks that he is still comfortable, then lies down where they have made room.

He has been thinking about this all afternoon and is suddenly shy of asking.

Yuka turns towards him.

“What?” ʸ

“Can I hold you a little?” ᴹ

She moves closer, keeps her weight off the places that still need it, guides his arm to where it can rest without pulling.

It is less than the embrace he remembers.

It is enough to feel the warmth of her through her clothes, her breath against his wrist, the little adjustment she makes when her hair catches beneath her cheek.

He lets out a sound she feels before she hears it.

“Okay?” she asks. ʸ

He nods carefully.

“Very.” ᴹ

* * *

Shoko returns for the check she promised.

Yuka gets up without making him wonder whether she has changed her mind about the bed. She tells him where she is going, comes back with the things Shoko wants, sits again when the work is done.

There is still work to do tomorrow.

He asks about one part instead of deciding he can manage it alone. Shoko answers without making his question sound like a new failure.

Then she leaves them, and Rimuru calls through the sitting room that he is still there if they need him.

“We know,” Yuka calls back. ʸ

Mau smiles into the pillow.

* * *

He wakes after the lamp has been lowered.

The room is unfamiliar for one sharp moment. Too soft beneath him. Warm on the wrong side. A hand against his shirt where the field never let anything stay gentle for long.

He opens his eye.

Yuka is asleep beside him.

He doesn't have to find the chair. She has one hand resting near his, the ring catching what little light is left. The drawer is shut. His shirt is still where he saw it.

His breath begins to settle before he has finished naming the things.

*Home,* Noise says softly. ᴺ

Mau looks at her face.

*Yes,* he answers.

He takes the next breath without waking her to make the room true.

When pain pulls him awake later, he calls for help.
