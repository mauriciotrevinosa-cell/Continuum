## Chapter 15 — What He Wants After Breakfast

*When: Mau wakes, Yuka has left him most of the blanket.*

He looks at it with suspicion.

Her shoulder is just beyond his hand. She is turned towards him, hair loose against the pillow, one foot tucked into a place she has negotiated out of the supports and the extra sheet Shoko wanted beneath his leg.

He remembers where he is before looking at the window.

Their room.

There is pain. There is morning light. Neither has made the other disappear.

He tells Ciel about the part that has changed since the night. She asks two questions, gets answers that are longer than *fine*, then tells him Shoko will check it when she comes.

It can wait until then. He doesn't have to decide that by himself.

He looks at Yuka again.

*Quiet?* Noise asks. ᴺ

Mau feels it close by, interested in the room without putting anything new into him.

*For a little while,* he answers.

He stays looking at his wife until she opens her eyes.

* * *

She takes a moment to find him, then smiles.

“Morning.” ʸ

“Something's wrong.” ᴹ

The smile stops.

He moves his fingers against the blanket.

“You left me some.” ᴹ

She looks down, then back at him.

“Temporary generosity.” ʸ

“I was concerned.” ᴹ

She laughs, puts her face against the pillow so it won't be too loud. He can feel the little movement through the mattress.

Then she looks at him properly.

“And actually?” ʸ

He tells her about the ache. What Ciel asked. What they are waiting for Shoko to check.

Yuka listens without having to pull the answer out of him.

“Thank you,” she says. ʸ

He nods, watching her settle back into the pillow.

There is no speech after it.

He has told her he hurts. She is here. They can go on having a morning.

* * *

Shoko comes before breakfast.

Yuka gets up to give her room. Mau tells her which part he had wanted to explain in the night and which part changed after sleep. Shoko checks what Ciel has kept, examines him, makes the adjustment he needs.

The plan for walking is a little smaller today.

He doesn't like that.

He says so.

Shoko nods as though disliking the plan is an answer he is allowed to give.

“We'll see how you are after you rest.” ˢʰ

He looks towards the window, then at the blanket over his foot.

“Okay. Not secretly more.” ᴹ

Shoko gives him a look over the edge of the record.

“An unusually specific assurance.” ˢʰ

“Targeted.” ᴹ

Yuka puts her hand over her mouth. Shoko tries to remain disapproving and mostly manages it.

Before she leaves, she tells him when she will return. He doesn't ask whether the smaller plan means yesterday's work is gone.

He asks which part he can still do.

* * *

Breakfast comes with a complaint from Rimuru about the number of trays that now belong to this room.

“You could stop bringing things on a new one every time,” Yuka points out. ʸ

“That sounds dangerously close to admitting I caused it.” ᴿ

“A promising start,” Mau says. ᴹ

Shizue takes the empty tray Rimuru has been carrying under his arm since he came through the door.

“We'll begin with this one.” ˢᶻ

Rimuru looks at it as though it has betrayed him.

Mau laughs, then makes a small sound when it pulls at his side.

Yuka looks at him.

He tells her what happened. Waits until it settles. They don't have to abandon the meal to work out whether he is hiding a disaster.

Rimuru lingers long enough to make sure he isn't needed for anything, then says where he and Shizue will be.

The door stays partly open after they go.

Mau likes the sound of their cups in the sitting room.

* * *

Yuka sets the bowl where he can reach.

He takes the spoon himself. His grip is not the one he remembers from before the trial. It is workable. He can ask for the bowl to move, and does.

She brings it closer.

For a moment the light is in the wrong place.

He knows the table in the other room, knows what was allowed to stay gentle until he wanted it too much. The spoon pauses above the bowl.

Yuka notices.

He speaks before she has to ask what he is hiding.

“Can you talk to me?” ᴹ

She looks at his face.

“About what?” ʸ

“Something here.” ᴹ

Yuka glances round the room, as though he has set her a difficult task and she intends to answer it properly.

“Mai thinks the thing I like is impractical. Momo says she can make it work. Neither has asked the other which part she means yet, so I think they might argue about it all afternoon.” ʸ

Mau looks up.

“You going?” ᴹ

“Later. I want to see who works it out first.” ʸ

“Mai.” ᴹ

“I said that. She told me to stop helping.” ʸ

He smiles.

The light is where it belongs again. Warm across the blanket. Grey on the cup. Yuka, in all the colour he has.

He takes a mouthful.

She keeps telling him about the argument they haven't had yet.

* * *

There is a knock before he has finished.

Frieren stands outside with Ori beside her, carrying a book she would like to show him. Not because it will make his leg better. Because there is something in it she wants him to see.

“Now?” Frieren asks. ᶠ

Mau looks at Yuka, then at the space beside the bed.

“A little while.” ᴹ

Ori comes in as though those are ordinary words that mean exactly how long she can stay.

She asks before climbing onto the safe part of Yuka's side. Frieren takes the chair and holds the lower edge of the book so Ori doesn't have to balance it against Mau's leg.

He listens to the explanation.

It is not very orderly. Ori has begun in the middle, stopped to tell him something from the beginning, and then remembered the part she actually came to show him.

Yuka helps her turn back to the page she wants.

Mau looks from them to Frieren.

He had rehearsed how to keep these people out of one another's way.

Here they are, taking up his bed with a book.

His eye fills.

Ori notices.

“Does that hurt?” she asks. ᴼ

He shakes his head gently.

“No. I like having you here.” ᴹ

She looks satisfied and goes back to the page.

Frieren stays where she is.

Yuka rests her hand against his for a moment beneath the book, then helps Ori find the line again.

* * *

He tells Ori when he is tired.

She marks the page, asks if she can leave the book so he won't forget which part comes next, and takes Frieren's hand when it is time to go.

“See you later, Dad.” ᴼ

“Later.” ᴹ

It doesn't feel like a promise he has made too large to keep.

Frieren pauses at the door to tell them where they will be before lunch. Mau thanks her. She nods and leaves without making him say why it helps.

Yuka closes the book and puts it where he asks.

Then she sits back beside him.

* * *

“Are we all right?” he asks. ᴹ

She turns towards him.

He has asked versions of that question in pain, in panic, half awake and trying to get an answer small enough to keep beside the pillow.

This one comes slowly. He is looking at her, not at the door.

Yuka thinks before she speaks.

“We have things to keep doing,” she says. “I need you to keep telling me before you've already decided. I need the talking to still matter when you feel better.” ʸ

He nods.

“It will.” ᴹ

She studies his face.

He doesn't reach for a greater promise. He has given her something he can practise when he is well, when the bed isn't doing most of the work of keeping him in reach.

“I'm not waiting to decide whether I want you,” she says. “I want you. I'm here because I love you. We had a fight, and we found out how much was still hurting. We're doing something about it.” ʸ

His fingers close a little round hers.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“So yes. We're all right. We can have work left and be all right.” ʸ

He looks at her for a long moment.

Then he leans the small distance he can manage, and she comes the rest of the way to kiss him.

* * *

There are scars under his shirt that did not exist in this room before.

His foot still needs help. The quiet can still turn sharp without warning, and there are memories he will have to choose how to approach, with people who now know to ask instead of accepting the first answer he can make sound ordinary.

But Yuka has heard what he thought she wanted.

She has told him what she wants.

He believes her.

When he looks round the room now, he doesn't start removing himself from it.

* * *

“What do you want after breakfast?” she asks. ʸ

He starts to look towards the book, the cup, the little list of things that could be made easier for somebody else.

Then back at her.

“You. For a little while before you go.” ᴹ

She smiles.

“You've got me.” ʸ

“And the window open.” ᴹ

She reaches to open it a little farther. The sounds of the house come in, somebody calling across the courtyard, a door closing, a conversation that has been going on without requiring him to hold it together.

Yuka lies beside him in the place they made room for.

His hand settles against her waist, light enough that he doesn't have to hold anything still except himself.

They stay there until she wants to tell him another thing about the afternoon.

He listens.
