# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 73
## Chapter 1 — Leave It for Two

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 73
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S12E08
**Continuity state:** B+229 to B+236, followed through ordinary rehabilitation milestones. Mau plans a late second anniversary of taking Yuka's hand. Shoko permits an ordinary evening and a short seated Kaisel outing within his improved daily limits. Mau and Yuka revisit the existing future-family question with Shoko, Ciel and DIO by joint choice. DIO isolates only Mau's acquired constructions from possible inheritance as previously offered. This is neither conception nor a medical guarantee. No combat clearance or new technique.

*When: Mau asks Mai if she can make him something, she tells him to stop looking over his shoulder.*

“She's with Maki,” Mai says. “I checked for you.” ᴹᵃ

Mau lowers the piece of paper he has been holding against his chest. Mai expects a drawing. It turns out to be a list, with *two* written three times and underlined the last time.

“We missed the day,” he says. “The one when we held hands. I'd like to take her to dinner.” ᴹ

She looks back at the list. He has drawn the table after all. It is a rectangle with a dark mark where one of the legs goes.

“That's the little one by the washroom. It rocks,” he says. “I want to take it out to the east terrace.” ᴹ

“You need a wedge.” ᴹᵃ

“I also need something that keeps the candle lit if the wind's coming up the hill. And for you to tell me if a candle is a stupid idea.” ᴹ

Mai folds the paper along its old crease. He watches her do it. She knows the date too. Yuka had mentioned it over laundry once, then asked her where she was supposed to put a shirt with a hole in it if she wanted the person who wore it to stop wearing it.

“It's a good idea,” she says. “I'll make the wedge and a little cover. Take your good shirt out before she notices you're doing laundry.” ᴹᵃ

He looks alarmed. She had only been guessing.

* * *

They have a few days before Shoko will agree to the outing.

Mau keeps his appointments. He gets far enough along the outside wall to argue that the bench has been moved and then sits down on it anyway. The next day he takes the turn by the stairs with Yuka, puts his hand on the rail, and tells her before his foot begins to drag. She sits on the step with him and finishes telling him something about Mai's window while the feeling comes back.

They also go back to DIO with the question they'd asked before the gate. Yuka brings it up on the way home from one of the checks. She says she's been thinking about the answer and would like to hear how much of it still holds. At the table, with Shoko and Ciel there, DIO goes over the part she offered to help with. She can put a boundary around what was built into Mau so that it would stay his, if they ever had a child. Yuka asks her to say what happens on her side of that boundary. DIO tells her that anything a child could ordinarily inherit from her would still be possible.

“And we can have you do that now, even if we're still deciding when?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Yes. If you both want me to.” ᴰ

Mau checks Yuka's face. She already has her hand out for his. They give their answers, ask the last of their questions, and let DIO finish the work they'd come to discuss. Afterward they go back to the room and spend most of an hour trying to decide whether a basket will fit in the space beside the wardrobe. Yuka wants it for washing. He had thought she meant the other kind, which explains why he'd started measuring a different part of the room.

She laughs at him. He can hardly blame her.

* * *

Shoko catches him in the yard on the afternoon of the outing.

“You were going to sneak in and ask me about the dragon,” she says. ˢʰ

“I was going to walk in through the door and ask you about the dragon.” ᴹ

“Good. A short ride, seated. Come back before your foot makes the decision for you. You've got a whole evening after that.” ˢʰ

Mau nods. Shoko walks beside him as far as the kitchen. Through the open door he can hear Rem counting something and Momo Ayase suggesting that if they counted it by eye the work would already be done.

“Are you coming in?” he asks. ᴹ

Shoko looks at the kitchen. He's asked it the same way he asked her to stay that night, only this time he's got a dish towel over his shoulder and a whole afternoon he could spend somewhere else.

“If there's a chair.” ˢʰ

“There's mine.” ᴹ

“Get me another one. Sit in yours when you need it.” ˢʰ

He goes to find her a chair. Later she sits with a cup in both hands and watches him take instruction from Rem on a task that clearly matters to him. She stays long enough to hear him laugh at the first thing he gets wrong.

* * *

Rem makes Mau taste the sauce before he asks what it's missing. He's the one who's going to serve it. She waits with the spoon still out until he actually thinks about it.

“It tastes like I put the whole lemon in,” he says. ᴹ

Momo looks at the cutting board.

“You did put the whole lemon in.” ᴹᵒ

“It was a small lemon.” ᴹ

Rem reaches for a clean pan. She tips some of the sauce into it, shows Mau how much and tells him he gets to do the rest. Momo takes over the cutting board, finishes the vegetables and puts one little heel of bread by his elbow so he can taste the next batch with something to eat it on. He takes a bite and wants another one. That seems like the first sensible answer he's had all afternoon.

Out in the gallery, Yuka comes by looking for him. Maki asks her to hold the other end of a measuring tape. Yuta arrives with a box which turns out to contain photographs and starts asking her where one of them was taken. By the time she has corrected him twice, all three of them are sitting down, and Yuta has taken out a picture she hasn't seen since before the wedding. Maki catches Mau passing the doorway with two covered plates. She looks directly at him and asks Yuka to tell the story from the beginning.

Yuka does. Mau can still hear her when he gets to the bottom of the stairs.

* * *

Ori has drawn two routes on the back of a sheet Fern was done with. One is very straight. The other has a loop so wide that it goes right through a word on the opposite side.

“This one,” she says, with her finger on the loop. ᴼ

“What happens out there?” ᴹ

“You can see our windows over the tree. Only for a little bit. You have to tell him to do the turn slow, because if he does it like he usually does, the tree gets in the way again before you can count them.” ᴼ

Mau studies the page. There is a mark beside the part where he needs to ask Kaisel to slow down. She's drawn a pair of eyes there, with lashes going in every direction.

“Have you told him?” ᴹ

“Yes. I'm telling you.” ᴼ

He takes her very seriously for the rest of the briefing. At the end she folds up the sheet and puts it in his pocket herself.

Mai has put the little wedge in place by the time he gets back to the table. There's a glass cover waiting over the candle, open at the top, with a small handle so he can lift it without catching his sleeve. Momo has written the finishing time on the edge of a scrap. Rem has left two pieces of bread under the cloth, including a new one in place of the heel he ate.

“Do you want us to light it when you come back?” Mai asks. ᴹᵃ

Mau looks at the match beside the plate.

“I'd like to do that.” ᴹ

She leaves the match where he can get it. He sets out two glasses, shifts one because he knows which way Yuka is going to turn when she sits down, and goes to ask her if she has plans for dinner.
