# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 73
## Chapter 5 — Before She Goes In

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 73
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S12E09
**Continuity state:** B+238 early morning to afternoon. Hinata remains away after the departure in V70 #5. Anko makes an ordinary invitation by the existing correspondence route, talks with Nazuna and Ko, and spends time with Mau at home. The plant is from this branch. No restoration of the old timeline's plant or shared memories, and no visit confirmed.

*When: Anko brings her letter to breakfast, Nazuna asks why it needs three pieces of paper.*

“The top one's the letter,” Anko says. ᴬⁿ

Nazuna looks at the two beneath it.

“And the others?” ᴺᶻ

“Also the letter.” ᴬⁿ

Ko takes a bite of toast. He has been listening long enough to know that he should finish it before he says anything.

“Is it the same letter three times?” he asks. ᴷ

Anko moves the bottom two pieces out of reach. She could explain that one is entirely about dates and one is what she actually wants to ask. The third manages to contain both, at the cost of sounding like somebody invited Hinata to inspect a road and remembered at the bottom that she might enjoy breakfast.

“Hinata asked if I'd come see her,” she says. “I think I'd like to know when she's coming here again too.” ᴬⁿ

“Tell her that,” Ko says. ᴷ

Nazuna takes the crust he's left on his plate.

“And tell her to bring the good jam.” ᴺᶻ

“I don't care about the jam,” Anko says. ᴬⁿ

“That's why you need me.” ᴺᶻ

Anko looks down at the top letter. There is a sentence about how Hinata shouldn't trouble herself if the day she has in mind is already full. She crosses out the second half of it.

“What if I ask her to stay an extra morning next time?” ᴬⁿ

Ko looks at her.

“Would you like her to?” ᴷ

“Yes.” ᴬⁿ

He doesn't have anything else to add. It's annoying how well the question works. She writes it down the way she would say it if Hinata were right here, and adds one line at the end asking whether she'd like breakfast with her, even if they have to get up early to have it.

When she puts the pen down, Nazuna has taken the last of the crust and left the soft part for Ko. Anko gets up to put another piece on the plate.

“I'll see you both tonight,” she says. ᴬⁿ

Nazuna tips her chair back far enough to look up at her.

“Are we doing something?” ᴺᶻ

“I'd like to.” ᴬⁿ

Nazuna puts the chair back on all four legs. Ko asks if there will be jam. This time Anko throws the folded scrap of her first letter at him. It goes past his shoulder and he picks it up for her before it can go under the table.

* * *

Mau finds her later on the outside step with a plant in its old pot and a new pot sitting beside it. She'd asked him to come when he got done walking. He arrives with his sleeve rolled up, ready to help, and looks at the two pots before he sits down.

“I can take the bottom out of the small one,” he says. ᴹ

“Then there'd be a hole in the bottom.” ᴬⁿ

“Right. You could lift it out without touching the roots.” ᴹ

She looks at the pot. It has an old crack, but she'd like to use it for something else afterward. She gives it to him to hold while she works around the rim with her thumb.

“How was your morning?” she asks. ᴬⁿ

“Good. I got to the corner and back. Shoko made me stop before I asked if I could do it again.” ᴹ

“I meant the rest of it.” ᴬⁿ

He thinks. Then he tells her Yuka stole both pieces of toast he'd set aside for later because she had to run to help Mai. He says it as if Anko might want to know how she carried them. Anko does. One in each hand, apparently. She asks what he was going to eat on the way home, and he tells her he hid a third piece while Yuka was putting her shoes on.

The pot comes free. She gets the plant over the bigger one, and Mau holds it up while she tips a little more soil in around it. A leaf is caught under his thumb. She frees it, then reaches for a cup of water and realises she left it out on the other end of the step.

Mau starts to get up.

“It can wait,” she says. “I wanted you to sit with me.” ᴬⁿ

He settles again. He still has dirt on the pads of his fingers. She gives him the cloth she had beside her knee and lets him decide what to do about it.

“I sent Hinata a letter,” she says. ᴬⁿ

“What'd you tell her?” ᴹ

Anko rests her elbows on her legs. It's easier to say it now that she has sent it. She tells him she wanted Hinata for a morning of her own, and that it felt peculiar to put it in writing when she can already ask her for much more complicated things in a room full of people. Mau listens all the way through. Then he asks what she would make for breakfast.

“I don't know,” she says. “Maybe I'd ask her to help.” ᴬⁿ

“Then she gets the morning too.” ᴹ

Anko looks at him. He has the cloth folded over one hand and the other one hanging loosely between his knees. She can remember him coming back to their end of the table so tired he forgot who he was handing things to. It is good to see him here with enough left over to ask her about breakfast.

“Can I ask you a question?” he says. ᴹ

She nods.

“What do you want to do next week? Something here. You, me. An actual thing on a day.” ᴹ

She didn't have a second letter ready for that one. She looks down at her hands, thinks about telling him to choose, and comes up with something she'd like.

“Walk with me to get a pot for the next one,” she says. “I'll buy you something to eat after.” ᴬⁿ

“I'd like that.” ᴹ

He stays while the earth settles around the plant. After a while he asks her what it is. She tells him she bought it from a woman who couldn't tell her either. They both look at the leaves. Mau guesses it will get taller. Anko says that's exactly what she was told when she bought it. They give it some water before he goes.
