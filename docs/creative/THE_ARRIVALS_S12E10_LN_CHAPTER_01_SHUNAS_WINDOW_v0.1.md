# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 73
## Chapter 7 — Shuna's Window

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 73
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S12E10
**Continuity state:** B+240. Shion and Shuna are at their established homes in the village. Shuna asks Yuka and Shion for an ordinary favor and reserves her afternoon for her own work. Yuka and Shion resume their festival rivalry through a limited, unpowered practice. No new ability, duty or travel.

*When: Shuna asks for a second pair of hands, Shion brings Yuka and starts counting hers as extra.*

Shuna is trying to see the difference between two colors of thread in the light that comes into her room. It is a decent room for this after breakfast. In the afternoon, the curtain cuts off the sun just when the corner of her table has warmed up. She has spent the last two days moving the whole table to get around it.

“I want to hang it farther to the left,” she says. “Yuka, can you hold this end?” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka puts down the practice sword she had been carrying and takes the rod. Shion moves the stool herself. She had come to find Yuka for the practice and spent the walk explaining that she remembered exactly how far her foot went over the line last time.

“You can put the sword in the corner,” Shuna says. “It's my room. You can come in even if you've already got something else to do.” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka looks around for the corner she means. There's a strip of cloth tacked over the chair back with two colors running through it. She had thought the colors were the same until she put the sword where Shuna pointed and came back into the light.

“Is that what you're making for the window?” she asks. ʸ

“That's for me,” Shuna says. “I'm trying something I couldn't see properly yesterday.” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka leans far enough to look at the stitches without letting go of her end. The darker color runs under the lighter one, then comes up at the last edge of the pattern and stays there. She tells Shuna which part she noticed first. Shuna gets off the stool to see it from where Yuka is standing.

“Oh,” she says. “Then I think I'll leave it.” ˢʰᵘ

Behind them Shion is still holding the far end of the rod above her head. She asks if she should continue to do it until supper. Shuna returns to the stool, fits the hook into place and says that for the rest of the afternoon, both of them can try to solve their differences where she can't hear them.

* * *

In the practice yard, Shion draws a line about as wide as her finger and steps back to admire it.

“You're taking a line with you everywhere now?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“You seem to need one before you'll admit I got close.” ˢʰⁱ

Yuka laughs and picks up her sword. They have one line for each of them, wooden swords, and enough room at either side to step off without backing into anything. They agree to stop if the blade touches skin. Shion asks if it counts if a sleeve was in the way. Yuka tells her it counts if she can convince herself.

The first pass is short. Yuka keeps the point high enough to make Shion bring her shoulder around, then goes low at the last moment. Shion catches the change. Both swords end up pointed down and they spend a second looking at each other over them.

“You used to do that later,” Shion says. ˢʰⁱ

“You used to fall for it.” ʸ

Shion grins. She comes in with the same opening on the next pass, only her front foot stops short. Yuka reads the shoulder and moves anyway. Shion gets the flat of the sword on her sleeve. It's there long enough for Yuka to count it herself.

“That was good.” ʸ

Shion looks pleased. Then she lowers the sword.

“Again?” ˢʰⁱ

Yuka wants another round. Her arm has started to do that thing where she thinks she's holding it level and it drifts a little lower every time. She puts the sword down on the bench.

“After water.” ʸ

Shion looks at the line, then at the bench. She goes to get both cups. When they come back to the line, Yuka waits for her to do the same thing again. Shion waits long enough that she eventually has to look up. They're both smiling when they make contact.

* * *

Shuna is still by the window when they bring the cups back. The cloth has grown by about the width of her palm. Shion goes around the chair to see what she did and ends up leaning against the wall, her hands behind her back so she won't touch anything that's still in progress.

“She got me twice,” Yuka says. ʸ

“You left out the third one,” Shion says. ˢʰⁱ

“The third one was my sleeve.” ʸ

Shuna keeps her eyes on the cloth. She can tell by their voices that they're both hoping she'll say something about the sleeve. She puts the thread down, gets her cup from Yuka, and asks if either of them would like to see what she made while they were out.

They come to stand where she points. Now the light reaches far enough across the floor for all three of them to see the colors. Yuka wants to know how she made them change places without leaving a lump. Shuna pulls another chair up to the table and shows her. Shion keeps standing until she gets to the difficult part. Then she asks if there's room for one more chair.
