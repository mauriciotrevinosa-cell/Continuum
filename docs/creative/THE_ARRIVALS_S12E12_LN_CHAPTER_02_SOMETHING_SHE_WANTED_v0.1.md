# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 73
## Chapter 12 — Something She Wanted

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 73
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S12E12
**Continuity state:** B+245 afternoon. Yuka initiates a private conversation with Mai about the anniversary and being ready to start trying. Mau tells Yuka about his conversation with Shoko. Their early attempts do not result in pregnancy. Yuka initiates ordinary affection without stepping back from her own time with Mai.

*When: Yuka brings the candle cover back, Mai asks if she's going to spend all afternoon washing it.*

Yuka turns the glass in her hands. She'd washed it before she came down. There is a mark on it now because she's been holding it in the same place while Mai makes room on the workbench.

“I wanted to talk to you,” she says. ʸ

Mai puts her tools down. She uses her foot to push the door a little nearer to shut and turns the lamp so they can both see where to put things.

“Does that belong to the conversation?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks down at the glass again. She puts it on the bench, right between the two of them. Mai moves it to the side. There is room to sit now. Yuka sits.

“Mau and I, after the anniversary,” she says. ʸ

Mai waits for the rest. Yuka has seen her wait out a person who could not think of a better way to tell her that they'd broken something. She had expected this to be easier once she'd started.

“We slept together.” ʸ

Mai looks at her.

“Yes, that way,” Yuka says, before Mai can possibly have said anything else. ʸ

“I thought you might have.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka forgets to be embarrassed for long enough to sit up.

“How?” ʸ

“The day after, I was looking for both of you. I found one of you for about four seconds, and then she said she'd come see me tomorrow. And she did.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka remembers that. Mai had been on the stairs when she'd gone down to find out where Shizue kept the other blanket. She remembers how good it felt to say *tomorrow* and go back upstairs.

“Was I that obvious?” she asks. ʸ

“I know you. Is something wrong?” ᴹᵃ

“No.” ʸ

Mai lets her think about it for a little longer.

“You didn't do anything wrong,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“I know. I think I came because I wanted to tell somebody I was happy.” ʸ

She'd had the words all morning and still feels the difference once she says them aloud. Mai sits back a little. There is a very small smile at the corner of her mouth. She looks pleased for her, which is what Yuka wanted and somehow still hadn't quite expected.

“Do you want to tell me what made you ready?” Mai asks. ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks down at the candle cover. The first time she'd seen it, there was a flame inside it and Mau was trying to tell her how he'd thought about her for months without knowing if she wanted him to come home. She had expected him to need to be convinced for longer than she could possibly give him in one evening. Then he said he was always going to love her, and a knot she'd been carrying for weeks came loose.

“I heard what he thought I meant,” she says. “I heard how long he was in there believing it. And I was still trying to figure out how to tell him how much I wanted him home when he sat down with me and told me he was going to love me forever.” ʸ

Mai stays still. Yuka looks up to find out what her face is doing and sees that she is listening in the same way she had when they'd talked after the honeymoon, with both hands free and her attention exactly where Yuka wanted it.

“I wanted to be closer than I'd ever been,” Yuka says. “It was the first time I didn't have to think of anything else before I could say it.” ʸ

Mai puts her chin in her hand. She lets Yuka see the smile properly now.

“I'm glad you got to say it,” she tells her. ᴹᵃ

Yuka laughs a little. Then she tells her about the other thing they decided, and how she's been catching herself trying to imagine a whole life at breakfast. Mai asks what part she likes to imagine most. Yuka says she likes the thought of seeing Mau with someone small enough to fall asleep in his arms before he can get to the end of a story.

“He'll keep telling it,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“I know. I'll get to hear the rest.” ʸ

They spend a little longer at the bench. Eventually Mai reaches for the candle cover and asks if Yuka means to give it back. Yuka says she wants to keep it. Mai checks the handle anyway, then puts it in her hands herself.

* * *

Mau is reading by the time she gets home. He's in the chair that only looks comfortable after you have taken the cushion from the other one, and he has done that. Yuka puts the glass on the windowsill and asks how he spent his morning.

“I asked Shoko if we were going to have a baby already,” he says. ᴹ

She turns round. He has his finger between the pages. His expression is hopeful about a different thing now, which is that she might understand how he got as far as asking the question.

“What did she say?” ʸ

“She asked if I'd ever had this explained to me.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs. He puts the book down. By the time he's told her the rest, she's close enough to take his hand off the arm of the chair and make a little room for herself. He gives it to her. She sits across his legs and lets her weight settle only after he's said he has room for her.

“Did you want to ask her together sometime?” he says. ᴹ

“Maybe. I talked to Mai today. I wanted her to know why I was so happy.” ʸ

Mau looks at her. She can feel the moment the words reach him because his fingers stop moving in hers.

“Are you still?” he asks. ᴹ

She leans in and kisses him. Then she gets the book from the arm of the chair and puts it back in his hand, because she wants to stay there for a while and thinks he ought to be able to finish the page.

He tries. It takes him a long time to find his place.
