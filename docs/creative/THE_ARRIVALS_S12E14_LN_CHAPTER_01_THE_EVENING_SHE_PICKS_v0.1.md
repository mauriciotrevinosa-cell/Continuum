# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 73
## Chapter 15 — The Evening She Picks

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 73
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S12E14
**Continuity state:** B+248 late afternoon. Rimuru and Shizue have their first chosen date on the lower bank inside the village's protection. They agree they want another. No relationship declaration to the house, change of residence, office or teaching schedule.

*When: Shizue sees the knot on the cloth, she asks who taught Rimuru to tie it.*

“Mau lent me the cloth. The knot is mine.” ᴿ

“I thought it might be.” ˢᶻ

He looks down at it. She reaches to take one of the ends and tells him she likes that the food is still inside. Then she pulls it free. The knot falls apart with considerable dignity.

They have come down far enough to hear the grass moving across the bank. The top of it comes to her shoulder when she sits. She can see the feet of people taking the higher path to the common room. They stay in sight for about three steps at a time and then vanish. She understands why he thought of this place. When she told the students she was going out, they asked her which of the four things they thought she still had to do she was going to finish. Out here she can hardly remember the second one.

Rimuru passes her a piece of bread with something still warm wrapped inside it. She takes a bite. He'd found the food himself after all, a piece of the filled bread they had at Tempest and talked about twice since.

“You remembered what I liked in it.” ˢᶻ

“I remembered what you picked out of it.” ᴿ

She turns it to check. He got it right. It has the good part all the way to the end. She moves a little nearer when she sits up again so he can share the cloth.

They have to talk over the grass a little at first. He tells her he'd been thinking of bringing a cup each and then discovered the cloth had room for only one. She says she can think of a way around it. He takes a drink and hands it to her. There is a moment after she drinks when they both look at the cup. She keeps it a little longer than she needs to.

“How long were you thinking about asking me?” she says. ˢᶻ

“Properly? Since the Fort.” ᴿ

“I was hoping you'd stay for the last story.” ˢᶻ

“I was hoping you'd ask.” ᴿ

She looks at him. He had sat down beside her early, while there were still better cushions elsewhere. When Milim finished her story, Shizue had kept her hand on the edge of his cushion for a moment so she could ask whether he planned to get up. He'd been looking for something on the floor. She had been able to see that his eyes never quite got to what he was supposedly looking for.

“I thought if I moved my hand you'd get up,” she says. ˢᶻ

“I thought if I stayed too still you might move it.” ᴿ

She laughs, then sets the cup down so she can find his hand. He turns it over for her. His fingers are still a little cool from holding the cup. She likes how long it takes them to warm up.

* * *

He asks if she misses anything about the place she used to live.

“A few people,” she says. “The first minute of a walk before anybody recognises you. Some things I don't miss until somebody does them exactly the way they used to.” ˢᶻ

He waits. She had thought she'd want to finish the list if he gave her the chance. For now it feels complete.

“What about you?” she asks. ˢᶻ

“I miss bits of Tempest at odd times. And sometimes I go back and find the people I was missing have moved the table or picked a different place to eat.” ᴿ

She lets his thumb pass once over the side of her hand.

“Did you still like being there?” she asks. ˢᶻ

“Yes. I think I liked seeing what they wanted to do while I was gone.” ᴿ

She thinks about that for a while. It's part of what she likes about him here, that he can show somebody a place and hear them say what they want to do with it. Sometimes she has to wait while he realises they had a different idea. She's gotten fond of the face he makes when he finds out he likes theirs.

“Next time I'd like to pick,” she says. ˢᶻ

“The place?” ᴿ

“The place and what we do there. I have somewhere I like going after class. I think you'd like it too.” ˢᶻ

He looks at her for a second. She can see him paying more attention to the first two words than to the rest of what she said.

“Then I'd like a next time,” he says. ᴿ

She squeezes his hand. There's someone going up the high path in a hurry now. They hear both feet, then a pause while the person remembers which turn to take. Shizue leans her head against Rimuru's shoulder, and he shifts far enough that she can stay there without slipping down the bank.

* * *

The cloth is easier to tie when they are going home. Shizue puts the cup inside it and brings the corners round while Rimuru holds the rest. She asks him if he wants to see the place she picked on a morning or an evening. He asks when she likes it best.

“An evening,” she says. “I like having time to get there.” ˢᶻ

He asks which evening. She tells him, and at the top of the path he reaches for her hand again. She lets him take it. There are plenty of people to meet between here and their door. She finds she is quite happy for the walk to take as long as it takes.
