# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 74
## Chapter 4 — The Name She Heard

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 74
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S12E18
**Continuity state:** B+259. Reze picks up the house's earlier answer to her letter, hears a plausible lead about Denji at the crossing and chooses to follow it. No reunion, confirmed location, house visit, pact with the road office or change in her release terms.

*When: Reze asks if there's a reply for her, the man at the crossing asks whether she has filled out the sheet.*

She looks at the sheet. It had been weeks since she last came this far along the road, and she doesn't remember one on this table. She'd read one on the other side of the river three days ago, while the person in front of her tried to decide what to put under the part about shelter.

“I'm picking up a letter,” she says. ᴿᶻ

“It will be easier if you have a place on record.” ᴬ

She keeps her hand on the counter. She asks again if there's a reply for her. The man looks towards the basket where he has been putting the things people are meant to collect. Somebody has set a flat envelope there without putting it under the stack. He checks the name and gives it to her.

She opens it outside. They have written back about the examination. Shoko will tell her what she wants to ask before she asks it. If she would rather meet somebody outside the house, she can say so. She reads the last part twice. Somebody else would have said she could go anywhere now, and then told her which way to walk.

She folds the answer and keeps it.

* * *

She's still deciding what to eat when she hears the name.

The person saying it is under the long part of the awning, trying to get his friend to remember which of them paid the last time they stopped for food. Reze walks past once to see whether she'll hear it again. The second time, she goes all the way up to them.

“Who were you looking for?” she asks. ᴿᶻ

They look at each other. She lets them finish the argument about who paid. Then the one who'd said it tells her they met a boy who gave them his name and asked where he could get something to eat.

“Denji,” Reze says. ᴿᶻ

“That's it.” ᴬᵍ

He shows her which way the boy went. Reze wants to ask what he looked like, who was with him, whether he'd told them what he was going to do after he ate. She asks the first one. The man gives her an answer which could be a good description of several people if you were only going to look at them from across the road. She asks if he remembers what the boy wanted to eat.

“Whatever we had,” he says. “He asked if we had enough to give him two.” ᴬᵍ

She puts her hand in her pocket and feels the fold of the letter. She can remember him asking for two of something and eating both while she was still deciding whether she wanted one. It had seemed so ordinary at the time. Now she has to look towards the road for a second before she can ask how long ago this happened.

The man tells her. It could already be too late to get there. It could be a different boy, hungry in the way half the people at the crossing are hungry. She thanks him, waits until she is sure which path he meant and starts to walk.

She comes back as far as the awning before she goes. The woman behind the counter asks how much bread she wants. Reze was going to say one. She puts the money down for two and takes them with her.
