# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 74
## Chapter 6 — Her Half

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 74
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S12E19
**Continuity state:** B+260. Denji follows a rumor he previously dismissed and reunites with Power. She remembers what Makima did to her. The two eat and agree to stay together. Reze gives them time and remains outside the village. Their account and arrival points are independent of Makima's prior testimony. No attack or formal group yet.

*When: The woman at the counter says the thief wanted paying, Denji stops counting his money.*

Reze had asked about something to eat. The woman told her what there was, then went into a description of a person who stole enough cheese for three people and waited near the door to be congratulated. Denji waits to see whether she has more of the story.

“She said I owed her a second piece,” the woman says. “For bringing my cat back.” ᴬ

“Did she bring it back?” Denji asks. ᴰᵉ

The woman looks down. There is a cat asleep on the empty shelf underneath the counter.

“It was already in here.” ᴬ

Denji looks at Reze. Somebody told him a story a little like this one two days ago and he'd walked on before he heard the end. He asks which way the girl went this time. The woman holds out her hand for the money before she tells him. He gives her enough for two pieces and asks if she can wrap them up.

They find the side of the building she meant. There is an empty box upside down beside the door, and the person sitting on it has her feet planted in front of her as if she had been waiting for a different person to come out.

Denji goes as far as the end of the step. For a second he sees the girl before he sees the horns. It turns out that is enough time for his knees to get very strange about what they are supposed to do. Reze is still beside him. He puts the bag against his chest and manages one more step.

“Power.” ᴰᵉ

Power turns her head.

“I was waiting for somebody else,” she says. ᴾᵒ

He doesn't move. Her eyes come back to his face.

“Denji?” ᴾᵒ

“Yeah.” ᴰᵉ

She gets off the box. He thinks she'll come to him, then realises she's waiting to see if he's going to come the rest of the way himself. He does. By the time he gets there she's got both hands in his shirt. She pulls him far enough down to look at his face properly.

“You took a long time,” she says. ᴾᵒ

He puts his arms around her. She stays very stiff for a moment. Then she presses her forehead so hard into his shoulder that he can feel it even through the shirt. He holds on. On the other side of the yard, he hears Reze ask the woman where she can sit. He is glad she didn't ask him.

* * *

Power eats both ends of her piece first. Denji remembers her doing this with things she wanted to leave on his plate afterward. This time she finishes it and goes right on looking at what he has.

“You can have half,” he says. ᴰᵉ

“I can have all of it.” ᴾᵒ

He gives her half. She eats it while he gets to the middle of his own. For about a minute neither of them has to do anything else. There is a cat on the wall above them now. Power wants him to acknowledge that she could have caught it if she had wanted to.

“I know,” he says. ᴰᵉ

She looks down at him. He finishes his bite and waits for her to tell him what he was supposed to say.

“Did you see what happened?” she asks. ᴾᵒ

He doesn't need to ask which thing.

“Yeah.” ᴰᵉ

Power puts her hands on the empty paper. She smooths it twice with both palms.

“I remembered the door,” she says. “I wanted to tell you I remembered it.” ᴾᵒ

His throat closes. He had thought about what he might tell her for so long that by now he should have been able to get all the way through something. She sits there with the paper in her hands and lets him be quiet. Eventually he reaches over to take one of the corners from her, so it can lie flat between them.

“We can eat again tomorrow,” he says. “If you want to stay.” ᴰᵉ

Power looks out at the yard. Then she puts her shoulder against his.

“You have to buy more next time.” ᴾᵒ

He says he will. She folds the paper down the middle, dividing the grease mark into two uneven halves, and asks him how much more he can buy today. He laughs. It surprises him how easy it is to get the sound out this time.
