# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 74
## Chapter 7 — The Cup She Keeps

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 74
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S12E20
**Continuity state:** B+261. Satella and the other witches continue living in the established tea court. Fern shares what she knows about the road declaration and asks before including anyone's name. Satella chooses how she would like the household to answer a question about her home. No summons or demand for surrender yet.

*When: Mau tries to take Satella's cup to the kitchen, she catches it by the handle before he lifts it.*

“I was going to have some more,” she says. ˢᵃ

He puts it back. It is a cup with a bit missing from the foot. Satella keeps a folded scrap of cloth on the table for it so she can put it down without having to look. He'd brought three empty cups out of the kitchen with him. He moves one aside to make room for hers.

“I can bring the pot here,” he says. ᴹ

“I can reach it.” ˢᵃ

She can. She pours enough to warm her hands around the cup and passes him the pot afterward. Across the table Daphne is cutting bread and asking Echidna if she would like to have any before she starts reading. Echidna says she will have a piece later. Daphne cuts her one anyway and puts it far enough away that she has to get up if she changes her mind.

Mau has come to have tea. For the first few minutes he does. He finds out what Satella did with the second chair at her window. She moved it across the room so she could see the window from it instead of having to turn her head. He asks whether she would like anybody to sit in it. She tells him she put it there to see whether she liked the place. If she does, she might ask someone to try the one beside it.

“You can ask me,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at him. Then she asks how long he thinks he could sit down without somebody coming to find him.

“Long enough for a cup.” ᴹ

“That's a start.” ˢᵃ

He laughs. She brings the pot back nearer him.

* * *

Fern comes through the open gate in the court. She has the letter she brought home from the lower road and another one from a courier who stopped at the crossing. She puts both flat on the table and shows them which sentence she wanted to ask about.

It is the one about the people who give shelter. The person who wrote back to her says he has seen the same question on another road. He couldn't find out who would receive the answers. She is going to ask him which office the people at the table named when they offered help.

Satella reaches for the letter only after Fern has finished pointing to it.

“Have they asked you about us?” she asks. ˢᵃ

“Nobody has asked me for your names,” Fern says. “I'd want you to hear it from me if somebody did.” ᶠᵉ

Satella puts the letter down. She looks at the page for long enough that Mau starts thinking about the things he might want to say if somebody did ask. He has got through two of them when she speaks.

“I live here,” she says. ˢᵃ

“Yes,” says Fern. ᶠᵉ

“I had to think about it for a while before I could say it like that.” ˢᵃ

Mau keeps his hand around his cup. He can hear people on the far side of the court coming in for bread. Whoever it is has been trying to shut the door with an armful of something, and Daphne gets up to help.

“If somebody asks what you do here, what would you like me to tell them?” Fern asks. ᶠᵉ

Satella considers it. Echidna has lowered the page she was reading, but she keeps her finger on her own place.

“You can say I live with my family,” Satella says. “They can ask me the rest themselves.” ˢᵃ

Fern nods. She gives Satella time to look through the second letter before she puts either of them away.

* * *

Later, when most of the cups are in the kitchen and Daphne is giving somebody bread to take home, Satella asks Mau if he still has time.

“For another cup?” he says. ᴹ

“For a chair.” ˢᵃ

He goes with her as far as the window. There is room between the two chairs for a little table. She asks him to sit in the one she's been trying while she sits in the other and sees how far she has to turn to look at him. It turns out she hardly has to move at all.

“That's better,” she says. ˢᵃ

He looks out the window. He can see a narrow strip of path and a corner of the place where they hang things out to dry. After a while someone goes past with a cloth trailing out of a basket. Satella gets up to catch it before the end drags in the dirt. She comes back with it over her arm and sits down again. Mau stays until the person comes back to get it.
