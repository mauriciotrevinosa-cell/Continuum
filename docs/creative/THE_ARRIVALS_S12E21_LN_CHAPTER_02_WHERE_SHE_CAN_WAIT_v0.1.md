# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 74
## Chapter 10 — Where She Can Wait

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 74
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S12E21
**Continuity state:** B+264. Ione's sister answers via the bread carrier. Ione chooses to meet her at the open storeyard the following day and asks Makima to walk part of the road with her. Fern receives a separate letter indicating several crossing clerks now treat the declaration as a requirement, without identifying its origin. Makima's alert-only shadow remains active.

*When: Ione opens the letter, Makima goes as far as the door and finds she can still hear the paper shake.*

She waits there. It has taken the man with the bread three days to get back with an answer. There was enough time for Makima to go and see whether anybody at the crossing had asked him what he was carrying. He would have told her, if she'd asked. She did ask how much he wanted for bringing the bread. Then she took it inside without touching the letter.

“She can come tomorrow,” Ione says. ᴵᵒ

Makima turns.

“Would you like her to come here?” ᴹᵏ

Ione looks at the letter again. Makima has offered her the room and told her what she can have sent to it. There is a chair near enough to the door for somebody to sit and listen to people coming up the corridor. Ione has put it on the other side, where the bed is.

“I'd like to meet her at the storeyard,” she says. “She can get there without having to go past the crossing table.” ᴵᵒ

Makima knows the yard. It is usually open, with one low wall and a space at the other end where the owner ties up a cart. She asks what time Ione wants to be there. Ione tells her, then looks down at the letter as if the next thing were written there too.

“Will you walk with me as far as the wall?” Ione asks. ᴵᵒ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

She hears herself answer before she's thought about what happens if the sister doesn't come, or if she asks for something Makima doesn't want her to have. Ione folds the letter. For a moment she seems to wonder whether there is something else she has to show Makima. Then she puts it away.

* * *

At the village, Fern reads a second account of the tables at the crossings. This one came back with someone who usually sends her the price of flour, written under a question about what he ought to tell people who want to go on. Two of the clerks are already asking travelers to fill in the line about shelter before they give out directions. When he asked who wanted the sheets, one clerk pointed at the blank where somebody was supposed to put a date.

Fern sends word asking whether he can give her the name of the clerk who did that. Then she brings the letter to Rimuru.

“Can we tell if anybody's using the answers yet?” he asks. ᴿ

“I can tell somebody has started collecting them.” ᶠᵉ

She holds the edge of the letter down while he reads it again. The people who put out the sheets have made it easy for someone on the road to answer one question at a time. Fern has enough copies now to look at what all the questions would tell somebody if they put the answers together.

“I'd like to tell the house before we send anything back,” she says. ᶠᵉ

Rimuru nods. He waits while she folds the letter so they can take it with them.

* * *

Makima goes to the storeyard before it gets dark. She has time to walk around it while the man who keeps it is bringing a cart in. She asks if there's somewhere two people can sit tomorrow without getting in his way. He points to the low wall. She thanks him and goes to stand by it.

Her shadow looks the way it looked yesterday. She can see the shape of her hands in it if she moves far enough into the sun. Nothing about the way it falls gives away what it could do if she asked.

She takes her hand off the wall and starts back. When she gets to Ione's door, she tells her there will be room to wait for her sister. Ione asks which side of the yard she'll want to stand on. Makima tells her where the cart was and lets her pick the side.
