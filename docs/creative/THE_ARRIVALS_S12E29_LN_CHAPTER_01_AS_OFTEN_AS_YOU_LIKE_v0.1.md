# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 75
## Chapter 4 — As Often as You Like

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 75
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S12E29
**Continuity state:** B+273 morning. Mau arrives for the hearing. Testarossa represents Tempest. Elmesia gives him a reusable pass for the island hotel and accepts a revocable emergency shadow anchor. Mau prepares a separate existing ant at his return point.

*When: On the morning of the hearing, Fern gives Mau a copy of the question she'd like somebody to answer.*

Fern has left half a page empty beneath it. Mau sets his bread down to read the line at the top. It asks what happens to a name once somebody writes it on a declaration at one of the crossings. There's a copy of the declaration underneath, with Fern's copy of the notice from the bend beside it.

“Do they have both?” he asks. ᴹ

“They have what I sent back when the invitation came. I'd like you to have a set you can put on the table yourself.” ᶠᵉ

He lifts the notice. Where the date is supposed to be there are still two little strokes with room for numbers between them. He remembers standing there with Fern while she copied those too.

“If somebody says they need names to look into it, what can I give them?” he asks. ᴹ

“The places where we saw the sheets. They can ask the clerks who told them to collect them. If they want to speak to the people who filled them in, those people get to decide if they want to come.” ᶠᵉ

Mau looks at the half page. “I'll ask what happens to the names.” ᴹ

“I'd appreciate that.” ᶠᵉ

She gets up to put the other set away. Yuka comes in while Mau is trying to fold the two papers so that the line Fern wants is on the outside. He shows her what he's doing. She holds the corners even for him until he gets the crease all the way across.

“Will you eat the rest of that here?” she asks. ʸ

He looks at his bread. He'd been about to wrap it and find out if there was somewhere to finish it at the hall. He sits back down. Yuka takes the chair opposite him and tells him what Mai said about tomorrow while he eats. By the time he reaches for his coat, he knows what time the two of them are meant to be at Mai's and what Yuka thinks they'll have for supper tonight.

* * *

There's an outer lodge where the carriages are turned around. Mau stops there and asks one of the ants to wait with him while he sees how far the hall is from the gate. With all six feet on the ground, the ant's head comes up to Mau's knee. It angles its round abdomen out of the way of the next carriage and looks up at him. From the top of the steps he can still see the flag over the lodge. He goes back down, points out the open doors at the top in case it finds itself in the hall, and lets it decide whether it wants to wait inside or out. It puts one foot on the warm stone outside and settles in.

Before he leaves it, Mau walks the last few steps with it at its pace. There's a lane for people who came on foot, where the wheels stay on the other side of a low stone edge. He shows it the gap by the post. The ant goes through and comes back on its own. Then it turns to face the sun and stays exactly where he can find it again.

On the second trip up he finds Testarossa leaning on the balustrade. She's looking down at the exact spot where he's left the ant.

“I thought you might have brought Fern,” she says. ᵀᵉ

“Fern asked if I wanted to bring her. It sounded like an offer to come, so I almost said yes. Then I realised she was asking if I wanted somebody to hold the paper and point to the question they'd actually written down.” ᴹ

“Do you?” ᵀᵉ

“I took the paper.” He holds it up. “I put my finger on it all by myself this morning.” ᴹ

Testarossa holds out her hand. He gives her Fern's copy. She reads the question first, then goes to the bottom of the declaration as if somebody might have remembered to sign this one since the last time she saw it.

“Some of them came to ask that question,” she says. “I passed two at the lodge who were comparing copies. Some came to ask you for an account of the storeyard. I heard a man offer to give his before he found out that the woman he was talking to knew where the yard was.” ᵀᵉ

“Had he been there?” ᴹ

“He left to find his witness.” ᵀᵉ

She hands the papers back. Mau waits while a clerk takes two names at the door and sends the people who gave them to a bench inside. They gave the same territory, and one carried both their copies of the invitation. A man behind them tries to tell the clerk where they should sit. The clerk looks down at the list for long enough that the man goes in to see for himself.

“When I speak for Tempest, you'll hear me say so,” Testarossa says. “If one of them asks you a question, you get to hear the whole question before you decide what to do with it.” ᵀᵉ

“And if they ask me what Tempest thinks?” ᴹ

“You can send them to the lady with the sign.” ᵀᵉ

She smiles and moves over to make room for him. Behind them, an attendant is trying to carry the sign for Tempest and a second chair at the same time. Testarossa puts the chair down for him and asks him where he'd like it. He hesitates. The chair has Mau's name on it, and the place it is meant to go faces every other place in the room. Mau takes a look from the threshold. There'll be room for the ant to get out.

“You can put mine there,” he says. ᴹ

The attendant asks if he needs a table of his own. Mau looks at how far away he'd have to put the papers if he said yes. He asks if there's room at the end of the one everybody else will be using. The attendant measures it with his eyes and says he can move a lamp. Mau goes in with him long enough to see him do it. When he puts his hand down, he can reach the table and look straight at the person opposite him.

They hear Elmesia before they see her. She has found a member of her delegation halfway to a wrong door and is telling him that if he ends up hosting a meeting about grain, he had better remember the price they agreed on. When she reaches them she passes her own papers to the corrected man and asks Mau how he's been.

“Better,” he says. “I can go down the steps now without putting both feet on each one. I still think about putting both feet on each one.” ᴹ

“You can do that here. There's nobody at the bottom keeping time.” She studies his face a moment. “The last time I saw you, I was close enough to hear the quarry after you both went down. I went home to people who were frightened somebody might come to their city and do it to them. They didn't have to find out what that would have been like. I owe you a favour for it. You can ask me in this hall or send me a letter when you think of something.” ᴱˡ

Mau thinks about it for the time it takes a clerk to go past them with the next stack of names.

“Could I take Yuka back to the hotel on the island?” ᴹ

Elmesia looks at Testarossa. Testarossa's eyebrows go up.

“Is there something you'd like to discuss there?” Elmesia asks. ᴱˡ

“I have unfinished business with a bird.” ᴹ

Elmesia waits. He has to explain the fruit, and the fact that the bird took it close enough for him to see which piece it had picked out. By the time he gets to Yuka's account of whose fault it was, the man with the papers has come back to get Elmesia and she has to ask him to give her a moment. She takes a card from her case, writes on the back and holds it out.

“How long can we go?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“As long as you like. Show them my seal when you arrive. Your room, meals, anything you want to do while you're there. You're my guests. Bring everyone if you want. Just write ahead so nobody has to find a spare room for half of Tempest at dinner.” ᴱˡ

“You've saved me a lot of anniversary ideas.” He takes it with both hands. “Thank you. Can I offer you something while we're out here?” ᴹ

She looks at the card in his hand before she looks back up at him.

“One of my soldiers can stay in your shadow. It can call me if you ask or if you're in immediate danger. It won't tell me where you go or what you say. You can send it away, and an alert doesn't mean I can always reach you.” ᴹ

“Only for emergencies.” ᴱˡ

“Only for emergencies.” ᴹ

She asks how she would send it away. Mau explains and calls one of his ordinary soldiers far enough from the doorway to give them room. Elmesia asks it to come out of her shadow as soon as it goes in. It does. She tries once more after taking three steps, then asks what would happen if she sent for him and the two of them were standing somewhere he couldn't reach.

“I'd know you asked. I might have to tell somebody closer to you.” ᴹ

She thinks about that. “That's still something I'd like to have.” ᴱˡ

She gives the soldier permission to return. Mau has the card in his hand while she does it. He hasn't decided whether to put it in his coat or somewhere he can find it again when he changes out of the coat. Elmesia asks if he intends to carry it through the meeting. He tells her he thinks he'll put it somewhere safe.

The attendant comes out to tell her the clerk is ready. This time the man with her papers has found the right door. She goes in with him and leaves Mau at the threshold with Testarossa.

“Did you think of the bird first?” Testarossa asks. ᵀᵉ

“I thought of Yuka seeing the water again.” ᴹ

“And then the bird?” ᵀᵉ

“Almost at the same time.” ᴹ

Testarossa picks up the sign. Mau takes out his invitation for the clerk. He puts the card away first.
