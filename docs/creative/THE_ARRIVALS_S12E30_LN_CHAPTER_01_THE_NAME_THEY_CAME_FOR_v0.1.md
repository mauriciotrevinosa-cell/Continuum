# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 75
## Chapter 6 — The Name They Came For

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 75
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S12E30
**Continuity state:** B+273. Lugunica demands Satella. Mau refuses and identifies her as family. Reinhard's sword recognises Mau. The wheel is raised briefly and lowered after clarification. No attack takes place.

*When: The envoy asks the clerk to record the name in full. Mau stays seated.*

“Satella,” the envoy says. “We have received testimony that she is living in the territory under your protection. Will you hand her over to us?” ᴰˡ

“No.” ᴹ

Nobody is holding the pen any more. The clerk picks it back up as if it's his own idea. The envoy turns a page. Mau had expected the first thing they'd ask about Satella to be what she had done since she arrived. He can see the envoy has a second page with the same name on it.

“You understand that the people who sent me have lived with that name longer than you have?” ᴰˡ

“Yes. I heard one of them say it himself when he came to live with us.” ᴹ

“Then you understand that some of them cannot accept an assurance from the man sheltering her. What is she to you that you would make enemies of several kingdoms over one woman?” ᴰˡ

“She lives with us. She eats with us. She has a place where she sleeps, and if she wanted a better chair to read in I'd ask her which one. She's family. If your question is whether I'll give you somebody from my family because you're threatening to become my enemy, the answer is no.” ᴹ

A small sound carries over the table. It's metal against its fitting, just once. Mau's eyes go to the sword. Reinhard is close enough to the envoy for the envoy to turn his head and see him. The sword is still at Reinhard's side. Mau puts his thumb on the fold in his invitation and the wheel appears over his shoulder.

The envoy's next breath is loud in the chamber. Reinhard looks at Mau before he looks at the wheel.

“Nice sword,” Mau says. “Are you going to use it, or did it just recognise me?” ᴹ

“It recognised you,” Reinhard says. “I haven't drawn it.” ᴿʰ

Mau holds his eyes for one breath. Then the wheel is gone. Reinhard moves his open hand farther from the sword. Down by the end of the table, the woman who'd asked about the letter takes her pen out again.

“That's the wheel in the testimony,” someone says. ᴰᵉ

“Yes,” Mau says. He smooths the fold with his thumb. “You can ask him what just happened if you need two accounts.” ᴹ

The man looks at Reinhard and chooses to go on talking to Mau. There are decisions, he says, that a single man has no right to make. For an incident on the road there should be an account of the incident. For the castle there should have been consent. And for the sort of man who can summon the thing they just saw, there ought to be terms under which every government in the room can require him to act or refrain from acting.

Mau notices that Reinhard is still watching him. He doesn't have the look of someone waiting for Mau to make good on a threat. He keeps an eye on the envoy each time the man leans far enough to brush the scabbard. When the envoy finally sits back, Reinhard can let his arm rest. Mau wonders how many times somebody has stood in a room hoping that sword would give them the answer they wanted to hear.

“Are you asking me to turn myself over to you, or offering to tell me what to do from here?” ᴹ

“Either would be a start.” ᴰᵉ

“I won't do either.” ᴹ

The man has started to say it again when Mau turns his head towards Elmesia. She's waiting for him to catch her eye.

“Am I doing this right?” he asks. ᴹ

“You could be doing it worse,” she says. ᴱˡ

He gives that the consideration it deserves. The envoy from Lugunica has got his papers back into order. The man beside him is still speaking, this time to the rows beyond Mau, and someone from the eastern bench asks why a house that shelters him should be treated any differently from a kingdom that gives him a place to stand.
