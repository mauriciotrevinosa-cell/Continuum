# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 75
## Chapter 6 — The Name They Came For

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 75
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S12E30
**Continuity state:** B+273. Lugunica demands Satella. Mau refuses and identifies her as family. Reinhard's sword recognises Mau. The wheel is raised briefly and lowered after clarification. No attack takes place.

*When: The envoy asks the clerk to record the name in full. Mau stays seated.*

“Satella,” the envoy says. “We have received testimony that she is living in the territory under your protection. Will you hand her over to us?” ᴰˡ

“No.” ᴹ

Nobody is holding the pen any more. The clerk picks it back up as if it's his own idea. The envoy turns a page. Mau had expected the first thing they'd ask about Satella to be what she had done since she arrived. He can see the envoy has a second page with the same name on it.

“You understand that the people who sent me have lived with that name longer than you have?” ᴰˡ

“Yes. I heard one of them say it himself when he came to live with us.” ᴹ

The envoy lifts his eyes at that. One of the people beside him turns a page, looking for the name Mau hasn't given. The envoy asks whether the person is prepared to say what he knows in front of the delegations. Mau asks what they would want to ask him. When the envoy says that will depend on who he is, Mau keeps his hand on his own paper.

“If he wants to come, he can tell you his name himself.” ᴹ

The envoy goes back to the page he's been holding. He explains that in Lugunica there are people who can tell you what their grandparents did when a rumor about the Witch got into the village. He knows a woman who was taught which way to run before she was old enough to know why. When the first report reached his office here, she came to ask whether the roads were still open. He wants to be able to tell her something she can use.

“What did the first report say she'd done here?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“It said you'd given her a place to stay.” ᴰˡ

Mau waits. The envoy looks at him a little longer. Then he puts the page down and tells the clerk that the request was made on the strength of the identification. The clerk writes that down. Across from Mau, a woman who had drawn a small mark beside the word *testimony* lays the point of her pen beside it again.

“If you have a question for Satella, I can carry it back,” Mau says. “She told us you could ask her yourself. I can give her the choice about where and how she'd answer.” ᴹ

“Will you guarantee she answers?” ᴰˡ

“I'll guarantee she gets the question if you give it to me.” ᴹ

The envoy waits long enough for the people beside him to see that he's considering the offer. He looks down at the first page again. It has a space near the bottom where somebody would put the name of the officer authorised to take custody. The page with Satella's name is the one he keeps in front of him.

“My people are asking for custody,” he says. “What is she to you that you would make enemies of several kingdoms over one woman?” ᴰˡ

Mau remembers holding a cup while Fern asked Satella what she wanted them to say about her. Someone had come into the court for bread with both arms full. Daphne went to hold the door while Satella thought it over. There had been time for Mau to come up with an answer of his own and hear hers before he used it. He remembers how she said *I live here*, and how long she said it had taken her to be able to say it.

He puts Fern's paper down. This part he knows in Satella's words.

“She lives with us. She eats with us. She has a place where she sleeps, and if she wanted a better chair to read in I'd ask her which one. She's family. If your question is whether I'll give you somebody from my family because you're threatening to become my enemy, the answer is no.” ᴹ

A small sound carries over the table. It's metal against its fitting, just once. Mau's eyes go to the sword. Reinhard is close enough to the envoy for the envoy to turn his head and see him. The sword is still at Reinhard's side. Mau puts his thumb on the fold in his invitation and the wheel appears over his shoulder.

The envoy's next breath is loud in the chamber. Reinhard looks at Mau before he looks at the wheel.

“Nice sword,” Mau says. “Are you going to use it, or did it just recognise me?” ᴹ

“It recognised you,” Reinhard says. “I haven't drawn it.” ᴿʰ

Mau holds his eyes for one breath. Then the wheel is gone. Reinhard moves his open hand farther from the sword. Down by the end of the table, the woman who'd asked about the letter takes her pen out again.

For several seconds the only person who moves is the clerk. He takes his inkwell farther from the edge. Then someone asks whether they ought to get the guards from outside. Reinhard says he doesn't need them. He looks towards the door when he says it, so that the attendant who's put his hand on the latch can hear him.

“Would you mind saying what you saw?” Testarossa asks him. ᵀᵉ

Reinhard looks from her to the clerk. “My sword moved in its fitting. Mau asked if I meant to draw it. When I said I hadn't, he put the wheel away.” ᴿʰ

“Before or after you took your hand away?” the woman at the end asks. ᴰᵃ

“Before.” ᴿʰ

The clerk puts that down in two short lines. Mau lets his hand come off the fold in the invitation. It has left a white line across his thumb. He hadn't known he was pressing that hard.

“That's the wheel in the testimony,” someone says. ᴰᵉ

“Yes,” Mau says. He smooths the fold with his thumb. “You can ask him what just happened if you need two accounts.” ᴹ

The man looks at Reinhard and chooses to go on talking to Mau.

“We asked for an account of the yard. You told us you were there. We have an account of the castle, too. Who gave you consent to take it down?” ᴰᵉ

“If you're asking what happened at the castle, I'll tell you. What are you asking me to agree to today?” ᴹ

The man draws his list towards him. He points to the four places on it, one by one.

“There ought to be terms that every government here can invoke if you do what you did at any of these places again. We should be able to require you to act or refrain from acting.” ᴰᵉ

“Who would make that decision?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“We'd have to agree on that part.” ᴰᵉ

Mau notices that Reinhard is still watching him. He doesn't have the look of someone waiting for Mau to make good on a threat. He keeps an eye on the envoy each time the man leans far enough to brush the scabbard. When the envoy finally sits back, Reinhard can let his arm rest. Mau wonders how many times somebody has stood in a room hoping that sword would give them the answer they wanted to hear.

“Are you asking me to turn myself over to you, or offering to tell me what to do from here?” ᴹ

“Either would be a start.” ᴰᵉ

“I won't do either.” ᴹ

The man asks the clerk to make a note that they have sought his cooperation. Testarossa asks him to specify which of the two things he sought. He says both can go in the record. By the time he has to hear the clerk read back the part about turning Mau over, two delegates on his own bench have stopped passing his list along.

One of them says she came to ask who gave the orders at the crossings. If the meeting is now considering custody, she'll need to go back to the people who sent her before she can answer for them. The man tells her there can be a vote when there is a question to put. She asks whether he intends to let the man they're voting about leave before then. He says they'll come to that.

Mau looks down at the paper in front of him. Fern's question is still visible beyond the clerk's hand. He folds his own copy and starts to put it back inside his coat.

While the man tells the clerk where he'd like the two requests put down, Mau turns his head towards Elmesia. She's waiting for him to catch her eye.

“Am I doing this right?” he asks. ᴹ

“You could be doing it worse,” she says. ᴱˡ

He gives that the consideration it deserves. The envoy from Lugunica has got his papers back into order. The man beside him is still speaking, this time to the rows beyond Mau, and someone from the eastern bench asks why a house that shelters him should be treated any differently from a kingdom that gives him a place to stand.
