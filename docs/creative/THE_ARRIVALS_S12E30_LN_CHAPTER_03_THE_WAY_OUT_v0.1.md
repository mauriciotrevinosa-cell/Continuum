# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 75
## Chapter 8 — The Way Out

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S12 v0.1
**Volume:** 75
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S12E30
**Continuity state:** B+273. Testarossa leaves the chamber with Mau's ant. End of Season 12. The house has yet to hear Mau's account.

*When: Testarossa is close enough to see the ant put its forelegs on the edge of the chair.*

It drops to the floor before anybody has decided what to do about the chair. A man standing between it and the door moves both feet to let it pass. The ant takes the space he gives it, skirts the end of the bench and stops behind a man who hasn't seen it coming.

“Did he just leave that here?” asks the man who wanted the door closed. ᴰᵉ

Testarossa rises so she can see what he's looking at. He has his eyes on the chair. The ant is already nearly out from under the table.

“I believe it's leaving too,” she says. ᵀᵉ

The man turns to the clerk. He says the record ought to show that Mau departed before the delegations had reached a decision. The clerk has his pen over the last line he wrote. He asks what decision the man would like him to put there.

“Whether we accept his refusal.” ᴰᵉ

“I had you down as saying you would come for his head,” the clerk says. ᶜˡ

The man brings both hands down on the table, palms first. Nobody on his bench speaks while he does it. The woman at the end asks the clerk for a copy of that part. She'll need it to explain to the people who sent her why she couldn't get an answer about the crossings.

Testarossa watches the clerk set a scrap of paper at the right place. He had been trying all morning to put down enough of what people said that they could recognise it afterward. Some of them are going to wish he'd done a little less of that.

“There was a question about the roads,” Luminous says. ᴸ

Her packet of tea is still holding the notice down. She lifts it so the clerk can slide the notice free. She asks for a copy of the page on the forms along with the one the woman requested. The clerk says he can have both ready if she can give him a little time. She takes her packet back and sits down to give it to him.

The Lugunican envoy has been collecting his pages. He has the one about Satella by itself. Reinhard stays beside him while he folds it and goes towards the door. The envoy pauses halfway there to ask whether he intends to come. Reinhard looks at the floor between them first. He takes one step back. The ant uses the opening, goes a little farther and stops beside the attendant's leg.

“I'll be along,” Reinhard says. ᴿʰ

The envoy takes his pages out. Testarossa sees one of the people from his bench get up a moment later, stop at the end of the table and put a paper in front of the clerk.

“That's the question I came with,” she says. “Can you send me anything they tell you about it?” ᴰᵇ

The clerk asks for a place to send it. She gives him one. Testarossa has her own place ready when he looks in her direction.

“I'd like the account of the yard, the question about the forms and what was said about closing the roads,” she says. “Send those to Tempest.” ᵀᵉ

He writes it down. She considers asking for the rest, but the clerk already has a request from Elmesia for everything that went in the book. They can compare the two when she sends it. For now, the copy she wants is the one her people will be able to read before they hear that the people on the roads are in trouble because somebody has voted to put them there.

The man who wanted the door closed has got as far as gathering his own copies when he asks for a page he can send to the lodges on his way back. The clerk gives him a spare sheet from under the book. The man asks whether he can have it stamped before he goes.

“What are you sending?” Testarossa asks. ᵀᵉ

“A notice that we met.” ᴰᵉ

“Your office can give notice that you met,” she says. “Tempest would like its name kept off anything you issue.” ᵀᵉ

For a moment Testarossa thinks he's going to ask again. He folds the spare sheet around his own papers and takes them out under his arm.

The clerk asks Elmesia whether she wants her copy sent on ahead or if she'll take it when it's ready. She tells him she'll wait. He brings a chair from beside the doorway nearer the table for her. From there she can see the part of the book he's copying.

Testarossa takes a moment to tell the clerk where in Tempest his letter should go. She gives him the name of the person who will open it. If anyone brings him another sheet with an empty place for a date, he can send that too. He writes the address in the margin of his own notes and underlines the name.

She goes round the table to meet the ant. It's still by the attendant. She stops just short of its antennae and leans down to catch its eye.

“Would you like help finding the way out?” ᵀᵉ

The ant raises its head. After a moment it gives her a nod. She goes ahead of it as far as the doorway. The attendant takes another step back and holds the door wide enough for them both.

Outside, it starts towards the middle of the steps. Testarossa points out a way down along the side where the stone is warm. The ant tries the first drop and finds it can manage it. She takes the next step while it works out how to make the turn.

Below them, a driver calls up to ask whether he ought to bring the carriage around. Somebody tells him to wait. Testarossa can see the low stone edge where the lane for people on foot runs past the lodge. An ordinary morning's traffic is still using it. Nobody down there has closed a road. Nobody has yet had time to go home and tell the people who use it what was proposed at the table.

The ant takes another step. When it looks up, she's still there.

“Your king secured several holidays and acquired several threats,” she tells the ant once they're out on the steps. “In that order.” ᵀᵉ

The ant makes a small sound.

Testarossa laughs. “Yes. He is a curious man.” ᵀᵉ
