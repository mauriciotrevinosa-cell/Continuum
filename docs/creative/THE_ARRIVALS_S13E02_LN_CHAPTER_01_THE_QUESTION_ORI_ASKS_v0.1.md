# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 76
## Chapter 3 — The Question Ori Asks

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 76
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S13E02
**Continuity state:** B+274 morning. Ordinary routes remain open and the reinforced watch reports. Ori asks whether the house will kill. The household agrees to stop the attackers, protect the injured on either side and seek out those who gave the orders. No attack date is known.

*When: The next morning, Ori has a question nobody put on Fern’s page.*

Fern has brought two cups to the table and keeps having to move the one she hasn't used. She wants to lay the report from the watch beside the notes Mau gave her yesterday. The report fits on half a page. It says when the cart left with the fixed axle, when the woman who brings bread got home and when the first extra group of shadows took over from the others.

“Anybody turn back?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“Nobody your shadows saw. I still want to know what they were saying at the other end of the road.” ᶠᵉ

She slides the cup over again. Ori puts it down on the other side of the notes, where Fern can reach it. She has come in with her drawing rolled up in both hands. She finds a place to put it against the wall so she can sit beside Frieren. The paper opens a little on its own.

“Are we changing which way we go to the Fort?” she asks. ᴼ

“No,” Rimuru says. “Muster's forty are still using the same doors. Bellion put the reserve out on the roads.” ᴿ

Ori looks at the report from where she's sitting. Fern turns it for her. She reads the bit about the axle slowly and gets to the part about the woman who brings bread. Then she asks whether the people at the meeting will know all the ways to the house.

“They know the main roads,” Mau says. “If they try to use them to come for somebody, we'll know as soon as one of mine sees them.” ᴹ

Ori puts her hand on the table. There are three little marks of pencil along one side of it, from yesterday. She tries to rub one off with her other thumb.

“Are we going to kill them?” she asks. ᴼ

“No,” Mau says. ᴹ

He says it so quickly that he hears it just after Ori does. She's looking right at him. He sets his hand down where she can see it.

“We're going to stop them. If somebody gets hurt, we'll help them. I want to get to the people who told them to come.” ᴹ

“What if they're the ones who get hurt?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“We help them too.” ᴹ

Frieren rests a hand on the back of Ori's chair. Ori lets the pencil mark alone and looks down the table. Maki has her cup halfway up. She takes a drink, puts it down and asks Mau what he thinks stopping them will mean when they can't get to the person giving the orders.

Mau had been thinking of the man with both hands on the table. He'd wanted him to put his name down, and the man had looked for somebody else who could give him permission first.

“It might mean we have to hold a road for longer than I'd like,” Mau says. “Or let somebody go when we could've followed him. If we know who's giving the orders, I want to stop him giving another one. I'd like somebody besides us to hear what he did.” ᴹ

“Where would you take him?” Maki asks. ᴹᵃᵏ

He hasn't worked out the answer. He says so. The people in the hall will have things to say about it, and some of them were already asking the man whether he had the authority to give the order he wanted. Fern asks if Mau would let those people have a say in what happens to the ones who gave it. He says he'd want that.

Shoko takes the cup Fern keeps moving and asks if she can have the rest of the water in it. Fern pushes it over. Shoko drinks and sets it down before she asks where he intends to bring the injured.

“Some of them may be able to go home with help. Some are going to need somebody to meet them right where they are. If you carry one in without warning, I'm going to have to ask you questions while you're trying to get him down off your shoulder.” ˢʰ

Mau puts both hands on the table. “What would you like us to tell you first?” ᴹ

“Where they are and whether they can be moved. Then I'll tell you what I need.” ˢʰ

He looks at Ori. She had asked whether they were going to kill anybody. She listens to what Shoko says about being called early, and when Shoko is finished she takes Fern's pencil and writes *ask first* in the margin beside her own name. Fern has to lean over to see it. She leaves it there.

“We're all deciding this, aren't we?” Ori asks. ᴼ

Maki looks at her. “Yes. And if anybody at this table thinks they can't do what we just said, now would be a good time to say so.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Nobody speaks right away. Mau lets the silence go on. It takes a while for him to get his own thoughts in order. The parts he knows he can do are the ones that involve reaching a place in time. The parts he hasn't thought through yet will decide what the others have to do when he isn't there.

“I'm going to need help with that,” he says. ᴹ

“So are the rest of us,” Rimuru says. “We can work it out here.” ᴿ

“Start with me, then,” Maki says. “Suppose I'm on the road and someone goes down. You know I can hold the road. You don't know if I saw them fall. Who do you call?” ᴹᵃᵏ

Mau looks at Shoko. She's looking at him already. The answer that came to him first was that he'd go and see for himself. He has no idea where he was when Maki asked the question. He could have been on a different road, with somebody else asking him the same thing.

“I call you,” he says to Maki. “You tell me what you can see. If you can hold, we tell Shoko where to send somebody. If you can't, we have to get you both some help.” ᴹ

Maki gives him a nod. Ori uncurls the corner of Fern's notes to look at what she wrote. She draws a second line beneath it. Mau starts to ask if she'd like another piece of paper, then sees she's still thinking.

“What if they want somebody from their side to come?” Ori asks. ᴼ

Shoko considers it. “If that person knows how to help, I'd be glad to have another pair of hands.” ˢʰ

Ori leaves the second line empty. Mau can see she has something she wants to put there. She borrows the pencil long enough to put a little mark beside it so she'll know where to start when she's got the words.

* * *

When they get up, there is enough time left in the morning to do the things they'd said they would do the night before. Yuka goes to get her shoes. Mai is still going to look at the heel she fixed yesterday, and Yuka intends to give her a chance to be pleased with herself before she says anything about the mud.

Mau asks Fern whether she'd like him to stay while she writes back to Testarossa. Fern tells him she sent it earlier. She shows him the place where she kept a copy. She asked for the record, the names of the people who said they'd look for an order, and whether the ant made it out.

“You put that in the same letter?” he asks. ᴹ

“I wanted the answer to all three questions.” ᶠᵉ

He can see the line at the bottom. It has as much room as any of the others. He thanks her for putting it there.

On his way out he finds Ori standing by the drawing. She's rolled it back up, with the pencil tucked inside this time. He asks if she wants him to carry it. She gives him the pencil instead.

“I'm going to show it to Milim,” she says. “She says the wings count.” ᴼ

Mau puts the pencil in his pocket where he can find it when she wants it back. They take the short way to the Fort, through the door Chloe said worked. It opens the same way it did yesterday. On the other side, Milim already has a ruler.
