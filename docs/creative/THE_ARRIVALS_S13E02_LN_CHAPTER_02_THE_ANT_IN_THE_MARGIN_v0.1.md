# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 76
## Chapter 4 — The Ant in the Margin

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 76
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E02
**Continuity state:** B+274 afternoon. Testarossa, Luminous and Elmesia confirm they will stay alongside the house. Testarossa requests the ant’s company, Mau agrees subject to its choice, and she names it Antonio. No transfer of shadow allegiance. No attack date yet.

*When: Testarossa has answered Fern’s question about the ant in a letter of its own.*

The three letters arrive before the last of the lunch things are put away. Fern takes them by the door. One is addressed to Rimuru and has a second name underneath. She reads both, then goes to find Mau. She brings him back to the table, where Yuka is sitting with Mai's notes on one side of her plate and half a slice of bread on the other.

Testarossa has written for Tempest first. The permits for the house still stand. Tempest heard the proposal in the hall, discussed it and will keep recognising its people on the roads. Anyone who has trouble with a permit can come to a Tempest post. Testarossa gives the clerk's wording about what was proposed and what was actually decided. She gives it its own paragraph. Fern reads that one twice.

“What does the second page say?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Fern turns it over. The second page is folded the other way. Testarossa wanted Mau to know that his ant got down the steps and went all the way to the lodge with her. It chose to come along a little farther when she asked. She finds it agreeable company and would like to know whether it can stay.

Mau reads that bit for himself. An ink print takes up most of the space beneath the question. He can see where Testarossa must have held the page steady for the ant to put one foot on it. There's a faint second print on the corner where it stepped off.

“It went with her?” asks Yuka. ʸ

“I saw it coming down the steps with her.” ᴹ

He asks Fern for something to write on. Before he gets the page back from her, a message comes from the post. Testarossa sent the letters on ahead. She's come to speak to Rimuru about the record and would like to know where he wants her to wait.

“Here, if she has time,” Rimuru says from the doorway. ᴿ

Fern takes his cup off the place he was about to set it. She brings another chair to the end of the table.

* * *

The ant comes in at Testarossa's knee. Its antennae sweep the legs of the chair Fern brought over. Mau sits back to give it a little room and offers a hand. It puts one forefoot in his palm. He holds it long enough to ask if it's happy where it is.

It makes a small sound and looks back at Testarossa.

“Then you can stay,” he says. He lowers his hand so it can go back to her. “She knows where to bring you if you change your mind.” ᴹ

It goes around the table to where Testarossa has set her case down. She takes out a folded cloth and puts it on the floor. The ant wipes its front feet on it without being asked.

“Thank you,” she says. “Antonio, you've left footprints on my good paper.” ᵀᵉ

The ant turns around at once. Mau looks from it to her.

“Why Antonio?” ᴹ

“Because he's an ant and he looks like an onion. Antonio.” ᵀᵉ

It reminds Mau of a man who could make himself small and had given one of his ants a name. He can't remember whether the man ever explained the name to anybody. He thinks he'd have remembered this explanation.

She says it in exactly the voice she'd used for the trade permits. Mau takes a second to see if anything on her face is about to change. Nothing does. Yuka folds the edge of Mai's notes down with her thumb and starts to laugh.

“Do you see the onion?” Mau asks her. ᴹ

“I'm trying.” ʸ

Antonio settles beside the cloth. He raises his head when Testarossa says his name a second time. Mau laughs at that too. By the time Fern asks whether there is anything else in the case, Antonio has put both forefeet over the clean edge of the cloth, as though waiting to be told he's finished.

There is a copy of the clerk's record in the case. Fern takes it and lets Testarossa finish telling Rimuru what happened after Mau left. Elmesia waited for the clerk to get through the pages she wanted, and Luminous took the parts about the roads with her. The man who proposed the restrictions asked for a stamp on a sheet he wanted to send. He went out with his own papers and an empty one.

“Did anybody give him authority to close anything?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“Not in that room.” ᵀᵉ

Rimuru asks where to send news if somebody tries it anyway. Testarossa writes the place on the bottom of his copy. She waits while Fern puts it beside the address from the letter, where either of them can find it.

“I meant what I said about taking some distance,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know.” Testarossa sits back from the table. “We talked about who would have to explain it if we did. There are people on our roads who know your soldiers by the way they hold the gate while a cart goes through. I couldn't tell them why we'd ask those soldiers to go home at the first sign that somebody might need them.” ᵀᵉ

Mau thinks of the ant taking the steps while she went on beside it. He'd expected to have to call it back before he left. He hadn't expected to be sitting across from her the next day asking whether he could make things easier by disappearing from her road.

“What should I tell the ones at the gate?” he asks. ᴹ

“That we're grateful. And that the people who relieved them yesterday will come at the usual time.” ᵀᵉ

He tells her he'll pass it on. She bends down to open the other side of the case. Antonio puts one forefoot beside her boot and taps the cuff of her sleeve with an antenna. She puts her hand on his head while she looks through the rest of the papers.

* * *

Luminous has included the name of the merchant who sells her tea. Fern is to write to him at the post on his own road if there is trouble there. If someone needs a place to be attended, the posts will give them one. Mau reads the rest aloud at Fern's request. Luminous says she has given the same answer to her people that she is giving to him. She considers the roads open and expects anyone who says otherwise to bring her something with a name on it.

Fern asks if she can make two copies of the directions to the posts. One for the watch and one for Shoko. Mau reads her the first address slowly enough for her to get it down. Yuka goes to get another pencil when the one Fern has stops leaving a line.

By the time she comes back, Mau has opened the last letter. He sees the same hand that wrote on the hotel card. Elmesia tells him that if a Thalion paper stops being accepted on a road, that is a matter for Thalion to take up. She'll hear about it from her own people. She wants to hear about it from his, too.

He stops halfway down. Yuka sets the pencil beside Fern and touches the bottom edge of his page.

“Can you read me that part?” she asks. ʸ

He looks at her. Then he goes back to the beginning of the line.

“‘I meant what I said about the island. I mean this, too. If I only make room for you when things are going well, what kind of friend would that make me?’” ᴹ

He can hear it better out loud. He reads the sentence again to himself while Yuka keeps her hand on the bottom of the letter. Then he folds it once, carefully, and gives it to Fern long enough for her to take down the part her people need for the road.

“I'm going to write back to her,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka nods. “I'll leave you some room on my page.” ʸ

He tells her he might need quite a lot. She pulls the spare sheet he'd asked Fern for over to her side of the table and turns it so they can both use it. He has time to write Elmesia's name before the messenger from the outer post arrives.
