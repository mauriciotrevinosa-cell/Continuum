# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 76
## Chapter 5 — The Day She Gives Them

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 76
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E03
**Continuity state:** B+274 late afternoon into evening. Makima names B+280 evening as the planned attack and gives a verifiable lead. She requests residence in the village. Fern corroborates a road booking for that evening, not its purpose or all participants. Makima waits outside the ward while the house considers her request.

*When: The message from the outer post comes while Mau still has the pencil in his hand.*

Makima asked to speak to Rimuru and Fern. She asked for Mau as well if he was willing to come. She is waiting outside the ward, where they met the last time. Rimuru reads the message and gives it to Fern. Mau looks at the first line he's written to Elmesia. There isn't enough of it yet to tell her why he's writing.

“I'd like to hear what she came to tell us,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka takes the pencil from beside his hand. “Will you come back and tell me?” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

They find Makima at the post in the late afternoon. There's a bench under the open side of the shelter. She gets up when she sees all three of them and moves to one end. Fern keeps her papers in her hand.

“Six days from today,” Makima says. “At dusk.” ᴹᵏ

Mau stops with his hand on the end of the bench. Yesterday he'd heard somebody ask for permission to take people off a road. This is the first time anybody has given him a day when they mean to do it.

“That's when they plan to come?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“Yes. They had the date before the hearing. Some of the men asking for authority were hoping to have it by then.” ᴹᵏ

“Who gave it to you?” Fern asks. ᶠᵉ

Makima tells her the part she can have checked. There's a woman at a tollhouse who keeps a copy of the bookings she makes after the clerks take their book upstairs. She remembers a man asking how many carts could arrive together after the last of the ordinary traffic. The clerk told her to keep the road free six evenings from now. She thought somebody wanted room to bring cattle through. When she asked which village the cattle were coming from, she was told to leave the reason off the copy.

“Did you ask her yourself?” Fern says. ᶠᵉ

“She spoke to someone I know. She might be glad to tell you herself if you ask about her copy first.” ᴹᵏ

Fern takes down the name of the tollhouse. She asks which side the woman works on. Makima tells her. Rimuru waits until Fern has folded the page.

“That tells us when somebody wants the road clear,” Fern says. “What tells you why?” ᶠᵉ

Makima looks out through the opening at the side of the post. The path there runs straight for a while before it turns back towards the village.

“The people I heard were asking how long it takes your people to come out when somebody calls for help,” she says. “They wanted an answer from somebody who'd actually seen you do it.” ᴹᵏ

Mau sits down. He can think of three occasions somebody might have meant. He leaves the question of which one alone until Fern asks if Makima heard anything about where they expected the call to come from. Makima tells her she didn't hear that part.

“What else did you come for?” he asks. ᴿ

“I want to live here.” ᴹᵏ

Mau looks from her to the ward. A person on the other side is walking home with both hands under a parcel. They slow to let somebody pass and then go through the same door they'd been using yesterday.

Rimuru sits at the end of the bench Makima left free. “Tell us what you're asking for.” ᴿ

“A room inside. Somewhere I can have a key and come home when I want to.” ᴹᵏ

“Why ask today?” ᴿ

She looks at Mau. He thinks she's going to answer him even though it was Rimuru who asked. She looks back at Rimuru first.

“Because today you have a reason to say yes.” ᴹᵏ

Fern's fingers close over the paper. Mau watches Makima leave the sentence where she put it. She has told them enough that they can go and ask a woman what she was told to do. There may be other things she is waiting to see if they will ask for.

“Would you tell us the rest if the answer was no?” he asks. ᴹ

“I came here expecting to be asked that,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He waits. Rimuru does too. The sun has reached the side of the shelter. Mau can see the lower edge of the post make a line across Makima's sleeve as she leans forward.

“I would tell you if the day changed,” she says. “I want to be here when you decide what to do with the rest.” ᴹᵏ

Fern asks if she can take the name now and get somebody to ask the question before the woman goes home. Makima says yes. Rimuru tells her they will talk at the house and come back with an answer. She asks whether they want her to wait at the post. Rimuru says she can if she likes. She sits back and folds her hands on her knee.

* * *

When Mau gets home, Yuka is by the door where they left the letters. She stands up when she sees him. He takes his coat off first because he'd like to sit down with her and the coat always ends up between them if he forgets.

“She says they're coming in six days,” he tells her. “Fern is checking. She wants to live here, too. She brought both things together.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at the place he has left beside him and sits down.

“Do you want her to?” she asks. ʸ

“I want the information. I think she could help us work out what to do with it. I don't know yet if I want what she's asking for.” ᴹ

She takes his hand. “Then say all of that when we get there.” ʸ

He thought he had to have the rest of the answer by the time he got home. He looks at their hands for a while. Then he tells her he'd like to hear what she thinks before they go down.

“I'm angry she picked today to ask,” Yuka says. “I think she knew some of us would hear the date and forget we could still tell her where she could go.” ʸ

“I don't want us to forget.” ᴹ

“I know. I want to be there when we talk about it.” ʸ

He sits with her until they hear Mai come up to tell them Fern got an answer. The tollkeeper has seen the order and can give them the date without seeing what Makima said. She can also name the clerk who took the original upstairs. Fern has kept the two accounts apart so that they can see where they agree.

Yuka picks up the paper they'd started to write to Elmesia and puts it somewhere it won't get covered by the others.

“Six evenings,” she says. ʸ

Mau knows what he's going to say when they sit down. He knows he will still have to listen to everything that comes after it. He takes Yuka's hand, and they go down together.
