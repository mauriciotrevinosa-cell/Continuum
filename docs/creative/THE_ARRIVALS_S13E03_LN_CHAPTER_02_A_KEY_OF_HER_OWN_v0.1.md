# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 76
## Chapter 6 — A Key of Her Own

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 76
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S13E03
**Continuity state:** B+274 evening into B+275 morning. After hearing objections and asking Makima to disclose what she knows, the house consents to residence in a room of her own. Access is on the household’s terms and no new power or command is granted. B+280 dusk remains the expected attack time.

*When: Umaru asks to hear what Makima would have asked for if she had come yesterday.*

Mau tells them he would like to hear the rest of what she knows. He says the part about the room afterward. Yuka is beside him. He can see a corner of the letter to Elmesia sticking out under the clean sheet she brought down for Fern.

“Do we have to answer her tonight?” Umaru asks. ᵁ

Rimuru pulls out the chair opposite hers. “We owe her an answer. We can take the time to give her one we mean.” ᴿ

Umaru moves her cup a little closer to herself. “What would she have asked for if she'd come yesterday?” ᵁ

Mau thinks of the bench on the other side of the ward. He remembers the way Makima looked at him before she went back to Rimuru to give him her answer.

“The same room, probably,” he says. ᴹ

“I'd have had something different to say about it yesterday. I don't like that she gets to pick which day I have to say it on.” ᵁ

Mau leaves his hand flat on the table. The first thing he wants to tell her is that he thinks Makima could do some good here. He'd still like to tell her that once he's heard what else she has to say.

“You're allowed to be angry about the day she picked,” he says. ᴹ

“I am. And I'm going to be angry tomorrow, too.” ᵁ

Rem has been listening from the seat beside her. She moves her cup out of the way when Umaru starts looking for somewhere to put her elbow.

Satella comes in while Fern is reading back what the tollkeeper told her. She stops when she hears the day. Maki gets up to give her a seat and tells her which part they are still waiting to hear. Satella takes it. She asks whether the people who came for her name at the hearing are the same ones coming up the road.

“We can check the date against an order the woman actually saw,” Fern says. “Nobody has given me a list of everybody who made it. I'll tell you when I know whose name is on it.” ᶠᵉ

Satella leaves one hand on the chair for a while after she sits. Then she asks if she can come with them when they speak to Makima. She'd like to hear for herself whether anything else was said about the people who live here. Rimuru asks whether anyone else would like to come. Umaru says she does. When she gets up, she waits for Rem to decide whether she wants to come too.

* * *

They bring one lamp to the post. By the time they get there Makima has moved from the bench to the step so the man who is waiting for the next watch can sit down. He gets up when he sees Rimuru. Rimuru tells him to finish what he's doing and finds a place beside the post where they can set the lamp.

Fern tells Makima what the tollkeeper confirmed. She leaves out the bit about where she keeps her other copies. Then she asks whether Makima has heard anyone name the people they mean to take.

“I heard them ask whether Satella leaves the ward,” Makima says. “I heard somebody say the people who come out to help her might be useful alive.” ᴹᵏ

Satella looks at her. “Did you hear me named by somebody who will be coming?” ˢᵃ

“I heard your name from somebody who will be sending people.” ᴹᵏ

“Can you tell me who?” ˢᵃ

Makima tells her. Fern asks her to spell the name for the page. Mau watches her give the letters to Fern one at a time while Satella waits beside the lamp. He'd been worrying about how to tell Satella what this meant. She asks two more questions before he has anything to add.

When they finish, Umaru asks what happens to the information if they tell Makima she can't have the room. Makima looks down at the page. She'd already given them the name. She puts a finger on the end of the bench and draws it back before she answers.

“The day is yours either way. The names I've heard are yours too. I want you to say yes to the room.” ᴹᵏ

“Why?” Umaru asks. ᵁ

“I'd like to come in at the end of a day and have somewhere I can go without asking a person to let me in.” ᴹᵏ

Umaru holds her eyes for a while. “What if I don't want you at my table?” ᵁ

“Then I can eat somewhere else.” ᴹᵏ

Umaru sits on the edge of the step. Rem stays beside her. Mau can see her thinking of another question and then deciding she'd rather keep it until she needs it.

Rimuru says there is a room off the gallery with a door of its own. It is close enough that Makima can get to the common yard and far enough that nobody has to walk past her door to get to their own. He tells her how they will handle visitors. He tells her what he expects from her if somebody asks her to give them room. When he gets to the way the ward works he leaves that part to Maki, who tells Makima she can ask at the post if a rule there is unclear.

“And your meetings with Mau?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Makima turns to her. “I'd still like to have them.” ᴹᵏ

“Then you still ask. A key to your room is a key to your room.” ʸ

Makima leaves that answer alone long enough for Yuka to see she heard it. Then she says she understands.

Mau tells the people who came what he thinks. He thinks she can help them understand what is coming. He thinks they ought to be able to go on saying what they think to her when she lives here. He tells Umaru that he intends to listen if she wants to talk to him about it again tomorrow.

“You might be busy tomorrow,” Umaru says. ᵁ

“Find me anyway.” ᴹ

She looks down at Rem's hand beside hers. She takes it, then says she can live with the room. She'd like them to talk again about how things are going after they've actually done it for a while. Maki says she'd like that too. Rimuru tells Makima he can bring her the key in the morning. Makima asks what time he usually gets there. He tells her when he'll be there tomorrow.

* * *

In the morning she comes back with one bag. She waits at the post until Rimuru comes out to meet her. He gives her the key there. She checks which way he's going before she lifts the bag and walks in with him.

In the gallery there is a window between her door and the common yard. Someone has put the little iron latch on backwards. Makima finds it that way when she rests a hand on the sill to let Rimuru point out the steps.

“I can have somebody look at that,” he says. ᴿ

She asks if he minds if she looks at it herself first. He says she can. Then he goes back the way they came and she takes her key to the door. She gets it into the lock on the first try. For a little while she stands with the bag in one hand and the door open in front of her. Then she takes the bag inside.
