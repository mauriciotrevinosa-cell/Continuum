# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 76
## Chapter 9 — The Part That Felt Right

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 76
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S13E05
**Continuity state:** B+276 morning. Mau volunteers his remembered account of the alteration from the previous life. Shuna and Frieren distinguish the false sense of familiarity from erasure and devise a tentative test. No deliberate exposure to the attacker or confirmed immunity.

*When: Shuna asks Mau to tell it in the order he felt it, including the parts that had seemed ordinary.*

Shuna has asked for a morning when Mau can stop halfway through. Frieren brings her own cup and puts it on the sill, well away from the papers. Yuka walks with him as far as the door. He asks her whether she wants to come in.

“I do. Do you want me to?” she asks. ʸ

“Yes. If you have the time.” ᴹ

She takes the chair he can reach. He finds that he needs to ask for her hand before he starts. She gives it to him while Shuna is still getting her pencil out, and he doesn't have to look up from what their hands are doing to say the first thing.

“I didn't think I'd forgotten anyone,” he says. ᴹ

Shuna writes that down before she asks him what he could remember.

“Faces. Rooms. What people ate. If you asked where someone slept, I could tell you.” He stops. There is a part of the memory he has to get to by thinking of how cold the field was. “I'd have told you I liked everyone there. I thought they'd given me a place to stay and I ought to thank them for it.” ᴹ

“Did you notice feeling different?” Shuna asks. ˢʰᵘ

“No. That's what I remember most clearly.” ᴹ

Frieren picks her cup back up. Mau can hear the very slight sound it makes when her sleeve brushes the rim. He knows which person he saw standing in the frost that night. The person sitting in front of him now has asked to hear what he remembers, and has taken enough time with him before asking that he can look at her when he tells her.

“I knew your name,” he says. “I thought knowing your name was the same as knowing you.” ᴹ

Frieren looks at the cup for a while. Then she asks if anybody said something he expected to mean more than it did.

He remembers Kamish calling him *small king*. He'd answered as though somebody had called him from the other end of a room. The old dragon had left a silence where he would usually have said the rest. Mau had known it was Kamish, and he'd thought the silence meant he'd changed his mind about asking for something.

“What about Ori?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Mau can feel her thumb on the inside of his palm. He tells her about the paper Ori had folded up. The names were all things he could recognise if she held it in front of him. He hadn't understood what it cost her that he could.

He gets quiet. Shuna puts the pencil down. Outside, somebody calls to another person to hold a door, and somebody else tells them they're coming. It takes a little while for the door to shut.

“Do you want to stop?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“For a minute.” ᴹ

She leaves her hand where it is. He takes the whole minute. When he looks up, Shuna is still sitting the way she was before. He asks what she'd like to know about next. She tells him she wants to go back to the first breath he remembers taking after the change.

* * *

They go through it again more slowly. Mau remembers frost. He remembers a cookie in his pocket. He remembers wanting to give a second one to somebody who already had one in her hand. The field had been full of people a little before that. He remembers knowing they had gone home. That had seemed to him an answer he could give the whole house.

Shuna asks when he remembers first hearing the people near him. He takes a while over it. There was a voice that could have been behind a wall or part of a dream. He'd let it pass because the world he'd woken into had made enough sense already.

“Would a warning from inside your head have helped?” she asks. ˢʰᵘ

“I might've made sense of that too.” ᴹ

Frieren puts her cup down beside Shuna's papers this time. “What would somebody standing next to him have been able to see?” ᶠ

Shuna turns the paper over. She's put a little line through each place she wanted to ask him to go back to. On the other side there are none. She starts by writing down that somebody could have heard him answer. She gets as far as how they would know what the answer was supposed to be and stops.

“Something held outside of him,” she says. “And somebody who knows how to ask him without telling him what answer they're waiting for.” ˢʰᵘ

Mau lets Yuka's hand go so she can take the paper when Shuna passes it down. Yuka reads it. She asks whether he remembers being in pain before or after anybody spoke to him. He doesn't remember enough to tell her. She hands it back without giving him a reason he might have felt one.

Frieren asks Shuna if she can mark what it looks like when one of her wards takes a small touch. Shuna says she can. Then Frieren asks whether she can mark where it was touched from if they use more than one. Shuna brings a second sheet across and puts the two side by side. She could try. It would take someone standing far enough away to make the differences matter.

“Would you use me for that?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“I'd like to use the paper first,” Shuna says. “We can make a mark move on a piece of paper without asking you to remember anything you don't want to remember.” ˢʰᵘ

He looks down at the lines she drew while he was talking. It's a little easier to see why she asked him to take it one breath at a time. She wanted to hear how completely an ordinary thing could still feel ordinary.

“If you do need to ask me again later, you can,” he says. ᴹ

Shuna looks at him, then puts the blank sheet over the page with the lines so he doesn't have to keep seeing it. Frieren takes both and moves them over to the window, where there is light enough to see the edge of one through the other.

“Come back after lunch if you want to see whether it worked on the paper,” she tells him. ᶠ

Yuka gets up with him. At the door he asks if she meant to go somewhere else this morning. She tells him yes. She wants to see Mai about the shoe again, and if he likes he can walk over there with her. He takes her hand without having to ask for it this time.
