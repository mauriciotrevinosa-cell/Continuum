# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 76
## Chapter 14 — The Man in the White Helmet

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 76
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S13E07
**Continuity state:** B+280 dusk. First confrontation at the lower road. A wounded attacker chooses treatment. Mau meets an independent rescue worker and confirms the house will let his group reach the injured. No casualties confirmed.

*When: The first man Mau goes out to meet has come to carry somebody away.*

The carts stop well before the bridge. There is a woman with a basket between the last cart and the first line of men on the road. One of Bellion's soldiers is walking beside her. When the soldier sees Mau, it lifts a hand and keeps going at the woman's pace. The basket tilts. She puts her other hand under it and gets across the bridge without putting anything down.

Mau waits for her to be past him. There had been a nice clean space for him to stand when Fern showed him the map. In person the space belongs to a woman who has been on her feet all day and has a basket to get home. He leaves it to her. She looks back just once, and by then he's moved far enough that she can see the soldier beside her again.

A man at the front of the line calls for Satella by name. Mau asks who sent him. The man reads from a page he holds far enough away to see it over the rim of his shield. There is a place in what he reads where a name would fit. He calls it the authority of the nations and goes on to the part about how long they have to answer.

“Which nations?” Mau asks. ᴹ

The man looks up. Somebody behind him calls to him to finish reading. He gets as far as the first word when the man to his left comes forward without him.

Mau had expected a line to move like a line. The first three don't wait for the fourth. One of them comes close enough to swing. Mau brings the surface of the sky up between them and feels the blade go past his shoulder into empty air. He takes the man's wrist when it comes back down. The man lets go of the sword much later than Mau expects. Mau holds him through the part where he still thinks he can get it back, then lets him sit down on his own.

Bellion's soldiers move. Mau hears three of them pass behind him. A fourth reaches the cart and points to a pair of hands gripping its side. Mau signals the shadows to hold until the owner gets out. A moment later the hands are gone and the cart begins to roll towards the place the woman with the basket came from.

From the other side of the road comes a cry. Mau turns in time to see a man on the ground holding his leg. Someone in a white helmet is running towards him. The helmet has a blue mark on it, two open hands with a little space between. The man has got a strap over his shoulder and is trying to get it off while he runs.

Mau steps between him and the line.

The man stops so hard the strap goes over his head by itself.

“I'm going to him,” he says. ᴴ

Mau looks down. The strap is attached to a bag. The man on the ground has seen it too. He is trying to get his heel off the road and can't hold it up long enough to make room for a hand underneath.

“What do the hands mean?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“We go for the hurt. Anybody who's hurt. I've got three others down by the turn.” ᴴ

Mau moves aside. The man gets to his knees next to the injured soldier. He asks whether the man can feel his foot. Mau waits for the answer. When it comes, the man in the helmet looks back at him.

“Can you keep that lot from coming through here while I do this?” ᴴ

“Yes,” Mau says. ᴹ

He calls to Bellion. The nearest shadows spread far enough apart for the man on the ground to see the road. They turn to face the people still standing. A man from the line shouts the injured soldier's name. He gets no farther than the ditch before one of the shadows steps into his path and raises a hand. Mau tells the man in the helmet that somebody is calling. The injured man closes his eyes.

“Don't tell him where I'm going,” he says. ᴵⁿ

The man in the helmet stops with one hand over the bag. He asks the soldier whether he wants to go anywhere yet. The soldier opens his eyes and looks at Mau.

“He's going to take me as a prisoner,” the soldier says. ᴵⁿ

“I can send for somebody to look at your leg. If you need help sooner than he can give it here, we'll take you to the closest place that can give it.” Mau leaves enough space for the man to hear the rest. “You can ask somebody to come for you when you're ready.” ᴹ

“Somebody I pick?” ᴵⁿ

“Yes.” ᴹ

The man says a name very quietly. The rescuer asks if he can write it on the back of the card he's using to keep track of where they found him. The man nods. Mau waits while he gets it down.

Then Mau calls Fern and tells her what the blue hands mean. She asks whether the rescue workers have agreed to cross. He puts the question to the man beside him, who looks at the four shadows and says he would if his people can find the same way back out.

“They can,” Mau tells him. “Tell them to show the hands. I'll tell my soldiers to let them through.” ᴹ

He sends the order to Bellion with a description of the helmet. Bellion passes it to Muster for the gate and down the line to the shadows on the road.

Fern repeats what he told her. Mau hears her ask for Shoko while he's still holding the connection. By then the white helmet is bent over the injured man's leg. Mau goes to the edge of the little space the shadows made and stays there until he hears another set of feet coming down the road.
