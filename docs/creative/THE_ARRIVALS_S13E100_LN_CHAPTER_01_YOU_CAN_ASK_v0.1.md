# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 23 — You Can Ask

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S13E100
**Continuity state:** Ori reveals withheld stay request. Mau/Yuka/Frieren listen and apologize for assumptions without blaming mother or child; no impossible availability promise. Ori chooses whole Fort castle siege, scheduled with preparation time; Mau assigned Kamish aide under play rules.

*When: B+403, late afternoon before dinner; continuous after homecoming.*

Ori comes to their room before dinner.

Mau has the last of the clean clothes in his hands. Yuka is standing in front of the open drawer, telling him that putting something away does not mean placing it on top of the thing she is trying to find.

He is preparing a defence when they hear the knock.

Yuka opens the door.

Ori has a page held flat against her chest. She looks from one of them to the other before asking whether she can come in.

“Of course,” Yuka says. ʸ

Mau puts the clothes down and sits on the edge of the bed. Ori comes to him, shows him the picture she has brought and begins explaining the part which did not fit on the earlier letter.

It is the beach town, drawn from above. The shell door is much larger than the houses around it. Lantern occupies a stretch of water which Mau believes may once have been a road, although Ori tells him the arrangement is deliberate.

“He has to fit.” ᴼ

“A very sensible planning decision.” ᴹ

She nods, satisfied, and shows Yuka the place where she has put her.

Yuka sits on the other side and leans in to see.

“There I am.” ʸ

“You're next to Dad.” ᴼ

Mau's hand rests lightly against the back of Ori's shirt. She continues pointing out the other people, checking that each one is recognized before moving to the next.

At the end, she folds neither the page nor the conversation away.

“Can I ask something?” ᴼ

Mau looks at her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

---

“If I asked next time, could I stay?” ᴼ

Yuka turns toward her fully.

“On the island?” ʸ

Ori nods.

“With us?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Another nod. She looks down at the picture, smoothing one corner against her leg.

“I would ask Mama.” ᴼ

“We'd talk to her,” Yuka says. “What made you think about it?” ʸ

Ori presses the corner flat again.

“I wanted to before.” ᴼ

Mau feels the little movement beneath his hand stop.

“Before you came home?” ᴹ

“When you were talking about the mornings.” ᴼ

He remembers the small meal near the beach. Yuka describing the days she wanted with him. Ori beside Frieren, looking at the shell.

He had seen her there.

“Yuka told you that you could come to us,” he says, more quietly. “Before we left. Did something make you think that changed?” ᴹ

Ori shakes her head immediately.

“I knew I could ask. She told me.” ᴼ

Yuka moves closer, listening.

“Then what made it hard?” she asks. ʸ

Ori looks at them with a sincerity which leaves Mau nowhere comfortable to put the answer before it arrives.

“You were happy.” ᴼ

Yuka's fingers close against the edge of the bed.

“I didn't want you to stop,” Ori says. ᴼ

Mau looks at his wife. The hurt in her face is the same shape as the thing he has not found words for yet.

Ori sees the look and begins to draw the page back.

“It was okay. I came with Mama. Chloe was here.” ᴼ

“I know,” Mau says at once. ᴹ

He puts a hand over the page before she can fold it, then moves it so that he can take hers instead.

“You didn't do anything wrong by coming home.” ᴹ

Ori looks up.

“And you wouldn't have done anything wrong by asking to stay.” ᴹ

Her face changes slowly.

Yuka moves closer.

“We should have asked what you wanted before we finished making the arrangements,” she says. ʸ

“You wanted the mornings,” Ori says. ᴼ

“I did,” Yuka answers. “I still like having mornings with him. I like having mornings with you too.” ʸ

Ori looks between them.

“You can be happy if I stay?” ᴼ

Mau draws her into his arms before the question can get any smaller.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

She holds onto him. Yuka puts a hand against her back, warm and steady, and waits until Ori turns enough to include her.

“You're never a nuisance because you want to be with us,” Yuka says. ʸ

Ori's mouth begins to tremble. Mau feels the effort she makes to keep it still and holds her closer without asking her to explain any more before she can.

---

They ask whether she wants Frieren.

Ori says yes. Yuka goes to find her, leaving the picture open on the bed rather than putting it somewhere the conversation might appear to have ended.

Mau sits with Ori across his lap. She has one hand caught in his shirt and is looking at the page with the grave attention she gives things she wants to get right.

“Sometimes we might say not that day,” he tells her. “Or we might have to work out how. That doesn't mean we don't want you.” ᴹ

She turns toward him.

“You can still ask. You don't have to make yourself not want it first.” ᴹ

She thinks about that.

“Even if you're doing something?” ᴼ

“You can ask. We can tell you when we'll listen properly.” ᴹ

“And then you'll listen?” ᴼ

He looks at her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Ori rests her forehead against his chest. He puts his chin lightly above her head and waits while she holds him, the little hand in his shirt loosening only when she has found a comfortable place.

When Frieren arrives, she comes directly to the bed.

Yuka has told her enough. Frieren kneels where Ori can see her face, and the girl immediately reaches toward her.

“I didn't know you wanted to ask,” Frieren says. ᶠ

“You wanted to come home.” ᴼ

“I did. We could still have talked about what you wanted.” ᶠ

Ori looks down.

Frieren touches the side of her face, waiting for her to look back.

“You don't have to choose which of us is allowed to hear you want something.” ᶠ

Ori's breath catches. She goes into her mother's arms, and Frieren holds her without looking at the others for an answer which belongs to the child.

Mau stays close enough that Ori can keep her hand on him. Yuka sits beside them, the picture between her knee and the place where Ori was sitting.

They do not hurry the quiet which follows.

---

After a while, Ori asks whether they can have a day.

Mau looks at her over Frieren's shoulder.

“What kind of day?” ᴹ

“I pick.” ᴼ

Yuka's smile arrives through the little bit of sadness still on her face.

“You pick,” she agrees. ʸ

Ori straightens enough to think. The seriousness of it begins to change. There is something else behind her eyes now, a plan she has apparently been carrying in a much less painful place.

“The Fort,” she says. ᴼ

“All right.” ᴹ

“And Kamish.” ᴼ

Mau pauses.

Frieren looks at Yuka.

Ori turns toward him.

“He has a castle.” ᴼ

“I have a castle,” Mau says. ᴹ

“He needs one for the war.” ᴼ

Yuka covers her mouth with one hand.

“Which war?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Ori has found the part of the plan she is certain about.

“Ours.” ᴼ

He looks at Frieren. She is watching her daughter with an expression which suggests that the planning decision is no longer his to prevent.

“Do I have a role?” he asks. ᴹ

Ori considers him carefully.

“You're with Kamish.” ᴼ

“A proud appointment.” ᴹ

“But you can't do anything.” ᴼ

Yuka begins laughing. Mau looks at his daughter, then at the picture of the beach town on the bed.

“I see.” ᴹ

“It's a rule,” Ori explains. ᴼ

“Of course it is.” ᴹ

They choose the day together, leaving time to prepare the field and ask the others. When Ori goes downstairs afterward, she takes both carvings to show Chloe and tells Frieren that she has something important to discuss with the president.

Mau watches them go.

Yuka rests her head against his arm.

“We missed that,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

She turns toward him, and he puts his arms around her before either can try to turn the ache into a reason to deny what Ori has asked for next.

They stay there for a little while.

Then Yuka lifts her face and says, “We need to warn Kamish.” ʸ

Mau starts laughing, very softly, against her hair.
