## Chapter 24 — The Castle in His Pocket

The castle starts with a doorway in the grass.

Ori stands beside Chloe and watches Mau lower it into place. For a moment it is a perfectly ordinary door with nowhere to go on either side. Then a section of wall appears beside it, and the doorway becomes the entrance to something which has been waiting in his inventory for considerably longer than she has been waiting to invade it.

“That one?” he asks. ᴹ

Ori consults the drawing. She has brought the beach town, turned the page over and made a much less peaceful arrangement on the back.

“It needs to be a castle.” ᴼ

“I'm getting there.” ᴹ

He lowers the next marked section. Maki checks the space behind it while Senku compares the old dismantling marks. They are using the sound parts of the stored building, joining a low ring of rooms around the broad courtyard. The upper stairs which lead nowhere are closed off. The pieces which would put a tower over the workshop stay where they are.

Mai walks through the first doorway, tests the floor and looks back at Ori.

“How many rooms are you planning to conquer?” ᴹᵃ

Ori looks at Chloe.

“How many can have sweets?” ᴼ

Mau pauses with one hand raised. Yuka, beside him with the list, starts laughing.

“A very different construction question,” he says. ᴹ

Umaru has already called an assembly. She has a pencil, the good side of the long table and Rem beside her with the paper she keeps losing beneath other paper. Coco and Suika have drawn a banner. Bocchi is trying to decide whether adding a little hamster to it would constitute unauthorized alteration.

“It needs one,” Ori tells her. ᴼ

Bocchi draws the hamster. It looks apprehensive about the military appointment.

Milim arrives carrying three different balls.

“No,” Umaru says before she reaches the table. ᵁ

“You don't know what I was going to say.” ᴹⁱ

“The little one.” ᴹⁱ

Mau sits at the end with Yuka. She has been given the Fort's copy of the arrangement; he has been given the page headed Kamish, and discovers a second name underneath it.

His.

“You help him.” ᴼ

“An honour.” ᴹ

“Water is allowed,” Ori rules. ᴼ

“And if someone falls?” ᴹ

“Then you help,” Yuka answers at once. ʸ

Ori nods hard enough to make the pencil behind her ear fall onto the table.

“That's not playing anymore.” ᴼ

Mau retrieves the pencil and gives it back. She takes it, puts it somewhere less temporary and gets on with explaining how he is forbidden from winning.

Mau has brought the page outside and reads the relevant parts aloud. At the rule forbidding the use of actual fire, the dragon lowers his head until the great eye is level with him.

“You have accepted this?” ᴷ

“I'm your attaché.” ᴹ

“That does not answer the question.” ᴷ

“With pride.” ᴹ

Kamish looks toward the common-room windows. Several faces withdraw from the glass too late to become inconspicuous.

“And the stronghold is mine?” ᴷ

“For the day.” ᴹ

“A treasury. Several kitchens. An unreasonable quantity of small biscuits.” ᴹ

“Against my own household.” ᴷ

“Apparently they've declared themselves an invading power.” ᴹ

Kamish considers the castle. The broad opening at the back of the courtyard gives him room to rest his head above the low inner wall without trying to fit the rest of his body down a passage designed for people.

“Then they will have to negotiate their surrender.” ᴷ

Mau looks at the windows again. Ori is visible now, making no attempt to hide. Yuka has joined her with a cup in her hand.

“I don't think they came prepared for that outcome.” ᴹ

“They should learn.” ᴷ

The soldiers receive their orders before anyone puts decorations on them.

Mau chooses ordinary knights for the open rooms. He shows them where to stand, which simple challenge belongs to each door and when to hand over the token. He demonstrates a stop signal. Each soldier repeats it. No one needs to speak to make the meaning clear.

Bellion watches the second demonstration, then sends the remaining details down the line exactly as Mau gives them.

The actual watch stays at its posts. These are a different detail for a different job. The children may call the doorway a frontier; the grounds outside it remain the grounds of their house.

Yuka comes through while Mau is giving a knight a wooden serving spoon.

“He's going to defend the kitchen with that?” ʸ

The knight holds the spoon upright. Mau examines the effect.

“I think we should be concerned.” ᴹ

She stands beside him while he finishes. When the last soldier moves to the assigned room, she slips both arms around his waist and catches him before he can follow.

“You can sit down now.” ʸ

He looks toward the courtyard. Maki has finished her inspection. Senku and Byakuya are arguing about whether the banner belongs on the left or right side of the gate. Mai settles the dispute by giving them a second banner.

Mau lets Yuka turn him toward the bench outside the kitchen wing.

“Did I look tired?” ᴹ

“You looked finished. I'm taking advantage.” ʸ

She sits first and pulls him down beside her. He rests his head against her shoulder while she reads the supply list aloud. At the fourth kind of sweet he puts a finger beside the name.

“We have that one. And the next two.” ᴹ

“Then we can put different things in each room.” ʸ

“How different?” ᴹ

She turns the page.

He begins laughing before she has finished reading. The list has become an argument for keeping several rooms open after lunch so that nobody has to choose between finding the next surprise and playing the game already in front of them.

“This one,” she says, tapping the name of the biscuits they brought home from the island. “Save some for later.” ʸ

“For Ori?” ᴹ

“For me.” ʸ

He turns to kiss her. She receives it with one hand behind his neck and keeps him close when he begins to draw away.

“That's not a payment,” she tells him. ʸ

“I was going to do it for free.” ᴹ

“Good policy.” ʸ

Shuna finds them when they bring the list back inside.

She has an empty baking tray under one arm and has been thinking about the little cakes since Yuka described the courtyard. If they want fillings, she can prepare those too. There is room in the kitchen this afternoon, and she would like to try the version with ginger.

“I can help with the sweets,” she tells Mau. ˢʰᵘ

“Oh. I think we already have some.” ᴹ

“These too.” ᴹ

“How many do you have?” ˢʰᵘ

There are fruit chews beneath those, chocolate bars in flat stacks, little cakes sealed for travel and bags of the crisp things Umaru insists are essential supplies.

He gives Shuna a ginger sweet to try while he finds the box he meant to show her.

She accepts it without looking away from his hands.

The boxes continue arriving.

Yuka gets her list out from under a stack and sits on the end of the bench. Mau pauses long enough to move the empty tray, checks that Shuna has room to step back and fills the space with two more open crates.

Hundreds of packets are visible now. Several of the tins have smaller sweets packed beneath the first layer. The table has disappeared under neat, improbable little towers, and the last two crates stand on the floor beside Mau's chair.

By late afternoon the rooms have acquired their own character.

The first knight guards a little bell and a deep tray of wrapped sweets. The next sits beside a shallow water channel with paper waiting at one end and tins of biscuits beneath the window. Someone has put a crown on the helmet in the kitchen wing; the spoon remains impressive beneath it. Every open room has something different to discover, even the ones that do not hold a token. Prepared boxes stand behind the serving tables so the next group will find them as full as the first.

The three tokens still open the treasury. Nobody has to finish every challenge before they can have something to eat. Mau makes that part clear while Ori decides which little guards are meant to look most suspicious.

Mau goes through with Ori before the game. She checks the closed stair, the route to the courtyard and the place where Lantern can watch without blocking a passage. He shows her where the ordinary doors lead. She tells him which ones should feel secret even though everyone can see them.

At the treasury, she stops.

It is a real stone room. Tomorrow there will be sweets in the chest beneath its window, but right now there are only three places on the board for the wing tokens and a heap of ribbon waiting to become decorations.

Ori puts her hand on the door.

“You really kept it.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“I thought maybe it would be little.” ᴼ
