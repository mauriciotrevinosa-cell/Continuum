# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 25 — Matching

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 25
**Source episode:** S13E101
**Continuity state:** Ori offers Beru matching explorer clothes and later Mau-Ori-Beru dungeon. Beru chooses Fort side for play with Mau blessing; traitor joke immediately and explicitly reassured. Mai constructs fitted jacket with collaboration, existing birthday hat remains. No real command defiance, unit upgrade or forced outing date.

*When: B+404, after dinner; preparation for the following morning.*

Beru finds Ori after dinner with the hat in his hands.

She is sitting on the common-room floor beside Chloe. The map has acquired three wings, several rooms which do not fit any of them and a tunnel represented by a question mark. Umaru is on the sofa behind them, consulting Rem about the difference between conquering a pantry and eating in it.

Beru stops at the edge of the paper.

“Princess.” ᴮᵉ

Ori looks up.

“Could I petition for a place beside my king?” ᴮᵉ

Across the room, Mau lowers the cup he has been about to drink from.

Yuka looks at him, then at the enormous ant standing before their daughter with the solemnity of someone requesting leave to cross a hostile border.

“For the game?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“For the defence of the stronghold.” ᴮᵉ

Umaru leans over the sofa arm.

“The enemy has a recruitment problem,” Umaru says. “We've already taken his pastry department.” ᵁ

“Umaru,” Rem murmurs. ᴿᵉ

“An observation.” ᵁ

Ori puts the pencil down. She looks past Beru to Mau, who lifts one hand to show that he is not going to decide this part for her.

“You could be with us,” she says. ᴼ

Beru keeps the hat against his chest.

“My king would be greatly outnumbered.” ᴮᵉ

“He has Kamish.” ᴼ

“A formidable defender. I would nevertheless—” ᴮᵉ

“We could have matching clothes.” ᴼ

Beru stops.

Mau sees the precise moment the argument reaches him. He puts the cup down before the rest of it can become dangerous to the upholstery.

Ori has begun looking toward Mai.

“Like my exploring ones,” she explains. “For both of us.” ᴼ

Mai, who has been listening from the table, studies the shape of Beru's shoulders.

“That would take a little adjustment.” ᴹᵃ

“We have time,” Ori says. ᴼ

Beru looks at Mau. Then at Ori. Then at Yuka, who has made no attempt to conceal how much she is enjoying the negotiations.

Ori adds the final offer while he is still recovering from the first.

“And we could ask Dad to go exploring with us. Another time. You and me and him.” ᴼ

Mau covers his mouth.

Beru turns fully toward him.

“My king.” ᴮᵉ

Mau removes his hand. He makes a serious attempt at answering with the dignity the occasion deserves.

“I won't hold it against you.” ᴹ

The ant bows so deeply that Ori puts a hand on the map to keep the hat's brim from moving it. Then he steps around her, takes a position behind her shoulder and faces the room.

“Traitor,” Mau cries. ᴹ

Beru goes perfectly still.

The laughter escapes before Mau can stop it. Yuka catches his forearm. Across the room Beru looks from the king's face to the hand she has over her own mouth.

“Was that in earnest?” he asks. ᴮᵉ

“No. No, Beru, I'm joking.” Mau is still laughing as he gets up. “You're the best. She's made an excellent offer.” ᴹ

He crosses the room and puts a hand against the ant's arm, waiting until Beru looks directly at him.

“I mean it. Go and have a good time with her.” ᴹ

Beru's shoulders lower. The hat rises to its proper position with renewed care.

“I shall ensure the princess does.” ᴮᵉ

Ori catches his claw before that can become a promise to stand behind her doing nothing all day.

“You too.” ᴼ

He looks down at her.

“Yes, Princess.” ᴮᵉ

Umaru makes a firm mark on the page.

“A major diplomatic success.” ᵁ

---

Mai brings Ori's explorer outfit from the room where Frieren has been keeping it.

Ori touches the familiar pocket before she puts it on. There is nothing in it now. On the last expedition it held a collection of things she considered essential and Mau was required to take seriously.

“You'll need room in yours,” she tells Beru. ᴼ

Mai has already seen the problem. A human sleeve would sit across a joint that needs to move; a human collar would disappear beneath the shape of his neck. She draws a short over-jacket which leaves the shoulders free and puts the useful pockets where he can actually reach them.

Momo Nishimiya sits beside her with the board tilted toward the lamp. When Mai holds out a hand for the pencil, Momo puts it between her fingers without looking away from the drawing.

“This closes here?” she asks. ᴹⁿ

“Under the arm. Otherwise it catches.” ᴹᵃ

Momo points to another corner. Mai follows her finger, adjusts the line and leaves her hand resting near Momo's when she has finished.

Mau sees it from beside Ori and looks back down at the buttons he is sorting. He is beginning to think Momo may not need his advice after all.

The cloth takes shape as Mai works. She asks Beru to turn, checks the edge beneath the new pocket and shortens it before the weight of whatever Ori gives him can interfere with his movement. Mau helps build the simple fastenings when she asks, following the version she has just shown him.

Ori has chosen the same broad band for both outfits. Mai gives her the two lengths to compare.

“Matching?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

Ori holds one against Beru, then the other against herself.

“Matching.” ᴼ

Chloe straightens the band on Ori's shoulder while Beru holds still for Mai. Frieren comes to the table to see the result and finds her daughter trying to reach the pocket on the ant's new jacket.

“You can ask him to bend,” she says. ᶠ

“I wanted to see if I could.” ᴼ

“And?” ᶠ

Ori regards the distance.

“He should bend.” ᴼ

Beru kneels at once. She puts the little pencil from her own pocket into his, stands back and nods at what this has accomplished.

Mau is not quick enough to avoid being called for the inspection.

Ori takes his hand and brings him in front of them. Beru rises, the pencil still in his pocket and the old hat on his head.

“Well?” Yuka asks from beside Frieren. ʸ

Mau looks between the two explorers. He can make a joke. Several have offered themselves. The sight of Beru waiting for his answer with the same grave attention as Ori makes him choose the simpler one.

“You look wonderful.” ᴹ

Ori beams. Beru puts a hand to his chest and bows just enough to keep the pencil where she has put it.

---

Kamish receives the news while Mau is putting the last supplies into the treasury.

“You have lost your general,” Kamish says. “And I am told the kitchen has declared against us.” ᴷ

“There were uniforms involved in the first one. I still don't understand the second.” ᴹ

“You displayed the treasury to the enemy.” ᴷ

“They're the people I bought it for.” ᴹ

Kamish considers him.

“Your appointment was premature.” ᴷ

“You offered him none?” ᴷ

“I hadn't realized we were competing.” ᴹ

“We are defending a castle.” ᴷ

Mau looks around the room. The shelves hold rows of biscuit tins, bags of sweets from the shop and the packets they brought back from the island. Three broad trays wait for the cakes Shuna is bringing in the morning. He has put a labelled parcel aside for each person and kept Yuka's packet apart, although he suspects it will be discovered and requisitioned long before he gets to give it to her.

The extra crates are already divided between the wings. The food for the shared lunch remains with Shuna, who has apparently managed to join the invasion without losing interest in feeding the defending dragon.

“I may have underestimated the campaign.” ᴹ

Kamish lowers his head beyond the window.

“What advantage remains to us?” ᴷ

“You can argue with Umaru without needing to reach for your glasses.” ᴹ

“A slender foundation for victory.” ᴷ

Mau starts laughing. The sound comes back from the empty room, ordinary and ridiculous beneath the dragon's enormous voice.

Yuka finds him there a few minutes later. She has come to get the list, but stays when Kamish asks her whether the opposing queen intends to honour the declared limits of the conflict.

“Which ones are you worried about?” she asks. ʸ

“Your husband.” ᴷ

She looks at Mau. He spreads both hands.

“I'm not allowed to do anything.” ᴹ

“We'll keep him occupied,” she promises. ʸ

She takes his hand before he can investigate how much worse that answer might make his appointment. He turns the lamp down, gives Kamish the place to watch over the closed courtyard and goes back to the house with her.

At the door, Ori is waiting to tell Beru where to meet her in the morning.

He listens to every word.
