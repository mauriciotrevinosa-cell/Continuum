# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 1 — Eighty-One

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E104
**Continuity state:** B+406, the morning after the Fort's castle day. The house goes back to ordinary routine. Yuka is clingier than ever after the island: she wakes him by sitting on him, carries her chair round the table to his side, steals from his plate. Mau has a small mark on the bedroom calendar: the day Yoru's ninety-day contract ends, at the same hour, B+487. Eighty-one days. Yuka sees him looking at it and asks; he tells her the truth instead of "it's fine". Mai opens the notebook she started when she learned his eyes cost nothing (V80 #28, "Everything") and asks for the first project: a second floor for the workshop, built with her Construction on his reserves under their agreement. Momo Nishimiya is unconvinced about the stairs. Kiss completed in the Work revision.

*When: B+406. Morning to early afternoon. The castle has gone back into storage. The Fort's banner is still hanging in the common room, slightly crooked, because Umaru won't let anyone straighten it.*

He wakes up because something is sitting on him.

Not on his chest. Lower, across his stomach, with a knee on either side of him and its full weight settled in a way that suggests it has been there a while and has no plans to move. He knows the weight. He'd know it in the dark, in a gate, in a room at the bottom of the world.

He opens his eyes.

Yuka is sitting on him in his own shirt, the old grey one with the hole at the collar, with her hair everywhere and her arms crossed and her chin up, looking down at him like a queen who has been kept waiting.

“You overslept,” she says. ʸ

“What time is it?” ᴹ

“Late.” ʸ

He turns his head. The light coming through the curtain is the light of very early morning, thin and pale, the light he usually wakes up to.

“It's not late,” he says. ᴹ

“It's late for *me*,” says Yuka. “I've been awake for ages. I was bored.” ʸ

“So you sat on me.” ᴹ

“So I sat on you.” ʸ

She doesn't move. She uncrosses her arms and puts both hands flat on his chest instead, on the long line from shoulder to hip, and leans forward until her face is above his and her hair falls round them both like a curtain.

“Good morning,” she says. ʸ

“Good morning.” ᴹ

“Say it properly.” ʸ

He puts a hand at the back of her neck and draws her the last little distance down.

Yuka stops holding herself above him. Her elbows settle beside his shoulders, her hair catching against his cheek as she kisses him. He has time to smile before she does it again, more firmly, as though the first attempt has not quite met the morning's requirements.

“Good morning,” he murmurs against her mouth. ᴹ

“Better.” ʸ

“Was there a complaint?” ᴹ

“You were asleep.” ʸ

He lifts his free hand toward the pale line beneath the curtain, hoping the evidence will help him.

“In our bed. At night.” ᴹ

“It's morning now.” ʸ

She pushes his hair back from his forehead with both hands. He catches one, kisses her palm and keeps it beside his face while she studies him with an expression which has lost most of its royal indignation.

He brings her down for the next kiss himself.

Yuka makes a small pleased sound, then laughs when her hair gets between them. He frees the strand from the corner of his mouth. She gathers the rest over one shoulder and bends to him again, carefully this time, with her free hand against his cheek.

When she finally sits up, he keeps his hands at her waist.

“Breakfast?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks toward the window, apparently considering whether that requires too much movement.

“In a minute.” ʸ

“You told me I was late.” ᴹ

“You've caught up.” ʸ

He laughs. She lies down against him, tucks her head beneath his chin and takes a much longer minute than either of them intends to admit downstairs.

---

She makes him carry her downstairs.

She doesn't ask. She just gets off him, goes to the door, stands in it with her arms out, and waits.

“You can walk,” he says, pulling on his trousers. ᴹ

“I can,” she agrees. ʸ

“You walked across a cavern floor made of nests.” ᴹ

“I was very brave.” ʸ

“So you can walk down the stairs.” ᴹ

“I could,” says Yuka. “But I've decided not to.” ʸ

He picks her up. She settles against him with her arms round his neck and her cheek on his shoulder and makes a small satisfied noise, like someone getting into a warm bath. On the landing they pass Beru, who is wearing the explorer jacket Mai made for him over his armour, and who stops, and steps aside, and inclines his head.

“My king. My queen,” Beru says. “You are very late.” ᴮᵉ

“See?” says Yuka. ʸ

The kitchen is loud.

It's always loud after a big day. People reliving it. Milim on the bench explaining to Shion, who wasn't there, exactly how the tunnel worked, with diagrams drawn in spilled tea. Umaru announcing to anyone who'll listen that the Fort's victory will be studied by historians. Senku asking whether anyone saw where he put the paper boat, and Ori, from the doorway, telling him very firmly that it lives by the shell door now and isn't for touching.

Mau puts Yuka down at the table. She takes the chair beside his.

Then she gets up, picks the chair up, and moves it six inches closer, so that her knee is against his under the table. Then she sits back down.

“There was room,” he says. ᴹ

“There was too much room,” says Yuka. ʸ

Shuna puts plates in front of them. Eggs, the bread she made yesterday, the small sharp pickles Mau likes and pretends not to. He picks up his fork.

Yuka's hand arrives at his plate before his fork does.

She takes a pickle. She eats it. She doesn't look at him while she does it; she looks straight ahead, at Milim's tea diagram, with an expression of perfect innocence.

He looks at his plate. At the gap where the pickle was.

He doesn't say anything. He just moves the rest of the pickles, without thinking, to the side of the plate nearest her.

She notices. Of course she notices. She takes another one, slower this time, and he feels her smile against his shoulder before she's even finished chewing.

Across the table, Shoko is drinking her tea and watching them over the rim of the cup, and her face is doing the thing it does when she's decided not to say something out loud.

---

The calendar is on the wall of their room, beside the wardrobe.

It isn't a real calendar. Senku made it for the household a long time ago, when the B-count got too big to keep in anyone's head: a long sheet of paper with the days ruled off in blocks, and the house marks things on it. Birthdays. Gate days. The day the castle went back into storage, with a small drawing of a flag.

There's a mark on it near the bottom that nobody else made.

He made it the day they came home from the island. A small dot, in pencil, very faint, on a square a long way down the sheet. He didn't write anything beside it. He didn't need to.

*B+487. The same hour.*

Yoru's contract. Ninety days of terms, signed at a table by the sea, with Asa watching through the same eyes. Ninety days, and then whatever Yoru wants next.

He goes up after breakfast to get his jacket, and he stops in front of the calendar, and he looks at the dot.

He counts.

Eighty-one.

“What are you looking at?” ʸ

She's in the doorway. He didn't hear her come up. She's still holding the last piece of bread from breakfast, which she has evidently decided is hers.

He could say *nothing*.

The word is right there. It would be so easy. *Nothing. The calendar. Just checking a date.* She'd believe him, or she'd pretend to, and she'd come and lean on him, and the morning would go on.

He doesn't.

“The dot,” he says. ᴹ

She comes in. She stands beside him and looks at the calendar, and finds it, the faint pencil mark at the bottom, and he watches her know what it is.

“Yoru,” she says. ʸ

“Eighty-one days.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a moment.

“You've been counting,” she says. ʸ

“Since the island.” ᴹ

“Every day?” ʸ

“Most days.” ᴹ

“And not saying.” ʸ

He thinks about that.

“There wasn't anything to say,” he says. “It's just a number. It gets smaller.” ᴹ

She puts the bread down on the windowsill. She turns to face him. She doesn't look angry. She looks like she's thinking very carefully about what to do with her hands.

What she does with them is put them on either side of his face.

“Then say it to me when it gets smaller,” she says. “Every morning, if you want. *Eighty-one.* *Eighty.* I don't care. Just so it isn't only in your head.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“That's a strange way to start the day,” he says. ᴹ

“You've had stranger,” says Yuka. ʸ

She pulls his face down and presses her forehead against his, hard, the way she does when she means something, and stays there.

“Eighty-one,” she says. ʸ

“Eighty-one,” he says. ᴹ

She lets go. She picks up her bread. She takes his hand on the way out of the room, and she doesn't let go of it down the stairs, even though she has to walk sideways on the narrow bit by the landing to keep holding it.

---

Mai is waiting for him in the workshop doorway.

She has a notebook. A small one, with a cracked cover, that she didn't have before the gate. He's seen her writing in it at meals, in the yard, in the evenings by the fire with Momo's feet in her lap. He's never seen inside it.

She holds it against her chest.

“You said,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“I said a lot of things.” ᴹ

“In bed. After the gate. When Gojo told me your eyes don't cost anything.” She narrows her own. “I asked how much you could give me. You said *how much do you need*. And I told you not to say that, because you didn't know what I'd ask for. And you said *ask anyway*.” ᴹᵃ

He remembers. He remembers her going down the stairs one step at a time, writing, not looking where she was going.

“I remember,” he says. ᴹ

Mai opens the notebook.

The first page says *EVERYTHING* across the top, in her small hard capitals, underlined twice.

Under it is a list.

He doesn't read all of it. She doesn't let him. She puts her thumb over most of the page and only shows him the top line, but he sees enough of the rest, before her thumb comes down, to understand why she told him not to say *how much do you need*. Bridges. Something with the word *aqueduct*. Something with the word *Tempest* and three question marks. A line that just says *a better kettle* and has been crossed out and rewritten four times.

The top line says: *Workshop. Second floor.*

“That one first,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Behind her, through the door, the workshop is exactly as it always is. Too small. Senku's bench and hers and the forge and the shelves to the ceiling, every inch used, the box with the ring pattern on the top shelf where nobody touches it, Momo's broom leaning in the corner because Momo has started spending more time in here than she admits.

“A second floor,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Up. Not out. If I go out, I take the yard, and Ori will never forgive me.” Mai looks up at the low ceiling. “Stairs from the back corner. A floor that can hold the forge if I ever move it. Windows on the side the light comes from. I've done the drawings. Senku's done the arithmetic. He says it's possible. He says it's *ten billion per cent* possible.” ᴹᵃ

“It's at least ten billion,” Senku calls, from somewhere inside. ˢᵉ

“The only thing I don't have,” says Mai, “is enough cursed energy to build a floor out of nothing in one go. My Construction costs. A whole floor costs more than I've got in a month.” ᴹᵃ

She closes the notebook.

“Under our agreement,” she says. “I build it. My technique, my design, my hands. You give me the energy. The way we already do it, only—” ᴹᵃ

“Only a floor,” he says. ᴹ

“Only a floor,” Mai agrees. ᴹᵃ

He doesn't answer right away.

He follows the connection they already use, checking how Mai wants to draw on it while she works. His eyes leave his own expenditure almost nothing; her Construction will still use the energy he supplies. He has more than enough for what she is asking.

“You want the whole frame first?” he asks. ᴹ

“With Senku watching the joins. Then the floor. We keep the stairs clear until I know where they're going.” ᴹᵃ

He looks at the line beneath her thumb, the one she has shown him, and at the workshop beyond her.

He likes the thought of seeing what she does with that much room.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Mai lets out a breath.

“You're not going to ask what else is on the list?” she says. ᴹᵃ

“Would you tell me?” ᴹ

“No.” ᴹᵃ

“Then no.” ᴹ

Something happens at the corner of her mouth.

“The stairs,” says Momo Nishimiya, from the corner by the broom. ᴹⁿ

They both look at her.

She has her arms crossed. She's been there the whole time, he realises, leaning on the wall by the forge in the way she leans on things when she's pretending she just happened to be passing.

“What about the stairs?” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

“You said the back corner,” says Momo. “The back corner is where the forge vents. You'll be going up stairs through the heat every time you go up. You'll cook.” ᴹⁿ

Mai opens her mouth.

She closes it.

She opens the notebook again, finds the drawing, looks at it for a long time.

“She's right,” says Senku, from inside, with enormous delight. ˢᵉ

“I *know* she's right,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

She takes the pencil from behind her ear and draws a line through the back corner, and moves the stairs to the side wall, by the window, where the light comes in. Then she looks at Momo.

“You could have said that this morning,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“You didn't ask this morning,” says Momo. ᴹⁿ

“I'm asking now. What else?” ᴹᵃ

Momo uncrosses her arms. She comes over to the doorway. She looks at the drawing over Mai's shoulder, very close, closer than she needs to, and Mai doesn't move away.

“The windows are too small,” says Momo. ᴹⁿ

Mau leaves them to it.

---

He finds Yuka on the porch.

She's in the old chair, his chair, with her feet tucked under her and her notebook on her knee, and she isn't writing anything. She's watching the yard. Ori and Beru are at the far end by the training posts, Ori on a crate, explaining something with large gestures, Beru nodding gravely in his jacket.

She looks up when he comes out.

“Well?” she says. ʸ

“A second floor.” ᴹ

“For the workshop?” ʸ

“Up, not out. Because of Ori and the yard.” ᴹ

“And you said yes.” ʸ

“I said yes.” ᴹ

She doesn't get up. She just shifts in the chair, very deliberately, toward the arm, leaving exactly enough room for him, and looks at the space and then at him.

He sits. She arranges herself across him, the way she does, with her legs over his and her head on his shoulder and the notebook on her stomach.

“What else was on the list?” she says. ʸ

“She wouldn't tell me.” ᴹ

“Did you see?” ʸ

“A little.” ᴹ

“And?” ʸ

He thinks about *aqueduct* and *Tempest* and the four crossed-out kettles.

“We're going to need a bigger house,” he says. ᴹ

She laughs into his neck.

They stay there a long time.

The yard goes on without them. Ori finishes her explanation and Beru salutes. Shuna comes out with washing. Gojo crosses the yard toward the orchard with his hands in his pockets and his blindfold on, whistling something, and waves at them without looking, and Yuka waves back without lifting her head.

At some point she opens the notebook. She turns to a new page. She writes, at the top, in her neat small letters:

*81.*

She doesn't say anything about it. She just shows him the page, and then closes the notebook, and puts it back on her stomach, and puts her hand over his on top of it.

Tomorrow the page will say *80*. He knows it will. She'll write it before he's even awake.

He leans his head back against the chair and closes his eyes, and lets himself be sat on.
