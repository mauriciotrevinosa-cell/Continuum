# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 2 — Bad Company

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S13E104
**Continuity state:** B+407 ("80"). Ciel asks Mau to come to the shared inner room. She finished the last of the Mini Ciel repairs overnight (the partial filter has been in use since B+392 on the island, with the cloth for tired days). She tells him gravely that Mini Ciel "will not survive" the final step, lets it land, and then laughs. Mini Ciel is whole: rebuilt, steadier than before the overload, so the cloth becomes optional rather than needed. Mau asks whose idea the joke was; Noise, at the table, is very innocent. Mau tells Noise it's been keeping bad company. Ciel and DIO both object. Yuka hears all of it through her link and laughs out loud in the yard. This frees enough headroom for the next step Noise has been waiting on (Maki's Heavenly Restriction, next chapter). No new power beyond the finished filter.

*When: B+407. Mid-morning. Yuka wrote "80" on the new page before he woke up. Outside, the first beams for Mai's second floor are lying in the yard, and Ori has fenced them off with string.*

“Can you come in?” Ciel says. ᶜ

He's on the porch with his tea, watching Mai and Senku argue over a beam. Yuka is somewhere in the yard with Ori, inspecting the string fence. It's an ordinary morning. He likes ordinary mornings more than he used to.

“In?” he says. ᴹ

“The room. I'd like to show you something. It's better at the table.” ᶜ

She doesn't sound worried. She sounds the way she sounds before she gives him a number she's proud of. He puts the tea down on the railing and settles back into the porch chair. His body stays there, the yard still within reach of his attention while he follows the invitation to the room inside.

*Shall I include Yuka?* Ciel asks through the practical channel. ᶜ

*Yes.* ᴹ

*I want to see,* Yuka answers when the invitation reaches her. *Ori and I are staying by the fence.* ʸ

“Five minutes,” he tells Ciel. ᴹ

“It will take less,” says Ciel. ᶜ

---

The room is the same as always.

The table. The chairs, one of them the one Noise adjusted once so Ciel could sit without moving the cup. The cup itself, which nobody has ever quite finished. The door Ashborn keeps closed. Makima's chair by the far wall, empty this morning; she's outside, sitting on the bottom porch step in her own body, where Noise keeps her.

Noise is by the table. The shadow of it, the outline. It looks, if a shadow can look anything, very composed.

DIO is at the window end of the table with her chin in her hands. She's wearing her hat indoors, which she's started doing since the island. Nobody has told her not to.

Ciel is in her chair.

She has a sheet of something in front of her: not paper, the room doesn't really have paper, but the idea of a page, the kind of thing she uses when she wants him to look at a shape rather than hear a number. Lines on it. A small, intricate structure, layered, like the inside of a shell.

He knows it. He'd know it anywhere.

“Mini Ciel,” he says. ᴹ

“Mini Ciel,” Ciel agrees. ᶜ

He sits down.

He knows the history of that shape better than he'd like. The filter Ciel built a long time ago, from what she could see behind Gojo's eyes, so that his own eyes wouldn't drown him. The earlier overload, before the north, when it gave way and he went down in Shoko's clinic with blood on his lip. The work it took to recover from that. Then the Rulers' gate: his eyes reaching their full efficiency, and the filter failing again beneath what they could receive. The cloth afterward. And then the island: Ciel rebuilding it in pieces, a little at a time, testing the layers until it was usable. Partial. Enough that he only needs the cloth when he's tired, or when the world starts arriving faster than he can choose.

He's worn the cloth less every week since. He still keeps it in his pocket.

“I finished the last layer last night,” Ciel says. ᶜ

He looks up.

“While you were asleep, in the calibration window we agreed on. With Yuka asleep on you, which made that part easier; your attention was very still.” ᶜ

“Finished,” he says. ᴹ

“Finished,” says Ciel. “There's one step left. Fitting it. Bringing the last layer in under the others, so it's whole.” ᶜ

She folds her hands on the table.

“I need to tell you something first,” she says. ᶜ

Her voice changes.

He's known Ciel for a very long time. Longer than anyone except Frieren. He knows every voice she has: the crisp one for numbers, the dry one for Gojo, the careful one she uses in the clinic, the very quiet one from the gate when she told Yuka why she counted.

This is the careful one.

“When I bring the last layer in,” Ciel says, “the old structure has to give way to make room. All of it. I can't build the new one around it; it's too damaged. The filter you've been using since the island, the partial one, the one I rebuilt in pieces—” ᶜ

She pauses.

“Mini Ciel won't survive it,” she says. ᶜ

The room goes very quiet.

He looks at the page. At the small layered shape. At the place in it where he can see, now that he's looking, the old pieces: patched, mended, held together with care.

He doesn't know why it lands the way it does. It's a filter. It's a structure. It's a thing Ciel built so he could see without screaming. It isn't a person.

But it's been with him a long time. It held for years. It broke because he pushed it too far, and she put it back together piece by piece, and it held again.

“Okay,” he says. His voice is a little rough. “If that's what it takes.” ᴹ

He swallows.

“Thank you,” he says. “For—all of it. It did a lot.” ᴹ

Ciel looks at him.

Her face is perfectly composed.

Then the corner of her mouth goes.

Then the rest of it.

She laughs.

Ciel, who got through the entire explanation with a perfectly composed face, puts her hand over her mouth and laughs so hard her shoulders shake. At the far end of the table, DIO has put her face down on her arms and is making a sound like a kettle.

He stares at them both.

“It's fine,” Ciel manages. “It's *fine*. It survives. Of course it survives. The last layer goes in *under* it. Nothing gives way. It's whole now. It was whole the moment I finished; the fitting took four seconds while you were asleep.” ᶜ

“You said—” ᴹ

“I know what I said.” ᶜ

“You said it *wouldn't survive*.” ᴹ

“I know.” She's still laughing. “I'm sorry. Your *face*. You thanked it. You said *it did a lot*.” ᶜ

“It *did* do a lot,” he says, with as much dignity as he can find. ᴹ

That sets DIO off again.

He sits there while they finish.

It takes a while. Every time Ciel almost stops, DIO lifts her head and says *it did a lot* in a low, solemn voice, and Ciel goes again.

When it's finally quiet, he looks at Ciel.

“Whose idea was that?” he asks. ᴹ

Ciel straightens. She smooths the page in front of her. Her face is composed again, mostly.

She doesn't answer. She doesn't need to. Her eyes go, very briefly, to the shadow by the table.

So do his.

Noise is very still.

Noise is the stillest he has ever seen it. A shadow standing beside a table in the posture of someone who has been standing beside a table all morning, minding its own business, with no idea what anyone could possibly be talking about.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes,” says Noise. ᴺ

“Was it you?” ᴹ

A long pause.

“My idea,” Noise admits. ᴺ

Ciel looks down at the page, far too late to hide her smile.

“He wanted you to remember the announcement,” she says. ᶜ

“I do,” Mau tells them. ᴹ

He looks at Noise for a long time.

Noise, his first construct. The thing he built before he knew he was building anything, out of the parts of himself that never stopped watching. The thing that used to talk only in reports. That once told him *help* in a voice he'd never heard it use, because two people were sitting too close to it at a table.

That just planned a prank.

“You've been keeping bad company,” he tells it. ᴹ

The objection is immediate and from two directions.

“*Bad*—” says DIO, lifting her head from her arms with her hat crooked. ᴰ

“I beg your pardon,” says Ciel, very crisply. ᶜ

“I'm not bad company,” says DIO. “I'm *excellent* company. I'm the best company in this room. Elmesia says I'm very good company.” ᴰ

“Elmesia says,” Ciel repeats, looking at her. ᶜ

DIO looks very hard at the window and straightens her hat.

“And I,” says Ciel, turning back to Mau, “have been delivering accurate information to you for longer than you have been able to remember your own name. One joke does not make me *bad company*.” ᶜ

“You laughed for two minutes,” says Mau. ᴹ

“It was a very good joke.” ᶜ

“My joke,” Noise says, very quietly, and Mau realises it sounds pleased. ᴺ

From outside, through the link, faint and delighted, comes another laugh.

Yuka.

She stayed in the conversation Ciel invited her to join, listening from the yard while the three of them tried to keep straight faces. He can feel her out in the yard, beside the string fence, bent over with her hands on her knees, laughing so hard that Ori is asking her what's wrong.

*It did a lot*, Yuka says down the link, in a very fair imitation of his voice. ʸ

“Not you too,” he says out loud, to the room. ᴹ

*Me too*, says Yuka. *Ciel, that was wonderful. Do it again sometime.* ʸ

“I will consider it,” says Ciel. ᶜ

---

He turns his attention back to the porch afterward, his hand still resting beside the tea he left on the railing.

His tea is cold. Mai and Senku have stopped arguing about the beam and started arguing about a different beam. Yuka is coming up the steps toward him with Ori behind her, both of them still grinning.

He takes the cloth out of his pocket.

He looks at it. Plain, grey, soft at the edges from weeks of tying and untying. He's carried it every day since the clinic. He put it on in the dark on the island, on bad mornings. He took it off for Yuka on the beach when the sun went down.

He doesn't put it on.

He looks at the yard instead. Properly. Without it. The beams. The string fence. Ori. The light on the grass. Every grain of the wood and every thread of the string and the slow movement of the air over the yard, all of it arriving, all at once, the way it always arrives.

And underneath it, steady, holding, the filter. Whole. Not patched. Taking the flood and letting it through in a shape he can stand.

It's quiet in his head.

Not silent. Quiet. Like a room where the window's open, but the wind has dropped.

Yuka sits down on the arm of the chair. She sees the cloth in his hand.

“Not today?” she says. ʸ

“Not today,” he says. ᴹ

She takes it out of his hand. She folds it, very carefully, into a small square, and puts it in the pocket of her own jacket.

“I'll keep it,” she says. “For tired days.” ʸ

“There might not be any.” ᴹ

“There'll be some,” says Yuka. “You're you.” ʸ

She leans against him. Ori climbs up the other side and wedges herself into the space under his arm.

“Ciel made a joke,” Ori tells him seriously. ᴼ

“I heard,” he says. ᴹ

“Mama said Ciel doesn't make jokes.” ᴼ

“She didn't,” says Mau. “Noise did.” ᴹ

Ori considers this.

“Noise is getting funny,” she decides. ᴼ

*I heard that*, says Noise, inside him, and he doesn't need to see a shadow to know it's pleased. ᴺ

At the bottom of the porch steps, Makima has been watching the whole thing with her hands folded in her lap.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't ask if she can speak. But when he looks down at her, she inclines her head, very slightly, toward the yard: toward the beams and the fence and the light, all of it, in focus.

He nods back.

Then Ciel says, inside, crisp again, all business:

*Since you can take it now*, she says, *Noise has something it's been waiting to finish. It's been waiting for the headroom. I think you'll want to sit down for this one. Not a joke.* ᶜ

*Not a joke*, Noise agrees. ᴺ

He looks at the yard a moment longer.

“Tomorrow,” he says. ᴹ

*Tomorrow*, says Ciel. ᶜ
