# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 3 — What Maki Feels

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S13E105
**Continuity state:** B+408 ("79"). With Mini Ciel whole, Noise finally finishes what it has been building quietly since it began watching Maki train: a model of how Maki's Heavenly Restriction makes her body sense the world (heat, air, weight, the shift of a foot before a strike), folded into Mau's body **without** the restriction itself, so he loses no cursed energy and nothing is traded away. Mau asks Maki first, the way he asked Mai. Maki's answer is short. The first test is in the yard with Maki and Yuta; Maki’s bodily sensing and Mau’s Six Eyes combine. He can resolve nearby matter atom by atom and distinguish its ordinary motion from cursed energy and mana even where all three overlap. The combined input overwhelms him until Mini Ciel and Noise attenuate it. Yuka, playful, asks what he can feel on *her*; he tells her the truth (her heartbeat and the warmth climbing her neck), and she flees behind her grandmother. Maki is proud and pretends not to be. Kiss completed in the Work revision.

*When: B+408. Morning in the yard, by the training posts. Yuka wrote "79" before breakfast and drew a small sun next to it because the weather was good.*

“I have to ask her first,” he says. ᴹ

He's sitting on the edge of the bed with his boots half laced. Noise has shown him the model, turning one part toward him and then another while Ciel explains how the pieces fit. They have stayed with it until he can follow the distinction that matters.

*You do*, Ciel agrees. ᶜ

*Her senses,* Noise says. ᴺ

He shows Mau the pattern again: the little change before Maki catches a falling cup, the foot shifting before Yuta moves, several observations laid beside one another until Mau can see what Noise has kept.

“And you can put it in me.” ᴹ

*No trade,* Noise says at once. *Just senses.* ᴺ

*Maki's present body works without cursed energy,* Ciel explains. *Noise has modelled how it receives information. Fitting that pattern into yours does not impose her restriction or remove your energy. Receiving the additional information is the part we had to prepare for.* ᶜ

*With the filter whole*, says Ciel, *you can carry it. Before, you couldn't. It would have been one more flood.* ᶜ

Yuka is lying across the bed behind him with her head propped on her hand. She asked to join the explanation and has been following it through their chosen channel.

“Granny's going to say something rude,” she says. ʸ

“Probably.” ᴹ

“Can I come?” ʸ

“You were always coming.” ᴹ

She looks pleased.

---

Maki is in the yard.

She's always in the yard in the mornings. She trains the way some people pray: every day, the same hour, whether or not anyone is watching. Today it's the long staff, the plain one, moving through the forms against nothing, and Yuta is on the bench by the posts with a cup of tea, watching her the way he's watched her for longer than Mau can remember anything.

She sees them coming and doesn't stop.

“What,” she says, mid-form. ᴹᵃᵏ

It isn't a question. It's an invitation to get to the point.

“Noise built something,” Mau says. “From watching you. I'd like to ask if I can use it.” ᴹ

The staff stops.

Maki turns round. She looks at him. Then at Yuka, beside him, holding his hand. Then back at him.

“Built what?” ᴹᵃᵏ

He tells her.

All of it. The way Noise put it to him. Not the restriction; not the trade. Just the way her body listens. Heat and air and weight. The shift in a foot before a strike. The thing she does every time she catches something without looking.

Maki listens with her arms crossed and the staff in the crook of her elbow.

When he's done, she's quiet for a moment.

“You asked Mai,” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Before her technique. For the ring.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And she smacked you.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“Twice. Once for the clip, once for the ring.” ᴹ

Maki considers this.

“Will it make you weaker?” she asks. ᴹᵃᵏ

“No,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Will it cost you anything?” ᴹᵃᵏ

“No loss of energy or strength. More information to get used to. That's why we waited for the filter.” ᴹ

“Will it make you better at keeping her alive?” ᴹᵃᵏ

She tips her head, very slightly, at Yuka.

Mau doesn't look away.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Maki uncrosses her arms.

“Then take it,” she says. “It was never mine to keep. I just had to live in it.” ᴹᵃᵏ

On the bench, Yuta has put his tea down.

He doesn't say anything. But Mau sees him look at his wife, the way he looks at her when she's said something she'll pretend later she didn't mean as much as she meant it.

Noise does it right there, in the yard.

There isn't much to see. Noise doesn't need much. It's been ready for weeks, waiting for the headroom; now there's room. Mau stands by the training posts with his eyes closed and feels it go in, not like the ring going into the katana, not like Construction, but like something he already knew how to do and had forgotten. Like remembering the way home.

*Open,* Ciel says through the private channel. ᶜ

He opens his eyes.

It's too much.

He knew it would be. Ciel told him; Noise told him; he still wasn't ready.

He knows the Six Eyes. He knows the flow of cursed energy, the point where a technique takes shape, the difference between that flow and mana. Mini Ciel is whole again and he knows how to live with what comes through it.

Then his body gives each of those readings a place to land.

The *air*. He can feel the air. Not see it: *feel* it, against every part of him at once, the way you feel water when you get into a bath, only the bath is the whole yard and the water is moving. He can feel the warmth coming off the training posts where the sun has been on them. The cool coming up out of the shade under the porch. Yuta's tea, cooling on the bench, the heat leaving it in a slow thin column. Maki's weight on the ground, ten paces away, shifting from one foot to the other.

Maki rolls her staff half a turn. He feels the air yield around it before he notices her fingers move. His eyes pick up the same change and keep following it. The disturbance breaks into the paths of molecules. He can separate those into atoms, follow the ones closest to the staff and see where their movement carries the heat from her palm into the cooler air.

At the bench Yuta lifts his cup. The new sense catches the small change in how his feet bear his weight. The eyes give him that movement in pieces: the muscle tightening at the wrist, the tea coming up the inside of the cup. Cursed energy shifts through Yuta's hand at the same time. Mau can follow the energy through the fingers without losing the movement of the fingers themselves. Each belongs to its own reading, even within the same hand.

Mana lies shallow across the training post beside him, a trace left where a spell sealed a split in the wood. He feels the sun's warmth coming off the post. He can follow the grain down to the atoms in the wood, then follow the mana along the seam where it crosses the grain. He knows exactly where the two meet. When the wind crosses the post, he still has the air, its temperature and the pull of it on his sleeve, all moving across that same little patch of yard.

He tries to look back at the cup. Yuta hasn't even got it to his mouth. Mau has the tea cooling, the cursed energy in the hand that holds it and the mana along the old split in the post. His body is still receiving the rest of the yard. Every time he fixes on one of those readings, the eyes give him enough detail to split it again.

He can decide to follow a single atom. He hasn't yet learned how to leave the rest alone—

He lowers himself onto the grass before the next breath can bring him another yard's worth of things to sort through.

“*Mau*—” ʸ

Yuka's hand is on his shoulder before he's finished sitting.

“Too much,” he says. His voice comes out strange. “It's okay. It's too much. Give me a—” ᴹ

*Turn it down*, says Ciel. *You can. The filter will take some of it. Noise, the sensing first.* ᶜ

*I know*, says Noise. *I've got it.* ᴺ

Noise lowers the new sense first. The grass beneath him settles from a thousand small pressures into somewhere to sit. Mini Ciel brings the eyes down to the scale of the things he can actually use. One outline for the cup, one for the hand carrying it, each energy flow kept separate and ready if he asks for it. The finer detail is still there. He can return to the split in the post and see what the mana is doing at a single point without having to follow every atom in the yard on the way there.

He can still feel it. All of it. Underneath. Waiting.

But it's quiet.

He breathes out.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

“Did you feel him pick up the cup?” asks Maki. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Yes. Could you feel the tea turning inside it?” ᴹ

“I felt him put more weight on his left foot. His grip tightened before he lifted it.” She watches Mau look from the cup to the post. “What else did you get?” ᴹᵃᵏ

“His cursed energy. Mana in the post. I could follow the air between them atom by atom while I felt you shift your foot.” ᴹ

Maki glances down at her boot, then back at him.

“Your eyes followed all that?” she asks. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Until I sat down.” He takes another breath. “The part you feel. Is that there all the time?” ᴹ

“All the time.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“How do you stand it?” ᴹ

She shrugs.

“I've had to learn it,” she says. “You're receiving all of this for the first time. I've spent my life working out what my body tells me, including when it changed.” ᴹᵃᵏ

She spins the staff once, idly.

“You turned it down,” she says. “I can't do that.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks up at her. He hadn't thought of that.

“Do you want to?” he asks. ᴹ

Maki thinks about it for longer than he expects.

“No,” she says at last. “It's mine.” ᴹᵃᵏ

---

They test it properly after that.

Not much. Shoko would kill him. Just enough. Maki moves round him with the staff, slow at first, then not slow, and he keeps his eyes closed, and he doesn't need them. He feels the air move before the staff does. He feels the weight shift in her back foot a long time before the strike, and steps out of the way of it, and the staff goes past his ear with a sound like a breath.

She hits him on the third one anyway.

Not hard. A tap, on the shoulder, from a direction he felt coming and still couldn't get out of.

“Feeling it isn't the same as being fast enough,” says Maki. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴹᵃᵏ

She taps him again, on the other shoulder, for emphasis.

On the bench, Yuta laughs out loud.

---

Yuka has been watching all of it from the edge of the yard.

She waits until Maki has stepped back and leaned on her staff. Then she comes over, slowly, with her hands behind her back and a very particular look on her face. He knows that look. That look has made him buy a sweet from every page of the Shop.

She stops in front of him. Close. Closer than she needs to.

“So,” she says. ʸ

“So.” ᴹ

“You can feel things now. Heat. Weight. All that.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Without looking.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She tilts her head.

“What can you feel on *me*?” she asks. ʸ

He could say *nothing*. He could say *it's turned down*. He could make a joke.

He doesn't lie to her anymore.

He closes his eyes. He lets the sense come up, just a little, just for her.

“Your heartbeat,” he says. “It's fast. It got faster when you walked over here.” ᴹ

Her face does something.

“And there's heat,” he says, “coming up your neck. From your collar. It's still climbing. It's at your ears now.” ᴹ

He opens his eyes.

Yuka is staring at him, scarlet to the roots of her hair, the only colour in the yard, with her mouth slightly open.

Then she turns round and walks very fast across the yard and puts herself behind her grandmother.

Maki looks down at her granddaughter, hiding behind her with her face in the back of her shirt.

Then she looks at Mau.

“You did that on purpose,” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“She asked,” says Mau. ᴹ

“He *told* me,” says Yuka, muffled, into Maki's back. “He just *told* me. Out loud. In the *yard*.” ʸ

“You asked,” says Maki. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I didn't think he'd *answer*.” ʸ

On the bench, Yuta has put his face in his hands. His shoulders are shaking.

Maki reaches behind her without looking and finds Yuka's head and pats it, twice, the way you'd pat a dog that has done something both stupid and endearing.

“Go on,” she says. “Go and get him. He's going to be insufferable now.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka peers out from behind her.

“He's already insufferable,” she says. ʸ

“He's going to be *more* insufferable,” says Maki. “He's got my sense. You'll never sneak up on him again. You'll never steal anything off his plate without him feeling your hand coming.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka goes very still.

Then she looks at Mau with dawning horror.

He doesn't say anything. He just smiles.

She comes back eventually.

She does it slowly, with great dignity, still pink, and stops in front of him again, and pokes him once in the chest with one finger.

“You're going to *let* me,” she says. ʸ

“Let you what?” ᴹ

“Steal things. Off your plate. You're going to feel my hand coming and you're going to *let* me.” ʸ

“I always let you.” ᴹ

“You're going to let me *more*.” ʸ

He catches her hand. The one that poked him. He holds it against his chest.

“I can feel your heartbeat in your fingers,” he says. ᴹ

“*Stop it*.” ʸ

He closes his mouth, but the smile stays.

Yuka looks down at the hand he is holding. Her finger is no longer accusing him of anything. He turns it over and rests her palm against him instead, warm beneath his own.

“You're enjoying this,” she says. ʸ

“You came over to ask me.” ᴹ

“I came over to be charming.” ʸ

“You were.” ᴹ

That leaves her quiet for just long enough that he bends toward her. She catches his collar before he can make her wait and kisses him, still pink, her other hand rising to the back of his neck.

He draws her closer. She stops worrying about what her face is doing when he follows her into the second kiss, his arm around her waist and her fingers warm against the short hair at his nape.

Behind them, Yuta makes a badly suppressed sound.

Yuka pulls back an inch.

“You can look at your own wife,” she tells him. ʸ

“I was,” Yuta manages. ʸᵗ

Maki gives him a look which starts him laughing all over again. Yuka turns back toward Mau with an expression of injured dignity.

“We should go inside.” ʸ

“To steal something off my plate?” ᴹ

“You're supposed to offer.” ʸ

He kisses her once more, briefly this time, and takes her hand. She keeps his arm close against her as they cross the yard, still explaining the terms under which she intends to enjoy his improved perception.

---

Later, when they've gone in, Maki is still in the yard.

She's finished her forms. She's sitting on the bench beside Yuta now, with the staff across her knees and his cup of tea, cold, in her hands.

“You gave it to him,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

“I didn't give him anything,” says Maki. “Noise watched me. Noise built it. All I did was say yes.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“You said *it was never mine to keep*.” ʸᵗ

Maki drinks his cold tea.

“It wasn't,” she says. “None of it was. My family made sure of that. Everything I had, I had to take.” ᴹᵃᵏ

She looks at the door Mau and Yuka went through.

“That's the first time,” she says, “anyone's asked whether they could have this part.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuta doesn't say anything. He just puts his hand over hers on the cup, and they sit in the yard in the sun, and in the house, faintly, they can hear their granddaughter shouting *I said stop it* and laughing.
