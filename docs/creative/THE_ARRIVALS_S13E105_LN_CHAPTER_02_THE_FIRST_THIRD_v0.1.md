# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 4 — The First Third

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E105
**Continuity state:** B+409 ("78"). Keeping the promise from his birthday (V80 #10), Mau sits down with Yuka, Umaru and Jin-Woo to look at the points: everything saved from level 2 to 5, worth 300 levels of the old scale. Umaru has a plan on paper for every point. Jin-Woo repeats what he said before, "keep some back", and adds why. They agree on a first third, spread across the body rather than all in one place. Mau feels it land in his body over the evening: heavier, quicker, steadier, the arm Shoko rebuilt finally as strong as the other. The rest stays unspent ("you'll know when"). B+410 adds physical calibration with Jin-Woo and Ori's route planning before the outing. Shoko checks him and is cross that there's nothing to fix. No CE or mana change; this is the body alone. The stat page uses plain field names; exact numbers are not recorded here.

*When: B+409, after lunch through night; B+410, the following morning and early afternoon. Yuka writes "78" and "POINTS DAY", then "77" the next morning.*

Umaru has brought paper.

A lot of paper. A stack of it under her arm, and three pencils, and a ruler, and what looks like a homemade chart drawn across four sheets taped together. She puts it all on the common-room table with the air of someone laying out surgical instruments.

“You promised,” she says. ᵁ

“I know.” ᴹ

“At your birthday. In front of witnesses. *Later. All of you. With Yuka first.*” ᵁ

“I know, Umaru.” ᴹ

“It's been six weeks.” ᵁ

“There was a gate,” says Yuka, sitting down beside him with her notebook. “And an island.” ʸ

“There's *always* a gate,” says Umaru. “There's always an island. Sit.” ᵁ

He sits.

Jin-Woo comes in last, with his tea.

He doesn't bring any paper. He sits down at the end of the table, where he can see everyone, and puts the cup down, and waits. He's good at waiting. Mau has seen him wait at the edge of a dungeon for an hour without moving, watching, until he knew exactly what was inside.

“Open it,” says Umaru. ᵁ

Mau opens the page.

It looks the way it has always looked since level five. The name at the top. The level. And the fields under it, the ones that used to be smudged, filled in now: plain words, in the system's flat clear hand.

*Strength. Speed. Endurance. Perception. Mana.* And a few smaller lines under those that the system hasn't explained and Umaru has spent six weeks theorising about.

And at the bottom, the box.

> **[Unallocated points: accumulated from Level 2 to Level 5.]**
>
> **[Equivalent on the former scale: 300 levels.]**

Only Mau has the page in front of him. He reads it out loud, field by field, the numbers too, and Umaru writes every one of them down on her chart with the ruler, and Yuka copies them into the notebook, and Jin-Woo listens.

When he's finished, there's a short silence.

“Okay,” says Umaru. “Here's what we do.” ᵁ

She has a plan.

Of course she has a plan. She's had a plan since the birthday. She's had, by the look of the chart, several plans, most of them crossed out, and one circled very firmly in the middle.

She explains it. At length. With estimated breakpoints, and curves, and something she calls *diminishing returns*, and something else she calls *the strength trap*, which she says most people fall into, and which involves putting everything in one place because it feels good and then discovering you can hit very hard and can't get out of the way of anything.

“You won't fall into it,” she says, “because I won't let you.” ᵁ

“I wasn't going to—” ᴹ

“You *were*.” ᵁ

He wasn't. He was going to put everything into speed. He doesn't say so.

“Nothing in mana,” says Umaru. ᵁ

She's drawn a thick line through the *Mana* row on her chart. Twice.

“Why not?” says Yuka. “It's on the page.” ʸ

Umaru looks at her as if she's asked why you don't pour water into the sea.

“Because it's *infinite*,” she says. “The Black Heart. He's got a cup with no bottom. What are you going to do, put points into it? Infinite plus ten? You'd be making it *more infinite*. That's not a stat, that's a waste. Every point in that row is a point that could've gone into his legs.” ᵁ

* * *

Senku is passing the doorway with a coil of wire over his shoulder.

He stops.

“Not necessarily,” he says. ˢᵉ

Everyone looks at him.

He comes in. He puts the wire down on the end of the table, next to Jin-Woo's tea, and picks up one of Umaru's pencils without asking, and turns over a sheet of her chart.

“Gabriel's horn,” he says. ˢᵉ

He draws. A curve, falling away to the right, getting closer and closer to the line without ever touching it. Then he turns it, round the line, into a long thin trumpet shape that goes on forever.

“This shape,” says Senku, “goes on forever. Infinitely long. And the surface of it, the outside, if you painted it, is infinite. You'd never have enough paint.” ˢᵉ

He taps the inside of the horn.

“But the *volume*,” he says. “What it holds. That's finite. You could fill it with a bucket. Ten billion per cent true. Infinite on one measure, finite on another, *same object*.” ˢᵉ

He looks at Mau.

“So when somebody says *infinite*,” says Senku, “the first question a scientist asks is: infinite *what*? Maybe your mana cup has no bottom. Fine. That's the volume. Does that mean you can pour it out infinitely *fast*? Does the spout have no limit? Those aren't the same thing. One could be infinite and the other one isn't, and the row on that page could be measuring the one that isn't.” ˢᵉ

Umaru opens her mouth. Closes it. Looks at her thick line through the *Mana* row with sudden doubt.

* * *

Mau thinks about it.

He thinks about Zoltraak. About the quarry, and the porch, and every time he's reached into the Black Heart and found it full. He's never once found the bottom. He's never once found the spout either.

“How many spells,” he says, “would I have to cast at the same time to run out?” ᴹ

Senku looks at him.

He opens his mouth.

He doesn't say anything.

He looks at the horn he's drawn. At the infinite surface and the finite inside. He looks at Mau, who has a cup with no bottom that he's never once managed to empty and has never needed to, and something goes across Senku's face that looks very much like a man who has just been handed a problem he can't solve and doesn't like it.

He puts the pencil down.

He picks up his wire.

He leaves.

“Senku?” says Umaru, to the empty doorway. ᵁ

From the hall, very faintly, irritated: “*Ten billion per cent annoying.*” ˢᵉ

Yuka puts her face in Mau's shoulder and shakes.

Umaru looks at her chart. At the *Mana* row. She doesn't rub the line out. But she adds, very small, beside it: *(ask Senku later when he's calmed down)*.

Jin-Woo waits until she's finished.

Then he leans forward and puts one finger on the chart, on the circled plan, at the bottom, where Umaru has written *ALL OF IT* with an arrow.

“Not all of it,” he says. ᴶʷ

Umaru opens her mouth.

“It's a good plan,” Jin-Woo says. “It's the plan I'd use. For a third.” ᴶʷ

He sits back.

“I told you at the birthday,” he says to Mau. “Keep some back. I didn't tell you why.” ᴶʷ

“You said the best thing you ever did was have points left the day you needed them.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴶʷ

He turns his cup round on the table. Once. Twice.

“He's done it before,” says Yuka. ʸ

Jin-Woo looks at her.

“A big jump,” says Yuka. “All at once. In the red gate. Before you came.” ʸ

Mau goes still.

He has. The red gate. The trap, the one with Makima at the end of it. He came out of it a hundred levels higher than he went in, all at once, and for days afterward he was afraid to touch anything. Afraid of the cup in his hand. Afraid of the door handle. Afraid, most of all, to hold Yuka, in case his arms didn't know their own strength yet and something in her broke.

Yuka remembers. She remembers him lying beside her in the dark that week with his hands held carefully away from her, as if they belonged to someone else.

“How many?” says Jin-Woo. ᴶʷ

“A hundred,” says Mau. ᴹ

“And after?” ᴶʷ

“I didn't want to touch anyone for a week,” says Mau. ᴹ

Jin-Woo nods slowly. He hadn't known. He takes it in the way he takes in the shape of a dungeon from its edge.

“Then you already know,” he says. “A third of what you've got now is a hundred again. Points aren't just numbers. They go into your body. Your body has to learn them. A hundred, you can learn in a few days. You've done it. If you put three hundred levels in at once, today, at this table, you'll spend the next month learning to walk again. Not badly. But you'll reach for a cup and break it. You'll step off the porch and land in the orchard.” ᴶʷ

Yuka makes a small sound.

“And if something comes in that month,” Jin-Woo says, “you'll be fighting in a body you don't know yet. That's worse than fighting in a weaker one you do.” ᴶʷ

He looks at Mau.

“A third now,” he says. “Let your body learn it. The rest—” ᴶʷ

“I'll know when,” says Mau. ᴹ

Jin-Woo almost smiles.

“You'll know when,” he agrees. ᴶʷ

Umaru looks at her chart for a long time.

Then she takes her pencil and, very slowly, divides the circled plan into three. She labels the first part *NOW*. She labels the other two *LATER* and *LATER (DON'T BE AN IDIOT)*.

“A third,” she says. “Fine. But we do it *properly*.” ᵁ

“Properly how?” ᴹ

“Spread out,” says Umaru. “Not all in one field. A bit in each. You don't want to be fast with a body that can't take the speed. You don't want to be strong with joints that can't hold it. Everything goes up together, or something breaks.” ᵁ

Jin-Woo nods.

“She's right,” he says. ᴶʷ

“I'm always right,” says Umaru. “People just don't listen.” ᵁ

Yuka checks the four allocations against the amount they agreed to spend. She makes Umaru add them again before Mau confirms anything. The first column takes one third of the saved pool; the other two thirds stay where they are. Mau reads that total back from the page so they can compare it with what they wrote down at the start.

Yuka has been very quiet.

She's holding the notebook. She's written down every number. She's been looking, the whole time, not at the chart but at him.

“Will it be like last time?” she asks. “After the red gate. When you wouldn't—” ʸ

She stops. She doesn't say *wouldn't hold me*. She doesn't have to.

Mau looks at her.

“Same size jump,” he says. “So, probably. A few days of not trusting my hands.” ᴹ

“But you know what it is this time,” says Jin-Woo, from the end of the table. “That helps more than you'd think.” ᴶʷ

Yuka nods slowly. She looks back at Mau.

“Then do it,” she says. “But I'm holding your hand while you do. And you're not letting go of it after, either. I don't care how strong your arms are.” ʸ

He does it with her hand in his.

It isn't dramatic. There's no light. There's no sound. He reads Umaru's numbers off the chart, field by field, a little in each, and puts them where she says, and the box at the bottom of the page changes to show what's left, and that's all.

For a moment, nothing.

Then he feels it start.

It begins in his hands.

A warmth, deep in the bones, the way his hands feel when he comes in from the cold and holds a cup. It spreads. Up his arms. Into his shoulders. Down his back. His legs. Everywhere at once and nowhere in particular, like the feeling of a long stretch after sleeping wrong.

He looks down at his left arm.

The one Shoko rebuilt. Eleven pieces after the north, set and re-broken and set again in Elmesia's palace and then in the clinic, still weaker than the other one all through the gate. He's been favouring it for so long he doesn't notice he's doing it anymore.

He closes his left hand.

It closes the way his right one does.

He stares at it.

“What?” says Yuka. “What is it? Is something wrong?” ʸ

“No,” he says. ᴹ

He opens the hand. Closes it again. Holds it up.

“It's the same,” he says. “As the other one. It's the same now.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at his hand.

Then she puts both of hers around it, very hard, and doesn't say anything for a while.

---

It goes on all afternoon.

Not the warmth; that fades in the first hour. The learning. Jin-Woo was right. He gets up from the table and the floor is closer than it should be, or further, he can't tell which. He reaches for his tea and his hand gets there before he expected it to, and he has to stop himself before he knocks the cup over. He goes out onto the porch and down the steps and takes them two at a time without meaning to, and nearly ends up in the yard.

Beru, on the porch, watches all of this with great interest.

“My king,” he says. “You are walking like a newborn calf.” ᴮᵉ

“Thank you, Beru.” ᴹ

“A very strong newborn calf,” Beru adds kindly. ᴮᵉ

By evening it's better.

His body is learning. He can feel it learning: each step a little closer to where he means it, each reach a little more exact. Jin-Woo, watching from the bench, gives him small corrections without being asked. *Shorter step. Don't look at your feet. Let the hand go where it wants; it already knows.*

Umaru follows him round the yard with a pencil, writing things down.

Yuka doesn't follow him. She sits on the porch steps with her notebook on her knees and watches him walk, round and round the yard, getting it right.

She draws a line in the notebook every time he gets it right. By sunset there's a lot of lines.

---

Shoko comes out at dusk.

She has her bag. Of course she has her bag. Someone told her, probably Umaru, probably very loudly, and she has come out to check.

She checks.

Both hearts, listening to each separately. Lungs. Joints. Every one of them. The left arm, for a long time, turning it in her hands, pressing on the old breaks with her thumbs, looking for the weakness that has been there for as long as she's known it.

It isn't there.

She lets go of his arm. She looks at him.

“There's nothing wrong with you,” she says. ˢʰ

She says it like an accusation.

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“The arm—” ˢʰ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I spent three weeks on that arm.” ˢʰ

“I know, Shoko.” ᴹ

He turns the hand so she can see it close again. The old marks stay where they were. So does the crooked line of the damaged fingers.

“I was using it because of you long before today,” he says. ᴹ

She stops looking for the weakness for a moment and looks at his face instead.

“I know what you gave me,” he adds. ᴹ

Her thumb rests beside the old break. Then she squeezes his hand once, tests the movement one more time and lets herself breathe out.

She looks at the arm. Then at him. Then she puts the bag down on the step and sits down next to Yuka, heavily, and takes out a cigarette she isn't going to light.

“Next time,” she says, “tell me *before*.” ˢʰ

“There were witnesses,” says Umaru. ᵁ

“I'm not a witness,” says Shoko. “I'm his doctor.” ˢʰ

---

That night, he opens the page one more time.

Yuka is asleep against him. The house is quiet. He opens it in the dark, the way he opened the Shop on the porch a long time ago, and looks at the box at the bottom.

Two thirds. Still there. Waiting.

He thinks about Jin-Woo's finger on the chart. *Not all of it.* About the cup he nearly knocked over, and the stairs he nearly fell down, and how much worse that would be in the middle of something that wanted him dead.

He closes the page.

Yuka stirs. Her hand finds his left arm, the way it always does, and runs down the line on it from the elbow to the wrist, the old habit, the one from the clinic, checking.

It stops. She's noticed something, asleep.

Her fingers press, very lightly, on the place where the old weakness used to be.

“Same,” she murmurs, without opening her eyes. ʸ

“Same,” he says. ᴹ

She smiles against his chest, and goes back to sleep, and doesn't let go of the arm.

---

The next morning, Yuka writes *77* and finds him sitting on the edge of the bed, opening and closing his left hand.

She takes it on her way past, kisses the knuckles and tells him Jin-Woo is already outside.

“Did he bring a chart?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Umaru did.” ʸ

“Of course she did.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo has brought a wooden training dagger and a line drawn across the packed earth. Mau puts his real daggers back into the inventory before taking the blunt one. The exercise begins with a step, a turn and stopping where Jin-Woo wants him to stop.

The first time, Mau passes the line.

The second time he stops too soon, pleased with the correction until Jin-Woo taps the unguarded side of his wrist.

“You were thinking about your feet,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“They've been very demanding.” ᴹ

“Give them less to do.” ᴶʷ

They shorten the movement. No reinforcement, no techniques carrying him across the distance. His body takes the first part too readily; he has to learn where to begin slowing it rather than wait until he has arrived.

Umaru writes down the first two attempts. By the fifth she has stopped writing long enough to watch his hand.

“That one,” she says. ᵁ

“Again,” Jin-Woo tells him. ᴶʷ

Mau does it again. This time Jin-Woo catches the wooden blade against his own instead of needing to reach across and stop him.

They stay with that movement until Mau can answer a question while doing it.

The question comes from Ori.

“Are you busy tomorrow?” ᴼ

He turns toward her voice and nearly loses the line. Jin-Woo lowers the trainer, laughing, before correcting him.

“I thought I had orders,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ori holds up the map. Beru stands behind her with both explorer hats in his hands.

“I wanted to check.” ᴼ

Mau gives Jin-Woo the wooden dagger and goes to see. Yuka comes off the porch steps to join them. She has brought water, and Ori has more to explain than fits in the little space at the bottom of her drawing.

They sit beside the posts. Mau holds the map while Ori follows the road with a finger, changing one turn after Beru asks which bank of the river she wants. Yuka remembers the place where the path divides. She asks Ori to draw that part larger, and gets a very serious explanation of why the other path is not on this expedition.

When Ori has gone to show Frieren, Mau stays with Yuka. She turns his newly steady hand over, puts the cup into it and watches him take a drink without having to correct the last little part of the reach.

“There,” she says. ʸ

He offers her the cup back. She takes it and sits closer, one knee against his.

For a while he watches Umaru and Jin-Woo compare what she wrote. Then his head settles against Yuka's shoulder. Her hand moves into his hair before he has found a place for it himself.

“Tired?” she asks quietly. ʸ

“Of concentrating on where my feet are.” ᴹ

She laughs and turns to kiss his forehead.

“I'll tell you if they escape.” ʸ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

She keeps him there while he finishes the water. Across the yard, Ori has persuaded Beru to stand where she can measure how far above his head the map will be.

He holds still with enormous happiness.
