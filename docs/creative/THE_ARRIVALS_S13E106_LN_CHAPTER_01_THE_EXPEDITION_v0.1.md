# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 5 — The Expedition

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E106
**Continuity state:** B+411 ("76"). The exploring trip Ori promised Beru when she offered him the matching clothes (V82 #25). The plan was Mau, Ori and Beru. Frieren comes because it's Ori. Yuka comes because it's Mau. Fern comes because Frieren would get lost, so Stark comes, so Himmel comes. Destination: the old stone ruins and the cave behind them in the hills past the river, which Ori has been mapping from the ridge for weeks; outside the house's protection but well inside Mau's reach, with Kamish overhead. Beru carries Ori on his shoulder the whole way. Everyone takes orders from a child on someone's shoulder. Beru holds back tears of happiness and mostly fails. A small real moment in the cave: Ori finds a carved mark on the wall and Frieren reads it. No combat beyond a startled bat. Mau's new body, one third in, gets its first long walk. Kiss note for Work.

*When: B+411. Sunrise to dusk. Yuka wrote "76" and drew a map pin next to it. The workshop's second-floor frame is up; Mai has forbidden anyone to look at it until it's finished.*

Ori is ready an hour before anyone else.

Mau knows this because she comes into their room at first light, fully dressed, in the explorer clothes Mai made her, hat, jacket, boots, the satchel with her map in it, and stands at the end of the bed and says, in a whisper that could be heard in the orchard:

“*Dad. It's today.*” ᴼ

Yuka, asleep on his chest, doesn't move.

“I know,” he whispers back. ᴹ

“Beru's ready.” ᴼ

“Is he?” ᴹ

“He's been ready since *yesterday*. He slept in the jacket.” ᴼ

Yuka's shoulders begin to shake. She's awake. She's laughing into his shirt and trying very hard not to make a sound.

“Five minutes,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ori considers this.

“Four,” she says, and leaves. ᴼ

* * *

Beru is in the yard.

He is, as reported, wearing the jacket. Mai made it for him to match Ori's: the same cut, the same pockets, the same small brass buttons, only enormous, fitted over his armour with a care that must have taken her days. On his head, the hat Ori gave him for his birthday, the good one, brim turned up at one side the way Ori turns up hers.

He's standing very straight. He's been standing very straight, Shuna says, for some time.

When Ori comes out of the door, he goes down on one knee.

“My commander,” he says. ᴮᵉ

Ori stops on the porch steps. Her whole face lights up.

She doesn't say anything. She just walks down the steps and across the yard and stops in front of him, and Beru lowers his head, and she reaches up and straightens his hat, very seriously, the way you'd straighten someone's collar before something important.

“There,” she says. ᴼ

Beru's mandibles make a small sound.

* * *

The plan was three.

Mau. Ori. Beru. That's what Ori promised, at the Fort, when she was bargaining for the matching clothes: an expedition, the three of them, somewhere real. She's been planning it for weeks. She has a map.

The plan lasts about four minutes.

Frieren is on the porch when they come out. She has her staff and a small bag and the expression of someone who has been told nothing and has worked everything out.

“I'm coming,” she says. ᶠ

“Mama—” ᴼ

“You're going to the ruins past the river,” says Frieren. “The ones with the cave. You've been drawing them from the ridge for three weeks. There are old marks on the stones there. I want to see them.” ᶠ

Ori looks at her for a long moment.

“You just want to come,” she says. ᴼ

“Yes,” says Frieren. ᶠ

Ori thinks about this, and then nods, very gravely, like a general accepting reinforcements.

Yuka comes out behind Mau with her boots in her hand.

“I'm coming,” she says. ʸ

“I know,” says Mau. ᴹ

“I promised. Every dungeon.” ʸ

“It isn't a dungeon. It's a cave.” ᴹ

“It's a cave you're going into without me,” says Yuka, sitting on the step to pull on her boots. “Same thing.” ʸ

* * *

Fern comes out of the kitchen with a sandwich.

She looks at Frieren with her staff. She looks at the map in Ori's hand. She looks at the hills.

“Frieren-sama,” she says. ᶠᵉ

“Fern.” ᶠ

“You'll get lost.” ᶠᵉ

“I won't.” ᶠ

“You got lost in the orchard last week.” ᶠᵉ

Frieren doesn't answer that.

Fern sighs, very deeply, the sigh of a person who has been making this decision for most of her life, and puts the sandwich in her pocket and goes to get her coat.

“If Fern's going,” says Stark, from the window, “I'm going.” ˢᵗ

“Nobody asked you,” says Fern, without turning round. ᶠᵉ

“I'm going anyway.” ˢᵗ

And then, from the far end of the porch, where he has been sitting all morning with a book he hasn't turned a page of, Himmel closes the book and stands up and stretches.

“Well,” he says, cheerfully. “I can't be the only one left behind.” ᴴⁱᵐ

Frieren looks at him.

He smiles at her.

She looks away, and doesn't say *no*.

* * *

So it's eight.

Eight people on the road past the orchard and down to the river, in the early light, with the grass still wet. Ori counts them twice. She seems to find the number satisfactory. She adds them to the map in pencil, in a list at the bottom, under the heading *EXPEDITION*.

Then she looks up at Beru.

She doesn't say anything. She just looks. And Beru, who has known her since she was very small, understands at once, and goes down on one knee again, and holds out his hand.

She steps onto it.

He lifts her up, and up, and sets her on his shoulder, on the broad flat plate of armour beside his head, and she sits there with her legs dangling down the front of his jacket and one hand on the brim of his hat for balance, higher than anyone, higher than the hedge, higher than the gate.

She looks down at all of them.

“Forward,” she says. ᴼ

* * *

They go forward.

Mau walks at the back. He doesn't decide to; he just finds himself there, with Yuka's hand in his, watching the expedition in front of him.

Beru, in front, with a commander on his shoulder. Ori with the map open across his head, which Beru doesn't seem to mind, giving directions in a clear carrying voice. *Left at the big stone. Mind the ditch. Stark, that's the wrong way.* Frieren beside them, staff in hand, looking at the hills in the way she looks at things when she's remembering a different set of hills a long time ago. Himmel next to her, close but not too close, talking about nothing. Fern and Stark behind them, arguing in low voices about the sandwich, which Stark wants half of and Fern will not give him.

And all of them, every one of them, doing what the child on Beru's shoulder says.

“Stark. *Left*.” ᴼ

“I *am* going left—” ˢᵗ

“Your *other* left.” ᴼ

Stark goes his other left.

Yuka squeezes Mau's hand. He looks at her. She's watching Ori, and her face is doing something soft and bright.

“Look at her,” she says quietly. ʸ

“I'm looking.” ᴹ

“She's *running* it.” ʸ

“She's running it,” he agrees. ᴹ

* * *

It's Beru he keeps looking at.

Beru walks very carefully, with Ori on his shoulder. More carefully than Mau has ever seen him walk, in the tower or the gates or the long war in the north. He places each foot like the ground might give. He keeps one hand up, not touching her, just there, near her back, in case.

And every so often, when Ori gives an order, or leans forward to point at something, or laughs, Beru's head turns, very slightly, toward her. And his mandibles move. And he makes a small sound, low in his chest, which he seems to be trying very hard to keep in.

On the riverbank, when Ori says *we stop here for water, everyone, that's an order*, Beru stops, and stands there with her on his shoulder, and Mau sees him lift his free hand and press it, briefly, against the side of his own face, where his eyes are.

“Beru,” Mau says quietly, coming up beside him. ᴹ

“My king,” says Beru, not looking at him. His voice is not entirely steady. “I have dust in my eyes.” ᴮᵉ

“All of them?” ᴹ

“All of them,” says Beru. ᴮᵉ

* * *

His body is different on the road.

He notices it after the river. The long climb up into the hills, which used to pull at his side and his left arm by the second hour, doesn't pull at anything. He walks it the way he used to walk the road with Frieren, a long time ago, before anything was broken. His left hand, when he puts it out to steady Yuka on a loose stone, closes on her arm as firmly as his right.

And the new sense is there too, turned low, underneath everything. The warmth of the sun on the rocks. The cool coming out of the ground where the path goes into shade. The weight of eight people and a giant ant on the hillside, all moving, all shifting, all safe.

Overhead, high up, so high he's only a shape, Kamish turns in a slow wide circle against the sky.

*Nothing for a long way*, Ciel says. *A few goats. A very angry bird.* ᶜ

“Good,” Mau says. ᴹ

* * *

The ruins are on a shoulder of the hill, just below the ridge.

Old stone. Older than the house, older than anything Mau has seen in this valley except the trees. A ring of broken walls, half fallen, with grass growing through the floors. And behind them, set into the hillside, a dark opening in the rock: the cave Ori has been drawing from the ridge for three weeks.

Ori goes very quiet on Beru's shoulder.

She looks at the ruins. Then down at her map. Then at the ruins again.

“I drew it right,” she says, almost to herself. ᴼ

Frieren looks at the map over her shoulder.

“You did,” she says. “Better than I would have.” ᶠ

Ori pulls her hat down over her eyes and says nothing at all.

* * *

They go into the cave together.

Beru first, ducking, with Ori pressed down flat against his helmet so she won't hit the roof, laughing. Then Mau and Yuka. Then the rest. Frieren makes a light, a small soft one, the kind she taught Mau a long time ago, and it floats ahead of them along the passage and shows them the walls.

There's a bat.

Just one. It comes off the ceiling when the light reaches it and goes past them all in a panic, and Stark yells and ducks and walks straight into Fern, and Fern says something under her breath that Ori is not supposed to hear, and Ori hears it and repeats it delightedly, and Frieren says *Ori* in a voice that stops her.

Then the passage opens out.

* * *

It's a small chamber. Round. Dry. The floor smooth with age.

And on the far wall, cut into the rock, a mark.

Ori sees it first. Of course she does. She slides down Beru's arm before anyone can stop her and goes across the chamber and stands in front of it, in the floating light, with her hat pushed back.

It's a shape. Lines and curves, cut deep and worn soft. Not a picture. Something that might be writing, or might once have been.

“Mama,” she says. “What is it?” ᴼ

Frieren comes and stands beside her.

She looks at the mark for a long time. Her face goes very still, the way it goes when she's somewhere a long way back.

“It's a road sign,” she says at last. ᶠ

Ori looks up at her.

“An old one,” says Frieren. “Very old. People used to cut them into caves along the roads, so travellers would know where they were. Where the water was. How far to the next shelter.” ᶠ

“What does this one say?” ᴼ

Frieren reads it. Her finger moves along the lines without touching them.

“It says *rest here*,” she says. “And then, under it. Someone added this later. It's in a different hand.” ᶠ

She stops.

“What?” says Ori. ᴼ

“*We did*,” Frieren reads. “It says *we did*.” ᶠ

* * *

Nobody says anything for a moment.

Then Himmel laughs, very softly, behind them.

“That's a good sign,” he says. ᴴⁱᵐ

Frieren doesn't turn round. But Mau, from where he's standing, sees her face, in the light, and sees the corner of her mouth go up.

Ori takes out her map. She takes out her pencil. Very carefully, on the drawing of the cave, in the place where the chamber is, she writes:

*REST HERE. WE DID.*

Then, underneath, in smaller letters:

*(expedition 1)*

* * *

They eat in the ruins, in the sun.

Fern gives Stark half the sandwich after all. Himmel produces, from somewhere, a bag of small hard sweets that Frieren pretends not to like and eats four of. Ori sits on a broken wall between Beru and Mau, swinging her legs, eating an apple, explaining to Beru exactly where the next expedition will go, which involves the ridge, a stream, and a place on her map marked only with a question mark.

Beru listens to all of it with his whole attention.

Yuka sits on the grass with her back against Mau's legs and her head tipped back against his knee, eyes closed in the sun.

“Good day?” he says. ᴹ

“The best,” she says, without opening her eyes. ʸ

She reaches up without looking and finds his hand and pulls it down onto her shoulder, and holds it there.

* * *

On the way home, Ori falls asleep.

She falls asleep on Beru's shoulder, about halfway down the hill, with her cheek against the side of his helmet and her hat slipping over one eye and the map still in her hand.

Beru stops walking.

He stands very still on the path, in the long evening light, and doesn't move, and everyone behind him stops too.

Then, very slowly, very carefully, he reaches up with his free hand and takes the hat off her head before it can fall, and holds it against his chest, and goes on down the hill so gently that she doesn't wake.

Mau walks at the back with Yuka, and doesn't say anything.

He doesn't have to. He can see Beru's shoulders, from behind, and the way they're shaking, just slightly, all the way home.

* * *

In the yard, Beru lowers Ori into Frieren's arms without waking her.

Then he straightens, and turns to Mau, still holding the small hat against his chest.

“My king,” he says. ᴮᵉ

“Beru.” ᴹ

“That was the best day,” says Beru, “since the beginning.” ᴮᵉ

Mau looks at him.

“You said that about the gate,” he says. “On my birthday.” ᴹ

Beru considers this.

“I was wrong,” he says. “It was the second best.” ᴮᵉ

He looks down at the hat in his hands.

“She said *expedition one*,” he says, very quietly. “On the map. *One*.” ᴮᵉ

“That means there's going to be a two,” says Yuka. ʸ

Beru doesn't answer. He just stands there in the yard in his matching jacket, holding a child's hat, with the evening coming down, and Mau doesn't need the eyes or the new sense or anything at all to know that the dust has got into all of them again.
