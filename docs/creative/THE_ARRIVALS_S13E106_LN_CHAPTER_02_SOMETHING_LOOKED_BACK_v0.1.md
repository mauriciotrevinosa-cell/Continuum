# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 6 — Something Looked Back

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S13E106
**Continuity state:** B+414 ("73"). A letter arrives from Subaru, the first word from him and Tsurugi in a long time. They went looking for Emilia across this world, not other worlds. They've found her, and Puck, and Beatrice. They're coming home. And they aren't alone: Tsurugi met people on the road, and some of them are "not from here, not from anywhere". That night on the porch, with the new sense turned low and the eyes open, Mau feels something enormous a long way off to the north-east: bright, like light, and under it something with no word, pure intent. It notices him noticing, and looks back. Short closing section from far away: a Simurian warrior on a mountain, honing himself, who doesn't remember where he learned half of what he knows, feels a small hard point to the south-west that is hiding almost everything, and smiles. No names yet on that side.

*When: B+414. Afternoon, then late night. Yuka wrote "73" and nothing else; she was in a hurry to get to the window because she'd heard the bird.*

The bird comes in at lunch.

Not Elmesia's. Mau knows Elmesia's bird: sleek and grey and unimpressed by everyone. This one is smaller and scruffier, with one ragged wing feather, and it comes in through the kitchen window and lands in the middle of the table, directly in the butter, and looks around at all of them with an expression of tremendous self-importance.

There's a letter tied to its leg.

Rem gets to it first. She always gets to things first. She unties the letter and turns it over, and her face changes.

“It's Subaru's writing,” she says. ᴿᵉ

* * *

The kitchen goes quiet.

Subaru and Tsurugi have been gone a long time. Since after the Final Trial. They went together, the two of them, looking for Emilia: not across other worlds, but across this one, over the hills and past the places on Senku's maps where the maps stop. The house knew. The house has always known. But nobody has heard anything in months, and the house has been careful, very careful, not to talk about how long it's been.

Ram, at the end of the table, puts down her cup.

Rem looks at her sister. Then at the letter. Then she holds it out across the table, to Mau.

“You read it,” she says. ᴿᵉ

He looks at her.

“Out loud,” says Rem. “For everyone.” ᴿᵉ

* * *

He reads it out loud.

Subaru's handwriting is terrible. It always was. It slopes, and it crowds, and it breaks into capitals for no reason halfway through words. Mau has to stop twice and squint.

*To everyone at the house,*

*We found her.*

There's a pause in the kitchen that you could hear a pin drop in.

*We found Emilia. She's okay. She's really okay. She was a long way north, past the big lake, in a village that didn't know what she was and liked her anyway, which is the most Emilia thing that has ever happened. Puck is with her. He says hello. He also says he wants to know who's been looking after Rem and Ram, and he's very fluffy about it, in a threatening way.*

*And BEATRICE. Beako was with them. She says she was NOT waiting for me. She was absolutely waiting for me.*

Somebody at the table laughs, wetly. Mau doesn't look up to see who.

*We're coming home. All of us. It'll take a while; the roads are bad and Puck refuses to be carried. Tell Rem and Ram. Tell them I'm sorry it took so long. Tell them I have a lot of things to say and I want to say them properly, in person, so I'm not going to write them here.*

Rem has put her hand over her mouth. Ram, beside her, is looking at the table, very hard, and her jaw is tight.

Mau keeps reading.

* * *

*One more thing,* the letter says.

The writing changes here. Still Subaru's. But smaller. More careful.

*Tsurugi met some people on the road.*

*I'm going to be honest, because Tsurugi says Mau likes it when people are honest now, and Tsurugi is very insistent about this. They're not from here. They're not from anywhere. They came down out of the sky somewhere in the north, a lot of them, and most of them stayed where they landed, and a few of them didn't.*

*Two of them are travelling with us. Brothers. Twins. One of them wants to talk. The other one wants to fight everyone and Tsurugi keeps telling him no.*

*They've heard about the house. Tsurugi told them. Tsurugi told them about Mau.*

*I'm sorry, Mau. He talks a lot when he's excited. He told them you're the strongest person in this world and that you're basically a king. I tried to stop him. He doesn't stop.*

*There's a third one. He isn't travelling with us. He's not travelling with anyone. But he heard Tsurugi talk about you, and he got very quiet, and the next morning he was gone, and the brothers say that means he's interested.*

*The brothers say that's bad.*

*See you soon.*

*Subaru*

*P.S. Beako says she's coming to the house to see if the library is any good, NOT because she missed anyone. Please have a library.*

* * *

He puts the letter down.

The kitchen is very quiet for a moment. Then it isn't. Then everyone is talking at once: Rem crying and laughing, Ram saying *he'd better have a lot of things to say* in a voice that isn't steady, Milim demanding to know who the twins are and whether they're strong, Umaru demanding to know whether the house has a library and, if not, why not.

Mau sits in the middle of it with the letter in front of him.

*He told them you're the strongest person in this world.*

Yuka's hand finds his knee under the table.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't have to. She's read the last part over his shoulder.

“Seventy-three,” she says, very quietly. Just to him. ʸ

“Seventy-three,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

That night, he can't sleep.

He doesn't try very hard. He gets up, carefully, so as not to wake her, and goes down through the dark house and out onto the porch, and sits in the old chair with his feet on the railing and the night all round him.

It's a clear night. Cold. The stars are out over the hills in the way they only come out in the dry part of the year, sharp and close.

He closes his eyes.

He lets the sense come up. Just a little. Just to see.

* * *

The yard first. The warmth still coming off the stones where the sun was. The cool in the grass. Beru, at the end of the porch, very still, keeping watch; the steady slow weight of him. Kamish, high up and far off, turning against the stars. The house behind him, full of people sleeping, all their warmth and breathing like a great soft animal at rest.

Then further.

The orchard. The river. The hills. He lets the sense go out, the way you'd let a rope out, further than he's let it go before. Mini Ciel holds the flood. Noise holds the sense. The eyes read everything it touches.

The valley. The ridge where Ori draws her maps. The ruins with *rest here, we did* cut into the rock. Further. The empty country past the ridge, where the maps stop.

And then, a very long way off, to the north-east, beyond anything he's ever reached—

Something.

* * *

He opens his eyes.

He doesn't mean to. His body does it, the way his body sat down on the stair in the gate when he saw the Shop was open. His heart is going very fast.

*You felt it*, says Ciel. Not a question. ᶜ

“What is it?” ᴹ

*I don't know.* ᶜ

Ciel doesn't say *I don't know*. Ciel says *I'm looking* or *insufficient data* or *give me a moment*. She doesn't say *I don't know*.

He closes his eyes again. He goes back.

* * *

It's still there.

Very far. So far that it's hardly anything: a point, at the edge of the edge of what he can reach. But it's *bright*. That's the first thing. Not bright the way a fire is bright, or the sun on the rocks. Bright the way the eyes see light: a pressure, a shape, something that the eyes read as *light* the way they read Infinity as *distance*.

And under the brightness, something else.

Something the eyes don't have a word for. They try. He feels them try, the way they tried with the Warden and found nothing to hold. Only this isn't nothing. This is the opposite of nothing. It's full. It's pressing. It's—

*Intent*, says Noise, very quietly. *It's just intent. Pure. Wanting to kill. Shaped like a thing.* ᴺ

* * *

He holds still.

He holds very still in the old chair on the porch, with his eyes closed and the sense out further than it's ever been, and looks at the bright hard point a very long way off.

And the point looks back.

He feels it. Unmistakably. The way you feel someone's eyes on you across a room. The brightness *turns*. The intent under it sharpens, and narrows, and comes round toward him across all that distance, the way a dog's head comes round at a sound.

It knows he's there.

It knows he's looking.

*Pull back*, says Ciel. *Now. Slowly. Don't let it follow the line.* ᶜ

He pulls back.

Slowly. The rope coming in, hand over hand. The empty country. The ridge. The ruins. The river. The orchard. The yard. The porch.

He opens his eyes.

* * *

Beru is standing beside his chair.

He didn't hear him move. Beru is very quiet when he wants to be. He's standing there in the dark with his wings half open and every eye fixed on the north-east, on nothing, on the hills.

“My king,” he says. ᴮᵉ

“You felt it too.” ᴹ

“I felt *you* feel it,” says Beru. “And then I felt it look at you.” ᴮᵉ

He doesn't fold his wings.

“I did not like it,” says Beru. ᴮᵉ

* * *

The door opens behind them.

Yuka. Barefoot, in his shirt, with the blanket from the bed round her shoulders and her hair everywhere and her face still soft with sleep. She stops in the doorway and looks at him, and at Beru with his wings open, and at the hills.

She doesn't ask what's wrong.

She comes across the porch and sits down in his lap, blanket and all, and wraps it round both of them, and puts her cold feet on his shins, and her head on his shoulder.

“Tell me,” she says. ʸ

So he tells her.

All of it. The sense. The point. The light, and the thing under the light. The way it turned and looked at him.

She listens without moving.

When he's done, she's quiet for a while.

“Is that one of them?” she says. “The ones Subaru wrote about?” ʸ

“I think so.” ᴹ

“The third one. The one who went off by himself.” ʸ

“I think so.” ᴹ

She's quiet again.

Then she pulls the blanket tighter round both of them and settles her head more firmly on his shoulder, like someone digging in.

“Then he can look,” she says. “He can look all he wants. You're mine.” ʸ

* * *

A very long way to the north-east, on a mountain with no name, someone opens their eyes.

He has been sitting on the bare rock at the top for three days. He sits there often. It's high and cold and nothing lives on it, and nothing interrupts him, and he can turn inward, the way he's learned to, and go over everything he knows.

There's a great deal of it.

More than there used to be. More than he can account for, sometimes. Techniques he doesn't remember learning. Ways of moving that his body knows and his memory doesn't. A way of mending himself from the inside, of closing a wound by turning something back on itself, which he can do as easily as breathing and has no idea who taught him. A space he can open around himself, a world of his own, which he has never once opened and knows exactly how to.

He remembers honing. That's all. Long days of honing, on mountains like this one, against nothing, against himself. Pushing, and adapting, and pushing again, against something that was always just a little stronger, that was always changing to meet him, always turning a great slow wheel—

He doesn't remember the wheel.

He doesn't remember anything about it, except that sometimes, at the edge of sleep, there's a sound like something heavy turning, and he wakes with his hands up.

* * *

He felt something just now.

A long way off. To the south-west. Beyond the empty country.

Not a light, like his. Nothing like his. Something small, and hard, and very carefully closed, like a fist. Something that showed almost nothing at all: a little cursed energy, a little of the other thing the humans here call mana, barely more than the boy Tsurugi had. Nothing worth turning his head for.

But it was *looking*.

That was the thing. It had reached all the way out here, across all that distance, further than anything here should be able to reach, and it had found him. And when he looked back, it had pulled away. Carefully. Hand over hand. Not afraid. Just careful. Like something that knew exactly what it was doing, and was hiding almost everything, and had decided, for now, not to show him.

The boy Tsurugi had told him about a king.

He had laughed. Kings were for his people, and his people's king was him.

He isn't laughing now.

He sits on the bare rock under the stars, a long way from anywhere, and looks toward the south-west, where the small hard fist had been.

And for the first time in a very long time, he smiles.
