# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 8 — Permanent

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E107
**Continuity state:** B+420 ("67"). The day after the arrival. Subaru asks Mau to sit with him while he talks to Rem and Ram, because he doesn't trust himself not to run. He apologises properly. Rem forgives him with her whole heart. Ram forgives him too, and tells him plainly that forgiving isn't the same as fixed: the damage to what was between them is permanent, and he'll have to live next to the crack. He accepts it. Emilia has tea with Satella, with Yuka and Mau carrying the cups; Satella answers what she can and says "not yet" to the rest. Beatrice inspects the house's "library" (Frieren's shelves, Senku's notebooks, the grimoire Frieren keeps for Mau) and moves in among Frieren's books without asking. In the afternoon the Fort inducts Emilia as a member, with Ori presiding. Cross is at the edge of every room Yuka is in.

*When: B+420. Morning to evening. Yuka wrote "67". Subaru has been awake since before dawn, sitting on the back steps.*

Subaru is on the back steps when Mau comes down.

He's been there a while. Mau can tell by the way he's sitting: elbows on his knees, hands hanging, the cup of tea beside him gone cold and untouched. He looks up when the door opens.

“Hey,” he says. ˢᵇ

“Hey.” ᴹ

Mau sits down beside him. He doesn't say anything. He's learned, over a long time and from a lot of people, that sometimes the best thing to do with someone on a step at dawn is sit next to them.

Subaru looks at the yard for a while.

“I have to talk to them,” he says. “Rem and Ram. Properly. I said I would. In the letter.” ˢᵇ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I've had months. I had this whole thing worked out. On the road. Every night. What I'd say.” ˢᵇ

He laughs, not very well.

“I've forgotten all of it,” he says. ˢᵇ

* * *

Mau doesn't know everything about it.

He knows enough. The house knows. Subaru and Rem, and Subaru and Emilia, and the long tangle of it from before they all came here. Rem, who loved him with everything. Subaru, who loved Emilia. The things he promised, and the things he couldn't keep, and the way it all came apart in this house, a long time ago, in a way that hurt everyone and that nobody has ever quite put back.

And Ram. Who watched all of it happen to her sister. Who has never once said what she thought of it, out loud.

“Will you sit with me?” Subaru says. ˢᵇ

Mau looks at him.

“When I talk to them,” says Subaru. “Not to say anything. Just—be there. I don't trust myself not to run.” ˢᵇ

He looks at the cold tea.

“I ran a lot,” he says quietly. “Before. When things got bad. You've heard.” ˢᵇ

“I've heard.” ᴹ

“I don't want to run from this one.” ˢᵇ

Mau thinks about it.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

They talk in the small sitting room at the end of the house, the one with the window on the orchard.

Rem and Ram on the couch. Subaru in the chair across from them. Mau in the corner by the window, where he can see all three of them and none of them have to look at him.

Subaru talks.

It doesn't come out the way he planned it. Mau can tell. It comes out in pieces, out of order, starting in the middle and going back to the beginning and getting lost and starting again. He says he's sorry. He says it a lot. He says he's sorry for the promises and sorry for the leaving and sorry for the way he left, and sorry it took him this long to come back and say so. He says he knows sorry doesn't fix it. He says it anyway.

Rem listens with her hands in her lap and her eyes on his face.

Ram listens with her eyes on the window.

* * *

When he's finished, Rem gets up.

She crosses the little room and kneels in front of his chair, and takes both his hands in hers, and holds them.

“I forgive you,” she says. ᴿᵉ

Simply. Without any conditions on it. The way Rem does everything: all the way, with her whole heart, no part held back.

Subaru's face crumples.

“Rem—” ˢᵇ

“I forgave you a long time ago,” says Rem. “I was only waiting for you to come home so I could tell you.” ᴿᵉ

She squeezes his hands. Then she lets go, and stands, and goes back to the couch, and sits down beside her sister.

They both look at Ram.

* * *

Ram doesn't say anything for a long time.

She's still looking at the window. At the orchard, where the light is coming up through the old trees. Her hands are folded in her lap, very neatly, and very still.

“I forgive you too, Barusu,” she says at last. ᴿᵃ

Subaru lets out a breath.

“But,” says Ram. ᴿᵃ

He stops breathing again.

She turns her head. She looks at him. Directly, for the first time since he came through the gate.

“Forgiving you isn't the same as it being fixed,” she says. “I want you to understand that. I'm not going to pretend. You hurt my sister. I watched you do it. And something between you and me broke when you did, and it isn't going to unbreak because you came back thinner with a scar on your face and said sorry a great many times.” ᴿᵃ

Subaru doesn't look away. Mau will remember that, later. He doesn't look away.

“The damage is permanent,” says Ram. “What we had before: it's gone. It isn't coming back. We'll be something else now. I don't know what yet. Something with a crack in it. And you'll have to live next to the crack, every day, and not pretend it isn't there.” ᴿᵃ

She pauses.

“That's what I can give you,” she says. “Forgiveness, and the truth. I think it's more than you expected.” ᴿᵃ

* * *

The room is very quiet.

Then Subaru nods. Slowly.

“Yeah,” he says. His voice is rough. “Yeah. It's more than I expected.” ˢᵇ

He wipes his face with the back of his hand.

“Thank you,” he says. “For not pretending. I'd rather have the crack than the pretending.” ˢᵇ

Ram looks at him for a moment longer.

Then something happens at the corner of her mouth. Not a smile. Not quite.

“Good,” she says. “Then you can start by eating something. You look terrible.” ᴿᵃ

She stands up and leaves the room.

Rem stays a moment longer. She looks at Subaru, and then, very briefly, at Mau in the corner, and inclines her head to him, a small *thank you*. Then she follows her sister.

* * *

Subaru sits in the chair with his face in his hands.

Mau doesn't say anything. He just stays in the corner, by the window, until Subaru lifts his head.

“You didn't run,” Mau says. ᴹ

Subaru laughs, a cracked, wet sound.

“No,” he says. “I didn't.” ˢᵇ

* * *

Emilia has tea with Satella in the afternoon.

Yuka arranges it. She doesn't ask anyone; she just decides, the way she decides things now, and appears in the kitchen with a tray and a very firm expression and says *Mau, you're carrying the pot*. So he carries the pot. Out onto the side porch, where Satella is sitting in the shade, alone, in the chair she always uses.

Satella looks up when they come round the corner.

She sees Emilia behind them.

She goes very still. The same stillness as yesterday, in the doorway.

“You said there was tea,” says Emilia. Her voice is careful. “When I was ready. I think I'm—I'd like to try.” ᴱᵐ

* * *

Mau pours. Yuka hands round the cups. Then they sit on the porch steps a little way off, close enough to be there and far enough not to be part of it, Yuka leaning against his side with her head on his shoulder.

They don't listen. Not on purpose. But the porch is small.

Emilia asks questions. Careful ones, at first. Where Satella came from. How long she's been here. Whether the house knew, when she came, what she was.

Satella answers. Some of it. She tells Emilia how she arrived, and who was kind to her first, and how long it took before she could sit at the long table without everyone going quiet. She doesn't tell her everything. Mau can hear the places where she stops.

Then Emilia asks the question she came to ask.

“Why do we look the same?” ᴱᵐ

* * *

Satella holds her cup in both hands.

She looks at it for a long time.

“I don't know all of it,” she says. “And the parts I do know, I don't think you're ready to hear. Not today. Not on your second day here, after a road that long.” ˢᵃ

Emilia's face falls, just slightly.

“Not yet,” says Satella gently. “That's all. Not *no*. Not yet.” ˢᵃ

She looks up.

“You can ask me again,” she says. “Whenever you want. I'll always tell you *not yet* or I'll tell you the truth. I won't ever tell you something that isn't.” ˢᵃ

Emilia looks at her.

Then, slowly, she nods, and drinks her tea.

* * *

Beside Mau, Yuka has gone very quiet.

“She's good at that,” she murmurs. “*Not yet*. She does it with Ori too.” ʸ

“She does it with everyone,” says Mau. ᴹ

Yuka is quiet for a moment.

“I want to be better at it,” she says. ʸ

He looks down at her. She isn't looking at him. She's looking across the yard, toward the training posts, where Cross is standing alone with his arms crossed, not training, not doing anything, just standing in the sun.

Looking at the porch.

Mau looks too. Cross looks away.

Yuka doesn't notice. She's already turned back to the tea, and to Satella, and to Emilia, and she's tucked herself more firmly against Mau's side, and she's smiling.

* * *

Beatrice finds the library at four in the afternoon.

There isn't one. Not really. There's Frieren's room, with its shelves of grimoires up to the ceiling. There's Senku's corner of the workshop, with its stacks of notebooks. There are the books in the common room, which are mostly Umaru's and mostly about games.

Beatrice inspects all of it. She does it slowly, with her arms crossed and her chin up and an expression of profound disappointment, room by room, shelf by shelf, while Subaru follows behind her apologising to everyone.

She saves Frieren's room for last.

She stands in the doorway and looks at the shelves for a long time. At the old bindings. At the books by the window, where the light is good. At one in particular, wrapped in an old cloth, on a shelf by itself.

“What is that one?” she asks. ᴮᵉᵃ

“It's Mau's,” says Frieren, from her chair. “I'm keeping it for him.” ᶠ

Beatrice looks at Frieren. Then at the book. Then at Frieren again.

Something in her expression changes. Not much. Just enough.

“This,” she says, “is acceptable.” ᴮᵉᵃ

And she walks into the room, and climbs onto the window seat beside the shelves, and takes down a book without asking, and opens it, and doesn't come out again until dinner.

Frieren watches her for a while.

Then she goes back to her own book, and doesn't say anything, and when Fern comes to call them both for dinner she finds them sitting a foot apart in total silence, each reading, and both of them, she reports later, looking extremely pleased.

* * *

The Fort inducts Emilia at sunset.

Ori presides. She stands on a crate in the yard in her explorer clothes, with the banner from the castle day held up behind her by Milim and Umaru, and the Fort girls in a row, and the rest of the house watching from the porch. Emilia stands in front of her, looking slightly bewildered, with Puck on her shoulder looking extremely amused.

“Do you,” says Ori, very seriously, “promise to defend the Fort?” ᴼ

“I—yes,” says Emilia. “I promise.” ᴱᵐ

“Even against Kamish?” ᴼ

Emilia looks at the enormous dragon lying in the orchard. Kamish opens one eye.

“Even against Kamish,” says Emilia bravely. ᴱᵐ

“And do you promise,” says Ori, “never to tell anyone where the tunnel is?” ᴼ

“There's a tunnel?” ᴱᵐ

“*There was never a tunnel*,” says Kamish, from the orchard, without lifting his head. ᴷ

The whole porch laughs.

Ori waits, with great dignity, for it to stop. Then she climbs down off her crate, and goes to Emilia, and reaches up, and pins a small paper badge to her coat: a castle, drawn in pencil, very carefully.

“Welcome to the Fort,” she says. ᴼ

Emilia looks down at the badge. Her face does something soft.

“Thank you,” she says. ᴱᵐ

* * *

Mau watches from the porch with Yuka in his lap.

She's laughing at Kamish. She's clapping for Emilia. She's leaning back against him with her whole weight and her head under his chin, warm and loose and happy, the way she's been every evening since the island.

He holds her and watches the yard.

At the far end of it, by the posts, Cross is watching too.

Not the induction. Not Emilia. Not Ori on her crate.

Yuka.

He watches her laugh. He watches her lean back against Mau. He watches Mau's arms round her. And his face, in the long evening light, is very still, and very hard to read.

Yuka doesn't see. She's turned her face up to say something to Mau, something about Kamish and the tunnel, and she's laughing so hard she can barely get it out.

He laughs with her. He kisses the top of her head.

And over her head, across the yard, he meets Cross's eyes.

Neither of them looks away for a long moment.

Then Maru comes and stands beside his brother and says something quietly, and Cross turns, and goes inside.
