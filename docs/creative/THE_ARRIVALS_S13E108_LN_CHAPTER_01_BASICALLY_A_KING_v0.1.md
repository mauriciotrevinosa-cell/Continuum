# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 9 — Basically a King

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S13E108
**Continuity state:** B+421 ("66"). The first real talk with the Simurians, at the long table after dinner, with Rimuru as the house's diplomat and Mau at the head because everyone looks at him. Maru explains: his people came down from the sky in the north; most stayed where they landed; they want to live, not fight; but there's one who left. Dabura, the strongest of them, the one they call king. He's been honing himself alone on a mountain, and since Tsurugi's stories he's been asking about Mau. Tsurugi, delighted, retells his stories about Mau to the whole table, and Mau wants to sink through the floor. Yuka sits in Mau's lap through the whole negotiation and won't move. Cross says almost nothing; when he does, it's to Yuka. Maru asks Mau, formally, to meet Dabura before Dabura decides to come by himself. Mau agrees to think about it. The meeting ends with Yuka claiming a kiss and Cross watching from the doorway.

*When: B+421. After dinner, into the night. Yuka wrote "66" and, underneath, "TALKS", underlined, then crossed out and replaced with "TALKS (BORING)".*

Rimuru calls it a meeting.

Nobody else does. To everyone else it's just dinner going on longer than usual, with the plates cleared away and the lamps lit and the twins sitting at the long table with the house all round them. But Rimuru has a notebook and a pen and the face he wears when he's being a ruler, and he's put himself at the side of the table where he can see everyone, and he has, very deliberately, left the chair at the head of the table empty.

Everyone looks at Mau.

He looks at the chair.

“I'm not sitting there,” he says. ᴹ

“You're sitting there,” says Rimuru. ᴿ

“Why?” ᴹ

“Because they're looking at you,” says Rimuru, not unkindly, “and if you sit anywhere else, they'll keep looking at you anyway, and it'll be awkward.” ᴿ

He sits there.

Yuka sits on him.

She doesn't ask. She just comes round the table from where she was helping Shuna with the plates, and arrives at the head of the table, and sits down in his lap, sideways, with her legs over the arm of the chair and her back against his shoulder and her notebook on her knees. As if this is a normal place to sit in a diplomatic meeting. As if she does it every day.

She does do it every day. Just not usually at meetings.

Rimuru looks at her.

“Yuka,” he says. ᴿ

“I'm taking notes,” says Yuka. She holds up the notebook. ʸ

“You could take notes from a chair.” ᴿ

“This is a chair,” says Yuka. ʸ

Gojo, at the far end of the table, puts his face down on his arms and starts shaking.

Mau doesn't say anything. He puts one arm round her waist so she won't slide off, and looks across the table at the twins, and waits.

Maru is looking at them with polite interest.

Cross isn't looking at them at all. He's looking at the table. At his own hands, flat on the wood. His jaw is tight.

“In my experience,” says Maru, carefully, “kings do not usually hold meetings with someone sitting on them.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“He's not a king,” says Yuka. “He's my husband.” ʸ

Maru considers this.

“Both, I was told,” he says. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“Tell us,” says Rimuru, before Tsurugi can open his mouth. ᴿ

So Maru tells them.

He tells it slowly, choosing every word, the way he spoke at the gate. His people came down out of the sky. A long time ago now, by their counting; not so long, by anyone else's. They came down in the north, a great many of them, in a place with mountains and snow and nothing else. They didn't know where they were. They didn't know anyone. They didn't know if anyone would want them.

“Most of us stayed where we landed,” says Maru. “We built. We waited. We are not a warlike people, whatever you may have heard. Most of us only want to live.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

He looks at his brother.

“Most of us,” he says again. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Cross doesn't look up.

“And the one who isn't most of you,” says Rimuru. ᴿ

Maru is quiet for a moment.

“Dabura,” he says. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

The name sits on the table between them.

“He is the strongest of us,” says Maru. “Our people call him king. He has always been strong. But he has become—more. Since we came here. Much more. He went up a mountain alone, a long time ago, and stayed there, honing himself. When he came down, he was different. He knew things he had not known before. He could do things no one had taught him.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Mau stops moving his thumb over Yuka's waist.

*Things no one taught him.*

“Does he remember learning them?” he asks. ᴹ

Maru looks at him sharply.

“No. He remembers going away to improve. He can't tell us when some of it became possible.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“Something looked back at me a week ago,” Mau says. “From that direction. I was on the porch, looking through one of my shadows farther out.” ᴹ

The table goes quiet.

Maru stares at him. Even Cross looks up.

“From *here*?” says Maru. “He is—that mountain is weeks from here. On foot. Days on the fastest of us.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“I wasn't standing next to him,” Mau says. “I can't tell you his technique from that glimpse. I can tell you he noticed the attention.” ᴹ

“And you think it was him.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“The direction fits. So does what you're describing. I'd like to check the rest before I decide anything.” ᴹ

Maru sits back in his chair.

He looks at Mau for a long time. Then at Yuka in his lap, still writing in her notebook, apparently unbothered. Then at Tsurugi, at the far end of the table, who is practically vibrating.

“Tsurugi,” says Maru slowly. “When you told him. On the road. About this house. What exactly did you say?” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Tsurugi beams.

Mau closes his eyes.

Tsurugi tells them.

All of it. To the whole table. With gestures.

He tells them about the duel. About Sukuna, and the quarry, and a technique that cut the world, and Mau standing back up anyway. He tells them about the gate that wanted to kill Yuka, and the Warden, and Mau being cut in half and putting himself back together before the halves could fall apart. He tells them about the Monarchs in the north, and the shadow army, and seven kings in an ambush, and a dragon raised from the dead, and a city saved. He tells them, with particular relish, about the Frost Monarch.

Yuka's pencil stops. Mau looks down at the notebook, then at his brother-in-law.

“You heard about that one,” he says. ᴹ

“From very reliable people.” ᵀˢ

“Name two.” ʸ

Tsurugi points at them both. Yuka stares at him. Across the table, Jin-Woo puts his cup down before he spills it.

He gets about a third of it wrong. He tells it all as if it happened yesterday, and as if he saw all of it, which he didn't, and as if Mau did it all smiling, which he very much didn't.

“And then,” says Tsurugi, “he put his hand on the dome, like *this*—” ᵀˢ

“Tsurugi,” says Mau. ᴹ

“—and it just *folded*—” ᵀˢ

“*Tsurugi*.” ᴹ

“What? It's true. Isn't it true?” ᵀˢ

“It's mostly true,” says Yuka, without looking up from her notebook. “You've got the order wrong. The dome was the birthday gate. The Warden was the other one.” ʸ

“See?” says Tsurugi, triumphantly, to the whole table. “Mostly true.” ᵀˢ

Mau would like very much for the floor to open.

“So you told Dabura,” says Maru, “that he is a king.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“I said *basically* a king,” says Tsurugi. “Basically. There's a difference.” ᵀˢ

“There is no difference,” says Maru, “to Dabura.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Tsurugi's face falls, just slightly.

“The word *king* means something to him,” Maru says. “He has been our king a long time. No one has ever been stronger. When you told him there was someone here—in this world, in a house—who was the strongest, who was a king—” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

He doesn't finish.

“He went quiet,” says Tsurugi. “And then he was gone in the morning.” ᵀˢ

“Yes,” says Maru. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Cross speaks for the first time.

“He's coming,” he says. ᶜʳ

Everyone looks at him.

He's still looking at the table. At his hands. His voice is flat.

“He doesn't care about the house,” says Cross. “He doesn't care about any of you. He cares about one thing. Whether there's anyone in this world stronger than him. And now he's heard there is.” ᶜʳ

He looks up.

Not at Mau. At Yuka.

“He'll come,” Cross says. “And he won't care who's standing next to the king when he does.” ᶜʳ

The table goes very quiet.

Yuka has stopped writing.

She looks at Cross across the table, and something passes over her face that Mau can't read. Then she looks down at the notebook again, and writes something, and doesn't say anything.

Mau's arm tightens round her waist. Just slightly. He doesn't decide to.

She notices. She puts her hand over his, on her stomach, and leaves it there.

“Then we meet him first,” says Maru. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

He sits forward. He puts his hands flat on the table, the way his brother did, but open.

“That's why we came,” he says. “That's what my brother and I want. Not war. We've had enough of falling out of the sky into places that don't want us. We want to talk. But Dabura won't talk to anyone he doesn't respect. And he only respects strength.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

He looks at Mau.

“If you would meet him,” he says. “On your terms. In a place you choose. Before he decides to come here by himself. Then perhaps there can be talking instead of the other thing.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Mau doesn't answer right away.

He thinks about the bright hard point in the north-east. The intent with no word. The way it turned and looked at him.

He thinks about the house. Ori on Beru's shoulder. Rem and Subaru on the back steps. Emilia's paper badge. Beatrice in the window seat.

He thinks about the dot on the calendar, at the bottom, very faint. *Sixty-six.*

He thinks about Yuka in his lap, with her hand over his.

“I'll think about it,” he says. ᴹ

Maru inclines his head.

“That is more than I hoped for,” he says. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

The meeting ends. People get up. Chairs scrape. Rimuru closes his notebook with the face of a ruler who has got through a difficult evening without anyone setting anything on fire.

Yuka doesn't get up.

She stays where she is, in his lap, at the head of the long table, while the room empties round them. She closes her notebook. She puts it down. She turns, sideways, so she can see his face.

“You're going to meet him,” she says. ʸ

It isn't a question.

“Probably,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Somewhere away from the house.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a moment.

“I'm coming,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I promised. Every dungeon.” ʸ

“It's not a dungeon.” ᴹ

“It's a monster who wants to fight you,” says Yuka. “Same thing.” ʸ

She puts her arms round his neck.

She catches his next objection with a kiss. Mau's hand comes up to the back of her head; the other stays at her waist. She has turned far enough across his knees that the notebook starts sliding off her lap. He catches it against the table without leaving her mouth.

Yuka smiles into the kiss. He feels it and kisses her again, slower, until she forgets what she was about to say about the notebook.

“Very diplomatic,” he murmurs. ᴹ

“The talks were boring.” ʸ

“You wrote six pages.” ᴹ

“I had opinions.” ʸ

She takes the book from him and sets it well out of the way. Her palm returns to his jaw. For a moment she studies his face; he waits for whatever she wants to ask.

“Tell me when you decide,” she says. “Before you arrange all of it.” ʸ

“I will.” ᴹ

She presses her mouth to his once more, briefly this time, then settles with her cheek against his neck. He moves his chair back so her feet no longer catch the underside of the table. She takes advantage of the space and curls closer.

“You can stand up,” he says. ᴹ

“I could.” ʸ

He holds her there. He has no particular wish for her to demonstrate it.

Across the empty room, by the door, Cross has stopped.

Maru is already in the hall. Cross was following him. Now he's standing in the doorway with his hand on the frame, looking back.

At the head of the table. At Yuka with her arms round Mau's neck.

Mau sees him. Over her shoulder. He doesn't look away.

Cross doesn't look away either.

Then Cross turns, and goes out, and closes the door behind him, very quietly. Too quietly. The way you close a door when you want to slam it.

Yuka, her face in Mau's neck, doesn't hear it at all.
