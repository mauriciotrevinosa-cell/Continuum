# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 10 — Six Months

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E108
**Continuity state:** B+422 ("65"). Yuka's point of view. Cross asks to talk to her alone, and she says yes without thinking about it. They sit on the orchard wall. He tells her what happened after she vanished: he looked for her; he was shot by a sorcerer while the talks were collapsing and nearly died; he thought she'd died of the tumour somewhere alone. She tells him the truth as canon has it: the operation worked, the tumour came out, and then the coin found her and brought her here. He's the only person in this world who saw her when she was dying and frightened and pretending not to be. She feels something she doesn't have a name for (being *seen*, from before Mau) and decides it's just an old friend. She goes back to Mau and is extra clingy all evening. Mau, who didn't listen, asks nothing, and she doesn't notice that he doesn't. No romance from Yuka; the feeling is Cross's.

*When: B+422. Late morning on the orchard wall, then the evening. Yuka wrote "65" and drew a small dragon beside it, for no reason, because she was in a good mood.*

He asks her at breakfast.

Not in front of everyone. He waits until she gets up to take her plate to the sink, and he gets up too, with his own plate, which he hasn't touched, and stands beside her at the sink, a little too close, the way he always used to stand, because nobody had ever taught him how far away people liked you to be.

“Can we talk,” he says. ᶜʳ

Not a question. Cross never asked questions. He just said things and waited to see what happened.

She looks at him.

He's taller than she remembers. Or maybe she's shorter. It's been years for her; she doesn't know how long it's been for him. His face is the same, though. Hard and closed and not very good at hiding anything, which he thinks he is.

“Sure,” she says. ʸ

She doesn't think about it. Why would she? It's Cross.

She puts her plate down. She looks back across the kitchen to where Mau is sitting, still at the table, with Ori beside him explaining something about the next expedition. He's listening to Ori. He looks up when Yuka looks at him, and she smiles at him, and he smiles back.

“I'll be back in a bit,” she tells him across the room. ʸ

“Okay,” says Mau. ᴹ

She goes out with Cross.

They sit on the orchard wall.

It's the same wall where Mau sat with Yuta, the night after the ring. Old stone, warm in the sun, with the two old trees behind it and the oval of light between them, humming quietly again now, back to the shape she gave it. Something warm in it, somewhere.

Cross sits too close. She moves along a little and rests a hand on the stone between them. He doesn't move closer again.

For a while he doesn't say anything. He looks at the trees. At the oval. At the house.

“You're alive,” he says. ᶜʳ

“You said that when you arrived.” ʸ

“I'm still saying it.” ᶜʳ

He picks at the moss on the wall with one long finger.

“I looked for you,” he says. ᶜʳ

She didn't know that.

She knew he'd been there, at the end, before. She knew he'd come to the hospital with her when she asked him to, because she couldn't ask anyone else, because if she'd asked Tsurugi he'd have known, and if she'd asked Granny it would have been real. Cross didn't know what a hospital was. He didn't know what a tumour was. He sat in the plastic chair in the corridor with his knees up round his ears and listened to the doctor say *six months* and didn't understand any of it except her face.

She remembers his face. Afterward. In the corridor. When she told him what it meant.

She didn't know he'd looked for her.

“When you were gone,” Cross says. “After. You just stopped being anywhere. Your brother didn't know. Your grandparents didn't know. Nobody knew. And I thought—” ᶜʳ

He stops.

“I thought you'd gone somewhere to do it alone,” he says. “The dying. I thought you'd gone somewhere nobody could see, so nobody would have to watch. That's the kind of stupid thing you'd do.” ᶜʳ

Yuka doesn't say anything.

It *is* the kind of stupid thing she'd do. That's the worst part. He's right.

“So I looked,” says Cross. “For a long time. Everywhere. And then the talks happened. My brother's talks. And everything went wrong.” ᶜʳ

He lifts his shirt.

She flinches. She can't help it.

There's a scar on his side. Two of them, close together. Round and puckered and old.

“One of yours,” he says. “A sorcerer. With a gun. Right in the middle of my brother shaking hands with someone.” ᶜʳ

He lets the shirt fall.

“I nearly died,” he says. “I don't remember much of it. And when I woke up I was here, in this world, in the snow, with my brother, and all the others, and I thought: well. That's that. She's dead and I'm here and I'll never know where she went.” ᶜʳ

He looks at her.

“And then your brother started talking about a house,” he says. “And a man who was basically a king. And his *wife*. And he said your name.” ᶜʳ

Yuka looks at her hands.

“I'm sorry,” she says. ʸ

“Don't,” says Cross. ᶜʳ

“I didn't know you'd—” ʸ

“Don't,” he says again. “I don't want sorry. I want to know what happened. How you're alive. You said six months.” ᶜʳ

So she tells him.

She tells him what happened. She and Mau have spent too much time learning how to say difficult things to each other for her to turn the operation into an easy little miracle now.

She tells him about the operation. The one the doctors said probably wouldn't work, that she said yes to anyway, because six months of waiting seemed worse than a chance. She tells him about waking up afterward with her head shaved and bandaged and a doctor at the end of the bed with a face she didn't understand at first, because it was the face of someone who couldn't believe their luck.

“It came out,” she says. “All of it. They got all of it.” ʸ

Cross is very still.

“And then?” he says. ᶜʳ

“And then I went home,” she says. “To recover. To my room. And my ring was across the room, off the cord, on the desk, and I was going to get it. And something—found me. A coin. And I was here.” ʸ

She looks at the house. At the porch.

“And then I was here,” she says again. ʸ

Cross doesn't say anything for a long time.

He looks at her. Really looks. At her face, her hair, which has grown back long, her hands. As if he's checking. As if he's making sure every part of her is still there.

She lets him. She doesn't know why. Nobody looks at her like that. Except—

No. Mau looks at her like that. But differently. Mau looks at her like she's the only colour in the world, which she is, to him. Cross looks at her like she's a ghost that turned out to be real.

“You were so scared,” Cross says. ᶜʳ

She goes still.

“In the corridor,” he says. “After the doctor. You were so scared. You kept laughing. You kept telling me it was fine. You told me it was *fine*, Yuka. Six months. And your hands were shaking so much you couldn't open the door.” ᶜʳ

He looks away.

“I didn't know what a tumour was,” he says. “But I knew what your hands were doing.” ᶜʳ

Something happens in her chest.

She doesn't know what it is. She doesn't have a word for it.

It isn't love. She knows what love is now. Love is Mau carrying her down the stairs when she can walk. Love is the plate with her portion on her side. Love is a tent and a lamp and *it's ugly* and *both*.

This is something else.

This is being *seen*. From before. From when she was the most frightened she'd ever been in her life and pretending not to be, and nobody saw it, nobody at all, not her brother, not her grandparents, nobody, except a boy from another planet who didn't know what a hospital was and sat in a plastic chair with his knees up round his ears.

Nobody has ever seen her like that. From then. Mau never saw her then. Mau didn't know her then. Mau has seen her frightened since, a hundred times, but never *that* frightened. Never when it was her who was dying.

Cross saw.

She looks at him. At his hard closed face, looking at the trees.

“Thank you,” she says. ʸ

He looks back at her, surprised.

“For coming,” she says. “To the hospital. I never said. You were the only one I could ask.” ʸ

Cross doesn't say anything.

But his face does something she's never seen it do. It opens. Just slightly. Just for a moment. Like a door left ajar.

Then it closes again, and he looks at the trees.

She goes back to the house just before lunch.

She feels light. That's the strange thing. She feels light and warm and a little bit wrung out, the way you feel after you cry, except she didn't cry. She walks up through the orchard with the sun on her back, and she decides, on the way, what the feeling is.

*An old friend.* That's all. Someone from before. Someone who knew her when. She's allowed to be glad an old friend is alive. The house has made room for people from everyone's before; she has watched Mau sit through reunions without making anyone explain the whole history first. She can tell him hers too.

That's all it is.

She's very sure.

Mau is on the porch.

He's in the old chair, with a cup of tea, watching Ori and Beru at the training posts. He looks up when she comes round the corner.

He doesn't ask.

She notices that later. Much later. She doesn't notice it now. Now she just sees him, and the warm wrung-out feeling in her chest goes somewhere and turns into something else, something she *does* have a word for, and she walks straight up the porch steps and climbs into his lap and puts her arms round his neck and her face in his shoulder and holds on.

“Hi,” he says. ᴹ

“Hi,” she says, into his shoulder. ʸ

He puts his arms round her. He doesn't ask.

She stays there for a long time.

She's clingy all evening.

She knows she is. She doesn't care. She follows him to the kitchen and sits on the counter while he helps Shuna with the vegetables. She steals a piece of carrot off his board every time he cuts one. At dinner she drags her chair round to his side and puts her knee against his and eats half of everything off his plate. On the sofa afterward she lies across him like a blanket and refuses to move when Umaru wants to change the film.

Mau moves the carrots to her side of the board. She reaches for the next slice before it is ready; he lifts the knife away and gives her the piece with his other hand.

“Predatory,” he says. ᴹ

“Married.” ʸ

At dinner he hooks his ankle behind hers. On the sofa he draws her back when she stretches to reach the blanket, getting it from inventory before she can stand. She kisses his chin in thanks and tucks it around them both.

“You're blocking the subtitles,” Umaru says. ᵁ

They move together, barely, until she can see. Yuka resettles with her face against Mau's shirt. He has laughed with her, caught her hand, brought her closer. She has no reason to think those things cost him anything tonight.

At bedtime, she's lying on his chest in the dark, drawing shapes on his arm.

“Cross told me what happened,” she says. ʸ

“Yeah?” ᴹ

“After I was gone. He looked for me. He got shot. He thought I was dead.” ʸ

Mau's hand stops moving on her back for a second. Then starts again.

“That's a lot,” he says. ᴹ

“It is a lot.” ʸ

She waits a moment before continuing. His hand is moving again, and she takes the small nod beneath her cheek as an invitation to go on.

“He was the only one who came to the hospital,” she says. “Before. When they told me. I never told you that.” ʸ

“No,” says Mau. ᴹ

“He saw me really scared,” she says. “The worst. Before you.” ʸ

Mau doesn't say anything.

She yawns. She settles her head more comfortably on his chest. She's warm and full and sleepy and happy, and she has an old friend back, and her husband's hand is on her back, and everything is fine.

“I'm glad he's alive,” she says. ʸ

“Me too,” says Mau. ᴹ

She falls asleep a minute later.

---

Mau stays awake after her breathing settles. His hand keeps moving on her back, slowly, steadily. Once she stirs and presses closer; he bends his mouth to her hair until she relaxes again.

She doesn't see his face.

*Before you.*

He lies there until morning.
