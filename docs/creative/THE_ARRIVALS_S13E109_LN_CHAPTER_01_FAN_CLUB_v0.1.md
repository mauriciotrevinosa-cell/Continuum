# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 11 — Fan Club

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E109
**Continuity state:** B+424 ("63"). Mau asks Maru everything he knows about Dabura, and Noise, who has been quietly obsessed since the night on the porch, asks through Mau far more questions than are polite. Light that is a technique, and the thing under it that has no word. Gojo comes to listen and joins in. By the end Mau and Noise are openly delighted by a man who wants to kill them, and Yuka calls it a fan club. Mau agrees to meet Dabura, on his terms, at the quarry where he fought Sukuna. Then, at dinner, in front of everyone, Cross asks Yuka to walk with him tomorrow, to see the country he crossed looking for her. She says yes. Mau says it's fine. It's the first "it's fine" of the arc, and nobody but Makima notices it.

*When: B+424. Afternoon on the back porch with Maru, then dinner. Yuka wrote "63" and a question mark, because Mau had told her he'd be asking Maru about Dabura.*

“Light,” says Maru. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

They're on the back porch, out of the wind. Maru on the bench, sitting very upright, the way he always sits, as if he's been told chairs are for humans and is being polite about it. Mau in the old chair across from him. Yuka on the steps with her notebook, pretending not to listen and writing down every word.

“His technique is light,” Maru says. “That is the nearest word. He makes it. He shapes it. It goes where he sends it, faster than anything should go, and what it touches—” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

He stops.

“It doesn't burn,” he says. “It just stops being there.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

*Ask how fast*, says Noise. ᴺ

“How fast?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Maru looks at him.

“Fast,” he says. “I don't have a better word. When we were children, he could cross the valley before the sound of him leaving reached the other side.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

*Ask how much of a valley*, says Noise. ᴺ

* * *

It goes on like that.

For an hour. Maru answers what he can, carefully, choosing every word, and Noise, inside, has a new question for every answer, and Mau asks them, one after another, because he wants to know too. He wants to know very badly.

What the light does to cursed energy. Whether it can be bent. Whether Dabura has to see where it's going or whether he can feel his way. Whether there's a delay, even a small one, between deciding and doing. Whether he gets tired. Whether he gets *bored*.

“Yes,” says Maru, to that one, with feeling. “Very easily.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

*Interesting*, says Noise. ᴺ

“And the other thing,” Mau says. “Under the light. The thing I felt on the porch.” ᴹ

Maru goes quiet.

“We don't have a word for that,” he says at last. “None of us do. It isn't a technique the way the light is. It's—him. What he wants. Made into something that can touch you.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“Intent,” says Mau. ᴹ

Maru looks at him sharply.

“Yes,” he says. “If he wants you dead badly enough, the wanting is a weapon. It reaches you before the light does.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

* * *

Inside him, Noise makes a sound.

He's never heard Noise make that sound before. It's a small, involuntary sound, like someone seeing a very beautiful piece of machinery for the first time.

*Oh*, says Noise. ᴺ

“Noise,” says Mau, under his breath. ᴹ

*It's a technique made out of wanting*, says Noise. *Mau. It's a technique made out of WANTING.* ᴺ

“I know.” ᴹ

*I want to take it apart*, says Noise. *I want to take it apart so badly.* ᴺ

“I know,” says Mau, and realises, with some alarm, that he's smiling. ᴹ

* * *

Gojo arrives somewhere in the second hour.

He doesn't announce himself. He just appears on the railing, cross-legged, with his blindfold pushed up and a bag of sweets, and listens for a while, eating them, and then starts asking his own questions.

His are different. He wants to know what Dabura does with his hands when he's thinking. Whether he blinks. What his feet do before he moves. Whether he talks during fights, and if he does, whether he talks *more* when he's winning or when he's losing.

“Less,” says Maru, frowning, trying to remember. “When he's losing, he goes quiet. When he's winning, he laughs.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“Has he ever lost?” says Gojo. ᴳ

Maru thinks about it.

“Not that I've seen,” he says. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Gojo eats a sweet.

“Then nobody knows what he does when he loses,” he says cheerfully. “That's fun. Isn't that fun?” ᴳ

He looks at Mau.

Mau doesn't say anything. But he finds he's sitting forward in the chair. He finds his hands are clasped very tightly between his knees. He finds that the itch, the old one, the one from the porch before the birthday gate, is back in his fingers.

Gojo grins at him.

* * *

“You two,” says Yuka. ʸ

They all look at her.

She's still on the steps with her notebook. She's been writing for two hours. She looks up at the three of them: Maru upright on his bench, Gojo on the railing with his sweets, Mau sitting forward in the chair with his hands clasped and a look on his face she has seen exactly twice before, both times right before he went into a gate.

“You're a fan club,” she says. ʸ

“We're not—” ᴹ

“You *are*. You've been asking about him for two hours. You asked if he *blinks*.” ʸ

“Gojo asked if he blinks.” ᴹ

“Noise asked how big the valley was,” says Yuka. “I heard. Through the link. Noise was making noises.” ʸ

*I was not making noises*, says Noise. ᴺ

*You were absolutely making noises*, says Ciel. ᶜ

Yuka points her pencil at Mau.

“You think he's *cool*,” she says. ʸ

Mau opens his mouth.

He closes it.

“He's very cool,” says Gojo helpfully. “He's got a technique made of wanting. That's extremely cool. I'd join the fan club.” ᴳ

“There's no fan club,” says Mau. ᴹ

“I'm writing it down,” says Yuka. “*Fan club. Members: Mau, Noise, Gojo.*” ʸ

*Put me down as a reluctant member*, says Ciel. ᶜ

Yuka writes it down.

* * *

Maru, on the bench, is looking at them all with an expression of slow bewilderment.

“He wants to kill you,” he says to Mau. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“I know.” ᴹ

“He is the strongest of my people. He has never lost. He crossed a valley faster than sound when he was a child. He has a technique made of wanting you dead.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“I know,” says Mau. ᴹ

“And you're—*pleased*.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Mau thinks about it. Honestly.

“Not pleased,” he says. “Interested.” ᴹ

Maru looks at Gojo.

Gojo shrugs, and eats another sweet.

“That's how you know he'll be fine,” Gojo says. “The ones who aren't interested are the ones who lose.” ᴳ

* * *

“The quarry,” says Mau. ᴹ

Maru turns back to him.

“If he wants to meet,” Mau says. “On my terms. That's my terms. The quarry, past the hills. Where I fought Sukuna.” ᴹ

Something goes over his face when he says the name. He feels it. He doesn't hide it. Yuka, on the steps, stops writing.

“It's far enough from the house,” he says. “It's already broken. Nothing lives there that can't move. And I know the ground.” ᴹ

He knows the ground very well. He's been under it, in pieces, with his shadows holding him together.

“You'll meet him,” says Maru slowly. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“I'll meet him,” says Mau. “To talk. If he wants to talk.” ᴹ

“He won't want to talk.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“Then I'll meet him for the other thing.” ᴹ

Maru looks at him for a long moment.

Then he stands up, and bows, very formally, the way his people must bow, from the waist, with his hands open.

“I'll send word,” he says. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

* * *

Yuka comes up the steps when he's gone.

She doesn't sit in his lap. She stands in front of the chair and looks down at him, with her notebook against her chest.

“The quarry,” she says. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Where you nearly died.” ʸ

“Where I didn't.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long time.

Then she does sit in his lap. Heavily. All at once. And puts her forehead against his, hard.

“I'm coming,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Every dungeon.” ʸ

“It's not a—” ᴹ

“*Mau*.” ʸ

“Every dungeon,” he agrees. ᴹ

* * *

Cross asks at dinner.

In front of everyone. Mau will think about that later. He doesn't wait until she's at the sink, the way he did before. He just sits across the long table from her, with his plate untouched again, and when there's a gap in the noise, he says, quite loudly:

“Walk with me tomorrow.” ᶜʳ

The table doesn't go quiet. It's too loud for that. But the part of it near them does.

Yuka looks up from her plate.

“Walk where?” she says. ʸ

“North,” says Cross. “The road we came down. There's a ridge a few hours up where you can see all the way to the lake. Where I looked for you. I want to show you.” ᶜʳ

He says it flatly, the way he says everything. But his hands are flat on the table again, and they aren't quite still.

“I walked all of it,” he says. “Looking. I want you to see what I walked.” ᶜʳ

* * *

Yuka hesitates.

Just for a moment. Mau feels it, through the contract. A small pause. And then she turns, and looks at him, sitting beside her with his knee against hers and half her dinner on his plate.

“Can I?” she says to him. ʸ

She's asking. She always asks. That's the rule they have.

Mau looks at her.

He looks at Cross, across the table, with his hands not quite still.

He thinks about the orchard wall. About *before you*. About lying awake until morning.

“It's fine,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Yuka smiles at him.

Bright and easy. She leans over and kisses his cheek, quickly, right there at the table, and then turns back to Cross.

“Okay,” she says. “Tomorrow.” ʸ

Cross nods. Once.

He picks up his fork. For the first time since he came through the gate, he eats.

Yuka goes back to her dinner. She steals a piece of bread off Mau's plate. She leans against his shoulder while she eats it.

Mau eats his dinner.

* * *

At the bottom of the porch steps, after dinner, Makima is sitting where she always sits.

He comes out for air. He doesn't mean to stop. But she looks up at him as he passes, and he stops.

She doesn't ask if she can speak. She just looks at him for a long moment, with her hands folded in her lap.

“You said *it's fine*,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He goes very still.

“You haven't said that in a long time,” says Makima. “Not like that. I felt it. Through the thread.” ᴹᵏ

He doesn't answer.

She doesn't push. She just looks at him, the way the best strategist in her world looks at a board, seeing every piece, and then she looks away, at the yard, at the dark.

“Sit down,” she says. “If you want. You don't have to say anything.” ᴹᵏ

He sits down on the step beside her.

They don't say anything. They sit there in the dark, side by side, until the house goes quiet behind them. And then he goes up to bed, and Yuka is already asleep, curled on his side of the bed with her hand on his pillow, waiting for him.

He gets in carefully, so as not to wake her.

She wakes anyway, just enough to move over and put her head on his chest.

“Tomorrow,” she mumbles. “Back by dinner. Promise.” ʸ

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

She's asleep again before he can say anything else.
