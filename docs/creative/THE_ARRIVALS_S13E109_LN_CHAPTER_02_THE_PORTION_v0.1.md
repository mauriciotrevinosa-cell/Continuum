# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 12 — The Portion

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S13E109
**Continuity state:** B+425 ("62"). The day of Cross's walk. Mau spends it at home doing ordinary things and not thinking about it. Yuka comes back at dinner, as promised, glowing, still in the middle of a story she and Cross were telling on the road, and sits down across the table beside Cross to finish it. She doesn't come round to Mau's side. She doesn't steal anything. Mau, who once said the only thing that would disappoint him is if she stopped stealing off his plate (V80 #6), sets her portion aside on her side of his plate the way he always does, leaves it untouched, and walks out of dinner without a word. Shuna sees the plate. Once Mau is out of sight, the pressure he's been carrying turns into a panic attack. Makima feels it through their bond and stays beside him until he can speak. He calls her his friend and offers his hand. She tells him what it meant to have everyone come when she called and no one sit with her by choice. Yuka sees him only after he has returned. No intimate scene.

*When: B+425. Morning to night. Yuka wrote "62" and, beside it, "back by dinner", underlined.*

She's gone before he's properly awake.

Not gone. Getting ready. He opens his eyes to the sound of her boots on the floor and her moving round the room in the half light, finding things, putting them in a small bag. Water. Bread. The middle jacket.

She sees he's awake. She comes over and sits on the edge of the bed and bends down and kisses him on the forehead.

“Back by dinner,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Promise.” ʸ

“I know, Yuka.” ᴹ

She smiles and bends to kiss his forehead again. He catches her hand before she can get up.

“Missed,” he says. ᴹ

She looks down at him, then at his mouth.

“Did I?” ʸ

This kiss lands where he wanted. She gives him another before he can complain about the first one being too short. When she finally stands, she keeps hold of his fingers until the bed makes her let go.

“Dinner,” she says again, softly. ʸ

He nods and watches her go.

He lies there and listens to her go down the stairs. The front door. Two sets of footsteps on the porch, and then on the path, and then nothing.

He doesn't think about it.

That's the plan. He has a lot of plans, and that's the main one. He gets up and makes the bed. He goes down to breakfast and eats it. He helps Mai lift the last beam into place on the second floor, holding it with Authority while she builds the joint round it with her Construction, and she thanks him in her way, which is to say *don't move* fourteen times.

He doesn't think about it.

He takes Ori and Beru to the orchard after lunch and lets Ori explain the second expedition again, all of it, with the map. He sits with Frieren for an hour in her room and reads three pages of the grimoire she keeps for him while Beatrice watches him from the window seat with deep suspicion. He goes down to the clinic with nothing wrong. Shoko looks up from the cupboard and points at the kettle with her chin.

“If you're visiting, make yourself useful.” ˢʰ

He fills it. She goes back to counting clean dressings. By the time she sits down, he has two cups on her desk and the lid back on the tin.

She opens it again.

“That's the one Satoru said he finished.” ˢʰ

“He did.” ᴹ

“And you replaced it.” ˢʰ

“You like that one.” ᴹ

“I liked knowing who to be annoyed with.” She shuts the tin. “Now he'll think it refills.” ˢʰ

“It does. Bad design.” ᴹ

She laughs into her cup. For a little while he can sit with his mother while she complains about the cupboard Gojo never closes, and have an answer that doesn't require arranging his whole face first.

When she asks whether he slept, he looks down at the cup. She draws the tin back across the desk and stays with him while it steeps.

He doesn't think about it at all.

At four, the sun moves round to the north side of the house, and he finds he's standing at the window looking up the north road.

He doesn't remember deciding to.

*It's a long way to the ridge*, says Ciel, quietly. *Four hours there. Four back. If they left at dawn, they'll be on the way down.* ᶜ

“I wasn't asking.” ᴹ

*I know*, says Ciel. ᶜ

He goes and helps Shuna with the vegetables.

She's back at dinner.

Exactly at dinner. He hears the door as Shuna is putting the plates out. Two voices in the hall, one of them laughing. Hers.

She comes into the kitchen with her cheeks pink from the wind and her hair everywhere and her whole face lit up, the way it lights up after something good. Cross behind her. Cross, who has spent every meal since he arrived with his plate untouched and his jaw tight, is—not smiling. But something near it.

“We made it,” says Yuka, to the room. “Back by dinner. I *said*.” ʸ

She's talking before she's finished sitting down. She's in the middle of a story. Something about a stream on the way up, and a log across it, and Cross insisting it would hold him and it not holding him, and Mau can tell from the way she's telling it that she's told half of it already, on the road, and is in a hurry to get to the end.

She sits down.

Across the table. Beside Cross. In the empty chair there, because that's where she was standing when she sat, because she's in the middle of the story and he's in the story and she's turning to him to say *tell them what you said when you went in*.

Cross tells them.

The table laughs.

Yuka laughs hardest.

Mau looks at the chair beside him.

It's empty. He'd moved it, before she came in. Six inches closer. The way she always moves it. He didn't think about doing it; his hands did it while Shuna was putting the dishes out.

She doesn't come round.

She doesn't look round. She's leaning toward Cross, telling the next part, the part where they got to the top of the ridge and you could see all the way to the lake, all of it, the whole road Cross walked, looking for her.

“You can see the whole road from up there,” she's saying. “We took all morning to walk a piece of it and from the ridge it looked like nothing. There was a bit where the wind was coming so hard off the lake I couldn't get my lunch out of the bag.” ʸ

Mau looks at his plate.

It's a good dinner. Shuna made the small sharp pickles. The ones he likes and pretends not to.

He moves them to the side of the plate nearest Yuka's chair. The empty one. He doesn't decide to. He's done it every meal for a long time. His hands do it.

He takes another spoonful of stew. The chair still has room for her.

Across the table, Yuka is laughing at something Tsurugi said. She's eating her own dinner. Off her own plate. Bread, and the stew, and the little sharp pickles from her own portion, which she eats without looking, the way you eat something that's just there.

She doesn't reach across. She doesn't steal anything. She doesn't need to. She isn't next to him.

He looks at the pickles, on the side of his plate nearest the empty chair.

*Never*, he said, in the dark, in their bed, with her laughing into his shoulder. *Unless you stop stealing food off my plate. Then I'd be very disappointed.*

It was a joke.

It was a *joke*.

He puts his fork down.

He doesn't say anything. He doesn't make a sound. He just pushes his chair back, very quietly, and gets up, and leaves the plate where it is: half eaten, with the small sharp pickles set aside on the far edge, untouched, in a little pile, the way he always leaves them.

He walks out of the kitchen.

Nobody stops him. Nobody really notices. The table is loud, and Tsurugi is telling a story about the road now, and Milim is demanding to know whether the log was a real log or a magic log, and Yuka is laughing so hard she has to hold onto Cross's arm.

She doesn't see him go.

Shuna sees the plate.

She's clearing. She comes to his place with the stack in her arms and stops. Looks down. Half a dinner. And the pickles, set aside on the edge nearest the empty chair, in a small neat pile, the way they've been set aside at every meal for longer than she can remember.

She looks at the empty chair beside it.

She looks across the table, at Yuka, laughing, holding Cross's arm.

Shuna starts to call his name, then looks toward the back door. He has already gone beyond it. She picks up the plate very carefully, so the little pile doesn't fall over, and carries it to the counter, and stands there for a moment with her back to the room, looking at it.

Then she covers it with a cloth, and puts it to one side, and doesn't throw it away.

He makes it as far as the wall past the training posts. He'd meant to sit there in the cold for a minute. There's a good flat stone with enough room for his elbows. He can see it. He puts one hand down on it and suddenly can't get enough air to do anything else.

His shirt pulls against his sternum. He gets two fingers under the collar. The cloth comes free and he's still dragging at it. The pressure goes deeper than the cloth. He waits for the part where it lets up and instead his other hand goes numb against the wall.

For an instant there is grit on his tongue and something warm under the ribs. His body remembers bearing down on it with both hands. His eyes can give him every piece of the wall. A flake of mortar. The little cold gap under the capstone. There's grass caught in it from the last time someone cut the yard. He can see it all and he can't make the grass more real than the pressure in his hands.

He tries to say Ciel's name. What comes out is one broken breath.

*Here*, says Ciel. ᶜ

He hears her. There is something terrible about how relieved he is to hear her, and he can't tell her why.

*Lower*, says Noise. The yard softens by degrees. Mau can still see the capstone but he no longer has to see through it. ᴺ

Then Makima is at the far end of the wall. She rounds it, checks where his hand is and stops in front of him. Her own breath comes in short. He knows she's feeling some of his and still can't quite understand how she got here from the other side of the house.

She puts one hand on the stone. There's a gap between her hand and the one he can no longer feel.

“Can I stay here?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

He nods. It does very little to his head and it must be enough because she sits against the wall, with her knees drawn up. The yard is dark here. From the kitchen all anyone would see is the capstone and the tops of the posts.

Someone starts laughing inside. His chest tightens again. Makima doesn't ask him to explain it. When his legs give a little, she moves her feet and makes room for him to get down next to her. He goes the last few inches hard and she steadies his elbow long enough for him to find the ground.

“That one went all the way out,” she says a while later. ᴹᵏ

He doesn't know what she means until the next breath leaves him without catching. Nothing else happens for a bit. The kitchen quiets and starts up again. He leans his head back far enough to touch the wall and finds that he can feel the cold through his hair.

“Did you feel all that?” he asks. The words are thin but they're words. ᴹ

“Enough to come looking for you.” ᴹᵏ

She gives him time. He goes on sitting with his palms open on his knees until the feeling comes back in his fingertips. When he can put a sentence together, the one he gets out has practically nothing to do with the part that frightened him.

“It was a joke,” he says. ᴹ

She waits.

“The plate,” he says. “I told her once. In bed. The only thing that'd disappoint me is if she stopped stealing off my plate. It was a joke. She laughed.” ᴹ

He looks at his hands.

“And she didn't,” he says. “Tonight. She sat over there and ate her own and didn't even look. And it's *stupid*. It's a stupid thing to—it's food. It's pickles. She had a good day. She came back by dinner. She *promised* and she did it.” ᴹ

“Yes,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“So it's fine.” ᴹ

Makima doesn't say anything.

She doesn't have to. He can feel her not saying it, the way you can feel someone not looking at you.

“It's not fine,” he says, much more quietly. ᴹ

“No,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

They sit there a long time.

The house is lit up behind them. Loud. He can hear it from here: the long table, the laughing, someone starting a song and someone else telling them to stop. All the warmth of it, through the windows, falling across the dark yard in long bars.

He's on the outside of it. He knows he did that. He walked out.

He did that once before. A long time ago. He doesn't remember it the way he remembers things now, clearly; he remembers it the way you remember a room you lived in as a child. Moving his cup out of a room. His clothes. A few books. Because someone new had come, and he thought the room should be theirs now, and he thought he was being kind.

He was wrong then. He knows that. Frieren told him, much later.

He doesn't know if he's wrong now.

“You're a good friend,” he says. ᴹ

He says it without looking at her. Casually. The way you'd say *it's cold* or *the stars are out*. He doesn't think about it.

She stops moving beside him, their shoulders almost level against the wall.

“I was glad when I saw you,” he tells her. “Before I knew what you were going to do. I think I would've been glad if you just sat there.” ᴹ

“I did just sit there,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He looks at her. She looks like she's waiting to hear if sitting there was enough.

“Yeah,” he says. “You did.” ᴹ

He holds out his hand. Sideways. Without looking. The way you'd hold your hand out to a friend at the end of something, to shake on it.

“Thanks,” he says. ᴹ

Makima looks at his hand for a long time.

Then she takes it.

Not with her fingers through his. Not the way Yuka holds his hand. Palm to palm, thumb over the back of his hand, firm. The way you'd shake hands with someone on a deal you both meant. And then she doesn't let go. She keeps holding it, like that, between them on the wall, a friend's hand.

“Before you, I'd never had one,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He looks at her.

“A friend,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

She's looking at the dark. Her face, in the bars of light from the house, is very still.

“In my world,” she says, “I had everyone. Everyone did what I said. Everyone came when I called. Every person I ever met, I could make into something I needed. And I did. All of them.” ᴹᵏ

She pauses.

“I had everyone,” she says, “and I didn't have anyone. Not one. Not one person who sat on a wall with me because they wanted to.” ᴹᵏ

Her hand tightens, very slightly, on his.

“So I don't know what a good friend is,” she says. “I'm learning. From watching. From you.” ᴹᵏ

He doesn't say anything.

He doesn't have to. He holds her hand, like that, a friend's handshake, on the wall at the bottom of the yard, and looks at the dark with her, and lets the house be loud behind them.

After a while, she says, still not looking at him:

“You should go back in. Before she notices.” ᴹᵏ

“She won't notice.” ᴹ

“She will,” says Makima. “Eventually. And when she does, it would be better if you were in the house.” ᴹᵏ

He thinks about that.

Then he lets go of her hand and gets up. In the time it takes him to get his legs under him, there's a little water from the grass on the side of his trousers. He brushes it off without getting much of it. By the time he crosses the yard, he can get to the door on an ordinary breath.

Yuka is on the sofa when he comes in.

She's lying across it with her head on a cushion, still talking to Tsurugi about the ridge. She sees him in the doorway and her whole face lights up and she holds out her arms.

“There you are,” she says. “Come here. Tsurugi doesn't believe you can see the whole lake from up there.” ʸ

He goes there.

She pulls him down onto the sofa and arranges herself across him, the way she always does, her head on his chest and her legs over his and her hand on the line on his arm. Warm and happy and tired from the road. She tells him about the lake. All of it. How big it was, and how it caught the light, and how far you could see.

Her hair smells like the wind. He puts his hand on her back and listens, testing how much pressure he needs to keep it still. After a while she settles her thumb over his wrist and goes on telling him about the lake. Her feet have gone cold. He pulls the blanket down to cover them.

In the kitchen, Shuna puts the covered plate away.
