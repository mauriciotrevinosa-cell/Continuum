# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 13 — Someone Has To

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S13E110
**Continuity state:** B+426–429 ("61"–"58"). Yuka trains with Cross and begins spending more of her days with him; her affection for Mau remains easy at bedtime. Mau follows through on cooking with Shuna. Rem asks him for help picking a song and Umaru keeps him for a movie. They make room for him without asking him to account for his feelings. Ori notices his empty chair and sits with him at a different dinner. She tries one of his pickles and Mau tells her she doesn't have to eat food she hates to keep him company. She asks if everything is okay. "A lot in my head." "The duel?" "Partly." Ori leaves him a note under his cup the next morning.

*When: B+426 to the morning of B+429. Yuka writes "61", "60", "59" and then "58", one each morning.*

The notebook is on the windowsill when he wakes up.

Open. Today's page. *61*, in her neat small letters, and beside it, a tiny drawing of a stream with a log across it, half sunk.

She's already gone downstairs. He can hear her in the kitchen, laughing.

He looks at the number for a long time. Then he closes the notebook and puts it on her side of the bed, where she'll find it, and goes down.

She's in the yard all morning.

With Cross. He sees them from the porch. She's showing him the training posts. Then the orchard, and the two old trees, and the oval of light between them that she gave its shape. Then the Fort, where Ori is lying in wait with a stick and demands to know if he's friend or foe, and Cross looks at Yuka, completely lost, and Yuka laughs so hard she has to sit down on the grass.

He watches her laugh.

He's not sure what his face is doing. He makes it stop doing whatever it is and drinks his tea.

In the afternoon she trains with him.

Cross has never seen Ten Shadows. Not properly. In her world he'd only seen her once, he tells the yard, at the end, when everything was going wrong; just the white dog, once, very fast, before she was gone. He asks to see it. All of it.

So she shows him.

The dogs, both of them. The toad. The others, one at a time, out of her shadow and back. Cross watches every one with the same expression Noise had on the porch with Maru: a kind of hungry wonder.

Then he asks to fight one.

She lets him.

Mau watches from the porch as the black dog goes for Cross and Cross meets it with his bare hands, long-limbed and wrong-jointed and very fast, and throws it, and it lands and turns and comes back, and Cross laughs hard enough to lose his grip. The dog takes advantage of it.

Yuka laughs too.

“Again,” says Cross. ᶜʳ

“*Again*,” says Yuka, delighted. ʸ

She doesn't look at the porch.

At night she's exactly the same.

That's the thing he can't make fit. He lies in bed and she comes up after him and climbs in and finds him in the dark the way she always does, her head on his chest, her hand on the line on his arm, her cold feet on his shins. She tells him about the day. The Fort. Ori and the stick. Cross throwing the black dog.

“He's never seen anyone use their shadow like that,” she says. “His people don't do anything like it. He was so *interested*.” ʸ

“Mm.” ᴹ

“You'd like him. If you talked to him.” ʸ

“Maybe.” ᴹ

She kisses his chest, sleepily, through his shirt.

“I love you,” she says. ʸ

“I love you,” he says. ᴹ

She sounds like herself. She kisses his chest once more and settles against him. He has no reason to call those words a lie. He still cannot make them fit the day he has spent watching her from the porch.

He could ask her to stay awake a little longer. The question reaches his mouth. He kisses her hair instead.

Everything is fine.

She falls asleep. He lies awake.

The next day he's at the workshop when he smells dough warming near the stove. He takes the pencil from behind his ear and sets it next to Mai's level. She asks where he's going until she catches the smell too.

“Focaccia,” she says. “Bring a corner.” ᴹᵃ

“You know I promised to help make dinner. It's still at the stage where it isn't bread.” ᴹ

“Then I can expect a *warm* corner.” ᴹᵃ

In the kitchen, Shuna has the bowls laid out for the work she'd meant to do after putting the dough in. Mau washes his hands and asks how much of the afternoon she wants back.

“What's the offer?” she says. ˢʰᵘ

He puts a deep tray from the Shop on the counter. The paper over it smells of garlic and pepper. Shuna lifts a corner and looks underneath.

“Is it good?” ˢʰᵘ

“I was hoping you'd tell me. I thought we could keep the bread and do it in the same oven.” ᴹ

There's chicken in the tray, already braised. Shuna puts a spoon into the sauce before he can find her a plate. She tastes it twice. Then she opens the icebox, pushes the covered bowl of cut peppers toward him, and asks him to put those in too.

“Is that a yes?” ᴹ

“Put the peppers in.” ˢʰᵘ

He does. When she goes out to get rosemary, he keeps the oven door cracked with a folded cloth and watches for the edges to start catching. She's back with the rosemary and half the people she'd gone outside intending to eat with. They fit round the kitchen counter because there's no point in taking the bread to the table at the rate Mai is tearing it apart.

Yuka has paint on her thumb from touching up the Fort sign. It gets on the handle of Mau's serving spoon when she takes it from him. He holds out his apron for her to wipe it off. She takes the apron strings and pulls him in first.

“I was coming to get you,” she says. ʸ

“For what?” ᴹ

“I thought we'd all eat outside.” ʸ

She has a piece of bread in her free hand. She splits it and gives him the end with the good crust. They get as far as the back step with their plates before Ori calls Yuka over to find a banner pole, and Mau gets up to make room for someone coming down the steps. By the time he sits again, it is three places farther along. He can see Yuka still tearing bits off the bread he gave her. He can't hear what she's saying.

Rem sits down beside him with two folded pieces of paper on her plate.

“Can you listen to something for me after dinner?” she asks. ᴿᵉ

He lifts his plate to make room for the paper. She wants to know which of two arrangements she could ask the girls to try at the next practice. When they go to listen she gets him to sit with his eyes shut. He picks the one where he can hear the smaller voice all the way to the end. Rem plays that one again so he can tell her which bit lost him the first time.

When he gets up to go, Umaru has his place on the other sofa covered in a blanket.

“It's a very good movie,” she tells him, moving over just enough. ᵁ

“Rem already told me it was terrible.” ᴹ

“Rem doesn't have my commentary.” ᵁ

He sits down. In the first ten minutes she tells him which man has stolen the car, stops it to show him what the car looked like before it was stolen, and asks if he can get her a soda while it's paused anyway. He produces two. The second makes her look at him with a gratifying amount of suspicion.

“I'm having one,” he explains. ᴹ

“I was going to give you one.” ᵁ

“You were going to let me hear you open it.” ᴹ

She hits play and makes it almost to the end of the car chase before asking him how much he thinks a good custom horn costs. From the other end of the sofa, Rem says she left the room to get away from this exact question. Mau slides her the second soda. She takes a sip and gives it back to him without looking away from the screen. Yuta comes in during the second chase and pulls up a chair. He takes the glass of water Mau offers without asking a second time who Mau was looking for at the door.

The day after, he goes through the next part of the grimoire with Frieren and Beatrice. Beatrice has put a finger over the word Frieren just read.

“That means *return*, I suppose.” ᴮᵉᵃ

“In the margin,” Frieren says. “The line in the body is *retain*.” ᶠ

Mau turns the page toward the window. There are two hands in it. The later writer has squeezed an instruction between the original lines.

“So it says to keep the spell here and send the answer back?” he asks. ᴹ

The two of them look at the page.

“That,” Beatrice says, “is what I said.” ᴮᵉᵃ

“It wasn't,” Frieren says. ᶠ

Mau keeps his head bent until the small smile is safe. Beatrice borrows his pencil without asking and writes her reading on the loose sheet, not in the old book. Frieren moves her cup before the cuff reaches it.

By the time Fern calls them downstairs, Mau has read three lines properly. He leaves a mark where he can start tomorrow, and Beatrice adds a very small correction to it. He takes Ori and Beru to the river to plan expedition two.

When Rimuru asks about the next talks, he gives him the date and the names. When Rimuru stays to ask about him, he says *it's fine*.

He says *it's fine* when Shoko asks how he's sleeping.

Ciel asks if he wants her to keep the sense turned down when he comes in for dinner. He says he can manage. A minute later he asks her to keep it where Noise left it the other night. She does. He means to thank her aloud and only gets as far as thinking it.

Ori notices other things.

He doesn't know when. That's the thing about Ori; you never know when she starts noticing. She doesn't say anything. She just watches. At breakfast. In the yard. At the long table. Her eyes going from her father to Yuka, at the far end, and back.

On the third night, at dinner, Yuka sits at the far end again.

She comes in with Cross and Maru, comparing the places they'd considered for the river crossing. One of the drawings is already spread across the far end of the table. Yuka catches Mau's eye, blows him a kiss and goes over to stop Cross putting his wet cup on Rimuru's notes.

The chair beside him is empty.

He's moved it six inches closer. His hands did it.

He looks at it.

Ori gets up from her place beside Frieren.

She doesn't say anything. She walks round the table, past Gojo, past Milim, past Umaru arguing with Senku about something, and stops at the empty chair beside her father.

She looks at it. She looks at him.

Then she pulls it out, and climbs up onto it, and shuffles it, with enormous effort, six more inches closer, until her knee is against his.

She sits.

She looks at his plate.

It's grilled fish, with a spoonful of rice and two of the little sharp pickles left at the bottom of the jar. He could have finished them while he was waiting for his fish. He moved them to the edge instead.

Ori looks at the pickles.

She looks up at him.

Then she reaches over, very deliberately, and takes one. And eats it. Looking straight ahead, at nothing, with an expression of perfect innocence.

He looks down at her.

She doesn't look at him. She takes another one.

She doesn't even like them. He knows she doesn't. She makes a face every time Shuna puts them out. She's making the face now, very slightly, chewing, trying not to.

She reaches for the bread and eats half a slice standing up on the rung of her chair. He hears himself laugh. She has a hand held under her chin to stop him seeing her chew fast enough to get rid of the taste.

Ori hears it. She looks up at him, finally. Pleased with herself.

“They're horrible,” she informs him, quietly. ᴼ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm eating them anyway.” ᴼ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Someone has to.” ᴼ

He puts his arm round her. She leans against his side, small and warm and making a face at the taste of pickles, and they eat their dinner like that, the two of them, while the table is loud round them, and he lets himself have it. Just this. Just his daughter, at his side, stealing off his plate.

He doesn't look at the far end of the table.

For a while, he doesn't need to.

After dinner she stays.

Everyone else gets up. Chairs scrape. The table empties toward the sofas and the fire. Ori stays in the chair beside him, swinging her legs, with her hand still in his sleeve.

“Dad,” she says. ᴼ

“Mm.” ᴹ

“Is everything okay?” ᴼ

He looks at her.

She's looking up at him with Frieren's eyes. Frieren's patience. Frieren's way of asking a question and then just waiting, for as long as it takes, for the real answer.

He could say *it's fine*. He's been saying it for three days. It's right there.

He can't. Not to her.

“I've got a lot in my head,” he says. ᴹ

Ori nods slowly, as if this is a perfectly sensible answer.

“Is it the duel?” she asks. ᴼ

She means the meeting at the quarry. Everybody in the house knows by now. Maru's word came this morning: Dabura will come, in his own time, and he won't say when. Ori has been asking about it all day.

He thinks about it.

“Partly,” he says. ᴹ

Ori looks at him for a long moment.

She doesn't ask what the other part is. She doesn't look at the far end of the table, where Yuka is on the sofa now with Tsurugi and Cross, laughing. She just looks at him.

Then she nods again. Very seriously. Like a general receiving a report.

“Okay,” she says. ᴼ

She slides down off the chair. She stands in front of him, and reaches up, and hugs him, hard, round the neck, the way she hugs people she's decided about.

Then she goes to find Frieren.

On B+429, there's a note under his cup.

He finds it when he picks the cup up. A small square of paper, folded twice, in Ori's careful handwriting. The same paper she uses for the *welcome home* notes he keeps on the bedside table.

He unfolds it.

*Dad.*

*You don't have to say what the other part is. But you can if you want. I'm good at listening. Mama says so.*

*Also next time it should be something I like. I thought that over.*

*Ori.*

*P.S. They are still horrible.*

He reads it twice before Ori comes in with Frieren. He catches her sleeve as she passes and bends to kiss the top of her head.

“You don't have to keep eating something you hate,” he says. ᴹ

“I said I would.” ᴼ

“I like having you beside me. You can have the bread.” ᴹ

She considers the exchange with appropriate seriousness, then takes a piece from his plate. He holds it steady while she tears off the end. Frieren stays beside her chair, watching him thank their daughter without handing her the rest of what is in his head.

He folds the note carefully and puts it in his pocket. Not on the bedside table with the others. In his pocket. Where he can feel it.

Across the kitchen, Yuka is at the window with her tea, talking to Cross about the orchard. She sees Mau and smiles at him, bright and easy, and blows him a kiss.

He smiles back.

He keeps his hand in his pocket, on the note, the whole time.

Upstairs, on the windowsill, the notebook is open to today's page.

*58.*
