# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 14 — What Everyone Sees

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S13E110
**Continuity state:** B+429 ("58"). Yuka's point of view. Mai corners her in the new upstairs workshop: she's swapping Mau for Cross, and everyone can see it. Maki, blunter, joins in; Yuta, gentler, tries to explain what the house is seeing (the chair, the plate Shuna kept, Ori eating pickles she hates). The house understands both of them: they also say Mau should have said something and is falling back into old habits, but when could anyone have sat him down? Yuka digs in: Cross is a friend, Mau said "it's fine", she's done nothing wrong. Her anger is terror, and also something else she won't name: feelings she never let herself have about being seen at her worst. She leaves and goes straight to Mau and clings to him; he asks nothing. She doesn't notice Ori's note in his pocket.

*When: B+429. Mid-morning, upstairs in the finished second floor of the workshop. Yuka wrote "58" and nothing else; she was late, because Cross had asked to see the dogs again before breakfast.*

Mai asks her up to see the new floor.

That's what she says, anyway. She comes and finds Yuka in the yard, where she's been showing Cross the white dog again, and says *come and see it, it's finished*, in a voice that doesn't leave much room. So Yuka goes.

It's lovely.

She says so. It is. The stairs on the side wall by the window, where Momo said they should be. The windows bigger than the drawings, because Momo said that too. The light coming in across a clean new floor, and the smell of fresh wood, and Mai's workbench already up here by the window with her tools laid out the way she likes them. A second floor built out of nothing, out of Mai's hands and Mau's reserves.

“It's beautiful,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Sit down,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks at her.

Mai is standing by the bench with her arms crossed. She hasn't looked at the floor once since they came up.

“I didn't bring you up here for the floor,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

* * *

Yuka sits down on the bench.

She doesn't know why her heart has started going faster. It's Mai. It's the workshop. Nothing's wrong.

“What,” she says. ʸ

Mai doesn't sit. She stays standing, arms crossed, looking down at her.

“You're swapping him,” she says. ᴹᵃ

Yuka stares at her.

“What?” ʸ

“Mau. For Cross.” Mai says it flatly, the way she says *don't move* when she's building. “Everyone can see it. The whole house. You're swapping your husband for your old friend, and you can't see it, and he won't say anything, and I'm tired of watching.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

For a moment, Yuka can't breathe.

Then she can, and it comes out as a laugh. A short, sharp, wrong-sounding laugh.

“That's ridiculous,” she says. ʸ

“Is it,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

“I'm not *swapping* anyone. Cross is my *friend*. He came all this way. He looked for me for *months*. I'm allowed to—I'm showing him the house. That's all. That's all it is.” ʸ

“You sat with him at dinner,” says Mai. “Three nights out of four.” ᴹᵃ

“Because he doesn't know anyone else—” ʸ

“He knows his brother. He knows Tsurugi. He knows Subaru. He walked here with all of them.” ᴹᵃ

“That's not—” ʸ

“And you didn't steal anything off Mau's plate,” says Mai. “Not once. Three nights.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

Yuka goes very still.

“How do you know about that,” she says. ʸ

Mai looks at her.

“Everybody knows about that,” she says. “Everybody in this house has watched you steal off his plate for two years. It's the most obvious thing in the world. And Shuna found his plate the night you came back from the walk, with your pickles set aside on your side, where they always are. Untouched. He'd left the table. You didn't see him go.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka opens her mouth.

“She kept it,” says Mai. “The plate. Under a cloth. She didn't know what else to do with it.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

There are footsteps on the new stairs.

Maki. And behind her, Yuta. Maki comes up the last step and looks at the two of them, at Mai standing and Yuka on the bench, and doesn't look at the floor either.

“You told her,” Maki says to Mai. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I told her,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

“Good.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Maki comes and stands beside Mai. Yuta stays at the top of the stairs, with one hand on the rail, the way he stands in doorways when he's not sure he should come in.

Yuka looks from one of them to the other.

“You *planned* this,” she says. ʸ

“Nobody planned anything,” says Maki. “We just all saw the same thing at the same time and nobody else was going to say it.” ᴹᵃᵏ

* * *

“Granny—” ʸ

“Ori's been eating pickles,” says Maki. ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka stops.

“Every night,” says Maki. “Since the night after the walk. She takes the chair beside him. Your chair. She moves it closer. And she eats the pickles off his plate. She hates them. She makes a face every time. She does it anyway.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka didn't know that.

She was at the other end of the table. She didn't see.

“A child,” says Maki, “is filling in for you. At dinner. And you didn't notice.” ᴹᵃᵏ

* * *

Yuka stands up.

She doesn't decide to. She's just standing, with her hands in fists at her sides, and her heart going so fast she can hear it in her ears.

“He said it was *fine*,” she says. ʸ

Her voice is too loud for the room. She doesn't care.

“I *asked* him,” she says. “Before the walk. At dinner. In front of everyone. I asked *can I* and he said *it's fine*. He said it. I didn't do anything without asking. I never do anything without asking. That's our rule.” ʸ

Maki looks at her for a long time.

“Mau says everything's fine,” she says quietly. “When has that ever meant fine?” ᴹᵃᵏ

* * *

Yuta speaks from the top of the stairs.

“He should have said something,” he says. ʸᵗ

They all look at him.

He comes the rest of the way up. Slowly. He stands a little apart from his wife, nearer to his granddaughter.

“I mean it,” says Yuta. “He's not blameless. He should have told you. He told you everything, after the gate. He promised. And now he's gone back to *it's fine* and putting things in his pocket and lying awake. We can all see that too. He's falling back into the old habits. The ones from before you.” ʸᵗ

Yuka stares at him.

“But Yuka,” says Yuta gently. “When could he have told you?” ʸᵗ

* * *

She opens her mouth.

“You're with Cross all day,” says Yuta. “In the yard. In the orchard. On the walk. And at night you're so happy, and so tired, and you curl up on him and tell him about your day. All of it. About Cross. And then you fall asleep. When was he supposed to find the gap?” ʸᵗ

“He could have—” ʸ

“He could have,” Yuta agrees. “He should have. I'm not saying he shouldn't. I'm saying we can all see why he didn't.” ʸᵗ

He looks at her with an expression she's seen on his face before. When she was small. When she'd done something she didn't understand yet was wrong.

“We see both of you,” he says. “That's all. We see him not saying it, and we see you not seeing it. And we love you both. And we're worried.” ʸᵗ

* * *

The room is very quiet.

Yuka stands in the middle of the new floor, in the light from the big windows Momo said should be bigger, with her hands in fists.

She's shaking. She can feel it.

She doesn't know why she's shaking. She isn't frightened. There's nothing to be frightened of. Her husband is downstairs and he loves her and she loves him and she's done nothing wrong.

Cross is her friend.

Cross saw her in the corridor, with her hands shaking too much to open the door.

Nobody else saw that. Nobody. Not Granny. Not Grandpa. Not Tsurugi. Not Mau, who didn't know her then, who has seen her frightened a hundred times since but never like that, never when she was the one dying.

That's all it is. Someone who saw. She's allowed to have someone who saw.

She's allowed.

* * *

“You're wrong,” she says. ʸ

Her voice cracks in the middle. She doesn't care about that either.

“All of you. You're *wrong*. I'm not swapping anyone. I love him. He *knows* I love him. I tell him every night. He's my *husband*.” ʸ

“We know,” says Maki. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Then *stop*.” ʸ

Maki doesn't say anything.

Mai looks at the floor.

Yuta looks at her with that face.

Yuka turns round, and goes down the new stairs, too fast, nearly missing the bottom one, and out of the workshop, and across the yard.

* * *

She doesn't go to Cross.

She'll remember that, later, and hold onto it. She doesn't go to Cross. She goes straight to the porch, where Mau is in the old chair with his tea, watching Ori and Beru at the posts, and she climbs into his lap without a word, and puts her arms round his neck, and her face in his shoulder, and holds on so tightly it must hurt.

He puts his arms round her.

“Hey,” he says. ᴹ

She doesn't answer.

She's shaking. She knows he can feel it. She waits for him to ask.

He doesn't.

He just holds her. His hand on her back, moving slowly, steadily, the way it always does. He doesn't ask what happened. He doesn't ask why she's shaking. He doesn't ask anything at all.

She's grateful for that. She tells herself she's grateful.

She doesn't notice that he's stopped asking things.

* * *

Later, when she's stopped shaking, she sits up a little.

“Do you think I spend too much time with Cross?” she says. ʸ

She means it lightly. She makes it sound light. She looks at his face to see what it does.

His face doesn't do anything.

“You're catching up,” he says. “It's been a long time. It's fine.” ᴹ

There it is again. *Fine*.

She hears it this time. She does. Just for a moment, she hears it, the way Maki said it in the workshop: *when has that ever meant fine*. And something cold goes through her.

Then he smiles at her, the small one, the real one, the one she knows.

And she lets the cold thing go.

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

She puts her head back on his shoulder.

* * *

He has his other hand in his pocket.

She doesn't notice that. She doesn't notice the shape of something folded under his fingers: a small square of paper, the kind Ori uses for notes, that he hasn't taken out of his pocket since yesterday morning.

She doesn't notice, either, across the yard by the training posts, Cross. Standing very still. Watching the porch.

She closes her eyes.

On the windowsill upstairs, the notebook is open.

*58.*
