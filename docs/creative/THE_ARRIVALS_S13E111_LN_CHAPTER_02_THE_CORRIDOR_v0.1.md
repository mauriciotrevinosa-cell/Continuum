# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 16 — The Corridor

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S13E111
**Continuity state:** B+430, evening. Yuka's point of view. Shoko asks her to the clinic with nothing wrong, the way Mau goes. Not a lecture: Shoko talks to her the way a mother would. She tells her what Gojo saw (without numbers): Mau answering the question next to the question, and touching a note from Ori in his pocket. Shoko doesn't call Cross anything. She asks Yuka what she wanted from a second walk with Cross. Yuka talks about wanting one day she could spend with him while both of them were well. Shoko tells her it's possible for that day to be good and still leave Mau needing something from her. She asks what their next conversation could sound like. Yuka, about twenty-one, has wanted to be more grown-up for a long time. She half hears it. She decides she'll talk to Mau properly, tomorrow, when there's a moment. There isn't one.

*When: B+430. After dinner, in the clinic, with the lamp lit. Yuka chose to sit beside Mau at dinner. Ori stayed in her own seat by Frieren.*

Shoko asks her after dinner.

Quietly, at the sink. Not the way Mai did. Not *I didn't bring you up here for the floor*. Just:

“Come to the clinic for a bit. If you want. There's nothing wrong.” ˢʰ

Yuka looks at her.

“Nothing wrong?” ʸ

“Nothing wrong,” says Shoko. “That's the point. I'm told it's a thing people do now.” ˢʰ

Yuka almost smiles. It's what Mau does. Since the gate. He goes to the clinic with nothing wrong, because Shoko asked him to, and they drink tea.

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

The clinic smells of the things it always smells of. Clean cloth. The sharp stuff Shoko uses on cuts. The cigarettes she doesn't light.

Shoko puts the kettle on. She gets out two cups, not her clinic cups, the good ones, the ones with the chips in the rim that she won't let anyone throw away. She doesn't sit at the desk. She sits on the end of the bed by the window, the one Mau has sat on more than any other bed in the house, and pats the space beside her.

Yuka sits.

They drink tea for a while. Shoko doesn't say anything. Yuka doesn't either. Through the window, the yard is dark, and the light from the common room falls across it in long bars.

“Gojo came to see me,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

Yuka's cup stops halfway to her mouth.

“He fought Mau this morning,” says Shoko. “Past the river. Said it was training.” ˢʰ

“I know. Mau told me. He said it went fine.” ʸ

“Did he,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

She doesn't say it the way Maki said it. *When has that ever meant fine.* She just says it, quietly, and lets it sit.

Yuka looks at her tea.

“Gojo said Mau was somewhere else,” says Shoko. “The whole time. Fast, and good, better than he's ever been, and somewhere else. Gojo kept getting through. Little taps. In the gaps.” ˢʰ

She turns her cup in her hands.

“So he stopped, and sat down, and asked Mau what was going on,” says Shoko. “And Mau told him. Every reason. His physical guard is late on one side while he sorts the new sense. His heart rate's up because of the points. His Shrine goes where you'd be standing because of the gate. All of it. Very precise. Like Ciel.” ˢʰ

She looks at Yuka.

“And nothing else,” she says. “Gojo said it was the most technical answer he'd ever heard. To a question that wasn't technical at all.” ˢʰ

Yuka doesn't say anything.

She thinks about yesterday. About climbing into Mau's lap on the porch, shaking, after the workshop. About waiting for him to ask what was wrong. About him not asking.

About being grateful that he didn't.

“Gojo said one more thing,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

Yuka waits.

“He said Mau keeps touching something in his pocket,” says Shoko. “When it gets bad. A note. From Ori. He doesn't know he's doing it.” ˢʰ

Something cold goes through Yuka.

The same cold thing from yesterday. From *it's fine*. From the porch.

She didn't know about the note. She didn't know Ori had written him a note. She doesn't know what it says.

She knows the notes they keep on the bedside table. *Welcome home.* The chocolate wrapper from the red gate, the letter from Elmesia. She's read all of them. She's the one who keeps the notebook. They've made room to tell each other more since the gate, even the ugly things. She has started treating that as a certainty that nothing important can pass her by.

She didn't know about the note.

“I didn't know,” she says. ʸ

Her voice is small.

“I know,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“Mai said I'm swapping him,” Yuka says. ʸ

She didn't mean to say it. It just comes out.

“For Cross,” she says. “Then Granny asked if he was sleeping and I couldn't even say yes. And Grandpa had this look on his face—” ʸ

She stops.

“I'm not,” she says. “I'm *not*. I love him. I tell him every night. He's my husband. Cross is just—” ʸ

She stops again.

Shoko doesn't say anything. She doesn't say *you are* or *you aren't*. She just waits, the way Mau waits, the way Ori waits.

“I thought it was a good day,” says Yuka finally. “When we went to the ridge.” ʸ

“What did you want out of it?” Shoko asks. ˢʰ

Yuka looks at her.

She could tell Shoko the same story she told the table. The view, the log. The way they'd got there and sat down expecting to be tired and ended up going a little farther because the day was good. She has the uncomfortable feeling that Shoko would happily listen and still be waiting for an answer when she finished.

She thinks about it.

She thinks about it properly. Because it's Shoko. Because Shoko asked it like she actually wanted to know.

“I wanted to do one whole thing with him without anybody getting bad news,” she says. “He did a lot of waiting with me, before.” ʸ

Shoko waits, too. Yuka puts her cup between both hands and tries again.

“At the clinic where they told me, there was a door with a little metal latch. I kept trying to turn it and my hands were shaking. It took him a minute, but he opened it. When we went up on the ridge he put his hand out once, at a steep bit. I went up on my own and waited for him at the top. I wanted to be the one waiting for him for a change.” ʸ

Shoko is quiet for a long time.

Then she puts her cup down on the windowsill. She turns on the bed, so she's facing Yuka properly. She takes Yuka's hands, both of them, cup and all, in her own.

“That's real,” she says. ˢʰ

Yuka stares at her.

“Of course you'd want him to see you do that,” says Shoko. “He was your friend when getting up a flight of stairs meant something else entirely.” ˢʰ

Yuka's eyes have gone hot.

“And it's allowed,” says Shoko. “You're allowed to have that. You're allowed to be glad he's alive. You're allowed to want to sit with someone who knew you then.” ˢʰ

“But,” says Yuka. ʸ

It isn't a question. She can hear it coming.

“But it isn't the same thing,” says Shoko gently, “as what you have with Mau. And you know that. You know exactly what you have with Mau. You've got a notebook full of it.” ˢʰ

She squeezes Yuka's hands.

“The problem,” she says, “is that *he* can't see the difference. Not from where he's standing.” ˢʰ

Yuka goes very still.

“I saw you looking for him when you came in tonight,” says Shoko. “When was the last time he saw you do it?” ˢʰ

Yuka thinks of walking out of the workshop and crossing straight to the porch. She hadn't told him then what she needed. She'd asked a question where it felt like the answer to both their afternoons depended on him saying fine. At the time she'd been glad he'd said it.

“I told him about the hospital,” she says. “The night after we went to the village. He listened.” ʸ

“I'm glad you told him,” Shoko says. “I think he's had something to say since then and it hasn't come out.” ˢʰ

“He wouldn't step away from me because of one bad dinner.” ʸ

Her voice is barely there.

“He has,” says Shoko. “Before. In the other timeline, long before he'd ever heard of me. With Frieren. When Fern came. He moved his cup out of their room. His clothes. His books. Because he thought Frieren needed the space for someone new, and he thought he was being kind.” ˢʰ

Yuka looks up. “How do you know that?” ʸ

“He told me.” Shoko shrugs, very slightly. “One of those afternoons he comes down to the clinic with nothing wrong with him. He sits on the end of the cot and I point at the kettle, and sometimes he talks. He told me that one a while ago. Like it was a funny story about someone else.” ˢʰ

She lets go of Yuka's hands. She picks her cup back up.

“He was wrong,” says Shoko. “Frieren told him, much later. But he did it. Without asking her. Without saying anything. He just quietly made himself smaller until there was room.” ˢʰ

She drinks her tea.

“That's what Gojo was worried about,” she says. “In the field. He saw Mau stop answering him. We don't get to know what Mau's thinking until he says it, but I don't want either of you sitting alone deciding for the other.” ˢʰ

Yuka sits on the bed by the window with her hands round her cup.

She wants to cry. She doesn't. She's twenty-one. She's been wanting to be more grown-up for a long time now, for years, since before the island, since before the gate. Since before Mau, really. She's been wanting to be the kind of person who can hear something hard and sit with it and not run.

She sits with it.

It's horrible.

“What do I do?” she says. ʸ

Shoko looks at her.

“Talk to him,” she says. “Tell him where you were coming from when you asked if he minded. See what he would have said if he'd thought he could be difficult for a minute.” ˢʰ

She puts her cup down.

“Tell him the ugly thing,” says Shoko. “The way he tells you. That's what you promised each other, isn't it? Even the ugly things.” ˢʰ

Yuka nods.

She sees herself falling asleep against him after talking about the ridge. She can't think of anything he told her that night about his own day. He was awake when she stirred later, and he put the blanket back over her as if he'd been waiting to.

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

She stands up. She puts the cup down on the windowsill beside Shoko's.

“I'll talk to him,” she says. “Tomorrow. When there's a moment. Properly.” ʸ

Shoko looks at her for a long time.

“Tomorrow,” she says. ˢʰ

“Tomorrow.” ʸ

She goes upstairs.

Mau is already in bed. On his back, with his arm under his head, looking at the ceiling. He turns his head when she comes in, and smiles at her: the small one, the real one.

She looks at him.

She thinks about the note in his pocket. She thinks about *making himself smaller*. She thinks about saying *I need to tell you something* right now, tonight.

She's so tired.

She gets into bed. She finds the place on his chest where she always fits. She puts her hand on the line on his arm.

“Tomorrow,” she murmurs, into his shirt. “I want to talk to you. Properly. About something. Tomorrow.” ʸ

She feels him go still under her.

Just for a moment.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

She falls asleep.

She doesn't know what he will make of *I want to talk to you about something*, left without the rest of it in the dark.

She doesn't know he lies awake until morning, again, with his hand on her back, deciding what the something is.

She doesn't know what he decides.

In the morning, he's gone before she's awake.
