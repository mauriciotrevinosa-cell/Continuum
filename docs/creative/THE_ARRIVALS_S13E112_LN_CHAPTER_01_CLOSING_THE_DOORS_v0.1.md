# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 17 — Closing the Doors

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E112
**Continuity state:** B+430 ("57"), before dawn. Mau closes his direct personal relays and the external contract access, including Yuka’s borrowed techniques/Exchange and Mai’s supply. Contracts remain intact. The shadow watch and its existing protection orders continue; Ciel’s independent communications and emergency reports remain available. Ciel/Noise ask him not to isolate them too, and he agrees. Makima accompanies him outside; DIO returns to her room inside Mau after the argument. Mau remembers he offered to make Shoko tea and asks Ciel to relay that he won't be there. He leaves without a note for Yuka. On the porch Mau struggles to orient himself; Makima makes him wait until he can see the road and he holds on to the known count of ten floors.

*When: B+430. The hour before dawn. Yuka hasn't written today's number yet. She's asleep, on his side of the bed, with her hand on his pillow.*

He goes to the room.

Not the bedroom. He's already left that. He got up so carefully she didn't stir, and went down the dark stairs, and sat on the bottom step in the hall with his back against the wall. And then he went inward. To the other room. The shared one, inside him, with the table and the chairs and the cup nobody ever finishes.

They're all there.

Noise by the table, the shadow of it, very still. Ciel in her chair. DIO at the window end, without her hat, which means she's worried. Makima in her chair at the far end of the table, upright, hands folded, watching him come in.

Nobody says anything.

They have seen what reaches the shared room: missed meals, the work his body is doing without sleep, the requests he keeps putting aside. Makima has felt his distress through their particular bond. None of them has heard the conversation Yuka meant to have. He has spent the night supplying its ending himself.

He sits down at the table.

“It's all coming up,” he says. ᴹ

Nobody answers. He didn't expect them to.

“Everything,” he says. “The room with Frieren. When Fern came. I moved my cup out. I moved my clothes. I didn't remember I'd done that until the wall with Makima, and now I can't stop remembering it.” ᴹ

He puts his hands flat on the table.

“And the Trial,” he says. “The year. The hands. The cheapest tent. All of it I said I'd find out about with her. It's all just—coming. All at once. And the gate. And her face at the gate when she saw him. And *before you*.” ᴹ

His voice is very level. He notices that from a distance.

“I can't think,” he says. “With everything coming in. The eyes. Maki's sense. The house. Her. I need it to stop. Just for a while. Just until I can sort it.” ᴹ

“What do you want to do?” says Ciel. ᶜ

Her voice is careful. The clinic voice.

He doesn't answer right away.

He looks at the doors.

There are doors in the room. There always have been. Not real ones; the room doesn't really have walls. But he knows where they are, the way you know where the doors are in your own house in the dark. The ones that lead out. The threads. The contracts.

One goes to Yuka.

He can see it. Feel it. The thread of her contract: everything he built for her over the years, one piece at a time. The techniques she can borrow. The second Limitless inside him that's hers. The handle Noise put on her side, so she can pull him to her. The link, so the voices in him can reach her. All of it running out through that door, out of him, to her, all the time.

He notices the familiar connection still available while she sleeps. He has not opened her thoughts. The fact that she could reach him is enough to make him imagine the words she might say.

Another goes to Mai. The agreement. Her Construction, drawing on his reserves instead of hers, so she can build the second floor and the kettle and the aqueduct and everything else on her list.

And more. Threads to Rimuru. To the soldiers in people's shadows. To everyone.

“I want to close them,” he says. ᴹ

The room goes very quiet.

“Close,” says Ciel. ᶜ

“Not break.” ᴹ

He says it fast. Firmly. He needs them to understand.

“I'm not breaking anything,” he says. “Not her contract. Not Mai's. Nothing. Everything stays. Everything's still there. I just—close the doors. For a while. So nothing comes through. So I'm not waiting for her to reach me while I'm trying to sort all this out. So the requests stop for a while. So I can think.” ᴹ

“And when you open them?” says Ciel. ᶜ

“Then they're open,” he says. “Same as before. Nothing lost.” ᴹ

“And Yuka?” ᶜ

He doesn't answer that.

DIO stands up at the end of the table.

“Mau,” she says. ᴰ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You don't,” says DIO. “You don't know. If you close hers, she'll feel it. She'll reach for something of yours and it won't come. She'll reach for *you* and you won't—” ᴰ

“I know,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Then don't,” says DIO. ᴰ

He looks at her.

He's never seen her face like this. DIO, who is all brightness and hats and plants and bouncing. Her face is very still, and very frightened, and very much like Ori's when Ori is trying not to cry.

“I have to,” he says. “I can't think with it open. Every time I notice the connection I think about the walk and the ridge and the corridor and the chair. I need it to stop. Just for a while.” ᴹ

He closes his eyes.

He closes the door.

It doesn't make a sound.

He'd thought it might. It doesn't. The thread to Yuka is there, and then it's still there, and then it's still there but nothing is coming through it. Like a window with the shutters closed. The house is still on the other side. He just can't hear it.

Her side no longer has a way to reach him directly.

He sits very still at the table and waits to feel better.

He doesn't.

He closes Mai's door.

He closes the personal relay to Rimuru. He quiets the ordinary reports from the soldiers in people's shadows. He does not call them back or cancel their protection orders. The watch remains Bellion's to run.

“Emergencies still reach me,” Ciel says. “And I can speak to Rimuru and Yuka myself. You are asking to close your conversation, not mine.” ᶜ

He nods. Those paths stay. One by one, he closes the invitations that reach him directly.

And then he reaches for the last ones. The ones closest. The ones in the room.

“*No*,” says Noise. ᴺ

Mau stops.

Noise has moved. The shadow by the table isn't by the table anymore; it's in front of him, between him and the place where the next door is. Not threatening. Just *there*. The way Beru stands in front of things.

“Not us,” says Noise. ᴺ

It doesn't say it in its report voice. It says it in the voice it used once, a long time ago, saying *help*, when two people were sitting too close to it. A small voice. Uncertain.

“Not us,” says Noise again. “Please.” ᴺ

Ciel has stood up too.

She's standing beside her chair with one hand on the back of it, very straight, the way she stands when she's about to give him a number he won't like.

“We're not outside you,” she says. “We're not a door. If you close us, you won't be sorting anything. You'll just be alone in here with all of it, and nobody to help you carry it, and nobody to tell you when it's too much.” ᶜ

She pauses.

“I counted every breath of a year once,” she says, “so you'd know you were still there. Don't make me sit in here and not be able to count.” ᶜ

He looks at them.

At Noise, the shadow of the first thing he ever built without knowing he was building it. At Ciel, who has been with him longer than he can remember. At DIO, standing at the end of the table with her hands pressed together, not crying, trying not to.

At Makima, in her chair at the far end, who hasn't said anything, and who is watching him with the eyes of the best strategist her world ever had, seeing every piece on the board.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is very tired.

“Okay. Not you.” ᴹ

Noise doesn't move for a moment. Then, slowly, the shadow goes back to the table.

Ciel sits down.

“One thing,” he says. ᴹ

Ciel looks at him.

“From now on,” he says, “just tell me the technical things. What's coming. What's broken. Where things are. Numbers. I'll listen to all of that.” ᴹ

He stands up.

“Don't ask me how I am,” he says. ᴹ

Ciel opens her mouth.

“Please,” he says. ᴹ

She closes it.

“Understood,” she says, after a long time. The crisp voice. The number voice. But he can hear what's under it, and he doesn't let himself listen. ᶜ

He turns to the far end of the table.

“Makima,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“I'm going to a dungeon.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“Ciel found one,” he says. “Weeks ago. In the hills past the ruins. Wild. Nothing to do with the house gate. Medium. I'm going to clear it.” ᴹ

“Now?” ᴹᵏ

“Now. Before anyone's up.” ᴹ

“Why?” ᴹᵏ

He thinks about it.

“To clear my head,” he says. “And the Hamster War cost me a lot of money. I'll earn some back.” ᴹ

Makima looks at him for a long, long moment.

She knows it's not the money. He knows she knows. He's rich; the Shop is full; the war cost him nothing that matters. She doesn't say so.

“If you go far, I come inside or I come with you,” she says. “This body can't stay behind at the house.” ᴹᵏ

“I know. You can stay in here if you want.” He looks at the place across the table from her. “Would you come with me instead?” ᴹ

She gets up. “I was about to ask if I could.” ᴹᵏ

That gets him to look at her. She stands in front of him with her hands loose at her sides.

“I want to see a Gate with you,” she says. “I get to see a lot through you. I want to be the one who gets to point something out first.” ᴹᵏ

“You'll have to go a bit faster than me for that.” ᴹ

“Will I?” ᴹᵏ

He nearly laughs. He'd thought he'd have to explain what he was going to do with himself before someone could say yes. Makima looks at him long enough to catch the little change in his mouth.

“And if I tell you to stop for a minute, can I have the minute?” she asks. “You were standing up at the wall for a long time before you knew your legs were going. I felt it. I'd like to be able to say something before it gets that far today.” ᴹᵏ

“If you ask me,” he says. ᴹ

He nods.

He doesn't say *thank you*. He can't, right now. She doesn't seem to need him to.

He comes back out of the room.

He's sitting on the bottom stair in the hall, in the grey before dawn, with his back against the wall. The house is silent round him.

He's never felt it this silent.

He can't reach Yuka through the route they chose to share. He is used to knowing he can speak to her, across a yard or at the edge of sleep, if either of them opens it. He has taken that choice off her side without warning her.

It isn't there.

He sits on the stair in the silence and waits to feel the relief he closed the door for.

It doesn't come.

He stands up anyway. There's a spoon and the tea Shoko likes on the kitchen shelf, behind the one Gojo uses. He can see the rim of the tin from the stairs. He told her he'd make it this morning.

*Will you tell Shoko I won't be in for tea?* he asks Ciel. ᴹ

*I can tell her where we'll be, if she asks.* ᶜ

*Yes.* ᴹ

He could have Ciel say the same to Yuka. He lets the moment to ask go and stands there long enough to know he's let it go.

He doesn't go upstairs.

He doesn't wake her. He doesn't leave a note. He thinks about it. He stands at the bottom of the stairs and looks up toward their room, toward the windowsill, where the notebook is. Where today's number isn't written yet.

He doesn't go up.

On the back step he puts his foot down a little too soon. His heel hits hard. His next thought is to get his other foot on the dry part of the stone, before the blood reaches it.

There's no blood. He can see where his boot has pressed the dew out. He knows exactly how much water is on it. He has to pick each foot up separately to get to the bottom.

Makima is there, in the body Noise holds for her. She has one hand flat against the porch rail.

“Which way did you mean to go?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

He looks at the house. The back door's still open. He'd come down five steps. He can count the marks his shoes left, but he can't remember why he thought he'd be able to make the tea.

“The gate in the hills,” he says at last. “It's ten floors, right?” ᴹ

“That's what Ciel found.” ᴹᵏ

Ten. Down to the end and then back out. There would always be a next thing, and he could tell it was over when there was no floor after ten. He can think as far as the road if he holds on to that.

Makima takes her hand off the rail. “Look at me a second. Can you tell me where we are?” ᴹᵏ

“Home.” He gets it on the first try. It takes him long enough to say it that she puts her hand back. ᴹ

“Wait with me until you can see the road from here.” ᴹᵏ

She stays on the bottom step with him while the light gets far enough into the yard to reach the gap in the orchard. When he points it out to her, she stands. She walks on the side of him that faces the house.

DIO has gone back to her room inside him. He passes her door without knocking. Beru is at the end of the porch, very still, watching.

“My king,” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

“Stay with the house, Beru.” ᴹ

Beru looks at him for a long time with all his eyes.

“Yes, my king,” he says. ᴮᵉ

He doesn't move. He doesn't stop him. But Mau feels him watching all the way across the yard, all the way to the orchard, all the way up the hill road, until the trees close behind them.

Upstairs, in the room where he isn't, Yuka sleeps.

Her hand is on his pillow. The sheet beside her is going cold.

On the windowsill, the notebook is open to yesterday's page.

*58.*
