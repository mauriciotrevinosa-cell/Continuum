# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 18 — Earning It Back

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S13E112
**Continuity state:** B+430, dawn to late afternoon. Makima wants to explore the wild Gate with Mau and they agree she can ask him to stop. They clear it without changing the house Gate’s separate rules. The clear credits his balance; physical floor drops go to inventory. He wants to break an optional deeper seal; Makima stops him. He says he fears being replaced. They record the seal for a later planned survey and leave through the ordinary exit; the cleared gate closes. They return wealthy, tired and still emotionally unsettled.

*When: B+430. From first light in the hills to the late afternoon, when the light goes long. At the house, the notebook now says "57", in Yuka's hand; she wrote it when she woke up and found the bed empty.*

The dungeon is in a fold of the hills, a long way past the ruins.

Ciel marked it during an earlier survey of the hills: a wrongness in the ground, like a stone in a shoe. A wild gate. Nothing to do with the house, or the Rulers, or the oval between the two old trees. Just one of the holes this world opens sometimes in empty places, with things living in it, waiting.

Medium, Ciel said. Ten floors. Nothing that matters.

He stands at the mouth of it in the grey light with Makima beside him.

“Numbers,” he says. ᴹ

*Ten floors on the entry record*, says Ciel. *The survey puts water on four and moving stone on seven. Guardian marked on ten. Those are the readings we have, not a guarantee about what hasn't shown itself.* ᶜ

“Nothing outside the survey?” ᴹ

*Nothing verified. I'll tell you when that changes*, says Ciel. *You asked for technical things.* ᶜ

He goes in.

Ciel tells him when both feet are on the first floor. He already has a dagger out. He sets it against the stone just long enough to feel the point catch and turns the grip in his hand. Nothing here looks like Shoko's steps. He can make sense of the first thing coming at him.

It's fast.

He's never cleared anything this fast. Not the birthday gate with Beru. Not the quarry. He goes through the first floor like it isn't there. Things come out of the walls and he's already moved. Things drop from the ceiling and the daggers are already where they'll land. He doesn't open anything big. He doesn't need to. Blue the size of a fist, Shrine in single lines, Authority to move things out of his way, the new body doing what he tells it before he's finished telling it.

When the dark under his feet closes over a dropped core, he can hear the scrape it makes against the rock. Noise checks the inventory on its way past. Ciel gets the floor count up before he asks for it.

Floor two. Floor three.

Makima walks behind him through all of it.

She doesn't do much. She can't go far from him; Noise holds her body, and Noise is busy. Once, on floor three, something comes at her from a side passage while he's in front, and she looks at it, just looks, and it stops. Not dead. Just stopped, the way a dog stops when someone it respects says *no*. He kills it on the way back past.

He goes to step past it and sees her looking down. The thing had come out of a slot no wider than her arm. It's lying where it came out, a long piece of bone showing through a slit in the side.

“I was going to tell you it had one foot facing back the way it came,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Mau lowers the dagger. “I said you'd have to go faster.” ᴹ

“I saw it first. You killed it first. I think that's two separate things.” ᴹᵏ

He bends far enough to see the foot. It does turn the wrong way. He'd seen the joint and filed it away with all the things he hadn't needed to know to get them past the slot. She was waiting to see what he'd make of it.

“It can probably go through there backwards,” he says. ᴹ

“Can you?” ᴹᵏ

“Probably.” ᴹ

“Good. You can show me on the way out.” ᴹᵏ

He walks past her. After a few steps he slows down so there's room for her to catch him. “I can show you. I'm choosing a better hole than that one.” ᴹ

She comes up level with him as they get to the stairs. By the bottom she's describing two things she saw on the first floor that he's going to have to let her count too.

Floor four has water.

He crosses it on Sky without getting his boots wet. Things move under the surface. He doesn't look at them. He reads them, with the eyes, and with the sense turned down very low, and he goes round the places where they are, and doesn't stop.

Halfway across, he thinks about the drowned level in the Rulers' gate. Carrying her on his back the whole way. Her arms round his neck. Her voice in his ear: *four, two, left*.

He goes faster.

On six the staircase breaks halfway up and starts again at the next wall. Mau takes a step where there ought to be stone, turns on Sky and comes down the other way round on the first intact stair. He looks back to see whether Makima saw the turn.

“Very good,” she says from below. “Can you show me where you expect me to put a foot?” ᴹᵏ

He comes back without turning this time. He holds out a hand and steps from dry stone into the gap as easily as he'd cross a room to get a cup. She takes it, goes exactly where he goes, and steps down on the other side. When she sees how far they carried each other past the broken part she looks at him instead of looking back.

“I liked doing that with you better than I liked watching it,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He laughs on the last step. She keeps her hand in his until she has both feet down.

Floor seven has the ceiling.

It comes down while he's in the middle of the room, the whole thing, slow and then fast, stone grinding on stone. He doesn't run. He puts Infinity over himself and Makima and lets it come down on that, and holds it there, and walks out the far side with the ceiling resting on nothing above their heads.

On the far side, Makima looks back at the room. The ceiling is lying on the floor now, where it dropped when he let go. He has the sense of water climbing behind it, getting in above his boots. He listens for the direction of it until he hears his name the second time.

“Is it still coming in?” he asks. ᴹ

“There's no water on this floor.” Makima comes round where he can see her. “That's dry stone where you're standing.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at the grit at the end of his boot. He moves his foot across it. It leaves the kind of mark feet make when the floor's been dry for years.

“You didn't have to do that,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Do what?” ᴹ

“Hold it. You could have just walked out quickly. You were faster than it.” ᴹᵏ

He doesn't answer.

He held it because it felt good to hold something heavy and not have it move. He doesn't say that either.

The guardian on ten is a great armoured thing like a beetle the size of a house.

He kills it in four moves.

He doesn't even think about it. It comes at him, and he moves, and moves again, and opens Shrine in a single long line exactly where the eyes say the joint in its armour is, and it comes apart along the line like a seam unpicked, and falls.

The guardian falls. Above the exit that forms behind it, the notification opens.

> **[Dungeon cleared.]**
>
> **[Currency acquired.]**

There's a lot of currency. More than the Rulers' gate. More than the Hamster War cost him, by a long way.

The clear credits it to his balance. He closes the notification without checking the total.

He doesn't leave.

He stands in the middle of the guardian's chamber, among the remains, and the eyes are still out, still reading, and they've found something.

Under the floor.

Another level. Deeper. Not part of the dungeon's ten floors; something older, underneath, sealed. The eyes can see the seal. The sense can feel the cold coming up through the stone. Something down there that isn't medium. Something that, Ciel is already telling him in her careful number voice, is very much not medium.

*There's a sublevel*, says Ciel. *Sealed. Under this floor. The readings are well above anything in the dungeon. I'd estimate it's—* ᶜ

“I can see it.” ᴹ

He puts his hand on the floor.

“I'm going to open it,” he says. ᴹ

“No,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

He looks up.

She's standing in front of him. Right in front of him, on the other side of his hand, between him and the floor. He didn't see her move. Noise must have moved her; or she moved, and Noise let her.

“Get out of the way,” he says. ᴹ

“No,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“It's sealed. Something's down there. If I don't open it, someone else will find it eventually and—” ᴹ

“Ciel, was this under the guardian on your survey?” Makima asks. ᴹᵏ

*No. I can see its outline from here. I don't have a classification.* ᶜ

He puts a little less weight on the palm that's on the floor. “I can take a look.” ᴹ

“On seven you had to turn and look at me after I said your name. Twice. Then you asked me if the water was still coming in. We'd already left it behind.” Her foot stays where she put it, over the first part of the outline. “I can come back with you to look at this. We'll bring Gojo and Yuta. We'll let Shoko know where we've gone. Today I want the minute you said I could ask for.” ᴹᵏ

He stands up. She has to lift her head a little to see his face. The stone behind her foot is very cold. He can make out an uneven line in it where he'd need to put the first cut.

“Would you move if I told you I'm good enough to kill it?” he asks. ᴹ

“I think you probably are,” she says. “I watched you win all the way down here. You looked up to see if I'd caught what you did with the step on six. On the last floor you didn't even look when the exit opened.” ᴹᵏ

His eyes go toward the ordinary exit, a thin place in the light behind the guardian. He remembers reading the notice. He can't remember whether he'd looked past it.

“Stay with me for a minute,” she says. “I want to hear what you're going down there for.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at her for a long time.

Then he sits down. On the floor of the guardian's chamber, among the pieces of the beetle, with his back against a broken column.

He doesn't say anything.

After a moment, Makima sits down too. Beside him. Not touching. The way she sat on the wall.

“I can feel it,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He doesn't ask what.

“On seven I felt your ribs tighten before the ceiling started moving,” she says. “I thought it was the stone. Then I felt it again when you heard water running down into the floor. It was the same thing as on the wall, only you were still walking.” ᴹᵏ

She looks at her hands.

“I'm not going to make you say it,” she says. “I know it hurts. I don't know what you think is happening unless you tell me.” ᴹᵏ

He's quiet for a long time.

Then he says it anyway.

“I think I'm being replaced,” he says. ᴹ

He hears it come out of his mouth. Very quiet. Very level. In the voice he used for Gojo's numbers. The truth, the real one, in the wrong voice.

Makima doesn't say anything.

“It happened before,” he says. “With Frieren. When Fern came. I thought—Frieren had someone new now. Someone who needed her more. Someone she'd want the room for. So I moved my things out. Before she could ask. So it wouldn't hurt when she did.” ᴹ

He looks at the remains of the beetle.

“She didn't want me to,” he says. “She told me later. Much later. She'd done the same thing. She thought I'd want the space. We both got out of each other's way and we were both wrong.” ᴹ

He breathes out.

“I like him better than I thought I would,” Mau says. He can hear how unlikely that sounds, from him, today. “I'm glad she had somebody with her back then. I meant it when I told him so. Then I think about how I sat beside her last night and tried to work out what she could possibly want me to give back.” ᴹ

His hand has gone to his pocket. He doesn't notice.

“So I'm getting out of the way,” he says. “Before she has to ask. So it doesn't hurt when she does.” ᴹ

Makima is quiet for a long time.

“You're wrong,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He laughs. It isn't a very good laugh.

“You don't know that.” ᴹ

“I do,” says Makima. “I've watched her. I've watched her the whole time I've been in this house. I've never seen anyone look at anyone the way she looks at you. Not in my world. Not here.” ᴹᵏ

She pauses.

“Would you believe it if I went on telling you?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

His fingers have folded round the note in his pocket. He lets go of it. “I'd want to hear it from her.” ᴹ

She turns her head and looks at him.

“And you closed her door,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He doesn't answer.

He looks at his hands. At the right one, which closes the way it always has. At the left one, which closes the same way now, which the points fixed, which used to be weaker.

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“Then open it,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“Not yet.” ᴹ

“Why not?” ᴹᵏ

He thinks about it.

“Because if I open it,” he says, “and she tells me the something, and the something is what I think it is—” ᴹ

He doesn't finish.

Makima doesn't push. She looks at the floor, where the seal is, where the cold is coming up.

After a while, he holds out his hand.

Sideways. Without looking. The way he did on the wall.

She takes it. He feels her thumb settle across his knuckles.

They sit there like that, on the floor of the guardian's chamber, among the pieces of the beetle, with a sealed thing under them that neither of them is going to open, and say nothing for a long time.

“Thank you,” he says eventually. ᴹ

“For what?” ᴹᵏ

“For standing in the way.” ᴹ

Makima looks at their hands.

“At the wall I could sit,” says Makima. “I knew how to do that. Today you asked me to move and I had to choose what would happen if I did.” ᴹᵏ

“Do you wish you'd moved?” ᴹ

She thinks about it. “I wish there were fewer legs round the place we sat down.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at the remains of the beetle. By the time he looks back at her she's looking at them too, trying to arrange her mouth as if she hadn't said anything funny. It gives him one good breath all on its own.

He gets up far enough to see whether there's a part of the column she can have without getting her coat dirty. There isn't. He puts his other hand out and she takes it so he can help her up.

“I did want to come,” she says. “You can ask me again when you want to go somewhere and you haven't got anything to clear.” ᴹᵏ

He could say she only came because he'd already made up his mind. She had asked him to look at a foot on the third floor. He wonders how many things he'd have got to see if he'd gone as slowly as she wanted.

“You could point something out before we go back,” he says. “On the road. Somewhere you want to stop.” ᴹ

Makima straightens her cuff where it caught against the column. “I've already seen a place.” ᴹᵏ

Before they leave, Ciel records the unopened seal and its position. Mau marks it on their map for a later survey with the house. The ordinary exit is still available; he takes it rather than breaking a second way deeper. Once they are both outside, the cleared gate closes.

They come out in the late afternoon.

The light is long and gold over the hills. He can't see gold. He knows it's there. Ciel tells him the angle of the sun without being asked, because it's a technical thing, and he listens.

*You've been gone ten hours*, Ciel says. *The household will have noticed. You left no note.* ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

*That's a technical observation*, says Ciel. ᶜ

“I know, Ciel.” ᴹ

There's a flat place where the rain has gone down between the rocks and left the smaller stones on top of each other. Makima had walked past it in the morning. She stops there now and shows him a grasshopper on the tallest stone. It's turned so that it can see both of them. Mau crouches to look and it goes round to the side she was on before he moved.

“It saw you first,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes,” says Makima. She moves her foot and it comes back round. “I think we should count it.” ᴹᵏ

They stay just long enough for it to jump. After that they walk home over the hills, his inventory carrying the floor drops, his balance higher by an amount he didn't need and didn't check. Makima falls a step behind him when they get to the narrow part of the path.

He doesn't feel better.

He feels the way he felt on the stair this morning: like something has been switched off. Quiet. Too quiet. He can't feel the house through the closed doors. He can't feel Yuka. He can feel Makima behind him, and Ciel and Noise inside, and DIO, very subdued, saying nothing.

That's all.

He walks home in the long light, with the silence where she used to be, and doesn't let himself think about what he'll find when he gets there.
