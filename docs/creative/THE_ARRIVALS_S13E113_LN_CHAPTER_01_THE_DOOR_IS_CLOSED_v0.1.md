# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 19 — The Door Is Closed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S13E113

*When: B+431. Morning to late afternoon. When she woke, the bed was cold on his side; she didn't think anything of it. She wrote "56" and a small sun.*

The bed is empty when she wakes up.

She doesn't think anything of it. He gets up early sometimes. He goes down to sit on the porch with his tea before anyone else is awake, or he goes to help Shuna with the bread, or he's in the yard with Beru. She reaches over and his side of the sheet is cold, which means he's been gone a while, and she thinks: *he couldn't sleep again*.

She thinks: *tonight*. *I'll tell him tonight.* *Properly*. *About the corridor. The way Shoko said*.

She gets up. She finds the notebook on the windowsill, open to yesterday's page.

*57.*

That's strange. He usually leaves it open to a blank page for her. She turns to the next one and writes the number, and draws a small sun beside it, because it's a nice morning.

*56.*

She goes downstairs.

* * *

He isn't on the porch.

He isn't in the kitchen. Shuna hasn't seen him. Beru is at the end of the porch, very still, looking at the orchard, and when she asks him, he turns his head toward her slowly, with all his eyes, and doesn't answer straight away.

“He went out early, my queen,” he says at last. ᴮᵉ

“Out where?” ʸ

Beru is quiet for a moment.

“He asked me to stay with the house,” he says. ᴮᵉ

It isn't an answer. She doesn't notice that it isn't.

“Okay,” she says. “When he comes back, tell him I'm in the yard.” ʸ

“Yes, my queen,” says Beru. ᴮᵉ

He goes back to looking at the orchard.

* * *

Cross is in the yard.

He's always in the yard in the mornings now. Waiting for her, she thinks, though he'd never say so. He's by the training posts with his arms crossed, and he uncrosses them when he sees her coming.

“You said you'd show me,” he says. ᶜʳ

“Show you what?” ʸ

“The other thing. His things. That you can use.” ᶜʳ

She told him about it yesterday. On the walk back from the ridge, maybe; she can't remember. About the contract. About how she can borrow Mau's techniques, any of them, his Blue, his Red, his Infinity, the second Limitless he built inside himself just for her. About how she can pull him to her through her own shadow, from anywhere.

Cross didn't believe her. She could tell. His face did the thing it does when he thinks someone is exaggerating.

“Okay,” she says, grinning. “Watch.” ʸ

* * *

She holds out her hand.

She's done this a hundred times. More. In the Rulers' gate, every floor. In the yard, on the porch, for fun, to make Ori laugh. She reaches into the contract, the way you'd reach into a pocket you know is there, for his Blue, the small one, the first one she ever threw on the first floor of a gate built to kill her.

Nothing comes.

She stops.

That's wrong. She reaches again. Further. For the place where the contract always is, where his things always are, waiting, warm, easy to take.

It's there. She can feel it's there. The thread. Everything he built for her, all of it, still there.

But it's closed.

Like a door. Like a door she's walked through a thousand times and has always been open and now, without any sound, without any warning, is shut. Not locked. Not broken. Just shut. She can put her hand flat on it. She can feel that the room is still on the other side.

She can't get in.

* * *

“Well?” says Cross. ᶜʳ

She doesn't hear him.

She reaches for the Exchange. The handle Noise put on her side, so she can pull him to her or go to him from anywhere. The thing she used on the bell tower to save herself. She reaches for it, and it's there, the shape of it, she can feel the shape, but when she closes her hand on it nothing turns.

Closed.

She reaches for him.

Not through the contract. Through the other thing. The link in her head, the one Ciel built on the contract day so the voices in him could reach her and she could reach them, and through them, *him*, the warm steady weight of him at the edge of her that's always, always there. Even asleep. Even across the house. Even when he's in the bath and she's in the yard and she just wants to know he's there.

She reaches.

There's a door there too.

Closed.

* * *

She's sitting on the grass.

She doesn't remember sitting down. She's just there, on the grass by the training posts, with her hands flat on the ground on either side of her, and the yard is very bright and very far away, and her heart is going so fast she can hear it.

“Yuka,” says Cross. ᶜʳ

He's crouched in front of her. She can see his face. She can't hear him properly.

*Ciel*, she says. Down the link. Down whatever's left of it. *Ciel. Noise. Are you there? Is anyone there?* ʸ

* * *

*We're here*, says Ciel. ᶜ

She almost sobs.

Ciel's voice. Crisp, careful. Coming through clear, the way it always does, from somewhere inside Mau, wherever he is.

*Ciel, his door's closed*, she says. *His things. The Exchange. Him. I can't reach him. I can't feel him. What happened? Is he hurt? Where is he?* ʸ

A pause.

A long one.

She doesn't know that inside Mau, in the room with the table, Ciel and Noise are watching him clear a dungeon floor in silence, and feeling everything he's ever locked away come up through him at once, the room he emptied for Fern and the year in his hands and *before you*, all of it, like a tide. She doesn't know that Ciel promised him, this morning, technical things only. She doesn't know that Ciel is choosing her words while drowning in his.

*He's not hurt*, says Ciel. ᶜ

Yuka breathes out.

*He's asked not to be reached*, says Ciel. *For now.* ᶜ

* * *

Yuka goes very still.

*Not to be reached*, she says. *By who?* ʸ

Another pause.

*He needs some time*, says Noise. ᴺ

Noise's voice. Gruff and careful. She's never heard it this careful.

*He doesn't want anyone near him right now*, says Noise. *He's closed everything. Not just yours. He just—needs some time. Away.* ᴺ

* * *

*Anyone*, Noise says.

*Everything*, Noise says. *Not just yours.*

She doesn't hear those words.

She hears *he doesn't want*. She hears *near him*. She hears *away*.

She hears a closed door where her husband always is. She hears *I want to talk to you about something*, last night, in the dark, and him going still under her, just for a moment. She hears Maki saying *when has that ever meant fine*. She hears Mai saying *you're swapping him*. She hears Shoko saying *his old habit of deciding the kindest thing he can do for someone he loves is get out of the way*.

She hears *me*.

He doesn't want *me* near him.

* * *

*Yuka*, says Ciel, sharply. *Yuka, that isn't what—* ᶜ

But she's stopped listening.

She's sitting on the grass with her hands flat on the ground and she's shaking, all over, so hard her teeth knock together, the way she shook in the cavern after the domain, the burnout, when her shadow wouldn't come and his things were too—no. That was wrong. His things weren't too heavy. She chose not to take them, because the weight would land on him.

She can't take them now.

Not because she's choosing.

Because he closed the door.

* * *

“Yuka.” ᶜʳ

Cross. Still crouched in front of her. He's put his hands on her shoulders. Big hands, long-fingered, wrong-jointed, warm. Holding her still.

“Look at me,” says Cross. ᶜʳ

She looks at him.

His face. Hard and closed and not very good at hiding anything. Looking at her the way he looked at her in the corridor, a long time ago, in another world, when her hands were shaking too much to open the door.

“Breathe,” he says. ᶜʳ

She breathes.

“Again,” he says. ᶜʳ

She does.

He doesn't ask what's wrong. He doesn't need to. He saw her face change. He saw her reach for something and not find it. He's seen her like this before. He's the only one who has.

He stays there, crouched on the grass in front of her with his hands on her shoulders, until she stops shaking.

* * *

She doesn't lean into him.

She'll remember that later and hold onto it, the way she'll hold onto *I didn't go to Cross after the workshop*. She doesn't lean into him. She doesn't put her face in his shoulder. She sits on the grass and breathes, and lets him hold her still, and when the shaking stops she takes his hands off her shoulders, gently, and stands up.

“Thank you,” she says. ʸ

Her voice is very small.

“Where is he?” says Cross. ᶜʳ

She doesn't know.

She goes to find out.

* * *

Nobody knows.

Or nobody will say. She asks Shuna, and Shuna looks at her with a face Yuka can't read and says *he went out early*. She asks Frieren, who looks up from her book and says *I don't know* in a voice that means she does. She asks Gojo, on the wall, and Gojo pushes his blindfold up and looks at her for a long time with the bright eyes and says, for once, nothing at all.

She asks Beru again. Beru says *he asked me to stay with the house*.

She asks about Makima.

Makima's gone too. Nobody's seen her at the bottom of the porch steps since dawn.

Yuka stands in the middle of the yard and doesn't let herself think about that.

* * *

She waits on the porch all afternoon.

In the old chair. His chair. With her knees drawn up and her arms round them and the notebook in her lap, closed, and the north road and the hill road and the orchard path all in front of her, all empty.

Ori comes and sits on the step below her for a while. She doesn't say anything. She just sits there, with her back against Yuka's legs, the way she sits with Mau.

After a while, she gets up and goes inside.

When she comes back she has a plate. With bread on it, and two of the small sharp pickles, the ones Ori hates.

She puts the plate in Yuka's lap. She looks at her very seriously.

Then she goes back inside.

* * *

Yuka looks at the pickles.

She doesn't understand. Then, slowly, she does.

*Ori's been eating pickles*, Maki said. *Every night. In your chair. She hates them. She does it anyway. A child is filling in for you.*

She looks at the plate in her lap.

She doesn't eat them.

She holds the plate, and looks at the empty roads, and waits.

* * *

The sun goes round to the west.

The light goes long and gold across the yard.

At the very edge of the hill road, where it comes out of the trees, two shapes.

One of them is walking a step behind the other.

She's on her feet before she knows it, the plate sliding off her lap and the pickles going everywhere. She's halfway down the porch steps.

Then she sees who the second shape is.

She stops.
