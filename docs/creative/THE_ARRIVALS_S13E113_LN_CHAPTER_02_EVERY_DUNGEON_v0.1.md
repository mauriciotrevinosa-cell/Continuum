# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 20 — Every Dungeon

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S13E113
**Continuity state:** B+430, sunset, in the yard. The break. Yuka sees Makima walking a step behind Mau on the hill road, smells the dungeon on them, and understands where he's been all day: a dungeon, without telling her, with Makima, after she promised at the quarry to go with him into every one. She's furious and frightened. Mau starts to explain: he didn't want to bother her, she was with Cross. She hears "with Cross" and gets defensive before he can finish, and it turns into an attack. She says she might have made a mistake marrying him, and she brings up Makima: he'd rather have her; Yuka had *started to trust her*. Makima, stricken, takes a step and ends up half behind Mau, like Power behind Denji. That breaks Yuka. Mau says he loves her when she turns to leave. Yuka hears him, tells him she knows, and asks Cross to walk with her. Mau stands in the yard with the S1 belief confirmed: stepping away was the right thing, she has someone else. The house sees all of it. No physical fight.

*When: B+430. Sunset. The light is going long across the yard. Most of the house is on the porch or at the windows, because Yuka has been waiting all afternoon and everyone knows it.*

He sees her before he reaches the yard.

He doesn't need the sense. He doesn't need the eyes. She's on the porch steps, halfway down, with a plate on the ground at her feet and something scattered round it, and she's stopped dead, staring at the hill road.

At him.

At Makima, one step behind him.

He keeps walking. He doesn't know what else to do.

She doesn't come to meet him.

She waits at the bottom of the porch steps. She has her arms crossed. She's very pale, and very still, and there's something in her face he's never seen there before, not even in the gate, not even when the box turned red.

He stops in front of her.

Makima stops behind him.

The yard goes quiet. He can feel it go quiet: everyone on the porch, everyone at the windows, Beru at the end of the porch with his wings half open, Ori in the doorway with her hand in Frieren's sleeve. Cross by the training posts, arms at his sides.

“Where were you,” says Yuka. ʸ

It isn't a question.

He could lie.

He doesn't. He's been calling things fine that aren't. This answer, at least, comes out plainly.

“A dungeon,” he says. “Past the ruins. A wild one. Medium.” ᴹ

Her face does something.

“A dungeon,” she says. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Today. All day.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She looks past him. At Makima.

“With *her*,” she says. ʸ

“Yuka—” ᴹ

“You went to a *dungeon*,” she says. Her voice is getting louder. “Without telling me. Without a note. You were gone before I woke up. And you took *her*.” ʸ

“She has to come. Noise holds her body; she can't be far from—” ᴹ

“I promised you,” says Yuka. ʸ

Her voice cracks.

“At the quarry. I said *from now on I'm coming with you*. Every dungeon. *Every one*. I said it at the bell tower. I said it at the warm house. I said it in the *tent*, Mau, with the lamp. And you went without me. And you took her instead.” ʸ

He opens his mouth.

“I didn't want to bother you,” he says. “You were—” ᴹ

He stops. He doesn't know how to finish it. *You were busy*. *You were happy*. *You were with—*

“You were with Cross,” he says. ᴹ

He sees it land.

He sees her face go white. Then not white. He can see colour on her; he's always been able to see colour on her. He sees it flood up her throat and into her face, all at once, the way it did in the yard when he told her he could feel her heartbeat. Only it isn't that. It's nothing like that.

“*Don't*,” she says. ʸ

“I just meant—” ᴹ

“*Don't you dare*,” says Yuka. “Don't you dare make this about Cross. You *closed your door*. You closed *me* out. I reached for you this morning and you weren't there. I reached for your things and they were shut. I reached for *you*, Mau, in my head, where you always are, and there was a *door*. And Ciel told me you didn't want anyone near you. And I sat on the grass and couldn't breathe.” ʸ

She's shaking.

“And you were in a dungeon,” she says. “With her. All day.” ʸ

He doesn't know what to say.

Everything he could say is the wrong thing. He can see that. He can see it the way he sees the flow in a technique: every line of it, every way it could go, and all of them going wrong.

*I closed it because I couldn't think.*

*I closed it because every time I noticed the link I imagined what you were about to tell me.*

*I closed it because you said you wanted to talk to me about something and I thought I knew what the something was.*

He doesn't say any of it.

He stands there.

“Say something,” says Yuka. ʸ

He can't.

“*Say something*,” she says. “You always say something. You always tell me. You *promised*. Even the ugly things. Say it.” ʸ

He looks at her.

The only colour in the world.

He opens his mouth, and nothing comes out.

She looks past him again.

At Makima.

He feels Makima behind him. Through the thread. Very still. Very quiet. She hasn't said a word. She's been standing there the whole time with her hands folded in front of her, exactly where Noise put her, a step behind his shoulder.

“You'd rather have her,” says Yuka. ʸ

Her voice has gone very quiet. Worse than loud.

“That's it, isn't it. When it's bad, you go to her. She sits with you on the steps. She goes where you go. She can't be far from you. You *built* it that way.” ʸ

“That's not—” ᴹ

“I had to hear it from Beru,” Yuka says, quieter still. “Shoko got a message about her tea. Even she got a message.” ʸ

He could say there was only one person he couldn't bear to leave a message for. He'd have to tell her why.

“I let her in,” says Yuka. ʸ

Her voice breaks.

“I made her a rule,” she says. “On the contract day. I let her in my *head*. I said good night to her in the gate. I gave her a whole day on the island. I let her hold your hand.” ʸ

She looks at Makima.

“I had *started to trust you*,” she says. ʸ

Makima flinches.

He feels it. Through the thread. Something in her goes very small and very cold, all at once, like a candle going out.

She doesn't say anything. She can't, he thinks. She hasn't asked if she can speak, and it isn't important, not the way the rule means, and Yuka didn't come to her.

“Yuka.” Maki's voice comes from the steps. “You can be angry with him without doing this to her.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka's eyes move toward her grandmother and back.

Makima takes a step.

Not toward Yuka. Not away. Sideways. Closer. Until she's half behind him, at his shoulder, the way a child stands behind someone when someone else is shouting. Her face does something he's never seen Makima's face do. It crumples. Very slightly. She draws breath, measures the room that is really a yard full of people, and finds no place in it that she can stand without worsening the argument.

She puts one hand on the back of his shirt.

Just one. Small. Holding on.

Yuka sees it.

He watches her see it.

Makima, half hidden behind her husband, holding the back of his shirt, with her face crumpled. The best strategist her world ever had, who controlled everyone, hiding behind Mau like a scolded child.

He knows what it looks like.

He knows exactly what it is. Ori does it behind Frieren when Gojo is too loud. A friend hiding behind a friend.

That's all it is.

He knows that's not what Yuka sees.

Something in Yuka's face breaks.

He watches it happen. He'll see it for a long time afterward, at night, with his eyes closed. Not anger. Past anger. Something giving way.

“Maybe I shouldn't have married you,” she says. ʸ

The yard stays quiet. Somewhere off the porch, a cup that was already tipped over rolls far enough to stop against a step.

Mau looks at her left hand. The ring is still there. They'd had half a dozen mornings since the island when she put that hand flat on his chest and told him to stay in bed. One of those mornings was yesterday.

She can see where he's looking. She curls her fingers in as if she's only just found out what she said. “You let me make a whole life around being the person you'd come to. What's left for me to do if you can manage without me?” ʸ

He tries to get out *I couldn't*. He's been trying since he got down the hill. His hand opens where she can see it. He'd have been glad if she put anything in it at all.

“Yuka.” It's Shoko this time, from the steps. “He heard you.” ˢʰ

Yuka searches his face. He can't tell if she's waiting for him to take it away from her or for what she's going to say next. She looks beyond him for long enough that he checks who's there. Cross has one hand on the training post, exactly where he was before she said it.

“Did he?” she asks Shoko. “Everybody keeps telling me what he's heard. He can ask me himself.” ʸ

Shizue sets a dishcloth down over the rail. “You said you wanted to ask him where he'd been.” ˢʰⁱ

“I asked him for more than that.” Yuka takes one breath like she's ready to say what it was. Then she looks at Makima again. “Cross was there this morning too. He actually heard what I was asking.” ʸ

Mau sees Shizue's fingers tighten at the edge of the cloth. He can't remember saying anything that would let her tell Yuka where he was in all of this. He still has his hand partway out.

“Fine,” she says. ʸ

Very quietly.

“Fine. Go. Go with her. That's what you want, isn't it? You closed the door so I'd stop bothering you. So go. She'll go with you everywhere. She *has* to.” ʸ

She steps back.

“I'll stop bothering you,” she says. ʸ

She turns round.

“I love you, Yuka.” ᴹ

He hears his own voice and knows he's said it out loud because she stops. He hadn't gotten as far as thinking he would say her name. All of it has come out at the volume he'd use if she were standing close enough to hear him from the other side of their pillow.

She looks back. He sees her lower her shoulders, as if she's known for a while that he would say it, and now she's heard him she can stop waiting.

“I know you do, Mau,” she says. ʸ

He waits for the rest in the time it takes her to turn back. He knows how quickly she usually says it. Sometimes he hasn't finished before she does.

“Cross,” she says. “Will you walk with me?” ʸ

She's looking at Cross when she asks. Mau sees him get one foot off the ground before she even starts across the yard. She leaves him room to come up beside her. Neither of them looks back to ask whether Mau has something else to say.

Shoko comes down a step.

“Stay on the grounds tonight,” she says. “We'll talk when you can hear it.” ˢʰ

Yuka gives a sharp nod without turning round. Cross catches up to her at the last training post. Mau can't hear what Cross says. He can hear Yuka answer him.

Mau stands in the yard.

He doesn't move. He can't. The light is going long and gold across the grass, and he can't see gold, and he can see her, small and far away, going into the orchard with Cross a step behind her.

The only colour in the world, walking away.

*Go with her*, she said. *I'll stop bothering you.*

He thinks: *I knew*.

He thinks it very clearly. Very calmly. In the voice he gave Gojo the numbers in.

The band on her hand moves when she turns it to go. He notices the small line of light on one edge and remembers getting it stuck halfway over her knuckle the day he put it on. She helped him with the last part before he'd had time to decide there was anything to be embarrassed about. He'd remembered that before without it hurting.

She said she'd tell him when she was angry. He said he'd tell her when he needed her. He can still hear his own voice getting through that part in front of everyone. Right now there is one person who needs to hear it and he can't get it out.

He knew, and he got out of the way, the way he got out of Frieren's way when Fern came. Before she had to ask. So it wouldn't hurt when she did.

He was right.

It hurts anyway.

Behind him, Makima's hand is still on the back of his shirt.

It's shaking.

He reaches back without looking, and finds it, and takes it. Not the way Yuka holds his hand. Palm to palm, thumb over the back. A friend's handshake. The way he did on the wall.

He holds it.

He doesn't know who's holding whom up.

On the porch, nobody moves.

Maki has her hand over her mouth. Mai is looking at the ground. Yuta has his arm round both of them. Shoko is standing in the clinic doorway, very still, with an unlit cigarette in her fingers that she isn't going to light.

Gojo is on the wall with his blindfold off, looking at Mau with the bright eyes, and for once his face isn't doing anything at all.

Frieren has gone very pale. She's looking at Mau the way she sometimes looks at Himmel across the table, as if she's watching something she understood too late, happening again to someone else.

Ori is in the doorway with her hand in Frieren's sleeve.

She lets go of the sleeve.

She comes down the porch steps, past the mug on its side by the step, across the yard, to her father. She doesn't say anything. She takes his other hand.

He stands in the yard in the last of the light, with Makima's hand in one of his and Ori's in the other, and the orchard empty in front of him where Yuka went.

Maru finds him there, at dark.

He comes across the yard from the north road, quickly, the way he doesn't usually move, and stops in front of Mau, and looks at the three of them, and at the orchard, and at Mau's face. Whatever he sees there, he doesn't ask about.

“Word came,” he says. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Mau looks at him.

“Dabura,” says Maru. “He's on his way. He'll be at your quarry three mornings from now, at dawn. He says—” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

He hesitates.

“He says he's tired of waiting to see if the king is real,” says Maru. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Mau doesn't say anything for a long time.

Then he nods.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is very level.

“Three mornings,” he says. “At dawn. Tell him I'll be there.” ᴹ

Maru looks at him. At his hands, in Makima's and Ori's. At the orchard.

“Are you—” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“It's fine,” says Mau. ᴹ

Inside him, very quietly, Ciel says nothing at all.
