# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 21 — The Day Between

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S13E114
**Continuity state:** B+430 night to B+431 late morning. Ori sleeps beside Mau by choice. Yuka stays with Frieren in Ori's room. Makima asks Mau to stay home until the duel. At the workshop the next morning, Mai confronts the unannounced cut and they restore the supply on its own with Ciel. The personal lines stay closed.

*When: B+430, late night, into B+431, after breakfast. The notebook is still open to "57".*

She doesn't come to bed.

He waits. He sits on the edge of the bed in the dark with his boots still on, and listens to the house going to sleep round him, and waits for her footsteps on the stairs.

They don't come.

At some point, very late, the door opens.

Not Yuka. Ori. In her nightdress, barefoot, with her pillow under one arm.

She closes the door behind her very quietly. She crosses the room. She climbs onto the bed, on Yuka's side, and puts her pillow where Yuka's pillow is, on top of it, and lies down.

“Yuka's in my room,” she says, into the dark. ᴼ

He doesn't say anything.

“She's asleep,” says Ori. “She cried first. Then she slept. Mama's with her.” ᴼ

She pulls the blanket up to her chin.

“So I'm sleeping here,” she says. “I wanted to be with you.” ᴼ

He lies down beside her.

He doesn't take his boots off. He doesn't think about it. He just lies on his back on top of the blanket with his boots on and his daughter beside him, very small and very determined, on Yuka's pillow.

She finds his hand in the dark. She holds it.

“Dad,” she says. ᴼ

“Mm.” ᴹ

“Is it the duel?” ᴼ

He thinks about it.

“Partly,” he says. ᴹ

“And the other part,” says Ori. ᴼ

He doesn't answer.

She doesn't push. She lies there holding his hand, and after a while her breathing slows, and her hand goes loose in his, and she's asleep.

He lies awake.

He gets up at the darkest part of the night.

He does it carefully, so as not to wake her. He tucks the blanket round her. He goes down the stairs in his boots, which he never took off, and through the dark house, to the back door.

He knows what he's going to do.

The quarry. Now. Tonight. Not three mornings from now at dawn, the way Maru said. Now, while it's dark, while everyone's asleep, while nobody can follow him. He'll go to the quarry and wait there, and when Dabura comes, early or on time, he'll be there, alone, and he'll do it, and it'll be done.

He doesn't need anyone. He doesn't want anyone. He closed the doors so nothing would reach him, and nothing will.

He opens the back door.

Makima is sitting on the porch steps.

In the body Noise made for her, with her hands folded in her lap, facing the yard. Waiting.

She doesn't turn round.

“You felt it,” he says. ᴹ

“You were very loud,” says Makima. “You've been very loud for an hour. Deciding.” ᴹᵏ

He stands in the doorway.

“Move,” he says. ᴹ

“No,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

He comes down the steps. He stops beside her.

She still doesn't look up. She's looking at the yard. At the dark shape of the training posts. At the orchard beyond, where Yuka went.

“If you go now, Ori wakes up and you're gone,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He grips the door. A draught is coming in along the frame. He had checked there was enough blanket over Ori's shoulder and never once pictured her waking up without him there.

“I have to go.” ᴹ

“You go in two days,” says Makima. “At dawn. With Gojo and Frieren and Jin-Woo watching from the ridge. With the house knowing where you are. Not now. Not in the dark. Not alone, two days early, so you can stand in a quarry by yourself and wait for someone to come and kill you.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at her.

“You want to stop waiting,” Makima says. “That much reaches me. It doesn't tell me what you'll do when you get there.” ᴹᵏ

She finally turns her head and looks up at him.

“I want to come back,” he says. ᴹ

“Then let someone see you leave. Let them know when to expect you. You know how to make that plan.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at the dark yard.

“Then come in for a minute,” she says. “You can be angry at me for asking.” ᴹᵏ

He stands there a long time.

Then he sits down on the step beside her.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't take his hand. She just sits there, beside him, in the dark, the way she sat on the wall.

After a while, he puts his face in his hands.

He doesn't make a sound.

Makima doesn't say anything. She doesn't touch him. She just stays, a friend on a step, while the sky starts very slowly going grey over the hills.

He gets as far as breakfast on B+431.

He spends it in the workshop with Mai. He doesn't decide to; he just goes there, after breakfast, which he doesn't eat, and Mai looks at him and gives him a beam for the storage rack she's adding beneath the new floor. He holds it while she checks the dry joint. She has not started her Construction yet.

Mai doesn't ask him to explain Yuka. She puts the first beam down before she touches her Construction.

“My supply stopped yesterday,” she says. “The joint was empty, so I could stop. You have to tell me before you do that.” ᴹᵃ

He looks at the tools. The prepared socket. The work she has set aside.

“I didn't think about where you were in it.” ᴹ

“I noticed.” ᴹᵃ

“I'm sorry.” He rests a hand beside hers on the bench. “Can we put that part back without opening the rest?” ᴹ

Ciel answers privately: the supply path and the personal conversation can be separated. They check it with Mai before she begins. She draws a little, tests the joint and lets it go. Nothing is taken from her own reserve.

“Tell me if you're going to stop it again,” she says. Then she puts the next beam in his hands. ᴹᵃ

He holds it still while she builds. There is work they can do together without either of them pretending the rest is settled.

Yuka is in the orchard.

He knows without looking. He can't feel her; the door's closed. But he can see the orchard from the workshop window, if he lets himself look, and he does let himself, once, around noon.

She's on the wall. The same wall. With her knees drawn up and her arms round them, and Cross sitting beside her, not too close for once, not talking. Just sitting.

She's very still.

He looks away.

He doesn't look again.
