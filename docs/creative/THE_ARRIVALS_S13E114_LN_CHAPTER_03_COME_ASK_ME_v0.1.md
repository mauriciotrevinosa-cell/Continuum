# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 22 — Come Ask Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S13E114
**Continuity state:** B+431. Yuka and Mau see each other after the yard while the house arranges a river handoff. He has told the household where he's going and asks her whether she has time to talk. She defends having left with Cross. Maru and Rimuru continue the river work; Shuna and Maki keep care moving inside the house. Yuka's personal link stays closed. Dabura is due at dawn on B+433.

*When: B+431. After Mai starts on the rack beneath the workshop.*

The handles on the empty crates say how many people it takes to carry them. The first one Ori brings Yuka has three handles. It has been made out of another crate and one side of something that used to be a drawer.

“Which one do I hold?” asks Yuka. ʸ

Ori sets it down to look. “The good one. I'll take the other good one and then we won't need the third.” ᴼ

They get the crate into the yard that way. The weight is all on Yuka's end. Ori puts both hands under hers and decides out loud that they can bring it back empty without ever having put anything in it. Yuka sets it down where Rimuru is sorting cloth over the backs of two chairs. It's going up to the ford. Maru knows a woman who can mend one of the cart covers if they send her enough to replace the bad part.

There's a little straight stretch of table left for a cup. Yuka puts hers there. The apple she took out of Shuna's basket at breakfast is untouched in her pocket.

Mau comes down from the workshop with Mai's level in his hand. Momo meets him at the first step and puts a hand up for it. He gives it to her. He pauses long enough to make sure both her hands are free to get it up the stairs. By the time he turns back he's looking right at Yuka.

She gets ready to hear him say her name. She has to put a great deal of effort into standing the way she'd been standing a moment earlier, with one hand around her cup and all her weight on one foot.

“Maru wants to show me where they can bring the cart down,” Mau says. “I'll be on our side of the ford till lunch. From there I'm coming back to the house.” ᴹ

She waits. There's no sound except Ori scraping the crate away from the feet of the good chair.

“Is that for me?” ʸ

“Yes,” he says. “If you've got a minute before I go, I'd like to talk to you.” ᴹ

She was ready for him to look past her, or ask Rimuru to tell her where he was going. Now he's done neither and she still can't take her hand off the cup.

“I couldn't ask you where you were yesterday,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Can I ask you today?” ʸ

“Here I am.” He looks toward the far side of the post. There are two people working at the table. “We could go round the side if you'd rather.” ᴹ

She reaches for him without moving. It's what she used to do in the middle of something, when she wanted to know if he'd noticed what she had. There is still nothing on the other end. He sees her hand tightening on the cup. He doesn't reach for it either.

“I’d rather be able to talk to you the way I could on the day before yesterday,” she says. ʸ

Mau takes a moment. “I can hear you here,” he says. ᴹ

“Until you go somewhere I can't.” ʸ

The apple is cold against her hip. She knows she could put the cup down and go round the post with him. She thinks of having to say she walked out, of hearing herself say Cross's name, and suddenly she has all the courage in the world for the argument she knows how to have.

“I had to ask people where you were. Do you know what that felt like? In my own house?” ʸ

“I should have told you where I was going,” he says. ᴹ

“Then open it.” ʸ

She hears herself ask it like an instruction. She could ask again and make it sound different. She watches him rub the inside corner of his thumb over the other hand. He doesn't look down until he answers.

“I don't want to open it in the middle of this.” ᴹ

“What am I meant to do if you decide you don't want me to find you again?” ʸ

“Come ask me,” he says. “I'll tell you.” ᴹ

She sets the cup down too close to the cloth. It gets caught up under the handle. She works the corner free so she can hear how much she's raising her voice.

“Cross didn't make me ask him twice last night. He came with me because I was upset. He could see that.” ʸ

His thumb goes still.

“I could see that,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Then why didn't you come?” ʸ

“You told me to go with her.” He lets his hand fall. “I thought I'd heard you properly.” ᴹ

The edge of the cup is still damp where she touched it. She can remember Cross turning his head when she went past him. She'd kept her pace until she heard him behind her, then slowed down. She hasn't told anybody that part. She'd wanted someone there, and she knew who would follow without asking what she'd meant.

“You'd already made your choice by then,” she says. ʸ

Mau waits. There is time here, even now, for her to tell him which choice she means. She hasn't managed to say whether it was the gate, or what she'd seen when he came home, or why she hadn't gone upstairs to ask. She looks at the table. Maru is standing with both palms flat on the cloth.

“Will you give me a minute with her, Maru?” asks Mau. ᴹ

“Of course.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Yuka knows how it would feel if the whole yard emptied and left them right here. She picks up her cup.

“I don't want to talk right now,” she says. ʸ

Mau turns to her. There is a beat where he keeps both feet where they are. His mouth barely moves when he says her name. She can still hear it when she puts her first foot on the porch step. She keeps going, into the kitchen.

* * *

Inside, Shuna has put the big spoon down on a folded cloth so the handle won't roll off the counter. She has onions going in one pan and something else in the oven, and somebody has put the lid on the pot she needs to see into.

“Get that off for me,” she says when she sees Yuka. ˢʰᵘ

Yuka lifts it. Steam gets to the bottom of her eyes. She leaves her face there for too long. Shuna pulls the spoon closer to her end of the counter.

“Did you eat your apple?” she asks. ˢʰᵘ

Yuka takes it out of her pocket. One side has picked up the shape of the seam.

“No.” ʸ

“Cut it in half. Leave me some.” ˢʰᵘ

She sets it on the board. The knife goes through the bruise first. Both halves are perfectly fine past that little dark place, and she turns one so Shuna can see where to pick it up. Nobody comes in from the yard to tell her what a fool she's made of herself.

Maki comes in with Ori. Ori has a piece of cloth from the bottom of one of the crates. She only wants Maki to say whether it could be a new flag if she drew on the good side. Maki tells her to spread it out and see what fits. Then she leans a hip against the counter, takes half the apple Shuna hasn't gotten to yet, and cuts a piece off for Ori.

“He told you where he was going,” she says to Yuka. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Yes.” ʸ

“You can still be angry about yesterday.” Maki wipes the knife. “Today he asked if you'd talk.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“He wants me to decide when it's over and he won't even let me reach him.” ʸ

“He was standing in front of you, Yuka.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka doesn't have a good answer for that. She takes the spoon Shuna leaves next to her and stirs the pot so nothing sticks. Across the kitchen, Ori is trying to see if a giraffe would fit upright on the flag. Before she finishes, Maki has to move both their cups.

* * *

By late afternoon the empty crates have gone out on the cart. Cross comes back with a piece of the old cover that he kept so Shuna can see how much is left to mend. He finds Yuka where she's putting the good chairs back. He helps by taking the far side of one. It goes through the door crooked and they have to back up and turn it. She laughs. She makes the sound before she's ready for someone to hear her do it.

“You can come round with me,” she says when the chair is in. “I need to get out for a minute.” ʸ

They go down as far as the orchard wall. There is a shadow soldier at the far end with a rope for the next cart. Cross gives it room. Yuka wants to know whether Mau went up as far as the ford like he said he would. She can ask Ciel. She asks Cross.

“He did. He was back when he said he'd be back.” ᶜʳ

She can look up from where she stands and see the gate he came through last night. Nothing is waiting there. She could go home with the piece of canvas, put her cup down on the table beside him, and tell him he was right about one thing.

“I was trying to tell him why I was angry,” she says. ʸ

Cross looks down at the cloth over his arm. “Did he let you tell him?” ᶜʳ

“He wanted to go round the side and talk.” ʸ

“Then he had a good idea where he wanted that conversation to go.” ᶜʳ

She looks at him. “He usually just asks me what I think.” ʸ

“Does he?” asks Cross. ᶜʳ

Yuka can hear that he means for her to think about it. It feels like being handed a question she could answer any way she wanted. She doesn't tell him about Mau asking for the extra minute. She knows what it feels like to ask after Mau when he's late getting back. Tonight he's home and she has walked out again. If she goes up there with a story about what happened in the yard, he'll have one too. She isn't ready to hear where his starts.

“Can we make one more round before we go in?” she asks. ʸ

Cross takes the path away from the house. She walks beside him.

* * *

The light in Ori's room is on when Yuka goes upstairs. Ori is still cutting the flag. Frieren sits by the window with its edge on her knee so Ori can use both hands on the scissors. There's an empty place beside them on the floor, big enough for Yuka and a pile of offcuts.

Yuka stops in the hall. She can see the brass handle on her own door from here. Last night Mau was in there when she came halfway up the stairs and changed her mind. Tonight she could knock while she knows where he is. She would have to wait for him to answer, and this time stay if he asked her what she'd meant.

“Can we have the big scissors?” Ori asks. ᴼ

Yuka gets them from the other room. Mau's boots are gone. The window is open, and the notebook is still on the sill. The number is the one she wrote the morning he went into the gate.

She brings the scissors to Ori. She gets down where Frieren has left her room on the floor, turns the blades toward herself, and asks Ori to tell her where to start.
