# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 23 — A Place to Look

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S13E114
**Continuity state:** B+432. The house prepares the quarry approach. Mau offers Yuka the route and asks whether she still wants to come if the argument remains unresolved. Yuka holds on to the earlier promise and brings the gate back into the conversation. Shoko and Makima help him later that day. Ori asks for a goodbye before dawn. Mau leaves the remaining two thirds of his points unspent.

*When: B+432. The last full day before Dabura.*

Shuna gets an elbow under the bottom of the door before Mau can close it. She has three bowls stacked to her chin, two of them empty. He takes all three. The empty ones are warm from the stove.

“Are you coming down to eat or are we doing this one on the stairs?” she asks. ˢʰᵘ

“Are there people on the stairs?” ᴹ

“You. In a minute.” ˢʰᵘ

He puts the warm bowls on his knee and sits on the top step. She puts one of the long spoons from the kitchen in his hand and sits down on the step below. It's still morning. There's a little light over the top of the hall door and he's counted every tile that catches it.

Maki passes them twice. The first time she's looking for a strap to mark where the quarry trail gets narrow. The second time Ori is with her, holding the folded end of it like it's in danger of being drafted for something else. Shuna has to move her legs to let them go by. She tells Ori to let her know if there's anything left over for a peg bag.

Mau eats enough to give himself a reason to put the spoon down. He picks it up again while Shuna gets up and puts her hands out for the empty bowls. She takes those two downstairs. He takes the bottom one himself.

* * *

Rimuru has both routes out of the quarry on the table. Jin-Woo asks where the soil gives way above the lower turn. Maki finds the spot with a finger and Ori brings her the strip of cloth. The person who goes up first can fix it to something that won't move. The paper curves up underneath her hand. Frieren keeps one end down with a cup. There's nothing in it. When Mau gets close enough to see, a loose hair has caught underneath the edge.

“Gojo and I walked it,” says Jin-Woo. “Nobody has to take the lower path. It gives us somewhere to go if the ridge gets cut off.” ᴶʷ

Mau looks along the edge of the page. He can picture how much of the stone the ridge will let him see.

“Is the low shelf clear?” he asks. ᴹ

Frieren follows his finger. “That bit?” ᶠ

“I told Yuka I'd save a place where I could see her.” ᴹ

“Well inside the scouts,” says Jin-Woo. “Tell her where to go.” ᴶʷ

Mau takes an extra piece of paper from by Rimuru's elbow. He draws the upper trail again. He marks the place and has gone as far as starting a second line from it before he realises he wants space to write something. Nothing he wants to say will fit in the gap he's left.

Frieren stays after the others take the big map. She sees him folding the small one and puts the empty cup back on the page.

“I thought there would be time,” she says. ᶠ

He looks up.

“With Himmel. I thought I could understand him later, when it was convenient. So I didn't ask. I let him be kind and I was kind back and neither of us said anything.” ᶠ

He has a fold in the middle of the ridge. He gets it out with his nail.

“It took me ten years and a funeral to find out what I'd missed,” says Frieren. “I don't want it to take you that long.” ᶠ

She puts the cup in his hand on her way out. He takes it back to the kitchen. A minute later he picks up the map and goes to find Yuka.

* * *

She's sitting on the inside of the low wall with one of Ori's planks across her knees. She's drawing the edge of something on it and rubbing marks out with her thumb. He stops far enough back to see the whole drawing. It's a row of teeth. Ori will probably want those done in white.

“I've got the way up for tomorrow,” he says. ᴹ

She looks over at him. He has the map in his hand already. She braces the wood on the wall so it won't fall when she lets it go, and he has one wholly unreasonable second of hoping she'll ask him to sit before she takes the paper.

She unfolds it against the wood. He points to the entrance without leaning on her knee. She has her finger on the exact spot from the first time she looks at it.

“This was where you wanted me,” she says. ʸ

“Still is.” ᴹ

She lets the bottom of the paper lift. He can see the two places where he drew the same bit of the ridge.

“Will you come if we're still mad in the morning?” he asks. ᴹ

Her eyes come up.

“I said I'd be there when you looked up.” ʸ

“I know you said it.” He has to make himself finish. “You also told me to go.” ᴹ

She looks down at the drawing. She presses her thumb into the place with the teeth, which moves the wood away from its support. He catches it by the end. It stays between them at an angle.

“You went before I told you anything,” she says. “That's the part I can't get past. You keep acting like it started out here.” ʸ

“No,” he says. “I left and I didn't tell you. I'm asking you now, in front of me, because I want you there.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment. Then she goes to put the map on the wall, underneath the chalk, where he can take it back.

“I don't want to go up there and find out you're fine without me,” she says. ʸ

He could have answered that. He's spent most of two nights answering it into the dark. Somehow what he hears in time to speak is the same thing he heard in the yard, that she sees him choose somewhere else before he knows what he's chosen.

“I wouldn't have drawn this for you if I was fine without you.” ᴹ

She looks at him. Something in her face gives a little, and he almost bends his knee to sit next to her. She picks up the chalk first.

“It's easy to tell me that when all I have to do is watch,” she says. ʸ

His hand is on the bottom of the plank. He gets it level against her knee before he takes his fingers away.

“I was asking if you'd come,” he says. ᴹ

“I know what you were asking.” ʸ

He puts the map back next to her. It will stay there if she keeps the chalk on it. She sets the chalk down without looking at his hand.

“You can use it,” he says. “It isn't going to matter to anyone which way you decide to go up.” ᴹ

He makes it around the end of the wall. Before there's a chance she might look back, he thinks of three other things he could have said. At the bend he stops trying to work them into sentences.

* * *

Shoko finds him by the pump. He has the handle up with one hand and the jug under the spout. The water has been going past the jug long enough to soak the sleeve of his other arm.

“Can I take that?” she asks. ˢʰ

He lets her get a hand around the handle before he lets go of it. There's no force left in his fingers by the time she puts it down. He puts his hand on the post. He thought he could come get water without needing to hear anybody say his name.

*Both feet,* says Noise. ᴺ

He checks. One of them is braced against the step, with nothing under the heel. He gets it down. He stands long enough to tell Shoko he isn't dizzy enough to fall. She slides the stool out from under the pump with her foot anyway.

“I asked her,” he says. ᴹ

“About the quarry?” ˢʰ

He nods. The wet sleeve has started to stick to him.

“Did you tell her you want her there?” ˢʰ

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Her hand settles on the back of the stool, close enough to him that he doesn't have to say anything when he sits. She waits until he's able to pick up the jug before she takes him across to the clinic and gives him a towel for his sleeve.

Makima comes by when he's finished turning it back the right way. She tells him there's time to get as far as Bocchi's house and back before supper. Bocchi has wanted him to come along and help her father settle something about a cable, and asked Makima if she knew where he'd gone. Makima sits on the step while he decides. When he comes out, she stays sitting until he has got both feet on the path.

He goes. Futari has got two completely different plugs lined up on the floor and decided that one of them belongs to a kettle. She announces this with enough certainty that he gives the plug another look. By the time Bocchi's dad digs out the matching one, Mau is on the carpet with Futari trying to identify the sound of the stupidest appliance in the house without looking. Bocchi's mother wins from the hallway. Mau stays another ten minutes. When he leaves, Bocchi walks him to the door and makes him promise to come back when they have pie. He asks if her dad knows about the pie yet. She says that's what she's going to tell him now.

* * *

At dinner Ori sits across from his chair, beside Frieren. She has a piece of bread she can't tear without pulling the whole loaf off the plate. He holds the other end while she gets it apart. She gives him half on the condition that he comes to find her before he goes over the hill.

“I'd like that,” he says. “I'll come find you.” ᴹ

She's put the good half of the bread by his elbow. He gives Frieren the heel she likes at the same time, before Ori can think of the next question. Near the back door Milim is telling Gojo that she can count how many sweets are in his pocket from here. She gets the number right. Gojo makes her prove it by taking them all out and putting them on the bench one by one. Ori wants to see what happens when she gets to the last one.

Mau stays to finish the bread.

That night, alone in the bedroom, he opens the page.

The stat page. The name at the top. The level. The fields: *Strength. Speed. Endurance. Perception.* Each raised by part of the first allocation. One third of the saved pool spent. The rest stands where he put it the first time he looked.

> **[Unallocated points remaining: two thirds of the accumulated total.]**

*You'll know when,* Jin-Woo had told him when they cleared the map away.

He closes it. He doesn't spend anything.

Ori comes in when Frieren brings her up to say goodnight. Her boots go neatly under the chair and her pillow goes on top of Yuka's again. He's still awake when her hand slips off the blanket. He puts it back where she'd have it if she were looking for him.

The last number in the notebook is still *57*. Across the hall, there are two steps by Yuka's door and he knows the sound both of them make. He gets all the way to dawn listening.
