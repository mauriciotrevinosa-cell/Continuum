# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 23 — Forty Seconds

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S13E115
**Continuity state:** B+433, the first phase of the fight. Dabura's light moves faster than sound, faster than the eyes can follow; Mau survives on Infinity while the eyes and Noise read the light and start building copies of what they see. Infinity holds the first exchanges. Dabura is delighted and gets *interested*, which is bad: he stops being careless. Then he finds the trick: the wordless intent can reach Mau's cursed energy itself and cut it, just for an instant, and in that instant Infinity thins. He finds the thin place on Mau's right side, the one Gojo warned about, and goes through it. Mau takes the first real hits. The daggers and the hands (one third of the points) work in the gaps. RCT is always on, so the wounds close, but the hits are getting closer together. On the ridge, Gojo: "That's my Infinity." Frieren: "He's alive because he hid first." No domains yet.

*When: B+433. The first minutes. On the ridge, Yuka has both hands over her mouth now.*

The first one doesn't touch him.

He doesn't see it. He can't. It's light; it moves the way light moves, and the eyes, which can see the flow of everything, which can read a technique before it's finished forming, are still telling him it's coming when it's already arrived.

It hits Infinity.

It stops. An inch from his chest. A line of pure bright pressure, stopped against the folded distance round him, the way everything has always stopped against it.

The quarry floor on either side of him isn't so lucky. The light that missed the Infinity goes on past, into the stone, and the stone stops being there. Not broken. Not melted. Just *not there*: two long smooth grooves in the floor of the quarry, running back to the wall behind him, and through the wall, and out the other side into the morning.

He looks at the grooves.

He looks at Dabura.

“Huh,” says Dabura. ᴰᵇ

* * *

“Again,” says Mau. ᴹ

Dabura grins.

* * *

It's fast.

It's faster than anything. Faster than Sukuna, who could cut the world. Faster than the Warden, who was a rule. Dabura doesn't move so much as stop being where he was and start being somewhere else, and the light comes from every direction at once, lines and bars and needles of it, and every single one of them hits the Infinity, and stops.

Mau doesn't move.

He doesn't need to. Not yet. He stands in the middle of the quarry with the light breaking on the folded distance round him like rain on a window, and lets the eyes look.

*I'm reading it*, says Noise. *Keep him busy. I need time.* ᴺ

“How much?” ᴹ

*More than he's going to give us.* ᴺ

* * *

The eyes read it.

That's what they do. That's what they've always done, since Gojo's eyes became his: see the shape of a technique, the flow, the *how*, and give it to Noise, and let Noise build. He's copied things that way his whole life. Sukuna's cuts. Makima's Bang. The way Maki's body listens.

The light is hard.

It's hard because it's fast, and because it's simple. There's nothing to it. No elaborate shape, no flow to follow. Just light, made, and sent, and arriving. The eyes look at it the way you'd look at the sun, and the information comes back too bright to hold.

*I've got the shape of the making*, says Noise. *Not the sending. The sending's too fast. Give me more.* ᴺ

He gives it more.

He stands there and lets Dabura throw everything.

* * *

On the ridge, Gojo has stopped eating.

He's sitting forward on his rock with his blindfold off and both eyes open, and his face isn't grinning. It's very still.

“That's my Infinity,” he says. ᴳ

Frieren doesn't look at him.

“He's standing in the middle of a technique that erases stone,” says Gojo, “and it's stopping an inch from his chest. Every time. That's *my* Infinity. I taught him that. On a porch.” ᴳ

“He's alive,” says Frieren, “because he hid first. If Dabura had known what he was from the start, he'd have come in at full. He didn't. He came in laughing. He's still warming up.” ᶠ

Gojo looks at her.

“You're keeping score,” he says. ᴳ

“So are you,” says Frieren. ᶠ

* * *

On the quarry floor, Dabura is having the time of his life.

Mau can see it. Through the eyes, through the sense turned low, through everything. Dabura is *delighted*. Every line of light that stops against the Infinity makes him laugh. He comes in closer, and closer, trying angles, trying speeds, trying everything, the way a child tries every key on a ring to see which one opens the door.

And then he stops laughing.

Not because he's angry. Because he's interested.

Mau feels it happen. The change. The looseness goes out of the way Dabura moves, and something else comes in. Attention. Focus. All of that vast bright speed, narrowing.

*He's stopped being careless*, says Ciel. *That's bad.* ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

*Jin-Woo said make him bored*, says Ciel. *You made him interested.* ᶜ

“I *know*, Ciel.” ᴹ

* * *

Dabura stops.

Fifty paces off. Standing on the quarry floor with his hands at his sides and the light gone quiet in him, looking at Mau. At the Infinity. Studying it, the way Mau studied the light.

“That's not a wall,” says Dabura. ᴰᵇ

Mau doesn't answer.

“I thought it was a wall,” says Dabura. “It isn't. Nothing's stopping the light. It just never gets there. There's more space between you and it than there should be. Isn't there?” ᴰᵇ

Mau doesn't answer.

Dabura smiles.

“And it's made of the cursed stuff,” he says. “Your energy. The thing you were hiding.” ᴰᵇ

He tilts his head.

“What happens,” he says, “if I touch *that*?” ᴰᵇ

* * *

The other thing comes up in him.

The thing with no word. Mau feels it before he sees it, before the eyes can read it: a pressure, enormous, pure, *wanting*. Wanting him dead. Not angrily. Not cruelly. Just wanting, the way water wants to go downhill.

It doesn't come at him.

It comes at the Infinity.

Not to break it. To *reach* it. To reach through it, past the folded distance, to the thing the Infinity is made of. His cursed energy. The flow of it, running out of him into the space round him, keeping everything an inch away.

The wanting touches it.

And for an instant, for less than an instant, the flow *stops*.

* * *

It's like a hand on a tap.

That's the only way he'll ever be able to describe it afterward. Something reaches in and closes the tap, for a heartbeat, and the cursed energy that's always flowing out of him into the Infinity just—doesn't.

And the Infinity goes thin.

Not everywhere. Not all at once. It goes thin first where it was already thin. On the right side. Where he's been letting more through so he can feel the air with Maki's sense. Where Gojo tapped him three times in the field. Where Gojo said *Dabura won't give you forty seconds*.

He doesn't.

He doesn't give him four.

* * *

The light comes through.

On the right. Through the thin place. A single line of it, very fine, very fast.

It goes through his right shoulder.

He feels it go through. Not a burn. Not a cut. A *subtraction*. A line of him that was there, and then wasn't: a hole, clean, smooth-sided, the width of a finger, front to back, through the meat of his shoulder below the collarbone.

He doesn't fall.

He's standing in the quarry with a hole in his shoulder, and the RCT is already there, already in the hole, because it's always on now, ticking over, the way Gojo taught him on the porch. It follows the subtraction in. It fills it. He feels the flesh come back, from the inside out, the way it came back after the Warden.

The hole closes.

It takes about two seconds.

Dabura watches it close.

His eyes go very wide.

* * *

“You *heal*,” he says. ᴰᵇ

“So do you,” says Mau. ᴹ

Dabura stares at him.

“I can see it,” says Mau. “Inside you. The same thing. Turned back on itself. You don't remember learning it.” ᴹ

Something goes across Dabura's face. Too fast to read. Then it's gone.

“No,” says Dabura slowly. “I don't.” ᴰᵇ

He looks at Mau for a long moment.

Then he laughs, short and sharp and delighted, and comes in.

* * *

Now it's a fight.

Not a wall and a storm. A fight. Dabura isn't throwing light from fifty paces anymore. He's *close*. In and out, faster than sound, and every time he comes in, the wanting comes first, a hand on the tap, and the Infinity goes thin, and the light comes through.

And Mau moves.

He has to. He can't stand still anymore. The first third of the points is in his body; he can feel it, the new speed, the new strength, his left hand as strong as his right. He moves the way he's never moved, out of the line of the light, round it, under it, and the daggers are in his hands, his own, real, the ones from the shadow.

Dabura comes in through the thin place on the right, and Mau isn't there. He's on the left, under Dabura's arm, and the dagger goes in.

Not deep. Dabura is fast. But it goes in.

Dabura makes a sound.

Not pain. Surprise.

* * *

They go back and forth across the quarry floor.

It's too fast to follow. Mau knows that from the outside, later, from what the people on the ridge tell him. Two shapes going across the quarry like lightning across a sky. Lines of light carving the walls. Pieces of the quarry floor going up into the air and coming down somewhere else.

From the inside, it's very simple.

The wanting comes. The tap closes. The Infinity thins. He has less than a heartbeat to not be where the light is going. And then the tap opens again, and the Infinity comes back, and he has a heartbeat to get the dagger in, or the fist, or Red, small, a flick, to push Dabura back half a step.

Over and over.

The holes start coming faster.

* * *

One in his side. Closes.

One through his left forearm, the one the points fixed. Closes.

One across the top of his thigh, a long shallow groove. Closes.

Every one closes. The RCT is always on. It doesn't wait. It follows every subtraction in and fills it.

But they're getting closer together.

*He's learning*, says Ciel. *The interruptions are getting longer. Not much. A fraction each time. He's figuring out how to hold the tap closed.* ᶜ

“How long?” ᴹ

*Until he can hold it closed long enough to put the light through something the RCT can't follow?* ᶜ

A pause.

*Not long*, says Ciel. ᶜ

* * *

*I need more time*, says Noise. *I've got the making. I've almost got the sending. Give me—* ᴺ

“I *know*,” says Mau. ᴹ

He's breathing hard. He notices that from a distance. His heart is going very fast.

And under everything, at the back of everything, the noise.

Not Noise. The other noise. The eyes, wide open, pouring in everything. Maki's sense, turned up now because he needs it, because he needs to feel the air move before Dabura does, pouring in everything else. Mini Ciel holding the flood. Noise, busy with the light, building, not holding anything else.

And under all of that, the other thing.

The room he emptied for Fern. The year in his hands. The plate. The chair. *You were with Cross. Don't you dare. I had started to trust you. I'll stop bothering you.*

All of it.

Coming up.

* * *

The tap closes.

He's a fraction late.

The light goes through his right side, low, under the ribs, and this one is wide. Not a finger. A hand. And it goes through something that matters, and he feels it, and he goes down on one knee on the quarry floor.

The RCT goes in after it. It fills the hole. It takes four seconds this time.

Four seconds on one knee.

Dabura doesn't come in.

He stands ten paces off, light quiet in him, looking down at Mau on one knee on the broken floor of the quarry where Mau once lay in pieces.

He isn't smiling.

* * *

On the ridge, Yuka makes a sound.

He can't hear it. He's too far, and the door's closed. But he looks up, because he can't not, because he always looks up, and she's standing on the edge of the quarry with both hands over her mouth, and her whole body is leaning forward, toward him, like someone about to step off a cliff.

Cross has his hand on her arm.

Holding her back.

* * *

Mau looks away.

He stands up. The hole is closed. His side is whole. His heart is going very fast.

Dabura is still looking at him.

“You're somewhere else,” says Dabura. ᴰᵇ

Mau goes very still.

“You were here,” says Dabura. “At the start. When you let it all go. You were *here*. All of you.” ᴰᵇ

He tilts his head.

“Now you're somewhere else,” he says. “Half of you. More than half. You keep looking up at the ridge.” ᴰᵇ

He doesn't come in.

He just stands there, with the light quiet in him, looking at Mau with an expression Mau has never seen on him. Not amusement. Not interest.

Something almost like disappointment.

“I came all this way,” says Dabura, “to fight a king. Not half of one.” ᴰᵇ
