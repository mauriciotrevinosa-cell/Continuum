# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 28 — Nothing Twice

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 28
**Source episode:** S13E115
**Continuity state:** B+433. Mau answers an opponent who adapts by never doing the same thing twice. Every exchange is a new combination: Dabura's own stolen light ("That's mine." "It was."), the katana with Rika, whole, out for the first time in a real fight, Authority on the quarry itself, Bang, Sky. And once, Frieren's first and most useless spell, the one that makes flowers bloom, opened under Dabura's feet, so that his body wastes a whole adaptation on something harmless while the real strike comes from behind. Frieren, on the ridge, has never been prouder. Dabura stops adapting in time and starts getting hit. He bleeds, closes, and laughs: "Finally something that doesn't break!" Mau understands him exactly. Pushed past everything his body knows, Dabura opens the domain he's never opened.

*When: B+433. Late morning going on noon. Ciel has started counting the exchanges, because Mau asked her to tell him if he ever repeats one.*

The first thing he shows him is his own.

The light. The point of it in Mau's palm, very small, very bright. He sends it the way Noise built it, the way Dabura sends his, faster than sound.

Dabura has never had his own technique thrown at him.

It takes him high in the left side and goes through. He looks down at the hole. It's closing. He looks up at Mau, and his face is doing two things at once: something enormous and delighted, and under it, outrage.

“That's *mine*,” he says. ᴰᵇ

“It was,” says Mau. ᴹ

*One*, says Ciel. ᶜ

* * *

The second thing is the katana.

He takes it out of the shadow. Kaisel's scale in the spine. Beru's chitin in the guard. Kamish's tooth ground into the edge. The ring at the base, three rings and the web between them, holding cursed energy and mana side by side.

And Rika.

He feels her wake. All of her, every form, the ones Yuta's ring held and the katana couldn't until the workshop, until Mai's corrected curve.

She comes up out of the blade behind him.

The great pale shape of her rises over the quarry floor. The arms. The face that is terrible and gentle at once. For the first time in a real fight, she isn't half of herself.

Dabura stops in the middle of the air.

“*What* is that,” he says. ᴰᵇ

“A friend's,” says Mau. ᴹ

Rika goes for him from above. Mau goes for him from below with the blade.

Dabura has never fought two things at once that weren't the same thing. He meets Rika with light, because she's the bigger shape. And the tooth-ground edge goes through his thigh from underneath, where nothing was looking.

*Two*, says Ciel. ᶜ

* * *

On the ridge, Tsurugi grabs Gojo's arm.

“That's Grandpa's,” he says. “That's *Rika*. How does he—” ᵀˢ

“The workshop,” says Frieren, without looking away. “Mai's curve.” ᶠ

* * *

Dabura learns Rika.

Of course he does. The third time she comes down, he's already gone, and the fourth time he's waiting for her arm and sends light up it, and she screams the way she screams and goes back into the katana like smoke going home.

So Mau doesn't call her a fifth time.

*Three. Four*, says Ciel. *You haven't repeated anything.* ᶜ

* * *

He takes hold of the quarry.

Not a rock. The quarry. Authority, the grip that isn't Blue, all of it, on the whole tier of stone under Dabura's feet. He doesn't throw it. He *tilts* it. The ground Dabura is standing on goes from flat to steep in the space of a breath.

Dabura's body knows how to dodge things coming at it. It has never once had the floor change its mind.

He slides.

Mau is waiting at the bottom with two fingers raised.

“Bang,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

It isn't a technique the way the others are. It's Makima's, the version he copied from her a long time ago: a small sharp nothing off the end of his fingers that arrives before it leaves. It takes Dabura in the shoulder, and the shoulder goes back, and he spins.

*Five*, says Ciel. ᶜ

Dabura comes up out of the spin already sending light, and Mau isn't there. He's above, on Sky, and when Dabura looks up he's not above, he's to the left, on a piece of air Dabura didn't know could be stood on.

*Six.* ᶜ

* * *

It goes on.

He doesn't repeat. He doesn't let himself. Light from the right, then the katana from the left, then Authority on Dabura's own arm, then Blue, but alone this time, small, on the far side so it pulls Dabura *toward* the katana instead of away. Then the stolen light again, but from Rika's hands, as she rises behind him for one breath and sends it and goes.

Every exchange new. He has practised holding two independent techniques at once, long enough to keep Infinity between himself and the light while Shrine cuts. The pressure builds behind his left eye anyway. He lets each cut finish before he gives his full attention to the next technique. Ciel counts the exchanges and checks how much of that pressure the RCT has time to relieve.

And it works.

Dabura's body, the body that learned against something that always changed to meet it, is trying to learn faster than Mau can change. It reaches for the last thing to prepare for the next, and the next thing is never the last thing. It's getting there. Each answer comes faster. But Mau is a breath ahead, then half a breath, then just barely, and Dabura is bleeding from a dozen places and closing them, bleeding and closing, and he can't get *ahead*.

*Eighteen*, says Ciel. *Nineteen.* ᶜ

* * *

On the twentieth, he gets it wrong.

Not badly. A fraction. He has Infinity up and Shrine cutting, the two he can hold, and Dabura comes in from a direction he didn't read in time, and his hand reaches for Blue before the Shrine has gone quiet.

Blue and Infinity are both Limitless. He's already held them together today. This time he tries to redirect the same technique while its flow is still being interrupted and Shrine is still cutting. His hand finishes the change before he's sorted out which part of the flow can carry it.

Ciel starts to tell him to let Shrine go.

Something behind his left eye *tears*.

He feels it go. The old damage, the eye that was never quite right since the duel. A line of white-hot pressure across the back of it, and the left half of the world going dark and bright and dark, and something warm running out of his nose, fast. Not the slow ache the RCT keeps ahead of all day. A sudden thing. Faster than the healing.

He drops the Blue. Drops the Shrine. Holds only Infinity.

The RCT is already there. It was always there. It goes into the tear and starts closing it. But it's *behind*. For three seconds, four, it's chasing the damage instead of keeping ahead of it, and the left side of the quarry flickers. Dabura's intent cuts the flow on that side and his light goes through Mau's shoulder before Mau finds his footing.

Five seconds. Six.

Then the RCT catches up. The tear closes. The left side of the world comes back.

*That*, says Ciel, very quietly, *is what a mistake costs.* ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

*The refresh keeps up with use*, says Ciel. *Not with errors. If that had been the domain, or a full combination, it wouldn't have been an eye.* ᶜ

“I *know*, Ciel.” ᴹ

He wipes his nose with the back of his hand. Dabura, ten paces off, is watching him with his head on one side. He saw. He saw something go wrong, and he doesn't know what, and he's very interested.

Mau goes back to two.

He doesn't reach for a third again. Not by accident.

*Twenty*, says Ciel. ᶜ

* * *

Then Mau does the stupid thing.

He doesn't plan it. It comes up out of his hands the way the cheapest tent did: from somewhere deeper than his head.

He opens the flower spell.

* * *

Frieren's. The first spell she ever gave him, a long time ago, on the road. Not Zoltraak. Before Zoltraak. A spell that does nothing at all except make flowers bloom. She taught it to him because she likes it. That was the only reason. *It's a good spell*, she said. *It's pretty.*

He opens it on the quarry floor, under Dabura's feet.

And the stone blooms.

* * *

Flowers. Hundreds of them. Pouring up out of the cracks in the broken floor in a wave, soft and bright, petals everywhere, a whole field of them in the middle of a fight to the death.

Dabura's body reacts.

He sees it with the eyes, perfectly. The new thing under Dabura's feet, the thing he's never seen, the thing that came up out of the ground from Mau's mana: his body does what it's done all morning. It reads the change as the start of an attack. Dabura angles his hips away from the first stalks and lowers his guard to cover the cracks they came through. He has already committed to that answer by the time one of the petals brushes his hand. His weight shifts. His light comes up, aimed down, at petals.

At nothing.

And behind him, very quietly, Mau puts the katana through his back.

* * *

On the ridge, Frieren makes a sound.

It isn't a word. It's a sound Mau has never heard her make, though he'll hear about it later from Gojo, at length, many times. A sort of hiccup. Her hand has gone over her mouth, and her ears have gone so high they're nearly straight up, and her face is doing something that on anyone else would be crying and on Frieren is something no one has a name for.

“That's mine,” she says, through her fingers. “That's the *flower* spell. That's the first one. I taught him that because it was *pretty*.” ᶠ

Gojo stares at her.

“He used a *flower spell*,” says Gojo. ᴳ

“He used my flower spell,” says Frieren, “to make the strongest Simurian alive waste his whole body on *flowers*.” ᶠ

Gojo looks at the quarry floor. At the field of flowers. At Dabura with a katana through him, looking down at petals with an expression of total bewilderment.

“Okay,” says Gojo slowly. “That one's yours.” ᴳ

* * *

Dabura pulls himself off the blade.

He does it forward, the way you'd step off something, and turns round, and the wound in his chest is closing, and he's standing in a field of flowers up to his knees.

He looks down at them.

He picks one. A small one. He holds it up between two long fingers and looks at it.

“What *is* this?” he says. ᴰᵇ

“A flower,” says Mau. ᴹ

“What does it *do*?” ᴰᵇ

“Nothing.” ᴹ

Dabura stares at it.

Then at Mau.

And then he laughs. Harder than he's laughed all morning. Harder than when he thought there was nothing there. He stands in the field of flowers with one in his hand and a katana wound closing in his chest and laughs until he has to bend over with his hands on his knees.

“*Nothing*,” he says. “It does *nothing*. And my body—I *answered* it—I put everything into—” ᴰᵇ

He can't finish. He's laughing too hard.

*Twenty-one*, says Ciel, and Mau thinks she sounds like she might be laughing too. ᶜ

* * *

When he straightens up, he's bleeding.

From a dozen places. All closing. But more of them than there were, and closing slower, and the light in him is coming up hot, and he's breathing hard for the first time all morning.

He looks round at the quarry. At the walls carved from rim to floor, glowing where his light has hit them. At the floor, which isn't a floor anymore, just holes and pieces and flowers. At Mau, standing in the middle of it with the katana in one hand and nothing in the other.

“*Finally*,” he shouts. Across the quarry. Up at the sky. “*Finally!* Finally something that doesn't *break*!” ᴰᵇ

Mau knows exactly how he feels.

He doesn't say so. But he's smiling too. He can feel it on his face. Two people who've spent their whole lives holding back because everything round them was too fragile, finally standing in front of something that isn't.

* * *

And then Dabura stops.

The laugh goes. The light goes quiet.

He stands in the field of flowers and looks at Mau for a long time. Not delighted. Not outraged. Something else. Something that looks almost like a man at the edge of a cliff he didn't know was there.

“I can't keep up with you,” he says. ᴰᵇ

It sounds like a confession.

“Every time,” he says. “Every time I learn it, you're already on the next thing. I've never—nothing's ever done that. Not since—” ᴰᵇ

He stops. The edge-of-sleep look, very brief.

“Not ever,” he says. ᴰᵇ

* * *

He brings his hands together in front of his chest.

Slowly. The way you'd hold something very fragile.

“I know how to do this,” he says. “I've always known. I've never needed to.” ᴰᵇ

Mau feels it before it happens. The shape folded small at the centre of Dabura, the one the eyes found at dawn. The world of his own he's never opened.

“Domain,” says Ciel. ᶜ

“I need to now,” says Dabura. ᴰᵇ

And the world goes white.
