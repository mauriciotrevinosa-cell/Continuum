## Chapter 32 — Can I Speak?

*When: B+434. Before dawn to dinner. Yuka writes "53" by lamplight: B+434, the day after the fight. The last written count was "57" on B+430.*

She writes the number before dawn.

She does it by the lamp, on the windowsill, in a room with only her in it. He slept in Ori's room last night; she heard Ori take him there, very firmly, the way Ori does things. She didn't go after them.

She counts back. Fifty-seven, the last one she wrote. Then fifty-six and fifty-five, the two days she left blank. Fifty-four on the day of the fight, yesterday. Today is fifty-three.

She turns to a new page.

*53.*

She doesn't draw anything next to it.

She looks at it for a long time. Then she closes the notebook and puts it on his side of the bed, where his pillow is. Where he'll see it.

Makima is on the porch steps.

At the bottom, in the body Noise made for her, with her hands folded in her lap. She's been there since before light. Yuka saw her from the landing window when she came to find the lamp. No voice announced where Makima was; she was simply there, waiting beneath the porch light.

Yuka comes down the steps.

She stops one step above Makima.

Makima doesn't look round. She doesn't say anything. She doesn't ask if she can speak.

Yuka sits down on the step beside her.

They sit for a while.

The yard is grey. Cold. The training posts. The orchard. The long field going down to the river, where the mist is.

Yuka's heart is going very fast.

She thinks about the rule. The one she made on the contract day, sitting in Mau's lap with the notebook. *If it's important, you talk to me. If it isn't, I'll come to you. And if you want to talk to me about something that isn't important, you can ask. Can I speak? And I'll say yes or no.*

She made that rule for Makima.

She's never once had to use it herself.

“Makima,” she says. ʸ

Makima turns her head.

“Can I speak?” says Yuka. ʸ

Makima goes very still.

Yuka watches it happen. The strategist's stillness, the one that sees every piece on the board. And then something else under it. Something that looks, for just a moment, very young.

“Yes,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“I said I'd started to trust you,” says Yuka. ʸ

Her voice is small. She makes it go on anyway.

“In the yard. I said it like—like it was over. Like you'd broken it. Like you'd done something.” ʸ

She looks at her hands.

“Ciel said you were holding something for him from inside,” she says. “When the domain came up I was here, trying to cut an apple in half. I still had the knife in my hand. I kept asking her what was happening, and she told me he'd stopped going down because you were there.” ʸ

“She could have said he was getting up,” Makima says. “I wanted him to stay down until he could feel his hand again.” ᴹᵏ

Makima doesn't say anything.

“You're his friend,” says Yuka. “That's all it ever was. I knew that. I knew it in the yard when I said it. I said it anyway. Because I wanted to throw something.” ʸ

She looks up.

“I'm sorry,” she says. “I want to trust you. I hate that I said it like the choice was all mine and I could just take it away to hurt him.” ʸ

Makima looks at her for a long time.

She doesn't say *it's fine*. Yuka will think about that later. She doesn't say *it's fine* or *don't worry about it* or *I understand*. She just looks at Yuka, very steadily, the way the best strategist in her world looks at something she's going to tell the truth about.

“It hurt,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka flinches.

“When you said it,” says Makima. “I've been hurt a great many times. By people who wanted to. That was the first time anyone ever hurt me by taking back something they'd given me.” ᴹᵏ

She pauses.

“I didn't know I had anything that could be taken back,” she says. “Until you did it.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's eyes have gone hot.

“I'm sorry,” she says again. ʸ

“I know,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

She looks out at the yard.

“I hid behind him,” she says. “In the yard. I didn't mean to. My body did it. I've seen someone do it, a long time ago, in my world, a girl who hid behind her friend whenever anyone shouted. I used to think it was ridiculous.” ᴹᵏ

Something happens at the corner of her mouth.

“I understand it now,” she says. “I'm sorry it looked like something else.” ᴹᵏ

They sit there.

The mist on the river is starting to lift. The sky over the hills is going from grey to something lighter.

“He's not talking,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her.

“To anyone,” says Makima. “Not to Ciel. Not to Noise. He tells them technical things. He tells Gojo numbers. He tells me—” ᴹᵏ

She stops.

“He told me one true thing,” she says. “In the dungeon. On the floor. I won't tell you what it was. It's his. But I'll tell you this.” ᴹᵏ

She turns and looks at Yuka.

“I told him I wasn't the one who could answer it,” she says. “That there's only one person he'd believe it from.” ᴹᵏ

She looks back at the yard.

“And he closed her door,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka goes to the workshop next.

Mai is already there, upstairs, on the new floor, at her bench by the big window. Maki is with her, on a stool, with a cup of tea. They both look up when Yuka comes up the stairs.

Neither of them says anything.

Yuka stands at the top of the stairs. In the light from the windows Momo said should be bigger.

“I didn't go,” she says. “Yesterday. I had my boots on and I let Cross talk me out of it.” ʸ

Mai puts down her tool.

“It was still my choice,” she says. “I know it was. At the time it felt like the only way I wasn't going to embarrass myself. And then he came home and wanted to know if I'd remembered he'd saved me a place.” ʸ

She's saying it all without looking at either of them. She makes herself look up for the next part. “I'm still angry he left without telling me before the gate. I thought I had to stop being angry before I could go talk to him. I think I was using that as another reason to wait.” ʸ

Maki looks at her granddaughter for a long time.

Then she puts her tea down and gets up off the stool and crosses the floor and puts her arms round her. Hard. The familiar firm hold Yuka has seen her give Yuta when he comes home tired and still tries to say he isn't.

“Good,” she says, into Yuka's hair. ᴹᵃᵏ

That's all. *Good.*

Over Maki's shoulder, Mai is looking at her.

“What are you going to do?” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

Yuka thinks about it.

“Not talk,” she says. “Not first.” ʸ

Mai frowns.

“If I ask him,” says Yuka, “he'll tell me. Everything. What I did. The plate. The chair. All of it. He'll make himself say every ugly thing, because he promised.” ʸ

She takes a breath.

“I want one minute where I can sit by him and say what I did before I ask for anything,” she says. ʸ

Dabura's knees don't fit under the long table. By midday Rimuru has asked for two chairs, a bench and finally for somebody to take the table extension out so they can push one of the legs farther down. Yuka can carry the extension. She goes in backward with Shizue calling where the table ends and meets Ori going the other way with a stack of cups at her chin.

Mau has his palm down near the edge of the table. He moves it when Yuka comes through so she can lower her end. A thin strip of cloth has come out over his wrist. He's put his cuff back over it before he says thank you.

Dabura gets the bench farther away with one enormous foot. He looks at Mau across the width of the table. “When would you have stopped, if I hadn't?” ᴰᵇ

Mau breathes in. Maru has stopped taking the extension from Yuka. It hangs between everybody for a second, as if they all have to stand where they started until they've heard the answer.

“When you asked me to,” says Mau. ᴹ

Dabura looks down at the extra length of wood and lifts his end of it himself. Yuka takes two cups from Ori and sets them where Shizue points. One of them is the cup Mau held when he came home yesterday. She wants five minutes with him. She can have five minutes if she asks him to get up in the middle of the conversation and come out. He would still get up for her.

Mau lifts his eyes. For a second she's looking at him with no idea what expression he's seeing. Ori puts the last cup in her hand. She brings it round to Mau's side of the table and sets it beside him. When her finger hits the handle the contact is loud enough for him to hear. He slides the cup closer.

Rimuru asks whether the offer about the road still stands, and Maru says it does. There is a patch on one of the wagon covers at the ford, he tells them. It took all morning to finish, but when they came to set off the woman who made it wanted to know who was coming back for the next load. Mau asks him how long she wants to stay there. Yuka realises he'll spend as long as they need talking about one woman with a piece of canvas. She used to like that about him. It was why she could ask him for anything.

She wants to be angry about the line for another minute. There's still something in her that wants to feel whether he needs her before she'll go and say she needs him. She stops reaching for it when she notices what she's doing.

Shizue catches the swinging door for her on the way out. “Can you ask Shuna if there's enough for five when they're done?” ˢʰⁱ

“There'll be enough,” says Yuka. She goes to find out what she can do before they all come out again.

The plan is very small.

She thought about it all night with the curtains open on his side of the room. She thought about grand things. Letters. Speeches. Going into the common room in the middle of the talks and saying it in front of everyone.

None of it was right.

What's right is small.

Dinner. Her chair. Six inches closer. His plate.

That's all. That's the whole plan. She'll sit beside him, where she always sat, and she'll reach over, the way she always reached, and she'll steal something off his plate. And he'll know. He'll know without her saying anything. Because it's theirs. Because it belonged to their ordinary dinners long before anyone else noticed its absence.

And then, after, when he knows, she'll say it. The ugly thing. All of it.

But first, the plate.

The talks end at dusk.

She hears the common-room doors open. Voices in the hall. Dabura's laugh, enormous, going toward the kitchen. Rimuru saying *that went well, I think, I genuinely think that went well*. Maru, quieter, saying something careful.

She's already in the kitchen.

She's been in the kitchen for an hour. Helping Shuna. Setting the table with an attention that makes her check the same cup twice. Putting the plates out. His at the head, where he'll sit, because everyone looks at him.

Her chair beside it.

She moved it six inches closer. Then six more. Then she sat in it, to check, and her knee was where his would be, and she got up again with her heart going so fast she had to hold the back of the chair.

Ori is sitting in it when the others come in.

Of course she is. She sat there the night they had the map out. And last night she found the courage to ask him upstairs when Yuka couldn't finish a sentence.

She's swinging her legs. She's waiting for her father.

She sees Yuka standing by the stove.

She looks at her. At Yuka's face. At Yuka's hands, which are holding the edge of the counter very tightly.

Ori doesn't say anything.

She slides down off the chair. She pushes it back in, very neatly, six inches closer than anyone else would push it. She looks at Yuka once more, very seriously, like a general handing over a command.

And she goes back round the table, past Gojo and Milim and Umaru, to her own seat beside Frieren, and sits down, and folds her hands.

Frieren, beside her, puts a hand on her head.

Mau comes in last.

He looks tired. More tired than she's ever seen him. The fight yesterday; the talks today; another night of not sleeping. His body is whole and new and stronger than it's ever been and he looks like he could lie down on the kitchen floor and not get up.

He goes to the head of the table.

He sees the chair beside his.

Empty. Pushed in. Six inches closer.

He stops.

He looks at it for a long moment. Then he looks round the table, looking for Ori. Ori is beside Frieren, with her hands folded, looking at her plate.

He sits down.

Yuka comes away from the stove.

She sits beside him.

She doesn't say anything. She sits in her chair, six inches closer, and her knee is against his under the table, and she feels him go completely still.

He doesn't look at her.

She doesn't look at him either. She looks at his plate. Crisp potatoes with rough edges. Mau has split the last one down the middle. The good half sits beside the rim nearest her.

Waiting.

She reaches over.

Her hand is a few inches from his plate when Beru speaks.

From the porch. Through the open kitchen door. Not loud this time. In the voice that has stopped her before: in the Rulers' gate, the night before the bottom; on the porch, the night something bright looked back from the north-east; and once, a long time ago, before her.

“*My king*,” says Beru. ᴮᵉ

The kitchen goes silent.

And in the yard, with a sound like a sheet being shaken out, Kaisel comes down out of the dark and lands on the grass, and turns his long head toward the gate, and stays there.

Pointing.

Yuka's hand is still out over his plate.

Mau stands up.

His chair goes back. He's already moving. He doesn't look at her. He doesn't look at her hand, or the pickles, or the chair six inches closer. He goes to the kitchen door in three steps, and Makima is already rising from the bottom of the porch steps, and Gojo is already on his feet, and Dabura has stopped laughing.

Yuka's hand is still out over his plate.

She hears Mau say, in the doorway, very quietly, not to her:

“It's her.” ᴹ

And then, from the gate, out of the dark, a voice she's only heard once, at a table by the sea:

“Good evening. I hope I'm not interrupting dinner.” ʷᵈ

Yuka looks at her hand.

A few inches from his plate. Reaching. Half a potato with salt on the skin, on her side of the plate.

She didn't reach them.

She pulls her hand back, very slowly, and holds it against her chest, and stands up, and goes to the door after him.
