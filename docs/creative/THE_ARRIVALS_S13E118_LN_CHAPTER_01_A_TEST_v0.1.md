# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 83
## Chapter 33 — A Test

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 83
**Reading order:** 33
**Source episode:** S13E118
**Continuity state:** B+434 night. Yoru reports independently moving devils and tries to frame the danger as her test. The B397 pact explicitly bars arranged/proxy killing, weapons intended for covered killings, attacks on the household and withdrawable lethal instructions. Mau challenges that boundary. Yoru claims she sought later leverage and ordered no present attack, expected deterrence, told them to stop and failed; this is her account, not narrator certification. Ordinary independent devils may not be her proxies, but calling an arranged attack a test would not exempt it. Her presence does not override DIO’s ward; protected grounds retain their old footprint. Scout sight corroborates the openings. The mechanism of erased devils returning is unverified, not an established single hole at the house. Makima tells Yoru the couple aren't separated, then uses a remark about marrying by proxy to draw Yoru's attention off Yuka. Mau states for himself that he's married to Yuka. Makima goes to Yuka afterward and explains what she was doing.

*When: B+434. Night. The kitchen door is open behind Mau; dinner is on the table, untouched; the light from inside falls across the yard to the gate.*

She's standing at the gate.

Exactly at it. Not through it. Just outside, where the road meets the yard, with her hands clasped behind her back and her head tilted, looking at the house the way you'd look at a painting you've been told about.

Asa's body. Dark hair, a school uniform from a world that isn't this one, a scar across the face. And Yoru's eyes looking out of it.

Mau knows those eyes. He sat across a table from them by the sea and signed his name.

He stops on the porch steps.

“You can't come in,” he says. ᴹ

“I can, actually,” says Yoru. ʷᵈ

She steps through the gate.

The barrier doesn't stop her.

He feels it not stop her. The familiar pressure holds over the grounds. Nothing in it parts for the contract; no attack crosses with Yoru. Mau remains between her and the porch.

She walks across the yard.

“The contract,” she says, conversationally. “Ninety days, signed at a table by the sea. Asa watching. You remember. Until the same hour on the four hundred and eighty-seventh day. I may not harm anyone in your house. Not you. Not her. Not the child. Not the dragon.” ʷᵈ

She stops ten paces from the porch.

“I am not attacking anyone,” she says. “So your Goddess's little fence lets me in. It's very literal. I like that about it.” ʷᵈ

Behind him, the house has come to the door.

He can feel them without looking. Gojo on the porch, blindfold off. Dabura in the kitchen doorway, very still, looking at Yoru with the eyes of a man who has met something like her before and didn't like it. Maru behind the warrior. Frieren with her staff. Beru at the end of the porch with his wings half open. Kaisel in the yard, head low, between Yoru and the house.

Makima at the bottom of the steps.

And in the kitchen doorway, behind him, Yuka.

He doesn't look round.

“What do you want,” he says. ᴹ

“To tell you something,” says Yoru. “Out of courtesy. I'm told you like people to be honest now.” ʷᵈ

She smiles. It's Asa's mouth, but the smile isn't Asa's.

“I told my world about you,” she says. ʷᵈ

He doesn't say anything.

“What I saw,” says Yoru. “In your memory. The old one. The inn. The yard. What happened there, in the recollection you chose to show me. And what I saw at the quarry, yesterday, from the rim.” ʷᵈ

She tilts her head.

“And what I knew already,” she says. “That you once stood in front of the thing that erases. The Chainsaw. The one that eats devils and takes them out of the world so they never were. And it ate *at* you. Pieces of what you could do, gone, the way things it eats are gone. And you got them back.” ʷᵈ

The yard is very quiet.

“My world has no reason to expect an erasure like that to give anything back,” Yoru says. “Then I told them what you did.” ʷᵈ

“So they're afraid,” says Mau. ᴹ

“So they're afraid,” Yoru agrees. ʷᵈ

She smiles again.

“Fear makes devils hungry,” she says. “You know that. Makima knows that.” ʷᵈ

At the bottom of the steps, Makima doesn't move.

“Some of them,” says Yoru, “are very afraid. And very hungry. And they aren't bound by my contract.” ʷᵈ

“You are,” Mau says. “Including instructions you can withdraw. Means you provide for a killing you intend. People you send to do it for you.” ᴹ

She stops smiling for a moment.

“I haven't sent them. I haven't armed them. I told them there was something here that gave back what Chainsaw took, and that I wanted it left alive.” ʷᵈ

“Until when?” ᴹ

“Until I could deal with it on my own terms. Later.” Her eyes stay on him. “I expected fear to make them wait. Some of them decided it meant they shouldn't.” ʷᵈ

“Then tell them to stop.” ᴹ

“I did. They aren't taking my orders. I can't make them, any more than you can make a stranger who dislikes you part of my army.” ʷᵈ

Mau lets Ciel set the words beside the completed terms. The distinction exists: an independent attacker is not automatically her proxy. It does not prove that her account is complete.

“If this is something you've arranged, the agreement still applies,” he says. “Calling it a test won't change that.” ᴹ

“Then find out,” Yoru says. ʷᵈ

She looks north. Past the house. Past the orchard. At the dark hills.

“They're pushing through,” she says. “In the empty country to the north. Past the ridge your daughter draws. Things that were eaten. Things that shouldn't exist anymore, because the Chainsaw took them. Finding routes through this converged world. I don't know the whole mechanism yet. Because they heard there's something here that survived what they didn't.” ʷᵈ

She looks back at him.

“They want you,” she says. “Very badly. They'll be through by morning.” ʷᵈ

He hears Yuka make a sound behind him.

He doesn't turn round.

“And you came to warn me,” he says. ᴹ

“I came to make it a test,” says Yoru. ʷᵈ

She takes a step closer.

Kaisel's head comes up. A low sound in his chest. Yoru looks at the dragon with polite interest and doesn't come any closer.

“You go alone,” she says. ʷᵈ

The yard goes very still.

“Alone,” says Yoru. “To meet them. In the north. Before they come looking.” ʷᵈ

She holds up one finger.

“Not quite alone,” she says. “Those who live in you may go. Your clever voice. Your first construct. And her.” ʷᵈ

She looks at Makima. Makima looks back.

“They're part of you,” says Yoru. “I don't count parts. But nobody else. Not the man with the blindfold. Not the elf. Not the ant. Not your wife.” ʷᵈ

“Why,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Because they are looking for one opponent,” Yoru says. “If they see a household deploying with him, some will try the easier targets. You'll be chasing them in different directions before you've seen what came through.” ʷᵈ

She looks at the house behind him. At the people in the doorways.

“Your protection covers these grounds,” she says. “Not every road beyond them. Not the river where your daughter plans her expeditions. Not the ridge where your wife stood yesterday.” ʷᵈ

He goes very cold.

“If they see you first,” Yoru says, “you may keep their attention on what they came for. Bring a group and you give them choices.” ʷᵈ

“That's not a test,” says Gojo, from the porch. “That's a threat.” ᴳ

Yoru turns her head and looks at him.

“The threat exists,” she says. “I didn't need to invent it. I want to watch what he does with it.” ʷᵈ

She looks back at Mau.

“And I'll be watching,” she says. “Of course. That's all I'm allowed to do. You let me watch the quarry. This time I'm asking to watch the answer to something else.” ʷᵈ

She tilts her head again.

“I saw something,” she says, more quietly. “In the memory. At the inn. You were alone there too. Everyone dead in the yard. And you went out looking for a door, a rule, anything. Alone. Because you thought being near people was what killed them.” ʷᵈ

He doesn't move.

“And yesterday, at the quarry,” says Yoru, “you weren't alone. You had her at your shoulder. You let her help. You took her hand.” ʷᵈ

She looks at Makima.

“That's different from the memory,” says Yoru. “That's new. A point where what you are now doesn't match what you were.” ʷᵈ

She smiles.

“I want to see what happens when I press on it,” she says. “I want to see what breaks.” ʷᵈ

Something moves in Asa's face.

Not Yoru. Something underneath. The muscles round the eyes, the set of the mouth, changing, as if someone else is trying to come up from the bottom of a well.

“*I didn't agree to this*,” says Asa. ᴬˢ

Her voice is different. Younger. Frightened. Coming out of the same mouth.

“*I said watching*,” says Asa. “*You said you'd only watch. This isn't watching. This is—*” ᴬˢ

“I'm not sending them,” Yoru says through the same mouth. “I wanted them to wait. They're coming anyway. I can still watch what he does.” ʷᵈ

“*You're making him go alone*—” ᴬˢ

“He hasn't agreed that it's safest,” Yoru says. “He's listening.” ʷᵈ

Asa's face goes still again.

Yoru looks at Mau.

“She's very sweet,” she says. “She worries.” ʷᵈ

Mau looks at her for a long time.

At Asa's body with Yoru's eyes. At the scar across the face. At the hands clasped behind the back, relaxed, patient.

He thinks about the north. The empty country past the ridge. *REST HERE. WE DID.* The cave where Ori's map says *expedition 1*.

He thinks about Ori at the river. Yuka in the yard with the notebook. Jin-Woo's family on the road to the house. The protection ends long before those roads do.

He thinks about the inn. The yard. Everyone dead.

*Because you thought being near people was what killed them.*

“I'll look at what's coming through,” he says. “I'm not accepting your account without checking it. And you don't choose whether my people protect the roads.” ᴹ

Behind him, someone moves.

He feels it. Through nothing, because the door is closed. He just feels it, the way you feel someone step into a doorway behind you in a dark room.

“*Mau*,” says Yuka. ʸ

He doesn't turn round.

“Mau, wait. Please. I need to—” ʸ

“Not now,” he says. ᴹ

It comes out very quietly. Very level.

He hears her stop.

Yoru looks past him at Yuka.

“Why are you all the way back there?” she asks. “By the sea you were practically in his lap.” ʷᵈ

Yuka's fingers slip off the doorframe.

“I kept expecting you at the quarry,” Yoru says. “Every time he looked up at that ledge, I thought, *oh, there she is*.” ʷᵈ

For a second Mau can see the ledge from where he stood with Dabura across from him. There'd been a patch of pale stone at the lip. He'd picked it out before the first exchange so he could find her fast.

Yoru turns the smile on him.

“Beat me one day and I might consider marrying you. You wouldn't have had to look twice for me.” ʷᵈ

“They aren't separated,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

Yoru's eyes travel back to the porch. She nearly gets all the way to Yuka.

“And if any of the Horsemen were going to marry him, it would be me,” Makima adds. “By proxy, obviously.” ᴹᵏ

Yoru turns her whole head. “Since when do I need you to tell me who I can marry?” ʷᵈ

“Why? Did you ask Asa first?” ᴹᵏ

Yoru takes her eyes off the doorway. Something at the corner of her mouth tightens. Her sister is still standing at the bottom of the steps with her arms at her sides, waiting for a reply as if she'd meant to ask the question all night.

“I'm married to Yuka,” says Mau. ᴹ

He can say it while Yoru is looking elsewhere. He can still hear how quietly Yuka said she shouldn't have married him. The words reached him from about where she's standing now. He keeps his eyes in front of him. He doesn't need her to say it with him.

“When are they coming through?” he asks. ᴹ

“Morning,” says Yoru. “They'll be through by morning. The ridge past your daughter's ruins. You'll feel them. You feel everything now.” ʷᵈ

She turns round. She walks back across the yard, past Kaisel, who turns his whole body to keep facing her, and out through the gate.

She stops on the other side.

“Good luck, king,” she says, without turning round. ʷᵈ

And she's gone, into the dark, down the road.

The yard is silent.

Nobody moves. Nobody says anything.

Mau stands on the porch steps and looks at the gate.

Then he looks north. At the dark. At the hills. He takes the sight Bellion's northern scouts can give him, keeping the feel of the porch separate beneath it.

Far off. Past the ridge. Past the ruins. In the empty country where the maps stop.

The shared view catches a distortion above the empty ground. It is growing. Noise records what reaches the scout; this part of the warning is real.

Something is pressing against the world from the other side.

Not one thing. Many. Old, and wrong, and hungry. Things that shouldn't exist. Things that were eaten and remember being eaten.

And all of them, every one, turned toward him.

Behind him, in the doorway, Yuka is standing with her hand pressed against her chest. The hand that reached for his plate.

She heard all of it.

She heard *not now*.

Rimuru asks for a lamp and a map and Mau goes back down the steps to meet him. Yuka gets their bowls out of the way before anyone has to ask. She stacks them too close together. The bottom one ends up making a wet ring on the inside of the top one and she has to pull them apart again.

“Can I speak?” asks Makima. ᴹᵏ

Yuka sets the bowl down. For a second there's no room on the counter to put the other one. Makima waits until she finds a place.

“Yes.” ʸ

“I didn't think you needed to hear anything about another wedding from me,” says Makima. “I said it because I could see where Yoru was looking. I told her you were married and she was still looking past me at you.” ᴹᵏ

“You said we weren't separated.” ʸ

“I did. I think she was waiting to hear how much trouble you'd have saying it yourself.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka puts a little distance between the bowls so they won't pick up the same mark again. “And if you'd stopped there?” ʸ

“She'd have tried something else. She didn't even have to look at him to tell you where he kept looking in that quarry. I wanted her to have a question for me instead.” ᴹᵏ

“So you told her you'd marry him.” ʸ

“I told her I'd have a better claim than she did. She'd spent all that time looking for a way to put herself on the other side of you. I know what it feels like when someone tells you they got there first.” ᴹᵏ

“Did you mean it?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Makima gives that question the time it takes to put both bowls right side up. “I want to keep the friend I have.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka gets a corner of the cloth into the first bowl. She moves it round in a slow circle while she thinks about where she's heard Makima use that voice before.

“You liked knowing she'd turn around,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Yes.” After a moment Makima adds, “I saw your face when I said it. I didn't like that. I came in to tell you why instead of leaving you to ask him what I meant.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks over at the map table. Mau is leaning in so Rimuru can see something he's drawn under the ridge. He told Yoru he was married to her. He'd said it without ever looking at her.

“I was going to tell him that I put the band back on because I wanted to,” she says. “That's about as far as I got before she came.” ʸ

Makima looks down at Yuka's hand. Then she looks up again. “I'd tell him that when you can.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka dries both bowls and puts them back in the cupboard. After Makima goes back out she takes the notebook upstairs. She opens it to the number she wrote this morning. There are a lot of things she could get into the space under it and let Mau find on his pillow. There is still something she wants to have to say to his face.
