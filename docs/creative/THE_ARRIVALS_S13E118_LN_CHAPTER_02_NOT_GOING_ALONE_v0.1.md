## Chapter 32 — Not Going Alone

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1 (end of volume)

*When: B+435. The hour before dawn. Nobody slept. The kitchen table is still set from dinner; Shuna hasn't cleared it, and nobody has asked her to.*

He gets ready in the dark.

Shoko has checked him twice during the night. The fresh bleeding has stopped; the strain from the compression has not become an old injury just because he can walk.

“No doing that again today,” she told him. “Ciel calls it if the repair starts falling behind.” ˢʰ

He agreed. She made him eat before she let him go upstairs for his boots.

There isn't much equipment to get ready. Everything he needs is in the shadow. The daggers. The katana with Rika in it. The tent, which he doesn't think he'll need. The thick blanket, which he doesn't take out.

He sits on the edge of the bed in the room where Yuka isn't and laces his boots.

The notebook is on his pillow. Where she left it. He saw it last night when he came up and didn't open it. He opens it now, by the lamp, and finds the page.

*53.*

In her hand. No drawing next to it.

He looks at it for a long time.

Then he closes it, and puts it back on the pillow, exactly where she left it, and goes downstairs.

The house is in the kitchen.

All of it. Nobody slept. They're sitting round the long table, still set from dinner, plates untouched, in the lamplight, and they all look up when he comes in.

Nobody says anything at first.

Then Gojo stands up.

“I'm coming,” he says. ᴳ

“No,” says Mau. ᴹ

“I'm not asking.” ᴳ

“You heard her.” ᴹ

“I heard a devil threaten you with a rule she made up.” Gojo's voice is very level. No grin. “I've been threatened by people who make up rules before. You go anyway, and you bring friends, and you break the rule.” ᴳ

“The fence covers the house,” says Mau. “Not the roads. Not the river. Not the ridge.” ᴹ

Gojo turns toward the north window. Ciel's scout images have been passed through Rimuru during the night. There are several openings now, separated by empty country. The first shapes that crossed them did not all stay together.

“If you follow me,” says Mau, “they'll see you. They'll see where you came from. They'll see who you are. And then they'll know about Ori at the river, and Jin-Woo's family on the road, and everyone who ever walks out of that gate.” ᴹ

He looks round the table.

“They've come looking for me,” he says. “I want to catch their attention before they spread.” ᴹ

Gojo stands there for a long moment.

“Then I hold the route back,” Gojo says. “You keep Ciel talking to us.” ᴳ

“Technical reports,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Reports,” Rimuru agrees. “Your private conversations can stay closed. The watch isn't guessing where you've gone.” ᴿ

They settle the first positions over the map. Bellion's ordinary soldiers keep the distant views; the house holds the roads and travellers inside the existing protection until they know which way the devils move. Gojo is ready at the return point. Nobody from outside Mau goes forward with him.

“I want the next image before you cross that ridge,” Gojo says. “Not after you've found out she left something out.” ᴳ

Mau agrees. Rimuru marks the place on the map, then turns it so Bellion can see the route the watch will hold.

Gojo finally sits, but leaves the map open.

Dabura is at the end of the table.

He hasn't said anything. He's sitting with his huge hands folded on the wood, looking at Mau.

“You beat me yesterday,” he says. ᴰᵇ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And today you're going to fight a war. Alone. To keep them looking at you.” ᴰᵇ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Dabura considers this.

“Kings are very strange in this world,” he says. ᴰᵇ

Maru, beside him, puts a hand on his arm.

Ori is by the door.

She's in her nightdress, with her explorer jacket over it, standing very straight. She has something in her hand.

She doesn't say anything. She just holds it out.

A note. Folded twice. The same paper.

He takes it.

He doesn't open it. Not here. He puts it in his pocket, with the other one, the one that's been there for a week, and the two of them sit there together against his chest.

Ori looks at the pocket.

Then she looks up at him.

“Come back,” she says. ᴼ

“I'll come back.” ᴹ

“Promise.” ᴼ

He thinks about it. Properly. The real answer.

“I'll try with everything,” he says. ᴹ

Ori looks at him for a long time with Frieren's eyes.

“That's the real one,” she says. ᴼ

She hugs him round the waist, hard, and lets go.

Frieren stops him at the door.

She doesn't hug him now. She stands in the doorway with her staff, looking up at him, and her face isn't doing anything at all.

“The last time you went out alone looking for something,” she says, “at the old inn, you didn't come back for a long time.” ᶠ

He goes very still.

“I know how long it took you to come back after you showed her that memory,” Frieren says. “I don't need to have seen what she saw.” ᶠ

She reaches up. She puts her hand flat on his chest. Over the pocket. Over the two notes.

“Come back sooner this time,” she says. ᶠ

She takes her hand away and steps aside.

The yard is grey.

Not light yet. The grey before light, cold, with mist on the grass and the training posts dark shapes and the orchard a darker one beyond.

Makima is at the bottom of the porch steps.

She stands up when he comes out. She doesn't say anything. She looks at him, and then north, at the hills, at the dark, at the thing pressing against the world on the other side.

*It's important*, she says, inside, through the thread. *So I won't ask. They're closer. A few hours.* ᴹᵏ

*I know*, he says. ᴹ

DIO waits inside, her borrowed body withdrawn. Her attention stays near Ashborn's protected space. She has not asked to go forward as a fighter.

*Ciel's counting*, says Makima. ᴹᵏ

*I know.* ᴹ

He's halfway across the yard when Yuka says his name.

He stops.

He doesn't turn round. He hears her come down the porch steps behind him. Fast. Barefoot on the wet grass. She stops a few steps behind him.

“*Mau*,” she says. ʸ

He turns round.

She's in his shirt.

The old grey one with the hole at the collar. The one she was wearing the morning she sat on him to wake him up. Her hair is everywhere. Her face is white, and there are marks under her eyes, and she's holding the notebook against her chest with both arms.

The only colour in the world.

She looks at him. He looks at her.

“I need to tell you something,” she says. ʸ

Her voice is shaking.

“I know it's not—I know you have to go. I know. But I need to tell you before you do. It's not what you think. It's not *I want to talk to you about something*. It's—” ʸ

She stops.

She's trying. He can see her trying. Every word coming up out of her slowly, the way his do now. The ugly thing. The way they promised.

“The plate,” she says. “I saw the plate. Shuna kept it. I—” ʸ

Behind him, Makima says, very quietly, inside: *They're moving. Faster. Now.* ᴹᵏ

He feels it.

To the north. The pressure. All of it at once, the old wrong hungry things, surging, the way water surges when a dam starts to go. The ridge. The ruins. *REST HERE. WE DID.* Ori's map.

He looks back at Yuka.

She's still trying. Her mouth is open. The next word is right there.

“It's fine,” he says. ᴹ

She stops.

He sees it land. He sees her face change. He doesn't understand why, not yet. He'll understand later, much later, alone in the north with Makima's hand in his. But right now he only sees her face go still, the way it went still in the yard four nights ago, just before she said *I'll stop bothering you*.

He keeps going. He means it kindly. He means it to make her feel better. He wants her to know he isn't going to be alone in the dark like at the inn. He's learned. He accepts help now. He has someone.

“I can solve it,” he says. “I'm not going alone.” ᴹ

He tips his head, slightly, back toward the bottom of the porch steps.

“Makima's coming with me,” he says. ᴹ

The yard is very quiet.

Yuka stands on the wet grass in his shirt with the notebook against her chest.

*I'm not going alone. Makima's coming with me.*

He means *don't worry*. He means he has accepted help, the thing she once asked him to do.

Yuka knows why Makima can go. She apologised to her this morning. She doesn't take that apology back.

It still hurts to hear her own name absent from the reassurance. *Her. I couldn't reach you, and she can.*

She hears the yard. *You'd rather have her. When it's bad, you go to her. She goes where you go.*

She said that. It wasn't true when she said it.

The words have come back in a shape she wasn't ready for.

He doesn't know how they landed.

She doesn't cry.

She doesn't say anything. She stands very still on the wet grass and looks at him, and he can't read her face. He's always been able to read her face. He can't read this one.

Then she nods.

Once. Very small.

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

Makima is beside him.

She's come across the yard, quietly, the way Noise lets her move. She's at his right shoulder. Where she steadied the reconnection in the domain.

She holds out her hand.

Not to Yuka. To him. Sideways, without looking, the way he held his out to her on the wall.

He takes it.

Palm to palm. Thumb over the back. A friend's handshake. Firm. And then neither of them lets go, because where they're going is very dark, and very far, and full of things that want him gone from the world, and you hold on to a friend's hand in a place like that so you don't lose each other.

That's all it is.

He knows that's all it is.

He doesn't look at Yuka's face as she watches them do it.

They go north.

Across the yard. Past the training posts. Into the orchard, under the two old trees, past the oval of light humming between them. Up the hill road. Toward the ridge, and the ruins, and the empty country where the maps stop.

He doesn't look back.

He doesn't need to. He knows exactly where she is.

She's standing in the middle of the yard in his shirt, with the notebook against her chest, watching him go into the dark with another woman's hand in his.

The sky goes from grey to pale.

The house comes out onto the porch, one by one, and stands there, looking north. Gojo with his blindfold on. Frieren with her staff. Shoko in the clinic doorway with a cigarette she isn't going to light. Ori, holding Frieren's sleeve. Beru at the end of the porch with his wings half open, not moving, the way he stood when he wasn't allowed to follow into the Rulers' gate.

Nobody says anything.

Yuka is still in the middle of the yard.

She hasn't moved.

After a long time, she sits down.

Right there. On the wet grass. In his shirt.

She opens the notebook.

She finds today's page. She takes the pencil from where it's tucked into the spine.

She writes:

*52.*

She looks at it.

Then, under it, she starts writing something else.

Not a number.

A list.

Her hand isn't shaking anymore. It's very steady. She writes the first line, and the second, and the third. Small, neat letters, the way she writes everything she means to keep.

Nobody on the porch can see what it says.

Ori, holding Frieren's sleeve, watches her write it, and after a while, very quietly, lets go of the sleeve, and goes down the steps, and across the wet grass, and sits down beside her.

Yuka doesn't look up. She moves the notebook slightly, so Ori can see.

Ori reads it.

She nods, very seriously, like a general receiving a plan.

And they sit there together in the middle of the yard as the sun comes up over the hills, writing, while to the north, past the ridge, past the ruins, past everything, something old and hungry comes through into the world, and turns toward the only man it was ever looking for.
