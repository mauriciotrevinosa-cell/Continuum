# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 1 — Not Really Killing

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E119
**Continuity state:** B+435, dawn into early morning ("52"). Mau walks north with Makima at his side; Noise and Ciel are with him inside, and DIO stays by Ashborn's protected space. The legion stays folded in his shadow; the plan doesn't need it. Kamish is the exception when they get to the basin. He left Beru on the porch on purpose, for Ori. On the hill road Makima tells him plainly to kill them. Mau says no. Makima explains how devils work: one that dies on Earth goes to Hell, and one that dies in Hell is born again on Earth, remembering nothing: not the house, not him. So it isn't really killing. Mau asks what else there is. Makima: they're fears with bodies, concepts; there isn't much else to do. Mau doesn't forget Yuka's "Okay". Ciel reports to Rimuru: technical only.

*When: B+435. Dawn, and the first hour after it. Yuka wrote "52" on the porch and then, under it, something that isn't a number.*

They don't talk on the hill road.

Not out loud. There's no need to. Makima walks at his right shoulder, his hand still in hers, and the road goes up through the orchard and out past the last fence post and into the long wet grass where the hill starts properly, and neither of them says a word.

Inside, it's different. Inside, the room is busy.

Ciel is at the table with the scout images laid out in front of her like cards, sorting them, passing the short version through to Rimuru every few minutes. *Eleven openings. Four have closed again. The shapes that came through are moving north-west, away from the house, toward the ridge. Consistent with Yoru's account.* Then, to Mau only: *Your first pulse is higher than walking explains. The Black Heart is steady.* ᶜ

*I know*, he says. ᴹ

Noise has taken the chair next to Ciel. She slides each scout image across when it comes in, and he puts a fingertip on the places where the shapes change direction. Outside, he keeps Makima's body steady while she walks. When Mau stumbles half a step on the wet grass, Noise looks up before Ciel does.

DIO isn't at the table. She's taken a chair near the door to Ashborn's protected room, where she can hear when Ciel has news. She waved once when they left. Not a fighter. She never asked to be.

And under all of it, under the table and the cups and the scout images, folded down into the dark where the shadow goes when he isn't using it, the army.

Igris, with the companies that know this work. Deep behind him, the ranks added in gates Mau has never had reason to name aloud. There are more ways to answer the sight in front of him than there are people at the table.

He doesn't call them.

He's thought about it most of the night, lying on the bed in the room where Yuka wasn't. He could flood the north with them. Line every ridge. Make a wall of them from the ruins to the river. And every one of them would be something the devils could see, something they could follow back, something that says *there are more of them and they came from that direction*.

No. The plan doesn't need an army. The plan needs to be over before anyone has time to look round.

He left Beru on the porch.

On purpose. Last night, after Yoru left, he went out to where Beru was standing at the end of the porch and said it quietly, the way he says the things that are orders and also aren't: *Ori. Whatever happens. You stay with Ori.* And Beru looked at him for a long time with his wings half open, and then folded them, and went and stood at the foot of the stairs that go up to Ori's room, and was still standing there at dawn.

Yoru said *not the ant*. Mau would have left him anyway.

* * *

At the top of the hill, where the road bends and the house drops out of sight behind it, Makima stops.

He stops with her. Their hands are still joined.

She looks back the way they came. Not at the house. You can't see the house from here. At the place where it would be.

*She said okay*, says Makima. Inside. Not a question. ᴹᵏ

He doesn't answer.

*She said okay*, Makima says again, *the way you say it's fine.* ᴹᵏ

He feels that go into him, somewhere under the ribs, and stay.

*I know*, he says. ᴹ

He does know. He saw her face. He just doesn't know what to do with it yet, and there isn't time, and there are things to the north that are coming whether or not he understands his wife. So he puts it where he puts things. Behind a door. He closes the door very carefully, the way you close a door on a sleeping child, and he walks on, and Makima walks with him.

She doesn't push. She doesn't need to. She lives on the other side of that door, too. She can hear it rattle.

* * *

The sun comes up over the eastern hills when they're halfway to the ridge.

It doesn't change much, for him. The world goes from dim to bright. The grass goes from one grey to a lighter grey, with the dew on it catching the light like broken glass. Somewhere behind him, in a yard he can't see, the only colour in the world is sitting on the wet grass writing in a notebook. He knows that without looking. He can feel the shape of the house the way he feels his own hands.

Ahead, the ridge. Ori's ridge. The one on her map, with the ruins drawn on it in careful pencil and the words she copied from the old stone, *REST HERE. WE DID.*

And past it, something wrong.

He can feel them now without Ciel's images. A pressure, low and cold, the way the air feels before a storm, except that it isn't weather. It's hunger. Lots of it, all pointed at the same place. All pointed at him.

“How many?” he says. Out loud, for the first time since the yard. ᴹ

“More than a hundred,” says Makima. “Less than a thousand. Most of them are small.” ᴹᵏ

She says it the way she'd say the weather. She's turned her head toward the ridge, and her face, in profile, has gone very calm in a way he's learned to recognise. The face she wore in the world she came from. The one that made rooms go quiet.

“Small,” he says. ᴹ

“Small fears.” She considers it. “The fear of a particular kind of dog. The fear of a stair in the dark. The fear of a sound behind a wall. Things that were eaten and should never have come back.” ᴹᵏ

She looks at him.

“Kill them,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

He doesn't answer straight away.

It's the plainest thing she's ever said to him. No angle. No hook. She isn't trying to make him into something. She's telling him what she would do, as someone who has done it for a very long time, in a world where it was the only answer anyone had.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

She doesn't argue. She waits.

“I don't kill,” he says. “Not if there's another way. I didn't kill Dabura. I didn't kill the—” ᴹ

He stops, because the list is long, and she knows it. She's heard him refuse before. She already knows where the sentence ends.

“I know,” says Makima. “That's why I'm going to tell you how it works.” ᴹᵏ

She lets go of his hand.

Not far. She steps round in front of him on the road, so she can see his face, and she stands there with the wind off the ridge moving her hair, and she explains it the way you'd explain it to someone you respect. Slowly. Properly. Nothing held back.

“A devil isn't a person,” she says. “Not the way you mean. A devil is a fear that's been given a body. The more people fear the thing, the stronger the devil. The fear of guns makes the Gun Devil. The fear of darkness makes the Darkness. As long as people fear it, it exists. Do you understand?” ᴹᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“When a devil dies here,” says Makima, “on Earth, in a world with people in it, it doesn't stop. It goes to Hell. And when a devil dies in Hell, it comes back. Here. Somewhere. Born again, out of the same fear, because the fear is still there. The same devil. The same name. The same shape, more or less.” ᴹᵏ

She pauses.

“And it remembers nothing,” she says. “Nothing of this life. Not who it fought. Not who it hated. Not what it wanted. Not the house. Not you.” ᴹᵏ

The wind moves the grass.

Mau stands very still on the road and thinks about that.

“So if I kill them,” he says slowly, “they don't die. They go round. And come back.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“Without remembering where they were going.” ᴹ

“Without remembering that there was anywhere to go,” says Makima. “Without remembering that a man in the north gave back what the Chainsaw took. Without remembering the house, or the child at the river, or the road where your friend's family walks. They'd be small fears again, somewhere, starting over. With no reason on earth to come here.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at her.

“So it isn't really killing,” he says. ᴹ

“They lose this life,” says Makima. “The fear survives it. The next one won’t remember what brought this one here.” ᴹᵏ

Mau looks down the road. He can still hear the word he chose, *really*, doing more work than he meant it to.

* * *

He thinks about it for a long time.

Long enough that Ciel, inside, puts the scout images down and looks at him. Long enough for Noise to set the next image down without sliding it over.

“And if I don't,” Mau says. “If I just push them back. Hold them. Send them away.” ᴹ

“They come back,” says Makima. “Remembering. Hungrier.” ᴹᵏ

“If I talk to them.” ᴹ

Something moves at the corner of her mouth. Not quite a smile. Not unkind.

“You can't talk a fear out of being afraid,” she says. “I've tried. With people. It doesn't work on people either.” ᴹᵏ

She steps closer again. Not touching. Just closer.

“They aren't Dabura,” she says. “They aren't a king who got bored on a mountain. They aren't someone who's been lied to. There's nothing in them to reach. They're concepts. Fears with bodies. They don't change their minds, because they don't have minds the way you mean, only the thing they're afraid of and the thing they want to eat.” ᴹᵏ

She holds out her hand again.

“There isn't much else to do,” she says. “I'm sorry. I know you'd like there to be.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at the hand.

He thinks about the house. About Ori at the river with her map. About Jin-Woo's family on the south road. About the fence that covers the grounds and nothing past them. About a hundred, or a thousand, small hungry things that would come back remembering, and come back again, and again, until one day one of them found the river at the wrong hour.

And he thinks, under all of that, about the alternative. That they go round. That they come back somewhere far away, small and new and knowing nothing. That nobody remembers anything, and nobody has to.

There are worse things than forgetting. He's lived through some of them.

He takes her hand.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

He hears it as he says it. *Okay.* The same word. In a different mouth, on a different road, meaning something else entirely.

He puts that behind the door, too.

* * *

*Then we need a plan*, says Ciel, inside. *One that ends before anything can run.* ᶜ

*I have one*, says Mau. ᴹ

He tells them. Noise looks down at the stack of scout images and pulls out the one that shows the entire basin.

*Kamish will like that*, he says. ᴺ

He turns the image so Ciel can see where he wants the dragon to come in. He gives Mau a little pleased look before he goes back to the picture.

*Your body*, says Ciel carefully. *Shoko said not again today.* ᶜ

*Not the domain*, says Mau. *Not the compression. This is something else.* ᴹ

*It asks more of your concentration than any other single release*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*For a few seconds*, says Mau. *Then it's over.* ᴹ

Ciel looks at him across the table for a long moment. Then she picks the scout images up again and squares them into a neat stack.

*I'll count*, she says. *If the repair falls behind, I'll call it. And you'll stop.* ᶜ

*I'll stop.* ᴹ

*Say it like you mean it.* ᶜ

He thinks about Ori by the door in her nightdress. *That's the real one.*

*I'll stop*, he says, and means it. ᴹ

Makima, beside him, has gone quiet. She's heard the plan too. She's looking at him with an expression he can't place, something that sits between amusement and something much older and colder.

*You want me to do it*, she says. ᴹᵏ

*I want you to do the part only you can do.* ᴹ

She considers that.

*They'll all hear me*, she says. *Everything past that ridge. And I don't know what they'll think, afterward. Whether they'll remember who it was.* ᴹᵏ

*They won't remember anything*, says Mau. *You just told me.* ᴹ

For a moment, something crosses Makima's face that looks almost like surprise. As if she has been caught out by her own explanation.

Then she laughs. Short, quiet, real. The way she laughs now, sometimes, since she started being bad company instead of something worse.

*Yes*, she says. *I did.* ᴹᵏ

They walk on toward the ridge.

Behind them, a long way back, past the bend in the road and the hill and the orchard and the fence, in a yard full of people standing on a porch, Ciel's latest report reaches Rimuru: *Approaching the ridge. Plan set. Expected duration: short.*

Rimuru reads it out to the porch.

Nobody believes the last word.
