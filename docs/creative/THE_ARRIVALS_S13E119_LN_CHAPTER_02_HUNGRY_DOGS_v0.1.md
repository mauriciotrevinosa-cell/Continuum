# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 2 — Hungry Dogs

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S13E119
**Continuity state:** B+435, morning. Mau and Makima reach Ori's ridge and the ruins with *REST HERE. WE DID.* on the stone. Yoru is already there, sitting on the broken wall in Asa’s body. Mau gives her leave to watch from there. She tells him again that this is her test and that she didn't send them. The ones coming aren't the strong devils. They are the hungry ones, small fears that were eaten and came back, running to destroy something before it can grow. Mau doesn't argue with her about whose test it is. He walks down into the basin past the ruins, alone with Makima, and waits for them to come to him, so that nothing gets past him toward the house. Ciel counts. The first of them comes over the far rim.

*When: B+435. Mid-morning. The scout report Rimuru reads to the porch says only "At the ridge."*

Ori's ruins are smaller than she drew them.

They always are, Mau thinks, the places children draw. On her map they were a castle. Here they're a few walls of old stone, waist-high, knee-high, the corners rounded off by a long time of wind, standing on the crest of the ridge with the grass grown up through the floor. A doorway with no door. A step that goes nowhere.

And on the biggest stone, the one at the front, the words she copied so carefully into her notebook. He remembers standing here while she sounded the inscription out after Frieren. He had been looking at Ori then. This time he notices how deep somebody cut the words into the stone.

REST HERE. WE DID.

He stands and looks at them for a moment longer than he has time for.

Then he looks at the person sitting on the wall beside them.

* * *

Yoru has made herself comfortable.

She's sitting on the broken wall with her knees up, in Asa's body, in Asa's school uniform, with Asa's scar across her face and her own eyes looking out of it. She has a stalk of grass in her mouth. She's been here a while; there's a little pile of picked-apart grass beside her on the stone.

“King,” she says pleasantly. ʷᵈ

“You said you'd watch.” ᴹ

“I'm watching.” She spreads her hands. “Look. Nothing in them. I can't harm anyone in your house. You know that. I'm not even allowed to throw a stone. I'm just sitting on a wall.” ʷᵈ

“On her wall,” says Mau. ᴹ

Yoru glances at the words cut into the rock beside her. Then back at him, with interest.

“The child's,” she says. “The one with the map.” ʷᵈ

He doesn't answer.

“I'll get off it if you like,” Yoru says, and doesn't move. ʷᵈ

Makima, at his shoulder, has gone very still. She hasn't looked at Yoru once since they came over the crest. She's looking north, past the ruins, down the far side of the ridge, into the long empty basin beyond where the grass gives way to stone and the stone gives way to the place where the maps stop.

“They're close,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“They are,” Yoru agrees. ʷᵈ

She takes the grass out of her mouth and points with it.

“There. And there. And over there, see where the air's wrong. More of them every minute. They smelled you the moment you crossed the hill.” ʷᵈ

Mau sees. With the Six Eyes, the far side of the basin looks like water just before it boils: small disturbances, a dozen of them, two dozen, the world dimpling where something pushes through from somewhere it shouldn't be. And behind each dimple, the pressure. The cold, low hunger.

“This is my test,” says Yoru. ʷᵈ

“You keep saying that.” ᴹ

“Because it is.” She tilts her head. “I didn't send them. I told you. I told my world there was something here that gave back what the Chainsaw took, and I said I wanted it left alone until I could look at it properly. Some of them heard *left alone* as *left for later*. Some of them didn't wait.” ʷᵈ

She smiles. It isn't Asa's smile.

“I'm not responsible for them,” she says. “I'm only responsible for watching.” ʷᵈ

“And calling it a test,” says Mau. ᴹ

“And calling it a test,” Yoru agrees. “A test is just a thing that happens while someone pays attention. I'm paying attention.” ʷᵈ

He looks at her for a long moment.

He could argue. He argued last night, in the yard, and the argument went round and round the edges of her contract and came out where it started: she can't touch the house, she didn't send them, she can watch. Arguing again would cost time he doesn't have, and it would give her what she came for, which is to see him off balance.

“Watch, then,” he says. ᴹ

He turns away from her and looks down into the basin.

* * *

*Who are they?* he asks Makima. Inside, where Yoru can't hear. ᴹ

Makima is still looking north.

*Not the ones you're afraid of*, she says. *Not the strong ones. The strong ones are patient. They'd wait. Watch. Find out what you are first.* ᴹᵏ

She lets the shapes in the basin come into focus for him, the way she does sometimes, laying her sight over his.

They're small. Most of them. Shapes he can almost name and then can't: something like a dog with too many legs. Something like a door that walks. Something that's mostly teeth and a sound. Something that looks like nothing at all, just a place where you'd rather not put your foot.

*Hungry dogs*, says Makima. *That's all. The ones that got eaten because they weren't careful, and came back still not careful. They heard there's something here that could make the world they lost come back. And they're frightened of what that means, so they're running at it.* ᴹᵏ

*To do what?* ᴹ

*To destroy it.* She says it simply. *Before it grows. Before it gets any bigger than it is. That's what frightened things do to something new. They try to kill it while it's still small.* ᴹᵏ

He thinks about that.

*Small*, he says. ᴹ

*To them*, says Makima. And then, after a moment, with that edge in her voice that sits between amusement and something much colder: *They haven't seen you.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

He walks down the far side of the ridge.

Makima comes with him. Yoru doesn't. She stays on Ori's wall with her knees up, watching, the stalk of grass back in her mouth.

The basin is wide and shallow, like a bowl that someone set down in the hills and forgot. The grass thins out as he goes down. Then it's stone, flat and old and cracked, with dust in the cracks. No trees. No river. No road. Nothing out here that anyone would miss.

That's why he chose it. When he asked Ciel last night for the emptiest place between the openings and the house, she showed him this.

He stops in the middle.

Makima stops beside him. Close. Her shoulder an inch from his.

He looks back once, up at the ridge. The ruins against the sky, small and broken. Yoru, a little shape on the wall. And past the ridge, past the hills, past the orchard and the fence, the house, where Gojo is standing at the return point on the hill road, and Rimuru is reading reports out loud to the porch, and Beru is standing at the foot of Ori's stairs, and a woman in his grey shirt is sitting on the grass with a notebook, writing a list.

Everything behind him. Nothing in front of him except the basin and the hungry things coming over the far rim of it.

That's the point. Everything past him has to go through him.

*Ciel*, he says. ᴹ

*Counting*, says Ciel. *Heart rate high but stable. The repair is keeping up. The strain from yesterday's compression is still present in the left side. Don't use that side for anything you don't have to.* ᶜ

*I won't.* ᴹ

*Noise.* ᴹ

Noise slides the last scout image between himself and Ciel. His finger rests on the far rim, where they agreed to bring Kamish in. Mau feels his attention settle as the first shapes come over it.

*Ready*, says Noise. ᴺ

*Makima.* ᴹ

She doesn't answer in words. She reaches over without looking and takes his hand again, the way she did in the yard, palm to palm, thumb over the back. A friend's hand.

*Whenever you say*, she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

The first of them comes over the far rim.

It's the thing like a dog. It stops at the top, on the edge of the bowl, with its too-many legs splayed out, and looks down at him across the whole width of the basin, and he feels it look. He feels it recognise him. He feels the hunger in it turn, all at once, like a compass needle finding north.

It howls.

And behind it, all along the rim, from left to right, the others come.

They come over the edge like water over a weir. Dozens. Then a hundred. Then more. Small and wrong and fast, pouring down the sides of the basin toward the one man standing in the middle of it, the thing they came all this way to destroy while it was still small enough to destroy.

Up on the ridge, on Ori's wall, Yoru takes the grass out of her mouth and leans forward.

Mau lets go of Makima's hand.

He lifts both of his.
