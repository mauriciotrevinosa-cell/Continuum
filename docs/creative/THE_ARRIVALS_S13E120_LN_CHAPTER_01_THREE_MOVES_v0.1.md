# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 3 — Three Moves

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S13E120
**Continuity state:** B+435, mid-morning. Yoru's point of view from Ori's wall. Mau draws Blue and Red together and pours everything into one Hollow Purple, unlimited: all his concentration, all of it, a trade-off that leaves him unable to move for the seconds it takes to hold and steer. Anyone could punish that opening. Makima says "Stop", and everything in the basin stops: every devil, all at once. Yoru knows that voice; it is her sister's. The Purple goes through the basin and erases almost all of them. Shadow soldiers rise along the rims and corner the few left at the edges into the middle; Kamish comes out of the sky and burns them. It is over in less than a minute. Three moves, and two of them weren't his. Yoru says "How boring" and leaves. Her ninety days still stand.

*When: B+435. Mid-morning, for less than a minute.*

Yoru has been looking forward to this.

That's the thing nobody in that house understands about her. They think she wants to hurt him. They think the contract is a leash and she's straining at it. They don't understand that she enjoys *watching*. That a war is the most interesting thing in any world, and the most interesting part of a war is the moment before it begins, when nobody knows yet what anyone will do.

She has a very good seat.

Asa's body is cold. Asa doesn't like heights much, or cold stone, or being awake this early. Yoru can feel her somewhere underneath, complaining in the small resentful way she complains about everything. Yoru ignores her. She leans forward on the child's wall with the grass in her teeth and watches the king lift his hands.

She saw the quarry two mornings ago. She saw what he could do to a Simurian when he was willing to stand still and take the punches and learn. She knows what he is. Or she thought she did.

She wants to see him *enjoy* it. That's the test, really, under all the talk. She wants to see what happens when a man who's been told all his life to hold back is finally given a basin full of things nobody will miss and told *go on*. She wants him to stretch. To try things. To pull out the domain and the shrine and the shadows and the dragon and play. To take an hour. To take all morning. To find out, in front of her, that he likes it.

She has seen that in men before. It's her favourite thing.

The hungry dogs come down the sides of the basin.

He lifts his hands.

* * *

There's something in his left hand.

She can't see it, exactly. She feels it. A pull, a drag, the air of the whole basin leaning very slightly toward his left palm, the dust lifting off the stone in thin lines and sliding toward him. Something that wants everything to come closer.

And in his right hand, the opposite. A push. A pressure, the air bending away from his right palm in a shimmer, the grass at the edge of the basin flattening outward as if a door had been opened onto a high wind.

Both at once.

She sits up a little.

She's heard about this. Everyone in her world who's been listening has heard about this. The boy with the eyes. The one who can pull and push at the same time and put the two together and make something that erases. She's heard about it the way you hear about weather in another country.

She has never seen anyone do it like this.

He brings his hands together.

And then he does something she doesn't expect. He doesn't throw it. He doesn't fire it off and move. He *keeps pouring*. She can see it in the way his whole body goes still, rooted to the stone as if he's grown out of it. All of him, everything, every scrap of whatever he runs on, going into the space between his palms. More and more and more of it. Not the clean quick thing she heard about. Something enormous. Something being made very slowly and very carefully by a man who has decided, for these few seconds, not to keep anything back at all.

His feet don't move. His shoulders don't move. Nothing moves except the thing between his hands, swelling, and the air of the whole basin bending in toward it, and the light going strange.

Yoru understands, with a slow delighted chill, what he's done.

He's made himself *helpless*.

For these few seconds, the most dangerous man she's ever looked at is standing in the open in the middle of a bowl full of things that want to eat him, and he can't so much as turn his head. All of him is in his hands. If anything touches him now, he can't stop it. If the dog with the legs reaches him in the next three seconds, it will find a man who can't lift a finger to push it away.

And the dogs are almost there.

She leans forward so far she nearly slips off the wall.

*This*, she thinks. *Now. What do you do now, king?*

* * *

The woman beside him speaks.

Yoru doesn't see her move. She doesn't need to. She's been watching the king so hard she nearly forgot the woman was there at all: standing at his shoulder, close, her hand an inch from his. She doesn't lift that hand. She doesn't step forward. She doesn't even look at the dogs.

She just says, in a quite ordinary voice, as if asking someone to pass the salt:

“Stop.” ᴹᵏ

Everything stops.

Everything.

The dog with the legs stops in the middle of a leap, a body-length from the king's throat, and hangs there, and comes down on its feet and doesn't move again. The thing like a door stops with one hinge lifted. The thing that's mostly teeth stops with them open. The ones still pouring over the rim stop on the slope, all of them, mid-stride, mid-howl, mid-hunger, frozen on the sides of the bowl like a picture of a flood.

Hundreds of them. All at once. In a word.

Up on the wall, Yoru goes very still too.

Not because she's been told to. Nobody told her. She's not in the basin and she's not hungry and she isn't something that voice could put a hand on. She goes still because she knows that voice.

She has known it for longer than this world has existed. She's heard it at the tops of stairs and the ends of corridors and in her own head, in the dark, in a place without light. She has hated it. She has been afraid of it, a little, which she has never admitted to anyone.

*Sister*, she thinks.

Down in the basin, Makima doesn't look up at the ridge. She doesn't need to. She's looking at the king, very calmly, waiting.

And the king, who could not move, who had made himself helpless, who had trusted the woman at his shoulder with the three seconds in which anything at all could have killed him, opens his hands.

* * *

Yoru has seen a great many things erased.

She's seen the Chainsaw eat. She's watched names go out of the world like lamps, watched whole fears stop having ever been. She knows what nothing looks like.

This is different.

The thing between his palms doesn't fly. It *opens*. It goes out from him in a wide flat sweep, like a breath, like the first push of a tide, and everything it passes over stops being there. Not broken. Not burned. Not thrown. Gone. The dog with the legs is there and then the place where it was is there instead. The thing like a door, gone. The teeth, gone. The frozen flood on the north slope of the basin, row on row on row of small, still, hungry things that couldn't run because a voice had told them not to, gone, gone, gone, in one long silent stroke from left to right, as if someone had drawn a hand across a slate.

The stone underneath isn't touched. He's steering it. Even now, even with everything he has in it, he's holding the edge of it a hand's width off the ground, so the floor of the basin doesn't go with them.

It takes perhaps two seconds.

Then he closes his hands, and it's done, and he lets out a breath Yoru can hear from the ridge.

Almost all of them. Nine in ten. More.

* * *

Not all.

A few at the edges. The ones that were still coming over the rim when the voice spoke, and stopped half over the lip, outside the sweep. A dozen. Twenty. Waking up, now that the voice has let go of them, and finding the basin empty in front of them, and understanding, the way even the stupidest fear understands, that something has gone very wrong.

They turn to run.

The ground at the rim opens under them.

Not the ground. The shadow. Long lines of it, running out from the king's feet across the basin floor faster than anything should run, and at the end of each line, at the rim, something stands up out of the dark.

Soldiers. Yoru counts thirty, forty, then stops counting. Knights in shadow-armour. Things with spears. A tall one with a sword at the front, who moves like a man who's done this ten thousand times. They don't attack. They don't need to. They just *stand*, all round the edge of the bowl, shoulder to shoulder, and the fleeing things hit that wall and turn back, and run the other way, and find the wall there too, and are herded, without a single blow struck, down the slope and into the middle of the empty basin, into a tight frightened knot in the place where the floor is still bare.

Yoru realises what the shadows are doing a moment before it happens.

She looks up.

The sky above the basin has gone dark in one place.

Something comes down out of it.

It's the dragon. The one that lies in the orchard and lets the child climb on it. She's seen it there, lying along the bottom of the orchard like a range of low hills, with a little girl in an explorer's jacket asleep in the crook of one foreleg. It is not curled now. It comes out of the high air with its wings back and its jaws open, and it doesn't even land. It passes over the basin once, low and fast, and breathes.

The fire doesn't miss.

It fills the middle of the basin from wall to wall of the shadow soldiers, and burns for a long moment, and goes out.

When it's gone there's nothing in the middle of the basin but a wide black scorch on old stone, and smoke going up thin and straight in the windless morning.

The soldiers sink back into the shadow. The dragon wheels once, high up, and dives, and is gone too, folded back into the dark at the king's feet like a coat put on a hook.

The basin is empty. The names are still there. Yoru can think them, even the small, stupid ones. The bodies will have to start over elsewhere. Chainsaw would have taken the names with him.

The king is standing in the middle of it, with his hands at his sides, and the woman at his shoulder, exactly where they were.

The grass she spat off the wall is still drifting down the side of it. Less than a minute.

* * *

She sits back on the child's wall.

She's aware, distantly, of Asa underneath, making the small horrified sound Asa makes. She ignores it.

Three moves.

She counts them again, to be sure.

The erasing. That was his.

The voice. That was her sister's.

The fire. That was the dragon's.

Three moves, and two of them weren't even his. He didn't open a domain. He didn't open the shrine. He didn't take anything out of that great dark bag of a shadow except a few dozen soldiers to stand in a ring and look at things. He didn't try anything new. He didn't *play*.

He stood in the middle of a bowl full of things that wanted him dead, made himself completely helpless for three seconds because he trusted the person next to him to cover him, and then ended it, cleanly, the way you'd clear a table, and stood there afterward with his hands at his sides as if he'd done nothing more interesting than take out the rubbish.

Down in the basin, he turns, and looks up at the ridge. At her.

He doesn't say anything. He doesn't need to.

Yoru takes the grass out of her mouth and looks at it, and drops it on the pile.

“How boring,” she says. ʷᵈ

She means it. She means it from the bottom of whatever she has instead of a heart. She came to watch a man discover that he loves war, and she has watched a man discover that he doesn't need it.

She stands up on the wall. She brushes the dust off Asa's skirt.

“Ninety days,” she calls down, conversationally, across the whole empty basin. “Still. Don't forget. I'll think of something better.” ʷᵈ

She doesn't wait for an answer.

She steps down off the far side of the child's wall, and walks away along the ridge, and doesn't look back.

Behind her, on the stone, the words the child copied so carefully are still there, deep-cut and old and very slightly crooked. Yoru doesn't read them.

REST HERE. WE DID.
