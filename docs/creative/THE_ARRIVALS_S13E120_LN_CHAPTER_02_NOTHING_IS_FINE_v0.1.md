# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 4 — Nothing Is Fine

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E120
**Continuity state:** B+435, late morning. The house expected to wait all day. Ciel's report reaches Rimuru: done. Mau and Makima are back on the hill road before anyone has finished believing it. On the road Mau asks to hold onto Makima, lets her take his weight and falls asleep against her. She carries him as far as the return point, where Gojo takes his weight and helps him walk once he's awake. The weight of the Purple and the quarry's compression catches up with him after two nights without sleep. He doesn't go to the porch. He doesn't go to Yuka. Before he can think, he goes straight to the clinic. Makima sits on the clinic step; Noise holds her body there. Mau tells Shoko everything at once, the whole bundle together, though she already knows much of it: everything he had closed, Cross, the room with Frieren, and the Final Trial, when he heard Yuka's "If you won't let me be with you when it hurts, I don't know how to stay in this" as leaving and spent ~368 local days (one day outside) working out how to say goodbye. Shoko sees him nodding off, bandages his split palms, makes him drink water and gives him something to sleep properly. He holds her hand and asks how it's going with Gojo. Before he falls asleep he asks her: if Yuka or Ori ask, tell them the truth. Nothing of "it's fine". He sleeps holding Shoko's hand. In the clinic he empties everything onto Shoko except one thing: the band coming off in the yard on B+431, which he has replayed for four days and still sees though it was back on her finger yesterday. He has the words and swallows them.

*When: B+435. Late morning. Rimuru has read "Done" out loud twice, and the second time nobody laughed.*

The hill road is longer on the way back.

It isn't, really. Mau knows it isn't. It's the same road, the same bend, the same wet grass going lighter grey as the sun climbs. But his legs have started to disagree with him about it, somewhere around the top of the ridge, and by the time the orchard comes into sight below them they're arguing with every step.

*Repair is keeping up*, says Ciel. *Barely. You put everything into that.* ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*Your hands.* ᶜ

He looks at them. He hadn't noticed. Both palms are split across, the skin opened in long fine lines where the thing he made pressed back against him. Not deep. Bleeding a little. The RCT closing them, slowly, and opening again every time he flexes his fingers.

*It's fine*, he starts to say, and stops.

He doesn't finish it.

Makima, beside him, has his elbow. Not his hand. His elbow, the way you'd hold someone who's had too much to drink, so lightly that he can pretend she isn't. He doesn't pretend. He lets her.

“You can sit down,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“At the bottom.” ᴹ

“You said that at the top.” ᴹᵏ

He keeps walking. For a while he can work out where to put his next foot from the way the gravel moves under the other one. Then the grass comes in across the road and gets both of them wet to the knee. He steps off it a little too quickly. Makima tightens her hand at his elbow before he can put a split palm down to catch himself.

“How far to Gojo?” he asks. ᴹ

“One bend. You can see the post from here.” ᴹᵏ

He lifts his head. There's a fence post a little below them with an old rusted staple in it. The one at the turn has two staples. He can see the second one plainly the next time his eyes open.

“Wait,” he says. ᴹ

They stop. He has her sleeve between two fingers. He means to use it to keep from swaying while Ciel shows him where he is. He's still waiting for the road to make sense when she takes half a step nearer.

“Can I?” He gets his hand up as far as her shoulder. She waits until he puts it where he meant to. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

He puts both arms round her and rests his forehead against her shoulder. There's a noise he's been keeping in since the first piece of stone came back under his feet that morning. He lets it out into the fabric. Her hand comes up between his shoulder blades. He can feel her shift one foot in the gravel as he leans harder.

“I can't go another bend yet,” he says. ᴹ

“Then don't.” ᴹᵏ

He has spent the whole walk waiting until he can see the next place to put his foot. He leaves his last one where it is. By the time his grip loosens, she's already holding him up on her own.

His next memory is of her arm underneath his knees and the fence going past behind her.

* * *

He comes round enough to feel the walking. Each step lifts him a little in her arms. He can feel the place his boot keeps knocking against her leg. He tries to pull it in. She shifts her arm so he can stop trying.

“Did I fall?” he asks. ᴹ

“No. You fell asleep.” ᴹᵏ

“How long have you been carrying me?” ᴹ

“I've got about twelve steps left before he says something.” ᴹᵏ

He turns his head. It takes her a little more than twelve. Gojo comes off the bank at the return point with his blindfold pushed up on his head and his mouth open. The first thing out of it is Mau's name. He says it again when he sees Mau's eyes are open, this time like a question.

“I can get down,” says Mau. ᴹ

Gojo puts a hand on his shoulder. “Hang on. How's she got you?” ᴳ

Makima shows him with a small lift of the elbow under Mau's back. Gojo comes in from that side while she lowers him. His feet meet the road so gently he doesn't know he's standing until he can feel a stone through the worn place in his boot. Then he notices he's holding Gojo's forearm. Gojo has turned it so Mau can lean on the sleeve without having to close his fingers.

“That's it,” says Gojo. He waits to feel how much of Mau's weight is on him. “Let me have the rest for a bit.” ᴳ

It's a lot more than Mau would have said it was. Gojo takes it. Makima keeps her hand at Mau's other elbow and eases him forward when he moves.

They go about as far as the first tree. Mau stops there. He knows where he is now. He used to hear the gate from here, before he could see anyone coming out to hold it for him. He's expecting a latch to lift. A shoe moving to make room for his. The sound gets tangled up with a boot he listened for at the quarry until he heard every other pair get there.

“Take me to Shoko,” he says. ᴹ

Gojo glances back down the path to the house. “All right. I'll go in with you if you want.” ᴳ

“Just to the step.” ᴹ

“Step it is.” ᴳ

Mau takes another breath against his sleeve. “Could somebody tell Ori I'm coming home? Before she sees everybody run.” ᴹ

*I'll ask Rimuru to go to her first*, says Ciel. ᶜ

He lets her do it while he watches Gojo step over a root. He puts his own foot in the same place. Makima reaches over and moves the collar that got folded under his neck while he slept. When he turns to thank her she has her eyes on another root, one he hasn't seen yet. She waits for him to get across it.

Kamish's tree is empty. The dragon is folded away in the dark. Mau can feel him there, warm and pleased with himself. He'll have to go and lie under the tree later and let Ori climb on him and tell him he was good.

Later.

* * *

The house sees them before they reach the gate.

He hears it before he sees it. Somebody on the porch shouting, then lots of somebodies, then feet. Then he's out from under the trees and there they all are, coming off the porch and across the yard, Rimuru at the front, Fern, Stark, Umaru with a dressing gown on over everything, Mai with her arms folded too tight. Frieren, not running, walking very fast with her staff. Beru at the top of the stairs by the door, with Ori's hand in his claw. Rimuru told her he was coming, and she has both slippers on, which means somebody got her to stop long enough to put them on. She calls to him from the stairs. He lifts his hand far enough to show her he heard.

And in the middle of the yard, on the grass, where he left her.

Yuka.

Standing up now, with the notebook against her chest. In his shirt. Still in his shirt. The only colour in the world.

She's not running. She's not moving at all. She's looking at him the way you look at a door you've been staring at all morning, waiting for it to open, when it finally opens.

He looks at her.

And something in him that he has been holding very carefully, all morning, all night, all of the last week, through the panic at the wall and the empty place at the quarry and the way she said *okay* this morning, goes.

Not all at once. Not dramatically. It just stops being held. The way your hand opens when you fall asleep holding something.

He can't.

He can't go to her. Not like this. Not with his hands bleeding and his legs going and his head full of everything he hasn't said. If he goes to her now, he'll say *it's fine*. He knows he will. He can feel the words already in his mouth, waiting, the way they've waited every day for a week. And he can't say it to her again. Not today. Not after this morning.

So he doesn't think.

He turns left, past the training posts and toward the clinic. His knee goes soft on the first step. Gojo takes him under the arm and keeps walking when Mau can walk again. At the door Mau has to look down to find the step he asked them to take him to.

He doesn't look back.

He knows exactly where she is.

* * *

Shoko is in the doorway.

Of course she is. She was in the clinic doorway this morning when he left, with an unlit cigarette. She's in the doorway now, with the same cigarette, still unlit, and she looks at him coming across the yard, and then she looks past him, once, at the middle of the grass, and then back at him.

She holds the door. Gojo waits until Mau gets both feet across the sill. For a second Mau still has his sleeve. Gojo stays where he is until Mau is the one who lets go.

He goes in.

Makima stops on the step.

She can't come far from him, not with her body outside. Noise holds her there. She sits down on the clinic step with her knees together and her hands in her lap, facing the yard, the way a dog sits outside a door it isn't allowed through, and doesn't move.

Gojo stays outside too. Mau hears him say something low to Makima, and Makima say something back, and then nothing.

The clinic is quiet. It always is. The cot by the window. The cupboard. The kettle. The smell of whatever Shoko uses to clean things, sharp and clean and familiar.

He sits down on the end of the cot.

He doesn't remember deciding to.

Shoko closes the door.

* * *

She doesn't ask what happened. She knows what happened; Rimuru has been reading the reports out loud all morning. She takes his hands, turns them over, looks at the palms.

“What did this?” ˢʰ

“Purple.” ᴹ

“How much of you did you put in it?” ˢʰ

“All of it.” ᴹ

She looks at him over his hands.

“I told you not again today,” she says. ˢʰ

“It wasn't the domain.” ᴹ

“I know it wasn't the domain.” She lets go of his hands and goes to the cupboard. “That's why I'm only going to shout at you a little.” ˢʰ

She doesn't shout. She comes back with a bowl and a cloth and the roll of bandage, and pulls the stool over with her foot, and sits down in front of him, and starts to clean his palms.

It stings. He watches her do it.

And then, without meaning to, without any decision at all, he starts to talk.

* * *

“I closed everything,” he says. ᴹ

Shoko doesn't look up. She keeps cleaning.

“I closed the relays,” he says. “The contracts. Yuka's techniques. Mai's supply. I closed them all because I thought—” ᴹ

He stops.

“Go on,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

So he goes on.

Most of it she knows. That's the strange part. He can see her knowing it as he says it: Gojo told her about the field, and he told her about the room with Frieren himself, one of those afternoons on this cot, and she was in the room after the Trial, when he told a small chosen few what it had been, and the next day, when he and Yuka told her the worst of it. None of it is news to her.

He tells her anyway. All of it. Not in order. Not one thing and then the next, the careful way he tells things when he's decided what they mean. All of it at once, in one pile, the way you empty a bag onto a table because you can't carry it another step.

He starts with the cup on the windowsill in the other life and ends up telling her about the piece of grass in the crack in the wall. About being able to see it and not being able to feel either hand. He talks about it as if he'd gone out to sit down because the air inside was close. Shoko has stopped cleaning his hand by the time he gets to Makima finding him.

“That happens again,” she says, “will you come here?” ˢʰ

“I was across the yard. It's not far.” ᴹ

“You're telling me you couldn't get a breath or feel your hand. You don't have to decide how far it is. You can ask someone to get me.” She opens the cloth where it was folded in her palm. “What happened after that?” ˢʰ

He tells her about the seat at the quarry. He gets as far as sitting down in it himself. There's something awfully hard to say about having left a place for someone and then sitting in it with your feet on the rocks, checking where all the blood had dried while you waited until you could walk home.

“I know,” Shoko says, now and then. Not to stop him. Just so he knows she's still there. ˢʰ

And the Trial.

“She said she didn't know how to stay in this,” he says. “The morning before. *If you won't let me be with you when it hurts, I don't know how to stay in this.* She didn't mean it like that. She told me after. I know she didn't.” His voice does something. He waits for it to stop. “But I heard it like that. And then I went to the inspection and the door shut and it was the Trial.” ᴹ

“I know,” says Shoko. “You told us.” ˢʰ

“A year,” he says, as if she hadn't spoken. “Three hundred and sixty-eight days. And she was out here for one. And I spent all of it working out how to say goodbye to her properly. A guest room. How to keep away when Ori visited. How to keep protecting her from somewhere she didn't have to look at me.” ᴹ

Shoko's hands have slowed down on his palm.

“I told you all that,” he says. “I told her. We talked. I came back and I opened up and I've been trying, every day, and I thought it was *behind* me.” ᴹ

He looks at his hands in hers.

“I told her I loved her in the yard,” he says. “She said she knew. Then she asked Cross to go with her.” ᴹ

Shoko stops with the cloth folded across both her fingers. She waits for him to turn his other hand over himself.

“After the duel, when she said it back, I heard that too,” he says. “She brought a plate. I was sure Shuna had asked her. She'd put her boots on, she said. I couldn't ask for the rest of it. I could barely ask Yuta to get you.” ᴹ

Shoko takes his other hand once he's got it open. “He heard you,” she says. “He came straight over.” ˢʰ

He tries to turn his hand over. She gets there before he can.

“And then in the yard,” he says. “When I got home from the gate. She said *you'd rather have her. When it's bad you go to her.*” ᴹ

He's very quiet now.

“That's the thing I'm most afraid of,” he says. “Not that I'd rather have someone else. That she'd think I would. That she'd look at me and see someone who goes to other people when it's bad. Because that's what I did at the inn. I went *away*. I went away from everyone so they'd be safe, and they weren't, and—” ᴹ

He stops again.

“She took the thing I'm most afraid of,” he says, “and turned it round, and said it to me. And she didn't even mean it. I know she didn't mean it. I know she was hurt and she was frightened and she said the worst thing she could find. I *know* that.” ᴹ

He looks down at his hands in hers.

There's one more thing in the pile. It's been sitting on top of everything else since the yard, and he's been walking round it for four days.

He can see it now. He can see it any time he stops: by the table in the yard, the cup, her thumb going under the band. The place it stuck on her knuckle. The half a breath when she could have pushed it back down, and didn't, and slid her other thumb under it and kept going. Her fingers closing round it. Her hand going into the hoodie pocket.

He's seen it a hundred times. He saw it in the dark at the quarry with his back against the rock. He saw it this morning on the road. He'll see it again tonight. It doesn't get smaller when he looks at it. It just plays again.

And yesterday it was back on her finger. At the long table, carrying the extension in with Shizue. The band touched the handle of his cup, and he looked at it, sitting where it belongs, and he still watched it come off.

He opens his mouth to tell Shoko.

Nothing comes. Not because there aren't words. He has the words. *She took her ring off in front of me.* Nine of them. He can't make them go in the air between him and Shoko, where they'd be true out loud, and Shoko would ask what it meant, and he'd have to find out whether he knows.

He swallows it. It goes down like the rest of the things he swallows, and sits.

He looks up at Shoko.

“And this morning,” he says, “I said it back to her without meaning to. *Makima's coming with me.* I said it to make her feel better. And I watched it go into her. And she said *okay*.” ᴹ

He breathes out.

“And I don't know how to stop,” he says. “Getting out of the way. I don't know how to stop doing it. I've done it my whole life. Both lives. I keep putting things behind a door so I can keep going, and the door keeps opening, and every time it opens there's more behind it.” ᴹ

* * *

Shoko has finished cleaning.

She doesn't say anything for a while. She reaches for the bandage and starts to wrap his left palm, carefully, round and round, the way she does everything, as if she has all the time in the world.

She's watching him while she does it. He can feel her watching.

His head is very heavy.

He didn't notice it getting heavy. It just is. Everything is. The room has gone soft at the edges, and the light from the window is very warm on the side of his face, and his eyes keep closing on their own and opening again a moment later, surprised.

“Mau,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

He opens his eyes.

“You're falling asleep,” she says. ˢʰ

“I'm not.” ᴹ

“You've been falling asleep for the last five minutes. Mid-sentence. Twice.” She ties off the bandage on his left hand and starts on the right. “When did you last sleep?” ˢʰ

He thinks about it. He genuinely can't remember.

“Before Yoru came,” he says. ᴹ

“You lay down after the quarry because I made you. That isn't sleeping. And you didn't lie down at all last night.” ˢʰ

“Didn't I?” ᴹ

She finishes the right hand. She gets up, fills a cup from the jug on the side, and puts it into his bandaged hands, and closes his fingers round it with hers.

“Drink that,” she says. “All of it.” ˢʰ

He drinks it. All of it.

She takes the cup back. Then she goes to the cupboard again, and comes back with something small in a twist of paper, and a second cup.

“This is going to make you sleep,” she says. “Properly. Not the way you sleep when you're listening for things. Ciel's going to keep counting and Noise is going to keep watching and nobody is going to wake you up for anything less than the house being on fire. And if the house is on fire, Gojo's outside, and he can put it out.” ˢʰ

He looks at the paper.

He thinks about saying *I don't need it*. He thinks about saying *it's fine*.

He holds out his hand.

She puts it in his palm and watches him take it. Then she makes him lie down. She pushes him back on the cot with one hand on his chest, gently, the way you'd push a door shut, and puts the blanket over him, and sits back down on the stool beside him.

* * *

He's almost gone when he finds her hand.

He doesn't mean to. His bandaged hand just moves across the blanket and finds hers on the edge of the cot, and holds on. Not hard. Just holds. Like a child who's afraid of the dark and too old to say so.

Shoko doesn't take her hand away.

“How's it going,” he says. His voice is very slurred. “With Gojo.” ᴹ

There's a silence.

Then Shoko laughs. Short and surprised, out of somewhere she doesn't usually laugh from.

“You're asking me that,” she says. “*Now.*” ˢʰ

“You never tell me.” ᴹ

“You never ask.” ˢʰ

“I'm asking.” ᴹ

She looks down at him. At his face on the pillow, at his eyes trying to stay open, at the bandaged hand holding hers.

“Slowly,” she says at last. “It's going slowly. He's being very careful. He's never been careful in his life. It's strange to watch.” ˢʰ

“Good strange?” ᴹ

“Good strange,” Shoko says quietly. ˢʰ

“Good,” he says. ᴹ

His eyes close.

Then they open again. Just once. He looks at her, and there's something in his face she's seen before, in other patients, in the moment before the anaesthetic, when they remember the one thing they meant to say.

“Shoko,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm here.” ˢʰ

“If Yuka asks,” he says. “Or Ori. If they ask. Tell them the truth.” ᴹ

She goes still.

“All of it?” she says. ˢʰ

“All of it,” he says. “Everything I told you. Don't—” ᴹ

He's going. She can see him going.

“Don't tell them it's fine,” he says. “Nothing of *it's fine*. Not from you. Not about me. Not any more.” ᴹ

“I won't,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“Promise.” ᴹ

She thinks about Ori at the door this morning, in her nightdress and her explorer jacket. *That's the real one.*

“I promise,” she says. ˢʰ

He sighs. A long one, all the way out.

And then he's asleep. Really asleep. The kind of sleep she hasn't seen on him since she met him: no listening in it, no edge, no part of him still standing guard. His face goes slack and young on the pillow. His breathing slows and deepens. His hand, in hers, goes loose.

He doesn't let go of it.

Shoko sits on the stool beside the cot with her hand in his and looks at him for a long time.

Then she looks at the door.

On the other side of it, on the step, Makima is sitting with her hands in her lap, facing the yard. And past her, in the yard, the house is standing very quietly in a loose ring around the clinic, the way people stand outside a room where someone is very ill, not knowing whether they're allowed to come in.

And in the middle of them, a little apart, with a notebook against her chest, is a young woman in a grey shirt with a hole at the collar, looking at the clinic door with an expression that Shoko has only ever seen on the faces of people in corridors outside rooms like this one.

Shoko doesn't get up.

She'll wait until they ask.
