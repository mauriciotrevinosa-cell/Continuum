# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 5 — The Same Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E121
**Continuity state:** B+435, around noon. Yuka's point of view. She saw him come back and walk past her, straight to the clinic, and it frightened her more than the war did. When she knocks, all she is told at first is that he needs to sleep. Then she asks. Shoko, who promised, tells her the truth in the herb room behind the clinic, with Mau's permission and in Mau's words: everything he had closed, the plate, Cross, "before you", the room with Frieren in the other life, the red gate. Yuka remembers what she said before that gate, that she wanted to stop this, said in a way that sounded like leaving the marriage if he didn't open up. He spent a year inside with that question; she spent three days outside watching the gate turn red. Then, in the yard, she took the thing he fears most and said it to him. Nothing is forgiven or fixed in this chapter. Ori asks too, and gets the truth at her size. Ori stays with Mau and Shoko. Yuka goes to talk to the house.

*When: B+435. Around noon. On the page under "52" there are seven lines in Yuka's handwriting. She has crossed out the fourth.*

He walked past her.

That's the thing Yuka can't get past. Not the war. She'd been afraid of the war all morning, sitting on the grass with the notebook, listening to Rimuru read the reports out loud from the porch: *at the ridge*, *plan set*, *expected duration: short*. Nobody believed the last one. She didn't believe it. She sat there and wrote her list and crossed things out and wrote them again and waited for the report that would say something had gone wrong.

It didn't come. *Done*, Rimuru read. And then, a few minutes later, very quietly, *on the hill road*.

And then he came out from under the trees with Makima at one elbow and Gojo at the other, and she stood up with the notebook against her chest, and he looked at her.

He looked right at her. Across the whole yard. She saw his face. She saw something in it go, like a rope going slack.

And then he turned left and walked past the training posts to the clinic and went in and the door closed.

He didn't come to her. He didn't even try.

She has been standing in the same place ever since.

* * *

The house is round the clinic in a loose ring, the way the house stands when someone's hurt. Rimuru with his notebook closed. Frieren with her staff. Fern holding Stark's sleeve. Umaru in her dressing gown with her hands pressed over her mouth. Mai with her arms crossed so tight it must hurt. Gojo by the clinic wall, blindfold down, not saying anything to anybody.

And on the clinic step, with her knees together and her hands in her lap, Makima.

Yuka looks at her.

Makima looks back.

There's nothing in it. No triumph. No pity. Makima's face is very calm and very tired, and she looks at Yuka for a long moment and then, very slightly, moves her knees aside on the step. Enough to let someone past.

Yuka doesn't move.

She's afraid of what's behind that door.

Ori isn't.

Ori comes down the porch steps at last, with Beru behind her like a shadow with wings, and walks straight across the yard through the ring of grown-ups, and stops on the clinic step beside Makima, and knocks.

Small. Polite. Twice.

The door opens a hand's width. Shoko looks out. She looks at Ori, and then over Ori's head at Yuka, and her face doesn't do anything.

“He's asleep,” she says. “He needs to sleep. That's all.” ˢʰ

*That's all.*

Yuka hears it and something in her goes cold all the way down. Because that's what *it's fine* sounds like. That's what it sounds like when someone tells you nothing so that you'll stop asking.

She takes a step forward before she knows she's going to.

“No,” she says. ʸ

Everybody looks at her.

Her voice is shaking. She doesn't care. She walks across the grass to the clinic step, past the house, past Gojo, past Makima moving her knees aside, and stops in front of Shoko, and looks up at her.

“No,” she says again. “Not *that's all*. I'm asking. Please. I'm asking you. What's wrong with him?” ʸ

Shoko looks at her for a long moment.

Then she opens the door the rest of the way.

“He said you'd ask,” she says. ˢʰ

* * *

He's asleep on the cot by the window.

Really asleep. Yuka has seen him asleep a thousand times and never like this. He's lying on his back with the blanket up to his chest and his face turned a little toward the window, and there's nothing in it. No listening. No edge. His mouth is a little open. His hands are bandaged, both of them, white on the blanket, and one of them is lying open on the edge of the cot where somebody's hand must have just been.

She wants to go to him so badly that she has to hold on to the doorframe.

Shoko doesn't let her. Not unkindly. She puts a hand on Yuka's arm and steers her past the cot, through the little door at the back, into the herb room, where it smells of dried things and there's a bench under the shelves and a window onto the orchard.

She sits Yuka down on the bench.

She sits down beside her.

“He told me to tell you,” says Shoko. “Before he fell asleep. He said: *if Yuka asks, or Ori, tell them the truth. All of it. Don't tell them it's fine.*” ˢʰ

Yuka stares at her.

“He said that?” ʸ

“He made me promise.” ˢʰ

Yuka puts her hand over her mouth.

“So I'm going to tell you,” says Shoko. “What he told me. In his words, as near as I can. And you're going to sit there and hear it, and you don't have to say anything, and you don't have to fix anything. Not today. Today you just have to know.” ˢʰ

Yuka nods. She can't speak.

Shoko tells her.

* * *

She tells her about the doors.

That he closed everything, the relays, the contracts, her techniques, Mai's supply, all of it, because he thought he was getting out of the way. That he didn't do it to punish anyone. That he did it the way he does everything: so that whatever happened next wouldn't hurt anyone but him.

She tells her about the plate. That he saw it. That he'd been setting her portion aside on her side of his plate at every meal, the small sharp pickles he pretends not to like, and that the day she didn't reach over and steal them, he noticed, and didn't say anything, and kept setting them aside anyway.

She tells her about Cross. About *you're alive*, at the gate. About the face Yuka made then, that Mau had never seen on her. About *before you*.

She tells her about the room with Frieren. In the other life, the one this Frieren doesn't remember. The cup on the windowsill. The clothes. The books. How he moved himself out of the room when Fern came, before anyone could ask, so that it wouldn't hurt when they did. How it came back to him at the wall with Makima and hasn't stopped coming back.

And then Shoko tells her about the red gate.

* * *

Yuka knows what's coming.

She knew it the moment Shoko said *the red gate*. Her whole body knew it. She sits on the bench with her hands clenched on the notebook in her lap and hears it come, the way you hear a cart coming up the road long before you see it.

“He said you told him you wanted to stop,” says Shoko. “Before he went in. That you were tired of him not telling you things. And he said you said it in a way that sounded like you meant *us*. Like you'd go, if he didn't open up.” ˢʰ

Yuka closes her eyes.

She remembers. Of course she remembers. She remembers being so angry, so *tired*, after too many *it's fine*s and *don't worry*s and doors she couldn't open. She remembers looking for the hardest thing she could say. Not to hurt him. To make him *hear*. She remembers saying *I want to stop this*, and letting it sit there, ambiguous, letting him wonder what *this* meant, because she wanted him frightened enough to finally talk to her.

And she remembers him going very still. And nodding. And going into the gate.

And the gate going red.

“For you it was three days,” Shoko says. “For him it was a year.” ˢʰ

Yuka makes a sound she doesn't recognise.

“A year,” Shoko says quietly, “with that question. He told me. A whole year inside that thing, being taken apart, with *I want to stop this* and not knowing what it meant. Not knowing if there'd be anyone to come back to.” ˢʰ

Yuka has her face in her hands now.

Three days. She remembers the three days. She remembers them as the worst three days of her life.

She never once, in all the time since, thought about what three days was on the other side.

“He came back,” Shoko says. “He opened up. He told me that too. He's been trying ever since. Every day. And he thought you were all right. He thought *we* were all right. And then.” ˢʰ

She stops. She waits until Yuka takes her hands away from her face and looks at her.

“And then in the yard,” says Shoko, “four days ago, you told him *you'd rather have her. When it's bad you go to her.*” ˢʰ

Yuka can't breathe.

“That's the thing he's most afraid of,” Shoko says. “He told me. Not that he'd rather have someone else. That *you'd* think he would. That you'd look at him and see a man who goes away from people when it's bad. Because that's what he did at the inn. He went away from everyone so they'd be safe.” ˢʰ

She's very gentle. She doesn't soften a single word.

“You took the thing he's most afraid of,” she says, “and you turned it round, and you said it to him. He knows you didn't mean it. He said so. He said he knows you were hurt and frightened and you said the worst thing you could find.” ˢʰ

“I did,” Yuka whispers. ʸ

“He said that too.” ˢʰ

* * *

They sit in the herb room for a long time.

Shoko doesn't say anything else. She doesn't need to. She sits beside Yuka on the bench and lets her cry, and doesn't touch her, and doesn't tell her it's all right, because it isn't, and they both know it isn't, and Shoko promised.

Yuka cries the way she hasn't cried since the hospital. Not loudly. Not prettily. Hard and ugly and silent, with the notebook crushed against her stomach, until there's nothing left.

The door. That's what she keeps coming back to.

He puts things behind a door so he can keep going. She's known that about him for as long as she's known him. She used to think it was the thing she had to fix. She used to stand in front of that door and knock, and knock, and get angry when it didn't open, and say things to make it open.

And every time it opened, there was more behind it. Things she put there.

*I want to stop this.*

*You'd rather have her.*

*Okay.*

The same door. Opened over and over. And every time it opened, she was the one standing on the other side of it with the worst thing she could find in her hand.

“I didn't know,” she says at last. Her voice is wrecked. “About the year. I didn't—I never thought—” ʸ

“I know,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“Is he going to—” ʸ

She can't finish it. She doesn't know what she was going to ask. *Leave. Forgive me. Wake up. Ever look at me the way he did before.*

Shoko looks out of the window at the orchard for a moment.

“He's going to sleep,” she says. “For a long time, I think. I gave him something so he would. And then he's going to wake up, and he's going to still be carrying all of it. He doesn't forget things, Yuka. You know that. He just puts them away.” ˢʰ

She turns back.

“So I don't know,” she says honestly. “That's the truth. I don't know what he's going to do. I don't think he knows. I don't think this gets mended by one conversation, and I don't think it gets mended by you being sorry very hard at him. I think it gets mended slowly, if it gets mended. And I think it starts with you knowing what it actually is.” ˢʰ

Yuka looks down at the notebook in her lap.

The page with *52* at the top. And under it, the seven lines in her own handwriting. The fourth one crossed out.

She looks at the list for a long time.

* * *

When they come back out into the clinic, Ori is sitting on the end of the cot.

Nobody let her in. Somebody must have. She's sitting there with her feet not touching the floor and her explorer jacket on over her nightdress, very straight, watching Mau sleep. Beru is crouched in the corner by the door, folded up as small as a thing that size can fold, which isn't very.

Ori looks up at Shoko.

“Is he hurt?” she says. ᴼ

Shoko looks at Yuka. Yuka nods.

Shoko crouches down in front of the cot, so she's at Ori's height.

“A little,” she says. “His hands. They'll heal. But that's not the main thing.” ˢʰ

Ori waits. She's very good at waiting for the real one.

“He's been carrying some worries,” says Shoko. “For a while. Heavy ones. And a lot of things happened this week that he understood wrong. Things that looked like one thing and were really another. And then, on top of all that, he had to go and fight. And he did it, and he won, and now he's very, very tired, and he needs to sleep for a long time.” ˢʰ

Ori looks at Mau.

“Worries about us?” she says. ᴼ

Shoko thinks about it. She promised.

“Some of them,” she says. ˢʰ

Ori nods slowly, the way she nods when she's been handed a plan and is checking it for holes.

“Can I stay?” she says. ᴼ

“Yes,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“Can you tell me things while he sleeps?” Ori says. “Not about him. Just things. So it's not quiet.” ᴼ

Something happens to Shoko's face. Yuka sees it, and has to look away.

“Yes,” says Shoko. “I can do that.” ˢʰ

She pulls the stool over and sits down beside the cot, next to Ori. Ori shifts along so she's sitting right at the edge, close enough that her knee is touching Mau's arm through the blanket. Then she reaches over very carefully and puts her hand on top of his bandaged one, where Shoko's was.

Mau doesn't wake. But his fingers move, very slightly, under hers.

“What's the strangest thing anybody's ever swallowed?” says Ori. ᴼ

Shoko looks at her.

“Do you want the true answer,” she says, “or the one that's suitable for children?” ˢʰ

“The true one,” says Ori, at once. “Always the true one.” ᴼ

Shoko starts to tell her.

* * *

Yuka stands in the doorway of the clinic for a moment and looks at them. Ori on the edge of the cot with her hand on his. Shoko on the stool, talking low. Beru in the corner. Mau asleep in the middle of it, with his face turned toward the window, young and slack and finally, finally not listening for anything.

She wants to stay. She wants to sit on the floor beside the cot and hold his other hand and not move until he wakes up.

She doesn't.

That's the old thing. The knocking at the door. Standing there so that the first thing he sees when he opens his eyes is her face, wanting something from him. Wanting him to make it all right so that she can feel better.

Not this time.

She looks down at the notebook. At the list.

Then she turns round and goes out onto the step.

Makima is still sitting there. She looks up.

The two of them look at each other.

Yuka doesn't say anything to her. Not yet. There's too much, and none of it's ready. But she nods. Once. And Makima, after a moment, nods back.

The house is still standing in its ring round the clinic. All of them. Rimuru. Frieren. Fern and Stark. Umaru. Mai. Maki and Yuta, who must have come out while she was inside. Gojo by the wall. Shuna in the kitchen doorway, with a cloth in her hands.

They're all looking at her.

Yuka holds the notebook against her chest.

“I need to talk to all of you,” she says. ʸ

Her voice still sounds wrecked. She lets it.

“Not here,” she says. “Inside. Where he can't hear.” ʸ

She looks round at them. At every face.

“I did something,” she says. “Lots of things. And I need help. I don't know how to fix this by myself. I don't think I'm supposed to.” ʸ

Nobody says anything for a moment.

Then Shuna puts the cloth over her shoulder and holds the kitchen door open.

One by one, the house goes in.
