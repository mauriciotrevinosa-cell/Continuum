# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 7 — Battle Hoodie

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E122
**Continuity state:** B+437 ("50"), morning to late afternoon. Yuka's point of view. She is afraid, and she knows she's the one who got it wrong, so she has to be the one who stands in front. She puts on her battle armour: the anthracite and papaya McLaren hoodie. She doesn't go into the clinic. She helps Shuna make Mau's breakfast and lets Ori carry it in. Breakfast in the clinic: Mau, Ori, Shoko, Gojo, Frieren, and Makima, whom Mau calls through the bond to come and eat and tell her part. Yuka, on the clinic step with the window open, hears Makima tell the war: the Stop, the three seconds he trusted her with. It hurts, and it's also the thing Yuka asked him for: he accepts help now. Mau goes back to sleep. All afternoon Yuka moves a little closer, one step at a time, and doesn't go in. Ori notices.

*When: B+437. "50" in the notebook, in Yuka's hand, written at dawn at the kitchen table. Under it, the list. The fourth line is still crossed out. There is a fifth line now with a tick beside it: "breakfast".*

The hoodie is a decision.

Yuka stands in front of the wardrobe in Ori's room at dawn, where she's been sleeping since the night he didn't come up, and looks at it on its hanger for a long time.

Anthracite and papaya. The 2021 colours. It's too big for her. The sleeves come down over her hands and the hem comes down to her thighs and when she pulls the hood up it falls over her eyes. It's the thing she puts on when she has to be brave and isn't.

Her battle armour. Mai calls it that. Mai isn't wrong.

She takes it off the hanger and puts it on.

It still smells of him, faintly, under the smell of the cupboard. She stands there with the sleeves over her hands and her face in the collar for a moment longer than she meant to.

Then she pulls the hood back and goes downstairs.

* * *

She's afraid.

She's been afraid since yesterday, in the herb room, with Shoko's voice going on and on very quietly and not softening anything. She was afraid all last night, lying in Ori's bed without Ori, because Ori had gone back to the clinic for the third time and Shoko had stopped sending her away. She was afraid when she walked across the yard in the dark and put the notebook on the clinic step beside Makima and walked away again without saying anything to her.

She's afraid now, standing in the kitchen doorway at dawn in her battle armour, watching Shuna lay out the breakfast things.

Afraid of going into the clinic and seeing his face do what it did in the yard. Go slack. Turn away.

Afraid of *not* going in. Of being the woman who stands in the yard while her husband walks past her and doesn't follow him, because it's easier to be hurt than to be in the way.

Afraid most of all of the thing Shoko said at the end, in the herb room, looking out of the window at the orchard. *I don't think this gets mended by you being sorry very hard at him.*

She's been sorry very hard at him before. She knows what it looks like. It looks like knocking on a door until it opens, and then standing there wanting something.

She doesn't want to want something from him today.

She wants to be the one who stands in front. She got it wrong. Twice. She's the one who has to come to him, not the other way round, and she has to do it in a way that doesn't make him do any of the work.

She doesn't know how to do that yet.

So she starts small.

* * *

“I want to help,” she says to Shuna. ʸ

Shuna looks up from the bread. She looks at the hoodie, and her face does something very kind and very quick, and then she hands Yuka a knife.

“The fish,” she says. “The way he likes it. You know how.” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka knows how.

They make his breakfast together, without saying much. The fish the good way, the way Shuna makes it when someone's been ill. The rice soft. Eggs. A little pot of the plum paste he pretends not to like and always finishes. A bowl of pickles, the small sharp ones, on the side.

Yuka looks at the pickles for a long time.

Then she puts them on the tray anyway. Because that's what goes on his tray. Because he'll set them aside. Because she wants him to.

She stands back and looks at it. The whole tray. She made most of it. It looks exactly like any other breakfast.

“Will you take it?” says Shuna, very gently. ˢʰᵘ

Yuka thinks about it. About carrying the tray across the yard and up the clinic step and through the door, and his face when he looks up and sees who's carrying it.

“No,” she says. “Not yet.” ʸ

Shuna nods as if that's a perfectly reasonable answer.

“Then who?” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka looks out of the kitchen window, across the yard, at the clinic.

The door's opening. A small figure is coming out of it, in a nightdress and an explorer's jacket, rubbing her eyes, with a much larger figure folded up behind her like a shadow with wings.

“Ori,” says Yuka. ʸ

* * *

Ori takes the tray very seriously.

She stands in the kitchen in her boots and looks at it, all the dishes, the pot of plum paste, the pickles. She looks at Yuka in the hoodie. She looks at the tray again.

“You made this,” she says. ᴼ

It isn't a question.

“Most of it,” says Yuka. ʸ

Ori considers this with the face of a general reviewing intelligence.

“Do you want me to tell him?” she says. ᴼ

Yuka's throat closes up. She shakes her head.

Ori nods once, briskly, as if that's a perfectly sound strategic decision. Then she picks up the tray, which is too heavy for her and which she carries anyway, with Beru hovering at her elbow ready to catch it, and walks very slowly and very carefully across the yard to the clinic, and doesn't spill anything.

At the clinic step she stops, and looks back over her shoulder at the kitchen window, where Yuka is.

Then she goes in.

* * *

Yuka doesn't mean to follow.

She tells herself she's going to stay in the kitchen. Then she tells herself she's going to stand in the doorway. Then she's halfway across the yard with her hands in the hoodie pocket, and she stops at the training posts, and tells herself she's going no further.

Then she's sitting on the clinic step.

Not in front of the door. At the end, by the corner, where the window is. Where she can't be seen from inside unless someone leans out. Where Makima sat yesterday, all day, with her hands in her lap.

The window's open. It's a warm morning.

She can hear everything.

* * *

They're having breakfast in there. All of them.

She can hear Gojo first, because Gojo is always loudest. He's come in from somewhere with a bag of something and is complaining that nobody saved him any fish, and Shoko is telling him that's because it wasn't his breakfast, and Gojo is saying everything is his breakfast if he's in the room, and Ori is telling him with great severity that the fish is *specifically* for Mau.

She can hear Frieren, saying very little, the way Frieren does. The scrape of a chair. *I'll have tea.* The small sounds of Frieren putting sugar in it and stirring for too long.

She can hear Mau.

Not much. He's quiet. He says *thank you* to Ori for the tray, and Ori says *you're welcome* in her most formal voice, and then there's a silence while he looks at it, and Yuka holds her breath.

He doesn't say anything about the tray.

But she hears, after a moment, the small particular sound of a pair of chopsticks moving something from one side of a bowl to the other. Setting something aside.

She puts her face in her knees.

* * *

“Where's Makima?” says Gojo. ᴳ

“Outside,” says Shoko. “She sat on the step all yesterday. Then she went to her room.” ˢʰ

There's a pause.

Then Mau says something Yuka can't hear. Not out loud. She knows that pause. It's the pause he makes when he's talking to the people who live inside him. To Ciel. To Noise. To DIO.

To Makima.

A minute later, footsteps across the yard. Yuka lifts her head.

Makima is coming from the north wing. She's walking quickly, for her, which is to say at a perfectly ordinary pace instead of her usual unhurried one. She reaches the clinic step and sees Yuka sitting at the end of it in the hoodie.

She stops.

The two of them look at each other.

Makima doesn't say anything. She inclines her head, very slightly, the way she did yesterday. Then she goes past Yuka and up the step and through the door, and Yuka hears Gojo say *there she is*, and Mau say, out loud now, quite clearly:

“Sit down. Eat. And tell them your part.” ᴹ

* * *

So Makima tells them.

Yuka sits on the step with her arms round her knees and listens.

Makima tells it the way she tells everything: calmly, precisely, without decoration, as if reading from a report she has written very carefully. The ridge. The ruins. The words on the stone. Yoru on the wall with a piece of grass in her mouth. The basin, empty, and then not empty. The hungry ones coming over the rim.

“And he put everything into the Purple,” says Makima. “Everything. He couldn't move. For three seconds, he couldn't turn his head.” ᴹᵏ

“That's idiotic,” says Gojo. He sounds delighted. ᴳ

“Yes,” Makima agrees. ᴹᵏ

“What did you do?” Ori asks. ᴼ

There's a little pause.

“I told them to stop,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“All of them?” ᴼ

“All of them.” ᴹᵏ

“And they *did*?” ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

Ori takes a long breath, audibly, the way she does when something is so good she needs extra air to hold it.

“Do they remember?” she says. “The devils. That it was you?” ᴼ

“No,” says Makima. “Nothing. They'll be born again somewhere far away, small and new, and they won't remember anything. Not him. Not the house. Not you.” ᴹᵏ

Ori considers this.

“Good,” she says. “Then they won't be sad.” ᴼ

There's a silence in the clinic. Yuka, on the step, finds she has her hand over her mouth.

Then Frieren says, very quietly, “He made himself helpless.” ᶠ

“Yes,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“For three seconds,” Frieren says. “In the middle of all of that. And he trusted you with them.” ᶠ

“Yes,” says Makima again. ᴹᵏ

And then, after a moment, more quietly, in a voice Yuka has never heard her use:

“I didn't ask him to. He just did it. He didn't even look at me first. He knew I'd be there.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

It hurts.

Of course it hurts. Yuka sits on the step and lets it. *He knew I'd be there.* Three seconds of his life, held in another woman's hands, and he didn't even look.

And under the hurt, like a stone under water, something else. Something she can't look away from.

*I wanted him to do that.*

That's what she said. Over and over. For a long time. *Let us help you. Let me be with you when it hurts. Stop going alone. Stop deciding the answer before anyone else gets to say anything.* That's what she asked for, in the morning before the Trial, with her finger bleeding. That's what she was so angry about, in the yard, four days ago.

He did it.

He went into a basin full of things that wanted him dead and made himself helpless and trusted someone else to keep him alive, without looking, because he knew they'd be there.

That isn't the man who goes away from people when it's bad.

That's the man she asked for.

He just didn't think he could ask it of *her*. Not that morning. Not after the yard. Not after *you'd rather have her*.

She did that.

She puts her forehead against her knees and breathes, slow, in and out, through the thick soft fabric of the hoodie, until it stops feeling like she's going to come apart.

* * *

He goes back to sleep after breakfast.

She hears it. Shoko's voice saying *bed*, and Mau saying *I've only been awake an hour*, and Shoko saying *and you've been awake an hour, and that's enough*. The cot creaking. Ori saying *I'll stay*, and Shoko saying *I know you will*.

Gojo comes out first. He stops on the step and looks down at Yuka sitting at the end of it in the hoodie.

He doesn't say anything. He doesn't grin. He just reaches down and pats her once on top of the head, the way you'd pat a student who's finally doing the hard thing, and doesn't make a joke of it.

Then he goes.

Frieren comes out next. She stands on the step a moment, looking out at the yard.

“He moved the pickles,” she says, to nobody in particular. ᶠ

Then she goes too.

Makima comes out last.

She stops beside Yuka. She doesn't sit. She looks down.

“He's asleep,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka nods.

Makima stands there a moment longer. As if she's going to say something else. Then she doesn't. She goes back to the north wing at her ordinary unhurried pace, and Yuka is alone on the step.

* * *

She stays.

All morning. Into the afternoon. Shuna sends Umaru out with a plate for her at lunch, and she eats some of it. Mai comes and stands at the training posts with her arms crossed and looks at her for a long time, and then nods, once, and goes back to the workshop. Milim comes bounding across the yard to ask what she's doing and Rimuru catches her by the collar halfway and carries her off, still talking.

Yuka stays.

At noon she's at the end of the step, by the corner.

At one she's moved to the middle of it. Not on purpose. She just shifted, at some point, to get out of the sun.

At two she's at the top of it, with her back against the doorframe.

At three, without quite deciding to, she reaches up and pushes the door open a hand's width. Not to go in. Just so it isn't closed.

Through the gap she can see the end of the cot. His feet under the blanket. Ori sitting cross-legged beside them with her notebook, writing something very small and very serious. Beru in the corner.

She doesn't go in.

She sits in the doorway with the door open a hand's width and her back against the frame, in the hoodie, with the sleeves over her hands. Close enough to hear him breathe.

That's as far as she gets today.

* * *

Ori looks up from her notebook.

Their eyes meet through the gap in the door.

Ori doesn't say anything. She doesn't call out. She doesn't wave. She looks at Yuka in the doorway for a long, measuring moment, and then she looks down at her notebook, and writes something else.

Then she tears out the page, folds it twice, and gets up. She walks very quietly to the door and holds the folded paper out through the gap.

Yuka takes it.

Ori goes back to her place by the cot without a word.

Yuka unfolds the paper.

It's a map. Ori's kind of map. The clinic, drawn as a box. The cot, drawn as a smaller box, with a stick figure lying in it. A smaller stick figure sitting beside it with a notebook. A dragon-shaped scribble in the corner that's meant to be Beru.

And outside the box, at the door, a stick figure in a hood. With an arrow going from it, very carefully, in pencil, across the floor of the clinic, to the empty chair on the other side of the cot.

Under the arrow, in Ori's best handwriting:

*NEXT EXPEDITION.*

Yuka folds the map up again very carefully, along Ori's folds, and puts it in the pocket of the hoodie, against her chest.

She doesn't go in.

Not today.

But she doesn't move from the doorway either, until Shuna rings the bell for supper.
