# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 8 — Stay

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E122
**Continuity state:** B+437 ("50"), supper. Mau's point of view. He wakes for the bell and walks to the kitchen on his own legs for the first time in two days. His chair at the long table is beside Yuka's, as it always is. He stops behind it and thinks about taking the empty one at the far end, beside Ori, so she won't have to sit next to him. Yuka, in the hoodie, says, "Stay." He sits. He sets the small sharp pickles aside on her side of his plate, the way he always has, without thinking. After a long moment she reaches over and takes them. Her first small step. Nobody at the table says a word. Nothing is talked about yet.

*When: B+437. Supper. Shuna rang the bell twice, the second time very gently, toward the clinic.*

The bell wakes him.

Not the first time. The first time he only half hears it, somewhere far off, and turns over into the warm place where Ori was and finds it empty. The second time it's closer, or softer, or both. Two notes, very gently. The way you'd knock on the door of someone who might be asleep.

He opens his eyes.

The light through the curtain has gone long and low.

*Supper*, says Ciel. *You've slept the afternoon. Shoko says you can walk to the kitchen if you want to walk to the kitchen. She says if you don't want to, someone will bring you a tray.* ᶜ

He lies there for a moment.

He thinks about the tray. About Ori carrying it across the yard this morning, step by careful step, with Beru hovering. About the fish done the good way. The plum paste. The pickles on the side.

He knows who made that tray. He knew the moment he lifted the cloth. Shuna makes the fish the good way, but she cuts the skin off. Yuka leaves it on, because he likes it crisp. She's the only one who knows that, because she's the only one who's ever watched him eat closely enough to notice.

He didn't say anything this morning. He couldn't. He just ate it, all of it, and set the pickles aside on the edge of the bowl where they always go, and Ori watched him do it with her general's face and didn't say anything either.

*I'll walk*, he says. ᴹ

* * *

The doorway is empty when he gets up.

He knows somebody was sitting in it. The door is still open a hand's width, the way he never leaves it. There's a dent in the dust on the step by the doorframe where somebody sat for a long time with their back against the wood. A single thread of something caught on a splinter of the frame, at about the height of a shoulder.

He doesn't touch it.

He walks across the yard. Slowly. His legs work. His left side works, which it hasn't properly in a week. His hands, in the bandages, don't hurt at all. The evening is warm and the yard smells of cut grass and woodsmoke, and the house is lit up in every window, and through the kitchen windows he can see them all, gathering round the long table, scraping chairs, arguing about something.

He stops at the kitchen door.

He's afraid.

He hasn't let himself say that word, even inside, even to Ciel. He says it now, standing at his own kitchen door with his hand on the frame. He's afraid to go in. Afraid of the room going quiet when he does. Afraid of her face.

*She was in the doorway all afternoon*, says Makima. Very quietly. Inside. *She didn't come in. She didn't leave.* ᴹᵏ

He knows. He saw the dent in the dust.

He goes in.

* * *

The room doesn't go quiet.

That's the first thing. He'd braced himself for it, for the whole table turning to look at him, the chairs stopping, the silence. It doesn't happen. Umaru is in the middle of explaining something to Milim about a game, and keeps explaining it. Senku is arguing with Rimuru about the boiler. Gojo, at the far end, lifts a hand without looking round, as if Mau's just come back from fetching something from the porch.

They're being very careful. He can feel it. The whole house being so carefully ordinary that it hums.

He loves them for it so much that for a moment he can't move.

Then he looks at the table.

His chair is where it always is. Halfway down the long side, under the window, where the light falls in the evenings. It's been his chair since the house was built.

And beside it, the chair next to his. Where it's always been.

She's in it.

* * *

She's in the hoodie.

He sees her the way he always sees her, the only colour in the room. Her face. Her hair, coming loose. Her hands, where they come out of the too-long sleeves, folded very tightly on the table in front of her. Everything else in the room is grey, the way it always is: the table, the bowls, the lamplight, the people he loves. The hoodie is grey too. He can't see what colour it is. He's never been able to. He knows which one it is from the shape of it, the size, the way the sleeves fall over her hands. He knows what it means when she wears it.

She's sitting very straight. She isn't eating. She isn't looking at him.

She knows he's there. He can tell from her shoulders.

He stands behind his own chair with his hand on the back of it.

And he thinks, very clearly, the way you think things at the top of a stair in the dark: *I could sit somewhere else.*

There's an empty chair at the far end. Beside Ori. Ori would like that. Nobody would say anything. It would just be a man sitting by his daughter, who'd spent all night and all day beside him in the clinic. Ordinary. Kind.

And Yuka wouldn't have to sit next to him.

She wouldn't have to sit there, all through supper, with him at her elbow, after everything Shoko told her. After the Trial and the guest room and the year of rehearsing goodbye. After the yard. She wouldn't have to be brave about him for an hour in front of everyone. She could eat. She could breathe.

He could give her that. He could get out of the way.

His hand tightens on the back of the chair.

He's already half turned toward the far end of the table when she speaks.

* * *

“Stay.” ʸ

One word. Very quiet. She still isn't looking at him. She's looking at her hands, folded on the table.

But she said it.

He stops.

The table, very carefully, doesn't stop. Umaru keeps explaining. Senku keeps arguing. Milim, who has noticed, is being held very firmly by the back of her collar by Rimuru, and is vibrating.

Mau stands with his hand on the back of his chair.

“Stay,” says Yuka again. A little louder. Her voice shakes on it, and she doesn't stop it shaking. “Please. Here. Not—not somewhere else.” ʸ

He looks at her.

She lifts her head and looks back.

Her eyes are red at the rims. She's been crying, and stopped, and decided not to hide that she had. She looks at him with her whole face, the way she used to before any of this, and it's frightened, and it's sorry, and it isn't asking him for anything except the one thing.

*Stay.*

He pulls out the chair. He sits down.

Beside her. Where he always sits.

Something goes through her when he does. He feels it more than sees it. A breath let out that she must have been holding since the bell.

Neither of them says anything else.

* * *

Shuna puts a bowl in front of him.

Rice. The stew she makes with the root vegetables. Bread. And on the side, in a little dish of their own, the small sharp pickles.

He looks at them.

He doesn't think about it. That's the thing. Afterwards, lying in the dark, he'll try to remember deciding to do it, and he won't be able to, because he didn't decide. His hands just do what they've done at every meal for as long as anyone can remember. He picks up his chopsticks. He picks up the pickles from the little dish, one by one, the small sharp ones he likes and pretends not to. And he sets them aside, in a little pile, on her side of his bowl.

Where she can reach.

He realises what he's done when he's finished. He looks at the little pile, sitting there on the edge of his bowl, on her side. Waiting.

The way they've been waiting at every meal for a week, while she didn't reach over. While she sat at the other end of the table. While she didn't come to supper at all.

He puts his chopsticks down.

He doesn't move the pickles back. He doesn't eat them himself. He doesn't know what to do. He just sits there, very still, with his hands in his lap, looking at the small grey pile on the edge of the bowl.

* * *

It takes a long time.

Long enough that he thinks she isn't going to. Long enough that Umaru finishes her explanation, and Milim gets free of Rimuru and is recaptured, and Gojo asks for the bread twice. Long enough that he picks up his own chopsticks again, and starts to eat his rice, slowly, so his hands have something to do.

Then, out of the corner of his eye, he sees her hand move.

Out of the too-long sleeve. Across the small space between her bowl and his. Shaking a little. She doesn't hide that either.

She takes one.

Just one. The smallest. She picks it up off his bowl the way she has a thousand times before. The thing they had. The thing she stopped doing.

She puts it in her mouth.

She makes the face. The one she always makes, because they're very sharp and she doesn't actually like them very much and never has. The one Ori learned from her, and makes too.

And she reaches over and takes another.

He doesn't look at her. He can't. If he looks at her he doesn't know what his face will do. He keeps his eyes on his rice and eats it, very slowly, one grain at a time, while beside him his wife steals the pickles off his plate one by one until the little pile is gone.

* * *

Nobody says anything.

Not a word. The whole table goes on being ordinary at the top of its voice. But Mau can feel it, all round him, like warmth from a fire. Umaru has her hands pressed flat on the table and is staring very hard at Milim. Mai, across the table, has put down her spoon and is looking at the ceiling with her jaw set, blinking a great deal. Shuna has turned round to the stove and isn't doing anything at it.

And at the far end, beside the empty chair where he nearly sat, Ori has stopped eating altogether.

She's watching them. Her pencil is in her hand.

She opens her notebook under the edge of the table, where she thinks nobody can see, and makes a very small, very careful mark. A tick.

Then she closes the notebook and goes back to her supper as if nothing has happened, and makes the face at her own pickles, the one she learned from Yuka.

* * *

They don't talk.

Not about anything that matters. Not at supper. He passes her the bread once, and she says *thank you*, and he says *you're welcome*, and that's all. It's the most careful conversation they've ever had.

When she's finished, she doesn't get up straight away. She sits there beside him with her hands back in her lap, in the hoodie, until he's finished too.

Then she gets up. She stands by her chair for a moment.

“Goodnight,” she says. ʸ

She doesn't touch him. She doesn't ask for anything. She just says it, very quietly, to the side of his face, and waits.

He makes himself look up.

“Goodnight,” he says. ᴹ

Her face does something. Not a smile. Something smaller and more fragile than that, a thing that might be a smile someday.

Then she goes. Up the stairs, toward Ori's room. Not their room. He hears her feet on every step.

He sits at the table with the empty edge of his bowl in front of him.

All six pickles. Gone.

* * *

He asks Shoko for the clinic bed again.

She's at the sink, washing her cup. She looks round at him.

“Your hands are healed,” she says. “Your side's healed. You don't need it.” ˢʰ

“I know.” ᴹ

She looks at him a moment longer.

“All right,” she says. She doesn't ask why. ˢʰ

He doesn't know how to explain it anyway. That he isn't ready to go up to the room where the notebook was on his pillow. That there's a conversation he has to have first, and it isn't with Yuka. Not yet.

It's with the people who live in him.

He has a lot to say to them, and they've waited long enough.
