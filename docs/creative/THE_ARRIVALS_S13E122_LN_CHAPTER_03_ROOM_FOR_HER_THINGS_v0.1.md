# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 9 — Room for Her Things

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S13E122
**Continuity state:** B+437 after supper. Mau goes upstairs for a shirt before returning to the clinic. Yuka finds him putting away more than he needs for one night. They talk briefly about what he heard in the yard and what she chose on the morning of the duel. Neither asks to reopen the line. He sleeps at the clinic. Yuka stays with Ori.

*When: B+437. After the dumplings.*

At the sink, Shoko takes a loose end of bandage off his thumb.

“What are you planning to do with that?” she asks. ˢʰ

He looks down. His hands have healed. He's been winding the old bandage around his fingers while she washes her cup, keeping it neat enough to use again.

“Leave it,” she says. “There's enough clean cloth in this house.” ˢʰ

He leaves it beside her cup. Then he goes up for a shirt.

There's still a bit of light upstairs. Somebody has left the window at the end of the hall open. The door to Ori's room is shut. He hears her chair drag across the floor and waits for her voice. Whatever she's doing in there, she does it quietly. Yuka must be with her.

He gets their own door open without making a sound. He knows how high to lift the handle to keep it from catching. Yuka learned it from him a long time ago. Once he woke up to her getting it open with her shoulder because she had a cup in each hand and wanted to stay in bed with him long enough to finish both.

The window inside is open. The bed is straight. Yuka's socks are lying beside one another on the back of the chair, where she can reach them with her foot if she's cold and doesn't want to get up. He can still see the little dip in his pillow where she set the notebook.

He takes a shirt out of the drawer. He does up the button at the throat and folds the sleeves across it. He's doing it the way he does when he wants to be able to reach into inventory for a change of clothes in the dark. He has others in there already. He got dressed out of it the morning he left for the gate. He could have done the same tonight.

He takes out another shirt. That gives him room to pick up the trousers underneath without dragging them against the runners. The drawer is very shallow once the clothes are out. There's a sock at the back from some day when he put everything away too fast. He takes it too.

She only uses the top drawer for a few things. There would be space here for the rest of them. She wouldn't have to stand at the end of the bed deciding how much to take to Ori's room. She's already been there long enough for Ori to start keeping the little lamp on all night. He knows because he crossed the yard twice in the dark and saw it burning both times.

The stack goes into inventory all at once. He puts the shirt he's going to wear on top of the dresser and opens the drawer again. There's nothing in it now.

When Yuka said *stay*, she held on to the edge of the table. He'd looked at that hand to find out what she was asking for. It would stretch as far as the bowl between them. He knows how far it would stretch because he watched it go there. The table will be there tomorrow, too. He can keep eating beside her if she asks. There are lots of ways to stay with somebody without obliging them to share a room with you.

He pulls the drawer the rest of the way out to get at the paper underneath. One corner has folded over. He runs his thumb along the crease.

*You closed the door so I'd stop bothering you.*

Until then he'd been thinking about what she said to him. With her voice the way it went at the end. Now he can hear the question inside it. She'd thought he was leaving her all morning. Every hour he'd spent in the gate with Makima had told her the same thing. And when he came back, the person Yuka saw on the other side of the yard was a man who could go away without telling her and come back holding somebody else up.

He can't put his thumb anywhere useful anymore. He takes it off the paper. The next thing she said was *go with her*. Then *she has to*. She'd found the part of what he did that nobody ever got to say out loud. There was the woman who followed him because she had to, and there was the woman who could go. He had put himself in the yard right between them and held absolutely still while one of them left.

He pushes the drawer in too hard. It goes shut with a sound that bites back through his hand.

“Did you catch your finger?” ʸ

He turns. Yuka is standing in the doorway with a pair of clean socks in her hand. He looks down at that hand for too long. She brings it up a little so he can see what she's brought. Ori thinks there was a stone in one of her slippers. They went looking for socks instead.

“No,” he manages. “I got the drawer.” ᴹ

She looks at the dresser. He puts the shirt under his arm. He's still standing across from the place where she'd set the notebook, which makes him want to talk a little faster than he can think.

“You can put your things down there,” he says. “I cleared it out.” ᴹ

Yuka puts her hand on the open door. She could close it from where she's standing. For a second he needs to know which way she's going to move.

“Why?” she says. ʸ

“So you can come back up. You don't have to stay with Ori on account of me.” ᴹ

“Where are you going?” ʸ

“The clinic tonight. After that, wherever you'd rather I went.” ᴹ

He means to do something with the shirt when he's finished saying it. Put it over his shoulder so he can pass her with both hands at his sides. He looks down and finds that it has slipped and he's holding it against the dresser by one of the buttons. If he lets it go, there'll be a shirt on the floor.

Yuka comes in as far as the chair. Her socks are right there, the ones she'd left days ago. She touches the toe of one without picking it up.

“I thought if you wanted me here I'd know it,” he says. “I keep waiting to know.” ᴹ

The shirt drops. He bends to get it and she gets there first. She takes it by the two shoulders and shakes it out. The button clicks against the dresser. She sets it loose over the back of the chair where he can pick it up.

“I do want you here,” she says. ʸ

He hears her. His hand stays flat against the wood. He wants to lean into her badly enough to have to look at where his feet are.

She starts to put a hand out toward him. Halfway there she closes it. The band's there. He looks right at it and still sees the finger she worked it off. It's the little turn she gave it when it stuck on her knuckle that keeps coming back. He could ask her about it right now. By the time her arm comes back down he still hasn't found a way to start.

“Why didn't you come that morning?” he asks. ᴹ

Her eyes come up to his. She has a sentence ready. He can see it in her face before she tries again.

“I put my boots on,” she says. “Cross said I could stay. I was angry, and I let him tell me you'd want me to. Then I took them off. It was me who took them off.” ʸ

He looks down at her socks. There were still wet footprints in the dirt by the foot of the quarry path when he came up behind Gojo. He had picked them out from where he was standing and looked up to see whether any of them had stopped on the patch he'd saved. He could tell who carried weight on the outside of a boot. He could tell that nobody had crossed between those two rocks.

“I kept turning to the wrong side,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka's lips part.

“Even after I saw you weren't there. When there was time to look. It took me a while to stop.” ᴹ

“How many times?” she asks. ʸ

He could tell her exactly what he was doing every time there was enough room in the fight to turn his head. He was there for each one. His grip had slipped on the dagger at the end, and even then he'd known why he wasn't going to throw his weight onto that side. From there he could see the whole of the ledge.

“I don't know,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka closes her eyes, as if she's tried to see it and can't get past him looking up. He wishes for a moment he'd left it to another night before he said a word. Then she sits down so hard on the edge of the chair that the clean socks slide off her knee. She picks them up. He has to give her a minute to be the person who took her boots off before she can say anything else.

He picks up his shirt. The fabric is warm at the shoulders where she held it.

“Can I still go back to Shoko's?” he asks. ᴹ

“Yes.” Yuka draws her feet in so he can pass the chair without having to turn sideways. “Your things can stay here. If you want. I can leave the drawer open.” ʸ

He puts two shirts back. After a moment he opens inventory again and fishes out the one sock from the back. He drops it next to the shirts. He leaves the drawer a little out himself.

“Could you shut the window when you come back for your socks?” he asks. ᴹ

“I can do it now.” ʸ

He waits while she does. Its catch has gone stiff in the cold. She presses it with the flat of her hand. When it sits down, the whole room goes quiet enough that he can hear Ori ask on the other side of the hall where the socks are.

Yuka gets up with the clean pair in her hand. “Goodnight, Mau.” ʸ

He says it back. She lets him go down first.

* * *

The lamp in the clinic is where Shoko left it. He changes out of the shirt he wore to dinner and puts the one from upstairs on. The button won't sit right when he does it up. He leaves it undone.

He sits on the cot and draws the blanket across his lap. Upstairs he put something back. He can still feel the drawer runner coming level with the tips of his fingers. Earlier in the week he closed everything and went down the steps with the entire house at his back, before anybody could see what he was doing. He can see, now, that there's more than one person he left up there.

*Ciel?* he says. ᴹ

She comes as soon as he says her name. She would have done it any night this week.

*I need a minute with all of you. Could you ask them to come sit with me?* ᴹ

She asks. He lies down. When he closes his eyes he keeps his hand loose at his side, the way Noise leaves the chair beside him when there's room at the table.
