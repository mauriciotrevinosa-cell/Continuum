# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 9 — Only Technical

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S13E123
**Continuity state:** B+437 night ("50"). The clinic bed again, by his own request. Mau goes into the inner room and sits at the table with Ciel, Noise and DIO; Makima comes and stands by the wall. He apologises properly, all of it. Noise: "You didn't close us when we asked." Mau: no, but he asked them for technical reports only, as if they were instruments; that was wrong, his biggest mistake of the week, and he'll make it up to them. Ciel says what it was like to report his heart rate to Rimuru while he wouldn't speak to her. DIO, who withdrew inside, is still hurt and says he owes her something. Then Mau reopens Mai's personal line and, before he sleeps, writes her a letter: it wasn't that he thought she wouldn't respect his space; once he began to see the contracts and the lines that join them, he could feel every one of them pulling, and he couldn't think, and he had to put everything down. He leaves the letter for Shoko to take to the workshop in the morning.

*When: B+437, night. Shoko left the lamp on low and her own door open a hand's width.*

The inner room is quiet when he comes in.

It's always been a strange place to arrive. There's no door, exactly. There's no coming in. One moment he's lying on the clinic cot in the dark with the blanket over him, and the next he's standing at the edge of the room with the table in front of him, and the chairs, and the cup, the way you're suddenly in a dream without remembering how you got there.

The room hasn't changed. It never really changes. The table in the middle. The chairs round it, more than there used to be, because people keep arriving. The cup, which is always there and always warm and which nobody has ever been seen to fill. The corridor at the back that leads to the little Inn where DIO has her room, and her plant, and the door to Ashborn's space that stays closed.

They're all there.

Ciel is at the table. She has her body, the one DIO gave her, and she's sitting very upright in her chair with her hands folded in front of her, and when he comes in she looks up and doesn't say anything.

Noise is by the wall. Where he always is. The outline of someone standing guard.

DIO is in the corridor mouth, leaning against the frame with her arms crossed. She has her back half turned to the room. She's looking at her plant through the open door of her room, as if it's very interesting.

Makima is by the other wall, the far one. She came in a moment before him. She doesn't sit. She stands with her hands behind her back like a guest at a gathering she wasn't sure she'd been invited to.

Nobody speaks.

He sits down at the table.

* * *

He's had a whole day and a half of sleep to think about what to say, and he has nothing.

He looks at his hands on the table. In here they aren't bandaged. In here they're just his hands.

“I'm sorry,” he says. ᴹ

It sounds very small in the quiet room.

Nobody answers. Not unkindly. They're waiting for the rest.

So he gives them the rest.

“Six days ago,” he says, “before dawn, I closed everything. The relays. The contracts. Yuka's techniques. Mai's supply. I closed them all, and I didn't tell anyone first, and I didn't leave a note. And when you asked me—” ᴹ

He looks at Ciel. Then at Noise.

“When you asked me not to shut you out too, I said yes. I said I wouldn't. And you're the ones who stayed. You stayed open the whole time. And I let you think that was enough.” ᴹ

* * *

“You didn't close us,” says Noise. ᴺ

It's the first thing he's said. His voice, the way it always is, low and even, coming from the wall.

“When we asked,” Noise says. “You didn't. You kept us.” ᴺ

“No,” says Mau. “I didn't close you. That's true.” ᴹ

He makes himself keep looking at the shape by the wall.

“But I asked you for reports,” he says. “Technical reports. That's what I said. Heart rate. Repair rate. Where the devils were. The quarry. I told Rimuru you'd keep the house informed, *technical only*. And I let you be that. Instruments. Something I'd use to tell other people I was all right.” ᴹ

His voice does something. He lets it.

“You're not instruments,” he says. “You've never been instruments. You're the first thing I ever made, and you turned into the best thing I ever made, and I asked you to read me my own pulse and pass it on and not talk to me.” ᴹ

He looks at Ciel.

“Both of you,” he says. “That was the worst thing I did all week. Worse than the doors. Worse than not leaving a note. I had you right here, the whole time, and I made you into a gauge.” ᴹ

Silence.

“That was my biggest mistake,” he says. “I know it was. And I'm going to make it up to you. I don't know how yet. But I will.” ᴹ

* * *

Ciel unfolds her hands.

She puts them flat on the table. She looks at them. For a long time she doesn't say anything.

“I read your heart rate to Rimuru,” she says. “Every twenty minutes. For six days. He wrote it down in his notebook, and he read it out to the porch, and they all looked at the numbers, and Yuka looked at the numbers.” ᶜ

Her voice is very level. Ciel's voice is always level. That's what makes it so hard to hear what's under it.

“And you wouldn't talk to me,” she says. “I was the one who could see it. All of it. What the numbers meant. Why they were going up. I could feel you sitting on the edge of the bed in the dark at three in the morning, and I had to tell Rimuru *heart rate elevated, repair keeping pace*, because that was what I was allowed to say.” ᶜ

She lifts her head and looks at him.

“I didn't mind the reports,” she says. “I would have sent them anyway. I minded that they were all I was allowed to be.” ᶜ

“I know,” he says. “I'm sorry.” ᴹ

“I know you are.” She looks back down at her hands. “I counted for you in the basin. I counted, and I didn't have to call it, and when you put everything into that Purple, I thought—” ᶜ

She stops.

Ciel doesn't stop. Ciel never stops in the middle of a sentence. He's never once heard her do it.

“I thought, *he's trusting Makima with three seconds*,” says Ciel, “*and he won't trust me with one sentence that isn't a number.*” ᶜ

By the far wall, Makima goes very still.

Mau puts his hands over his face.

* * *

Nobody says anything for a while.

Then he hears a chair scrape. He takes his hands away.

Noise has come away from the wall.

He doesn't do that. Mau has seen him do it maybe three times in all the time they've had this room. He's a shape that stands by walls. He stands by walls the way the wall stands; it's what he's for. But he's come away from it now, the whole outline of him, and he's standing at the end of the table, and he pulls out the chair there, slowly, as if he's not sure how chairs work, and sits down in it.

Ciel looks at him in astonishment.

Noise doesn't look at anyone. He puts his shadow-hands on the table, the way Mau's are.

“Home,” he says. ᴺ

Mau stares at him.

“You came back,” says Noise. “The basin. You came back. You said you'd stop if Ciel called it, and you meant it, and she didn't have to call it.” ᴺ

He turns the outline of his head toward Mau.

“You're sorry,” says Noise. “Good. Make it up. But you came back. That's the thing. That's always the thing.” ᴺ

Then he does something Mau has never seen him do in all the time this room has existed.

He reaches over and picks up the cup.

The cup that's always there and always warm and that nobody has ever been seen to fill. He picks it up in his shadow-hands, and he holds it out across the table to Mau.

Mau takes it.

It's warm. It's always warm. He's never known what's in it.

He drinks.

It tastes like nothing he can name. It tastes like the kitchen at supper, with the lamps lit and everyone talking at once.

* * *

He puts the cup down. He looks at the corridor mouth.

DIO hasn't turned round.

She's still leaning against the doorframe with her arms crossed, looking at her plant. She's been looking at it this whole time with tremendous concentration. It's a small plant. It isn't doing anything.

“DIO,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm busy,” says DIO. ᴰ

“With the plant.” ᴹ

“It needs watching.” ᴰ

“It's a plant.” ᴹ

“Plants need watching,” says DIO, with great dignity, “if one wants them to understand that one is still there.” ᴰ

He gets up. He goes over to the corridor mouth, and stands beside her, and looks at the plant too.

It's grown. He can see that. A new leaf since the last time he looked. Small, pale, uncurling.

“I'm sorry,” he says. “You went inside because I closed things. You pulled back so I'd have the range I needed. You didn't ask to fight. You didn't ask for anything. And I didn't even say thank you.” ᴹ

DIO's jaw is set.

“I went to my room,” she says. “That's all. I have a room. I went to it.” ᴰ

“For six days.” ᴹ

“It's a nice room.” ᴰ

He waits.

DIO uncrosses her arms. She crosses them again.

“You didn't knock,” she says at last. Very low. Not looking at him. “Once. In six days. You were right there. I could hear you in the dark. I could hear you not sleeping. And you didn't knock once.” ᴰ

He closes his eyes.

“No,” he says. “I didn't.” ᴹ

“I would have opened it,” DIO says. ᴰ

“I know.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a long moment.

Then she turns her head, finally, and looks at him. Her face, the one she made for herself, is very proud and very hurt and doing its best to be only the first one.

“You owe me,” she says. ᴰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Something,” DIO says. “I haven't decided what. Something large.” ᴰ

“Okay.” ᴹ

“Don't say *okay* like that.” She frowns at him. “Like you'll just give it. I want to have to argue for it.” ᴰ

He almost laughs. It gets most of the way this time.

“Then argue,” he says. “Whenever you've decided. I'll argue back. Properly.” ᴹ

DIO considers this.

“Acceptable,” she says. ᴰ

And she looks back at her plant, with her arms still crossed. But she leans, very slightly, until her shoulder is against his.

* * *

Makima hasn't moved from the far wall.

He looks at her across the room. She looks back.

She doesn't need an apology from him. Not for this. She was the one who walked beside him; she was the one he trusted with the three seconds. He can feel her knowing that, and feel her knowing what it cost Ciel to watch.

She inclines her head toward Ciel, very slightly.

*Her*, she says. Inside the inside. *Not me. I had it easy this week.* ᴹᵏ

He goes back to the table.

He sits down next to Ciel. Not across from her, the way he sat before. Next to her. Close enough that their shoulders touch.

“No more *technical only*,” he says. ᴹ

Ciel doesn't look at him.

“You'll forget,” she says. “The next time it's bad. You'll close the door and ask for numbers.” ᶜ

“Maybe,” he says. “Then tell me. Don't send it to Rimuru. Tell *me*. Say *you're doing it again*. Out loud. In here. As loud as you want.” ᴹ

Ciel turns her head and looks at him.

“As loud as I want,” she says. ᶜ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Something happens at the corner of her mouth.

“You'll regret that,” says Ciel. ᶜ

“Probably.” ᴹ

She looks at him a moment longer. Then she puts her head down on his shoulder. Just for a second. Then she lifts it again, and folds her hands back on the table, and is Ciel.

That's enough. That's more than enough.

* * *

He comes out of the room the way he went in, without a door.

He's lying on the clinic cot in the dark. The lamp is low. The house is asleep. Shoko's door is open a hand's width.

There's one more door.

He finds it the way he found them all, six days ago, in the dark before dawn. The line. Thin and steady, running out of him across the yard toward Mai. He closed this one. He reopened the supply the next day, when she came and stood in front of him with her arms folded and asked him why. But not the line. Not her. He left that closed.

He opens it.

Gently. The way you'd open a door on someone sleeping. He doesn't push anything through it. He doesn't wake her. He just lets it be open, the way it was before, so that if she reaches for it in her sleep, it's there.

Then he gets up and finds paper in Shoko's drawer, and a pencil, and sits down at the clinic table in the low lamplight, and writes.

* * *

*Mai,*

*I'm sorry I closed the line without asking you. I'm sorry I didn't tell you why when you came and asked about the supply. I'm writing it now because I don't think I can say it in the workshop without making a mess of it, and you deserve it said properly.*

*It wasn't you. I want you to know that first. I didn't close it because I thought you'd push, or wouldn't respect it if I asked for space. You always have. You're the person in this house who's best at leaving a thing alone when it needs to be left alone.*

*It was the eyes. Since they changed, I can see the contracts. All of them. Yours, Yuka's, Makima's, the soldiers', the anchors. Not as words. As lines. Running out of me to everyone, thin and bright, and I can feel each one pulling a little, the way you'd feel a thread pulling on a sleeve. Usually it's fine. Usually it's good. It's how I know where everyone is.*

*But last week I couldn't stop feeling them. Every single one. All at once. All pulling. And I couldn't think. I couldn't hear myself over it. So I put them all down. Every one, all at the same time, the way you'd drop everything you were carrying because your arms had given out. I didn't choose yours. I didn't choose anyone's. I just put everything down.*

*I've opened yours again tonight. You don't have to do anything with it. It's just there, the way it was.*

*Thank you for coming to ask about the supply. Thank you for being angry about it out loud instead of quietly. I needed somebody to be.*

*I'll come to the workshop when I can. If you want me to.*

*Mau*

* * *

He folds it twice. He writes *Mai* on the outside.

He leaves it on the clinic table, under the edge of the lamp, where Shoko will see it in the morning.

Then he goes back to the cot and lies down.

He doesn't fall asleep straight away. He lies there in the dark with his eyes open, feeling the lines. All of them, open now, or almost all. The soldiers. The anchors. Mai's, thin and quiet. Makima's, close. The ones inside, Ciel and Noise and DIO, warm as the cup.

And one that's still closed.

He doesn't touch it. He lies there and feels where it is, the way you feel where a door is in a dark house that you know very well. Across the yard. Up the stairs. In Ori's room. A line he closed six days ago before dawn, without a note, and hasn't opened.

Not tonight.

That one isn't his to open first.

*You're doing it again*, says Ciel. Quietly. Inside. ᶜ

He almost laughs.

*No*, he says. *I'm not. I'm waiting. It's different.* ᴹ

A pause.

*Yes*, says Ciel. *It is.* ᶜ

He closes his eyes.
