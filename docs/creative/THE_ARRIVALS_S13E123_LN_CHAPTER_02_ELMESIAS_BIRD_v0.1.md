# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 10 — Elmesia's Bird

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E123
**Continuity state:** B+438 ("49"), morning to early afternoon. Breakfast in the kitchen; Yuka beside him; she steals the pickles again, and it's still careful. Elmesia's bird, sleek and grey and unimpressed by everyone, lands on the table with a letter for Mau: she would like to talk to him, alone, about something that isn't a crisis. DIO, inside, notices the letter doesn't mention her. This time Mau tells Yuka where he's going and when he'll be back, out loud, before he goes. "Come back." "I will." He crosses by Exchange to the soldier anchor Elmesia has carried since V75. In her study, Elmesia asks what he thinks about DIO, and how she can know what DIO feels: whether it's really her DIO wants to be near, or only gratitude for being treated with respect. Mau tells her what he knows: DIO never learned the difference between kinds of love; respect can look like romance to her; she'll want to argue for things she wants. In answering, he hears his own week. He asks when to bring DIO. Elmesia: in a couple of hours, after supper.

*When: B+438. Breakfast. "49" in the notebook. The notebook is on the kitchen table, beside Yuka's bowl, where anyone can see it.*

She steals the pickles again at breakfast.

It's still careful. Everything is still careful. She sits down beside him without asking, the way she did last night, in the hoodie, and he sets them aside without deciding to, and after a while her hand comes out of the sleeve and takes one. Then another. She makes the face.

He still doesn't look at her while she does it. He isn't ready for what his face would do. But he moves his bowl an inch closer to her side, so she doesn't have to reach so far.

She notices. He feels her notice.

Neither of them says anything about it.

Across the table, Ori writes something very small in her notebook and doesn't look up.

* * *

The bird comes in at the end of breakfast.

He knows it before it lands. Elmesia's bird. Not the scruffy little thing with the ragged wing feather that crashed into the butter a few weeks ago with a message from the north. This one is sleek and grey and unimpressed by everyone. It comes in through the open kitchen window in one smooth line, lands on the table between the bread and the teapot without disturbing either, folds its wings, and looks round at all of them with an expression of tremendous disdain.

Then it looks at Mau, and holds out its leg.

“It knows you,” says Umaru, delighted. ᵁ

“It knows the soldier,” says Mau. ᴹ

He unties the letter. The bird watches him do it as if it's checking his technique.

Elmesia's hand. Elegant and quick and full of loops, the way she talks.

*Mau,*

*I heard that you went north and came back very quickly, and then slept for a day and a half, which is exactly what you should have done. I am pleased. I hope you are well enough today for a visit. Not a crisis. Not a war. Nothing is on fire. I would simply like to talk to you about something, alone, if you can spare the morning. The anchor you left with me is still in my shadow, and still very polite. Bring nothing. I'll feed you.*

*E.*

*P.S. The bird would like a piece of bread. He won't ask. He considers asking beneath him.*

Mau tears off a piece of bread and puts it on the table. The bird looks at it for a long moment, as if considering whether it's beneath him. Then it eats it.

* * *

Inside, DIO has gone quiet.

He feels it. Not a word. Just the particular shape of DIO noticing something and deciding not to say anything about it, which is louder than most people shouting.

*She asked for you*, says DIO at last. Very offhand. ᴰ

*She asked me to come and talk*, he says. ᴹ

*Alone.* ᴰ

*That's what it says.* ᴹ

A pause.

*She didn't say anything about me*, says DIO. ᴰ

*No.* ᴹ

*Not even hello.* ᴰ

*No.* ᴹ

*Well*, says DIO. *I expect she's very busy. Being a queen. With a bird.* ᴰ

And she withdraws, not far, just to her room in the little Inn at the back, and he feels her door close. Not slammed. Just closed, very precisely, the way you close a door when you want someone to notice that you closed it.

*She's going to sulk*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*You could tell her it's probably about her*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*I don't know that it is.* ᴹ

Ciel gives him the look she gives him when he's being deliberately slow, which is an expression she has perfected in the last two days now that she's allowed to make it out loud.

*Mau*, she says. *What else would Elmesia want to talk to you about, alone, that isn't a crisis?* ᶜ

He doesn't have an answer to that.

* * *

He doesn't go straight away.

That's the thing he notices about himself, standing in the kitchen with the letter in his hand. A week ago he would have gone. He'd have put the letter in his pocket and stood up and been halfway to the Exchange before anyone had finished their tea, and if anyone asked later he'd have said *I left a note*, and he wouldn't have.

He doesn't go.

He turns round.

She's still at the table. She's watching him over the rim of her cup, in the hoodie, very carefully not looking like she's watching him.

He goes back to the table. He doesn't sit. He stands by his chair, beside hers, and holds out the letter so she can see it.

“Elmesia,” he says. “She wants me to come and talk. About something that isn't a crisis. I think it's about DIO.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at the letter. Then at him.

He makes himself keep going.

“I'm going by the anchor,” he says. “To her palace. I'll be there this morning. Then I'll come back here. Then I think I'm going back again tonight, after supper, with DIO. I don't know how late.” ᴹ

He stops. He doesn't know what else there is.

“I wanted you to know,” he says. “Where I'll be. Before I go.” ᴹ

Yuka puts her cup down very slowly.

He watches her face. He watches her understand what he's doing. He watches her understand that he's never once done it before, not like this, not out loud, at the table, before. He watches something go through her eyes that makes them go bright, and watches her decide not to let it out, not here, not yet.

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

The word. That word.

But it isn't the same. It isn't the *okay* from the yard, flat and closed. It's quiet and it shakes a little and it's the other kind. The kind that means *I heard you*.

And then she says something else. Something he didn't expect.

“Come back,” she says. ʸ

He hasn't heard her say that since the morning he went to find a gate and it turned red. *Come back for dinner.* Before the Trial, before Cross, before any of it. In another season.

“I will,” he says. ᴹ

Her mouth does the small fragile thing again. The thing that might be a smile someday.

Then she picks up her cup and drinks her tea and looks out of the window as if nothing has happened, and he goes, before his face can do anything.

* * *

The Exchange is easy now.

It wasn't always. It used to cost him. Now it's a step, and a turn, and the world folding round him like a coat, and then the smell is different and the light is different and he's standing somewhere else, exactly where a soldier was standing a moment ago, while the soldier stands in the kitchen at home where he was.

He's in a study.

Tall windows. A great deal of light. Bookshelves to the ceiling, and a desk covered in papers and maps and something that might be the plan for a bridge. A tray of tea already on a side table, steaming, two cups. A long low couch under the window, piled with cushions.

And Elmesia, behind the desk, with her elbows on a pile of very important-looking documents and her chin in her hands, beaming at him.

“There you are,” she says. ᴱˡ

She comes round the desk. She looks at him properly: the bandage marks on his hands that haven't quite faded, the way he's standing, his face. Her own face does something kind and quick.

“You look better than I expected,” she says. “And worse than I'd like. Sit. Eat. I had them make the small cakes you liked last time.” ᴱˡ

He sits on the couch. She pours tea.

She doesn't sit behind the desk. She sits on the other end of the couch, with her legs tucked under her, holding her cup in both hands. Not a queen. Just someone who wants to talk.

“I'll get straight to it,” she says, “because if I don't I'll talk about the bridge for an hour, and you didn't come here to hear about the bridge.” ᴱˡ

“I don't mind the bridge.” ᴹ

“Nobody minds the bridge. That's the problem with the bridge.” She takes a breath. “It's about DIO.” ᴱˡ

*Told you*, says Ciel. ᶜ

* * *

Elmesia isn't quick about it, for once.

She turns her cup round and round in her hands. She looks out of the window. She starts twice and stops twice. Mau has never seen her do that. Elmesia is the fastest talker he knows; she thinks with her mouth.

“She writes to me,” Elmesia says at last. “Did you know that? Since the island. Every week. Sometimes twice. About her plant, mostly. Whether it's getting enough light. Whether she should move it. She asks me what my gardeners do.” ᴱˡ

He didn't know.

“And I write back,” says Elmesia. “I tell her what the gardeners do. And I tell her about my week. And she tells me about hers. And she asks questions about the most ridiculous things, *why do people in your city carry umbrellas when it isn't raining*, and I answer them. And I find that I look for the bird on the days when it's due. I look at the window.” ᴱˡ

She stops.

“I'm not asking you about me,” she says. “I know what I feel. I've known for a while. I'm old enough to recognise it when it comes in through the window.” ᴱˡ

She looks at him.

“I'm asking about her,” she says. “You know her better than anyone. She lives in you. And I don't know how to tell what she feels. Whether it's me she wants to write to, or whether it's only that I was kind to her, and she's never had much kindness, and she doesn't know the difference.” ᴱˡ

Her hands are very still on the cup.

“I don't want to be someone she's grateful to,” says Elmesia. “If that's all it is, I'll be that. Gladly. But I need to know which it is, before I say anything. Because if I'm wrong, I think it would frighten her very badly. And I don't want to frighten her.” ᴱˡ

* * *

He thinks about it for a long time.

He thinks about DIO in the corridor mouth last night, looking at her plant with tremendous concentration. *Plants need watching, if one wants them to understand that one is still there.*

“She doesn't know the difference,” he says. “You're right about that. She never learned it.” ᴹ

Elmesia waits.

“A long time ago,” he says, “before she was what she is now, she was made into something that only knew one kind of love. The kind you'd die for. The kind you'd break the world for. And she thought that was all it was. That if you cared about someone, that was what it meant. So when she made Ori, the first thing she wanted was for Ori to love her like that.” ᴹ

He watches Elmesia take that in.

“Ori decided for herself,” he says. “How she wanted to love people. That was Ori's whole road. And DIO had to watch it, and learn from it, and she's still learning. She still mixes them up. When someone treats her with respect, when someone's kind to her and doesn't want anything back, she can mistake it. She thinks it must be the big kind. Because that's the only kind she was given words for.” ᴹ

Elmesia's face has gone very still.

“So I'm right to be afraid,” she says. ᴱˡ

“No,” says Mau. ᴹ

She looks at him.

“You asked how you'd know,” he says. “I think I know how you'd know.” ᴹ

* * *

He takes a moment. He wants to get it right.

“When she's grateful,” he says, “she's very polite. She thanks people. She does what they ask. She doesn't want to be any trouble. She's like that with Rimuru, and with Frieren. She's like that with most of the house. She's careful, because she's afraid of being sent away.” ᴹ

He thinks about last night again. *You owe me. Something large.* *Don't say okay like that. Like you'll just give it. I want to have to argue for it.*

“When it's real,” he says, “she argues. She sulks. She says *you owe me*. She wants to have to fight for it, because if she has to fight for it, it means she's allowed to want it. She doesn't sulk at people she's only grateful to. She sulks at people she's sure won't leave.” ᴹ

He looks at Elmesia.

“She's sulking right now,” he says. “In her room. Because your letter didn't say hello to her.” ᴹ

Elmesia puts her cup down.

She puts it down very carefully, on the table, without looking at it, and it rattles a little in the saucer, and she doesn't seem to notice.

“She's *sulking*,” she says. ᴱˡ

“She closed her door,” says Mau. “Very precisely. So I'd notice.” ᴹ

Elmesia puts her hand over her mouth.

She doesn't say anything for a long moment. Then she laughs, behind her hand, a small helpless laugh that has something wet at the edges of it, and shakes her head.

“Oh,” she says. “Oh, that's—oh, *no*. I didn't put her name on it because I didn't want her to read it. I didn't want her to know I was asking about her. And she thinks I forgot her.” ᴱˡ

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

“I'm an idiot,” says Elmesia. ᴱˡ

“A bit,” says Mau. ᴹ

She laughs again, properly this time.

* * *

It's only afterward, eating one of the small cakes while Elmesia talks happily and at enormous speed about what DIO might like to see in the palace gardens, that he hears what he said.

*She doesn't sulk at people she's only grateful to. She sulks at people she's sure won't leave.*

*She wants to have to fight for it, because if she has to fight for it, it means she's allowed to want it.*

He puts the cake down.

He thinks about a woman in a too-big hoodie, sitting in a doorway all afternoon with her back against the frame. Not coming in. Not going away. Moving an inch closer every hour.

He thinks about *stay*.

He thinks about how, in the yard, she went for the worst thing she could find. Not the cruelest. The worst. The thing that would make him *hear*. And how she only ever does that, he realises, with the people she's sure of. She's never said anything like it to Cross. Never to Mai. Never to anyone she's afraid of losing.

Only to him. Twice.

Because she was sure he'd still be there to hear it.

He sits on Elmesia's couch with the half-eaten cake in his hand and doesn't move.

“Mau?” says Elmesia. ᴱˡ

He looks up.

“Sorry,” he says. “I just—understood something.” ᴹ

Elmesia looks at him. She's very quick, Elmesia. She's quicker than almost anyone he knows.

“About her?” she says. “Or about you?” ᴱˡ

He doesn't answer.

Elmesia doesn't press. She just pushes the plate of cakes an inch closer to him, the way he moved his bowl closer to Yuka at breakfast.

* * *

When he gets up to go, he asks.

“When should I bring her?” ᴹ

Elmesia looks out at the sun, judging it.

“A couple of hours,” she says. “After supper. Mine is early; I'm a queen, I get to have it whenever I like. Come when you've had yours. I'll have the garden lit.” ᴱˡ

She stops.

“Will she come?” she says, and for a moment she doesn't sound like a queen at all. “If she's sulking?” ᴱˡ

Mau thinks about DIO's door, closed very precisely.

“Leave that to me,” he says. ᴹ
