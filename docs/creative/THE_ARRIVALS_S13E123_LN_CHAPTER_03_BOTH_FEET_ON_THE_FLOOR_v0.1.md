# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 11 — Both Feet on the Floor

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E123
**Continuity state:** B+438 ("49"). Mau wakes at the clinic after speaking to Ciel, Noise, DIO and Makima. Mau calls for Ciel twice. Her brief hesitation takes him back to being hurt at the Trial bridge. Noise finds a familiar sound, then shows him the wedding, which brings back the ring and the yard. Mau asks Makima to stay while he calls for Shoko. He calls Shoko Mom when she arrives. Shoko, Gojo and Rem offer practical company as Mau tries to go about his day. Yuka's direct line remains closed. Late that evening Makima gets up to let Mau rest. He asks her to stay, draws her onto his lap and lets her hold his head against her chest. He falls asleep. She settles him on the cot and returns inside before anyone sees them.

*When: B+438. A wet morning, before the road dries.*

The thing that wakes him is two people laughing outside.

The clinic window is open a little. He can hear somebody on the stones by the pump telling somebody else to turn the handle again. It's the voice of a woman who takes the bread out some mornings when Shuna has her elbows in dough. She's got water in her shoe. Both of them laugh again when she takes it off and pours it out.

Mau lies where he is. He used to like waking up to that. He used to be able to tell which way people were going, whether it was worth getting up to steal the warm bit of whatever they'd left on the board. Sometimes Yuka got as far as the door before him and caught him by the waist as he went through.

*I'll stop bothering you.*

He opens his eyes.

The cold floor catches both feet at once. He has his shirt on from last night, his trousers on the back of the chair, and time to get dressed before anybody comes through that door. He picks up the trousers. He sets them down again. There was a pair of boots at the foot of their bed the evening he left. He could go upstairs and see whether she'd put them back there. He knows where to look.

His hand stays on the back of the chair. He can see the end of each finger against the wood and can't feel anything past the first joint. Somebody works the pump again. The knock it makes is the sound of somebody putting their heel down on a flat stone.

When he'd come out onto the quarry floor he'd looked for Yuka on the low shelf of rock. He'd thought she'd have a foot up where she could fix her laces. He could throw something up to her from where he stood and see if she caught it. He used to know the exact moment she'd raise her hand.

Gojo had left the whole place clear for her. Even his sweets were on the ground on the other side of him.

He puts his bare feet wider on the floor. His chest is moving much faster than he thought it was. He waits for it to slow down far enough for him to make up his mind about getting dressed. The chair starts to scrape. He lets it go, puts both hands on the cot, and sits.

*Ciel?* ᴹ

He'd told her last night to say it out loud next time. There are a few seconds in which he can hear the pump and the woman looking for a dry stocking, and Ciel waits to hear whether he has another request for numbers. He'd made it very plain at the Gate what he wanted from her. She'd been trying so hard to leave him room to ask for something different that he has to ask twice.

On the whole other side of those seconds there is stone under his shoulder and the end of a sound he can't place. He'd called for Ciel from that ground until he couldn't get his lungs to do it. He'd thought if he made it to the other side of the bridge she might be able to hear him there. The hand he'd tried to drag himself with kept slipping.

He looks down to see where it slipped this time. It's wrapped around the edge of Shoko's cot. He can feel the rail through his skin and the stone through the heel of the same hand. His feet are both on the floor. His stomach still drops like he's trying to stand up on nothing.

“Ciel!” ᴹ

*I'm here.* The words come fast enough to run into each other. *Mau, I can hear you. I'm right here this time.* ᶜ

This time. He tries to take the breath she heard him get away with. It stops short, and he opens his mouth for another one before the first is out.

*Give him the room,* says Noise. ᴺ

In the place where they can all see it, Noise puts a cup down on their table with a small, deliberate click. At the clinic Mau is still holding the cot. He tries to follow the smaller sound, and Noise makes it again for him. Outside, the pump has stopped. The latch hasn't moved.

*I was waiting for you to ask,* says Ciel. She's nearer than she was. *You did. I heard the first one too.* ᶜ

He wants to tell her he knows why she couldn't answer at the bridge. Right now he needs to hear her answer twice even though she just did.

In the Trial he'd had a morning with his back against cold stone and the heel of one boot planted where he could push himself up off it. He'd got the bleeding to stop again. He'd made himself think of a little room somewhere Yuka could bring Ori without having to tell either of them how long they'd get to stay. He'd been able to lay his dagger down, make a light, and imagine what the room would look like if he survived long enough to find it. Noise had listened until the light guttered. Then Noise had asked him why, in all his imaginings, he kept leaving Yuka out of the part where he asked her what she wanted.

He'd hated the question. He'd gone on hoping there would be somebody to ask.

He can still see the pale bit of rock where he looked next, in case she'd chosen a different place.

*She saw me standing there,* says Mau. *That night in the yard. I keep remembering that part.* ᴹ

The cup moves somewhere inside. Noise comes out of the side of his vision and sets it on the table.

*I saw,* says Noise. ᴺ

Mau tries to straighten his leg. It comes forward a little and stops. In the Trial he'd have been looking for his dagger by now. Here he could undo some distance with barely a thought, put his hand on the table upstairs and see what she'd left for him. He can't go as far as the water on Shoko's shelf.

Noise pulls the chair beside him out a little. He puts a hand on the back of it. Before their eyes the light changes around the table.

*Mau. Look.* ᴺ

It's Mai coming forward with the rings. There was a crease in the cloth under them. He remembers seeing it and knowing there was something he had to do with his hands, and hearing Yuka laugh because for once he was going to have to take his time. The band catches on her knuckle. She puts her finger on the back of his to help.

He can hear her voice. A moment earlier than the rings. *I promise to tell you when I'm angry. And when I'm afraid.*

For a second it's the only thing he can hear. Then her hand turns, the same ring catches the light off the dirt in the yard, and he hears her say *maybe I shouldn't have married you*. The next morning, she gets it over the same place where it caught at the wedding. He remembers lifting his hand while she did it, as if there might be something left for his hand to do.

“I heard her,” he says, aloud in the clinic. ᴹ

In the yard she'd said it that plainly too. *I know you do, Mau.* He can hear the space she left for Cross to answer.

The memory is still there. Noise has found something real for him, and Mau can no longer see how he is meant to hold it without holding the other one. On the day she put the ring on him he could turn to her in front of the whole house. In the yard there had been nearly as many faces, and they'd all been close enough to hear her.

“I thought if she told me when she was angry I'd hear what she actually wanted.” His breath goes halfway into that sentence and won't make it through the next one. He puts the heel of his hand up against his mouth. ᴹ

Noise's fingers close on the chair back.

*Mau?* ᴺ

The light goes out over the memory. Noise's hand comes away from the chair. He looks as if he doesn't know where else he can put it.

*Mau, listen.* Ciel comes nearer. *I heard her too. I know who was standing where. You don't have to show me again.* ᶜ

He hears every word. The room still tilts toward the window. He puts one foot flat and holds his knee down when it starts moving.

*Did she take it back before she went?* he asks. ᴹ

He's looked for that moment every time the yard comes back. He knows the answer well enough to hear it in the minute Ciel gives him before she speaks.

*She walked away,* she says. ᶜ

Mau's lips part. He hasn't heard anybody that close to him say it so plainly. Noise takes a step, still with his hand open.

“Mau,” says Makima, from the step. ᴹᵏ

He can see her through the gap. She pushes the door open with one hand. The other stays low where he can see it. She comes in as far as the cot and sits on the floor in front of him, so he doesn't have to lift his head to find her.

“You can give me your hand if you need to put it somewhere,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He puts it in hers. At first all he can feel is her hand under his wrist. He works his fingers into the gaps as soon as he can feel them. Inside, Noise hasn't moved away.

“I tried to look at something good,” he tells her. ᴹ

Makima leans just far enough to set the curtain back against the wall. A strip of light moves across his feet.

“I know,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He calls for Shoko. His voice breaks on it and he tries again, loud enough this time that somebody in the yard stops speaking. In the back of his head he's already counting how long it ought to take her. There are too many places he could be when she gets to the door.

*I have her,* says Ciel. She keeps speaking while she waits for the answer. *She's in the kitchen. She heard you. She's coming across now.* ᶜ

“Makima.” He has to look down to see if she's still holding on. “Stay when she comes in.” ᴹ

“I'll stay.” ᴹᵏ

Makima keeps her hand where he left it. Shoko's foot comes down on the step. Only then does he manage to look up at the door.

“Mom?” ᴹ

It's out before he knows who else heard it. He knows who he meant. He's never needed a word for what she is to him as badly as he needs her to come the last few feet now.

“I'm here, Mau,” says Shoko. She takes the chair a little closer to the cot and sits. Her knees are beside Makima's. Nobody asks him to get one hand back before he can put the other out. ˢʰ

* * *

Shoko has flour between the lines of her hands. She must have wiped them on a cloth as she came. She asks him how long he's been awake. He can't answer that yet. She asks if he'd be able to drink out of a cup if she put one beside him.

He moves his head once. She puts it down, and leaves the trousers where they are.

“I heard the pump,” he says eventually. ᴹ

“I can shut the window.” ˢʰ

“Leave it. I want to know if they found the shoe.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him for a second. Then she leans back to have a look outside, as if that really is the next thing they need to find out.

“It's on the wall,” she says. “She'll get it when it dries.” ˢʰ

He gets a hand round the cup.

* * *

Gojo brings lunch. Shoko has gone back for her share. There's soup and what looks like half a loaf of bread balanced across a bowl. He puts the tray down, puts two spoons in one of the bowls by mistake and sits like that was his plan.

“You can get a chair,” Mau tells him. ᴹ

“I got this one last night,” says Gojo. “It took a lot of work.” ᴳ

It's the chair Gojo pushed up with his foot when he came to see if the cot was going to be comfortable last night. He hasn't moved it since. Mau looks at his soup. The spoon has warmed where the handle lies across the bowl.

“What do you do,” he asks, “when you're here and she's there and you know what you said the last time and you've gone around it so many times you can't tell which bit she heard?” ᴹ

Gojo takes the bread off the bowl and breaks it across his knee. A piece falls between them and he picks it up before either of them can step on it.

“Sometimes I do something so stupid she hears that instead,” he says. ᴳ

Mau almost smiles.

“Sometimes I ask if she wants to have lunch,” Gojo says. “Give me your bowl if you're only going to get the spoon hot.” ᴳ

Mau picks it up. He eats half a spoonful and sits back before he takes another. Across the yard somebody on the porch shouts for Rem. Gojo gets as far as saying somebody can go get her. Then he sees Mau with his chin up, having turned at the first sound even with soup about to run down his wrist.

“Want me to close it a little more?” asks Gojo. ᴳ

Mau shakes his head.

He eats the soup. His shoulders come down after a while. By the time Gojo takes both bowls away the sound outside is tolerable enough that he can tell a laugh from a shout again.

* * *

Rem brings a folded sheet to the clinic before supper. It's the thing he circled when she asked him to listen to the two pieces. Somebody has tried out a lower part on it. The pencil's gone through a note at the bottom and she has a finger across it to keep the hole from catching when she unfolds it.

“It's yours till I'm done,” she says. “You said this line got lost once they all came in. Can you still hear it if we leave one voice out until the end?” ᴿᵉ

“I can come listen,” he says. ᴹ

He means it. He gets his trousers from the chair, puts them on and comes to the step with her. He gets as far as the two paces where the clinic wall stops catching the wind. The porch is on his left. He can hear the speed Yuka's speaking at, telling somebody how far something is, and his body is already turning in that direction before he remembers.

Rem stops beside him. She waits for him to tell her where to go.

“Can we do it out here?” he asks. ᴹ

She sits on the step and opens the sheet across both her knees. He sits lower than she does. She shows him where the hole is and starts from the line before it.

The low part sounds good on its own. A little further on there's a line that needs to come in high, and she asks him to try it so she can hear how it fits. He hasn't been able to get a whole breath for very long today. The first note scarcely carries as far as the folded paper. Rem comes in under it where she'd planned to, and he stays with her long enough to make the turn.

Inside the house somebody clears two places at the small table. Afterward Rem goes to see if there's still enough for them both. He stays on the step to hear where she stops on the way back. He gets the second note up by the time she reaches him again.

* * *

Rem leaves the music with him after supper. She'll need it tomorrow. He tells her he knows where to find her and sets it under his cup so the corner won't lift in the draught. By the time he takes his trousers off again the lamp has burned down far enough to leave soot on one side of the glass. He can see the room perfectly well. He gets up to turn the lamp down anyway, because when he lies down the light is always exactly where he has to look.

When he sits back on the cot, Makima is in Shoko's chair. She's been turning back the cuff on one sleeve. She straightens it once with the tips of her fingers and it goes right back.

“Did you get to sing it?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

“I got the second part.” ᴹ

“I heard you.” ᴹᵏ

He looks down at the music under the cup. He was sure none of it got past the end of the clinic step.

“Towards the end,” she says. “When Rem came back.” ᴹᵏ

He nods. He'd thought about asking her to listen. By the time he could sing it properly, he'd thought if he went back out one more time he might find the courage to go farther than the step.

“I should let you sleep,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

She gets up. He knows where she's going. He'll be able to find her when she gets inside, and he knows he could ask her any of the things he meant to ask this morning. He watches her take two steps and thinks he can keep looking at the side of the lamp until he forgets there's a yard on the other side of the wall.

His hand closes on hers before she gets past the cot.

“Wait,” he says. ᴹ

She stops. He pulls a little. She comes back within reach and waits for the rest.

“Can you sit with me?” he asks. He could still make room next to him and have it mean the chair, or the far side of the cot. He lets go of her hand long enough to tap his own knee. “Here. If you want to.” ᴹ

For a second he thinks he's going to have to say why. He knows exactly how it would look if somebody opened the door. He can still hear Yuka asking him if he'd rather have Makima. He tried to tell her in the yard and nothing came out. Now he has both hands open and can barely stand to look at them.

“I can stay here,” says Makima. She puts a hand by his hip to steady herself as she comes up onto the cot. “Is this where you want me?” ᴹᵏ

She sits facing him, a knee on each side of his. The mattress goes down a little and his feet settle harder against the floor. He has somewhere to put his hands now. He rests them on her back and stops with his arms halfway round her.

“I don't know if I should ask you for this,” he says. ᴹ

She studies his face a second. Then she takes her hand off the cot and puts it on the back of his head.

“Like this?” ᴹᵏ

He nods into her palm. She brings him forward until his cheek is against her chest. Her other arm goes round his shoulders. She rests her head against the top of his. He stops with his mouth shut and his eyes open, still keeping most of his weight on the tips of his feet.

“I can't keep it up tonight,” he tells her. ᴹ

“I can hold you.” ᴹᵏ

His heels come down.

* * *

The rest of him follows so suddenly Makima has to tighten both arms to keep him where he is.

She'd thought she'd known everything that could come across the bond at once. She had been in him when he woke and couldn't feel the room. She'd felt him recognize Shoko's voice and wait for the part of a breath that would let him answer. She hadn't known how much of himself he was still holding up even then.

She feels it go now. His arms close all the way round her waist. The breath he's been holding comes out against her shirt and takes the next one with it. He tries to get a word out round it. For a moment she thinks he needs her to answer something. Then she feels her shirt go wet against his cheek and he takes his face a little farther into it. He can't even do that without her feeling how badly he wanted not to cry where she could see him. The next breath makes a sound too. He lets it.

There was a time when she could spend a whole afternoon figuring out what she could take away from him so that the next time he wanted anything, he'd have to come to her. The first time he thanked her, she thought about it for days. She hadn't been able to make him say it again.

Now he has asked her for the thing she wanted him to ask for. She's never been closer to him. For a moment she's so glad he asked that she forgets where they are. Then he moves his hand on her back and she can feel how hard it is for him to stop trying to put himself back together. She wishes she'd never had a reason to hear him ask like that.

“Can you stay till I'm asleep?” he asks. He has to start it again after *stay*, as though he'd thought that might be enough to tell her. ᴹ

“Yes,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He makes a little sound against her when she moves to give him more room. She settles with her head still resting on his.

“I love you,” he says into her shirt. ᴹ

“I love you too,” says Makima. She hears the whole sentence only after it's out. ᴹᵏ

His fingers loosen against her back. A while later his breath comes on its own. She can move her thumb across the back of his head without his hands closing harder on her.

He's asleep.

She stays with his weight against her through the last of the voices in the kitchen. Somebody laughs, once, out by the pump. He sleeps through it. When the noise at the far end of the yard begins to come closer, she gets one arm along his back and takes her knee off the cot. It gives her room to lower him without waking him. She gets his feet up, pulls the blanket to his shoulder and puts the empty cup on the sill so there'll be room when Shoko brings him another.

She goes inside before anyone comes up to the clinic.

The table is where it always is. Ciel has left a cup at the place Makima uses. She puts her hand round it. A minute ago she knew where to put both her hands. Here she has to move the cup twice before she can set them both down.

Noise looks up. Makima looks back at him. There must be an answer he is waiting for. She can't think what the question could be.

She sits there until he moves the cup within reach again. She keeps looking at it. She's still glad Mau asked. She can't think of anything to say after that.
