# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 11 — Both Feet on the Floor

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E123
**Continuity state:** B+438 ("49"). Mau wakes at the clinic after speaking to Ciel, Noise, DIO and Makima. Noise helps him through the panic that follows an ordinary morning sound. Shoko, Gojo and Rem offer practical company as Mau tries to go about his day. Yuka's direct line remains closed.

*When: B+438. A wet morning, before the road dries.*

The thing that wakes him is two people laughing outside.

The clinic window is open a little. He can hear somebody on the stones by the pump telling somebody else to turn the handle again. It's the voice of a woman who takes the bread out some mornings when Shuna has her elbows in dough. She's got water in her shoe. Both of them laugh again when she takes it off and pours it out.

Mau lies where he is. He used to like waking up to that. He used to be able to tell which way people were going, whether it was worth getting up to steal the warm bit of whatever they'd left on the board. Sometimes Yuka got as far as the door before him and caught him by the waist as he went through.

*I'll stop bothering you.*

He opens his eyes.

The cold floor catches both feet at once. He has his shirt on from last night, his trousers on the back of the chair, and time to get dressed before anybody comes through that door. He picks up the trousers. He sets them down again. There was a pair of boots at the foot of their bed the evening he left. He could go upstairs and see whether she'd put them back there. He knows where to look.

His hand stays on the back of the chair. He can see the end of each finger against the wood and can't feel anything past the first joint. Somebody works the pump again. The knock it makes is the sound of somebody putting their heel down on a flat stone.

When he'd come out onto the quarry floor he'd looked for Yuka on the low shelf of rock. He'd thought she'd have a foot up where she could fix her laces. He could throw something up to her from where he stood and see if she caught it. He used to know the exact moment she'd raise her hand.

Gojo had left the whole place clear for her. Even his sweets were on the ground on the other side of him.

He puts his bare feet wider on the floor. His chest is moving much faster than he thought it was. He waits for it to slow down far enough for him to make up his mind about getting dressed. The chair starts to scrape. He lets it go, puts both hands on the cot, and sits.

*Noise.* ᴹ

*Here.* ᴺ

It takes him a moment to work out what he wanted from calling. He hears the pump stop. His head comes up before he can stop it. The latch doesn't move.

In the Trial he'd had a morning with his back against cold stone and the heel of one boot planted where he could push himself up off it. He'd got the bleeding to stop again. He'd made himself think of a little room somewhere Yuka could bring Ori without having to tell either of them how long they'd get to stay. He'd been able to lay his dagger down, make a light, and imagine what the room would look like if he survived long enough to find it. Noise had listened until the light guttered. Then Noise had asked him why, in all his imaginings, he kept leaving Yuka out of the part where he asked her what she wanted.

He'd hated the question. He'd gone on hoping there would be somebody to ask.

He can still see the pale bit of rock where he looked next, in case she'd chosen a different place.

*She saw me standing there,* says Mau. *That night in the yard. I keep remembering that part.* ᴹ

The cup moves somewhere inside. Noise comes out of the side of his vision and sets it on the table.

*I saw,* says Noise. ᴺ

Mau tries to straighten his leg. It comes forward a little and stops. In the Trial he'd have been looking for his dagger by now. Here he could undo some distance with barely a thought, put his hand on the table upstairs and see what she'd left for him. He can't go as far as the water on Shoko's shelf.

Noise pulls the chair beside him out a little. He puts a hand on the back of it. Before their eyes the light changes around the table.

*Mau. Look.* ᴺ

It's Mai coming forward with the rings. There was a crease in the cloth under them. He remembers seeing it and knowing there was something he had to do with his hands, and hearing Yuka laugh because for once he was going to have to take his time. The band catches on her knuckle. She puts her finger on the back of his to help.

He can hear her voice. A moment earlier than the rings. *I promise to tell you when I'm angry. And when I'm afraid.*

For a second it's the only thing he can hear. Then her hand turns, the same ring catches the light off the dirt in the yard, and she says *go with her*.

“I heard her,” he says, aloud in the clinic. ᴹ

The memory is still there. Noise has found something real for him, and Mau can no longer see how he is meant to hold it without holding the other one. On the day she put the ring on him he could turn to her in front of the whole house. In the yard there had been nearly as many faces, and they'd all been close enough to hear her.

“I thought if she told me when she was angry I'd hear what she actually wanted.” His breath goes halfway into that sentence and won't make it through the next one. He puts the heel of his hand up against his mouth. ᴹ

Noise's fingers close on the chair back.

*Mau?* ᴺ

The light goes out over the memory. Noise's hand comes away from the chair. He looks as if he doesn't know where else he can put it.

*Mau, listen.* Ciel comes nearer. *I heard her too. I know who was standing where. You don't have to show me again.* ᶜ

He hears every word. The room still tilts toward the window. He puts one foot flat and holds his knee down when it starts moving.

*Did she take it back before she went?* he asks. ᴹ

He's looked for that moment every time the yard comes back. He knows the answer well enough to hear it in the minute Ciel gives him before she speaks.

*She walked away,* she says. ᶜ

Mau's lips part. He hasn't heard anybody that close to him say it so plainly. Noise takes a step, still with his hand open.

“Mau,” says Makima, from the step. ᴹᵏ

He can see her through the gap. She pushes the door open with one hand. The other stays low where he can see it. She comes in as far as the cot and sits on the floor in front of him, so he doesn't have to lift his head to find her.

“You can give me your hand if you need to put it somewhere,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He puts it in hers. For a while he can only feel where his wrist rests on her fingers. She keeps her hand there while he gets the rest of it back. Inside, Noise hasn't moved away.

“I tried to look at something good,” he tells her. ᴹ

Makima leans just far enough to set the curtain back against the wall. A strip of light moves across his feet.

“I know,” she says. ᴹᵏ

The next breath comes far enough for him to call Shoko's name. It doesn't carry past the door. Makima turns her face toward the kitchen as if he's spoken to her in a perfectly ordinary voice.

*I'll ask her to come*, says Ciel. ᶜ

Makima stays to hold his hand. This time, before he has to decide whether to get up and prove he can, he hears Shoko on the actual step.

* * *

Shoko has flour between the lines of her hands. She must have wiped them on a cloth as she came. She sits where he can see her and asks him how long he's been awake. He can't answer that yet. She asks if he'd be able to drink out of a cup if she put one beside him.

He moves his head once. She puts it down, and leaves the trousers where they are.

“I heard the pump,” he says eventually. ᴹ

“I can shut the window.” ˢʰ

“Leave it. I want to know if they found the shoe.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him for a second. Then she leans back to have a look outside, as if that really is the next thing they need to find out.

“It's on the wall,” she says. “She'll get it when it dries.” ˢʰ

He gets a hand round the cup.

* * *

Gojo brings lunch. Shoko has gone back for her share. There's soup and what looks like half a loaf of bread balanced across a bowl. He puts the tray down, puts two spoons in one of the bowls by mistake and sits like that was his plan.

“You can get a chair,” Mau tells him. ᴹ

“I got this one last night,” says Gojo. “It took a lot of work.” ᴳ

It's the chair Gojo pushed up with his foot when he came to see if the cot was going to be comfortable last night. He hasn't moved it since. Mau looks at his soup. The spoon has warmed where the handle lies across the bowl.

“What do you do,” he asks, “when you're here and she's there and you know what you said the last time and you've gone around it so many times you can't tell which bit she heard?” ᴹ

Gojo takes the bread off the bowl and breaks it across his knee. A piece falls between them and he picks it up before either of them can step on it.

“Sometimes I do something so stupid she hears that instead,” he says. ᴳ

Mau almost smiles.

“Sometimes I ask if she wants to have lunch,” Gojo says. “Give me your bowl if you're only going to get the spoon hot.” ᴳ

Mau picks it up. He eats half a spoonful and sits back before he takes another. Across the yard somebody on the porch shouts for Rem. Gojo gets as far as saying somebody can go get her. Then he sees Mau with his chin up, having turned at the first sound even with soup about to run down his wrist.

“Want me to close it a little more?” asks Gojo. ᴳ

Mau shakes his head.

He eats the soup. His shoulders come down after a while. By the time Gojo takes both bowls away the sound outside is tolerable enough that he can tell a laugh from a shout again.

* * *

Rem brings a folded sheet to the clinic before supper. It's the thing he circled when she asked him to listen to the two pieces. Somebody has tried out a lower part on it. The pencil's gone through a note at the bottom and she has a finger across it to keep the hole from catching when she unfolds it.

“It's yours till I'm done,” she says. “You said this line got lost once they all came in. Can you still hear it if we leave one voice out until the end?” ᴿᵉ

“I can come listen,” he says. ᴹ

He means it. He gets his trousers from the chair, puts them on and comes to the step with her. He gets as far as the two paces where the clinic wall stops catching the wind. The porch is on his left. He can hear the speed Yuka's speaking at, telling somebody how far something is, and his body is already turning in that direction before he remembers.

Rem stops beside him. She waits for him to tell her where to go.

“Can we do it out here?” he asks. ᴹ

She sits on the step and opens the sheet across both her knees. He sits lower than she does. She shows him where the hole is and starts from the line before it.

The low part sounds good on its own. A little further on there's a line that needs to come in high, and she asks him to try it so she can hear how it fits. He hasn't been able to get a whole breath for very long today. The first note scarcely carries as far as the folded paper. Rem comes in under it where she'd planned to, and he stays with her long enough to make the turn.

Inside the house somebody clears two places at the small table. Afterward Rem goes to see if there's still enough for them both. He stays on the step to hear where she stops on the way back. He gets the second note up by the time she reaches him again.
