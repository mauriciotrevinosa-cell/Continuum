# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 12 — Out Where He Can Hear

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S13E123
**Continuity state:** B+439 ("48"), before Elmesia's letter. A broken washing line brings Yuka and Mau into the same part of the yard. Shuna, Mai and Frieren help with the work. Yuka sees how readily Mau expects her to want him gone. Maki asks Yuka what she was trying to accomplish by taking off the band in his sight. Later, Mau asks Makima to stay while he eats. Yuka brings him the bread he asks for. She and Mau make a small plan for the following morning. Mau still sleeps at the clinic; Yuka stays with Ori, and their direct line remains closed.

*When: B+439. The yard is still wet underneath yesterday's sun.*

One end of the washing line comes down before lunch. Yuka gets an armful of wet sheet against the face. The far end is still tied to the post, so she can hold most of it off the grass by standing very straight and trying to get all the rest of it up with her knee.

“Pull toward me,” says Shuna, from somewhere on the other side of it. ˢʰᵘ

“I'm inside it.” ʸ

“Stick a hand out.” ˢʰᵘ

She does. Shuna takes hold of it and walks her round the edge, feet first. That puts both of them where they can see the bottom of the sheet, heavy and dark where it's caught under the overturned stool. Behind them Ori is already going for clean cloths so they can put the laundry down.

Mau is on the clinic step. Yuka sees him because the sheet stops coming further down just before she has to shout. He's put his shoulder under a different part of it. The whole wet edge is against his neck, where it keeps dripping into his collar.

“Can you get it clear?” he asks Shuna. ᴹ

“Nearly. Yuka, up a little.” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka takes it higher. Mau is close enough to count the threads from where he is. He's looking at where Shuna is getting the corner past the stool. The sheet gets lighter all at once and goes into both their hands.

For a second she's standing there with him the way she's stood with him in their room a hundred times, folding one end toward the other. She takes a step in. He takes the same one. Then she sees his head turn, looking for a place to put his end down.

“I can stay if there's more,” he says to Shuna. ᴹ

“Could you bring the rope round when I've got this in the basket?” Shuna gathers a fold between her wrists. “We can set the clean side out by the kitchen to dry.” ˢʰᵘ

He nods, still holding it. Yuka realises a beat too late that he's waiting for her to tell him whether she wants to stay as well.

“I'll be there,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks at her then. It lasts as long as it takes Shuna to say the next corner is clear. He folds his end in, puts it down in the basket and goes after the rope.

* * *

There's enough left of the old line to reach if they put it on a different hook. Frieren brings the empty basket back out to hold the end in while Mai sets a new hook farther along the wall. Ori wants to hold the other end and has been informed that her arms won't stay that high once someone asks her a question. She deputises Frieren and asks her one. Frieren answers without lowering either arm.

At the foot of the post, Mau is trying to unpick the knot.

“She's coming,” says Mai, because she sees Yuka come round the corner with the damp sheet. “Hold up a second.” ᴹᵃ

Mau gets up so fast that he pulls the knot in tighter. He sets the rope down on the post and moves, leaving a way through for Yuka and the whole basket. His foot catches at the lip of the path. He rights himself and leans back against the wall.

She stops just long enough to see that he's steady. Then she comes over with the basket and sets it where he left the rope.

“I brought you a dry shirt,” she says. “Shuna put it on top when I said your collar was wet. Where do you want it?” ʸ

He looks past the shirt to the door where his other one hangs on a peg in the clinic. There are two of his cups on Shoko's sill so he can use whichever one is clean. Somebody has moved his comb there too. Yuka hadn't known he'd brought it down.

“Could you hand it to me?” he says. ᴹ

She does. She sees him collect the piece of rope again with the edge of his sleeve, thumb working at the knot through cloth. He keeps working at it while she stands there. He doesn't ask her to hold the other side.

“I thought you were leaving when Mai said I was coming,” she says. ʸ

“I was giving you the path.” He gets the end through. “I can get this for them from the other side if that's easier.” ᴹ

Easier for whom, she wants to say. It would sound like a fight. She has the whole sentence in her head. She can hear the voice she'd use if she said it. She puts both hands on the basket and waits until she can say the next thing plainly.

“I keep wanting you to stay,” she says. “I never seem to tell you till you're already standing up.” ʸ

He turns the rope over. “I don't know which times you mean it.” ᴹ

She straightens. He's still looking down. She can see how hard he is holding that filthy little length of rope he's made into a reason to be there.

“I meant it at supper,” she says. “I mean it now.” ʸ

He looks toward the hook where Mai is still working. His weight comes off the wall. At the far end Ori wants to know how many towels the new hook can hold. Shuna asks her to count the ones she actually has and start there. Mau gets the clean shirt under one arm and holds the rope out to Yuka with the other.

“Could you get it over that end?” he asks. ᴹ

She can. When she gets it there she looks back, and he's still there holding the other end.

* * *

They go in different doors afterward.

Cross is at the back door. He's got one hand on the frame, as if he was going to hold it open for Yuka. Mai gets there with the wet basket first.

“Did you want me?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“I thought you might want to get out of the house for a bit.” ᶜʳ

Mai steadies the basket against her hip. “We're about to eat. You can come in and ask her at the table.” ᴹᵃ

Cross drops his hand from the frame. He says he'll see her later. She watches him go far enough to hear him ask Shuna where Maru is. The invitation would have sounded kind yesterday. She notices how much easier it is to decide what she wants when he gives her room to finish deciding.

Inside, Mai has a clean towel across her lap, drying off after the last of the line. She holds it out with the tips of her fingers when she sees that the sheet has left a wet stripe across Yuka's cheek. Yuka takes it and stands there for long enough that Mai moves her work to the end of the bench.

“He has a comb at Shoko's,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Yeah?” ᴹᵃ

“He takes it there every night. He probably has a place he puts it. I didn't look long enough to see.” ʸ

Her voice goes on that last word. She sets her forehead against the towel. Mai waits while she gets it out. There's a long enough gap in the kitchen noise for Yuka to hear Shuna arguing that a plate with one piece of bread on it counts as a plate that needs washing.

“I want him home,” she says when she can. ʸ

“Then tell him that when he's got room to hear it.” Mai pushes a cup farther in so Yuka won't knock it over. “He still has to get a say in where he sleeps tonight.” ᴹᵃ

“I know that.” ʸ

“Do you?” Maki has been on the other end of the bench the whole time, picking dirt out of the hem of her trousers. She stops long enough to look straight at Yuka. “You waited for him to ask you to stay after you took the band off. What were you going to tell him if he asked?” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuka gets as far as opening her mouth. That morning she'd been so sure he'd have something to say to her once he saw the ring in her hand that she hadn't thought about what she'd say back.

“I thought I'd know when he said it.” ʸ

“He had something to say that morning. You watched his face while you took it off. There's a difference between wanting him to talk and wanting to see if you can make him.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Mai puts her work down. “And when you get a minute with him, let him have an answer you didn't come to hear.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka gets her face out of the towel. She gets enough air to open her hands and see the marks she left pulling the cloth tight between them.

“Can you come back and eat with me?” she asks. ʸ

Mai looks at the bit she's been working on for a whole second. Then she gets up and brings it with her. Shuna fits another stool between the two already at the end of the table. They move over to give her space for her own plate when she comes back.

* * *

There are still two loose pegs on the clinic sill after supper. Yuka takes them back before Ori can ask if anyone has counted. She catches the door before it pushes all the way in and knocks on the frame.

Makima is sitting on the low chair by the cot. Yuka can see one of her shoes where the lamplight falls across the floor. Mau's leaned forward so he can hold a bowl against both his knees. His hand is out to one side, empty. When he hears the knock, his fingers slip between Makima's. It's done by the time he sees who knocked.

Yuka feels the urge to take one step back. There's nobody behind her. She stays.

“I brought the pegs. Can I do anything while I'm in here?” ʸ

Mau looks down at the bowl. The spoon has slipped far enough in that she can only see the end of the handle.

“Could you put the stool on this side?” he asks. ᴹ

She goes round the cot. The stool drags on one foot, so she lifts it and carries it the last little distance. She would have set it right against his leg. She can see him measuring where the bowl's going to go with the angle of his wrist.

“Here?” she asks. ʸ

“A little farther forward. Thanks.” ᴹ

He gets the bowl onto it. Makima starts to let go and he holds on.

“Stay a bit?” he asks her. ᴹ

Makima settles back onto the chair. “Yes.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka feels the edge of the peg in her palm. She knows the exact height at which she used to put her hand out and have Mau take it without checking whose it was. There'd been a whole afternoon on the island when she was the one who told Makima it was fine to hold his hand for a while. Tonight he asked for it in front of her, and it doesn't take either of them a second to understand him.

She sets the pegs on the foot of the cot before she can let herself ask for that hand back. “There's bread in the kitchen. Do you want some?” ʸ

“One edge piece if there's any left,” he says. ᴹ

Shuna is scraping the board when Yuka goes in. She offers to put a little butter on the bread so he can eat it with the soup. Yuka nearly tells her she has to go right back. She waits while Shuna goes for the butter and takes the piece herself. When she sets the plate on the stool at the clinic, he turns his wrist to take it without letting the bowl move.

“You found the good end,” he says. ᴹ

“I asked Shuna.” ʸ

He looks up. He's usually the person who saves the heel for Frieren. She can't tell if that's what he's just remembered. He breaks the bread with one hand, gets a little of it into the soup and eats. Yuka has time to decide if she's going to ask him while he's got a reason to want her to go.

“Could we eat together tomorrow?” she asks. “Early.” ʸ

Mau puts the bread down on the edge of the plate. Makima leaves her hand where it is.

“At the table?” he asks. ᴹ

“Yes. I'll come sit with you, if you're there first.” ʸ

He thinks about it. There have been a lot of moments she could spend making herself guess what his face is doing. She lets him get to his own answer.

“Come sit with me,” he says. ᴹ

She puts the pegs on the sill. He breaks the rest of the bread small enough to go straight into the bowl. He's quite methodical about it. She has time to say tomorrow once more before she goes. He's already told her to come.

She goes back to the house and up to Ori's room, where Ori still has to be told that her feet should point toward her own end of the bed.

* * *

Makima takes the bowl when he finishes. At the washstand she tips a little water into it and turns it slowly to get the last of the soup off the side. She could do it a second time. She could dry the spoon separately.

“Did you eat?” asks Mau. ᴹ

She looks back. He's moved over on the cot so he can see her without turning his neck. Last night she had her cheek against the top of his head. All day she'd been able to remember the spot where the ends of his hair touched her mouth. She'd tried out the words quietly by the pump before she came in. *Could I hold you again?* She'd wondered if he might want it again on a night when he could get up and go anywhere he liked.

The trouble was that he could tell her no.

“I did,” she says. ᴹᵏ

She sets the bowl down. Her hands have dried all except where the water ran under one cuff. She could ask now. She lifts her eyes and finds him setting the good end of the blanket out of the way, absently, to make a seat.

“Come sit with me a little longer,” he says. “I want to tell you what Ori said while Frieren was holding the wash up.” ᴹ

“Why she had to hold it so high?” ᴹᵏ

“No. She'd seen two birds arguing down by the pump. I think we were supposed to know one of them personally.” ᴹ

Makima sits. He's barely a sentence into it when she realises he already told her some of this on his way in. He stops at the same spot to search for the sound Ori made when she did the second bird. This time Makima remembers it before he does. He turns to look at her properly when she gets it right.
