# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 11 — A Room With a View

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E124
**Continuity state:** B+438 ("49"), afternoon into evening. Mau comes back from Elmesia's and knocks on the door of DIO's room inside him. She is down. He tells her a small lie to spare Elmesia: he misread the letter, and Elmesia had assumed DIO would come along inside him and step out when they arrived. DIO's face turns into Ori's face when Ori has misunderstood something and has just heard exactly what she wanted. Mau says sorry; DIO says that's two he owes her; he tells her Elmesia wants to see her plant. DIO picks up the pot from her other room at the Guest Inn before they leave. After supper at home (Yuka beside him; he tells her again where he's going) he crosses back. This time he arrives in a small room off Elmesia's garden and leaves a soldier there permanently, so that future arrivals don't frighten anyone. The room has a very good view. DIO and Elmesia walk the lit garden with the plant. Nobody names anything. Mau waits on a bench far enough away not to hear, with Ciel, who is allowed to tease him out loud now.

*When: B+438, from early afternoon. "49." Ori has drawn a small bird beside it.*

He knocks.

That's the first thing. Afterward, when he thinks about the day, he'll think that was the whole of it: that he sat down in the clinic and went inside, walked past the shared table to DIO’s own door, and knocked.

He didn't knock for six days. He knocks now.

Silence.

Then, from inside, very dignified: “Who is it?” ᴰ

“You know who it is.” ᴹ

“I don’t know anything,” says DIO. “I’ve been in my room. Being very busy.” ᴰ

“Can I come in?” ᴹ

A long pause.

“It isn't locked,” says DIO, in the voice of someone who wants it on record that she didn't say yes. ᴰ

* * *

She's sitting on the end of her bed with her knees drawn up. The chair she mentioned last night is beside her desk. She hasn't put anything on it. He notices that before he notices the watering notebook lying closed on the bed beside her.

He sits on the floor with his back against the wall, so he isn't standing over her.

“You went to see her,” says DIO. ᴰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Was it nice?” ᴰ

“She had the small cakes.” ᴹ

DIO's jaw tightens.

“I'm sure that was very nice for you,” she says. ᴰ

He looks at her. At the set of her shoulders. At the way she's holding her knees, the way Ori holds hers when she's trying very hard not to cry about something that she has decided doesn't matter.

He thinks about Elmesia's face behind her hand. *I didn't put her name on it because I didn't want her to read it. I didn't want her to know I was asking about her.*

He can't tell DIO that. It isn't his to tell. It's Elmesia's, whenever she's ready, in her own words, in her own garden.

So he lies.

* * *

“I got it wrong,” he says. ᴹ

DIO looks round.

“The letter,” he says. “I read it wrong. I read *alone* and I thought she meant just me. I went by myself. And when I got there she was looking past me. Behind me. Like she was waiting for someone else to come through.” ᴹ

DIO goes very still.

“She thought you'd be with me,” he says. “She thought you’d come with me and step out when we got there. She didn’t put your name on because she didn’t think she needed to. She thought it was obvious. She said *of course DIO's coming, why would I need to write that down*. And I'd left you behind sulking in your room, and she was very disappointed in me.” ᴹ

He watches it land.

And DIO's face changes.

He's seen that change before, on another face. On Ori's, at the kitchen table, once, when she'd misunderstood something about the Fort and thought she was in trouble, and then someone explained what had really happened and it turned out to be the exact opposite of what she'd feared. That face. The one that goes from braced to open all at once, from thunder to sun, so fast you can practically hear it. The face of someone who has just heard exactly what she most wanted to hear and doesn't quite dare believe it yet.

DIO made Ori. He's always known that. He's never seen it so clearly as now.

“She thought,” says DIO slowly, “I'd be with you.” ᴰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“She was *disappointed*.” ᴰ

“Very.” ᴹ

“In *you*.” ᴰ

“Mostly in me.” ᴹ

DIO uncurls. She puts her feet on the floor. She picks up the watering notebook and tries to look as if that was what she meant to do all along.

“Well,” she says. “That's your fault, then.” ᴰ

“Yes,” he says. “I'm sorry.” ᴹ

“You should be.” She stands up. She smooths her clothes down. She is, all at once, entirely herself again, chin up, magnificent. “That's two.” ᴰ

“Two?” ᴹ

“Two you owe me,” says DIO. “The door. And now this. Two large things. I'm keeping count.” ᴰ

“I know you are.” ᴹ

* * *

“She wants to see the plant,” he says. ᴹ

DIO, halfway to the door, stops.

“My plant,” she says. ᴰ

“She asked about it. She said you've been writing to her about it. She said she wants to see how it's getting on. With her own eyes.” ᴹ

That part's true. Elmesia did say it, between the bridge and the cakes. *Tell her to bring the plant. I want to see whether my gardeners are any good.*

DIO turns round with the notebook in her hands. She opens it to the page with the new leaf, then runs a thumb along the little drawing.

“It's not ready,” says DIO. “It's not big enough. It's not—she has a *palace*, Mau. She has gardens. She has gardeners who've been gardening for three hundred years. And this is—” ᴰ

She stops.

“This is mine,” she says, much more quietly. “It's the first thing I ever kept alive.” ᴰ

He doesn't say anything for a moment.

“I think,” he says carefully, “that's why she wants to see it.” ᴹ

DIO looks at him. She closes the notebook with both hands, carefully keeping her pencil in the right place.

“After supper?” she says. ᴰ

“After supper.” ᴹ

“I’ll need to go to the Guest Inn first. I want to check if it needs water.” ᴰ

“There's time.” ᴹ

* * *

Supper is careful again.

Yuka sits beside him. When he asks whether he can put the bread between them, she moves both their cups to make it fit. Ori tries to put one of the pieces up on its end and the whole pile slides towards Mau. Yuka catches it before it lands in his lap. She looks at him with both hands full of bread and tells him she did it on purpose.

When he's finished, he turns to her.

“I'm going back,” he says. “To Elmesia's. With DIO this time. She's bringing her plant.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him. He watches her register, again, that he's telling her. Before. Out loud.

“How long?” she says. ʸ

“I don't know. A few hours. I'll be back tonight.” ᴹ

She nods.

“DIO's plant,” she says. And something happens at the corner of her mouth, something with a little light in it, the first he's seen there in over a week. “Is it—are they—” ʸ

“I don't know,” says Mau. ᴹ

“You *do* know.” ʸ

“It isn't mine to say.” ᴹ

The light at the corner of her mouth gets a little bigger.

“Okay,” she says. The good kind. “Come back.” ʸ

“I will.” ᴹ

* * *

DIO is already at the Guest Inn when he goes to fetch her. She lifts the pot off the sill in her room and turns it so the new leaf faces him. It is smaller than the one in her drawing. He decides to keep that to himself.

At Elmesia’s palace, he doesn’t arrive in the study this time.

He thought about it all through supper. Every time he comes through, it's a soldier vanishing from someone's shadow and him standing where it stood. In Elmesia's study that's one thing; she expects him. But if he's going to bring DIO, and if this is going to happen again, and he has a feeling it's going to happen a great deal, then he can't keep appearing out of a queen's shadow in the middle of her study. Someone's going to walk in with a tray one day and drop it.

So he asked Elmesia, before he left this morning, for a room.

Any room. Somewhere quiet. Somewhere nobody would be frightened if a man suddenly appeared in it.

She gave him one. Before he left, she took him up to see it herself, and he asked one of the ordinary soldiers to wait there for the evening Exchange.

It's small. Hardly bigger than a cupboard. A plain wooden floor, a chair, a hook for a coat, at the top of a narrow stair in the old part of the palace, where the gardens begin. Nobody uses it. Nobody comes up here except the cats.

And it has a window.

He comes through with DIO's hand in his and her plant held against her chest in its pot, and the world folds and unfolds, and they're standing in the little room, and he looks up.

The window looks out over everything.

The whole garden, laid out below them, every path lit with lanterns, the way Elmesia promised. And past the garden, the city, rooftop after rooftop going down the hill toward the river, every window lit. And past the city, the river itself, wide and slow and bright in the dusk, with the last of the sunset going out over the hills on the far side.

He can't see the colours. He never can. But he can see the shape of it, and the light, and how far it goes, and he knows, standing there, that it's one of the most beautiful things he has ever looked at.

DIO has stopped breathing beside him.

* * *

He brings the landing soldier back through his shadow. It had traded places with him for the crossing. He has another anchor at the house for the way home, and this one can take up its post now.

It rises in ordinary shadow armour and goes down on one knee in front of him.

“This is your room now,” Mau tells it. ᴹ

The soldier looks at him.

“Stay here,” he says. “When I come through, I'll come here. So I don't frighten anyone. You don't have to do anything else. Just be here.” ᴹ

The soldier inclines its head.

Then it turns, very slightly, and looks out of the window.

At the garden. The city. The river going bright in the dusk.

Mau feels something go through it. Not words. Soldiers don't have words. But he knows them, all of them, the way he knows his own hands, and what goes through this one is something very like the feeling of a man who has been given a job he was dreading and found out it comes with the best seat in the house.

“Yes,” Mau says. “It's a very good view.” ᴹ

The soldier settles into the chair by the window, and doesn't move again.

*You've given a shadow a holiday*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*It deserves one.* ᴹ

*They're all going to want this room now.* ᶜ

*They can take turns.* ᴹ

* * *

Elmesia is waiting at the bottom of the narrow stair.

She's changed out of the queen's clothes she wore this morning into something simpler, and her hair is down, and she's holding a lantern of her own, and when she sees DIO coming down the stairs with the plant held against her chest she doesn't say anything at all for a moment.

Then she says, “You came.” ᴱˡ

DIO stops on the second-to-last step. She lifts her chin.

“I was *invited*,” she says. “Apparently. Mau read the letter wrong.” ᴰ

Elmesia's eyes go to Mau, over DIO's shoulder, very quickly. He doesn't do anything with his face.

“He's very bad at letters,” says Elmesia gravely. ᴱˡ

“He's very bad at a lot of things,” DIO agrees. “I'm keeping count.” ᴰ

Then she holds out the plant.

She does it all at once, abruptly, the way you'd hand someone something hot. Both hands. The pot thrust out between them in the lantern light.

“This is it,” she says. “It's not very big. Your gardeners will laugh at it.” ᴰ

Elmesia puts the lantern down on the step.

She takes the pot in both hands, carefully, the way DIO picked it up, the way you'd take something sleeping. She lifts it up into the light. She looks at it for a long time. At the small stem, and the leaves, and the new one, the smallest, still uncurling.

“It has a new leaf,” she says. ᴱˡ

“I noticed it yesterday,” says DIO. ᴰ

“Yesterday,” Elmesia repeats. She looks at DIO over the top of the plant. “Nobody is going to laugh at it. Come and see the garden. I want to show you where I think it would like to be. If you ever wanted it to have a holiday.” ᴱˡ

DIO hesitates. Then she comes down the last step.

* * *

He doesn't follow them.

He finds a bench at the edge of the garden, under a tree, far enough away that he can't hear what they say. He can see them, if he looks. Two figures walking slowly down one of the lit paths, one carrying a lantern, the other carrying nothing now because the other one has the plant. Stopping by a bed of something. Bending over it. One of them pointing. The other one shaking her head, and then nodding.

He doesn't watch for long. It isn't his.

He sits on the bench and looks up at the little window at the top of the narrow stair, where the light is on, and a shadow is sitting very still in a chair, looking out at the city.

*She's going to work it out*, says Ciel. *DIO. Sooner or later.* ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*And when she does, she's going to be furious with you for lying about the letter.* ᶜ

*That'll be three.* ᴹ

*She'll keep count.* ᶜ

*I know she will.* ᴹ

Ciel is quiet for a moment.

*You understood something, this morning*, she says. *In the study. With the cakes.* ᶜ

He doesn't answer straight away.

*She doesn't sulk at people she's only grateful to*, he says at last. *She sulks at people she's sure won't leave.* ᴹ

*Yes.* ᶜ

*Yuka went for the worst thing she could find*, he says. *Twice. She's never done that to anyone else. Only me. Because she was sure I'd still be there to hear it.* ᴹ

*Yes*, says Ciel again. Gently. ᶜ

*That doesn't make it all right.* ᴹ

*No*, says Ciel. *It doesn't. But it means it was never the thing you were afraid of. It was the opposite.* ᶜ

He sits on the bench in Elmesia's garden and looks at the lanterns.

Somewhere down the lit path, very faintly, carried on the evening air, he hears DIO laugh. A real one. The first he's heard from her in six days.

He closes his eyes.

He wants to go home.

Not yet. DIO isn't ready. The garden is very big and Elmesia has a great deal to show her.

But soon.
