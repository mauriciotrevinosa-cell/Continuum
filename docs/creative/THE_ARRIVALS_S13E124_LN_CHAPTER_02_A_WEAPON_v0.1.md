# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 15 — A Weapon

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S13E124
**Continuity state:** B+440 ("47"), the same afternoon, between Mau's two trips; Yuka's point of view. Mau is home but inside, in DIO's room inside him; his body sits in the clinic chair with his eyes closed. Makima, whose body can't go far from his, is free in the house. Cross finds Yuka on the orchard wall and tries: Mau went to a queen this morning, came back, and still went to someone else instead of her; he and Maru are going north soon, and she doesn't have to wait in doorways. Yuka calls her wheel. It turns once over her head, ready to throw hands. She sets the limit: she's grateful for what he saw then, and always will be, but she sees now that he's using it as a weapon to pull her away from her husband, and she won't let it be used that way again, by him or by herself. She takes responsibility for removing her band in Mau's sight and staying away after he came to ask her himself. Maru takes Cross away. Makima, who heard the wheel, sits with Yuka on the wall and tells her about finding him during the panic attack before he left for the wild Gate. Mau gave Shoko leave for Yuka to know everything, so this is a legal grey area, and Makima chooses to stand in it.

*When: B+440. Mid-afternoon. Ori has drawn a bird beside "47".*

The orchard wall was their place. Hers and Cross's.

It's the first thing she understands, sitting on it in the afternoon sun with the hoodie sleeves over her hands. She came here without thinking. She came here because it's where she sits when she wants to be alone and not alone at the same time, and it's where Cross found her the day after he arrived, and it's where he told her about the corridor and the plastic chair and *you just stopped being anywhere*.

It used to be her place with Mau. Before that.

She'd forgotten that. Somewhere in the days since the walk she'd stopped thinking about that.

She sits on the wall and looks at the clinic across the yard. The door's open. She can see Mau in the chair by the window, with his eyes closed and his hands loose in his lap. Not asleep. She knows what he looks like asleep. This is the other thing: the way he looks when he's gone inside, to the room with the table and the cup, to talk to the people who live in him.

He's been there an hour. He knocked on DIO's door, Ori told her. Ori is very well informed.

Yuka doesn't mind. She sits on the wall and watches his face, and she doesn't mind at all.

* * *

“He's back, then,” says Cross. ᶜʳ

She didn't hear him come. She never does. He moves the way his people move, too fast and too quiet, and then he's just there, leaning on the wall beside her with his arms folded on the top of it.

“He's back,” she says. ʸ

Cross looks across the yard at the clinic. At Mau in the chair.

“From the queen,” he says. ᶜʳ

“From Elmesia's.” ʸ

“He went to a queen's palace this morning,” says Cross, “and came back, and went straight inside to talk to—who is it this time? The one with the plant?” ᶜʳ

Yuka doesn't answer.

“And tonight he's going back,” Cross says. “To the queen. With another woman inside him.” ᶜʳ

He says it lightly. As if it's nothing. As if he's only remarking on the weather.

“He told me,” says Yuka. “Where he was going. Both times. Before he went.” ʸ

“He *told* you.” Cross nods slowly. “That's good. That's something.” ᶜʳ

He's quiet for a moment.

“It's just,” he says, “I keep noticing. He goes to everyone. The queen. The devil. The voices. The doctor; he went straight past you to the doctor, the other day, I saw it. He goes to all of them. And you sit in doorways.” ᶜʳ

She has to take her hand out of her sleeve. It won't lie flat while it's folded in there.

“I told him I loved him after the quarry,” she says. “I didn't say it back before. I wanted him to hear it.” ʸ

Cross looks at her for a second. “Did you get to keep talking?” ᶜʳ

“I left him his supper. He was so tired he couldn't hold a cup straight.” ʸ

“And now you're working out how much of it you need to say again so he'll come upstairs.” He looks toward the chair. “He said it in the yard and he's barely had to say another thing to you about it since.” ᶜʳ

* * *

It goes into her like a splinter.

That's what's terrible. It goes in. Because she sat in a doorway all afternoon when she brought him breakfast. Because she did watch him walk past her to the clinic. Because some small frightened part of her, the part that said *you'd rather have her* in the yard, still believes it. Hears it and nods.

Cross sees it go in. She watches him see it.

“I'm not trying to hurt you,” he says, more gently. “I'm not. I just remember what it was like, before. When you were sick and nobody could see it. When you were sitting outside a door because nobody would let you in. And I don't want you to be that again. Not for anyone.” ᶜʳ

He looks at her.

“Maru and I are going north soon,” he says. “Back to our people. Not today. But soon. And there's room. You don't have to wait in doorways, Yuka. You never had to. Not for him. Not for anyone.” ᶜʳ

* * *

She calls the wheel before she climbs down from the wall.

She feels it first, at the back of her head, the way you feel a sneeze coming. A pressure. A turning. And then it's there, over her head in the afternoon light: the great eight-spoked shape, solid and enormous and old, the one she learned to call on a white stone in another world. Not the whole of Mahoraga. Just the wheel. Hanging over her like a halo made of something that has broken gods.

It turns once.

The click carries across the whole yard.

Cross takes his arms off the wall. Slowly. He steps back. One step. His hands open at his sides, the way his people hold their hands when they're ready to move very fast.

Across the yard, on the porch, Rimuru stops talking mid-sentence. Maki puts down the staff she was oiling. In the kitchen doorway, Shuna goes very still.

Yuka slides down off the wall and stands up.

She doesn't raise her hands. She doesn't need to. The wheel is enough.

“Say it again,” she says. ʸ

Her voice is quite calm. It surprises her.

“Say the part about doorways again,” she says. “I want to hear exactly what it sounds like.” ʸ

* * *

Cross doesn't say it again.

He looks at the wheel. Then at her. He's very pale.

“Yuka,” he says. ᶜʳ

“No,” says Yuka. “You've said enough. It's my turn.” ʸ

She takes a breath.

“You saw me,” she says. “In the corridor. When I was the most frightened I'd ever been and pretending I wasn't, and nobody else saw it. Not my brother. Nobody. You did. You sat in that stupid plastic chair with your knees up round your ears and you saw me, and you didn't look away, and I'm grateful. I'm going to be grateful for that my whole life. I want you to know that. I'm not taking it back.” ʸ

Cross's face does something.

“But you're using it,” says Yuka. ʸ

He goes still.

“You're using it,” she says again, and her voice shakes now, and she lets it. “You're taking the one thing you gave me that nobody else could, and you're using it like a knife. To get between me and my husband. I had his map under my hand when you told me to wait to be asked. He'd already asked me. I told you he did. You made me listen to the last thing he could get out in the yard and hear it like he'd worked out a way to make me stay.” ʸ

She swallows. He has his hands open beside him. She remembers that Mau had his at his sides that night. He hadn't even taken a step.

“He couldn't get a whole word out for Shoko when he came home. I was in the hallway when he tried. How do you make that sound like something he did to me?” ʸ

The wheel hangs above her. Cross has backed up. She can say the rest without taking another step.

“And I let you,” she says. “That's the worst part. I let you. I sat on this wall and I let you, because it felt good to be seen from before. And I went home and said the worst thing I could find to the man who's seen me every single day since.” ʸ

* * *

“You'd already taken it off,” says Cross. His eyes drop to her hand. “I saw it was gone when you came out to move the chairs. I thought you'd made up your mind.” ᶜʳ

Her thumb comes up against the band. There's a place between it and Grandpa's where she can get the tip of her nail if she turns her hand just so. She'd done the turn before she knew if the band would come off cleanly. Once it caught, she had a whole breath she could have used to push it down again. She'd spent the breath making sure Mau was still watching.

“I wanted him to ask me to put it back on,” she says. She hasn't prepared for what it sounds like to tell the person she went to instead. “I wanted him to have to ask.” ʸ

“He'd gone. You had every reason to be angry.” ᶜʳ

“He was standing by the cup asking me to talk.” ʸ

Cross has his mouth open. She can hear what she used to say before he had to say it for her. *He went through the Gate without me.* She can still be angry about that tomorrow. She won't give him that sentence to say for her again.

“After he saw me do it, he came to the wall with the map,” she says. “After I stayed home with it, he came back and asked if I'd remembered where it was. He had to ask if I'd even thought about him that morning.” ʸ

She can see a little of the map coming out under the glove again. She had known all along how to get there. The only thing she'd been waiting for was for him to come down the hill and ask her to do the thing he'd already asked for. He was going up that same hill toward a man who could kill him. She hasn't let herself put those facts next to one another until now.

The wheel stops turning. She leaves it where it is. Across the yard she can see the clinic window, and a little corner of the blanket on the cot. She wants to open the curtain on his side of the bed again and have it actually mean he's coming upstairs. Before she can ask for that, she's going to have to tell him what she'd wanted him to see.

“I'm staying here,” she tells Cross. “I meant it when I put the band back on. I owe Mau the part that says why I took it off.” ʸ

She looks at Cross.

“Don't do it again,” she says. “Don't use what you saw to pull me away from him. Not ever. If you want to be my friend, you can be my friend. I'd like that. I mean it. But not like this. Not as a weapon.” ʸ

* * *

Cross doesn't say anything for a long time.

He looks at the wheel, still and enormous over her head. He looks at her face. He looks, very briefly, across the yard at the clinic, where a man is sitting in a chair by the window with his eyes closed, not knowing any of this is happening.

“I thought you were dead,” he says. ᶜʳ

His voice cracks on it.

“I know,” says Yuka. ʸ

“For six months. I thought you'd gone somewhere to do it alone. And then I woke up here, and you weren't dead, you were—happy. With someone. And I didn't know what to do with that. I didn't know where to put it.” ᶜʳ

“I know,” Yuka says again, more gently. ʸ

“So I put it there,” says Cross. “On him. And on you. And I told myself I was looking out for you.” ᶜʳ

He rubs his face with one hand.

“I'm sorry,” he says. ᶜʳ

Behind him, somebody has come into the orchard. Very quietly, the way his people move. Maru, his twin, with his hands at his sides and his face full of something like relief.

Maru doesn't say anything. He just puts a hand on his brother's shoulder.

Cross looks round at him. Then he looks back at Yuka one more time.

“Friend,” he says. “If you'll have it.” ᶜʳ

“If you'll keep it that,” says Yuka. ʸ

He nods.

Maru takes him away, out through the far side of the orchard, toward the river. Neither of them looks back.

* * *

The wheel goes.

She lets it. It folds back down into her shadow like a held breath let out, and the orchard is just an orchard again, with the afternoon sun coming through the leaves and her standing in the middle of it in a too-big hoodie, shaking.

She sits down on the wall. She has to.

“That was very good,” says somebody beside her. ᴹᵏ

She jumps.

Makima is sitting on the wall too. A careful distance away. Knees together, hands in her lap, the way she sat on the clinic step. Yuka didn't hear her come either. She's starting to think nobody in this house walks like a normal person.

“The wheel,” Makima says. “I heard it from the north wing. I thought someone was about to die.” ᴹᵏ

“Nobody was about to die.” ʸ

“No,” Makima agrees. “But it was a very convincing wheel.” ᴹᵏ

They sit there for a moment.

Makima sets both hands on the stone. There is enough room between them for Yuka to stand up and go. Yuka puts an elbow on the wall and turns toward her.

“You can sit closer,” she says. ʸ

Makima moves a little way. She leaves her hands on the stone.

“Can I tell you something about him?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka looks at her.

“Shoko already told me,” she says. ʸ

“Shoko told you what he said,” says Makima. “I'm going to tell you what he felt. That's different. I was inside it.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka goes very still.

“He gave Shoko leave to tell you everything,” Makima says. “*If Yuka asks, tell her the truth. All of it.* He didn't say anything about me.” She tilts her head. “So I think that's a grey area. And I've decided I'm going to stand in it. You can tell me to stop.” ᴹᵏ

“Tell me what you felt,” Yuka says. “Anything he told you in private can stay with you.” ʸ

“The night you came back from the ridge,” says Makima, “do you remember where he was when you got in?” ᴹᵏ

“At the table.” Yuka has to think about whether she saw him get up. “He came in later. I was on the sofa with Tsurugi.” ʸ

Makima puts a palm flat on the wall between them. A little grass is growing in a seam right below it.

“He had his hand here when I found him. Farther down, past the training posts. He could see his fingers on the stone. He couldn't feel them.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at the place she's put her hand. Shoko said he remembered a piece of grass. She'd imagined him sitting out there cooling off, with a whole yard between him and the reason he'd needed air. She hadn't pictured him with both legs still trying to hold him up.

“I felt it from across the house,” Makima says. “I had trouble taking a breath on the way over. By the time I got there he was pulling on his collar with two fingers and it was already loose. I sat down because I could see he'd have to. It took a while before he could let his weight come down.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka has a hand up to her own throat by the time she thinks to take it away.

“Was he ill?” ʸ

“He was panicking.” Makima says it very plainly. “His body had taken him somewhere. He was trying to get back. I told him when one breath made it all the way out. That was how little there was to say to him for a while.” ᴹᵏ

“Was he asking for me?” ʸ

Makima looks out across the yard. “He thought you wouldn't notice if he stayed out.” ᴹᵏ

They can see the common-room window from the part of the wall they're on. Yuka can remember Tsurugi's elbow on the cushion while she was telling him about the lake. When Mau came through the door, she held out both arms and he came. She had seen him lift one leg over hers before settling and never wondered why there was wet grass on his trousers.

“That was the night of the walk,” she says. “I keep thinking it started when he went through the Gate.” ʸ

“He'd been here that whole day,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

Yuka tries once to think of something to say about who was with him now. The person she'd put on the other side of the argument was the person who sat with him until he could go back inside. Makima hadn't been able to take a full breath of her own and she went out to find him anyway. Yuka had been waiting on the sofa for him to come prove he still wanted the place she'd kept warm.

“He came in when he could walk,” she says. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka puts her thumb against her band again. She keeps it still this time.

“I took it off in front of him, later,” she says. “I thought he knew it was something I could put back when I wanted to. I didn't think about him having to wake up the next morning and look for me anyway.” ʸ

Makima looks at her hand and then at her. “I felt what happened in the yard when you told him he'd rather have me. Everything in him got quiet. I've been near him when he was angry. It felt like he'd stopped expecting an answer.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka lets her hand come down onto the stone. She'd started by trying to make him afraid she'd go. By the time she decided she wanted to stay, she'd given him plenty of reasons to stop asking.

“And this morning,” says Makima, “in the kitchen, when you said *come back*, I felt that too.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks up.

“He didn't move,” says Makima. “His face didn't move. He went to the anchor and he went through. But inside—” ᴹᵏ

She stops. For the first time, she seems to be looking for a word.

“I don't have a word for it,” she says. “I've been inside people's heads for a very long time. I've felt them be afraid, and obey, and want things. I've never felt that. It was like someone opening a window in a room that's been shut for years. All at once. All the air coming in.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka cries.

Not like in the herb room. Not hard and ugly. Just quietly, sitting on the wall in the sun, with the tears running down and her not doing anything about them.

Makima doesn't touch her. Makima waits.

“How do I help him,” Yuka says at last. “Properly. Not the way I've been doing it. Not knocking. Not being sorry at him. I don't know how else to do it.” ʸ

Makima thinks about it.

“Don't make him guess,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her.

“That's all,” Makima says. “He spends his whole life guessing. What people need. What they'll do. Whether they'll leave. He's very good at it, and it's killing him. You said something in the yard that made him guess wrong. So don't make him guess. When you talk to him, say the thing. The real one. The plain one. Even if it's ugly. *Especially* if it's ugly.” ᴹᵏ

She looks out across the yard, at the clinic.

“And don't let him take it from you,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Take what?” ʸ

“Your part,” says Makima. “He'll try. He'll try to say it was his fault. That he was the one who closed the doors and went to Shoko and didn't come up the stairs. He'll try to carry it for you, because that's what he does. He carried my contract for me. He carries everything for everyone.” ᴹᵏ

She turns her head and looks at Yuka directly.

“Don't let him carry yours,” she says. “It's yours. You did it. Say so. And then don't let him take it off you to make you feel better. That's the help. That's the whole of it.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka sits with that for a long time. Before she goes in she has to think what she would say if he never asked about the ring. She'd still have to tell him why he saw her take it off. She doesn't know how long it would take her to be ready to hear the answer if it were the other way round.

The sun moves. Across the yard, in the clinic, Mau opens his eyes. She sees him do it. He sits up in the chair and looks round the clinic as if he's checking where he is, and then he gets up, slowly, and goes out to the kitchen, toward the smell of supper.

He doesn't look toward the orchard. He doesn't know.

Makima gets up off the wall.

“I'm going to supper,” she says. “He'll want me there before he goes. So DIO doesn't have to be the only one he's worrying about.” ᴹᵏ

She hesitates. Then:

“The wheel was very good,” she says again. “Really. If I were still the kind of thing I used to be, I'd have been afraid of it.” ᴹᵏ

She goes.

Yuka follows as far as the porch. She sees Mau pull out a chair for DIO so there will be room for the pot when she comes in. There's a place for Yuka, too. He sees her looking and reaches for the empty cup beside it. She goes in before he can decide whether to set it out for her.

“Will you come and find me afterwards?” she asks. “When DIO's home. I'll be awake.” ʸ

He has both hands on the cup. He looks at her before he puts it down.

“Can I have supper with you first?” he asks. ᴹ

“Yes,” she says. “I wanted to ask you that, too.” ʸ
