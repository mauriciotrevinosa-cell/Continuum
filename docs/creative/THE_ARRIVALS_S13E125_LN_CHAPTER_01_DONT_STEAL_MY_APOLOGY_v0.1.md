## Chapter 16 — Don't Steal My Apology

*When: B+440, close to midnight. Ori's map is on the clinic windowsill, unfolded, held flat with a stone.*

DIO hums all the way home.

Very quietly, with the pot held against her chest. They come through the Exchange into the kitchen at home, out of the little room with the very good view, using the house anchor. The soldier with the view stays at its window. DIO frees a leaf caught against her sleeve.

She doesn't say anything about the garden. She doesn't say anything about Elmesia. She says *goodnight, Mau*, very graciously, like a queen dismissing a courtier, and walks to the Guest Inn with the pot. By now she knows how much moonlight reaches her windowsill.

And then, very faintly, through it, the humming.

*She's going to work it out*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*Not tonight.* ᴹ

*No*, says Ciel. *Not tonight.* ᶜ

* * *

The house is asleep.

He stands in the dark kitchen for a moment. The stove is banked. Somebody has left a lamp lit on the table, turned low, and a plate under a cloth beside it. Shuna, again. Always Shuna.

He doesn't lift the cloth. He isn't hungry. He's tired in the good way, the way you're tired after a long day of walking somewhere you wanted to go.

He thinks about going upstairs.

The stairs are right there. Their room is at the top. The notebook was on his pillow the morning he left for the basin, with *53* in her handwriting. She's not there. She's been in Ori's room since the night he didn't come up.

He doesn't go up.

He goes out across the yard to the clinic instead, the way he did last night, because he asked Shoko for the cot and she said *all right* and didn't ask why, and because he can still feel the drawer going shut against his hand.

The clinic door is open a hand's width.

He didn't leave it like that. He left it shut this morning.

He pushes it open.

* * *

She's in the chair.

The one on the other side of the cot. The empty one. The one Ori drew on her map with an arrow going to it across the whole floor of the clinic, and under it in her best handwriting, *NEXT EXPEDITION*.

She's sitting in it in the dark with the lamp on the windowsill turned very low, and the map unfolded beside it, held flat with a stone. She's in the hoodie. Her knees are drawn up. Her hands are out of the sleeves for once, holding the notebook in her lap.

She looks up when he comes in.

She doesn't get up.

He stands in the doorway. He doesn't know what to do with his hands.

“Hi,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Hi,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment. He sees her decide something. He sees it go through her like a held breath.

“Can you sit down?” she says. “I need to say something. And I need you to let me say all of it before you say anything. Can you do that?” ʸ

He thinks about it.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

He sits down on the edge of the cot. Across from her. Close enough to touch. He doesn't.

* * *

She opens the notebook. She doesn't read from it. She just holds it open, as if it helps to have it there.

“I keep starting with the Gate,” she says. “I did it in the kitchen when you came back from the quarry. I did it with Shoko. I can tell that part in a way that makes somebody want to say of course I was angry.” ʸ

He looks at her hands. Both of them are flat on the notebook. He can see which one she could put in her pocket if she needed a minute.

“I said maybe I shouldn't have married you. After that I told you to go with Makima. You said you loved me. I said I knew and I asked Cross to come with me. I knew you'd wait for me to say it back.” ʸ

She hadn't said the sentence about marrying him again since he heard it in the yard. He can tell by how much trouble she has getting all the words into one breath. He gives her the time. He'd wanted her to come back that night and say she hadn't meant it. It takes all he has now to wait for what she came here to say.

“I don't know when I started hearing the things Cross said in my own voice,” she says. “I heard myself say that to you. I didn't need to believe it to know exactly what it would do.” ʸ

Her voice has gone thin. She takes a breath and gets a little more of it.

“And in the morning you told me exactly where you were going. You asked me to stay for a minute. I took the band off while you watched. When it caught on my knuckle I could have stopped. I looked to see if you'd noticed and I kept going.” ʸ

He moves one hand under his thigh. He didn't tell Shoko. She can't have brought it in with her from anybody's account of what happened to him. She's describing what she did from the other side of the cup. He'd never been able to work out where she was looking after the band caught.

“I wanted you to ask me to put it back on,” she says. “Cross told me you knew I'd be there whenever you came back. He thought I should let you feel what it was like to have something to lose. I was at the rail when he said it. I looked straight at the band. The next day I made you watch me take it off.” ʸ

Mau's thumb moves up against the seam in his trousers. Some part of him is trying to work out what he'd have said if he could have got more than her name out.

“You already knew what it felt like,” Yuka says. “I knew because you'd told me. And you were standing right in front of me trying to talk when I decided you needed to feel it again.” ʸ

She pauses. He can hear a spoon go down in the house across the yard. Nothing else moves.

“The next day you came to find me again. You showed me where you'd see me when you looked up. I could still have gone. I even took the map down to where I was putting on my boots. I had to make myself believe I was still waiting for you to ask because I wanted a reason to stay angry.” ʸ

She looks down at the notebook. The corner has curled up under her finger. She keeps talking while she gets it flat again.

“When you came back, I told you I'd put my boots on. I was still waiting for you to ask me something. You thanked Shuna and you held onto Makima's hand. I left you sitting there with the part I still hadn't said. I remembered the place. Every line you drew. I knew what I'd made you look up and find.” ʸ

His shirt has a button at the throat. It's the one he brought down from the drawer. He leaves his hand in his lap instead of putting it there. The run of sounds from the yard gets as far as her shoes across the dirt. This time she's still in the chair when he looks at her.

He'd meant to leave her enough room that she'd only have to say it to him the one time. He sees the drawer again, all the way back to the lining at the bottom, his hand fixing the little crease. He'd been going to make that room for her, bit by bit, wherever she looked.

She sees him turn his hand over. She waits while he looks at it. Then she puts the notebook down flat on her knees and looks at him.

“I'm sorry, Mau. I'm sorry I did that to you. I put it back on because I want to be your wife. You've had to decide what that meant once already from looking at my hand. I want you here, with me. I want to hear what you were going to say when I walked away.” ʸ

She twists the notebook so he can see the flat of it and then leaves it alone. “I heard you in the yard,” she says. “I knew what you were waiting for and I answered something else. When I finally said it back after the quarry, I wanted to go straight to you and have you hold me. I needed to be able to do this part, too. I don't want you to have to give up the hurt before I've even asked where it is.” ʸ

* * *

He opens his mouth.

“I closed the—” ᴹ

“No,” says Yuka. ʸ

He stops.

She's looking at him, very fierce, through eyes gone wet and bright.

“Don't,” she says. “I know what you're going to say. You're going to say *I closed the doors* and *I didn't leave a note* and *I went to Shoko instead of you* and *I said Makima was coming with me*. You're going to take half of it, so I don't have to carry all of it. You're going to make it *both of us*.” ʸ

She shakes her head.

“Don't steal my apology,” she says. ʸ

He stares at her.

And then, out of absolutely nowhere, from somewhere so far down in him that he'd forgotten it was there, he laughs.

He doesn't mean to. It just comes up, all at once, the way a cough does. Not a big laugh. A short helpless one, almost a breath, with his hand coming up to his face too late to stop it. It catches him by surprise. He'd been getting ready to apologise without knowing whether she'd even let him finish. Somehow it matters that she wants him to sit here long enough to hear her out.

Yuka is staring at him now.

“That's—” she says. “You *laughed*.” ʸ

“Sorry,” he says, behind his hand. “Sorry. It's just—*don't steal my apology*—” ᴹ

“It's not *funny*.” ʸ

“No,” he says. “It's not.” And laughs again. ᴹ

Her mouth twitches.

She fights it. He watches her fight it. She presses her lips together and frowns at him and tries to keep her face serious and wet and fierce, and fails, and the corner of her mouth goes, and then all of it goes, and she's laughing too, with tears still running down her face, holding the notebook against her chest with both arms.

They sit there in the clinic at midnight and laugh at each other across the cot like idiots, with no idea why, until it runs out.

* * *

When it runs out, he wipes his eyes.

“I won't steal it,” he says. ᴹ

“Thank you.” ʸ

“It's yours.” ᴹ

“It is.” ʸ

“I heard it,” he says. “All of it. I heard it.” ᴹ

She nods. She's still crying a little. She doesn't do anything about it. One hand is flat on the cover of the notebook. He can see the band against it.

He had a question for her since she brought him the cup. *Did you want me to see?* She answered that one all by herself. He still wants to know if she would have put it back on if he hadn't come to look for her, or if he'd come home from the quarry and gone straight upstairs. The choices she made hurt him before she had to watch any of it happen. He can't tell when it began to matter to her in the way it matters to him. He thinks it must have been before she found the empty drawer. He wants it to have been before.

She takes a breath. He can still hear it catch on the way in. In another minute she'd try to tell him what he wanted to hear without him having to ask. He can see her getting ready to. He can see exactly how much he wants to believe it.

“It's—” He hears how readily he could finish it. Her shoulders are already starting to come down. He remembers them doing that in the yard, when he said he loved her and she knew which answer he was waiting for. “I heard you. I want to come upstairs with you. Can that be all I answer tonight?” ᴹ

Her thumb stops against the edge of the notebook. She looks down at it, moves her thumb off the ring, and looks back up.

“Yes,” she says. ʸ

He'd thought he might have to give her something more for her to put the book aside. She sets it beside her on the chair all the same. A moment later she picks it up again, holding it by a corner this time. His eyes go back to her hand. The question is still there, and he can't get his mouth round it. He has one other thing he came to tell her. He puts both hands on the edge of the cot so he can feel where he's sitting.

“Can I give you mine?” he says. “Separately. Not instead of yours. Next to it.” ᴹ

She looks at him a long moment.

“Separately,” she agrees. ʸ

* * *

He takes a breath.

“There are things I can tell you while I'm sitting here,” he says. “Then the pump starts up and I have to ask somebody where I am.” ᴹ

Her knees come together. He waits to see if she's going to ask him what she could have done differently that morning.

“Who do you ask?” she says. ʸ

“Makima. Sometimes Ciel, if I can hear her. The other morning I needed Shoko to come in.” ᴹ

He can see that it hurts to hear the names without hers in them. She leaves the next breath to him.

“Sometimes I can stand,” he says. “I couldn't the other morning. That part's hard to tell you.” ᴹ

He looks at the chair Makima had taken. He'd asked her to stay while Shoko saw him like that. He'd asked her again that night, with the lamp turned down and his feet barely keeping him on the edge of the cot. He's trying to tell Yuka what it cost him to ask anybody to stay that day. He can manage the part about the doors. The rest takes longer just to remember.

He looks at his hands.

“I kept thinking you wanted me out of the way,” he says. “So I did. That's the oldest thing I know how to do. Get out of the way before anyone has to ask. I did it at the inn. I did it with Frieren. I practised it for a year in the Trial. And the first time it got hard again, I went straight back to it, like it was a room I'd never really left.” ᴹ

He makes himself look up at her.

“I closed the doors without telling you,” he says. “I closed *you*. Your line. Without asking. I walked past you in the yard. I said *it's fine* when it wasn't. I said *Makima's coming with me* and I didn't even hear how it sounded, because I wasn't listening to you, I was listening to the room I'd gone back to.” ᴹ

He breathes out.

“That's mine,” he says. “Not yours. Mine. I'm sorry. And I'm going to try to be better. For you. I can't promise I won't go back there; I don't think it works like that. But I'll try to notice when I'm doing it. And Ciel's going to tell me. Out loud. As loud as she wants.” ᴹ

*I am*, says Ciel, inside, very quietly. ᶜ

“When I start wondering what you meant,” he says, “I’ll try to ask you. I should’ve asked you that night, before I spent it answering for you.” ᴹ

* * *

Yuka doesn't say anything for a while.

She looks at him across the cot in the low lamplight. He can see her taking it in. Turning it over. Not forgiving it straight away, not waving it off. Holding it, the way he held hers.

“Okay,” she says at last. ʸ

The good kind.

“I accept it,” she says. “Yours. Separately.” ʸ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

She looks down at the notebook in her lap. She runs her thumb along the edge of the page. Then she looks up again.

“There's one more door,” she says. ʸ

He goes still.

He knows which one. He's known all night. He felt it last night, lying here in the dark, the one line he didn't open, running out of him across the yard and up the stairs to Ori's room. He didn't touch it, because it wasn't his to open first.

“Will you open it?” Yuka says. “Please. Not because I've said sorry. Because I'm asking.” ʸ

He doesn't answer with words.

He finds it. The line. Thin and bright and closed, running from him to her across the little space between the cot and the chair, a hand's width, less. He closed it ten days ago before dawn without a note.

He opens it.

Not slowly. Not carefully. All at once, the way you'd throw open a window in a room that's been shut too long.

She gasps.

For a moment the line is simply there between them. Then she reaches through it herself.

*Here*, Yuka says. ʸ

He closes his eyes at the sound of her in that familiar place.

*Here*, he answers. ᴹ

When he opens his eyes she's crying again. Properly. With her hands pressed over her mouth and the notebook slipping off her lap onto the floor.

He reaches across the cot and takes her hand.

* * *

She gets up, still holding his hand, and comes round the end of the cot. He turns to make room for her. Her knee knocks the wooden frame on the way and she gives it a furious little look. It is so like her that he almost laughs again.

“Can I kiss you?” she asks. ʸ

“Yes.” He tightens his fingers around hers. “Come here.” ᴹ

She sits beside him. Her cheeks are wet and the cuff she tries to wipe them with is already wet too. He wipes the next tear away with his thumb. She turns her face into his palm for a moment before she leans in.

The kiss is brief. He still feels the warmth of it when she draws back. She's close enough that he can see her gathering herself to say something, so he waits.

“Will you come upstairs with me?” she says. “I want to sleep beside you.” ʸ

He looks at the chair she chose, at Ori's map under the stone. He puts his free hand over hers.

“I want that too,” he says. ᴹ

She lets out a breath against his cheek. He presses his mouth to her forehead and stays there a second longer, until she stands and takes him with her.

* * *

They go upstairs.

Together. Across the dark yard, past the training posts, past the orchard wall. Up the porch steps. Through the kitchen, past Shuna's covered plate. Up the stairs, every step of them, her hand in his.

To their room.

The bed is made. The window is shut where Yuka pressed the catch with the flat of her hand. The drawer is still open just far enough for him to see the sock he put back in it.

She stands in the doorway and looks at it.

“I've been in Ori's room,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Ori kicks.” ʸ

“I know.” He almost laughs again. “She fell asleep on me in the clinic. Twice.” ᴹ

“She talks in her sleep.” ʸ

“About the Fort reserves.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him. Then she laughs too, very softly, so as not to wake anyone.

She doesn't take the hoodie off. She gets into bed in it, sleeves and all, and he gets in beside her, and for a moment they lie there on their backs in the dark, side by side, not touching, the way two people lie who aren't sure yet what they're allowed.

Then she turns over and puts her face in his shoulder.

The way she used to. The way she did the first night home after the island, and a thousand nights before that, and never once since the yard.

He puts his arm round her.

He doesn't decide to. It's just where his arm goes.

* * *

He's almost asleep when she says it. Very quietly, into his shoulder, half under already.

“I'd imagined tomorrow,” she says. ʸ

He opens his eyes in the dark.

“With you,” she says. “All of it. A long way. That's why I was so frightened. I'd already let myself want it.” ʸ

He doesn't say anything. He holds her a little tighter.

“Ask me again tomorrow,” she says. “I think I have more to tell you when I've slept.” ʸ

“Tomorrow,” he says. ᴹ

Her grip loosens on his shirt. He has a thumb free to move the hair tickling the corner of her mouth. She waits for him to do it with her eyes already shut.

And she's asleep.

Just like that. All at once, the way Ori goes. Her breathing slowing and evening out against his shoulder, her hand fisted in his shirt.

He lies there in the dark for a while, feeling the line between them, open and bright and warm. Feeling all of them. Ciel and Noise still talking at the table. He thinks about DIO setting her pot back on the sill at the Guest Inn, humming to herself. Makima, in her room in the north wing, very still, and then, as if she's felt something, letting out a long breath and turning over. Beru at the foot of Ori's stairs. A soldier in a little room far away, sitting in a chair by a window, looking at a city.

All of them there.

*You're doing it again*, says Ciel, inside, very gently. ᶜ

*Doing what?* ᴹ

*Staying awake to check on everyone.* ᶜ

He almost laughs.

*Yes*, he says. *Sorry.* ᴹ

*Go to sleep, Mau.* ᶜ

He goes to sleep.

Neither of them wakes when the lamp in the hall goes out.
