# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 15 — Wake Up in Thirty

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S13E126
**Continuity state:** B+440 ("47"), morning. Yuka's point of view, then Mau's. She wakes alone in their bed and, for one terrible moment, thinks the last two days were a dream: the clinic chair, "don't steal my apology", the line opening. She runs downstairs in the hoodie. In the kitchen, Mau has bought out half the Shop's food and is making even more desserts with Shuna, Rem, Emilia and Umaru; not all of them survive Shuna's review. He says he thought she'd sleep later; he had a surprise ready and was going to carry her down. Yuka says nothing at all, turns round and goes back upstairs. From the landing: "I'm waking up in thirty." Through the open line, Mau feels her heart going a thousand miles an hour.

*When: B+440. Early. "47" in the notebook. Nobody has written it yet. The notebook is on the kitchen table under a bowl of whipped cream.*

The bed is empty.

Yuka knows it before she opens her eyes. She knows it the way you know a step is missing in the dark: her hand goes out across the sheet to where he should be, and finds the sheet, cool and flat, and nothing else.

She opens her eyes.

Their room. The window open a little. The morning coming in grey and early round the curtain. His side of the bed, empty, the pillow still dented.

And for one long, horrible moment, she doesn't know what's real.

She was in the clinic. Wasn't she? In the chair Ori drew. She said it, all of it, plainly. *Don't steal my apology.* He laughed. He laughed, really laughed, for the first time in two weeks. And then the line opened, all at once, like a window thrown wide, and she felt him on the other end of it, all of him, and she cried, and he took her hand across the cot, and they came upstairs together, and she fell asleep with her face in his shoulder.

And yesterday. The couch. The films. The sign on the door. Milim with the castle game. Stealing his towers.

All of it.

Or none of it.

Because she's dreamed it before. That's the thing. Every night for a week in Ori's bed, with Ori's feet in her back, she dreamed some version of it: walking into the clinic and finding him awake, saying the right thing, him turning to her. And every morning she woke up, and the dream went, and he was still in the clinic and she was still in the wrong bed.

She sits up.

She's in the hoodie. She's in their bed. That's something. That's real. Isn't it?

She reaches for the line.

And her hand is shaking so badly, inside, that she can't find it. She can't tell if it's there. She can't tell if it was ever there, or if she dreamed that too, the window and the warmth and him.

She's out of bed before she's decided anything.

* * *

She takes the stairs two at a time. Barefoot. Hood half up. Sleeves over her hands.

The house is awake. She can hear it. Voices, a lot of them, from the kitchen. A clatter. Someone laughing, high and delighted: Umaru. The smell of something sweet and burning and something else sweet and not burning.

She doesn't hear his voice. She can't pick it out. Her heart is too loud.

She comes round the bottom of the stairs and through the kitchen door at a run and stops so hard she nearly falls over.

* * *

The kitchen is full of food.

Every surface. The long table, the counters, the windowsill, the top of the cold box. Bags and boxes and jars and paper parcels from the Shop, opened and half opened, stacked three deep. Flour. Sugar in four different kinds. Chocolate, so much chocolate. Fruit she's never seen in this world, piled in a bowl the size of a cart wheel. Cream. Eggs. Butter. Something that might be an entire wheel of cheese.

And desserts.

Everywhere. Cakes cooling on the windowsill. Tarts on the table. A tower of small round things on a plate that Rem is in the middle of piping something onto with tremendous concentration. A bowl of something pale and wobbling that Emilia is poking with a spoon and saying *is it meant to do that?* to nobody. A tray of something that has very clearly been on fire and is now, as Shuna holds it up to the light and looks at it, being judged.

“No,” says Shuna, and puts the tray on the reject pile. ˢʰᵘ

The reject pile is large.

Umaru is sitting cross-legged on top of the counter beside it with a fork, eating the rejects, with an expression of perfect happiness.

“That one was *fine*,” Umaru says through a mouthful. ᵁ

“It was on fire,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

“*Briefly.*” ᵁ

And in the middle of all of it, in front of the stove, with an apron on over his shirt and flour on his face and a whisk in one hand and a bowl of cream in the other, is Mau.

* * *

He sees her.

He turns round at the noise of her coming through the door, and his face does something, and he says, “Oh,” in a voice of pure dismay. ᴹ

She can't move.

“You're up,” he says. “You're—I thought you'd sleep later. You slept till ten yesterday.” ᴹ

He looks round at the kitchen, at the chaos, at Rem frozen mid-pipe and Emilia with her spoon in the wobbling thing and Umaru on the counter with her fork. Then back at her.

“It was a surprise,” he says. Helplessly. “I was going to—everything was going to be on the table, and then I was going to go up and wake you, and carry you down, and you'd open your eyes, and it'd all be here.” ᴹ

He holds up the whisk, as if it explains something.

“You've ruined it,” he says. “You've completely ruined it. You weren't supposed to see it yet.” ᴹ

Yuka stands in the doorway.

She looks at him. At the flour on his face. At the apron, which is Shuna's and too small for him. At the whisk.

At the kitchen full of food. At the cakes on the windowsill. At the tower of small round things that Rem is piping, which, she sees now, she knows. They're the ones from the island. The ones she ate too many of and talked about for days afterward. He must have remembered. He must have found them in the Shop, or worked out how to make them, or got Rem to.

All of it. For her. Before she woke up.

He was going to carry her down.

She doesn't say anything.

She can't. There's nothing in her that can make words. Everything in her is very busy doing something else, something enormous and warm and shaking, that started in her chest when she came through the door and saw him and is still going and doesn't seem to have any intention of stopping.

So she doesn't say anything.

She turns round and goes back upstairs.

* * *

Mau stands in the middle of the kitchen with the whisk.

Nobody moves.

Rem has stopped piping. Emilia has taken her spoon out of the wobbling thing. Umaru has stopped chewing. Shuna is holding a tray of something that hasn't been judged yet and isn't looking at it.

They all look at him.

He looks at the empty doorway.

“Was that—” says Emilia, very carefully. “Was that bad?” ᴱᵐ

He doesn't know. He genuinely doesn't know. He stands there with flour on his face and the most terrible lurch in his stomach, the old one, the one from the yard. *She saw it and she left. She turned round and she left.* He feels the door start to open, the one in him where he puts things, and something behind it start to stand up.

And then, from the top of the stairs, very loudly, in a voice that cracks in the middle:

“*I'm waking up in thirty.*” ʸ

A door slams. Not angrily. Just very fast.

Silence in the kitchen.

Mau stands very still.

Because he can feel her.

The line. Open since the night before last, open the way he threw it open in the clinic, all at once, like a window. And down it, from upstairs, from their room, from the bed she's apparently just thrown herself back into, he can feel her heart.

It's going a thousand miles an hour.

Not frightened. He knows what frightened feels like down that line. He felt it for a week. This isn't that. This is something so fast and so warm and so completely out of control that it's knocking against him like a bird against glass, and under it, very faintly, so faintly he almost misses it, he can hear her.

Laughing. Into a pillow. Or crying. Or both.

He puts the whisk down.

“No,” he says to Emilia. “I don't think it was bad.” ᴹ

* * *

Umaru looks at the clock on the wall.

“Thirty minutes,” she says. ᵁ

She jumps down off the counter. She looks round at the kitchen. At the chaos. At the tarts on the table and the cakes on the windowsill and the half-piped tower and the reject pile, which has some very good things in it.

“Thirty minutes,” Umaru says again, in an entirely different voice. The voice of a general. ᵁ

Everybody looks at her.

“You heard her,” says Umaru. “She's waking up in thirty. Which means in thirty minutes, she's coming down those stairs, *asleep*, and she's going to open her eyes, and everything has to be *on the table*. Everything. Rem. How long for the tower?” ᵁ

“Ten minutes,” says Rem, already piping again, very fast. ᴿᵉ

“Emilia, is that thing meant to wobble?” ᵁ

“I don't know!” ᴱᵐ

“Then it's meant to wobble. Put it in the middle. It's a feature. Shuna, judge faster.” ᵁ

“I judge at the speed of quality,” says Shuna, and judges faster. ˢʰᵘ

Umaru turns round to Mau.

“And *you*,” she says. ᵁ

He looks at her.

“Wash your face,” says Umaru. “Take off the apron. You're going to carry her down. You said so. You *promised*. In twenty-eight minutes you're going up those stairs.” ᵁ

He looks at her for a moment longer. At Umaru, who has been eating his rejects all morning in her dressing gown, standing on the kitchen floor with her fork pointed at him like a sword.

“Yes, sir,” he says. ᴹ

And Umaru grins so wide her whole face goes with it.

* * *

The kitchen goes at it like a battle.

He's seen this house get ready for many things. Wars. Gates. The night before the quarry, all of them round the table in the lamplight, saying nothing. He's never seen it like this. Rem piping so fast her hands blur. Emilia arranging fruit on a plate with enormous seriousness and then rearranging it. Shuna sending things to the reject pile with a single word and then quietly rescuing two of them when nobody's looking. Umaru running between them like a general between regiments, issuing orders through a mouthful of the reject pile.

Other people start to appear.

Milim, drawn by the noise, who is immediately put in charge of carrying things and drops only one. Fern, who takes one look and starts setting the table without being asked. Stark, who is handed a bowl of cream and a whisk and told *don't stop until it's stiff* and is still whisking twenty minutes later with a look of grim endurance. Ori, in her nightdress and boots, who walks in, assesses the situation in one sweep, and takes charge of the napkins.

Frieren, who comes in, looks at everything, says “Oh,” very softly, and sits down at the end of the table to watch. ᶠ

Mau washes his face.

He takes off the apron.

He stands at the bottom of the stairs, with the whole kitchen behind him going at it, and feels her heart down the line, still going, a little slower now, but still fast, still warm, still knocking.

*Twenty-two minutes*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*You're smiling.* ᶜ

He is. He didn't notice. He puts his hand up to his face and finds it there, all the way across, and leaves it.

*Yes*, he says. ᴹ

*Good*, says Ciel. *Keep doing that. Twenty-one.* ᶜ
