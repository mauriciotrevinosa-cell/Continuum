# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 18 — Under Kamish's Tree

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S13E127
**Continuity state:** B+442 ("45"). Mau keeps the "later" from the day he came back: he goes and lies under Kamish's tree in the orchard and lets Ori climb on the dragon and tell him he was good. Yuka comes with the carved animals from town for the Fort. Ori is still trying to teach Kamish to sit. Mau tells Ori what she said in her sleep, the Fort reserves and the pickles, and she is mortified. Under it is a real fear: that if she wanted too much, the family would break. Mau tells her that nothing she wants breaks them; the family nearly broke from the grown-ups not saying what they wanted. Ori decides to keep the Fort reserves. Kamish sits, once, by accident.

*When: B+442. "45." Ori has drawn a dragon beside it. The dragon is sitting. It is aspirational.*

Kamish is lying under his tree when they come.

He always lies there in the afternoons. Not under it; nothing that size could be under anything. Kamish lies along the whole bottom of the orchard and out over the wall into the field beyond, longer than the house, a ridge of dark scales with the grass grown up against it, and the big old pear with the low wide branches is where he puts his head. His jaw rests on the grass beside its trunk. The tree barely comes up to his brow. His eyes, each one as big as a cartwheel, are half shut. His tail is somewhere out past the far hedge. Most of the house has stopped noticing him there. You walk round a dragon the way you'd walk round a hill.

Ori hasn't stopped noticing.

She's standing in front of his nose with her hands on her hips and her explorer hat on, and Beru is standing behind her with his arms folded in an exact imitation of her, and she is saying, very firmly, for what Ciel tells Mau is the fortieth time today:

“*Sit.*” ᴼ

Kamish opens one eye.

He looks at her for a long moment.

Then he closes it again.

Ori turns round to Beru with an expression of tremendous patience being tried.

“He understands,” she tells Beru. “He's just choosing not to.” ᴼ

Beru nods gravely.

* * *

Mau sits down on the grass beside Kamish's head.

He doesn't say anything at first. He just puts his hand flat on the side of the dragon's great face, on the scales below the eye, and leaves it there. Kamish's eye opens again. It rolls round to look at him.

Something goes through the dragon. Mau feels it the way he feels all of them, through the line that runs from him down into the shadow where Kamish folds himself when he isn't lying under trees. Warm. Pleased. Very slightly smug.

“You were good,” Mau tells him. ᴹ

The eye blinks. Slowly.

“*I know*,” says Kamish. His voice comes up out of the ground under them more than out of his mouth; it always has. ᴷ

“In the basin,” says Mau. “The fire. You came down out of the sky and you didn't even land. You didn't miss one. You were very, very good.” ᴹ

Kamish lets out a breath. A long one, through his nose, warm, that ruffles the grass all the way to the tree trunk and lifts Ori's hat brim. It sounds exactly like a cat purring, if a cat were the size of a hill.

Ori has stopped telling him to sit. She's watching.

“Was he?” she says. “Really?” ᴼ

“Really.” ᴹ

“Better than Beru?” ᴼ

Behind her, Beru goes very still.

Mau looks at Beru. Beru looks at Mau.

“Different,” says Mau carefully. “Beru stayed with you. That was the most important job. Kamish had the second most important job.” ᴹ

Beru unfolds his arms, slowly, with enormous dignity. Ori pats his leg.

“You had the most important job,” she tells him. ᴼ

Beru makes a sound that Mau has only ever heard him make about three times. Ori takes it as agreement.

* * *

Ori climbs up onto Kamish's head.

She doesn't ask. She's never asked. She goes up the side of his face the way she'd go up a familiar wall, one boot on his jaw, one on the ridge above his eye, and sits down between his horns with her legs on either side of his head and her hands on the scales.

Kamish doesn't move. He's used to this.

“You were good,” Ori tells him, leaning forward over the front of his head so she can see his eye upside down. “Dad says. So you were. That's official.” ᴼ

Kamish blinks at her.

“Now *sit*,” says Ori. ᴼ

He doesn't.

* * *

Yuka comes down the orchard in the middle of the afternoon with the basket.

She's in the hoodie again. She sits down on the grass on Mau's other side, close, with her shoulder against his and her legs crossed, and puts the basket in her lap.

“Fort members,” she announces. “Assemble.” ʸ

Ori is off Kamish's head before the word's finished. She stands to attention in front of the basket. Beru stands to attention behind her. Kamish opens both eyes.

Yuka takes the carved animals out one at a time.

There's one for everybody in the Fort. Mau found them yesterday at the stall, with her standing over him, and he chose each one himself, by shape and weight, because he couldn't see the paint. A small owl for Umaru, because she stays up all night. A rabbit for Emilia, because it looks surprised. A cat for Milim, because she'll pretend to be insulted and keep it in her pocket for ever. A fox for Rem. And for Ori, a dragon. A small one. Sitting.

Ori takes it in both hands.

She looks at the little carved dragon, sitting very upright with its tail round its feet. She looks at Kamish, lying under his tree, not sitting.

She looks at Mau.

“Did you pick this,” she says. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Because of the sitting.” ᴼ

“Ciel told me how it was going,” he admits. ᴹ

Ori looks at the little dragon for a long time.

Then she holds it up in front of Kamish's nose.

“*This*,” she says, “is what it looks like.” ᴼ

* * *

Afterwards, when the animals have been distributed into Ori's satchel for delivery to the Fort and Beru has been sent to stand guard over the satchel and Kamish has gone back to sleep, Ori lies down on the grass between Mau and Yuka with her head on Mau's leg and her boots on Yuka's lap.

She's quiet for a while. Looking up at the pear leaves.

Mau thinks about the clinic. About her face in his shirt in the dark. About *I'll eat less of the Fort reserves. I promise.*

“Ori,” he says. ᴹ

“Mm.” ᴼ

“You talked in your sleep. In the clinic. The first night.” ᴹ

Ori goes very still.

“No I didn't,” she says. ᴼ

“You said you'd eat less of the Fort reserves.” ᴹ

Ori goes from still to rigid.

“And no more pickles,” he says. ᴹ

Ori puts both hands over her face.

“I *didn't*,” she says, through her fingers. ᴼ

“You did,” says Yuka gently. “I heard about it. Shoko told me. It's all right.” ʸ

“It's *not* all right,” says Ori. “That's *private*. That's *Fort business*. You're not supposed to *hear* Fort business when you're *asleep*.” ᴼ

Mau doesn't laugh. He wants to. He puts his hand on her head instead, on her hair, and leaves it there, the way he left it on Kamish.

“Why did you say it?” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Ori doesn't answer for a long time.

She keeps her hands over her face. Mau keeps his hand on her hair. Yuka holds her boots.

“Because,” Ori says at last. Muffled. ᴼ

They wait.

“Because I thought maybe I wanted too many things,” Ori says. “The expedition. The matching clothes. The Fort. The pickles. The reserves. Everyone. I want everyone. All the time. And then you and Yuka—” ᴼ

She stops.

“And then you weren't talking,” she says. “And Yuka was in my room. And you were in the clinic. And I thought—maybe it was because there was too much wanting. In the house. And if I wanted less. If I gave some back. Then maybe there'd be enough.” ᴼ

Her voice goes very small on the last word.

Yuka has gone very still.

Mau takes his hand off Ori's hair. He takes her wrists, gently, and moves her hands off her face, so he can see it. Her eyes are wet. She's very angry about it.

“Ori,” he says. “Listen to me. This is the real one.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“Nothing you want breaks us,” he says. “Nothing. Not the expedition, or the clothes, or the Fort, or the pickles. You could want the whole world. It wouldn't break anything.” ᴹ

He makes himself say the rest.

“What nearly broke us,” he says, “was the grown-ups not saying what *they* wanted. Me not saying. Yuka not saying. Both of us deciding for the other one, and not asking. That was all it was. It was never you. It was never too much wanting. It was too little saying.” ᴹ

Beside him, he feels Yuka's breath catch. He feels it down the line too.

Ori looks at him for a long time with Frieren's eyes.

“That's the real one,” she says slowly. ᴼ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“So I can keep the reserves.” ᴼ

“You can keep the reserves.” ᴹ

“And the pickles.” ᴼ

“Yuka eats the pickles,” says Mau. “You'll have to fight her for them.” ᴹ

Ori looks at Yuka. Yuka, with her eyes very bright, looks back.

“I'll fight you,” says Yuka. “Every meal.” ʸ

Ori considers this.

“Good,” she says, with deep satisfaction, and puts her head back down on Mau's leg. ᴼ

Yuka sits back on her hands. One of Ori's buckles is pressed into the soft part of her palm. Mau moves the boot a little and slips the folded napkin from the basket under it.

“Luxury,” says Yuka. ʸ

“I can do better than that.” ᴹ

He takes a twist of paper from his coat, the extra sweets from town. Her eyes go straight to it.

“That's mine,” she says. ʸ

“I know whose it is.” ᴹ

She leans across Ori just far enough to kiss him. He brings the sweet to her mouth after, once she's stopped smiling enough to take it. When Ori raises her nose at the crackle of the paper, Mau puts the bag where all three of them can reach.

“Those are the sour ones,” Yuka warns her. ʸ

Ori takes two.

* * *

Kamish sits.

None of them see it happen. They're lying on the grass under the pear tree, the three of them, with the leaves moving and the afternoon going warm round the edges for Yuka and grey for Mau and whatever it goes for Ori, not talking, and somewhere behind them there's a great slow shifting sound, like a hill rearranging itself.

When Ori sits up and looks round, Kamish is sitting.

Upright. Tail round his feet. Exactly like the little carved dragon. Taller than the house; taller than the old pear by three times; his head up where the birds are, blotting out half the evening, his shadow falling over the whole orchard. Looking down at the three of them from that enormous height with an expression of profound and patient dignity.

Ori stares at him.

Then she screams. A full, high, delighted scream, the kind that brings people running from the house.

Kamish, startled, lies back down at once.

Nobody else sees it. Umaru arrives at a run and Milim arrives at a faster run and Beru arrives with the satchel, and they all want to know what happened, and Ori tells them, at length, with gestures, and nobody believes her.

Mau believes her. He felt it. Down the line, warm and pleased and very slightly smug.

*You did that on purpose*, he tells Kamish. ᴹ

The eye opens. Looks at him.

“*She asked forty times*,” says Kamish. “*It seemed rude not to.*” ᴷ

The eye closes.
