## Chapter 20 — Not Watching the Stairs

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84

“Again,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

He looks at the tray.

It isn't on fire. That's progress. It's the second attempt and it isn't on fire. It's merely a little dark at one corner and a little pale at the other and has sunk, very slightly, in the middle, like a tent with a broken pole.

“It's not burnt,” he points out. ᴹ

“It isn't anything,” says Shuna. “It's a tray of good intentions. Again.” ˢʰᵘ

From the counter, where she has been sitting cross-legged since breakfast with a bowl of something in her lap, Yuka laughs so hard she has to put the bowl down.

* * *

It's the tray from the morning of the feast.

The one Shuna judged first, before anything else, and sent to the reject pile with a single word. *No.* The one that was on fire, not because he burned it, Shuna told him afterwards, but because he was watching the stairs.

*I'll teach you that one properly*, she said. *Another day. When you're not watching the stairs.*

He thought she'd forgotten. Shuna doesn't forget things. She came and found him after breakfast this morning with an apron in her hands, his own this time, not hers, cut for his size and folded neatly, and said, “Today,” and that was that.

Yuka followed them into the kitchen.

She didn't ask if she could come. She just came, and climbed onto the counter by the window, and arranged herself there with a cushion she'd brought from the common room, and announced that she was *supervising*.

“Supervising what?” Shuna asked. ˢʰᵘ

“Him,” said Yuka. ʸ

Shuna looked at her for a long moment. Then she nodded, as if this were a perfectly reasonable role in a kitchen, and handed her a bowl of shelled nuts.

“Supervisors,” said Shuna, “don't touch the batter.” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka has been touching the batter all morning.

* * *

It's not a difficult tray. That's what's so humiliating.

It's a sort of flat cake. Layered. Something with fruit in the middle and something crisp on top, baked in a wide shallow tin. Shuna makes it in her sleep. He has watched her make it a hundred times without paying attention.

It turns out paying attention is the whole thing.

“You're thinking about something else,” Shuna says, halfway through the third attempt. ˢʰᵘ

“I'm not.” ᴹ

“You are. Your hands are slow. When you think about something else, your hands go slow.” ˢʰᵘ

He looks at his hands. They're in the batter. They're slow.

He was thinking about Ori, in the orchard, with her face in her hands. *Maybe I wanted too many things.*

He makes his hands go faster.

“Better,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

* * *

He gets flour on his face.

He doesn't notice. He reaches up to push his hair out of his eyes with the back of his wrist, the way he's seen Shuna do it a thousand times, and leaves a long streak of flour across his cheekbone.

Yuka notices.

She slides down off the counter. She comes over, barefoot on the kitchen floor in the hoodie, and stands in front of him, and looks at the flour on his face very seriously, the way a doctor looks at a symptom.

Then she reaches up and wipes it off with her thumb.

“Supervisors,” says Shuna, without looking round from the stove, “don't touch the cook.” ˢʰᵘ

“I was removing a hazard,” says Yuka, climbing back onto the counter. ʸ

Shuna doesn't answer. But Mau sees the side of her face, turned toward the stove, and the corner of her mouth.

He gets flour on his face four more times before lunch.

He's fairly sure at least two of them aren't accidents.

* * *

Rem and Umaru come in halfway through the third tray.

They're in the middle of an argument about something; Mau doesn't catch what. Umaru is walking backwards in front of Rem, explaining something with both hands, very fast, in her dressing gown, and Rem is following with an armful of washing and an expression of deep patience.

Umaru sees the tray in the oven and stops dead.

“Is that the fire one?” she says. ᵁ

“It isn't on fire,” says Mau. ᴹ

“*Yet*,” says Yuka, from the counter. ʸ

Umaru comes over and presses her nose to the oven door. Rem puts down the washing and comes to stand beside her, to look too.

And Mau, with nothing to do for a moment but wait for the tray, looks at them.

He's seen them together a thousand times. Every day. At meals, at the Fort, in the yard. Umaru dragging Rem into things. Rem following, sighing, smiling. He's never really *looked*.

He looks now.

Umaru is talking. About the tray, about the fire, about the reject pile and how it was the best part of the feast. Talking with her whole body the way she does, her hands going, her face going.

And Rem isn't looking at the tray.

She's looking at Umaru.

Not the way you look at someone who's talking. Not patient. Not tired. Rem is looking at Umaru the way Yuka, on the counter, is looking at him. Like something she can't quite believe she gets to have. Like something she's afraid to put down.

And Umaru doesn't see it. Umaru is looking at the tray.

Mau looks away.

He doesn't say anything. It isn't his. But he remembers what Rem said, the day after Subaru came, with Mau sitting beside him. *I love Umaru. I'm happy.* Said to Subaru. Said in front of Mau. Never to her.

*She hasn't told her*, says Ciel quietly. ᶜ

*No.* ᴹ

*She'll come to you first. She always does.* ᶜ

*I know*, says Mau. *When she does, I'll help.* ᴹ

* * *

The third tray comes out right.

He knows before Shuna says anything. He knows it when he opens the oven and the smell comes out, which is the smell of the trays Shuna makes and not the smell of the trays he makes. Even. Risen. The top crisp, the middle soft, the fruit just starting to come through at the edges.

He puts it on the board.

Shuna comes and looks at it. She looks at it for a long time. She cuts a corner off with her knife and tastes it.

“Yes,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

From the counter, Yuka bursts into applause. Umaru joins in. Rem, after a moment, smiling, joins in too.

Shuna cuts the tray into pieces. She gives the first one to Yuka, for supervising. The second to Umaru, for being there. The third to Rem. The fourth she holds out to Mau.

He takes it.

She doesn't let go of it straight away.

“You know what the house looks like,” she says, “from the stove?” ˢʰᵘ

He looks at her.

“Everyone's back,” says Shuna. “All day. All the time. That's what I see. Everyone's back, going in and out of that door. Going to the gate. Going to the quarry. Going north. And I stand here and make the food, and keep it warm, and wait to see whose back comes in again.” ˢʰᵘ

She lets go of the piece of tray.

“That week,” she says, “I made your plate every night. And every night I put it on the table. And every night you didn't come in. And I put it under a cloth and left it, because the plate's how I say it. That's all I've got. The plate.” ˢʰᵘ

He doesn't say anything. He can't.

“Today you came in the front,” says Shuna. “And stood at my stove. And burned two trays. And I got to see your face the whole time instead of your back.” ˢʰᵘ

She turns back to the stove.

“Lesson two is next week,” she says. “Bring the supervisor.” ˢʰᵘ

* * *

He eats the piece of tray.

It's very good. It's the best thing he's ever made.

On the counter, Yuka has finished hers and is looking at him over the empty plate with her chin in her hands and flour on her own nose, which he didn't put there and which she doesn't know about.

He goes over.

He wipes it off with his thumb.

She looks at him.

He doesn't say anything about being a supervisor, or a hazard, or the cook. He just leaves his thumb there, on the side of her face, for a moment longer than he needs to.

Behind them, very quietly, Umaru says, “*Oh*,” and Rem puts a hand over Umaru's mouth, and Shuna, at the stove, doesn't turn round.
