# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 28 — Beru's Cry

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 28
**Source episode:** S13E130
**Continuity state:** B+449 ("38"). Ori's point of view. Ori and Beru come back from her first run through the Fort's new door in the upgraded house gate (V84 #26–27), checked by Frieren. On the path through the orchard, Cross is waiting. He wants to explain; he says he wants Ori to understand him. Ori tells him plainly: he nearly broke her family, and she doesn't want him near her. When he steps closer anyway, Beru uses his cry, the Solo Leveling one that roots people where they stand. Cross can't move. Beru picks Ori up and carries her to find Mau. Mau doesn't walk to the river this time. He listens to all of it, thanks Beru, and goes to find Maru instead, and asks him, very calmly, to keep his brother away from his daughter. Maru says he will.

*When: B+449. "38." Ori wrote it herself, very large, and drew Beru beside it with his mouth open and lines coming out of it.*

The middle door is Ori's.

It's new. Since yesterday. Since Dad went for a walk after supper and came back an hour later having had tea with the Rulers, which Ori still doesn't entirely believe, and the house gate had three doors instead of one.

The first door is the one there always was: Dad's, the weekly one, the one he can tell what he wants. The third door is for the grown-ups who want to *scratch an itch*, Gojo says, and Shuna is already making a list.

And the middle one is for the Fort.

Safe for children. Safe for people who don't fight. It reads who comes in and won't make itself harder than they can finish, and it can't turn Red, even if Dad's there. If he wants something harder he has the door on the right. The gate said it couldn't give much in the way of treasure, so Dad said it could take its treasure out of his Shop, and now it can.

Frieren checked it first anyway. She went through it before lunch, alone, with her staff, and came out and said, *That's fine. Ori can go in. With Beru. Not past the second door. Yet.*

Ori has never gone past the second door. She's only been in once.

She keeps count anyway, in her notebook, on a special page, with a map of every room. The first room has slimes, the small slow kind that Beru can step on. The second room has bats. The third room is behind the second door and she has never seen it and she has a drawing of what she thinks is in it, which is a dragon, which is probably wrong.

Today she killed four slimes and a bat. The bat was mostly Beru. She's counting it anyway.

She comes out of the house gate and round the back of the house into the orchard with her explorer hat on and her wooden sword through her belt and Beru behind her, and she's telling him about the bat, how it went, how she'd have got it if it hadn't flown at her face, when she sees who's on the path.

* * *

It's Cross.

He's standing by the old pear tree. Kamish's tree. Kamish isn't there; he's folded away in Dad's shadow today. Cross is just standing there by himself with his hands in his pockets, looking at the cellar door, like he was waiting for it to open.

Ori stops.

Beru stops behind her.

She knows who Cross is. Everybody knows. He's the one from the gate who said *you're alive* to Yuka. The one who sat on the orchard wall with her every day for two weeks. The one Yuka called the wheel on. The one Dad walked across the yard toward with the grass going flat, until Yuka stopped him with toast.

Ori was at the kitchen window for that. She saw the grass.

“Hi, Ori,” says Cross. ᶜʳ

He smiles. It's a nice smile. It's the smile grown-ups use on children when they want something.

Ori doesn't smile back.

* * *

“I wanted to talk to you,” Cross says. “If that's all right. I know—I know you probably don't think much of me right now. And I don't blame you. But I'd like to explain. I'd like you to understand.” ᶜʳ

He takes his hands out of his pockets. He crouches down, a little way off, so his face is level with hers. Grown-ups do that too. Ori has noticed. They do it when they want you to think they're the same size as you.

“Your—Mau,” says Cross. “Your dad. He doesn't want me to talk to him. I understand. And Yuka's—Yuka's being kind, but she doesn't really want me near either. And I thought, you're the one who keeps the Fort. You're the one everyone listens to. If *you* understood—” ᶜʳ

Ori looks at him.

She thinks about the clinic. About the dark. About her face in Dad's shirt and saying things in her sleep about the reserves and the pickles. About lying in the orchard under Kamish's tree with her hands over her face. *Maybe I wanted too many things.*

She thinks about Yuka in her room, every night for a week. Not in Dad's room. In hers. Crying in her sleep with her face turned to the wall, when she thought Ori was asleep, and Ori wasn't.

“No,” says Ori. ᴼ

* * *

Cross blinks.

“I don't want to understand,” says Ori. ᴼ

Her voice comes out very clear. Clearer than she expected. The voice she uses for Fort business, when it's important, when she's standing on a crate and everyone has to listen.

“You nearly broke my family,” she says. ᴼ

Cross's face does something.

“You sat on the wall with Yuka every day,” says Ori, “and said things to her, and she went to my room instead of Dad's, and Dad went to the clinic, and nobody was talking, and I thought it was because there was too much wanting, so I said I'd give up the reserves. In my *sleep*.” ᴼ

Her voice wobbles on the last word. She doesn't let it.

“And it wasn't too much wanting,” she says. “It was you.” ᴼ

She lifts her chin, the way Beatrice does.

“So I don't want to understand you,” she says. “I don't want you to explain. I don't want you near me. Or near the Fort. Or near Dad, or Yuka. That's all. That's the real one.” ᴼ

* * *

Cross is very still.

His face isn't nice anymore. It isn't angry either. It's something else, something she doesn't have a word for, something that goes very flat and very careful all at once, like a pond when the wind stops.

“Ori,” he says. ᶜʳ

He stands up.

He takes a step toward her.

Only one. Not fast. His hands are open. He's still smiling a little.

But she just told him not to come near her, and he heard it, she watched him hear it, and he's coming anyway.

Behind her, Beru opens his mouth.

* * *

Ori has never heard Beru do it.

She's heard *about* it. Jin-Woo told her, once, when she asked why everyone in his old world was so afraid of an ant. *He has a cry*, Jin-Woo said. *It isn't a roar. It goes through you. Things that hear it stop.*

She didn't really understand. She does now.

It isn't loud. It's worse than loud. It comes out of Beru and goes *through* you, not into your ears but into the back of your neck, into your spine, into the part of you that's still an animal and knows when something much bigger has decided you're not going to move.

That's what happens to Cross.

* * *

Beru's cry goes through the orchard.

It goes through the pear leaves and the grass and the stones of the path, and every bird in the orchard goes silent at once, and somewhere far off in the house Ori hears something drop.

Cross stops.

Mid-step. One foot lifted. His hands still open at his sides. His mouth still half open on her name.

He doesn't move.

He can't. She can see him trying. She can see it in his eyes, the only part of him still moving, going wide, going down to his own feet, up to Beru. His whole body locked in place like someone has poured something round it and let it set.

Beru doesn't attack.

He doesn't need to. He just stands there behind Ori, enormous, with his wings half open and his head lowered, looking at Cross with those eyes, and lets the cry finish.

Then he reaches down and picks Ori up.

* * *

He doesn't ask. Beru doesn't ask, when it matters. He scoops her up in one arm, the way Dad scoops her off Kamish, and settles her against his chest, and turns, and walks away down the orchard path toward the house.

Not running. Walking. Slowly. With his back to Cross the whole way.

Ori looks back over his shoulder.

Cross is still standing under the pear tree. Frozen. Foot still lifted.

He'll be able to move soon. Jin-Woo said that too. It doesn't last.

But it's long enough.

She turns round and puts her face against Beru's chest plate, which is hard and cool and smells of the gate, and holds on to the edge of it with both hands.

“Thank you,” she says. ᴼ

Beru makes the sound he made when Dad said he had the most important job.

“I know,” says Ori. “You did.” ᴼ

* * *

Dad is in the kitchen.

He's sitting at the table with Yuka and a cup of tea, and he's already standing up when Beru comes through the back door with Ori in his arm. He was already standing up before they came in. Ori can tell. He felt it. He always feels it.

His face is very quiet.

Beru puts her down. Ori stands in front of Dad in her hat with her wooden sword through her belt and tells him.

All of it. Properly. Like a report. The gate. The slimes and the bat. The way round the back of the house. Cross under the tree. *I'd like you to understand.* What she said back. Him standing up. Him stepping forward after she'd told him not to. Beru's cry. The birds going silent. Cross frozen with his foot in the air.

Dad listens to all of it.

He doesn't move. He doesn't interrupt. Yuka, beside him, has gone very still, with both hands round her cup.

When Ori finishes, Dad is quiet for a long moment.

Ori waits for it. The thing from yesterday. The grass going flat. The air bending. She's ready to grab his sleeve. She's ready to stand in front of him like Yuka did. She hasn't got toast but she's got her sword.

It doesn't come.

* * *

Dad crouches down.

Not the way Cross crouched. Not to look the same size. He just gets down so he's close, and looks at her face, carefully, all over, the way Shoko looks at people. Checking.

“Did he touch you?” he says. ᴹ

“No.” ᴼ

“Did he scare you?” ᴹ

Ori thinks about it. Properly. The real answer.

“A little,” she says. “When he stood up. When he stepped. Not before.” ᴼ

Dad nods slowly.

“You said the real one to him,” he says. “Even though you were scared.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴼ

“That's very brave.” ᴹ

“I know,” says Ori. ᴼ

Dad almost smiles. Then he stands up and looks at Beru.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

Beru inclines his head.

* * *

Dad doesn't go to the river.

He doesn't go to the orchard either. He goes out of the kitchen door, past the training posts, past the split one from yesterday that Mai hasn't fixed yet, across the yard to the long room the Simurians share, at the end of the west wing.

Ori follows. Nobody tells her not to.

Maru is in the doorway. He's heard. Everyone in the house heard the cry. He's standing there very still with his hands at his sides, the way his people stand, and when Dad comes across the yard toward him his face goes tight, like he's ready for something bad.

Dad stops in front of him.

“Your brother,” says Dad. ᴹ

His voice is very calm. Ori has heard Dad angry. This isn't it. This is worse. This is the voice he used in the yard to Yoru.

“He went to my daughter,” says Dad. “After I told him not to come to me. He went to my *daughter*. And when she told him to stay away, he stepped toward her anyway.” ᴹ

Maru closes his eyes.

“I'm not going to do anything to him,” says Dad. “I'm telling you, not him, because he isn't to speak to me. And I'm asking you. Keep him away from her. Away from the Fort. Away from Ori, and from anything Ori loves.” ᴹ

He waits.

“Can you do that?” ᴹ

Maru opens his eyes.

“Yes,” he says. “I'm sorry. I'll keep him away. I promise.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Dad looks at him for a moment longer. Then he nods.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

And he turns round, and sees Ori standing behind him in the yard with her hat and her sword, and holds out his hand.

She takes it.

They walk back to the kitchen together.

* * *

That night, in her notebook, on the special page with this week's map of the gate, Ori draws a new thing.

Not in the gate. In the orchard. Under the pear tree. A stick figure, standing on one leg, frozen. With little lines round it to show it can't move.

And beside it, very large, a Beru. With his mouth open.

Under it, in her best handwriting, she writes:

*MOST IMPORTANT JOB.*

She shows it to Beru before she goes to sleep.

Beru looks at it for a long time.

Then he makes the sound.
