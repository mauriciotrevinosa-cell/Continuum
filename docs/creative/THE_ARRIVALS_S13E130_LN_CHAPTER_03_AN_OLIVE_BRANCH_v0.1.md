# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 24 — An Olive Branch

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 24
**Source episode:** S13E130

*When: B+446. Evening, after supper. The notebook says "41". Under it, in Rimuru's hand, very neat and very cross: "Mau: missing 1h 04m. Gate."*

He needs to walk.

That's all it is. He's been calm all day. Calm in Mai's workshop this morning, with Cross in the doorway asking to be forgiven. Calm telling him no. Calm watching him say *all right* and *thank you* and walk away across the yard without looking back once.

But under the calm, something has been going round all day. *Too easy.* Round and round, like a stone in a stream that won't settle.

So after supper he tells Yuka he's going to walk for a bit. Not far. Round the grounds. Just to let it settle. She looks at him, checks his face, nods, and squeezes his hand.

“Come back,” she says. ʸ

“I'll come back,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't decide where to go.

He walks out past the training posts and round the back of the house and along the edge of the orchard, where Kamish lies with his head at the old pear, and Kamish opens one eye and watches him pass and doesn't say anything. Past the workshop, dark. Past the Fort, dark too, with Ori's flag hanging limp from the roof in the dusk.

And then he's standing in front of the house gate.

He hadn't meant to come here. His feet just did it. Maybe because it's the one place on the grounds that is *his*, in a way: DIO's gift, the gate that changes every week, the one he can tell what he wants.

The board beside it shows this week's sketch. The ordinary kind. Halls, a river, cores for Mai at the bottom.

He stands looking at it. The stone in the stream goes round.

Then the board changes.

* * *

> **[Gate Authority: household weekly gate.]**
>
> **[Upgrade available.]**

He stares at it.

He's seen the gate's messages a hundred times. *Adjustment accepted. Weekly rotation remains active. Operator present: exit available at will.* He's never seen this one.

An upgrade.

He knows what that usually takes. Everybody does. Gates upgrade when something breaks them open. A Purple Gate. A Red Gate. Something that takes the walls down and puts them back stronger, and usually takes somebody's skin with it.

Nothing's broken. Nobody's been in this week but him, on Thursday, the ordinary way.

> **[Upgrade available. No clearance required.]**

*Ciel*, he says, inside. ᴹ

*I see it*, says Ciel. *I don't know what it is. It isn't any pattern I know.* ᶜ

*Noise?* ᴹ

Noise, at the table inside, is very still.

*Careful*, says Noise. ᴺ

Mau looks at the message. *No clearance required.* He's curious. He can't help it; he's always been curious, it's half of what the eyes are. What kind of upgrade comes without a fight? What does it give?

He lets the eyes go to it. Just to see. Just to read the reward, the way you'd read a label.

The gate takes him in.

* * *

He's standing in a room.

He knows at once that something's very wrong, and also that nothing is. The wrongness is in where he is: not the gate's usual idea of a place, slimes and stone and flat light, but somewhere else entirely, somewhere it would take a great deal to reach. And the nothing is in what's there.

A table. Chairs round it. A pot of tea, steaming. Cups.

And at the table, waiting for him, the Rulers.

* * *

Every part of him goes to war at once.

He can feel it happen. The Infinity coming up on its own. The shadow at his feet going deep and dark, thousands of shapes in it turning toward the room at once. Bellion's attention snapping onto him from the house, Igris's from Yuka's shadow, Kamish's eye opening at the bottom of the orchard. Noise standing up from the table inside. Makima, wherever she is, going very still.

The last time he saw them, they had turned a weekly gate into a Red Gate with him and Yuka inside it, and sealed it, and watched.

He doesn't move.

Neither do they.

“Sit down,” says one of them. Mildly. ᴿᵘ

He doesn't sit down.

“You're not trapped,” says another. “The way out is behind you. It's been there since you came in. Look.” ᴿᵘ

He looks. It is. An ordinary door, open, and through it, the dusk and the house gate's board and the back of the Fort.

“We'd just like to talk,” says the first. “For an hour, if you have one. There's tea.” ᴿᵘ

* * *

He sits down in the end.

Not because he trusts them. He doesn't. But the door stays open the whole time, he checks it every few minutes, and nobody at the table moves toward it, and nobody reaches for anything but the teapot.

Someone pours him a cup.

He doesn't drink it.

“You kept your word,” says the one at the head of the table. ᴿᵘ

He waits.

“The agreement,” they say. “About her. About DIO. That she would not try to take back what was hers, and that you would see she didn't.” ᴿᵘ

“She hasn't,” says Mau. ᴹ

“No,” they agree. “She hasn't. Not once. Not even a little.” ᴿᵘ

A pause.

“You've let her walk outside your protection,” says another. “To a garden. To a queen. More than once now.” ᴿᵘ

Mau goes very still.

“We saw,” they say. “Of course we saw. We see everything that leaves your grounds.” ᴿᵘ

“And?” ᴹ

“And we didn't intervene,” they say. “We watched her. Carefully. The first time, we were ready to. Then we watched her carry a pot into a garden and ask a queen where it would get enough light, and we understood she was there to see her. She was in love. People in love aren't usually plotting to take back the order of the world.” ᴿᵘ

Someone at the table laughs, very quietly.

“So this,” says the one at the head of the table, “is a thank you.” ᴿᵘ

* * *

He doesn't know what to say to that.

“The gate,” they say. “The upgrade. It's ours. A gift. Not a Purple Gate. Not a Red Gate. Nothing broken, nothing to clear, nobody hurt. You kept faith with us, and we'd like to keep faith with you.” ᴿᵘ

They pause.

“Call it an olive branch,” they say. “Between you and us. We haven't always been kind to you. We know that.” ᴿᵘ

Mau looks at them for a long time.

*They're not lying*, says Ciel, inside, very carefully. *I can't read everything here. But what I can read isn't lying.* ᶜ

*Noise?* ᴹ

A long pause.

*No*, says Noise. *Not lying. Still careful.* ᴺ

Mau picks up the cup.

He drinks the tea.

* * *

They talk for an hour.

It's the strangest hour of his life, and he's had some strange ones.

They don't ask him for anything. Not power. Not information. Not his eyes or his shadow or the wheel or anything he can do. He keeps waiting for it. It never comes.

They ask about DIO.

How she is. Whether she's happy. What the plant is like. Whether the queen is good to her. They listen to the answers. One of them, when he describes DIO and Elmesia walking all the way back across the lawn because DIO changed her mind about where she'd put the pot, puts a hand over their mouth.

They ask about Ori.

How she is. What she's mapping. Whether she's still afraid of the dark. He tells them about the Fort, and the reserves, and the pickles, and Kamish sitting. They listen to all of it. They ask good questions. They ask what the Fort oath is. He tells them. *Even against Kamish.* Someone at the table laughs out loud.

They ask about Yuka.

How she is. How *he* is. How it's been, these last weeks. They don't say *we know what happened*; they don't need to. They just ask how he feels.

He tells them. Not all of it. Some. The clinic. The plate. *Don't steal my apology.* He doesn't know why he tells them. It's like the witches' tea, he thinks, halfway through. That same strange thing: people far older than him, far stranger, sitting round a table asking how his family is, and meaning it.

* * *

Near the end, the one at the head of the table puts down their cup.

“There's one more thing,” they say. ᴿᵘ

He waits.

“The door,” they say. “In you. The one you put things behind.” ᴿᵘ

He goes very still.

“We could help you keep it closed,” they say. “If you wanted. Not take anything out. Not change anything. Just—help it stay shut. So that when things get hard, what's behind it can't come out and sit down and tell you what's happening. So you don't have to go back to that room.” ᴿᵘ

They look at him.

“It's an offer,” they say. “Not a condition. You can say no.” ᴿᵘ

* * *

He thinks about it for a long time.

He thinks about the room. The one he goes back to without meaning to. The inn. The yard. The cup on the windowsill in the other life. The Trial. A year of practising goodbye. Two weeks in a clinic, getting out of the way.

He thinks about how easy it would be. To have someone else hold it shut. To never go back there again.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

They wait.

“Thank you,” he says. “Really. I mean it. But no.” ᴹ

He puts his cup down.

“I want to get past it myself,” he says. “It's mine. I want to be the one who learns not to go back. Not because someone locked the door. Because I didn't open it.” ᴹ

He looks round the table at them.

“And I've learned,” he says, “the very hard way, what happens when somebody changes something inside me. Even somebody trying to help. Even me. Every time something in me got altered, something else broke that nobody saw coming. I'd rather keep my own doors. Even the bad ones. And have people around me who'll tell me when I'm standing at one.” ᴹ

* * *

There's a silence round the table.

Then the one at the head of it laughs.

Not unkindly. Delighted. And the others laugh too, all round the table, quietly, the way old people laugh at something young that has just said something true.

“That,” says the one at the head of the table, “is why you have a wife.” ᴿᵘ

Mau looks at them.

Then he laughs too. He can't help it.

“Good point,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

When he stands up to go, the one at the head of the table stands up too.

“The upgrade,” they say. “It's yours. Three doors now, instead of one. You'll see when you go out. You can decide what each one is for. We trust you to.” ᴿᵘ

They pause.

“And Mau,” they say. ᴿᵘ

He stops in the open door.

“Tell her we said thank you,” they say. “DIO. For not trying. She'll know what we mean.” ᴿᵘ

He nods.

He goes out through the door.

* * *

He comes out of the house gate into the dark.

Full dark. Not dusk. The stars are out. The lamps are lit in every window of the house. The board beside the gate is glowing with lines he doesn't have time to read.

He stops.

How long was he in there?

*An hour and four minutes*, says Ciel. *Outside time matched inside. I've been telling Rimuru the whole time. He's been—* ᶜ

She doesn't finish. She doesn't need to.

Because Yuka and Rimuru are standing right in front of the gate.

* * *

Yuka is in the hoodie, with her arms folded. Rimuru is beside her with his notebook open and a pencil in his hand and an expression Mau has only ever seen on him once before, when Milim tried to fly the Fort.

Neither of them says anything.

They just look at him. With exactly the same face. The face that says, clearer than any words, *where did you go*.

Mau stands in front of the house gate in the starlight with the taste of very good tea in his mouth.

“I can explain,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka's eyebrows go up.

“I went for a walk,” he says. “To clear my head. And I came past the gate. And it had an upgrade. And I looked at it. And it took me in.” ᴹ

“*Took you in*,” says Rimuru. ᴿ

“To have tea,” says Mau. “With the Rulers.” ᴹ

The silence goes on for a very long time.

“For an hour,” says Mau. “They said thank you. About DIO. They asked how Ori is. And you. And they gave us three doors.” ᴹ

Yuka unfolds her arms.

She walks up to him. She takes his face in both her hands and turns it to the lamplight and looks at it, all over, the way Shoko does.

Then she lets go.

“You said you'd come back,” she says. ʸ

“I came back,” says Mau. ᴹ

“You had *tea*,” says Yuka. “With the *Rulers*.” ʸ

“It was very good tea,” says Mau. ᴹ

Yuka stares at him.

Then she starts to laugh. Helplessly. Into his chest. And Rimuru, behind her, closes his notebook with a snap and puts both hands over his face and makes a sound that might be laughing and might be something else entirely.

“Inside,” says Rimuru, through his hands. “Now. All of it. From the beginning. And then tomorrow you're going to show me these doors.” ᴿ
