# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 24 — Three Doors

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 24
**Source episode:** S13E130
**Continuity state:** B+447 ("40"), morning. Mau tells DIO first: the Rulers said thank you, for not trying. She goes very quiet and then says, "They noticed," and goes to water her plant. After breakfast the whole house goes to see the house gate, which now has three doors. The house decides what they're for. The first, the old weekly one Mau can adjust, stays for the house: supplies, cores for Mai, the warm place. The second becomes the Fort's: safe for children and for anyone who doesn't fight. The gate warns it can't give much loot, so Mau connects its rewards to his Shop and inventory and supplies it himself. The third Mau can modify, and it refreshes every time it's completed: for anyone who wants to scratch an itch. Gojo wants it solo, Jin-Woo wants it, Yuta and Maki want it together, and within ten minutes Shuna is in charge, with a rota, a time limit, and a rule that if you don't finish in time you lose your turn. The rules, set with Rimuru and Ciel: the Fort door and the third (unless Mau is the operator inside) can never turn Red; nobody can make them harder than set; the gate reads who comes in and won't become more than they can complete. The rest of the house stays safe.

*When: B+447. Morning. "40." Under it, in Shuna's hand, a timetable. It goes on for half the page.*

He tells DIO first.

Before breakfast. Inside, at the table, while the house is still waking up. She's there with her watering notebook open, drawing a leaf. She looks up when he sits down.

“They said thank you,” he says. ᴹ

DIO's pencil stops.

“The Rulers,” says Mau. “Last night. They said to tell you. Thank you, for not trying. They said you'd know what they meant.” ᴹ

DIO doesn't say anything for a long moment.

She looks at the leaf she was drawing. She doesn't finish it.

“They noticed,” she says. Very quietly. ᴰ

“They watched you,” says Mau. “In the garden. With Elmesia. They said they could see you weren't trying anything. They said—” He stops. Decides to say it. “They said people in love aren't usually plotting to take back the order of the world.” ᴹ

DIO goes a colour he can't see.

She closes the notebook.

“I'm going to water my plant,” she says, with enormous dignity, and gets up, and goes. ᴰ

He feels her, a minute later, out in the world, in the Guest Inn, standing at the windowsill with the watering can, not watering anything, for a long time.

* * *

After breakfast, the whole house goes to see the doors.

Rimuru organises it. He has his notebook, and the face he wears when he's being a ruler, and he walks at the front of a crowd of about thirty people across the yard and round the back of the house to the house gate, like a man leading a tour of a building he isn't sure is safe.

The house gate has three doors.

It used to have one. A single opening, with the board beside it showing the week's sketch. Now there are three, side by side, each with its own board. The left one looks exactly like the old one. The middle one is smaller, and the light in it is softer, and someone, Ori presumably, in the night, has already stuck a paper flag beside it that says FORT. The right one looks like nothing in particular, and its board is blank.

Everyone stops and stares.

“Tea,” says Gojo, from the back. “You had *tea*. With the people who sealed you in a Red Gate. And they gave you *this*.” ᴳ

“It was very good tea,” says Mau. ᴹ

* * *

They decide what the doors are for.

All of them. Standing in front of the gate in the morning sun, arguing, the way the house decides things. Rimuru writing it down.

The left door stays the house's.

The old weekly gate. The one Mau can tell what he wants, once a week, when it changes. Harder or easier. Cores for Mai. Materials for Senku. Something warm in it, somewhere, a place to rest. That's what it's been for a long time and that's what it stays: the house's door, for supplies, for the ordinary Thursday run.

“Agreed,” says Rimuru, writing. ᴿ

* * *

The middle door is the Fort's.

Ori says so, before anyone else can. She steps out in front of the crowd with her sword in its sheath and points at the paper flag that says FORT, and looks round at everybody with an expression that dares anyone to disagree.

Nobody disagrees.

Mau goes to the middle board. He lets the eyes go to it, carefully, the way he didn't last night.

> **[Gate Authority: household gate, second door.]**
>
> **[Configure?]**

He tells it what he wants. Out loud, so everyone can hear.

“Safe,” he says. “For children. For anyone who doesn't fight. Hard enough to be fun. Never hard enough to hurt. Read who comes in, and never be more than they can finish.” ᴹ

> **[Configuration accepted.]**
>
> **[Notice: low-difficulty instance. Reward generation limited.]**

He reads it twice.

“It says it can't give much loot,” he tells Ori. ᴹ

Ori's face falls.

Mau looks at the board for a moment.

“Can you take rewards from somewhere else?” he asks it. “From my Shop. My inventory. If I put things in, can you give them out?” ᴹ

The board thinks about it. He can feel it think.

> **[External reward source: operator inventory/Shop. Link?]**

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

> **[Linked. Rewards for second door will be supplied by operator.]**

He turns round.

Ori is staring at him.

“So you're the treasure,” she says slowly. ᴼ

“I'm the vending citizen,” says Mau. “I have to supply.” ᴹ

“The *official* vending citizen,” says Ori, and turns round to the Fort, who are all standing behind her, and raises the paper flag, and the Fort screams.

* * *

The right door is the problem.

Because Mau reads its board, and the board says:

> **[Gate Authority: household gate, third door.]**
>
> **[Operator-modifiable. Refreshes on completion.]**

And he reads it out loud.

And Gojo, at the back of the crowd, goes very still.

“Refreshes,” says Gojo. ᴳ

“Every time it's completed,” says Mau. ᴹ

“So you finish it,” says Gojo, “and it's new. Immediately. And you can tell it what you want it to be.” ᴳ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And it's *not* the Fort one,” says Gojo. “It doesn't have to be safe for children.” ᴳ

Mau looks at him.

“I want it,” says Gojo. “Solo. Today. Now. Make it hard. I want to scratch an itch.” ᴳ

* * *

He isn't the only one.

“I'd like a turn,” says Jin-Woo, very calmly, from the side, in the voice he uses when he wants something very much. ᴶʷ

“Us,” says Maki, with her staff. “Me and Yuta. Together.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“I'd also like to—” begins Yuta. ʸᵗ

“*Together*,” says Maki. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Together,” Yuta agrees. ʸᵗ

“Can I go in?” says Milim, bouncing. ᴹⁱ

“*No*—” says Rimuru, out of pure habit. ᴿ

Mau looks at the board again. *Reads who comes in. Never harder than they can finish.* He turns it over in his head. Then he looks at Milim, bouncing on her toes in front of the third door with her whole face lit up, the way it's been lit up at every door in this house that everybody else got to go through first.

“It adapts,” he says. “To whoever goes in. It'll read her and become something she can fight. That's the point of it.” ᴹ

Rimuru opens his mouth. Closes it.

Milim has stopped bouncing. She's staring at Mau.

“So I *can*,” she says. ᴹⁱ

“You can,” says Mau. ᴹ

Milim lets out a sound that makes Kamish, at the bottom of the orchard, lift his head.

Then she points at Mau. Straight at him. With her whole arm.

“And *you*,” she says, “are coming with me.” ᴹⁱ

Mau looks at her finger.

“Am I?” ᴹ

“You are,” says Milim. “I've watched you go in every Thursday for a *year*. I've watched you go in with Yuka. I've watched Gojo get to fight you in a field. You're coming in with me, and we're going to break it, and then it's going to refresh, and we're going to break it *again*.” ᴹⁱ

Mau looks at Rimuru. Rimuru looks at the sky.

“Okay,” says Mau. ᴹ

Milim screams.

“Dabura wants a turn,” says Maru, from the back. “He hasn't said so. But he's looking at it the way he looked at the pears.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Dabura, beside him, is indeed looking at the third door with an expression of tremendous seriousness.

Gojo is already walking toward it.

* * *

Shuna stops him.

She doesn't raise her voice. She doesn't move fast. She just steps out of the crowd and stands in front of the third door, between it and Gojo, with a cloth over her shoulder and her hands folded in front of her apron.

Gojo stops.

He's Gojo Satoru. He could walk through her, or round her, or over her, without trying.

He stops anyway.

“A list,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

Gojo opens his mouth.

“A list,” says Shuna again. “And a time. And an order.” ˢʰᵘ

She takes a pencil from behind her ear and the notebook from Rimuru's hands, without asking, and turns to a fresh page.

“Gojo,” she says, writing. “Solo. Tomorrow. Jin-Woo, the day after. Maki and Yuta, the day after that. Dabura after them, but never on the day of the house run on the left door. Then Milim, *with Mau*, since he's apparently been volunteered. Anyone else, you ask me.” ˢʰᵘ

“*Tomorrow*?” says Gojo. ᴳ

“Today the Fort goes first,” says Shuna. “Ori asked before you did. Last night. In writing.” ˢʰᵘ

Gojo stares at her.

“And you each get a time,” says Shuna. “From when you go in. If you haven't finished by the time, you come out, and you lose your turn, and the next one goes. I'm not having anybody in there all night while their supper goes cold.” ˢʰᵘ

“How long?” says Jin-Woo, interested. ᴶʷ

Shuna considers.

“Until the bread's done,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

She closes the notebook and hands it back to Rimuru.

Nobody argues.

Not even Gojo. He looks at the third door, and at Shuna, and at the third door again, and then he does something nobody has ever seen him do.

He says, “Yes, Shuna.” ᴳ

* * *

The rules take another hour.

Rimuru and Ciel and Mau, at the kitchen table, with the boards' messages copied out and the eyes going over every line. Frieren listening from the end of the table. Shoko with her arms folded, adding things.

When they're done, Rimuru writes them out in his neatest hand and pins them beside the gate, on a board of their own.

*HOUSE GATE: RULES*

*1. Left door: the house's. Weekly. Mau adjusts it.*

*2. Middle door: the Fort's. Safe for children and anyone who doesn't fight. Reads who comes in. Never harder than they can finish. Rewards supplied by Mau. CANNOT TURN RED.*

*3. Right door: refreshes on completion. Mau can modify it. Turns by Shuna's list. Time limit: Shuna's bread. Reads who comes in; never harder than they can finish. CANNOT TURN RED unless Mau is the operator inside.*

*4. Nobody makes any door harder than it's set. Nobody. (This means you, Gojo.)*

*5. The rest of the house stays safe. Always.*

Under it, smaller, in Ori's handwriting, added later when nobody was looking:

*6. The Fort door is the best one.*

* * *

Before lunch, Mau stands in front of the three doors for a while.

The board on the left showing the week's sketch. The board in the middle with FORT stuck beside it and, already, a small drawing of a slime. The board on the right, blank, waiting for tomorrow.

He thinks about the room last night. The table. The tea. *We'd like to keep faith with you.*

*They meant it*, says Ciel, inside. ᶜ

*I know*, says Mau. ᴹ

*You're still careful.* ᶜ

*I'll always be careful with them*, says Mau. *But I drank the tea.* ᴹ

Behind him, across the yard, he hears Ori asking Frieren, very loudly, whether she can go in the Fort door *today*, *right now*, *please*. And Frieren saying *I'll go through it first*. And Ori saying *then go NOW*.

He smiles.

He goes in to lunch.
