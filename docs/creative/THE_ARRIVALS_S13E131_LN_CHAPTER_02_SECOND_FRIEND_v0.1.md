## Chapter 31 — Second Friend

*When: B+452. "35." Yuka and Makima spend part of the afternoon together.*

Makima is on the north-wing step at dusk.

Yuka knows she likes that step at dusk. Since the witches moved into the north wing and Makima got a room at the end of it, she often comes out there after eating, sits with her hands in her lap, and watches the light go.

She doesn't talk to anyone while she does it. Nobody's ever asked her why.

Yuka comes round the corner of the house in the hoodie and stops.

She's thought about this every time they've sat together since the orchard wall. Since Makima sat at the other end of it, not beside, near, and told her *don't make him guess* and *don't let him carry yours*, and was right about everything. Since Makima came down at a run from the north wing because she heard a wheel and thought someone was going to die.

Since Makima told Mau about Cross, and Mau walked toward the river, and Makima walked a step behind his shoulder the whole way, ready to go with him.

*I'd have had to go with you. It would have been very awkward.*

Yuka heard about that. Ciel told her. Ciel tells her things now, out loud.

She has a line in the notebook about Makima. It's been there since the first morning on the wet grass.

She'd written something before it and then rubbed it out. On the night at the gate she had thought she'd be up here trying to decide whether Makima really meant what she'd said about marrying Mau. Makima came to the kitchen herself. Yuka hadn't had to ask. And now some nights when the bread comes round, Yuka leaves a piece on the plate before it gets to Mau, because he's gotten in the habit of saving an end for Makima when she needs a minute to sit down.

She walks up behind the step.

* * *

Makima doesn't turn round. She knows who it is. She always knows.

“Yuka,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka doesn't answer.

She sits down on the step behind Makima, one step up, and puts her arms round her from behind. Round her shoulders. Tight. And puts her face against the back of Makima's head.

Makima goes completely still.

Yuka has never touched her. Not once. Not since the clinic, all those months ago, when she set her terms: *you don't touch me, you don't come into our room, you ask before you sit beside me*. Makima has kept every one of them. Has asked, every time, before she sat anywhere near.

Yuka never set any terms for herself.

She holds on.

“Thank you,” she says, into Makima's hair. ʸ

Makima doesn't move.

“Thank you,” says Yuka again, “for not letting him do something stupid. When I hurt him. When I said that in the yard and he went down inside. You were there. You were in there with him. You held him.” ʸ

Her voice cracks. She doesn't stop.

“And the basin,” she says. “You held his three seconds. And the road. And the clinic step. And the river, when he was going, you went with him, you'd have gone all the way. You held on to him the whole time I couldn't. Thank you. Thank you.” ʸ

* * *

Makima is quiet for a long moment.

Then, very carefully, the way you'd touch something you weren't sure you were allowed to touch, she lifts one hand from her lap and puts it over Yuka's, where Yuka's arms cross over her collarbone.

“You don't need to thank me,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I do.” ʸ

“No,” says Makima. “It's what friends are for.” ᴹᵏ

She says it simply. As if it's a rule she's learned and checked and found to be true.

Yuka laughs.

She doesn't mean to. It comes out of her wet and surprised, into Makima's hair. *What friends are for.* From Makima. The woman who sent a devil to get Yuka out of the way. The woman who stood in a basin and said *stop* and stopped a hundred hungry things. Sitting on a step at dusk saying *it's what friends are for* like a girl in a schoolyard.

“What?” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“Nothing,” says Yuka. “Nothing. It's just—” ʸ

She lets go. She shifts down onto the same step as Makima, beside her, close. Not near. Beside.

Makima looks at her.

“Can I ask you something?” says Yuka. ʸ

* * *

“The hands,” says Yuka. ʸ

Makima waits.

“You and him,” says Yuka. “When you went north that morning. I kept looking at the way he put his fingers between yours.” She makes herself keep going. “What is that? What are you doing?” ʸ

She's afraid of the answer. She's been afraid of it for two weeks. She watched him walk north into the dark with another woman's hand in his, and the fear came up in her so strong she sat down on the wet grass.

Makima doesn't look away. She doesn't look embarrassed, or careful, or sorry. She looks the way she looks when she explains anything: calm, precise, interested in getting it right.

“It's how we talk,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“You talk inside,” says Yuka. “You don't need hands.” ʸ

“Not that kind of talk,” says Makima. “The other kind.” ᴹᵏ

She holds up her hand. She turns it so Yuka can see the palm.

“At first it was like this. He held his out that night by the wall. In the Gate too. I'd put mine against it and he'd keep it there until we had something else to do.” ᴹᵏ

“And in the yard?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“After we came back. After you walked away. I was still holding his shirt. He reached back and I thought he wanted me to let go of it.” Makima looks down at her hand. “He went looking for my fingers. I could feel him waiting for me to take my hand back as soon as he touched it. He was afraid I'd go too.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka takes a breath. “But you're with him. Even if you go inside.” ʸ

“I can't disappear out of him. He knew that then. I could still go to my room and shut the door. I think he needed to feel me choose to stay beside him.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka can see the yard from the top step. There's a place where two people can go round the training posts abreast before one of them has to fall behind. She remembers hearing Cross catch up to her there.

“Do you still take it like that?” she asks. ʸ

“Sometimes we slide our fingers together. He can ask for me that way even when he can't say anything. Sometimes I reach first. There was a minute in the basin when I needed to know where he was too.” Makima turns her hand back over. “He's my friend, Yuka. If I'd seen it from where you were, I'd have wanted somebody to tell me why.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

“I don't have those feelings,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her.

“Romantic ones,” Makima says. “For anyone. I never have. I used to think something was missing in me. Then I thought it was a weakness in other people. Then I stopped thinking about it at all.” ᴹᵏ

She looks out at the yard, at the light going.

“What I wanted,” she says, “all my life, without knowing the word for it, was someone at my level. Someone who could stand level with me. Not under me. Not afraid of me. Not something I could make do things. Someone I couldn't control, who'd look at me and see me, and stay anyway. Someone I could call an equal.” ᴹᵏ

She's quiet for a moment.

“I tried to make that,” she says. “For a very long time. In my world. I tried to *make* someone into that. It doesn't work. You can't make an equal. That's the whole point of one.” ᴹᵏ

She looks at Yuka.

“And then he thanked me,” she says. “When he had no reason to. And then he forgave me. In a way. Not all of it. He never pretended it was all. But enough to let me stand next to him. And I found out what it was. What I'd been looking for. It was a friend.” ᴹᵏ

She turns her hand over again.

“That's all I want from him,” she says. “That's all I've ever wanted. Someone at my level who holds my hand in the dark so we don't lose each other. I have it. It's more than I ever thought I'd get.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka sits very still on the step.

She thinks about the yard. *You'd rather have her. When it's bad, you go to her.* She thinks about sitting on the wet grass watching them walk north hand in hand. She thinks about two weeks of being afraid of this woman. Of thinking she'd come into the house to take him. Of thinking that every time he went inside to the room with the table and the cup, Makima was there, and she wasn't, and that meant something.

And all the time, the thing she was afraid of was impossible.

Not unlikely. Not something Makima was resisting. *Impossible.* Makima doesn't have it to give. She never did. All she ever wanted was a friend, and she found one, and the friend was Yuka's husband, and that was never, not for one second, a door Yuka was standing on the wrong side of.

She was afraid of a room that didn't exist.

She puts her face in her hands.

Makima waits.

* * *

When Yuka takes her hands away from her face, she holds one of them out.

Makima looks at it.

“Show me the first one,” says Yuka. ʸ

Makima looks at her for a long moment.

Then she takes it. Palm to palm. Thumb over the back.

It's very ordinary. That's the strange thing. Yuka was expecting it to feel like something. It feels like holding someone's hand. Makima's hand is cool and dry and quite small, and she holds on firmly, not hard, the way you'd hold a rail on a stair.

*I'm here.*

Yuka looks down at their hands.

“Okay,” she says. “I get it.” ʸ

* * *

“He's got two now, then,” says Yuka. ʸ

Makima tilts her head.

“Friends,” says Yuka. “That hold hands. You and me. He'll have to share.” ʸ

Something happens at the corner of Makima's mouth.

“He's very good at sharing,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“He is,” says Yuka. ʸ

She doesn't let go of the hand. She looks Makima in the eye.

“But I'll tell you something,” she says. “Plainly. The way you told me.” ʸ

Makima waits.

“If I ever see you,” says Yuka, “*try* to kiss him the way Yoru tried. Once. Just once. I won't be afraid of you. I won't sit on the grass. I'll call the wheel, and I'll send you to hell myself. Personally. And then I'll go and get you back, because you're my friend now, and then I'll send you again.” ʸ

Makima looks at her for a long, long moment.

Then she laughs.

Not the short quiet one. A real laugh, startled out of her, the one from the night of the castle game. She puts her other hand over her mouth and it doesn't help.

“Accepted,” she says, through her fingers. “Those are very reasonable terms.” ᴹᵏ

“I mean it.” ʸ

“I know you do. That's why they're reasonable.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

They sit on the step a while longer, holding hands, watching the light go.

“Can I tell you something about him?” says Yuka. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“Rem,” says Yuka. ʸ

Makima turns her head.

“You know Rem,” says Yuka. “The quiet one. Umaru's. She came here a long time ago, out of a bad place.” ʸ

Makima's face doesn't change. But she's listening very carefully.

“He got her out,” says Yuka. “Mau. Before I was even here. He brought her home. And he never asked her for anything. Not once. Not gratitude. Not to pay him back. Nothing. He just asked her to be family. That's all. And she found out she could be. She found Umaru, and she found the house, and she's happy. And when Subaru came, and used what she'd only started to feel to pull her back, she could say no. She told him so. *I'm happy.* With Mau sitting right there.” ʸ

She squeezes Makima's hand.

“That's what he does,” she says. “That's who he is. He doesn't take. He doesn't make people into things. He just asks if they want to be family, and waits.” ʸ

She looks at Makima.

“He did it for you, too,” she says. “I didn't see it. Now I do.” ʸ

* * *

Makima doesn't say anything for a long time.

The light is almost gone. The lamps are being lit in the kitchen windows. Somewhere in the house Umaru is shouting about a tournament.

“Thank you,” says Makima at last. ᴹᵏ

Her voice is very quiet.

“For telling me,” she says. “About Rem. I didn't know.” ᴹᵏ

She looks down at their hands.

“And for this,” she says. “I didn't know I could have two.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka holds on.

“Neither did I,” she says. ʸ

* * *

When Yuka gets to the kitchen, she looks back through the window. She'd spent a long time deciding which questions she could ask Makima without getting an answer she wasn't ready to hear. Now there's the much smaller question of whether to bring her soup and sit out there with her a little longer.

Mau is at the stove holding a bowl so Shuna can pour. He's got his head bent to hear what she's saying about the bottom of the pot. Yuka waits until the bowl is on the counter, then goes right up behind him and puts her arms around his waist.

“Hi,” she says into his back. ʸ

He lays a hand over hers before he turns. “Hi. Do you want supper in a bowl or on a plate?” ᴹ

“I want to stay here.” ʸ

“You can stay here and still have supper.” ᴹ

She tests this. He puts the bread board within reach. She takes a piece without moving her other arm, feeds him a corner, and then brings her hand right back where it was.

She can see the north-wing step through the window. Makima has stayed to watch the last of the light. Yuka thinks she'll leave her to that and take her a bowl afterwards, when the quiet's had time to do its work.

“Get me an extra one for later?” she asks. ʸ

Mau puts a second bowl beside hers. When he turns around in her arms she tips her face up and he kisses her, all bread and still enough time before supper.
