# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 29 — First Pin

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 29
**Source episode:** S13E132
**Continuity state:** B+451 ("36"). The first use of the new clause. Mai comes to find Mau in the yard in the middle of the morning to tell him what she's going to make today, and he stops what he's doing, sits down on the porch step and listens, properly, to all of it: a set of pins for Maki's first lesson, and the exact cost. It takes twenty minutes. It's the best twenty minutes of Mai's week, though she'd never say so. That evening Maki makes her first pin. It comes apart. She makes another. It comes apart. On the eleventh try it holds. Mau feels the small cost land in his body through the line, a pinch like a needle in the thumb, and has never been so pleased to be charged for anything.

*When: B+451. "36." Under it, in Maki's handwriting, very large and slightly aggressive: "PIN".*

Mai comes to find him in the middle of the morning.

He's in the yard with Jin-Woo and Suho, teaching Suho how to fall. Not fight; fall. Suho is small and has decided he wants to learn to fight like his father, and Jin-Woo has decided that before anybody in his family learns to throw a punch they learn to hit the ground without breaking anything, and has asked Mau to help, because Mau has hit the ground more times than anyone else in the house.

Suho is very bad at falling. He keeps trying to land on his feet.

“You're meant to land on your *shoulder*,” Jin-Woo is saying, for the ninth time. ᴶʷ

“But that *hurts*,” says Suho. ˢᵘ

“Less than your head,” says Mau. ᴹ

And Mai is standing at the edge of the training ground with her arms folded and a piece of paper in one hand, not saying anything, waiting.

* * *

He knows what it is the moment he sees the paper.

Last week he'd have said *in a minute*. He'd have finished with Suho, and then with Jin-Woo, and then gone to look at the split training post, and then remembered Mai, and gone to find her in the workshop, and said *go ahead, it's fine, you don't have to tell me*, and not looked at the paper.

He stops.

He says, “Sorry, Suho,” and “Excuse me,” to Jin-Woo, and goes across the yard to her. ᴹ

Jin-Woo watches him go. He doesn't say anything. But Mau sees him notice.

* * *

“I'm going to make something,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

She holds out the paper. She doesn't look at him. She looks at a point somewhere over his left shoulder.

“Under the new clause,” she says. “I'm telling you. You said you'd—” ᴹᵃ

“Listen,” he says. “Yes.” ᴹ

He sits down on the porch step.

Mai looks at him sitting there. She looks at the step beside him. She looks at the paper.

Then she sits down too, not close, with the paper on her knees, and starts to explain.

* * *

It takes twenty minutes.

It's pins. For Maki's first lesson tonight. Twelve of them, the simplest thing Mai knows how to make, and she's going to make them first, herself, as patterns, so that Maki has something to copy. She explains why pins: because a pin is one piece, no joins, nothing to come apart in the middle. Because it's small, so the cost is small. Because if Maki gets it wrong, nobody gets hurt, except possibly Maki's thumb.

She explains the cost. Exactly. To the last fraction. How much CE each pin draws through the line. What it'll feel like in his body, when it lands. Where. *Left thumb, probably. A pinch. Like a needle. It goes away in about a minute.*

She explains why she's making twelve and not ten. Because Maki will lose two.

She explains the shape of the head, which is round, and why, which is so Maki can't stab herself with the wrong end.

He listens to all of it.

He doesn't say *it's fine*. He doesn't say *go ahead*. He doesn't say *you don't have to tell me*. He asks two questions: why the heads are round, which she's already explained, and whether Maki will be cross about starting with pins, which makes Mai's mouth twitch.

“Furious,” she says. “She'll be furious. She wanted to start with a spearhead.” ᴹᵃ

“You said no.” ᴹ

“I said a pin. A spearhead is two hundred joins. She'd blow your arm off.” ᴹᵃ

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

Mai looks at him sharply, as if checking whether he's joking. He isn't.

* * *

When she's finished, she folds the paper up again.

She sits there for a moment on the step with it in her lap, not getting up.

“That's all,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“Okay,” he says. The good kind. “Thank you for telling me.” ᴹ

Mai doesn't move.

“You listened,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“I said I would.” ᴹ

“You *listened*,” says Mai again, as if he hasn't understood. “You stopped. You came over. You sat down. You asked about the heads.” ᴹᵃ

She looks at the paper.

“Nobody's ever—” she starts. And stops. ᴹᵃ

He waits.

She doesn't finish it. She stands up very abruptly and brushes off her skirt.

“Twelve pins,” she says. “This evening. Left thumb. You'll feel it.” ᴹᵃ

And she goes back across the yard to the workshop at a fast walk, and doesn't look back, and he sits on the step and watches her go.

*That was twenty minutes*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*She'll talk about it for a week. She'll never say she's talking about it.* ᶜ

*I know*, he says. And then: *It was a good twenty minutes.* ᴹ

* * *

Maki is furious about the pins.

He hears about it at supper. Everyone hears about it. Maki sits at the table and eats very fast with her jaw set and explains to Yuta, at length, that she is a grown woman who has fought in a war and killed a curse the size of a house with a spear, and her sister is going to teach her to make *pins*.

“Small ones,” Maki says. “With *round heads*. So I don't *stab myself*.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuta, very wisely, says nothing.

Across the table, Mai eats her soup and doesn't look up, and looks extremely pleased.

* * *

The first pin comes apart in Maki's hand.

He's in the kitchen when it happens. He feels it: a flicker down the line, the beginning of a pinch in his left thumb, and then nothing. The construction starting, drawing a tiny thread of CE through him, and then falling apart before it finishes, and the CE coming back.

From the workshop, faintly, across the dark yard: “*It's GONE.*” ᴹᵃᵏ

And Mai: “I told you. You weren't holding the shape. Again.” ᴹᵃ

The second pin comes apart.

The third pin comes apart halfway and leaves a sort of bent wire, which Maki throws across the workshop.

He feels every one. A flicker. A half-pinch. Nothing.

Yuka, at the table beside him, watches his face.

“Is that her?” she says. “Every time your thumb does that?” ʸ

He looks down. His left thumb is twitching. He hadn't noticed.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka takes his left hand in both of hers. His wrist is a little high against the lip of the table, so she pushes her chair in and sets it across her lap. The next flicker jumps into her thumb. She rubs the place once.

He could take his hand back. He turns it palm up instead, and when she slides her fingers between his he leans over and kisses the part in her hair. She tips against his upper arm with a pleased little sound. Across from them, Maki has put both elbows back on the bench. Mai is showing her how to keep from opening her fingers too soon.

“Tell me when it works,” she says. ʸ

* * *

The fourth comes apart. The fifth. The sixth.

The seventh holds for a whole second before it goes.

“*Did you SEE that*,” says Maki, from the workshop. ᴹᵃᵏ

“It came apart,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

“But it *held*. For a *second*.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“A pin that holds for a second is not a pin. It's a rumour. Again.” ᴹᵃ

The eighth. The ninth. The tenth.

He sits at the kitchen table with Yuka holding his hand, and feels every one. Shuna brings him tea. Umaru comes in and asks what's happening and Yuka explains, and Umaru sits down at the table too, with her chin in her hands, staring at the window that looks out at the workshop, and doesn't go away.

Ori comes in. Then Rem. Then, after a while, Yuta, who sits down at the end of the table and doesn't say anything and is clearly listening as hard as everyone else.

The eleventh.

He feels it start. The thread of CE, drawing through him, very thin. The construction catching. Holding. The shape closing round itself, all the way, one piece, no joins.

And then the pinch. In his left thumb. Sharp. Small. Exactly like a needle.

It stays.

He feels it land in his body and stay, the way Mai said it would. The cost. Paid.

He laughs.

Out loud. At the kitchen table. With his thumb stinging.

Yuka looks at him.

“It worked,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

From the workshop, across the dark yard, there's a silence.

Then Maki's voice. Not loud. Quite small, for Maki. Almost wondering.

“It's a pin,” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

A pause.

“It's a pin,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

Another pause. Longer.

Then Maki, louder: “It's a *PIN*.” ᴹᵃᵏ

And then something that sounds very much like two people hugging, which is not a sound, exactly, but the whole kitchen hears it anyway.

Umaru starts clapping. Ori joins in. Then Rem, then Shuna at the stove, then Yuta at the end of the table, with his face doing something very soft. Yuka, still holding his hand, squeezes it.

He sits there with the sting going out of his left thumb, slowly, the way Mai said, about a minute.

He's been charged for a great many things. Gates. Domains. Purple. Things that cost him pieces of his body that took weeks to grow back.

He's never been so glad to pay for anything in his life.

* * *

Maki comes to supper the next night with the pin.

She's put it through the collar of her shirt. Where everyone can see it. It's very small, and very plain, with a round head, and it's slightly crooked.

Nobody mentions it.

Maki doesn't mention it either.

But every so often, all through supper, her hand goes up to it. Just to check it's still there.

And Mai, at the end of the table, watches her do it, and doesn't say anything, and eats her soup, and looks happier than anyone at the table has ever seen her.
