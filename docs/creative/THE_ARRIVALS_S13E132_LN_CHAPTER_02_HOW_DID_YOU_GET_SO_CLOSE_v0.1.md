## Chapter 33 — How Did You Get So Close

Satella is already close to him: she sits pressed beside him at every tea, she's in the Fort, and the only thing he ever stopped was her old "I love you", because he knew it wasn't true. What she envies is Makima's: the room inside him, talking with him in there, going wherever he goes. *When: B+454. "33." Under it, in a handwriting nobody in the house recognises, very small and very neat: "asked". It is Satella's. She has never written in the notebook before.*

Satella comes at dusk.

Makima hears her before she sees her. Not footsteps; Satella doesn't make much noise when she walks. Just the particular quiet that comes with her, the way a room goes quieter when Satella stands in the doorway of it, the way the air seems to hold its breath.

She stops a few steps from the north-wing step.

Makima doesn't turn round.

“Satella,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Makima.” ˢᵃ

A long pause.

“May I sit?” says Satella. ˢᵃ

Makima moves along the step. Satella sits down at the far end of it, carefully, with her hands in her lap. The same way Makima sits.

They look at the yard for a while. The light going. The lamps coming on in the kitchen.

* * *

Satella has lived in this house longer than Makima.

Much longer. Makima knows the history, because she makes a point of knowing histories. Satella came with the other witches, in the beginning, before there was a north wing, before there was a house, when there was only an inn and a field and a plan on the table with names going on it in ink. She has her own room, with an inner window onto the common room; she asked for it, so that even when she's up in her room she can still see everyone. She's at the family table every meal, the same as everyone; she's Fort, and she's house. She has tea with the others every week. She has Emilia now, who came back from the dead world and sat across a table from her and did not run.

She is loved here. Everyone knows it. She knows it too, Makima thinks, in the way you know something that you've been told many times and still don't quite believe.

“How did you get so close to him?” says Satella. ˢᵃ

* * *

Makima doesn't answer straight away.

She turns the question over. She looks at it from every side, the way she looks at everything. She hears what's under it.

“You're envious,” she says. ᴹᵏ

It isn't an accusation. She says it the way she'd say *you're cold* to someone shivering.

Satella's hands tighten in her lap.

“Yes,” she says. ˢᵃ

She doesn't deny it. Makima respects that. Most people would deny it.

“It's the oldest thing I know,” says Satella. “Envy. It was the first thing I was. Before anything else. It's what they called me. For four hundred years it was all I was.” ˢᵃ

She looks at the yard.

“I'm not angry,” she says. “I want you to know that. I don't hate you. I'm not going to do anything. I just—” ˢᵃ

She stops.

“You hurt him,” she says. “You sent something to hurt his wife. You came here with your patience and your terms and you got close to him like water getting into a wall. I watched it. I was here.” ˢᵃ

“Yes,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“And now,” says Satella, “you have a room. *Inside* him. You sit at his table and drink from his cup and talk to him in there, where nobody else can hear. You walk beside him everywhere. Into the dark. To the north. You hold his hand.” ˢᵃ

Her voice doesn't rise. It just gets very quiet.

“I'm close to him,” she says. “I know I am. I sit beside him at every tea. Right beside him. Closer than the others like. I'm in the Fort; Ori pinned the badge on me herself. I see him every day.” ˢᵃ

She looks down at her hands.

“I told him once that I loved him,” she says. “A long time ago. And he stopped me. Kindly. He said it wasn't true, and he was right, and I knew he was right while I was saying it. I don't want that. I never really wanted that.” ˢᵃ

She lifts her head.

“I want what you have,” she says. “The room. The talking, in there. Going where he goes. That's what I envy. Not him. That.” ˢᵃ

* * *

Makima thinks about it for a long time.

The light is almost gone. A lamp has come on in the window behind them, in Makima's own room.

“Walking beside him,” she says at last. ᴹᵏ

Satella waits.

“You think I go with him because I've earned it,” says Makima. “Because I'm close. Because he lets me.” ᴹᵏ

She turns her head and looks at Satella.

“I go with him because I can't not,” she says. “He and I are, really, one thing now. This body isn't mine. It's Noise's. He holds it in the world for me, and he can only hold it so far from Mau before I have to go back inside. I don't accompany him, Satella. I'm *in* him. When he goes north, I go north, because the rest of me is already there.” ᴹᵏ

Satella goes very still.

“The hand,” says Makima, more quietly. “That's real. That's friendship. But the walking beside him, everywhere, all the time? That isn't a privilege. That's a tether.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Satella doesn't say anything for a long while.

Makima watches it go into her. Watches her turn it over. The thing she has been envying for weeks, seen from the other side.

“Oh,” says Satella at last. Very small. ˢᵃ

“Yes,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

Satella looks out at the yard.

“Then it's only the room,” she says slowly. “The table. The talking in there.” ˢᵃ

She folds her hands.

“I feel as though we never spend any time together,” she says. “He and I. Outside, everyone has him. At tea, everyone's there. I sit right beside him and there are six others at the table and Pride is throwing something. I never get him to myself for a minute. And you have him all the time, in there.” ˢᵃ

Makima looks at her.

She could say: *you see him every single day. You sit pressed against his side at every tea. He asks after you. He counts you.* All of it true.

She doesn't. It isn't what Satella needs to hear tonight.

“Then ask him for the room,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Satella's hands tighten in her lap.

“Just like that,” she says. ˢᵃ

“Just like that,” says Makima. “He'll say yes. He always says yes. But you have to ask it yourself.” ᴹᵏ

She stands up.

“Come on,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

Mau is in the common room with Yuka.

It's late. Most of the house has gone up. He's on the couch with Yuka asleep in his lap, the way she falls asleep in his lap most nights now, and a book open on the arm of the couch that he isn't reading, and he looks up when Makima comes in with Satella behind her.

He knows at once that something's happening. He can see it in Makima's face. And in Satella's, which he's never seen like this. Satella, who sits pressed against his side at every tea and has never once looked nervous about it.

She looks nervous now.

She's standing in the middle of the common room in the lamplight with her hands clenched at her sides, and she looks like she's about to be sick.

“She wants to ask you something,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

Mau looks at Satella.

Satella opens her mouth.

Nothing comes out.

* * *

She tries three times.

He watches her try. He doesn't help. He knows what it is to have a thing in your mouth that won't come out. He holds still, with Yuka asleep on him, and waits, and doesn't look away.

Satella's hands are shaking.

Makima, beside her, sighs. Very softly.

“She'd like a room,” says Makima. “Inside. Like mine. She's been envious of it for weeks; she thinks the two of you never get any time together, and she'd like somewhere to talk to you where nobody's throwing spoons. She'd like mine. To visit. She'd like—” ᴹᵏ

Makima stops.

She turns to Satella.

“No,” she says. “You say the last part. I'll say everything else. But you say the last part yourself.” ᴹᵏ

Satella looks at her.

Then she looks at Mau.

“Please,” says Satella. ˢᵃ

One word. It comes out of her cracked and raw, like something pulled up out of very deep ground.

“Please,” she says again. “May I.” ˢᵃ

* * *

He doesn't make her wait.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Satella stares at him.

“There's a room,” he says. “Inside. Off the corridor, past DIO's door. It's empty. It's always been empty. It's got a window; DIO wants every room in there to have one, even though there's nothing outside them.” ᴹ

He shifts Yuka a little in his lap, carefully, so as not to wake her.

“It's yours,” he says. “To visit. Whenever you want. As a guest. Not to live in. You live here, in the north wing, with your window onto the common room. But when you want to sit at the table, and drink from the cup, and talk to Ciel and Noise and DIO and Makima, and be in there with us, you can. You just come.” ᴹ

He looks at her.

“You don't have to ask again,” he says. “You asked once. That's enough. That's the door open.” ᴹ

Satella doesn't say anything.

She stands in the middle of the common room with her hands at her sides, not clenched now, just hanging, and her face doing something he's never seen it do. Something that might be a smile someday. Something very like Yuka's face in the kitchen, the first morning, saying *stay*.

Then she puts both hands over her face.

Makima puts a hand on her back. Just for a moment. Then takes it away.

“Thank you,” says Satella, through her hands. ˢᵃ

“You're welcome,” says Mau. ᴹ

In his lap, Yuka stirs. She opens one eye and looks at Satella standing in the lamplight with her hands over her face, and at Makima beside her, and at Mau.

“What did I miss,” she mumbles. ʸ

“Satella asked for something,” says Mau. ᴹ

“*Satella* asked?” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at Satella for a long moment. Then she smiles, sleepily, all the way.

“Good,” she says, and goes back to sleep. ʸ

None of them see the inner window.

Satella's window. The one Wakana built into the wall of her room in the north wing because she asked for it, so that even from her room she could see the common room. Her room is empty tonight; Satella is down in the common room. And behind her window, in the dark, pressed close together, absolutely silent, are six faces.

Echidna, with a book under her arm and an expression of intense interest.

Pride, standing on a chair to see over the sill.

Daphne, eating something.

Sekhmet, who has been carried there by Minerva and is mostly asleep.

Minerva, holding Sekhmet, with her jaw set.

And Carmilla, at the very back, barely visible, peering round the edge of the frame.

They watch Satella take her hands away from her face.

They watch Mau say *it's yours*.

They look at each other.

“A *room*,” says Pride, in a whisper that is not at all a whisper. ᴾʳ
