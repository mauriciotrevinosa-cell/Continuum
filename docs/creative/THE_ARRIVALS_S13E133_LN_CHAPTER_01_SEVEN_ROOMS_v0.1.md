# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 31 — Seven Rooms

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 31
**Source episode:** S13E133
**Continuity state:** B+453 ("34"). At breakfast the six other witches present a formal protest: if Satella gets a room inside, they want one each. Echidna argues it like a philosopher, Pride like a child, Daphne with her mouth full, Minerva angrily, Sekhmet asleep, Carmilla barely audibly. Mau, laughing, agrees: seven rooms. Inside, Noise hears it as more friends. Mau tells him it'll be his job to entertain them. Noise asks how, and Mau reminds him how clingy DIO and Ciel were when they first arrived; Noise reconsiders. Then he sees seven innocent faces through Mau's eyes, and both of them know it won't stay quiet for long, and they accept. Yuka asks why he lets so many people live inside him. "I'm giving Noise options." Ciel makes the most offended sound possible: "How *dare* you." Mau laughs until he has to put his cup down. *When: B+453. "34." Under it, in seven different handwritings, seven small marks, as if the witches had signed a petition. One of them is a drawing of a sleeping face.*

They come to breakfast the way they always come to meals in this house: all of them. It's a family table, and everyone sits at it unless they're out doing something, and that was the rule long before Shuna. Now there's Shuna, and nobody in any world says no to Shuna.

So the witches are at the long table every morning, the same as everyone. Fort, family, house. Daphne beside the bread. Pride on her wobbly chair. Sekhmet asleep against Minerva's shoulder.

What Mau has never seen is this: all six of them putting down their spoons at once, standing up, and filing round to the end of the table in a line, with Echidna at the front holding a piece of paper.

The kitchen goes silent.

Echidna stops at the end of the long table, opposite Mau. The others arrange themselves behind her. Pride, small and fierce, standing on her toes. Daphne, holding a bread roll she has brought with her from her place. Minerva, arms folded, scowling. Sekhmet, upright only because Minerva has a hand on the back of her collar, eyes closed, breathing slowly. And Carmilla, behind them all, half behind Minerva, mostly invisible.

Satella isn't with them.

Satella stays in her seat, beside Emilia, with a cup of tea. She's gone very still and is looking at her cup.

“We have a protest,” says Echidna. ᴱᶜ

* * *

She unfolds the piece of paper.

“It has come to our attention,” she reads, “that a room has been granted. Inside. To one of our number. For visiting purposes.” ᴱᶜ

She looks up over the top of the paper at Satella, who doesn't look up from her cup.

“We do not object to this,” says Echidna. “Let that be clear. We are delighted for her. She asked. Out loud. In the common room. For a thing she wanted. We are very proud.” ᴱᶜ

Satella's ears have gone a colour that Mau can't see but can guess at from the way Emilia is looking at them.

“However,” says Echidna, and looks back at the paper, “in the interests of fairness, and of the long and well-documented principle that what is given to one witch is immediately wanted by the other six—” ᴱᶜ

“We want rooms,” says Pride. ᴾʳ

Echidna lowers the paper.

“I was getting to that,” she says. ᴱᶜ

“You were taking *forever*,” says Pride. ᴾʳ

* * *

They make their case.

All of them. At once, mostly. Mau will try to tell it in order afterwards, to Ciel, and fail.

Echidna argues it like a philosopher: a room inside a person is a question nobody has ever been able to study from within, and she would consider it the greatest privilege of her very long life to be allowed to sit in one and ask it questions.

Pride argues it like a child, which she is, a little, and isn't, a great deal: *Satella has one, so I want one, and that's fair, and if I don't get one I'll be sad, and if I'm sad you'll be sorry.*

Daphne argues it with her mouth full. Mau isn't entirely sure what her argument is. It seems to involve whether there is food inside, and if not, whether there could be.

Minerva argues it angrily, the way Minerva argues everything, with her finger pointed at him across the table: *you let a devil in there before you let us, and that's an insult, and I'm insulted, and I want a room so I can be insulted in it properly.*

Sekhmet doesn't argue. Sekhmet is asleep. At one point, without opening her eyes, she says, very slowly, “...a bed...”, and then nothing else. ˢᵉᵏ

And Carmilla, from behind the doorframe, says something so quietly that nobody hears it, and when Echidna turns round and asks her to say it again, says it again, slightly louder, and it turns out to be: “...we'd like to be near Satella, too...” ᶜᵃ

Satella puts her face in her hands.

* * *

Mau has been holding it in for a while.

He's been sitting at the head of the table with Yuka beside him, stealing his pickles, and a cup of tea in his hands, listening to seven of the most powerful beings in this world or any other argue for rooms in his head like children asking for a puppy, and he's been holding it in.

When Sekhmet says *a bed* in her sleep, he stops holding it.

He laughs.

All of it. Out loud. He has to put the cup down. He leans forward with his elbows on the table and laughs until his eyes water, and Yuka is laughing beside him, and then Umaru, and then the whole kitchen, and the witches stand at the end of the table looking offended and then, one by one, not.

“Yes,” he says, when he can. ᴹ

Echidna lowers the paper.

“Yes?” she says. ᴱᶜ

“Seven rooms,” he says. “One each. Next to Satella's.” ᴹ

He wipes his eyes.

“There's room,” he says. “There's always room. I don't know how. Ask Noise.” ᴹ

* * *

Inside, Noise has gone very still.

Mau can feel him. At the table, in the little room with the cup. Not by the wall today; at the table, where he's been sitting more and more since the night he picked up the cup and held it out. He's been listening to all of it, through Mau. And Mau can feel what's going through him, slow and astonished and warm, like water filling a dry bed.

*More friends*, says Noise. ᴺ

*Yes*, says Mau. ᴹ

*Seven.* ᴺ

*Yes.* ᴹ

A pause.

*Seven more friends*, says Noise again, and something in his voice that Mau has never heard. ᴺ

*And it's going to be your job*, says Mau, *to entertain them.* ᴹ

* * *

Noise goes silent.

*Entertain*, he says, eventually. ᴺ

*When they visit*, says Mau. *They'll sit at the table. They'll want tea. They'll want to talk. Echidna's going to ask you questions about what you are. Pride's going to want to play games. Daphne's going to want to know where the food is. Minerva's going to be angry at you for something. Sekhmet's going to want a bed. And Carmilla's going to sit in the corner and you won't know she's there until she says something.* ᴹ

Another silence.

*How?* says Noise. ᴺ

*You'll work it out*, says Mau. ᴹ

*I don't know how to entertain*, says Noise. *I stood by a wall for longer than anyone knows. I've only just learned to sit at the table.* ᴺ

*You did all right with DIO and Ciel*, says Mau. ᴹ

And then, deliberately, very gently, he lets Noise remember.

DIO, when she first came inside, following Noise round the little room like a duckling, asking him questions every second, *what are you, why are you a shadow, are you sad, do you want to be my husband*. Ciel, when she first got her body, sitting at the table every hour of every day, wanting to know everything, wanting to talk about everything, wanting to be *near*.

How Noise had stood by his wall and endured it, for weeks, the way a cliff endures the sea.

*Oh*, says Noise. ᴺ

*Yes.* ᴹ

*Seven of them*, says Noise. *Like that.* ᴺ

*Possibly worse*, says Mau. *Pride's very small and very loud.* ᴹ

Noise is quiet for a long time.

Mau can feel him reconsidering. Feel him standing up from the table, inside, and going back toward his wall, slowly, the old way.

* * *

Then Mau looks up.

He doesn't mean to do it for Noise. He just does it. He looks up from his tea at the end of the table, where the seven witches are standing in a row.

Not arguing now. Not protesting. Just standing there, waiting for him to finish talking to whoever he's talking to inside, the way the house has learned to wait.

Echidna with her paper. Pride on her toes. Daphne holding her roll. Minerva with her arms still folded but her face gone uncertain. Sekhmet with her eyes closed. Carmilla, peering round the doorframe. And Satella, at the table, who's taken her hands away from her face and is looking at them too.

And every single one of them, at that moment, wears the same expression.

Innocence. Pure, wide-eyed, absolutely unbelievable innocence. Seven faces that have between them caused the downfall of kingdoms and the end of ages and the deaths of armies, looking at him across a breakfast table as if butter wouldn't melt in any of their mouths.

He shows Noise.

Through his eyes. All seven faces.

Inside, Noise stops halfway out of his chair.

*They're lying*, says Noise. ᴺ

*Completely*, says Mau. ᴹ

*This isn't going to stay quiet*, says Noise. ᴺ

*Not for a day*, says Mau. ᴹ

A long pause.

Then Noise turns round, inside, and walks back to the table, and sits down.

*All right*, he says. ᴺ

* * *

“Why do you let so many people live in you?” says Yuka. ʸ

She's beside him, with the last pickle halfway to her mouth, looking at him with real curiosity. Not jealous. Just asking. The way she asks things now.

“Ciel,” she says. “Noise. DIO. Makima. Now Satella. Now seven. That's—” She counts on her fingers. “That's a lot of people in one head.” ʸ

He thinks about it.

He thinks about Noise, standing by the wall for longer than anyone knows, alone, guarding a door. About the night Noise came away from the wall and sat down and picked up the cup. About *seven more friends*, said in that voice.

“I'm giving Noise options,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks at him.

And inside, very loudly, so loudly that Mau actually flinches, Ciel makes a sound.

He's never heard anything like it. It's not a word. It's the sound of a person being as offended as it is possible to be. A sharp, high, outraged intake of breath that goes on for much longer than an intake of breath should be able to.

*How* dare *you*, says Ciel. ᶜ

He starts laughing again.

*Options*, says Ciel. *He has* options. *He has had options for* years. *He has* me. *I have been sitting at that table since I* had *a body. I have been* entertaining *him since before he knew what the word* meant—ᶜ

*I know*, says Mau, through the laughing. *I know, I'm sorry, I know.* ᴹ

*—and DIO, who is* also *very entertaining, and Makima, who is at least* interesting, *and you say* options *as if we were* furniture—ᶜ

He can't stop. He's leaning on the table again. Yuka is looking at him in total bewilderment, because she can't hear Ciel, and then she's laughing too, because he is, and doesn't know why.

*You'll pay for this*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*Loudly*, says Ciel. *You said I could be as loud as I wanted. I am going to be extremely loud.* ᶜ

Inside, at the table, Noise does something Mau has never once heard him do in all the time the room has existed.

He laughs.

* * *

When it's all calmed down, Echidna folds her paper up.

“We should clarify,” she says. ᴱᶜ

Mau looks at her.

“We don't want to *live* in you,” says Echidna. “Let that be clear as well. We have our rooms. In the north wing. We like them. Pride has her chair that wobbles. Sekhmet has her door to the bath. We're not leaving them.” ᴱᶜ

She looks round at the others. They nod. Even Sekhmet, in her sleep.

“We just want somewhere to visit,” says Echidna. “Sometimes. Not often. To sit at your table and drink from that cup that's always warm, and talk to your friends inside, and see what it's like. And to be near Satella, when she's there. Because she's ours, and she's been alone a long time, and we'd rather she wasn't alone in there either.” ᴱᶜ

Satella, at the table, has gone very still again.

“We'll be outside most of the time,” says Echidna. “As always. At our tea. On our step. In our rooms. You'll hardly notice we're there.” ᴱᶜ

“That,” says Mau, “is a lie.” ᴹ

Echidna smiles.

“Yes,” she says. “Mostly.” ᴱᶜ

* * *

That night, Noise builds seven rooms.

Mau feels him do it. Lying in bed with Yuka asleep on his shoulder, he feels it happening inside: the corridor getting longer, seven new doors opening along it, one after another, next to Satella's. Each one with a window. DIO insists.

He doesn't help. Noise doesn't want help. It's his.

And when they're finished, very late, Noise stands in the new corridor, inside, and looks at the seven doors.

*Ready*, he says. ᴺ

*Are you?* says Mau. ᴹ

A long pause.

*No*, says Noise. *But I want to be.* ᴺ
