## Chapter 33 — Two People

*When: B+455. "32." Late. The witches went home at dusk; Pride left a spoon on the table on purpose.*

DIO comes to find him after the house has gone quiet.

He's inside. He goes in most nights now, before he sleeps, to sit at the table for a while with whoever's there. Tonight it's only Noise, picking up spoons, and Ciel, pretending not to help him, and the cup.

DIO comes out of her door with her watering notebook.

She doesn't usually carry it at night. The plant itself is asleep on its windowsill in the Guest Inn; the notebook is where she keeps it with her. She sets it on the table, beside the cup, and sits down across from him, and folds her hands, and looks at him.

Then she unfolds them. Then she folds them again.

“I need to ask you something,” she says. ᴰ

* * *

“About Elmesia,” she says. ᴰ

He nods.

DIO looks at the notebook.

“When I'm in her garden,” she says, “I can't think properly. I say things I didn't plan to say. I hold the plant too tight. When the bird comes with a letter I read it three times and then I put it under my pillow, which is stupid, because I don't sleep on a pillow, I'm not that kind of thing.” ᴰ

She frowns at the notebook as if it's the notebook's fault.

“And I don't know what it is,” she says. “That's the problem. I don't know what it *is*. I know a lot of words for things. I've read most of Echidna's library. And I don't know which word it is.” ᴰ

She looks up at him.

“How do you know?” she says. “What you feel. Which kind it is. How do you tell?” ᴰ

* * *

He thinks about it for a long time.

He thinks about Yuka. The first time. Before he knew anything. Before he had words for it either.

“I don't think you tell by thinking about it,” he says. ᴹ

DIO looks at him.

“I think you tell by what you want,” he says. “Not what you feel. What you *want*. When it's a friend, you want them to be all right. You want them happy. You'd be glad if they were happy somewhere else, with someone else, as long as they were happy.” ᴹ

He looks at the cup.

“When it's the other kind,” he says, “you want them happy *with you*. You want to be the one who's there. You want to be the one they show things to. And if they were happy somewhere else, with someone else, you'd be glad for them, you would, but something in you would also break a bit.” ᴹ

He looks up.

“That's how I know,” he says. “With Yuka. I'd be glad if she were happy anywhere. But I want her to be happy *here*.” ᴹ

DIO is very still.

* * *

“Then what about Noise?” she says. ᴰ

Across the table, Noise drops a spoon.

DIO doesn't look at him. She keeps her eyes on Mau. Her face is doing the thing it does when she's very frightened of a question and has decided to ask it anyway.

“I always thought,” she says, “that what I felt for Noise was that. The other kind. From the beginning. I said so. I asked him if he wanted to be my husband. More than once. I *meant* it.” ᴰ

Noise has gone completely still in his chair, halfway through picking up the spoon.

“And now,” says DIO, “I don't know. Because I don't feel about Noise the way I feel in Elmesia's garden. And I don't know which one's wrong.” ᴰ

* * *

Mau is careful.

He's very careful. He can feel Noise behind him. He can feel Ciel, at the end of the table, not pretending anything anymore. He can feel what this means, to all three of them.

“Do you remember Ori,” he says. “At the start.” ᴹ

DIO's face goes tight.

“When you made her,” he says, gently. “You told me once. The first thing you wanted from her was for her to love you. The big kind. The kind you'd break the world for. Because that was the only kind you knew. You'd never been given words for any other kind.” ᴹ

DIO nods, very slightly.

“And Ori decided for herself,” he says. “How she wanted to love people. And you had to watch her do it. And learn.” ᴹ

He looks at her.

“I think with Noise,” he says, “you did the same thing. Noise was kind to you. He was there. He stood by his wall and let you follow him round and ask him a thousand questions. He never sent you away. And you'd never had anyone do that. So you thought it must be the big kind. Because that was the only word you had for someone who stayed.” ᴹ

He waits.

“But it wasn't,” he says. “I don't think it was. I think it was the other thing. The thing you have with Ciel. With me. With Makima. With Ori. You love him. You'd want him happy anywhere. That's a friend. That's a very, very good friend.” ᴹ

* * *

DIO doesn't say anything for a long time.

Beside Mau, at the table, Noise hasn't moved. Mau can feel him listening. Can feel something go through him that's very complicated and, underneath it all, very relieved.

“And Elmesia,” says DIO at last. Very quietly. ᴰ

“Do you remember what you were like?” says Mau. “Before the island?” ᴹ

DIO's chin comes up.

“I was perfectly dignified,” she says. ᴰ

“You were like a schoolgirl,” says Mau. ᴹ

DIO goes a colour that he can't see but can guess at.

“You checked the window every day for the bird,” he says. “You read her letters at the table and then pretended you hadn't. You asked me four times what to wear to the island. You made Ciel help you write back, and then you made her leave the room so she wouldn't see what you wrote.” ᴹ

*I saw anyway*, says Ciel. ᶜ

“And every time you made something,” says Mau, “every new leaf on that plant, every page of that notebook, every time you learned a new word from Echidna's books, there were only two people you always wanted to show. Every single time. Straight away. Before anyone else.” ᴹ

He holds up two fingers.

“Me,” he says. “And Elmesia.” ᴹ

* * *

Beside him, at the table, Noise makes a noise.

At the end of the table, Ciel makes a noise too. A pointed one. Sharper than Noise's.

And from the corridor, where he didn't know she was standing, Makima, who has come out of her room at the sound of DIO's voice, makes one as well. Smaller. Very dry.

Mau stops.

He looks at his two fingers.

“Five,” he says. “Sorry. Five. I counted you all in with me.” ᴹ

DIO stares at him.

Then she laughs.

Out loud, all at once, the way Makima laughed on the north-wing step. She puts both hands over her face and laughs into them, and her shoulders shake, and the notebook on the table slides an inch.

*Counted us in with you*, says Ciel. *Like* options. *Like* furniture. ᶜ

*I'm sorry*, says Mau. ᴹ

*You're not*, says Ciel. *You're never sorry. You just say it.* ᶜ

But she's smiling. He can feel it.

* * *

When DIO stops laughing, she takes her hands away from her face.

“Two people,” she says. “Out of everyone. Him and her.” ᴰ

“Him and her,” says Mau. ᴹ

“And I want her happy with me,” says DIO. Slowly. Testing it. Like someone putting their weight on a floor they're not sure of. “Not anywhere. With me. In her garden. Reading my letters. Looking at my plant.” ᴰ

She stops.

“That's the other kind,” she says. ᴰ

“I think so,” says Mau. ᴹ

DIO looks at her notebook for a long time.

“Oh,” she says. ᴰ

That's all. Just *oh*. Very small. The sound of something falling into place.

* * *

“You're not the first person to ask me that,” says Mau. ᴹ

He doesn't mean to say it. It just comes out. He's thinking about Rem. About every time Rem has come to find him, quietly, in the kitchen or on the porch, with that face. *Mau, I don't know what this is.* The date. The festival. Every step, she's come to him first.

DIO looks up sharply.

“Who else?” she says. ᴰ

“Nobody.” ᴹ

“You said I wasn't the first.” ᴰ

“I shouldn't have said that.” ᴹ

DIO's eyes narrow.

She looks at his face. Carefully. All over. The way she looked at the plant, in the Guest Inn, the first time it put out a leaf, as if it might be hiding something.

“There's a third,” she says. ᴰ

“I didn't say—” ᴹ

“There's a third,” says DIO. “You and Yuka. Me and—” She stops. Her face goes complicated. “Me and *her*. And someone else. In this house. Someone else came to you and asked you exactly the same thing.” ᴰ

He doesn't say anything.

That, he realises immediately, is the worst thing he could have done.

DIO's whole face lights up.

“*Who?*” ᴰ

“I'm not telling you.” ᴹ

“Is it Rem?” ᴰ

He doesn't move a single muscle in his face.

DIO looks at him for a long, long moment.

“It's Rem,” she says. “Rem came and asked you how to say it. To Umaru. They've been together for ages, they kiss on the porch when they think nobody's looking, and Rem has *never said it*. That's it, isn't it? That's the third. Rem looks at her like I look at the bird and she's never once told her.” ᴰ

“I didn't tell you that,” says Mau. ᴹ

“No,” says DIO, enormously pleased. “You didn't. I worked it out. All by myself.” ᴰ

* * *

She picks up her notebook.

She stands up. She holds it against her chest. She looks at him across the table, and her face is still a colour he can't see, and her eyes are very bright.

“Help me,” she says. ᴰ

He waits.

“With her,” says DIO. “I don't know how to say it. I've never said it. I don't know how it's done. You've done it. Help me.” ᴰ

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

DIO nods once, briskly, as if a contract has been signed.

Then she turns to go back to her room. At the door, she stops.

“Noise,” she says, without turning round. ᴰ

At the table, Noise goes still.

“I'm sorry,” says DIO. “For asking you all those times. If you wanted to be my husband. I think I was asking the wrong question.” ᴰ

A long pause.

“I think,” says DIO, “what I meant was, will you be my friend. For a very long time. And I didn't know how to ask that, so I asked the other thing.” ᴰ

Noise is quiet for a long moment.

“Yes,” he says. ᴺ

DIO nods again.

She goes into her room with her notebook and closes the door. Not precisely. Just closed.

Noise sits at the table with his hands round the cup.

*Are you all right?* says Mau. ᴹ

*Yes*, says Noise. A pause. *I think I've been waiting a long time for her to ask that one.* ᴺ
