# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 35 — Rem Asks

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 35
**Source episode:** S13E134
**Continuity state:** B+456 ("31"). Early morning, the kitchen, before anyone else is up. Rem comes to Mau the way she always has: first. Before the date, before the festival, she came to him; she comes now. She wants to tell Umaru she loves her. Out loud. To her face. She has said it once, to Subaru, with Mau sitting there, but never to Umaru. She's afraid, and it's not the fear of a no. It's the old one: that saying it hands Umaru something that could be used to pull her, the way Subaru once used what she was only beginning to feel. Mau tells her Umaru isn't Subaru; Umaru drags her into things because she wants her there, not because she's owed. He doesn't plan it for her. He asks her where she'd want to say it. Rem knows at once: the Fort, on the roof, where Umaru goes to watch the stars and pretends she's looking for satellites. Tonight.

*When: B+456. "31." Very early. The notebook is still on the kitchen table where Yuka left it last night. Rem doesn't touch it.*

Rem is in the kitchen before the sun.

He felt her come down the stairs while it was still dark. He tried to get his arm out from under Yuka without waking her. Her fingers closed on his cuff as soon as he moved.

“Are you coming back?” she asks. Her eyes are still shut. ʸ

“I think Rem wants tea.” ᴹ

Yuka opens one eye. It's too early for her to focus it very well. She finds the back of his neck by touch and pulls him down for a kiss that nearly takes him back to sleep.

“Go,” she says, with her mouth still against his. “I'll keep your spot.” ʸ

He tucks the warm edge of the blanket around her shoulder. She takes his pillow into her arms the moment his head clears it. In the hall he has to stop to find his sleeve, which she's managed to fold halfway back up to his elbow.

Then he goes down.

She's at the table with a cup of tea she hasn't drunk. In her plain dress. Hair still loose. Her hands round the cup.

She looks up when he comes in.

She doesn't look surprised. She never does. She's done this before. The night before the first date. The morning of the festival. Every step, every time, before she takes it, she comes down to the kitchen before the sun and waits, and he comes.

He sits down across from her.

“Mau,” she says. ᴿᵉ

“I'm here.” ᴹ

* * *

She doesn't say it straight away.

She turns the cup in her hands. She looks at the window, where the sky is going from dark to grey. She looks at the notebook on the table, which she doesn't touch.

“I want to tell her,” she says. ᴿᵉ

He waits.

“Umaru,” says Rem. “I want to tell her. That I love her. Out loud. To her face. Not—” ᴿᵉ

She stops.

“I said it once,” she says. “To Subaru. The day after he came. With you sitting there. *I love Umaru. I'm happy.*” ᴿᵉ

“I remember.” ᴹ

“I've never said it to her,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

She looks down at the cup.

“Isn't that stupid?” she says. “We've been together for so long. She holds my hand under the table. We kiss on the porch when we think nobody's looking. And I said it to him, to the one person in the world who least needed to hear it, and I've never once said it to her.” ᴿᵉ

* * *

“Why?” he says. ᴹ

He asks it gently. He already thinks he knows. He wants her to say it.

Rem is quiet for a long time.

“Because last time,” she says, “when I was only just starting to feel something, before I even knew what it was, someone took it. And held it up in front of me. And used it to pull me.” ᴿᵉ

Her hands tighten on the cup.

“*You feel this, so you owe me this.* That's what it was. He never said it like that. But that's what it was. And I said no, and I'm glad I said no, and I'd say it again. But I learned something. I learned that the moment you say it out loud, it isn't only yours anymore. It's in their hands too. And they can do anything with it.” ᴿᵉ

She looks up at him.

“I'm not afraid she'll say no,” says Rem. “I think—I think she won't. I think she knows. Everybody knows. Even DIO knows; she looked at me at breakfast yesterday like she'd solved a puzzle.” ᴿᵉ

He keeps his face very still.

“I told Ram,” she says. “Last night. Everything. She listened to all of it and then she said *about time* and went to sleep.” ᴿᵉ

Mau almost laughs.

“I'm afraid of the other thing,” says Rem. “Of giving it to someone. Of putting it in someone's hands. Even hers.” ᴿᵉ

* * *

He thinks about it for a long time.

He thinks about Umaru. Umaru on the counter eating the reject pile. Umaru with her fork pointed at him like a sword, *you're going to carry her down, you promised*. Umaru at the kitchen table at midnight with her chin in her hands, staring at the workshop window, waiting for Maki's pin to hold.

Umaru, dragging Rem into everything. The Fort. The tournaments. The games. The castle day. Every single thing, every time, she turns round and holds out her hand and says *Rem, come on*.

“Can I tell you what I see?” he says. ᴹ

Rem nods.

“Subaru wanted you to come back to him,” says Mau, “because he thought you owed him. Because of what you felt. He held it up and said *this means you have to*.” ᴹ

He leans forward.

“Umaru drags you into everything,” he says. “The Fort. The games. The tournament at midnight. She turns round every time and holds out her hand and says *come on*. And she's never once said *you have to*. She's never once said *you owe me*. She just wants you there. Because it's better with you there. That's all it's ever been.” ᴹ

Rem is very still.

“She doesn't want to pull you,” he says. “She wants you to come. That's different. That's the whole difference.” ᴹ

* * *

Rem looks at her tea.

Outside, the sky has gone from grey to pale. A bird starts up in the orchard.

“If I give it to her,” says Rem slowly, “she won't use it.” ᴿᵉ

“I don't think she'd know how,” says Mau. “I think if you gave it to her, she'd hold it like Ori holds the little carved dragon. Very carefully. With both hands. And show everyone.” ᴹ

Rem laughs. A small one. Surprised out of her.

“She would,” she says. “She'd *tell* everyone. She'd make a banner.” ᴿᵉ

“She'd make a banner,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

Rem puts her hand over her mouth. She's still laughing, a little. And her eyes have gone very bright.

* * *

“How should I do it?” she says. ᴿᵉ

He almost answers.

He has it ready. A whole plan. It came to him while she was talking: where, when, what she should say, how to get Umaru there without her guessing. He could lay it out on the table in front of Rem like a map, every step.

He stops.

*Next time it'll be before*, Yuka said. On the porch step. *Not during. Before.*

He doesn't lay it out.

“Where would you want to say it?” he asks instead. ᴹ

Rem blinks.

“It's yours,” he says. “Not mine. I'll help with anything. But I'm not going to decide it for you. Where would *you* want to be? When you say it?” ᴹ

Rem looks at him for a long moment.

Then her face changes. He watches it change. Watches her stop looking for an answer from him and start looking inside, for her own.

It doesn't take long. He can see it come to her. He can see her see a place.

“The roof,” she says. ᴿᵉ

He waits.

“The Fort roof,” says Rem. “At night. Umaru goes up there. Most nights, when it's clear, after everyone's gone to bed. She takes a blanket and lies on her back and looks at the stars. She says she's looking for satellites.” ᴿᵉ

Her mouth does something soft.

“There aren't any satellites,” she says. “Not here. Not in this world. She knows that. She looks anyway. She says one day there might be one, and she wants to be the first to see it.” ᴿᵉ

She looks at him.

“I go up sometimes,” she says. “And lie next to her. And she points at stars and tells me which ones she thinks might be satellites. And none of them are. And I never tell her.” ᴿᵉ

“Then that's where,” says Mau. ᴹ

* * *

“Tonight,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

She says it before she can stop herself. He sees her hear it. Sees her decide to let it stand.

“Tonight,” she says again, more firmly. “If it's clear.” ᴿᵉ

*It'll be clear*, says Ciel, inside. *I checked. Not a cloud all day.* ᶜ

“It'll be clear,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

Rem nods.

She picks up her tea, at last, and drinks it, although it's cold.

“Will you—” she starts. ᴿᵉ

Mau waits. Rem folds her hands one over the other.

“Could you be close?” she asks. “I'll see if she wants anyone out there. I just want to know where you are in case I get halfway up and have to come all the way back down again.” ᴿᵉ

“I can be there,” he says. “Do you want Yuka there, too?” ᴹ

“Yes. The two of you can go and sit where the ladder starts.” Rem lets her fingers go loose. “And afterwards. Will you be in the kitchen? In the morning? Before the sun?” ᴿᵉ

He looks at her.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Rem smiles. Properly. All the way.

“Good,” she says. “I'll come down and tell you how it went. I always do.” ᴿᵉ

* * *

The house comes down for breakfast an hour later.

Umaru is first, as always, in her dressing gown, yawning, with her hair everywhere. She stumbles into the kitchen and sees Rem at the table and lights up the way she always does, the way the whole room lights up a little when Umaru sees Rem, though Umaru herself has never noticed it.

“You're up *early*,” says Umaru. “Did you make the rolls? Are there rolls? Rem, there have to be rolls, I dreamed about rolls—” ᵁ

“There are rolls,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

Umaru flings herself into the chair beside her.

Rem passes her the rolls.

And under the table, where nobody can see except Mau, who sees everything, Rem's other hand is clenched very tight in her lap, and shaking, very slightly, all through breakfast.

He catches her eye across the table.

He doesn't say anything. He just nods. Once.

Rem takes a breath.

And at the other end of the table, DIO, who has come down for breakfast in her own body for the first time in a week, looks from Rem to Umaru and back again, and smiles into her tea like a cat that has found the cream.

* * *

Umaru finds him in the afternoon.

She comes into the workshop yard where he's sitting with Taihei, who has been helping Mai carry timber and has stopped for water, and she plants herself in front of the two of them in her dressing gown with her hands on her hips and her face doing something very determined and very frightened at the same time.

“I need to talk to you,” she says. “Both of you. My brothers. Now.” ᵁ

Taihei puts down his water.

Mau doesn't say anything. He has a feeling he knows.

He does.

It all comes out at once, the way everything comes out of Umaru. How long she's loved Rem. How she's never said the words, not once, not even with Rem's hand in hers under every table in the house. How she's afraid that if she says them, Rem will think she's being asked for them back, because of Subaru, because of what he did, and say them because she's kind and not because she's ready. How she wants to say it anyway. How she doesn't know how.

“How do I say it,” says Umaru, “so she knows I don't want anything for it?” ᵁ

Taihei looks at Mau.

Mau looks at the sky.

It's very clear. Not a cloud. Ciel was right this morning. It'll be clear all night.

He thinks about Rem at the kitchen table before the sun. *Where would you want to say it?* *The roof.* *Tonight.* He thinks about how it isn't his to say. Either half of it.

He doesn't say either half.

“Go up on the roof tonight,” he says. ᴹ

Umaru blinks.

“The Fort roof,” says Mau. “It'll be clear. Look at the stars. The way you always do.” ᴹ

“That's not *advice*,” says Umaru. “That's just what I *do*.” ᵁ

“If you want to,” says Mau. “And if you'd like me to come as far as the ladder, tell me. I can bring Yuka.” ᴹ

Umaru spends a little while picking a thread off her sleeve. “I'd like her to come,” she says. “You can sit next to her and be quiet until I get up there.” ᵁ

Umaru looks at him with deep suspicion.

Taihei, beside him, looks at Mau for a long moment, and then, very slowly, picks his water back up and drinks it, and doesn't say anything, and Mau can tell from the side of his face that he's understood exactly what Mau is doing and is trying very hard not to smile.

“And when you say it,” says Taihei, to his sister, gently, “say that part too. That you don't want anything for it. Out loud. She'll need to hear it.” ᵀᵃ

Umaru looks from one brother to the other.

“You two are being *weird*,” she says. ᵁ

“Roof,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Tonight,” says Taihei. ᵀᵃ

Umaru narrows her eyes at both of them. Then she turns round and stalks off across the yard in her dressing gown, muttering.

Taihei waits until she's gone.

“Rem came to you this morning,” he says. Not a question. ᵀᵃ

“I can't tell you that,” says Mau. ᴹ

“You just did,” says Taihei, and smiles into his water. ᵀᵃ
