## Chapter 33 — Satellites

*When: B+456. Night, clear. Not a cloud. Ciel was right.*

Umaru is already on the roof when Rem comes up.

She always is, on clear nights. Rem knew she would be. She climbs the ladder at the back of the Fort with the spare blanket over her shoulder, the way she has a hundred times, and puts her head up over the edge of the roof, and there's Umaru, flat on her back in the middle of the boards on her own blanket, with her hands behind her head and her dressing gown wrapped round her, looking up.

“Rem,” says Umaru, without looking round. “You came.” ᵁ

“I came,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

She climbs up. She spreads the spare blanket beside Umaru's. She lies down on it, on her back, close enough that their shoulders nearly touch.

The sky is enormous.

It always is, up here. No lamps. No clouds. The whole of it, from one edge of the world to the other, thick with stars, more stars than Rem ever saw in the world she came from. They go all the way down to the hills on every side.

“Okay,” says Umaru. “So. *That* one.” ᵁ

She points.

* * *

It's a game. It's always been a game.

Umaru points at a star and says it might be a satellite. And explains why: because it's moving, or because it's a funny colour, or because it's in the wrong place, or because she just has a feeling. And Rem looks where she's pointing, and says *maybe*, or *I don't know*, or *it looks like a star*. And Umaru says *you have no imagination*, and points at another one.

There are no satellites. Not here. There's never been anyone in this world to put them up. Umaru knows that. She knows it better than anyone; she's the one who explained to Rem, the first night, what a satellite even was.

She looks anyway. Every clear night. *One day there might be one*, she said. *And I want to be the first to see it.*

Rem has never told her she's wrong. Not once.

“That one's moving,” says Umaru. “See? Just a bit. Left. See it?” ᵁ

Rem looks.

It isn't moving. It's a star.

“Maybe,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

“You *always* say maybe.” ᵁ

“It might be.” ᴿᵉ

“It *might* be,” Umaru agrees, delighted, and points at another one.

* * *

Rem lies on her back on the blanket and doesn't look at the stars.

She looks at the side of Umaru's face. In the starlight. Her hair everywhere on the blanket. Her hand up, pointing. Her mouth going, explaining something about orbits, very fast, the way she explains everything.

She thinks about the kitchen. This morning. Before the sun. Mau across the table.

*She doesn't want to pull you. She wants you to come.*

She thinks about the yard, the day Subaru came. About the thing she was only starting to feel, before she knew what it was, being picked up and held in front of her face. *You feel this, so.*

She thinks about Umaru on the kitchen counter with a fork. Umaru at midnight with her chin in her hands. Umaru turning round, every time, every single time, holding out her hand. *Rem, come on.*

Never once *you have to*.

Never once *you owe me*.

Rem takes a breath.

“Umaru,” she says. ᴿᵉ

* * *

“Mm?” says Umaru. Still pointing. ᵁ

“I love you,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

* * *

Umaru stops pointing.

Her hand stays up, in the air, for a moment, as if it's forgotten what it was doing. Then, slowly, it comes down onto the blanket.

She doesn't say anything.

Rem has never, in all the time she has known Umaru, heard her not say anything. Umaru talks in her sleep. Umaru talks with her mouth full. Umaru talked all the way through the war, through the night before the quarry, through every silence the house has ever had. Umaru has never once in her life been quiet when there was something to be said.

She's quiet now.

She lies on her back on the blanket, perfectly still, looking up at the stars, and doesn't say anything at all.

Rem's heart is going very fast.

She doesn't take it back. She doesn't say anything else. She lies there beside Umaru and lets it be said, and lets it be in Umaru's hands now, and waits to see what Umaru does with it.

* * *

Umaru sits up.

Slowly. She sits up on the blanket with her dressing gown falling off one shoulder and her hair everywhere and looks down at Rem.

Her face is doing something Rem has never seen.

“Do you mean,” says Umaru. Very small. Smaller than Rem has ever heard her voice. ᵁ

She stops. She tries again.

“Do you mean it the big way?” she says. “Not the—not the friend way. Not the Fort way. The big way. Like Yuka and Mau. Like—” ᵁ

“The big way,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

Umaru looks at her.

And then her face crumples, all at once, the way a little girl's face crumples, and she bursts into tears.

* * *

Rem sits up too, fast, frightened.

“Umaru—” ᴿᵉ

“I *know*,” says Umaru, through the tears. “I know, I know, I'm sorry, I'm not—it's not—” ᵁ

She's crying so hard she can't get the words out. She puts both hands over her face. Her shoulders shake.

Rem doesn't know what to do. She reaches out, and stops, and reaches out again, and puts a hand on Umaru's back.

Umaru leans into it.

“I've loved you,” she says, through her hands, “since the first *racing tournament* you played.” ᵁ

Rem goes very still.

“The racing one,” says Umaru. “The first one. You didn't want to play. You said you didn't know how. And I made you. And you were *terrible*. You were the worst I've ever seen. You drove backwards for a whole lap. And you were laughing. You were laughing so hard you couldn't hold the controller.” ᵁ

She takes her hands away from her face. Her face is wet and red and completely undone.

“And I looked at you laughing,” she says, “and I thought *oh no*.” ᵁ

* * *

“Then why didn't you—” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

“Because of *him*,” says Umaru. ᵁ

She wipes her face on her dressing gown sleeve. It doesn't help.

“Because of Subaru,” she says. “Because of what he did. I heard about it. Everyone heard about it. That he took what you felt and used it to pull you. And I thought—” ᵁ

She stops.

“I thought, if I say it,” she says, “if I tell her, then she'll think I want something. She'll think I'm doing it too. She'll think *oh, here's another one who thinks I owe them because of what I feel*. And she'll—she'll be kind about it, because Rem's always kind, but she'll be careful of me after. And I'd rather have you not careful of me. I'd rather have you on the roof not knowing than careful of me knowing.” ᵁ

She looks down at Rem's hand, which is still on her back.

“So I didn't say,” says Umaru. “I just kept dragging you into things. Because I wanted you there. And I thought, if I just keep asking her to come, and never ask for anything else, maybe that's enough. Maybe that's all I'll ever get, and it's enough.” ᵁ

* * *

Rem doesn't say anything for a long time.

She sits on the blanket on the roof of the Fort under all the stars in the world, with her hand on Umaru's back, and feels it.

All this time.

Both of them. Lying here, night after night, side by side, looking at stars that weren't satellites. Both of them holding the same thing. Both of them afraid to put it in the other's hands, for the same reason, because of the same man.

Neither of them was ever going to pull.

Rem starts to laugh.

She doesn't mean to. It comes up out of her the way it came up in the first tournament, when she drove backwards for a whole lap. Helpless. Wet. She puts her other hand over her mouth and it doesn't help.

Umaru stares at her.

“*What*,” she says. ᵁ

“Nothing,” says Rem. “Nothing. It's just—both of us. All this time. Both of us afraid of the same thing.” ᴿᵉ

Umaru's face changes.

And then she's laughing too. Through the tears. The two of them sitting on the roof of the Fort in the middle of the night, laughing and crying, with Umaru's dressing gown half off and Rem's hair coming loose, until neither of them can breathe.

* * *

They lie back down, eventually.

Side by side on the blankets. Closer, this time. Rem's shoulder against Umaru's. Umaru's hand finding Rem's on the boards between them, and holding on.

They look at the sky.

After a while, Umaru lifts her other hand and points.

“That one,” she says. Her voice is still thick. “That one's a satellite.” ᵁ

Rem looks.

It isn't. It's a star. Like all the others.

“Yes,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

Umaru goes still.

“Yes?” she says. ᵁ

“Yes,” says Rem. “I see it. It's moving. Just a bit. Left.” ᴿᵉ

Umaru turns her head on the blanket and looks at her.

Rem keeps looking at the star.

“It's a satellite,” says Rem. “You found one. You were the first.” ᴿᵉ

Umaru doesn't say anything. She doesn't need to. She holds Rem's hand so tight on the boards between them that it hurts, and Rem lets it, and they lie there under the satellite that isn't, for a long, long time.

* * *

Rem comes down to the kitchen before the sun.

Mau is already there. At the table. With a cup of tea for each of them.

She sits down across from him.

He looks at her face. He doesn't need to ask. She can see him see it.

“She said it back,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

He smiles. All the way.

“She's loved me since the first tournament,” says Rem. “She didn't say because she thought I'd think she wanted something. Because of Subaru. We were both—” ᴿᵉ

She stops. She's laughing again.

“We were both afraid of the same thing,” she says. ᴿᵉ

Mau doesn't say anything. He pushes her tea across the table.

She drinks it. It's hot. He made it right before she came down. He always knows.

From upstairs, very faintly, comes a sound. A rustling. A thumping. Something being dragged across a floor. And then Umaru's voice, muffled, through the ceiling, saying something urgent to somebody, possibly Ori, about *paint*.

Rem looks at the ceiling.

“She's making a banner,” she says. ᴿᵉ

“I know,” says Mau. ᴹ

Rem puts her face in her hands and laughs until the sun comes up.
