## Chapter 37 — The Invitation

*When: B+457 and 458. "30", then "29". Beside "30" Umaru has stuck a small paper flag. It says REM.*

The banner goes up before breakfast.

Mau sees it from the kitchen window. Everybody sees it. It's hard not to. It's the length of the Fort roof, made of three bedsheets sewn together badly, and painted on it in letters as tall as Ori, in at least four colours that Mau can't see and can tell from the way Yuka screams with laughter are very loud ones:

REM SAID IT

And underneath, smaller, in different handwriting, clearly added later by someone standing on someone else's shoulders:

(AND SO DID UMARU)

Rem sees it at the same time as everyone else, because she's at the table with her tea, and she goes a colour that even Mau can guess at, and puts her cup down very carefully, and gets up, and walks out of the kitchen and across the yard to the Fort with tremendous dignity to take it down.

She's outvoted.

Umaru calls the vote from the roof. Agott gets far enough into the opposition's case to demand an end date. Chloe, sitting with the rest of the Fort on the steps, wants to know if the writing will wash out of their sheets. Ori argues to keep the banner through supper. There are enough hands in the air that Rem gives up trying to count them.

The banner stays.

Rem comes back to the kitchen, sits down, picks up her tea, and drinks it, with her face in her cup, and her shoulders shaking. Umaru comes in a minute later and sits down beside her as if nothing has happened, and steals a roll off her plate.

Under the table, Mau sees them holding hands.

* * *

By the second day, it's become a thing.

It starts at lunch. Umaru is on the couch in the common room in her hamster hoodie, the one with the ears, with a bag of chips in her lap that she has been guarding all morning with her life, and the Fort is circling.

Rem sits down beside her.

“Umaru,” she says, quite calmly. “You know I love you.” ᴿᵉ

Umaru goes still.

“Could you share your bag of chips with the house?” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

Umaru looks at her with an expression of profound betrayal.

Then she holds out the bag.

The Fort descends on it.

* * *

Umaru gets her revenge at supper.

Rem is about to get up to fetch the water jug from the far end of the table, and Umaru, who is lying with her head in Rem's lap and has no intention of moving it, looks up at her.

“Rem,” she says sweetly. “You know I love you.” ᵁ

Rem stops halfway to standing.

“Could you stay exactly where you are,” says Umaru, “so I don't have to get up?” ᵁ

Rem sits back down.

Milim fetches the water.

* * *

By the end of the day they don't say *please* anymore. Not to each other. Not once. Every favour, every small thing, every *pass the salt*, starts the same way now: a name, and *you know I love you*, and then the asking. Said lightly. Said laughing. Said, Mau notices, every single time, as if neither of them can quite get over being allowed to.

It isn't a lever. It never once is. It's the opposite of one. It's the thing Umaru promised on the roof, turned into a joke so they can say it fifty times a day without it ever getting heavy.

Mau watches them do it from across the table and doesn't say anything, and is so happy for the two of them that he has to look at his rice.

* * *

Umaru catches him at it.

She comes over after supper in the hamster hoodie, with what's left of the chips, and stands in front of his chair, and looks down at him.

She takes out one chip. She holds it out.

He takes it.

“You don't get to complain,” says Umaru, “about anything. Ever. You sent us both up the same roof.” ᵁ

“I'm not complaining,” says Mau. “I'm taking notes.” ᴹ

Umaru narrows her eyes.

Then she takes out another chip and puts it in his hand.

“Good student,” she says, and goes back to Rem. ᵁ

* * *

He writes to Elmesia after breakfast.

He's careful. He sits at the kitchen table with Ciel at his shoulder, inside, reading every word, and writes it three times before it's right. Because it isn't his. None of it is his to say. Not what DIO feels. Not what Elmesia feels. He knows both. He can't say either.

*Elmesia,*

*DIO would like to visit the garden again. With the plant. She'd like to see the place you said it might have a holiday.*

*She'd like it to be just the two of you this time. I'll bring her through the Exchange. But I'll stay in the little room with the very good view, with the soldier, and you won't see me. Unless you need me.*

*Tomorrow evening, if that suits you. Or whenever does.*

*Mau*

*P.S. She doesn't know I'm writing this. Well. She knows. She asked me to. She just doesn't know what I've said. Please don't tell her I said she'd like it to be just the two of you. She'll be furious.*

He reads it again.

*That's honest*, says Ciel. *And you didn't say anything that isn't yours.* ᶜ

*Except the postscript.* ᴹ

*The postscript is extremely yours*, says Ciel. *It's the most yours thing you've ever written.* ᶜ

He sends it with one of the soldiers through the Exchange anchor, to sit on Elmesia's desk.

* * *

The bird comes back the same afternoon.

Sleek and grey and unimpressed. It lands on the kitchen table between the teapot and the bread, holds out its leg, and looks at Mau as if he has kept it waiting.

He gives it bread first. It considers this beneath it. It eats the bread.

The letter is short. Elmesia's loops are tighter than usual, as if she wrote it fast.

*Yes.*

*Tomorrow evening. I'll have the garden lit.*

*E.*

*P.S. I won't tell her. I'm too busy being nervous to tell anyone anything. Is that normal? You're married. Tell me it's normal.*

He shows it to Yuka.

Yuka reads it twice and then has to sit down on the floor of the kitchen and laugh into her knees.

* * *

DIO panics for a day and a half.

Mau has seen DIO frightened before. Before the island. In the war. In the nights after the Trial, when she sat in the corridor inside, outside his door, because she didn't know how else to help.

He has never seen her like this.

She doesn't sleep. She paces the corridor inside all night, past her own door and Satella's and the seven witches' and back, and Noise, who is trying to tidy up the spoons, has to keep stepping out of her way. She changes her mind about what to wear eleven times. She makes Ciel come into her room and help her choose, and then makes Ciel leave because Ciel is looking at her *like that*, and then makes Ciel come back because she can't decide.

*I'm not looking at her like anything*, Ciel tells Mau, at three in the morning, offended. ᶜ

*You're a little bit looking at her like something*, says Mau. ᴹ

*I'm looking at her*, says Ciel, *like someone who has watched her fall in love with a queen through a window for six months and is tired of waiting.* ᶜ

* * *

Makima gives DIO advice.

Nobody asked her to. She comes and stands in DIO's doorway inside while DIO is holding up two dresses that look the same to everyone but DIO, and watches for a while, and then says:

“Say the plain thing.” ᴹᵏ

DIO turns round.

“That's my advice,” says Makima. “I gave it to Yuka. It worked. When you get there, and you don't know what to say, say the plain thing. Not the clever thing. You'll want to say the clever thing. Don't.” ᴹᵏ

DIO looks at her for a long moment.

“What's the plain thing?” she says. ᴰ

“You know what it is,” says Makima. “You've known since the night you asked him about it. You just haven't said it out loud yet.” ᴹᵏ

She turns to go. At the door, she stops.

“The blue one,” she says. “Not the other one. The blue one.” ᴹᵏ

“They're *both* blue,” says DIO. ᴰ

“No,” says Makima. “They're not.” ᴹᵏ

She goes.

DIO looks at the two dresses for a long time.

She chooses the one Makima meant.

* * *

Ori lends DIO the dragon.

The little carved one. The sitting one. The one Mau found for her at the stall in town, that she held up in front of Kamish's nose and said *this is what it looks like*.

She comes to find Mau on the porch on the evening of the second day, with it in both hands, and holds it out.

“For DIO,” she says. “For luck. For tomorrow.” ᴼ

He looks at it.

“You love that dragon,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” says Ori. “That's why it's lucky. Things you love are luckier. That's a rule.” ᴼ

“Whose rule?” ᴹ

“Mine,” says Ori. “I just made it. It's a Fort rule. It's official.” ᴼ

He takes the dragon.

“She has to give it back,” says Ori. “After. It's a lend. Not a give.” ᴼ

“I'll tell her.” ᴹ

Ori nods, satisfied, and goes back inside.

He sits on the porch with the little carved dragon in his hand and passes it inward, to the table, where DIO is sitting with her head in her hands. She looks at it for a long time. Then she picks it up, and holds it very carefully, and doesn't say anything at all.

* * *

He realises the problem late on the second night.

He's lying in bed with Yuka asleep on his shoulder, going over it. Tomorrow evening. The garden. The little room with the view. DIO with her plant and the dragon and the blue dress. Elmesia with the garden lit.

Two people who love each other, who have never said it, who are both so frightened of saying it that one of them hasn't slept in a day and a half and the other one wrote *is that normal?* in a postscript.

And him. In the little room. *Unless you need me.*

He thinks about how it went with Rem. *Where would you want to say it?* He didn't plan it. He didn't decide it. He asked, and she knew.

He thinks about how it went with DIO, at the table. *Help me. I don't know how it's done. You've done it.*

He has done it. Once. With Yuka. And looking back at it, he isn't sure he did it well. He isn't sure he knew what he was doing at all. He isn't sure *anyone* in that garden tomorrow is going to know what they're doing.

He needs someone who does.

He looks down at the face on his shoulder.

She's asleep. Her mouth is a little open. Her hand is in his shirt. She's the only colour in the room, even in the dark.

She's the one who knew. All along. Who stole the pickles and sat in the doorway and said *stay* and *don't steal my apology* and *I'll fight you every meal*. Who says the plain thing better than anyone he knows, now. Who knows exactly what it is to love someone so much it frightens you, and say it anyway.

“Yuka,” he says. ᴹ

She mumbles something.

“Will you come with me tomorrow?” he says. ᴹ

She opens one eye.

“Where?” ʸ

“To Elmesia's garden. With DIO. I think—” He stops. “I think I'm going to need an expert.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him for a long moment, one-eyed, in the dark.

Then she smiles. All the way. Even half asleep.

“Will there be food?” she says. ʸ

“It's Elmesia. There'll be cakes.” ᴹ

“Then yes,” says Yuka. ʸ

She closes her eye again. He thinks she's gone back to sleep. A second later she reaches up with both hands, finds his face, and brings it down far enough to kiss him.

“And at least one dance,” she says. ʸ

“In the garden?” ᴹ

“Somewhere nobody's waiting for you to say anything clever.” ʸ

“I'll find somewhere.” ᴹ

She takes this as settled. Her hands slide off his cheeks. He catches one before it goes back into his shirt and presses a kiss to her palm. She closes her fingers over it and is asleep before he can ask whether she wants him to bring the good shoes.
