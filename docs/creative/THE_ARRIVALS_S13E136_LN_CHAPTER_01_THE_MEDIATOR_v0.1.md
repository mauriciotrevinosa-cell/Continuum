# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 84
## Chapter 37 — The Mediator

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 84
**Reading order:** 37
**Source episode:** S13E136
**Continuity state:** B+459 ("28"), evening. Yuka's point of view. Elmesia's garden, lit. DIO with the plant, the blue dress and Ori's lucky dragon; Elmesia waiting at the foot of the narrow stair. Mau meant to stay in the little room with the soldier; within ten minutes he's been fetched down to a bench between the two of them, because neither can get a sentence out, and he ends up as mediator. He fetches his expert. The expert, Yuka, sits on the bench, eats the cakes, laughs, and says, "You two can do it." They can't. In the end Mau says it for them, plainly, because nobody else will: DIO loves Elmesia, the romantic way, and Elmesia loves DIO, the same way. Both of them go red to the ears, which Yuka can see and Mau can't, because neither of them managed to say it to the other. Then they say it properly, to each other, without him. DIO tells Mau he still only owes her two; the letter lie is forgiven. Mau and Yuka take their promised dance by the pool and then sit under a tree to wait. Yuka puts her head on his chest; a let go; they both fall asleep. They're woken by DIO, alarmed, because it's very late and they should have been home hours ago.

*When: B+459. Evening into night. "28." Under it, in Yuka's hand: "EXPERT". Underlined.*

It goes wrong in ten minutes.

Yuka knows it will. She knows it the moment they come down the narrow stair from the little room with the very good view, the soldier settling back into its chair by the window behind them, and Elmesia is standing at the bottom with her lantern, and DIO is standing on the second-to-last step with her plant.

They look at each other.

Neither of them says anything.

Yuka knows this. She's been this. Two people who've written each other a hundred letters standing face to face for the first time since they understood what the letters were, and every single word they know falling straight out of their heads.

“You came,” says Elmesia, at last. ᴱˡ

“I was invited,” says DIO. ᴰ

“Yes,” says Elmesia. ᴱˡ

Silence.

“The plant,” says DIO, and holds it out. ᴰ

“Yes,” says Elmesia, and takes it. ᴱˡ

Silence.

Mau, beside Yuka on the stair, makes a very small sound.

* * *

He's supposed to go back up.

That was the plan. He wrote it in the letter. *I'll stay in the little room with the very good view, with the soldier, and you won't see me.* He was going to bring DIO down, and go back up, and sit with the soldier and look at the city, and leave them to it. And Yuka was going to sit with him and eat the cakes Elmesia had sent up, and that would be her whole job.

He turns to go back up the stair.

“Mau,” says DIO. Very fast. ᴰ

He stops.

“Stay,” says DIO. “For a minute. Just—just for a minute. Until we—” ᴰ

She doesn't finish.

Elmesia, holding the plant, looks at him with an expression of naked relief.

“Yes,” she says. “Stay. Please. Just for a minute.” ᴱˡ

Mau looks at Yuka.

Yuka looks at him.

It is very, very hard not to laugh.

* * *

The minute lasts an hour.

They walk through the garden, the four of them. Down the lit paths, past the beds of flowers and the little fountains and the trees with lanterns hung in them. Elmesia shows DIO the place where she thinks the plant might like a holiday. A sheltered corner by a wall, with good light.

At least, that's what she's trying to do.

What actually happens is that Elmesia says, “This is the—” and stops, and looks at DIO, and DIO looks at the corner, and says, “It's very—” and stops, and they both look at Mau.

And Mau says, “The light's good here.” ᴹ

And Elmesia says, “Yes, that's what I—yes.” ᴱˡ

And DIO says, “The light. Yes.” ᴰ

And they walk on.

This happens at every single bed in the garden. Yuka counts. Eleven times.

By the eleventh time, Mau has given up walking a step behind them. He's walking between them. Literally between them, in the middle of the path, with DIO on one side holding the lucky dragon and Elmesia on the other holding the plant, both of them talking *to him*, about each other, as if the other one weren't there.

“Is she cold?” says Elmesia, to Mau. “Ask her if she's cold. There's a shawl.” ᴱˡ

“Are you cold?” says Mau, to DIO. ᴹ

“Tell her I'm not cold,” says DIO, to Mau. “Tell her it's a very good shawl, though. Tell her I noticed the shawl.” ᴰ

“She's not cold,” says Mau, to Elmesia. “She noticed the shawl.” ᴹ

Yuka, walking behind them, has to stop and hold onto a tree.

* * *

He sends for his expert after the fountain.

They've reached a bench by a big still pool with lanterns floating on it, and DIO and Elmesia have sat down on it, at opposite ends, as far apart as a bench allows, with the plant on the seat between them like a chaperone. And Mau is standing in front of them, in the lantern light, looking from one to the other with an expression Yuka has only ever seen on him once before: in the basin, when he saw how many devils were coming over the rim.

He turns round and looks at Yuka.

*Help*, his face says.

Yuka comes over.

She doesn't hurry. She comes over slowly, with the basket of cakes that Elmesia sent up to the little room on her arm, and sits down on the bench. Right in the middle. Between DIO and Elmesia. On the plant's side of things, with the plant in her lap.

She takes a cake out of the basket.

She eats it.

DIO and Elmesia look at her.

Mau looks at her.

Yuka finishes the cake. She takes another one. She looks at DIO on her left, and Elmesia on her right, both of them pink to the eyebrows and not looking at each other.

And she starts to laugh.

* * *

She can't help it.

She tries. She tries very hard. She puts the cake down and puts her hand over her mouth and it doesn't help. It just comes up out of her, helpless, the way it came up in the clinic chair. *Don't steal my apology.* Because she knows. She knows *exactly* what this is. She sat in a doorway all afternoon in a hoodie, moving an inch an hour. She knows what it is to have the most important sentence in the world in your mouth and not be able to get it past your teeth.

And here are two of the oldest, most powerful, most dignified beings she's ever met, a queen and a creature who was once a god, sitting on a bench in a lit garden, unable to say four words to each other, and asking her husband to pass messages about *shawls*.

“Yuka,” says Mau. Desperately. ᴹ

“I'm sorry,” she says, through her hand. “I'm sorry. I'm sorry. It's just—” ʸ

She can't finish.

DIO is staring at her. Elmesia is staring at her.

“You two can do it,” Yuka manages, finally. “You *can*. I promise. I've seen worse.” ʸ

She picks up her cake.

“I'll just be here,” she says. “Eating.” ʸ

And she eats.

* * *

They can't do it.

They try. Yuka watches them try. DIO opens her mouth and closes it. Elmesia starts a sentence about the fountain and turns it into a sentence about the weather halfway through. DIO says something about Echidna's library. Elmesia says something about the bridge. Neither of them says anything about anything.

Ten minutes. Fifteen.

Mau, standing in front of the bench, has gone very quiet.

Yuka knows that quiet. It's the quiet he goes into before he does something.

She stops eating.

* * *

“DIO,” says Mau. ᴹ

DIO looks up.

“Elmesia,” says Mau. ᴹ

Elmesia looks up.

Mau looks at them both. Very calmly. In the lantern light. The way he looked at Satella in the common room, waiting for her to ask. The way he looked at Rem across the kitchen table, before the sun.

“I'm going to say it,” he says. “Because nobody else is going to, and the cakes are going to run out.” ᴹ

Neither of them moves.

“DIO loves you,” Mau says to Elmesia. ᴹ

Plainly. The plain thing. Not the clever thing.

“Not the friend way,” he says. “The other way. The big way. She wants you happy *with her*. Not anywhere. Here. In this garden. Reading her letters and looking at her plant. She worked it out four nights ago at my table, and she's been too frightened to say it ever since.” ᴹ

He turns to DIO.

“And Elmesia loves you,” he says. ᴹ

DIO goes completely still.

“The same way,” says Mau. “She told me. The first morning I came here. Before you'd even met in the garden. She looks at the window for the bird on the days it's due. She didn't put your name on the first letter because she didn't want you to know she was asking about you. She was afraid you only liked her because she was kind to you, and you'd never had much kindness, and you wouldn't know the difference.” ᴹ

He stops.

“There,” he says. “That's it. That's what both of you have been trying to say for an hour. Now you don't have to.” ᴹ

* * *

They go red.

Both of them. At exactly the same moment. Yuka sees it, because Yuka can see colour, and Mau can't, and she'll tell him afterwards because he'll want to know.

It starts at DIO's collar and goes up. All the way up. Her neck, her jaw, her cheeks, the tips of her ears. Red. Bright red. Red the way the hoodie is papaya, impossible to miss.

And on the other side of Yuka, at exactly the same time, Elmesia. The same. Red from her collar to the tips of her ears, which are long, and which go red all the way along, like lanterns being lit one after another down a path.

Neither of them says anything.

They're not looking at each other. They're looking at Mau. Both of them. With identical expressions of absolute, scalding horror. Because he said it. Out loud. In front of everyone. In front of *Yuka*. The thing they'd both been holding in their mouths for an hour, for a week, for six months, and couldn't say. He just said it. Both halves. Like reading a shopping list.

Yuka puts her face in Mau's coat, where he's standing right in front of her, and shakes.

* * *

“You,” says DIO. To Mau. In a strangled voice. ᴰ

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

“You *said* it.” ᴰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“You weren't *supposed* to say it. *I* was supposed to say it. That was the *point*.” ᴰ

“You weren't saying it,” says Mau. “Neither of you was saying it. I waited an hour.” ᴹ

DIO opens her mouth to argue.

And then she stops. And turns her head. Slowly. Across Yuka, across the plant, to the other end of the bench.

Where Elmesia is sitting, red to the ears, looking back at her.

* * *

Yuka gets up.

She doesn't say anything. She takes the plant off her lap and puts it down on the bench, in the middle, where she was. She takes the cake basket. She takes Mau by the hand.

She leads him away.

Not far. Just across the lawn. Far enough that they can't hear. To a big old tree at the edge of the pool, with lanterns in its branches, and a wide low root you can sit on with your back against the trunk.

Behind them, on the bench, Yuka hears DIO say something. She doesn't hear what. She hears it's short. She hears it's plain.

She hears Elmesia say something back. Also short. Also plain.

Then nothing. For a long time.

Yuka doesn't look round.

* * *

She puts the cake basket down on the wide root of the tree and looks back. Mau is still standing there with his hand in hers.

“We have a minute,” he says. ᴹ

His feet are still turned toward the bench. A moment passes. No one comes to ask for him. He turns the rest of the way to her.

“You owe me a dance,” she says. ʸ

“I found somewhere.” He looks down at the strip of lawn between the root and the pool. “I was hoping you'd remember.” ᴹ

He holds out his other hand. She gives him the one he already has, steps straight into him, and puts the free one on the back of his shoulder. For a few steps she follows the sound of the water against the edge of the pool. Then he takes a step that she's taken too, and her shoe comes down across his. He stops. She goes up on her toes and kisses him with one foot still pinning him where he is.

“That's how you do it,” she says. ʸ

“I had the step right. You went the same way.” ᴹ

She kisses him again for arguing. He lets her have the next step, then takes one himself, the smallest one he can manage. She follows it with her cheek against his. By the time they reach the root again she has both arms over his shoulders and there hasn't been a recognizable step for quite a while.

She sits on the root and pulls him down beside her.

She leans against him. Then she shifts, and turns, and lies down with her back on the root and her head on his chest, the way she does on the couch, the way she did after the red gate, when he couldn't stop and she held him until he could.

He puts his arm round her.

“You said it,” she says. Into his chest. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Both halves. Out loud. In front of them.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You're the worst mediator in the world.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

She laughs. Into his chest. She can feel his heart under her ear.

“They went red,” she says. “Both of them. All the way up. Elmesia's ears. You should have seen.” ʸ

“Tell me,” he says. ᴹ

So she tells him. What red is. What it looked like. How it went up Elmesia's ears like lanterns down a path. How DIO went the same, the exact same, at the exact same moment.

He listens to all of it.

And then she stops talking, because she's warm and he's warm and the lanterns are moving very slowly on the pool and his heart is going under her ear, steady, steady.

“Let go,” she says. Very quietly. ʸ

His arm slides lower around her. She reaches behind herself to make room for it and feels him settle into the space she's made.

For a little while she plays with the edge of his cuff. He turns his palm over beneath her fingers and lets it lie there. His breath goes out warm against the top of her head. The next one takes longer to come.

He falls asleep.

She falls asleep a minute after, with her head on his chest and her hand in his shirt.

* * *

“*Mau.*” ᴰ

Somebody is shaking him.

Yuka opens her eyes. It's very dark. The lanterns in the tree have burned low. The lanterns on the pool have gone out, most of them. It's cold.

DIO is standing over them.

She's holding the plant. She's holding Ori's dragon. Her face, in the last of the lantern light, is still faintly red, and her hair is coming down, and her eyes are enormous.

Behind her, a little way off, Elmesia, holding a shawl, looking at the sky.

“Do you know what *time* it is?” says DIO. ᴰ

Mau sits up, slowly, with Yuka still on his chest.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

“It's past *midnight*,” says DIO. “We were supposed to be home *hours* ago. Ori's going to be *frantic*. Ori lent me her *dragon*. I have to give it *back*. It's a *lend*.” ᴰ

Yuka starts to laugh again.

“And *you*,” says DIO, to Mau, “were *asleep*. Under a *tree*. While I was—while we were—” ᴰ

She stops.

The red, in the lantern light, comes back up her neck.

“Never mind,” she says. ᴰ

* * *

On the way back up the narrow stair to the little room with the very good view, DIO stops.

She turns round on the step above Mau, so that for once she's taller than him, and looks down at him.

“The letter,” she says. ᴰ

He goes still.

“The first one,” says DIO. “You said you read it wrong. You said she thought I'd come along inside you. You said she was disappointed in *you*.” ᴰ

He doesn't say anything.

“That wasn't true,” says DIO. “Was it. She didn't put my name on it because she didn't want me to know. She told me. Just now. On the bench.” ᴰ

“No,” he says. “It wasn't true.” ᴹ

DIO looks at him for a long moment.

“That's a lie,” she says. “You lied to me. To make me feel better. To protect her. That's three.” ᴰ

He nods.

“That's fair,” he says. ᴹ

DIO keeps looking at him.

Then she shakes her head.

“No,” she says. “It's not three.” ᴰ

He looks up.

“It's still two,” says DIO. “That one doesn't count. That one was—” ᴰ

She stops. She looks back down the stair, toward the garden, toward the figure at the bottom holding a shawl and a lantern, waiting to say goodnight.

“That one was a good lie,” says DIO. “I'm keeping it. You can't have it back. It's still two.” ᴰ

She turns round and goes up the stair.

Yuka, beside him, takes his hand.

He doesn't say anything. He can't.
