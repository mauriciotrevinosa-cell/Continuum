## Chapter 1 — Membership

*When: B+460. "27." Under it, in Ori's hand: "NEW MEMBER". A small owl drawn beside it.*

DIO gives the dragon back at breakfast.

She does it formally. She comes down in her own body, which she has started doing every morning now, and walks the length of the kitchen to where Ori is sitting, and stops, and holds out the little carved dragon on her open palm.

“Returned,” she says. “As agreed. A lend, not a give.” ᴰ

Ori looks at the dragon. She takes it. She inspects it carefully, turning it over, checking the tail, checking the feet.

“It's all here,” she says. ᴼ

“Of course it's all here,” says DIO. ᴰ

“It worked?” says Ori. ᴼ

DIO goes a colour that the whole table can see except Mau.

“It worked,” she says. ᴰ

Ori nods, satisfied, and puts the dragon in her pocket.

“And this,” says DIO, and holds out her other hand. ᴰ

In it is a flower. Pressed flat, between two pieces of paper, very carefully. Small. Five petals. Mau can't see what colour it is.

“From her garden,” says DIO. “The corner where the plant's going to have its holiday. She said a lend should come back with interest. That's the interest.” ᴰ

Ori takes the flower. She looks at it for a long time.

Then she opens her notebook and puts it between the pages, very carefully, next to the two flowers Mai pressed in last week.

“Interest,” she says. “That's a good rule. That's a Fort rule now.” ᴼ

* * *

Ori is thinking about something else for the rest of breakfast.

Mau can see it. She has the face she gets when she's planning: eyes narrowed, chewing very slowly, looking at nothing. She keeps glancing down the table.

At Beatrice.

Beatrice is at breakfast. Beatrice is at breakfast every day now. For three weeks, since the night in the library when Mau made his small rule and she called it a terrible rule, she has come out every single day. To breakfast. To supper. To Frieren's window seat. To the rug in Frieren's room on rain days. To the kitchen, once, to tell Shuna that the bread was acceptable, in fact.

Nobody has had to come and fetch her. Not once.

Ori has noticed.

* * *

The Fort holds an emergency session after breakfast.

Mau knows because the bell rings. The Fort bell, which is a saucepan lid and a spoon, hung from the corner of the roof under the banner that still says REM SAID IT in letters as tall as Ori.

Three strikes. Emergency.

Every member comes running. Umaru in her dressing gown. Milim, very fast. Emilia, with Puck on her shoulder. Rem, who is now officially a member, because Umaru inducted her at midnight on the roof two nights ago and the vote was unanimous. Beru, who was already there.

And Ori, on her crate, with her spear and her notebook.

Mau watches from the porch with Yuka. Most of the house watches from the porch. Fort emergencies are rare and generally worth seeing.

“The Fort,” says Ori, “has identified a candidate.” ᴼ

There's a murmur.

“The candidate,” says Ori, “has been observed. For three weeks. The candidate has come out of the library every day without being fetched. The candidate shared a difficult book about mushrooms with a Fort member during a rain day. The candidate argued with Echidna and won.” ᴼ

“Partly won,” says Rem. ᴿᵉ

“Partly won,” Ori agrees. “The candidate is very good at doors.” ᴼ

She lifts her spear and points it at the kitchen door, where Beatrice has just come out onto the step with a book under her arm, and has stopped dead at the sight of the entire Fort turned round to look at her.

“The candidate,” says Ori, “is Beatrice.” ᴼ

* * *

Beatrice goes very still.

“Betty,” she says, “is not a *child*, in fact.” ᴮᵉᵃ

“Not all of us are children,” says Ori. “Emilia isn't a child. Beru isn't a child. Rem isn't a child.” ᴼ

“Umaru *is* a child,” says Beatrice. ᴮᵉᵃ

“I'm a *grown woman*,” says Umaru, outraged. ᵁ

“In fact,” says Beatrice, “Betty is four hundred years old. Betty kept a library for four hundred years. Betty does not need to be in a *Fort*.” ᴮᵉᵃ

Ori considers this.

“The Fort,” she says, “is not about needing. It's about wanting. Do you want to?” ᴼ

Beatrice opens her mouth.

And then she doesn't say anything.

Mau watches her not say anything. He knows that silence. He's seen it on Satella's face in the common room. On Rem's, across the kitchen table. On his own, in mirrors, for longer than he can remember.

The silence of someone who's been asked what they want and has never once in their life been asked before.

* * *

“Betty,” says Beatrice at last, very stiffly, “would not be *opposed*, I suppose.” ᴮᵉᵃ

The Fort erupts.

Milim screams. Umaru screams. Emilia claps. Puck, on her shoulder, does a small somersault. Beru makes the sound. Rem, smiling, comes down off the Fort steps and holds out her hand to help Beatrice up.

Beatrice looks at the hand. Then she takes it, as if entirely by her own decision, and lets herself be brought up onto the roof.

* * *

The induction is held at once.

Ori stands on her crate. Beatrice stands in front of her, chin up, with her book clutched to her chest, looking as though she's about to be executed.

Ori brings out the paper badge. It's been ready, Mau suspects, for some time. It has an owl drawn on it, and the word FORT, and underneath, smaller, BEATRICE (BETTY).

“Do you,” says Ori, very seriously, “promise to defend the Fort?” ᴼ

“Betty promises,” says Beatrice. ᴮᵉᵃ

“Even against Kamish?” ᴼ

Beatrice looks out across the orchard to the pear tree, where Kamish is lying in the sun. Kamish opens one eye.

“Even against Kamish,” says Beatrice, with enormous dignity. ᴮᵉᵃ

“Even,” says Ori, “against the library?” ᴼ

Beatrice goes still.

The whole Fort goes still.

Ori looks at her. Very steadily. Not unkindly. With Frieren's eyes.

“If the library ever wants you to stay inside,” says Ori, “and not come out. To keep it. To wait. Will you come out anyway? For the Fort?” ᴼ

Mau, on the porch, has stopped breathing.

He didn't tell Ori about the rule. Nobody told Ori. Ori worked it out on her own, watching Beatrice come out every day, watching her on the rug, watching her argue with Echidna about *waiting* and *keeping*.

Beatrice stands on the roof of the Fort for a long time.

“Even against the library,” she says. Very quietly. ᴮᵉᵃ

Ori pins the badge on her.

* * *

Umaru has the owl.

The little carved one. Mau chose it for her in town: an owl, because she stays up all night. She's had it in the pocket of her dressing gown every day since. Mau has seen her take it out and look at it at meals.

She takes it out now. She looks at it.

Then she holds it out to Beatrice.

“You can borrow it,” she says. “New members get a carved animal. That's the rule. And there isn't one for you yet. So you can borrow mine. Until Mau gets you one.” ᵁ

Beatrice looks at the owl.

She takes it. She holds it in both hands, the way Ori holds the dragon. She looks at it for a long time.

“Betty will give it back,” she says. ᴮᵉᵃ

Umaru looks at her. Then at Rem, beside her. Then back at Beatrice.

“No,” says Umaru. “Keep it. I don't need it anymore.” ᵁ

She doesn't explain. She doesn't need to. Rem, beside her, takes her hand.

Beatrice puts the owl in the pocket of her dress, beside the book, and doesn't say anything else, and doesn't need to.

* * *

Echidna watches from the north-wing step.

Mau sees her there. Sitting with a book on her knee, not reading it. Watching the Fort roof. Watching Beatrice stand under the REM SAID IT banner with a paper badge on her dress and an owl in her pocket and Ori's hand on her arm.

He can't see Echidna's face from the porch. But he sees her close the book.

And he sees her stay there, on the step, not reading, for a long time after the Fort has dispersed and Beatrice has gone back to the library through the pantry door.

* * *

Beatrice wears the badge to supper.

Nobody mentions it.

She sits in her place beside Frieren, very upright, with the badge on her dress, and eats her soup, and tells Shuna the bread is acceptable, in fact, and argues with Echidna, who has come to supper for once, about a word.

Halfway through, Mau catches her touching it. The badge. Just with one finger. Checking it's still there.

The way Maki touches her pin.

He looks away before she sees him see.

Beside him, Yuka steals his pickles and puts her foot on his under the table, and leans over, and whispers in his ear:

“You have to get her a carved animal.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“An owl. Her own one.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Tomorrow.” ʸ

“Tomorrow,” he agrees. ᴹ

Across the table, Ori writes something very small in her notebook, and puts a tick beside it.
