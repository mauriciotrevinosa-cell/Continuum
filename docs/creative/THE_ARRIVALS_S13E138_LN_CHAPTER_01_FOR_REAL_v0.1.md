# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 3 — For Real

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S13E138
**Continuity state:** B+462 ("25"), dawn. The field beside the old quarry, inside Frieren's barrier. The house on the hill with banners and baskets; Ori with her scoring columns; Shoko and Ciel ready to call it. Gojo and Mau, one on one, no army, no shadows, no Purple at each other. It starts with bodies and Infinity against Infinity: neither can touch the other, so they don't try; they fight the space between. Blue and Red in turn. Mau keeps two at once, as he's practised; the refresh keeps pace. Gojo is not what he was in his own world; something was taken on Day 133 and he has never said what. He doesn't need it. He's faster than Mau expects, and better, and he's laughing. Halfway through, Mau realises he's smiling too and can't stop. Ori awards style points. Shoko calls no stop. The chapter ends with Gojo, breathing hard and delighted, saying "Domain?" and Mau saying "Domain."

*When: B+462. Dawn. "25." Under it, in Ori's hand, the scoring table, already half full by the time anyone thought to write the date.*

The hill is full before the sun comes up.

Mau sees it as he walks out across the field: the whole house, spread along the slope above the quarry, on blankets, on Senku's chairs, on the grass. Two banners. One says GOJO in Umaru's enormous letters. The other says DAD in Milim's even bigger ones, with a drawing underneath that might be either of them, or a horse. Shuna's baskets in a row. Emilia, with Puck, explaining to Satella what a fight is for. The witches, all seven, on a blanket of their own at the edge, Pride standing on Minerva's shoulders to see. Jin-Woo with Suho on his knee. Dabura, sitting a little apart with his huge hands folded, watching with tremendous interest, Maru beside him.

Ori, at the front, on her crate, with her notebook and her spear.

Shoko, on the barrier's edge, standing, arms folded. Not sitting. Ready.

And Yuka, beside Shoko, in the hoodie, with her hands in the pocket.

She doesn't wave. She just looks at him across the field, and nods once.

*Enjoy this one*, she said. *For me.*

* * *

Gojo is already in the middle of the field.

Blindfold off. Hands in his pockets. Bouncing slightly on his toes, the way he does when he's been waiting for something for a long time and the waiting is almost over.

He watches Mau come.

“Ready?” he says. ᴳ

“Ready,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Shoko says *go* when she wants it to start,” says Gojo. “That's her rule. She added it last night. I think she just likes making me wait.” ᴳ

Up on the barrier's edge, Shoko doesn't say anything for a long moment. Long enough that Gojo starts to bounce higher. Long enough that someone on the hill laughs.

Then:

“Go,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

* * *

Gojo's gone.

Not gone. Moved. So fast that the place where he was standing is still there in Mau's eyes when Gojo is already on his left, low, coming in.

The Six Eyes catch it. Barely. They catch the shift of weight, the push off the ground, the line. Mau's body moves before he tells it to: half a step back, a turn, and Gojo's fist goes through the space where his ribs were and stops.

Stops on nothing.

Infinity. His. Up the moment Gojo moved, on its own, the way it comes up now. Gojo's knuckles hang in the air a hand's width from Mau's side, closer and closer and never arriving.

Gojo grins.

And Mau, without thinking, throws his own fist at Gojo's face.

It stops too. A hand's width away. Gojo's infinity. Closer and closer and never arriving.

They stand there for one second in the middle of the field, each with a fist stopped an inch from the other, each perfectly untouchable.

On the hill, somebody says, “*Oh*.”

* * *

“Well,” says Gojo. “That's the problem, isn't it.” ᴳ

He takes his fist back. So does Mau.

“Neither of us can hit the other,” says Gojo. ᴳ

“No,” says Mau. ᴹ

“So we don't hit each other,” says Gojo. ᴳ

And he moves again. Not at Mau. Past him. To the side, and back, and round, and Mau realises what he's doing a half-second too late: he's not fighting Mau. He's fighting the *ground*. The space. The air between them.

A pull. Behind Mau. Sudden and hard.

Blue.

Not at him; Infinity would take it. *Behind* him. A point of pull opened in the empty air a body's length back, dragging everything toward it: the dust, the grass, the loose stones, and Mau's own weight, his balance, the ground under his feet tilting toward the point like a floor going down a drain.

He goes with it. He has to. He can't stop the pull; it isn't touching him, it's touching everything else. He stumbles backward toward the blue point, and Gojo is there, already there, waiting, and his knee comes up—

And stops. An inch away.

But Mau's off balance now. And Gojo's laughing.

“You see?” says Gojo. “You can't touch me. But you can still *fall over*.” ᴳ

* * *

Mau learns fast.

He always has. It's the eyes. They see what Gojo's doing the second time he does it, and the third time they see it coming. And the fourth time, when Gojo opens a Blue behind him, Mau opens a Red in front of himself at the same instant: a push, sharp and hard, his own body thrown forward out of the pull, the two forces cancelling round him in a burst of dust.

Two at once. Infinity and Red. And then, a breath later, Red dropped and Blue up: a pull of his own, under Gojo's feet.

Gojo drops a foot, laughing, and catches himself on nothing.

*Refresh keeping pace*, says Ciel, inside, very calm. *Two at once is fine. You've practised. Your body knows.* ᶜ

*I know*, says Mau. ᴹ

*You're smiling*, says Ciel. ᶜ

He is.

He didn't notice. He's in the middle of a field with Gojo Satoru coming at him from three directions at once, the ground tilting, the air bending, dust everywhere, and he's smiling so hard his face hurts.

* * *

It goes on for a long time.

He loses track. The sun comes up over the hill. The dust hangs in the air in great slow clouds, lit up for everyone but him. They go up and down the field and up and down again. Blue and Red. Pull and push. Infinity against Infinity, neither of them touching the other once, both of them falling over a great deal.

Gojo is not what he was.

Mau knows that. Everyone knows that. On Day 133, after Rika and Maki broke the fight apart, the world reached into Gojo and took something. He's never said what. He's never complained. He's never once, in all the time since, let it show.

He doesn't need it.

He's faster than Mau expected. He's better. Not stronger; Mau thinks, in the middle of it, with a strange flat certainty, that he might be stronger now, after the points, after the quarry, after everything. But Gojo is *better*. He's been doing this since he was a child. Every movement he makes is one he's made ten thousand times. He knows exactly where Mau's weight is going before Mau does. He opens Blues in places Mau hasn't thought of yet. He fights the field itself as if it's an old friend he's arguing with.

And he's laughing. The whole time. Out loud. Not the performance laugh he uses on the porch. The real one. The one Mau heard once, a long time ago, on a wall, when Yuta told him something good.

Mau can't stop smiling either.

* * *

On the hill, Ori is keeping score.

Mau can hear her, in snatches, between one Blue and the next. Calling it out, very loudly, the way she calls out Fort business.

“GOJO. Style. Two points. For the one behind him that made him fall in the dust.” ᴼ

“DAD. Style. Three points. For doing the push and the pull at the same time and not falling over.” ᴼ

“GOJO. Minus one. For laughing at Dad when he fell over.” ᴼ

“That's not a *rule*!” Gojo shouts, mid-air. ᴳ

“It is NOW!” ᴼ

“DAD. Not dying. One point. GOJO. Not dying. One point.” ᴼ

“BOTH. Doing what Shoko says. Five points each.” ᴼ

“She hasn't *said* anything!” ᴳ

“That's why it's five,” says Ori. ᴼ

* * *

They stop, at last, in the middle of the field.

Not because anyone calls it. They just stop. Both at once. Standing a few paces apart in the settling dust, breathing hard. Mau's shirt is torn at the shoulder where he fell into a Blue the wrong way. Gojo has dust in his hair and a long scrape down one cheek from the ground, which he hasn't noticed, and which Shoko, on the barrier's edge, has very much noticed.

They look at each other.

Gojo is grinning so wide it looks painful.

“You're good,” he says. ᴳ

“You're better,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Yes,” says Gojo, delighted. “I am. But you're *good*. You're really good. You got good while I wasn't looking.” ᴳ

He wipes his face with the back of his hand and looks at the dust on his knuckles.

“And you're enjoying it,” he says. ᴳ

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

Gojo looks at him for a long moment.

“Good,” he says, quite softly. “That's all I wanted, really.” ᴳ

Then the grin comes back.

“*Domain?*” he says. ᴳ

* * *

Mau looks up the hill.

At Shoko, on the barrier's edge. At Ciel, inside, at the table, with her hand flat on the wood, watching his brain refresh.

At Yuka, beside Shoko, with her hands in the hoodie pocket, watching him.

She nods.

*Brain refresh is fine*, says Ciel. *The strain from the quarry is gone. If it falls behind, I'll say so. And you'll stop.* ᶜ

*I'll stop.* ᴹ

Shoko, on the edge, hasn't said anything. She's looking at Gojo. She's looking at the scrape on his cheek.

Then she unfolds her arms, very slightly. Not a yes. Not a no. Just: *go on. I'm watching.*

Mau turns back to Gojo.

“Domain,” he says. ᴹ
