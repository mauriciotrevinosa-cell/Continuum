# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 4 — Two Purples

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E138
**Continuity state:** B+462 ("25"), mid-morning. Gojo opens Unlimited Void. Mau opens his own domain at the same instant; he doesn't compress it this time, he just lets it stand, his space against Gojo's, and the two press on each other across the field. It's a contest of refinement, and Gojo is better at it. For twelve seconds Mau holds. Then both domains buckle at once and collapse, the way they did on Day 133, and both of them burn out, techniques gone quiet for a while. They fight with their hands and laugh. When it comes back to them, both, without a word, begin to prepare Purple, the way they did on Day 133, pointed at each other. And stop. And look at each other. And turn, together, and fire them straight up into the sky, crossing. Two lines of nothing going up through the morning. The whole hill on its feet. Shoko never had to call it. Ori declares a draw, for style. Gojo lies on his back in the dust and laughs until he cries.

*When: B+462. Mid-morning. "25." Under Ori's table, in her hand, underlined twice: "DRAW (STYLE)".*

Gojo opens his first.

He doesn't move. He doesn't need to. He just brings his hands together in front of him, one finger crossed over another, the old shape, and says, quite quietly, almost fondly:

“Domain Expansion.” ᴳ

And the world goes.

* * *

Mau has been here before. Once. On Day 133, a long time ago, before the nerf, before everything, with his own domain up against it and a shrine and a wheel and Rika screaming. He remembers almost nothing of what it looked like from the inside. There wasn't time to look.

There's time now.

It isn't dark. That's the first thing. He'd thought it would be dark. It's the opposite. It's everything. All at once. Every piece of information the world has ever held, all of it, poured straight in through his eyes at a speed nothing could keep up with. The field. The dust. The light. Every grain of every stone. The hill. Every face on the hill. Every thought behind every face. Every second that has ever passed, still passing, all at once, forever.

*Unlimited Void.*

His mind starts to go. He can feel it start. The flood coming in through the Six Eyes and the eyes not knowing how to stop taking it, the way they've never known how to stop taking anything. Seeing everything. Understanding everything. Doing nothing.

*MAU*, says Ciel. Very loud. Exactly as loud as he told her she could be. ᶜ

And he opens his own.

* * *

He doesn't compress it.

That's the important thing. At the quarry, against Dabura, he forced it: three territories crushed together into one space, so hard the strain cracked something in his left side that took Shoko a week to mend. He made a sure hit where his domain doesn't naturally have one, and he paid for it.

He doesn't do that now.

He just lets it stand. His space. The one he built himself, a long time ago, out of everything he's ever been. The boundary. The dark underneath it where the shadows live. The rule that says what's inside is his.

It goes up round him like a held breath, and meets Gojo's.

* * *

He's read about this. Everyone who uses a domain has.

When two domains open in the same place, they don't fight like fists. They *press*. Each one trying to be the one that's real. Each one trying to say *this is my space, my rules, not yours*. And the one that's better made, the one that's more refined, more complete, more *certain*, slowly overwrites the other, inch by inch, until there's only one.

Gojo's is better made.

Mau knows it in the first second. It's not a matter of power. He might have more power now. It's a matter of *craft*. Gojo has been building that Void since before Mau existed. Every edge of it is perfect. Every line of it is certain.

Mau's has been torn down and rebuilt more times than he can count. Always in a hurry. Always under pressure. Always out of pieces.

He feels it start to give.

* * *

He holds it.

He holds it with everything. Not compressing it. Not forcing the sure hit. Just holding the space. Refusing to let the edge go. Feeling Gojo's Void press against it like the sea against a wall.

The Six Eyes, inside his own domain, can see Gojo's. Every perfect line of it. Where it's strongest. Where it isn't. There's nowhere it isn't. It's beautiful. It's the most beautiful thing he's ever seen anyone make.

Four seconds.

Eight.

*Brain refresh at limit*, says Ciel. *Holding. Not falling behind. But at the limit.* ᶜ

Ten.

Twelve.

And across the field, through the two pressing spaces, through the dust and the light and the everything, he sees Gojo's face.

Gojo is looking at him with an expression of pure, delighted astonishment.

“You're *holding* it,” says Gojo. ᴳ

* * *

Both domains buckle at once.

Mau feels it happen. Not Gojo's Void winning. Not his own giving way. Both of them, at the same moment, under the pressure of the other, cracking like two panes of glass leaned too hard against each other.

The way it happened on Day 133. Exactly the way.

The world snaps back.

The field. The dust. The hill. The sun.

And inside Mau, where his techniques live, everything goes quiet.

Not gone. Quiet. Like a fire that's burned so hot it's used up everything near it and has to wait for more to be brought. He reaches for Infinity and it isn't there. He reaches for Blue and finds nothing. Burnout. The cost of a domain that collapses.

He looks up.

Across the field, Gojo is standing very still with his hands at his sides, and Mau can see the same thing has happened to him. His Infinity is down. For the first time in the whole fight, there's nothing between them.

They look at each other.

“Well,” says Gojo. ᴳ

“Well,” says Mau. ᴹ

Gojo grins.

And comes at him with his fists.

* * *

They fight with their hands.

No techniques. No Infinity. No Blue, no Red, no pull, no push. Just two men in a field covered in dust, hitting each other, because for once they can.

And it hurts.

Gojo hits like a wall. He's been trained to fight hand to hand since before he could read, and he's fast, and he's precise, and every punch lands exactly where he meant it to. Mau's body, with all its points, all its strength, takes the hits and doesn't break, and hits back, and lands some, and misses a lot, and gets thrown on his back in the dust twice.

The second time he's thrown, he's laughing so hard he can't get up.

Gojo stands over him with his hands on his knees, breathing hard, bleeding from the nose, and laughing too.

“You've never done this,” Gojo says. “Have you? Just hit someone. Without anything in it.” ᴳ

“Not for fun,” says Mau, from the ground. ᴹ

“It's the best part,” says Gojo. “Get up. I'm not finished.” ᴳ

Mau gets up.

On the hill, Ori is screaming points so fast nobody can follow them.

* * *

It comes back to them at the same time.

Mau feels it first, or thinks he does. A flicker. Somewhere in the quiet place inside where the techniques live. Like a coal under ash that someone has just blown on.

He looks at Gojo.

Gojo's looking at him. Same face. He felt it too.

And without a word, without even deciding to, the way their hands moved on Day 133, they both step back. Two paces. Three. And bring their hands up.

The left hand. A pull. Blue, coming back, small and shaky and real.

The right hand. A push. Red. The same.

And between them, slowly, the two meeting.

Purple.

* * *

The hill goes silent.

Mau feels it go silent. Every person on that slope has heard about Day 133. Everyone knows. The two of them in a field, burned out, flow-state, reaching past the burnout for the one thing they both have, the most dangerous thing either of them can make, pointed at each other.

He feels Shoko, on the barrier's edge, go completely still.

He feels Yuka, beside her, take her hands out of her pocket.

He looks at the thing forming between his palms. Small. Unsteady. Real. And past it, across the field, at the same thing forming between Gojo's.

Pointed at him.

His own pointed at Gojo.

Exactly the way it was.

* * *

He stops.

Gojo stops.

At the same moment. Neither of them looking away from the other. Both of them holding the Purple between their hands, not firing, not letting it go, not closing their hands either.

*No Purple at each other*, Shoko said. *Not once.*

Gojo's face, across the field, does something.

He smiles. Not the grin. Something much smaller and much older. Something that knows exactly what this is, and where they've been, and how far they came to get back here and *not* do it.

He tips his head. Very slightly. Upward.

Mau understands.

* * *

They turn together.

Both of them, at once. Their hands still cupped round the Purple. Turning from each other, not away, just up. Lifting their hands over their heads. Pointing at the sky.

They fire.

* * *

Two lines go up.

Two lines of nothing. Mau can't think of another way to describe it, afterwards. Two narrow beams of absolute absence, going straight up from the field into the morning sky, side by side, crossing high over the quarry in a long slow X, and through the X the sky looks *wrong*, looks like a door has been opened onto somewhere without any light in it.

They go up and up and up.

Then they're gone.

The sky closes behind them, slowly, like water closing over a stone.

* * *

The hill is on its feet.

All of it. All at once. Everybody. The whole house, standing, on the slope above the quarry, looking up at the place in the sky where the two lines crossed and are gone.

Nobody says anything.

And then Milim screams.

And then everybody screams. Umaru. Emilia. Pride, on Minerva's shoulders. Jin-Woo, standing with Suho held up in the air above his head so he can see. The banners waving. DIO, who has come out of Mau in her borrowed body to watch and didn't tell him, screaming louder than anyone. Dabura, on his feet, his huge hands raised over his head, roaring something in his own language that might be a blessing.

And Ori.

Ori standing on her crate with her spear raised and her notebook held up in her other hand, shouting at the top of her voice:

“*DRAW!*” ᴼ

“*DRAW! FOR STYLE! TEN POINTS EACH! TWENTY FOR DOING WHAT SHOKO SAID!*” ᴼ

* * *

Gojo lies down.

He doesn't fall. He just lies down, in the dust, in the middle of the field, on his back, with his arms flung out, the way he lay on the common room floor on the rain day, and looks up at the sky where the X was.

And laughs.

He laughs until he can't breathe. Until the tears are running down the sides of his face into the dust. Until he has to roll over on his side and hold his ribs.

Mau sits down beside him.

He doesn't say anything. He doesn't need to. He sits in the dust next to Gojo Satoru and looks at the sky and feels his own techniques coming back, slowly, coal by coal, and feels his face, which hasn't stopped smiling for an hour, and doesn't try to make it stop.

“We didn't,” says Gojo, eventually, through the laughing. ᴳ

“No,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Day 133,” says Gojo. “We had it in our hands. And this time we *didn't*.” ᴳ

“No.” ᴹ

Gojo wipes his face. It just smears the dust.

“That was the best fight of my life,” he says. ᴳ

Mau looks at him.

“Mine too,” he says. ᴹ

And Gojo laughs again, helplessly, and holds out his fist, lying there on his back in the dust, without looking.

Mau bumps it with his own.

No Infinity. Nothing in between.

Just knuckles.

* * *

Shoko is walking across the field toward them.

Not fast. She's got her bag. She's got the look on her face that she gets when she's about to be very angry with someone and also very relieved, and hasn't decided which to be first.

She stops over Gojo.

She looks down at the scrape on his cheek. At the blood under his nose. At the tears in the dust on his face.

Gojo looks up at her.

“You didn't call it,” he says. ᴳ

“You didn't make me,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

She crouches down. She takes his face in one hand, quite firmly, and turns it to look at the scrape.

She doesn't let go of it for longer than she needs to.

Mau gets up very quietly and walks away across the field toward the hill, where Yuka is coming down the slope to meet him, and doesn't look back.
