# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 3 — Good Strange

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S13E139
**Continuity state:** B+463 ("24"). Shoko's point of view. The morning after Gojo's turn on the house gate, turned all the way up. Gojo comes to the clinic with nothing wrong, the way Mau does, and sits on the end of the cot. She checks the scrape anyway. He tells her the thing he didn't say at the door: that for an hour yesterday he forgot about being the strongest, about holding back, and just played; and that the reason he could was that she was sitting on the step by the door and he knew she'd be there if it went wrong. *When: B+463. "24." Under it, in Shoko's hand, which is very small and very neat and almost never appears in the notebook: "clinic, 1 visitor, nothing wrong".*

He comes in the morning with nothing wrong.

Shoko knows he's coming before he knocks. She knows the sound of his feet on the clinic step. She's known it for a long time. Longer than this world. Since a different step, in a different school, when they were young and stupid and he used to come and sit on the end of the infirmary cot with nothing wrong, too.

He knocks anyway. He never used to knock.

“It's open,” she says. ˢʰ

He comes in. He doesn't say anything. He looks round the clinic the way Mau does, as if checking it's still there, and then he sits down on the end of the cot.

Shoko doesn't look up from the cupboard.

She points at the kettle with her chin.

* * *

He makes the tea.

He's terrible at it. He always has been. He puts too much leaf in and leaves it too long and the result is the colour of something that should be treated as evidence. She drinks it anyway. She always has.

She checks the scrape while it's brewing.

She doesn't ask. She just crosses the clinic and takes his chin in her hand and turns his face to the window to look at it. It's healed already. Mostly. A faint line along his cheekbone, where something in the gate took the skin off. He's let his RCT do most of it and left the last little bit, she can tell. Left it on purpose.

“You left that,” she says. ˢʰ

“I wanted you to look at it,” he says. ᴳ

She lets go of his chin.

She goes back to the cupboard.

* * *

“I forgot,” says Gojo. ᴳ

She doesn't turn round.

“Yesterday,” he says. “In the gate. For about an hour. I forgot.” ᴳ

“Forgot what?” ˢʰ

“All of it.” ᴳ

He's quiet for a moment. She hears him turn the cup in his hands.

“Being the strongest,” he says. “Holding back. I think about it every day. Every single day, Shoko. Since I was a kid. When I wake up. When I put the blindfold on. Every fight, every training round, every time I touch anything, there's a part of me measuring how much is too much. How much would break it.” ᴳ

He stops.

“I don't say it,” he says. “I don't think anyone's noticed. I'm very good at not saying it.” ᴳ

“I've noticed,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

A pause.

“Yes,” says Gojo. “I suppose you would have.” ᴳ

* * *

“And yesterday I forgot,” he says. “For an hour. I wasn't thinking about any of it. I was just—” ᴳ

She hears him look for the word.

“Playing,” he says. “I was playing. Like when we were young. Before any of it. Before I had to be the strongest. Before it meant anything. Just—running about in a place that could keep up, laughing. I told it to read all of me, Shoko. And it did. And it built something that could take it. I didn't have to measure anything.” ᴳ

He puts the cup down.

“I haven't done that since I was sixteen,” he says. ᴳ

Shoko closes the cupboard.

She turns round.

* * *

He's sitting on the end of the cot with his hands loose between his knees and his blindfold pushed up and his face doing something she hasn't seen it do since they were very young.

Not grinning. Not performing. Just looking at her.

“And you know why I could?” he says. ᴳ

She doesn't answer.

“Because you were at the door,” says Gojo. “Sitting on the step. Arms folded. Bag beside you. And I knew, the whole time, that if it went wrong, if I went too far, if anything happened, you'd be there when I came out. And it'd be all right.” ᴳ

He looks at her.

“I've never trusted anyone to stop me,” he says. “Not once. In my whole life. Not my teachers. Not the elders. Not Suguru. Nobody. I was always the one who had to be stopped, and nobody could, so I had to do it myself. Every time. Forever.” ᴳ

He looks down at his hands.

“And yesterday I didn't have to,” he says. “Because you were there. So I could just—play.” ᴳ

* * *

Shoko doesn't say anything for a long time.

She thinks about the step. An hour on it, with her arms folded and her bag at her feet, listening to the gate shake. The whole house pretending not to listen. Ori beside her with the notebook, writing down every boom. Twice the ground jumped. Once something on the other side of the door made a sound she has no name for, and her hand went to the bag, and stayed there.

And then the door opening, and him walking out, dusty and bleeding from one cheek and laughing so hard he had to hold the frame.

She didn't have to go in.

He didn't make her.

* * *

“It's still slow,” she says. ˢʰ

He looks up.

“This,” says Shoko. “Whatever this is. It's still slow. I'm not going to make it fast because you had a good day in a gate and said something nice in my clinic.” ˢʰ

She folds her arms.

“I've known you since we were fifteen,” she says. “I watched you be the strongest. I watched what it did to you. I watched you lose Suguru, and I watched you not cry about it, and I stood outside that box while you were in it, and there was nothing I could do but wait.” ˢʰ

Her voice doesn't change. It never does.

“And then I woke up here,” she says. “And you were here. And you were still doing it. Still not saying anything. Still grinning.” ˢʰ

She looks at him.

“So it's slow,” she says. “Because I'm not doing it the way we did everything before. Fast. Stupid. Without talking. I'm doing it slow, so I can see it.” ˢʰ

Gojo looks at her for a long moment.

“Slow is good,” he says. ᴳ

“Is it?” ˢʰ

“Slow is—” He stops. He tries again. “I've never done anything slowly in my life. I don't know how. I'm terrible at it. I keep wanting to rush.” ᴳ

He looks down at the cup of terrible tea.

“But I'd rather do it slow with you,” he says, “than fast with anyone else. And I'd rather you could see it.” ᴳ

* * *

Shoko looks at him.

She thinks about saying several things. Clever things. Dry things. The things she's said to him for years, at the end of every conversation that got too close, to make it not close anymore.

She doesn't say any of them.

She pulls the stool over with her foot, and sits down on it, in front of the cot. Close. Not touching.

She picks up her own cup of his terrible tea.

“Drink your tea,” she says. ˢʰ

He drinks his tea.

They sit there in the clinic in the morning light, not saying anything. Not touching. Just there.

After a while, without looking at him, she reaches out and fixes the collar of his shirt, which is folded wrong, the way it's always folded wrong, the way it was folded wrong when he was fifteen.

She takes her hand away.

He doesn't say anything about it.

But he doesn't move from the end of the cot until lunch.

* * *

Mau passes the clinic window at about eleven.

Shoko sees him. Out of the corner of her eye, crossing the yard with Ori on his shoulders and Yuka holding his hand. He glances at the window as he passes. He sees them in there: Gojo on the end of the cot, Shoko on the stool, two cups.

He doesn't stop.

He looks away very deliberately and keeps walking, and says something to Ori that makes her laugh, and steers all three of them round the corner of the house toward the orchard, out of sight of the window.

Shoko watches him go.

“He saw,” says Gojo, who has noticed too. ᴳ

“He looked away,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“He's learned,” says Gojo. ᴳ

“Yes,” says Shoko. “He has.” ˢʰ

She looks down at her tea.

“So have you,” she says. Very quietly. ˢʰ

He doesn't answer that. He doesn't need to.

Good strange, she thinks. That's what she told Mau, in the dark, with his hand in hers, when he asked. *Good strange.*

It still is.
