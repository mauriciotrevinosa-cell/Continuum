# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 1 — A Week from Here

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E13
**Continuity state:** B+286, the first day after the fighting ends.

*When: The next morning, while the kettle is still coming to a boil.*

The man from the road wants to know if the people with the blue hands found his sister's neighbor. He asked them to find the neighbor first because his sister works a long way from the road, and she can go get his daughter.

Shoko has already given him the answer twice. He asks again when Mau comes in because Mau is a different person and might have heard something later. Mau understands that well enough to pull the chair up and tell him the same thing all the way through.

“The neighbor's name is in the message. So is your daughter's. One of the people who carried you here is taking it home.” ᴹ

The man tries to straighten the fold in the sheet over his wrist. The fingers on that hand won't do quite what he wants yet.

“Did you tell them I was still alive?” ᵂᵒ

“Those are the first words.” ᴹ

They are, because Shoko let him dictate it and he kept putting the names of the men who had sent him here ahead of the thing his daughter needed to hear. The message went out before breakfast. Mau stayed to watch it go.

Downstairs, the kettle screams. It's in the kitchen, a long room away, and the man looks toward the sound before Mau does.

“That's the kettle,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know.” ᵂᵒ

After a moment the man lifts his fingers off the sheet and lets it stay creased.

Mau stays in the chair. The man looks as if he expected him to stand the moment he'd given the answer. Shoko passes the doorway and sees Mau still sitting there. She lifts a finger, asking for another minute before she brings a clean dressing in. Mau nods.

“I didn't think you'd tell her,” the man says. ᵂᵒ

Mau waits for the rest of it. The man is looking at the things somebody set by the window when they took his coat off. There's a little wooden horse with the front of its nose worn smooth. Shoko put it on top of the coat because she didn't know which pocket it came out of.

“I thought if you were keeping me here, there might be a way you wanted me to tell her. A thing you'd need me to say first.” ᵂᵒ

“I think she just wants to hear from you.” ᴹ

The man reaches toward the horse. Mau puts it on the bed where he can get to it. He tries twice before he gets his thumb around it the way he wanted. Then he moves the horse over to his other hand so he can feel the nose with his good thumb.

“If you see the little one who brought the pencil, tell her I used it.” ᵂᵒ

Mau says he will. At the door he steps aside to let Shoko through. She puts the dressing down, sees the horse in the man's hand, and asks whether he wants to keep holding it while she works. From the passage Mau hears him tell her yes.

By the time Mau goes down, Ori has taken the chair beside the kitchen door. She puts her book face down on the table.

“Can he go home?” ᴼ

“Shoko still wants him here today. He's asked for his daughter.” ᴹ

Ori thinks about that while he pours the hot water. Yesterday she wanted to know where to put the pencil once the injured people had told someone who they wanted. Today it's tucked behind her ear. He can see the teeth marks.

“Does she have to come alone?” ᴼ

“No. She can bring somebody.” ᴹ

Ori turns her book over again.

“Can you ask them to tell her that too?” ᴼ

The messenger has gone. There's still a place on the road where one of the ordinary shadows can leave a second note for the blue hands. Mau asks Ori if she wants to write it. She takes the pencil from behind her ear and tears off the ragged margin of the page so it will look a little better than the first one.

The reply from Elmesia comes while he is holding the paper flat for her.

Yuka carries it in against her chest, close enough that Mau can see where the envelope has picked up a crease. He catches himself making the same face at it that he has made every time someone brought a sealed letter into this room in the past six days.

“It's the island,” Yuka says. ʸ

His whole hand relaxes against the table.

“Open it.” ᴹ

“I was going to wait for you to come find me. I got as far as the kitchen.” ʸ

She sits beside Ori. Mau looks over her shoulder as she slides the letter out. Elmesia will be on the island in a week. They can stay as long as they like. She has checked the room herself and there is a stretch of water visible from the bed if they leave the balcony door open. She has also asked for them to be shown the smaller pool. The bird, in her estimation, probably still lives there.

“She remembered the table,” Yuka says. ʸ

“The bird remembers the table.” ᴹ

“Let me enjoy this for a minute.” ʸ

He settles his chin on her shoulder. She tips the letter a little so he can finish reading. Ori works out how many mornings a week is and mouths the numbers before she says anything.

“That's after my birthday.” ᴼ

“It is.” ʸ

Yuka looks up at Mau. They haven't agreed how long to stay. At the parliament he thought if he could bring her back for one afternoon and see her steal something off his plate there again, he could live on that for weeks. Now she's looking at him and silently asking him to make up his mind about a stretch of ordinary days.

“Six nights?” he says. ᴹ

Yuka reads the last line twice, smiling a little wider the second time.

“Yes.” ʸ

There's a small sound in the back of his head. DIO is considering how to interrupt.

*May I come with you? I want to see the island. I can do things on my own when you two are busy. I promise I'm quite capable of taking myself to lunch.* ᴰⁱᵒ

He relays the question to Yuka, including the part about lunch.

“I'd like you to come,” Yuka says, tipping her head back against him. “I want some days with just Mau. We can say which ones when we get there.” ʸ

*I thought you might want the entire week that way.* ᴰⁱᵒ

“Us wanting time alone doesn't mean you ruin the trip by coming,” Mau says. “We'd be happy to eat with you when we're all there. And Elmesia might want to see the island with you.” ᴹ

*Then I'd like to ask her myself.* ᴰⁱᵒ

Ori shifts her finished note over so that Yuka has a clear corner of the table to write on. By the time Mau takes it to send, Yuka has added a line at the bottom of hers and crossed it out again. He has only caught *Can we have* before she folds the letter.

“What did you change your mind about?” ᴹ

“Breakfast. I'm asking you instead.” ʸ

She hands him the envelope and tucks her cold toes beneath his leg under the table.

“First morning. Will you get up with me and go down to the water before we eat?” ʸ

He can imagine it well enough to hear her walking ahead of him, shoes in one hand.

“Wake me up when you're ready.” ᴹ
