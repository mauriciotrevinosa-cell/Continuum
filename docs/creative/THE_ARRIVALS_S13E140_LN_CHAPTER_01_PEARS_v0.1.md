# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 7 — Pears

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E140

*When: B+465. "22." Under it, in Ori's hand: "HARVEST (WAR)". A drawing of a pear wearing a helmet.*

The pears are ready.

Shuna says so at breakfast. She's been watching the old tree for a week, the one Kamish sleeps under, and the long row along the orchard wall, going out every morning to squeeze one or two, very gently, and frown, and come back in. This morning she came back in and didn't frown.

“Today,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

And Ori stands up on her chair.

* * *

Ori runs it like a war.

Mau has seen her run the Fort, and the expedition, and the scoring at the rematch. He's never seen her like this. She has a map of the orchard, drawn in the night apparently, with every tree numbered and a plan for every row. She has the house divided into units. She has a whistle, borrowed from somewhere, which she blows at the start of each phase and which makes Kamish jump every time.

“Unit One,” she says, standing on her crate at the edge of the orchard with her notebook. “Low branches. Rem, Umaru, Emilia, Satella.” ᴼ

“I can't reach anything,” says Umaru. ᵁ

“That's why you're on low branches,” says Ori. ᴼ

“Unit Two. Middle branches. Fern, Stark, Maki, Mai, Yuta, Jin-Woo.” ᴼ

“Unit Three. High branches.” She looks round. “Dad. And Kamish.” ᴼ

Kamish, under his tree, opens one eye.

“Unit Four,” says Ori. “Carrying. Beru.” ᴼ

Beru stands up very straight.

“Unit Five,” says Ori, and looks at the seven witches, sitting on a blanket at the edge of the orchard with a basket of their own and no intention of moving. “Eating.” ᴼ

Pride cheers. Daphne is already eating.

“And Milim,” says Ori. ᴼ

Milim, bouncing on her toes, looks up hopefully.

“Milim,” says Ori, very seriously, “does not shake the trees.” ᴼ

Milim's face falls.

“That's your whole job,” says Ori. “Not shaking them. It's the most important job. Nobody else could do it.” ᴼ

Milim considers this.

Then she stands up very straight, beside Beru, and folds her arms, and doesn't shake a single tree all day.

* * *

Kamish lowers the high branches.

Ori asks him to. She goes and stands in front of his nose with her map and explains it, at length, with gestures: the top of the old pear tree, where the best fruit is, where no ladder reaches. And Kamish looks at her for a long time with one eye.

Then he stands up.

He's enormous when he stands up. People forget. He unfolds from round the foot of the tree like a hill getting to its feet, and everyone in the orchard stops and looks up, and he reaches up, very slowly, with one great clawed forefoot, and hooks the top of the highest branch, and bends it down.

Gently. So gently. The way you'd bend down a branch for a child. The leaves barely shake. Not one pear falls.

He holds it there.

Ori, on her crate, looks at the branch hanging down in front of her, heavy with pears, at the height of her hands.

She looks at Kamish.

“Good,” she says. Very quietly. ᴼ

Kamish blinks, slowly, once.

* * *

Dabura discovers fruit picking.

He's been watching from the edge of the orchard, the way he watches most things in the house: sitting a little apart, with his huge hands folded, studying it. He's never picked fruit. Mau doesn't think he's ever done anything in his life that wasn't fighting or waiting to fight. Maru told them once, at the long table. *He's been alone on the mountain for so long, honing himself, he doesn't know how to do anything else.*

Halfway through the morning, Maru goes over and puts a pear in his hand.

Dabura looks at it.

He turns it over. He holds it up to the light. He smells it. He looks at the tree it came from, and the people picking, and the baskets filling.

Then he stands up, and goes to the nearest tree, and looks at it for a very long time with the expression of a man studying an opponent.

And reaches up, and picks a pear.

He looks at it in his enormous hand.

He picks another one.

By lunch he has filled four baskets on his own, very carefully, with tremendous seriousness, each pear placed in the basket as if it were a fragile and valuable weapon. He's started organising them by size. When Stark puts a slightly bruised one in his basket by mistake, Dabura takes it out again and looks at Stark with such profound disappointment that Stark apologises.

“The king,” says Maru, to Mau, watching, with something in his voice Mau hasn't heard before, “is enjoying himself.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“I can see,” says Mau. ᴹ

“He doesn't know he is,” says Maru. “Don't tell him.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

* * *

Beatrice opens the Fort door at noon.

She said she would. *The Fort should have a library door. That's obvious.* She comes out of the pantry in the middle of the harvest with a stack of books under her arm and marches across the orchard to the Fort, and stands in front of the small door at the back that leads to the cupboard where Ori keeps the reserves.

She puts her hand on the frame.

Mau feels it. The fold. The seam. Space pinched and stitched.

She opens the door.

Behind it, where the reserves cupboard was, there's a shelf. Just one. Low. At the height of a child. And past it, the dimness, going back and back.

Ori, who has come over at a run, stops and stares.

“Where are the *reserves*,” she says. ᴼ

“The shelf below,” says Beatrice. “Betty is not a monster, in fact.” ᴮᵉᵃ

Ori looks. The reserves are on the shelf below.

Beatrice puts her stack of books on the shelf above. All of them about fruit. How to grow it. How to pick it. How to keep it through the winter. A very old one, with pictures, about a pear that a king once traded his kingdom for.

“For the Fort,” says Beatrice. “Reference.” ᴮᵉᵃ

Ori looks at the books. She looks at Beatrice.

She doesn't say anything. She just takes the whistle out of her mouth and gives Beatrice a very formal Fort salute, which involves the spear, and which Beatrice returns, very stiffly, with her owl.

* * *

Yuka spends the afternoon on Mau's shoulders.

It's the only way to reach the top of the row along the wall, where Kamish can't fit his claws between the branches. She climbs up without asking, the way Ori climbs Kamish, and sits with her legs over his shoulders and her hands in the branches, and picks, and hands the pears down to him, and he hands them to Beru, and Beru carries them away.

She eats every fourth one.

“Supervisor's tax,” she says, when he points this out. ʸ

“You're not supervising.” ᴹ

“I'm supervising from up here. It's a very high supervisor's tax.” ʸ

He doesn't argue.

* * *

Halfway through the afternoon, she stops.

He feels her stop. Her hands go still in the branches. She doesn't pick the next pear. She just sits there on his shoulders, very quietly, and he can feel through the line that she's looking at something.

He looks up.

She isn't looking at the tree. She's looking round. At the orchard. At all of it.

Kamish, holding a branch down for Ori. Ori on her crate with her whistle. Dabura, at his fourth tree, organising pears by size, with Maru beside him not telling him he's enjoying himself. Rem and Umaru under the low branches, Umaru with a pear in each hand, Rem laughing at something she's said, their shoulders touching. Gojo up a ladder that Shoko is holding at the bottom, pointedly not looking at each other. DIO on the witches' blanket, writing a letter on her knee, which Mau doesn't need to ask about. Beatrice in the Fort doorway, reading her pear book. Makima on the orchard wall, with her hands in her lap, watching all of it, and, when she catches Yuka looking, raising one hand very slightly.

Yuka raises one back.

Then she goes on looking.

She doesn't say anything. She sits on his shoulders with her hands still in the branches and looks round at all of it, everyone, for a long time.

He feels something go through her. Down the line. Warm and quiet and very large. He can't name it. It isn't any one thing. It's the feeling of someone looking at a room full of people they love and understanding, slowly, all the way down, that they get to stay in it.

“Yuka?” he says. ᴹ

She looks down at him.

Her face is doing something he can't read. Something soft. Something thinking.

“Nothing,” she says. “Just looking.” ʸ

She picks the next pear. She hands it down.

But he notices. He notices for the rest of the afternoon. That every so often her hands stop in the branches. That she looks round again. That she's thinking something, slowly, all the way through, and hasn't finished yet.

He doesn't ask.

He's learned that much. When she's ready, she'll say the plain thing.

* * *

That evening there are pears in everything.

Shuna makes them into a tart. And a sauce, for the pork. And a cake. And something cold and sweet in a bowl that Umaru eats four helpings of. And a jam, in great quantities, in jars along the windowsill, glowing in the lamplight, enough to last the winter.

Dabura eats a whole pear at supper, slowly, with tremendous seriousness, and when he finishes it he sits for a while looking at the core in his hand.

“I picked this,” he says. Not to anyone in particular. ᴰᵇ

“You did,” says Maru. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Dabura looks at the core a moment longer.

“It's better,” he says, “than the ones you don't pick.” ᴰᵇ

Nobody laughs. Nobody says anything at all. But Mau sees Maru put his hand over his mouth, very quickly, and look away.

Beside Mau, Yuka is eating pear tart with her foot on his under the table, and not stealing his pickles, because there aren't any; Shuna made pear relish instead. She's quiet. Still thinking.

After supper, she takes the notebook out of her hoodie pocket and writes something in it, under *22*, at the bottom of the page.

He doesn't look.

But he sees her look at it for a long time afterwards, and close the notebook, and put her hand flat on the cover, as if keeping something in.
