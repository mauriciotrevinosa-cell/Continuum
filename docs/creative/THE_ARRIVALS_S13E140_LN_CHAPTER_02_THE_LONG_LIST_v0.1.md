# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 5 — Jam on Her Hands

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E140
**Continuity state:** B+466 ("21"). Yuka's point of view. The morning workshop with Mai, while Maki makes her nineteenth pin. Yuka has been thinking since the pear harvest. She asked for a breather after the false alarm before the island, close to three months ago, and Mau never once asked her to end it; he held her, and waited, and didn't make her guess. Now she has looked round the orchard at everyone she loves, at Rem and Umaru, at DIO writing letters on her knee, at Gojo up a ladder with Shoko at the bottom, and she has understood, all the way down, that she gets to stay. And she has said out loud that she let herself want a tomorrow with him. She tells Mai she thinks she's ready. Mai, who can't bear talking about this, listens anyway, and says the only useful thing: then tell him, in your own words, not his, and not the house's. Mai reminds her she'll have her own chance to say it to Mau. Yuka helps Mai work on the riding gear before joining Maki.

*When: B+466. A morning in Mai's workshop, after the pear harvest.*

Maki's nineteenth pin comes apart in her hand.

“*Again*,” she says, through her teeth. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Again,” Mai agrees, without looking up from her own bench. “You weren't holding the head. You're always thinking about the point. Think about the head.” ᴹᵃ

“The head is *boring*.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“The head is what stops it stabbing you.” ᴹᵃ

Maki makes a noise like a kettle and starts again.

Yuka, on her stool in the corner of the workshop with her knees drawn up and a bowl of Shuna's pear jam on a piece of bread, watches them and doesn't say anything.

She's started coming by on mornings when there's room at Mai's bench. She remembers asking Mai to eat with her after finding Mau's comb at Shoko's. Mai brought her work and made room for another plate. Now Yuka comes for the smell of cloth and metal and something that's always slightly burnt, and to hear Mai and Maki argue in short sharp sentences that are mostly love. Sometimes there really is something for her to hold.

This morning she's thinking.

She's been thinking since yesterday. Since the orchard. Since she sat on his shoulders with her hands in the branches and looked round.

* * *

She looked round and saw everyone.

That's all it was. She can't explain it better than that. She looked round at the orchard and saw them all. Rem and Umaru with their shoulders touching. DIO on the blanket writing a letter she wasn't going to tell anyone about. Gojo up a ladder and Shoko at the bottom of it, both pretending they weren't. Dabura with his pears. Beatrice in the Fort door. Makima on the wall, lifting one hand.

And Mau underneath her. Holding her up. So she could reach.

And she understood something.

It came slowly. Not like a door opening. Like a tide coming in. Like she'd been standing on a beach for a long time with her feet in the water and only just noticed it was up to her knees.

She gets to stay.

Not *she's allowed to*. Not *nobody's making her go*. She *gets to*. All of it. The orchard and the kitchen and the Fort and the notebook. Him. All of it, for as long as there is. Nobody is going to take it. Nobody is going to make her earn it. She doesn't have to be frightened of losing it in order to keep it.

She's been frightened for so long that she didn't know there was another way to hold something.

* * *

She thinks about the breather.

She asked for it before the island. After the false alarm. After hoping, and then not, and the floor going out from under her when it turned out to be nothing. She couldn't. She just couldn't. Not that part. Not then. She needed time with her feet on the ground, being close to him without that part, without the hoping, without being afraid of hoping.

And she told him. Plainly. And he said *okay*. The good kind. And he never once asked her to end it.

Not once. In close to three months. Not on the island. Not after the Rulers' gate. Not in the good weeks after, when she was all over him all day. Not in the bad weeks, when she was in Ori's bed and he was in the clinic. Not in the last three weeks, with her in his lap every evening and her face in his shoulder every night.

He never once made her guess whether he was waiting. He never once made her feel she owed it to him. He just held her, every night, and let her decide when.

She's never thought about that properly before.

She thinks about it now, in the workshop corner, with jam on her bread, and has to put the bread down.

* * *

“You're quiet,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks up.

Mai hasn't looked round. She's still at her bench, filing something small and complicated. But she's stopped filing.

“I'm thinking,” says Yuka. ʸ

“I know,” says Mai. “You've been thinking since yesterday. Your face does a thing.” ᴹᵃ

Across the workshop, Maki looks up from her twentieth pin.

“What thing?” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“A thing,” says Mai. “Make your pin.” ᴹᵃ

Maki looks at Yuka for a long moment with her grandmother's face. Then, to Yuka's enormous surprise, she puts the pin down, and gets up, and says, “I need air,” and goes out of the workshop door and closes it behind her.

Leaving the two of them alone.

Mai still doesn't turn round.

“Well?” she says. ᴹᵃ

* * *

Yuka tells her.

Not all of it. She doesn't know how to say all of it. She tells her about the orchard. About looking round. About getting to stay. About the breather, and how long it's been, and how he never once asked.

Mai listens.

She puts the file down. She sits on her stool with her back to Yuka and her hands flat on the bench, and listens, and doesn't say anything, and Yuka can see from the back of her neck that she would rather be absolutely anywhere else in the world.

Mai can't bear talking about this. Everyone knows that. Mai would rather talk about load-bearing joins for an hour than say one word about anything to do with anybody's heart.

She listens anyway.

“And I think,” says Yuka, at the end, “I think I'm ready. Again. I think I want to. I think I've wanted to for a while and I didn't notice because I was busy being frightened of other things.” ʸ

She stops.

“That's all,” she says. ʸ

* * *

Mai is quiet for a long time.

Then she turns round on her stool.

Her face is exactly the colour Yuka expected. She's looking at a point somewhere over Yuka's left shoulder. But she's turned round.

“Then tell him,” she says. ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks at her.

“That's all,” says Mai. “That's my whole advice. I'm not going to say anything else about it. Ever. I'm going to pretend this conversation never happened. But tell him.” ᴹᵃ

She hesitates.

“In your own words,” she says. “Not his. You'll want to say it the way he'd say it, carefully, so it doesn't land wrong. Don't. And not the house's words either. Not Umaru's, or Maki's, or anyone's. Don't go and ask anyone else how to say it. You've already asked me, and I'm telling you not to ask anyone else.” ᴹᵃ

She looks at Yuka, finally. Straight in the eye. Just for a second.

“Yours,” she says. “He's been waiting three months for your words. Not anybody else's. Give him yours.” ᴹᵃ

Then she turns round again, very fast, and picks up the file, and starts filing something so hard it squeaks.

* * *

Yuka sits in the corner with her bread and jam. There is a dent on the bench where Mai put down the edge of a buckle and leaned on it trying to make it take a straighter turn. Yuka runs the side of her thumb along the dent and thinks about how much she'd like to hear Mau come up the steps. He'd see the jam on her hand and get her something to wipe it off before he asked what she was doing here.

“Do you ever know before you ask?” Yuka says. ʸ

The file stops. It takes Mai a second to decide which of them Yuka's asking about.

“I knew what I wanted,” she says. “I still needed to know if Momo would say yes.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks at the buckle. “Did she?” ʸ

“She asked me why I'd spent a morning making two sets of gear if I meant to fly alone.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka can hear the bit of the answer Mai is choosing to leave out. She sets down the bread and goes to look for a cloth.

“What does that one need?” she asks, coming back with it. ʸ

“Hold it straight. Good. Now turn it round for me.” ᴹᵃ

She does. Mai has her start with exactly the piece she thinks she'll get wrong, and stays where she can see her do it. By the time Yuka finds the jam again she's got another strip of fabric looped over her wrist so Momo can see what they changed. She wipes her hand before she touches it. She thinks she can ask Mau to come and sit with her whenever she wants to tell him. That part she'd be able to say right now.

* * *

Maki comes back in from her air.

She looks at Yuka in the corner with the strap looped round her wrist. She looks at Mai at her bench, filing something into dust. She doesn't ask.

She sits down at her own bench and picks up a fresh pin.

“Think about the head,” says Mai, without looking up. ᴹᵃ

Maki thinks about the head.

The twentieth pin holds.
