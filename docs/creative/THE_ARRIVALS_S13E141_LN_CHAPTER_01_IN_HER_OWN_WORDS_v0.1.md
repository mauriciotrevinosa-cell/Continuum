She doesn't plan it.

That's the thing she decides, in the end, lying awake the night after the workshop with her face in his shoulder and the notebook under her pillow. She doesn't plan it. She doesn't choose a place, or a time, or a way. She doesn't think about what he'd want, or what would be romantic, or what Umaru did on the roof or what DIO said in the garden. She doesn't ask anyone else.

*Yours*, Mai said. *Give him yours.*

So the next day she just lives it. Breakfast. The workshop. Lunch. An afternoon on the couch with him reading out loud and Ori on the rug. Supper. Pear tart. His pickles, stolen, one by one.

And after supper, when the house is going in and the lamps are being lit and the yard is blue and cold, she takes his hand and pulls him out onto the porch step and sits him down.

* * *

He sits.

He looks at her. He doesn't ask. He's learned not to ask. He just waits, with his hand in hers, the way he's waited every night for three months.

She takes the notebook out of the hoodie pocket.

She opens it to the back. To the list. The long one. She turns it round so he can see. She holds it out.

She points to the last line. The one at the bottom. The one she left a space for on the first morning on the wet grass, and wrote yesterday, in the workshop, with jam on her fingers.

He looks at it.

She watches him read it. She watches it go into him. She watches his face go very still.

She's already decided she's going to say it out loud anyway. Writing it down isn't enough. Writing it down is still a little bit hiding.

* * *

“The breather's over,” says Yuka. ʸ

Her voice comes out steadier than she expected.

“I asked for it,” she says. “Before the island. After the false alarm. I couldn't do the hoping anymore, not then. And you said okay. And you never asked me to end it. Not once. Not in three months. You held me every night and you never once made me guess whether you were waiting.” ʸ

She takes a breath.

“That's part of why,” she says. “I want you to know that. You never pushed. Not even a little. So I got to decide. And I've decided.” ʸ

She looks at him.

“I'm not frightened of hoping anymore,” she says. “I worked that out in the orchard. I was frightened of everything for so long. Of losing you. Of losing the house. Of wanting things in case they got taken. And then I looked round, at all of them, at all of you, and I understood I get to stay. I get to keep it. I don't have to be frightened to hold on to it.” ʸ

Her hand tightens on his.

“So I'm not frightened,” she says. “And I want you.” ʸ

She doesn't make it softer. She doesn't make it clever. She says it the way Makima said to say things, the way Mai said: plain, in her own words, all the way out.

“I want you,” she says again. “Tonight. That's mine. I'm telling you.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't say anything for a moment.

She watches him not say anything. She can feel him, down the line. The line he threw open in the clinic, all at once, like a window. She can feel everything going through him, very fast, very warm, too much of it to name.

And under it, the one thing.

He asks it very quietly.

“Are you sure?” ᴹ

Not *do you mean it*. Not *are you ready*. He isn't asking her to prove anything. She can feel that. She can feel exactly what he's asking, the way she can feel his heart. He's asking because he's spent three months making sure she never had to guess about him, and he wants to make sure, once more, before anything, that she isn't guessing about herself.

She loves him so much it frightens her.

It doesn't frighten her.

“Yes,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He looks at her a moment longer.

Then he looks down at the notebook in her other hand, still open at the last line. He reaches out and closes it. Gently. With his thumb. And puts it back in the hoodie pocket for her, and his hand stays there, on the outside of the pocket, over the notebook, over her.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

The good kind. The best kind. The kind she's never heard him say quite like that before.

She stands up.

She doesn't let go of his hand.

* * *

They go in.

Through the kitchen. Past the lamp on the table and the jars of pear jam along the windowsill and Shuna, at the sink, who doesn't turn round. Past the common room, where Umaru is setting up a game and Rem is helping and Ori is asleep on Beru on the rug, and nobody looks up, and the whole room very carefully doesn't look up, so hard that Yuka can feel it like warmth from a fire.

Up the stairs. Every step.

To their room.

She stops in the doorway.

She looks at it. The bed. The window open a little. The lamp. The place on his pillow where the notebook was, once, with *53* in her hand.

She turns round to look at him.


“Come in,” she says. ʸ

His hand is still in hers. She takes one step backwards, and he takes one forwards, and the door is at his shoulder. He reaches round for it with his free hand. The latch catches on the first try. She has come up here with him a hundred times. Tonight she hears that little click all the way down to the soles of her feet.

There's cold air coming in off the yard. He glances at the window, and she draws his hand inside the cuff of her hoodie before he can go over to it. His knuckles are cold too.

“Are you cold?” he asks. ᴹ

“A bit. I want you here first.” ʸ

He comes close enough that the side of his shoe touches hers. She puts his hand at her waist, above the pocket. When she leans up to kiss him the corner of the notebook jabs her in the stomach.

“Ow.” ʸ

Mau draws back so quickly she has to catch his sleeve.

“The book,” she says. “You're doing fine.” ʸ

He looks down at the pocket with grave disapproval. She gets the notebook out and lays it on the table beside the lamp. It takes a moment to turn back, because she's laughing a little and wants enough breath for the kiss this time. When she does turn, he's smiling. She puts her palms on his cheeks to keep the smile where she can see it.

“Where were we?” he asks. ᴹ

“Here.” ʸ

She kisses him. She feels him smile again halfway through it, and the small, warm breath that goes with it. Her hands slide down to his collar. His thumb moves once at her waist. When she pulls back he follows a fraction before he catches himself. She kisses him again for that, quick and right on the mouth.

“Do you want to take your shoes off?” he asks. ᴹ

“Eventually.” ʸ

“Okay.” ᴹ

He gives her as much of himself as he can with both of them standing up in their shoes. She tucks her face into his neck, right where the porch cold is still on his skin, and feels the heat coming back under her cheek. She could make them stand here for a very long time. He'd probably let her. The thought makes her hold him harder.

“Actually,” she murmurs, “shoes.” ʸ

She sits on the edge of the bed to undo hers. Mau takes one off standing and has an entirely avoidable fight with the other. By the time he gets it free, she's pulled the blanket back on his side.

“Leave the lamp?” she asks. ʸ

His eyes go to hers. “Yes.” ᴹ

The mattress sinks beside her. For a little while their knees touch and that's all. She can hear a chair scrape downstairs, then the muffled rise of somebody laughing. The sound dies against the floorboards. Mau turns his hand over between them. She sets hers in it.

“You can tell me what you want, too,” she says. “I'd like to hear it.” ʸ

His thumb stops against the side of hers. In the lamplight she sees the colour come up along his cheek. It's there for two whole breaths before he speaks.

“I want you,” he says. “And I'd like you to come closer.” ᴹ

Her first instinct is to tease him about how little room he's left her to do it. Then she sees how carefully he's watching her, and how pink he still is, and leaves it for another time. She puts one knee up on the bed and turns towards him. He shifts back just enough to make space. She takes his face in her hands again.

“Closer than that,” she says. ʸ

He laughs, and she feels it right where her wrist rests against his throat. He brings his arm round her. She gets to hear him draw breath before she kisses him, and when the kiss ends she stays where she is, with her forehead against his.

“I might need a second now and then,” she says. ʸ

“Take it.” ᴹ

“You too.” ʸ

He presses his lips to her temple. “I will.” ᴹ

She takes the first second with her thumb resting in the little fold of his collar. She can feel the fabric move each time he breathes. He stays close enough for her to lean in the rest of the way, and when she does, he lets out a breath against her mouth. She had thought she'd need to sit back and collect herself. All she needed was to feel him wait while she made up her mind.

“I'm ready,” she says. “Come down with me?” ʸ

She scoots back until her shin clears the side of the bed. He takes her hand as she lowers herself onto the pillow and sets his other palm beside her shoulder. The dip in the mattress brings him close. She reaches up to wrap her arm round him and finds the blanket pinned under her elbow.

“Hang on. I've caught myself.” ʸ

Mau lifts his hand. She tugs the blanket free, and the corner of it lands across the back of his head.

“Did I do something?” he asks, from under it. ᴹ

“You were in the way.” ʸ

She pulls the blanket down herself. His hair comes with it, flattened over one eye. She uses two fingers to push it back. He's holding himself steady while she does it, trying to give her a straight face, and the effort is visible in his mouth. The first laugh is hers. She feels his follow against her forehead. By the time they settle, she has her arm round him and the blanket is wherever it is.

When they make love, she wants to keep looking at him. He lowers his head to kiss her, and she catches his cheek in her palm.

“Up a little,” she says. “There. I can see you now.” ʸ

In the lamplight she sees his lips part before she hears him breathe. He leans down again, slowly enough for her to meet him, and the kiss ends against the corner of her mouth. Her fingers close on his shoulder. She had meant to tell him to stay that close, and for a breath she forgets what she meant to say.

“A second,” she manages. ʸ

He goes still. His breath is warm along her cheek. She smooths her hand over the place where she's been gripping his shoulder and feels him relax into it by degrees.

“Can I hold you while we wait?” he asks. ᴹ

“Yes. Here.” ʸ

She turns onto her side and draws him down beside her. His hand leaves the mattress, and the pillow lifts a little under her head. She draws his hand round to her back, and his palm settles there, warm and quite steady. She takes another breath with her face against his jaw. It comes easier this time. She raises her head and kisses him back. The hand at her back draws her closer, and when he pulls away to see her she follows, just far enough to make him laugh.

“Now who needs a second?” she asks. ʸ

“Me, actually.” ᴹ

She keeps her hand on his cheek. The laughter takes a moment to leave his face. She gets to see it go, and to see what he looks like after it does.

“What do you want?” she asks. ʸ

“Your hand.” ᴹ

She starts to lift the one on his cheek. He catches her other wrist, and she understands. She opens that hand, and he threads his fingers through hers against the pillow.

“Both?” she says. ʸ

He turns his face a little into her palm. “Both.” ᴹ

She stays like that until he squeezes her hand and says her name. She says his back. The next time he leans in, she keeps her eyes open until he's too close for her to see him properly. She feels him smile at her mouth. She wants to keep him there and pulls him closer.

She keeps her fingers between his while their breathing settles. When she lifts their hands, the seam of the pillow has left a crease along the back of her wrist. She turns her hand to look at it. Mau rubs his thumb over the line once, then brings her knuckles to his mouth.

“Warm enough?” he asks. ᴹ

The air has cooled on the side of her face that was turned towards the window. She inches closer and brings his hand down to her waist.

“Now I am.” ʸ

He brings the blanket up to her shoulder, and she waits until his arm is settled before she reaches for the lamp. She gets an inch off the pillow. His arm tightens.

“Where are you going?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks at the lamp. Then she looks down at his arm.

“Nowhere, apparently.” ʸ

She settles back. He laughs into her shoulder. One of the corners of the blanket is folded in on itself, and when she reaches around him to straighten it, her fingers find the back of his shirt. The fabric is warm now. She leaves her hand there.

“Tomorrow,” she says. ʸ

He tips his chin up so he can see her.

“I'd like breakfast with you. Late, if we sleep late.” ʸ

“How late?” ᴹ

“I'll tell you when I wake up.” ʸ

He gives this a moment's consideration. “I can live with that.” ᴹ

She fits a hand behind his neck and draws his head down to her chest. He helps her a little, lifting his shoulder so she can get her arm underneath. She feels him settle, then feels the small part of his weight he's still holding up on an elbow.

“Let go,” she says, into his hair. ʸ

His elbow moves. All at once he's warm and heavy against her. She holds him there with her cheek against the top of his head, and after a few breaths the hand at her waist goes slack. His next breath is deep enough to move the blanket. She can see the lamp over his shoulder. It can burn a while longer.

Her fingers find the back of his shirt. She takes a little of the cloth in her hand and closes her eyes. By the time the house goes quiet, she's asleep too.

* * *

In the morning, she writes it in the notebook.

Under *20*. Under the word she wrote the night before and went over twice. In the space she'd saved at the bottom of the long list, beside the last line, she makes a tick.

Then she looks at the list. All of it. Every line. Every tick.

She closes the notebook.

She doesn't need the list anymore.

She puts it under her pillow anyway. Some things you keep.
