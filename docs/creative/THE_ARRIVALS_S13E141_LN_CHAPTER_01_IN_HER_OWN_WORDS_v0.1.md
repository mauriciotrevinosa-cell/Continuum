She doesn't plan it.

That's the thing she decides, in the end, lying awake the night after the workshop with her face in his shoulder and the notebook under her pillow. She doesn't plan it. She doesn't choose a place, or a time, or a way. She doesn't think about what he'd want, or what would be romantic, or what Umaru did on the roof or what DIO said in the garden. She doesn't ask anyone else.

*Yours*, Mai said. *Give him yours.*

So the next day she just lives it. Breakfast. The workshop. Lunch. An afternoon on the couch with him reading out loud and Ori on the rug. Supper. Pear tart. His pickles, stolen, one by one.

And after supper, when the house is going in and the lamps are being lit and the yard is blue and cold, she takes his hand and pulls him out onto the porch step and sits him down.

* * *

He sits.

He looks at her. He doesn't ask. He's learned not to ask. He just waits, with his hand in hers, the way he's waited every night for three months.

She takes the notebook out of the hoodie pocket.

She opens it to the back. To the list. The long one. She turns it round so he can see. She holds it out.

She points to the last line. The one at the bottom. The one she left a space for on the first morning on the wet grass, and wrote yesterday, in the workshop, with jam on her fingers.

He looks at it.

She watches him read it. She watches it go into him. She watches his face go very still.

She's already decided she's going to say it out loud anyway. Writing it down isn't enough. Writing it down is still a little bit hiding.

* * *

“The breather's over,” says Yuka. ʸ

Her voice comes out steadier than she expected.

“I asked for it,” she says. “Before the island. After the false alarm. I couldn't do the hoping anymore, not then. And you said okay. And you never asked me to end it. Not once. Not in three months. You held me every night and you never once made me guess whether you were waiting.” ʸ

She takes a breath.

“That's part of why,” she says. “I want you to know that. You never pushed. Not even a little. So I got to decide. And I've decided.” ʸ

She looks at him.

“I'm not frightened of hoping anymore,” she says. “I worked that out in the orchard. I was frightened of everything for so long. Of losing you. Of losing the house. Of wanting things in case they got taken. And then I looked round, at all of them, at all of you, and I understood I get to stay. I get to keep it. I don't have to be frightened to hold on to it.” ʸ

Her hand tightens on his.

“So I'm not frightened,” she says. “And I want you.” ʸ

She doesn't make it softer. She doesn't make it clever. She says it the way Makima said to say things, the way Mai said: plain, in her own words, all the way out.

“I want you,” she says again. “Tonight. That's mine. I'm telling you.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't say anything for a moment.

She watches him not say anything. She can feel him, down the line. The line he threw open in the clinic, all at once, like a window. She can feel everything going through him, very fast, very warm, too much of it to name.

And under it, the one thing.

He asks it very quietly.

“Are you sure?” ᴹ

Not *do you mean it*. Not *are you ready*. He isn't asking her to prove anything. She can feel that. She can feel exactly what he's asking, the way she can feel his heart. He's asking because he's spent three months making sure she never had to guess about him, and he wants to make sure, once more, before anything, that she isn't guessing about herself.

She loves him so much it frightens her.

It doesn't frighten her.

“Yes,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He looks at her a moment longer.

Then he looks down at the notebook in her other hand, still open at the last line. He reaches out and closes it. Gently. With his thumb. And puts it back in the hoodie pocket for her, and his hand stays there, on the outside of the pocket, over the notebook, over her.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

The good kind. The best kind. The kind she's never heard him say quite like that before.

She stands up.

She doesn't let go of his hand.

* * *

They go in.

Through the kitchen. Past the lamp on the table and the jars of pear jam along the windowsill and Shuna, at the sink, who doesn't turn round. Past the common room, where Umaru is setting up a game and Rem is helping and Ori is asleep on Beru on the rug, and nobody looks up, and the whole room very carefully doesn't look up, so hard that Yuka can feel it like warmth from a fire.

Up the stairs. Every step.

To their room.

She stops in the doorway.

She looks at it. The bed. The window open a little. The lamp. The place on his pillow where the notebook was, once, with *53* in her hand.

She turns round to look at him.

In the morning, she writes it in the notebook.

Under *20*. Under the word she wrote the night before and went over twice. In the space she'd saved at the bottom of the long list, beside the last line, she makes a tick.

Then she looks at the list. All of it. Every line. Every tick.

She closes the notebook.

She doesn't need the list anymore.

She puts it under her pillow anyway. Some things you keep.
