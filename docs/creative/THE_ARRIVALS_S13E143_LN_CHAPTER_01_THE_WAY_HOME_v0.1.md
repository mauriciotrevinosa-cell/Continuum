*When: B+470. "17". Ori drew the valley beside it, very small, with an arrow going off the edge of the page and the words "EXPEDITION THREE".*

Ori wakes up first.

She always does. Mau hears her before he opens his eyes: the tent flap untying, boots being pulled on in a hurry, the small fierce sounds of someone who has decided the day has started and everyone else is late.

He opens his eyes.

Yuka is asleep on the other side of the empty place where Ori was. Face in the blanket. Hand stretched out across Ori's side, toward him. She kept her word. In the tent, not on him. Her hand found its way over in the night anyway.

He doesn't move it.

Outside, Ori says, very loudly, to Kamish: “*Good morning*. You can sit now. Nobody's watching.” ᴼ

Kamish, apparently, does not sit.

* * *

Ori follows the stream.

After breakfast, which is cold pears and colder rolls and the last of the honey cake, she packs her satchel and takes her map and her spear and walks down the stream away from the camp, out from under the wild pear trees, to where the valley opens out.

Mau and Yuka follow. Beru goes in front of her, a few steps, the way he always does now when there's somewhere new.

The stream gets wider. The valley gets wider. The hills on either side get lower and lower and then stop.

And Ori stops too.

* * *

In front of her, the land just goes on.

More hills. Long and low and folded, going away to the north and the west as far as Mau can see, fold after fold, getting paler and paler until they stop being hills and start being sky. A river somewhere in the middle of it, the stream they've been following, getting bigger, going away. No roads. No smoke. No marks. Nothing.

Just more.

Ori stands at the edge of it with her map in her hand.

She doesn't say anything for a long time.

Then she unfolds the map. She looks at the valley she drew last night, with EVERYONE'S PEARS at the bottom. She looks at the edge of the page, where the valley stops.

She takes out her pencil and draws an arrow. Off the edge of the paper. Pointing north.

Under it she writes: *EXPEDITION THREE*.

She folds the map up.

“Not today,” she says. ᴼ

She looks up at Mau.

“It's too big for today,” she says. “It needs supplies. And a plan. A long plan. And more ropes.” ᴼ

“In case one rope,” says Mau. ᴹ

“In case *two* ropes,” says Ori, very seriously. ᴼ

* * *

Beru finds the stone on the way back up the valley.

He stops in the stream, up to his knees, and reaches down into the water, and comes up with something in his claw. He holds it out to Ori.

It's a stone. Flat and smooth and pale, the size of Ori's palm. And right through the middle of it, a hole. Worn through by the water, over who knows how many years, so round and clean it looks as if someone made it on purpose.

Ori takes it. She holds it up and looks through the hole. At the sky. At the hills. At Mau, through the hole.

Then she looks at Beru.

And gives it back.

Beru looks at the stone in his claw. Then at her.

“It's your find,” says Ori. “You found it. On an expedition. That's the rule. You keep your finds.” ᴼ

Beru looks at the stone for a long time.

Then he puts it somewhere about his person, beside the terrible pear he's been carrying since yesterday, and Mau feels something go through him, down the line, so large and warm that Mau has to look away.

* * *

The compass brings them home.

Ori holds it the whole way. She doesn't look at the map once. She just holds the compass out in front of her on its string, walking very fast, and follows the needle. Up the valley. Over the far rim. Across the basin, past the black ring, where she stops for a moment and looks at it, and then goes on. Up the near rim. To the ridge.

The ruins.

* * *

The pears are gone.

All three. The ones Ori left on the top of the stone yesterday, in a row, carefully, so they wouldn't roll off.

Gone.

Ori stops in front of the stone.

There's nothing on it. Just the words, cut deep. REST HERE. WE DID.

And round the foot of the stone, in the dust, small tracks. Lots of them. Going round and round the stone and away into the grass. Something small. Several somethings. Four feet each. Ori crouches down and looks at them very closely.

Mau looks at them too. With the eyes.

Nothing dangerous. Something like a fox, maybe. Or something like a big rabbit. Something small and wild that lives in the ruins, that came out in the night and found three pears on a stone where there were never pears before.

Ori stands up.

She looks at the empty stone. She looks at the tracks.

“Something was hungry,” she says. ᴼ

“Something was hungry,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

Ori nods slowly.

“They rested here,” she says. “And they were hungry. And there were pears.” ᴼ

She looks satisfied. More than satisfied. She looks the way she looked when Kamish sat.

“Good,” she says. ᴼ

She takes out her map and, beside the ruins, very small, she draws three pears and a set of small tracks going away.

* * *

Makima steps out at the ridge.

Mau feels her go: up from the table inside, and out, and into the world, at his right shoulder, exactly where she was yesterday. She looks round at the four of them. At Ori's muddy knees. At Yuka's hair, which has had a night in a tent. At Beru, walking with his claw pressed to something about his person.

“Well?” she says. ᴹᵏ

Ori tells her.

All of it. The basin and the valley and the wild pears and EVERYONE'S PEARS (TERRIBLE) and the tent and the stars and the edge where the hills go on forever and EXPEDITION THREE and Beru's stone and the empty stone at the ruins and the tracks.

It takes all the way down the hill road.

Makima listens to every word. She asks questions. Good ones. About the tracks, and the arrow, and whether the pears were really that terrible.

They were, says Ori. They were the worst pears she's ever eaten. She's going back for more.

* * *

The house is on the porch.

All of it. They come down the hill road at dusk and there it is, lit up in every window, and everyone standing on the porch, the way the house stands when someone comes home. Gojo waving both arms. Umaru with the banner. The witches on their step. Rem with her hand in Umaru's. Shuna in the kitchen door with a cloth over her shoulder.

And Frieren.

At the top of the porch steps. Not waving. Just standing there, with her staff, watching Ori come down the road.

Ori sees her.

She stops. Just for a second. Then she runs.

* * *

She reports to the Fort first. That's the rule. Fort business before anything.

She stands on her crate in the yard in the last of the light with her muddy knees and her map unfolded, and reports, and the Fort listens: Umaru and Milim and Emilia and Rem and Beatrice with her owl, and Beru, who was there, listening to it all again as if for the first time.

At the end, she folds up the map.

Then she gets down off the crate and goes across the yard to the porch steps, where Frieren is still standing.

She takes the compass off the string round her neck. She holds it out.

“Returned,” she says. “It's a lend.” ᴼ

Frieren takes it.

She doesn't look at the needle. She looks at Ori.

“Where did it point?” she says. ᶠ

Ori looks up at her.

“At you,” she says. “The whole time.” ᴼ

Frieren doesn't say anything.

She crouches down, slowly, so their faces are level, and takes Ori's face in both hands, very gently, the way you'd hold something you'd found after a long time looking.

Then she lets go, and stands up, and puts the compass in the pocket of her robe.

“Supper,” she says. ᶠ

Ori goes in to supper.

Frieren stays on the step a moment longer. Mau, coming up behind with Yuka, sees her take the compass back out of her pocket and look at it, in the light from the kitchen window.

He doesn't see where the needle's pointing.

He doesn't need to.
