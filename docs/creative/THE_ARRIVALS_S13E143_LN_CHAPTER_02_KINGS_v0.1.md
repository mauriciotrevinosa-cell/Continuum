# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 11 — Kings

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E143
**Continuity state:** B+471 ("16"). Dabura asks Mau to sit with him on the orchard wall in the evening. He wants to know what a king is in this world, since everyone keeps calling Mau one and Mau keeps saying he isn't. They talk about it, slowly, the way Dabura talks. Dabura says on the mountain a king was the one nobody could beat; here it seems to be the one who picks pears with everyone. Then, while they talk, Dabura's hands do something on their own: the fingers of his right hand trace a shape on the stone of the wall, over and over, eight lines from a centre. He doesn't notice. Mau does. It is the wheel. Dabura has no memory of the wheel; his body does. Seed only: the Rulers took his memory and missed his hands. Mau doesn't tell him. Ciel files it.

*When: B+471. "16." Dabura has drawn something beside it, in the clumsy careful hand of someone who learned to write very recently: a pear.*

Dabura asks him to the orchard wall in the evening.

He doesn't ask in words. He comes and stands in the kitchen doorway after supper, filling it, and looks at Mau, and then looks out toward the orchard, and then back at Mau. And waits.

Maru, at the table, says quietly: “He'd like to talk to you. Outside. He doesn't know how to ask.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

Mau gets up.

* * *

They sit on the orchard wall.

The same wall where Yuka sat with Cross, and called her wheel, and sat with Makima after. It's a long low wall of old stone at the bottom of the orchard, with the pear trees behind it and the fields in front, and in the evening the light comes across the fields flat and long.

Dabura sits on it carefully, the way he does everything that isn't fighting, as if he's afraid the wall might break. It doesn't. He's very big. His hands, on his knees, are the size of Mau's head.

He doesn't say anything for a long time.

Mau doesn't either. He's learned that with Dabura. You wait. The words come up slowly, from a long way down, like water in a deep well, and if you rush him they don't come at all.

* * *

“King,” says Dabura at last. ᴰᵇ

Mau waits.

“Everyone calls you that,” says Dabura. “Tsurugi. The small one with the spear. The ant. The dragon, when he speaks. *King*.” ᴰᵇ

“I'm not,” says Mau. ᴹ

“You say that,” says Dabura. “Every time. *I'm not.* And they keep saying it.” ᴰᵇ

He looks out at the fields.

“On the mountain,” he says, “I was king. That's what they called me, when they still came up to find me. Before they stopped coming.” ᴰᵇ

He turns his huge hands over on his knees and looks at the palms.

“King was the one nobody could beat,” he says. “That was all it meant. The strongest. The one at the top. The one nobody could stand in front of.” ᴰᵇ

He looks at Mau.

“So when they said you were king,” he says, “I came down. To see. To find out if you were the one nobody could beat.” ᴰᵇ

“And I beat you,” says Mau. ᴹ

“You beat me,” Dabura agrees. Without any bitterness at all. Just a fact. “And then you told me to get up.” ᴰᵇ

* * *

He's quiet for a while.

“But that isn't why they call you king,” he says. ᴰᵇ

Mau looks at him.

“I've been watching,” says Dabura. “Since I came down. Since the quarry. I watch everything. It's what I did on the mountain, for a long time, when there was nothing else. Watched.” ᴰᵇ

He looks back at the orchard. At the trees. At the baskets still stacked by the Fort door from the harvest.

“You picked pears,” he says. ᴰᵇ

“Everyone picked pears.” ᴹ

“Yes,” says Dabura. “Everyone. You too. On the ground. With a woman on your shoulders taking every fourth one. The small one gave orders and you did what she said.” ᴰᵇ

He shakes his head slowly.

“On the mountain,” he says, “the king did not pick pears. The king did not do what the small one said. The king sat at the top, alone, and was strong, and waited for someone to come and try.” ᴰᵇ

He looks at his hands again.

“I think,” he says slowly, “in this world, a king is something else. I think it's the one who picks pears with everyone. And everyone wants him to be there while they do it.” ᴰᵇ

Mau doesn't say anything.

He doesn't know what to say. He's never thought about it like that. He's never thought about it at all. He's spent so long saying *I'm not* that he never stopped to wonder what they meant when they said it.

“I'd like to be that kind,” says Dabura. Very quietly. ᴰᵇ

* * *

That's when Mau sees the hands.

Dabura doesn't know he's doing it. He's looking out at the fields, talking slowly about pears and kings, and his right hand has come off his knee and is resting on the stone of the wall beside him, flat, and one finger is moving.

Tracing something. On the stone.

A line, from a centre point, outward. Then another line, from the same centre, at an angle. Then another. Eight of them. Then back to the first, and round again. Eight lines from a centre. Over and over. The finger moving slowly and surely, the way you trace a shape you've traced a thousand times.

Mau goes very still.

He knows that shape.

He's carried it over his head into a quarry, into a library, into a domain clash. Eight spokes. A centre. Turning.

The wheel.

* * *

At the quarry, at the end, when Mau called the wheel and it rose over his head, Dabura stopped. Mid-step. Staring at it. *At a wheel he cannot place and already knows*, Mau thought, at the time. And afterwards, on his back, with the light at his throat, his hands still half raised toward it, as if waiting for something he couldn't remember.

Mau thought about it, afterwards. Then the war came. Then everything else. He put it behind a door.

The door is open now.

Dabura's finger traces the eight lines again. And again. Slowly. Surely. His eyes on the fields. He hasn't looked down once.

His mind doesn't know the wheel.

His hand does.

* * *

*Ciel*, says Mau. Inside. Very quiet. ᴹ

*I see it*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*He doesn't remember.* ᴹ

*No. Maru told us. He doesn't remember much of anything from before the mountain. A long time. Gaps.* ᶜ

A pause.

*Somebody took it*, says Ciel. *His memory. Very carefully. A long time ago. Whoever did it was very good.* ᶜ

*The Rulers.* ᴹ

*I don't know that*, says Ciel. *I'm not going to say I know it. But whoever it was, they took it out of his head.* ᶜ

*And missed his hands*, says Mau. ᴹ

*And missed his hands*, Ciel agrees. ᶜ

* * *

Dabura is still talking.

About the mountain. About the long years alone. About what it was like when the others stopped coming up, and then Tsurugi came with stories about a man in the south whom everyone called king, and he came down.

His finger keeps tracing.

Mau listens to all of it. He doesn't interrupt. He doesn't say *look at your hand*. He doesn't say *you know this shape*.

He doesn't know what it means yet. He doesn't know what was taken, or why, or who by, or what the wheel has to do with any of it. He doesn't know whether Dabura would want to know. Whether it would hurt him. Whether it's a door Dabura would want opened, or one he'd want left alone.

It isn't Mau's to open.

Not yet. Not without asking.

So he sits on the wall and listens to Dabura talk about kings, and watches the finger trace eight lines on the stone, over and over, until the light goes.

* * *

When they get up to go in, Dabura stops.

He looks down at the wall. At the place where his hand was.

There's nothing there. Fingers don't leave marks on stone. But he looks at it for a long moment anyway, with a small frown, the way you look at a place where you're sure you put something down.

Then he shakes his head and turns to go.

“Thank you,” he says. “For sitting.” ᴰᵇ

“Any time,” says Mau. ᴹ

Dabura nods. He walks back up through the orchard toward the house, enormous and careful, ducking under the low branches of the pear trees so as not to knock any fruit down.

Mau stays on the wall a moment longer.

He puts his own hand flat on the stone. Where Dabura's was.

*File it*, he says. ᴹ

*Filed*, says Ciel. *Not a report. A note. For when you want it.* ᶜ

*For when he wants it*, says Mau. ᴹ

A pause.

*Yes*, says Ciel. *For when he wants it.* ᶜ
