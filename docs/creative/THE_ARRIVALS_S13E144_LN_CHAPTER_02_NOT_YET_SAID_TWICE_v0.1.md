*When: B+473. "14." Under it, in Emilia's careful hand, which is still learning the letters of this world: "tea (and a feeling)".*

Emilia has tea with Satella every week.

She has since the second day. Since the day after the gate, when Mau and Yuka carried the cups and Emilia sat across a small table from the woman whose face she had been told her whole life she wore. The Witch of Envy. The one in the stories. The one whose shadow swallowed half the world.

Satella looked at her across the table and said only her name. *Emilia.* Very quietly. As if it were something precious she was afraid to drop.

And Emilia, who had been afraid of this face her whole life, looked back at it and found she wasn't afraid at all.

So she came back the next week. And the next. And every week she asks one question. And every week, when the question is a hard one, Satella says *not yet*. Not *no*. Never *no*. Just *not yet*.

Emilia has learned to like it. *Not yet* is a door that might open.

* * *

Today she's early.

She comes to the north-wing terrace where the witches have their table, with Puck on her shoulder, and Satella isn't there. The table is laid. Two cups. But the chair is empty.

Emilia looks round.

Mau is sitting on the porch step across the yard. Eyes closed. Hands loose in his lap. Not asleep; Emilia knows by now what that looks like. He's inside. In the room with the table and the cup.

And Satella's chair is empty because Satella is in there with him.

Emilia sits down at the tea table to wait.

* * *

She can't see inside. Nobody can, except Mau and the people who live there, and now the seven witches, who visit. But Mau has a way of letting things be heard, sometimes, when he forgets to keep them in. And across the yard, very faintly, the way you hear a party in another house, she can hear it.

Laughing.

Pride, loud and high. Something clattering, spoons maybe. Minerva shouting that something isn't fair. Echidna saying something dry. And under all of it, threading through it, a laugh Emilia has never heard before.

Satella's.

Not the small soft breath that is the nearest Satella usually comes to laughing. A real one. Bright and helpless, like water going over stones.

Emilia sits at the empty tea table on the terrace with her cup in her hands and listens to it.

And something goes through her.

Small. Sharp. Sudden. Under the ribs. Like a splinter.

She doesn't know what it is.

* * *

“That's envy,” says Puck. ᴾᵘ

Emilia looks at him.

He's on her shoulder, curled round the back of her neck, his tail over her collarbone, and he's looking at her with his small dark eyes, very wise and very amused.

“What is?” says Emilia. ᴱᵐ

“What you're feeling,” says Puck. “That little sharp thing. Right there.” He pats her collarbone with a tiny paw. “That's envy, Lia. Plain ordinary envy.” ᴾᵘ

Emilia stares at him.

“I'm not envious,” she says. ᴱᵐ

“You're envious of seven witches and a shadow and a man's head,” says Puck, “because Satella's laughing in there and she's never laughed like that out here with you.” ᴾᵘ

Emilia opens her mouth.

She closes it.

He's right.

She sits very still on the terrace with her cup and lets it be true. She, Emilia, who was feared and hated her whole life because she looked like the Witch of Envy. Who was called a half-elf witch and spat at in the streets and had stones thrown at her because of whose face she wore.

She's envious.

Of the people who make Satella laugh.

* * *

Satella comes out half an hour later.

She sits down across the tea table from Emilia, the way she always does, carefully, with her hands in her lap. Her face still has the end of the laugh in it. It fades as she looks at Emilia.

“I'm sorry I'm late,” she says. “Pride invented a new game. It went on.” ˢᵃ

“That's all right,” says Emilia. ᴱᵐ

Satella pours the tea.

Emilia watches her do it. Then she puts her cup down.

“Can I tell you something?” she says. ᴱᵐ

Satella looks up.

“It's not a question,” says Emilia. “It isn't one of my questions. You don't have to say *not yet* to it. It's just something I want to tell you.” ᴱᵐ

* * *

She tells her.

Plainly. The way Yuka does now, the way everyone in this house seems to be learning to. She tells her about sitting at the empty table, and hearing the laugh from across the yard, and the splinter under the ribs.

“Puck says it's envy,” she says. “I think he's right. I'm envious. Of them. Of whoever made you laugh like that. Because you've never laughed like that with me.” ᴱᵐ

She looks down at her cup.

“Isn't that silly?” she says. “Me. Envious. Of all things. I was hated my whole life for looking like you. For looking like *envy*. And here I am.” ᴱᵐ

She makes herself look up.

“I'm not angry,” she says. “I'm not asking you to do anything. I just—I've been learning in this house that you should say things. Out loud. So people don't have to guess. So I'm saying it.” ᴱᵐ

* * *

Satella goes very still.

She sits across the small table with her hands in her lap and her face doing something Emilia has never seen it do. Not the careful stillness. Not the quiet. Something wide open, as if a window has been thrown up in a room that's been shut for a very long time.

She doesn't say anything for a long moment.

Emilia waits for *not yet*.

It doesn't come.

“Thank you,” says Satella. ˢᵃ

Emilia blinks.

“For telling me,” says Satella. Her voice is very quiet. Very unsteady. “Thank you for telling me.” ˢᵃ

She looks down at her own cup.

“Envy was the first thing I was,” she says. “Before anything. It was what they made me into. It was all I was, for four hundred years. I envied everyone. Everything. Every person who was loved. Every person who was *seen*. It was the only thing I knew how to feel.” ˢᵃ

She looks up.

“And nobody ever envied *me*,” she says. “Not once. They feared me. They hated me. They ran. Nobody ever looked at me and wanted something I had.” ˢᵃ

Her voice cracks.

“You just did,” she says. “You're envious of the people who make me laugh. Which means you want to be one of them. Which means—” ˢᵃ

She stops. She can't finish.

Emilia reaches across the table and takes her hand.

* * *

They sit like that for a long time.

Puck, on Emilia's shoulder, has gone very quiet. His tail is curled tight round her neck.

“Would you like to see it?” says Satella, eventually. ˢᵃ

Emilia looks at her.

“The room,” says Satella. “Inside him. Mine. Next to the others. With the window that looks at nothing. I could ask. He'd say yes. He always says yes.” ˢᵃ

Her hand tightens on Emilia's.

“I'd like to laugh like that with you,” she says. “At that table. With the spoons. I'd like you to be there.” ˢᵃ

Emilia looks at her.

She thinks about Mau on the porch step with his eyes closed. About the laugh across the yard. About a woman with her face, who was only ever envy, sitting across a tea table saying *I'd like you to be there*.

“Yes,” says Emilia. ᴱᵐ

* * *

That evening, Satella goes and finds Mau.

She stands in front of him in the common room, the way she did the first time, with her hands at her sides. Not clenched. Not shaking.

“May I bring someone,” she says. ˢᵃ

She doesn't need Makima to say it for her.

She doesn't need to say *please*.

She just asks.

He says yes. Of course he says yes. And Emilia, standing behind Satella in the doorway with Puck on her shoulder, sees his face when he says it, and understands, for the first time, why everyone in this house calls him king.
