## Chapter 15 — Lesson Two

**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 15

**Continuity state:** B+475 ("12"). Shuna and Mau arranged a turn with the bread after finishing the fruit tray. They moved it around other people's turns and put it down for this morning. They make the house loaf. Yuka supervises from the counter, as before, and is a worse supervisor than ever because she keeps getting off the counter to stand behind him with her arms round his waist. “Bread,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

She puts the apron in his hands. His own. The one she cut for him for lesson one.

“The house loaf,” she says. “Maki likes to do the last fold twice. Rem leaves hers alone after she puts it down. You'll probably have something to tell me about both of them by lunch.” ˢʰᵘ

He opens his mouth.

“Lesson two,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

* * *

They kept moving their turn because one of them wanted to use the oven for something else. This morning Shuna came down with her sewing already put away. Mau got up when he saw the apron. Yuka got up when she saw him.

* * *

Yuka is on the counter before he's tied the apron.

The same counter. The same cushion, brought from the common room. Her legs crossed under her. A bowl of something already in her lap.

“Supervising,” she says. ʸ

Shuna looks at her.

“Supervisors,” says Shuna, “don't touch the dough.” ˢʰᵘ

“I know,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Or the cook.” ˢʰᵘ

“I *know*.” ʸ

Shuna looks at her a moment longer, with the expression of a woman who has been here before. Then she turns to the flour.

* * *

Yuka touches the cook within five minutes.

She doesn't even pretend it's a hazard this time. She slides off the counter while he's measuring flour and comes and stands behind him and puts her arms round his waist. Just that. Her arms round him from behind, her cheek against his back, her hands clasped over the front of the apron.

And stays there.

He stops measuring.

“You're supervising,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm supervising from here,” says Yuka, into his back. “It's a better angle.” ʸ

“You can't see anything from there.” ᴹ

“I can see *you*.” ʸ

Shuna, at the other end of the table, doesn't turn round.

“Within limits,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

Yuka doesn't let go.

* * *

He makes the bread with her arms round him.

It's harder than it sounds. Kneading is a whole-body thing, Shuna explains: you lean into it, you push from the shoulders, you fold and turn and push again. With somebody holding on round your middle, every lean becomes a lean for two.

Yuka doesn't care. She goes where he goes. When he leans forward, she leans forward. When he turns the dough, she turns with him. She hums something, very quietly, against his back. He can feel it through his ribs.

Shuna watches from the end of the table.

She doesn't say *don't touch the cook* again. She watches the two of them, swaying over the dough together like one odd creature with four arms, and her face does something it almost never does where people can see.

“Fold it,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

He folds it.

“Turn it.” ˢʰᵘ

He turns it.

“Again.” ˢʰᵘ

* * *

The dough rises.

They sit at the kitchen table while it does, under a cloth, by the warm stove. Yuka in his lap, because there's only one chair close enough to the stove and she has decided it's theirs. Shuna across from them with a cup of tea.

Nobody says much.

It's very warm. The kitchen smells of yeast and flour and the pear jam on the windowsill. Through the window, Ori is in the yard teaching Pickles, the small dented soldier, to salute. Pickles is very bad at it. Ori is very patient.

“You've gone quiet,” says Shuna, eventually. ˢʰᵘ

Mau looks at her.

“Last time,” says Shuna, “you were thinking about something else the whole time. Your hands were slow. I could see it.” ˢʰᵘ

She drinks her tea.

“Today you weren't,” she says. “You were just making bread.” ˢʰᵘ

He thinks about it.

She's right. He wasn't thinking about anything. Not Ori in the orchard. Not Cross. Not Yoru, with twelve days left. Not Dabura's finger on the stone. He was just making bread. With his wife holding on round his waist, humming.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Shuna nods, as if that's the answer to something.

* * *

The bread comes out right.

First try. He takes it out of the oven and puts it on the board and it's exactly the bread. The house loaf. The one they keep coming back to even when someone means to bring something different. Round. High. The crust cracked across the top in the pattern Shuna's always is.

Shuna looks at it.

She looks at it for a long time. She picks it up. She knocks on the bottom with her knuckle and listens. She puts it down. She looks at him.

“That's my bread,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“That's exactly my bread.” ˢʰᵘ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Shuna narrows her eyes.

“It sounds like mine,” she says. “And it sat on the board the whole time while you let her hold you. How long have you been doing this when I wasn't here?” ˢʰᵘ

He doesn't say anything.

“How,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

* * *

He tells her the truth.

“I've been watching,” he says. ᴹ

Shuna looks at him.

“I've helped Rem get it out before the bottom caught. Did the first fold one morning when you were looking for the other tin. And Maki showed me which corner Ori always asks her to cut.” ᴹ

He puts the loaf on its side. The last part has caught where it took him too long to turn it. “You know what you're looking for while you make it,” he says. “I wanted to know what you could see.” ᴹ

* * *

Shuna doesn't say anything for a long time.

She stands at the end of the table with her hand on the edge of the bread board, looking at him.

Shuna goes to get the knife they use for the crust. She gives it to Mau. He makes two cuts and she picks the piece from between them while it's still warm.

“I think it would like longer next time,” she says. “Before you put it in. Let it rise while you sit down.” ˢʰᵘ

“That's what I tried to tell him,” Yuka says, still holding on. “He said you'd tell.” ʸ

“I am telling,” Shuna says. She tastes her piece. “Sit down next time. Both of you.” ˢʰᵘ

* * *

They eat the bread at lunch.

All of it. The whole house. Shuna puts it in the middle of the table without saying who made it, and everyone eats it, and nobody notices anything, because it's exactly the bread.

Except Ori.

Ori takes a piece, and looks at it, and smells it, and looks at Mau across the table, and narrows her eyes.

“You made this,” she says. ᴼ

Everyone looks up.

“How can you tell?” says Mau. ᴹ

Ori considers it.

“It tastes like Shuna's,” she says. “But it tastes like someone was happy making it.” ᴼ

Shuna puts her own slice down long enough to cut Ori another piece.

Yuka, beside him, puts her face in his shoulder and doesn't say anything for a while.

Ori eats her bread.
