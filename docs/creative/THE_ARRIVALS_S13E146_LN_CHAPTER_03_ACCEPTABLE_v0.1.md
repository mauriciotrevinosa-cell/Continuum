# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 17 — Acceptable

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E146
**Continuity state:** B+477 ("10"), morning through the afternoon, before Himmel's hour. Milim and Mau accept the right-hand door's revised challenge. The dragon is comparable to Veldora in available power. It punishes their separate attacks and takes coordination to defeat. The gate reports the dragon is ineligible for extraction. No dragon shadow is acquired. Jin-Woo makes the comparison with Kamish. Third door only; the Fort door remains safe.

*When: B+477. The second run, with enough of the afternoon left for Himmel's hour.*

There is one room on the map.

Milim turns the board to see if there's anything behind it. There are four nails and a dark patch where the rain has got in.

“It's taking us seriously this time,” she says. ᴹⁱ

“The room's big,” says Mau. ᴹ

She looks again. He can see the moment she notices the scale at the bottom. He notices the moment she starts to smile.

Yuka has come down with him. She has a warm coat under one arm because she means to take Ori and Pillow the long way round the river after they leave. She gives Mau the coat to hold while she pulls the collar of his shirt flat.

“What did they give you?” she asks. ʸ

“A single chamber. I won't know what's in it till it sees us. If it changes its mind about that exit, I'll come back and tell you.” ᴹ

Milim taps the line at the bottom twice. The operator's exit is still available; the board wants them both to acknowledge it and the new limits before they go in. Mau puts his hand on the board. Milim adds hers. When he gives Yuka the coat back she takes him by the collar rather than taking hold of the coat. He gets his hand up in time to keep the coat off the ground while she kisses him.

“Tell me the good bit when you're out,” she says. ʸ

“I'll let you know when it happens.” ᴹ

* * *

A thin sheet of water covers the floor inside. They leave tracks for the first dozen paces, then get into water deep enough that the tracks stop. A sloping rock wall runs all the way up to an opening in the ceiling. Wind comes down out of it hard enough to make the surface of the water tremble.

There's a shape lying against the wall on the far side. Mau sees it first in the water. It takes him a second to realise he's looking at the reflection of a head held well above the level of his own.

The head turns. The ridge across it catches the light of their entrance. Each of the dragon's eyes comes to bear on one of them.

Ciel has time to give him a comparison. *Veldora's range.* Then the dragon inhales and the reading continues to climb. ᶜ

Milim hears Mau say her name. She is already going forward.

She drives in low along the floor, the way she did when he first saw her lean into a charge without taking a step. The bank of air in front of her moves up the wall. The dragon lifts off it. One wing comes down sideways against the moving air, using the whole force of her rush to turn its body. Mau sees the tail under the reflection a breath before it comes out of the water and hits her.

He moves Sky across the middle. The tail changes direction enough to keep Milim from hitting the wall headfirst. Her shoulder goes into it instead. She drops as far as the water. One hand takes her weight and she comes right back up grinning.

“Did it mean to do that?” she calls. ᴹⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴹⁱ

He feels the real pressure move through the water. The dragon is drawing a line from the floor up, a column that will take everything with it except the place where the wings have pressed the air flat.

Milim flies straight into the column. Mau runs on Sky toward the one bit of flat air and tries to take the underside of the nearer wing, before the dragon can turn it. His dagger has just reached the joint when the other wing closes against him. Infinity holds the edge off his body. He's still in the channel where the pressure goes through, and he loses the position he'd found. The dragon rolls slowly over him and breathes into the space where Milim will have to come out.

He gets her shoulder with Authority. He pulls hard enough to take her out of the line. He could have got himself clear and waited to see what happened if the dragon finished the breath. Doing both things takes him longer. By the time he's put his boots on the floor the first two fingers of his left hand have stopped gripping. Noise has the point of the dagger back in inventory before it hits the water.

Milim looks down at his hand. “Was that me?” ᴹⁱ

“That was me,” he says. “I thought you'd come out on the right.” ᴹ

“I was about to.” ᴹⁱ

He laughs once. She laughs much louder. The dragon drops back into four feet of water and throws it up both sides of the chamber.

* * *

They try it the other way round.

Mau goes first. He can see where the scale overlaps at the joint. This time he takes his dagger through the path of the wing and pulls the wing far enough out with Authority to give himself space. Milim looks to come in beside him when the flank presents. He hears her feet come off the floor and for one lovely moment he thinks they got the timing right.

The dragon gives him the joint.

It shifts one claw forward. Its weight comes down through the entire line he's holding across that side of the room. He has to let go or the wall behind him will take it, and Milim is about to be in front of that wall. He drops the hold. The dragon brings its whole wing across the chamber and takes the lift out from under her. Mau catches enough of her fall to keep her shirt from touching the water. She stands up in it with the rest of the shirt wet to the chest.

“This is where it's going to keep putting us,” he says. ᴹ

“Move, then.” ᴹⁱ

“I know where to go. I can't get there while it has the end of my arm.” ᴹ

Milim wipes water out of her eye. “Show me where you were going.” ᴹⁱ

He points. There's a stretch of wing that has to open before the dragon can turn under the ceiling. It's where he aimed the first time. If he comes in on the right and puts his foot on the right bit of Sky he can get a blade past it. Both times he tried, the dragon had seen the direction of his feet before he got there.

“That's the place you went *twice*,” says Milim. ᴹⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“I'll put you there this time.” ᴹⁱ

He looks at her. She holds a hand open beside her the way she did before she picked up the gate's grate and threw it across two hallways.

“Will you be watching where you put me?” he asks. ᴹ

She doesn't dignify it with an answer. The dragon is making a new bend in the air with its wing. She wades forward to get both feet under her. He steps into the flat of her hand.

“Wait till it sees me,” he says. ᴹ

“No. You put Sky behind me so it has to watch where I'm going.” ᴹⁱ

He lays it in as she comes up through the water. She takes off from his curve without once looking down. The dragon turns the wing to meet a force coming straight toward its ribs. She waits long enough for it to commit to the turn and then flings Mau high along the far side of that wing, the part the dragon can't see from under the ridge of its head. Mau lets one boot touch the curve of Sky. The second boot misses because Milim put him across it faster than he meant to go. He doesn't have to correct the landing. He lets the miss bring him round the end of the wing with both hands over the joint.

There it is. There's room for a blade and a couple of fingers after it. He takes the joint open and takes its angle away. The dragon starts the turn that caught him on the first pass. The wing loses the part that would normally take its weight.

“Now!” he calls. ᴹ

Milim hits it under the shoulder hard enough to send the sound through the water before the body moves. It lands. It draws air for another breath. Mau puts Sky up in front of the opening, across the line of the breath, and holds it long enough for Milim to drive the head sideways and take the rest of the strike herself.

He hears her shout from the other side of the wing. He can't see what she hit. He knows exactly when it landed. The wind out of the hole in the ceiling drops into the chamber like water out of a bucket.

The dragon's head comes down. Both of them wait. The rest of the water goes back toward the far wall. Ciel tells him the reading is falling. It keeps falling until the only part of the room still moving is the place where Milim shakes her hair in her eyes and sets off through the water toward him.

“Still got my hand?” he asks. He has been holding the dagger by two fingers for most of the last pass. ᴹ

“Which one?” ᴹⁱ

He looks. Both hands are there. One of them is not doing much apart from holding on. RCT is getting feeling back through his knuckles. He sets the dagger on the closed side of inventory so he can take the hand out of the light.

“Both,” he says. ᴹ

“Then tell her the good bit was when I threw you.” ᴹⁱ

He laughs so hard he has to sit down on the nearest piece of rock. Milim sits with him for half a breath, long enough to lean into his shoulder and knock him nearly off it. She gets back up to check on the dragon.

* * *

The room clears. Ciel has time to look at the transfer sheet while soldiers are collecting scales loose enough to carry. One has both arms up under a plate nearly as wide as the door. Milim goes to hold up that end for it. It takes her help, then follows the two going ahead to the exit.

Mau goes to the dragon.

He stands where its eye was when it first looked at him. The pupil has gone flat under the line of scales. There is an outline in the shadow as long as the wall behind it.

“Arise,” he says. ᴹ

He feels for the place where the bond would take weight. There is nothing to put his hand round.

The gate opens a notice in front of him.

> **[Dragon extraction unavailable. No persistent shadow pattern.]**

He says it once more, in case the room has only registered him standing there.

The notice stays up.

Milim is out of the gate before him. He comes through with the soldiers behind him and an ordinary crate of spoils held against his good hip. His own shirt is ruined one way or another, and he can still feel for the joint as though he is up there looking for it. Milim has caught Shuna at the upper end of the path and is explaining what happened while trying to show her the entire height of the ceiling with both arms.

“How was it?” asks Jin-Woo. ᴶʷ

“Acceptable,” says Milim. ᴹⁱ

Mau puts the box down. “It won't let me extract the dragon.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo waits. His eyes go straight to the open board.

“Did you try again after it said no?” ᴶʷ

Mau looks at him.

“Now you know how I feel about Kamish,” says Jin-Woo. ᴶʷ

Then he picks the box up by the other side, where Mau's hand can let go.

* * *

Yuka finds them on their way to the kitchen. She has sand on the hem of her coat and a paper bag from the Fort in one hand. She sees which hand Mau is using to walk with the box and comes round to that side. He has just got it on the step. She puts the bag down and helps him slide it the rest of the way rather than making him lift it.

“Have you had a drink?” she asks. ʸ

He remembers the bottle from yesterday. It's still where he left it in inventory. His finger misses the top when he takes it out. Yuka twists it off and passes it back. He drinks half and gives her what's left.

“Milim threw me,” he says. “That was the good bit.” ᴹ

“Thank you,” Milim says, loudly, from the kitchen. ᴹⁱ

Yuka gives him the last sip back. Her hands are cold from her walk. He can feel that even through the part of his fingers that isn't quite working yet.

“Do you have a minute before you go in?” she asks. ʸ

He puts the bottle down. She sits on the step and draws him down against her, one arm behind him so he can lean his full weight without getting the edge of the wood against his back. He rests his head against her and lets his eye close. He can hear Milim moving a chair out of the way and Jin-Woo telling her to sit in it before she starts talking about the dragon again.

“Are you going to make me wait for the rest?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“I need a little longer,” he says. ᴹ

She shifts her elbow until he can put more of his weight against her. “That's fine.” ʸ

He stays. When he starts telling her where the dragon was lying, he's still got his head down and her hand is still warm on the back of his neck. She can hear him perfectly well from there.
