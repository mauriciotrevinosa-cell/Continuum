## Chapter 19 — Ten Billion Percent

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1

*When: B+478. "9." Under it, in Senku's handwriting, which is tiny and slanted and fills the rest of the page: a diagram of a rocket, with notes.*

Senku has been annoyed for three weeks.

Mau knows. Everyone knows. Senku is annoyed the way other people are hungry: visibly, constantly, with his whole body. He's been annoyed since the morning after the Fort roof, when Umaru came down to breakfast and announced to the entire kitchen that she and Rem had found a satellite, and Ori asked what a satellite was, and Yuka explained, and Ori said *are there any here*, and Yuka said *no*.

Senku was at the table. He put his spoon down.

He hasn't said anything about it since. He's spent a great deal of time in the workshop, and in his corner with his notebooks, and on the roof of the Fort at night with a ruler and a lamp, looking up.

Mau recognises the look. He saw it on Senku's face the day of the points, when Senku drew Gabriel's horn on the back of Umaru's chart and couldn't answer how many spells it would take to empty a cup with no bottom. He left the room. *Ten billion per cent annoying.*

This is that, but more.

* * *

At supper, Senku puts a sheet of paper on the table.

He doesn't say anything. He walks round the long table to where Umaru is sitting, beside Rem, holding Rem's hand under the edge of the table the way they always do now, and he puts the paper down in front of them, between their plates, and stands back with his arms folded.

Umaru looks at it.

Rem looks at it.

The table goes quiet.

* * *

It's a plan.

Mau can see it from where he's sitting. Senku's handwriting, tiny and slanted and everywhere, filling the whole sheet. Diagrams. Numbers. Arrows. A timeline down one side.

At the top, a tube. With lenses. *Telescope (Year 1).*

Below it, a thinner tube, with fins. Going up, and an arrow curving back down. *Rocket, sub-orbital (Years 3–6). Goes up. Comes down. On purpose.*

Below that, a taller one. With stages. Going up. And an arrow that curves over and round and doesn't come down. *Rocket, orbital (Years 8–15).*

And at the very bottom of the page, small, round, with little panels sticking out of it like wings:

*Satellite (Year 15–20). Ten billion per cent.*

* * *

Umaru stares at it.

She doesn't say anything. She just stares. Her fork is halfway to her mouth and stays there.

“There aren't any satellites here,” says Senku. ˢᵉ

He says it the way he says everything: flat, fast, slightly irritated.

“That's what everybody keeps saying,” he says. “*There aren't any satellites here.* Nobody's ever built one. Nobody's ever sent one up. Fine. That's true. It's a correct statement of current conditions.” ˢᵉ

He taps the bottom of the page. The small round thing with the panels.

“Yet,” he says. ˢᵉ

* * *

Umaru puts her fork down.

She puts it down very carefully, beside her plate, and looks at the paper, and then up at Senku, and then back at the paper.

“You're going to build one,” she says. Very small. ᵁ

“I'm going to build one,” says Senku. “Obviously. It's annoying that there isn't one. I fix annoying things.” ˢᵉ

“That's twenty years,” says Umaru. ᵁ

“Fifteen if the materials cooperate,” says Senku. “Twenty if they don't. Maybe less. I haven't factored in his inventory.” He jerks his thumb at Mau without looking. “I'm not factoring in his inventory. It's cheating.” ˢᵉ

Umaru looks back down at the paper.

At the small round thing at the bottom. With the panels like wings.

And then her face crumples, all at once, the way it did on the roof, and she bursts into tears.

* * *

Rem doesn't let go of her hand.

She doesn't do anything else. She doesn't say anything. She just holds on, under the table, and with her other hand pulls the paper a little closer to Umaru, so she can see it better through the tears.

“It's a *satellite*,” says Umaru, through the crying. “A *real* one. Not a star. Not a pretend one. A *real* one.” ᵁ

“Obviously a real one,” says Senku. “I don't build pretend ones.” ˢᵉ

“And we'll *see* it,” says Umaru. “From the roof. One day. Moving. Just a bit. Left.” ᵁ

“Or right,” says Senku. “Depends on the orbit. I haven't decided the orbit.” ˢᵉ

Umaru laughs. Through the tears. She wipes her face with her sleeve, which doesn't help, and looks at Rem.

Rem is smiling. Her eyes are bright.

“You were the first,” Rem says to her, very quietly. “To see one. On the roof. I told you. You found one.” ᴿᵉ

“That was a *star*,” says Umaru. ᵁ

“It was a satellite,” says Rem. “It just hadn't been built yet.” ᴿᵉ

* * *

Ori is on her feet.

She's standing on her chair. She has her notebook in one hand and her pencil in the other and she is looking at Senku with an expression of absolute, burning seriousness.

“I want to be on the team,” she says. ᴼ

Senku looks at her.

“The satellite team,” says Ori. “The rocket team. All the teams. I want to be on them.” ᴼ

Senku considers this.

“You're very small,” he says. ˢᵉ

“I'll be bigger in fifteen years,” says Ori. ᴼ

Senku opens his mouth. Closes it. Looks at her.

“That's correct,” he says, after a moment, sounding faintly surprised. “That's a correct statement.” ˢᵉ

He thinks about it.

“The Fort,” he says, “can have a seat on the launch committee.” ˢᵉ

Ori goes very still.

“A *seat*,” she says. ᴼ

“One seat,” says Senku. “On the committee. You'd have to attend meetings. You'd have to learn things. Physics. Chemistry. Very boring things. For years.” ˢᵉ

“I'll learn them,” says Ori. ᴼ

“You'll hate it.” ˢᵉ

“I'll learn them *anyway*,” says Ori. ᴼ

Senku looks at her for a long moment.

Then something happens at the corner of his mouth.

“Fine,” he says. “The Fort's in. Ten billion per cent.” ˢᵉ

Ori sits down very fast and writes something in her notebook and underlines it four times.

* * *

“The inventory,” says Mau. ᴹ

Senku turns round.

“Whatever the Shop has,” says Mau. “Materials. Tools. Whatever you need. I can get it.” ᴹ

Senku narrows his eyes.

“That's cheating,” he says. ˢᵉ

“Is it?” ᴹ

“It's ten billion per cent cheating,” says Senku. “The whole point is to do it from first principles. From rocks. From what's here. You don't build civilisation by *buying* it.” ˢᵉ

Mau waits.

Senku looks at the paper. At Umaru, still crying a little, holding Rem's hand. At Ori, writing furiously in her notebook. At the small round thing at the bottom of the page, with the panels like wings.

At *Year 15–20*.

“...Fine,” he says. “Cheating accepted. But only for the boring parts. I'm building the interesting parts myself.” ˢᵉ

“Which parts are interesting?” ᴹ

“All of them,” says Senku. “I'll let you know.” ˢᵉ

* * *

After supper, Mau goes out onto the porch.

The sky is clear. The stars are out. All of them, thick and bright over the yard and the orchard and the dark hills beyond.

On the roof of the Fort, two figures are lying on blankets, side by side. Holding hands. Looking up.

And beside them, cross-legged, with a lamp and a ruler and a notebook, a third figure. Writing. Muttering. Pointing at a part of the sky and saying something about angles.

And on the ladder, halfway up, a fourth. Small. With an explorer's hat. Climbing very carefully so as not to drop her notebook.

Mau watches them for a long time.

*Fifteen years*, says Ciel, inside. *Maybe twenty.* ᶜ

*I know*, he says. ᴹ

*Will we still be here?* says Ciel. ᶜ

He thinks about it. About Yoru, with nine days left. About whatever comes after that, and after that. About everything.

*I want to be*, he says. ᴹ

A pause.

*That's the real one*, says Ciel. ᶜ

On the roof of the Fort, Umaru points at a star.

“That one,” she says. Clear across the yard. “That's where it'll go.” ᵁ
