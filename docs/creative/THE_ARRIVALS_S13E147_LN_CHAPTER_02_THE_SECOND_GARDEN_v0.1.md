# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 20 — The Second Garden

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S13E147
**Continuity state:** B+479–480 ("8"–"7"). DIO's second visit to Elmesia, the first since they said it. DIO takes the plant from the Guest Inn windowsill for its holiday in the sheltered corner of the palace garden. Mau brings them through the Exchange; Yuka comes for the company and the cakes. This time nobody needs a mediator. Mau and Yuka sit in the little room with the very good view, with View, who keeps the room between rota weeks and has to share his window. They watch DIO and Elmesia plant it together in the lit garden below, and argue about how deep, and laugh. DIO comes up the stair at the end with dirt on her hands and a face Mau can't see the colour of but can guess. She tells him she isn't counting the debts anymore. Then she says: still two.

*When: B+479, evening, into the small hours of B+480. "8", then "7". DIO wrote both herself, in a hand that has got much neater since she started writing letters.*

DIO goes to the Guest Inn for the plant at dusk.

Mau walks with her to the Guest Inn. She wants to carry the plant down herself. He waits in the hall while she goes up the stairs to her room, the physical one, the one with the window that actually looks out onto something, and comes back down with the pot in both hands.

The plant is bigger than it was. Four new leaves since the first visit. She's drawn every one of them in her watering notebook.

“It's going on holiday,” she tells him. ᴰ

“I know.” ᴹ

“To the corner. By the wall. Where the light's good.” ᴰ

“I know.” ᴹ

“She's going to plant it,” says DIO. “With me. In the ground. Properly. It's never been in the ground.” ᴰ

She looks down at the plant.

“I'm a little bit afraid it won't like it,” she says. ᴰ

“It'll like it,” says Mau. ᴹ

DIO looks at him.

“You don't know that,” she says. ᴰ

“No,” he agrees. “But it's going to be in a garden with someone who loves you. Things tend to do all right there.” ᴹ

DIO goes a colour he can't see.

“Shut up,” she says, and walks out of the Guest Inn very fast with the plant.

* * *

Yuka comes because there'll be cakes.

She says so. Openly. She's in the yard when they come out, in the hoodie, with a basket over her arm that she has packed herself.

“I'm not mediating,” she announces. “I'm not helping. I'm not doing anything. I'm sitting in the room with the window and eating whatever Elmesia sends up, and you two can do whatever you like.” ʸ

“You didn't help last time,” Mau points out. ᴹ

“I ate,” says Yuka, with dignity. “That's a kind of help.” ʸ

DIO looks at her.

“You laughed,” she says. “The whole time.” ᴰ

“I *did* laugh the whole time,” Yuka agrees, unrepentant. “It was the best night of my life. You were both so *red*.” ʸ

DIO goes red again.

* * *

They arrive in the little room.

View is in the chair by the window. He keeps the room between the rota weeks, and tonight is one of the nights between. He looks round when they come through, which he almost never does, and then back at the window, as if checking it's still there.

The room is small. There's one chair. View is in it.

Mau looks at View.

View looks at the city.

“We'll just stand,” says Mau. ᴹ

View doesn't move.

Then, slowly, with what Mau can only describe as reluctance, he shifts the chair. Not out of the way. Just to one side. Six inches. So there's room at the window for two more.

“Thank you,” says Mau. ᴹ

View doesn't answer. Soldiers don't answer. But Mau feels something go through him, down the line. Something very like a man who has given up six inches of the best view in the world and wants it noted.

* * *

DIO goes down the narrow stair with the plant.

Elmesia is waiting at the bottom. With her lantern. With her hair down. With a small spade in her other hand, the kind gardeners use, which she is holding as if she isn't entirely sure which end goes in the ground.

They look at each other.

And this time, somebody says something straight away.

Mau can't hear what. He's at the top of the stair, in the doorway of the little room, and they're at the bottom. But he sees DIO say it. Something short. And Elmesia laughs. Not nervously. Properly. And says something back.

And they turn, together, and go out into the lit garden.

* * *

Mau and Yuka watch from the window.

There's room, just, beside View. Yuka sits on the windowsill with the basket in her lap. Mau stands behind her with his arms round her shoulders and his chin on top of her head.

Yuka leans back an inch and finds him with the top of her head.

“Can you see over me?” she asks. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Then come down here.” ʸ

She moves the basket to the other end of the sill. He fits onto the ledge beside her, one foot against the floor, and she puts both of her legs across his. When the edge of the stone starts to dig into him, she folds the cloth out of the basket and pushes it under his hip. He catches her cheek with a quick kiss as she straightens. She feeds him a corner of her cake without looking down.

Below them, the garden. All the paths lit. The fountains. The lanterns in the trees. And in the sheltered corner by the old wall, where the light's good, two small figures kneeling in the earth.

They're arguing.

Mau can tell from up here. From the way DIO is pointing at the ground and Elmesia is shaking her head, and then Elmesia is pointing at a different bit of the ground and DIO is shaking hers.

“How deep,” says Yuka, eating a cake. ʸ

“What?” ᴹ

“They're arguing about how deep,” says Yuka. “Look at her hands. She's measuring. She thinks it should be deeper.” ʸ

He looks. DIO is holding her hands apart, very precisely, the width of something.

Elmesia brings them closer together.

DIO moves them apart again.

Elmesia, kneeling in the dirt in what is clearly a very expensive dress, puts down her spade and sits back on her heels and laughs so hard Mau can see her shoulders shake from three floors up.

“She's going to win,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Who?” ᴹ

“DIO. Look. Elmesia's letting her.” ʸ

She is. Elmesia picks up the spade again and digs deeper. The width of DIO's hands.

* * *

They plant it.

Together. DIO holding the plant. Elmesia holding the pot. Both of them easing it out, carefully, the way you'd take something sleeping out of its bed. Into the hole. DIO's hands in the earth, pressing it down round the roots. Elmesia's hands beside hers.

Then they sit back on their heels in the lantern light and look at it.

A small plant. In a big garden. In the ground for the first time.

Neither of them says anything for a long time.

Then DIO reaches over, without looking, and takes Elmesia's hand. With dirt on both of theirs.

Yuka, on the windowsill, makes a small sound.

Mau doesn't say anything. He holds on a little tighter.

Beside them, View looks down at the garden. At the two small figures by the wall, hand in hand. And Mau feels something go through him, down the line, that he doesn't have a word for and View doesn't either.

View moves his chair back another inch.

* * *

DIO comes up the narrow stair at midnight.

Alone. Elmesia is at the bottom, holding her lantern, waiting to say goodnight. DIO has come up first, to fetch them.

She stops in the doorway of the little room.

Her hands are covered in dirt. There's dirt on her dress, at the knees, and a smear of it on her cheek. Her hair is coming down. Her face is a colour Mau can't see but can absolutely guess from the way Yuka, on the windowsill, puts a hand over her mouth.

She looks at Mau.

“It's in the ground,” she says. ᴰ

“I saw,” says Mau. ᴹ

“It liked it,” says DIO. “I think it liked it. It's too soon to tell. But I think it did.” ᴰ

“It'll like it,” says Mau. ᴹ

DIO looks at him.

“You keep saying that,” she says. ᴰ

“I keep being right.” ᴹ

* * *

DIO is quiet for a moment.

“I've stopped counting,” she says. ᴰ

He looks at her.

“The debts,” says DIO. “What you owe me. The door. The letter. I've stopped counting them.” ᴰ

She looks down at her dirty hands.

“I was counting,” she says, “because I didn't know if you'd stay. If I kept count, then you'd always owe me something, and if you always owed me something, you'd always have to come back. That's how I thought it worked. That's how everything worked, before.” ᴰ

She looks up.

“I don't need that anymore,” she says. ᴰ

Mau doesn't say anything. He can't.

DIO looks at him a moment longer. Then her chin comes up. Magnificent. Entirely herself.

“Still two,” she says. ᴰ

Yuka bursts out laughing.

“You *just said*—” ʸ

“I said I'd stopped *counting*,” says DIO, with tremendous dignity. “I didn't say he'd stopped *owing*. It's still two. It's always going to be two. I just don't need to count them anymore.” ᴰ

She turns round and goes back down the stair to say goodnight to Elmesia.

Mau stands in the little room with Yuka laughing into his chest and View at the window and the garden lit up below, and the small plant in the corner by the wall, in the ground for the first time, and doesn't say anything at all.
