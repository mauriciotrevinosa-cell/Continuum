He asks her at breakfast.

He doesn't plan how. That's the thing. He planned everything else: the where and the when and the basket and the blanket and the way there. He spent two nights lying awake with her face in his shoulder working it out. But he didn't plan how to ask, because he's learned that much this month. You don't plan the plain thing. You just say it.

So at breakfast, while she's stealing his pickles, he says:

“Will you come out with me tonight?” ᴹ

“Out where?” ʸ

“It's a surprise.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“You planned something,” she says. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“By yourself.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“You didn't ask anyone how.” ʸ

“No,” he says. “Not even Ciel.” ᴹ

*He didn't*, says Ciel, inside, sounding faintly put out. *I've been extremely curious for two days.* ᶜ

Yuka eats the pickle. She makes the face.

“Yes,” she says. ʸ

Shuna packs the basket without asking questions.

He goes to her after breakfast and says *something for two, for tonight, to take somewhere*, and Shuna looks at him for a long moment and then nods and turns to the cupboard, and by the afternoon there's a basket on the kitchen table with a cloth over it, and a blanket folded on top. The thick one. Not the thin one.

He lifts the cloth.

Bread. The house loaf, the one he made. Cold fish, the good way, skin on. The honey and pear cake. Plum paste. A bottle of something. And in a small jar on its own, wrapped in a napkin folded into what is unmistakably a dragon: pickles.

He looks at Shuna.

Shuna is at the sink with her back to him.

“Ori folded the napkin,” she says, without turning round. ˢʰᵘ

“Ori can hear you,” says Ori. ᴼ

“That's for when you get back. You can put things in it that you didn't buy from the Shop.” ᴼ

“Something I can tell Lantern the story of. It doesn't have to be big. I want something to trade for what Mama finds on our walk.” She puts one corner back where it won't go in the pickles. “And leave some cake. I want cake with her after.” ᴼ

“I'll go to my room when you leave,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka is already at the door with her cloak over one shoulder. He tells her he'd like Makima to see the place and sit with them for a while, then he waits.

“Can she hear me too if I ask her something?” says Yuka. ʸ

“If you both open the line.” ᴹ

Yuka looks across at Makima. “Come for supper. I want him to show me what he planned and then I'd like a while with him.” ʸ

“I'll come for supper,” says Makima. She lets go of the cloth. “Will you tell me when you reach the good part?” ᴹᵏ

Makima is inside by the time the two of them get their shoes on. He finds her putting her cup down at the table by the time he and Yuka step into the yard.

He takes her by Exchange.

They stand in the yard at sunset, with the basket, and he holds out his hand, and she takes it, and the world folds round them like a coat.

Kamish is already there. He flew out an hour ago and settled himself on the far rim of the basin above the long valley, and folded his wings, and waited. His shadow is the anchor.

They come through into Kamish's shadow on the rim.

Yuka looks round.

And stops.

The valley goes down in front of them, long and falling, full of colour in the last of the sun for her and grey for him. The stream at the bottom catching the light. The old wild pear trees at the very bottom, heavy with small hard fruit.

Everyone's pears. Terrible.

She looks at him.

“Here,” she says. ʸ

“Here,” says Mau. ᴹ

* * *

They walk down.

Slowly, in the last of the light, with the basket and the blanket. Kamish stays on the rim. He puts his head down on his forelegs and watches them go, and doesn't follow. Mau told him he didn't need to. Kamish seemed to understand.

At the bottom, under the oldest pear tree, Mau spreads the blanket.

Yuka sits down on it. She looks up at the branches, heavy and low and tangled over her head. She reaches up and picks one of the small hard pears and looks at it.

“They're terrible,” she says. ʸ

“I know,” says Mau. ᴹ

She bites it. She makes the face.

“*So* terrible,” she says, delighted, and eats the whole thing. ʸ

*She ate it all?* asks Makima. ᴹᵏ

Mau looks at the stem in Yuka's hand. He gives Makima the view of that much. *Every bit except this.* ᴹ

*I can hear you,* says Yuka. She leans back far enough to see him around the branch. *I saved you both a good one.* ʸ

He takes it. The skin feels like it ought to warn him. The first bite makes the hinge of his jaw hurt. He gives Makima a second to decide if she wants to know.

*I'd like to try it,* she says. ᴹᵏ

He lets her taste the second bite. It takes her long enough to answer that he goes to take another.

*You can stop,* she says. *I felt it the first time.* ᴹᵏ

*Apparently it gets better near the middle,* he says. ᴹ

*Who told you that?* ᴹᵏ

Mau gets half the way through the rest of the pear because he can make Yuka laugh harder if he keeps telling her it must be about to improve.

* * *

They eat Shuna's basket in the dusk.

The bread. The fish. The cake. The bottle, which turns out to be something sweet and fizzy that Yuka likes and Mau doesn't and which she therefore drinks most of. The pickles, out of the jar with the dragon napkin, which Mau sets aside on her side of the blanket without thinking and she steals without thinking.

The first star comes out while they're wrapping the last of the bread. Yuka points it out so Makima can see it with them. He has to turn his head twice before he's got the part of the sky she means.

*Is this a good part?* Makima asks. ᴹᵏ

Yuka sits where she is with her chin up until she can find it again. *Yes. I'm glad you got to see it.* ʸ

Mau leaves the view there a little longer. *Can we have a little while to ourselves now?* he asks. ᴹ

*Of course.* She picks up her cup. *You owe Ori a thing for the napkin. Show me what you find on the way home.* ᴹᵏ

Yuka lies back on the blanket and looks up.

She lies on the blanket under the terrible pear trees and looks at him, and he feels it down the line: everything going through her, very fast, very warm, too much to name.

Then she rolls over and puts her face in his shoulder.

“I'm glad you told me some of it,” she says, muffled. ʸ
