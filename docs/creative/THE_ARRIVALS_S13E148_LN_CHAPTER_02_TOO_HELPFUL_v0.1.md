# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 22 — Too Helpful

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S13E148
**Continuity state:** B+482 ("5"). Ciel tells Mau what she's seen, as promised: not a report, a telling. Cross has kept every term. He hasn't spoken Mau's name. He hasn't gone near Ori. He has been helpful: to Shuna, to Mai, to Senku, to the witches. Too helpful, Maru said. Today Ciel noticed something else: Cross has been asking people small questions. Harmless ones. Where Mau goes in the mornings. Which nights he sleeps in the clinic. How far Makima can walk. When the house goes quiet. None of it is forbidden. All of it is a map. Mau doesn't confront him; there's nothing to confront. He tells Yuka, plainly. He tells Maru. He tells Makima, who listens with an expression Mau has seen once before, on her own face, in another life, at the outer post. "He's learning," Makima says. "Slowly. The way I did." Mau and the house have no proof yet and keep watching. The reader sees Cross writing to Yuuki. Yuuki's inquiries about Mau began after Sukuna. He sees an opening after Cross reconnects with Yuka and notices Mau withdrawing, then suggests using Mau's habit of getting out of the way to push him farther out. What Cross uses from his history with Yuka is his choice.

*When: B+482. "5." Nobody wrote anything under it. Ciel asked Mau not to.*

Ciel tells him in the morning.

She said she would. *Just an eye. Tell me if anything changes. Not a report. Tell me.* Five weeks ago, in Mai's workshop, after Cross walked away across the yard and didn't look back.

She's been watching since. Quietly. The way she watches everything. And this morning, while he's sitting at the table inside before breakfast, the way he does every morning now, she puts her hands flat on the wood and says:

*Something's changed.* ᶜ

* * *

*Tell me*, he says. ᴹ

She does.

*He's kept every term*, says Ciel. *Every one. He hasn't said your name. He hasn't greeted you. He hasn't come near Ori, or the Fort. When you've been in a group, he's spoken to the group and not to you. He's done exactly what you asked.* ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*And he's been helpful*, says Ciel. *To everyone. Shuna's water. Mai's floor. Senku's boiler. He fixed the training post. He's been carrying things for the witches; Pride likes him. He smiles at everyone. He says thank you for everything.* ᶜ

*Maru said*, says Mau. *Too helpful.* ᴹ

*Yes.* ᶜ

A pause.

*Yesterday*, says Ciel, *I noticed something else.* ᶜ

* * *

*He's been asking questions*, she says. ᶜ

Mau goes still.

*Small ones*, says Ciel. *Harmless ones. Nothing anyone would think twice about. He asks Shuna what time you come down in the mornings, so he knows when to bring the water in. He asks Senku which nights you sleep in the clinic, because he wants to fix the clinic shutter and doesn't want to wake you. He asked Pride, during tea, how far Makima can walk from you before she has to go inside. He said he was curious. Pride told him. She thought it was a nice question.* ᶜ

Mau doesn't move.

*He asked Umaru when the house goes quiet at night*, says Ciel. *For the stars, he said. He wants to look at them too. He asked Fern how long it takes the watch to change.* ᶜ

She's quiet for a moment.

*None of it is forbidden*, she says. *None of it breaks a term. Every question has a reason. Every reason is a good one. If you asked anyone in this house, they'd say he's been very kind.* ᶜ

*But*, says Mau. ᴹ

*But if you put all the answers on one piece of paper*, says Ciel, *it's a map.* ᶜ

* * *

He sits at the table inside for a long time.

He thinks about Mai, in the workshop. *I used to do that. I'd say yes, sir and thank you, sir and go away very quietly. And everyone thought I'd given up. I hadn't. I was waiting for them to stop watching.*

He thinks about Maru, on the training ground. *Or maybe he's waiting. For everyone to stop watching.*

He thinks about the outer post. A long time ago. Makima coming with her requests and her perfect calm, agreeing to every term, thanking him for every limit, asking small questions. Harmless ones. Every one of them with a good reason.

Every *yes* a step she didn't have to take by force.

*It might be nothing*, says Ciel. *I want to say that. He might really be sorry. He might really want to fix the shutter. People do ask questions.* ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*I'm not saying it's something*, says Ciel. *I'm saying it changed. You asked me to tell you if it changed.* ᶜ

*Thank you*, he says. And means it. ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't confront him.

There's nothing to confront. Cross hasn't broken a term. He hasn't said a word to Mau. He's carried water and fixed shutters and asked about the stars. If Mau walked up to him in the yard and said *what are you doing*, Cross would look at him with that carefully arranged face and say *I don't know what you mean*, and he'd be right, and everyone who saw it would think Mau was being unfair.

And he'd know he'd been seen.

So Mau doesn't do anything.

That's the old thing too, a little. Not saying. He knows that. He catches himself, halfway to putting it behind a door, and stops.

Not the old thing. Not this time.

He doesn't confront Cross. But he tells people.

* * *

He tells Yuka first.

After breakfast, on the porch step, plainly. All of it. What Ciel saw. The questions. The map.

Yuka listens. Her face goes very still.

“He's asking how far Makima can walk,” she says, at the end. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And when the house goes quiet.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a long moment.

“Thank you for telling me,” she says. ʸ

He looks at her.

“You told me,” she says. “Not after. Not when it's too late. Now. When there's nothing to do yet.” ʸ

She takes his hand. “When he asked if you'd asked me to come to the quarry, I said yes. By the time I took the boot off I was waiting for you to ask again. Nothing you did changed while I was sitting there with him.” ʸ

Mau looks at her. She got there without him having to ask. He wants to be able to put the last answer she gave him in the clinic next to this one and hear them both the same way. He leaves his hand where it is.

“Tell me if he asks you something,” he says. ᴹ

“I will.” She looks across the yard toward the workshop. “If it sounds kind, too. That's how it sounded the first time.” ʸ

* * *

He tells Maru.

At the river, where Maru and Tsurugi are up to their ankles in water trying to get a peg into the bank. The shallow run they've been using for wagons has lost another piece in the last rain. The first new timbers arrived that morning. Nobody trusts them enough yet to carry loaded carts.

Yuka goes down the bank to see what they're doing. She's spent enough time following Tsurugi down worse riverbeds that she takes the two steps to the bottom without looking where he put his feet. The bank goes out a little under the second step. Mau starts to put a hand out and she puts hers back for him, eyes still on the peg. He goes down after her.

They spend some of the afternoon feeding the guide rope across to Tsurugi and helping the man on the far side get the boards in the order he wants. He brought one of the wagons through the first time the water rose and says there must be a way to leave enough room for people on foot. Maru goes to see how wide the cart is at the axle. By the time he's back, Yuka has got a stick and is showing the two of them how far somebody would have to step down to let a wheel go past them. They look for a place to build a ledge, and settle on asking Rimuru if it can go on the new drawing.

When the rope is tight enough to leave, Mau asks Maru to stay. Yuka picks up the wet reel and goes up to help Tsurugi fit it under his arm. Mau waits until the two of them have started back together. Then he tells Maru what Ciel saw.

Maru doesn't say anything for a long time. He sits on the bank with his hands loose between his knees, looking at the water.

“I hoped,” he says at last. ᴹᵃʳᵘ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I still hope,” says Maru. “He's my brother. Maybe they're just questions.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

He looks at Mau.

“But I'll watch closer,” he says. “And if he asks me anything, I'll tell you.” ᴹᵃʳᵘ

* * *

He tells Makima last.

On the north-wing step, at dusk, where she always sits. He sits down beside her. Not close. Near. And tells her.

She listens with her hands in her lap. She doesn't interrupt. When he gets to the part about Pride, and *how far Makima can walk*, something goes across her face, very fast, and is gone.

When he finishes, she's quiet for a long time.

Then she says, “He's learning.” ᴹᵏ

Mau looks at her.

“Slowly,” says Makima. “The way I did.” ᴹᵏ

She looks out at the yard.

“I recognise it,” she says. “The questions. The kindness. The terms kept perfectly. Every *yes* a step. I did exactly this. At the outer post. For months. Small questions, every one of them with a good reason. I was very kind. Everyone thought so.” ᴹᵏ

She turns her head and looks at him.

“The difference,” she says, “is that I was very good at it. And he isn't. Not yet. Ciel saw it in three weeks. It took you months to see it in me.” ᴹᵏ

“Is that good?” says Mau. ᴹ

Makima considers.

“It means he's not dangerous yet,” she says. “It means he's still learning how.” ᴹᵏ

She looks back at the yard.

“I'll watch him,” she says. “I know what to look for. I know exactly what to look for. Better than anyone in this house.” ᴹᵏ

She's quiet a moment.

“And if he ever asks me how far I can walk,” she says, “I'll tell him the truth. Exactly as far as I need to.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Nobody moves.

That's the decision. Mau makes it with Ciel that night, at the table inside. Nobody moves. Nobody confronts. Nobody changes anything Cross can see. The house goes on being ordinary at the top of its voice.

But everyone who needs to know, knows.

Yuka. Maru. Makima. Ciel. Noise, who heard all of it and said nothing and pulled his chair, very slightly, closer to Mau's.

Everyone watches.

And across the yard, in the long room at the end of the west wing, Cross sits on the end of his bed in the dark with a piece of paper on his knee. A wagon is going to the northern ford in the morning. There'll be room for one more letter with the tally of rope and canvas.

Yuuki's last note sits open beside him. Cross had told him he'd seen Mau walking again and that Yuka was with him. Yuuki sent back a list of questions, with a few lines at the bottom about Sukuna. He'd been collecting names and accounts since then. He wanted to know who Mau had asked for when he couldn't get off the ground, and who was close enough that Mau believed them when they said he could.

*You can win the fight and still come home to an empty room,* he'd written on the back.

The first thing Cross wanted when he went up to the house was to see Yuka walk out to meet him. He'd been told she was alive and he hadn't quite believed it until he watched her lift Tsurugi's pack like nothing. Later, when she told him what had happened after the diagnosis, he brought up the corridor. She'd been surprised he remembered the door. That night he'd gone back over the whole conversation and couldn't remember when she'd last sounded that happy to see him.

By the time he wrote to Yuuki about her, she'd already started to tell him what was wrong at home. He'd written about the supper she came back for after their walk. How her husband got up before anyone asked him to, and how Yuka went on talking beside him. He'd been hoping she'd do exactly that again. He left that part out.

Yuuki had sent back one question. *If he already gets out of her way, how far do you think he'll go if he believes she wants him to?*

Cross had wanted to answer that one. Afterward it got easier to hear what Yuka was afraid of and ask her a question that would let her go on being afraid. Yuuki had never heard her talk about that corridor. Cross knew exactly which part she couldn't finish, and when to let her stop.

He writes that Makima can still reach Mau inside even if she isn't standing beside him. He starts a second line about the clinic. Then he puts the pencil down. Yuuki had asked if Yuka ever tried to go back to the orchard wall on her own. Cross can leave that place out of the next letter. He knows she'll come back there eventually, if he gives her enough time to miss it.

He folds the paper and puts it under his pillow. By morning he'll have the answer about the clinic in the margin.
