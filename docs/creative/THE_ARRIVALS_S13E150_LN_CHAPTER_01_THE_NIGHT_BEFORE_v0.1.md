*When: B+486. "1". Yuka wrote it at breakfast and drew a small sun next to it. Not a countdown anymore. Just a day.*

The last time, nobody slept.

He remembers it. The night before the war. The house round the kitchen table in the lamplight, plates untouched from supper, nobody saying anything. Shuna not clearing the table. Gojo standing up to say *I'm coming* and Mau saying *no*. The notebook on his pillow with *53* in it and no drawing. Yuka in the yard in his shirt, barefoot on the wet grass, trying to tell him something, and him saying *it's fine* and walking north with another woman's hand in his.

He remembers all of it.

This time is different.

He makes sure it's different.

* * *

He tells them at supper.

Not after. Not when everyone has gone to bed and it's just him on the edge of a mattress at three in the morning. At supper. At the table. In the middle of the food, with the lamps lit and the whole house there.

He puts his chopsticks down.

“Tomorrow,” he says. ᴹ

The table goes quiet. But not the old quiet. Not the frightened quiet. Everyone just stops talking and looks at him, the way you look at someone who's about to tell you something you need to know.

“Tomorrow's the end of the ninety days,” he says. “At the hour. Yoru will come. Or she'll send word. I don't know which.” ᴹ

He looks round at all of them.

“I'm going to tell you what I think will happen,” he says. “And what I'm going to do. And then you're going to tell me if I'm wrong.” ᴹ

* * *

He tells them.

Plainly. All of it.

That he doesn't think Yoru wants to fight tomorrow. That she said *how boring* in the basin and meant it, and *I'll think of something better*, and Yoru is the kind of thing that wants a war to be interesting, and tomorrow wouldn't be.

That he could be wrong.

That if she comes, he'll go to meet her. At the edge of the grounds, where the protection ends. Not in the north. Not alone. Not a day early in the dark. At the hour, in daylight, with the house knowing exactly where he is.

That he's going to ask her for time.

“Not to fight,” he says. “I'm not going to fight unless she makes me. I'm going to ask her for time. Before whatever she's planning. Time for the house. For all of you. To get ready, if there's anything to get ready for.” ᴹ

He stops.

“That's all,” he says. “That's the plan. Tell me what's wrong with it.” ᴹ

* * *

Nobody says anything for a moment.

Then Gojo stands up.

He does it the same way he did the night before the war. Pushing his chair back. Standing at the end of the table with his blindfold up.

“I'm coming,” he says. ᴳ

He says it the same way too. *I'm coming.* Not asking.

Mau looks at him.

He remembers saying *no*. He remembers why: because if Gojo followed him north, the devils would see where he came from, and they'd know about the river and the road and everyone who ever walked out of the gate.

That isn't true tomorrow. Tomorrow is the edge of the yard. Tomorrow is the house.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Gojo blinks.

He actually blinks. He was ready for *no*. Mau can see it. He had the whole argument ready, the one about people who make up rules and going anyway and bringing friends.

“Yes?” he says. ᴳ

“Yes,” says Mau. “To the edge of the yard. Where the protection ends. Stand there. If she does anything, you'll be the first one she sees.” ᴹ

Gojo looks at him for a long moment.

Then he sits down. Slowly. With his face doing something it almost never does.

“Okay,” he says. ᴳ

* * *

Shoko asks next.

She doesn't stand up. She just says, from her end of the table, without looking up from her soup: “I'm coming too.” ˢʰ

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

Shoko looks up.

“I'm not asking,” she says. “I'm telling you. In case she makes you do anything stupid. I want to be close enough to shout at you before it gets bad, not after.” ˢʰ

“I know,” says Mau. “Yes.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him for a long moment, the way she looks at a patient who is unexpectedly following instructions.

Then she goes back to her soup.

* * *

After that, the house decides.

Together. Out loud. At the table. It takes an hour and nobody minds.

Rimuru takes out his notebook and draws the yard, and everyone tells him where they'll be. Not hiding. Not waiting for reports. *Being there*.

Gojo at the edge, by the orchard, where the protection ends.

Shoko a step behind him.

Makima at Mau's right shoulder, where she always is.

Yuka at his left.

Jin-Woo at the south road, with his family behind him and his own shadows at the gate.

Frieren on the porch, with her staff, and Himmel beside her.

Maki and Yuta and Mai at the workshop. Yuta with the ring on, ten minutes ready.

Dabura at the north edge, where he can see the hill road. *If she comes from there*, he says slowly, *she'll see a king picking pears.* Nobody understands what he means except Mau.

Beru with Ori. Always Beru with Ori. And Lantern in her shadow.

Igris watching over Yuka, where Mau put him.

Bellion with the watch on the approaches, the legion's front rank at the edges of the grounds, the way he holds them whenever Mau isn't leading them himself.

Kamish across the bottom of the orchard, keeping the house, the way he promised.

Ori with her notebook, on the porch steps, *for the official record*.

And everybody else. The Fort. The witches. Rem and Umaru. Emilia and Satella. Senku with something that hisses that he says is *probably not necessary but just in case*. Everybody in the yard, at the hour, where Yoru can see them. All of them. Together.

Rimuru looks at the drawing when it's finished. A yard full of small marks. Every one a person.

“That's not a defence plan,” he says. ᴿ

“No,” says Mau. ᴹ

“That's just everyone standing in the yard.” ᴿ

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

Rimuru looks at him.

Then he smiles. The real one, not the ruler's.

“That's a better plan,” he says. ᴿ

* * *

Then everybody goes to bed.

That's the strangest part. Last time nobody slept. This time, when supper's finished and the plan's on paper and Shuna has cleared the table, the house just goes to bed. Not tense. Not waiting. Not lying awake staring at the ceiling.

Tired. From a long ordinary day. Going up the stairs, saying goodnight, closing doors.

Mau stands in the kitchen and listens to it.

*They're not afraid*, says Ciel. ᶜ

*No*, says Mau. ᴹ

*Because you told them*, says Ciel. *Everything. At the table. Before. So nobody has to lie awake guessing.* ᶜ

He thinks about it.

*Yes*, he says. ᴹ

* * *

He and Yuka sit on the porch for an hour first.

The yard dark. The stars out. The orchard a long shadow, and Kamish under his tree, and the lamp still lit in the kitchen window behind them, where Shuna will leave it all night, the way she always does.

Yuka is in the hoodie. She's leaning against him with her head on his shoulder and her hand in his.

“I'm not afraid,” she says. ʸ

He looks down at her.

“I want you to know that,” she says. “Last time I was. I was so afraid I sat down on the grass. I wrote a list because I didn't know what else to do. I was afraid you'd go and not come back, and afraid you'd come back and not want me, and afraid of everything.” ʸ

She turns her head on his shoulder and looks up at him.

“I'm not afraid now,” she says. “Not of tomorrow. Not of her. Not of any of it.” ʸ

“Why?” he says. ᴹ

Yuka thinks about it.

“Because you told me,” she says. “At the table. Before. With everyone there. What you think will happen and what you're going to do. And you said yes to Gojo. And yes to Shoko. And you're taking me.” ʸ

She squeezes his hand.

“And because if anything's wrong, you'll say so,” she says. “To my face. Out loud. And so will I.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't say anything for a while.

He looks at the yard. At the dark shape of the orchard wall, where Gojo will stand. At the place where the protection ends. At the hill road beyond it, going north, where he walked into the dark with Makima's hand in his and didn't look back.

“I'm not afraid either,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks up at him.

He checks. He goes inside himself, the way he's learned to, and looks. Under the ribs. Behind the doors. In the room he goes back to, sometimes, without meaning to.

It's quiet in there.

“It's true,” he says. “I looked. I'm not.” ᴹ

Yuka puts her head back on his shoulder.

“Good,” she says. ʸ

* * *

They go up at midnight.

At the top of the stairs, outside their door, she stops him.

“The dragon,” she says. ʸ

He looks at her.

“Ori's dragon,” says Yuka. “The lend. For luck. Have you got it?” ʸ

He takes it out of his pocket. The small carved dragon. Sitting.

Yuka looks at it for a long moment.

“Keep it on you,” she says. “Tomorrow. All day. It's a rule.” ʸ

“Whose rule?” ᴹ

“Ori's,” says Yuka. “She told me to tell you. She said things you love are luckier. She said that's official.” ʸ

He puts the dragon back in his pocket.

They go to bed.

And for the first time before something like this, in either of his lives, he sleeps all night.
