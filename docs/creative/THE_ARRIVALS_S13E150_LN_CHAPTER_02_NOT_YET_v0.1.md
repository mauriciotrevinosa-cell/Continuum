# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 85
## Chapter 29 — Not Yet

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1 (end of volume)
**Volume:** 85
**Reading order:** 29
**Source episode:** S13E150
**Continuity state:** B+487 ("0"), the hour. The ninety days end. Yoru comes in Asa's body to the edge of the grounds and finds the whole house standing in the yard, exactly as drawn in Rimuru's notebook. She says: not yet. She promised him something better than the basin, and something better takes longer. Being, she says, so very generous, she won't kill anyone while she prepares it. In exchange she wants one answer: why didn't he enjoy the fight with the devils? He tells her the truth: he hadn't slept in days, he'd just come from a fight where he did smile, the devils were irritating, and all he wanted was to go home and sleep. Yoru says nothing. She'll give the house warning, and rules, in good time; he asks for a month; she offers two weeks; he starts to say something and she says one word other than "thanks" and it's one week; he says "thanks." Asa surfaces to say hello and goodbye. Mau offers her a camping kit, a change of clothes and food, in case she has nowhere. Asa says they have a place, but it's far away; if the offer stands, she'll take it. Yoru, grudgingly: "Asa told me to say thanks." They go. The countdown in the notebook ends. The house goes in to lunch.

*When: B+487. The hour. In the notebook, in Yuka's hand: "0". And beside it, very large, in Ori's: "(NOT YET)".*

The hour comes at noon.

He knows the exact minute. So does everyone. The same hour on the four hundred and eighty-seventh day, Yoru said, at a table by the sea, with Asa watching. Ninety days. Signed.

At a quarter to, the house goes out into the yard.

Nobody hurries. Nobody runs. They come out of the kitchen and the workshop and the Fort and the north wing and go to the places they chose last night at the table, the places in Rimuru's notebook, the small marks that were every one of them a person.

Gojo at the orchard wall, where the protection ends. Blindfold up. Hands in his pockets.

Shoko a step behind him.

Jin-Woo at the south road, his family behind him, his shadows at the gate.

Frieren and Himmel on the porch.

Maki, Yuta and Mai at the workshop door. Yuta's ring on. Ten minutes ready.

Dabura at the north edge, by the hill road, sitting on the wall with a pear in his hand.

The Fort on the roof of the Fort, under the REM SAID IT banner. The witches on their terrace. Senku by the training posts with something that hisses.

Ori on the porch steps, with Beru behind her and Lantern in her shadow and her notebook open on her knees.

Bellion's front rank at the edges of the grounds. Kamish across the bottom of the orchard, keeping the house, his head up for once.

And Mau in the middle of the yard. Makima at his right shoulder. Yuka at his left, with Igris watching over her. A small carved dragon in his pocket.

Everybody. Together. In daylight. Where she'll see them.

* * *

She comes at noon exactly.

Up the hill road. On foot. Not hiding. Not fast.

Asa's body. The school uniform from a world that isn't this one. The scar across the face. Yoru's eyes looking out of it.

She stops at the edge of the grounds. At the place where the protection ends. Where Gojo is standing.

Gojo doesn't move.

Yoru looks at him. Then past him. At the yard.

At all of it.

At Dabura on the wall with his pear. At the Fort on its roof with its banner. At the witches. At the workshop. At the porch, with an elf and a hero and a small girl with a notebook and an enormous ant. At Jin-Woo on the south road with his family. At the middle of the yard, where a man is standing with a woman on each side of him.

All of them. Looking back at her.

Nobody's afraid. She can see that. Mau watches her see it.

Yoru tilts her head.

* * *

“Well,” she says. ʷᵈ

Her voice carries across the whole yard. She doesn't raise it.

“This is new,” she says. ʷᵈ

Mau walks to the edge.

Not alone. Makima at his right. Yuka at his left. Gojo steps aside a half-pace to let them stand beside him, and doesn't step any further.

Mau stops in front of her. At the line where the protection ends.

“It's the hour,” he says. ᴹ

“It is,” Yoru agrees. ʷᵈ

“The ninety days are over.” ᴹ

“They are.” ʷᵈ

He waits.

Yoru looks at him for a long moment. Then she looks past him again, at the yard, at all of them, at Ori on the porch steps with her pencil.

“Not yet,” says Yoru. ʷᵈ

* * *

Nobody says anything.

“I promised you something better,” says Yoru. “In the basin. Do you remember? *I'll think of something better.* And I meant it.” ʷᵈ

She spreads her hands.

“I've been thinking,” she says. “For seven weeks. Watching. And I don't have it yet. It isn't ready. Something better takes longer. Something worth watching takes much longer.” ʷᵈ

Her eyes go back to the yard.

“And now,” she says, “I'm going to have to think even harder. Because I came here expecting one man, and I find a house. Standing in a yard. In daylight. *All* of them.” ʷᵈ

Something moves in Asa's face that isn't Asa.

“You're very difficult to fight,” says Yoru, “when you won't fight alone.” ʷᵈ

* * *

“So,” says Yoru. “Not yet. I'll go away and prepare. It'll take a while.” ʷᵈ

She smiles. It isn't Asa's smile.

“And because I'm so very generous,” she says, “I'll make you a promise. Not a contract. Just a promise. In the meantime, while I'm preparing, I won't kill anyone. Not in your house. Not on your roads. Not anyone you love.” ʷᵈ

She tilts her head.

“That's very kind of me,” she says. “Don't you think?” ʷᵈ

Mau doesn't answer that.

“What do you want for it?” he says. ᴹ

Yoru's smile widens.

“One answer,” she says. ʷᵈ

* * *

“The basin,” says Yoru. ʷᵈ

He waits.

“I came to watch you enjoy it,” she says. “That was the whole point. I wanted to see what happened when a man who's been told all his life to hold back was given a bowl full of things nobody would miss and told *go on*. I wanted to watch you discover you liked it.” ʷᵈ

Her eyes narrow.

“And you didn't,” she says. “Three moves. Two of them not yours. And then you stood there with your hands at your sides like you'd taken out the rubbish.” ʷᵈ

She leans forward a little.

“Why?” she says. “Why didn't you enjoy it? I saw you at the quarry. With the Simurian. You smiled. In the middle of it. You *liked* it. Why not the basin?” ʷᵈ

* * *

He thinks about it.

The real answer. The plain one.

“I hadn't slept,” he says. ᴹ

Yoru blinks.

“For days,” says Mau. “I'd been up since before the quarry. I'd fought Dabura the day before. I did smile in that one. You're right. I liked it.” ᴹ

He shrugs.

“And then you turned up in my yard at night and told me I had to go and fight a war,” he says. “And I walked north in the dark and stood in a basin, and a lot of small hungry things came over the rim, and I looked at them, and I thought—” ᴹ

He stops. He makes himself say it exactly.

“I thought they were really irritating,” he says. ᴹ

Yoru stares at him.

“They were loud,” says Mau. “And they smelled. And they weren't interesting. They weren't Dabura. They didn't want to fight me, they wanted to eat me, and that's not the same. And I was so tired I could barely stand, and my wife was in the yard writing a list, and all I wanted, the whole time, the only thing in my head, was to finish it and go home and sleep.” ᴹ

He looks at her.

“So I finished it,” he says. “And I went home. And I slept for thirty-eight hours.” ᴹ

He waits.

“That's the answer,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Yoru doesn't say anything.

For a long time. She stands at the edge of the grounds in Asa's body with her hands at her sides and her face very still, looking at him.

Mau watches her not say anything.

He doesn't know what she's thinking. He doesn't think he'd understand if he did. But he thinks, maybe, from the way her face has gone still, that it's the most boring answer anyone has ever given her.

And that she believes it completely.

* * *

“Warning,” says Yoru at last. ʷᵈ

He waits.

“When I've thought of it,” she says. “The better thing. I'll give you warning. And rules. In good time. So your whole house can stand in the yard again, if that's what you want.” ʷᵈ

“How much time?” says Mau. ᴹ

“Enough,” says Yoru. ʷᵈ

“A month,” says Mau. ᴹ

Yoru looks at him.

“Two weeks,” she says. ʷᵈ

He opens his mouth.

He has an argument ready. About the house, and the children on the south road, and how long it takes to get Senku's hissing thing working properly.

Yoru holds up one finger.

“If you say one word,” she says pleasantly, “that isn't *thanks*, it's one week.” ʷᵈ

He closes his mouth.

Beside him, Yuka makes a very small sound. On his other side, Makima has gone very still in a way he recognises as trying not to laugh.

Behind him, at the orchard wall, Gojo isn't trying at all.

“Thanks,” says Mau. ᴹ

Yoru smiles.

“Good,” she says. ʷᵈ

* * *

Then something changes in her face.

Mau sees it happen. The eyes. Yoru's eyes, looking out of Asa's face, and then, between one breath and the next, not Yoru's. Different. Younger. More tired. More frightened. A girl in a school uniform with a scar across her face, standing at the edge of a stranger's yard, looking at a crowd of people who are all looking back at her.

Asa.

She blinks.

“Hi,” she says. ᴬˢ

Her voice is quite different. Smaller. Uncertain.

“Hi,” says Mau. ᴹ

Asa looks round at the yard. At all of them. She looks very much like she would like to be anywhere else.

“Sorry,” she says. “About—all of it. The devils. The—everything. She doesn't listen to me. Most of the time.” ᴬˢ

“I know,” says Mau. ᴹ

Asa looks at him.

“Bye,” she says. “I just wanted to say hi. And bye. Before she—” ᴬˢ

She stops.

“Bye,” she says again. ᴬˢ

* * *

“Wait,” says Mau. ᴹ

Asa stops.

He reaches into the dark at his feet. The shadow. He takes things out of it, one at a time, and holds them out.

A pack. The good one, from the Shop. A tent folded inside it. The thick blanket. Not the thin one.

A change of clothes. Plain ones. In her size, roughly. Yuka helped him guess last night.

A bag of food. Shuna packed it this morning without asking what it was for. Bread. Dried fruit. The honey and pear cake. A small jar wrapped in a napkin folded into a dragon.

He holds them all out over the line where the protection ends.

Asa stares at them.

“In case you've got nowhere,” he says. “To sleep. Or a change. Or food. I don't know where she takes you. I don't know if you've got anywhere.” ᴹ

* * *

Asa looks at the pack for a long time.

“We have a place,” she says at last. Quietly. “She has a place. It's—it's far. Very far. It's not—it's not very nice.” ᴬˢ

She looks at the blanket.

“If the offer stands,” she says. “Even if we have a place. Can I still—” ᴬˢ

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

Asa reaches out over the line.

She takes the pack. The clothes. The bag of food. Her arms full. She stands at the edge of the grounds holding all of it against her chest, the way Ori holds the little carved dragon. Very carefully. With both arms.

She looks at Mau.

She doesn't say anything. Her eyes are very bright.

* * *

And then they change again.

Yoru's eyes. Looking out of Asa's face. Looking down at the armful of things. The pack. The blanket. The jar with the napkin folded into a dragon.

Yoru's face does something complicated.

“Asa,” she says, through her teeth, “told me to say thanks.” ʷᵈ

Mau looks at her.

“So,” says Yoru, with enormous reluctance. “*Thanks*.” ʷᵈ

She turns round.

She walks away down the hill road, in Asa's body, with her arms full of a pack and a blanket and a bag of food, and doesn't look back.

* * *

The yard is very quiet.

Mau stands at the edge of the grounds, where the protection ends, and watches her go. Down the hill road. Over the rise. Gone.

Makima, at his right shoulder, lets out a long slow breath.

Yuka, at his left, takes his hand.

Gojo, at the orchard wall, says, to nobody in particular: “*Thanks*. She said *thanks*. Yoru. The War Devil. Said *thanks*.” ᴳ

“Asa made her,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“I know,” says Gojo. “That's the best part.” ᴳ

* * *

On the porch steps, Ori closes her notebook.

She's been writing the whole time. Every word. For the official record. She looks at the last page for a long time.

Then she opens it again, and turns to the front. To the page where Yuka wrote *90*, a long time ago, the night after the table by the sea, before the Simurians, before any of it. The first page of the countdown.

She turns all the way through. Every page. Every number. *90. 89. 88.* All the way down. Past *53*, with no drawing. Past *52*, with a list under it. Past *47* in whipped cream. Past *25*, *DRAW (STYLE)*. Past *20*, *mine*. Past *4*, *HI NOISE*.

To *0*.

She looks at it.

Then she takes her pencil and writes beside it, very large, in her best handwriting:

*(NOT YET)*

And under that, smaller:

*Two weeks' warning. He said thanks.*

And under that, smaller still:

*Countdown finished. Start a new notebook.*

* * *

Shuna rings the bell for supper.

The house goes in.

All of it. Out of the yard. Off the roof of the Fort. Down from the wall. In from the south road. Gojo and Shoko. Frieren and Himmel. Rem and Umaru. DIO, coming out of Mau in her own body to walk in beside him, already composing a letter to Elmesia in her head about all of it. Dabura, with his pear, which he still hasn't eaten.

Mau goes in last.

He stops at the kitchen door. He looks back at the yard. Empty now. The grass. The training posts. The orchard. Kamish under his tree. The hill road going north over the rise, where Yoru went with her arms full.

Two weeks' warning. Whenever that is. Something better. Something that takes longer.

Not yet.

*Are you afraid?* says Ciel, inside, very quietly. ᶜ

He checks. Under the ribs. Behind the doors.

*No*, he says. ᴹ

*Good*, says Ciel. ᶜ

He takes the small carved dragon out of his pocket and looks at it. Sitting. Ori's. A lend, not a give.

He'll give it back after supper.

He goes in.

* * *

Inside, Yuka is already at the table. In his chair. Which she's never done.

She looks up at him with a perfectly straight face and pats her lap.

He sits in her chair instead. Beside her.

She gives him a look, and before she can reach for his plate he tucks an arm around her and draws both chairs closer. The legs scrape. Shuna puts down the rice and goes straight back for two more bowls.

“Here,” he says, and kisses her when she leans back to find out what he wants. ᴹ

Yuka stays tucked under his chin until Ori comes with the empty notebook and asks if there's room. Mau shifts over. Yuka lifts the edge of the tablecloth for Ori's knees. The notebook goes down between their plates, and while Ori is deciding what to write on the first page, Yuka steals his pickles.
