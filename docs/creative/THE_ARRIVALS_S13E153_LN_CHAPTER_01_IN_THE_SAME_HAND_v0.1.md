# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 86
## Chapter 3 — In the Same Hand

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 86
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S13E153
**Continuity state:** B+502, six days before the lake. Rimuru and Fern start checking the ground from Yoru's notice. Mau makes time of his own to talk to Yuka in their room. He tells her what he believed he saw when she took her wedding band off and asks whether she would have come to find him if he'd stopped asking. Yuka answers as far as she can. Afterward they go back down together and keep their promise to help Ori with the ford cart.

*When: B+502. Early enough for the kitchen windows to mist over.*

Ori leaves the pear outside the door before breakfast. It's on a saucer with one side eaten. There's a note, flattened under the clean side, that says YOU CAN HAVE THAT BIT. The edge of the paper has been cut in the shape of teeth.

Mau takes everything back to bed. Yuka opens one eye when the saucer touches her knee. She has most of his shirt caught under her hip. He's been awake long enough to know he won't get it out by pulling the hem.

“Someone sent you breakfast,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at the pear. “Did she leave the part she didn't like?” ʸ

“She left us the part that was still good.” ᴹ

Yuka sticks her hand out of the covers and lifts the note. It sags in the middle. “That's evidence of teeth. We can probably charge her.” ʸ

He takes a bite while she puts the note where the juice can't get to it. By the time she turns back he's holding the pear at the height of her mouth. She takes the next bite exactly where his ended. He gets the saucer out of the way before he leans over to kiss her. She lets him get as far as the first kiss before she puts the heel of one foot against his shin and pulls him all the way back under the covers.

He hears footsteps on the landing. Small ones, then Beru's. They pass the room. Yuka slips one hand up the back of Mau's neck and kisses him again, more slowly now that she has him the way she wanted. He can taste the pear. He can also feel the cold place where she ran a hand down the back of his collar. He moves the sleeve out from under her, just enough to get his arm around her waist.

The footsteps pass a second time. There are two sets of small ones now. A muted discussion takes place outside the door about whether a person who has left you breakfast is allowed to ask you for the saucer back.

“Give us five minutes,” calls Yuka. ʸ

“Five from now or from the first pear?” asks Chloe. ᶜʰ

“Now.” ʸ

Mau can hear Ori taking this down. “We could give you six,” she says. ᴼ

“Tell her we'll take six,” Mau says into Yuka's hair. ᴹ

Yuka tells her. They spend the first minute making the bed worse and the next four trying to fix it with one of Yuka's arms still around him. The sheet comes free of the corner. They decide to leave it out and get dressed.

* * *

At breakfast there are two maps of the lake laid end to end. They agree about the shore on one side and about nothing on the other. Fern holds both flat with dishes. She'd like to know who last sent somebody far enough north to find out if the old track is still there.

Bellion turns one of the sheets so the top points out through the back of the house. “The scout came in from here. Somebody has put a fence across what used to be the road.” ᴮˡ

“Is the fence there for animals?” asks Rimuru. “I'd like to hear from whoever put it up before we decide nobody lives near the lake.” ᴿ

Mau moves the plate with his eggs on it so he can see which corner Fern is holding. The road they're talking about doesn't go to the ford. It would be easy to Exchange past it. He'd be able to tell them in a few minutes whether it still went all the way through.

Instead he asks Fern which road she wants him on today.

“I don't need you on either yet,” she says. “I need someone to check whether the people who use them got our notice.” ᶠᵉ

“I can do the ford after lunch,” says Mau. ᴹ

Fern puts a mark in the right place. She holds his plate up while Yuka pulls a map corner off the butter. Nobody asks him when he'll be able to go to the lake.

Makima is on her way out with two rolled copies from Rimuru. Mau catches her eye and asks whether she's seen the fence on the other side, the side the scouts didn't walk. She puts the rolls down long enough to show him where Rimuru started looking. He knows from the way she touches the paper that she'd like to come around and see it from where he's standing. He moves over. She takes the space and lays a finger along a little curve where there wasn't supposed to be a path at all.

“Fern can ask at that house,” she says. “Someone keeps moving the same gate.” ᴹᵏ

Mau bends until he can see that the scout marked a second position. He smiles as soon as he finds it. Makima feels him do it and picks the maps up again, already smiling herself.

Yuka folds his napkin and holds it out to him. He takes it in the same hand she has been able to feel through his sleeve since they came in.

“Do you have time upstairs before the cart?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks straight at him. He can feel her thinking about the towel and what he managed to say before Yoru came.

“Yes,” she says. “I'll tell Ori we'll get her after.” ʸ

“I can tell her. I'd like to go now, if you're done.” ᴹ

Yuka gets her cup up with both hands and drinks the last of it. She sets it down with enough room between it and the edge that she won't need to come back to push it in. “I'm done.” ʸ

* * *

In the room he gets as far as the bed and then stands up again. Yuka goes to the chair. It's the one he put his shirts on before he went back to sleep at Shoko's. She sits on it without clearing the socks off the back.

“I wanted to tell you before I go to the lake,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka puts her hands in her lap. “Because you're afraid?” ʸ

“Of going there and coming home with something else I have to tell you first.” He reaches for the window catch. He doesn't need to open it. He leaves it alone. “I'll fight her. I've wanted to know what she can do since the island. I know what I mean when I say that. This I keep having to start over.” ᴹ

Yuka can see him looking at the chair while he says it. She'd walked in here with clean socks for Ori and found him making space for her things. The drawer still doesn't shut unless someone pushes it twice. She'd meant to fix the catch and never did.

“What would help you start?” she says. ʸ

“Can I see it?” ᴹ

She knows which hand. She holds it out and turns it enough for him to see the top of the band. He comes over to her slowly, and sits on the edge of the bed facing the chair. The light reaches both their hands from there. When she sees he'd like to touch it, she sets her wrist on his knee. He holds the base of one finger between his thumb and the side of his own hand, just below the band.

“It stopped there,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸ

“On the morning you took it off. You turned it a little and used your other hand.” He moves his thumb the distance he'd watched her wrist move. “You did that the first time I put it on you. There wasn't anyone behind me I could look at for help. You did it for me.” ᴹ

She remembers. She'd been trying to keep both hands steady so he could finish putting it on. Her knuckle had got red, and she saw him look at it for long enough that she bit the inside of her cheek so she wouldn't start laughing at the altar. She can see the same care in his hand now. It makes it harder to hear what he tells her next.

“When it came off, I thought it meant I'd finally heard you properly. All night I could tell myself you'd said *maybe*. When you worked it past that knuckle, I thought you were showing me what you'd decided while I was waiting for you to change your mind.” ᴹ

“You thought I was done.” ʸ

“I thought you were done and I was the one still asking. I kept trying to work out how to make it easier for you to leave me. I knew what shirts I would need from here. I knew how to keep Ori out of it.” His thumb stops. “I couldn't work out how to be there when she came looking for you and found me instead.” ᴹ

Yuka takes a breath and feels it very far down. She has the impulse to put her arms round him and say all the things that have felt true every morning since she came to the clinic. His hold on her finger is light enough that she could do it without giving him time to get the rest out. She keeps her elbow on her leg.

“I wanted you to ask me to put it back,” she says. “I knew how much it would scare you, and I thought that was how I'd find out that it mattered to you. You were standing there asking me to give you time to talk. I made you watch me do it anyway.” ʸ

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

He says it into the quiet. She can feel the little effort it takes him to let it stay there.

“There was something else I couldn't ask at Shoko's,” he says. “If I'd stopped coming to find you, would you have come to get me?” ᴹ

Yuka looks down at her hand. She'd known where his bed was. She'd been able to see the lamp from Ori's room. When she went downstairs she sometimes sat far enough toward the wall that she could tell herself she wouldn't be in his way if he came in. She can see the length of the room from here. She knows how long a person can sit where she sat before a decision starts to feel like something that's happening to them.

“I want to say I would've come sooner,” she says. “I think I could have kept waiting. I kept telling myself you'd have to be the one who came first, even when you already had. That's what I was like that week.” She draws another breath. “I wish I'd come on my own while you still knew how to ask me.” ʸ

Mau looks up at her. She has to let go of her own knee to keep from reaching for him too quickly. He sits with the answer long enough for her to hear a latch go in the yard below, and a cart wheel roll a little before somebody stops it with a foot.

“Thank you for not giving me the answer you wanted to give me,” he says. ᴹ

She nods. For a little while he keeps her hand on his knee. He turns it over eventually. The band stays where it is. He slides his thumb over the warm skin of her palm and holds her hand there.

“Can I sit beside you?” she asks. ʸ

“Please.” ᴹ

She moves to the edge of the bed. He leans against her before either of them has worked out what to do with the space she left for him. It puts her sleeve against his cheek. She can feel him breathe for a while without looking down. When he lifts his head, she rests her forehead on his.

“How do you feel?” she asks. ʸ

“Like I told my wife something I didn't think I'd ever be able to tell her.” His eyes close for another breath. “A little less alone with it.” ᴹ

She kisses him where she's close enough to reach. He brings his hand up to her face and turns toward her properly. There's time for one more kiss before they hear Ori downstairs asking if anyone knows which of the two baskets they're meant to take.

* * *

Mau takes the bigger basket. Yuka gets into the cart before he can decide whether to lift it in after her or put it at her feet. She moves over with both hands on the seat and gives him half the blanket. He drops the basket in at the back and climbs in with her.

Ori is standing by the wheel, looking at all three of them. “Who’s driving?” ᴼ

Mau looks at Yuka. Yuka looks at the clear space beside Ori. The two of them get as far as making the same face before Rimuru calls that if they want to sit together the whole way they'll have to promise to unload at the other end.

Yuka agrees for herself. Mau agrees before she's finished. Ori gets up beside Rimuru and tells him where the bad stones start. At the turn in the road a bump puts Mau's shoulder into Yuka's. Her hand finds his under the blanket. He leaves it there and listens to Ori explain which bit of the ford she wants to show him first.
