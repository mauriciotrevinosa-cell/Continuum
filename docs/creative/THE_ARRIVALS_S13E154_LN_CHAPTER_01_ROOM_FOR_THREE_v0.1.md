# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 86
## Chapter 5 — Room for Three

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 86
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E154
**Continuity state:** B+504. Makima asks Mau to stay past the time he'd planned to leave. He asks her to see whether Yuka wants to join them. The three spend the rest of the afternoon on the porch after the bread has come out. Later Makima approaches Yuka herself and says she would like a date with Mau. Yuka says no and asks for time to think about the kinds of company she can be comfortable with.

*When: B+504. The bread takes longer to finish than Shuna expected.*

Rem brings the loaves in two batches. The first comes off the board while there is still enough good light to see all the cracks in it. The second leans into the first on its way out and has to be carried by somebody with two hands free. Makima puts her book face down, wipes flour off the end of her sleeve and takes it from Rem.

“Do you want any cut now?” asks Rem. ᴿᵉ

Makima taps the end of the loaf with one finger. “Will you show me where you'd begin?” ᴹᵏ

Rem gets the knife. At the head of the kitchen table, Ori has a plate and a shoulder bag spread out over the seats on either side of her. She's got the report from the far road folded into her notebook. Makima has been helping her keep the report flat while they copy parts of it for Fern. She knows which piece of bread Ori is going to ask for without having to turn round.

Rem cuts it too thick. Makima says that'll be the one for Ori. Rem puts both her hands on the edge of the board and laughs a little. She meant to cut all of them that thick. She cuts the next one thin on purpose and holds it up for Makima to approve.

“That's for Mau,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“He won't think so.” ᴿᵉ

“He won't have to know until he's been given the first one.” ᴹᵏ

Makima finds a smaller plate and takes both pieces out to the porch anyway. Ori brings the notebook after her. By the time Mau comes looking for them, Ori has spent half an hour drawing the fence from three angles and eaten everything she left on her plate. She takes the notebook to Bellion to see if he can explain why it doesn't show where the last gate went. Makima looks down at the thin piece of bread still resting against the thick one.

“I thought I'd be late,” says Mau. ᴹ

“I saved you the good end,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He can see it's a middle piece. He puts it against the other and pulls a face as though she'll have to answer for cutting off the crust. Then she lifts the cloth and shows him the bit she saved underneath. He takes that first, breaks it on the rim of the plate and gives her half.

For a good while they have this particular patch of sun to themselves. He has an opinion about the last thing in the book she brought out and wants to go back three pages to see if it holds up. It does until she makes him read out the bit between the two lines he remembered. She finds herself listening to the way he says the second line twice so he'll be able to hear where he'd gone wrong. When he asks if there's a third reason she picked the book, she says she wanted to see whether he'd stick up for the woman in it. He says he was ready to until the woman left her dog tied to a cart.

“She went back for the dog,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“Read me that part.” ᴹ

She finds it. He makes her finish the whole page even after it turns out the dog didn't want to be untied. He would like to know where the cart was going. She turns the page and there is no cart. There is a picture of a fish. He tries to get her to go back and she takes the corner in both hands to keep it where it is. They lean into each other for the few seconds it takes him to admit that the fish looks unreasonably good.

The light moves off the bottom step and onto the face of it. He shuts the book on the strip of cloth she was using for a mark.

“I said I'd go get Yuka after she put the drying rack away.” ᴹ

She'd known before he remembered. She can feel him ready to get up and has time to work out how many ways she could get him to spend another minute arguing about the fish. She leaves the book closed.

“Would you stay a little longer?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

He looks across at her without getting up. “Ask Yuka if she wants to come sit with us. I can keep your place in the book.” ᴹ

She has almost offered to go and ask a different question by the time he hands it back. She sets the book on the space between them. It will keep his place for her as well.

* * *

Yuka is at the back door trying to get the last foot of the drying rack flat. There's a heel of one shoe stuck through the bars. The other shoe is laid on its side out of the way, holding the cloth they put over the line when they don't want to leave it out at night.

“Do you need a hand?” asks Makima. ᴹᵏ

“Yes. Can you take the top when I take my foot out?” ʸ

Makima holds it. She can feel at once which part of the frame will come at Yuka's shin if she lets it go. Yuka turns her foot slowly until it's clear, then gets all the bars together with both hands. The rack comes upright between them. There is a spot worn clean where somebody always holds it to lift it.

“I asked Mau to stay out a little longer,” Makima says. “He said to ask you if you wanted to come.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka gets her shoe back under her foot and pushes the heel down with her other one. Makima sees a question cross her face and then another one on its way. Yuka takes the top of the rack so Makima can stand beside her.

“We could all sit out for a while,” Yuka says. “Did he eat the bread already?” ʸ

“Some of it. There was an end piece.” ᴹᵏ

“Then I need to go and check how much of the rest he saved for me.” ʸ

They put the rack away together. By the time they get round to the porch Mau has moved the book and is eating the last third of a slice right down the middle, which will make it impossible to set back on the plate without Yuka seeing the bite. She climbs over him far enough to get to it and he lifts one arm round her back. She takes the bread out of his hand, kisses him and sits sideways across his legs to eat it.

Makima holds her cup in both hands. She likes the look on Mau's face when he turns his chin to see whether Yuka got crumbs into his sleeve. She'd liked having his whole attention on the book, too. Her fingers are still round the cup when Yuka reaches for the cloth they left across the plate and passes it over to her.

“Read us the bit he tried to skip,” she says. ʸ

“How do you know he tried to skip it?” ᴹᵏ

“He always tells me to leave out the parts that make him look bad.” ʸ

Mau says he'd like to object to *always* and waves at Makima to go ahead and read. She opens the book where the cloth held her place. Yuka settles her head against his shoulder, and for as long as the light lasts they argue about whether the dog knew where it was going.

* * *

The steps are empty when Makima comes out again after supper. Yuka is bringing the cups back. She puts them in a row on the end of the bench, starting with the one she's been drinking from. There's a drop of tea coming off the handle. She catches it with a finger before it can run down the front of the cup.

“Can we talk for a minute?” asks Makima. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her, then goes back to the first cup and moves it far enough along the bench to sit where it was. There is enough room between her and the cups for one other person. Makima sits.

“I'd like to do that again,” she says. “Spend some time with both of you.” ᴹᵏ

“I had a good time.” Yuka turns her head to look at her. “There's something else.” ʸ

Makima has several ways to say it. She tried one after dinner while she was standing at the sink. It used the fact that Yuka had just laughed with her for half an hour. It would have made it easy to say yes without thinking about the next day. She watches Yuka move the cup again and lets that wording go.

“Sometimes I'd like to have him to myself a little longer. Sometimes I want to take him out on a date.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's fingers come down round the cup. She turns it once. Makima can feel something being carried from the kitchen to the yard through Mau's end of the line. She's been listening closely enough to know exactly how far away he is. She stops listening so she can hear Yuka take her next breath.

“When you say date, that's really what you mean?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka starts to nod, so slightly that it might have been the weight of the cup making her look down. Then she sets it on the bench. “I can give you an answer to that part. No. I can't picture being all right with it.” ʸ

Makima had expected to have another thing to say when she heard that word. She has to put the heel of her hand against the bench before she can tell how she was planning to sit through it.

“Will you still want me to come out for tea?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks up sharply enough that the question can surprise her too. “I wanted you there today. I want you there tomorrow. Give me some time to think about the rest, please.” ʸ

Makima nods. There are three cups left on the bench. She takes the two cooler ones and lets Yuka bring the one she's already holding. Inside, she puts them side by side on the cloth Shuna left for them. She can hear where Mau is at the far end of the yard. She stays to dry the last cup anyway.
