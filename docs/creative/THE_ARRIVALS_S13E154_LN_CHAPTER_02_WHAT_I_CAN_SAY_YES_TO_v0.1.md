# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 86
## Chapter 6 — What I Can Say Yes To

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 86
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S13E154
**Continuity state:** B+505. Yuka sees a familiar ease between Mau and Makima and hears Cross's old framing again. She talks to Mai before going to Mau herself. Mau explains how strong feelings come across the shared bond and says plainly what he wants with Yuka. They discuss a boundary around private plans. Mau asks whether they can talk to people they trust with Makima there too.

*When: B+505. Three mornings left before the lake.*

Yuka finds the good scissors before Mai finds her. She uses them to cut a strip of cloth for the table. In one corner, through no fault of the scissors, the strip comes out shaped like a spoon.

Mai opens her mouth. Yuka gives her the spoon-shaped end, and Mai shuts it again and puts the end where it won't do anybody any harm.

“Are we going to use this for the water?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“For the people carrying the water. When they come back, Shoko can put a strip on their sleeve if they're supposed to sit down before they go back out.” Mai pulls the cloth flat across her lap. “I know what you're thinking. You could make someone sit down without a strip.” ᴹᵃ

“I was thinking he would put a second sleeve on.” ʸ

Mai's laugh is short and surprised. Yuka likes getting her to laugh before she can decide if something's funny. They cut two more strips. One of them turns out quite straight.

Across the yard, Mau has gone to the end of the bench where Makima is sitting. He'd meant to bring Yuka a cup first. She can see both cups in his hand. He leans down to get a look at the paper Makima is marking. Somebody's made a note for Ori in a different hand. Makima puts her finger under it. He reads it and smiles before he remembers to tell anyone what it says.

As soon as he straightens, he catches Yuka looking. He comes to her with the cups. The tea he set out for her is exactly the one she likes when she hasn't eaten. He had to walk back to the pantry to get it, because there was something quite different in both pots on the stove. He's got her cup and his cup by their handles in one hand because his other hand is ready to get into her hair.

She lets him touch it. He gets the bit of thread out with his thumb and leans down to kiss her. It ought to give her everything she needs to know about how he'd come to stand here. It does, for the length of the kiss.

Then she can see him reading over Makima's shoulder again. Hear how he'd laughed. He'd wanted Makima to hear the thing too, even with his hands full of what he went to get for his wife.

Mau sets the second cup by her elbow. “I'll come back when we've put the notice up. Do you want me to bring Ori?” ᴹ

“I think she's got most of the pins.” ʸ

“Then I'll bring Ori and the pins.” ᴹ

He kisses her again before he goes. She puts two fingers on the place where his mouth was and feels stupid for a whole breath.

She picks the cloth up once he gets across the yard. It's the length of the mark Mai made. She should be able to cut it directly across, and instead she has lined the scissors up with the place where Mai's wrist is resting.

“Give me those,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

Yuka does. Mai sets them down on her other side, where neither of them can trip over the blade.

“Makima asked me if she could take him on a date,” Yuka says. ʸ

Mai sits with that. “What'd you say?” ᴹᵃ

“I said no.” ʸ

“Did you want to say no?” ᴹᵃ

“Yes.” It comes out very easily here. She can see the two cups on the table and the mark Mau made moving one of them closer to her. “I thought for a second I should say yes. She was there when I couldn't get him to look at me. She talked to me on the step. And he asks for her now even when he's all right. He likes being with her. I don't think I can ask him to stop liking it just because I didn't pay attention to what was happening.” ʸ

Mai gathers the cloth so it won't run off the end of the table. “What did you want a say in?” ᴹᵃ

“Whether they go somewhere alone together because Makima wanted it to be a date.” Yuka looks out at the back of Mau's head where he's talking to Ori. “And I think I want to talk about what counts as alone. There are things he can talk to her about in his head that I don't hear. I don't want to take that away. I wouldn't know where to put it if I did.” ʸ

“Did you tell him any of that?” ᴹᵃ

“No. I told her I needed to think.” ʸ

Mai gets the scissors back out. She cuts once to get the bad bit away from the good one. “You can use the time you asked for. Come with me to get the other cloth first. Then go find him while you know what you want to ask.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka takes the cut end and rolls it up with one hand. She thought Mai would want to tell her that she has an obvious right to be angry. She hadn't realised how relieved she'd be to find she can take a little longer deciding what comes after it.

“Cross would have told me that look meant I was right to worry,” she says. ʸ

Mai looks where Mau's gone. He and Ori are still trying to get the notice high enough for the last line to show. Makima has put a finger over the pin Ori dropped so they'll be able to find it when the paper moves.

“Is that what you thought when he came here with the tea?” asks Mai. ᴹᵃ

“For a second. It sounded exactly like him.” ʸ

“You can tell Mau it sounded like that.” Mai waits until the roll is straight. “Can you carry the cups? I only have room for the cloth.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka takes hers. She takes Mai's too. The other one belongs to Mau. When he comes looking for it later she'll be able to tell him where she left it.

* * *

He comes to find her first.

She hears the latch as he's carrying the empty basket back to Shuna. She has the new cloth stretched over a makeshift bench in the side yard, where the afternoon wind won't turn it over. Mau puts the basket down upside down and sits on it so he can hold the other end for her. She sees where one of the slats is starting to bend and very nearly tells him to get up. He sees her looking and sits farther back over the good edge.

“Did you need me for something earlier?” he asks. ᴹ

“I was trying to talk to Mai.” She smooths the end of the cloth. She has less to do with her hands than she thought she would. “Did Makima tell you what she asked me?” ʸ

“She said she asked you for a date with me. She said you were thinking about it, and that she heard you say no to the date.” He lifts his end a little so she can get hers square. “She came to tell me after supper. I wanted to hear from you before I said anything else about it.” ᴹ

Yuka lets the cloth rest on the bench. She can feel a small rush of relief and hasn't worked out what it means by the time he sets his end down too.

“Did it bother you that I said no?” she asks. ʸ

He thinks for long enough that she can almost find something else to say. Then he puts his elbows on his knees, one hand hanging between them. On the ridge he always puts both hands up so he can show whoever he's talking to where things ought to stand. Here she can see he's trying to decide how to do it with words.

“I like having time with her. I think there'd be a part of me that wanted that afternoon, whatever we called it.” ᴹ

Yuka swallows. “And what would you call it?” ʸ

“I'd have to know what I wanted to do with it before I could call it what she meant. I haven't figured all that out.” He looks at her. “When what she's feeling gets very strong I can feel it before she says anything. Sometimes I can't tell if it came across to me or it was already there. I know I love her. I know I want to be your husband. That second thing is mine, Yuka.” ᴹ

She waits, and he lets her. The wind gets one corner of the cloth up high enough to hit her on the knuckle before he puts it back.

“I still don't want you to go on a date,” she says. “And right now I don't want the two of you to make plans to be alone together. I almost said I could be fine with one of those things. I was thinking about what I did to you, and what she did for you afterward.” ʸ

“Did you think you couldn't ask me?” ᴹ

“I thought you might say it was for me to decide, and then one day I'd find out I decided something you didn't want.” ʸ

Mau turns the loose edge of the cloth under and gets his palm down on it. “I can agree about the plans. I can tell her myself, too. Sometimes I'll want to talk to her inside. Especially if something goes wrong at the lake.” ᴹ

“I want you to be able to do that.” Yuka runs her fingers along the place where the bench has bent, toward his. “What I want to know is if there starts to be something you won't tell me because you know how I'll take it.” ʸ

“I was about to wait till I could tell which feeling was mine.” He moves his hand so hers can fit over it. “I can see that might have made you wait a very long time.” ᴹ

She almost laughs. It hurts when it gets to her throat. “I started thinking of Cross again this morning. It was a second. You turned to look at her and I thought maybe he'd seen it before I did.” ʸ

Mau looks at the part of the yard where they were standing that morning. She can tell he has found it because his hand goes very still. For a moment she's afraid he's going to take his fingers out from under hers. He turns them all the way over and keeps her hand.

“Thank you for coming to me with it,” he says. ᴹ

She lets herself sit next to him. The basket creaks. He puts an arm around her waist so she can take the edge nearest him. When she rests against him she feels him kiss her hair before either of them has found where to put their feet.

* * *

Later he asks her if they can talk to somebody at home who has seen them together lately.

“About us?” says Yuka. ʸ

“About how I've been doing this. I'd like Makima to be there when I ask. And you. If either of you would rather do it in a smaller room, we can.” ᴹ

She thinks about the day she told them she needed help. She'd wanted everybody within reach and what she'd actually needed, to begin with, was a quiet place to say one thing she could stand to hear. She looks at Mau's face now. The part he can't hear from where he's sitting is what she's heard in the kitchen and what somebody who was paying attention might have been trying to tell them.

“Mai knows,” she says. “I'd ask Maki too. And Shizue, if she wants to come. I can tell you tomorrow if I want more people.” ʸ

“All right.” ᴹ

“You ask Makima. I think she'll want to hear it from you.” ʸ

His hand slides off the bench into her lap. She takes it and gets the cloth rolled with her other hand. “Come help me carry all this inside first,” she says. ʸ

He picks up the basket and swings it onto his arm. Before she can get the roll from the bench he has it balanced across the top. That leaves her a hand to fit through his elbow. She takes it and goes in with him.
