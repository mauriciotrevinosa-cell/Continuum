# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 86
## Chapter 7 — What Would You Have Said?

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 86
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E155
**Continuity state:** B+506. With Yuka and Makima's agreement, Mau asks a small group from the house for their honest opinion. They disagree about what each of the three owes the others. Makima says that she is in love with Mau and that she loves Yuka too. She offers a contract against deliberate interference with their marriage, asking to stay part of their life and the house. Yuka keeps the boundary on private plans and takes time to consider the terms. She makes room for Makima at supper.

*When: B+506. Two days before the lake.*

Rimuru is about to take the cloth off the back of a chair when Mau asks him to stay after the others leave.

“For the lake?” asks Rimuru. ᴿ

“We got the reply from the woman who collects stone. She's moving her visit until the road is open.” Mau takes the cloth first. It's still wet along one seam. “It's about something at home. Would you be able to listen for a bit after lunch?” ᴹ

Rimuru waits. There's a stool laid out beside the chair. He'd been going to put it under the notice where Ori can climb up and reach the bottom of the page. He pushes it toward Mau and sits on the chair.

“Everybody's all right?” ᴿ

“Everybody's all right. Yuka said to ask you. She asked Shizue. Mai and Maki can come after they put the rest of the cloth away.” ᴹ

“And Makima?” ᴿ

“I asked her yesterday. She said to tell you she can answer for herself if there's anything you want to know.” ᴹ

Rimuru looks at him for a few seconds. “Did you want me to know a part before you tell everybody?” ᴿ

“I think if I try to tell it twice I'll find a way to make one of them sound better.” ᴹ

Rimuru considers asking him something else. He sees Mau turn the wet edge of the cloth round so it won't get his trousers damp where he sits. He takes the cloth off his hands and drapes it on a hook out of the way.

“All right. I'll be there after lunch.” ᴿ

Mau lets him take the stool. In the yard Ori is checking whether there's enough room between the new notice and the old one to put something up about the pears. Rimuru carries it over to her. Before he puts it down he asks whether she wants four feet or only three of them on the grass. She gets the stones out of the way with the side of her boot and tells him he can have all four.

* * *

Yuka catches Mau by his shirt at the foot of the stairs. He was on his way to get the tray. She pulls him over far enough to sit down in the seat at the bend in the hall. There is a little space between the corner of the wall and where the cushion ends. He goes to fill it. She moves so that he can do it beside her.

“I asked them to go to the room by the kitchen,” she says. “Shizue put the kettle on. We've got a few minutes.” ʸ

“Do you still want to talk to them?” ᴹ

“I do. I just want you here first.” ʸ

He lets her pull his shoulder to hers. She puts her mouth on his cheek and then waits for him to look at her. As soon as he does she kisses him properly. The second kiss takes long enough that the person who was coming up the stairs decides to go back down. Mau runs his thumb behind her ear. There's a bit of hair he could put back and he's quite content to leave it there.

“Where did you put the cups?” she asks, when she can see his face again. ʸ

“I was going to get them.” ᴹ

“We can be late five minutes.” ʸ

“Rimuru lives here too. He can find cups.” ᴹ

She kisses him again for saying it. He presses his forehead to hers afterward and tells her he's going to be very bad at the first part of the conversation. She says he can tell her that again when she's had enough of waiting for him to start. They stay until they hear Shizue asking where the small teapot went. Yuka puts both hands out so he can pull her up and then she holds on to one of them as far as the room.

* * *

Makima has come early. There's a folded slip of paper beside her cup. She keeps one finger on it while the others get settled. Yuka can see there isn't anything written on the outside. She puts her chair in between Mau and the wall. When he reaches for the teapot, she holds the lid down for him without looking at where her hand goes.

He pours hers first, then Makima's. Maki takes the pot from him when he goes to do the same for everybody else. She sets it beside her chair, where he won't be able to fill another cup unless he asks.

“Do you want to start?” he asks Yuka. ᴹ

“I want to hear what you were going to ask me before you asked anybody else,” she says. ʸ

He lets his hand come down onto the table.

“I like having Makima close,” he says. “Sometimes I'm the one who goes looking for her. I ask her to stay inside if I think I'll need her. I went to her room the other day because I had a question I wanted to ask. I stayed because she held me and I wanted her to.” He gets a breath in and looks at Makima. She's left both hands where he can see them. “She asked Yuka for something I hadn't worked out how I felt about. And I found out I was waiting to know what I felt before I could tell Yuka there was anything to talk about.” ᴹ

Shizue's chair is a little farther back than the others. She hasn't moved it since she came in. “What did you want us to help you with?” she asks. ˢʰⁱ

“I want to know if I'm doing this wrong.” His eyes go to the band on Yuka's hand and come back to Shizue. “If you've seen me doing something I ought to talk about, can you tell me what it looked like? I'd rather hear it today.” ᴹ

Maki doesn't answer straight away. She brings the teapot up to fill her own cup and puts it down where it was.

“What did *you* think a date meant when she asked for it?” she asks. ᴹᵃᵏ

“She asked Yuka first. I didn't know until later.” ᴹ

“Okay. So, when you found out.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Mau puts the side of his hand flat on the table. “I know what one means when it's for me and Yuka. I don't think I could have meant it the way I mean it when I take my wife somewhere.” ᴹ

“What if Yuka had said yes?” ᴹᵃᵏ

The silence lasts long enough that Yuka reaches across him for the handle of her cup. Mau moves his elbow so she can get it without pulling it closer to him first.

“I'd still have had to answer Makima,” he says. He looks down. “I'm glad I didn't have to find that out by going.” ᴹ

“I'd have said yes for about the first two seconds,” says Yuka. She keeps the cup where both of them can get to it. “I felt like if I couldn't do that much for her after the clinic, I was being ungrateful. I had to sit there long enough to hear what I'd be saying yes to.” ʸ

Mai has her hands around an empty cup. She sets it down. “What about now?” ᴹᵃ

“Now I can say I love having her here. And I can say I don't want a date. I don't want the two of them making plans to be alone together right now.” Yuka looks at Mau long enough for him to see that she means to include him in the next part. “I told him already. I want to be able to say it with her here too.” ʸ

Makima bends her head once. A little tea moves in the cup she hasn't touched.

Rimuru turns in his chair until he's facing Mau across the shorter end. “Yesterday you called her to see where the wheat was. She was already next to Shuna. I've seen you do that a hundred times with the people who live in your head. I'd hate for either of you to have to think about a rule every time you need a bag of flour.” ᴿ

“I know,” says Mau. ᴹ

“Can you tell Yuka what you're actually agreeing to keep?” ᴿ

“I'll talk to her before I make the kind of plans we just talked about. And I can say I won't go on that date. I can agree to that myself.” Mau looks at Makima as he says the last part. She looks back at him. Yuka can almost hear his breath come in before he turns toward her. “If something changes in what I'm doing with Makima or what I want, I want you to hear it from me. Before I've sorted it out all the way if that's what it takes.” ᴹ

Shizue keeps an eye on him till he finishes. “I think you do have to tell her before you know what to call it. Yuka had to ask yesterday whether you'd wanted to go. You'd already known someone had asked for a date. It's very easy in that gap for somebody else to explain it to her.” ˢʰⁱ

Yuka puts her cup down hard enough to let them all hear it. She hadn't meant to.

“I thought of him anyway,” she says. “For a second. Before I knew Makima had anything to say to me, he made it sound like any time Mau turned toward her, I ought to ask whether I had a place left. Kind of got used to hearing it in my own voice.” ʸ

Shizue's chair comes forward an inch. “Did you tell Mau?” ˢʰⁱ

“Yesterday.” Yuka waits to see whether she'll be able to say the next part in front of all of them. Mau doesn't put a hand on her while she works it out. “I heard it. And I went to him to ask what he thought.” ʸ

There's a place on the table where the finish has worn away. Makima rubs the tip of her finger against it. She's been waiting for Yuka to finish. She knows how to begin in a way that would make it very easy for Yuka to get up at the end and thank her.

She looks at Yuka first.

“I did want it to be a date,” she says. “I'm in love with your husband.” ᴹᵏ

Mau feels something on the far side of the words, a surge so quick that he has the sense of having thought the same thing at the same time. Then she's looking right at Yuka. He hears what she said again. He doesn't remember reaching for his cup and has to put it down slowly so the others won't hear it shake.

Yuka looks hard at Makima for a breath. Makima lets her. She keeps her hands on the same side of the table while she says the next part.

“And I love you. The first time I could feel how much you mattered, I was feeling how much you mattered to him. When Ori and I go to look for you now, I know what I want to tell you when we find you. Sometimes it doesn't occur to me to check where he is first.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's mouth moves. She gets enough air to ask, “Were you going to tell me both of those things when you came in?” ʸ

“I thought about saying the second one first.” Makima looks down at the little fold of paper. “I knew I'd get a different answer out of you if I did.” ᴹᵏ

She turns the paper so it faces Yuka. She doesn't push it across.

“I've worked out what I'd ask you for, if you'd hear the terms of a contract. While the two of you are married, I won't do anything deliberately to separate you. I want to go on having supper with you. I want to be able to go looking for my friends when I have something I'd like to do with them. I want you to tell me what that can be with you, because I'd like to keep it.” ᴹᵏ

There is enough room in the silence to hear somebody take a plate off the shelf on the other side of the wall. Nobody does anything with the paper.

“A contract with me,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“Would I have to promise for all of them?” ʸ

“No.” Makima looks down again. She runs one finger along the crease she's made. “I know I have to ask them myself. I can live with that.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at Maki first. Maki is sitting very straight, with the teapot close to one knee. She can see something she'd like to ask. When Yuka takes a breath, Maki stays where she is.

“I want to think about it. I want to think about what I'd promise because I actually want to promise it. I don't want you two making plans to be alone together right now. That's still where I am.” Yuka looks at Makima. “Will you come to supper?” ʸ

Makima puts one hand on the folded paper. “Tonight?” ᴹᵏ

“Yes.” ʸ

She has to think about what time it's going to be in the kitchen for a second. The last time she went down hungry, Yuka had put the bread at her end of the table without knowing she'd come in. The chair she'd taken that night will be empty about an hour from now.

“Yes,” she says. “I'd like to.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

When the others have left, Mau is still sitting where Yuka can reach him. Makima has her cup in one hand and the fold of paper in the other. She sees him look at her and then back at his wife. She can feel a whole part of him warming toward her and a whole part coming up against something too large to explain. For once she leaves him time to decide what he wants to say in front of her.

“I felt it before,” Mau says. He looks at the cup Makima's holding and then at her face. “I hear you now.” ᴹ

She nods. She'd wanted him to hear it for a long time. There is more to that feeling than she had thought there would be. She leaves with the paper folded the same way it was when she came in.

After the door closes, Yuka picks her cup up and sets it back down on the spot it left. Mau puts his hand by hers.

“I know what I want when I come back from the lake,” he says. “I want to be here with you. I want to be your husband when I get home. I'd like to say that even if you're still thinking.” ᴹ

Yuka rests one finger against his wrist. “I am still thinking. I still want to see you when you get home.” ʸ

She leans forward far enough to kiss him. It takes a moment for him to close his eyes. She can feel his hand come up, familiar and careful, along the back of her neck. When she draws back he moves with her a little way, until she laughs into his cheek and lets him have another.

At supper, Makima comes late. She'd gone upstairs with the paper and has enough time to put it away before coming down. There's a chair with a cup in front of it in the usual place. One of Yuka's feet is on the rung to keep the chair far enough out for somebody to sit. Makima takes it. Yuka takes her foot off the rung and pushes the bread where they can both reach it.
