# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 86
## Chapter 8 — A Place on the Ridge

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 86
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E155
**Continuity state:** B+507, the day before the lake. Household preparations and a visit to the outer ridge make Mau and Yuka decide where they want to see each other before he goes down. Makima and Yuka talk about the unfinished offer and keep their own plans with Ori. Mau invites Makima to stay at the inner table when they leave tomorrow. No contract is signed. The terms of the fight are still to be established with Yoru at dawn.

*When: B+507. The day before Yoru comes to the lake.*

Yuka gets to Shoko's with the wrong kettle and has to stand there while Shoko explains what the right one looks like from farther away. When Shoko says the word *blue*, Mau leans around the clinic door with the right kettle in his hand.

“I wanted to see how long she'd let you go without saying it,” Shoko tells him. ˢʰ

“Until you asked me why the handle was different.” ᴹ

Yuka takes the kettle out of his hand. “I could have worked the colour out before you did.” ʸ

“You could have worked out I was holding it.” ᴹ

“I did. I wanted you to come closer.” ʸ

He does. She gets a kiss by the cheek, then pulls him around to get the other one where she wants it. When Shoko goes into the little room adjoining the clinic with the kettle, Mau can hear her put it down considerably louder than it would normally go down.

Maki has brought the strips of cloth for the people on the ridge. Half of them are as straight as the edge of the table. The other half are straight enough to go round a sleeve twice. Shoko puts a strip in each of the cups that went into the cart before Mau got there. They can take them out and use the cups when they've got the water through.

“Would you mind if I rode out with you to see the bank?” Yuka asks Mau. ʸ

He'd had it in his head to ask her the same thing. He agrees before Shoko has finished showing them how to make the last lid fit. As soon as he's done it, he realises that this time he doesn't need to think up a different way to ask. When he picks up the other basket, he asks if she'd like him to leave room beside him for the ride back too. She says he's lucky she hasn't made him look for a third place to sit.

* * *

The road goes over a low rise, twists twice and stops at the fence. The farmer has lifted the part of it that moves while they came up the hill. There's enough space for the cart. Fern takes it as far as the high part and gets down to find the old track. Rimuru stays back with the farmer and the reply they promised to bring him. Ori calls from the cart bed to ask which person is going to check her map when she comes down.

Frieren climbs up instead. She puts her hand on the page so Ori can hold the pencil without having to hold the map all the way flat.

Below them the lake is pale and dry. Mau can see the high waterline Yoru described. From up here there's an entire second stretch of ground hidden under the rise on the far side. He knows how long it would take him to get to it if he had to cross the flat first. He looks forward to asking Yoru where she thinks the fight will start. When she brought him the location, she'd had one hand on the latch. She knew what she had brought for him and it looked as though it took everything in her to agree to wait another week to show him.

Yuka gets down off the cart beside him. The ground falls far enough away that she has to take one long step after the little one. He goes with her till he can feel her weight settle on her back foot.

“I saw three places I could stand,” she says. “Do you want to see them?” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She leads him down as far as the bit where the grass turns in the wind and points them out one at a time. The first is too low to see from the lake bed. The second is on the path where Fern has just gone. The third is a shelf with a big flat stone at one end, far enough back that a person coming up the trail will have a whole ridge between them and the flat. Yuka puts her hand on the stone.

“Would you see me from where you're going?” ʸ

“Before I go down, yes. After that I might be looking at the other side.” ᴹ

“I could wave from here before you go.” ʸ

He thinks about how many times he turned his head at the quarry. About what he did with the information when he couldn't find her. From this spot she can see the track as far as the fence if she turns her back on the lake, and there are several other things on this road she might have to do when he goes down.

“I'd like to come here to see you first,” he says. “I don't want to wait till I'm on the other side to look for you. After that, go where Fern or Shoko needs you.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him with half a smile coming up on the side nearest him. He knows the smile he's going to get if he's close enough when it arrives. He takes the other step and kisses her while she's still leaning on the rock.

“What am I supposed to get done if you do that every time you want me to stay somewhere?” she says. ʸ

“Is it working?” ᴹ

“You've got a better chance if you stay another minute.” ʸ

He does. A little way down the path Fern calls that she's found something else the farmer must have moved. Yuka keeps one hand in his until the sound of the cart rises from behind them. Then she goes to see whether Ori needs a way to mark it.

* * *

Makima is at the kitchen window when they come back. She'd promised Ori that this time she'd come soon enough to have a say in how the map gets hung. There's a loose place in the frame where somebody took a pin out with a piece of wood still on it. She holds a hand over it while Ori puts a folded corner of paper under the good side.

“Are you staying for supper?” asks Ori. ᴼ

“I am.” ᴹᵏ

“Good. I want to show you what Mama saw on the road.” ᴼ

Makima has her fingertips against the window frame, where she can hear exactly who has come up the steps behind her. She'd thought she might spend the rest of the day listening for Mau. She wonders if Ori will see the road from this window when she comes down tomorrow. The light on the glass makes it hard to see beyond the trees. Makima holds the map steady while Ori decides by herself that it ought to go a little lower.

Yuka brings two cups out from the kitchen while they're still putting up the last pin. Ori takes hers and goes ahead to find Frieren.

“The paper you brought yesterday,” says Yuka. “Are you keeping it for later?” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“I thought about what I could say yes to. I thought about what you'd get if I did it because I didn't want you to leave.” Yuka holds the cup out to her. “I'd like to talk it through again after the lake. We can still have supper while I think.” ʸ

Makima puts one hand round the cup. She'd planned for an hour at the table yesterday. Then she could have left with an arrangement she could hold up to anybody who asked whether she still had a place here. Today she has a cup already poured and Ori has wanted her to come and look at the map since before she went out. She can see why Yuka wanted an answer she could go on giving.

“All right,” she says. After a second she adds, “I came because Ori asked me to. I was going to come if you didn't bring tea.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her. “You'd have had to get your own. Mine's good.” ʸ

Makima tastes it. It is very good. She follows Yuka into the kitchen before they can both spend another minute being careful about saying so.

* * *

Mau goes looking for Makima after supper. She is still out of doors, carrying the small cups round to the back with the handles balanced across her fingers. He takes the one that is working its way loose first. She gives him the other two after that.

“Do you want to come with me tomorrow?” he asks. “I'd like to find you at the table inside when I leave.” ᴹ

She had made a point of waiting for him to ask this time. She has an answer ready and doesn't need to find another one while she gives it. “I'll be there.” ᴹᵏ

He stands beside her a second longer with the cups. She can feel the point when he'd like to give them back. She's almost ready to reach for them when he puts them on the sill instead.

“Come in before your tea gets cold,” he says. “Ori left you half an apple.” ᴹ

They go in while Yuka is still putting the plates away. Mau finds her where she has to come up on her toes to reach the last one. He takes it from her, slips it in beside the others, and leaves his arm up for long enough that she can come in underneath it. She turns and puts both arms around him. He kisses her on the top of her head before she lifts her face for the rest of it.

Ori sticks her head round the door to say that there's a bite taken out of the half apple and it was like that when she found it. Makima tells her she'll need to show her the bite. The two of them go together to see the evidence.

At the bottom of the stairs Yuka catches Mau by the hem of his shirt. He'd been on his way to see which shoes she'd put out and whether one of them needed to be moved so they wouldn't trip over it in the dark.

“I can get my boots on by myself tomorrow,” she says. “I'd like you to wake me first.” ʸ

He takes her hand off his shirt and keeps it. “Before I put mine on?” ᴹ

“Before you see how far you can get without making a sound.” ʸ

He has time to tell her he was going to get both of them washed and put out by the end of the bed. Instead he goes up behind her to see how she's left them. They're right by his. She'd turned all four toes toward the door. He doesn't have to move a single one.
