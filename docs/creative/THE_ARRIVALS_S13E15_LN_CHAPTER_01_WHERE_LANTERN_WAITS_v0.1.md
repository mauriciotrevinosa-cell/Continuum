# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 4 — Where Lantern Waits

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E15
**Continuity state:** B+292, Lantern accepts a place with Ori and the family ends the birthday together.

*When: Later on Ori's birthday, while the first blanket is still being laid out.*

Mau looks down at the shadow the blanket casts over the grass, then backs them both away so Lantern has room. The dragon rises beyond the blanket, wings folded tight. He lowers his head until Ori can see both lights. One enormous claw settles on the trailing edge of the blanket, holding it still against the breeze.

She says his name before Mau can introduce him. Lantern turns to look at her and holds so still she can see the grass moving on either side of him.

“Last time you told me she named him. You said she saw the lights in his eyes.” ᴼ

“I remember how quickly he looked at her when she said it. He liked hearing it.” ᴹ

Ori says it again. Lantern leans closer. Mau takes his hand off the blanket and lets the two of them look at each other.

“He asked me if he could come to the birthday,” Mau says. “He wants to stay in your shadow sometimes. I asked him to come out so you could talk to him yourself.” ᴹ

Lantern sets his chin on the grass. Ori holds her palm out to him. He brings his nose the rest of the way to it, light enough that she feels his breath before she feels the scales. She studies where he set his claw. There isn't much room between it and the hem she wants to fold back.

“Is he going to go where I go?” ᴼ

“When you both want that. He can come home to me whenever he wants. You can ask him to come out and you can ask him for time by yourself. He's still one of mine. I told him that if he goes out with you he's got to listen to you about what you're doing. And I'd like you to listen to him if he asks to stop somewhere.” ᴹ

Ori peers at Lantern.

“How do I know if he asks me?” ᴼ

Mau waits. Lantern takes a moment, then taps twice on the grass with the side of his foot.

“Like that?” ᴼ

He taps twice more. She rests her palm against his nose another moment, then takes it away.

“Would you stay for lunch? I'm going to be busy picking where we put things. You can tell me if you want a blanket somewhere too.” ᴼ

He lifts his foot off the edge. Instead of leaving, he follows her around the tree and taps the ground at a patch that has a low branch overhead. Ori doesn't think much of it at first. She looks again and sees that somebody reading there can lean back and tuck their feet against the roots. It's a much better place for the books than the patch of sun she was going to pick. She tells him so. They put down the second blanket.

By the time the first tray goes out, the picnic has taken the whole tree. Milim is running around the outside because Ori drew the line of where things should go and she has decided that everybody who can see it will want to race it. Himmel and Frieren sit at the stones, taking turns holding a little pot of water steady. Chloe fits her clue at the fork between the routes. She asks Ori if she wants the ending to be a place or a person. Ori picks a person and makes her clue so difficult that Gojo walks past the answer twice.

The answer is Beru. He is still wearing the paper hat and has started giving people the wrong answer if they guess without reading the clue aloud. When Ori comes up behind him and puts a hand on his arm, he sits down immediately so she can tell him what's funny about the hat. They both forgot to take the price off the paper. The number is upside down over his forehead. He makes a solemn request that she put a second number there and tell people he's been promoted.

She draws one with her finger. He holds the hat out at the right angle for her.

After lunch Lantern follows her as far as the branches allow and settles with his chin close to the ground again. Ori wants to try calling him in front of Mau while she can still ask questions. She turns to see if Mau's looking and finds Frieren beside him, standing so close their sleeves touch. Frieren has a spot of paint on the back of her hand. When Ori catches her eye she turns the hand over to hide it and shows her the clean one instead.

She asks Lantern to go in first. He does, disappearing from the grass until all that's left in front of her is the shape the sun makes of her. Ori calls his name. The grass bends at her toes. He comes out in the clear space, turning his head enough to look down at her with one eye. There are a dozen things she could ask him to do to see if he will. She asks him to come back to the books with her. He follows her to the end of the blanket and settles with his chin right at the edge, where he can see what she's reading.

Her mother brings the painted stone over to put at the edge of the blanket. It has a set of yellow windows on it. Ori thinks she knows which house Frieren meant, but she's going to wait until it's just the two of them to ask.

Yuka comes out after carrying dishes in. She has one plate left and offers Ori the little end piece of cake. Ori takes it. Yuka crouches to get a better look at Lantern, who is nudging a fold in the blanket with his nose so nobody will sit on the pointy stone.

“When do I get a dragon?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Ori takes her time swallowing.

“There are levels.” ᴼ

Yuka is still crouched, so they turn at nearly the same height to see what Mau has to say. He's already halfway to Mai.

“Mai!” he yells. ᴹ

Mai puts her plate down on a bench and moves one step to the left, clearing the whole path between him and Yuka. Ori is up first. Yuka runs just fast enough to catch her at the bend, and together they go after him. Behind them Frieren tells Lantern that he's allowed to go the long way around the path. He tries it. By the time Mau has found out how little Mai intends to help him, Lantern is there with one wing lifted just far enough that Ori can duck under it and surprise Mau from the other side.

That night Ori says goodnight to her mother, to Lantern and to the paper hat, which she puts on the lamp so she can find it again. Before she gets into bed she goes out once more to find Mau. She wants to tell him about the stone while she can still remember the exact color of the windows. He takes his bag off the chair so she can sit down. Then he tells her that Frieren painted the first window over three times before she was satisfied with the light in it. Ori asks what was wrong with the other two, and stays to hear him answer.
