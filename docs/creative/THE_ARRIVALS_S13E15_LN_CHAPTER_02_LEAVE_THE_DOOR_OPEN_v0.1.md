# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 5 — Leave the Door Open

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E15
**Continuity state:** B+293, arrival and first evening on the island.

*When: The morning after Ori's birthday, with six nights booked.*

Yuka packs the wrong shoes on purpose. Mau finds this out because she makes him guess which ones she's got in her hand behind her back. He names the pair she's been wearing around the house all week. Those are on her feet. Then he names the ones from the wedding and she laughs so hard she has to put a hand on his shoulder.

“You want me to spend six days in those?” ʸ

“I remember taking them off you.” ᴹ

“That was your favorite part of the shoes.” ʸ

She reveals the thin sandals she bought on the first trip and complained about all the way home. The buckle sticks. The right sole has come away at one corner. She hands them to him.

“I want to put them on when we get there. Think you can keep them where I can find them?” ʸ

Mau sets the pair away in his inventory by themselves. It takes one second. The second after it, he checks behind her because there's a knock.

Rem has a little paper bag for them from Ori, who asked her to bring it up when she went to get sheets. Inside are two cakes with dots in the icing where somebody tried to draw windows. Rem says they might want to eat the cakes before the sun gets to them. Yuka says they'll make it their first thing at the beach. Then Rem looks at the empty spot on the floor and asks if they're really taking no bags.

“I have three,” Mau says. ᴹ

Rem looks down at his empty hands.

“And he knows where my shoes are,” Yuka says. ʸ

From the stairs they hear Ori ask where her mother put a yellow stone. Rem grins and says she'll see them next week.

They ride Kaisel until they can see the island. DIO watches with Mau on the way over, with long gaps between the things she says. There's enough wind to move Yuka's hair under his chin. He can't see her face clearly without leaning right around her. When she points something out she holds his wrist so she won't have to raise her voice to tell him where to look. Far below, a boat draws a line in the water that shines white and goes out slowly from the far end.

Elmesia meets them on the quiet end of the landing and waits while Yuka sits on a low wall and puts on the bad sandals. Mau has to close the right buckle for her. She puts her weight on it, declares it fine and takes his hand.

“I saved you a spot that gets a breeze,” Elmesia says. “I could give you a tour, but if you want to find your room first, I'll be downstairs for a while.” ᴱˡ

Yuka hesitates, still looking at the water.

“Could we find it on our own?” ʸ

Elmesia gives her the key. She has drawn two small turns on the paper that came with it and marked the route that goes outside. They walk it slow. There's a flight of stairs Mau remembers taking three at a time because they were late to something. Yuka stops on those stairs now and leans on the rail to see what else is below. There's a small pool with most of the people sitting along the far side and a ledge that looks out over the waves. She says, very quietly, that it's even better than the one she asked for.

They find the room. The curtains are pulled in so when Yuka opens the balcony door the wind lifts the bottom edge of one and moves it across her arm. The water is visible from exactly where Elmesia said it would be. Mau puts the bags down. When he turns around Yuka has already taken off the bad sandals and left them where the curtain can brush the tips.

“Leave the door open.” ʸ

He leaves it. They sit on the bed in the clothes they wore to fly. For a while she draws a line in the fabric of his shorts with one finger and tells him how she'd thought that part of the island was shaped when she'd been looking at the map at home. There's a place she wants to swim in the morning if the water's good. If it isn't good she wants to walk to it anyway. Mau puts his hand over hers and asks if she's hungry, and they both remember the cakes.

The one on Yuka's side of the bag has lost a corner. She eats the corner first.

Downstairs, later, Elmesia has ordered a plate of something salted and left a chair pulled out beside her. Mau asks if she has a minute somewhere private. Elmesia takes the plate to an alcove where someone could walk past on the other side of the wall and never see them.

DIO asks to speak for herself. Mau gives her the same offer he gave her in Tempest. He'll stay where she came out, and she'll be able to go back the moment she asks. The protection at home is still a long way away. He shows her the alcove and the way into it before she decides. She comes out beside his chair, waits until she can see the whole little space for herself, then takes the empty one. Elmesia sets a clean plate in front of her with an unhurried hand. DIO puts both elbows on the table.

“I thought I'd look at the far shore while these two are out one day. I'd like a place I can sit down to eat if I get there and decide to stay.” ᴰⁱᵒ

“Would you like me to come?” ᴱˡ

DIO starts smiling before she gives the answer. They have to establish where Mau will be and how much of the shore they can cover in that arrangement. Elmesia listens, asks to see the stretch on the map, and says they can spend the whole afternoon in that part of the island if they like. Then she asks DIO what she wants to eat. The menu they're now discussing is long enough that Mau has time to slide his chair closer to Yuka's, watch her take one of the salted things from his plate, and take a second one for himself before the others notice.

Mau and Yuka leave them talking and go down to the water with the rest of the cakes. Yuka wants to show him a spot she can see from the room. She gets there and finds the tide has covered it. They wait until the pull of the water uncovers just enough sand to stand beside each other, then lose the whole patch and have to move again. She laughs, catches his arm and nearly takes him with her.

They walk back with wet hems. In the room Yuka sits by the balcony while he gets her a towel. The wind is cooler now. She puts both bare feet on his when he comes back and holds him still long enough to take the towel from his hand.

“What are we doing tonight?” he asks. ᴹ

“This until I feel like moving.” ʸ

He stays. She doesn't move for a long time.
