They have lost the cover down by their feet. She eventually considers going after it. He lifts the part he can reach and pulls it up high enough for both of them. He has to turn a little to get the far corner over her elbow. She helps with that end. Then he lets his arm fall across her side.

“I think I got all of you,” he says. ᴹ

She checks. He has. The only cool place left is her hand on his shoulder. She slides it down under the cover and rests it against his chest.

“You did.” ʸ

Mau moves a little to get his knee out from under the fold in the sheet. Yuka lifts the fold for him. Then they both put their feet back down and discover they're on the same cold piece of it. He draws his feet up first. She follows him with hers because she knows now exactly where he's going to try to keep them.

“I got all of you and you put your cold feet on me.” ᴹ

“I had to find you under there.” ʸ

He rolls onto his side so his knees are between hers and the far edge of the bed. She could still get a foot around if she tried. She lets him have the victory for the time it takes him to turn his face toward her. Then she puts her foot against his ankle. He goes very still and looks straight at her. She makes it three breaths before she starts laughing.

He reaches out to get the hair off her face. She holds still until he has the last little bit of it behind her ear. His hand stays there. She sees him look at the place where his thumb comes to rest. She can tell he's about to ask if she likes that. She turns her face into his hand before he has to.

“There's still a bit of chalk on your wrist,” she says. ʸ

He turns his hand over. It's been there since Ori pulled him back to the bench so he could see what she'd written. He wipes at it and she takes his hand before he can make it worse. In this light she can see the little bit of dust in the lines along the side. She thinks of taking him back down to the sink and telling him she found something else that needed washing. She likes being here better. She brushes it away with the edge of her thumb.

“Did you want another kiss?” he asks. ᴹ

“I was going to ask you.” ʸ

“You can still ask.” ᴹ

She asks. This time he lets her come the whole way to him. She gets to feel his hand settle against her shoulder and to hear the little breath he takes when she moves closer. She pulls back to look at him and finds he's doing the same thing. Then she lays her head back on his shoulder, with enough room this time that he can get his arm around her without having to pull the sheet up again.

His breathing takes a while to settle. So does hers. She's half inclined to tell him the thing she keeps setting aside, because the room is quiet and there would be time to tell the whole story from the beginning. She tries the first words in her head. Before they get anywhere, Mau presses his mouth to her forehead and asks if she wants him to put the lamp out.

She looks. The ring dish throws a little shadow in front of the folded list. She knows exactly what she would have said if she'd had the news she wanted to have. She'd even picked a place on the island before she knew whether the stairs she'd drawn on her map were really there.

“Leave it a little longer,” she says. ʸ

He leaves it. She puts her head back down. There's a place at his shoulder where she can hear his voice all the way through him when he talks, and she puts herself right there.

“You know the part at breakfast when Beru came in?” he asks. ᴹ

She nods against him.

“Ori didn't take her hand out of mine until she had to go round the table. I keep thinking about it.” ᴹ

Yuka runs her thumb over the place where his hand was under the ribbon. The line is gone now. She remembers his face in the doorway when Ori told him she was awake. She'd had a hand on Ori's back and felt her take the little step forward the instant he held his arms out.

“You two both get that look when you want a hug,” she says. ʸ

“What look?” ᴹ

She lifts her head, keeps her arms exactly where they are and looks at him. He lasts about a second before he tries to get both arms around her again. She lets him do it.

“That one works pretty well,” he says. ᴹ

She turns her face into his shoulder so he can feel her laugh.

They lie still after that. The house settles around them a room at a time. First the pipe makes a noise in the wall. Then somebody pulls the back door to and has to lift it a little to get the latch in. She knows that one because she's spent an afternoon holding the door for Mai while Mai tried to find which hinge was giving them trouble. Whoever it is gets it shut on the second try. A little later a light moves along the gap under their door, stops and goes on toward the other end of the passage.

Mau says they should sleep. He says it into her hair so softly she thinks he might have said it to himself.

“Are you tired?” she asks. ʸ

“A little. Do you want me to be?” ᴹ

She laughs again. She moves over enough to see his face. He has a crease from the pillow below his ear. She touches it because she's only seen it on him once before, at a time when she couldn't stay long enough to see how quickly it went away. He holds still for her. When she takes her hand off, the little mark is still there.

“I want you to stay awake long enough to help me with something,” she says. ʸ

“The cases?” ᴹ

“Do you see a case anywhere on this bed?” ʸ

He rolls his eyes as far as he can without moving his head. Then he says he sees a person who's going to make him get up after all. She takes his hand and holds it on the pillow between them.

“I want you to show me how you got the water to come up that high that time we woke up.” ʸ

He studies her for a second. Then she realises she's said water because she's been thinking about the beach for most of the evening.

“Us. How you got us that high.” ʸ

“You know how I did it.” ᴹ

“I know you were happy. Show me what you do if you want it to happen on purpose.” ʸ

He looks toward the headboard. She sees him judge how far up it comes. They aren't close enough to the wall to bump into it unless something goes badly wrong, and she knows that he'll want to keep his hand on her whether he needs to or not. She turns on her side so they can stay facing each other.

“How high do you want to go?” he asks. ᴹ

“High enough I can tell we're doing it. Then you can put us down again.” ʸ

“Can I keep the pillow?” ᴹ

“I thought you said you could do this in your sleep.” ʸ

“I could. I'd like a pillow if I'm doing that, too.” ᴹ

She puts one hand on the edge of it to keep it between them. He looks down at where their knees are. After a second he asks her to tell him when she feels the mattress go away. She feels it first at her shoulder, where the weight of his arm stays the same but the pressure from under her eases. There is a breath when it seems like she'll drift away from him. His hand comes around to her back. The pillow rises with them and the loose corner of the cover falls until she catches it on her foot.

“There,” she says. ʸ

He stops. She doesn't feel them go any higher. She pushes a thumb down past the edge of the pillow to see how far it is to the bed. The gap is barely bigger than her hand. It feels much bigger because there isn't anything under her the whole way along.

“I can do this without holding onto you,” he says. ᴹ

“I know. Hold on anyway.” ʸ

He does. She takes her hand off the pillow and puts it between his shoulder blades. It takes a little while to get used to having the room stay where it is while their feet don't. She lets her own weight go, slowly enough that he has time to feel her do it. Nothing moves. There's a strand of her hair falling toward the bed. She can see where it ends.

“Do you feel different when you do it?” she asks. ʸ

“I have to know where the bed is. If I look at you for too long I start to forget how high I meant to stop.” ᴹ

“You could look at the bed.” ʸ

“I could.” ᴹ

He looks at her. She can see it take an effort to keep from smiling. They go up another inch before he brings them back down. Yuka closes her eyes just in time to feel the bed take the weight of her shoulder again. She leaves her hand on him for another breath.

“Think you could do it in the water?” she asks. ʸ

“I think the water would do most of the work.” ᴹ

“Will you let me try lying on you and see if we can both stay up?” ʸ

“Yeah. You'll have to tell me if you get water in your ears.” ᴹ

She considers that. She'll want to be able to hear him, even if neither of them is doing anything that needs talking about. She asks him if he'd be willing to just get in and try without deciding first what they have to get right. He laughs, because she was the one who asked him to try it just now, but he puts his head down where she can reach it and says he'll get in whenever she wants.

She kisses him once for agreeing. He gets her back before she can take her head off the pillow. When they stop she holds the sheet up near his chin and watches him breathe. A minute ago she was ready to tell him to go to sleep. Now she has another question and can tell by the way he's looking back at her that he does too.

“You first,” she says. ʸ

“What if you get there and you want to do something you didn't put on the list?” ᴹ

“I'll tell you.” ʸ

“What if I already got us something to do that morning?” ᴹ

“Then you'll tell me. And we'll see how much we want to do each one.” ʸ

His gaze moves to the dresser. The list is small even for the one they wrote. It only looks long because she left a line between things she was going to come back to and then wrote the rest along the side. She'd pictured him finding out about each thing a little at a time, the way he did when she first asked him to take the walk before breakfast. She hadn't put anything on it about what she hoped to tell him on the stairs.

“What if I want to stay in bed all day?” she asks. ʸ

He thinks about it. She can tell he's thinking seriously because his first reaction was to laugh and he catches it before it gets out.

“I'd go get us breakfast before you made me promise to stay.” ᴹ

“What if I make you promise now?” ʸ

“I'll bring breakfast to bed.” ᴹ

“Good answer.” ʸ

She turns onto her back and pulls his arm with her. He lets it go where she puts it. They look up at the ceiling from the same pillow. Yuka can see the light against the edge of the beam, and past it she can just make out the place they ought to have hung a lamp when they first moved into the room. They set a lamp by the bed instead. It still has the little button that gets warm if she leaves it on for too long.

“Do you remember the first thing we put in here?” she asks. ʸ

“The bed.” ᴹ

“Before that.” ʸ

“You put a cup on the windowsill.” ᴹ

She had. She'd brought water up and left it there in the only flat place near the window. Mau wanted to know whose it was because he didn't want to carry it out with the things somebody had left from working on the room. She told him she wanted to see what the light did through it. He went downstairs and got another one and filled it only halfway up so they could compare.

“It took us a long time to get the rest in,” he says. ᴹ

“You kept changing where you wanted the bed.” ʸ

“The first place was too far from the window. Then we put it where you couldn't get your feet down without hitting something.” ᴹ

“I hit you once.” ʸ

“You did.” ᴹ

She had forgotten that too. He was on the floor with one arm under the bed trying to see why it was rocking. She came down with both feet and got him in the back. He could have got up in an instant. He stayed exactly where he was and asked her to lift the other corner for him while she was there. She had laughed herself right off the bed.

“I thought we were getting better at putting a room together,” she says. ʸ

“We're good at this one.” ᴹ

He says it looking up at the same beam. She turns enough to see his face from the side. There's a moment when she wants to tell him what she'd been thinking about while Ori was by the tree. She gets as far as deciding how she'd have to begin. Then Mau turns and looks at her too. She finds she'd like to know the answer to something she can ask him right now.

“What did you think about when Ori said there were levels?” ʸ

“That you were going to ask for a dragon in front of everybody.” ᴹ

“I already had. I saw you look at me.” ʸ

“I was thinking about what you might do with one. You'd take Ori somewhere on the first day. Then you'd come home to tell me about it and find out you'd left my place on the dragon empty.” ᴹ

She rolls onto her side again. He follows her this time.

“I'd come back to get you,” she says. ʸ

“Where would we go?” ᴹ

She has a dozen answers at first. All the places she can think of have some part they wouldn't see if they went that high. Then she remembers what their own roof looked like the first time they came in low enough to see the windows. She'd spent the last bit of the flight pointing at rooms from the air, trying to find which ones still had people in them. She can think of something she'd like to be able to show him when it was her turn to take them up.

“Over the tree, after dinner. We'd fly as far as we could still see the house, and then you'd tell me when to turn.” ʸ

“I could tell you that now.” ᴹ

“I'd rather you wait till we get there.” ʸ

He nods. It takes him a little while before he says anything else. She puts a finger into the dip at the collar of his shirt and watches it come back out when he swallows.

“I want to be there when you do it,” he says. ᴹ

She has nothing ready to say to that. She moves closer and gets his hand between hers. It's a much easier thing to do than try to tell him what it means to hear that. Tomorrow she'll get on Kaisel and they'll go somewhere they've already been together. Tonight she can lie here and let him tell her how far he thinks they'd get on a flight over the tree before she wanted to come back for something she forgot. She makes him name three things. He knows the first two by heart and gets the third one wrong on purpose.

* * *

Mau thinks she's asleep when he reaches over her for the lamp. She says his name before his fingers get to it. He stops with his arm still over her. She takes a breath and turns until her face is pointing up toward his.

“Can you leave it until I get back?” she asks. ʸ

He brings his hand back. She goes to the bathroom with the cover still warm against her knees when she stands. She takes a while washing her face. There's a loose strand at the back of her hair that she wets and twists around one finger so it will stay out of the way. When she lets it go it sticks exactly where it was. She looks at herself in the glass and does it one more time. At some point she can hear Mau get up.

By the time she's back in the room he's found two glasses. There is water in both of them. He has also got the small case out from where she left it next to the large one. Her hand goes to the little bundle before she thinks about how that looks. He has put the case close to the dresser so they can get to the bed in the dark.

“I left it open. I didn't know how much else you wanted in it.” ᴹ

She closes the flap with one hand. There is still room to get at the bundle from the side if she needs it. She looks back at him. He gives her a glass and goes to his end of the bed, where he can sit without making the mattress tip her way.

“Did you get up because you thought I was going to want water?” ʸ

“I got up because I wanted water. I got two because you were going to ask why I only got one.” ᴹ

She tries it. It's cold enough to hurt her teeth. She puts it down and reaches for his. It tastes exactly the same. He watches her set it back down.

“What was that for?” ᴹ

“I wanted to see if you gave me the colder one.” ʸ

“I didn't know there was a colder one.” ᴹ

“There isn't.” ʸ

He gets a drink from his own glass, then gives it to her so she doesn't have to reach. She drinks again and gives it back. They set both glasses on his side so she can get in without taking one with her. The open case is in the first place she'd look if she got up in the night. She leaves it there.

In bed she lets her eyes rest on the list until Mau asks whether he'd be allowed to cross out the two things he remembers doing. She tells him he can do it in the morning. He wants to know which two she remembers. They each name one of the shirts. Neither of them can remember whether the other one went in or was still on the rug when they went for cake.

Yuka puts her hand out to switch off the lamp herself. It's farther away than she can get without getting out of bed again. Mau puts a hand around her waist to hold her while she reaches. She makes it to the switch. When the light goes out he brings her straight back against him. There's a little while where she can only see the square of the window and the light coming around it. Then she can see his face well enough to know that he's still awake.

“Do you want to know my favorite part of the birthday?” she asks. ʸ

“Was it when I ran to Mai?” ᴹ

“That's the second one.” ʸ

He waits for her to give him the first. It was the moment Ori asked if the other plans could go on the blankets. Yuka had thought she'd pick one thing and everybody else would get their ideas out of the way. Ori looked around at all the people who'd made something for her and found a place for them on the same map. Yuka got to see the moment Milim understood what she meant. She tells Mau about that part and what happened to Milim's face when she saw Chloe start drawing again.

“I think Ori was hoping to get more cake,” he says. ᴹ

“She did get more cake.” ʸ

He laughs very softly. Yuka can feel it where he has his arm around her. She's quiet long enough to hear someone go down the hall again. They walk slower this time. A door shuts and the house is quiet from their end of it all the way to the stairs.

“My favorite part's this,” he says after a while. ᴹ

“This wasn't at the birthday.” ʸ

“I know. It's still today.” ᴹ

Yuka reaches for his hand under the cover. She gets two fingers first. He gives her the rest of it as soon as he can work out where her hand is. She thinks about telling him to go to sleep again, but she likes the way he says her name when he's already half asleep and she moves just enough to hear him do it. She kisses him once more and puts her forehead against his.

The cases can stay open. She'll see what's in them when the sun comes in. When Mau finally goes quiet, she stays close enough to feel the moment he falls asleep.
