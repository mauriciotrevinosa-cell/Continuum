# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 7 — Yuka Takes the First Morning

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E16
**Continuity state:** B+294, island day two, Yuka chooses the walk, snorkeling and lunch.

*When: Before breakfast, on the first full morning on the island.*

Yuka puts a hand on Mau's back before she says his name. He opens one eye and rolls just far enough to see her across his shoulder. There's pale light at the edge of the curtain and a stripe of it across her forehead. Where it touches her he can see the blue.

“I found my shoes,” she says. ʸ

He has to think about where they are before he understands why that's funny. Then he turns all the way over and finds her smiling at him, ready to go. The bad sandals are on her feet. He's glad she didn't try to fasten the right one herself in the dark.

“Give me one minute.” ᴹ

She gives him three. It gives her time to stand on the balcony and see which direction the wind is going. By the time he gets there she has decided she wants to walk along the sand to the place she saw from the air. They'll go before the boats get loud. If they get hungry first, they'll turn back. Mau listens to the entire plan in the doorway, shirt still in his hand, and gets the same small rush of pleasure he gets when she asks him to stay awake a little longer to hear the end of a story. He is coming with her. That's the only part of the plan that needed him and she put it in first.

They get down to the water while the men from the hotel are setting the umbrellas out. The sand is cold enough to wake up the backs of his legs. Yuka follows the wet line and takes his hand when they have to step across a narrow channel. She doesn't let go when they're across it. He can see a set of tracks ahead of them already being filled by water. She takes off the sandals at the next channel.

“Is this the way you thought it went?” he asks. ᴹ

“No. I thought there was a rock halfway along.” ʸ

They find the rock at the far end. It has a shallow place behind it where the water is calm and clear to the bottom. Yuka stands beside it and looks at Mau with the delighted expression of somebody who knew a different thing was true until one second ago and likes being wrong better. There's a man at a little post just opening a box of masks. She wants to find out when he starts letting people out and if they can use that side of the rock.

They have to go back for breakfast after all. It gives them time to ask about the current and get the right masks. At the table Mau sees her eating as fast as she used to when somebody might want the same piece as her. She sees him watching and puts her fork down.

“I've never been able to see all the way to the bottom before getting in.” ʸ

“I wanted to see you see it.” ᴹ

“Then eat.” ʸ

He eats. She gets his last piece of bread while he's still laughing about it.

Yuka pays while he goes to rinse his hands. By the time he gets back she has stopped beside the rack of postcards outside the restaurant. She holds one by the corner so the light won't bounce off it. It's a picture taken from farther up the beach, showing the rock with the sea stretched out behind it. She holds it up to the view, then looks at him instead.

“What do you see when you look past the rock?” ʸ

“The farther water's a lot darker than the bit where we're going in. The shadow where that boat is. I know which blue the sky used to be when it looked like this. I'd have a hard time telling you whether I remembered the water right.” ᴹ

Yuka holds the picture up again. Mau waits. She follows the edge of the rock with her finger without touching the card.

“There it's green. Light enough I can see the sand through it. It goes blue farther out, but it keeps a little of the green where the waves come in.” ʸ

He looks as she talks. The line between the lighter and darker water really is where her finger stops. The man from the mask stand is checking his box again. He's got the lid open just far enough that he can bring a mask out and fit it over his face with one hand.

“Would you buy the card?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Do you want it?” ʸ

“I want to see how you draw it when we're back in the room. So I can tell which part you mean.” ᴹ

She puts it back. She has a pencil upstairs that she was going to use to write the names of things she liked in the margin of the menu. She can do what he wants on the back of the envelope the key came in. On the way to the mask stand she makes him tell her which part he's going to draw himself. He says the piece of the rock that's underwater. She tells him to look at that part on the way in, then, while she's getting used to the mask.

The man at the post shows them where the little drop is. They wade out over the first stretch, close enough that they can still talk. Yuka tries her mask at waist height and comes up with a strip of wet hair stuck to her lip. Mau takes it off for her with one finger. She asks if he can see underwater as well as he does on land. He says yes if he's looking for something. She tells him he can look at what she points to.

The first thing is a fish smaller than her thumb. It turns through a gap in the rock and two more come out of the same gap, so like it that Mau wouldn't have thought to distinguish them if Yuka hadn't stopped him there. They hang over that piece of rock until all three come back out. She makes a noise through the snorkel that he would have known was a laugh even without being able to see her shoulders move.

They find striped fish and a patch of plants that lean in one direction when the water moves. Mau sees something pale pull itself across the sand and gets Yuka by the hand before he can think to point. She sees it, kicks once too hard, and bumps him with her mask. They come up laughing, salt drying in the corners of their mouths. She wants to know where it went. He puts his face under and points with his foot because she hasn't let go of either hand. It takes her another minute to see it. Once she does, she hangs there with him while it crosses the whole stretch of sand on its own time.

On the way in she stops and presses the side of her mask. A little water's got in. He waits in waist-deep water for her to fix it. There's no reason to offer to do it for her while she has both hands on it, so he makes himself stand there and watch the little waves hit his stomach. She gets it to sit right and takes one last look in the shallow water before handing it over. He likes watching that last look. They can come back. She still wants it.

At lunch they get a table where they can see people leaving the masks by the little post. Yuka wants the fish with the sharp sauce they had the first time, and she asks whether they can put a little of it on the potatoes as well. Mau orders the other thing she was thinking about so she can try both. He turns his plate toward her when it comes. She makes a point of giving him the better part of her first bite.

“What do you want to do after this?” he asks. ᴹ

“Sit here till we know which thing we liked best. Then go back to the rock when the light changes. It might look completely different.” ʸ

He turns in his chair to find the rock. He can see the sun on the water in front of it. In the afternoon the whole side of it will be bright.

“Can we get the same table again when we come back?” ᴹ

Yuka puts her fork across the edge of his plate.

“We can come back before we leave this time. Or we can come back after we go home. I keep forgetting both are options.” ʸ

He looks at her for a second, holding his glass just off the table. When they were here on their honeymoon he kept wanting to be able to remember what the light looked like on her face because he didn't know when he'd see it again. He can still remember it. This morning he got to see her open the balcony door and turn toward a different light, and there are five mornings left.

“Then I want to come back after we go home, too.” ᴹ

She hooks her foot around his ankle. It's cold from the water. She leaves it there while she finishes the part of lunch he ordered for her.
