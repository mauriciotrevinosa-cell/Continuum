# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 7 — The Way the Dragon Carries

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E16
**Continuity state:** B+295, island day three, Mau chooses a sunset boat and asks about the giant dragon.

*When: The next afternoon, before Mau's boat leaves the pier.*

Mau chooses the boat that goes the long way around.

There's a smaller one that leaves later and gets back sooner. If the choice were only about seeing the sunset they'd do fine with that one. He watches the two boats for a while and sees that the long one has a flat bench at the stern, wide enough that Yuka could sit sideways with her feet up and talk to him. He asks what time they're allowed to get on.

Yuka finds him at the end of the pier with a receipt in his pocket and a cloth bundle in his lap. She doesn't look to see how much the ride cost. She asks what's in the bundle.

“Something for you if you get hungry halfway around.” ᵐᵃᵘ

“Halfway around what?” ʸᵘᵏᵃ

He points at the boat. She looks at it until it lifts far enough on the waves for her to see the bench. The corner of her mouth goes up.

“You thought of where I was going to sit.” ʸᵘᵏᵃ

“I want to sit there too.” ᵐᵃᵘ

She holds the bundle for him while he gets up. He bought two of the little rolls she'd eaten on the beach, a paper sack of the sharp sweets Elmesia had pointed out yesterday, and a soft fruit he only finds out is in danger when she takes a bite of it while he's still telling her about the boat. He makes her hold the bundle a bit longer so he can steal a bite from the same place.

Elmesia appears on the pier just as Mau is licking juice off his thumb. She has a strap over one shoulder and looks like she's been carrying something heavier than the bag it holds. He steps over to ask if he can help. That's when he sees the shadow on the water.

At first he expects to look up and find cloud. The shape in the water moves against the wind, too fast for that. Elmesia takes another step toward the end of the pier and raises her arm. A dragon circles beyond the boats. There is enough of it to fill the stretch of sky above the last few roofs, and the lift comes in smooth increments through its back before the wings so much as twitch. Mau watches one long pass. Before he can help it he's following the way the load shifts when the creature banks and the pattern that keeps the air from tearing loose around it.

*Oh,* says Noise. ᵗʰᵉ ⁿᵒⁱˢᵉ

Mau knows that sound. He can feel the attention sharpen all the way up into his eyes. He blinks once and the pattern is still there, clearer at the edge this time. The dragon's enormous back seems to tilt half the sky with it.

“Elmesia. My eyes have already started reading your dragon. Noise is doing it too.” ᵐᵃᵘ

She lowers her hand and looks at him with all the interest she'd been showing the dragon a moment ago.

“Can you stop it?” ᵉˡᵐᵉˢⁱᵃ

“I can stop looking. The first part is usually over before I know I'm doing it. I can ask Noise to leave what it saw alone.” ᵐᵃᵘ

The dragon passes the end of the pier. The water lifts against the supports. Yuka has gone still with the bundle in her arms. Mau can't tell whether she's watching Elmesia or the dragon.

“What did it see?” Elmesia asks. ᵉˡᵐᵉˢⁱᵃ

*How to carry something that should be too heavy for its wings,* says Noise. *And the place where it leans into a turn. I would like to look at the other side when it comes back.* ᵗʰᵉ ⁿᵒⁱˢᵉ

Mau gives her the answer. Elmesia looks up at the dragon for a little while before she replies.

“Then there might be more than one someday. You can look again. If you make anything from it, I'd like to see what you make.” ᵉˡᵐᵉˢⁱᵃ

The dragon turns. This time Mau looks on purpose. Yuka takes a little step to the left to get a better angle with him.

“Is this something I could get too?” she asks. ʸᵘᵏᵃ

Mau sees in one instant that she remembers everything Ori said about there being levels. She also sees that he remembers.

“Noise can see if there is a way to fit something like it into Ten Shadows. We know what worked with Agito. I'd want to show you the whole plan before we tried it.” ᵐᵃᵘ

“I'm going to remember you said that in front of Elmesia.” ʸᵘᵏᵃ

“Good,” says Elmesia. “So am I.” ᵉˡᵐᵉˢⁱᵃ

Mau laughs. He asks Noise if it can keep the plan at the stage where they can all still talk about it. Noise wants to know if it can draw something that's allowed to have its wings open. He tells it yes. By then the man from the boat is coming down the gangway to wave them over.

Yuka gets the bench exactly as Mau pictured. She takes one sandal off and tucks the foot under his leg. The boat pulls away a little at a time and he can feel each movement of the wood under his seat. For a while they watch the pier until they can't see Elmesia on it anymore. Then Yuka takes the rolls out and holds them both on her knees so he can choose which one he wants.

They take so long deciding what to talk about that she finally just asks him to say whatever he was thinking about when he picked the boat. He tells her about the bench. She says he already told her that with the way he looked at her feet. So he looks down at the water going under the boat and tells her the other part, that he was thinking of a place they'd have time to see everything go dark all at once.

The sun goes in pieces. First the orange on the deck goes away. Then the dark inside the clouds spreads down to the water. Yuka points out the last bright strip when Mau thinks the light has already gone. He leans closer so he can see what she sees. It's under the lowest cloud and he watches until neither of them can make it out anymore.

She stays tucked against him after the boat turns back. In the dark they can see lights along the shore and he tries to guess their room. He guesses wrong twice. The third time she tells him she can see which one it is because they left the curtain at an angle so it would let light through.

“We can fix it when we get back,” he says. ᵐᵃᵘ

“Leave it,” she says. “I want to find it again tomorrow.” ʸᵘᵏᵃ

He lets her move his hand to where she wants it at her waist. As the pier comes back into sight she stays there and doesn't turn around to look for the step.
