# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 8 — Where She Was Going to Tell Him

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E17
**Continuity state:** B+295, third night, Yuka tells Mau what she had hoped to share.

*When: After the boat comes back, on the third night.*

Yuka waits until Mau has locked the door behind them. He puts the key on the dresser and brings a glass of water out to the balcony. She sits sideways with her knees pulled up so he can get a chair in beside her. There's a low light on the pier where the man from the boat is still winding his rope.

“Do you remember where I stopped in the hall yesterday?” ʸ

“By the turn in the stairs?” ᴹ

She nods. She was going to ask him to stand there with her. From that spot she could have told him she had something to say and still looked out at the water while she said it.

“I thought I'd tell you there.” ʸ

Mau waits for her to say what. She sets the glass on the little table between them, looks down at her hands and then reaches for it again.

“I got my period before we left.” ʸ

He takes a breath. He knows how many mornings there have been since the first time they said they were ready to try. Sometimes he remembers when he's helping her find something in the bedroom. Sometimes he's out on an errand and sees a child try to hold two adults' hands without touching the ground between them. He hadn't realized how long she'd been looking for a place to tell him.

“I had it in my head that I was going to say it to you on the stairs. Even after I knew. I kept thinking about what you might do when you heard it.” ʸ

“Come here a little?” he asks. ᴹ

“Just let me finish. I think I'll lose it if I stop.” ʸ

He leaves his hand on the arm of his chair. If she wants it she can reach it. She looks down at the light on the pier. The rope is off the deck now.

“I know you never said there was a day it had to happen by. If you had, I'd tell you you were being stupid. But I could already see you on that step.” ʸ

“What was I doing?” ᴹ

“You'd ask me again because you wanted to hear it twice. And I'd be so happy I wouldn't even make fun of you for it.” ʸ

He can see himself doing exactly that. For a moment he's surprised by how badly he wants to tell her what he would say, how close he comes to describing it out loud as though they could make it happen just by getting the whole thing right.

“I was hoping it was this time, too,” he says instead. ᴹ

Yuka turns her face toward him. She grips the bottom of her shirt so tight he can see every finger under the fabric.

“Then I feel worse. I feel like I failed you.” ʸ

“What did you think I wanted you to do that you didn't do?” ᴹ

“I know the answer to that. I can tell you what Shoko would say. And then I think that one day you'll get tired of waiting or you'll see how much I want it and get tired of me. It's been in my head since before we got here and I don't know how to get it out.” ʸ

She runs one hand across her face as if that could stop the tears that are already coming. Mau moves his chair close enough for their knees to touch. When he turns his hand over between them, she puts hers in it.

“Tell me that last part again,” he says. ᴹ

“You might get tired of me.” ʸ

“I won't stop loving you if it takes longer. I won't stop loving you for telling me you wanted it to happen already, either. Shoko told me it could take time. I heard her, and I still pictured telling you I'd found a place for a crib.” ᴹ

Yuka closes her eyes. She cries more then, enough that she has to put her glass back down with both hands. He stays beside her without trying to stop it. Somewhere below the balcony there's a door opening and closing while two people say goodnight. The voices go apart. For a while he can hear only Yuka and the water.

“Where did you want to put it?” she asks. ʸ

“By the wall where I keep leaving the chair out. There's room if I move the chair, and we could both get up without hitting it.” ᴹ

“I always run into that chair.” ʸ

“I know. You kick it back under the desk.” ᴹ

Her fingers tighten and then ease on his. She rubs one cheek with her sleeve. He offers her a piece of the towel from the back of the other chair. She takes it and asks if he thinks she wants too many things at once.

“I like when you want things with me. The boat, the breakfast, the room at home that you're thinking about. I want all of that too.” ᴹ

“I want some of it so badly I can already feel what it would be like. I used to think if I could get to the next day that would be enough. Now there are all these days and I get to ask for things. I don't know how to want them a little.” ʸ

Mau remembers seeing her outside the kitchen on the first day she used to go looking for him by herself. She already knew the way and stood there anyway until she saw him turn his head. He hadn't known then how long they could make it last. If she'd asked him what he thought they'd be doing years later he couldn't have come up with this balcony.

“You can want it a lot. You can be sad about it with me when it doesn't happen. And if it takes longer, I still get to wake up with you tomorrow. I was looking forward to that, too.” ᴹ

She lets go of the glass and the towel and comes over. He brings her in against him. His chair has one arm low enough for her to get a knee over and she sits across his lap with her head on his shoulder. She keeps one hand against his chest. He waits until her breathing settles enough to ask if she wants him to bring her inside. She says in a minute, twice, and then doesn't say anything at all.

When she has been asleep long enough that the air is cooling her bare feet, he gets one arm under her legs and moves them to the bed. She stirs at the place where he has to put her down. He gets his arm free without trying to open the hand she has in his shirt.

He leaves the balcony door open a little. There'll be enough light to see the water when she wakes. He goes to sleep with the hand in his shirt still closed.
