# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 10 — Nothing on the Menu

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E17
**Continuity state:** B+296, island day four, their quiet morning and carajillo afternoon.

*When: The next morning, with nowhere either of them has to be.*

Mau wakes because he feels Yuka pulling the sheet higher over them both. The sun has already crossed the room. It stops at her elbow. She gets the sheet to her chin and gives him a look that tells him she has every intention of going back to sleep.

“Do you want breakfast?” he asks. ᴹ

“Yes. I don't want to get up.” ʸ

He closes his eyes again and finds her hand with his own. They stay like that until he can hear someone outside pushing a cart with one stubborn wheel. Then she puts her mouth to his arm and gives him a kiss for no reason he can see.

“How are you?” he asks. ᴹ

“Better. It comes back for a minute and then it goes again.” ʸ

“Do you want me to do anything when it comes back?” ᴹ

“Let me take your hand like this.” ʸ

She does. After a while he shifts enough to see the water through the open door. There's no wind yet and the bright part of the sea looks like it would feel warm to touch.

“We could do exactly this by the little pool. There are beds with curtains. We could lie down again and ask somebody to bring us food.” ᴹ

Yuka sits up. The sheet falls to her waist.

“Eggs on the toast with the sour butter, potatoes on the side. The red sauce, a little dish of it. Two juices with ice, one of them the orange thing you got the first day. I want the coffee when we get there, and then the eggs.” ʸ

“You memorized the menu.” ᴹ

“Put your shirt on before I remember the page with the cakes.” ʸ

He starts to get up and she catches him around the middle from behind. For a second he thinks she's changed her mind. She kisses the back of his shoulder and then lets him go. By the time he gets his shirt over his head she's out of bed and looking for the good sandals, the ones she admits are better when they have more than one flight of stairs to get down.

The little pool is around a wall with leaves coming down over it. It is set aside for adults, and at this hour most of the people who used it yesterday are somewhere else. There's no music close to the water. When Mau goes past the first daybed he can hear the hollow sound of his own foot on the deck. Yuka chooses one with its curtains tied back on the left. From that side she can see the sea if she turns her head. She gets up on her knees to reach the tie on the right and leaves that curtain half closed to keep the sun out of her eyes.

The person with the order pad comes over before Mau has finished getting their shoes off the deck. Yuka remembers the whole order, adds a little plate for the fruit, and watches him sit down.

“What are you getting?” she asks. ʸ

“You said two juices. I thought that meant I'd get some of the rest.” ᴹ

She takes his hand and turns it over, as if she's trying to read how he arrived at that idea.

“Get the sandwich with the fried potatoes.” ʸ

He orders it. When it comes she gives him one corner of her toast and takes a potato. He waits until she has opened the book she brought and takes back a different one. They are in no hurry to finish. They manage a stretch of conversation about a chair Yuka wants to move back under a certain desk, get as far as picking a corner for it, then both forget to decide anything else because the sun's made its way around the curtain. Mau gets up to pull it over. Yuka lifts her foot and holds the edge of his shorts in place with two toes until he comes back.

They get into the pool sometime after the cups are empty. The water is warm on top and cold near his feet. Yuka makes her way down the steps and straight to him, having decided he can take the first shock of the cold for both of them. He lets her hold on and eases them away from the wall. Once they're in the open he settles himself flat on his back. Authority keeps him just high enough that a wave doesn't go right over his mouth. Yuka watches him do it. Then she rolls over and puts her head on his stomach.

“Can I stay here?” ʸ

He adjusts until the water moves around her ears without getting in. He can see one of her feet just below the surface. It drifts out when she relaxes her knee. He holds them both for a little while, following the squares on the bottom whenever he needs a fixed point. Yuka's fingers rest on his side. She doesn't ask to be anywhere else.

His stomach growls directly under her head.

“Are you hungry?” she asks. ʸ

“I was thinking about it. Things keep disappearing off my plate.” ᴹ

“It could be the bird.” ʸ

He laughs hard enough that her head lifts. Water comes over the side of his mouth and he has to put his feet down. She turns over with him, still laughing, and they take a minute to find the steps. At the top she puts a towel around both of them and walks sideways all the way to the daybed so he can't get out of it.

Late in the afternoon somebody brings a drinks card. Yuka asks about the cold one with lime. Mau asks what a carajillo is and ends up with a short glass of coffee and liqueur over ice. The first sip makes him stop with the glass right in front of his face. He takes a second, gives it a chance to be less good than he thought it was, and puts the glass down with tremendous care.

“Oh, that's dangerous.” ᴹ

Yuka reaches for it.

“I want to know why.” ʸ

He gets the lime drink in return. It's just sweet enough to let the sharp part stay. When he hands it back she says she wants another sip of his later, after the ice has melted a little. He slides it to the middle of the small table where either of them can get to it. They take the whole length of the afternoon over the two drinks, with a glass of water each and a plate that gets lighter from both sides.

At some point Yuka puts her book down and takes his hand. She has the same expression she had when she said it would come back for a minute. He stays still. She holds it until she looks up again, and then asks which of the things on the plate was his favorite. He points to the last one. She breaks it in half and eats the smaller piece.

By the time they stand up, the curtains have gone still. Mau helps her find the other sandal, and she asks if they can go to dinner exactly as they are. He's still damp. She has the soft crease from the daybed on her cheek. He takes one look at her and says yes.
