# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 14 — Two Bowls

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S13E19
**Continuity state:** B+298, island day six, the bird accepts an offer and then reconsiders.

*When: The last full day on the island, at the table closest to the little pool.*

The bird makes Mau wait almost the entire morning.

He's already been down to the water with Yuka. They've already had breakfast once. They have moved to the table closest to the little pool because that's where she saw the bird go after it stole from him last time. He has checked three birds from far enough away to give them a head start. None of them has looked at the table quite the same way. He asks for a small bowl of cut fruit and gets their own plate first. Yuka sits with her foot hooked around his ankle and asks him how long he intends to give it.

“However long we're sitting here.” ᴹ

“You can eat while you're waiting. It might recognize you better if there's fruit in your hand.” ʸ

He picks one off the plate. It's the nicest part of the whole thing, soft through the middle and firm just under the skin. He takes a bite, leaving enough to hold. Yuka leans her elbows on the table. She watches the rail because she's enjoying herself now, too.

It comes in low from the other side. They both see the shadow move across the table. Mau puts his hand down so fast he almost loses his piece of fruit on his own. The bird settles with a flap on the rail. It looks past the plate and straight at his hand. Yuka grips his ankle with both feet.

“That's it,” she says. ʸ

He puts the fruit on the plate, palm up on the table. The bird takes three steps along the rail. He has time to think about how much room it will need under its wings. When it tries to fly, its feet land softly on the rail again. The bird takes two steps the other way. Mau changes the edge of the spell as Frieren taught him. The bird stops walking and looks at him over one wing.

“Could you move the bowl a little closer?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Yuka goes to pick it up and sees that her hand is empty. The extra bowl still hasn't come. For a second they're all looking at the plate. Yuka sets one piece aside on the edge of her saucer and makes no move to eat it. The person with the order pad appears at the right moment, sets a small bowl down and pauses to look at the rail.

“That's a big one,” he says. ˢᵛ

“It comes here a lot,” Yuka says. ʸ

The man has apparently heard worse things about the birds. He slides the bowl over, takes their empty glasses and walks off without asking why this one is still there. Yuka carries the bowl over to the rail, where the bird looks at it from one side and then the other. It can see what's in it. She sets it down on the broad flat post. Mau opens his fingers.

“Yours,” he tells the bird, pointing at the bowl. Then he points at his plate. “Mine.” ᴹ

The bird tries the bowl. He gets to see it lift a pale wedge, drop it and pick up a smaller piece. It eats. His own plate is as full as it was when he put his piece down. He gives Yuka a slow look from the side. She nods. She is trying very hard to be serious about how well he did.

The bird takes off from the post and lands on the edge of the table. Before Mau has moved his eyes, the piece he was eating has gone. It had been right beside his hand. The bird takes it across the pool and over the roof. Yuka doubles over in the chair. Mau is left pointing at the place where the fruit was.

“It already had some,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka gets one hand free from around her stomach and uses it to point at the bowl.

“You gave it a choice.” ʸ

“I meant a choice of the things in the bowl.” ᴹ

Yuka asks if he wants her to go and get half a pastry for reparations. He has to think for a second before he remembers the last half a pastry, and how quickly she got the bigger piece back from him.

“Which half?” he asks. ᴹ

She can't say it with a straight face. That gets him laughing before he even hears the answer.

She gets upright enough to take a drink and has to put the glass back down immediately. Mau looks back toward the roof. He can just see the tail over the edge. He's quite sure if he goes to look for another piece of fruit he'll be giving the bird exactly the afternoon it wanted.

When he turns back, Yuka is eating off his plate.

“Did you have to take the other ripe one?” he asks. ᴹ

“I waited until you were looking right at me.” ʸ

He puts his head down on his forearm. She leaves him that way just long enough to eat the piece. Then she reaches over and finds his hand and lets him laugh into his sleeve.

The extra bowl stays on the post as they finish. The bird comes back to it after they've stopped watching. Yuka sees it first this time and points with the corner of her glass so he won't have to move. It tips its head and eats the piece it dropped. They can see its throat work as it swallows. Mau can already imagine going home and trying to tell Frieren which part of the day she ought to be proud of.

“Want to come back to the same table next time?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Yes. I'd like to see you order the sandwich again.” ᴹ

She puts one arm on the back of his chair and leans into him. A little later she turns her face so he can kiss her in the shade of the umbrella. When the bird flies away for the last time that day, neither one of them looks up.

They spend the afternoon going back to two places they'd liked and leaving one unopened bag of sweets for the flight home. Elmesia finds them that evening beside the stairs. She wants the whole story. Mau gets as far as the first bowl. Yuka takes his elbow and gives Elmesia the end of it because she's already laughing too hard to wait for him to get there.

“Your invitation stands for as many visits as you want,” Elmesia says. “Both of you have to tell me when you're coming.” ᴱˡ

Yuka thanks her for the room and for the table. Then she asks whether she and DIO have plans to do anything with the last evening. They do. They found a stretch of shore where the lights from two buildings line up on the water. Elmesia wants to show her what it looks like from closer in. Mau offers to take a walk with Yuka along that part of the shore, close enough for DIO to go ahead with Elmesia. Elmesia says they'll take their time so the two of them can take theirs.

The four leave together. At the first turn Yuka hears Elmesia ask DIO which light she meant yesterday. She listens for the answer, then drifts farther from them with Mau. They can see both lights. There is a long space between them to walk through before they have to start walking back.
