# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 15 — A Few More Nights

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S13E19
**Continuity state:** B+299, morning of the planned departure, island day seven. Elmesia sees them at breakfast and asks them to stay longer; they accept for a few more nights. A letter goes home with the hotel's morning post, no new route or power. No danger at home. Mau forgets he already packed Yuka's sandals and she pretends to be offended.

*When: The morning they are supposed to leave, while the first boats are still being tied up at the pier.*

Mau has packed everything by the time Yuka wakes up.

He did it quietly. That was the whole point. She was asleep across most of the bed with one arm thrown over his pillow, and he wanted her to wake up to a room that didn't need anything from her. The bags are in his inventory. The towels are folded on the chair. The bad sandals are put away with the rest of the shoes.

Yuka sits up, looks around the room, and then looks at him.

“Where are my sandals?” ʸ

“Packed.” ᴹ

“I wanted to wear them to breakfast.” ʸ

He opens his mouth. Closes it.

She had told him that. Last night, walking back along the shore between the two lights, she had said she wanted to wear the bad sandals one last time down the steps, so that they could say goodbye to the stairs properly. He remembers her saying it. He remembers saying yes.

“I forgot,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka lies back down, slowly, and pulls the sheet up to her chin.

“Then I can't go to breakfast.” ʸ

“I can get them out.” ᴹ

“It's too late. The moment's gone. I'll just stay here.” ʸ

“Yuka.” ᴹ

“Tell Elmesia I said goodbye.” ʸ

She closes her eyes. Her mouth is doing something at the corner that she is trying very hard to stop.

Mau takes the sandals out of the inventory and kneels at the side of the bed with them held out on both palms, like something being presented at a ceremony.

“For the lady,” he says. ᴹ

She opens one eye.

“Put them on me.” ʸ

He does. The right buckle sticks, the way it has stuck all week. He gets it the second time. When he looks up she's watching him with her chin on her knee, and the thing at the corner of her mouth has won.

“You're forgiven,” she says. ʸ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“For now.” ʸ

* * *

Elmesia is at the table by the window when they come down.

She has a cup in one hand and the morning post in the other, and she puts both down when she sees them. She watches them cross the room. Yuka has her hand in the crook of Mau's arm and is telling him something about the stairs, and he is bent a little to hear it, and neither of them is looking where they're going.

Elmesia waits until they reach the table.

“Sit,” she says. ᴱˡ

They sit. A server brings coffee before anyone asks. Yuka orders the eggs with the sour butter without looking at the card.

“You're leaving this morning,” Elmesia says. ᴱˡ

“That was the plan,” Mau says. ᴹ

Elmesia looks at them for a moment longer. At Yuka's foot, which has found its way round Mau's ankle under the table. At Mau's hand, which is resting on the back of Yuka's chair as though it has nowhere else it would rather be.

“I have run this hotel for a long time,” she says. “I have had a great many people stay in it. Kings. Generals. People who came here because they had won something, and people who came here because they had lost everything else.” ᴱˡ

She picks up her cup.

“I don't think I've ever had anyone as happy as the two of you are.” ᴱˡ

Yuka goes pink. Mau can see it, the color rising up her neck, the one thing in the room that has any.

“Stay,” Elmesia says. “A few more nights. The room is yours. Nobody is waiting for it.” ᴱˡ

* * *

Yuka looks at Mau.

Mau looks at Yuka.

It is a whole conversation. He has learned to read her face well enough by now that he can see the yes in it before she has decided to let it out, and the little worry behind the yes, the one that has a name and a map with a cross on it and is probably at that exact moment asking Rem whether she can have the last bun.

“Ori,” Yuka says. ʸ

“We can write,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Will she mind?” ʸ

Mau thinks about Ori. About the map she drew of the island before they left, with a cross on it that said *bring me something from here*. About the way she said *you came back before lunch* the last time they went somewhere without her, as though it was the best possible thing that could have happened.

“She'll mind a little,” he says. “And then she'll want a shell.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs.

“The post goes out with the morning boat,” Elmesia says. “It'll be at your house by tonight.” ᴱˡ

Yuka looks at Mau one more time.

“A few more nights,” she says. ʸ

“A few more nights,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

Elmesia smiles into her cup, the satisfied smile of someone who knew how this was going to end before she asked.

* * *

They write the letter together, after breakfast, at the little table on the balcony.

Yuka holds the pen. Mau holds the paper flat against the wind with two fingers. They argue gently over the first line. He wants to start with *we're fine*. She says that's exactly how he'd start a letter if they weren't fine, and the whole house would know it, and Shoko would be on Kaisel by noon.

“What do you want to start with?” he asks. ᴹ

“The truth.” ʸ

She writes it:

*We are having such a nice time that Elmesia asked us to stay a few more nights, and we said yes.*

Underneath, in smaller letters, for Ori:

*We'll bring you a shell. A good one. Don't let Beru wear the hat to bed.*

Mau takes the pen long enough to add a line for Frieren.

*The spell worked. The bird came back.*

Yuka looks at him.

“You're leaving out the part she'll ask about first,” she says. ʸ

“She can ask me when I get home.” ᴹ

He gives her the pen back and reads the whole letter over her shoulder.

“Should we say how many nights?” ᴹ

Yuka thinks about it.

“No,” she says. ʸ

“Why not?” ᴹ

“Because I don't know yet.” ʸ

She folds the letter and puts it in his hand, and then she doesn't let go of his hand when he takes it. He looks at her.

“I don't want to know yet,” she says. “Is that all right?” ʸ

He turns his hand over under hers.

“That's all right,” he says. ᴹ

The wind catches the fold just as he takes the letter. Yuka catches it against his chest with both hands. Mau puts a hand over hers and gets as far as looking for something to weigh the paper down before he realizes she's leaning in.

He meets her halfway. The first kiss is quick because the corner of the letter comes up between them and touches his chin. Yuka slips the folded sheet into his shirt pocket and smooths it down. Her hand stays there.

“Now you can pay attention,” she says. ʸ

He touches the side of her face and kisses her again. The wind is cold on the back of his hand. It's warm where she leans into his palm. He keeps his eyes closed a little past the moment she draws back, and when he opens them she's still close enough that he can feel her laugh.

“You know you can go back to bed after breakfast?” he says. ᴹ

“I'm aware. I was going to ask you to come with me.” ʸ

He looks down at the pier. The boatman is still carrying a crate across the gangplank. There's enough time for Mau to take the letter down without running. He stays where he is for one more kiss.

* * *

He takes the letter down to the pier himself, stopping at the desk for a sleeve with the hotel's seal.

The boatman sees the seal and tucks the letter into the morning post. He promises it will be at the house by evening. Mau stands on the pier for a while after the boat goes, watching it get smaller.

When he turns round, Yuka is at the top of the steps.

She is wearing the bad sandals. She has come all the way down from the room in them, the right buckle almost certainly undone again, so that she could stand at the top of the steps to the pier and wait for him.

“You said goodbye to the stairs,” he says, when he reaches her. ᴹ

“I said hello to them again.” ʸ

She holds out her foot. The buckle is undone.

He kneels and does it up.
