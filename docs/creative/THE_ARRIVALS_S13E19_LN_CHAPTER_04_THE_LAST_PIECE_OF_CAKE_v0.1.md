# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 16 — The Last Piece of Cake

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S13E19
**Continuity state:** B+300, island day eight. A day of doing nothing at the little pool. Yuka sits on Mau's lap while he reads and falls asleep on his shoulder on the daybed. He eats the cake she saved for later; she plays offended and the bird finishes the second one. No danger, no new topic.

*When: The first morning of the nights they didn't plan, with nowhere on the island they have to be.*

The first thing Yuka says when she wakes up is:

“We don't have to do anything today.” ʸ

She says it the way someone else might say *it's my birthday*. As though it's a present she has found at the foot of the bed.

“Nothing at all?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Nothing. Not even the rock.” ʸ

“What about breakfast?” ᴹ

She considers it.

“Breakfast isn't doing anything. Breakfast is breakfast.” ʸ

He laughs. She rolls over and puts her face into his shoulder and stays there, warm and heavy, until somebody's cart with the stubborn wheel goes past outside and she decides that's a sign.

* * *

They take the daybed by the little pool again. The same one, with the curtain tied back on the left so she can see the sea if she turns her head.

Mau brings a book. He found it on the shelf in the lobby the evening before, a thick one about the islands with pictures he can't see the colors of and long paragraphs about which fish live where. He thought he might learn the name of the pale thing that crossed the sand.

He gets three pages in.

Then Yuka, who has been lying beside him with her eyes closed and her feet in the sun, sits up, looks at him for a moment, and climbs into his lap.

She does it without asking, the way she does at home. She sits sideways across his legs with her back against his chest and her head tucked under his chin, and settles there like a cat that has found the warm spot on the floor. The book is now on the other side of her.

“I was reading,” Mau says. ᴹ

“You can read.” ʸ

“The book is over there.” ᴹ

She reaches out, picks it up off the daybed, and holds it open in front of both of them. Upside down.

“There,” she says. ʸ

He turns it the right way up, very gently, with one finger. She lets him. Then she leans her head back against his shoulder and looks at the pictures with him.

“What's that one?” she asks, pointing. ʸ

He reads her the caption. It's a fish that changes color when it's frightened. She asks what color. He tells her the book says it goes from blue to almost white.

“Like your hair,” she says. ʸ

“My hair isn't blue.” ᴹ

“Almost white,” she says. “When you're frightened.” ʸ

He doesn't know what to say to that. She doesn't seem to need him to say anything. She turns the page herself and asks about the next one.

* * *

At some point she falls asleep.

He doesn't notice the exact moment. One minute she is asking him to read the bit about the turtles again, and the next her hand has gone loose on the edge of the book and her breathing has slowed against his chest.

He closes the book and puts it down very slowly.

She doesn't wake.

He adjusts, carefully, until he is lying back against the cushions with her still on top of him, her head on his shoulder and one arm across his chest. The sun has moved around the curtain. It is falling across her legs now. He reaches up and pulls the curtain over, one-handed, and the light goes soft.

She makes a small sound in her sleep. Then she turns her face into his neck and holds on.

He stays very still.

He has slept beside her a hundred times now. He has woken up to her in the clinic, in their room, in this hotel. He still doesn't have a word for what it is when she falls asleep on him like this, as though there is nothing in the world to keep watch for, as though he is the safest place she knows.

He doesn't need a word. He lies on the daybed with the curtain moving in the breeze and her weight on his chest, and listens to her breathe, and lets the morning go by.

* * *

She sleeps for an hour.

Somewhere in the middle of it the server comes round. Mau orders very quietly, with his free hand. A coffee for himself. A slice of the cake with the soft layers that Yuka liked on the fourth day, which she said, the night before, she wanted to have *later*, as though later were a place she had planned to visit.

The cake comes on a little plate with a fork.

It sits on the low table beside the daybed.

He looks at it for a long time.

Yuka is asleep. She will be asleep for a while yet. The cake is there, and it smells very good, and he is hungry, and it is, after all, cake. He can order another one. He can order another one before she wakes up.

He eats one bite.

Then he eats the rest of it.

* * *

Yuka wakes up a little while later and the first thing she sees, before she sees him, is the empty plate.

She lifts her head off his shoulder. She looks at the plate. She looks at the fork, which still has a little of the soft layer on it. She looks at Mau.

“Was that my cake?” ʸ

“It was a cake.” ᴹ

“Was it the cake I said I wanted later?” ʸ

“It is later,” Mau says. “Technically.” ᴹ

Yuka sits up. She is still sitting on him. She folds her arms.

“You ate my cake while I was asleep on you.” ʸ

“I was going to order another one.” ᴹ

“When?” ʸ

“Before you woke up.” ᴹ

“I'm awake.” ʸ

He has no answer to that.

She looks at him very seriously for a long moment. Then she lies back down on top of him, all at once, with her full weight, and pins him to the cushions.

“Now you can't order anything,” she says into his chest. ʸ

“Yuka.” ᴹ

“You'll just have to lie here and think about what you did.” ʸ

He laughs so hard she bounces.

* * *

He orders two more slices in the end.

He has to do it lying down, with her still on top of him, calling to the server over her shoulder while she refuses to move. The server is very professional about it. He brings two plates and puts them on the low table and goes away without looking at either of them.

Yuka sits up and takes one plate in both hands.

“This one's mine,” she says. ʸ

“And the other one?” ᴹ

“Also mine. As an apology.” ʸ

“That seems—” ᴹ

“Fair,” she says. “It seems fair.” ʸ

He lets her have it. He watches her eat the first slice slowly, with great ceremony, making sure he can see every bite. Then she puts the empty plate down and reaches for the second.

It isn't there.

They both look at the low table.

On the rail of the pool, a few steps away, the bird is standing with half a slice of soft-layered cake in its beak. It looks at them. It doesn't look sorry.

Then it flies away over the roof.

There is a long silence.

“That,” Mau says, “was not me.” ᴹ

Yuka puts her face in her hands and starts to laugh, and keeps laughing, until she has to lie back down on top of him again because she can't sit up any more.

She gets a hand on his shoulder and pushes herself up just enough to kiss him. She's still laughing when their mouths meet. He catches the next laugh against his lips and loses his own breath with it. Her knee slips in the gap between the cushions. Mau slides a hand behind her back to steady her and she uses the help to stay exactly where she is.

“You taste like my cake,” she says. ʸ

“So do you.” ᴹ

She looks at him as if she's about to object. Then her thumb brushes the corner of his mouth and she gives him a slower kiss. This time they get all the way through it before she starts laughing again.

“You're still ordering me another one,” she says. ʸ

“I think we should eat that one inside.” ᴹ

Yuka turns her head toward the roof. Mau feels the movement all the way down the cushion where she's still lying on him.

“I'll order yours while he's looking the other way,” he says. ᴹ

“We can share it,” she says. “If you're awake.” ʸ

* * *

In the afternoon they get into the pool.

They don't do anything in it. They stand in the shallow end with the water up to their chests and lean on the edge and look at the sea. Yuka puts her arms round his neck from behind and hangs off his back with her feet off the bottom, and he walks her up and down the length of the shallow end like that while she tells him where to go.

“Left,” she says. ʸ

“There's a wall on the left.” ᴹ

“Then right.” ʸ

He goes right. She tightens her arms and puts her chin on his shoulder.

“I like this,” she says. ʸ

“The pool?” ᴹ

“Not doing anything.” ʸ

He thinks about all the days in his life when there was something to do. Something to fix, something to learn, something coming. He can't think of many days, before her, that were like this one.

“Me too,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

That evening they eat on the balcony. Room service. Neither of them wants to get dressed for the restaurant, and Elmesia's card covers it, and Yuka says that if she has to put on shoes again today she will go back to the cake argument and win it properly this time.

They eat with their feet up on the railing. The sea goes dark below them. Mau can hear it more than see it, the long pull and push of it on the sand, steady as breathing.

Yuka puts her plate down and climbs into his lap again.

He doesn't say anything this time. He just moves the plate out of the way and puts his arms round her.

She settles against him and watches the dark water, and after a while she says, very quietly:

“I'm glad we stayed.” ʸ

He puts his chin on the top of her head.

“Me too,” he says. ᴹ
