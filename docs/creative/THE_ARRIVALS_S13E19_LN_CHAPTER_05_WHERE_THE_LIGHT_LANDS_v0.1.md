# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 17 — Where the Light Lands

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E19
**Continuity state:** B+301, island day nine. Instead of the hotel's evening, Mau takes Yuka out on Kaisel to a high empty headland on the far side of the island for the sunset. He cannot see the sunset's colors; he sees them only where the light falls on her, the one thing in color for him. Return late to the room. Mau forgets the blanket; Yuka teases him. No danger, no new technique.

*When: The hotel's card for the evening offers a lantern walk by the pier, and Mau puts it face down on the table before Yuka can read it.*

“What was that?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Nothing.” ᴹ

“You turned it over.” ʸ

“It was the lantern walk.” ᴹ

“I like lanterns.” ʸ

“I have a better idea.” ᴹ

She looks at him over the rim of her cup. He can see her deciding whether to make him tell her or let it be a surprise. She chooses the surprise, which he knew she would, because she has never once in two years been able to resist one.

“When?” she says. ʸ

“Before the sun goes down.” ᴹ

“Do I need shoes?” ʸ

He thinks about it.

“Yes. Not the bad ones.” ᴹ

She looks so betrayed that he has to kiss the back of her hand before she'll finish her breakfast.

* * *

He spends the afternoon asking questions.

He asks the man at the mask stand, who has lived on the island all his life, where a person would go to see the whole sky with nobody else in it. He asks Elmesia, who smiles and draws him a line on a scrap of paper to the far side of the island, past the last village, to a headland that the map calls by a name he can't pronounce. Nobody goes there, she says. There's no road. There's nothing there except grass and the edge of the cliff and the sea going all the way out.

“How do people get there?” he asks. ᴹ

“They don't,” Elmesia says. “That's why it's empty.” ᴱˡ

She looks at him.

“Your dragon could,” she says. ᴱˡ

* * *

He calls Kaisel from the empty end of the beach, where nobody will mind the wind.

Yuka is waiting on the sand with her good shoes on and a cloth bag over her shoulder that she has packed herself and won't let him look in. When Kaisel comes up out of the dark and puts his great shoulder down for them, she looks at Mau with her eyebrows up.

“We're flying?” ʸ

“We're flying.” ᴹ

“To where?” ʸ

“You'll see,” he says, and then, because he can't help it: “Well. You'll see. I'll mostly see you.” ᴹ

She doesn't understand that yet. She climbs up in front of him anyway, and settles back against his chest, and takes hold of his wrist the way she does when she wants to be able to point at something without letting go.

Kaisel lifts.

* * *

The island goes past underneath them.

The hotel first, its little pools catching the late light. The pier, the boats. The village with its low roofs and the stall by the water that Elmesia said to go to hungry. Then hills, soft and folded, with sheep on them that scatter when Kaisel's shadow passes over. Then nothing: grass, and rock, and the edge of the land coming up fast, and the sea beyond it going out and out.

Mau brings Kaisel down on the headland, a long way back from the edge.

It's exactly what Elmesia said. Grass, bent all one way by the wind. The cliff. The sea. Not a building or a boat or a person anywhere, all the way to where the sky comes down to meet the water.

Yuka slides off Kaisel and stands in the grass and turns all the way round, slowly, once.

“Oh,” she says. ʸ

That's all. Just *oh*. He has heard her say a great many things about a great many views, and he has never heard her say only *oh*.

He lets Kaisel go back into the dark. The wind fills the space where he was.

* * *

The bag has a blanket in it.

Mau finds this out because he goes to spread out the blanket he brought and discovers he didn't bring one. He was so busy thinking about Kaisel and the headland and the map Elmesia drew that he forgot the blanket entirely. It's still on the chair in their room, folded, where he put it so he wouldn't forget it.

Yuka watches him look at his empty hands.

“You forgot the blanket,” she says. ʸ

“I didn't forget it. I left it in a safe place.” ᴹ

“In the room.” ʸ

“Where it's very safe.” ᴹ

She takes a blanket out of her own bag and shakes it out in the wind, and it billows up between them before she gets it down on the grass.

“I knew you'd forget,” she says. ʸ

“How did you know?” ᴹ

“Because you planned everything else so carefully. You always forget the thing next to the thing you were thinking about.” ʸ

He sits down on her blanket. She sits down beside him, close enough that her shoulder is against his, and takes two of the little rolls out of the bag, and a bottle of something, and the bag of sharp sweets they didn't finish on the boat.

“You packed dinner,” he says. ᴹ

“I packed a picnic. You packed a dragon.” ʸ

“That's quite a lot to pack.” ᴹ

“It is,” she agrees, and puts a roll in his hand. “That's why I did the rest.” ʸ

* * *

The sun goes down.

He watches it the way he watches everything now: in grey. The sky is a great pale field going darker at the top. The sea is a darker grey below it, with a line of brightness across it that runs from the horizon almost to the foot of the cliff. The sun is a white disc going down into the water, slowly, the edge of it flattening as it touches.

He knows, somewhere, that it must be a color. That everybody else who has ever watched a sun go down over a sea like this has seen something he can't. He has heard people describe it. He has a memory, from a long time ago and a long way off, of what that kind of light looked like.

He doesn't look at the sun for long.

He looks at Yuka.

* * *

The light is on her.

It comes across the water and up the cliff and through the grass and lands on her face, on the side of her face turned towards the sea, and where it lands he can see it.

Warm. That's the first word. Not white, not grey. A warmth on her skin that he can see, as though the light has found the one thing in the whole world it is allowed to be the color of. It's on her cheek. It's on her mouth. It's caught in her hair where the wind has pulled a strand loose, and the strand is lit up at the edges like a thread held to a lamp.

He can't see the sunset.

He can see where it lands.

She is looking at the sea. She doesn't know he's looking at her. She has her knees up and her arms round them and her chin on her arms, and the light is on her, and she is the most beautiful thing he has ever seen in either of his lives.

* * *

“You're not watching,” she says, after a while, without turning. ʸ

“I'm watching.” ᴹ

“You're watching me.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She turns her head.

The light moves across her face as she does it. He sees it go from her cheek to her eyes, and he sees her eyes in it, and he forgets for a moment how to breathe.

“What's it like?” she asks. ʸ

He knows what she means. She has asked him before, in other ways, at other times. *What do you see.* *What does it look like to you.* She asks because she wants to know, and because she is the only person he can answer honestly.

“The sky's grey,” he says. “The sea's grey. The sun is a white circle going into the water.” ᴹ

She waits.

“And it's on you,” he says. “The color. It's on your face. That's the only place I can see it.” ᴹ

She doesn't say anything for a moment.

Then she turns towards him on her knees. One of them is still pointed toward the sea, so when she leans across him she catches the light down one side of her face. She puts her hands on either side of his.

“Then look,” she says. ʸ

He looks.

He can see the last of it on her knuckles, too. He turns his head enough to kiss one of them, and Yuka keeps that hand right where it is.

“What color?” she asks. ʸ

He wants to answer before it changes. There's a little of the color her hair goes when it's wet and the sun gets to it. There's a deeper color on her cheek, one he recognizes from that morning when Elmesia asked them to stay. He's thinking about how to put both of those things in a sentence when she runs her thumb under his eye.

“Tell me after,” she says. ʸ

She leans in. He sets his hand on the blanket to give her something steady to come towards. Her nose touches his first, cold from the wind. Then her mouth is warm against his and his free hand goes to her elbow. He feels her relax into it. The grass keeps moving around them, and every so often the loose corner of the blanket taps the back of his wrist.

When she draws away she goes only far enough to look at him. He's still holding her elbow. She slides her hand from his face to the back of his neck and leaves it there while she takes a breath.

“After which one?” he asks. ᴹ

She lets out a laugh. He gets his hand all the way round her this time before she kisses him again. He had thought he ought to keep looking for as long as he could see the light. He closes his eyes the instant she touches him. He can feel where she is from the weight of her hand and the warmth coming through both their sleeves. He has nowhere he needs to be when he opens them.

They stay close enough for him to answer without raising his voice.

“Gold, I think. Here.” ᴹ

He brushes the side of her cheek with a finger. Her eyes follow his hand when he lowers it.

“Is it still there?” she asks. ʸ

“A little. Put your head here and I'll tell you when it goes.” ᴹ

She sits down beside him again and fits her head against his shoulder. She tells him that right now the clouds at the edge look like the thin part of a peach when you hold it up to a window. He turns his cheek into her hair and listens. By the time she gets to the water, the gold on her face is almost gone, and he finds he wants to hear about the water anyway.

* * *

They stay until the last of the light has gone off the water.

He can tell when it goes because it goes off her. One moment there's warmth on her cheek, and then it's fading, and then she is the color she always is to him, the color of herself, with the grey dark coming down all around her.

She lies down on the blanket with her head on his chest and they watch the first stars come out. Those he can see. Stars are white, and white is one of the things he has.

“Thank you,” she says. ʸ

“For the dragon?” ᴹ

“For the place. For thinking of it.” ʸ

She is quiet for a while.

“For telling me what you saw,” she says. ʸ

He puts his hand in her hair.

“You're what I saw,” he says. ᴹ

She laughs, very softly, into his shirt.

“That's so cheesy,” she says. ʸ

“It's true.” ᴹ

“I know. That's why it's so cheesy.” ʸ

* * *

Kaisel brings them back in the dark.

The island is all small lights now, the village and the pier and the hotel, little warm points in the black that Mau can see as points even if he can't see what color they are. Yuka sleeps against him most of the way. He keeps one arm round her the whole time, and Kaisel flies lower and slower than he needs to, as though he knows.

In the room, the blanket Mau forgot is still on the chair, folded, very safe.

Yuka sees it and starts laughing again.

“You can bring it next time,” she says. ʸ

“There'll be a next time?” ᴹ

She takes his hand and pulls him towards the bed.

“There'd better be,” she says. ʸ

Yuka sits down on the end of the bed and holds on to his hand while she tries to get one shoe off with the heel of the other. The knot holds. Mau kneels and puts her foot on his thigh so he can get at it.

“I can do that,” she says. ʸ

He looks up. She hasn't taken her foot away.

“Want to?” he asks. ᴹ

She considers it. “No. And there's sand in the other one.” ʸ

There is. He shakes it out over the mat and sets the shoes together. When he comes back she's pulled her feet up under her and made a place for him beside her. He sits. The flight has cooled the ends of her hair. She pulls it all over one shoulder and asks if he'll get the tie she can feel coming loose. He works it down a little at a time, feeling for the caught strands so he won't pull. By the time he gets it free she has her eyes closed.

“That good?” he asks. ᴹ

“That good,” she says. “Do my hair tomorrow too.” ʸ

He puts the tie on the table where she'll be able to find it, then feels two fingers close on the cuff of his shirt. She tugs him back the few inches he moved to reach the table.

“You can tell me where you want me,” he says. ᴹ

“I was showing you.” ʸ

“Show me again.” ᴹ

She kisses him once, takes a look at him, and kisses him again. The little space she left between them the first time is gone by the end of the second. He feels her fingers uncurl from his cuff and settle inside his palm.

“You're smiling,” she says. ʸ

“You said there'd better be a next time.” ᴹ

“I was talking about the cliff.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

She thinks about that, then buries her face in his shoulder to laugh. He feels it through his shirt. When she lifts her head, he asks if she wants the lamp on. She looks at the lamp, then back at him.

“For a little longer,” she says. ʸ

Later, Mau feels her hand moving about on the sheet. He takes it, and she draws his arm up a little higher until it's where she wants it. One of their pillows is on the floor. He can see its corner from where he's lying. He could reach it if he let go.

“I left your hair tie on the table,” he says. ᴹ

“I know. You put it on top of the card.” ʸ

“You had your eyes shut.” ᴹ

“You say that like you were the only one looking all night.” ʸ

He laughs. She rests her forehead against his, and the pillow stays on the floor. A little while later she asks if they can sleep in tomorrow. Mau asks how late. She thinks about it for long enough that he thinks she's fallen asleep.

“I'll tell you when I wake up,” she says. ʸ
