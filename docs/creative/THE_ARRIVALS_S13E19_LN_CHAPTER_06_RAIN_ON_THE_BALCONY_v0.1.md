# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 18 — Rain on the Balcony

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S13E19
**Continuity state:** B+302, island day ten. A rainy day kept indoors. Cards borrowed from the desk; Yuka accuses Mau of reading the deck with his eyes, then cheats openly herself. Room service, a long nap with her lying on him, rain on the open balcony door. No danger, no new topic. Claude, by Mauri's request; bracketed notes are for Work.

*When: Yuka opens the balcony door in the morning and the rain comes straight in.*

She shuts it again very fast.

Mau, still in bed, watches her stand there with her hand on the frame and the front of her shirt wet.

“It's raining,” she says. ʸ

“I heard.” ᴹ

“It wasn't raining yesterday.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

She turns and looks at him as though this is his fault. He holds up both hands.

“I didn't do it.” ᴹ

“You could have checked.” ʸ

“How would I check the weather?” ᴹ

“You have very good eyes. Everyone says so.” ʸ

He laughs. She comes back across the room and gets into bed, wet shirt and all, and puts her cold feet on his legs on purpose and watches his face while she does it.

* * *

It rains all day.

Not a storm. A steady grey rain that comes down past the balcony in long straight lines and hisses on the railing and fills the room with the sound of it. The sea below is a blur. The pools are empty. Somewhere downstairs a door bangs every so often as people run from one building to another with their arms over their heads.

They don't go anywhere.

After breakfast, which comes up on a tray because neither of them is willing to run, Yuka opens the balcony door again, just a crack, just enough that they can hear the rain properly and smell it without it coming in. Then she goes down to the desk in a borrowed coat and comes back with a deck of cards and a look on her face that Mau has learned to be careful of.

“Do you know how to play this?” she asks, holding it up. ʸ

“Which game?” ᴹ

“Any of them.” ʸ

“Some of them.” ᴹ

“Good,” she says. “Then you can lose at them.” ʸ

* * *

She teaches him a game Maki taught her.

It involves a great many rules, several of which Mau suspects she is making up as she goes. They play on the bed with the cards spread out on the blanket between them. He loses the first round. He loses the second round. On the third round he starts to understand the game, and wins it by a lot.

Yuka looks at the cards. Then at him.

“You're using your eyes,” she says. ʸ

“I'm not.” ᴹ

“You are. You can see the backs. You can see which ones are which.” ʸ

“The backs are all the same.” ᴹ

“To me.” ʸ

He tries to explain that his eyes don't work like that. That they see energy and structure and the shape of things, not the faint difference between one printed card back and another. That even if they did, he wouldn't. She listens to all of it with her arms folded and her chin up and an expression of deep, patient disbelief.

“Prove it,” she says. ʸ

“How?” ᴹ

She takes a pillow and puts it over his face.

“Now play,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He plays the fourth round with a pillow on his face.

She holds his cards up for him and tells him what they are, and he tells her which one to play, and she plays it. Except that halfway through he realizes she isn't playing the one he tells her. She's playing whichever one she likes, and telling him she played the one he said.

He pulls the pillow off.

Yuka is looking at him with three of his cards in one hand and four of her own in the other, and her face is perfectly calm.

“You're cheating,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm helping.” ʸ

“You're playing my cards for me.” ᴹ

“You had a pillow on your face. You couldn't see.” ʸ

“You put the pillow on my face.” ᴹ

“To prove you weren't cheating.” ʸ

He looks at her. She looks back. Then she lays down the winning hand, his cards and hers, all together, and says:

“I win.” ʸ

He falls back on the bed and covers his eyes with his arm and laughs until his ribs hurt, which they still do, a little, when he laughs too hard. She climbs over the cards and lies down on top of him and pulls his arm away from his face so she can watch him do it.

* * *

They give up on cards after that.

She doesn't get off him. She lies with her chin on his chest and her ear to the rain, and he lies with one arm behind his head and the other across her back, and they listen to it come down.

“What do you want to do now?” he asks. ᴹ

“This.” ʸ

“All day?” ᴹ

She thinks about it.

“Until I'm hungry,” she says. “Then lunch. Then this again.” ʸ

He can't think of a better plan.

* * *

They have lunch on the floor.

The tray comes up and Yuka says the table is too far from the bed, so they sit on the rug at the foot of it with their backs against the frame and the tray between them, close enough to the balcony door to feel the cool damp air coming in. She eats from his plate and he lets her. He eats from hers and she lets him, until he takes the last of the little fried things she was saving and she slaps his hand.

“That one was mine.” ʸ

“You took two of mine.” ᴹ

“Yours were smaller.” ʸ

“They were the same size.” ᴹ

“They were smaller because they were yours,” she says, as though this settles it. ʸ

He gives her the fried thing back. She eats half of it and puts the other half in his mouth before he can say anything.

* * *

After lunch, they sleep.

He doesn't mean to. He isn't tired. But the rain is steady and the room is dim and she curls up against his side on the bed with her head on his shoulder and her hand flat on his chest, and her breathing slows, and his slows with it, and the next thing he knows the light has changed.

He wakes first.

The rain is still falling. Softer now. The room has gone grey and blue, he guesses, with the afternoon, though to him it's only grey. She is still asleep against him. At some point in the nap she has moved so that she is half on top of him again, one leg thrown over his, her face pressed into his neck.

He doesn't move.

He lies and listens to the rain and to her, and thinks about the house. Ori will be in the Fort, probably, if it's raining there too. Rem will be baking, because Rem bakes when it rains. Beru will be somewhere he shouldn't be, wearing a hat.

He thinks about them, and finds he doesn't need to be there yet.

He is exactly where he wants to be.

* * *

Yuka wakes up an hour later, and looks at him, and says:

“Did I sleep on you again?” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Did you eat anything while I was asleep?” ʸ

“No,” he says. “I learned my lesson.” ᴹ

She looks at him suspiciously for a long moment. Then she puts her head back down.

“Good,” she says. ʸ

The rain keeps falling. Neither of them gets up.
