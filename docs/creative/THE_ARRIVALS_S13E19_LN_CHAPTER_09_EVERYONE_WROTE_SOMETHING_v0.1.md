# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 77
## Chapter 21 — Everyone Wrote Something

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 77
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S13E19
**Continuity state:** B+305, island day thirteen. A letter from the house arrives with the morning boat, written in many hands, asking when they plan to come back; nothing is wrong at home. Mau and Yuka start to miss everyone and decide to go home the next morning. Last day: the pool, the bird steals from Yuka this time, dinner with Elmesia and DIO, a last night. Claude, by Mauri's request; bracketed notes are for Work.

*When: The morning boat comes in while they are still at breakfast, and the boatman walks up from the pier with a letter in his hand.*

The letter is thick.

That's the first thing. The one they sent home was a single sheet, folded once. This one has been folded three times and still bulges, and the outside of it is covered in handwriting. Not one hand. Several. Somebody has written their names on the front. Somebody else has drawn a small boat underneath. Somebody else has written *OPEN AT BREAKFAST* in very large letters along the side, and underlined it twice.

“Ori,” Yuka says, looking at the underlining. ʸ

“Ori,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

They open it at breakfast.

* * *

It isn't one letter. It's many, written on the same sheets, one after another, in different inks and different hands. Everyone has passed it round the kitchen table and added a piece.

Yuka reads it aloud. Mau leans his shoulder against hers to look.

The first part is Rimuru:

*Got your letter. Ori read it to the whole kitchen at supper. Twice. Nobody minds that you're staying. We do want to know when you're coming back, but only so we can plan dinner. Come home when you want to. Not before.*

Then Ori, in big careful letters that slope downhill:

*Beru did NOT wear the hat to bed. He wore it to breakfast. Mai says that's allowed. Lantern and I finished the book. I am keeping a space on the windowsill. When are you coming home. Is the shell good.*

“Is the shell good,” Yuka repeats, and her voice wobbles. ʸ

“The shell is very good,” Mau says. ᴹ

She keeps reading.

* * *

Umaru:

*A report on the snacks is required. Which ones did you try. Which ones are you bringing. This is official Ministry business.*

Rem, small and neat:

*I made the bread you like on the morning you were supposed to come back. I'll make it again. Tell me the day.*

Gojo, a single line, sprawling across half the page:

*Bring coffee. Bring a lot of coffee. Shoko says I'm not allowed to say why.*

Shoko, directly underneath, in a sharp hand:

*He's had four cups already today. Mau, eat properly. Yuka, make him.*

Maki:

*Yuta misses you. Not me.*

And underneath, in a different, softer hand:

*She does. — Yuta*

Yuka has to stop reading for a moment. She puts the letter down on the table and covers her mouth with her hand and laughs, and her eyes are wet.

* * *

There's more.

Frieren, one line, in the exact center of a blank space:

*Tell me about the bird.*

Mai:

*If you bring cloth, bring enough to take apart.*

Fern:

*The house is in order. The register is up to date. Ori asked me to write that she has been very good. She has been mostly good.*

And then, at the very bottom, in Fern's hand again but clearly not Fern's words:

*Beru asked me to write this for him, as his hands are occupied with the hat. He says: "My king. The hat is safe. The house is safe. Everyone asks about you. I tell them you are well, because you are with your queen, and my king is always well when he is with his queen. Come home when you wish. We will be here."*

Mau reads that part twice.

Then he takes the letter out of Yuka's hand, very gently, and folds it, and holds it against his chest for a moment without saying anything.

Yuka puts her head on his shoulder.

* * *

They sit there for a while, in the bright breakfast room, with the boats going out beyond the window and the letter between them.

“I miss them,” Yuka says. ʸ

She says it quietly, as though she has only just found out.

“Me too,” Mau says. ᴹ

He hadn't, until now. He has been here thirteen days and has thought about the house every day, fondly, the way you think about a place you know is waiting for you. But he hasn't missed it. He hasn't needed to.

Now he can see Ori at the kitchen table with her careful downhill letters, asking whether the shell is good. He can see Beru with the hat on, dictating to Fern. He can see Gojo with his fourth cup and Shoko taking it away, and Maki writing *not me* and Yuta writing *she does* underneath, and Rem's bread on the morning they were supposed to come back.

He misses them so suddenly that his chest hurts.

“Tomorrow?” Yuka says. ʸ

He turns and looks at her.

“Tomorrow,” he says. ᴹ

She nods. She doesn't look sad about it. She looks like someone who has had exactly enough of something wonderful and is ready to go home and tell everyone about it.

* * *

They spend the last day doing everything one more time.

The little pool. The daybed with the curtain tied back on the left. Yuka on his lap with the island book open upside down until he turns it the right way. Lunch at the table by the rail, where the bird stole his fruit three times this week.

The bird is there.

It lands on the rail while they're eating and looks at Mau's plate, and Mau puts his hand over it protectively, and the bird looks at him for a long moment.

Then it hops along the rail, lands on the edge of the table by Yuka, and takes the piece of fruit right out of her fingers.

Yuka stares at her empty hand.

The bird flies off over the roof.

Mau puts his head down on the table and laughs until he can't breathe.

“It's not funny,” Yuka says. ʸ

“It's very funny.” ᴹ

“It stole from me.” ʸ

“It's been stealing from me all week.” ᴹ

“That was *funny*,” Yuka says. “This is *theft*.” ʸ

He gives her his fruit. All of it. She eats it with tremendous dignity while he tries to stop laughing.

* * *

They have dinner with Elmesia and DIO.

Elmesia has saved them the table by the window again, the one from the anniversary. DIO comes with a cloth bundle under her arm that turns out to have all the sea glass in it, every piece they found, sorted by size. She lays them out on the table so Yuka can tell Mau the colors, and Yuka does, one by one, and DIO listens to every word.

“Thank you for keeping us,” Yuka tells Elmesia over dessert. ʸ

“I didn't keep you,” Elmesia says. “You stayed.” ᴱˡ

“You asked.” ʸ

“I asked,” Elmesia agrees. “You're the ones who were happy enough to say yes.” ᴱˡ

She looks at them across the table. At Yuka's hand, which has found its way into Mau's on the tablecloth. At Mau, who is looking at Yuka instead of at his dessert.

“Come back,” she says. “Whenever you want. Bring the whole family next time if you must. But come back.” ᴱˡ

“We will,” Mau says. ᴹ

He means it. He can see from Yuka's face that she means it too.

* * *

That night, on the balcony, they don't say much.

The sea is dark below them. The lights of the village are small and warm along the shore. The bad sandals are lying on the balcony floor where Yuka kicked them off, and the enormous hat is hanging on the back of the chair, and the shell for Ori is in Mau's inventory on its own, waiting.

Yuka is in his lap. She has been in his lap a great deal this week. He has stopped noticing when she climbs on, the way you stop noticing your own heartbeat.

“It was good,” she says. ʸ

“It was very good.” ᴹ

“I don't want it to be over.” ʸ

“It doesn't have to be over,” he says. “We can come back.” ᴹ

She thinks about that.

“I keep forgetting,” she says, “that we get to come back.” ʸ

He holds her a little tighter.

“We get to come back,” he says. ᴹ

She turns in his lap and puts her arms round his neck and her face against his shoulder, and stays there.
