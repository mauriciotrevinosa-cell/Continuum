# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 1 — The Room Next Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E22
**Continuity state:** B+306, late night, after the first movie. Ori is carried to bed asleep. Mau and Yuka go up to their room for the first night home after thirteen nights on the island. They remember that Rimuru and Shizue now sleep next door. The night itself is left for Work, with the full context, below.

*When: The movie ends a little after the house usually goes to bed. Shuna is already collecting cups, and Ori fell asleep somewhere near the middle.*

Ori is asleep with her feet in Yuka's lap and her head on the arm of the couch, and one hand is still in the popcorn bowl.

Nobody saw exactly when it happened. Umaru claims it was during the long scene on the bridge. Milim says it was before that, because she turned to ask Ori a question and got no answer, and decided Ori was ignoring her on purpose and was very offended for about a minute. Chloe, from the floor, says it was right after the dog. Chloe is usually right about these things.

The screen goes dark. Senku pulls the curtain back. The room fills up again with the ordinary light of lamps and the sound of people standing up slowly, the way people stand up after sitting through something together, as if they're not sure yet that they're allowed to talk.

“I liked it,” Milim announces to the room. ᴹⁱ

“You talked through the whole middle,” Umaru says. ᵁ

“I was *helping*.” ᴹⁱ

Mau gets the popcorn bowl out from under Ori's hand without waking her. It takes two tries. On the first one her fingers close around a kernel and don't let go, and he has to wait until her grip loosens on its own. Yuka watches him do it with her chin on her fist.

“You've done that before,” she says quietly. ʸ

“On the island. You fell asleep holding a piece of cake.” ᴹ

“I did not.” ʸ

“You did. The bird was very interested.” ᴹ

She can't argue with that, so she doesn't. She lifts Ori's feet from her lap, slides out from under them, and then stands there for a second looking down at the small sleeping person on the couch as though she is checking that everything is where she left it.

* * *

Mau carries her up.

Ori doesn't wake. She turns her face into his shoulder somewhere around the landing and says something that might be *shell* and might be *Beru*, and then she's quiet again. Lantern's shape stirs once in her shadow and settles. Beru follows them up the stairs at a respectful distance with the cloth hat still on his head, because nobody has told him to take it off and he isn't going to take it off without orders.

Yuka goes ahead and turns down the bed.

On the windowsill the shell is sitting where Ori finally put it after an hour of trying it in every possible place: between the biggest piece of sea glass DIO brought her and a stone Ori found herself in the creek last summer, and which she says is the same shape as Mau's thumb. Mau has looked at the stone. He doesn't see the resemblance. He has never said so.

He puts Ori down. Yuka pulls the blanket up. Ori's hand comes out from under it and finds the edge of Mau's sleeve and holds on, and then lets go.

They stand in the doorway a moment.

“She missed us,” Yuka says. ʸ

“She wrote *is the shell good* on a letter that went across the sea.” ᴹ

“She did.” Yuka leans against the doorframe. “I think she was worried we wouldn't come back.” ʸ

Mau doesn't answer right away. He has been gone before. Longer than thirteen nights, and in places that didn't send letters.

“We came back,” he says. ᴹ

“We did.” ʸ

Beru takes up his post in the hall outside Ori's door, hat and all.

“Good night, Beru,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Good night, my queen. Good night, my king.” ᴮᵉ

* * *

The hallway outside their room is dark.

Halfway along it, Yuka stops so suddenly that Mau nearly walks into her back.

She's looking at the door beside theirs. There's a thin line of lamplight under it, and from the other side comes the low murmur of two people talking. Shizue laughs at something. Rimuru says something back, too quiet to make out.

Yuka turns around very slowly.

“I forgot,” she whispers. ʸ

“Forgot what?” ᴹ

She points at the door with her whole arm, like someone pointing out a bear.

Mau looks at the door. Then he remembers too. While they were on the island, Rimuru and Shizue moved into the room beside theirs, because the old one was too close to the kitchen and Shizue wakes up whenever Shuna starts the bread. Rimuru wrote it in the house letter, between the part about dinner and the part about the snacks, as though it were nothing.

It was nothing, at the time. It was a line in a letter.

It is less nothing now.

Yuka has both hands over her mouth. Her shoulders are shaking.

“Don't,” Mau whispers. ᴹ

“I'm not doing anything.” ʸ

“You're about to laugh.” ᴹ

“*You're* about to laugh.” ʸ

He is. He can feel it starting somewhere under his ribs. They stand in the dark hallway of their own house like two people who've broken into it, trying not to make a sound, and on the other side of the wall Shizue laughs again at something Rimuru said, and that's what does it. Yuka makes a small strangled noise into her hands. Mau grabs her by the wrist and pulls her the last few steps to their own door, and gets it open, and gets them both through it, and closes it with exaggerated care, the latch clicking into place as softly as he can make it.

Then they lean against the inside of the door and laugh until they can't breathe, with their hands over each other's mouths, which doesn't help at all.

* * *

When they can stand up straight again, Yuka wipes her eyes.

“The walls aren't thin,” she says. ʸ

“The walls aren't thin,” he agrees. ᴹ

“Senku built them. He told me. There's something in them.” ʸ

“There's something in them.” ᴹ

“So it's fine.” ʸ

“It's completely fine.” ᴹ

They look at each other.

“We could ask Ciel to check,” Yuka says. ʸ

“We are *not* asking Ciel to check.” ᴹ

That nearly sets her off again. She turns away from him to hide it and starts pulling the pins out of her hair, and he goes to the other side of the room to get out of his shirt, which still smells faintly of the boat.

The room is exactly as they left it. Somebody — Shuna, probably — has aired it out and put fresh sheets on the bed, and on Yuka's side there's a small folded note that turns out, when she opens it, to say *welcome home* in Ori's handwriting, with a drawing underneath of two people on a boat, one of them wearing an enormous hat.

Yuka shows it to him without a word.

He looks at the hat. He looks at her.

“She hadn't seen the hat yet when she drew this,” he says. ᴹ

“I wrote about it in the letter.” ʸ

“You wrote about the hat in the letter.” ᴹ

“It was an important hat.” ʸ

She props the note up against the lamp, where she'll see it when she wakes up. Then she goes back to her hairpins, and he goes back to his shirt, and for a while there's only the small quiet noises of two people getting ready for bed in the same room after a long day, the sound of the house settling around them, and, very faintly through the wall that isn't thin, nothing at all.

* * *

Later — much later — Mau lies awake for a little while in the dark, listening.

The wind is in the orchard. Beru shifts his weight in the hallway. Somewhere below, a door closes, and Shuna's footsteps go along the passage toward the kitchen, which means it is either very late or very early. Through the wall, nothing. Senku really did put something in it.

Yuka is asleep against him, one arm thrown across his chest as though to stop him getting up. Her hair is everywhere. Some of it is in his mouth. He doesn't move it.

Thirteen nights on the island, and every one of them good. And still, there was a part of him that wasn't quite asleep on any of them, that was listening for the sound of this house. For Beru in the hall. For Shuna and the bread. For the particular creak of the third stair, which Ori always steps on, and which Rimuru has offered to fix three times, and which Ori has asked him three times not to fix, because then how would anyone know she was coming.

He hears it now, very faintly. The third stair.

Then small feet in the hallway, and Beru's voice, very low: “Little one. It is the middle of the night.” ᴮᵉ

And Ori's voice, sleepy and certain: “I just want to check.” ᴼ

The door doesn't open. She doesn't knock. There's only a pause outside, long enough for someone to stand there and listen to two people breathing on the other side of a door, and then the small feet go back the way they came, and the third stair creaks again, and the house is quiet.

Yuka hasn't woken. Mau lies there with his eyes open and something in his chest that he doesn't have a name for, and isn't sure he needs one.

*She just wanted to check.*

He falls asleep listening for the stair.
