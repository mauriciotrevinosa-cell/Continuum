# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 2 — Saving a Seat

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S13E22
**Continuity state:** B+307, morning to afternoon. First full day home. Breakfast. Yuka takes the island cloth to Mai; Mau keeps her place on the bench and pulls her back down beside him the moment she's done. Rimuru and Shizue make a point of saying nothing about the wall. Mau is restless after thirteen days of not moving much; Gojo notices.

*When: Breakfast on the first morning home. Shuna has made the bread Rem promised in the letter, and Rem is pretending she isn't watching to see if they like it.*

Rimuru says good morning in a completely normal voice.

That's how Mau knows.

It's the most normal good morning he has ever heard. It's so normal it could be used to teach other people how to say good morning. Rimuru says it from the stove, where he's helping Shuna with the eggs, and he doesn't turn around while he says it, and beside him Shizue is buttering toast with enormous concentration, as if the toast has asked her a difficult question.

Yuka, beside Mau in the doorway, has gone completely still.

“Good morning,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Sleep well?” Rimuru asks the eggs. ᴿ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Good. Good.” ᴿ

Shizue's shoulders are shaking very slightly. She finishes the toast and puts it on a plate and hands it past Rimuru to Shuna, and still doesn't look up.

“The walls,” Yuka says faintly. ʸ

“Are excellent,” Rimuru says at once. “Senku did a wonderful job. I've never heard a single thing through them. Not one. Have you, Shizue?” ᴿ

“Not one,” Shizue says to the butter. ˢᶻ

There's a short, dreadful silence. Then Shuna, who has been listening to all of this with a perfectly calm face, sets down the spatula and says, “I'll bring the bread out,” ˢʰᵘ and leaves the kitchen at a speed that suggests she isn't going to be able to keep the calm face much longer.

Yuka takes Mau by the hand and walks him straight past the stove and out to the table without another word.

* * *

The bread is very good.

Rem watches them try it from the far end of the table while pretending to read the household register over Fern's shoulder. Fern lets her pretend. When Mau looks up and nods, Rem ducks her head and turns a page of the register that Fern hasn't finished reading yet. Fern turns it back.

The table is full. It's the first breakfast in two weeks where everybody is in one place, and the noise of it is something Mau didn't realise he had missed until it was all around him again: Milim telling Ori about a dream she had in which the screen was the size of the orchard; Gojo asking Shoko if three cups is still a reasonable number on a day when somebody has come home from a trip; Shoko telling him it isn't; Maki stealing a piece of Yuta's bread while he's looking the other way, and Yuta letting her, and both of them knowing it. Frieren is reading at the end of the bench with a cup of tea that has gone cold. She looks up once when Mau sits down and says, “You owe me the bird,” ᶠ and goes back to her book.

“After breakfast,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I'll hold you to that.” ᶠ

Yuka has the bundle on her lap. She brought it down from the room wrapped in the island's paper, the one with the little printed boats on it: two lengths of the woven stuff from the market, the one with the stripe that the woman at the stall told her was the colour of the lagoon at noon. Mau has seen it. It looks to him like the paper of a book that's been left out in the light, a pale grey with a darker grey running through it. Yuka says it's the most beautiful blue she has ever seen, and he believes her.

“I want to give it to Mai before I forget,” she says, and stands up, and takes the bundle down the table to where Mai is sitting between Senku and Momo. ʸ

Mau watches her go.

Mai looks up. Yuka puts the bundle down in front of her and unwraps it, and Mai's whole face changes. She lifts one end of the cloth, rubs it between her fingers, holds it up to the window. Says something Mau can't hear over Milim. Yuka says something back and sits down on the end of the bench beside her, and Mai pulls the second length out and holds it against the first, and they bend their heads together over it.

Mau moves his cup.

He doesn't think about it. He just moves it, and his plate, a little to the left, so that the space on the bench beside him gets wider. Then he puts his arm along the back of the space, where her shoulders would go if she were sitting there, and leaves it there.

“You're saving a seat,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“I'm not saving a seat.” ᴹ

“You moved your plate.” ᴳ

“My plate was in the way.” ᴹ

“Of what?” ᴳ

Mau doesn't answer that. Shoko, beside Gojo, hides a smile in her cup.

Down the table, Mai is explaining something with her hands. Yuka is nodding. The cloth is spread out over half the table now, and Senku has leaned over to look at the weave, and Momo is saying something about a skirt. It goes on for a while. Mau eats his bread. Yuka laughs at something Mai says, and he looks up, and looks down again.

Gojo is watching him with an expression of enormous satisfaction.

“What,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Nothing.” ᴳ

“Say it.” ᴹ

“You're watching the door like a dog.” ᴳ

“There's no door.” ᴹ

“You know what I mean.” ᴳ

He does. He doesn't say so.

* * *

When Yuka finally stands up, Mai is still holding the cloth. She says something over her shoulder about coming to the workshop later, and Mai nods without looking up, already lost in it, and Yuka starts back down the table.

She doesn't get there.

She's still two steps away when Mau reaches out and takes her by the wrist and pulls, very gently, and she lands on the bench beside him exactly in the space he left, with his arm already there behind her shoulders, as though the whole thing had been planned with Ciel's help.

Yuka looks at the arm. She looks at the space. She looks at the plate he moved.

“Were you waiting for me?” she asks. ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“He was waiting for you,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“He moved his *plate*,” Milim adds, from three seats away, with her mouth full. ᴹⁱ

Yuka turns back to Mau. Her face is doing something complicated. He watches it go from surprised to pleased to deeply, unbearably smug, and then she leans into his side, all the way, until her head is on his shoulder and her whole weight is against him and he has to put his cup down to keep from spilling it.

“You missed me,” she says. ʸ

“You were *right there*. I could see you.” ᴹ

“You missed me anyway.” ʸ

He did. That's the stupid part. She was eight people down the same table, in plain view the entire time, close enough that he could hear her laugh, and he missed her. Thirteen days of having her within arm's reach every minute has done something to him, and he isn't sure yet whether he wants it undone.

“I'll go back if you want,” Yuka offers. ʸ

His arm tightens around her before he can stop it.

Gojo laughs so hard he has to put his head down on the table. Shoko pats him on the back without looking at him. Ori, who has been watching all of this with great interest, turns to Chloe and says, very seriously, “That's what I do with Lantern when he wants to go back in my shadow,” ᴼ and Chloe says, “I know,” ᶜʰ and that's the end of breakfast for Gojo, who has to leave the table.

* * *

The morning goes slowly, the way first mornings back usually do.

Mau keeps his promise to Frieren and tells her about the bird properly, sitting on the edge of the porch while she listens with her eyes half closed: the leaves first, then the sparrow-thing that wouldn't hold still, then the big grey one with the opinions, and the bowl, and the fruit, and the fact that at the end it chose to steal from Yuka instead of him, which Frieren considers for a long moment.

“It liked you better,” she says at last. ᶠ

“It stole from her.” ᴹ

“Birds don't steal from people they're afraid of. It stopped being afraid of her first. You were the one it had to keep an eye on.” ᶠ

Mau thinks about that.

“That's a compliment,” he says slowly. ᴹ

“It's an observation.” ᶠ

“From you that's a compliment.” ᴹ

She doesn't deny it. She goes back to her book, and he sits beside her on the porch for a while longer, watching the orchard and the yard and the people moving through it, and slowly becomes aware that his leg is jiggling.

He makes it stop. A minute later it starts again.

He isn't sure when it began. Sometime this morning. Maybe last night. On the island he barely used anything: a touch of Authority to win a fight about a fish, Frieren's spell on a bird, Kaisel to the cape and back. For thirteen days his body did nothing harder than swim, and it was wonderful, and now it has noticed. It feels like the moment after a long sleep, when the muscles want to stretch before the rest of you has decided to get up.

He stands. Walks to the end of the porch. Walks back.

“You're pacing,” Frieren says, without looking up. ᶠ

“I'm not pacing.” ᴹ

“You've walked past me four times.” ᶠ

He sits down again. His leg starts jiggling.

* * *

Gojo finds him in the yard in the early afternoon, doing nothing in particular next to the training posts.

Mau isn't training. He has Shoko's rules, and he's been good about Shoko's rules, and nobody has cleared him for anything heavier than swimming since the island. He's just standing there. Looking at the posts. Thinking about how far he could throw one of them if he wanted to, and whether the wall at the end of the yard would stop it, and whether Senku would be angry.

“Itchy?” Gojo asks. ᴳ

“A little.” ᴹ

Gojo nods slowly, as though Mau has confirmed a diagnosis. He walks over and leans against the nearest post, which creaks.

“Thirteen days of sun and fruit and sleeping late,” he says. “The body gets ideas.” ᴳ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You could spar with me.” ᴳ

“Shoko would kill us both.” ᴹ

“She'd kill *you*. She likes me.” ᴳ

“She does not like you that much.” ᴹ

Gojo concedes this with a tilt of his head. He looks at the posts, then back at Mau, and there's something in his face under the blindfold that Mau has learned to read over the years, the part of Gojo that isn't joking.

“Go somewhere,” Gojo says. “Not today. You only just got back, and she'd want you here. But soon. Find a gate nobody wants. Clear it. Come back tired.” ᴳ

“That's your medical advice.” ᴹ

“That's my *teacher* advice. You're the kind that needs to run.” ᴳ

He pushes off the post and goes back toward the house, and Mau stands there a while longer, thinking about it.

A gate nobody wants.

He hasn't been in one since the recovery clears, and those were small, with Bellion counting his steps and Fern waiting outside with the register. Before that — before that was the long one. The one with the drop of water. He waits to see if the thought of a gate brings any of that back with it, and finds that it doesn't, quite. There's something there, a weight in the background, but it isn't the thing he expected. It's quieter than that.

What's in the front, mostly, is the itch.

* * *

He's still standing there when someone comes up behind him and puts both arms around his waist.

He knows who it is before she's finished doing it. He could tell with his eyes closed, and his eyes are open, and she's the only thing in the yard with colour, so he could have seen her coming if he'd been looking. He wasn't looking. He was thinking about gates.

Yuka's chin settles on his shoulder from behind. Her arms lock in front of him.

“Where are you going?” she asks. ʸ

“Nowhere.” ᴹ

“You were looking at the wall like you wanted to go through it.” ʸ

“I was thinking.” ᴹ

“About going somewhere.” ʸ

He doesn't answer. She tightens her arms.

“Not today,” she says into his shoulder. “Today you're mine. You can go through walls tomorrow.” ʸ

He puts his hands over hers where they're locked in front of him. Her fingers are warm. She doesn't let go, and he doesn't try to make her, and after a while he notices that his leg has stopped jiggling. He isn't sure when that happened either.

“Gojo says I should find a gate nobody wants,” he says. ᴹ

“Gojo is right,” Yuka says. “*Tomorrow*.” ʸ

“Tomorrow,” he agrees. ᴹ

“Or the day after.” ʸ

“Or the day after.” ᴹ

“Come and help me take the shells to Mai. She wants to put them on something.” ʸ

She still doesn't let go. He turns around inside her arms, which she allows, and then she takes his hand and leads him back toward the house, and he goes, and doesn't look back at the posts.
