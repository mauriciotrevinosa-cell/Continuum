# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 5 — A Gate Nobody Wants

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E24
**Continuity state:** B+309, morning. Fern finds an unclaimed gate in the register. Mau goes in alone with a day's supplies and two daggers, tells Yuka and Fern when he'll be back. Inside, the gate turns: Noise reports the light has changed to red. The Shop is unreachable; the inventory opens but holds only what he packed. Time inside is running much faster than outside. He decides to clear it fast.

*When: After breakfast. Fern has the register open on the kitchen table before Mau has finished asking.*

“This one,” Fern says, and turns the book around. ᶠᵉ

Mau looks at the entry. Her handwriting is very small and very straight, and the line she's pointing at has three marks beside it: one that means *reported*, one that means *assessed*, and one that Mau doesn't recognise.

“What's that one?” he asks. ᴹ

“It means nobody wants it,” Fern says. “Souei's people found it eleven days ago. North of the river, past the old quarry. Small entrance. The assessment says the inside is cold and empty and there's nothing in it worth carrying out. Two parties looked at it and decided to go somewhere else.” ᶠᵉ

“Cold and empty.” ᴹ

“Cold and empty.” ᶠᵉ

He thinks about it. It sounds perfect.

“I'll take it,” he says. ᴹ

Fern writes his name next to the entry, and the date, and the time, and then looks up at him over the edge of the book.

“When will you be back?” ᶠᵉ

“Dinner.” ᴹ

She writes *dinner* in the margin. Then, after a moment, she writes something underneath it in even smaller letters that he can't read upside down.

“What's that?” ᴹ

“A note,” Fern says, and closes the book. ᶠᵉ

* * *

He packs light.

That's on purpose. The whole point is to go somewhere and come back tired, and you don't come back tired from a gate if you bring the house with you. Two daggers on his belt, the ones Jin-Woo has been teaching him to hold properly. A day's food in the inventory: bread from this morning, cheese, two apples, a flask of water, a bar of the chocolate Yuka likes, because she put it in his hand at the door and closed his fingers over it and said *for later*. A blanket, because Shoko would ask. Nothing else. The Shop is there if he needs it. It always is.

Yuka walks him to the edge of the yard.

“Dinner,” she says. ʸ

“Dinner.” ᴹ

“If you're late, I'm eating your share.” ʸ

“You'll eat my share anyway.” ᴹ

“That's not the point.” ʸ

She hugs him. It's a short hug, a see-you-later hug, nothing like the ones from before the long dungeon. He notices that, and is grateful for it, and doesn't say so. Then she lets go and pushes him lightly in the middle of the chest, the way you push a boat away from a dock.

“Go and get tired,” she says. ʸ

He goes.

* * *

The gate is where Fern said it would be.

It's small. The old quarry is a bowl of cut stone with a pool of rainwater at the bottom and scrub growing out of the cracks, and the gate sits against the far wall like a door someone painted on the rock: a tall oval of light about twice his height, faintly humming. Mau stands in front of it for a moment and looks at it with the eyes, letting them read what there is to read.

Small. Low density. A single layer, as far as the eyes can tell from outside. Cold on the other side, like Fern said. Nothing unusual.

“Anything?” he asks. ᴹ

“Nothing,” Noise says. ᴺ

“Ciel?” ᴹ

“Nothing I can see from here,” Ciel says. “It looks like what the assessment says it is.” ᶜ

He steps through.

* * *

It's cold.

That's the first thing. Not the cold of a winter morning, but the deep, dry, bone-level cold of a place where it has never been anything else. His breath comes out in front of him in a cloud. The ground under his boots is hard and uneven, frozen, and the air smells of nothing at all, which is worse than smelling of something.

It's empty, too. That's the second thing. He's standing at the edge of a wide flat plain that runs out in every direction until it disappears into a haze. There are shapes in the distance that might be hills or might be ruins. Above him there's a sky, or something doing the job of one, a flat even ceiling of light with no sun in it.

“Cold and empty,” he says aloud. ᴹ

His voice doesn't echo. The plain swallows it.

He stands there and waits for the itch to go, for the restlessness to settle now that he's somewhere he can move, and it does, a little. He rolls his shoulders. Bounces once on the balls of his feet. Thinks about how far he could run across this plain before he got tired, and whether the hills at the edge are hills, and what might be living in them.

Then Noise says: “Mau.” ᴺ

It's not the usual voice. It's flatter. Quicker.

“The light changed,” Noise says. ᴺ

Mau looks up.

The ceiling looks the same to him as it did a moment ago: a flat sheet of grey, even and sunless. He looks at the plain. It looks the same. He looks at his hands. The same.

“Changed how?” ᴹ

“Red,” Noise says. “The whole field went red. Just now. When you finished stepping through.” ᴺ

Mau stands very still.

He can't see it. Of course he can't. He sees grey; he's always seen grey, apart from her. If the whole world went red around him he'd never know unless someone told him. But he knows what the word means, here, in a gate. Everyone does. Jin-Woo told him once, sitting on the porch steps, in the voice he uses for things he'd rather not remember.

*If the gate turns red, you don't get out until it's done. And it doesn't care how long that takes.*

He turns around.

The gate is gone. Where he came in there's just more plain, flat and frozen, running away into the haze.

* * *

“Shop,” he says. ᴹ

Nothing happens.

He tries again, properly this time, the way he's done it a thousand times, reaching for it the way you reach for a door handle in the dark. There's always a door there. There's always been a door there, ever since the market, ever since the first box of rations. He reaches.

There's no handle. There's no door. There's a smooth blank wall where the door used to be.

“The Shop is unavailable,” Ciel says. Her voice is very calm, which is how he knows she's worried. “I can see where it should be. I can't reach it. Neither can you.” ᶜ

“Inventory?” ᴹ

He reaches for that, and it opens. Relief goes through him like warm water. He looks inside: bread, cheese, two apples, a flask, a bar of chocolate, a blanket. Exactly what he packed. Not a crumb more.

“Inventory works,” Ciel says. “But there's nothing in it you didn't bring.” ᶜ

“One day of food,” Mau says slowly. ᴹ

“One day,” Ciel agrees. ᶜ

“Time?” ᴹ

There's a pause. He can feel Ciel and Noise working on it together, the way they do, two processes leaning over the same problem.

“It's not running at the same speed as outside,” Noise says at last. “It's much faster in here. I can't give you an exact number yet. A lot faster.” ᴺ

“Days in here for every hour out there?” ᴹ

“More than that,” Ciel says. “Possibly a great deal more. I'll know better in a while.” ᶜ

He thinks about Fern's register. *Dinner*, in the margin, and the note underneath in letters he couldn't read.

He thinks about Yuka at the edge of the yard. *Go and get tired.*

He thinks — briefly, he can't help it — about another place where the door closed behind him and the clock ran differently and nobody outside could add anything to what he had. A drop of water. A room with a bed. He waits for the thought to pull at him, the way those thoughts sometimes do, and feels it touch him and pass, like a hand brushing his shoulder in a crowd.

This isn't that. This is a gate. A red one, but a gate. Jin-Woo got out of one. So will he.

“All right,” he says. ᴹ

He takes the chocolate out of the inventory, looks at it, and puts it back.

“For later,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

“Plan?” Ciel asks. ᶜ

He's already walking. Toward the hills, or the ruins, or whatever they are. The cold bites at his face and his hands, and he pulls the blanket out and wraps it round his shoulders like a cloak and keeps going.

“Find out what's in here,” he says. “Find whatever's in charge. Kill it. Go home.” ᴹ

“That's the whole plan?” ᶜ

“That's the whole plan.” ᴹ

“And food?” ᶜ

“Whatever lives here eats something. I'll eat what it eats. Or I'll eat it.” ᴹ

“You hate cold food,” Noise observes. ᴺ

“I'll learn to love it.” ᴹ

Ciel makes a small sound that might be a laugh.

“Fast or careful?” she asks. ᶜ

He thinks about that one honestly. Careful is how you survive a long dungeon. Careful is rationing, and resting, and not spending what you can't replace. He knows how to be careful. He spent a year learning how to be careful, in a place that punished him every time he got good at anything.

But careful is also slow. And every day he spends in here is some number of minutes at home: a few minutes where Fern looks at the clock, a few minutes where Yuka glances at the door. Not many. But some.

And there's a part of him, a part he isn't proud of, that just wants to *run*.

“Fast,” he says. ᴹ

“How fast?” ᶜ

The plain stretches ahead of him, frozen and grey and endless. Somewhere in the haze the shapes on the horizon have resolved into something like broken towers, and something is moving between them, too far away for even the eyes to make out.

He drops the blanket back into the inventory. Rolls his shoulders. Feels thirteen days of sun and fruit and sleeping late sitting in his muscles like a held breath.

“As fast as I've got,” he says, and starts to run. ᴹ
