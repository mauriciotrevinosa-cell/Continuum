# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 7 — As Fast as He Has

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E25
**Continuity state:** Inside the red gate, local days 7–20. Mau clears tower after tower at full pace toward a structure at the centre of the plain, sleeping in short stretches. The packed food runs out by day 10; he lives on tower lizards. Frostbite in two toes and the edge of one ear; the wheel turns once at the cold and the next exposures bite less. A long cut on his left forearm from a creature that came from below. The Pillow follows him as far as the inner ring and will not cross it; he says goodbye. The chocolate is still uneaten.

*When: The second and third weeks inside. About ten minutes outside, all told.*

On the seventh day he stops walking between towers and starts running.

Not jogging. Running the way he used to run in the yard when nobody was watching, long and low and fast, with Sky under his feet to take the jar out of the frozen ground. The plain unrolls under him. The towers come up out of the haze one after another, and he hits each one without slowing, and goes through whatever comes out of it, and keeps going.

It's not elegant. Jin-Woo would have something to say about the footwork. Gojo would have something to say about the waste: he's spending more than he needs to on every fight, throwing Blue where a dagger would do, holding Infinity open the whole time instead of only when something's about to touch him. Careful is cheaper. He knows that.

He isn't being careful. He's being fast. Every day he saves in here is another day he doesn't have to spend working out where his next meal comes from. Outside, it's hardly enough time for Yuka to glance at the door and go back to whatever she's doing.

* * *

He learns the long things.

After the fifth tower he knows how they move: the way their limbs unfold from the joint nearest the body first, the half-beat of hesitation before they commit to a direction, the way they always try to wrap rather than strike. After the eighth he knows how they think, as far as they think at all. They don't like the fire. They don't like Blue, which pulls them off balance and makes their long limbs tangle. They don't understand Infinity at all, and every one of them that touches it stops for a moment and tries to work out why it can't close, and that moment is all he needs.

After the tenth, he sits with his back against a tower and asks Ciel to pull up two of the fights. Movement Learner sets one beside the other. In the first he blocks high and has to wrench his knife free. In the second, a stray step brings him inside the reach of the joint. He watches that step three times before he gets up.

“You're dropping your shoulder on the left,” Ciel tells him, on the eleventh day, after a fight he thought went well. ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You didn't last week.” ᶜ

“I'm tired.” ᴹ

“I know,” Ciel says. ᶜ

* * *

The food runs out on the tenth day.

The last of the bread on the eighth. The cheese on the ninth. He eats the apples on the tenth, one in the morning and one at night, core and all, standing in the lee of a tower with the Pillow breathing behind him. After that it's lizards.

He doesn't mind as much as he expected. They taste like nothing, and there are never quite enough of them, and his belt goes in a notch and then another. But he's eating, every day, and he has water, and the fire, and the Pillow at night. He's been much hungrier than this. He doesn't let himself think about where.

The chocolate stays in the inventory. He checks on it every night before he sleeps. He doesn't take it out.

“You could eat it,” Noise says once. ᴺ

“It's for later.” ᴹ

“It's been later for ten days.” ᴺ

“Not that later.” ᴹ

* * *

On the thirteenth day the cold gets into his feet.

He doesn't notice until it's done. He's been running so hard and so long that his body has stopped telling him things, and when he finally stops at the end of the day and pulls off his boot to look, two of the toes on his left foot are white and hard and don't feel like they belong to him.

The edge of his left ear is the same. He touches it and can't feel his fingers touching it.

“Frostbite,” Ciel says. ᶜ

He knows. He sits by the fire with his foot almost in it and holds the toes in his hands and feeds them reverse cursed energy, slowly, a little at a time, the way Shoko taught him: not trying to fix all of it at once, just enough to bring the blood back. It hurts more coming back than it did going. He sits there with his teeth clenched while the Pillow watches him with one small eye.

Then he feels it. Somewhere above and behind him, out of sight, the wheel turns.

One click. That's all. A small, definite, grinding sound that nobody else would hear, the sound of something learning the shape of what hurts him.

The next day the cold bites less. Not much. But less.

“It turned for the cold,” Noise says. ᴺ

“It turned for the cold,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

He's not sure how he feels about that. The wheel doesn't care what it adapts to. It adapted to Sukuna's cuts. Now it's adapted, a little, to a frozen plain in a gate that nobody wanted. It will remember both the same way.

* * *

On the fifteenth day he gets careless.

He's clearing the thirteenth tower. There are more of the long things than usual, a dozen, maybe more, and they're coming at him from the openings in the walls in waves. He's moving better than he has all week. The first time he sees a joint unfold high he makes the step he'd watched, and the rest comes easily. He's watching all of them. He has all of them.

He doesn't have the one underneath.

It comes up out of the ground. There's a crack in the frozen earth at the base of the tower that he didn't see, or saw and didn't think about, and the thing comes up through it like water through a hole in a boat. Its arm goes past Infinity at an angle the neutral doesn't read in time, because it's not moving the way the others move, it's *rising*. Something like a blade at the end of its limb opens his left forearm from wrist to elbow.

He kills it. He kills the rest. Then he sits down very suddenly in the snow with his arm in his lap and blood running between his fingers, and the whole world tilts sideways for a second.

“Pressure,” Ciel says sharply. “Now.” ᶜ

He puts pressure on it. He closes it with RCT, carefully, from the inside out, the way Shoko taught him, and then he wraps it in a strip torn off the bottom of his shirt because he has nothing else, and then he sits there and breathes.

“You got careless,” Noise says. ᴺ

“I got tired.” ᴹ

“You got careless *because* you got tired.” ᴺ

He doesn't argue. Noise is right. He's spent fifteen days running at the edge of everything he has and his body has started making mistakes. He knew it would. He's been waiting for it.

“Do you want to slow down?” Ciel asks quietly. ᶜ

He thinks about it. He really does. He sits there in the snow with his arm throbbing and the cold coming up through his trousers and he thinks about the arithmetic one more time. Slow down, and he'll be in here longer. Longer means more lizards and fewer of them. Longer means more days like this one, and more chances for something to come up out of the ground.

Or go faster. Finish it. Go home tired.

“How far to the middle?” he asks. ᴹ

“Five towers,” Noise says. “Maybe six. Then whatever's in the centre.” ᴺ

He stands up.

* * *

On the eighteenth day, he reaches the inner ring.

He can see it now: the thing at the centre of the plain. It's not a tower. It's something lower and wider and older, a ring of standing stones taller than the towers were, arranged in a circle around something he can't make out from here. The air around it is different. Colder, if that's possible. Heavier. The eyes read it and come back with nothing useful, just pressure, layered and dense, like trying to see through deep water.

The long things don't go there. He hasn't seen one in a day.

And the Pillow stops.

It's been following him the whole way, all eighteen days, a big shaggy patient shape at his back. Now it stops at the edge of an invisible line about two hundred paces from the stones, and won't go any further. He walks on a few steps and looks back, and it's standing there with its nose swaying, looking at him with its small dark eyes.

He goes back.

“You can't come in there,” he says. “I know.” ᴹ

The Pillow groans.

He puts his hand on its broad shaggy forehead. It's warm, the way it's always been warm, the warmest thing in this whole frozen place.

“Thank you,” he says. “For the nights.” ᴹ

He stands there a moment longer than he needs to. Then he lights the little fire spell one last time, right there in front of the Pillow, and feeds it as much mana as he can spare, enough to last a long time — a day, maybe two — and steps back.

The Pillow looks at the fire. Then it lies down in front of it, slowly, with a long contented groan, and closes its eyes.

He walks toward the stones without looking back. He doesn't have to look back. He can hear it breathing all the way to the edge of the ring.

* * *

He stops at the edge of the circle on the twentieth day and sits down to rest before he goes in.

He's thinner. He can feel it in his ribs, in the way his belt sits, in the way his hands look when he holds them up in front of his face. The forearm has closed into a long tight line that pulls when he makes a fist. Two toes he's not sure about. The edge of his ear. A dozen small cuts he's stopped counting. He's so tired that the tiredness has become a kind of floor he stands on.

And under all of that, still, he can feel it: the itch is gone. Completely gone. He's spent every bit of it, and some he didn't know he had.

“Ready?” Ciel asks. ᶜ

He takes the chocolate out of the inventory.

He looks at it for a long time. The wrapper is the kind Yuka likes, with the little drawing on it that she says is a bird and he says is a cloud. It's been in the inventory twenty days, untouched, exactly as she put it in his hand. *For later.*

He puts it back.

“After,” he says. ᴹ

He stands up, and walks into the ring.
