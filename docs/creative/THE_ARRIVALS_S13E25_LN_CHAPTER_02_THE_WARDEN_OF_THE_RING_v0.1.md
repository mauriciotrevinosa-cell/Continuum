# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 8 — The Warden of the Ring

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E25
**Continuity state:** Red gate, local days 20–21. The ring's guardian: a seated armoured figure of ice that rebuilds itself from the standing stones and pulls heat out of everything near it, including CE in motion. Shrine cuts close as fast as they open; Blue and Red buy space; the wheel turns twice more at the cold. Noise finds the one warm point in the ring, a core under the seat, which the warden rebuilds around. Mau breaks the stones one by one with Dismantle and Authority so it has less to rebuild from, then puts Purple through the core. CE almost gone; he passes out briefly on the stone. Injuries: left hand frozen to the grip and torn free, cracked ribs on the right, the old forearm cut reopened.

*When: The twentieth day inside, going into the twenty-first. About thirteen minutes outside.*

The ring is quiet.

Inside the standing stones the air doesn't move at all. The cold is a weight, pressing on his shoulders, on the top of his head, on the inside of his chest every time he breathes. The ground is not frozen earth any more but smooth stone, black and polished, so smooth he can see a dim grey shape of himself in it when he looks down.

At the centre there's a seat.

It's carved from the same black stone as the floor, high-backed and plain, and there's someone sitting in it.

Something. It's tall, taller than Gojo, taller than the door of the house. It's dressed in armour, or it is armour, plates of something that looks like old iron furred over with frost, and between the plates where a body should be there's ice: clear and deep, with a faint dim shine inside it like light trapped in a frozen lake. It has a helm with no face. Across its knees lies a spear as long as the Pillow, its blade a single sheet of ice.

It doesn't move when he steps into the ring.

It doesn't move when he walks halfway across the floor toward it.

“It's watching you,” Noise says. ᴺ

“I know.” ᴹ

He stops ten paces from the seat. He doesn't draw the daggers yet. He looks at it with the eyes wide open and lets them read.

There's a lot to read. The ice is old. Older than the towers, older than the long things. The armour is a shell; what's inside it is cold the way the plain is cold, deep and total, a cold that doesn't just sit in a place but *takes*. He can feel it pulling at him from here, at the heat in his skin, at the warmth in his breath. At the CE moving in his body. He can feel it reaching for that, too.

“It eats heat,” Ciel says. “Any kind. Including yours. Including what you use.” ᶜ

“I noticed.” ᴹ

The helm turns. Slowly. Toward him.

And the warden stands up.

* * *

It's fast.

He doesn't expect that. Nothing that big should move that fast, but it does, the spear coming off its knees and around in one long flat sweep that would take his head off at the neck if Infinity weren't open. The blade stops a finger's width from his throat, held in the space that never closes.

And the space starts to frost over.

He can see it happening. The cold is reaching into Infinity itself, into the CE that holds the neutral open, and drinking it. The space between the blade and his skin is shrinking. Slowly. But shrinking.

He throws himself back. The blade comes after him. He puts Blue in the air between them and the warden lurches toward the pull and the spear goes wide, and he's ten paces away, then twenty, breathing hard, watching the frost melt off the edges of his neutral as he feeds it more.

“That's going to be expensive,” Noise says. ᴺ

“Everything's going to be expensive.” ᴹ

* * *

He tries Shrine.

Dismantle first, a clean line across the warden's chest where two plates meet. It goes in. He sees it go in, the ice parting like water. And then he watches the cut close behind it, the ice flowing back together from both sides, the plates sliding back into place, as though nothing had happened at all.

Cleave. A heavier cut, measured for the toughness the eyes are reading. It goes deeper. The warden staggers. One arm comes away at the shoulder and hits the polished floor with a sound like a dropped bell.

Then the arm drags itself back across the stone and reattaches.

He watches it happen with his mouth open.

“It's rebuilding,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“From what?” ᴹ

“Look at the stones.” ᶜ

He looks. One of the standing stones at the edge of the ring, the one nearest the warden's fallen arm, has a fresh scar down its face, a long shallow scoop where something was taken out of it. As he watches, a thin line of frost runs from the scar across the floor to the warden's shoulder and disappears into the joint.

“It's drawing on the ring,” Noise says. “Every stone is a reserve. You'd have to break all of them.” ᴺ

He counts. Twenty-four stones. Each one taller than a tower.

“Great,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

The warden doesn't give him time to think.

It comes at him again, the spear moving in long efficient arcs, and he goes back and back across the polished floor, opening and closing the neutral only where the blade will land so the cold has less to drink. Red sends it skidding. Blue drags it off balance. Zoltraak goes through its helm and out the back and the helm reforms around the hole. He gets inside the spear's reach, once, with the daggers, and opens three of the joints between plates in three quick strokes, and the cold comes up through the dagger handles into his hand so fast that when he tries to let go, he can't.

His left hand is frozen to the grip.

He tears it free. He feels the skin of his palm stay behind on the handle. He doesn't let himself look.

Above and behind him, the wheel turns. *Click.*

The next touch of cold bites less. Not much. Enough.

* * *

“There's something under the seat,” Noise says, somewhere in the middle of it. ᴺ

He's on one knee, breathing in short sharp gasps because the cold has got into his chest and every breath feels like swallowing ice. His ribs on the right side are cracked; the spear's haft caught him there two exchanges ago. The long cut on his forearm has opened again and he can feel the warmth of the blood running down his wrist, the only warm thing on him.

“What,” he gets out. ᴹ

“Warm,” Noise says. “The only warm point in the ring. Under the seat. It's small. Every time the warden rebuilds, it draws from the stones, but the shape it rebuilds *around* is that.” ᴺ

He looks at the seat.

He can't see anything. Black stone, high-backed, plain. But when he reaches with the eyes, past the cold and the pressure and the frost, there it is: a tiny point, buried deep. Like a coal in the ash of a fire that went out a thousand years ago. Like the one warm thing on a frozen plain.

He thinks of the Pillow, lying down beside the fire.

“If I hit that,” he says, “does it stop?” ᴹ

“If you hit it while it still has stones to rebuild from, it rebuilds the core too,” Ciel says. “You'd have to take the reserve away first. Or most of it.” ᶜ

Twenty-four stones.

He stands up.

* * *

He does it the slow way, because there isn't a fast one.

He goes round the ring. That's the whole plan. He leads the warden around the edge of the circle, staying just out of the spear's reach, and as he passes each stone he breaks it.

Dismantle doesn't do it alone; the stones are too big, and too cold, and the cuts close. So he cuts and then he *pulls*. Authority takes hold of the upper half of each stone as the cut goes through it and drags it sideways, off its base, so that by the time the frost can flow back into the gap there's nothing on the other side for it to flow into. The stone falls. It hits the polished floor and shatters into pieces too small and too scattered to be any use.

One. Two. Three.

The warden understands what he's doing by the fourth. It stops chasing him and starts trying to get in front of him, between him and the next stone, and that's worse, because now it's coming at him head-on.

Seven. Eight.

The wheel turns again somewhere around the tenth. He barely notices.

Twelve.

He's running on nothing now. The CE is going down faster than it ever has, the cold drinking it from every technique the instant it leaves him, and there's a bright hard pain behind his eyes that he knows from the duel, the warning that comes before the brain starts to pay. Ciel is saying something about it. He hears her. He keeps going.

Sixteen. Eighteen.

The warden is smaller now. He can see it. Every time it rebuilds, it rebuilds a little less: the armour thinner, the ice duller, the spear shorter. It has less to draw from and it knows it.

Twenty-two. Twenty-three.

He stops in front of the last stone. The warden is between him and it, its spear raised, the ice inside its armour gone almost dark.

He doesn't break the stone. He goes past the warden instead, under the spear, so close he feels the cold of it graze the back of his neck, and stands in front of the seat.

“Mau,” Ciel says. “If you do this now—” ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

He holds out both hands. The left one is raw and bleeding where the skin tore away. He doesn't look at it.

Blue in the left. Red in the right. He's done it so many times now: in the yard, with Gojo watching, and once in a place where it almost didn't work at all. He brings them together.

Purple goes through the seat and the floor and the small warm point buried under them, and through the last standing stone behind, and out the far side of the ring into the haze.

* * *

For a moment there's no sound at all.

Then the warden comes apart. Not broken; *undone*, the ice running out of its armour like water out of a cracked jar, the plates falling one by one onto the polished floor with a sound like distant bells. The spear melts in mid-air. The helm hits the stone last, rolls a little way, and stops.

The cold lets go of him.

He feels it go. All at once, like a hand that's been holding him underwater suddenly opening. The air in the ring is still cold, but it's ordinary cold now, winter cold, the kind you can breathe.

He sits down. He doesn't decide to. His legs just stop.

He lies back on the polished stone and looks up at the flat grey ceiling, which Noise tells him is still red, and which looks to him the same as it always has, and breathes. In. Out. Each breath hurts on the right side where the ribs are cracked. Each breath is warmer than the last.

“Mau,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“I'm here.” ᴹ

“You're almost empty.” ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Stay awake.” ᶜ

“I'm trying.” ᴹ

He isn't, really. The floor is cold and smooth under his back and he's so tired that the tiredness has become a kind of warmth of its own, and the last thing he thinks, before the grey ceiling goes darker and further away, is that he still hasn't eaten the chocolate.
