# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 9 — Operator

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S13E26
**Continuity state:** Red gate, local day 21, then outside. Mau wakes on the stone after a short faint. The system speaks: it confirms Mau as the operator and states that system improvements must be completed by the operator. The clear reward grants control over this gate's future difficulty and loot. Loot: frost cores from the warden's armour and the long things, a shard of the spear-ice that will not melt, plates of the frosted armour. He eats the chocolate. The gate opens. Total inside ≈ 21 days; outside ≈ 14–15 minutes. He walks out into the quarry and goes home.

*When: The end of the twenty-first day inside. Outside, Fern has looked at the clock twice.*

He wakes up because something is talking.

Not Ciel. Not Noise. It's a voice he hasn't heard in a long time, flat and clear and without any warmth in it at all, the voice that comes with boxes of light in front of his eyes. He used to hear it every day, at the beginning. He hears it rarely now.

He opens his eyes. There's a box hanging in the air above him, against the grey ceiling.

> **[Gate cleared.]**
>
> **[Guardian of the Red Ring defeated.]**

He lies there and looks at it.

“How long was I out?” he asks. ᴹ

“Four minutes,” Ciel says. “In here.” ᶜ

“So about four seconds out there.” ᴹ

“About that.” ᶜ

He laughs, which hurts the ribs, so he stops.

* * *

The box changes.

> **[Operator identity confirmed.]**

He frowns at it.

> **[Notice: System improvements must be completed by the operator.]**

“What does that mean?” he asks. ᴹ

“I don't know,” Noise says. ᴺ

That's unusual enough that he turns his head to look at nothing, the way he does when he wants to look at Noise and can't.

“You don't know?” ᴹ

“I can read the words,” Noise says. “I can't read what's behind them. It's not saying it to me. It's saying it to you.” ᴺ

“Operator,” Mau says slowly. ᴹ

The system has never been interested in what to call him. It tells him what he got and what it cost. It has never used that word.

The box changes again before he can think about it any further.

> **[Clear reward: Gate Authority (this gate).]**
>
> **[The operator may adjust the future difficulty and reward tier of this gate.]**

And underneath, smaller:

> **[Adjustment available on exit.]**

He reads it three times.

“I can change it,” he says. “The gate. I can make it harder. Or easier.” ᴹ

“And what it drops,” Ciel says. “That's what it says.” ᶜ

“Can it do that?” ᴹ

“Apparently it can.” ᶜ

He lies there on the cold black stone, with the broken armour of the warden scattered around him and the last of the standing stones lying in pieces at the edge of the ring, and thinks about a gate that nobody wanted, that he could make into anything. A training ground. A place for Yuka to practise where the walls wouldn't fall on her. Somewhere Maki and Yuta could go together. Somewhere that drops what Mai needs, or what Senku needs, or what the house needs.

“Ramiris will lose her mind,” he says. ᴹ

“Ramiris will want to take it apart,” Noise says. ᴺ

“She can look at it. She can't take it apart.” ᴹ

* * *

He sits up eventually.

It takes a while. The ribs are bad. The left hand is worse: the palm raw and weeping where the skin stayed on the dagger handle, the fingers stiff and swollen. He closes what he can with RCT, which isn't much, because there isn't much left. Enough to stop the bleeding. Enough to make a fist, if he doesn't mind the fist hurting.

He finds the daggers. One of them still has a patch of his skin frozen to the grip. He looks at it for a moment and then wipes it on his trousers and puts the dagger back on his belt.

Then he goes looking for loot, because Fern will ask.

There's a lot of it. The warden's armour, for a start: twenty or thirty plates of old iron furred with frost that doesn't melt when he touches it, cold enough to sting his good hand. He stacks them in the inventory with a label Fern will appreciate. *Armour, frost, do not touch bare-handed.* Inside each plate, where the ice was, there's a small hard round thing like a stone, clear as glass, with a faint dim shine at its centre.

“Cores,” Noise says. “They hold cold. A lot of it. Mai will want them.” ᴺ

“Mai will want *all* of them.” ᴹ

He gathers them up. He finds one more on the floor near the seat, bigger than the others, as big as his fist. And beside it, half-buried in what's left of the seat, a long thin shard of the spear-blade: clear ice, sharp as a razor, that hasn't melted even though the warden that held it is gone.

He picks it up carefully. It's cold, but it doesn't bite the way the warden's cold did.

“It's not going to melt,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“Senku,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Senku,” Ciel agrees. ᶜ

On the way out of the ring he finds the cores of the long things, too, where the bodies he left have finally dissolved into the frozen ground over three weeks: dozens of them, smaller and duller than the warden's, scattered across the plain like pebbles. He doesn't go back for all of them. He takes the ones in the towers he passes.

* * *

When he's done, he sits down on a fallen piece of stone at the edge of the ring and takes the chocolate out of the inventory.

He holds it for a while.

The wrapper has the little drawing on it. The one Yuka says is a bird. He's always said it was a cloud. Looking at it now, in the flat grey light at the end of a frozen plain, after twenty-one days, he thinks it might be a bird after all. It has a beak, sort of. If you squint.

He opens it.

It's broken in two places, from being in his pocket before it went in the inventory. He doesn't care. He eats the first piece very slowly. It tastes like the house. It tastes like the porch in the afternoon, and Rem's kitchen, and the bottom of the bag Umaru always leaves for him. It tastes like Yuka's hand closing his fingers around it at the door.

*For later.*

He eats all of it. Every piece. He licks the inside of the wrapper. Then he folds the wrapper very small and puts it back in his pocket, because she'll want to know he ate it, and this is how he'll prove it.

“Better?” Ciel asks. ᶜ

“Much better.” ᴹ

* * *

The gate opens.

He feels it before he sees it: a change in the air, a pressure lifting. Then there's a tall oval of light standing on the plain about fifty paces away, faintly humming, exactly where there was nothing a moment ago.

A box hangs beside it.

> **[Exit available.]**
>
> **[Gate Authority: adjust now?]**

He looks at it for a long moment.

“Not now,” he says. ᴹ

> **[Adjustment deferred. Gate Authority retained.]**

“Good,” Ciel says. “Ramiris will want to look at that first. And Noise.” ᶜ

“And Yuka,” Mau says. “She'll want to pick.” ᴹ

He stands up. His legs hold. He looks back once, across the plain, toward the haze where the towers are, and the place beyond them where a big shaggy shape is lying in front of a little fire that will burn for another day or two. He can't see it from here. Even the eyes can't reach that far.

“Goodbye,” he says anyway. ᴹ

Then he walks through the gate.

* * *

The quarry is exactly as he left it.

The pool of rainwater at the bottom. The scrub in the cracks. The cut stone walls. The air is mild and smells of wet rock and green things and the faint smoke of somebody's chimney a long way off, and it's so warm compared to where he's been that for a moment he just stands there with his eyes closed and breathes it.

“How long?” he asks. ᴹ

“Fourteen minutes and a little over,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“Fourteen minutes.” ᴹ

He opens his eyes. Looks at the sky. It's the same sky, grey and soft, with the same light in it as when he left. The same morning.

Twenty-one days. Fourteen minutes.

He starts to laugh, and the ribs stop him again, and he stands there in the bottom of the quarry with his arm round his side, laughing as much as the ribs will let him, which isn't much.

* * *

He walks home.

He could use Sky. He could call Kaisel. He doesn't. He walks, slowly, because he wants to; because his body has done everything he asked of it for three weeks and it would like, now, to do something slow. The road from the quarry goes along the river and up through the fields and past the orchard, and he takes all of it at a walking pace, with his raw hand held against his chest and his ribs complaining at every step and the folded chocolate wrapper in his pocket.

He's so tired he can feel the edges of the world going soft.

He can see the house from the top of the last field. The roof. The chimney, smoking. The porch. Somebody on the porch.

He knows who it is before he's close enough to see. She's the only thing on the whole hillside with colour.

She's standing up. She's been sitting, and now she's standing, and she's shading her eyes against the light to look down the field toward him. He sees her see him. He sees her start down the steps, and stop, and start again, faster.

Fourteen minutes.

He thinks, distantly, that she can't have been worried. Fourteen minutes is nothing. Fourteen minutes is a cup of tea.

Then he gets close enough to see her face, and understands that she saw him walking — that she saw the way he's walking — and that fourteen minutes doesn't matter at all.
