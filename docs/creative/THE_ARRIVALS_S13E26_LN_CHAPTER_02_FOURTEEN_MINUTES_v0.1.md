# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 10 — Fourteen Minutes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E26
**Continuity state:** B+309, late morning. Yuka's point of view. Mau comes home fourteen minutes after he left, three weeks thinner. Shoko sees to the hand and ribs on the couch. When she's done, Mau sits; Yuka climbs onto his lap facing him, sees how tired he is before he can say he's fine, draws his head against her chest. He lets himself fall into her and is asleep in moments. Yuka tells people who come in, quietly, that he's sleeping. Mai stays and talks with her in low voices. Yuka falls asleep with her head on his shoulder. The chocolate wrapper.

*When: Fourteen minutes after Mau walked out of the yard. Yuka had just sat down on the porch with a cup of tea she hadn't touched yet.*

She'd only just sat down.

That's what Yuka keeps thinking about, afterward. She had walked him to the edge of the yard and pushed him in the chest and told him to go and get tired, and then she had gone inside and helped Shuna with the breakfast plates and listened to Milim and Ori argue about whether dragons sleep, and then she had made a cup of tea, and taken it out to the porch, and sat down. She had been sitting for maybe a minute. The tea was still too hot to drink.

And then there he was, coming up over the top of the field.

At first she thought something had gone wrong at the gate — that he'd got there and found it closed, or taken, or not what Fern had said, and turned round and come home. That happened. It was a perfectly ordinary thing. She stood up to call out and ask him what had happened.

Then she saw the way he was walking.

She has seen Mau walk like that before. Not often. Enough. She knows the shape of it: one arm held in close against his side, his shoulders slightly hunched over it, every step placed a little carefully, as if the ground might not be where he expects it. She saw him walk like that after the Sukuna fight. She saw him not walk at all, after the long one.

She's down the porch steps before she's decided to go.

* * *

He's thinner.

She sees that before she's halfway down the field, and it stops her for a moment, because it doesn't make sense. He left fourteen minutes ago. People don't get thinner in fourteen minutes. But his face is narrower than it was at breakfast, the bones of his cheeks sharper under the skin, and his shirt hangs differently, and there's a long strip torn off the bottom of it that's wrapped round his forearm and stiff with something dark.

Then she's close enough to see his eyes, and he's smiling at her. Tired. Enormously tired. But smiling.

“Hi,” he says. ᴹ

“*Hi?*” ʸ

“I'm fine.” ᴹ

“You are *not*—” ʸ

“Three weeks,” he says. “It was red. Twenty-one days in there. Fourteen minutes out here. I'm fine. I'm just tired.” ᴹ

She stares at him.

“A red gate,” she says. ʸ

“A red gate.” ᴹ

“You went to a gate *nobody wanted*.” ʸ

“Fern's going to have to add a mark,” he agrees. ᴹ

She doesn't know whether to hit him or hold onto him. She does the second, carefully, because she can see how he's holding his ribs, and he lets out a long breath against her hair, and she feels his whole weight lean into her for a moment before he straightens up again.

“Shoko,” she says. ʸ

“Shoko,” he agrees. ᴹ

* * *

Shoko is already coming out of the house by the time they reach the porch. Somebody must have seen them from a window. She takes one look at Mau and says, “Couch,” ˢʰ in a voice that leaves no room for anything else, and he goes to the couch.

It takes her a while.

The ribs first: three cracked on the right, which Shoko feels with her fingers while Mau stares at the ceiling and doesn't make a sound. Then the forearm, where she unwraps the strip of shirt and looks at the long tight line underneath and says something under her breath that Yuka doesn't catch. Then his left hand.

Yuka has to look away from the hand. The whole palm is raw. Not cut, not burned, just *gone*, the skin stripped off it as though he'd pressed it against something and pulled it away too fast.

“Frozen to a handle,” Mau says, when Shoko asks. ᴹ

“And you pulled it off.” ˢʰ

“It was trying to kill me.” ᴹ

“The *handle* was trying to kill you?” ˢʰ

“The thing holding the other end.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him for a long moment. Then she bends over the hand and starts working, and Yuka watches the raw red surface slowly film over and close, and holds Mau's other hand, and doesn't say anything.

The toes. The edge of his ear. A dozen small cuts. Shoko finds all of them. She asks how long he went without eating properly, and he says the food ran out on the tenth day, and Shoko doesn't say anything to that either, but Yuka sees her jaw tighten.

“Lizards,” Mau adds helpfully. ᴹ

“Lizards,” Shoko repeats. ˢʰ

“They tasted like nothing.” ᴹ

“Eat something real today. A lot of something real. And then sleep. Nothing else.” ˢʰ

“Yes, Shoko.” ᴹ

She packs up and goes, and stops in the doorway to look back at him, and then at Yuka, and Yuka understands that look perfectly, and nods.

* * *

When it's just the two of them, Mau sits up on the couch and puts his feet on the floor.

He's going to say something. Yuka can see it coming. He's going to say *I'm fine* again, or *I should tell Fern*, or *I should take the loot to Mai*, and then he's going to get up and go and do it, and fall over somewhere in the hallway.

She doesn't let him.

She climbs into his lap. Not sideways, the way she sits when she wants to lean on him. Facing him, a knee on either side of his hips, her hands on his shoulders, so that he has to look at her.

“Yuka—” ᴹ

“Don't.” ʸ

She looks at him properly.

Up close, he's worse. There are shadows under his eyes like bruises. His face is thinner, much thinner, and there's a looseness in it she recognises, the look of somebody who has been holding himself up by force for so long that he's forgotten how to stop. His eyes keep wanting to close. She watches them try and watches him drag them open again.

He was going to tell her he was fine.

She puts her hand on the back of his head, very gently, and draws it forward until his face is against her chest.

For a second he holds himself there, stiff, as though he isn't sure what she wants. Then she puts her other arm round his shoulders and holds on, and she feels him understand.

He lets go.

It's the strangest thing. She's held him a thousand times. She's held him when he was hurt, and when he was asleep, and when he was so far away inside himself she wasn't sure he knew she was there. But she has never felt him do this: just *fall*. All of him, all at once, into her, as though she's the floor. His arms come up and go round her waist and hold on. His whole weight settles against her. She feels the long breath go out of him, and then another one, slower, and then another, slower still.

He's asleep before the fourth.

She holds his head against her chest and doesn't move.

* * *

People come in.

They don't mean to. It's the middle of the day and the common room is where people go. Milim comes in first, loudly, already halfway through asking whether Mau's back, and Yuka looks at her over Mau's head and puts a finger to her lips.

Milim stops in the doorway. Her eyes go wide.

“He's asleep,” Yuka whispers. ʸ

Milim nods very seriously and backs out of the room on her toes, which for Milim is an act of enormous self-control.

Umaru comes in a while later, sees them, and puts a bag of chips down on the table within Mau's reach without a word, as if he might wake up and want it. Then she goes away. Gojo looks in from the hallway, takes in the whole scene in one glance, and smiles in a way Yuka has never seen him smile before, small and real, and goes away without saying anything at all. Fern comes to ask about the register and stops in the doorway and changes her mind.

Ori comes in and stands very still beside the couch for a long time, looking at Mau's face where it's pressed against Yuka.

“Is he hurt?” she whispers. ᴼ

“A little. Shoko fixed it. He's just tired.” ʸ

Ori thinks about this. Then she takes the blanket off the back of the armchair and climbs up on the couch beside them and tucks it, very carefully, around Mau's back. It takes her three tries. When it's done to her satisfaction, she climbs down and goes away as quietly as she came.

Yuka has to close her eyes for a moment.

* * *

Mai comes in last.

She's carrying the box from the workshop, the one she keeps her best scraps in, because she wanted to show Yuka something about the island cloth. She sees them and stops. Yuka watches her take it in: Mau asleep with his face against Yuka's chest, his arms round her, the blanket Ori tucked in, the bag of chips on the table.

Mai puts the box down very quietly and sits in the armchair.

They don't say anything for a while.

“He looks like he weighs nothing,” Mai says at last, very low. ᴹᵃ

“He weighs a lot,” Yuka whispers. “I can't feel my legs.” ʸ

Mai smiles. “Do you want me to—” ᴹᵃ

“No.” ʸ

It comes out faster than she means it to. Mai's smile gets wider.

“No,” Yuka says again, more quietly. “I'm all right. I want him to sleep.” ʸ

Mai nods. She leans back in the armchair and looks at them both, and for a while they just talk, the way they used to talk in the workshop before everything got so busy: about the island cloth, and what Mai is going to make with it, and the hat, which Mai has heard about from at least four different people and wants to see. About Momo's skirt. About whether Senku will notice if they borrow his good scissors. About nothing at all.

Yuka didn't know how much she'd missed this. Mai's voice, low and dry and patient. The workshop smell that's always on her hands. The way Mai listens, with her whole face, as though whatever you're saying is the most interesting thing anyone's said all day.

She says so. Not all of it. Just: “I missed you.” ʸ

Mai looks at her.

“I missed you too,” she says. “It was too quiet in the workshop. Nobody to tell me the colour of things.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka laughs, very softly, so as not to wake Mau. His arms tighten round her in his sleep and then relax.

* * *

At some point, Yuka's own eyes start to close.

She doesn't notice it happening. The room is warm and quiet and Mai's voice is low, and Mau's breathing under her hands is slow and even and steady, like the sea at the island, and somewhere in the middle of Mai telling her about a stitch, Yuka's head goes down onto Mau's shoulder, and stays there.

She's half aware of Mai getting up. Of the box being picked up. Of something being put on the table beside the chips: something small, which rustles.

She wakes, much later, with the light gone long and gold across the floor, and Mau still asleep under her, and on the table beside the chips, there's a chocolate wrapper folded very small. Somebody must have taken it out of his pocket when they put the blanket over him. Ori, probably. Or Mai.

Yuka unfolds it with one hand. It's the one with the little bird on it. It's empty. Licked clean.

*For later.*

She folds it back up and holds it in her fist and puts her head back down on his shoulder, and doesn't move again for a long time.
