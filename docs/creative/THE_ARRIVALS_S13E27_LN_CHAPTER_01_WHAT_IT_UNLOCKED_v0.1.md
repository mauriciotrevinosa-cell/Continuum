# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 11 — What It Unlocked

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E27
**Continuity state:** B+309, evening. Mau wakes and eats everything Shuna puts in front of him. The loot goes to the table: frost cores (Mai), the unmelting spear-shard (Senku), frost-iron plates (Fern registers). Fern adds a fourth mark to the register. Ramiris and Noise read the Gate Authority: a standing permission over that one gate, deferred until chosen. The house argues about what to make it; Yuka gets the final word on the first setting. Mau tells the Pillow story at dinner.

*When: Mau sleeps until the light is long across the floor. When he wakes up, Yuka is asleep on his shoulder, and his legs have gone numb under her.*

He eats four plates.

Shuna keeps bringing them. She doesn't ask whether he wants more. She just watches the plate in front of him empty and takes it away and brings another one, and he keeps eating, and around the third plate he realises that the whole kitchen has gone quiet to watch.

“Lizards,” Yuka explains to the room. ʸ

“For eleven days,” Mau adds, with his mouth full. ᴹ

There's a general murmur of horror. Rem goes to the pantry and comes back with a whole loaf and puts it down next to his elbow without a word.

He eats half of it.

* * *

After dinner, he empties the inventory onto the big table.

The plates come out first. Twenty-eight of them, thick and heavy and furred with a fine white frost that doesn't melt even in the warm kitchen. He sets them down carefully, with his good hand, and tells everyone not to touch them bare-handed, and Milim immediately touches one bare-handed and yelps and sticks her finger in her mouth.

“I *said*—” ᴹ

“I wanted to see how cold.” ᴹⁱ

“How cold was it?” ᴹ

Milim considers. “Very,” she says, around her finger. ᴹⁱ

Then the cores, and Mai makes a sound Mau has never heard her make, a sort of low involuntary *oh*, and comes round the table so fast she nearly knocks over Senku. There are dozens of them. The small dull ones from the long things, and the clearer brighter ones from the warden's armour, and the big one, as big as Mau's fist, which he puts down last, in the middle of the table. Mai picks up one of the small ones and holds it up to the lamp.

“It's holding cold,” she says. “It's *full* of it. Mau, do you know what I could—” ᴹᵃ

“They're yours.” ᴹ

“All of them?” ᴹᵃ

“All of them.” ᴹ

Mai looks at him. Then at the cores. Then at him again. Her face does something complicated, and then she starts gathering them up into the front of her apron with both hands, very fast, as if he might change his mind.

He puts the shard down last.

It's as long as his forearm and as thin as a blade, clear all the way through, and it lies on the wooden table without a single drop of water forming under it. Senku leans in. He doesn't touch it. He looks at it from one side and then the other, his face very close, his eyes narrowed.

“It's not melting,” he says. ˢᵉ

“No.” ᴹ

“It's ice.” ˢᵉ

“Noise says it's ice.” ᴹ

“Ice melts. That's the *whole thing* about ice.” ˢᵉ

“This one doesn't.” ᴹ

Senku straightens up slowly. There's a look on his face that Mau has seen before, usually right before Senku disappears into the workshop for three days and comes out with something that changes everyone's life.

“Can I have it?” Senku asks. ˢᵉ

“You can have it.” ᴹ

“Can I take it apart?” ˢᵉ

“It's a piece of ice, Senku. You can do whatever you want with it.” ᴹ

Senku picks it up with a cloth, very carefully, and carries it out of the kitchen like a man carrying a newborn, and doesn't come back.

* * *

Fern writes it all in the register. Every plate. Every core. The shard. When she's done she turns back to the entry from this morning, the one with three marks beside it and *dinner* in the margin, and adds a fourth mark.

“What's that one?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“It means *red*,” Fern says. ᶠᵉ

“You have a mark for that?” ᴹ

“I do now.” ᶠᵉ

She turns the book around so he can see. Beside the new mark, in her very small, very straight handwriting, she's written: *Cleared. Mau. 21 days / 14 min. Do not send anyone else without asking him first.*

And under that, the note she wrote this morning that he couldn't read upside down. He can read it now.

*Make sure he eats.*

He looks at it for a long time. Fern doesn't say anything. She just turns the book back around and closes it.

* * *

Ramiris arrives after dinner, because Rimuru sent word, and she comes in through the window instead of the door because she says it's faster, which it isn't.

“Show me,” she says. ᴿᵃᵐ

He doesn't know how to show her. It isn't a thing. It's a box of light that only he can see, and a word the system has never used before, and a permission that's sitting somewhere at the edge of him, waiting.

So he tells her. All of it. *Operator identity confirmed. System improvements must be completed by the operator. Gate Authority (this gate). Adjustment deferred.* Ramiris listens with her small face screwed up in concentration, hovering over the table at the level of his eyes, and when he's done she doesn't say anything for a long moment.

“Noise,” she says finally. “What can you see?” ᴿᵃᵐ

Noise answers through Mau, the way it does when someone outside asks it directly. “A permission,” it says. “Attached to him. Attached to one gate. It's not doing anything. It's waiting for him to tell it what to do.” ᴺ

“Can you see what it *can* do?” ᴿᵃᵐ

“Some of it.” ᴺ

They talk for a long time after that. Ramiris asks questions and Noise answers them, and sometimes Ciel joins in with something she's noticed, and sometimes Mau has to translate when Noise's answer is too strange for anyone else to follow. He doesn't understand all of it. He doesn't try to. He sits with Yuka leaning on his good side and listens to the shape of it emerge.

The gate is his, in a narrow way. Not owned. *Operated.* He can set how hard it is, roughly. He can set what it drops, roughly, within whatever the gate is willing to give. He can't make it into something it isn't. He can't move it. He can't give the permission to anyone else.

“You'd have to go in to change it,” Ramiris says. “Or stand at the entrance, at least. It's not something you can do from here.” ᴿᵃᵐ

“That's fine.” ᴹ

“And this other thing.” Ramiris frowns. “*System improvements must be completed by the operator.* I don't like that one.” ᴿᵃᵐ

“Why not?” ᴹ

“Because it's telling you something,” Ramiris says, “and it's not telling you what. Things that do that usually want something from you later.” ᴿᵃᵐ

Nobody says anything for a moment.

“We'll watch it,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“We'll watch it,” Noise agrees. ᴺ

Ramiris looks at the space where she imagines Noise must be, somewhere around Mau's left ear. “You don't know what it means either,” she says. ᴿᵃᵐ

“No,” Noise admits. ᴺ

That bothers Mau more than anything else Ramiris has said.

* * *

Then the arguing starts.

It's a good argument. Everyone has an opinion about what the gate should be, and most of the opinions are loud.

Maki wants it hard. As hard as it'll go. “Somewhere Yuta and I can actually push,” she says, “without having to worry about the walls falling on somebody's grandmother.” ᴹᵃᵏ Yuta agrees, more quietly.

Mai wants it to drop cores. More cores. As many cores as it can be convinced to give, forever.

Gojo wants it to have a snack bar.

“It's a *dungeon*,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“A dungeon with a snack bar,” Gojo says. “Imagine how popular it would be.” ᴳ

Fern wants it predictable. Ori wants it to have a slide. Milim wants it to have a dragon, and when someone points out that Milim *is* a dragon, more or less, she says that's exactly why she wants another one, so she can fight it.

Shoko says, from the end of the table without looking up, “I want it to not send him home with no skin on his hand,” ˢʰ and that quiets everyone for a moment.

Mau lets them argue. He leans back in his chair and listens. He's still so tired he could fall asleep where he's sitting, and the voices are warm and loud and familiar, and every one of them is talking about what to do with a thing he brought home, and he finds that he likes that more than he expected to.

Then Yuka says, “He should decide,” ʸ and everybody looks at him.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

“It's yours.” ʸ

“It's the house's. I just went and got it.” ᴹ

He looks at her.

“You pick,” he says. “The first setting. You pick, and then if it's wrong, we change it.” ᴹ

Yuka opens her mouth. Closes it.

“Me?” ʸ

“You.” ᴹ

She thinks about it. Everybody watches her think about it. Maki looks hopeful. Mai looks very hopeful. Gojo makes a small gesture that might mean *snack bar*.

“Hard enough that I have to try,” Yuka says at last, slowly. “Not so hard anybody comes home without skin on their hand. Cores for Mai. And—” ʸ

“And something warm in it,” she says. “Somewhere. A place to rest. So nobody has to sleep on the cold floor.” ʸ

Mau looks at her for a long time.

“That's a good setting,” he says. ᴹ

“It's a *boring* setting,” Gojo says. ᴳ

* * *

He tells them about the Pillow at the end of the night.

He didn't mean to. Ori asks what lives in the gate, and he starts to tell her about the long things, and then he gets to the part where something big started following him on the third day, and suddenly the whole table is listening.

He tells it properly. The shape in the haze. The two nights without sleeping. The way it lay down a hundred paces off and breathed like wind through a doorway. The fifth day, when it walked up to the fire and lay down practically on top of him, and groaned, and closed its eyes.

“It wanted the *fire*?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“It wanted the fire.” ᴹ

“For three days?” ᴼ

“For three days.” ᴹ

Ori starts to laugh. Then Milim. Then the whole table, all of them, Gojo with his head down on the wood again, Maki with her hand over her mouth, Rem crying into Umaru's shoulder, and Mau sits in the middle of it with Yuka laughing against his arm and finds that he's laughing too, even though it hurts the ribs, even though he's so tired he can barely see.

“Did you bring it home?” Ori asks, when she can breathe. ᴼ

“No. It lives there. It eats the walls.” ᴹ

“Can I go and see it?” ᴼ

He thinks about the gate. About Yuka's setting. *Something warm in it, somewhere. A place to rest.*

“Maybe,” he says. “One day. If it's still there.” ᴹ

Ori nods, very satisfied, as though that settles it.

That night he falls asleep before Yuka has finished getting into bed. She tells him in the morning that he started snoring before his head was all the way down on the pillow, and he doesn't believe her, and she says she has witnesses, and he asks who, and she says Beru, and he can't argue with Beru.
