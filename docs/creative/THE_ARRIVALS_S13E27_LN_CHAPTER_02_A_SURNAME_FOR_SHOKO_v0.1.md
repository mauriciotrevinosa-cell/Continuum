# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 12 — A Surname for Shoko

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S13E27
**Continuity state:** B+310, morning. Shoko's check-up after the gate. Mau tells her about the island and about how he's been with Yuka. Shoko demands he take her to the island. He brings up Anko; she asks about Hinata; Gojo can come if he behaves. Mau considers taking Ieiri because Shoko is the closest thing he's had to a mother, as he told her in S9. She looks out the window, thinks through the idea aloud and answers her own questions. When he doesn't reply to a direct question about Yuka, Shoko finally turns to him. His gaze is vacant and his nose is bleeding. She catches him as he falls.

*When: The morning after the gate. Shoko wants to look at the hand and the ribs again in daylight, and Mau goes to the clinic without being asked twice, which she notes.*

The clinic smells of the soap Shoko uses and the faint smoke of a cigarette she isn't supposed to have had.

“Sit,” she says. ˢʰ

He sits on the edge of the bed. She takes his left hand and turns it over and looks at the palm, which is new and tender, the skin thin as paper. She presses it in two places with her thumb and watches his face while she does it.

“Hurts?” ˢʰ

“A little.” ᴹ

“Liar. It hurts a lot.” ˢʰ

“It hurts a medium amount.” ᴹ

She snorts and lets go of the hand and moves on to the ribs. He lifts his shirt. She runs her fingers along the right side, slowly, feeling for something, and he stares at the ceiling and counts the cracks in the plaster.

“Better,” she says. “Not good. Better.” ˢʰ

“That's what you always say.” ᴹ

“Because it's always true. You've never once come in here *good*.” ˢʰ

* * *

She doesn't tell him to go when she's done.

She sits down on the stool by the window instead and takes out a cigarette and doesn't light it, just turns it over in her fingers, which is what she does when she wants to talk and doesn't want to look like she wants to talk. He knows the signs by now. He stays where he is on the edge of the bed.

“So,” she says. “The island.” ˢʰ

He tells her.

He doesn't mean to tell her as much as he does. But she asks the right questions, the way she always does, short and sideways, and the answers come out of him one after another. The hotel. The little pool. The bird. The cape on the other side of the island, at sunset, which he doesn't describe in detail and which she doesn't ask about. The rain day. The rock. The turtle. The letter from the house, and how they read it at breakfast, and how Yuka had to stop and cover her mouth.

“She cried at Beru's part,” he says. ᴹ

“Everyone cried at Beru's part. Fern wrote it out. She cried while she was writing it.” ˢʰ

That stops him.

“Fern cried?” ᴹ

“Don't tell her I told you.” ˢʰ

He won't. He files it away somewhere he keeps things he'll never mention but will always know.

“And you,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

“What about me?” ᴹ

“How have you been. With her.” ˢʰ

She's still turning the cigarette. She isn't looking at him. That's on purpose; he knows that too. Shoko has always been better at asking hard questions when she's looking at something else.

He thinks about it.

“Good,” he says. Then, because that isn't enough, and she'll know it isn't: “Different. I don't know. Before the island I kept thinking — every time she was close, I kept thinking about when she wasn't. About the dungeon. About the door. I kept checking that she was still there.” ᴹ

Shoko nods slowly.

“And now?” ˢʰ

“Now I just—” He stops. Starts again. “Yesterday I came home and she climbed on me and put my head on her and I just fell asleep. Like that. I didn't check anything. I didn't think about anything. I just let go.” ᴹ

He hears how it sounds. He goes a little warm in the face and looks at the floor.

Shoko doesn't laugh. She's quiet for a moment.

“That's good, Mau,” she says. “That's very good.” ˢʰ

“Yeah?” ᴹ

“Yeah.” She finally looks at him. “That's the first time I've heard you say *let go* about something that wasn't your grip on a cliff.” ˢʰ

He laughs, and it hurts the ribs, and he doesn't care.

* * *

“I want to go,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

“Go where?” ᴹ

“The island. You're taking me.” ˢʰ

He blinks at her.

“You've been talking for ten minutes about little pools and fruit and doing nothing all day,” she says. “I've been patching this house together for as long as there's been a house. I want a little pool. I want fruit. I want to do nothing all day where nobody can find me.” ˢʰ

“Elmesia said we could bring people.” ᴹ

“Good. Bring me.” ˢʰ

“I'll ask.” ᴹ

“You'll *tell*. She likes you.” ˢʰ

He thinks about Elmesia at the table by the window, saying *bring the whole family next time if you must*. He thinks it'll be fine.

“Anko would want to come,” he says. ᴹ

Shoko's face does something interesting.

“Anko,” she says. ˢʰ

“She's been asking where we went. She'd lose her mind over the market. And the snorkelling. She'd try to fight the turtle.” ᴹ

“She would absolutely try to fight the turtle.” ˢʰ

“So?” ᴹ

Shoko turns the cigarette over twice. “Fine. Anko. If she promises not to fight the turtle.” ˢʰ

“She'll promise.” ᴹ

“She'll lie.” ˢʰ

“She'll lie,” he agrees. ᴹ

Shoko is quiet for a moment. Then, very casually, much too casually, she says: “And Hinata?” ˢʰ

He looks at her.

She's looking out the window. Very interested in the window.

“Hinata,” he says slowly. ᴹ

“She's been working too hard. Since the war. Somebody should make her rest.” ˢʰ

“Somebody should,” he agrees. ᴹ

“I'm just saying. If there's room.” ˢʰ

“There's room.” ᴹ

He waits. She doesn't turn around. He decides not to push.

“And Gojo,” he adds. ᴹ

That turns her around. “*Gojo?*” ˢʰ

“He'll find out. He always finds out. And then he'll sulk for a month.” ᴹ

Shoko closes her eyes in an expression of deep suffering.

“If he behaves,” she says. “If he behaves *perfectly*. If he says one word about little pools in that voice, he's swimming home.” ˢʰ

“I'll tell him.” ᴹ

“Tell him *exactly* that.” ˢʰ

They're both laughing now. He doesn't remember the last time he made Shoko laugh like this, properly, with her head tipped back, the cigarette forgotten in her fingers.

* * *

“Yuka wants to pick a name,” he says, when they've calmed down. ᴹ

“A name for what?” ˢʰ

“For us. A surname. She tried a few on the island. Neither of us liked any of them.” ᴹ

Shoko raises an eyebrow. “You don't have one.” ˢʰ

“I don't have anything. I just have Mau.” ᴹ

“And she has hers.” ˢʰ

“She doesn't want to keep it. She wants something that's both of ours. Or something new. She hasn't decided.” ᴹ

Shoko considers this with the seriousness she gives to any medical question.

“Take Gojo's,” she says. “He'd be unbearable about it for the rest of his life.” ˢʰ

“That's why I won't take Gojo's.” ᴹ

“Rimuru doesn't have one either. Not a real one.” ˢʰ

“Tempest,” Mau says. “That's a country, not a name.” ᴹ

“Frieren?” ˢʰ

“She doesn't have one. Elves don't.” ᴹ

“Then make one up.” ˢʰ

“That's what Yuka said.” ᴹ

He thinks about it. He thinks about the people in this house, and the names they brought with them from places he's never been and will never see. Ieiri. Zen'in. Okkotsu. Names that carry whole families behind them, whole histories, whole worlds.

“I could take yours,” he says. ᴹ

He's had it in his head since the island. Saying it in front of her feels different from trying it in his head on the way down to the pool. He waits while she decides what to do with it.

Shoko goes very still.

“Mine,” she says. ˢʰ

“Ieiri. Mau Ieiri.” He tries it out. “Do you remember when you asked me if I meant like a mother?” ᴹ

“I remember Gojo deciding he was your father. He's made it very hard to forget.” ˢʰ

He smiles. “You asked me first. I said you were the closest I'd had. I meant it then, too.” ᴹ

She puts the cigarette down on the sill. Her hand stays there a little longer than it needs to.

“There are a lot of names at that table,” Mau says. “Yours is the one I'd be happy to give if somebody asked where I got it.” ᴹ

“That's stupid,” she says. ˢʰ

“Is it?” ᴹ

“I'm still deciding whose idea that makes stupid.” ˢʰ

She puts her elbow on the sill and turns to face the window. He can see from the way she does it that she's decided how much of her face he gets to see. He lets her have the window.

* * *

From the clinic window Shoko can see the basket that somebody left on the porch rail. The wind has got hold of the cloth over it. One corner lifts and settles again.

“What would we tell Gojo?” she asks the glass. Her mouth twitches before she finishes the thought. “Nothing. He'd hear it once at dinner, put his hand on your shoulder and tell you how proud he is to have given you such an excellent name.” ˢʰ

She gives him room to laugh. The light has caught her reflection in the glass and she watches it closely. If she can see herself smile, she can tell how long it takes to get it under control.

“What would it be like to see it written down?” She looks at her hand on the sill as if she's already writing it. “Same as on the forms at school, I suppose. It'd look a little less lonely on the page.” ˢʰ

She hadn't meant to say the last part. She considers taking it back. There isn't a way to do that with him in the room. He's got a habit of letting a thing sit when it took someone a while to say it, and right now she's grateful for that.

“You wouldn't have to keep it on my account,” she goes on. “Suppose you and Yuka choose something else next year. Do you think I'd expect you to come tell me you changed your mind? I'd ask if she was bringing you round for tea.” ˢʰ

Her reflection is still smiling. She can work with that.

“Ask Yuka,” she says. The words come out more quietly than the rest. “I'd like to know what she thinks. Did you tell her you were thinking about Ieiri?” ˢʰ

She starts to turn her head, then gives him another moment. It's a direct question. Usually he gets as far as the first word of an answer before she's finished saying Yuka's name.

The basket cloth lifts again. She watches it settle. The silence is still there.

“Mau?” she says, and turns. ˢʰ

And Mau is looking at nothing.

His eyes are open. He's sitting exactly where he was, on the edge of the bed, with his hands in his lap. But he isn't looking at her any more, or at the window, or at anything in the room. His eyes are fixed on a point somewhere past her shoulder, wide and blank and unfocused, and they've changed. The blue at their centres is too bright. Much too bright. As though there's a light behind them that's been turned up and up and can't be turned down.

And there's blood on his lip.

It's coming from his nose. Not much. A thin bright line running down from one nostril over his upper lip, beading at the corner of his mouth.

He doesn't seem to notice.

“Mau,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

He doesn't answer.

“*Mau.*” ˢʰ

She's already moving. She's off the stool and reaching for him when his eyes roll up and he tips forward off the edge of the bed, all at once, like a puppet with the strings cut.

She catches him.

He's heavier than he looks. She goes down on one knee under his weight and gets her arms round his chest and holds him, and his head falls against her shoulder, and she can feel his heart going under her hand, much too fast, like something trying to get out.

“Ciel,” Shoko says, into his hair, very calm, very clear, the voice she uses in the worst moments, because she has learned that calm is the only thing that helps. “Ciel, something's wrong.” ˢʰ

And somewhere inside him, she hears something crack.
