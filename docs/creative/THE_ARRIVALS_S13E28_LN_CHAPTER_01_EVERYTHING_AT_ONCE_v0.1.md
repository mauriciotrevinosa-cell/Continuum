# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 13 — Everything at Once

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S13E28
**Continuity state:** B+310, morning, in the clinic. Shoko's point of view. Ciel hears the rupture and alerts Rimuru. Barriers and Mini Ciel fail; Mau's techniques begin to fire at once (neutral flickering, Blue pulling, shadows spilling, the wheel appearing). Shoko sees a domain starting to form. With what consciousness he has left, Mau tries to hold it in to protect her. Noise contains the rest and, with Ciel, stops the domain. Mau vomits blood and collapses. Rimuru, Yuka, Yuta arrive. They stop the bleeding and put him to sleep. Ciel and Noise explain: the refinement operation they had run a thousand times, the belief that the change in his eyes gave the load an outlet; it had only changed what he could process without noticing, and the surplus kept building.

*When: A few seconds after Shoko catches him. She will later work out that the whole thing took less than a minute.*

The crack is the first thing.

It isn't a sound, exactly. Shoko will try to describe it afterward, to Rimuru, to Yuka, to Ciel, and she'll never get it right. It's something she feels through her hands, through the place where they're holding him, a sudden deep *give* somewhere inside him, the way a frozen pond gives under a foot. Like something holding a great weight has just stopped holding it.

Then the air in the clinic goes strange.

* * *

“Rimuru,” Ciel says. ᶜ

Shoko hears it. She isn't supposed to be able to hear Ciel. Nobody outside Mau can, except when Ciel chooses to speak aloud through him or through Rimuru. But she hears this, clear as a bell, in the air of the clinic, as if Ciel has stopped caring who listens.

“Rimuru. Clinic. *Now.*” ᶜ

And then, quieter, faster, to someone else: “Noise. The barriers—” ᶜ

“I know,” Noise says. ᴺ

Shoko has heard Noise speak perhaps a dozen times in all the years she's known Mau. She's never heard it sound like this.

* * *

The first thing that goes is the neutral.

She knows what Infinity feels like. She's treated him through it, on bad days, when he couldn't remember to let it down; it's like reaching for something through very thick, very clear honey. Now she feels it flicker on around him, sudden and hard, so that her arms are pushed half an inch away from his chest. Then off. Then on again, harder, so that she nearly loses her grip. On, off, on, like a light with a broken switch.

“Mau,” she says. “Mau, let it down—” ˢʰ

He can't hear her. His eyes are still open, rolled up so that she can see only the whites under the lids, and the blood is coming faster from his nose now, running down over his lip and chin and dripping onto her sleeve.

Then the Blue.

She doesn't see it. She feels it. A pull, small and vicious, somewhere off to the left. The tray of instruments on the table by the window slides an inch toward it, all at once, with a crash. A glass jar tips over and rolls and doesn't stop rolling; it rolls up the side of the table in a way glass jars don't roll, and hangs there in the air for a second, and then drops.

Then the shadows.

They come out of him like water out of a broken pipe. She sees them pour across the floor: his own shadow first, spreading and deepening, then others, shapes rising out of it and falling back, a helm, a hand, the edge of a wing, something huge that's there and gone before she can tell what it was. Beru's voice from somewhere beyond the door, raised, saying *my king* in a tone she's never heard from him. Shapes trying to come out that he didn't call. Shapes trying to come out because everything in him is trying to come out at once.

And above his head — she sees it, she actually sees it, which she never has before — the wheel.

It's there for a second. Eight-spoked and pale and turning, hanging in the air above him like a halo made of something old and grinding. It turns once. Twice. Not at anything. At *everything*. Turning and turning at whatever is hurting him, and what's hurting him is himself.

* * *

The door slams open. Rimuru.

He takes it in all at once; she watches him do it. Then he's across the room, and his hand is on Mau's back, and something comes off him, something huge and patient and controlled, like a hand closing gently around a candle flame.

“Barrier,” he says. ᴿ

“It won't hold,” Ciel says. “His own barriers have already failed. Mini Ciel's down. He has nothing filtering.” ᶜ

“Then I'll *make* it hold.” ᴿ

He does. Shoko feels it go up around them: a skin of something invisible, tight around Mau, tight around her. The jars stop rolling. The tray stops sliding. The shadows on the floor press up against the inside of it like water against glass.

And then the air inside the barrier starts to *change*.

* * *

Shoko knows what a domain looks like.

She's seen Gojo's. She's seen the first trembling attempts of students, years ago, in another life, at the school. She knows what it looks like when the world starts to fold.

This is that. It's starting. Around them, inside Rimuru's barrier, the air is going thick and dark and layered, and there's a shape beginning to come through it, the shape of something being built from the inside out. She can't tell whose. It doesn't look like Gojo's. It doesn't look like Sukuna's. It looks like both of them and neither, and something else as well, something with edges like broken glass, and it's growing.

And she's inside it.

She understands that very clearly. She's on her knees on the clinic floor with her arms around him, and whatever is opening around him is opening around her too, and she doesn't know what it does. She doesn't know if it cuts. She doesn't know if it closes.

She doesn't let go of him.

* * *

Then she feels him move.

His hand. His left hand, the one with the new skin on the palm. It comes up off the floor, slowly, shaking, and closes on her sleeve.

His eyes are still rolled back. There's blood all over his face. He can't be awake. Nobody could be awake in the middle of this. But his hand closes on her sleeve, and holds on, and she feels him *pull*, not at her but at the thing growing around them. She feels him reach into the middle of it and take hold of it and try to drag it back inside himself.

He's trying to close it.

Not to stop it; he can't stop it. She can feel that he can't. But he's trying to pull it in. Away from her. Trying to make it small enough that it only takes him.

“Mau, *don't*—” ˢʰ

His whole body jerks. Blood comes out of his mouth, sudden and bright, all down the front of her coat.

“Noise,” Ciel says. “*Now.*” ᶜ

“I've got it,” Noise says. ᴺ

And it does.

She doesn't know how. She'll never know how. She only feels it: something that isn't Mau and isn't not-Mau, something that has been inside him for as long as she's known him, quiet and watching. It reaches past him, past his shaking hand, into the half-built thing in the air, and takes hold of it with a grip that is absolutely, impossibly precise. Not crushing it. *Unmaking* it. Taking it apart from the inside, layer by layer, edge by broken edge, faster than she can follow, while Ciel holds the pieces still.

The domain comes apart around them like frost melting off a window.

The air goes thin and ordinary again. The shadows on the floor sink back into his shadow, all at once, like water draining. The wheel above his head turns one last time and is gone. The neutral flickers once more, weakly, and goes out.

And Mau's hand lets go of her sleeve.

* * *

He's bleeding from everywhere.

That's what she sees, when she can see again. His nose, his mouth, the corners of his eyes. Thin bright lines of it. He's breathing in short wet gasps and every one of them brings up more blood, and she has him on his side before she's thought about it, his head on her knee, her fingers in his mouth to clear it, her other hand already flat against his chest and pouring everything she has into him.

“Inside,” Ciel says, very fast. “Vessels in the brain. Behind the eyes. His lungs—” ᶜ

“I can feel it.” ˢʰ

“Shoko, the one behind the left eye—” ᶜ

“*I can feel it.*” ˢʰ

She finds it. She closes it. She finds the next one, and the next, small bleeds everywhere, like a hundred tiny cracks in a dam, and she closes them one at a time, as fast as she can, faster than she has ever worked in her life, and it isn't fast enough, there are too many—

Then there's another pair of hands on his chest. Yuta's. She didn't hear him come in. His cursed energy floods into Mau alongside hers, strong and clean, and the cracks start closing twice as fast.

And then Yuka is there.

Shoko doesn't see her come in either. She's just suddenly there, on her knees on Mau's other side, both her hands on his face, her thumbs wiping the blood away from under his eyes, and she isn't saying anything. Her face is white. Her hands are steady.

* * *

They get the bleeding stopped.

It takes a long time. Shoko doesn't know how long. When she finally sits back on her heels her hands are shaking and her coat is ruined and there's blood on the floor in a wide dark pool that she's kneeling in, and Mau is breathing. Slowly. Unevenly. But breathing, and not bringing anything up.

“Sleep,” she says. “He needs to sleep. Deep. Now.” ˢʰ

Rimuru nods. Something passes from his hand into Mau, and Mau's ragged breathing evens out, and his face goes slack.

Nobody moves for a while.

Yuka is still holding his face. She hasn't let go once. Yuta is sitting against the bed with his head tipped back and his eyes closed. Rimuru is standing very still, his hand still on Mau's back. Through the door, Shoko can see Beru, in the hallway, kneeling. Behind him, faces. Maki. Gojo, without his blindfold, which she has almost never seen. Ori, being held back by Fern with both arms.

“What was that,” Yuka says. ʸ

Her voice is perfectly flat.

“What *was* that.” ʸ

* * *

It's Ciel who answers, aloud, so that everyone can hear.

“It was a refinement,” she says. “Something Mau and Noise have done a thousand times. Every day, for a long time. It processes what he takes in. What the eyes take in, mostly. It keeps him from drowning in it.” ᶜ

“And it broke,” Yuka says. ʸ

“No,” Noise says. “It worked. It always worked. That was the problem.” ᴺ

Everyone looks at Mau, because there's nowhere else to look.

“We thought the change in his eyes was giving it somewhere to go,” Noise says. “The load. When his eyes changed, they started handling more, and we thought the overflow was going out through them. That it was being spent.” ᴺ

“It wasn't,” Ciel says quietly. ᶜ

“It wasn't,” Noise agrees. “The eyes changed how much he could take in without noticing. Not how much he could *hold*. It kept building. Underneath. Where neither of us was looking. Because we thought we'd solved it.” ᴺ

There's a long silence.

“How long,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“I don't know,” Noise says. “A long time.” ᴺ

“And the gate?” Shoko asks. “Yesterday. He spent everything.” ˢʰ

“The gate didn't cause it,” Noise says. “It was already full. The gate was just—” ᴺ

It stops. For a moment it doesn't say anything at all, and in that moment Shoko understands something she never thought she'd understand about Noise, which is that it's afraid.

“It was already full,” Noise says again, much more quietly. “And I didn't see it.” ᴺ

* * *

They carry him to his own room. Yuka walks beside the stretcher with her hand on his chest the whole way. Shoko follows with her bag and her ruined coat.

They put him in the bed. Shoko checks everything twice. Three times. His breathing is slow and steady. His heart has settled. Nothing is bleeding.

“He's stable,” she says, finally. ˢʰ

She says it to Yuka, who's sitting on the edge of the bed holding his hand, and to Rimuru, in the doorway, and to Ori, who has finally been let through and is standing very small and very still at the foot of the bed.

“He's stable,” she says again, because she can see that Yuka needs to hear it twice. “The bleeds are closed. He's asleep. Now we wait.” ˢʰ

Yuka nods without looking up.

Ori climbs onto the end of the bed and sits by Mau's feet and puts her hand on his ankle through the blanket.

Shoko sits down in the chair by the window, finally, and looks at her hands, which are still shaking, and thinks about Mau's hand on her sleeve. About the domain closing around them. About him, with no consciousness left, with blood coming out of his eyes, reaching into the middle of it to drag it away from her.

*I could take yours.*

She puts her face in her hands.
