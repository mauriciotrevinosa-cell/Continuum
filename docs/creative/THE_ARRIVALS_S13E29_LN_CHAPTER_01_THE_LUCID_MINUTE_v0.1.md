# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 15 — The Lucid Minute

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S13E29
**Continuity state:** B+311, the small hours. Makima comes to the door and asks to speak while Mau has a lucid moment between convulsions. She offers a contract: she merges with him to hold what is building, and in exchange stays bound to his body for as long as it lives. Yuka says it's Mau's to decide. In the lucid minute, Mau understands, sets terms (she harms no one in the house, does not control him, Yuka above all), and accepts. Mau can sign contracts outside dungeons. Makima merges with him. The convulsions stop; the bleeds stop reopening. First half Yuka's point of view, the lucid minute in Mau's.

*When: Somewhere past two in the morning. The fourth fit has just ended. Shoko has been working for more than two hours without stopping.*

Makima knocks.

That's the strangest part, Yuka will think, much later. In the middle of all of it, with Shoko grey and shaking and Yuta's hands glowing on Mau's chest and Ori crying in Fern's arms in the corner and blood on everything, Makima stands in the open doorway and knocks, twice, lightly, on the frame. As if it were any door. As if she were asking whether it was a good time.

Beru is in front of her. He doesn't move.

“I'd like to speak,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

Nobody answers her.

“I'd like to speak to him,” she says. “Before the next one. There may not be many more where he can listen.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka looks at her.

She knows that face. She has spent months learning not to flinch from it at dinner. She knows the polite little smile and the calm eyes and the voice that never rises, and she knows what's underneath, or some of it. She knows about the devil that came for her, sent by those same calm eyes, back when Makima thought that taking her out of the way would bring Mau closer. She remembers Mau's face when he found her.

Makima isn't smiling now.

“What do you want,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima looks at Mau. At the blood. At Shoko's hands.

“I can hold it,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

She explains. She does it quickly, quietly, without any of the usual small politeness; Yuka has never heard her speak so plainly.

What's building in him has nowhere to go. Noise said so. It's his, and it's part of him, and it can't be taken out without taking him too. But it can be *held*. Ordered. Kept in its place, so that it doesn't press against everything else until things break.

“That's what I am,” Makima says. “That's the whole of what I am. I make things stay where they're put.” ᴹᵏ

“You control people,” Maki says, from the wall. Her voice is very flat. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I control *things*,” Makima says. “People are one kind of thing. This would be another. I wouldn't be controlling him. I'd be controlling *it*.” ᴹᵏ

“From where?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

Makima's eyes go to Mau.

“From inside,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Silence.

“I'd have to join him,” she says. “Merge. Become part of his body, so I can reach it where it lives. And once I've done that I can't undo it. I'd be bound to him. To his body. For as long as it lives.” ᴹᵏ

“And you'd want that,” Yuka says. ʸ

It isn't quite a question.

Makima looks at her for a long moment. Yuka can't read her face. She doesn't think Makima can read it either.

“I don't want him to stop,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

“Can she do it?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

He's asking Ciel. Ciel takes a moment to answer.

“I think so,” Ciel says. “I can see how it would work. I can't see everything. It would take a contract. A real one.” ᶜ

“He can't sign contracts,” Fern says suddenly, from the corner. “In the dungeon. His name won't hold.” ᶠᵉ

“That's only in dungeons,” Ciel says. “Out here he can sign anything.” ᶜ

“Noise?” Rimuru says. ᴿ

There's a pause.

“I can't stop what's happening,” Noise says. Its voice is very quiet. “She can. I don't like it. I don't like her. But I can't stop it, and she can.” ᴺ

Everyone looks at Yuka.

She knows they're looking. She can feel it. Shoko, Yuta, Rimuru, Maki, Fern, all of them, waiting to see what she'll say. As if it's hers to say.

It isn't.

“Ask him,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima's eyes come to her.

“It's his body,” Yuka says. “It's his to decide. Not mine. Not yours.” ʸ

She looks down at Mau's face in her lap. His eyes are closed. His lips are moving.

“Ask him,” she says again. “When he can hear you. And whatever he says, that's what happens.” ʸ

* * *

He comes up out of it a few minutes later.

—

There's a room. He knows the room. He doesn't know how he knows it. It's dark and full of people and smells of blood, and someone is holding his head, and someone else has their hands on his chest, and they're warm, and there's a light behind his eyes so bright and so loud that it's like a scream that doesn't stop.

Somebody says his name.

He finds the voice. It's very hard. Everything is very hard. It's like trying to see through deep water with a scream in it.

“Mau.” ʸ

He knows that voice. He knows it better than his own. He opens his eyes and there she is, upside down above him, her face very close, and she's the only thing in the room with any colour, and she's been crying.

“Yuka,” he says, or tries to. ᴹ

“Listen to me. You have to listen. Makima wants to tell you something.” ʸ

*Makima.* He knows that name too. It comes with a shape: calm eyes, a polite smile, something that hurt Yuka. He tries to turn his head and can't.

Then Makima's face is there, beside Yuka's. Close. Kneeling by the bed.

She tells him.

He doesn't understand all of it. The light is too loud. But he understands enough. *Hold it. From inside. Bound to your body. As long as it lives.*

“Why,” he says. ᴹ

Makima looks at him.

“I don't know,” she says. “I don't want you to stop. That's all I know.” ᴹᵏ

He believes her. He doesn't know why he believes her. Maybe because it's the first thing she's ever said to him that sounded like she didn't know the answer.

He looks at Yuka.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't nod. She doesn't shake her head. She just looks at him, and holds his head, and waits. And he understands, through the scream and the deep water, that she's leaving it to him. That she's decided not to decide. That whatever he says, she'll hold on.

*Live*, her face says. That's all. *Live, and I'll handle the rest.*

* * *

“Terms,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is barely there. Makima leans closer.

“You don't hurt anyone in this house,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“Not with your hands. Not with anything. Not with—with *anyone you send*.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“You don't control me.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“You don't—” ᴹ

The light surges. He feels the edges of the room go soft and dark and pulls himself back by force, by Yuka's hands on his face, by the colour of her.

“Yuka,” he says. “Above everything. Yuka. You don't touch her. You don't go near her unless she says. You don't—” ᴹ

“I understand,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“Say it.” ᴹ

“I won't harm her. I won't harm anyone in this house. I won't control you. I'll hold what's in you and keep it where it belongs. And I'll stay. For as long as your body lives.” ᴹᵏ

Her eyes are very steady.

“Do you accept?” ᴹᵏ

He looks at Yuka one more time.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

—

Yuka feels it happen.

She'll never be able to describe it, either. It isn't violent. It isn't loud. Makima puts one hand flat on Mau's chest, beside Shoko's and Yuta's, and closes her eyes, and then she *isn't there* — not gone, not vanishing, just thinning, the way mist thins in the morning, as if she were never quite solid to begin with. Her edges go soft. Her colour fades. For a moment Yuka can see the wall through her.

And then she sinks into him.

Into his chest, through Shoko's fingers, into the place where his heart is beating too fast. The way the soldiers went into the shadows, two days ago, in the yard. Like stepping into water.

Mau's whole body jerks once.

Then it goes still.

Not slack. *Still*. The shaking stops. The rigidness goes out of his back. His jaw unclenches. And the blood under his eyes, which was running, stops running. Yuka watches the last bright drop bead at the corner of his eye and *not* fall, and then it's just a drop, drying.

“Shoko,” Ciel says. ᶜ

Shoko's hands are still on his chest. She doesn't move them.

“They've stopped,” she says slowly. “The bleeds. They've stopped opening.” ˢʰ

“The load,” Noise says. ᴺ

Its voice is very strange.

“It's still there. It's all still there. But it's—” ᴺ

It stops. Tries again.

“It's *still*,” Noise says. “Something's holding it. Something's holding it exactly where it is.” ᴺ

* * *

Nobody moves for a long time.

Mau is breathing. Slowly. Deeply. His face is slack and peaceful under the blood, the way it is when he sleeps after a long day, and his hand in Yuka's has gone warm.

Shoko sits back on her heels. She looks at her own hands for a while. Then she puts them over her face and stays like that, her shoulders shaking, and Yuta puts his arm around her and doesn't say anything.

Ori is crying again in the corner, but it's a different kind of crying.

Yuka looks down at Mau's face. She wipes the drop from the corner of his eye with her thumb. She thinks about Makima's face, kneeling beside the bed. *I don't know. I don't want you to stop.*

She thinks: *She's in there now. She's in him.*

She doesn't know what she feels about that. She doesn't try to know. Not tonight.

She holds his head in her lap and counts his breaths, and they're slow, and even, and they don't stop.
