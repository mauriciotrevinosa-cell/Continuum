# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 16 — From the Inside

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S13E29
**Continuity state:** B+311, small hours to dawn. Makima's point of view, inside Mau. She finds the load and takes hold of it. She meets Noise, and then Ciel. Noise, who does not like her, builds her an outer body able to touch and live alongside the house; she remains bound to Mau's body. Through the bond she feels a memory of Mau finding Yuka hurt by the devil Makima sent, and the anguish of it, and cannot understand why it hurts her. She steps out near dawn. Her obsession with Mau is unchanged.

*When: The first hours after the contract. Makima will never be able to say how long it felt from inside.*

It's very loud in here.

That's the first thing Makima understands. She has been inside people before, in a manner of speaking: inside their wants, their fears, the small levers that move them. She knows what a person sounds like from close up. She has never been *inside* one like this, in the meat and the light of them, and she had not expected it to be so loud.

Everything is moving. Blood, breath, a warm beat she recognizes and a colder, heavier one answering from deeper in his chest. She counts three of each before she can listen to anything else. Cursed energy running in channels like rivers. Mana in a different current, cold and ancient. The eyes, behind everything, pouring in and pouring in, a ceaseless flood of what-is that would drown anyone who had to look at it directly.

And under all of it, the thing that's killing him.

* * *

She finds it quickly. She doesn't have to look hard. It's the loudest thing here.

It sits in him the way water sits behind a dam. Vast. Patient. Pressing. It isn't malicious; it isn't anything. It's simply *more*: more than he was built to hold, poured into him a little at a time by the eyes, pass after pass of refinement, with no room made for what he took in, until there's nowhere left for it to go. It presses against the walls of him. Where the walls are thin, it seeps. Where it seeps, things break.

She has never seen anything like it. She has never seen anything so much like a thing that wants to be *ordered*.

She puts her hands on it.

That's not quite what happens, here, where she has no hands. But it's the closest she can come. She reaches into the middle of it with the part of herself that has always reached into things, and she takes hold of it, and she says to it, very quietly, in the language underneath languages: *Stay*.

And it stays.

It's like holding back a sea with a word. She feels the whole weight of it pressing on her, and she holds it, and it doesn't break her. It's too big to break her. It's simply *heavy*, and she has always been good at carrying heavy things.

The walls of him stop seeping. She feels the small bright breaks start to close on their own.

* * *

“You're in my house,” says a voice. ᴺ

It isn't a voice, exactly. It's a presence, very close, very large, wrapped around everything. She had felt it from the moment she came in, and had been too busy to look at it. She looks at it now.

It has no shape. It's everywhere. It's watching her from every direction at once with an attention so total and so precise that, for the first time in a very long time, Makima feels *seen*.

She knows what it is. She's heard Mau speak to it. She's heard it speak, once or twice, in his voice.

“You're Noise,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I'm Noise.” ᴺ

“I'm holding it,” Makima says. “The thing that was killing him.” ᴹᵏ

“I know.” ᴺ

“You couldn't.” ᴹᵏ

A pause. “No.” ᴺ

“I can.” ᴹᵏ

“I *know*.” ᴺ

She has heard many things in many voices over the years. Contempt. Fear. Desire. Worship. She has never heard anything quite like this: a vast, precise intelligence that does not like her at all, and is not afraid of her, and is grateful anyway, and hates being grateful, all at once.

She finds it rather charming.

* * *

“You can't stay like this,” Noise says, after a while. ᴺ

“I made a promise. I stay as long as his body lives.” ᴹᵏ

“Not *like this*. Inside. Without a shape.” ᴺ

She considers that.

“You'll go mad,” Noise says. “Everything comes through here. His eyes. His blood. Everything he feels. You weren't made to live in it. Neither was I, at first. It took me a long time.” ᴺ

“And what do you suggest?” ᴹᵏ

Another pause. Longer. She has the sense of something very large making a decision it doesn't want to make.

“I'll build you a body,” Noise says. ᴺ

Makima is silent.

“Outside,” Noise says. “Something you can live in. Something that can touch things, and eat, and sit at a table. You'll still be bound to him. Your anchor stays here, holding that. But you won't have to *be* here. Not all the time.” ᴺ

“You can do that?” ᴹᵏ

“I built myself one, once. For an evening.” ᴺ

“Why would you do that for me?” ᴹᵏ

“Not for you,” Noise says. ᴺ

She waits.

“For him,” Noise says. “He'd want you to have somewhere to go. He'd want you to be able to leave a room. He doesn't like it when things are trapped.” ᴺ

* * *

Another presence comes while Noise is working.

Smaller. Brighter. Very sharp. It stands somewhere at the edge of things and watches Makima the way a guard watches a door. She knows this one, too, by reputation.

“Ciel,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“If you hurt him,” Ciel says, “I'll know. If you hurt anyone he loves, I'll know. I'm everywhere in here. I can't make you leave. But I'll know.” ᶜ

“I made a promise.” ᴹᵏ

“I heard it.” ᶜ

“Then you know I'll keep it.” ᴹᵏ

“I know you said it,” Ciel says. ᶜ

Makima smiles, a little. She can't help it. There's something very pleasant about being distrusted this precisely.

* * *

It happens while she's waiting for Noise to finish.

She's holding the sea. That's all. Keeping it still. Not looking at anything else, because she doesn't need to, and because everything else in here is very loud.

But she's *in* him now. Bound to him. And things come through.

A memory. Not hers. It rises out of the noise like a fish breaking the surface, without warning, and it's so vivid that for a moment she forgets she's holding anything.

A place. Grey, the way everything is grey for him. And on the ground, Yuka.

Hurt. Badly. The only thing in the whole grey world with colour, and the colour is wrong: there's too much of one colour on her, spreading. And standing over her, something Makima recognises, because Makima sent it. A small devil. A tool. Something she pointed at a problem and let go, a long time ago, before she knew him well, because the girl was in the way.

She watches him see it.

She *feels* him see it.

It goes through her like a blade. Not the seeing. What comes after. A feeling so large and so total that there's no room in it for anything else: not anger, though there's anger; not fear, though there's fear. Something under both. Something that says *not her, not her, anything, take anything, not her*. Something that would have torn the world in half with its bare hands if tearing the world in half would have helped.

Makima has never felt anything like it.

She has watched people feel things for her whole long life. She has studied it, catalogued it, used it. She knows what love looks like from the outside. She knows how to make it, how to bend it, how to point it at a target.

She has never felt it from the *inside*.

It hurts. That's the strange thing. It hurts *her*. She's holding his memory and it's tearing at her as if it were hers, as if the girl on the road were someone she — and she doesn't understand. She doesn't understand at all. It's his. It's not hers. Why should it hurt *her*?

And under the hurt, quieter, colder: *I did that. I sent that.*

She stays very still for a long time, holding the sea, with his grief tearing at her, and doesn't understand.

* * *

“It's ready,” Noise says. ᴺ

Makima comes back to herself slowly.

“What was that?” Noise asks. It's very quiet. ᴺ

“Nothing,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“It wasn't nothing.” ᴺ

“It was something of his,” she says. “It came through.” ᴹᵏ

A pause. She can feel Noise looking at her, at whatever she is in here, with that total precise attention.

“Yes,” Noise says. “Things do.” ᴺ

It doesn't say anything else. She's grateful for that, in a way she doesn't examine.

* * *

She steps out a little before dawn.

It's like stepping out of deep water. One moment she's everywhere, holding the sea, surrounded by the roar of him; the next she's standing on a floor, on feet, in a body, and the room is very quiet and very small and smells of blood and soap and old wood.

She looks down at herself. Hands. Arms. The same as before, or nearly; Noise has been precise, as Noise is precise about everything. She flexes her fingers. They work.

And she can still feel it. Behind her. Inside him. Her anchor, holding the sea, exactly where she left it. A thread runs from here to there, from this body to that one, thin and absolutely unbreakable.

The room is full of sleeping people.

Shoko, slumped in the chair by the window, her bloody hands in her lap. Yuta on the floor against the wall. Ori curled at the foot of the bed with Lantern's shape coiled around her. Beru at the door, not sleeping, watching her with his many eyes.

And Yuka, sitting up against the headboard with Mau's head in her lap, awake. Watching her.

They look at each other across the bed for a long moment.

Makima thinks about the road. The grey. The girl on the ground with the wrong colour spreading on her.

She opens her mouth to say something. She doesn't know what.

“Not now,” Yuka says quietly. ʸ

Makima closes her mouth.

“Not now,” Yuka says again. “He's sleeping.” ʸ

Makima nods.

She looks at Mau. His face is peaceful. His breathing is slow and deep. Inside him, she can feel her own hold on the sea, steady and sure, and around it the vast roar of him, and somewhere in the roar, faint and far away, a thread of warmth that she's beginning to understand is what it feels like to be glad someone is alive.

*I don't want you to stop.*

She still doesn't know why. But she knows, now, that she meant it.

She goes quietly out of the room, past Beru, who lets her pass, and down the stairs, and sits on the bottom step in the dark, alone, in a body she's had for less than a minute, and waits for the sun.
