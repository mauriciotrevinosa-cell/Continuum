# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 17 — The One with Colour

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E30
**Continuity state:** B+311, afternoon. Mau wakes with gaps. He recognises faces without knowing from where, and knows names without knowing whose. He doesn't know who Yuka is. She is the only thing in the room with colour and he doesn't know why. He sits beside her, takes her hand, by habit. Shoko and Noise: the memories are there; the paths to them are swollen after the bleeds; they should come back, no promise when. The house decides not to push him.

*When: The afternoon after the contract. He has slept for most of a day.*

He wakes up in a room he knows.

He's sure of that. He knows the ceiling: the crack in the plaster above the window, the one shaped like a river with a branch. He knows the weight of the blanket. He knows the smell, wood and soap and something sweet, like fruit left in a bowl. He knows the sound the window makes when the wind pushes on it.

He doesn't know whose room it is.

He lies there and looks at the crack in the ceiling and waits for the rest of it to arrive. It's coming. He can feel it coming, the way you can feel a word on the tip of your tongue. The room is his. It must be his. He knows it too well for it not to be.

The rest doesn't arrive.

* * *

There's someone in the chair by the window.

A woman. Tired. Dark hair tied back, a coat too big for her, hands in her lap. She's asleep with her chin on her chest. He knows her face. He knows it very well. He's seen it close up, frowning, many times; he knows the shape it makes when it's about to say *liar*.

He doesn't know her name.

No. That's wrong. He does know a name. It's sitting right there. *Shoko.* He's sure of the name. He's just not sure it belongs to her. It feels like having a key in his hand and a door in front of him and not knowing if they go together.

“Shoko?” he says. ᴹ

His voice comes out cracked and dry. The woman in the chair jerks awake.

She looks at him. Her face does several things very fast.

“Mau,” she says. ˢʰ

*Mau.* That's him. He knows that one. That one fits.

“Is that right?” he asks. “Shoko. Is that your name?” ᴹ

Her face goes very still.

* * *

There are more people, after that. They come in quietly, one or two at a time, because the woman — Shoko, it *is* Shoko, she told him so, and he's holding onto it — asks them to.

He knows all of them. That's the strange part. He knows every face that comes through the door. A tall man with something over his eyes and a smile that's trying very hard. A girl with glasses and a scar and a stare like a blade. A boy with soft hair who stands behind her. A small girl with her hair in two long tails and her arms full of a blanket. Faces he has seen across tables and in doorways and on the other side of fights, faces that fit into the shape of his life like pieces into a puzzle.

He knows some of the names. *Gojo*, he says, to the man with the blindfold, and the man's smile goes crooked, and he says *yeah, that's me*. *Maki.* *Yuta.* He gets those. He doesn't get the girl's. She tells him it's Milim, and then she sits down hard on the floor by the bed and starts to cry, very loudly, and the boy with soft hair has to take her out.

He doesn't know where any of them come from. He doesn't know how he knows them. He knows their faces the way he knows the crack in the ceiling. He knows that they belong here. He doesn't know what *here* is.

* * *

And then there's her.

She's been in the room the whole time. He doesn't know how he missed her at first. She's standing by the door, against the wall, a little apart from everyone else, with her arms wrapped around herself.

She's the only thing in the room with colour.

He stares at her.

Everything else is grey. It's always been grey. He knows that the way he knows his own name; it's not a memory, it's just how the world is. The walls, the people, the blanket, his own hands. Grey, in a thousand shades. That's what the world looks like.

And she's not grey.

Her hair is a colour. Her skin is a colour. Her eyes — he doesn't have words for her eyes. He doesn't have words for any of it. He's never needed them. Everything he's ever looked at has been grey, and he has no names for anything else, and here's someone standing by the door who's full of it, *made* of it, glowing with something he can't name, the way a lamp glows in a dark room.

He doesn't know who she is.

He doesn't know her face. He doesn't know her name. He tries. He looks at her and waits for the key to fit the door, and there's no key. There's no door. There's just her, by the wall, in colour, looking at him with an expression he can't read.

“Who are you?” he asks. ᴹ

* * *

She doesn't answer right away.

He watches something happen to her face. He doesn't know what it is. He doesn't know her well enough to know. But it goes through the whole room like a cold wind; he sees the tall man with the blindfold turn his head away, sees the girl with glasses close her eyes, sees Shoko's hands go tight in her lap.

“I'm Yuka,” she says. ʸ

Her voice is very steady. Too steady.

*Yuka.* He holds the name up and looks at it. It doesn't fit anything. It's just a word.

“I'm sorry,” he says. “I don't—I don't remember.” ᴹ

“That's okay,” she says. ʸ

It isn't. He can tell it isn't. Even without knowing her.

“Why are you—” He stops. He doesn't know how to ask it. “You're different. From everything else. Why are you different?” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment.

“I don't know,” she says. “You never knew either.” ʸ

* * *

Later, when most of the others have gone, she comes and sits on the edge of the bed.

She does it carefully. As though she's not sure she's allowed. She sits at the very edge, not touching him, with her hands folded in her lap, and looks at the window instead of at him.

He doesn't think about it.

He just moves over. A little. Enough that there's more room on the bed beside him. And then his hand goes out and finds hers in her lap and takes it, and holds it, and it's done before he's thought about any of it at all.

She goes very still.

He looks down at his hand holding hers. He doesn't know why he did that. It felt like the most natural thing in the world, like breathing, like putting his foot on a stair he knows is there in the dark. His hand knew where hers was. His hand knew it was supposed to be holding it.

“Sorry,” he says. “I don't know why I—” ᴹ

“Don't,” she says. ʸ

Her voice cracks on it.

“Don't let go,” she says. “Please. Just—don't.” ʸ

He doesn't let go.

They sit like that for a long time. She doesn't look at him. He doesn't know what to say to her. Her hand in his is warm and small and it fits there exactly, as if it was made to, and he has no idea why, and he doesn't let go.

* * *

Shoko explains it to him that evening, as well as she can.

Noise helps. He knows Noise. That's the strangest thing of all, maybe. He doesn't know who the woman in colour is, but he knows the voice in the back of his head that's been there as long as he can remember. It feels like the floor. Like the one thing that didn't move.

“The memories are there,” Noise tells him. “All of them. I can see them. Nothing's gone.” ᴺ

“Then why can't I—” ᴹ

“The paths are swollen,” Shoko says. She's sitting in the chair by the window again. She looks very tired. “There was bleeding in your brain. A lot of it. We stopped it, but the tissue around where it was is swollen, and it's pressing on things. On the routes between where your memories are kept and where you reach for them.” ˢʰ

“So they'll come back.” ᴹ

“They should come back. As the swelling goes down.” ˢʰ

“When?” ᴹ

Shoko doesn't answer right away.

“I don't know,” she says. “I can't promise. A day. A few days. Maybe longer for some things than others.” ˢʰ

He nods slowly. He looks at his hand. Yuka went downstairs a while ago, to eat something, because Shoko made her. He can still feel where her hand was.

“Her,” he says. “Yuka. Who is she?” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him. Noise is very quiet.

“That's not mine to tell you,” Shoko says at last. “And I don't think it should be told. I think it should come back.” ˢʰ

* * *

He finds out later — from the girl with the two tails, Milim, who sneaks back in after dark to apologise for crying, and stays to talk, and can't keep anything inside her for more than about a minute — that the house has decided not to push.

“We're not supposed to tell you stuff,” Milim whispers, sitting cross-legged on the end of his bed. “About who everybody is. Shoko says if we tell you, you'll remember us telling you, not the real thing. So you have to remember by yourself.” ᴹⁱ

“That makes sense,” he says. ᴹ

“It's *awful*,” Milim says. ᴹⁱ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

She looks at him with enormous eyes.

“You really don't remember Yuka,” she says. ᴹⁱ

He shakes his head.

Milim opens her mouth. Closes it. Opens it again. He can see her trying very, very hard not to say something, and he can see the exact moment she wins.

“Okay,” she says, very small. “Okay. Good night, Mau.” ᴹⁱ

She climbs off the bed and goes to the door. Then she stops and turns around.

“She stayed all night,” Milim says. “Last night. She held your head the whole time. She didn't sleep at all.” ᴹⁱ

And she's gone before he can say anything.

He lies there in the dark for a long time, looking at the crack in the ceiling shaped like a river, thinking about a woman in colour who held his head all night, and whose name doesn't fit anything, and whose hand his hand already knew.
