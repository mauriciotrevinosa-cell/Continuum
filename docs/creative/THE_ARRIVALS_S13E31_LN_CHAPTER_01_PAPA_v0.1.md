# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 19 — Papa

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S13E31
**Continuity state:** B+313, afternoon. Third day of the gap. Ori's last attempt breaks into a tantrum through tears; she calls him "Papa", the word she used before "Dad", and tells him that he loves her. Milim grabs him and shakes him, crying for him to remember. Yuka is about to step in when Mau tells Milim to stop. Then he sees Yuka, and knows her, and sees Ori crying, and everything comes back, including the three days of the gap. His anger is at the time lost and what Ori had to go through. He holds her, says sorry, says he loves her. The joke about the shake comes when Ori can breathe.

*When: The third afternoon. Ori said she'd come back. She came back yesterday, and the day before. Today she has a plan.*

Ori has a plan.

He can tell, because she comes in with a piece of paper and stands at the foot of the bed and reads from it.

“Things you know,” she says. ᴼ

She clears her throat. Behind her, in the doorway, Yuka is leaning against the frame with her arms crossed, watching. Milim is beside her. Further back, on the landing, he can see Beru's hat.

“You know I'm Ori,” Ori reads. “You know Lantern. You know the shell is very good. You know the stairs. You know the bread is Shuna's.” ᴼ

She looks up.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

“So you know some things.” ᴼ

“I know some things.” ᴹ

She nods, as if this confirms a theory, and turns the paper over.

“Things you *don't* know,” she reads. Her voice wobbles a little and she steadies it. “You don't know how I got here. You don't know about before. You don't know about my birthday. You don't know about Beru's hat, even though I made it, with Mai, and you were there.” ᴼ

She lowers the paper.

“You don't know who I am to you,” she says. ᴼ

He looks at her. She looks back at him, chin up, eyes very bright, the paper crumpling in her fist.

“I'm sorry,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

It doesn't go well after that.

She tries. She tries so hard. She tells him about before, when she didn't have a house; he doesn't remember it. She tells him about the day he found her; he doesn't remember it. She tells him about the bench in the courtyard where she first called him something, and the porch where she called him something else, and every single night since then that he's said goodnight at her door. She tells him about the letter, *is the shell good*, and the island, and the hat.

He listens to all of it. He believes all of it. He can see every word of it is true, in her face, in Yuka's face behind her, in the way Milim has her hands pressed over her mouth.

None of it comes back.

“I'm sorry,” he says again. “I'm sorry, Ori. I want to remember. I'm trying.” ᴹ

And something in her breaks.

* * *

“*You're not trying hard enough!*” ᴼ

It comes out as a scream. She throws the paper at him. It hits the blanket and slides off onto the floor.

“You're *not*! You're just *sitting* there! You're just sitting there being *nice* to me like I'm somebody's *kid* that came to visit!” ᴼ

“Ori—” ʸ

That's Yuka, from the door, starting forward. Ori doesn't hear her.

“I'm not somebody's kid!” Ori is crying now, really crying, the kind that comes from the bottom of the chest and shakes everything on its way out. Her face is red and wet and twisted up, and she's standing at the foot of the bed with her fists clenched at her sides, and she's shaking. “I'm *yours*! I'm *your* kid! You *said*! You said it on the bench and you said it on the porch and you said it every *night*, and now you don't even—you don't even—” ᴼ

She can't finish. She makes a sound like something tearing.

“*Papa!*” ᴼ

The word goes through the room like a bell.

Not *Dad*. He doesn't know why he knows that. He doesn't know why it matters. But it does. *Dad* is what she says now, every day, at breakfast, at the door, at bedtime. *Papa* is older. *Papa* is the bench in the courtyard. *Papa* is what she called him first, before she decided she didn't need to be formal with him any more.

She's gone back to it. All the way back, to the very first one.

“You love me,” she sobs. “You *love* me. You told me. You *told* me, you *said* so, you love me, you do, you're *supposed to*—” ᴼ

He can't breathe.

* * *

Milim moves.

She's across the room before anyone can stop her. Her hands come down on his shoulders and grab, hard, harder than she probably means to, and she *shakes* him. Once. His head snaps back. His teeth click together.

“*Remember!*” ᴹⁱ

She's crying too. Great hiccupping sobs. Her face is right in front of his, her eyes enormous and streaming.

“Remember her! Remember *Ori*! You *have* to, you *have* to, she's been coming every day and you just keep saying *sorry* and you have to *remember*—” ᴹⁱ

She shakes him again.

“Milim!” ʸ

Yuka is coming across the room. Fast. Her hand is out, reaching for Milim's arm, her face white.

And he says, “Milim. Stop.” ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't know where it comes from.

It isn't loud. It isn't angry. It's just his voice, very quiet and very certain, the voice he uses — the voice he *used*, he *uses*, when somebody in the house is about to do something they'll regret and he wants them not to. He knows that voice. He's used it a thousand times. On Milim, mostly. Milim, who stops dead with her hands still on his shoulders, staring at him.

He's not looking at her.

He's looking past her. At Yuka.

She's frozen halfway across the room with her hand still out. And he looks at her, at her face, at the colour of her, the only colour in the whole grey world—

—and he *knows* her.

* * *

It doesn't come back slowly.

It comes back all at once, like a door blown open in a storm. Like a dam breaking.

*Yuka.*

Yuka at seventeen, in the doorway of a house that wasn't this house yet, small and furious and dying and refusing to admit it. Yuka laughing on the roof. Yuka in the hospital bed. Yuka the day the cancer was gone. Yuka on the porch with her feet in his lap. Yuka at the door of the long dungeon, every single day, though he told Gojo to tell her she didn't have to be. Yuka in a white dress. Yuka on the island in an enormous hat, telling him the sea was a colour he'd never see, and him seeing it anyway, on her, where the light landed.

Yuka two days ago, standing by the wall, saying *I'm Yuka* in a voice too steady to be real, while he looked at her like a stranger.

And Ori.

Ori the day she came. Ori on the bench. *Papa?* Ori on the porch, later, deciding, with great seriousness, that *Dad* was better because it was less fussy. Ori's birthday. Ori's shell. Ori's stone, which is not at all the shape of his thumb, which he has never once told her, which he never will.

Ori, three days ago, coming up the third stair in the middle of the night to stand outside a door and listen to him breathe. *I just want to check.*

Ori at the foot of the bed yesterday, holding the shell, while he said *it's very good* like a stranger being polite.

Ori this morning with her list.

Ori right now, standing there, screaming *Papa* at a man who didn't know her.

It all comes back. All of it. Including the gap. Including every second of the last three days, every time he looked at her and didn't know her, every time she said *that's okay* and went away down the stairs and stepped on the third one.

* * *

The first thing he feels is anger.

He doesn't expect that. It comes up through him so hot and so sudden that his hands close into fists on the blanket. Not at Milim. Not at Ori. Not at anyone in this room.

At the time.

At three days. Three days of his life gone into nothing, three days of being a stranger to the people who love him most. Three days of Yuka by the wall with her arms around herself. Three days of Ori coming up the stairs with something in her hands — a shell, a stone, a list — and going back down with nothing. Three days of a child trying to make her father remember he was her father.

She shouldn't have had to do that. Nobody should have to do that. *She* shouldn't have had to do that. She's already lost enough.

“Ori,” he says. ᴹ

His voice comes out wrong. Rough. She's still crying, gulping, her fists at her sides.

He gets out of the bed.

Shoko would kill him. He doesn't care. His legs are shaky and his head swims and he doesn't care about that either. He gets out of the bed and goes down on his knees on the floor in front of her, so that his face is level with hers, and he puts his arms around her and pulls her in.

She goes rigid. Then she goes completely limp against him, and the crying gets worse, much worse, enormous gasping sobs into his shoulder.

“I'm sorry,” he says. “I'm sorry. I'm sorry, Ori. I'm here. I remember. I remember everything. I'm here.” ᴹ

“You *didn't*—” ᴼ

“I know. I know I didn't. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry.” ᴹ

He holds her. He holds her so tight she can probably barely breathe, and she holds him back just as tight, her small arms locked around his neck.

“I love you,” he says into her hair. “I love you. You're right. You were right. I do. I always do. Even when I couldn't find it. I love you, Ori.” ᴹ

She cries harder. He doesn't let go.

* * *

Yuka is kneeling beside them.

He doesn't see her get there. She's just there, on the floor beside him, her hand on his back, her other hand on Ori's head. Her face is wet. She isn't making a sound.

He looks at her over Ori's shoulder.

“Hi,” he says. ᴹ

Her face crumples.

He gets one arm free from around Ori and puts it around Yuka and pulls her in too, and holds both of them, there on the floor at the foot of the bed, and none of them says anything for a long time.

* * *

It's a long time before Ori can breathe properly.

When she can, she pulls back a little — not far, not out of his arms — and looks at him with her face swollen and blotchy and her nose running.

“You remember,” she says. ᴼ

“I remember.” ᴹ

“The stone?” ᴼ

“The stone is exactly the shape of my thumb,” he says solemnly. ᴹ

Ori stares at him for a moment. Then she laughs. It's wet and hiccupping and not very steady, but it's a laugh.

“It's *not*,” she says. “You never said that. You always just *looked* at it.” ᴼ

“I'm saying it now.” ᴹ

“You're *lying*.” ᴼ

“A little.” ᴹ

Behind them, Milim is still standing exactly where she was when he told her to stop, frozen, both hands over her mouth, tears still running down her face. He looks up at her.

“Milim,” he says. ᴹ

She flinches. “I'm sorry—” ᴹⁱ

“Next time,” he says, “could you shake a little *less*? I think you loosened something.” ᴹ

Milim stares at him. Ori giggles. Yuka makes a sound against his shoulder that might be a laugh or a sob or both.

“I *fixed* you,” Milim says, after a moment, with a slowly dawning wonder. “I shook you and it *fixed* you.” ᴹⁱ

“That's not what—” ᴹ

“I'm telling *everybody*.” ᴹⁱ

“Milim—” ᴹ

But she's already gone, out the door and past Beru and down the stairs at full speed, shouting at the top of her voice that he *remembers*, he remembers, she shook him and he *remembers*, and from somewhere below there's a sudden explosion of noise, voices and footsteps and somebody dropping a pan.

Ori puts her head back down on his shoulder.

“She didn't fix you,” she says. ᴼ

“No.” ᴹ

“*I* did.” ᴼ

He holds her tighter.

“Yeah,” he says. “You did.” ᴹ
