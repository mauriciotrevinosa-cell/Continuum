# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 20 — Like Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S13E31
**Continuity state:** B+313, evening. Makima comes up after the house has had its turn. She hugs Mau before he knows what she did. He sees her whole, in a body. Noise explains that it built her a body and that she lives with him now, "Like me." Ciel adds, "And me." Mau feels the hold inside him and understands what the contract cost her and what it keeps from him. Makima asks to apologise to Yuka; Yuka says she wants to be with Mau first and will hear her later with Maki and Yuta present.

*When: After supper, which everyone ate in Mau's room or on the stairs outside it, because nobody wanted to be downstairs.*

The house comes up in waves.

Milim's shouting brings the first one: everybody who was in the kitchen, all at once, up the stairs in a crowd, so that for a few minutes the room is so full that Shoko has to stand on a chair and tell them all to get out before she does something she'll regret. They get out. Then they come back one or two at a time, which Shoko allows.

Rimuru first, who doesn't say anything, just hugs him, hard, and then holds him at arm's length and looks at him and says, “You scared me,” ᴿ in a voice that makes it clear he means it.

Gojo, who leans in the doorway and says, “Found the light switch?” ᴳ

“Found it,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Told you.” ᴳ

Maki, who punches him in the shoulder, not lightly, and then sits on the end of the bed with her arms folded and doesn't leave. Yuta, who sits beside her. Rem, crying, with bread. Umaru, with chips. Fern, with the register, because she wants him to sign beside the entry for the red gate, and he signs it, and sees that underneath her note about making sure he eats, she's added another line: *He came back. B+313.*

Shoko, last, who checks everything twice and tells him that if he gets out of bed again without asking she'll tie him to it, and then sits down in the chair by the window and doesn't say anything for a long time, and finally says, very quietly, “You tried to close it. In the clinic. You tried to pull it in so it wouldn't take me.” ˢʰ

He doesn't remember that part. He remembers the conversation. *I could take yours.* And then nothing.

“Did I?” ᴹ

“You did.” ˢʰ

“Good,” he says. ᴹ

Shoko looks at him for a long moment, and then turns to look out the window, and doesn't say anything else.

* * *

Ori won't leave. She's on the bed beside him with her head on his arm and her shell in her lap, and every time anyone suggests she might want to go to bed she says *no* without looking up, and eventually they stop suggesting.

Yuka won't leave either. She's on his other side. She hasn't let go of his hand since the floor.

It's late when Makima comes.

* * *

He doesn't see her come in. He's looking at Ori, who has fallen asleep at last with her mouth open, drooling slightly on his sleeve. And then there's someone at the side of the bed, and he looks up.

It's Makima.

She looks the way she always looks. That's the first thing he thinks. The same calm face, the same coat, the same long braid over her shoulder. The same eyes, ringed and steady. He knows her. He remembers her: the visits in the clinic, the yard of the warehouse, the anchor in her shadow, the devil she sent, all of it, everything, the whole long tangled knot of her.

And then, before he can say anything, before he can ask what she's doing here, she leans down and puts her arms around him.

He goes completely still.

It isn't like Yuka at the window. It isn't the stiffness of a stranger. He knows exactly who this is; that's why he goes still. Makima has never touched him like this. Makima has never touched him at all, except once or twice, carefully, a hand on his arm in front of other people. And now she's holding him, her arms around his shoulders, her cheek against his hair, and she's holding him the way you'd hold something you thought you'd lost.

He doesn't know what to do.

Yuka, beside him, has gone still too.

* * *

“Makima,” he says. ᴹ

She lets go. She straightens up. Her face is perfectly calm, the way it always is. But there's something underneath it, something he's never seen there before, something that looks almost like relief.

“I'm glad you're back,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“What are you—” ᴹ

And then he feels it.

It's inside him. He's been feeling it since he woke up, three days ago, without knowing what it was. A weight. A presence. Something very large and very still, sitting in the deepest part of him, holding something. He thought it was part of being hurt. He thought it was the bleeding, or the swelling, or the fog.

It isn't. It's a *hand*. Holding a sea.

He feels the sea. Vast and heavy and pressing, the thing that broke in the clinic, the thing that was going to kill him. It's all still there. He can feel the edges of it, the sheer size of it. And holding it, keeping it exactly where it is, calm and absolute and unmoving: her.

He looks at Makima. He looks at her body, standing beside the bed. Then he looks inward, at the hand holding the sea. They're the same. He can feel the thread running between them, from her standing there to her inside him, thin and unbreakable.

“What did you do?” he says. ᴹ

* * *

“She made a contract with you,” Noise says. ᴺ

He knows that voice better than any voice in the world. It's the floor. It's the one thing that never moved, not even in the fog.

“You were dying,” Noise says. “You had a minute where you could hear. She offered. You set terms. You accepted.” ᴺ

He remembers, then. Faintly. Deep water and a scream and Yuka's face upside down above him. *Yuka. Above everything.* Makima's face, close. *I don't want you to stop.*

“She's holding it,” Mau says slowly. ᴹ

“She's holding it,” Noise agrees. “From inside. She's bound to your body. For as long as it lives.” ᴺ

He looks at Makima. She looks back, calm.

“Then how is she—” He gestures at her. At her standing there, solid, real, with her hands folded in front of her. “How is she *here*?” ᴹ

There's a pause.

“I built her a body,” Noise says. ᴺ

He stares at nothing.

“*You* did?” ᴹ

“She couldn't live inside you all the time. Nobody can. It took me years. So I built her somewhere to go. She still holds it from inside. But she can walk around. She can eat. She can sit at the table.” ᴺ

“You built *Makima* a body.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴺ

“You don't even *like* Makima.” ᴹ

“No,” Noise agrees. ᴺ

A pause.

“She lives with you now,” Noise says. “Like me.” ᴺ

And then, from somewhere else, quieter and sharper, a little dry: “And me.” ᶜ

* * *

He sits there for a long time.

Like me. And me. He thinks about that. He thinks about the three of them, Noise and Ciel and now Makima, all living in him. All holding things. Noise holding everything, as it always has. Ciel watching, as she always has. And Makima, now, holding the sea that was going to kill him, so that he can sit up in bed with Ori drooling on his arm and Yuka holding his hand.

He thinks about what she gave up. *Bound to your body. For as long as it lives.* Wherever he goes, now, she has to go. Whatever happens to him happens to her.

He thinks about what she did to Yuka.

He looks at Yuka. Yuka is looking at Makima. Her face is very still.

* * *

“I'd like to apologise,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

She isn't saying it to him. She's saying it to Yuka.

“Not for this,” Makima says. “For before. For what I sent. For trying to take you out of the way.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka doesn't say anything.

“I know it isn't enough,” Makima says. “I know you have no reason to listen. I'd like to say it anyway. Properly. When you're ready to hear it.” ᴹᵏ

Something happens in Makima's face, very briefly. He almost misses it. A flicker of something he's never seen there. Not the polite smile. Not the calm. Something that looks, for half a second, as though it hurts.

He thinks: *She felt it.* He doesn't know how he knows. Something in the thread between them. *She felt something of mine. Something about Yuka. And it hurt her.*

He doesn't say anything.

Yuka looks at Makima for a long time.

“Not now,” Yuka says finally. ʸ

Makima nods.

“I want to be with him now,” Yuka says. “Tonight. Just him. I've had three days of him not knowing who I am, and I want him back, and I'm not going to share that with anyone.” ʸ

“I understand.” ᴹᵏ

“Tomorrow,” Yuka says. “Or the day after. When he's sleeping. I'll hear it.” ʸ

She pauses.

“With Maki and Yuta,” she adds. “Not alone.” ʸ

“Of course,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

She inclines her head, very slightly, to Yuka. Then to Mau. Then she turns and goes to the door.

She stops there.

“I'll be nearby,” she says, without turning around. “I can't be very far. But I'll stay out of the way.” ᴹᵏ

And she goes.

* * *

When she's gone, nobody says anything for a while.

Maki and Yuta get up. Maki squeezes Mau's shoulder on her way past. Yuta lifts Ori off his arm, very gently, and carries her out, and she doesn't wake. Shoko follows them. The door closes.

It's just the two of them.

Yuka doesn't say anything. She just moves. She climbs up the bed and lies down next to him and puts her head on his chest and her arm across him, the way she does, the way she has a thousand times, and holds on.

He puts his hand in her hair.

“Hi,” he says again. ᴹ

“Hi,” she says into his chest. Her voice cracks on it. ʸ

“I'm sorry.” ᴹ

“Don't.” ʸ

“You stood by the wall. You said your name like—” ᴹ

“*Don't*,” she says. ʸ

He stops.

She's crying. He can feel it, soaking through his shirt. She isn't making a sound. She's just lying there with her face against his chest, crying, holding on.

“You held my hand,” she says at last. “The first day. You didn't know who I was, and you held my hand anyway. You moved over to make room.” ʸ

“My hand knew,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“The rest of me was just slow.” ᴹ

She laughs, a small wet sound, and then she's crying harder, and he holds her, and she holds him, and outside the window the wind pushes at the glass the way it always does, and inside him, very deep, very still, a hand he didn't choose holds back a sea, and he's alive.
