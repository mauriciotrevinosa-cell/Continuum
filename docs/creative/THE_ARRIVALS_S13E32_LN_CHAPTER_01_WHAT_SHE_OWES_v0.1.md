# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 21 — What She Owes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S13E32
**Continuity state:** B+314, afternoon, while Mau sleeps. Yuka's point of view. In the workshop, with Maki and Yuta present, Makima apologises concretely for sending the devil to get Yuka out of the way. She says what she felt through the bond and that she doesn't understand it. Yuka hears her out. She does not forgive her today; she thanks her for Mau's life, separately. Her terms: Makima doesn't come into their room, doesn't touch her, asks before she sits beside her. Makima accepts. Yuka leaves room for later. Maki is ready for a fight that doesn't come. Yuta speaks once.

*When: The day after Mau came back. He has slept since lunch. Shoko says he'll sleep most of the next few days, and that it's the best thing for him.*

They do it in Mai's workshop, because Mai offered it, and because it's the one room in the house where Yuka has never been afraid of anything.

Mai isn't there. She said she'd go for a walk. She left the lamp lit and put the kettle on the stove and a plate of the little biscuits Rem makes on the bench, and Yuka understood that this was Mai's way of being there without being there.

Maki stands by the door. Arms crossed. She has her weapon with her, propped against the wall within reach, and she's making no secret of it. Yuta sits on the stool by the window, quiet, his hands loose between his knees. He hasn't said anything since they came in.

Yuka sits at Mai's bench, where she's sat a thousand times, with the island cloth folded at her elbow.

Makima comes in last.

* * *

She stands in the middle of the room.

She doesn't sit. There's a chair, and Yuka watches her look at it and decide not to. She stands with her hands folded in front of her, the way she always stands, and looks at Yuka, and for a long moment she doesn't say anything at all.

Yuka waits. She's good at waiting. She learned.

“I sent a devil to hurt you,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

Plain. No softening. No *I'm sorry if*. Yuka hadn't expected that.

“I sent it because you were in the way,” Makima says. “I wanted Mau. I wanted him near me, and close, and mine, in the way I wanted things then, and you were the reason he wasn't. So I decided to remove you. Not kill. I told myself not kill. Hurt badly enough that you'd be gone for a while. Long enough.” ᴹᵏ

Maki shifts by the door. Yuka doesn't look at her.

“I didn't think about you,” Makima says. “At all. You weren't a person to me. You were a problem in a place I wanted to be. I pointed something at the problem and let it go.” ᴹᵏ

She stops.

“That's what I did,” she says. “That's all of it. I'm not going to tell you there was a reason that makes it better. There wasn't.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka looks at her for a long time.

She thinks about the day. She doesn't like to. She's mostly managed not to, these last months. But she lets herself now. The pain first, sudden and enormous and everywhere. The ground. The taste of the ground. And then Mau's face, above her, and something in his eyes she had never seen before and has only seen once since, in the dungeon, near the end.

“Why now,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Because he asked me not to harm you,” Makima says. “In the contract. Above everything. He said it three times.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka didn't know that. She was there. She didn't hear it. She was holding his head.

“And because of something else,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

She hesitates.

Yuka has never seen Makima hesitate. In all the months of dinners and visits and carefully neutral conversations at the far end of the table, Makima has never once looked as though she didn't know exactly what to say next.

“When I went into him,” Makima says slowly, “things came through. His things. Memories. I wasn't looking for them; they came. And one of them was that day.” ᴹᵏ

She looks at the floor. Then back at Yuka.

“I saw him find you,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

The workshop is very quiet.

“I felt it,” Makima says. “What he felt. When he saw you.” ᴹᵏ

Her voice is perfectly even. Her hands are perfectly still. But there's something wrong with her face, something Yuka can't name, as if the calm is a lid on something that's boiling.

“I've watched people love each other for a very long time,” Makima says. “I know what it looks like. I know how it works. I've used it. I'm very good at using it.” ᴹᵏ

She stops.

“I'd never felt it,” she says. “From inside. I didn't know it was like that. I didn't know it was so—” ᴹᵏ

She can't find the word. Yuka watches her look for it and not find it.

“It hurt,” Makima says at last. “It hurt *me*. It was his, and it was about you, and it hurt me as if it were mine. And I don't understand why.” ᴹᵏ

She looks straight at Yuka.

“And I did that,” she says. “The thing that made him feel that. I sent it. And I understand, now, a little, what it was that I sent it at.” ᴹᵏ

“I'm sorry,” Makima says. “I'm sorry I hurt you. I'm sorry I hurt him through you. I'm sorry I didn't see you. I see you now. I'm sorry it took this.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Nobody says anything for a long time.

Yuka looks at her hands on the bench. At the island cloth, folded at her elbow, the colour of the lagoon at noon.

She thinks about forgiving. She thinks about what Mau would want. She thinks he'd want her to do whatever she wants, and mean it, and that he'd stand behind her whichever way she went. She knows that about him the way his hand knew hers.

She thinks about what *she* wants.

“I don't forgive you,” she says. ʸ

Makima's face doesn't change. “I didn't expect you to.” ᴹᵏ

“I might. Someday. I don't know.” Yuka looks up. “But not today. Not because you said the right words in Mai's workshop. I'm not going to forgive you because you finally felt something.” ʸ

“No,” Makima agrees. ᴹᵏ

“I heard you,” Yuka says. “I heard all of it. I believe you meant it. That's what I can give you today. I heard you, and I believe you meant it.” ʸ

Makima inclines her head.

“That's more than I'd have given me,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

“And there's something else,” Yuka says. ʸ

Maki, by the door, uncrosses her arms.

“You saved his life,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima looks at her.

“Three nights ago,” Yuka says. “He was dying in my lap and nobody could stop it. Not Shoko. Not Yuta. Not Noise. And you could. And you did. And you're going to be holding that, inside him, for as long as he lives.” ʸ

She takes a breath.

“That's separate,” she says. “That's not part of the apology. That doesn't make up for anything. It's a different thing. And for that—” ʸ

She stops. It's harder to say than she thought it would be.

“Thank you,” Yuka says. “For his life. Thank you.” ʸ

Makima doesn't move.

For a moment Yuka thinks she isn't going to say anything at all. Then something happens to her face again, the thing from before, the lid lifting off whatever's boiling. Just for a second. Just long enough to see.

“You're welcome,” Makima says quietly. ᴹᵏ

* * *

“Terms,” Yuka says. ʸ

She didn't plan to use that word. It just came out. It's Mau's word. She heard him say it, upside down in her lap, with blood in his eyes. *Terms.*

Makima nods. “Tell me.” ᴹᵏ

“Our room is ours,” Yuka says. “You don't come in. Not ever. Not if he's sick, not if he calls, not if the house is on fire. If he needs something in there, someone else brings it.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“You don't touch me. Not my hand, not my shoulder, nothing. Not until I say.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“If you want to sit next to me, at the table, anywhere, you ask first. And if I say no, you don't ask why.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“And him.” ʸ

Yuka stops. She thinks about it. About Makima's arms around him last night, before he knew what she'd done. About the way Makima looks at him, that hasn't changed and won't.

“I know you're going to be near him,” Yuka says. “You have to be. You're bound. I know you're going to feel things through that, and I know you still want him. I'm not stupid.” ʸ

Makima doesn't deny it. Yuka is almost grateful.

“He's my husband,” Yuka says. “He chose me. I keep choosing him, too. When something comes through that belongs to him, leave it with him. If it's about me and you want to know more, you can ask me.” ʸ

“I know,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“Then that's the last one,” Yuka says. “Wait for me to answer.” ʸ

* * *

Yuta speaks then, for the first time. Quietly, from the stool by the window.

“Makima,” he says. ʸᵗ

She turns.

“When you felt it,” Yuta says. “What he felt about her. You said it hurt.” ʸᵗ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

Yuta looks at her for a moment, steady and mild.

“That's how it's supposed to feel,” he says. “That's how you know it's real.” ʸᵗ

Makima doesn't say anything. But she looks at Yuta for a long time, and Yuka sees something go across her face that might be the beginning of understanding, or might just be confusion. It's hard to tell.

* * *

Makima leaves first.

She stops at the door, beside Maki, who hasn't moved and hasn't relaxed and doesn't look like she's going to.

“Thank you for listening,” Makima says to Yuka. ᴹᵏ

“Thank you for saying it,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima goes.

Maki lets out a long breath and finally picks up her weapon and leans it back against the wall, further from her hand.

“I was ready,” she says. “If she'd tried anything.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“I know,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I'm still ready.” ᴹᵃᵏ

“I know.” ʸ

Maki looks at her. Then she comes over and sits down on the bench beside her and puts an arm round her shoulders, which Maki almost never does, and Yuka leans into her.

“You did good,” Maki says. ᴹᵃᵏ

“I don't know if I did.” ʸ

“You did.” Maki squeezes. “You said exactly what you meant. That's the whole thing.” ᴹᵃᵏ

Yuta gets up and pours three cups of tea from Mai's kettle and brings them over, and the three of them sit at Mai's bench and drink it and eat Rem's biscuits, and don't talk about Makima any more.

When Mai comes back, an hour later, she doesn't ask how it went. She looks at the three of them, and at the empty plate, and nods once, and sits down on the other side of Yuka and picks up the island cloth and starts showing her where she wants to cut.
