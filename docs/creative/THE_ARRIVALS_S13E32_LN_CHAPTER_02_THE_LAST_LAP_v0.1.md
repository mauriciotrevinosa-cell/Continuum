# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 22 — The Last Lap

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S13E32
**Continuity state:** B+316. Shoko lets Mau downstairs for the afternoon on condition he stays on the couch. Umaru organises a racing tournament on the new screen. Mau and Yuka end up in the final; she cheats openly; he lets Authority twitch once and is disqualified by Umaru. When he gets tired, he lets his head drop against Yuka and she holds him up; when he makes room, she climbs on. Makima sits at the far end of the room and asks before she sits anywhere. Noise is extremely competitive.

*When: Two days after he came back. Shoko has given him the afternoon downstairs, on the couch, and nowhere else.*

Umaru has drawn a chart.

It's pinned to the wall beside the screen, on a large sheet of paper Mai gave her, and it has every name in the house on it, arranged in pairs, with lines going up from each pair to a single box at the top. The box at the top says CHAMPION in letters so large and so carefully coloured that Mau can see, even in grey, how much effort went into it.

“Tournament,” Umaru announces, to the room. “Single elimination. Three laps. No items on the last lap.” ᵁ

“Why no items?” Milim demands. ᴹⁱ

“Because the last lap should be about *skill*.” ᵁ

“The *items* are skill.” ᴹⁱ

“The items are chaos. The last lap is sacred.” ᵁ

Milim opens her mouth to argue and Rem puts a biscuit in it.

* * *

Mau is on the couch.

Shoko was very clear. *The couch, Mau. Not the floor. Not the kitchen. Not the yard. The couch.* He's been on it since lunch, with a blanket over his legs that Ori insisted on and a cushion behind his back that Rem insisted on and a bag of chips at his elbow that Umaru insisted on, and he hasn't been allowed to get up even to fetch a drink. Every time he shifts, someone in the room looks at him.

He doesn't mind as much as he'd expected.

The room is full. It's the first afternoon since the clinic that the whole house has been in one place without anybody being hurt, and the noise of it is like warm water. Milim and Ori arguing about the chart. Gojo explaining to Shoko why he should be seeded first. Senku and Byakuya at the back, discussing whether the race physics are accurate, which they are not. Satella reading in the corner, looking up whenever somebody laughs. Fern keeping score on a separate sheet, because she doesn't trust Umaru's chart.

At the far end of the room, by the window, in a chair by herself, Makima.

She came in after everybody else. She stopped in the doorway and looked at the room and then at Yuka, who was sitting on the arm of the couch beside Mau, and said, “May I sit in the chair by the window?” ᴹᵏ and Yuka looked at the chair, which was nowhere near anything, and said, “Yes.” ʸ And Makima went and sat in it, and has been there ever since, with her hands folded, watching the screen with an expression of mild polite interest, as if she has never seen anything like it, which she probably hasn't.

Nobody has said anything about it. That's how the house does things. It notices, and it lets you be noticed, and it doesn't say anything until you're ready.

* * *

The first round takes an hour.

Ori beats Milim, which nobody expected, least of all Milim, who stares at the screen for a long time afterwards and then announces that she *let* Ori win, which nobody believes. Gojo beats Shoko by a margin so enormous that Shoko puts the controller down and says, “You're using the eyes,” ˢʰ and Gojo says, “I'm *always* using the eyes,” ᴳ and Shoko says, “Then you're disqualified for being yourself,” ˢʰ and Umaru rules in Shoko's favour, and Gojo sulks.

Maki beats Yuta. Yuta doesn't seem to mind.

Mau beats Taihei. He doesn't use anything. He just drives. It turns out that he's good at it, which surprises him; he doesn't remember ever being good at games. Ciel tells him privately that it's because his reaction time is absurd, and that he shouldn't tell anyone, and he doesn't.

Yuka beats Rem, very sweetly, and then apologises for beating her so sweetly that Rem laughs and says it's fine.

* * *

“You're good at this,” Yuka says, in the second round, watching him take a corner. ʸ

“I'm all right.” ᴹ

“You're *good*. Why are you good?” ʸ

“Reflexes.” ᴹ

“That's cheating.” ʸ

“It's not cheating. It's my body.” ᴹ

“Your body is cheating.” ʸ

He beats Maki in the second round, barely, on the last lap, and Maki stares at the screen with an expression of profound betrayal and then punches him in the shoulder again, harder than yesterday. Shoko tells her to stop punching the patient. Maki says the patient started it by winning.

“He's competitive,” Ciel tells him quietly. ᶜ

“Who?” ᴹ

“Noise.” ᶜ

He hadn't noticed. He pays attention. And it's true: there's something at the back of him, very focused and very intense, watching every corner, every line, every opponent's habits. Noticing that Maki always brakes too late on the third turn. Noticing that Yuka always looks at him instead of the screen right before she tries something.

“Noise,” he says, under his breath. ᴹ

“What.” ᴺ

“Are you *helping*?” ᴹ

“I'm *observing*.” ᴺ

“You're helping.” ᴹ

“I'm observing *very closely*.” ᴺ

He laughs out loud. Yuka looks at him suspiciously.

* * *

It's in the semifinal, against Gojo — who has been reinstated on appeal after promising not to use the eyes, a promise nobody believes — that he gets tired.

He doesn't notice it coming. One moment he's fine, taking a corner, Gojo's car right behind him. The next his hands feel heavy on the controller, and the screen goes a little far away, and the edges of the room soften. He blinks. He makes the corner. He almost doesn't make the next one.

It's been two days. Shoko said it would happen. *You'll be fine and then you won't. When you won't, stop.*

He doesn't stop. He's in a semifinal.

But he leans. Without deciding to. His head tips sideways until it's resting against Yuka, who's sitting beside him, her shoulder exactly where it needs to be. Her arm comes round him at once. She doesn't say anything. She just takes his weight, and holds it, and lets him lean.

He finishes the race with his head on her shoulder. He wins by half a car's length.

“Unbelievable,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“You were using the eyes,” Mau says, into Yuka's shoulder. ᴹ

“I was *not*.” ᴳ

“You were. I saw you.” ᴹ

“*You can't see me, I'm under a blindfold.*” ᴳ

“I could feel it.” ᴹ

Umaru, consulting with Fern, rules that the result stands.

* * *

The final is Mau against Yuka.

Nobody planned it that way. It's just how the chart came out. Yuka beat Ori in the other semifinal, after Ori got distracted by Lantern poking his head out of her shadow to watch the screen, and now the two of them are side by side on the couch, controllers in hand, and the whole room has gone quiet.

Even Makima, by the window, is leaning forward slightly.

“Ready?” Umaru asks. ᵁ

“Ready,” Yuka says. ʸ

Mau's still leaning on her. He hasn't moved. Her arm is still round him.

“You're going to lose,” Yuka tells him. ʸ

“We'll see.” ᴹ

“You're tired. You're leaning on me. I can feel you yawning.” ʸ

“I'm not yawning.” ᴹ

He yawns. The room laughs.

The race starts.

* * *

She cheats on the first lap.

Openly. Shamelessly. As they come into the second turn, neck and neck, she takes one hand off her controller and puts it over his eyes.

“*Yuka—*” ᴹ

“What?” ʸ

“That's cheating!” ᴹ

“It's *strategy*.” ʸ

He crashes into a wall. She takes the lead. The room erupts. Umaru, consulting with Fern again, rules that there's nothing in the rules about hands over eyes, because nobody had thought anybody would be that shameless, and that the race continues.

He catches her on the second lap. He drives one-handed, with his other arm round her, keeping her hand pinned so she can't do it again, and she laughs and squirms and tries to get free, and he holds on, and they go round the track side by side with nobody on the couch actually looking at the screen properly.

The last lap. No items. *Sacred.*

They're side by side. Neither of them can pull ahead. The final turn is coming up, and Mau knows, absolutely knows, with Noise screaming silently in the back of his head about the angle, that if he takes it tight he wins and if he takes it wide he loses.

And he feels it twitch.

Authority. Not much. Not on purpose. Just the barest flicker, a reflex, the same one that made him nudge the fish on the island. Somewhere on the coffee table, the bag of chips shifts half an inch toward Yuka's elbow, and her elbow bumps it, and her thumb slips on the controller.

He takes the turn tight. He wins.

There's a short, terrible silence.

“*Disqualified*,” Umaru says. ᵁ

“What?” ᴹ

“I saw that. The chips moved. *You* moved the chips.” ᵁ

“I didn't mean to—” ᴹ

“Authority on the last lap,” Umaru says, with the gravity of a judge. “On the *sacred* lap. Disqualified. Yuka is champion.” ᵁ

The room explodes. Yuka throws her controller in the air and shrieks. Milim tackles her from the side. Ori is jumping up and down on the couch. Gojo is laughing so hard he's crying. Shoko has her face in her hands. Fern is writing *Yuka — CHAMPION — by disqualification (Authority, chips)* on her score sheet in very small, very straight letters.

At the window, Makima has put one hand over her mouth. Mau can't tell, from here, whether she's laughing.

* * *

Later, when the room has calmed down and Umaru has written YUKA in the champion box in large, grudging letters, Mau is still on the couch.

He's so tired now he can barely keep his eyes open. He's lying sideways, his head on the cushion Rem gave him, the blanket over his legs. Yuka is sitting on the floor beside the couch, leaning back against it, eating the chips from the bag he moved, with tremendous ceremony, one at a time.

He shifts. Moves his legs. Makes a space.

She looks at the space. She looks at him.

“Is that for me?” ʸ

“It's for the champion,” he says. ᴹ

She climbs up. She doesn't sit in the space; she lies down on top of him, all of her, the way she did on the island, her head on his chest and her legs tangled with his and the bag of chips held carefully above both of them so it doesn't spill.

“Comfortable?” he asks. ᴹ

“Very.” ʸ

“You cheated.” ᴹ

“*You* cheated. I just cheated first.” ʸ

He puts his arm round her. He can feel her laughing against him, very quietly. He can feel the chips crunch. He can feel the room around them, warm and loud, and inside him, very deep and very still, a hand holding back a sea.

“Noise,” he says, under his breath. ᴹ

“*What*.” ᴺ

“You're sulking.” ᴹ

“I'm not sulking. We had that turn. We had it *clean*.” ᴺ

He falls asleep laughing, with Yuka on top of him and the champion's chips on his chest.
