# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 23 — Chocolate for Whoever Comes In

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S13E33
**Continuity state:** B+317. Shoko clears Mau for the kitchen, sitting. Yuka decides it's a chocolate day because there's no date that needs one. With Rem and Umaru they choose what to try: Shop chocolate Mau buys, Rem's cake, Umaru's demand for something with chips in it. It turns into making something for everyone who walks in, each person something different. Yuka hugs him from behind at the counter when he tries to stand up. Mau keeps the wrapper with the bird on it in his pocket now.

*When: The next day. No birthday, no anniversary, nothing on the calendar. Yuka says that's exactly why.*

“Chocolate day,” Yuka announces at breakfast. ʸ

Everyone looks at her.

“What's the occasion?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“There isn't one.” ʸ

“Then why—” ᴿ

“That's the occasion.” Yuka puts down her spoon. “We've had a red gate and a clinic and three days of him not knowing my name. Nothing's happening today. Nobody's birthday. Nobody's hurt. I want a day where the only thing that happens is chocolate.” ʸ

There's a short pause.

“Seconded,” says Umaru. ᵁ

“Seconded,” says Rem, a little shyly. ᴿᵉ

“Thirded,” says Ori, who doesn't know what it means but likes the sound of it. ᴼ

That settles it.

* * *

Shoko clears him for the kitchen.

“Sitting,” she says. “On a stool. At the counter. If I come in and you're standing, I'm taking the stool away and you can sit on the floor.” ˢʰ

“Yes, Shoko.” ᴹ

“And you don't lift anything heavier than a spoon.” ˢʰ

“What about a bowl?” ᴹ

Shoko considers. “A small bowl.” ˢʰ

So he sits on a stool at the end of the long kitchen counter, with a small bowl in front of him and a spoon, and watches the kitchen fill up.

* * *

The planning takes longer than the cooking.

Rem has opinions. Rem always has opinions in the kitchen; it's the one place where she's never shy. She wants a cake. A proper one, dark, three layers, the kind she used to make in a house a long way from here for people she doesn't talk about very much. She describes it with her hands and her eyes go somewhere else while she does it.

Umaru wants something with chips in it.

“Chocolate *and* chips,” she says. “Salty and sweet. It's science.” ᵁ

“It's not science,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

“Senku said it was science.” ᵁ

“Senku said that to make you stop asking him.” ᴿᵉ

Yuka wants to try everything.

That's her whole plan. She sits on the counter beside his stool, swinging her legs, and when he opens the Shop and starts reading the listings out loud — the catalogue that's been expanded since the market, the one with the snacks and the sweets and the long pages of things from places he's never been — she says *yes* to every single one.

“Dark?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸ

“Milk?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸ

“White?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸ

“There's one with chili in it.” ᴹ

She hesitates for exactly one second. “Yes.” ʸ

“There's one shaped like a fish.” ᴹ

“*Yes.*” ʸ

He buys one of each. Then, because she's looking at him like that, he buys two of the fish.

* * *

It starts with Rem's cake and Umaru's chips, and it doesn't stay there.

Shuna comes in to start dinner, sees the counter, and stops. She looks at the bars laid out in a long row, at Rem's three bowls of batter, at Umaru crushing chips with the bottom of a cup with enormous concentration.

“I'm making dinner,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

“You can make dinner *after*,” Yuka says. “Try this.” ʸ

She holds out a square of the dark one. Shuna looks at it. Then she takes it, because nobody can say no to Yuka on a chocolate day, and puts it in her mouth, and her face changes.

“Oh,” Shuna says. ˢʰᵘ

“Right?” ʸ

Shuna stays.

And after that, it just keeps happening. Somebody comes through the kitchen door, and Yuka sees them, and says *wait*, and makes them try something. And then she looks at their face while they try it, and turns to Mau, and says, “What would they like?” ʸ

“What?” ᴹ

“What would Shuna like? Not this. Something *for* her.” ʸ

He looks at Shuna, who's still standing there with her eyes half closed, tasting.

“Something warm,” he says slowly. “She's always cold in the mornings. She makes the bread and her hands are cold all day.” ᴹ

Yuka's face lights up. “Hot chocolate.” ʸ

“With the dark.” ᴹ

“With the *dark*,” she agrees, and slides off the counter to find a pot. ʸ

* * *

That's how the afternoon goes.

Gojo comes in looking for coffee and gets something with coffee *in* the chocolate, which he declares the finest invention of any world. Shoko comes in to check that Mau is sitting and gets a single square of the chili one, plain, no fuss, because Mau says she'd want something that bites back, and she eats it and looks at him and says, “Fine. Good,” ˢʰ which from Shoko is a speech.

Milim gets the fish. Both fish. She eats one and puts the other in her pocket for later and is still finding chocolate in her pocket a week afterwards.

Ori gets the white one, melted and poured over a plate of strawberries, which Yuka tells Mau are bright red and shiny, and which he can only see as small dark shapes in a pale pool. Ori eats every one and then licks the plate, and nobody stops her.

Frieren comes in, very late, and stands in the doorway, and looks at the chaos of the counter.

“Something old,” Mau says, before anyone asks him. “Something from a long time ago. The plainest one. Just chocolate. No milk. Nothing in it.” ᴹ

Yuka finds it. Frieren takes a square and holds it for a while before she eats it, and when she does, she closes her eyes.

“Himmel bought me this once,” she says quietly. “In a town in the south. I didn't like it.” ᶠ

“Do you like it now?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Frieren opens her eyes. She looks at the square in her hand.

“Yes,” she says. ᶠ

She takes another one, and goes away with it.

* * *

He tries to stand up once.

Rem's cake is coming out of the oven, and it's heavy, and Rem is small, and he sees her struggling with it and his body moves before his head can tell it not to. He's halfway off the stool when arms close round his waist from behind.

“*Sitting*,” Yuka says into his shoulder blade. ʸ

“She needs help with the—” ᴹ

“Rimuru's helping her. Look.” ʸ

He looks. Rimuru is already there, lifting the tin out of the oven with Rem directing him. Rem doesn't need him at all.

He sits back down. Yuka doesn't let go. She stays there, standing behind his stool, her arms round him and her chin on his shoulder, watching Rem turn the cake out onto the board.

“You were going to stand up,” she says. ʸ

“I was going to *help*.” ᴹ

“You were going to stand up and Shoko was going to come in and take your stool away and you were going to sit on the floor and I was going to have to sit on the floor with you.” ʸ

“You'd sit on the floor with me?” ᴹ

“Obviously.” ʸ

He puts his hands over hers.

“You'd hate it,” he says. ᴹ

“I'd complain the whole time,” she agrees. “But I'd do it.” ʸ

She still doesn't let go. He doesn't ask her to.

* * *

Makima comes in at the very end.

The kitchen is a wreck by then. Every bowl in the house is dirty. There's chocolate on the counter, on the floor, on Umaru's face, in Milim's hair. Shuna has given up on dinner entirely and is sitting at the table with her third cup of hot chocolate and her eyes closed. Rem's cake is two-thirds gone.

Makima stops in the doorway.

She looks at Yuka. Yuka, who's sitting on the counter again beside Mau's stool, licking a spoon. Yuka looks back.

“May I come in?” Makima asks. ᴹᵏ

There's a pause. The kitchen goes a little quieter.

“Yes,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima steps in. She stands at the end of the counter, not too close to anyone, with her hands folded.

Yuka looks at her for a moment. Then she turns to Mau.

“What would she like?” Yuka asks. ʸ

The kitchen goes very quiet.

Mau looks at Yuka. Yuka looks back, steady, the spoon still in her hand. She isn't smiling. She isn't frowning. She's just asking, the same question she's asked about everyone who's come through the door all afternoon.

He looks at Makima.

He thinks about it honestly. He thinks about the calm face and the folded hands and the three days she spent holding a sea inside him while he didn't know who anyone was. He thinks about the thing he felt through the thread last night: a flicker of something that hurt her, and that she didn't understand.

“Something she's never had,” he says. “She's tried everything that's expensive. Everything people give to someone important. She's never had anything just because it was a nice afternoon.” ᴹ

He reaches into the box. Finds the last of the milk chocolate. Plain. Ordinary. The kind that comes in a paper wrapper, the kind you'd buy at a counter on the way home.

He doesn't hand it to her. He hands it to Yuka.

Yuka looks at it. Then she breaks off a square, and holds it out across the counter.

Makima looks at the square for a long time. Then she takes it, carefully, without touching Yuka's fingers.

She eats it.

Her face doesn't change. Not much. But something in it goes very slightly soft, at the edges, for just a moment.

“Thank you,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“It's chocolate day,” Yuka says. “Everybody gets some.” ʸ

Makima nods. She doesn't say anything else. She goes and sits at the table beside Shuna, at the very end, and Shuna, without opening her eyes, slides the pot of hot chocolate toward her.

* * *

That night, when Yuka is asleep, Mau takes the wrapper out of his pocket.

The one from the red gate. The bird, or the cloud. Folded very small. He's kept it in his pocket every day since. He unfolds it now and looks at it in the light from the window.

It's a bird. He's sure of it now. It has a beak. If you squint.

He folds it back up and puts it in the drawer by the bed, next to the note from Ori that says *welcome home*, and goes to sleep.
