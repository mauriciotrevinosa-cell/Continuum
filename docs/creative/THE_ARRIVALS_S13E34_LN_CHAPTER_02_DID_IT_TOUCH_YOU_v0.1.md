# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 26 — Did It Touch You?

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 26
**Source episode:** S13E34
**Continuity state:** B+322. Shoko clears Mau for a supervised dungeon with Yuka and Yuta: a harder gate Fern has kept for Yuka's training. Yuka leads; Mau holds back on purpose. Deep in, Yuka sees an opening and goes for it overconfident. Mau already has Sky running under her footing; he puts Infinity on her as well and pays the known cost of two techniques at once (pressure, heat, a nosebleed he wipes before she sees). Yuta kills the attacker. Mau goes straight to Yuka: "Did it touch you?" She says it reached. Not a scratch. On the way back she stays glued to him.

*When: Eight days after he came back. Shoko signs the clearance with a face that says she'll regret it.*

“She leads,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuta looks at him. Yuka looks at him.

They're standing at the mouth of the gate, the three of them, in the cold early light. It's a bigger gate than the red one was, and a meaner one; Fern has been keeping it in the register for weeks, marked with a small careful note that says *for Yuka, when she's ready*. The assessment says it's full of things that move fast and hit hard and come from directions you don't expect. Good practice. Not too dangerous, with backup.

“I lead?” Yuka says. ʸ

“You lead. Yuta's on your left. I'm behind. I don't do anything unless you're about to die.” ᴹ

“That's very specific.” ʸ

“Shoko made me promise.” ᴹ

“You promised Shoko you'd only save me if I was about to *die*?” ʸ

“I promised Shoko I'd only use anything if I absolutely had to. I'm adding the dying part myself.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him for a long moment. Then she grins.

“Okay,” she says. “I lead.” ʸ

* * *

She's good.

He knew she was good. He's watched her train for years, in the yard and in the small gates and with Maki and Yuta in the woods past the stream. He's seen her technique grow from something desperate and wild into something with shape. He's seen her hold the line in the war with Mahoraga beside her and nothing left in her.

He hasn't watched her *lead* before. Not like this.

She goes in fast and careful at the same time, which is the hardest thing there is. She reads the first room before she's through the door. She calls to Yuta without looking at him — *left, two, low* — and Yuta goes left and takes two low, and she's already moving on to the next. When something comes out of the ceiling at her she's not where it lands. When something comes out of the floor she's already turning.

He stays behind. Ten paces. He keeps his hands in his pockets. He has Sky running, very faint, under her feet: just enough that the floor is always where she thinks it is, that a slick patch or a loose stone won't take her ankle. That's all. That's the only thing he's doing. She doesn't know it's there.

“You're grinning,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“I'm not.” ᴹ

“You've been grinning since the second room.” ᶜ

He is. He can't stop. She's *beautiful* at this.

* * *

Deep in, it changes.

They've been going for an hour. The rooms have been getting bigger and the things in them faster, and Yuka has been getting better with every one. She's found her rhythm. She's moving like water now, Yuta flowing around her, the two of them clearing room after room so cleanly that Mau, ten paces behind, has nothing to do but watch.

Then they come into a long hall.

It's wide and high and dim, with pillars down both sides, and at the far end there's something big. A shape. Crouched. Still. The eyes read it: heavy, dense, armoured on the front, soft at the back. Fast. Very fast. Something that waits for you to come close, and then doesn't wait any more.

“Careful,” Yuta says quietly. ʸᵗ

Yuka doesn't answer. She's looking at the thing. Mau can see her looking. He can see her read it, the way he's taught her, the way Maki's taught her: armour front, soft back.

And he sees her see the opening.

It's real. He sees it too. The thing is turned slightly, its armoured front toward the pillar on the left, and there's a line, a clean line, from where Yuka is standing along the right side of the hall, behind the pillars, to the soft place at its back. If she's fast enough. If she goes now.

She goes.

* * *

She's fast. She's very fast. She goes down the right side of the hall behind the pillars in a blur, low and silent, and she's halfway there before Yuta can say her name.

Mau sees it before she does.

There's a second one. Behind the first. He didn't see it; the eyes didn't see it. It was so still and so dense it read as part of the wall. And it's moving now, unfolding from the dark behind the pillar Yuka is about to pass, and it's faster than the first, and it's coming at her from the side, from the one direction she's not looking.

She sees it. Too late. He sees her see it. He sees her face change.

He already has Sky running. Under her feet. One technique.

He reaches for Infinity.

* * *

He knows what it'll cost.

He's done it before. Two techniques at once, both his own, both running at the same time in the same body. Noise built the support for it, back before the long dungeon. They tested it: the neutral and a small patch of Sky, two objects, a couple of heartbeats. It worked. It cost more than both of them separately. It made his head hot and his eyes ache and Gojo watched him very closely afterward.

This isn't a small patch. This is Sky holding her footing at full speed and the neutral wrapped round her whole body, at forty paces, on something that's moving.

He doesn't think about it.

He puts Infinity on her.

* * *

The thing hits her.

He sees it hit. Its whole weight, its armoured shoulder, coming in from the side at a speed that would have broken every bone on that side of her body. It hits.

And stops.

A hand's breadth from her ribs. Held in the space that never closes. Hanging there in the air, confused, its momentum going nowhere, the way everything hangs in his Infinity.

Yuka is standing on the floor that Sky is holding steady, with the neutral wrapped round her, completely untouched, staring at the thing a hand's breadth from her side.

And something in Mau's head goes white-hot.

It's not the scream from the clinic. It's not that. But it's close enough to make his stomach drop. Heat, behind his eyes, pouring in. Pressure in his temples. The world tilting slightly. Something warm on his upper lip.

He holds both. He holds both for one breath. Two.

And then Yuta is there.

* * *

He doesn't see Yuta move. Yuta is just suddenly *there*, beside the thing hanging in the air, and his sword goes in at the soft place where the armour doesn't reach, and out the other side.

The thing goes still.

Mau lets go. Of the neutral first. Then Sky. Both at once, like setting down two heavy bags. The heat behind his eyes drops away so fast it leaves him dizzy.

He wipes his lip with the back of his hand. It comes away dark. He wipes it on his trousers before anyone can see.

Then he's moving.

He doesn't run. He doesn't remember deciding to cross the hall. He's ten paces behind and then he's not; he's there, in front of her, his hands on her shoulders, her arms, her ribs on the side where it hit, feeling for anything wrong.

“Did it touch you?” ᴹ

His voice comes out strange. Rough. Too fast.

“Did it touch you, Yuka, did it—” ᴹ

“It reached,” she says. ʸ

He stops.

She's looking at him. Her face is very pale. Her eyes are very wide. She isn't hurt. He can see she isn't hurt; he can feel it under his hands. Not a scratch. Not a bruise.

“It reached,” she says again. “It was right there. And then it wasn't. You—” ʸ

She looks down at herself. At the space beside her ribs where the thing stopped.

“You put it on me,” she says. “The thing. The Infinity. On *me*.” ʸ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

“And you had—you already had something—” ʸ

“Sky. Under your feet. The whole time.” ᴹ

She stares at him.

“You had *two* going,” she says. “At once. That's the thing Shoko said you're not supposed to—” ʸ

“For one breath,” he says. “Two. That's all.” ᴹ

* * *

She looks at his face for a long time. Then she reaches up, very slowly, and touches his upper lip with her thumb.

He wiped it. He thought he wiped it.

She looks at her thumb. There's a faint dark smear on it.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't get angry. She doesn't tell him he shouldn't have. She just looks at the smear on her thumb, and then at him, and then she steps forward and puts her arms round him and her face against his chest and holds on.

He holds her back.

Behind them, Yuta is cleaning his sword. He doesn't look at them. After a while he says, very mildly, “There were two,” ʸᵗ to nobody in particular, and goes to check that there aren't three.

* * *

She doesn't lead the rest of the way.

She doesn't want to. She walks beside Mau, close, so close their shoulders bump every few steps, and when they come to a room she doesn't go in first. She waits for Yuta. She stays where Mau is.

He doesn't say anything about it.

She takes his hand on the way out. She doesn't let go of it at the gate. She doesn't let go of it on the long walk home across the fields, even when he has to climb a stile one-handed and nearly falls over. She doesn't let go of it when they come up past the orchard and the house comes into view.

“I'm sorry,” she says, finally, at the edge of the yard. ʸ

“For what?” ᴹ

“I went too fast. I saw the opening and I just—went. I didn't check. I was so sure.” ʸ

“The opening was real,” he says. ᴹ

“There were *two*.” ʸ

“The eyes didn't see the second one either,” he says. “Neither did Yuta. It was a good opening. It was the right call with what you could see. You just couldn't see enough.” ᴹ

She thinks about that.

“You're not angry,” she says. ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“You *should* be angry.” ʸ

“I'm not.” He looks at her. “You were beautiful in there. All of it. Every room. I couldn't stop watching.” ᴹ

She goes red. He can see it, because she's the only thing in the world he can see in colour, and it goes all the way up to her ears.

“Shut up,” she says. ʸ

“You were.” ᴹ

“*Shut up.*” ʸ

She doesn't let go of his hand.
