# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 26 — Two Plates

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 26
**Source episode:** S13E34
**Continuity state:** B+320, afternoon. Mau walks to Mai's workshop to meet Yuka. Mai gives him a pocket sleeve made from the island cloth, and he uses it to keep the folded chocolate wrapper from the Red Gate. He invites Yuka out for food. They make a short walk first and choose where to stop. Seven days after his memory returned, he can move comfortably and say when he has had enough. Ori, Milim and Umaru make room for them at the table. Mau put a second piece of toast on his own plate because he knew Yuka would take the first.

*When: The afternoon after the test in the yard. The workshop door is open and Mai has put her foot against it to keep it that way.*

“He's waiting for you,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Yuka has a pin between her lips and both hands occupied. She looks up at the window first. Then at the door, which is open just far enough for her to see half of Mau. He's leaning on the frame with his hands in his pockets. From that angle she can't tell how long he's been there.

She sticks the pin into the cushion. “Did you come all the way down here yourself?” ʸ

“I got up myself, too.” ᴹ

“Incredible. Next time ask me how to put your shoes on,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Mau puts one foot in the doorway for her inspection. Both laces are tied. One is longer than the other and he has, as usual, stuffed it into the top of the shoe rather than untie it and try again. Yuka knows exactly how long that arrangement will last on the orchard path.

“I wasn't sure you'd be done,” he says. “I can wait.” ᴹ

She looks down at what she's holding: the corner of a long seam, one edge a little crooked. An hour ago she would have wanted to put the whole thing down at the sound of his shoes in the hall. Now she wants to see if the corner opens out properly. Mai holds the other end against the bench while she works the fabric around her thumb. They pull it straight between them. The corner is square.

“Now I'm done.” Yuka puts it down flat. “What are we doing?” ʸ

“I was going to take you out to eat.” ᴹ

“Where?” ʸ

“I know somewhere that serves toast whenever you get there.” ᴹ

She looks at him. “Have you told Shuna she's running a restaurant?” ʸ

“I asked if she had enough for two. She said there was enough until Umaru found out there was toast.” ᴹ

Mai lifts her foot off the door. Mau catches it before it swings shut.

“Hang on.” Mai turns away from the bench and picks something up off the shelf. “If you're here, I may as well give it to you.” ᴹᵃ

It's a sleeve about the width of two fingers, with a folded lip to hold it shut. Yuka recognises the cloth before she sees the stitches. Mai has turned the edge in so neatly that she can't find where the seam starts.

“I made it from the bit we cut off,” Mai says. “For whatever's making that noise in your pocket.” ᴹᵃ

Mau takes his hand out. The paper from the Red Gate comes with it, flattened into a tiny square. The crease across the middle of the bird's back has gone white.

“That loud, huh?” ᴹ

“You do it when you're thinking.” ᴹᵃ

He opens the sleeve very carefully and tries the paper in it. It slips down to the fold and stays there. Then he turns the whole thing over in his hand and looks at the cloth.

“Tell him,” Mai says to Yuka. ᴹᵃ

Yuka sets her elbow on the bench beside him. She pulls the loose end of the cloth itself into the light, where they can both see it move.

“It's the colour of the water by the steps, before it gets deep. Remember when you dropped the strap on your sandal and I could see it all the way down?” ʸ

“I remember you telling me to go in after it.” ᴹ

“You were already wet.” ʸ

“Up to my ankles.” ᴹ

She laughs. “That colour. It's a little lighter around the fold. It changes when you move it.” ʸ

Mau tips the sleeve from side to side. The light moves on it, much the way it moves on water even if all he gets is the light.

“I can keep that,” he says. He looks up at Mai. “Thank you.” ᴹ

Mai clears both the pins and the cushion off a part of the bench that was clear already. “Bring it back if you tear it. It'll take me as long to fix it as it took to make it.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka is out in the hall with him before she thinks to ask how long that was. Behind them, Mai drops the latch back into place.

* * *

They take the long way to the kitchen. The day has warmed enough that the stone in front of the house feels dry under their feet. On the porch Yuka waits for Mau to take the orchard path. He goes straight down the steps, all three at his usual pace, and looks back when she doesn't follow.

“You coming?” ᴹ

“Looking at your laces,” she says. ʸ

He looks down too. The long one has come out. He puts his foot on the lowest step and ties it properly this time. Yuka makes it down the stairs while he's still bent over and lets the back of her hand brush his shoulder on her way past.

They make it as far as the tree with the branch low enough for Ori to sit on. Someone has left a wide board across two stumps there. It makes a good seat if you don't mind the bark on the back of your legs.

“Can we sit here a minute?” Mau says. “I've got about this far in me today.” ᴹ

Yuka sits. She settles at one end, and he fits himself next to her. They've made it farther than he did two days ago. She knows because that day she watched him from the kitchen and had to peel the same apple twice, once for the skin and once to give her hands something to do while he got back up the steps.

“I had enough for the walk out,” he says. “I wanted to have some left for the afternoon.” ᴹ

“Good,” she says. “I'm going to eat for most of it.” ʸ

He waits another minute while she tells him what they finished in the workshop, including the corner he saw her turn. There's going to be enough cloth for something for their room, if she and Mai can agree where it should go. Mau asks what they've agreed on so far.

“The colour,” Yuka says. ʸ

He laughs. When they get up, he has both feet under him in one movement. He waits while she picks the bark off her skirt and then they go in together.

* * *

Shuna is at the table with two plates and a jar of plum jam. There's butter on the first piece of toast already. Yuka reaches past Mau toward it and he slides the plate nearer to her, an old reflex so fast he does it before either of them says hello.

“Did you have a nice walk?” Shuna asks. ˢʰᵘ

“We did,” Mau says. “Could I have an extra piece on mine?” ᴹ

Yuka gets herself a knife. She's spreading jam when Ori comes in carrying three cups against her chest and asks how many seats they're going to need. She has apparently already asked everyone else in the room. Milim is bringing the chairs from the other table. Umaru has moved the racing chart far enough to make five places and has written *ME* on the space nearest the butter.

“I thought it was two,” Shuna says, amused. ˢʰᵘ

“It was,” Mau says. “They've got us surrounded.” ᴹ

Ori sets the cups down and brings a chair over from the other side of the table. She gets it next to Yuka's. The legs scrape. Something is wrong with the angle, so she tries again.

“This is yours,” she tells Mau. “So she'll be able to reach you.” ᴼ

Yuka sees him consider asking what she means. He looks at the chair. He looks at Yuka. He sits where he's been put.

Milim has decided to butter both sides of her toast. Umaru points out that she will have to put one of the sides down somewhere. They are still trying to settle this when Yuka finishes her own first piece and takes a corner off one of Mau's. She eats it slowly, watching him over the edge of her cup.

He takes a drink.

She takes the rest of that piece. There underneath it, cut into the same shape and with exactly the amount of butter she likes, is the second.

“You ordered that for me,” she says. ʸ

“I ordered it for me. The one you took was yours.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at her own empty plate. “I had one.” ʸ

“You had an opening argument,” Umaru says, buttering her third. ᵁ

Yuka laughs, and then catches Mau looking at her with so much quiet satisfaction that it takes her a second to remember what she'd been about to say. She moves her chair into the space Ori made. Her elbow finds his. He eats his toast while it's hot. She lets him finish almost all of it.

Afterward, while Ori is trying to persuade Milim that an upside-down cup has no bottom, Yuka looks down and sees a thin strip of blue at the mouth of Mau's pocket. She pulls it just far enough into the light for him to notice her hand.

“That was a date,” she tells him. ʸ

He looks across at their table. Umaru has drawn a little car in the jam on her plate. Milim has the cup upside down on her head and is trying to pretend she doesn't.

“I was hoping you'd think so,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka puts the cloth back in his pocket. She keeps her hand there for a second, flat over it. Under the table he turns his knee until it touches hers.
