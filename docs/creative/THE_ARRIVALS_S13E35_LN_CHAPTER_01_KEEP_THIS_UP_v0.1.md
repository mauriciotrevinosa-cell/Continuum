# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 27 — Keep This Up

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S13E35
**Continuity state:** B+322, afternoon. Home from the gate. Mau tells Shoko about the two techniques and the nosebleed before Yuka can; Shoko checks him and finds nothing new, and is angry anyway. Then Yuka settles on top of him on the couch and announces, with complete seriousness, that he has earned having her there all day and feeding her. He takes a dessert out of the Shop. "Keep this up and you'll get yourself a wife." He takes out a second. She laughs. The afternoon of desserts; Gojo's tally; Yuta tells the house about the second monster in his own way.

*When: Early afternoon. They're back before lunch is cleared away. Shoko is waiting on the porch, because Fern told her when they went in and Fern can count.*

He tells Shoko himself.

He decides on the walk home, somewhere around the stile. If Yuka tells her, it'll be a report. If Yuta tells her, it'll be a list. If he tells her, it'll be a confession, and Shoko is better with confessions than with reports.

So when they come up onto the porch and Shoko looks at him with her arms folded, he says, “Two techniques. At once. For two breaths. My nose bled. I wiped it.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him.

Yuka, beside him, says, “I was going to tell her.” ʸ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“I had a whole speech.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

Shoko unfolds her arms.

“Inside,” she says. “Couch.” ˢʰ

* * *

She checks everything.

She takes a long time about it. Her hands on his head, his temples, the back of his neck. Her fingers at the corners of his eyes. Ciel talking to her quietly the whole time, aloud, numbers and pressures. Noise, once, saying something about the hold, about Makima's hand on the sea, that it didn't move. Not even a little. Not even during.

“Nothing,” Shoko says finally. “Nothing new. The vessel that bled was small and it's already closed. The hold didn't shift.” ˢʰ

She sits back.

Then she hits him on the arm. Not hard. Hard enough.

“*Ow.*” ᴹ

“Two techniques,” Shoko says. “Eight days after you bled out of your eyes in my clinic. Two techniques, at forty paces, on a moving target.” ˢʰ

“She was about to get hit.” ᴹ

“I know she was about to get hit. Yuta told me in about four words on his way past.” ˢʰ

“What were the four words?” ᴹ

“*He did the thing.*” Shoko hits him again. “I'm not angry that you did it. I'd have done it. Anybody in this house would have done it. I'm angry because you *could* do it, and I didn't know you could do it at that scale, and now I'm going to lie awake wondering what else you can do that nobody's told me about.” ˢʰ

He doesn't have an answer to that.

“Bang,” he offers. ᴹ

“I know about Bang. I was *there*.” ˢʰ

“Then that's everything.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him for a long moment, the way she looks at a wound that's healing better than she expected and that she doesn't trust.

“Rest,” she says. “The whole afternoon. If you get off that couch I'll know.” ˢʰ

She goes. At the door she stops and looks back at Yuka.

“Make sure he doesn't get up,” she says. ˢʰ

“Oh,” Yuka says, “he's not getting up.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't understand what she means at first.

He's lying on the couch, on his back, with his head on the arm and his feet hanging off the other end, because the couch was built for people shorter than him. He's tired. Not the bone-tired of the red gate, or the fog-tired of the clinic. Just ordinary tired, the kind you get from a long morning, with a dull ache behind his eyes and a pleasant heaviness in his arms.

Yuka stands beside the couch and looks down at him.

Then she climbs on.

Not beside him. On him. She lies down on top of him, all of her, from his chest to his knees, her elbows planted on either side of his head, her chin propped on her fists, so that her face is right above his and her hair falls down around both of them like a curtain.

She looks at him with an expression of enormous seriousness.

“You saved my life,” she says. ʸ

“It reached,” he says. “You said it reached.” ᴹ

“It *reached*. It didn't *hit*. Because you saved my life.” ʸ

“Yuta killed it.” ᴹ

“Yuta *killed* it. You *saved* me. Those are different.” ʸ

He doesn't argue. She's very close and very serious and the curtain of her hair is warm and smells of the gate and the wind across the fields.

“So,” Yuka says. ʸ

“So?” ᴹ

“So you've earned it.” ʸ

“Earned what?” ᴹ

She settles more firmly on top of him. She makes a small satisfied sound, like a cat finding the warm spot on a bed.

“Me,” she says. “Here. All day. You've earned having me right here on top of you for the entire rest of the afternoon. And you've earned feeding me.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“*I've* earned feeding *you*?” ᴹ

“It's a privilege,” Yuka says, with great dignity. ʸ

“It's a privilege.” ᴹ

“A very great privilege. Not everybody gets to feed me. Only people who've saved my life.” ʸ

“That's a short list.” ᴹ

“It's a *very* short list,” she agrees. “It's you. You're the list.” ʸ

* * *

He opens the Shop.

He doesn't think about it. He just reaches past her, past the curtain of her hair, and opens it the way he opens a door he's opened a thousand times, and finds the page with the sweets, and finds the first thing on it that she'd like.

A little tart. Small, round, in a paper cup, with something glossy on top and a pale cream underneath. Yuka has told him before that the glossy part is the colour of the sun going down. He's never seen it. He knows she likes it.

He takes it out. He holds it up between them.

Yuka looks at it.

Then she looks at him.

Her face does something. He watches it go from surprise to delight to something much softer, something that makes her eyes go bright and her mouth go small.

“Keep this up,” she says, “and you'll get yourself a wife.” ʸ

He reaches past her again. Opens the Shop. Takes out a second one. Holds it up beside the first.

Yuka stares at the two tarts. Then at him.

And she starts to laugh.

It comes up out of her all at once, helpless, her whole body shaking with it on top of his, her forehead dropping onto his chest. She laughs so hard she can't breathe. She laughs so hard he has to hold both tarts up out of the way so she doesn't knock them out of his hands. She laughs until he's laughing too, under her, the two of them shaking on the couch, and he doesn't even know exactly what's so funny, except that it's her, and it's this, and he married her, and she's lying on top of him in the middle of the afternoon telling him he might get himself a wife.

“You already have one,” she gasps, into his chest. “You *already have one*, and you took out a *second*—” ʸ

“You said keep it up.” ᴹ

“I didn't mean—” ʸ

She can't finish. She's laughing too hard.

* * *

He feeds her.

It takes a long time, because she keeps laughing, and because she insists on doing it properly: he has to hold the tart, and she takes a bite, and then he has to wait, and she takes another bite, and when she gets cream on her chin he has to wipe it off, and when she says the glossy part is very good, he has to agree, even though he hasn't had any.

“You can have some,” she says magnanimously, halfway through the second one. ʸ

She holds it out to him. He takes a bite. It's sweet and tart at once, the glossy part sharp and the cream underneath cool and soft.

“Good?” ʸ

“Very good.” ᴹ

“It's the colour of the sun going down,” she tells him. ʸ

“I know. You told me.” ᴹ

“I'm telling you again.” ʸ

She takes the rest of it.

People come in. Of course they do. It's the common room in the middle of the afternoon.

Milim comes in first, sees them, and stops dead.

“Why is she *on* you?” she demands. ᴹⁱ

“I saved her life,” Mau says. ᴹ

“He saved my life,” Yuka confirms, from his chest. “I'm his reward.” ʸ

Milim considers this with great seriousness.

“Do I get to lie on someone if I save their life?” ᴹⁱ

“You have to ask them first,” Yuka says. ʸ

Milim thinks about it, nods, and goes off, presumably to find someone whose life she can save.

Gojo comes in a while later. He stops in the doorway. He takes in the scene: Mau flat on his back on the couch, Yuka lying on top of him from chest to knees with her chin on her fists, two empty paper cups balanced on Mau's stomach, a third tart in Mau's hand because Yuka has requested another one.

Gojo takes out a small notebook and makes a mark in it.

“What was that?” Yuka asks suspiciously. ʸ

“Nothing,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“That's the tally. Mai told me about the tally.” ʸ

“There's no tally.” ᴳ

“How many?” ʸ

Gojo looks at the notebook.

“Forty-seven,” he admits. “But this one counts double. Because you're *on* him.” ᴳ

“That's not fair—” ʸ

“My tally, my rules.” ᴳ

He goes away whistling.

* * *

Yuta comes in last, with Maki, and sits down in the armchair across from the couch. Maki perches on the arm.

Ori is right behind them. She's heard. Everyone's heard. Ori sits on the floor beside the couch, by Mau's head, and looks up at Yuka.

“Yuta says there were two,” she says. ᴼ

“There were two,” Yuka agrees. ʸ

“And you didn't see the second one.” ᴼ

“Nobody saw the second one.” ʸ

Ori turns to Yuta. “Tell it,” she commands. ᴼ

Yuta looks at her. Then at Maki. Then, very slightly, he smiles.

“There was a hall,” he says. “There was a monster at the end. Yuka saw a gap and went for it. She was very fast.” ʸᵗ

He pauses.

“There was a second monster,” he says. “It was very quiet. It came out of the wall. It hit her.” ʸᵗ

Ori gasps.

“And it stopped,” Yuta says. “In the air. A hand away from her. Because Mau put his Infinity on her. From the other end of the hall. While he was already doing something else.” ʸᵗ

“And then?” Ori demands. ᴼ

Yuta shrugs.

“And then I killed it,” he says. “And Mau ran across the whole hall and grabbed her and said *did it touch you* about six times. Very fast. In a funny voice.” ʸᵗ

“It wasn't a funny voice,” Mau protests. ᴹ

“It was a *very* funny voice,” Yuka says into his chest. ʸ

“It was the funniest voice I've ever heard you use,” Yuta says mildly. “And I've heard you sing.” ʸᵗ

Maki starts laughing. Ori starts laughing. Yuka, on top of him, is shaking again.

Mau lies on the couch with his wife on top of him and a tart in his hand and the whole room laughing at him, and thinks that he's been laughed at by better people, in worse places, and that he's never once minded it less.

“Another one,” Yuka says, holding out her hand. ʸ

He opens the Shop.
