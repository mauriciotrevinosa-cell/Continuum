# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 29 — The Best Part of the Day

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 29
**Source episode:** S13E35
**Continuity state:** B+323, seventeen days since the island return and thirteen since the collapse. Mau and Yuka share breakfast, part of the morning at the workshop and the afternoon in the kitchen. Mai asks Mau directly to try a clasp she made for herself, and wants him there for the first moment it finally closes the way she intended. After supper Mau and Yuka take their usual walk together. He tells her why he considered Ieiri. They decide it stays on the list to choose together.

*When: B+323. The morning after the dungeon with Yuta, Yuka says she'd like to go to the orchard with Mau after supper. Then she takes a piece of his toast and asks if he's coming to the workshop with her.*

“Are you coming to the workshop with me?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“After Senku's done with me,” he says. “Mai asked me to look at something.” ᴹ

Yuka puts the toast back long enough to take his hand. “Mine's later. I have to see Rem about lunch.” ʸ

“You'd better take the rest of that, then.” ᴹ

She looks down at his plate. He moves it close enough for her to take the whole piece without letting go of his fingers. They finish breakfast like that, passing the plate back and forth with the hands they have left.

Senku gets him for ten minutes on the porch. He wants to know whether the shard from the red gate is colder at the tip or at the base. Mau reaches out with his good hand; by the time he's settled on the tip, Yuka has come back to the door and is waiting for him with a cup of tea. He takes the cup. She keeps the handle until she's sure he's got it.

“The tip,” he tells Senku. ᴹ

“You're sure?” ˢᵉ

Yuka tests both ends with one knuckle. She draws it back, points at the tip, and makes a face.

“The tip,” she agrees. ʸ

Senku writes it down. Mau gives her the cup back so she can get warm again. They bring it with them all the way to the workshop.

* * *

Mai waits for Yuka to go and see Rem. Then she pushes a wooden box toward Mau with both hands.

“I want to see whether you can open this,” she says. ᴹᵃ

He looks at the little clasp she made for it. “Is it supposed to keep me out?” ᴹ

“Only if you do it the wrong way.” ᴹᵃ

The clasp is steel, a curved piece small enough for Mai to close her hand round. He recognises where she's left the edge thick enough to hold with the pad of a finger. He tries it once with his good hand and once with the other one. The second time it catches before he quite gets it open.

“Where?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

He sets it half open so she can feel the place for herself. She does. Then she takes a little file out of the groove on the other side of the bench. He holds the box down while she takes just enough off the edge.

“What's going in it?” he asks. ᴹ

“My things,” she says. “I keep ending up with everyone else's fittings in the drawer. I wanted one I could leave open and put something down in and find it when I came back.” ᴹᵃ

He looks for somewhere beside her to put the latch she's taken apart. She pushes the chair with her foot until he can sit down and reach it without stretching.

“Am I still doing it with the bad hand?” ᴹ

“With whichever hand you want. I'd like you to stay till I've got it right.” ᴹᵃ

He stays. She tries the latch twice before she likes how it sounds. On the third try she leaves her hand on the lid and looks sideways at him, waiting for him to hear what she did.

It opens with the softest click. He lets her enjoy the sound first.

“That's good,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” she says. Then she opens it again, just to hear it do the same thing twice. ᴹᵃ

* * *

Later, he finds Yuka in the kitchen with Rem. She's got flour to both elbows and a dab of it on her cheek.

“Can you get this?” she asks, offering him the wrong cheek. ʸ

He wipes the right one. She lifts a bare patch of her wrist to his nose so he can smell the orange she's been peeling. Then she puts a bowl near his elbow.

Rem wants the peel in long strips for the top, rather than in whatever shape Mau can manage on his first try. Yuka shows him how she wants him to hold it. He gets four good ones in a row. She feeds him a segment of orange and puts her hip against his side so she can reach his bowl while she works.

“Did Mai's clasp work?” she asks. ʸ

He tells her about the little click. She smiles down at the peel, pleased enough on Mai's behalf to forget the next thing she was going to say. They stay at the counter till Rem has a bowlful of peel for the tart and the two of them have eaten nearly all the oranges.

* * *

Some nights, once the plates are cleared, Mau and Yuka end up on the porch. Once it was the roof, because she wanted to see if the stars were in the same places as on the island. Tonight he's been thinking about what Shoko asked and how he'd like to tell Yuka where they can hear each other.

At supper, she's across the table between Maki and Mai. Gojo is telling a story about a curse that turned out to be a very large cat. Yuka catches Mau's eye when Ori asks if she can have one. He knows exactly how much effort it takes her to keep a straight face. He passes her the bread when she asks for it and ends up smiling at her across the table anyway.

Gojo makes a mark in his notebook.

“That's not a tally thing,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Everything's a tally thing,” Gojo says. ᴳ

* * *

Then the dishes, and Shuna sitting down, and Ori being sent to bed and arguing about it, and the house going quiet.

He goes out to the porch.

She's already there.

She's leaning on the rail with her back to the door, and she doesn't turn round when he comes out. She just holds out her hand behind her, without looking, and he takes it.

“Orchard?” she says. ʸ

“Orchard.” ᴹ

* * *

They walk.

The orchard at this hour is cool and quiet and smells of the grass under the trees. The light is going. He can see it going: the way it lies along the ground and thins and lifts, the shadows of the trunks stretching out long and soft across the grass. Grey on grey. And her beside him, in the middle of all that grey, in colour, the last of the light caught in her hair.

They don't talk for a while. They don't need to. She swings their joined hands a little as they walk, the way Ori does.

“I have to tell you something,” he says. ᴹ

She looks up at him.

“It's not bad,” he says quickly. “Shoko told me something about the morning at the clinic. About what she said before I fell. I keep thinking I should ask you.” ᴹ

“Ask me what?” ʸ

“About the name.” ᴹ

* * *

He tells her.

Sitting with Shoko in the clinic. Talking about the island, and who to bring, and then about surnames they'd tried on the island. And then telling Shoko which name he'd thought about and why. *I could take yours*.

“*Ieiri*,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Mau Ieiri.” ᴹ

She stops walking.

He waits for it. He doesn't know what he's waiting for. Then her face does something, and she puts her free hand over her mouth, and she starts to laugh.

“What?” ᴹ

“You *asked Shoko* if you could take her *name*?” ʸ

“I didn't ask. I just said it.” ᴹ

“And what did she say?” ʸ

“She said it was stupid.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs harder.

“Then she looked out the window for a long time. She told me later she'd said *ask Yuka* when I was sitting there. I missed that part.” ᴹ

Yuka stops laughing.

* * *

She doesn't say anything for a while. They start walking again, slower.

“She meant it,” Yuka says finally. ʸ

“I think so.” ᴹ

“She looked out the window?” ʸ

“For a long time.” ᴹ

Yuka nods slowly. She's quiet. He can see her thinking about it, properly, the way she thinks about things that matter: with her head a little on one side and her lower lip caught in her teeth.

“It's a good name,” she says. ʸ

“It's a good name,” he agrees. ᴹ

“You told her she's the closest thing you've had to a mother?” ʸ

“I told her a long time ago. I wanted her to know I still meant it.” ᴹ

“And she'd hate it,” Yuka says. “She'd pretend to hate it for the rest of her life. And she'd love it.” ʸ

He laughs.

“But,” Yuka says. ʸ

He waits.

“I want—” She stops. Starts again. “I think I want it to be something we both picked. Even if it's the name you've been thinking about since the island. Even if we end up picking it anyway.” ʸ

She looks up at him.

“Is that stupid?” ʸ

“No,” he says. ᴹ

“You can say if it's stupid.” ʸ

“It's not stupid. It's what I want too.” ᴹ

He means it. He didn't know he meant it until she said it. But he does.

“So it's on the list,” Yuka says. ʸ

“It's on the list.” ᴹ

“Near the top.” ʸ

“Near the top.” ᴹ

“And we keep looking.” ʸ

“We keep looking,” he says, and she squeezes his hand. ᴹ

* * *

The third tree from the left has a branch that's good for sitting on.

They've sat there before. It's low and thick and grows out sideways almost level before it turns up, and Yuka knows exactly where he puts his hand before he sits down beside her.

He lifts her onto it. She lets him. Then he sits beside her, and the branch takes both of them, and they sit there with their legs hanging down and their shoulders touching and look back through the trees at the house.

The lamps are coming on. One window, then another. The kitchen. The common room, where someone is playing a game and Umaru is shouting. Upstairs, Ori's window, where Ori is supposed to be asleep and isn't, because there's a small shape at the glass looking out at the orchard.

Yuka sees it too. She lifts her hand and waves.

The small shape at the window waves back, frantically, and then disappears very fast, as if someone has come in.

Yuka laughs and leans her head on his shoulder.

“This is my favourite part,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't ask of what. He knows.

“Mine too,” he says. ᴹ

“Do you remember when I asked if we could bring the last piece of bread out here one night?” she says. ʸ

“We ate it before we got to the tree.” ᴹ

“You ate it before we got to the tree.” ʸ

“You gave it to me.” ᴹ

“I told you to carry it.” ʸ

She puts her face in his shoulder and laughs until the branch shakes. When she straightens, his arm is already round her. She sets her hand over it and looks back at the house. There's still enough light in the kitchen window to see someone's elbow moving at the sink.

* * *

They stay until the lamps are all lit and the orchard has gone dark around them, and the only light is the house, warm and soft through the trees.

Then she slides down off the branch and holds her arms up, and he comes down after her, and she catches him, sort of, the way she caught him on the couch on the first night home when he came back from being a slime. They stand there in the dark under the tree for a moment with her arms round his neck.

For the first few seconds he's still got his hand on the branch behind him. She feels him check his footing, then takes one step into the space between his shoes. His hand comes down to her waist.

“You managed to spend a whole evening with me without giving me homework,” she says. ʸ

“We have a list.” ᴹ

“We can lose it whenever we want.” ʸ

He smiles. “I'd like to keep it.” ᴹ

That is what gets her. He says it with a little crease between his eyes, as if she might actually throw it out and he'd have to go looking for it in the dark. She pulls him within reach and kisses him. She can feel the laugh leave him before he kisses her back. His hand moves from her waist to the middle of her back. She leans into it and stays long enough to forget that there are lights on in the house at all.

“Tomorrow,” Yuka says, against his collar. ʸ

“Tomorrow,” he agrees. ᴹ

“Same time.” ʸ

“Same time.” ᴹ

They walk back to the house holding hands, slowly, the long way round, and when they get to the porch, Gojo is sitting on the steps with his notebook open on his knee, and doesn't say anything, and makes a mark.
