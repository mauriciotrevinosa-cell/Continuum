# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 32 — Badly Arranged

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 32
**Source episode:** S13E36
**Continuity state:** B+324, after supper. The common room is free; the house is at Umaru's tournament in the kitchen. Mau and Yuka do nothing: a movie, just the two of them. He settles on the couch; she climbs on top of him and immediately informs him he is badly arranged and she can't see. He fixes everything she names with Authority without getting up: cushions, blanket, lamp, popcorn, the table, the curtain, himself. Every instruction obeyed. She is delighted. Halfway through she falls asleep; he watches the rest of the movie with no idea what it's about.

*When: After supper on Maomao's chocolate day. Umaru has moved her second tournament into the kitchen because Senku wanted to test whether the screen works on a different wall, and the common room is empty.*

The common room is empty.

That never happens. He stands in the doorway for a moment just looking at it: the couch, the armchairs, the screen dark on the wall, the curtain half drawn. Nobody on the floor. Nobody arguing about the chart. Through the wall he can hear the tournament in the kitchen, Umaru announcing a disqualification, Milim protesting, the whole house laughing.

The common room is empty, and it's the best part of the day, and he's standing in it.

He goes and finds Yuka.

* * *

She's in the hall, already coming to find him. They meet halfway and both stop.

She's still in her shorts and the old McLaren with the papaya across the shoulders, which is what she calls that part of it so often he can put a name to the panel as soon as he sees it. He's got the good blanket folded over his forearm. He has to push it down far enough to free one hand from the sleeve of his Ferrari hoodie.

“That one,” she says, touching the blanket. “You remembered.” ʸ

“I brought the bowl, too.” He lifts the bowl he's got by the rim. ᴹ

She looks at the bowl. It has enough room for exactly the amount she'd have claimed was too much popcorn while he was getting it, then eaten half of. She comes the rest of the way down the hall and takes the heavy end of the blanket.

“The common room's empty,” he says. ᴹ

Her eyes go wide.

“*Empty* empty?” ʸ

“Empty empty.” ᴹ

She grabs his hand and pulls.

* * *

He gets to the couch first.

He lies down on it the way he likes to, on his back, his head on the cushion at one end, his feet hanging off the other because the couch was built for shorter people. He puts the blanket over his legs. He finds the controller for the screen. He's very comfortable. He's arranged everything exactly how he wants it.

Yuka stands beside the couch and looks down at him.

Then she climbs on top of him.

She does it the way she always does: all of her, from his chest to his knees, settling in with a small satisfied sound. She props her chin on his chest and looks up at the screen, which is behind his head.

There's a pause.

“You're badly arranged,” she says. ʸ

“I'm what?” ᴹ

“You're badly arranged. I can't see.” ʸ

“You're facing the wrong way.” ᴹ

“I'm facing the right way. *You're* facing the wrong way. The screen's behind you.” ʸ

“I'm lying down.” ᴹ

“Then lie down *better*.” ʸ

He looks at her. She looks back at him, chin on his chest, completely serious.

He thinks about getting up. Rearranging himself. Turning round on the couch so his head is at the other end, with her on top of him the whole time, both of them shifting and elbowing and her complaining about it.

He thinks about Authority.

* * *

“Fine,” he says. ᴹ

He doesn't move. He just lets Authority take hold of the couch, very gently, and turns it.

The whole thing. Couch, him, her, blanket and all, swinging slowly round on the floor like the hand of a clock, until his head is at the end facing the screen and his feet are toward the window. It makes a faint scraping sound on the boards. Yuka grips his shirt and squeaks.

The couch stops.

She looks at the screen, which is now in front of her, past his feet.

“Better,” she says, in the tone of someone giving a reluctant grade. ʸ

* * *

“The cushion,” she says. ʸ

“What about it?” ᴹ

“It's under your head. It should be under *my* head.” ʸ

“Your head's on my chest.” ᴹ

“Your chest is bony.” ʸ

He lifts the cushion out from under his own head with Authority and slides it in under her chin, between her face and his chest. His own head drops onto the bare arm of the couch with a small thump.

Yuka settles her chin on the cushion and sighs with deep contentment.

“Better,” she says. ʸ

“My head's on wood now.” ᴹ

“You have a very hard head. You'll be fine.” ʸ

He takes the second cushion, the one from the armchair across the room, and floats it over and tucks it under his own head. She watches it cross the room in the air with great interest.

“That's cheating,” she says. ʸ

“You told me to rearrange.” ᴹ

“I told you to rearrange *me*.” ʸ

* * *

“The blanket,” she says. ʸ

The blanket is over his legs. It's not over her at all; she's on top of it.

He lifts it out from under both of them, which takes some doing, and makes the blanket rise up into the air and hang there, and then settles it down again over both of them, carefully, tucked in around her shoulders, so that only her head is sticking out at the top, on the cushion, on his chest.

“Oh,” she says. ʸ

She wriggles. The blanket stays.

“That's good,” she says, quietly, with real surprise. “That's actually very good.” ʸ

“I'm very good,” he says. ᴹ

“You're very *badly arranged*, but you're good at the blanket.” ʸ

* * *

“The lamp,” she says. ʸ

It's on the table by the window. It's bright, and it's throwing a pale glare across the screen.

He reaches out with Authority and turns the little key at the side, and the flame goes down, and down, until it's just a soft glow.

“Lower,” she says. ʸ

Lower.

“A little higher.” ʸ

A little higher.

“*There.*” ʸ

He leaves it there.

* * *

“The curtain,” she says. ʸ

He draws it the rest of the way.

“The popcorn.” ʸ

“We don't have popcorn.” ᴹ

She looks up at him from the cushion, over the edge of the blanket, with an expression of profound disappointment.

He opens the Shop. He finds the popcorn, the one in the paper bag, the one she likes with too much salt. He buys it. He lifts it out of the air with Authority and floats it gently down until it settles on top of the blanket, on her back, between her shoulder blades, where she can't reach it.

“I can't reach it,” she says. ʸ

He moves it a hand's breadth. Then another. It slides down the blanket until it's resting beside her face on the cushion, exactly where she can reach it without moving anything but one hand.

She takes a piece. She eats it.

“Good,” she says. ʸ

* * *

“The table,” she says, a little later. ʸ

“What's wrong with the table?” ᴹ

“It's too far. I want to put my drink on it.” ʸ

“You don't have a drink.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

He opens the Shop again. The cold tea, the one in the glass bottle, the one she always takes from the kitchen when she thinks Rem isn't looking. He buys it. He floats it over. He picks up the low table with Authority and walks it across the floor, its legs barely touching the boards, until it's right beside the couch, exactly at the height of her hand, and sets the bottle down on it.

She looks at the table. At the bottle. At him.

“Anything else?” he asks. ᴹ

She considers this very seriously.

“You,” she says. ʸ

“What about me?” ᴹ

“You're still badly arranged.” ʸ

He laughs. He can't help it. She's lying on top of him under a blanket he put on her, with a cushion he moved under her chin and a bag of popcorn he bought her by her face and a table he walked across the room beside her hand, and the lamp exactly where she wanted it, and the curtain drawn, and the couch turned round, and she's telling him *he's* badly arranged.

“How?” he asks. ᴹ

“Your arm,” she says. “It's just lying there. It should be round me.” ʸ

He puts his arm round her.

He doesn't use Authority for that one.

“*Better*,” she says, and settles in, and finally, finally, presses the button to start the movie. ʸ

* * *

He doesn't know what the movie is about.

He tries to follow it, at first. There are people, and a city, and something to do with a ship. Somebody is angry at somebody else. There's a song. He watches it the way he watches everything on the screen, a bright moving grey with shapes in it, and listens to the voices.

But mostly he watches her.

She's watching it properly. Her eyes on the screen, following everything. She laughs at the funny parts, a little before he gets them. She goes very still at the sad parts. When something exciting happens she grips his shirt under the blanket. She eats the popcorn one piece at a time without looking. Every so often she reaches out for the cold tea, and he moves the bottle a finger's width closer to her hand before she gets there, and she never notices.

He doesn't need to know what the movie's about. He has this.

* * *

Somewhere in the middle, she falls asleep.

He feels it happen. Her grip on his shirt loosening. Her breathing going slow and deep against his chest, against the cushion. Her head getting heavier.

He doesn't move.

The movie keeps going. He watches it. He still doesn't know what it's about. Somebody is on the ship now. There's a storm, or something like a storm, and music, and then a quiet part, and then people talking for a long time.

Through the wall, the tournament has ended. He hears Umaru declaring a champion. He hears the house laughing and going up to bed, footsteps on the stairs, the third one creaking, Ori being told to go to sleep and saying she *was* asleep.

Nobody comes into the common room. He thinks maybe they looked in, and saw, and went away again.

He turns the lamp down a little more, with Authority, very carefully, so the key doesn't click.

* * *

The movie ends. The screen goes dark.

He doesn't turn the lights up. He lies there on the couch, which is facing the wrong way round from how it's supposed to face, with the table beside it that isn't where it's supposed to be and the armchair missing its cushion, and Yuka asleep on top of him under the blanket, her face on the cushion he gave her and one hand still curled in his shirt.

He thinks about getting up. Carrying her upstairs. That's what he should do.

He thinks about how she said he was badly arranged.

He stays.

* * *

She wakes up a little before dawn.

He's dozed off himself by then. He wakes up because she's lifting her head, slowly, her hair in his face, looking around at the dark common room with the confusion of somebody who doesn't remember falling asleep.

“Did I miss the end?” she mumbles. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“What happened?” ʸ

He thinks about it.

“There was a ship,” he says. ᴹ

She waits.

“That's all I've got,” he admits. ᴹ

She stares at him. Then she drops her face back down onto the cushion and starts to laugh, quietly, helplessly, so that he can feel it all through his chest.

“You didn't watch it,” she says. ʸ

“I watched part of it.” ᴹ

“What were you watching?” ʸ

He doesn't answer. He doesn't have to.

She stops laughing. She lifts her head again and looks at him in the grey light, for a long moment.

Then she puts her head back down, and pulls the blanket up over both their shoulders, and says, very sleepily, into the cushion, “We're watching it again tomorrow. And this time you're arranged properly from the *start*.” ʸ

“Yes, Yuka.” ᴹ

She's asleep again before he's finished saying it.
