“It'll take ten minutes.” ᴹ

“Then I'll be there for ten minutes.” ʸ

She picks her spoon back up. Conversation over. Across the table, Maki raises her eyebrows at Yuta, and Yuta very carefully doesn't raise his back.

“Can we see it?” Yuka asks. ʸ

They only go as far as the first bend. There's a little alcove there, dry and warm enough that Yuka takes her hands out of her sleeves as she steps into it. She presses her palm to the stone.

“I made that,” she says quietly. “I said it, and it's *there*.” ʸ

“You picked a good place to stop,” he says. ᴹ

She stays long enough to warm both hands. On the way out, Mau holds back a step to let her go through first. The opening stays there for him, and a moment later he's outside with her. He circles the rest spot on the sketch so Fern can check it later.

“I thought I'd go see if the quarry's still open,” he says. “I wanted to see the Pillow.” ᴹ

Yuka takes his hand. “I wanted to see where you were.” ʸ

* * *

She doesn't tell him the rest of it until they're at the quarry.

They walk there. Neither of them suggests Kaisel. It's a long walk, along the river and up through the fields, and the morning is cool and bright, and she holds his hand the whole way and talks about nothing: Mai's new dress pattern, whether Ori is old enough for a knife of her own, the ship in the movie, which she has decided was a metaphor for something, though she won't say what.

Then they come over the lip of the quarry and look down at the bowl of cut stone with the rainwater at the bottom and the scrub in the cracks, and the place against the far wall where the gate stands, a tall faint oval of light, humming.

She stops talking.

She looks at it for a long time.

“That's it,” she says. ʸ

“That's it.” ᴹ

“It looks so small.” ʸ

“It's small from outside.” ᴹ

She keeps looking at it. He watches her face. He thinks she's thinking about the porch, and the fourteen minutes, and the way he walked up the field.

“From now on,” she says, “I'm coming with you.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't answer right away.

“To gates,” she says. “Dungeons. All of it. Wherever you go when you go somewhere I can't see. I'm coming.” ʸ

“Yuka—” ᴹ

“I don't mean I'll fight.” She turns to look at him. “I mean I might. If I can help, I'll help. But even if I can't. Even if I just sit there. Even if I do *nothing*.” ʸ

He waits.

“You were in there three weeks,” she says. “On your own. And the long one, before that. A whole year, on your own. With nobody. And that's—” ʸ

Her voice catches. She stops, and swallows, and starts again, steadier.

“If you get stuck again,” she says, “I want you not to be alone. That's all. I don't care if I'm useful. I want to be *there*. So that if the door closes, it closes on both of us.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't know what to say.

He thinks about the room with a bed. The drop of water. The four hundred and thirty-seven. The frozen plain and the long nights and the Pillow breathing beside a fire. All the places he's been stuck, alone, where nobody could reach him.

He thinks about saying no. About telling her that the whole point of going alone is that nobody else gets stuck. About telling her that if the door closes on both of them, then there are two of them behind it instead of one.

He looks at her face, and doesn't say any of it.

“I'm not alone,” he says instead, gently. “When I'm in there. I've got Noise.” ᴹ

“I'm here,” Noise says at once. ᴺ

It says it aloud. Not loud. But aloud, so she can hear. And there's something in the voice he's never heard before. Something almost like pride.

“I'm always here,” Noise says. “I was there the whole three weeks. And the year. And before.” ᴺ

Yuka looks at the space beside Mau's head where she imagines Noise must be.

“I know,” she says. “Thank you.” ʸ

“You're welcome,” Noise says, and Mau could swear it sounds pleased with itself. ᴺ

* * *

“I'm sometimes there too,” says Ciel. ᶜ

Quieter. A little dry. A little, he thinks, put out.

Mau almost laughs.

“Yes,” he says. “Sometimes.” ᴹ

“*Sometimes*?” ᶜ

“You weren't in the long one.” ᴹ

“That wasn't my *choice*.” ᶜ

“I know. Sometimes.” ᴹ

There's a pause.

“Sometimes is fair,” Ciel admits. ᶜ

Yuka is laughing now, quietly, with her hand over her mouth. She looks at Mau and then at the empty air on both sides of him, as though she can see them all standing there, the three of them, arguing about who gets to keep him company.

“Then that's four,” she says. ʸ

“Four what?” ᴹ

“Four of us. Noise always. Ciel sometimes. Me from now on.” She thinks about it. “And Makima, I suppose, because she can't leave. But she doesn't count.” ʸ

“She counts a little,” Noise says. ᴺ

“She counts a *little*,” Yuka concedes. ʸ

“Four and a little,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Four and a little.” Yuka takes his hand again. “Will you show me where you met it?” ʸ

* * *

He stands in front of the gate.

He gets close enough for the eyes to read the way through. The return opened when the warden fell. It's still open now. He lets Ciel check it too before he says anything.

“We can turn straight round,” he says. “I cleared the trial. The way back is still there.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at the gate. Then at him. Then back at the gate.

“So we could go in,” she says. ʸ

“We could go in.” ᴹ

“Just to look.” ʸ

“Just to look.” ᴹ

She's already walking toward it.

* * *

The cold takes his breath as soon as he steps in. He'd remembered it as a thing he was carrying by the end. He'd forgotten how quickly it could get at his ears. The plain is still flat and grey and wide, the ceiling still flat and sunless. In the distance, the broken towers. Yuka stands at the edge with her breath clouding in front of her and looks at all of it with enormous eyes.

“It's red,” she says. ʸ

“Is it?” ᴹ

“It's *very* red. The whole sky.” She turns to him. “You were in this for three weeks and you didn't even know it was red?” ʸ

“Noise told me.” ᴹ

“That's not the same.” ʸ

“It's not,” he agrees. ᴹ

She looks at the sky a moment longer. Then she takes his hand.

“It's ugly,” she says decisively. “It's an ugly red. Like a bad bruise. You didn't miss anything.” ʸ

He laughs, and his breath clouds, and hers does, and they walk out onto the plain together.

* * *

Three long things come out of the first tower. He'd left bodies in every room of that tower the first time around. These three come toward them with that same loose jointed rush, and he steps forward, and Yuka says, “*Wait*.” ʸ

He waits.

“Let me,” she says. ʸ

He lets her.

She takes the first one with her own technique, clean and quick, the way she cleared the halls in the second gate. The second one tries to wrap her and she's not there. The third one gets close, closer than he likes, its long arm reaching, and he has Authority on it before he's thought about it, a small grip on its wrist, a hand's breadth off its line—

And she's already put it down.

She turns round and looks at him. At his hand, half raised.

“I had it,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You *grabbed* it.” ʸ

“A little.” ᴹ

“I said I was coming to do *nothing*.” ʸ

“You did a lot.” ᴹ

“*You* were supposed to do nothing. *I* was supposed to sit there.” ʸ

He lowers his hand. “I'll do nothing,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at him suspiciously. He puts his hands in his pockets.

He does nothing for the next tower too. It's very hard.

* * *

Yuka finds shelter in a shallow hollow near the base of the broken tower. It's lined with smooth stone, and once she steps down into it the wind goes straight over her head. She holds out a hand to Mau from the bottom.

“Can you make the fire it liked?” she asks. ʸ

He can. He uses the spell Frieren taught him and leaves it between two stones, where the warmth spreads slowly enough to get into the rock. Yuka settles beside it and holds her palms out. After a while she takes her hands away and rests one on the stone.

“I see why it wanted to follow you,” she says. ʸ

And then something huge and shaggy comes round the side of the tower.

* * *

Yuka goes very still.

It's enormous. Taller than Mau at the shoulder. Three times as long. Covered from end to end in thick shaggy fur. A broad low head with two small dark eyes and a long swaying nose.

It walks toward them. Slowly. It doesn't hurry. It looks at Mau with one small eye for a long moment.

Then it walks past them both, and steps down into the warm hollow, and lies down in it with a long, deep, contented groan, like a door that needs oiling.

“Is that—” Yuka whispers. ʸ

“That's the Pillow.” ᴹ

“That's the *Pillow*?” ʸ

“That's the Pillow.” ᴹ

She stares at it. The Pillow closes its eyes.

“You said it was big,” she says. ʸ

“I said it was very big.” ᴹ

“You didn't say it was *that* big.” ʸ

He goes down into the hollow. The Pillow opens one eye and watches him come. He puts his hand on its broad forehead, the way he did at the edge of the ring, and it groans again, deeper.

“Hello,” he says. “You found it.” ᴹ

* * *

Yuka comes down after a while. Slowly. Carefully. She stops beside him and looks at the Pillow, and the Pillow looks back at her.

“Can I?” she asks. ʸ

“Ask it.” ᴹ

She holds out her hand, palm down, the way you'd hold it out to a dog you don't know. The Pillow looks at her hand. Then it lifts its long nose and sniffs her fingers, very carefully, and then lays the end of its nose across her wrist, heavy and warm, and leaves it there.

Yuka makes a small sound.

She puts her hands into its fur. Both of them. Up to the wrists. He watches her face as she does it.

“It's so *warm*,” she says. ʸ

“I told you.” ᴹ

“You told me. You didn't say it was like *this*.” ʸ

She sits down against its side. Just like that, in the hollow, with her back against the Pillow and her hands still in its fur. The Pillow doesn't seem to mind. It groans contentedly and settles.

She looks up at him and holds out her hand.

He sits down beside her.

* * *

They stay a long time.

He doesn't know how long. A few hours, inside. A few seconds, at home. He doesn't care. They sit in the warm hollow at the foot of the broken tower with their backs against the Pillow, which breathes long and slow beneath them like the sea, and Yuka leans on his shoulder, and the ugly red sky that he can't see hangs over everything.

“This is where you slept,” she says. ʸ

“Some of it.” ᴹ

“On the cold ground. Next to it.” ʸ

“Next to it. It was the only warm thing.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a while.

“Not any more,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't ask what she means. He knows. She means the hollow. She means herself. She means both.

“Not any more,” he agrees. ᴹ

* * *

Later — much later, or a few seconds later — they walk out of the gate into the quarry.

The morning is exactly as they left it. Bright and cool. The rainwater still at the bottom of the bowl. Their footprints still on the path down.

Yuka stops at the lip of the quarry and looks back.

“We're coming back,” she says. ʸ

“We're coming back.” ᴹ

“With Ori. She wants to see it.” ʸ

“With Ori,” he agrees. ᴹ

“And we'll take Mai through ours when she's ready. For the cores.” ʸ

“We can show her where to sit.” ᴹ

“And next time—” ʸ

She turns to him, and takes both his hands, and looks up at him very seriously.

“Next time *you* get to do nothing,” she says. “The whole time. And I'll do everything. And you'll sit in the warm place with the Pillow and *watch*.” ʸ

He thinks about it.

“That sounds terrible,” he says. ᴹ

“That's the point,” Yuka says, and leads him home. ʸ
