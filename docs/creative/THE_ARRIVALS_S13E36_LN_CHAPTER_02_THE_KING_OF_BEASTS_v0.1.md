# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 30 — The King of Beasts

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 30
**Source episode:** S13E36
**Continuity state:** B+331, afternoon, the citadel of Thalion. Rakan's point of view. Yogumunt's plan: strike the elf queen the shadow-host loves; the host will come; while he is gone, Yogumunt and Querehsha take the Shadow Monarch's family. Rakan resents being bait and means to kill the host himself. He comes over the citadel wall, kills guards, meets Elmesia's magic and is impressed. Yuka gets civilians and servants down from the terrace with her Mahoraga. Elmesia, seeing what he is, asks for help through the anchor. Rakan watches the shadow under the table break open: a wheel first, tearing upward through it, then a figure. Hair that looks red streaked with white. Eyes: blue at the centre, ringed with orange, black where the white should be. Before Rakan finishes raising his hand, there is a cut on his shoulder.

*When: Early afternoon. About the time Mau and Jin-Woo are getting the trainers out in the yard, far to the north.*

Yogumunt explained the plan three times.

Rakan listened to the first explanation, because he was obliged to. He did not listen to the second. By the third, he was looking out of the cave mouth at the bright foreign sky and imagining the taste of the city at the end of the valley.

*It is simple*, Yogumunt said, in that thin dry voice of his, the voice of a thing that had worn too many faces to remember its own. *The shadow-host loves certain people. He has put pieces of himself in their shadows, like a man leaving lamps in windows. When one of them calls, he comes. Always. Without thinking. It is his weakness.*

*So you strike the elf. The queen in the white city. He loves her a little; he has given her a lamp. When she calls, he will come. And while he is there, three days' march from his house, Querehsha and I will go to the house of the one who killed us. And take what he loves.*

*And the shadow-host?* Rakan asked. It was the only question he'd bothered with.

*Keep him busy. Keep him there. As long as you can.*

*And if I kill him?*

Yogumunt had looked at him for a long time with eyes that changed colour as Rakan watched.

*If you can kill him*, Yogumunt said, *by all means. But you will not.*

Rakan had laughed.

* * *

The city is beautiful.

That's the first thing he notices, coming over the outer wall in the heat of the afternoon. He hates that it is beautiful. Pale stone and bright roofs and gardens hung from every terrace, and the smell of it, so many soft two-legged things packed together, food and flowers and sweat. It makes his teeth ache.

He goes up through the rings of the city like a fire going up a dry hill.

He doesn't bother with the streets. He goes over the roofs, from one to the next, faster than the eye, and where the city's guards try to stop him he goes through them. They're slow. They're so slow. They have spears and swords and some of them have magic, little flickers of light at their fingertips, and none of it matters. He's the King of Beasts. He was old when their world was young.

By the time he reaches the citadel wall, there are bells ringing all over the city.

He goes over it.

* * *

She's on the terrace.

He knows her at once. The elf. The queen. Tall and pale, in robes that catch the light, standing at the head of a long table under an awning with a cup still in her hand. Around her, guards, many of them, already moving, already shouting. And beside her, another woman, younger, smaller, human, frozen halfway out of her chair.

The queen turns and looks at him.

He expects fear. He likes fear. He's been tasting it all the way up the hill.

She isn't afraid. She looks at him the way a cat looks at a dog that's got into the garden: with contempt, and calculation, and no surprise at all.

“You're one of them,” she says. “The ones the Rulers warned about.” ᴱˡ

“I am the King of Beasts,” Rakan says. ᴿᵏ

“You're in my garden,” the elf says. “Get out.” ᴱˡ

And she raises her hand.

* * *

He's surprised.

He'll admit that later, to Yogumunt, if he lives to see Yogumunt again. The elf is *strong*. Stronger than anything he's met in this world so far. The magic that comes off her hand isn't a little flicker of light; it's a wall, a great hammer of force and fire that takes him full in the chest and throws him back across the terrace into the parapet so hard that the stone cracks.

He gets up. He's smoking. He's laughing.

“Good,” he says. ᴿᵏ

He comes at her.

She's good. She's very good. She fights the way old things fight, with no wasted motion: shield and strike and shield again, magic spun out of the air faster than he can see it come, her guards falling back around her in a ring and adding what they can. She holds him. For the length of three breaths she *holds* him, the King of Beasts, on her terrace among the overturned chairs and the spilled cups.

Then he gets through.

One claw, past her shield, across her arm. Not deep. Enough. He sees the blood. He sees her face change, just slightly, the contempt giving way to something more careful.

* * *

Behind her, the young human woman is moving.

He sees it out of the corner of his eye. He doesn't pay attention at first; she's nothing, a soft thing in a chair. But then something rises up behind her out of the air. Something huge and pale, with a blade along one arm and a wheel above its head, like the idols of some other world's old gods. It stands over her. It doesn't attack him. It turns its back on him entirely and spreads its great arms over the servants and the guards who've fallen, the ones cowering under the long table and against the terrace wall, and herds them toward the stairs.

The woman is shouting. Directing it. Getting them out.

He could kill her. He thinks about it. It would take a moment.

But the elf is in front of him, and the elf is the job, and the woman will keep.

* * *

The elf sees what he is. He watches her see it.

She's fought him for ten breaths now. She's bleeding from the arm and the shoulder and there's a cut on her cheek. Her magic is still strong, but it's not getting any stronger, and his isn't getting any weaker, and she knows it. He watches her do the arithmetic in her calm old face.

And then she does something he doesn't expect.

She doesn't call for her guards. She doesn't call for her soldiers, or her generals, or whatever this world's queens call for. She glances down. Just for a moment. At her own shadow, stretched out across the flagstones under the long table.

And she says, very quietly, so quietly he almost doesn't hear it: “Mau.” ᴱˡ

*Ah*, Rakan thinks. *There's the lamp.*

He bares his teeth, and waits for the shadow-host to come.

* * *

He's been told about the shadow-host. He's been told a great deal.

Yogumunt watched for days. Yogumunt sent things into the valley to look, small things, birds and insects, things nobody would notice. Yogumunt told him what they saw. A tall thin man, scarred, who limps a little on bad days. Brown hair going white. Plain clothes. Quiet. Who sits on porches with a cup of something hot and lets a girl climb onto his lap. Who trains in the yard with the one who killed them and loses as often as he wins.

*Ashborn chose him*, Yogumunt said. *A second time. In a second world. I want to know why.*

Rakan doesn't care why. Rakan has been told: a tall thin man who limps a little. Rakan is ready to kill a tall thin man who limps a little.

The shadow under the long table ripples.

* * *

Something comes through it.

Not a man.

The first thing out of the shadow is a wheel.

It tears up through the dark like a blade through cloth: eight-spoked, pale, enormous, spinning. The shadow doesn't open to let it through. The shadow *breaks*. It rips upward from beneath, the darkness splitting around the turning spokes with a sound like tearing silk, and the long table above it is flung aside as though it weighs nothing, and the cups and plates scatter across the terrace in a ringing rain.

The wheel rises. It hangs in the air above the broken shadow, turning, turning.

And under it, rising after it, out of the wound in the dark, something stands up.

* * *

Rakan has seen many things in his long life. He has seen Ashborn himself. He has seen the end of worlds.

He is not prepared for this.

The figure is tall, and thin, the way Yogumunt said. But Yogumunt said *brown hair going white*, and this hair is not brown. It's red. A deep, dark, burning red, like the inside of a wound, falling loose to the shoulders. And through it, in long streaks, white. Bone white. Ash white. Red and white together, like blood on snow.

And the eyes.

The centre of each eye is blue. A blue so bright it hurts to look at, a blue like the hottest part of a flame, like the sky seen from very high up, and it's *moving*, it's pouring, there's something behind it looking out that sees everything at once. Around the blue, ringing it, orange: concentric circles of it, gold and orange and amber, ring inside ring inside ring, like the eyes of something that commands. And where the white of the eye should be, where any ordinary eye is white, there is only black. Black as the shadow he came out of. Black all the way to the edges.

Blue in orange in black. It looks at Rakan.

And Rakan, the King of Beasts, who was old when this world was young, feels something he has not felt since the day the one who killed him walked into his den.

He feels *prey*.

* * *

The figure doesn't speak. It doesn't move. It just stands there under its turning wheel in the wreck of the shadow, looking at him with those eyes.

Then it looks past him. Once. At the elf, bleeding against the parapet. At the stairs, where the young human woman has stopped halfway down with her pale god behind her and turned around and is staring back up at the terrace.

Rakan sees something go through the figure's face when it looks at the woman on the stairs. He doesn't know what it is. It's there and gone in an instant.

Then the eyes come back to him.

He's already moving. He's the King of Beasts; he doesn't wait. His hand is coming up, claws out, the whole weight of his body behind it, faster than anything in this world, faster than the elf could follow, faster than—

There's a cut on his shoulder.

He looks at it.

It's deep. Very deep. A clean straight line, from the point of his shoulder down across the top of his chest, so fine and so sharp he didn't feel it go in. The blood hasn't even started to come yet. It's just a line, perfectly straight, opening slowly as he watches.

His hand is still only halfway up.

He didn't see anything move. The figure is standing exactly where it was. It hasn't lifted a hand. It hasn't taken a step. It's just standing there under the wheel, with its red and white hair and its blue and orange and black eyes, looking at him.

The blood starts to come.

And Rakan understands, all at once, with total clarity, what Yogumunt meant.

*You will not.*
