# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 30 — Do Not Eat Yet

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 30
**Source episode:** S13E36
**Continuity state:** B+324, breakfast through early afternoon. Maomao runs a blind chocolate tasting with Jinshi, Mau, Yuka and Ori. The experimental chocolate is labelled and offered only after Maomao explains its brief effect. Mau and Yuka choose to try it. Yuka asks for ordinary things much more openly and Mau is delighted to oblige. Both notice the effect ending before leaving the kitchen. A separate tin contains regular chocolate for later.

*When: The morning after the walk in the orchard. Jinshi makes a claim at the breakfast table and Maomao puts her chopsticks down.*

“I'd know yours,” Jinshi says. ᴶⁱ

Maomao has just told him what Rem went to the trouble of saving from the last chocolate day: the remaining bars, one folded page of notes and a little bowl of the crushed things Umaru insisted went in with the salt. She would have liked more of the notes. There is a question mark halfway through them and she has thought about it four mornings in a row.

“Know mine how?” ᴹᵐ

“If everybody made some. I'd know which one was yours.” ᴶⁱ

She watches him pick up his tea. He is looking exactly as pleased with himself as he did at the table yesterday when he insisted a merchant would take his first offer.

“You can start after breakfast,” she says. ᴹᵐ

He lowers the cup before he drinks. “Start what?” ᴶⁱ

“Finding out.” ᴹᵐ

* * *

By the time Mau and Yuka come through the kitchen, Rem has supplied two trays from the last batch and Maomao has made a third. She has also stopped Jinshi from helping twice. The first time he was holding the knife she needed; the second time he was doing a very good job of drying cups where he could see what was going into all three trays.

“Are we allowed in?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“You're allowed to tell him which one you think is mine before he eats any,” Maomao says. ᴹᵐ

Yuka puts her head around Mau's shoulder. She looks at the three trays. Two of them are cut in squares. The third has small pieces about the width of a finger, each with one end tapered where the knife went in at an angle.

“That one,” she says. ʸ

Jinshi looks wounded. “She hasn't even tasted it.” ᴶⁱ

Maomao looks at him. “Which one did *you* think it was?” ᴹᵐ

He looks back at her for several seconds. He might have got through it if he hadn't glanced at the third tray halfway through.

“Hand me the knife,” Yuka says, laughing. “I'll cut more. Let me put them all on the same plate.” ʸ

Mau goes to take the tray down from the shelf for her. On the way his eyes go past a fourth plate by the window, then go straight back to it.

The card says *DO NOT EAT YET*. He sees the first three words from far enough away that he gets a hand around Yuka's wrist the instant she reaches for a corner.

She looks down at their hands. He looks down too.

“I wanted to turn the card round,” she says. ʸ

“It's a good card.” He takes his hand off hers. “Very clear.” ᴹ

“There is a fourth word,” she says. ʸ

Maomao lifts the plate and puts it farther back on the sill. One piece still leans slightly into the next.

“It's still setting,” she tells him. “Why, have you been here before?” ᴹᵐ

For half a breath he can see another plate, on a table that doesn't exist anymore. He remembers going outside afterwards and not understanding why everybody stayed so close to the door. He can understand it now, and the distance between the two things still surprises him.

“Once.” He looks at the card again. “The other one didn't say *yet*.” ᴹ

Yuka catches his sleeve. “You may let go of the tray *now*, if you like.” ʸ

He hands it over. He keeps his empty hand near hers long enough that she takes it herself before she puts him back to work.

* * *

Jinshi gets Maomao's chocolate right on the second try, too.

Maomao checks his plate for some clue Yuka might have missed. The cuts are all the same. Nothing is written on the underside. Ori has wandered in with a pencil and is recording everybody's answers on a scrap of wrapping paper. She suspects Ori would rather stop the whole competition than help one person win it.

“Why that one?” Maomao asks. ᴹᵐ

“You had yours with tea this morning. It's got the same thing underneath it. The slightly burnt part.” ᴶⁱ

“I didn't burn anything.” ᴹᵐ

“That was his best word for it,” Mau says. He has a second piece in his hand and he's looking from it to Maomao. “The good edge. Like the bottom of bread when you leave it on the pan a little longer.” ᴹ

Maomao has a third piece herself. He's right. It's the bit she kept going back to this morning while everybody else was still eating breakfast. She hadn't known Jinshi had noticed she'd taken any, let alone what she'd taken it with.

Ori turns the paper so they can see it. There is a line for Jinshi, then two marks beside his name, and farther down there are five marks beside what looks like a drawing of a rabbit.

“What's the rabbit?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“That's you,” Ori says. “Those are for how many times you helped and didn't enter the competition. You can have one more for cutting.” ᴼ

Yuka turns to Mau. “I could beat him without eating any chocolate at all.” ʸ

“You could,” Mau says. He sets the piece he was holding on the edge of her plate. “You don't have to.” ᴹ

She eats it. She leaves a little bit in her fingers long enough to give it back to him.

At the far end of the table, Jinshi sets the last of his where Maomao can reach it and says he'd like to see the wrapping paper before Ori declares anyone the winner.

* * *

Maomao takes down the plate from the window after Ori has left with a tin from Rem's batch for the Fort. She turns the card over and writes on the blank side before she shows it to anyone.

“The tin Ori took is all ordinary chocolate. These I made for a separate tasting,” she says. “They make it easier to say whatever you were about to swallow back. A little while, then it wears off. I'd like to hear what you notice if you choose to try one.” ᴹᵐ

Yuka looks at the card, then at the piece she was about to take by accident. “Is this the one without the *yet*?” ʸ

“This one's the one with an explanation,” Mau says. ᴹ

Jinshi stays in his chair, nursing the tea he's refilled twice. “I'll be useful right here, listening to what you all decide to do with your afternoon.” ᴶⁱ

Maomao puts down her pencil. “You're welcome to keep your afternoon for yourself,” she says, and means it. ᴹᵐ

Yuka looks at Mau. He leaves the plate where it is until she makes up her mind, then takes a piece from beside the spot where she took hers. They both eat. The pastry tin on the side still has squares of the plain chocolate for later, and Maomao moves that one to the other end of the table so she can put the tasting plate away.

For a few minutes they talk about the film Umaru picked for the evening. Then Yuka looks down at the little space between her chair and Mau's.

“Come closer,” she says. ʸ

His chair scrapes against the boards before anybody has time to look.

“Closer than that. There. Now put your arm round me. I want to stay like this while I finish my tea.” ʸ

He's got room to get his arm between her and the back of the chair. He tucks it there. When she leans, he leans too, until she has his side and shoulder to rest against. He nearly has to put his own cup down before he spills it.

“Do you want to go round the orchard later?” he asks, looking down at her. ᴹ

“Yes. I also want your other hand now. Can you put the cup down without letting go of me?” ʸ

He can. He puts it down at an awkward angle and gets his hand back to her before the tea has stopped moving. She examines the way he's done it. Then she nudges his cup into a safer spot with her free hand and goes straight back to holding him with it.

Maomao looks down at her notes. She has written *a second request, then a third*. By the time she gets to the third word, Yuka has asked Mau to lend her the sleeve he isn't using because the wood of the chair is cold where her elbow touches it. He's rolling the cuff down to get it underneath her.

“I can get you a cushion,” Jinshi says. ᴶⁱ

“Then I'd only have a cushion,” Yuka tells him. “I've got Mau.” ʸ

Mau looks down at her. She can see him consider whether he ought to laugh. He gets distracted before he decides.

“Is it all right if I stay even after you finish the tea?” he asks. ᴹ

She puts her still-full cup down. The smile starts on his face before she gives him the answer.

“I wanted you to stay anyway.” ʸ

* * *

The situation improves, or gets worse, depending on where in the kitchen everybody is trying to work. Shuna asks them to move two chairs out of the way. Mau offers to move the ones they're sitting on. Yuka says she would prefer to travel as she is. Shuna sees that he was actually about to do it and gives them the bench by the window instead.

Yuka gets up first. She gets as far as the bench, turns round, and holds up both arms as if he might otherwise have trouble knowing where he's wanted. Mau sits right down beside her. She brings one leg across his and reaches up to fold over the edge of his collar.

“I've wanted to fix that all morning,” she says. ʸ

“I wish you had.” ᴹ

She looks right at him. “Why?” ʸ

“You do this with your hands. Then you look at me from right there.” ᴹ

She was still holding the collar. Neither of them had needed it fixed quite that much.

Across the room, Jinshi turns back to Maomao, who has gone to check whether there's anything left of the ordinary chocolate. He asks quietly whether that's what she was expecting.

“From what I've seen of them at breakfast?” she asks. “About that, yes.” ᴹᵐ

He laughs. She gives him a square off the last ordinary plate before he asks for any. He stops laughing long enough to tell her exactly which tray that was from.

* * *

By the time they bring their cups back, the light on the kitchen floor has moved past the leg of the table. Maomao asks them what they notice now.

Yuka thinks about it. Earlier everything she wanted just seemed to come out at the same speed she thought it. Now she can spend long enough choosing a word to watch Mau wonder what she's going to say.

“I can hold a thought in my mouth again,” she says. ʸ

Mau puts his cup alongside hers. “I can finish a cup with her sitting next to me.” ᴹ

Yuka looks in the cup. It is completely empty. She pokes him once through the sleeve for making such an impressive demonstration. He takes her hand as it's coming away and leaves his thumb between two of her fingers.

“Do you want to do the orchard now?” he asks. ᴹ

“Maybe another day.” She watches his thumb for a second. “I still want to sit with you.” ʸ

“Good,” he says. ᴹ

Maomao folds the card over the empty tasting tray. She has a whole page of notes and she thinks Jinshi is going to ask to see them, but he carries the last few cups to the sink and stays to help her put things away. Yuka asks if she can take two squares from the ordinary tin. Maomao says she can take the tin too if she brings it back tomorrow.

Mau holds the kitchen door for her. She takes his sleeve this time instead of his hand. That way she can walk next to him all the way upstairs without having to put the tin down.
