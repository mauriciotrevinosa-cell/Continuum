# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 32 — The Door Under the Porch

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 32
**Source episode:** S13E37
**Continuity state:** B+331, afternoon. On the ninth breath Mau takes hold of Rakan with Authority and both hands and Exchanges with the soldier under the Sungs' porch, bringing the Monarch with him (precedent: V74, returning accompanied through an existing anchor). He arrives in the Sungs' yard: Querehsha's swarm over the house, Yogumunt at the gate, Jin-Woo's shadows holding the door with Hae-In, the family inside. Jin-Woo is not there. Mau calls Bellion and the reserve to carry the family up to the main house, where Gojo and Rimuru are. Then he turns around and stands alone in front of the door with three Monarchs in the yard.

*When: The ninth breath. Three days' march north of the citadel, it is the same afternoon, a little after Mau left the training posts.*

He moves on nine.

Not toward the shadow. Toward the wolf.

It doesn't expect that. Nothing that's fought him today has expected anything he's done. It's braced for him to run, braced for him to vanish back the way he came. It's grinning. *Too late.*

He closes the distance in one step and takes hold of it.

Authority first: a grip on the whole of it, the way he'd hold a cup or a broom or a cart, except that this is a king of beasts and it's like trying to hold a falling tree. It fights the grip so hard he feels something tear behind his eyes. He doesn't let go. Then his hands, both of them, in the thick fur at its throat and shoulder, the way you'd grab a dog that's about to bite a child.

The wolf snarls. Its claws come up.

He finds the anchor under the Sungs' porch. Far away. North. In the dark under the porch roof, where the light never quite reaches.

He goes. And he takes the wolf with him.

* * *

It's like being thrown down a well.

He's done this before, bringing someone with him. Once. Makima, in the warehouse yard, out of a fight she couldn't win alone. But Makima wanted to come. The wolf doesn't want to come. It fights the whole way, and the whole way is *nowhere*, a long dark falling that has no length, and its claws are in his arm and his side and his chest, and he holds on.

Then ground.

Grass. Afternoon light. A porch roof above him. Smoke.

He lets go of the wolf and it rolls away from him across the yard, snarling, and comes up on all fours, and stops.

Because it's not alone any more. Neither is he.

* * *

The Sungs' house is under a cloud.

That's what the eyes see first. Not a real cloud: a swarm. A vast, moving, humming darkness hanging over the roof and the yard and the trees, millions of small bodies, wings and legs and stings. It's pouring down over the house in waves, beating against the walls, the windows, the door.

And something is holding it back.

Jin-Woo's shadows. He knows them. The knight who played with Suho, standing at the foot of the steps with his sword out, cutting the swarm apart as fast as it comes. The big ant, Jin-Woo's ant, the one that isn't Beru, crouched on the porch roof with its wings spread over the windows, taking the stings on its armoured back. A dozen others he doesn't know, in a ring around the house.

Holding. Barely.

And in the doorway, a sword in her hand, Hae-In.

She's fighting. She's fighting *well*, cutting down everything that gets past the knight, her face set and white and furious. Behind her, through the open door, he sees Jin-Ah with her arms round Suho and their mother with her arms round both of them.

Suho sees him.

“*Mau!*” ˢᵘ

* * *

There are two more of them in the yard.

The eyes find them. One at the gate: tall, very thin, in long robes that seem to shift colour when he looks at them. A face that won't hold still. It changes as he watches. A man. A woman. Something with too many eyes. A man again. It's standing perfectly calm with its hands folded in its sleeves, watching the house, the way you'd watch a play.

*Yogumunt.* Jin-Woo's voice in his head. *Wears faces that aren't his.*

The other is above the house, in the middle of the swarm. A woman's shape, if a woman were built of shining chitin, with wings like a wasp's and a crown of something that might be antennae. The swarm moves around her like a dress. When she lifts her hand, it pours. When she closes it, it strikes.

*Querehsha. Queen of insects. Doesn't care what dies.*

And Jin-Woo isn't here.

He looks. He looks with everything. The yard, the house, the path up through the orchard, the trees. Jin-Woo was in his yard. Jin-Woo was *right there*, on the training post, ten minutes ago, a few hundred paces up the path. He should be here. He'd feel his own shadows fighting. He'd be here in seconds.

He isn't.

“Where is he,” Mau says. ᴹ

It isn't a question for anyone. But the thing at the gate answers.

“Looking for his son,” says Yogumunt. ʸᵒ

It smiles, and its face settles, just for a moment, into a face Mau knows.

Hae-In's.

* * *

He understands.

He doesn't need it explained. He can see it all, the whole shape of it, as clearly as he saw the split on the terrace. Ten minutes ago. Jin-Woo on the training post, staring at the place where Mau vanished. And then Hae-In, running up the orchard path, white-faced. *Suho's gone. He went to the quarry. The gate. He said he wanted to see the Pillow.*

And Jin-Woo, who would never in a thousand years leave his family undefended, who left a dozen shadows round his house for exactly this, who had Mau put a soldier by his door — Jin-Woo would *go*. Of course he would. His son. A gate.

The quarry is miles north. The red gate is still there. Mau left it there, deferred, waiting.

*He'll try to split us.*

He split them three ways.

* * *

“Bellion,” Mau says. ᴹ

He doesn't raise his voice. He doesn't have to. Bellion is always listening.

The reserve comes up out of his shadow.

Not all of it. Thirty of them. Ordinary soldiers, faceless, the absence of light given shape, and Bellion at their head, tall and silent in his dark armour. They come up out of the grass around Mau's feet and stand.

“The family,” Mau says. “All of them. To the house. To Gojo and Rimuru. *Now.*” ᴹ

Bellion doesn't answer. Bellion doesn't need to.

The soldiers move. They go past Mau, past the wolf, toward the house, and the swarm comes down on them, and they go through it. Two of them fall, stung to pieces, and dissolve back into his shadow; he feels them come home. The rest reach the porch. Jin-Woo's knight steps aside to let them through without a word. Shadows recognising shadows.

Hae-In sees them coming. She sees Mau behind them.

She doesn't argue. She turns and shoves Jin-Ah and Suho and their mother toward the soldiers, and the soldiers take them, simply pick them up, and run.

Up the orchard path. Toward the main house. Toward Gojo.

Suho's face over a soldier's shoulder, looking back. Looking at Mau.

“Sir Shadow!” Suho shouts. “Mau, Sir Shadow *told* you, he *told* you—” ˢᵘ

“He told me,” Mau says. ᴹ

And they're gone, into the trees.

* * *

Hae-In doesn't go.

She stays in the doorway with her sword. She looks at Mau across the yard. She's bleeding from a dozen small stings, and her face is set like stone.

“Go,” Mau says. ᴹ

“My husband—” ᴴᶦ

“Is coming,” Mau says. “He'll feel it. He'll come. And he'll want you safe when he gets here. Go.” ᴹ

She looks at him a moment longer. Then at the three things in the yard.

“Don't die,” she says. ᴴᶦ

She goes. Jin-Woo's ant comes off the roof and goes with her, wings spread over her like a shield, and the swarm follows, and Querehsha lifts her hand to send it after them, and Mau says, “*Bang*,” ᴹ and a hole opens in Querehsha's wing.

The swarm stops.

* * *

They all look at him.

All three of them. The wolf, crouched on the grass, healed now, blood matted in its fur. The queen above the house, one wing torn, her faceless chitin head turned toward him. The thing at the gate, its face rippling, its hands still folded in its sleeves.

Jin-Woo's shadows close in round the empty house behind him. The knight at the foot of the steps. The others in their ring. Holding the door, though there's no one left behind it.

He stands in front of them.

He's alone. He understands that. Jin-Woo is miles away, at a gate, looking for a son who's being carried up an orchard path by Mau's soldiers. Yuka is three days south, with Elmesia, getting servants down a staircase. Gojo and Rimuru are at the house, and that's where they need to be; that's where the family is going; that's where Ori is.

There's nobody else here. There's nobody else in front.

His back is bleeding. His arm is bleeding where the wolf's claws went in during the jump. There's a bright hard pain behind his eyes from holding the wolf with Authority. The form is still running, red and white and burning, and the wheel is turning above his head, and Bang is in his hand, and inside him, very deep, very still, Makima's hand is holding the sea.

*Four hundred and thirty-seven*, something in the back of him says. *You know how to do this. You've always known how to do this.*

“Three,” Yogumunt says, from the gate. Its face settles into something almost amused. “And one of you.” ʸᵒ

“Yeah,” Mau says. ᴹ

He doesn't move from in front of the door.

“Don't worry,” he says. “I've done worse.” ᴹ
