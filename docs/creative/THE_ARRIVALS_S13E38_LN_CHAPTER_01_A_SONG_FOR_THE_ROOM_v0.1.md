# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 34 — A Song for the Room

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 34
**Source episode:** S13E38
**Continuity state:** B+329, late morning and evening. Bocchi and Kita finish a song that grew out of the tune Mau hummed when they got back from the island. Byakuya, Mau and Yuka help Bocchi arrange the seats so she can see the porch door. Bocchi chooses her own audience and plays before the film, then asks to play the ending again.

*When: Late morning. Byakuya has put a chair by the screen for Bocchi. Bocchi has moved it twice.*

Mau is outside the music room when the door opens the width of a hand.

“Do you have a minute?” Bocchi asks. ᴮ

“I've got until lunch,” he says. ᴹ

The door stays where it is. He can see her thumb against the edge of it and a little of the paper she's holding.

“Is Yuka with you?” ᴮ

“At Mai's. Did you want her too?” ᴹ

The door opens a little further. “I might. I don't know yet.” ᴮ

He can work with that. He waits for her to open it the rest of the way before he comes in.

Kita is sitting on the floor with three pages laid out in front of her. Byakuya left a stack of the cards he's using to catalogue the films, and there are three clipped to the top of three pages. Two have titles. On the third, Bocchi has rubbed out whatever was there so many times that the card has gone soft in the middle.

“The song you asked me about when you got back,” Bocchi says. “The bridge part. I could only remember what you hummed. I tried to write the rest down, and I don't think it's the same song anymore.” ᴮ

“We tried to make it the same,” Kita says. “Then we played it without stopping and it was better when she wasn't trying.” ᴷⁱ

Bocchi puts the page she's holding down beside the others. “It's easier in here. Once I go out where the screen is, I keep hearing myself stop.” ᴮ

Mau looks at the page. He knows where the chorus probably is from how much writing there is around it. None of the writing tells him what it's going to sound like.

“Can I hear it in here first?” he asks. ᴹ

Bocchi plays it sitting in the chair she always uses. Kita sings part of it with her eyes on the page, a few low words that don't sound like they belong to the piece he remembers. Then there's an interval between the chords, room for one breath, and he can hear the tune he tried to hum on the day they got home. It comes back in a different place than he expects. He waits all the way to the end to tell her that he heard it.

“That's where I stopped trying to find the old part,” Bocchi says. ᴮ

“Leave it there,” he says. “I like when it comes back now.” ᴹ

Kita reaches for a pencil. Bocchi gets to it first and writes something beside the empty measure.

Then she picks up the soft card and turns it over so the rubbed place is facing down.

“Byakuya said we could play before the film tonight. I said maybe.” ᴮ

“What would make it a yes?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Knowing who's going to be there.” Bocchi says this to the soft card. “And I'd like to see the door. I think I could play if I could see the door.” ᴮ

Kita glances at the narrow space behind the music stand. “We could move the chair over again.” ᴷⁱ

Bocchi touches the fretboard with one hand. She hasn't played a note since the song ended. Then she says, “Can we move where the people sit instead?” ᴮ

* * *

Byakuya is happy to move anything as long as someone tells him where.

Mau helps him carry the bench out of the common room. They get as far as the hallway, where they have to set their end down because Ori is coming the other way with a box she can't see over. A spool comes out of the box and rolls down the hall between their feet. She sets the box on the bench and chases it.

Yuka comes out of the workshop carrying the spool. Ori swerves back toward her.

“Mai sent you for something out of this box,” Yuka says. “Did she ask for the whole box?” ʸ

“She said I'd know it when I saw it.” ᴼ

“Was it the blue thread?” ʸ

“Yes,” Ori says with great relief. ᴼ

Yuka gives her the spool. Ori runs it back to Mai, leaving the box exactly where she put it. Yuka lifts it out of their way.

“Did you just get recruited as furniture?” she asks Mau. ʸ

“You're early,” he says. ᴹ

“Mai's taking a break. I came to get you for lunch.” ʸ

“Can I borrow you for a minute before you get me?” ᴹ

It takes all four of them to try what Bocchi asks. They turn the front row sideways and leave the edge by the porch clear. The screen still works from every chair except one. Yuka sits in that chair, leans forward, leans back, and declares that it belongs to whoever talks the most during the movie because they'll have no business looking at it.

“Gojo,” Yuka says. Mau gets the same word out half a breath after her. ʸ

Bocchi laughs. It comes out before she has time to cover it. Then she looks down the gap they left for her. If she looks straight up, she can see out through the glass of the porch door. The chair by the screen stays where she put it the second time.

“Do you still want to know who's coming?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“I want to ask them myself,” Bocchi says. “It's easier if I can say what I'm asking them to come for.” ᴮ

“What are we coming for?” Yuka asks. ʸ

Bocchi tries the answer once very quietly. Kita gets up from a chair and moves to the back of the room so she'll have to say it loud enough to travel.

“I made a song. Could you come a little before the movie and hear it?” ᴮ

“Yes,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I'd like to, too,” Byakuya says from behind the screen. ᵇʸᵃᵏᵘʸᵃ

Bocchi didn't know he was there. She jumps. Byakuya pokes his head round the edge and asks what to put on the card if there's going to be a song as well as a film.

“Give me one more day to think of the name,” Bocchi says. “I'm still playing it tonight.” ᴮ

* * *

At lunch Yuka sits next to Mau and tells him what she and Mai were doing before Ori came looking for the thread. She tried to make a pocket by herself. She got three sides out of four exactly right.

“Where did you put the fourth?” he asks. ᴹ

“Through the back of the thing I was sewing. Now it has a pocket you can't get into.” ʸ

“I can get into it.” ᴹ

“With Authority?” ʸ

“With scissors.” ᴹ

She elbows him. He gives her the last piece of cucumber off his plate, and she eats it and tells him she already tried scissors. This time she managed to take out only the stitches she wanted to take out, which is why she was so pleased when she came to find him. She wants to put a pocket in the next thing she makes just to see if she can do it twice.

“I'll give you something to put in it,” he says. ᴹ

“What if it's for you?” ʸ

“Then I'll put something in it myself.” ᴹ

She sees him smiling at his plate and finds, with genuine irritation, that she'd actually been trying to keep that part a surprise.

* * *

Bocchi has asked five people by the time somebody asks whether they can bring somebody else. She'd had her answer ready in case nobody wanted to come. She has to think for a minute about this one.

“Would you tell her it's before the film?” she asks Ori. “And I'll tell her myself where to sit, if she comes early.” ᴮ

Ori goes to ask Milim. Bocchi can hear the question travel all the way along the porch, at the volume Ori usually reserves for news about dragons.

Kita is trying very hard to get a sheet of music to stand up on the borrowed stool. It keeps folding over, page first. Bocchi puts one of Byakuya's blank cards against the back of it.

“Are we going to practise again?” Kita asks. ᴷⁱ

“We could try it out here once.” ᴮ

They do. It's strange how familiar the chair feels and how little the rest of the room does. Halfway through, a spoon falls in the kitchen. Bocchi hears it bounce twice. She knows she's missed the next chord because Kita has taken the breath to come in and kept it. The two of them stop in different places.

“Back to the start?” Kita asks. ᴷⁱ

Bocchi looks at the first measure. She's played it so many times today that she'll be able to hear it when she shuts her eyes tonight. She moves the card down behind the paper until the corner sticks out by the place they stopped.

“Could you sing from here?” she asks. ᴮ

Kita finds the place and sings one line by herself. Bocchi waits for the second breath, comes in partway through the line and finds the chord under it. They get to the end. She wants to know if it will work twice, so she asks again, and this time Kita keeps singing when she hears Bocchi's fingers catch on a string. There's a little sound Bocchi wouldn't have chosen between two notes she would. By the time they get to the end, she's stopped thinking about it.

Ori is at the porch door with Milim by the hand. She has heard enough to know that she's come back in the middle of something. She stands on the other side of the glass, eyes wide, and mimes clapping with her hands as far apart as she can get them.

Bocchi has to put the guitar down to laugh. The sound carries farther than she expects. Ori hears it and opens the door.

“Was that the end?” she asks. ᴼ

“That was practice,” Bocchi says. She looks at Kita. “Do you want to do it like that if I miss it tonight?” ᴮ

Kita nods. “I'll keep singing till you catch me.” ᴷⁱ

This time Bocchi asks Milim herself. Milim wants to know if the song has drums. Bocchi tells her it might, in a few weeks, if she can find a way to hear the voice she likes underneath them. Milim sits down on the floor to demonstrate that she can, if asked, be very quiet. Byakuya has to walk around her with a stack of programmes. She doesn't move so much as a finger.

* * *

Ori arrives for the song with both hands in her coat pockets. She has promised to clap at the end and she's taking precautions.

Milim sits cross-legged on the floor where the bench used to be. Gojo sits in the bad chair. He takes one look at the sliver of screen he can see and opens his mouth. Yuka holds up a plate of something fried. He reaches across and takes one. He doesn't say a word.

Mau is in the second row with a place beside him. Yuka stays standing long enough to get a good look at the room from where Bocchi is sitting. All the way across to the glass you can see the lamps on the porch. No shoulders in the way. Nobody standing in the gap.

She sits down.

Bocchi comes out with Kita. She sits in the chair and starts to put one ankle behind the other, then changes her mind and sets both feet flat on the floor. Kita doesn't count them in. She watches for Bocchi to start.

The opening is nearly quiet enough to miss. You have to listen on purpose. Mau leans a little forward. Yuka can see how much he's waiting for something through the whole middle, though she has no idea what until he lifts one finger on his knee at the instant a familiar line rises through the new one. She's heard him sing that part under his breath when he's getting ready in the morning. Never the next part. Kita sings through a snag in the chord. Bocchi looks up at her, smiling already, and comes back in under her voice. Yuka likes the way this part sounds enough that she tries it silently while they play it.

Bocchi leaves a little time after the last chord. Her hands stay where they are. Yuka waits. Then Bocchi looks up and takes her left hand off the neck of the guitar.

Ori gets both hands out of her pockets in time.

The clapping lasts longer than Bocchi seems to have planned for. She looks at Kita. Kita smiles at her and stays where she is. Byakuya goes to the screen and asks whether he should start the film. There's a pause.

“Could we play the last part again?” Bocchi asks. ᴮ

Byakuya sits on the bench they've left against the wall. “Of course.” ᵇʸᵃᵏᵘʸᵃ

The second time, Bocchi looks up before the part she liked best. She sees the whole room looking back at her. Her fingers keep moving.

When the film finally starts, Mau reaches for Yuka's hand. She gives him her wrist instead, sliding her arm through his until she can rest against him and still see. On the other side of the room Ori keeps tapping one foot. Yuka can hear where the rhythm goes wrong. Mau can too. He nudges her once with his elbow and neither of them points it out. Ori works out where it goes by the time the opening titles are over.
