# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 35 — Tall Enough

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 35
**Source episode:** S13E39
**Continuity state:** B+330, afternoon. Anko and Mau plant the plant they first repotted in V73 #6. Anko wants to see Hinata on purpose rather than inventing an errand, and asks Mau to come see her on purpose too. Yuka brings food and helps tie the plant to its support. Mau asks Anko to see how it is doing together next week.

*When: Mid-afternoon. Mau said he'd meet Anko on the outside step when he finished clearing away lunch. She has gone out to the step with two empty cups and a trowel.*

Anko puts one foot on the edge of the pot to keep it from tipping. The plant comes over anyway, listing out of the dirt without bringing the pot with it. She rights it with two fingers and holds it until Mau comes round the far end of the porch.

He's carrying tea.

“I thought those were for water,” Anko says, looking at the cups. ᴬⁿ

“I thought they were for us.” ᴹ

She moves one to the other end of the step and takes the one without a chip on the rim. He pours both and finds a flat place for the pot to stand. The plant leans another inch in the direction of his hand. He looks at it for a long time.

“I told you it would get taller.” ᴹ

“You could have told it to get thicker while you were at it.” ᴬⁿ

He sits beside her, leaving the length of the trowel between them. She hands him a stick about as long as his forearm. He takes it and looks around for a second one.

“Just that one. I thought if I took it out of the pot, we could give it something to lean on while it gets used to the ground.” ᴬⁿ

“Where do you want it?” ᴹ

“I thought you'd help me choose.” ᴬⁿ

He picks up the tea and looks out past the steps. There's a place in the strip along the wall that gets the sun early and another, farther over, where the shadow of the porch stays until late afternoon. He points to the first one and she tells him that was her first choice too. Then she picks up the trowel and stays on the step.

He drinks his tea.

She drinks hers.

“There's another thing,” she says after a while. ᴬⁿ

“Okay.” ᴹ

“I want to get better at going to see people.” ᴬⁿ

He waits. She likes that he waits. She also likes that he keeps drinking his tea while he's waiting. It gives her something to look at besides a person trying very hard to look patient.

“When I was going everywhere,” she says, “I was usually looking for someone who'd done something. I'd find them before they knew I was there. It's very easy to go and see someone like that. You don't have to tell them when you're coming, and if they want you to go home, you were expecting that anyway.” ᴬⁿ

“You're good at turning up in a room,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Thanks. I worked hard at that.” ᴬⁿ

He smiles. She gets to smile about it too without having to explain what she was working at or why. Then she sets her cup down.

“I want to see Hinata when she isn't the one coming here. I know how to ask. I still sit there and work out whether I could make it an errand first, so if she has to go after five minutes, I was going to be there anyway.” ᴬⁿ

Mau lets that sit for the length of a sip. “Would you like five minutes if she had them?” ᴹ

“I'd like the whole morning.” ᴬⁿ

“Yeah,” he says, with a little laugh in his voice. ᴹ

“You laughed.” ᴬⁿ

“I wanted Yuka for a walk the other day and ended up waiting outside the workshop long enough to hear Mai say, 'He's waiting for you.' I had already changed my mind twice about what to ask her to do.” ᴹ

“What did you ask her to do?” ᴬⁿ

“Eat toast with me.” ᴹ

Anko looks at him. He looks mildly embarrassed, which is interesting. He leaves that very rare expression on his face instead of covering it up with a joke.

“I thought you went out for the plant,” he says after a moment. ᴹ

“I did,” she says. “And I wanted to see you. I put it in the sun this morning so I'd remember to ask.” ᴬⁿ

He sets his empty cup down between them. “You can ask me any time.” ᴹ

“You have to do it too,” she says. ᴬⁿ

He looks up.

“Sometimes I hear you've been back for three days because someone tells me what Shoko said in the common room. Or I see you're sitting with Yuka and I figure you'll come over when you're done. And then you're off doing something again. Ask me to do something where you already know you don't need me, once in a while.” ᴬⁿ

He listens to all of it. At the end he takes the stick she'd set on the step and balances it across his two hands.

“I'm going to want to see how this thing gets on in the dirt,” he says. “Can I come and see it with you next week?” ᴹ

“You'd better,” Anko says. ᴬⁿ

* * *

The earth next to the wall is stony down to one knuckle and unexpectedly good after that. Anko digs. Mau holds the stem gently while she gets the plant out of the pot. There are more roots than she expected. They fill out the hole so closely that she has to get him to lift the plant back out while she makes it wider.

One of the roots slips over his finger, pale and thin as a strand of thread.

“This one needs more room.” ᴹ

She scrapes a little more away on that side.

Yuka comes round the corner and finds the two of them kneeling by the wall. She's got a clean cloth over one arm and two pieces of bread with sugar on top.

“Shuna said you took tea out here without anything to eat.” ʸ

Anko takes one piece and bites it. Yuka holds the other in front of Mau, who looks at his hands and then opens his mouth. She feeds him the corner. He goes on holding the plant. A little sugar sticks to his lip and she wipes it off with her thumb.

“What is it?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“He's supposed to tell me that when it flowers,” Anko says. ᴬⁿ

“I made no such promise,” Mau says through the bread. ᴹ

“You will, if I ask you in front of her.” ᴬⁿ

Yuka looks at the plant for a moment. “I'll give you until it flowers.” ʸ

Mau takes the second bite she offers anyway.

Between them they get the roots into the hole. Anko presses the soil in. Mau holds the stick steady and Yuka takes a strip out of the clean cloth, then thinks better of tearing the whole thing and takes a short length of sewing thread from her pocket instead. She winds it round the plant once. Anko can get a finger under the thread. There's room for the stem to grow.

They stand up and look at it. The plant is taller than Anko's knee. It is untidy, with two leaves sticking out in an entirely different direction from the rest. She thinks she knows what part of it she is going to see first from her window.

Yuka waits until they've both had a good look.

“Will you come in with me when you're done?” she asks Mau. ʸ

“I will.” ᴹ

She leaves the cloth where they can both reach it and goes back round the corner. Anko takes it first. Her hands are worse.

Mau looks down at the empty pot. “We can put something else in this.” ᴹ

“Leave it,” she says. “We'll see if there's anything we want in it when you come next week.” ᴬⁿ

He stays while she gathers the trowel and works the mud out of the edge with a piece of bark. It takes another minute. He lets it take the whole minute. Then he takes the two cups, she takes the empty pot, and they go in by the long way so they can look at the plant through the window on the other side.
