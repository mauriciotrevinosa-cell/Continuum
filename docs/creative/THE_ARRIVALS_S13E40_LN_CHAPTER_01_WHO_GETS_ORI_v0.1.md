# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 1 — Who Gets Ori

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E40
**Continuity state:** B+331, morning to afternoon. They wake late, tangled; he still hasn't found out whether the man in the book gets the horse. Breakfast side by side. The Pillow, raised as Mau's shadow at the quarry, has claimed the common-room fire and Ori; Lantern sulks in Ori's shadow and the two of them compete for her. Mau and Yuka referee from the couch with her in his lap. Gojo's tally resumes. No new units beyond the Pillow already recorded in V78.

*When: The first morning after the chair by the lamp. Shuna has already started the bread; Ori has already been downstairs for an hour, which nobody is supposed to know.*

He wakes up because she's reading his book.

Not reading it, exactly. She has it open on his chest, propped against his ribs, and she's lying with her cheek on his shoulder squinting at the page upside down from the wrong side. Her hair is all over both of them. One of her feet is hooked round his ankle as if to stop him leaving in his sleep.

“He doesn't get the horse,” she announces, when she feels him move. ʸ

“You don't know that.” ᴹ

“I skipped ahead. There's a picture. He's walking.” ʸ

“Maybe he's walking *to* the horse.” ᴹ

She considers this with great seriousness. Then she turns her face into his shoulder and laughs, and he feels it all through his chest, and the book slides off onto the blanket and neither of them picks it up.

“You read eleven pages in a month,” she says into his collarbone. ʸ

“I get interrupted.” ᴹ

“By who?” ʸ

He doesn't answer. He doesn't have to. She lifts her head to look at him with an expression of enormous innocence, and he puts his hand on the back of it and draws it down again, and she lets him, and for a while nobody reads anything.

She finds the corner of the book with her toes, pushes it farther down the blanket and settles back against him. He tries to remember what page they were on. She catches the look and laughs into his shoulder again.

* * *

They're late to breakfast. Everybody notices. Nobody says anything except Gojo, who doesn't say anything either, but takes a small notebook out of his pocket as they come in and makes a mark in it with enormous ceremony.

“I thought Shoko burned that,” Yuka says. ʸ

“She burned the first one,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“There's a *second* one?” ʸ

“There's always a second one.” ᴳ

Shoko, beside him, closes her eyes like a woman counting to ten.

There's a space on the bench. There's always a space on the bench now; somewhere around the island, the house stopped trying to fill the place beside Mau, the way you stop putting things on a chair that's always got a cat on it. Yuka slides into it and her shoulder lands against his arm and stays there. He moves the bread closer to her side. She takes the first piece. He'd put two on his plate already, because he knew she would.

“You did that on purpose,” she says, looking at the second piece. ʸ

“I always do it on purpose.” ᴹ

She takes that one too.

* * *

The trouble starts after breakfast, in the common room, and it's eight feet long and covered in shaggy fur.

The Pillow has been home for six days. Six days since the quarry and the old fire and the moment Mau put his hand on its broad forehead and asked, and felt it say yes in the only way it knew how, by groaning and closing its eyes. Six days since it stood up again out of his shadow, a long low shape of darkness that was still, unmistakably, the Pillow.

In those six days it has made one decision, and it has made it with its whole body. The fire in the common room is its fire. The rug in front of the fire is its rug. And the small person who reads on that rug every morning before anyone else is up is *its* small person.

Lantern does not agree.

* * *

Mau sees it as soon as he sits down.

Ori is cross-legged on the rug with a book in her lap and the Pillow curled around her like a wall, its long nose resting on her knee. She's reading aloud. The Pillow's small dark eyes are half shut. Every so often it sighs, a long contented groan like a door that wants oiling, and the whole room seems to settle a little.

And in Ori's shadow, under her knees, something is moving.

Not much. A ripple. The shadow at the edge of her skirt goes deeper, then flattens, then deepens again, like water when something large turns over beneath it. A curve of scale rises a finger's width out of the dark, and sinks.

“He's sulking,” Yuka whispers. ʸ

She's in his lap. She came over to sit beside him on the couch and then didn't, and he's stopped noticing when she does that, the way you stop noticing your own heartbeat. Her back is against his chest and her head is under his chin and she's watching the rug like it's the screen on a movie night.

“Who?” he whispers back. ᴹ

“Lantern. Look.” ʸ

The curve of scale rises again. Higher this time. An eye opens in the dark, round and bright as a lamp, and looks across Ori's lap at the Pillow's nose on her knee.

The Pillow opens one small eye and looks back.

Neither of them moves.

“Oh no,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Oh *yes*,” Yuka says, delighted. ʸ

* * *

It goes on all morning.

Ori turns a page and Lantern's head comes up out of her shadow, just far enough to rest his chin on her other knee. The Pillow shifts its nose half a hand closer to her. Lantern lifts his head higher, so that it's above the Pillow's nose. The Pillow lets out a groan so deep it rattles the cups on the side table. Lantern makes a sound Mau has never heard him make, a low annoyed whistle, like a kettle that wants to be noticed.

Ori, between them, goes on reading out loud as if nothing were happening.

“She knows,” Yuka whispers. ʸ

“She knows,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

“She's *enjoying* it.” ʸ

“She's enjoying it very much.” ᴹ

Milim comes in, sees the standoff, and sits down on the floor with her chin in her hands to watch. Then Chloe. Then Umaru, with chips, which she passes along the back of the couch to Yuka without taking her eyes off the rug. Yuka takes a handful and feeds them to Mau one at a time over her shoulder without looking, so that he has to catch them, and drops one down his collar, and says *oops* in a voice that makes it clear it wasn't.

“Bet,” Milim says, at the end of the second chapter. ᴹⁱ

“On what?” Umaru asks. ᵁ

“Who gets her. When she finishes the book.” ᴹⁱ

“The Pillow,” says Umaru. ᵁ

“Lantern,” says Chloe. “He was here first.” ᶜʰ

“The Pillow's *bigger*.” ᵁ

“Lantern's a *dragon*.” ᶜʰ

They all look at Mau.

“I'm not betting against my own shadows,” he says. ᴹ

“Coward,” says Yuka, comfortably, from under his chin. ʸ

* * *

At the end of the book, Ori closes it.

She looks down at the Pillow's nose on her one knee, and at Lantern's head on the other, both of them watching her. She looks at them for a long time with the seriousness she gives to things that matter.

Then she puts the book down on the rug, and puts one hand on the Pillow's nose and one on Lantern's head, and says, “You can *both* stay. But you have to *share*.” ᴼ

The Pillow groans. Lantern whistles.

Neither of them moves away.

“That's not an answer,” Milim objects. “Who *won*?” ᴹⁱ

“Ori won,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ori looks up at him across the room. She doesn't say anything. She just smiles, the small private smile she gives him when she's done something she's proud of and wants him to know that she knows he noticed.

He smiles back.

“She's *you*,” Yuka murmurs. ʸ

“She's not me.” ᴹ

“She just made two monsters share by being reasonable at them. She's *completely* you.” ʸ

* * *

He takes the Pillow out in the afternoon, because it should see the yard properly, and because Ori asks to walk it.

“You don't walk it,” Mau says. “It's not a dog.” ᴹ

“I know it's not a dog. It's a *Pillow*.” ᴼ

So they walk it. Ori in front with one hand buried in its fur, Mau beside her, the Pillow ambling between them at its own unhurried pace, stopping to put its long nose on anything that interests it: the training posts, the porch steps, Beru's hat, which Beru does not appreciate. Lantern rides in Ori's shadow and occasionally rises out of it to make his kettle noise at the Pillow's back.

Yuka walks on Mau's other side with her arm through his.

She doesn't say much. Every so often she leans her head on his shoulder. Once, when they stop at the edge of the orchard so the Pillow can investigate a fallen pear, she goes up on her toes and says, very quietly, into his ear, “I like this.” ʸ

“The Pillow?” ᴹ

“*This.*” ʸ

She means all of it. The yard and Ori and the shadows bickering and the long ordinary afternoon with nothing in it. He knows she does. He feels it too, so much that it's almost uncomfortable, a fullness under his ribs that he doesn't have anywhere to put.

“Me too,” he says. ᴹ

The Pillow eats the pear. Ori tells it that's disgusting. Lantern whistles in agreement.

* * *

At supper, Gojo makes another mark in the notebook.

“That one wasn't anything,” Yuka protests. “I was just passing him the salt.” ʸ

“You were passing him the salt with your *head* on his *shoulder*.” ᴳ

“That's how I pass the salt.” ʸ

“That's how you do *everything*.” ᴳ

She doesn't argue with that. She doesn't move her head either.

Across the table, Shoko is watching the two of them with an expression Mau has seen on her before, usually right after she's finished closing something up and found it closed better than she expected. She sees him notice, and looks down at her plate.

Down by the fire, two shapes are lying on the rug with a small sleeping person between them, one shaggy and one scaled, both of them perfectly still, both of them pretending very hard that the other isn't there.
