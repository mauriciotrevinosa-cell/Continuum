# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 2 — Shuna's Day Off

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S13E40
**Continuity state:** B+332. Rimuru talks Shuna into a day off. Mau and Yuka volunteer to make breakfast for the house, with Rem supervising from a chair she isn't allowed to leave. Yuka can't cook and is proud of it; Mau can, a little. Flour, a burned first batch, Authority used to flip things, Yuka sitting on the counter as "quality control". In the afternoon Jin-Woo trains with both of them, as a pair, and is quieter than usual; he doesn't say why. Shuna comes home and finds the kitchen clean, which is the part that makes her cry.

*When: B+332. The next morning, before dawn. Rimuru has spent the previous evening convincing Shuna that the house will survive one day without her, and she has agreed on the condition that she doesn't hear about it.*

“We'll do it,” Yuka says. ʸ

She says it the night before, at supper, when Rimuru is halfway through explaining to Shuna for the third time that she's allowed a day off and Shuna is halfway through explaining for the third time that she doesn't need one. Both of them stop and look at her.

“Breakfast,” Yuka says. “Tomorrow. Mau and me. You sleep.” ʸ

Mau looks at her. He didn't know he was volunteering. He finds he doesn't mind.

Shuna looks at Yuka for a long moment with an expression of polite, deep concern.

“Yuka,” she says gently. “You can't cook.” ˢʰᵘ

“I *know*,” Yuka says, with enormous pride. “That's why he's coming.” ʸ

* * *

They're in the kitchen before the sky has started to lighten. Rem is already there.

She's sitting on a chair in the corner by the stove with a blanket round her shoulders and her hands folded in her lap and an expression of grim determination. Rimuru put her there. Rimuru told her she could supervise but not touch, and that if she touched anything he would tell Shuna, and Rem, who would rather die than make Shuna worry, has been sitting in the chair ever since with her hands clenched together so that they can't escape.

“Good morning,” she says faintly. ᴿᵉ

“Good morning, Rem,” Mau says. ᴹ

“The flour is on the second shelf. The eggs are in the cold box. The pan for the bread is the big one with the dent, and you have to grease it all the way into the corners or it sticks, and the oven runs hot on the left side, and—” ᴿᵉ

“Rem,” Yuka says kindly. “Breathe.” ʸ

Rem breathes. Her hands stay clenched.

* * *

Yuka can't cook. She was telling the truth.

She can't cook in a way that's almost impressive. She cracks an egg and most of the shell goes into the bowl and she looks at it with real surprise, as though the egg has done this to her on purpose. She measures flour by holding the bag over the bowl and saying *that looks like enough*. She stirs so enthusiastically that a third of the batter ends up on the counter, on the wall, on Mau.

“You're very bad at this,” he says. ᴹ

“I *told* everyone I was bad at this.” ʸ

“You were very proud of it.” ᴹ

“I'm still proud of it.” ʸ

He takes the bowl away from her. She lets him. Then she hops up onto the counter beside him, swinging her legs, and declares herself *quality control*.

“What does quality control do?” ᴹ

“Tastes things.” ʸ

“And?” ᴹ

“And tells you if they're good.” ʸ

“And if they're bad?” ᴹ

She thinks about it. “Eats them anyway, so nobody else has to suffer.” ʸ

From the corner, Rem makes a small involuntary sound that might be a laugh.

* * *

He's not a good cook either. But he's a better one than she is, which isn't hard.

He learned on the road, a long time ago, beside Frieren, who never much cared what she ate; you learn to cook when the alternative is eating whatever Frieren decided was food. He learned more in a room with a bed, from a Shop that only sold four kinds of thing. He knows how to make bread that's edible, and eggs that aren't burned, and porridge that doesn't stick.

The first batch of bread sticks.

He didn't grease the corners of the pan. Rem told him. He forgot. He takes it out of the oven and turns it over and half of it stays in the tin, and he stands there looking at it, and in the corner Rem's hands are clenched so hard her knuckles have gone pale.

“Quality control,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuka slides off the counter. She looks at the ruined loaf. She pulls off a piece of the half that came out, blows on it, and eats it.

“It's very good,” she says, with her mouth full. ʸ

“It's stuck to the pan.” ᴹ

“The part that's not stuck is very good.” ʸ

“That's half of it.” ᴹ

“Then it's half very good.” ʸ

She feeds him a piece, holding it up to his mouth, and he eats it from her fingers because his hands are covered in batter, and it is, in fact, quite good. She wipes the crumb off his lip with her thumb. He greases the corners of the next pan very, very carefully, all the way in, while Rem watches with the intensity of a hawk.

* * *

He uses Authority for the eggs.

He doesn't mean to, at first. He's got three pans going and two hands, and one of the pans needs turning, and he reaches for it without thinking and it turns. Just lifts a little off the stove, flips its contents, and settles back down.

Yuka stares at it.

“Do that again,” she says. ʸ

He does it again. The eggs flip.

“Do it with *all* of them,” she says. ʸ

So he does. He stands in the middle of Shuna's kitchen with his hands in the bread dough and three pans on the stove lifting and flipping and setting down in turn, like something out of a juggler's act, while Yuka sits on the counter clapping and Rem, in her corner, gives up on dignity entirely and puts her face in her blanket and laughs until she shakes.

“Don't tell Shuna,” Rem gasps. “Please. She'd have a heart attack.” ᴿᵉ

“We won't tell Shuna,” Mau promises. ᴹ

“We're *definitely* telling Shuna,” says Yuka. ʸ

* * *

The house comes down to breakfast one by one, and every single one of them stops in the doorway.

There's food. That's the first surprise. There's bread, the second batch, perfectly turned out, and the half-stuck first batch that Yuka has arranged artfully on a separate plate with a label that says *QUALITY CONTROL APPROVED (MOSTLY)*. There's eggs. There's porridge. There's a pot of tea that Yuka made herself, and nobody is quite sure what she put in it, and it's actually not bad.

The second surprise is the two of them.

They're behind the counter, side by side, serving. Mau with flour on his face and in his hair and down his shirt. Yuka with batter on her sleeve and a smear of something on her nose that Mau keeps reaching over to wipe off and missing. Her hip is against his. Every time someone holds out a plate she puts something on it and announces what it is and how good it is in the voice of a market seller, and he stands beside her and lets her.

Gojo takes his plate and looks at it.

“Who made the eggs?” he asks. ᴳ

“The pans,” says Yuka. ʸ

Gojo turns his blindfolded face to Mau. Mau looks innocent.

“You used *Authority* to make *eggs*,” Gojo says slowly, with dawning wonder. ᴳ

“They're very good eggs,” says Yuka. ʸ

Gojo takes a bite. Considers it.

“They are,” he admits. ᴳ

He takes out his notebook and makes a mark in it, and Yuka points the serving spoon at him and says that one doesn't count because they were *working*, and Gojo says everything counts, and the whole kitchen laughs.

* * *

In the afternoon, Jin-Woo trains with them.

Both of them. Together. He's started doing that since the island, since Yuka asked him once in the yard whether he'd show her what he shows Mau, and he looked at the two of them and said *not separately*, and that's been the arrangement ever since. Mau and Yuka on one side of the yard. Jin-Woo on the other. No practice swords, no borrowed blades: Yuka has asked for balance, timing and the way the two of them cover each other, not another lesson in weapons. Jin-Woo tests them with empty hands, feints, changes of direction and the sudden weight of a shadow at their backs. Ten minutes at a time.

They're good together. Mau knows it and so does Jin-Woo. Yuka has her own technique, the second Limitless Mau made for her inside himself, and the reserves she draws from him under their arrangement; she knows how to use the weight of what he gives her without it costing her the edge she brought from home. Mau knows where she is without looking. When Jin-Woo comes at her left he's already moving to cover it; when Jin-Woo comes at him, she's already there.

Jin-Woo gets through them twice in an hour. Usually it's four or five.

He doesn't say much. That's not unusual. Jin-Woo never says much while he's working. But afterwards, sitting on the training posts with the three of them breathing hard and the sun going low, he's quiet in a way that's different. He keeps looking at the hills to the north.

“What?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Nothing.” ᴶʷ

“You keep looking at the hills.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him. Then at Yuka, sitting on the post beside Mau with her knee against his. Then at the hills again.

“Nothing,” he says. “Probably nothing.” ᴶʷ

Yuka glances at Mau. He glances back. Neither of them pushes. Jin-Woo will say when he's ready; he always does.

But Mau files it. Somewhere he keeps things he might need later.

* * *

Shuna comes home at dusk.

She's been in the village all day with Shion, who took her to see a weaver and to eat at the place by the bridge and to sit in the sun by the water and do absolutely nothing, which Shion reports she managed for almost an hour. She comes in through the back door with her cheeks flushed from the walk and her basket full of thread, and she stops.

The kitchen is clean.

Not just clean. *Clean.* Every pot scrubbed and back on its hook. Every bowl washed and stacked. The counter wiped. The floor swept. The dented bread pan greased and waiting, the way she leaves it every night. On the table, under a cloth, a plate of the second batch of bread, and a cup of the tea Yuka made, and a note in Yuka's round handwriting that says *for the chef — from quality control*.

Mau and Yuka are sitting at the end of the table. Yuka is asleep against his shoulder. He has flour in his hair still.

Shuna looks at the kitchen for a long time.

Then she sits down very suddenly on the nearest chair, puts her basket on the floor, and starts to cry.

“Shuna—” Mau starts, alarmed. ᴹ

“No,” she says, waving her hand. “No. It's—” ˢʰᵘ

She can't finish. She points at the clean pots. At the greased pan. At the cloth over the bread.

“Nobody,” she says, through her tears, “*nobody* ever cleans up after.” ˢʰᵘ

Yuka wakes up at the sound of her voice. She takes one look at Shuna crying in a chair in her own clean kitchen, gets up, crosses the room, and hugs her, flour and all.

Mau stays where he is and lets them. When Shuna can breathe again she looks at him over Yuka's shoulder.

“The eggs,” she says. “Rem told me about the eggs.” ˢʰᵘ

“Rem promised not to tell you about the eggs.” ᴹ

“Rem told me in the *first five minutes*,” Shuna says, and starts laughing, and doesn't stop for a while.
