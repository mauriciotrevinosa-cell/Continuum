# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 4 — A List of Names

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E41
**Continuity state:** B+334, morning to afternoon. Mau keeps his promise not to look. Unable to sit still, he starts a list of names in his head; Noise takes it very seriously, and Ciel adds her own. Makima, who feels his hope through the bond, offers one too. Mau tells her she's helping, but that doesn't make her anything less than his friend. Yuka is clingier than ever all day, and neither of them says a word to the house. Gojo's tally suffers.

*When: The morning after the arithmetic. Neither of them slept much. Yuka is pretending, very badly, that nothing is different.*

He doesn't look.

It's harder than anything he's done in weeks. Harder than the warden in the ring. Harder than holding a gate shut. She's everywhere he turns, all morning, because she won't let him out of arm's reach, and every single time she's close enough to touch, the eyes want to do what they do. They want to *read*. They read everything; that's what they're for. Walls and floors and people and the small invisible weather of cursed energy moving through all of it. They don't know how not to.

He makes them not.

He fixes them on her face. On her hands. On the bread she's buttering for him. On the ceiling. He narrows the filter as far as Mini Ciel will let it go, until the world through it is almost as plain as an ordinary person's. He holds it there.

“You're doing the thing with your jaw,” Yuka murmurs, at breakfast, leaning on his shoulder. ʸ

“What thing?” ᴹ

“The thing where you're trying very hard not to do something.” ʸ

“I'm not doing anything.” ᴹ

“I know,” she says, and squeezes his arm. “That's why your jaw is doing the thing.” ʸ

* * *

He needs something to do with his head. So he starts a list.

He doesn't mean to. It just starts. He's walking back from the yard with a bucket and a name comes into his mind from nowhere, the way a song does, and before he's thought about it he's turning it over, testing it, holding it up next to *Ieiri* and *Okkotsu* and nothing at all, to see how it would sound.

“That's not a good one,” Noise says. ᴺ

He stops in the middle of the yard.

“What?” ᴹ

“The one you just thought. It's not a good one. It sounds like a sneeze.” ᴺ

“You were *listening*?” ᴹ

“I'm always listening. You were thinking very loudly.” ᴺ

He stands there with the bucket. Then, because there's nobody in the yard and nobody to hear him, he says, very quietly, “What would you pick?” ᴹ

There's a pause.

“I'll need time,” Noise says. ᴺ

It says it the way it says things like *I'll need time to analyse the gate* or *I'll need time to build the model*. With total seriousness. With the air of something rolling up its sleeves.

* * *

By noon, Noise has forty.

It doesn't tell him all at once. It brings them to him one at a time, through the morning, while he's doing other things, the way a cat brings things into the house. He's holding a ladder for Senku and Noise says *Haru*. He's carrying water for Shuna and Noise says *Ren, but only if it's a boy, and only if you don't mind that it's also the word for lotus*. He's sitting on the porch with Yuka's head in his lap and Noise says *Mio* and then, a minute later, *no, not Mio, there's already a Mio somewhere in Bocchi's stories and it would be confusing*, and crosses it off.

“You're making a *list*,” Mau says under his breath. ᴹ

“You *asked*,” Noise says. ᴺ

“I asked what you'd pick. Not forty.” ᴹ

“Forty is a reasonable number for a first pass. I'm going to narrow it.” ᴺ

“How?” ᴹ

“By meaning, by sound, by whether it can be shouted across a yard without embarrassment, and by whether it works with both surnames you're considering. And with neither.” ᴺ

Mau puts his hand over his eyes.

Yuka, in his lap, opens one eye. “Who are you talking to?” ʸ

“Nobody.” ᴹ

“You're talking to Noise.” ʸ

“Noise is making a list.” ᴹ

She lifts her head. “Of *what*?” ʸ

He doesn't answer. He doesn't have to. Her face does something soft and stricken, and she puts her head back down in his lap very quickly so he can't see it, and he puts his hand in her hair and leaves it there.

* * *

Ciel adds three in the afternoon.

She doesn't announce them. She just puts them on the list, quietly, in the place where Noise keeps it, and Noise finds them there and is outraged.

“She *added* to it,” Noise reports. ᴺ

“Is that allowed?” ᴹ

“It's *my* list.” ᴺ

“I thought it was my list.” ᴹ

“It's *our* list,” Ciel says, very primly. “You both started it. I can't be expected to listen to the two of you arguing about whether Sora is too much like Shiro all morning without contributing.” ᶜ

Mau laughs out loud, alone on the porch steps. Ori, passing with the Pillow, looks at him strangely.

* * *

Makima finds him in the late afternoon.

She doesn't come close. She never comes close any more without asking; that's part of what Yuka set, and part of what she's chosen to keep on her own. She stops at the bottom of the porch steps where he's sitting and stands with her hands folded.

“May I?” she asks, looking at the step. ᴹᵏ

He nods. She sits on the bottom step, two steps below him, and doesn't look at him.

They sit there a while.

“You're hoping,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He goes still.

“I can feel it,” she says, still not looking at him. “Through the thread. All day. It's very loud. I don't know what it's about. You haven't said and I haven't asked. But it's there, and it's warm, and it's been there since yesterday morning.” ᴹᵏ

He doesn't say anything.

“I'm not asking,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I'm only telling you that I can feel it. So you know. So you don't think it's a secret from me when it isn't.” ᴹᵏ

He thinks about that. About the thread that runs from her to the deep still place in him where she's holding the sea. About how much of him comes through it whether he wants it to or not.

“Thank you,” he says. “For telling me.” ᴹ

She inclines her head.

Then, after a long pause, very quietly, still not looking at him: “Asa.” ᴹᵏ

He looks at her.

“It means morning,” she says. “In one of the languages from my world. I thought—you've been looking at the window a great deal. In the mornings. I thought it might belong on whatever list Noise has been muttering about all day.” ᴹᵏ

Noise goes completely silent.

Mau sits there on the porch steps, two steps above the woman who once sent something to hurt the person he loves most, who is now holding back the thing that would have killed him, who has just offered him a name for a child she doesn't know exists.

“You're helping,” he says. ᴹ

She looks up at him for the first time.

“You're helping,” he says again, “and I don't know how I feel about that yet. But that doesn't change that you're my friend.” ᴹ

Something goes across her face. He's seen it a few times now, since the pact. The calm lifting, just for a second, off something underneath.

She looks back down at her hands.

“Noise can cross it off,” she says. “If it doesn't fit.” ᴹᵏ

“Noise,” Mau says. ᴹ

A long pause.

“It stays on the list,” Noise says, grudgingly. “*Provisionally*.” ᴺ

* * *

Yuka doesn't let go of him all evening.

She's always close. She's been close since the island. But tonight it's different, and the whole house can tell; he can see them telling, out of the corners of their eyes, all through supper. She sits so close on the bench that there's no space between them at all. She eats with her left hand so her right can stay on his knee under the table. When he gets up to get more water she gets up with him. When he sits back down she sits back down.

Gojo makes a mark. And another. And another. And then puts his pen down and stares at them with his blindfolded face and says, “I can't keep up,” ᴳ in a voice of genuine despair.

“Then stop counting,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

“I *can't* stop counting. It's a *compulsion*.” ᴳ

Rimuru laughs. Milim laughs. Yuka, against his side, doesn't laugh; she smiles, very slightly, and doesn't look up from her plate, and squeezes his knee.

Neither of them says a word. Nobody asks.

But when Mau looks up from his plate, he finds Shoko watching him across the table, very still, with her cup halfway to her mouth. Shoko, who notices everything. Who has had her hands on him more times than anyone in this room. Who has seen his face in every state a face can be in.

She looks at him, and then at Yuka's hand on his knee, and then back at his face.

Then she drinks her tea, and looks away, and doesn't say anything at all.
