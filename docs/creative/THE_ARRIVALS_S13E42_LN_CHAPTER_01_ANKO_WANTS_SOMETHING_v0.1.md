# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 5 — Anko Wants Something

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E42
**Continuity state:** B+335, supper. Shoko has told Anko about the island trip Mau promised her (V78 #12). Anko spends the whole of supper buttering Mau up; he doesn't understand what she wants; she finally says it straight: the island. Within a minute the whole table wants to go and is arguing about it. Mau and Yuka slip out to the porch while the house argues, and decide that if it's something, they'll tell everyone on the island. That night they write the letter to Elmesia together, in bed, Yuka dictating and Mau writing.

*When: B+335. The next evening. Anko has been in a suspiciously good mood since lunch. Hinata visited yesterday.*

Anko is being nice to him.

It starts at the beginning of supper and it doesn't stop. She sits down across from him, which she never does; she usually sits at the far end with Nazuna, where she can complain about the food without Shuna hearing. Tonight she sits directly across from Mau and Yuka and smiles at him.

“Mau,” she says. “You look well.” ᴬⁿ

He looks at her. “Thank you?” ᴹ

“Rested. Healthy. Very—” She searches for a word. “—tall.” ᴬⁿ

“I've always been tall.” ᴹ

“And you wear it *so well*.” ᴬⁿ

Yuka, beside him, has stopped eating and is watching Anko with the intense interest of someone watching a play.

* * *

It goes on.

Anko passes him the bread before he asks for it. Anko passes him the salt. Anko compliments Shuna's stew in a loud voice and then turns to Mau and says that of course Shuna's stew is wonderful but it must be nothing compared to the food in *other places*, places he's *been*, places with *fruit*. Anko asks him how his hand is, the one from the red gate, and listens to the answer with an expression of deep sympathy. Anko laughs at something he says that isn't a joke.

He has no idea what's happening.

He looks at Yuka. Yuka is biting her lip. He looks at Shoko, two seats down, who is looking very hard at her stew. He looks at Nazuna at the far end, who has her chin in her hand and is watching Anko with the long-suffering patience of a roommate.

“Anko,” he says finally. ᴹ

“Yes, Mau?” ᴬⁿ

“Are you sick?” ᴹ

Yuka makes a sound into her spoon.

Anko's smile goes a little fixed.

“I'm *not* sick,” she says. ᴬⁿ

“You're being very nice to me.” ᴹ

“I'm always nice to you.” ᴬⁿ

“You told me last week that I eat like a raccoon.” ᴹ

“That was *affectionate*.” ᴬⁿ

Down the table, Gojo has put his spoon down to listen properly.

* * *

“Is there something you want?” Mau asks. ᴹ

He means it sincerely. He has genuinely run out of explanations. He's considered that she might be ill, that she might have broken something of his and be about to confess, that she might need a favour with Hinata, and none of them seem to fit, and she's still smiling at him in that fixed bright way.

Anko looks at him for a long moment.

Then the smile drops off her face like a dropped plate.

“The *island*,” she says. ᴬⁿ

He stares at her.

“The *island*, Mau. The one with the little pool. And the fruit. And the turtle. Shoko told me you said I could come. Shoko said you *said*. And then you went into a red gate and blew up a clinic and forgot who your wife was for three days, and nobody's said a *word* about it since, and I've been *waiting*.” ᴬⁿ

“Oh,” Mau says. ᴹ

“*Oh*,” says Anko. ᴬⁿ

Yuka has given up. She has her forehead on the table and her shoulders are shaking.

* * *

There's a silence of about one second.

Then the whole table is talking at once.

“What island?” Milim demands, standing up. ᴹⁱ

“The one they went to,” Umaru says. “With the hotel. Elmesia's.” ᵁ

“*Shoko's* going?” Gojo turns to Shoko with an expression of profound betrayal. “You're going to the island and you didn't—” ᴳ

“You're coming too,” Shoko says, not looking up. “If you behave.” ˢʰ

“I *always* behave—” ᴳ

“You don't.” ˢʰ

“Can I come?” Ori asks. ᴼ

“Can *I* come?” Milim asks, louder. ᴹⁱ

“Is Hinata coming?” asks Nazuna, from the far end, very innocently, and Anko turns around and throws a piece of bread at her. ᴺᶻ

Rimuru is laughing. Shizue is laughing. Senku has his notebook out and is asking how far away it is and what the water temperature is like and whether the turtle is a native species. Frieren hasn't said anything at all, but she's put her book down, which Mau has learned means more than anything she could say.

It's chaos. It's wonderful chaos. Everybody wants to go.

He looks at Yuka.

She's lifted her head off the table. Her eyes are wet with laughing. She looks back at him across the noise, and something passes between them, and she tilts her head very slightly toward the door.

* * *

They slip out while the house is arguing about whether Beru would need a hat for the beach.

Nobody notices. Or everybody notices and pretends not to, which in this house is the same thing. The porch is cool and dark and quiet. The noise of the kitchen comes through the door behind them, muffled, warm. Somewhere out in the yard the Pillow is lying in the grass and Lantern is curled in a patch of shadow by the fence, and the two of them are ignoring each other with tremendous dignity.

Yuka leans on the rail. He leans beside her.

“Everybody wants to go,” she says. ʸ

“Everybody wants to go.” ᴹ

“Elmesia's going to kill us.” ʸ

“Elmesia said bring the whole family.” ᴹ

“She said *if you must*.” ʸ

“I think we must,” he says. ᴹ

She laughs, very softly. Then she goes quiet. He waits.

“If it's something,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't move.

“If it's something,” she says again, and her hand finds his on the rail and holds it, “we could tell them there. On the island. All of them. When everyone's there.” ʸ

He thinks about it. The beach. The house in the sun. Everyone in one place, the way they almost never are, nobody hurt, nobody missing, nothing coming. Ori on the sand. Shoko in a little pool. Gojo behaving. And the two of them standing up at dinner, in Elmesia's hall, and saying it.

“On the island,” he says. ᴹ

“When it's real,” she says. “When we're sure.” ʸ

“When we're sure.” ᴹ

She turns her hand over under his and laces their fingers together, and they stand there on the porch with the house roaring behind them, holding a secret between them like something warm cupped in two hands.

* * *

They write the letter that night.

In bed. Yuka lying on her stomach with her chin on her folded arms and the lamp turned low. Mau sitting up against the headboard with the lap desk Mai made him and a sheet of good paper and a pen.

“*Dear Elmesia*,” Yuka dictates. ʸ

He writes it.

“*We hope you're well. We hope the bird is well. Mau would like it known that he has forgiven it—*” ʸ

“I haven't forgiven it.” ᴹ

“You have. You bought it a bowl. Write it.” ʸ

He writes it.

“*We'd like to come back.*” ʸ

He writes it.

“*We'd like to bring some people.*” ʸ

He writes it. He looks at her. “Some?” ᴹ

“*Some*,” she says firmly. “We'll tell her how many later. We'll break it to her gently.” ʸ

He writes *some*.

It takes them an hour. Partly because Yuka keeps changing her mind about words and making him cross them out. Partly because she keeps getting distracted and putting her chin on his knee instead of her arms, and then her whole head, and then she's lying across his legs looking up at him instead of at the letter, and he has to write the rest of it over the top of her hair. Partly because every few lines one of them says *if it's something* and then they both stop and don't say anything for a while.

They don't put that in the letter. They put in the bird, and the people, and Shoko's little pool, and a line from Yuka that says *Mau sends his regards and also says your hotel has the best fruit in any world he's been to*, which Mau did not say and does not cross out.

When it's done, she signs it. He signs it beside her. She folds it, very carefully, three times, the way Ori folds things that matter.

“Tomorrow,” she says. “The bird can take it tomorrow.” ʸ

“Tomorrow,” he agrees. ᴹ

He turns the lamp down. She doesn't move off his legs. She just pulls the blanket up over both of them where they are, him sitting up, her lying across his lap with her cheek on his knee, and closes her eyes.

“Don't look,” she mumbles. ʸ

“I'm not looking,” he says. ᴹ

He isn't. He's looking at the folded letter on the bedside table, and at the line of light from the window across the floor, and at the ceiling, and at everything in the room except the one thing he wants to look at most.

He falls asleep sitting up.
