# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 6 — The Bathroom Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S13E42
**Continuity state:** B+336, early morning. The day after they decided how to tell the house. Yuka wakes before him, says she's going to the bathroom, and doesn't come back. Her period has come. Mau finds her crying on the floor; when she sees him she breaks completely. She blames herself; he tells her it isn't her fault. She won't walk, so he carries her back to bed. He buys breakfast from the Shop and sends a message over the link that Yuka isn't well (not hurt, he'll explain later) and not to wait for them. Rimuru offers to bring breakfast; Mau says he already has something. Yuka falls asleep again holding his shirt. No intimate scene; the focus is Yuka.

*When: B+336. Just after dawn. The folded letter to Elmesia is still on the bedside table, waiting for the bird.*

She says she's going to the bathroom.

He hears it from very far down in sleep. Her weight shifting off the bed. Her voice, low and ordinary: *bathroom, go back to sleep*. Her bare feet on the boards. The door at the end of their room, the little one Senku put in when they moved the washroom, clicking shut.

He goes back to sleep.

* * *

He wakes up because she isn't back.

He doesn't know how long it's been. The light at the window has changed; it was grey when she got up and now it's a little less grey. Her side of the bed is cool when he puts his hand on it. The folded letter is still on the bedside table, exactly where she left it last night.

He lies there for a moment, listening.

Nothing. No water running. No sound of her moving around. Just the house waking up below them, faint and far away: Shuna in the kitchen, the third stair creaking under somebody's foot, Beru shifting his weight in the hall.

“Yuka?” ᴹ

No answer.

He gets up.

* * *

The washroom door isn't locked. He knocks anyway, softly, with one knuckle.

“Yuka?” ᴹ

Nothing.

Then, very small, from the other side of the door: a sound. Not a word. A breath that catches on something and doesn't come out right.

He opens the door.

* * *

She's on the floor.

Sitting with her back against the side of the tub and her knees pulled up and her arms wrapped round them. Her face is turned toward the wall. Her shoulders are shaking. She's crying, but very quietly, so quietly he didn't hear it through the door; the kind of crying you do when you've been doing it for a while and you're trying not to wake anyone.

He doesn't need the eyes. He doesn't need anything. He knows the moment he sees her.

He goes down on his knees beside her on the cold floor.

“Hey,” he says. ᴹ

She turns her head and looks at him.

And she breaks.

* * *

He's seen her cry before. He's seen her cry telling him about the hospital, about the years when she didn't think she'd have a tomorrow. He's seen her cry at the door of the long dungeon, every day for a year he didn't know about. He's seen her cry at weddings and at letters and at the end of movies and once, memorably, at a particularly good piece of cake.

He's never seen this.

It comes out of her all at once, as if the sight of his face has knocked out the last thing that was holding it. Her whole body folds. A sound comes out of her that he's never heard her make, low and torn and enormous, and she reaches for him with both hands and grabs his shirt and pulls herself into him and just *sobs*. Great heaving sobs that shake both of them. Her face in his chest. Her fingers twisted in his shirt so hard it's pulling against the back of his neck.

He holds her.

He doesn't say anything. There's nothing to say yet. He just gets his arms round her, all the way round, and holds on, and lets her shake against him on the cold washroom floor in the grey early light, and puts his hand on the back of her head, and waits.

* * *

“It came,” she says, eventually, into his chest. ʸ

He knows. He doesn't say so.

“It came. This morning. I woke up and I knew, I *knew*, before I even got up, and I thought maybe I was wrong, and I came in here and I wasn't wrong, and it—” ʸ

She can't finish. She shakes her head against him.

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“We were going to tell them. On the *island*. We had it all planned. You were going to put the bed by the window on my side—” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“—and Noise made a *list*—” ʸ

That's what does it, for both of them. The list. He feels his own throat close. He holds her tighter and doesn't let her see his face.

* * *

“It's my fault,” she says. ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“It is. It's me. Something's wrong with me. It's—” ʸ

“It's not your fault.” ᴹ

“You don't *know* that—” ʸ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

He pulls back just far enough to see her face. It's blotched and wet and crumpled. Her eyes won't meet his. He takes her face in both his hands, very gently, the way she does to him when she wants him to look at her, and waits until she does.

“It's not your fault,” he says again, slowly. “Nothing you did. Nothing you are. It's not your fault, Yuka. It's not anybody's fault.” ᴹ

She looks at him. He can see her wanting to believe it and not being able to. He can see the thing in her that's already decided it's her fault, and has been deciding it since before she opened the washroom door.

He doesn't argue with it. He doesn't tell her she's wrong to feel it. He just keeps his hands on her face and keeps saying it, quietly, as many times as it takes, until she closes her eyes and leans her forehead against his and stops trying to answer.

* * *

“Breakfast,” he says, after a long time. ᴹ

She shakes her head.

“Just a little. You don't have to come down. I'll get it. Just—let me take you back to bed first, and I'll get you something.” ᴹ

“I don't want to walk,” she says. ʸ

It's a very small voice. It's the voice of someone who has just found out that her legs don't feel like legs any more.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

He gets one arm under her knees and the other behind her back and stands up with her. She weighs nothing. She puts her arms round his neck and her face in his shoulder and doesn't lift it. He carries her out of the washroom and across the room and lays her down on her side of the bed, and pulls the blanket up over her, and she curls up under it on her side, small, with her knees drawn up, facing the window.

The folded letter is on the bedside table between them. He looks at it. He turns it face down.

* * *

He doesn't go downstairs.

He doesn't want to leave her even long enough to walk down and back up. So he sits on the edge of the bed beside her with one hand on her hip through the blanket, and opens the Shop.

He doesn't think about what to get. His hands know. The warm bread she likes, the soft kind. The tea in the glass bottle, the one she takes from the kitchen when she thinks Rem isn't looking. A little pot of the honey from the island catalogue. A bowl of the plain porridge, the one that's good when your stomach doesn't want anything. A heat pad, the kind you can buy from the Shop's household pages, because he remembers, from the island, when she had it before the trip and spent a morning curled up with a hot stone wrapped in cloth and said it helped.

He sets them all out on the bedside table. Quietly. One by one.

Then he reaches for the link.

* * *

It isn't words, exactly, the link. It's the thing Ciel built so the house can reach each other when someone's somewhere else. He only uses it when he has to. He keeps the message short.

*Yuka isn't well this morning. Not hurt. Nothing physical. I'll explain later. Please don't wait for us at breakfast.*

He sends it, and then sits there with his hand on her hip and waits.

It's Rimuru who answers first. Of course it is.

*Do you need anything? I can bring breakfast up.*

*I already got something from the Shop,* Mau sends back. *Thank you.*

A pause.

*Okay,* Rimuru sends. *We're here. Whatever it is.*

Mau closes the link. He doesn't know what to do with how that feels, so he doesn't do anything with it. He just sits there.

* * *

He gets her to eat a little.

Not much. A few spoons of the porridge, sitting up against him with the blanket round her shoulders and her head on his chest, while he holds the bowl. Half a cup of the tea. She won't touch the bread. He puts the heat pad against her stomach under the blanket and she makes a small sound and presses her hand over it.

She doesn't say anything else. She doesn't cry any more. She just leans on him, very still, with her eyes on the window, where the light has gone from grey to the pale flat white of a morning that doesn't care what's happened in this room.

After a while her eyes close.

He waits until her breathing goes slow. Then he tries to ease her back down onto the pillow, so she can sleep properly, so he can sit beside her and not crowd her.

Her hand closes on his shirt.

She doesn't wake. She just holds on. Her fingers twisted in the fabric over his chest, the way they were on the washroom floor.

He stays where he is.

* * *

He sits like that a long time.

Her asleep against him, holding his shirt. The porridge going cold on the table. The letter face down beside it. Downstairs, the house having breakfast without them; he can hear it, very faintly, quieter than usual. Somebody has told them to be quiet. Probably Rimuru.

He looks at the window.

He doesn't cry. He doesn't do anything. He just sits there and lets it be in him: the bed by the window on her side, the shallow water, the small weight on his shoulders screaming at every wave. All of it, still there, right where he left it last night, and nowhere to put it now.

“Noise,” he says, very quietly, so as not to wake her. ᴹ

“I'm here,” Noise says. ᴺ

Its voice is very soft.

“The list,” Mau says. ᴹ

A long pause.

“I've put it away,” Noise says. “Somewhere safe. I'm not deleting it. Just putting it away.” ᴺ

Mau closes his eyes.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

He holds her, and she holds his shirt, and outside the window the morning goes on getting lighter without either of them.
