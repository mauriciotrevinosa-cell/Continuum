# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 7 — What He Does Instead of Crying

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E43
**Continuity state:** B+336, late morning to night. While Yuka sleeps, Mau does what he does instead of crying: he looks for a solution. Without looking at her, he asks Noise and the wheel what could cause this and what could be done, and makes them promise not to tell Yuka, because the answer could hurt. Noise says there are too many possible causes and they can't know anything without looking at her. Makima feels his grief through the thread and comes to the door without coming in; Mau goes out to her. She doesn't understand what she's feeling. He tells her it's what he feels, only she's living it. He hugs her, the only one of the four who feel it with him that he can hug. She goes stiff. Yuka wakes in the evening, eats a little, says almost nothing, and holds his shirt all night.

*When: B+336. The same day. Yuka sleeps through most of the morning. The house is quieter than he's ever heard it.*

He doesn't cry.

He sits on the edge of the bed with her asleep against him for an hour, two, and he doesn't cry. He doesn't do anything. There's a thing in his chest that's the size of the room, and he doesn't know where to put it, so he leaves it where it is.

And then, because he can't sit there with it any longer, he starts looking for a way out.

* * *

That's what he does. He's always done it. He did it in a field with Frieren at the very beginning, when he couldn't remember anything and the only thing he could think of was *what do I do next*. He did it in the Trial, four hundred and thirty-seven times. He did it in a room with a bed. When something happens that he can't hold, he looks for the door. The way through. The thing that can be fixed.

“Noise,” he says, very quietly. ᴹ

“I'm here.” ᴺ

“What could cause it?” ᴹ

A pause.

“Don't tell her I asked,” he says. “Whatever the answer is. Don't tell her. Not unless she asks. It might hurt.” ᴹ

“I won't tell her,” Noise says. ᴺ

“Promise.” ᴹ

“I promise.” ᴺ

* * *

Noise tells him.

Not all at once. It doesn't know all at once. It tells him the way it tells him about a gate it's only just started to read: carefully, with everything it doesn't know marked as not known.

It tells him it could be nothing. That this happens. That it happens to people who are perfectly well, for no reason anyone can find, over and over, until one day it doesn't. That two months is not very long. That the body keeps its own calendar and doesn't tell anyone what's on it.

It tells him it could be something. That there are a great many somethings, and most of them have names Noise has only read about in the medical books Shoko keeps in the clinic, and some of them are on her side and some of them are on his, and some of them are nobody's, just the shape of two people from two different worlds.

“I can't tell you which,” Noise says. “Not without looking.” ᴺ

“I promised not to look.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴺ

“Can *you*? Without me?” ᴹ

“No,” Noise says. “I see through your eyes. If you don't look, I don't see.” ᴺ

* * *

He asks the wheel.

He's never done that before. The wheel doesn't answer questions. The wheel doesn't do anything except turn at what hurts him and learn its shape. But he asks anyway. He sits there with his eyes closed and reaches for it, up and behind, the place where it hangs when it's out, and asks it: *is there something here you can adapt to?*

Nothing.

The wheel doesn't turn. It doesn't move. There's nothing hurting him that it can learn. Nothing with a shape. There's just her, asleep against him, and a thing in his chest the size of the room.

“It doesn't work like that,” Ciel says gently. ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You asked anyway.” ᶜ

“I asked anyway.” ᴹ

* * *

He goes through everything he has.

All morning. He sits with her asleep against him and goes through it in his head, piece by piece. Reverse cursed energy: what it can mend, what it can't. Shoko's books: what Noise has read, what it hasn't. The Shop: medicines, the household pages, anything that might help, anything that might hurt. The things in his inventory he's forgotten. The things other people in the house can do. Maomao. Shoko. Rimuru's healing. Frieren's spells. The things he took from the duel and the Trial and the war and everywhere else.

He makes a list of things that might be worth knowing. He makes a list of people who might know them.

He doesn't find anything. Not because there's nothing. Because there's no way to know what to look for without doing the one thing he promised not to do.

He keeps going anyway. He goes through it again. And again.

It doesn't help. He knows it doesn't help. It's just the only thing he knows how to do with a feeling this big: turn it into a problem, and then work the problem until his head hurts more than his chest does.

* * *

Around noon, he feels someone at the door.

Not see. *Feel.* Through the thread that runs out of the deep still place in his chest, where a hand is holding back a sea. There's a pull on it. Small. Hesitant. Like someone standing on the other side of a door with their hand raised to knock, not knocking.

He eases Yuka's fingers out of his shirt. It takes three tries. She doesn't wake. He puts the pillow where he was, and her hand closes on that instead.

He goes out into the hall.

* * *

Makima is standing at the top of the stairs.

Not at the door. Yuka's terms. *Our room is ours. You don't come in.* She's standing as far from it as she can get while still being on this floor, with her hands folded in front of her and her face perfectly composed.

Except it isn't.

He's never seen her face like this. It isn't the calm. It isn't the polite little smile. It's something trying very hard to be the calm and not managing it, the way a lid rattles on a pot.

“I'm sorry,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He doesn't say anything.

“I didn't come to—I'm not here to come in. I know I can't. I'm not asking.” ᴹᵏ

She stops.

“I can feel it,” she says. “Since this morning. Since before you woke up. It's—” ᴹᵏ

She doesn't finish. She looks at her own hands as though they belong to somebody else.

“I don't understand what it is,” she says. “I don't know what's happened. I'm not asking you to tell me. But it's in me. It's been in me all morning. And I don't understand it, and I can't put it down.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

He looks at her.

He thinks about the thread. About everything that comes through it, whether either of them wants it to. About a woman who's spent her whole long life knowing exactly what other people feel from the outside, and using it, and who has never, until a few weeks ago, felt any of it from the inside.

“It's what I feel,” he says. ᴹ

She looks up.

“That's all it is,” he says. “It's mine. You're just—living it. Because you're in there. Because you're holding the rest of me, and this came through with everything else.” ᴹ

“It hurts,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

“It *hurts*, Mau. It doesn't—there's nothing to *do* with it. It doesn't go anywhere. It just—” ᴹᵏ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

He thinks about the four of them.

Noise, who's always been there. Ciel, who sees through him. DIO, whom Noise tells. And Makima, who's holding the sea. Four of them, all feeling what he feels, all living in him or beside him, all carrying a piece of this thing in his chest whether they want to or not.

He can't hug Noise. Noise is everywhere and nowhere. He can't hug Ciel. Ciel is a voice and a presence and a sharp bright watching. DIO is downstairs in her room in the Inn, and he can't go that far right now, he can't leave Yuka that long.

Makima is standing at the top of the stairs.

He crosses the hall and puts his arms round her.

* * *

She goes completely stiff.

He feels it. Every muscle in her body locking at once, the way a cat goes rigid when someone picks it up who's never picked it up before. Her arms stay at her sides. Her breath stops.

He doesn't let go.

He doesn't say anything. He just holds her, the way Yuka held him in the clinic, the way Shoko held him on the floor, the way he held Yuka on the washroom floor three hours ago. He holds her because she's the only one of the four of them he can hold, and because she's standing there not understanding something that's tearing her up, and because she didn't come into the room.

After a long time, her breath starts again.

She doesn't hug him back. Her arms stay at her sides. But something in her shoulders lets go, just slightly, a fraction of an inch. And her forehead comes down, very slowly, until it's resting against his shoulder.

They stand like that at the top of the stairs.

Then he lets go, and steps back.

“Thank you,” he says. “For not coming in.” ᴹ

She looks at him. Her face is doing something he can't read, something that isn't the calm and isn't the lid rattling either. Something new.

She doesn't answer. She just inclines her head, very slightly, and turns, and goes down the stairs.

He watches her go. Then he goes back into the room.

* * *

Yuka wakes in the evening.

She doesn't say much. She lets him help her sit up. She eats a little of the soup Shuna has left outside the door with a note that says *no need to bring the bowl back*. She drinks some water. She looks at the window for a long time.

Once, she says, “You smell like Makima.” ʸ

He goes still.

“I hugged her,” he says. “In the hall. She was—she could feel it. All of it. She didn't understand what it was. So I hugged her.” ᴹ

Yuka is quiet for a while.

“Okay,” she says finally. ʸ

That's all. She doesn't ask anything else. She doesn't say whether it's okay or not. She just leans back against him, and puts her hand flat on his chest, over the place where the thread comes out, and leaves it there.

* * *

She holds his shirt all night.

He doesn't sleep much. He lies there in the dark with her curled against him and her fingers twisted in the fabric over his heart, and goes through it all again in his head. The books. The Shop. The people. Everything he has.

He doesn't find anything. He doesn't stop looking.

Toward dawn, Noise says, very quietly, so as not to wake her: “You can stop.” ᴺ

“I can't.” ᴹ

“I know,” Noise says. “I just wanted you to know you could.” ᴺ

He lies there with that for a long time. Then he closes his eyes, and puts his hand over hers on his shirt, and for an hour or so, until the window goes grey again, he stops.
