# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 8 — It Could Be Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E43
**Continuity state:** B+337. The second day. Yuka cries, blames herself, apologises to Mau, gets angry at herself and at nothing. At one point Mau says the problem could be him; she shouts at him that it isn't. He never tells her what she feels is wrong. He gets her into the bath, washes her hair, feeds her, and she sleeps again. The focus stays on Yuka. No intimate scene.

*When: B+337. The second morning. She wakes before he does again, but this time she doesn't get up. When he opens his eyes she's lying on her side looking at him, and her face is already wet.*

“I'm sorry,” she says. ʸ

It's the first thing she says. Before *good morning*. Before anything. She's lying on her side facing him with her hands tucked under her cheek, and her eyes are red, and she's been awake for a while, he can tell; long enough to have decided what to say first.

“For what?” he asks. ᴹ

“For this. For yesterday. For—” ʸ

She can't find the end of it. She tries a few times.

“For all of it,” she says. ʸ

“You don't have anything to be sorry for.” ᴹ

“I *do*.” ʸ

He doesn't argue. He just reaches across the space between them and puts his hand on her cheek, and she closes her eyes and turns her face into his palm, and starts crying again.

* * *

It comes in waves, all morning.

She cries. Then she stops, and lies very still, looking at the window. Then she says something, and the something starts her crying again. He stays with her through all of it. He doesn't go anywhere. When she cries he holds her. When she's still he lies still with her. When she talks he listens.

“It's my fault,” she says, in one of the still stretches. ʸ

“It's not.” ᴹ

“You keep saying that.” ʸ

“Because it's true.” ᴹ

“You don't *know* that it's true.” ʸ

“I know you,” he says. “I know you didn't do anything. Whatever this is, you didn't make it happen.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment.

“My body did,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't have an answer to that. He doesn't try to make one. He just holds her hand.

* * *

“I'm sorry,” she says again, later. “I'm sorry I made you hope. I shouldn't have told you so fast. I should have waited until I was sure. Last time I waited and it was—and this time I didn't wait, and I made you—you put the *bed* by the *window*, Mau, you—” ʸ

“I wanted to hope,” he says. ᴹ

She stops.

“You didn't make me,” he says. “I wanted to. If you'd waited I'd have been angry. I'd have wanted those two days. I *did* want them. They were good days.” ᴹ

Her face crumples.

“They were good days,” she says, very small. ʸ

“They were very good days.” ᴹ

She puts her face in his chest and holds on and doesn't say anything else for a long time.

* * *

In the late morning, she gets angry.

It comes out of nowhere. One minute she's lying against him, quiet, and the next she's sitting up, pushing the blanket off, her face hot.

“It's not *fair*,” she says. ʸ

He sits up too.

“It's not fair,” she says again, louder. “Everyone—people just *have* them. People who don't even want them. People who don't even *try*. And we tried, we've been trying since the *hand*, and I did everything, I did everything Maomao said and everything Shoko said and I counted and I waited and I didn't drink the island wine and I—and it just—” ʸ

She hits the mattress with her fist. Not hard. Hard enough.

“*Why*,” she says, to nobody. ʸ

He doesn't tell her to calm down. He doesn't tell her it's okay. It isn't okay, and she isn't wrong to be angry, and he isn't going to take that from her.

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“You don't know. You can't know. It's not *your* body that—” ʸ

She stops. She looks at him, stricken.

“I'm sorry,” she says. “I didn't mean—” ʸ

“You can be angry,” he says. ᴹ

She stares at him.

“You can be angry,” he says again. “At me. At it. At whatever. You're allowed.” ᴹ

* * *

That's when he says it.

He doesn't mean to. It's been sitting in him since yesterday, since he went through everything and found nothing and Noise said *some of them are on his side*. It's been sitting in him like a stone. And now she's sitting up in bed, angry and crying and saying *it's not your body*, and it comes out before he can stop it.

“It could be me,” he says. ᴹ

She goes very still.

“It could be,” he says. “We don't know. I'm not—I don't even know what I am. Nobody does. I don't know where I came from or what I'm made of or if I'm—if I'm even the kind of thing that *can*. It could be me. It could be my fault, not yours. You shouldn't be carrying it on your own when it could just as easily be—” ᴹ

“*NO.*” ʸ

It comes out of her so loud that it rings off the walls. Down the hall, he hears Beru shift.

She's on her knees on the bed, facing him, her hands fisted in the front of his shirt.

“*No*,” she says again. “Don't you *dare*. Don't you dare say that. It's not you. It's *not*. You don't get to—you don't get to take this. You don't get to make it yours so I don't have to—” ʸ

She can't finish. She's shaking him. Not hard. Hard enough.

“It's not you,” she says, through her teeth, crying. “It's not you, it's not you, it's *not you*.” ʸ

He doesn't argue.

He puts his arms round her and pulls her in, and she fights it for a second and then stops fighting and collapses against his chest, sobbing, still saying *it's not you* into his shirt until she runs out of breath to say it with.

He holds her. He doesn't say it again.

But he doesn't take it back either. And she knows he doesn't.

* * *

In the afternoon, he gets her into the bath.

She doesn't want to. She doesn't want to do anything. But she's been in bed for a day and a half and she's been crying for most of it, and he can see, in the set of her shoulders and the dullness of her hair, that the bed has stopped being a place to rest and started being a place to sink.

“Just the bath,” he says. “That's all. Then you can come back.” ᴹ

“I don't want to.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I don't want to *move*.” ʸ

“You don't have to. I'll carry you.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“You're very annoying,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

He carries her.

* * *

He runs the water hot, the way she likes it. He sits on the floor beside the tub while she sits in it with her knees pulled up and her arms round them, staring at the water. He doesn't say anything. After a while he takes the jug from the shelf and fills it and asks, “Can I?” ᴹ and she nods without looking at him, and he pours the water slowly over her hair.

He washes it. He's never done that before. He's not good at it. He uses too much of the soap Shuna makes and gets some in her eye and she says *ow* and he says *sorry* and she says *it's fine* in a voice that almost, almost has something in it besides sadness.

He rinses it. Three times, because there's too much soap. He combs it out with his fingers, very carefully, working through the tangles from two days in bed.

She sits very still and lets him.

Halfway through, without turning round, she reaches back and finds his wrist and holds it, and he goes on combing her hair one-handed.

* * *

He feeds her after.

She's back in bed, in a clean shirt of his because she wanted one of his, with her wet hair spread on a towel on the pillow. He sits beside her with a bowl of the soup Shuna left outside the door again, with another note: *Rimuru says you're not to bring the bowl back either. — S.*

“I can feed myself,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

He holds out the spoon anyway.

She looks at him. Then she opens her mouth.

He feeds her the whole bowl. She eats all of it. It's the most she's eaten in two days. When it's done she lies back against the pillow and looks at him with her damp hair and her red eyes and her face that's still wrecked, but less wrecked than it was this morning, and says, “You're really annoying.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You keep saying *I know*.” ʸ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

And something happens at the corner of her mouth. Not a smile. Not yet. The place where a smile would be, if there were one.

She takes his hand and pulls it down onto the blanket over her stomach and holds it there with both of hers, and closes her eyes.

She's asleep in minutes.
