# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 10 — Mai Doesn't Knock

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E44
**Continuity state:** B+338, afternoon. While Yuka sleeps, Mau tells Maki and Yuta in the hall. Then Mai arrives at their door without anyone telling her anything. She sits on the floor and talks: about the workshop, the cloth, nothing. She waits for Yuka to answer her, and talks with Mau while she waits. When Maki and Yuta come in, Yuka tells the three of them herself. Maki, instead of being tough, just hugs her. Mau starts to slip out to give them room; Maki and Yuka both grab him. He says he's going to the bathroom. No intimate scene.

*When: B+338. The afternoon after the eyes. Yuka has slept since. It's the first sleep in three days that doesn't look like hiding.*

He tells Maki and Yuta in the hall.

He doesn't plan it. He comes out of the room to get water, and they're there, both of them, sitting on the floor at the top of the stairs with their backs to the wall, the way they've been sitting on and off for three days. Nobody told them to. They just came.

Yuta looks up when the door opens. Maki doesn't, at first. She's looking at her hands.

He sits down on the floor across from them.

He tells them. Not all of it. Enough. The two weeks. The bathroom door. The treatments. The eyes, and nothing.

Yuta doesn't say anything. He just closes his eyes for a moment, and opens them, and reaches across and puts his hand on Mau's knee.

Maki looks up.

He waits for it. For the hard thing. Maki's way: *of course it wasn't the treatments, did you really think*, or *two months, are you kidding me*, or something sharp and true and meant to make him stand up straighter. She's been doing it to him since the beginning. It's how she loves people.

She doesn't say anything.

She just looks at him, and her face does something he's never seen it do, and then she looks at the door behind him for a long time.

“Is she asleep?” she asks finally. ᴹᵃᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Good,” Maki says. Very quietly. ᴹᵃᵏ

That's all.

* * *

Mai comes an hour later.

Nobody told her. He's almost sure nobody told her. Maki and Yuta went downstairs; Rimuru knows only that Yuka isn't well; nobody else knows anything at all. But an hour after he sits back down on the bed beside Yuka, there's a sound in the hall, and then a soft scrape at the door, like someone sitting down against it.

He waits.

Nothing. No knock. Just the sound of someone settling in, and then a voice through the door, low and dry and patient.

“It's cold in the workshop,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Yuka, beside him, opens her eyes.

* * *

“I didn't light the stove,” Mai's voice goes on, through the door. “Again. I keep forgetting. You usually remind me. You come in and say *Mai, it's freezing* and I say *warm hands get lazy* and you say *my hands aren't lazy, they're cold*. And then I light it. So it's been cold for three days.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka doesn't say anything. She's lying very still, looking at the door.

“I cut the rest of the island cloth,” Mai says. “I didn't mean to. I meant to wait for you. But I needed something to do with my hands. I made a dress. It's for you. It's not finished. It's missing the colour. You have to tell me what colour the thread should be for the hem, because I can't decide, and every time I hold a thread up to it I hear you saying *no, Mai, that's the wrong blue*.” ᴹᵃ

A small sound from Yuka. Not quite a laugh.

“Also,” Mai says, “Senku took his good scissors back. He noticed. He was very upset. He gave me a lecture about borrowing. It was twenty minutes long. You missed it. It was the best thing that's happened all week.” ᴹᵃ

The small sound again. A little louder.

Mai doesn't say anything else for a while. She just sits there, on the other side of the door. He can hear her breathing. Patient. Waiting.

* * *

He gets up after a while and goes to the door.

He doesn't open it. Yuka's terms are Yuka's terms, and Mai has always understood them better than anyone. He sits down on the floor on his side of it, with his back against the wood, so that the two of them are sitting back to back with the door between them.

“She's awake,” he says quietly. ᴹ

“I know,” Mai says. “I heard her laugh. Almost.” ᴹᵃ

“Almost,” he agrees. ᴹ

They sit there.

“How are you?” Mai asks. ᴹᵃ

He doesn't know what to say. Nobody has asked him that. Shoko asked about Yuka. Maki asked if she was asleep. Rimuru asked if they needed anything. Nobody has asked him how *he* is.

“I don't know,” he says. ᴹ

“That's a real answer,” Mai says. “Most people say *fine*.” ᴹᵃ

“I used to say fine.” ᴹ

“I know,” she says. “You were very bad at it.” ᴹᵃ

He laughs. Very quietly. It surprises him.

* * *

They talk for a while like that. Through the door, back to back. Low. About nothing. About the frost-iron from the red gate, which Mai has started making into something she won't describe because she wants it to be a surprise. About Senku's lecture, which she reproduces in part, with the hand gestures, which he can hear even through the door. About Ori, who has been bringing the Pillow up to the top of the stairs every morning and sitting there with it for ten minutes before Frieren comes to take her away.

He doesn't talk about the bathroom door. Mai doesn't ask.

Behind him, he can feel Yuka listening. Not saying anything. Just listening, the way you listen to rain on a roof when you can't sleep.

* * *

“Mai,” Yuka says. ʸ

Her voice is hoarse. Small. It's the first thing she's said to anyone but him in three days.

On the other side of the door, Mai goes very still.

“Yes,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“It's not the wrong blue,” Yuka says. “The thread. For the hem. Use the one that's like—like the inside of a shell. The light part. It's in the third box. The one with the broken lid.” ʸ

A pause.

“The inside of a shell,” Mai repeats. ᴹᵃ

“The light part.” ʸ

“Okay,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Her voice does something on the word. Mau hears it through the door, through his own back. Something catching.

“You can come in,” Yuka says. ʸ

* * *

Mai comes in.

She doesn't rush. She opens the door slowly and steps through it and closes it behind her and stands there, looking at Yuka in the bed. In his shirt. With her hair still a little damp on one side from the bath. With her eyes swollen and her face pale and her hand, still, flat on her stomach.

Mai doesn't say anything. She just comes over and sits down on the floor beside the bed, close enough that Yuka can reach her, and puts her chin on the edge of the mattress, and waits.

A minute later there's a knock on the door. Two knocks, firm, the way Maki knocks.

Yuka looks at Mau. He looks back.

“Them too,” Yuka says. ʸ

* * *

Maki and Yuta come in. Maki first. Yuta behind her. They stand at the foot of the bed.

Yuka looks at the three of them. Mai on the floor with her chin on the mattress. Maki standing with her arms crossed, very straight. Yuta beside her, quiet. The three people in the house who were hers before they were anybody's. Her grandmother. Her grandfather. And the friend who sat in a cold workshop for three days because nobody was there to tell her to light the stove.

She tells them.

She does it herself. He doesn't help. He sits on the edge of the bed with his hand on her back and lets her. She tells them about the counting, and the hallway, and *don't look*, and the bed by the window on her side, and the list. She tells them about the bathroom door. She tells them about the treatments, and the hospital, and being afraid it was her, and the eyes, and nothing.

Her voice breaks four times. She keeps going each time.

When she's done, nobody says anything.

* * *

Maki uncrosses her arms.

He watches her do it. He watches Maki, who has never once in all the time he's known her said a soft thing when a hard one would do, who loves people by making them stand up straighter, who has been sharp and steady and unbreakable through every bad day this house has ever had. He watches her look at Yuka in the bed, small and wrecked and holding her own stomach.

He watches her decide not to say anything at all.

She comes round the side of the bed. She sits down on the edge of it, on Yuka's other side. And she puts her arms round her.

That's all. No words. No *two months is nothing*. No *you'll be fine*. Just Maki, holding her granddaughter, with her chin on top of Yuka's head and her eyes closed and her jaw tight.

Yuka makes a small sound and turns her face into Maki's shoulder and holds on.

* * *

He starts to get up.

Quietly. This isn't his. This is theirs. Maki and Yuta and Mai and Yuka, the four of them, the ones who came from the same place, or near enough. He'll go downstairs. He'll get more water. He'll give them the room.

He's halfway to standing when two hands grab him.

One is Yuka's. It shoots out from between her and Maki and closes on his wrist without her even lifting her face from Maki's shoulder.

The other is Maki's. It lets go of Yuka's back for exactly as long as it takes to grab the back of his shirt, and then goes back.

He stops. Half standing. Held in place by two women who aren't even looking at him.

“Where are you going,” Maki says. Not a question. ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks at the hand on his wrist. At the hand on his shirt. At Mai on the floor, who has put her face down on the mattress and is shaking with something that might be laughter or might not. At Yuta, at the foot of the bed, who is looking at the ceiling very carefully.

“Bathroom,” Mau says. ᴹ

There's a pause.

On the floor, Mai's shoulders shake against the mattress. Yuta keeps looking at the ceiling with enormous concentration.

Yuka doesn't laugh. Not yet. But her hand tightens on his wrist, hard, the way it tightened on his shirt on the washroom floor, and doesn't let go.

Maki's mouth twitches.

“You can go to the bathroom later,” she says. ᴹᵃᵏ

He sits back down.

Neither of them lets go.
