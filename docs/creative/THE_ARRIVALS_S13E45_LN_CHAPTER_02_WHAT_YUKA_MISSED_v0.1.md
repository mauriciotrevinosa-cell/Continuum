# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 12 — What Yuka Missed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S13E45
**Continuity state:** B+342. Frieren lets Ori visit. Ori sits on the bed with the Pillow's nose on the blanket and Lantern sulking in her shadow, and tells Yuka everything the Fort and the house did while she was in bed: the Fort's treaty with the Pillow, Milim's attempt to ride it, Senku's lecture about scissors (again), Umaru's tournament rules, Beru's hat. Yuka laughs, really laughs, for the first time. Then she asks Ori to take her to see what she missed, and Ori does. Mau, left behind on purpose, goes to the workshop and talks with Mai. No intimate scene.

*When: The seventh day. Frieren knocks on the door herself, which she has never done, and stands aside to let a small person through.*

Ori comes in very carefully.

She has clearly been told a great many things on the way up the stairs. Mau can see them on her face: *don't jump on the bed, don't be loud, don't ask her why she's sad, don't stay too long*. She stops just inside the door, holding the edge of it, and looks at Yuka sitting up against the pillows in Mau's shirt with the blanket round her.

Behind her, filling most of the doorway, the Pillow.

It ambles in past her without being asked. It walks across the room to the bed with its slow rolling gait and puts its long nose on the blanket beside Yuka's knee, and leaves it there, and groans, very softly, like a door that wants oiling.

Yuka looks at it.

“Hi,” she says to the Pillow. ʸ

The Pillow opens one small dark eye and looks back.

* * *

“Can I come in?” Ori asks. ᴼ

She's still holding the door.

“You're already in,” Yuka says. ʸ

“My *body* is in. I'm asking if I can come *in* in.” ᴼ

Yuka looks at her for a long moment. Something happens at the corner of her mouth.

“Yes,” she says. “Come in in.” ʸ

Ori lets go of the door. She crosses the room very properly, the way she's been told, and climbs up onto the end of the bed, and sits cross-legged on the blanket, facing Yuka, with the Pillow's nose between them.

In her shadow, something stirs. A curve of scale. A bright lamp-like eye, looking resentfully at the Pillow's nose.

“Lantern's still sulking,” Ori explains. “About the Pillow. It's been a whole week.” ᴼ

* * *

Mau is in the chair by the window. He doesn't say anything. He's decided not to say anything. This is Ori's.

“What did I miss?” Yuka asks. ʸ

And Ori, who has clearly been waiting a week to be asked exactly that, takes a deep breath.

* * *

“Okay,” she says. “So.” ᴼ

“So.” ʸ

“So the Fort had a meeting. About the Pillow. Because it keeps lying in front of the fire and nobody can get to the fire. And Kenya said it should have to move, and Alice said it was there first, which isn't true, it was only there for a week, and Chloe said that it didn't matter who was first because it was *bigger*, and Ryota cried because he wanted to sit on it, and Taihei said—” ᴼ

“What did Taihei say?” ʸ

“Taihei said we should make a *treaty*.” ᴼ

“A treaty.” ʸ

“With the Pillow. So now the Pillow gets the fire in the morning and the evening, and we get it in the afternoon, and it has to move its nose if someone needs to put a log on, and in return we give it a pear every day. Rimuru wrote it down. The Pillow put its nose on it. That means it signed.” ᴼ

Yuka is looking at her with her mouth slightly open.

“The Pillow signed a treaty,” she says. ʸ

“With its *nose*.” ᴼ

* * *

“And then,” Ori says, “Milim tried to ride it.” ᴼ

“Oh no.” ʸ

“She got on it in the yard. She said she was going to ride it to the orchard. And the Pillow just—” Ori demonstrates with her whole body: a slow, enormous, utterly unbothered sag. “—lay *down*. With her still on it. And went to *sleep*. And she couldn't get off because her legs were stuck under its fur on both sides and it was too heavy to push. She was stuck there for an *hour*.” ᴼ

Yuka puts her hand over her mouth.

“Nobody helped her because everyone was laughing too hard. Gojo took a picture. Umaru made a sign. And when the Pillow finally woke up it just stood up and walked to the fire with her still on it, and she said that *counted*, she *rode* it, and Shoko said she didn't, she was *luggage*.” ᴼ

Yuka's shoulders are shaking.

* * *

“And Senku gave Mai *another* lecture about the scissors,” Ori goes on, unstoppable now. “Because she took them *again*. It was even longer than the first one. He made a *diagram*. Of the scissors. With arrows. And Mai fell asleep in the middle of it and he didn't notice for ten minutes.” ᴼ

Yuka makes a sound into her hand.

“And Umaru made new rules for the tournament,” Ori says. “Nobody's allowed to use Authority. And nobody's allowed to cover anyone's eyes. And nobody's allowed to *tickle*. She put them on a sign. The sign has a drawing of Dad on it with a line through him.” ᴼ

Mau, in the chair by the window, closes his eyes.

“And Beru,” Ori says. ᴼ

She pauses for effect. She's learned that from Gojo.

“Beru,” she says, “got a *second hat*.” ᴼ

“A second—” ʸ

“For the *rain*. Mai made it. It has a *brim*. He wears it over the first hat. *Both at once*. He says the first hat is too important to get wet.” ᴼ

* * *

And Yuka laughs.

It comes out of her all at once, the way the crying came out on the washroom floor: as if something that's been holding it in has suddenly given way. She bends forward over her knees with her hand still over her mouth and laughs, really laughs, with her whole body, until there are tears running down her face for an entirely different reason, until the Pillow lifts its head off the blanket in alarm and Lantern pokes his whole snout out of Ori's shadow to see what's happening.

Ori watches her, very serious, very still.

Then Ori starts laughing too.

Mau, in the chair by the window, doesn't laugh. He can't. He's got his hand over his own mouth, and he's looking at the ceiling, and he's breathing very carefully through his nose, because it's been seven days and she's *laughing*, and he doesn't trust his face.

* * *

When she can breathe again, Yuka wipes her eyes on the sleeve of his shirt.

She looks at Ori for a long moment. At the small serious person sitting cross-legged on the end of her bed with a monster's nose in her lap and a dragon sulking in her shadow.

“Two hats,” Yuka says. ʸ

“*Two hats*,” Ori confirms. ᴼ

“I have to see that.” ʸ

Ori goes very still.

“You want to see it?” ᴼ

“I want to see all of it,” Yuka says. “The treaty. The sign. The hats. Milim's picture. I want you to show me everything I missed.” ʸ

Ori looks at her. Then at Mau, by the window. Then back at Yuka.

“Now?” she asks. ᴼ

Yuka pushes the blanket off her knees.

“Now,” she says. ʸ

* * *

She stands up.

She's unsteady. Seven days in bed. She puts her hand on the bedpost and her knees wobble a little, and Mau is half out of the chair before he's thought about it, and she looks at him across the room and shakes her head.

“I've got her,” Ori says. ᴼ

She hops off the bed and takes Yuka's hand. Small hand in bigger one. And the Pillow gets up too and comes round to Yuka's other side and leans against her, very slightly, the way it leaned against Mau in the red gate for warmth, so that she has something to steady herself on.

Yuka looks down at it.

“Thank you,” she says to the Pillow. ʸ

The Pillow groans.

At the door she stops. She looks back at Mau, still standing by the chair.

“You're not coming,” she says. ʸ

It isn't a question. It's a decision. He can see her making it, right there in the doorway: that she's going to go downstairs, for the first time in a week, with Ori, and see what she missed, and that she wants to do it without him holding her up.

And he can see what it costs her. He can see her want to say *come with me* and not say it.

“I'll be in the workshop,” he says. ᴹ

She nods.

“Don't go far,” she says. ʸ

“I won't.” ᴹ

She lets Ori lead her out of the room. He hears them on the stairs. He hears the third one creak under Ori's foot, and then, a moment later, under Yuka's.

* * *

He goes to the workshop.

Mai is there. Of course she is. The stove is lit. That's the first thing he notices. For the first time in a week, the stove is lit, and the workshop is warm, and Mai is sitting at her bench in her shirtsleeves with a length of island cloth across her knees and a needle in her hand and a spool of pale thread beside her: the light part, the inside of a shell.

She looks up when he comes in.

She looks at his face for a long time.

“She laughed,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“She laughed.” ᴹ

“I heard it. All the way down here. Through two floors.” ᴹᵃ

He sits down on the stool beside her. Hard. All at once. As if his legs have just decided they're done.

Mai doesn't say anything. She just reaches over without putting down the needle and puts her hand on his back, between his shoulder blades, and leaves it there.

“Tell me about the hats,” he says, after a while. His voice comes out strange. ᴹ

“Which hats?” ᴹᵃ

“Beru's. Ori says there are two now.” ᴹ

Mai's mouth twitches.

“There are two now,” she confirms. ᴹᵃ

And she tells him. She tells him about the rain hat, and the brim, and Beru's absolute refusal to take the first one off even to try the second one on, and the hour they spent working out how to fit one over the other with room for the antennae. She tells him about Senku's diagram. She tells him about Milim stuck under the Pillow, and Gojo's picture, and Umaru's sign with Mau's face crossed out.

He listens. He laughs, sometimes. Once he has to put his hand over his eyes.

Through the window, across the yard, he can see the common room. Through its window, he can see a small figure leading a bigger one toward the fire, where a shaggy shape is lying down and a scaled one is pretending not to notice. He can see the bigger figure stop in front of a sign on the wall. He can see her bend over, and put her hands on her knees, and shake.

“She's looking at the sign,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“She's looking at the sign,” he agrees. ᴹ

They sit there in the warm workshop and watch her laugh through two windows, and neither of them says anything else for a long time.
