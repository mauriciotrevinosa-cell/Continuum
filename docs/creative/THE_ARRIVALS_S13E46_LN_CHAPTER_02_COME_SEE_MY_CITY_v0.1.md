# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 14 — Come See My City

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S13E46
**Continuity state:** B+345. Elmesia's reply arrives. She'll gladly have the house at the island, but she wants to see Mau and Yuka first: to talk, and to show them her city, which in all the time they've known each other they've never seen. She asks them to stay a few days, and asks whether DIO, who can pass through Mau's protection, would come too. DIO is thrilled. Makima asks to come. Yuka: she's part of the little group that lives inside Mau, so she goes because she goes, but that doesn't mean Yuka wants to be touched. Yuka and Makima go over the terms; Makima explains Mau can change the contract, and Yuka's terms hold because Makima accepts Yuka is his wife. Yuka can change them if she wants; Makima would give her opinion. Yuka doesn't want to change them yet. Makima: okay.

*When: B+345. Two days after the bird. The same bird. It lands on the porch rail in the middle of the morning and looks at everyone present as though it has been personally inconvenienced by all of them.*

It's the same bird.

Mau knows it the moment it lands. The big dark one with the band on its leg and the expression of a creature that has been sitting in the rain for three days and has not forgotten. It lands on the porch rail beside where he and Yuka are sitting, and folds its wings, and looks at him.

He looks back.

“It remembers you,” Yuka says. ʸ

“It definitely remembers me.” ᴹ

The bird holds out its leg. There's a letter tied to it. It doesn't take its eyes off Mau while he unties it. When he's done, it doesn't fly away. It just sits there on the rail, watching him, like a creditor.

“I think it wants something,” Yuka says. ʸ

He opens the Shop and buys the most expensive fruit on the snack page and holds it out. The bird looks at the fruit. Looks at him. Takes it, with great dignity, and eats it in three bites, and then flies away without a backward glance.

“I think that was an apology,” Yuka says. ʸ

“From who?” ᴹ

“From you. For making it wait.” ʸ

* * *

She reads it aloud. She's in his lap. She's always in his lap now; he's stopped noticing when she climbs on, the way you stop noticing your heartbeat.

“*Dear Mau and Yuka*,” she reads. ʸ

Elmesia's handwriting is beautiful. Long, looping, perfectly even, the kind of hand that has been writing letters to kings for longer than most kingdoms have existed.

“*I received your letter. The bird received your fruit, eventually. It has asked me to tell you that it does not forgive you, but I am told by the keeper of my aviary that it has never once in its life eaten fruit from anyone but me, so I believe it is lying.*” ʸ

Mau laughs.

“*You may bring some people,*” Yuka reads on. “*I note the word 'some'. I have been reading letters for a very long time, and I know what 'some' means. It means 'everyone'. You may bring everyone. The hotel has more rooms than you think, and fewer than you will need, and I will make it work.*” ʸ

“She knows,” Mau says. ᴹ

“She knows,” Yuka agrees, delighted. ʸ

* * *

“*But before that*,” Yuka reads, and her voice changes slightly, “*I would like to see the two of you. Just the two of you. First.*” ʸ

She looks up at him. He looks back.

“*In all the time we have known each other,*” she reads, slowly, “*you have never seen my city. You have seen my hotel, and my island, and my table by the window, and once my anger in a parliament, which I regret you had to see. But you have never seen where I live. Where I come from. Where I am, when I am not on holiday being charming to the two of you.*” ʸ

She stops reading for a moment.

“*I would like to show it to you,*” she reads. “*Before the island. Before everyone. Come and stay a few days. Let me walk you through the streets. Let me show you the gardens and the towers and the place where the old library used to be before I had it moved. Let me be your host for once in a place that is actually mine. And then we will talk about the island, and the 'some' people, and how many rooms I need to find.*” ʸ

Yuka lowers the letter.

“She wants to show us her city,” she says. ʸ

“She wants to show us her city,” he agrees. ᴹ

“We've never seen it.” ʸ

“Never.” ᴹ

Something is happening in Yuka's face. Something bright and a little bit stunned, the way she looked on the boat the first time they saw the island come up out of the haze.

“I want to go,” she says. ʸ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

“*One more thing*,” Yuka reads. “*DIO. Would she come? I understand that she can pass through your protection, Mau, and that she prefers to stay near you. I would very much like her to see my city too. We have had many conversations about building things. I would like to show her something I built.*” ʸ

He doesn't have to answer that. He can feel it already, through the place in him where DIO's attention lives: a sudden bright wash of excitement, like a child who's been told about a trip to the sea.

“She wants to come,” he says. ᴹ

“How do you know?” ʸ

“She's—” He searches for the word. “She's *bouncing*.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him. Then she laughs, and puts her face in his shoulder.

“DIO's bouncing,” she says. ʸ

“DIO is very excited about the city.” ᴹ

* * *

Makima asks that evening.

She finds them on the porch after supper. She stops at the bottom of the steps, the way she always does now, and doesn't come up.

“I heard there's a trip,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka, in Mau's lap, looks at her.

“To Elmesia's city,” Makima says. “Before the island. I heard DIO is going.” ᴹᵏ

“DIO is going,” Yuka agrees. ʸ

Makima is quiet for a moment.

“I'd like to ask if I can go too,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka doesn't answer right away.

Mau doesn't say anything. This is Yuka's. He's learned that; she's made sure he's learned it. Where Makima is concerned, Yuka decides, and he stands behind whatever she decides.

Yuka looks at Makima for a long time.

“You're part of the little group,” she says finally. ʸ

Makima tilts her head.

“The little group,” Yuka says, “that lives inside Mau. Noise and Ciel and DIO and you. Wherever he goes, all of you go. You can't really not.” ʸ

“No,” Makima agrees. “I can't be very far from him.” ᴹᵏ

“So you're going,” Yuka says. “Because you're going. Because he's going. That's not really a question.” ʸ

She pauses.

“But that doesn't mean I want you to touch me,” she says. ʸ

Makima doesn't flinch. She inclines her head.

“I know,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

“Can I ask you something?” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima waits.

“The terms,” Yuka says. “The ones I set. In Mai's workshop. My room. Not touching me. Asking before you sit. Can those change?” ʸ

Mau goes very still.

Makima looks at Yuka for a long moment with those calm, ringed eyes.

“Yes,” she says. “They can change.” ᴹᵏ

“How?” ʸ

Makima considers the question with the seriousness she gives to anything that's actually a question.

“The contract I made is with him,” she says, and nods slightly toward Mau. “The one that binds me to his body. That one, he could change, if he chose to. It's his. He set the terms of it, in the night, with you holding his head.” ᴹᵏ

“And mine?” ʸ

“Yours aren't a contract,” Makima says. “Not in that way. I keep them because of something else.” ᴹᵏ

“What?” ʸ

Makima looks at her very steadily.

“Because you're his wife,” she says. “I accepted that. When I went in. When I felt—what I felt. I accepted that you are his wife, and that what you ask for, where he's concerned, I respect. That's why I keep your terms. Not because anything makes me. Because I accepted you.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka doesn't say anything for a long time.

Mau can feel her thinking. Her hand is flat on his chest and she's very still and he can feel her heartbeat, slower than usual, the way it goes when she's turning something over very carefully.

“So if I wanted to change them,” she says. ʸ

“You could,” Makima says. “Whenever you wanted. You'd tell me. I might have an opinion. I'd tell you what it was, if you asked. But they're yours. You'd decide.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka nods slowly.

“Okay,” she says. “I don't want to change them. Not now.” ʸ

“Okay,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

That's all. No pressure. No *maybe someday*. Makima just inclines her head, as if Yuka has told her the weather, and turns to go.

At the bottom of the steps she stops.

“Thank you for letting me come,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I didn't let you,” Yuka says. “You were coming anyway.” ʸ

Makima looks back at her. Something goes across her face, very briefly. Not the calm. Something that might, in a different person, have been the beginning of a smile.

“Thank you anyway,” she says, and goes. ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka is quiet for a long time after.

“You didn't say anything,” she says eventually. ʸ

“It wasn't mine to say.” ᴹ

“You could have.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

She settles back against his chest.

“She said *because I accepted you*,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I heard.” ᴹ

“She doesn't say things like that.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

Yuka is quiet a while longer. Down in the yard, the light is going long and gold across the grass; he can see it on her hands where they rest on his chest, the only place the gold shows.

“I still don't want her to touch me,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“But I'm glad she said it,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't answer. He just puts his arms round her, and she pulls them tighter, and they sit on the porch steps and watch the evening come down over the orchard, and somewhere inside him, very far away, DIO is still bouncing.
