# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 15 — Has Gojo Behaved

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S13E47
**Continuity state:** B+346. At breakfast, the island list. Mau remembers Shoko's condition: Gojo goes if he behaves. He calls Shoko over, whispers in her ear; they sit together with a notebook and begin questioning Gojo on his conduct. Before Gojo can give his speech, Shoko answers every question herself. Then Yuka reveals that she's been keeping her own count of things. Frieren asks, quietly, to go: she wants to meet Mau's opponent, the bird, which she calls the enemy of the state. Ori, beside her, is enormously proud, and the table realises who gave the bird its title. Yuka stays in Mau's lap through all of it.

*When: The next morning. The bird's letter is pinned to the wall of the common room under Umaru's tournament sign, and everyone has read it, and everyone has noticed the word "everyone".*

The house has read the letter.

Of course it has. Yuka pinned it to the common-room wall last night, under Umaru's sign with Mau's face crossed out, so that nobody could say they hadn't been told. By breakfast, every single person in the house has read it at least twice, and the kitchen is louder than it's been in two weeks.

“*Everyone*,” Milim keeps saying. “She said *everyone*. That means me.” ᴹⁱ

“It means everyone,” Umaru agrees. ᵁ

“So it means me.” ᴹⁱ

“It means you, Milim.” ᵁ

“I *know* it means me. I'm just saying it out loud so it's *official*.” ᴹⁱ

* * *

Mau is at the end of the bench. Yuka is in his lap. She's eating her breakfast in his lap, which she has never done at the table before, and nobody has said a word about it, and Gojo has made four marks in his notebook since she sat down.

He's watching Gojo.

He's been thinking about it since last night. About the clinic, a lifetime ago, before the red gate and the overload and the bathroom door. About Shoko sitting by the window turning a cigarette over in her fingers, and saying *if he behaves*. *If he behaves perfectly.*

He catches Shoko's eye across the table. He tilts his head, very slightly. *Come here.*

She looks at him suspiciously. Then she gets up, and comes round the table, and leans down.

He whispers in her ear.

Shoko straightens up. She looks at him. She looks at Gojo, across the table, who is in the middle of explaining to Senku why the island's turtles are definitely a native species.

A slow smile spreads across Shoko's face.

* * *

She sits down beside Mau. Yuka scoots over in his lap to make room. Shoko takes a small notebook out of her coat pocket, the one she uses for medicine, and a pen, and opens to a fresh page, and clears her throat.

The kitchen goes quiet.

“Satoru,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

Gojo stops mid-sentence. He turns his blindfolded face toward her with the slow wariness of a man who has heard his first name in that voice before.

“Yes?” he says. ᴳ

“Mau and I,” Shoko says, “have some questions. About the island.” ˢʰ

Gojo looks at Mau. Mau looks back, perfectly serious.

“Regarding,” says Mau, “your conduct.” ᴹ

* * *

“First question,” Shoko says, pen poised. ˢʰ

Gojo opens his mouth. He's clearly been preparing for this. He has the look of a man who's been preparing a speech since the moment he heard the word *island* a week ago at the dinner table, and has refined it every night since.

“In the past month,” Mau says, “has the candidate eaten more than his share of anything in the kitchen without asking?” ᴹ

“Now that's a matter of—” ᴳ

“Yes,” says Shoko, writing. “Twice. The chocolate on chocolate day. And Rem's cake.” ˢʰ

Gojo closes his mouth.

“Second question,” Mau says. “Has the candidate made any comments about little pools in a voice?” ᴹ

“I haven't even *seen* the little—” ᴳ

“Yes,” says Shoko. “Three times. Once at supper. Once to Ori. Once to *himself*, in the yard, when he thought nobody was listening.” ˢʰ

“You were *listening*?” ᴳ

“I'm always listening, Satoru.” ˢʰ

Down the table, Maki has put her face in her hands.

* * *

“Third question,” Mau says. “Has the candidate been kind to anyone who needed it?” ᴹ

The kitchen goes very quiet.

Gojo opens his mouth, and this time he doesn't say anything. He just sits there.

Shoko looks at the notebook for a long time.

“Yes,” she says. ˢʰ

She doesn't write it down. She just says it, very quietly, not looking at him.

“He went to the top of the stairs every day,” Shoko says. “Last week. He didn't go in. He didn't say anything. He just stood there for a few minutes. Every day. And then went away.” ˢʰ

Gojo has gone very still.

“And he stopped counting,” Shoko says. “The tally. For seven days. He didn't make a single mark.” ˢʰ

Nobody says anything.

Yuka, in Mau's lap, has turned to look at Gojo. She's looking at him with an expression Mau has never seen her give him before.

Gojo doesn't look back. He looks at the table.

“It wasn't anything,” he says. ᴳ

“It was something,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

* * *

Then Yuka says, “I've been counting too.” ʸ

Everyone turns.

She has her own notebook. Mau didn't know she had a notebook. She takes it out of the pocket of his shirt, which she is still wearing and which she is never going to give back, and opens it, and clears her throat exactly the way Shoko did.

“Since the island,” she says, “I've been keeping my own tally.” ʸ

“Of what?” Gojo asks warily. ᴳ

“Of every time *you* make a mark in *your* tally.” ʸ

The kitchen bursts.

“It's at a hundred and twelve,” Yuka announces, over the noise. “Not counting today. A hundred and twelve marks. In a month. Which means you've been watching us a hundred and twelve times. Which means *you're* the one who can't stop looking.” ʸ

Gojo stares at her.

“That's not—that's a completely different—” ᴳ

“A hundred and *twelve*, Gojo.” ʸ

“I'm *observing*—” ᴳ

“You're *obsessed*,” says Yuka, and Shoko laughs so hard she has to put the notebook down. ʸ

* * *

“Verdict,” Mau says, when the kitchen has calmed down enough to hear him. ᴹ

Shoko wipes her eyes. She picks the notebook back up. She looks at Gojo across the table for a long moment, the way she looks at a patient whose results have come back better than expected and whom she still doesn't entirely trust.

“He can come,” she says. ˢʰ

Gojo lets out a breath.

“*If*,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

Gojo stops breathing again.

“If he doesn't say *little pool* in *that voice* once. Not *once*. The entire trip.” ˢʰ

“I can do that,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“You can't,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“I *can*.” ᴳ

“You *can't*, Satoru. You physically can't. It's going to come out of you on the first day and you won't be able to stop it.” ˢʰ

Gojo puts his hand over his heart with tremendous solemnity.

“Watch me,” he says. ᴳ

Yuka writes something in her notebook.

“What are you writing?” Gojo demands. ᴳ

“A new tally,” Yuka says sweetly. ʸ

* * *

It's Frieren who goes quiet first.

She's at the far end of the bench, where she always sits, with her book open and her tea gone cold. She hasn't said anything all through breakfast. She doesn't usually. But Mau, who has known her longer than anyone in this house, notices when she puts the book down.

She doesn't make a speech. She doesn't campaign. She just says, into a small lull in the noise, “I'd like to go too.” ᶠ

The table goes quiet. Everyone looks at her.

“To the island,” Frieren says. ᶠ

“Of course you're coming,” Yuka says at once. “You don't have to ask. You're—of *course* you're coming.” ʸ

Frieren nods, as though this settles something.

“Good,” she says. “I'd like to meet him.” ᶠ

“Meet who?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Frieren looks at him with her old, patient, completely serious eyes.

“Your opponent,” she says. ᶠ

Mau blinks.

“The bird,” Frieren says. “The one that stole your fruit for thirteen days and then stole from your wife. I would like to see it for myself.” ᶠ

She pauses.

“The enemy of the state,” she says. ᶠ

* * *

There's a short silence.

“The *what*?” Mau says. ᴹ

“The enemy of the state,” Frieren repeats calmly. “That's what it's called. Isn't it?” ᶠ

Beside her, Ori has gone very still and is looking very hard at her porridge.

Mau looks at Ori.

Yuka looks at Ori.

The whole table, one by one, turns and looks at Ori.

Ori looks up from her porridge. Her ears have gone hot and her eyes are shining and she is clearly trying very, very hard not to look proud of herself, and failing completely.

“I said it,” she says. “When you were gone. When the letter came that said it stole from Yuka. I said it was the *enemy of the state*. Because it stole from the *queen*.” ᴼ

“And I liked it,” Frieren says, “so I kept it.” ᶠ

* * *

Yuka puts her face in Mau's shoulder.

He feels her shaking. He feels himself start too. He tries to stop it and can't. Across the table Gojo has started laughing, and Milim, and Shoko, and Rimuru, and Maki with her face in her hands, and Beru in the doorway with both his hats on making a sound that might, in an ant, be a laugh.

Ori sits beside Frieren, very hot in the face, very proud, swinging her legs.

“It *is* the enemy of the state,” she says, to nobody in particular, with great dignity. ᴼ

“It is,” Frieren agrees, and goes back to her book. ᶠ

* * *

That night, in bed, Yuka is still laughing about it.

She's lying on him. She's always lying on him now. Her chin on his chest, looking up at him in the dark, and every few minutes she remembers something — Gojo's face at *a hundred and twelve*, Frieren saying *your opponent*, Ori's *because it stole from the queen* — and starts laughing again, quietly, so that he can feel it all through him.

“The enemy of the state,” she says. ʸ

“The enemy of the state,” he agrees. ᴹ

“She called me the *queen*.” ʸ

“Beru calls you the queen.” ᴹ

“Ori never called me the queen before.” ʸ

He doesn't say anything. He puts his hand in her hair.

“A week ago,” she says, very quietly, after a while, “I didn't think I'd laugh again.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“And today I laughed so hard I almost fell off you.” ʸ

“You did fall off me. Briefly.” ᴹ

“I climbed back.” ʸ

“You did,” he says. ᴹ

She lies there a while longer. Then she says, so softly he almost doesn't hear it, “Thank you for not letting go.” ʸ

He doesn't answer. He just holds on tighter.

She lifts her head and kisses him goodnight. It's brief; afterward she stays close enough that their foreheads touch.

“I'm not going anywhere,” he murmurs. ᴹ

“You'd better not.” ʸ

She says it with her eyes already closing. He smiles against her forehead, and she settles back onto his chest.

She shifts until her ear rests over his heart, takes his hand and draws it under her cheek. He stays awake long enough to feel her breathing settle. She falls asleep on him. He lets her.
