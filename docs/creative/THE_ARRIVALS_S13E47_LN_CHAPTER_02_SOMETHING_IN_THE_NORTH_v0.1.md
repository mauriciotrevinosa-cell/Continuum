# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 16 — Something in the North

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S13E47
**Continuity state:** B+347–348. Packing for Thalion: Yuka choosing clothes and making Mau carry them in the inventory, DIO bouncing, Makima quiet. Hae-In mentions to Yuka that Jin-Woo has been having nightmares again. On the second day, Jin-Woo feels something, leaves to follow it, and comes back at dusk. He tells Mau, Yuta and Gojo: presences in the far north hills, old ones, ones he knows. Yuta and Gojo will stay near the house in case. Jin-Woo asks Mau to come with him. Mau tells Yuka all of it. They decide: Yuka goes to Elmesia on Kaisel with DIO, as planned; Mau goes with Jin-Woo and joins her when he can. Makima, who can't be far from Mau, stays with him.

*When: B+347–348. The two days before they were meant to leave. The bird has been and gone again, with a reply saying they're expected in three days.*

“The blue one,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I can't tell which one is the blue one.” ᴹ

“The one on the left.” ʸ

He picks up the one on the left. She looks at it. She looks at him holding it. She shakes her head.

“No. The other blue one.” ʸ

“There are two blue ones?” ᴹ

“There are *four* blue ones,” Yuka says, as though this is something he should have known, and holds out her hand. ʸ

* * *

They're packing.

Or she's packing and he's the inventory. She's standing in front of their wardrobe with every piece of clothing she owns spread across the bed, the chair, the floor and him, picking things up and holding them against herself and putting them down again. Every so often she hands him something and says *that one*, and he opens the inventory and puts it in, and she says *wait, no, take it out*, and he takes it out.

“You could just bring all of it,” he says. ᴹ

“I can't bring *all* of it.” ʸ

“It doesn't weigh anything. It's in the inventory.” ᴹ

“That's not the *point*, Mau. The point is *choosing*.” ʸ

He doesn't understand the point. He holds out his hands for the next thing.

Inside him, very far away, DIO is bouncing again. She's been bouncing for two days, ever since the bird came back with Elmesia's second letter, the one that said *three days* and drew a little map. He can feel it the way you feel a child kicking the back of your seat on a long drive. Constant. Delighted. Mildly exhausting.

“Should I bring a hat?” DIO asks him, from inside. ᴰ

“For Thalion?” ᴹ

“For the sun. The letter said gardens. Gardens have sun.” ᴰ

“Then bring a hat.” ᴹ

“I don't have one,” says DIO. “I'm going to make one. For the trip.” ᴰ

He decides not to ask how.

* * *

Makima is quiet.

She comes to the bottom of the porch steps in the afternoon the way she does every day, and asks if she can sit, and sits. She doesn't say much. She doesn't pack; she doesn't have anything to pack. She just sits on the bottom step and watches the yard, and every so often Mau feels something come through the thread from her that isn't quite excitement and isn't quite nerves.

“You've never travelled,” he says. ᴹ

She considers this.

“I've travelled a great deal,” she says. “I've never gone anywhere to *see* it.” ᴹᵏ

He thinks about that. About a woman who's spent her whole long life going places to use them.

“You'll like the gardens,” he says. ᴹ

“You haven't seen the gardens.” ᴹᵏ

“Elmesia drew them on the map. She drew a lot of gardens.” ᴹ

Makima looks at him. Something happens at the corner of her mouth.

“I'll tell you if I like them,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

Hae-In comes up the orchard path in the evening with a basket.

She's brought bread. Jin-Woo's mother made it, for the trip. She gives it to Yuka on the porch, where Yuka is sitting in Mau's lap with a list of gardens from Elmesia's map, and stays for a while on the step below them, and they talk about the city and the gardens and whether Elmesia's hotel will have enough rooms for *everyone*.

It's near the end, when the light is going, that Hae-In says it.

“He's been having them again,” Hae-In says. Quietly. Not quite to Yuka. ᴴᶦ

“Having what?” ʸ

A pause.

“Nightmares,” Hae-In says. “He doesn't tell me. He thinks I don't notice. But he wakes up at the same time every night and goes and stands at the window and looks north.” ᴴᶦ

Yuka doesn't say anything.

“He did it before,” Hae-In says. “In our world. Before—a long time ago. When things were coming.” ᴴᶦ

* * *

Jin-Woo leaves on the second morning.

Mau sees him go. He's on the porch with his tea and Yuka is upstairs choosing between two of the four blue things, and Jin-Woo comes up the orchard path at a walk and stops at the bottom of the steps and stands there for a moment, looking north.

Then he looks at Mau.

“I'm going to check something,” he says. ᴶʷ

“What?” ᴹ

“I don't know yet.” ᴶʷ

He doesn't say anything else. He turns and walks away north, past the yard and the training posts and the edge of the orchard, and then his own shadow rises around him and he's gone.

Mau sits there with his tea going cold.

“He felt something,” Ciel says quietly. ᶜ

“Did you?” ᴹ

A pause. “No,” Ciel says. “Nothing I can see. But he's been looking north for days. Hae-In wasn't wrong.” ᶜ

* * *

Jin-Woo comes back at dusk.

He doesn't come to the porch. He sends a shadow to the yard, a small one, one of his, which stands by the training posts and waits until Mau comes out. Then it takes Mau to the clinic, where Jin-Woo is sitting on the edge of Shoko's examination table with Yuta and Gojo already there, Yuta leaning against the wall, Gojo sitting backwards on Shoko's chair.

Shoko isn't there. Mau suspects Jin-Woo asked her to step out.

“Close the door,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

Mau closes the door.

* * *

“There's something in the north,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

He's very calm. Too calm. It's the calm Mau has seen on him only a few times, all of them right before something very bad.

“Past the hills,” he says. “A long way past. Where nobody lives. I felt it a few days ago. I thought I was imagining it. Then it got stronger.” ᴶʷ

“What is it?” Yuta asks quietly. ʸᵗ

Jin-Woo doesn't answer right away.

“Presences,” he says. “More than one. Old ones. Older than anything in this valley except the elf and Ashborn.” ᴶʷ

He looks at his hands.

“I know them,” he says. ᴶʷ

The clinic is very quiet.

“From your world,” Gojo says. Not a question. ᴳ

“From my world.” ᴶʷ

“How many?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“I don't know. I couldn't get close enough to count without them feeling me. Several. Maybe more.” ᴶʷ

He looks up.

“They shouldn't be here,” he says. “They shouldn't be *anywhere*. I killed them.” ᴶʷ

* * *

Nobody says anything for a long time.

“What do you want to do?” Gojo asks finally. ᴳ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

“Find out what they want,” he says. “Before they come here. My family is here. Everyone's family is here. The house has the protection, but the roads don't. The village doesn't, past the line. I want to know what they're doing before they do it.” ᴶʷ

“Then we stay,” Gojo says. ᴳ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

“Yuta and me,” Gojo says. “We stay near the house. If anything comes over that ridge while you're gone, it meets us first.” ᴳ

Yuta nods from the wall. He doesn't say anything. He doesn't need to.

Jin-Woo lets out a breath.

Then he looks at Mau.

“I'd like you to come with me,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

Mau doesn't answer right away.

He thinks about the trip. Three days. The bird's map with all the gardens on it. Yuka upstairs choosing between two of the four blue things. Elmesia waiting to show them a city they've never seen. DIO bouncing.

He thinks about Jin-Woo standing at the window every night looking north. About *I killed them*. About a man who has fought more wars than Mau has, and has been alone at the front of all of them.

“When?” he asks. ᴹ

“Tomorrow,” Jin-Woo says. “Early. I don't want to wait.” ᴶʷ

“I was supposed to go to Elmesia's tomorrow.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴶʷ

Jin-Woo doesn't say anything else. He doesn't say *I'm sorry*. He doesn't say *you don't have to*. He just waits.

“I need to talk to Yuka,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

* * *

He tells her everything.

All of it. On the bed, among the piles of clothes, with the four blue things laid out in a row between them. He doesn't soften it. He doesn't say *it's probably nothing*. He tells her what Jin-Woo said, word for word, and what Jin-Woo's face looked like when he said it, and what Hae-In said about the window.

She listens to all of it without interrupting.

When he's done, she's quiet for a long time.

“You're going,” she says. ʸ

“I want to,” he says. “If you're okay with it.” ᴹ

“And if I'm not?” ʸ

He doesn't answer right away.

“Then I'll tell him I'm sorry,” he says. “And I'll go with you to Elmesia's. And Gojo and Yuta will be here.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

She can see he means it. He can see her see it. He means it the way he meant *forever*: not as a thing he decided, but as a thing that's true.

“He asked you,” she says slowly. ʸ

“He asked me.” ᴹ

“He doesn't ask anyone for anything.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

She looks down at the four blue things on the bed. She picks one up. Puts it down.

“Go,” she says. ʸ

* * *

They work it out together.

She'll still go to Elmesia's. That's hers; she's been waiting for it since the bird came, since the letter, since before the bathroom door. She'll go on Kaisel tomorrow, as planned, with DIO, who can't wait another day, who might actually explode. She'll tell Elmesia what's happened. She'll start seeing the city.

He'll go with Jin-Woo. Find out what's in the north. And then come to her, as soon as he can. Elmesia has one of his soldiers in her shadow; so does Yuka. He can be there in nine breaths, if he has to.

Makima stays with him. She has to. She can't be far from him.

“DIO goes with you,” he says. ᴹ

“DIO goes with me,” Yuka agrees. “She'll protect me.” ʸ

“With a hat,” he says. ᴹ

“With a very good hat,” Yuka says. “She's been making it for two days. She showed me. It's lopsided.” ʸ

He laughs. He can't help it. She doesn't laugh, but she smiles, a little, and then the smile goes away and she just looks at him.

“You come back,” she says. ʸ

“I'll come to you,” he says. “Not back. *To* you. As soon as I can.” ᴹ

“And you tell me,” she says. “Whatever happens. Like the war. As soon as you can.” ʸ

“As soon as I can.” ᴹ

She picks up one of the blue things and puts it in his hands.

“That one,” she says. “For when you get there. So I have something to wear when you see the city for the first time.” ʸ

He puts it in the inventory. He doesn't ask which blue it is. He'll know when he sees it on her.
