# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 17 — The Fourth Breath

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E48
**Continuity state:** B+348, night. Yuka's point of view. Mau has spent the evening with the eyes at full, searching the north for the faintest sign of what Jin-Woo described, and comes to bed exhausted. Yuka, twice as clingy as before, remembers the day of the red gate, when he let go, and does it again on purpose: climbs onto him, takes his head and draws it against her chest. He lets go, the same way. Just before he falls asleep an "I love you" slips out of him. She goes red, holds him tighter, and hears a snore. Closeness, no intimate scene (five days out of the low days; she's only just getting her strength back).

*When: The night before they leave in two directions. The packed inventory is ready. The four blue things have been reduced to one.*

He comes to bed very late.

She hears him on the stairs. Slow. Heavy. Not his usual tread, which is light for someone his size, almost silent, the way he learned to walk in places where sound got you killed. Tonight he's walking like someone carrying something up the stairs, and when he opens the door she sees that he isn't carrying anything at all.

It's his eyes.

He's been on the roof since supper. She knows; she watched him go up. He told her he wanted to look north. He didn't tell her how long he'd be looking, and she didn't ask, and now it's past midnight and he's standing in the doorway with his hand on the frame and his face grey with something that isn't the light.

“Did you find anything?” she asks. ʸ

He shakes his head.

“I looked as far as I could,” he says. “All of it. Everything Jin-Woo said. I had them open all the way. All evening.” ᴹ

He rubs his eyes with the heels of his hands.

“There's nothing there,” he says. “Nothing I can see. Which means either there's nothing, or there's something good enough at hiding that I can't.” ᴹ

He doesn't say which he thinks it is. He doesn't have to.

* * *

He sits on the edge of the bed and starts pulling off his boots.

He does it badly. He gets one off and then just sits there holding it, staring at the floor, and she can see the exhaustion sitting on him like a weight. Not his body. His body's fine. It's the eyes. Five hours of them open all the way, reading everything, the whole north, mile after mile, searching for the smallest flicker of something that might not be there. She's seen him after the eyes before. She's never seen him like this.

He's going to say he's fine.

She can see it coming. He's going to turn round and say *I'm fine, just tired, go to sleep*, and lie down on his side of the bed, and stare at the ceiling for three hours thinking about the north.

She doesn't let him.

* * *

She remembers.

It comes back to her all at once, sitting up in bed watching him hold his boot. The day of the red gate. Fourteen minutes. Him coming up over the top of the field, thinner, holding his ribs. Him on the couch in the common room, about to say *I'm fine*, about to get up and go and tell Fern about the loot and fall over in the hallway.

What she did.

She did it without thinking, that day. She just climbed into his lap and took his head and put it against her and held on. And he *let go*. All of him. All at once. Like she was the floor. She'd never felt him do that before. She's thought about it every day since.

She wants it again.

Not for herself. For *him*. She wants him to be able to put it down. The north, and Jin-Woo's face in the clinic, and the eyes, and the bathroom door, and all of it. She wants him to have one place in the whole world where he doesn't have to hold anything up.

She gets up.

* * *

She takes the boot out of his hand and puts it on the floor.

He looks up at her, surprised. She doesn't say anything. She climbs onto the bed behind him, and then round in front of him, and into his lap, facing him, her knees on either side of his hips, exactly the way she did on the couch that day.

“Yuka—” ᴹ

“Don't,” she says. ʸ

The way she said it then.

She puts her hands on his face. She looks at him. Up close he's worse than she thought: shadows under his eyes like bruises, a looseness in his jaw, a tremor at the edges of his hands where they're resting on her knees. He's been holding something up for five hours and he doesn't know how to stop.

She puts her hand on the back of his head and draws it forward, slowly, until his face is against her chest.

* * *

For a second he holds himself there, stiff, the way he did that first time. She feels him not understand.

Then she puts her other arm round his shoulders and holds on, and she feels him understand.

He lets go.

It's exactly the same. It's exactly as enormous. All of him, all at once, falling into her like she's the floor. His arms come up round her waist and hold on. His whole weight settles against her. She feels the long breath go out of him, and then another, slower. She holds his head against her heart and doesn't move.

*Two*, she thinks.

He breathes out again. Slower still. She feels his shoulders loosen under her arm.

*Three.*

His hands go slack on her back.

And just before the fourth breath, so low and so slurred she almost doesn't catch it, into the front of his own shirt that she's wearing, against her heart:

“I love you.” ᴹ

* * *

She goes red.

She feels it. All at once, from her collarbones to the tips of her ears, hot as if she'd stood too close to a fire. Her whole face. Her whole neck. He's said it before. He's said it a hundred times. He said it on the porch and in the bed and *forever* in the dark. But never like this. Never like something that wasn't decided, that just came out of him on its own as he went under, the way a breath comes out.

She holds him tighter. Both arms. Her chin on top of his head. She holds on so tight she's probably squeezing him, and she doesn't care, and she opens her mouth to say it back—

He snores.

* * *

It's a small snore. Not loud. A soft, ragged, completely unselfconscious sound, the sound of a man who has been awake for twenty hours and spent five of them with his eyes open all the way and has just, finally, put everything down.

She stares at the wall over his shoulder.

He snores again.

She starts to laugh.

She doesn't make a sound. She can't; she'd wake him. She just shakes with it, silently, holding his head against her chest with her face burning and her eyes wet and him snoring into her shirt, and laughs until her stomach hurts.

He doesn't wake up.

* * *

She doesn't move for a long time.

She thinks about tomorrow. About Kaisel and the city and Elmesia waiting and DIO in her new hat. About him going north with Jin-Woo, toward something old enough that even the eyes can't see it. About *as soon as I can*.

She thinks about the bathroom door, a week and a half ago. About lying on the floor of the washroom feeling like the bottom had fallen out of the world. And about this: him asleep against her, heavy and warm and completely let go, with *I love you* still somewhere in the air between them.

The bottom didn't fall out. It's right here. It's him. And tonight, for one night, she gets to be it for him back.

* * *

Eventually she lets herself lie back.

She does it slowly, so slowly, taking him with her. His head stays on her chest. His arms stay round her waist. She gets the blanket up over both of them one-handed, and settles, and lies there in the dark with his weight on her and his breath going in and out against her heart.

He snores again. Softly.

“I love you too, idiot,” she whispers into his hair. ʸ

He doesn't hear her. She doesn't mind. She'll tell him again tomorrow, when he can.

She falls asleep with her hand on the back of his head, still a little red, still smiling.
