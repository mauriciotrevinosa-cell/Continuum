# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 18 — Two Directions

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S13E48
**Continuity state:** B+349, dawn. The yard. Kaisel comes up out of Mau's shadow for Yuka and DIO, who has made herself a hat for the trip. Ori, Frieren, the Sungs and half the house come out to see them off. Gojo and Yuta take their posts near the house. Yuka checks that her anchor (the quiet soldier from the north wall) is in her shadow; Elmesia's is in hers. The blue thing is in Mau's inventory for when he arrives. Goodbye. Then Mau, Jin-Woo and Makima (who can't be far from him) walk north. Jin-Woo carries one of Mau's soldiers in his shadow, placed weeks ago.

*When: B+349. Dawn, the morning after the fourth breath. Yuka woke first and didn't climb on him; she lay there with her hand on his chest until he opened his eyes, and then said, "Today."*

Kaisel comes up out of the ground in the middle of the yard.

He does it the way he always does: the shadow under Mau's feet deepening and spreading until it's wider than the training posts, and then something enormous rising out of it, scale by scale, wing by wing, the long head last. He shakes himself like a dog coming out of water. The training posts creak in the wind he makes.

Ori, on the porch steps in her nightclothes with the Pillow beside her, says “*oh*” the way she says it every time, even though she's seen him a hundred times. ᴼ

Kaisel lowers his head to the ground.

“Good morning,” Yuka tells him. ʸ

Kaisel blinks at her, very slowly.

* * *

DIO has made a hat.

She comes out of him in the yard this morning in her own body and stands beside Yuka wearing it. It's wide-brimmed and slightly lopsided and there is a sea-glass pin stuck in the band.

Yuka looks at it.

“DIO,” she says. ʸ

“It's for the sun,” DIO says. ᴰ

“It's lovely,” Yuka says, with total sincerity. ʸ

DIO beams, the way a child beams when she's been told her drawing is good.

He doesn't say anything about the hat. He's learned.

* * *

Half the house is in the yard.

Nobody arranged it. People just came, the way they always come. Shuna with a basket of food Yuka didn't ask for and will eat all of. Rem beside her. Umaru in a blanket, still half asleep. Milim on the porch rail, swinging her legs, loudly asking whether she can come instead, or as well, or in a bag. Maki and Mai side by side. Frieren on the bottom step with her book closed on her knee.

The Sungs came up the orchard path at first light. Hae-In with her hand on Suho's shoulder, Suho craning to see Kaisel, Jin-Ah with her notebook, their mother with a second basket. Jin-Woo behind them, already dressed for the road, very quiet.

Gojo and Yuta are at the edge of the yard, by the gate to the north path. Not saying goodbye. Taking their posts. Gojo has his blindfold pushed up on his forehead, which he almost never does, and his eyes are open and pointed north.

“We'll be here,” he says, to nobody in particular. ᴳ

Yuta nods beside him.

* * *

Yuka checks her shadow.

She does it the way he taught her, the first morning, in front of the whole house: closes her eyes for a moment and reaches for the small quiet thing that lives there. The soldier from the north wall. The one that doesn't like to be noticed.

He feels her reach. A tug under his breastbone, small and clear. *Here.*

“Still there,” she says, opening her eyes. ʸ

“Still there,” he agrees. ᴹ

“And Elmesia has hers.” ʸ

“Elmesia has hers.” ᴹ

She nods. She already knew all of that. She asked anyway, because asking is a way of holding on.

* * *

He opens the inventory and checks the blue thing.

It's there. Folded on its own, where he put it two nights ago, separate from everything else, the way he keeps the chocolate wrapper and the letter from the house and the stone Ori says is the shape of his thumb. He doesn't know which of the four blues it is. He'll know when he sees it on her.

“Don't take it out,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I'm just checking.” ᴹ

“You've checked it four times since yesterday.” ʸ

“Five,” says Ciel, very quietly, so only he can hear. ᶜ

He closes the inventory.

* * *

Then there's nothing left to do but say goodbye, and neither of them is any good at it.

She stands in front of him in the middle of the yard with Kaisel's great head beside them and the whole house pretending very hard not to watch. She's wearing her travelling clothes and the bracelet with the flowers and his old jacket, the one with the racing team's name on it, which she took from the wardrobe this morning without asking and he didn't say anything about.

She looks up at him.

“Tell me,” she says. ʸ

“Tell you what?” ᴹ

“The rule.” ʸ

He knows the rule. It's hers. She made it in the war, and again on the night of the Rulers, and again two nights ago on the bed among the four blue things.

“If something happens,” he says, “I tell you. As soon as I can.” ᴹ

“And?” ʸ

“And I come to you. Not back. To you.” ᴹ

She nods.

She puts both arms round his waist and her face in his chest and holds on. He holds her back. Neither of them says anything. Behind them, Kaisel breathes out, slow and warm, and the training posts creak.

Yuka rises onto her toes and kisses him goodbye. Her fingers are still knotted in the back of his jacket; he holds her carefully, as though letting go too soon could change the rule they have just made.

“Not back,” she says against his forehead. “To me.” ʸ

“To you,” he answers. ᴹ

Behind them, somebody on the porch clears a throat far too loudly. Yuka doesn't turn around. She takes his hand for another moment, presses it between both of hers, and only then steps back toward Kaisel.

* * *

She lets go first. She's always better at that part than he is.

She climbs onto Kaisel's neck without help. DIO settles behind her, hat and all. Ori runs down off the porch steps and stands right under Kaisel's head and shouts *bring me something* at the top of her voice, and Yuka shouts back *a shell* and Ori shouts *a better one* and Yuka laughs.

Kaisel lifts.

The wind of it flattens the grass. The training posts creak. Milim yells something from the porch rail that gets lost in the beat of the wings. And then Kaisel is up, above the orchard, above the roof, wheeling once over the house so that Yuka can wave, and then heading south and west with the dawn behind him.

Mau watches until he can't see the colour of her any more.

It takes a long time. She's the only colour there is. He can see her long after Kaisel is just a dark shape against the grey sky, a small bright point of something that isn't grey, getting smaller, getting smaller.

And then she's gone, and the sky is just sky.

* * *

“Ready?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶʷ

He's standing at the north gate beside Gojo and Yuta. He hasn't said goodbye to his own family yet; Mau watches him do it now. Hae-In, briefly, her forehead against his. His mother, longer. Jin-Ah, who says something about her notebook that makes him smile. Suho, who throws himself at his father's legs and has to be peeled off by Igris, Jin-Woo's Igris, who then stands beside the boy with one gauntleted hand resting on his shoulder.

Then Jin-Woo looks at Mau, and Mau nods.

Makima comes down the porch steps.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't need to; she can't be far from him, and everyone knows it. She's wearing her coat and she has nothing in her hands. She walks across the yard and stops beside Mau, a little behind him, the way she stood at the top of the stairs.

“You'll like the hills,” he tells her. ᴹ

“You haven't seen the hills,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Nobody has. That's the point.” ᴹ

Something happens at the corner of her mouth.

* * *

There's one more thing.

He checks it before they go, the way he checked Yuka's: reaches for the soldier in Jin-Woo's shadow. It's been there for weeks. Since the porch past the orchard, the afternoon Jin-Woo laughed and said *I have an army*, and then, later, said *put one by the door*. And then, a few days after that, without being asked, stood in the yard and said *and one in mine*. Not for him. For Mau. *So you know where I am, if you need to.*

It's there. Quiet. Steady. A door, if a door is ever needed.

“Still there,” Jin-Woo says, watching his face. ᴶʷ

“Still there.” ᴹ

* * *

They walk north.

Gojo and Yuta don't say goodbye. They just step aside to let them through the gate. Frieren doesn't look up from her book. Ori waves with both arms from the porch steps until they're past the orchard. The Pillow groans once, deep and long, a sound that rolls after them across the grass like distant thunder.

The path goes up through the fields and over the first ridge, and then there's nothing but hills.

Long bare hills, grey on grey, rolling north until they disappear into a haze that might be more hills or might be the sky. No houses. No people. No smoke. Just grass and rock and wind and the three of them walking, and somewhere ahead, far beyond what even the eyes can see, something old enough to make Jin-Woo stand at his window every night.

Mau opens the eyes.

Not all the way. Not yet. Just enough to read the ground as it comes. The filter thin, the world sharpening, the long empty hills unrolling in front of him layer on layer.

Behind him, very far away and getting farther, the place at the edge of him where Kaisel always is. Not a call. He always knows where his shadows are, the way you know where the doors in your own house are in the dark. And she's on that one.

South and west. Still flying.

He keeps walking north.
