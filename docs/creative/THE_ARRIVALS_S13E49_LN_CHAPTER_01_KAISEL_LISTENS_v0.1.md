# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 19 — Kaisel Listens

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S13E49
**Continuity state:** B+349, morning to evening. Yuka's point of view. The flight south and west on Kaisel, Yuka talking to him the whole way; he doesn't answer but tilts his head when she says Mau's name. DIO in her hat. The capital of Thalion comes up out of the haze: a walled city on a hill in rings, the citadel at the top. Elmesia meets them on a terrace and, before anything else, walks them through her city herself: the gardens, the place where the old library was, the market, a fountain she designed. She tells Yuka she's kept a room. Yuka keeps the blue dress for when Mau arrives. That night she holds her anchor without pulling it: it can't tell her where he is, only call him if she needs him.

*When: B+349. The same morning. Kaisel flies faster than Mau ever lets him fly with Yuka on his back; Yuka suspects DIO asked him to.*

She talks to Kaisel the whole way.

She doesn't plan to. It just happens. They're up above the clouds within a few minutes of leaving the yard, and the world goes quiet and white and huge beneath them, and the wind is too loud to talk to DIO, who is sitting behind her holding her hat on with both hands. So she leans forward along the great scaled neck, and puts her cheek against it, and talks.

About nothing, at first. The clouds. How cold it is up here. How Mau never lets Kaisel go this fast when she's riding, and how she thinks DIO must have asked him to, and how she's not going to tell Mau.

Kaisel doesn't answer. Kaisel never answers. He's one of the ones who don't talk, the big quiet ones; Mau says he understands everything and chooses to say nothing, which Yuka thinks is very wise and also very like Mau.

But he listens. She can feel him listening. The way his neck shifts under her cheek when she changes what she's talking about.

* * *

“He's going north,” she tells Kaisel, after a while. ʸ

Kaisel's wings beat.

“With Jin-Woo. Something's there. Something old. Jin-Woo knows what it is and he won't say.” ʸ

The wind.

“He'll be fine,” she says. “He's always fine. He comes back worse than he left and he says he's fine and then he's fine. Eventually.” ʸ

She's quiet for a while.

“I hate it,” she says. ʸ

Kaisel's neck moves under her. Just slightly. A shift, as if settling her more firmly.

“I hate it,” she says again. “Every time. I hate it so much. And I told him to go anyway. Because Jin-Woo asked. Because Jin-Woo never asks anyone for anything. And because he'd have gone with me instead if I'd said, and he'd have been miserable, and I'd have been miserable watching him be miserable.” ʸ

She lies there with her cheek on his scales.

“Mau,” she says, very quietly. ʸ

Kaisel's head tilts. Just slightly. Just once. The way a dog's head tilts when it hears its name.

She stares at the back of his head.

“You heard that,” she says. ʸ

Kaisel flies on.

“You *did*. You know his name.” ʸ

Nothing. Wind. Wings. And then, very slightly, the head tilts again, as if to say: *obviously*.

She laughs, out loud, into the wind, and DIO behind her asks what's funny, and she can't explain it.

* * *

The city comes up out of the haze in the late morning.

She sees it before DIO does. A smudge on the horizon that resolves, as Kaisel comes down out of the clouds, into a hill. And on the hill, walls. And inside the walls, more walls. Rings of them, one inside the other, climbing the hill like the rings of a tree cut open, each ring a street and each street a wall of pale stone houses with roofs in a hundred colours.

Blue roofs. Red roofs. Green ones with copper edges gone soft with age. Gardens hung from every terrace like green shelves. And at the very top, the citadel: a cluster of white towers with gold at their tips, catching the sun.

“Oh,” Yuka says. ʸ

DIO, behind her, has stopped holding her hat. She's looking. Yuka can feel her looking, the way you can feel someone holding their breath.

“She built that,” DIO says, very softly. “Some of it. She told me. The library. The waterways. The way the streets turn so the wind can't get in.” ᴰ

Kaisel circles once over the city. People in the streets look up. Some of them point. Some of them wave.

Yuka waves back.

* * *

Elmesia is on a terrace at the top of the citadel.

She's alone. That surprises Yuka. She expected guards and courtiers and people in robes. There are guards, at a distance, at the doors and along the walls. But on the terrace there's only Elmesia, in a plain dress, with a hat of her own against the sun, standing at the parapet with her hands folded, watching them land.

Kaisel settles on the flagstones. He lowers his head.

Elmesia comes forward. She doesn't look at Yuka first. She looks at Kaisel. She looks at him for a long moment, the great dark head, the slow-blinking eyes, and then she puts out her hand, palm down, the way you'd offer it to an animal you respect.

Kaisel looks at the hand. Then he lowers his head the last few inches and lets her touch his nose.

“He's beautiful,” Elmesia says. ᴱˡ

“He knows,” Yuka says. ʸ

Elmesia laughs.

* * *

“Where is he?” Elmesia asks, when Yuka has climbed down and DIO has climbed down and Kaisel has sunk back into the flagstones as though into water, back to wherever he goes. ᴱˡ

“North,” Yuka says. “With Jin-Woo. There's—something. He'll come as soon as he can.” ʸ

Elmesia looks at her face for a long moment. She doesn't ask anything else. She just nods.

“Then I'll show you the city first,” she says. “And when he comes, I'll show it to him again. I don't mind. I like showing it.” ᴱˡ

* * *

She shows them everything.

Herself. Not a guide, not a courtier. Elmesia, queen of Thalion, walks them out of the citadel and down through the rings of the city on foot, in her plain dress and her sun hat, with two guards trailing a long way behind trying to look as if they're not trailing.

She shows them the gardens: terraces of them, one above another, green and gold and full of flowers Yuka doesn't have names for. She shows them a fountain in the third ring that she says she designed when she was very young and very bad at fountains, and which still sprays slightly to the left, and which she has refused to let anyone fix for nine hundred years.

“Why?” DIO asks. ᴰ

“Because I was very proud of it,” Elmesia says. “And because it reminds me I was once very bad at something.” ᴱˡ

DIO thinks about this very seriously.

She shows them the market, which is loud and crowded and smells of spice and bread and something frying, and she buys them both a pastry from a stall without paying, because the woman behind the stall refuses to take her money and Elmesia, apparently, has been trying to pay at that stall for three hundred years and failed every time.

She shows them the place where the old library used to be. It's an empty square now, with grass in it and children playing. She had the library moved, she says, stone by stone, to higher ground, because the river used to flood here, and she didn't want to lose a single book.

“Did you?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Two,” Elmesia says. “I still think about them.” ᴱˡ

* * *

In the late afternoon they sit in a garden at the top of the city, under a tree with silver leaves, and drink something cold out of tall glasses, and look out over the rings of roofs going down the hill.

“It's beautiful,” Yuka says. ʸ

“It is,” Elmesia agrees. ᴱˡ

“I wish he could see it.” ʸ

Elmesia looks at her.

“He'll see it,” she says. “I'll make sure. I've kept a room. The one I wanted to give you both. It has a balcony that looks over the whole city. You can see the sea from it, on a clear day. I want him to see it with you.” ᴱˡ

Yuka doesn't say anything for a moment.

“I brought a dress,” she says. ʸ

Elmesia raises an eyebrow.

“For when he gets here,” Yuka says. “A blue one. He can't see the blue. He can't see any of it. Everything's grey for him except me. But on me, he can see it. So I brought it, so that when he gets here and sees your city for the first time, there'll be one thing in it he can see properly.” ʸ

Elmesia looks at her for a long time.

“You're going to make me cry in my own garden,” she says. ᴱˡ

“Sorry.” ʸ

“Don't be,” Elmesia says, and doesn't cry, but looks out over the city for a while without saying anything. ᴱˡ

* * *

That night, in the room with the balcony, Yuka can't sleep.

DIO is in the next room, already asleep, or doing whatever DIO does instead of sleeping. Elmesia's palace is quiet. Below the balcony the city glitters, ring on ring of small warm lights going down the hill to the dark.

She stands on the balcony in his jacket and closes her eyes.

She reaches for the soldier in her shadow. The quiet one. It's there; she can feel it the way you feel a key in your pocket.

It only goes one way. She knows that. He explained it the first morning, in front of the whole house: it can tell him where she is, if she needs him. It can't tell her where he is. She can't feel him through it at all.

But she knows that if she pulled, he'd feel it. Wherever he is. However far north. A hand on his sleeve.

She doesn't pull. She just holds it, the way you hold a key in your pocket, for a long time.

“Come to me,” she says to the dark. Not loud. Just enough. ʸ

The thread doesn't answer. It doesn't go that way. But it's there, and if she needed him he'd come, and that's enough to let her go back inside and lie down on the bed that's meant for two and fall asleep with her hand on his jacket.
