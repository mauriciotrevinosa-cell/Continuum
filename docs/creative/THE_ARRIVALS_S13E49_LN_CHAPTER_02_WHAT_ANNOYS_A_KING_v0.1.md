# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 20 — What Annoys a King

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S13E49
**Continuity state:** B+349 night to B+350 morning, the northern hills. They walk all day and find nothing the eyes can see. At the fire, Jin-Woo tells Mau about the Monarchs he killed, one by one, and what each of them hated. The Frost Monarch was the proudest: what he could never stand was being cut on the shoulder, because it meant someone had reached him before he moved. Makima, who has never gone anywhere to see it, sits apart and watches the stars. In the morning Elmesia's anchor pulls. Mau tells Jin-Woo one of the ones he described is in the city. Makima steps back inside him, since her body can't follow an Exchange. Mau calls the form and the wheel and goes.

*When: B+349, from dusk. The three of them stop where two ridges meet, because Jin-Woo says it's the last place from which you can see both the house's direction and the north at once.*

They find nothing all day.

That's the worst part. Mau walks with the eyes half open from the first ridge to the last light, reading every fold of the land, every hollow, every rock. Wind. Grass. A hare that bolts. A hawk. Nothing that shouldn't be there. Nothing that hides.

Jin-Woo walks ahead of him and doesn't say anything. Every so often he stops and stands very still with his head slightly lifted, the way a dog stands when it's caught a scent on the wind. Then he walks on.

Makima walks behind them both. She doesn't say anything either. But when Mau looks back, every so often, she isn't looking at the ground or at them. She's looking at the hills. At the sky. At the long grey distance going on and on in every direction.

“You're looking,” he says, the third time he catches her. ᴹ

She considers this.

“I've never been anywhere that wasn't for something,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

At dusk they stop where two ridges meet.

Jin-Woo chooses it. From here, he says, you can see back toward the house and forward toward the north at the same time, and whatever's out there, if it moves tonight, they'll see which way it's going.

Mau makes a fire. The small spell, Frieren's, the one that burns without fuel. He makes it bigger than he needs to, for no reason, and sits beside it, and thinks about the Pillow.

Makima sits apart. Not far; she can't be far. But on a rock a little way up the slope, with her coat around her, looking at the sky, where the stars are coming out one by one over the hills.

Jin-Woo sits across the fire from him.

* * *

“Tell me about them,” Mau says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at the fire.

For a long time Mau thinks he isn't going to. Then he starts.

He doesn't tell it like a story. He tells it like a list. Like a man going through the names on a wall, one by one, saying each one so that he doesn't forget it.

The Monarch of Beasts. Fast. Savage. Hated anything that walked on two legs. Died angry.

The Monarch of Plagues. A queen of insects, cold, patient, who fought with swarms and didn't care what died in them.

The Monarch of Iron Body. Huge. Slow. Almost impossible to cut. Died standing.

The Monarch of Transfiguration. The oldest of them except one, and the cleverest, who wore faces that weren't his and never fought if he could make someone else do it for him.

The Monarch of the Beginning. A giant. Older than mountains.

The Monarch of White Flames. Proud. Loud. Burned everything.

“And the Monarch of Destruction,” Jin-Woo says, and stops. ᴶʷ

“Who's that?” ᴹ

Jin-Woo doesn't answer right away.

“The one I'm afraid of,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

“And the Frost Monarch,” Jin-Woo says, after a while. ᴶʷ

He's smiling slightly. It isn't a nice smile.

“The proudest of them,” he says. “Prouder than White Flames. He thought he was the most elegant thing that had ever lived. Moved like it. Fought like it. Every movement perfect.” ᴶʷ

“What did he hate?” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him across the fire.

“Being touched first,” he says. “Being *reached*. He was so fast and so sure of himself that the idea of anyone getting to him before he'd moved was unbearable. I cut his shoulder once. Just once. A small cut. Before he'd finished lifting his hand.” ᴶʷ

He shakes his head slowly.

“He lost his mind,” Jin-Woo says. “He stopped fighting well. He stopped fighting *smart*. All he wanted after that was to make me pay for the shoulder. It's the only reason I lived through that fight.” ᴶʷ

Mau thinks about that.

“The shoulder,” he says. ᴹ

“The shoulder,” Jin-Woo agrees. “If you ever meet him, and I hope you never do, cut him there. Before he moves. He won't be able to think about anything else.” ᴶʷ

* * *

They sit by the fire a long time.

Up the slope, Makima hasn't moved. She's still looking at the sky. Mau can feel her through the thread: something quiet and strange coming off her, something he doesn't have a name for and isn't sure she does either.

“She's never seen stars,” Jin-Woo says quietly. ᴶʷ

“She's seen them.” ᴹ

“Not like that.” ᴶʷ

Mau looks up the slope at her. A dark shape against a sky full of light. Still. Watching.

“No,” he says. “Not like that.” ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't sleep much. He lies by the fire with his eyes half open, reading the dark, and every so often he checks.

Elmesia's soldier: quiet, under a long table in a garden at the top of a city. Yuka's: quiet, on a balcony, in a room meant for two. Jin-Woo's: quiet, three paces away, in the shadow of the man sleeping on the other side of the fire with one hand on the ground, ready.

All quiet. All night.

Around dawn he falls asleep.

* * *

Elmesia's soldier wakes him.

It isn't a tug. It's a *pull*. Hard and sudden, a hook set somewhere under his breastbone and yanked, so violently that he's on his feet before he's awake. South and west. Very far. A city on a hill. It comes with a feeling, and the feeling is *now*.

And under it, a breath later, quieter, smaller: Yuka's.

Not calling. She hasn't called; he'd know. The quiet soldier from the north wall is just very close to something very bad, and it's trembling, and it's telling him so.

Jin-Woo is already sitting up. He looks at Mau's face.

“What,” he says. ᴶʷ

“The city,” Mau says. “Elmesia called. Yuka's there. Something's—” ᴹ

He stops.

He's reading it. Through the pull, through Elmesia's soldier, through what it's close to. It isn't seeing; the soldiers don't see for him. It's knowing, the shape of the thing, the way you know a stair in the dark is broken by how it gives under your foot.

Cold. Enormous. Old. And *proud*. A pride so dense it's almost a smell.

He looks at Jin-Woo.

“One of yours,” he says. “One of the ones you told me about. He's there. In the city.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo's face goes very still.

“Which,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Cold,” Mau says. “Proud. Very proud.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo closes his eyes for exactly one second.

“Go,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

Makima is beside him.

He didn't hear her come down the slope. She's just there, in her coat, her face perfectly calm, looking at him.

“I can't follow an Exchange,” she says. “Not like this. Not in this body.” ᴹᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then I'll come the other way,” she says. ᴹᵏ

She puts her hand flat on his chest. And thins. The way she did on the night of the contract: going soft at the edges, going faint, as though she were never quite solid to begin with. For a moment he can see the grey dawn hills through her. Then she sinks into him, into the deep still place where her hand is holding the sea, and the coat she was wearing falls empty to the grass.

He feels her settle. Feels her hand, inside, tighten on what it's holding.

*Go*, she says. Not aloud. From in there. ᴹᵏ

* * *

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” Noise says. ᴺ

“The form.” ᴹ

“With her inside, it'll be steadier than it's ever been,” Noise says. “I don't know what it'll look like.” ᴺ

“I don't care what it looks like.” ᴹ

He feels it come over him.

It's like stepping into cold water and then into fire. Something running out from the deep place in his chest where Makima is, along every channel, into every part of him. His hair moves on his scalp as if a wind has caught it. His eyes go hot, then hotter, then something past hot that doesn't have a word. The world tips and sharpens and goes so clear it hurts.

And above and behind him, without being called, the wheel.

He feels it come out. Feels it hang there, turning slowly, in the grey dawn over the northern hills.

Jin-Woo is looking at him. At his face. At his hair. At his eyes. Mau can't see what Jin-Woo is seeing. He'll never see it. But he sees Jin-Woo's face change, the way a face changes when it sees something it wasn't ready for.

“Go,” Jin-Woo says again, quieter. ᴶʷ

“You'll be all right?” ᴹ

“I've got your soldier,” Jin-Woo says. “And mine. Go.” ᴶʷ

Mau finds Elmesia's soldier. Far away. South and west. Under a long table, in a garden, at the top of a city.

He goes.
