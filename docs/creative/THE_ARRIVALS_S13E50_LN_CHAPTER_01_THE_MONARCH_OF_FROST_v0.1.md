# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 21 — The Monarch of Frost

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S13E50
**Continuity state:** B+350, morning, the citadel of Thalion. The Frost Monarch's point of view. The plan, made by the Monarch of Transfiguration: he strikes the elf queen the shadow-host has left a soldier with; the host will come, far from the one who killed them; meanwhile the others take the Shadow Monarch alone in the northern hills. He came over the walls before dawn and froze the outer gardens. Elmesia meets him on the terrace and holds him longer than he expected. A small human woman with a pale wheeled thing behind her takes the servants and wounded down the stairs. Elmesia, bleeding, looks once at her own shadow and says a name. Mau's emergence is the next chapter.

*When: B+350. A little before Mau wakes in the hills. The sun is not yet over the eastern wall of the city.*

He has always preferred cities in the morning.

Before the noise. Before the smoke of a thousand breakfasts. When the stone is still cold from the night and the air is thin and clean and you can hear a bird land on a roof three streets away. A city in the morning is a held breath. He likes to be the thing it was holding its breath for.

He comes over the outer wall in the blue hour, and the gardens on the first terrace freeze behind him.

He doesn't mean to freeze them. He never means to. It's simply what he is. Where he walks, the warmth goes out of things, and the dew on the leaves goes white, and the water in the little channels that run between the flowerbeds stops running and goes still and hard as glass. By the time the first guard sees him, there's a path of white behind him all the way to the wall, like the wake of a ship.

The guard opens his mouth to shout.

He doesn't finish. The Monarch of Frost does not let people finish things in his presence. It's a matter of style.

* * *

The plan isn't his.

Nothing clever ever is. It came from the Monarch of Transfiguration, who has worn so many faces that he no longer remembers which one he started with, and who explained it to the rest of them in a cave in the northern hills in that thin, dry, patient voice of his, three times, while the Monarch of Beasts paced and the Queen of Plagues hummed and the giant slept.

*The shadow-host leaves pieces of himself with the people he loves. Like a man leaving lamps in windows. When one of them calls, he comes. Always. Without thinking. It is the only predictable thing about him.*

*So one of us goes to the elf. The queen in the white city. She has one of his lamps. When she calls, he will come. And while he is there, far to the south, the rest of us will be waiting for the one who killed us. Alone. In the hills. Where no one can help him.*

*And who goes to the elf?* the Monarch of Beasts had asked, already sure it would be him.

The Monarch of Transfiguration had turned his shifting face to the Monarch of Frost.

*You,* he'd said. *Because you are the only one of us with enough patience to keep a host busy without killing yourself trying to impress him.*

The Monarch of Frost had accepted. Not because he was asked. Because he had been told, correctly, that he was the most elegant of them. Which he was.

* * *

The citadel is very beautiful.

He'll give the elf that. White towers. Gold at the tips. Gardens hung in tiers like green shelves. A fountain, in one of the lower rings, that sprays very slightly to the left, which offends him so much that he freezes it solid on his way past.

He comes up through the rings the way frost comes up a window: from the edges in, quietly, and then all at once. The guards come at him in twos and threes and he lets them reach him and then they don't move any more. The bells start ringing across the city when he's halfway up. He finds the bells a little vulgar. He lets them ring.

By the time he reaches the top, the whole city is awake, and afraid, and holding its breath.

Good.

* * *

She's on the terrace.

The elf. The queen. Tall and pale and dressed in something plain, with a cup still in her hand, standing at the head of a long table under an awning. She turns when he steps over the parapet and looks at him.

There are others at the table. Servants. A thing in a wide hat that might be a person and might not; he can't read it, which is interesting, and he files it away. And a small human woman, young, half risen from her chair, staring at him.

He looks at the elf.

She doesn't look afraid. That's the first disappointment. She looks at him the way a gardener looks at frost on her roses: with irritation, and calculation, and no surprise at all.

“You're one of them,” she says. “The ones he was afraid of.” ᴱˡ

He doesn't know who *he* is. He doesn't care.

“I am the Monarch of Frost,” he says. ˢⁱˡ

“You froze my fountain,” says the elf. ᴱˡ

“Your fountain was crooked.” ˢⁱˡ

Her eyes narrow very slightly.

“It was crooked,” she says, “on purpose.” ᴱˡ

And she raises her hand.

* * *

She's strong.

He'll admit that later, if anyone ever asks. Stronger than anything he's met in this world so far; stronger, perhaps, than some of the others in the cave. The magic that comes off her hand is not the flicker of a hedge-witch. It's a wall of force and heat that takes him in the chest and throws him back across the terrace into the parapet, and for a moment, a single moment, he is *warm*.

He hates it.

He rises out of the shattered stone with frost spreading from his feet across the flagstones in long white fans. The elf is already moving. Shield and strike and shield again, magic spun out of the cold air faster than he can freeze it, her guards falling back around her in a ring and adding what they can. She holds him. For the length of ten breaths she *holds* him, the Monarch of Frost, on her own terrace among the overturned chairs.

Then he reaches her.

Not deep. A sliver of ice across the back of her hand, and another across her shoulder, and a third along her cheek, very fine, very precise, the way he does everything. He watches her blood come up bright against her pale skin. He watches her face change, just slightly: the contempt giving way to something more careful.

* * *

Behind her, the small human woman is moving.

He sees it from the corner of his eye. He doesn't attend to it at first; she's nothing, a soft thing in a chair. Then something rises behind her out of the air. Huge. Pale. A blade along one arm, and above its head a wheel, eight-spoked, turning very slowly, like something from the shrine of a god he's never heard of.

It doesn't attack him. It turns its back on him entirely. It spreads its arms over the servants cowering under the long table and the guards who have fallen and the thing in the wide hat, and sweeps them toward the stairs.

The woman is shouting. Directing it. Getting them out.

He could kill her. It would take a breath.

But the elf is the job. The elf is the lamp. And the woman, he thinks, will keep.

* * *

He reads the elf doing arithmetic.

Twenty breaths now. She's bleeding from the hand and the shoulder and the face, and her magic is still strong, but it isn't getting stronger, and the cold is in her now, he can see it in the way her fingers move. She looks at her guards, failing. She looks at the stairs, where the human woman and her pale god are getting the last of the servants down.

And then she does what the Monarch of Transfiguration said she would.

She glances down. Only for a moment. At her own shadow, stretched across the frosted flagstones under the long table.

And she says, very quietly, so quietly he almost doesn't hear it: “Mau.” ᴱˡ

*There*, he thinks. *The lamp.*

He steps back. He folds his hands. He waits, with all the patience he was chosen for, for the shadow-host to come.

* * *

He has been told about the shadow-host.

The Monarch of Transfiguration watched for days. Sent things into the valley to look: small things, birds, beetles, nothing anyone would notice. Brought back what they saw. A tall thin man. Scarred. Brown hair, going white on one side. Limps a little in the mornings. Sits on a porch with a cup of something hot and lets a woman climb into his lap. Trains in a yard with the one who killed them, and loses about half the time.

*Ashborn chose him*, the Monarch of Transfiguration said. *Ashborn gave him something, in a world that was not ours. I want to know what. And I want to know why a host would sit on a porch.*

The Monarch of Frost doesn't care why. He has been told: a tall thin man who limps in the mornings. He is ready to be elegant at a tall thin man who limps in the mornings.

The shadow under the long table ripples.
