# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 22 — Just a Tuesday

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S13E50
**Continuity state:** B+350, morning, the citadel. The Frost Monarch's point of view, continued. The shadow under the table breaks: a wheel tears up through it first, then a figure rises. Hair that looks red streaked with white. Eyes blue at the centre, ringed with orange, black where the white should be. Before the Monarch finishes lifting his hand, there's a cut on his shoulder. The figure tilts its head the way Makima does and asks if he's going to get angry. He is. The elf and the woman look at it as if it were an ordinary morning; Elmesia tells Mau to use that form to visit more often, with Yuka and DIO; Mau, already firing, doesn't answer; Yuka says she'll remind him. Mau asks what part of the city they're in. The centre. He takes the Monarch up into the sky.

*When: B+350. The same moment. The shadow under the long table has just rippled.*

The first thing out of the shadow is not a man.

It's a wheel.

It tears up through the dark like a blade through cloth. Eight-spoked, pale, enormous, turning. The shadow doesn't open to let it pass; the shadow *breaks*. It rips upward from below with a sound like tearing silk, and the long table above it goes flying, and every cup and plate on it rains across the frosted terrace in a ringing shower of broken china.

The wheel rises into the air above the wound in the dark. It hangs there, turning, turning.

And under it, rising after it, something stands up.

* * *

The Monarch of Frost has seen a great many things in his long life. He has seen Ashborn. He has seen the end of a world. He has seen the one who killed him walk into his hall with a shadow army at his back.

He is not prepared for this.

It's tall. Thin, as he was told. But he was told *brown hair going white*, and this hair is not brown. It's red. A deep, dark, wet red, like something seen through a wound, falling loose about a narrow face. And through the red, in long streaks, white. Bone white. The white of old ash. Red and white together, like blood on snow.

And the eyes.

The centre of each eye is blue. Not a blue he has a name for. A blue like the hottest part of a flame, like the sky seen from so high up that it's almost black, and it is *moving*, it's *pouring*; there is something behind it that sees everything at once and is not troubled by any of it. Around the blue, ringing it, orange. Circles of it, ring inside ring inside ring, gold and amber and the orange of embers, like the eyes of a thing that commands and has never once been disobeyed. And where the white of the eye should be, there is only black. Black as the shadow it came out of. Black to the very edges.

Blue inside orange inside black. It looks at him.

And the Monarch of Frost, who was old when this world was young, feels something he has not felt since the day the one who killed him walked into his hall.

He feels *slow*.

* * *

He moves anyway. He is the Monarch of Frost. He does not wait for anything.

His hand comes up. Not fast; elegant. That is always the point. The whole perfect line of the motion, from the shoulder through the elbow to the long fingers, already gathering the cold, already beginning the thing that will turn this red-haired creature into a statue on the elf's terrace—

There is a cut on his shoulder.

He looks at it.

A line. Very fine. Very straight. From the point of his shoulder down across the top of his chest, so clean that he didn't feel it go in. It's only just beginning to open. A thin dark seam in the frost-pale skin of a king.

His hand is still only halfway up.

He didn't see anything move. The thing with the red hair is standing exactly where it stood, under its turning wheel. It hasn't lifted a hand. It hasn't taken a step.

It's looking at him.

And then, very slowly, it tilts its head.

Just slightly. To one side. Not a man's gesture; not this man's. It's someone else's, worn like a borrowed coat: the tilt of someone who finds you mildly interesting and hasn't decided yet whether you're worth her attention. The Monarch of Frost doesn't know whose gesture that is. He only knows that it's the most insulting thing anyone has ever done to him.

“Are you going to get angry?” it asks. ᴹ

* * *

He is going to get angry.

He can feel it. It's already happening. The cut on his shoulder is the smallest wound he has taken in ten thousand years and it is the only thing in the world. Someone *reached* him. Before he moved. Before he'd finished lifting his hand. Someone touched the Monarch of Frost *first*.

The cold comes off him in a wave. The frost on the terrace goes from white to blue-white to something that cracks the flagstones. The air in front of his face fills with glittering points of ice. The guards at the edge of the terrace stagger back from him with frost forming in their eyebrows.

The thing with the red hair watches all of this happen.

It doesn't look worried. It looks like it was expecting exactly this, and it's a little disappointed to have been right.

“Yes,” it says. “You are.” ᴹ

* * *

That's when he sees the women's faces.

The elf, bleeding against the broken parapet. The small human, halfway up the stairs again, having come back up after getting the last servant down, with her pale god towering behind her.

He expects terror. He has earned terror. *This* has earned terror. A thing with blood-red hair and eyes like a hole into a furnace, which came out of the dark under a breakfast table and cut a king before he could move.

They don't look afraid.

The elf looks *annoyed*. Mildly. The way she looked at him when he froze her fountain. She looks at the red-haired thing with its wheel and its burning eyes, and the expression on her face is the expression of a woman whose guest has arrived in the middle of a crisis wearing something unsuitable.

And the human woman. The little one. She looks at it, and her face goes soft and furious at the same time, the way the face of a wife goes when her husband walks in late, covered in mud, with no explanation.

As though this were normal.

As though this were a thing that happens. On a morning. Any morning. As though, wherever these women come from, a creature with the eyes of a devil tearing up out of the floor is just a slightly inconvenient way to start the day.

* * *

“Mau,” says the elf. ᴱˡ

The red-haired thing doesn't look at her. It's still looking at him.

“That's a very good look,” says the elf. “When this is over, I want you to use it more often. To come and see me. With Yuka. And DIO. You can come out from under my table every week if you like. I'll have the china replaced with something cheaper.” ᴱˡ

The red-haired thing doesn't answer.

It has lifted one hand. Two fingers out. Thumb up. Like a child pretending to hold a weapon.

“He's not listening,” says the human woman, from the stairs. ʸ

“He's very rude when he's working,” the elf agrees. ᴱˡ

“I'll remind him,” the human woman says. ʸ

“*Thank* you.” ᴱˡ

* * *

“Bang,” says the thing with the red hair. ᴹ

There's a hole in the Monarch of Frost's thigh.

He doesn't see it happen. He doesn't feel it go in. He just finds, all at once, that there's a hole the size of a coin through his leg, front to back, clean as a drill, and that his leg doesn't want to hold him.

He stops.

He doesn't decide to stop. He *has* to stop. That's what he is. When he's wounded, the cold closes the wound. The ice comes up from inside and seals it and knits it and he is whole again, perfect again, elegant again. But to do it he has to stop. To turn inward. For a breath.

He turns inward. The hole closes.

“Bang,” says the thing, while he's still closing it. ᴹ

His shoulder. The other one.

He stops. Closes it.

“Bang.” ᴹ

His side.

He stops. Closes it.

And he understands, closing his side, with the humiliating clarity of a king who has just realised that he's been dancing to someone else's music, that the thing isn't trying to kill him. Not yet. It's trying to make him *stop*. Over and over. Every time he begins to move, every time he lifts a hand, there's a new hole, and he has to stop and close it, and while he's closing it, the thing is—

The thing is *counting*.

He can see its lips moving. Very slightly. Under the blood-red hair.

* * *

“Elmesia,” says the thing. Its voice is calm. ᴹ

“Yes?” ᴱˡ

“What part of the city are we in?” ᴹ

The elf looks at it as though it's asked something very stupid.

“The centre,” she says. “The very top. Everything goes down from here. Every street. Every house.” ᴱˡ

The thing with the red hair considers this.

Then it looks up.

* * *

The Monarch of Frost feels the ground go out from under him.

Not the ground. The *sky*. The sky under his feet, which he didn't know was there, which he didn't know could *be* there, tilting. And then something taking hold of him. All of him. Not with hands. With something like hands, something that closes around the whole of him at once the way a fist closes round a stone. He fights it. It's like fighting a mountain.

He goes up.

The terrace drops away beneath him. The citadel. The white towers with their gold tips. The rings of the city, going down and down the hill, every roof, every street, every person in every street staring up.

And beside him, rising with him, hanging in the empty blue air above the city with its wheel turning slowly overhead and its red and white hair streaming in the wind, the thing with the devil's eyes.

It isn't looking at him any more.

It's looking down. At the city. At all of it. At every roof and every street and every person.

Then it looks back at him. And tilts its head again, just slightly, in that borrowed way.

“Up here,” it says, “nobody's underneath.” ᴹ

And raises two fingers.
