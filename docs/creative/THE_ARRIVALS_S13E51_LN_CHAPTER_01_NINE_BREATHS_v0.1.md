# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 23 — Nine Breaths

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S13E51
**Continuity state:** B+350, morning, the sky above Thalion. Mau's point of view. He holds the Frost Monarch up where nothing is underneath, on Sky and Authority, and buys the Exchange recharge with Bang: every shot makes the Monarch stop and close the wound. The Monarch's cold can't get through Mau's neutral, so he builds a cage of ice in the air instead; Mau shoots through it. On the eighth breath Jin-Woo's soldier pulls, hard: an ambush, many of them, in the hills. Mau understands the plan. He drops past the terrace long enough to tell Yuka, keeping the rule; she says go. On the ninth breath he takes the Monarch with him through the soldier in Jin-Woo's shadow.

*When: B+350. The same morning, a few hundred feet above the citadel of Thalion. Below, the whole city is looking up.*

*One.*

He counts it the way he counted in the duel. Not aloud. In the place under his breathing, where Noise keeps the numbers.

The Monarch hangs in front of him in the empty air, held. Sky under its feet, a floor where there was no floor. Authority around the whole of it, the grip he learned on brooms and cups and carts, closed now on a king. It fights the grip. It's like holding a falling mountain by the edges. Something behind his eyes starts to hurt, the bright hard ache he knows from the warehouse. He doesn't let go.

Below them, the city. Every roof. Every street. Every face turned up.

Nothing underneath them now but sky.

* * *

*Two.*

The cut on its shoulder has closed. He watched it close: the frost coming up from inside, filling the line, smoothing it over. The Monarch looks at the place where the cut was, and then at him, and its face, which is long and pale and very beautiful in a way that has nothing kind in it, does something he recognises.

He's seen it in a mirror. It's the face of something that has been reached first and will never forgive it.

*Thank you, Jin-Woo*, he thinks.

The Monarch opens its mouth to say something. Mau doesn't let it.

“Bang.” ᴹ

Its thigh. It stops. Closes.

* * *

*Three.*

The cold comes for him.

He can feel it coming. A wave of it, out from the Monarch in every direction, a cold so deep it isn't temperature any more, it's a kind of absence. The air between them goes white. The moisture in it turns to glittering dust and then to needles and then to a wall of ice building itself out of nothing, racing toward him across the empty air.

It stops at the neutral.

He's had it on since the gate in the yard, since before the island, always, the way a person always has their eyes open. Thin. Cheap. A finger's width of space that never closes. The ice reaches that finger's width and simply doesn't arrive. It piles up against the nothing, layer on layer, a frozen shell around him with him perfectly still inside it, untouched.

The Monarch stares.

“Bang,” Mau says, through the ice. ᴹ

The shot goes out through the shell like a needle through paper. Into the Monarch's shoulder. The other one. It stops. Closes.

* * *

*Four.*

The form is steadier than it's ever been.

He can feel it. Last time, in the warehouse yard, it wobbled; Noise had to hold it together with both hands, so to speak, and it cost him a week of Shoko looking at him like he might break. Now it sits on him like his own skin. Whatever it's doing to his hair and his eyes, he can't see it and doesn't care. But he can feel *her*, in the deep still place in his chest, holding the sea, and through her, the model the form was built from. Not a copy any more. The source.

*You're doing this,* he thinks at her. *Some of it.* ᴹ

*I'm holding the sea,* Makima says, from in there. *That's all. The rest is yours.* ᴹᵏ

He doesn't believe her. He doesn't argue.

“Bang.” ᴹ

Its side.

* * *

*Five.*

The Monarch changes tactics.

It's clever. Not clever like Jin-Woo said the Transfiguration one was clever; clever like a duellist. It stops trying to reach him. It starts building. The air around both of them fills with ice, not aimed at him but *around* him, pillars and bars and lattices of it, a cage the size of a house hanging over the city, closing in from every side, so that whatever he does next, he does it from inside a prison.

“Bang,” Mau says, and makes a hole in the cage. ᴹ

The cage closes the hole. The Monarch hasn't stopped. It didn't need to; the cage isn't its body.

He sees that, and adjusts.

“Bang.” ᴹ

The Monarch's hand. The one building the cage. It stops. It closes the wound. And for the length of that breath, the cage stops growing.

* * *

*Six.*

He looks down.

He doesn't mean to. It's the eyes; they go where they want. Down, through the ice, through the empty air, to the terrace at the top of the citadel, a small white square far below with tiny figures on it.

And one of them in colour.

She's at the parapet. He can see her from here. He'd be able to see her from anywhere. She's leaning over the stone with both hands on it, staring up at him, a small bright point of everything-that-isn't-grey in the middle of a white city. She's wearing his jacket. On her, it's a colour.

Elmesia beside her, bleeding, a hand on her shoulder.

He looks back at the Monarch.

“Bang.” ᴹ

* * *

*Seven.*

It's going to work. He can feel it working. The Monarch is slower now; not much, but enough. Every hole costs it something to close. Its face has gone from fury to something colder, something that's beginning to understand that it is being *managed*, and hates that more than the cut on its shoulder.

Two more breaths. Then he can take it somewhere with nothing in it for miles and finish this.

He reaches, already, for the Exchange. For the door. Ready for nine.

* * *

*Eight.*

Jin-Woo's soldier pulls.

Not a tug. Not even a pull. A *wrench*, so violent that he nearly loses his grip on the Monarch. North. Far north. The hills. Two ridges meeting. A soldier in a shadow, in the shadow of a man, and the man is—

He reads it. Through the soldier, through the shape of what it's close to. The way he read the city through Elmesia's.

Not one. *Many.* Cold and heat and swarm and stone and something vast and patient and so old it's barely a shape at all. And at the centre of them, alone, Jin-Woo, fighting.

He understands.

He understands all of it, hanging in the sky over a white city with a king held in his fist. The Monarch in front of him was never the point. It was the lamp. The bait in the window. Sent to pull him south while the rest of them waited in the north for the one man they actually wanted, alone, three days' walk from anyone who could help him.

*He'll try to split us*, Jin-Woo said, in another conversation, on another night. *He'll try to make sure we're never in the same place when they come.*

The Monarch is watching his face.

It sees him understand. And it smiles. A thin, cold, beautiful smile.

* * *

He doesn't answer the smile.

He drops.

Straight down, through the air, through the hole in the ice cage, dragging the Monarch with him in the grip, Sky folding away under them and refolding lower, lower, until they're level with the parapet of the citadel for one breath, close enough to see her face.

She's already looking at him.

“Jin-Woo,” he says. “It's a trap. The rest of them are on him. I'm going.” ᴹ

The rule. As soon as he can. Before, if he can.

She doesn't argue. She doesn't ask. She just looks at him with her hands white on the stone, and nods once, hard.

“Go,” she says. ʸ

“I'll come to you.” ᴹ

“*Go.*” ʸ

* * *

*Nine.*

He finds Jin-Woo's soldier. North. Far. In the shadow of a man surrounded.

He doesn't let go of the Monarch.

He goes, and takes it with him.
