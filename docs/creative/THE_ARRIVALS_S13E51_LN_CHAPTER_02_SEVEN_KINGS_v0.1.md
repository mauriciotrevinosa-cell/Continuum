# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 24 — Seven Kings

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 24
**Source episode:** S13E51
**Continuity state:** B+350, morning, the northern hills where two ridges meet. Jin-Woo's point of view. Minutes after Mau vanished south, they come for him: Beasts, Plagues, Iron Body, Transfiguration, the giant of the Beginning, White Flames, and, last and furthest back, Destruction. Seven. His hundred rise. He fights well; he is not at his peak, and he knows exactly how long he can hold. Transfiguration wears Hae-In's face and he doesn't look. He feels Mau's soldier in his shadow and thinks: don't come. Then the soldier is gone, and Mau is standing where it stood.

*When: B+350. A few minutes after Mau vanished south. The coat Makima left on the grass is still lying beside the cold fire.*

They come out of the ground.

That's how he knows it was planned. Not over the ridge. Not out of the haze. Out of the *ground*, in a ring around the place where two ridges meet, as if the hills themselves had been holding their breath for three days and have finally let it out.

He knows every one of them.

He doesn't need to look. He looks anyway. He makes himself. If he's going to die on a hill, he'll do it with his eyes open.

* * *

The Monarch of Beasts, first, because it's always first. Half a man, half a wolf, already moving, already snarling.

The Queen of Plagues, rising out of a cloud of her own making, a humming darkness of a million wings that blots out the dawn.

The Monarch of Iron Body, huge and slow and grey as a cliff, standing up out of the hillside with earth sliding off his shoulders.

The Monarch of Transfiguration, at the edge of the ring, hands folded in his sleeves, face rippling.

The giant. The Monarch of the Beginning. Not standing up so much as *unfolding*, out of the ridge to the east, rising and rising until his head is level with the clouds and his eyes look down at Jin-Woo like a man looking down at an ant.

The Monarch of White Flames, already burning, the grass around him going black in a widening circle.

And last, furthest back, on the crest of the north ridge, not moving at all, not doing anything at all, just *watching*: the one he's afraid of.

Destruction.

* * *

Seven.

He counts them the way you count your fingers after an explosion. Seven kings he killed once, one by one, in a world that was ending, each one of them nearly killing him first. Seven, on one hill, at one time, for one man.

Not eight. The proud one isn't here. The proud one is in a white city to the south, being the bait.

*Mau*, he thinks, and the thought is very calm. *You went. Good. You went where you were needed. Stay there.*

“Shadow Monarch,” says Transfiguration, from the edge of the ring. Its voice is thin and dry and patient. “We've missed you.” ʸᵒ

He doesn't answer.

He lets his shadow go.

* * *

His hundred rise.

Not the way Mau's soldiers rise, ordinary and faceless and quiet. His hundred are the ones he chose. Each one kept for a reason. Each one with a name, or a face, or a debt. Knights and mages and great beasts and things that were once kings in their own right. His Igris. His Beru. Rising out of the dark around his feet in a great black tide, row on row, filling the hollow between the ridges, until he's standing in the middle of an army and the army is standing in a ring facing outward at a ring of kings.

The Monarch of Beasts laughs.

“A hundred,” it says. “You used to have *thousands*.” ᴿᵏ

“I used to have a world to save,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“And now?” ᴿᵏ

He thinks about a porch. About a boy throwing himself at his legs and having to be peeled off by a knight. About a woman with her forehead against his at a gate.

“Now I have a family,” he says. ᴶʷ

And the hill explodes.

* * *

He fights well.

He knows he fights well. He doesn't need anyone to tell him. He's been fighting kings since before most of the people in that valley were born, and he knows how. Where to stand. When to move. How to set his hundred so that each one covers another and the gaps close before anyone can use them.

Beasts comes first, always, and he meets it with his daggers and his Igris, and Beasts goes back with a long line opened across its ribs.

Plagues comes with her swarm and his Beru meets it, his own ant king, and the swarm can't sting what has no blood, and the queen screams.

Iron Body comes slow and certain as a landslide, and four of his heaviest stand in its path and are broken, and dissolve back into his shadow, and he feels them come home.

White Flames burns. He doesn't let it reach him. That's all he can do with White Flames: not let it reach.

He holds them. For one minute. For two.

* * *

He isn't at his peak.

He knows it. He's known it since the day he chose to come here, to the valley, to a porch and a family and a life. He gave things up to do that. Power he used to have. A ceiling he used to be able to break. He doesn't regret it. He'd make the same choice a thousand times.

But he knows how long he can hold seven kings with what he has now.

Not long.

* * *

Transfiguration wears Hae-In's face.

Of course it does. He was waiting for it. It comes walking through the battle as though the battle weren't there, untouched, unhurried, in Hae-In's body, with Hae-In's walk, holding out Hae-In's hands.

“Jin-Woo,” it says, in Hae-In's voice. “Please. Stop. Come home.” ʸᵒ

He doesn't look at it.

He keeps his eyes on Beasts, and on the swarm, and on the cliff of Iron Body coming again, and on the crest of the north ridge where the one he's afraid of hasn't moved. He doesn't look at his wife's face on a thing that killed thousands.

It hurts anyway. It's meant to.

* * *

He feels Mau's soldier.

It's still there. In his shadow, under everything. The quiet one Mau placed weeks ago, *so you know where I am, if you need to*. It hasn't called. It doesn't call unless he's in immediate danger, and he is, he's been in immediate danger for two minutes, and it's been telling Mau so this whole time, and there's nothing he can do to stop it.

*Don't come*, he thinks at it. Uselessly. It isn't listening. It doesn't listen. It just tells.

*Don't come. You're with her. You're in the city. One of them is in the city. Stay. Stay there. Don't come here. Don't stand in front of this. Don't—*

The soldier is gone.

* * *

He feels it go. A small cold absence in his shadow, where something was.

And in the same instant, three paces to his left, where the soldier was standing in the dark under him, something is standing in the open air instead.

He turns.

He doesn't mean to. It's the one thing in the whole battle he didn't mean to turn toward.

It's Mau.

Or it's what Mau looks like when he's pulled everything out. Jin-Woo saw it at dawn, at the fire, and still isn't ready. Red hair, wet-dark, streaked with white. Eyes blue at the centre, ringed in orange, black where the white should be. A wheel turning over his head. And one hand closed, like a fist round a stone, on the throat of the Monarch of Frost.

Mau looks around the hollow. At the seven. At the army. At Transfiguration in Hae-In's body. At the giant, at the swarm, at the fire, at the crest of the north ridge.

Then he looks at Jin-Woo.

And Jin-Woo, who has been calm for two minutes in the middle of seven kings, feels the calm go out of him all at once, like water out of a cracked jar.

Because he knows that look. He's worn it. He wore it for years.

It's the look of a man deciding to stand in front.
