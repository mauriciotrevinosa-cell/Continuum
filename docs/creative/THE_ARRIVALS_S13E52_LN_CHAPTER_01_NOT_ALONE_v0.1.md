# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 25 — Not Alone

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 25
**Source episode:** S13E52
**Continuity state:** B+350, morning, the hollow between the ridges. Mau's point of view. He arrives thinking he's dragged the only Monarch into the middle of nowhere and finds seven more around Jin-Woo. He throws the Frost Monarch down among them and steps in front of Jin-Woo. Jin-Woo sees him decide it and loses his calm: "No. No, no, no. He is not going to die alone." Furious, he steps up beside Mau, not behind him. Mau calls his named shadows out: Beru, Igris, Bellion, Kamish, Kaisel, Tusk. Then, last, Rime: the copy of the Frost Monarch from S8. The Frost Monarch sees himself come out of Mau's shadow.

*When: B+350. The ninth breath. The same morning, three days' walk north of the city.*

He comes out of the dark into noise.

That's the first thing. The city was quiet, up in the sky; just wind and ice and a king breathing. This is the opposite. This is a hollow between two ridges full of roaring. A swarm, somewhere above, humming so loud it's in his teeth. Fire, crackling. Stone grinding against stone. Steel. The sound of a hundred shadows fighting, which isn't a sound at all but a pressure, a deep dark hum under everything else.

And the eyes, wide open, doing what they do.

He reads it in one breath.

* * *

He thought he'd brought the only one.

He really did. All the way through the dark, holding the Frost Monarch by the throat in Authority's fist, he thought: *one*. One king in a city, sent to pull him south. And the rest, the ones Jin-Woo was afraid of, the *many* he'd felt through the soldier, he thought maybe three. Maybe four. He thought he'd come out into a fight Jin-Woo was losing slowly, and turn it.

He counts.

A wolf-thing, savage, already turning toward him. A queen in a dress made of insects, high in a humming cloud. Something like a cliff that walks. A thin shape at the edge of everything with a face that won't stay still. A giant, rising out of the eastern ridge so tall its head is in the low cloud. A figure made of fire. And on the crest of the northern ridge, very still, very far, something so vast and so old the eyes almost can't hold it. It isn't doing anything. It's just watching.

Seven.

Seven, and the one in his hand.

And in the middle of them, in a ring of black shadow-soldiers holding the line, alone, Jin-Woo.

* * *

He doesn't think about it.

He's past thinking about it. Something older than thinking has already decided, the thing that decided in a Trial four hundred and thirty-seven times, the thing that kept him in a room with a bed for a year rather than let anyone else walk through the door.

He throws the Frost Monarch down.

Not gently. Out of Authority's grip, across the hollow, into the middle of the ring of kings, where it hits the ground hard enough to crack the earth and lies there, for half a breath, stunned. Then it's up, with frost spreading from its feet and its beautiful face twisted, and it's staring at him across the battle with a hatred so pure it's almost restful.

He doesn't look at it.

He steps forward. Past the last of Jin-Woo's shadows. Out of the ring. Into the open ground between Jin-Woo and seven kings.

In front.

* * *

“No,” says Jin-Woo. ᴶʷ

Mau doesn't turn round.

“No,” Jin-Woo says again, and his voice is wrong. Mau has never heard it like this. Jin-Woo, who is always calm, who stood in the clinic and said *I killed them* in a voice like a closed door. His voice is shaking.

“No, no, no,” Jin-Woo says. “*No*.” ᴶʷ

Mau hears him move. Hears him come out of the ring of his own army, fast, faster than a man that size should move. Feels him arrive, not behind him. *Beside* him. Shoulder to shoulder in the open ground, so close their arms touch.

He turns his head.

Jin-Woo isn't looking at him. Jin-Woo is looking at the seven kings, and the eighth, and the one on the northern ridge, and his face is a thing Mau has never seen on it before. Not calm. Not grief. Rage. White, total, shaking rage.

“He is not,” Jin-Woo says, to all of them, to the hills, to something older than the hills, “going to die *alone*.” ᴶʷ

* * *

The hollow goes quiet.

Not silent. The swarm still hums. The fire still crackles. But the kings have stopped moving. All of them. They're looking at the two of them standing side by side in the open ground.

“You idiot,” Jin-Woo says, very low, not taking his eyes off the kings. “You absolute idiot. You saw seven of them and you *walked out in front*.” ᴶʷ

“You were alone,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I was *fine*.” ᴶʷ

“You were alone,” Mau says again. ᴹ

Jin-Woo's jaw works.

“So are you,” he says. “So we're both alone. Together. Is that the plan?” ᴶʷ

Mau almost laughs.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

And lets his shadow go.

* * *

They come up out of the ground around him.

Not the ordinary ones. Not the faceless soldiers who hold walls and carry stretchers and sit in shadows waiting to be needed. These are the ones with names.

Beru first, because Beru is always first. Up out of the dark beside Mau's right hand, huge and armoured, wings snapping open, many eyes taking in the hollow, the swarm, the queen in her humming cloud. He's still wearing both hats. Mau didn't have the heart to tell him to take them off.

“My king,” Beru says. And then, looking up at the queen: “Ah.” ᴮᵉ

Igris, silent, his sword already drawn. Bellion, tall and dark, a step behind Igris, the way he always stands. Tusk, robed, his hands already moving in the shapes of something old and cruel. Kamish, rising last of the first rank and largest, wings spreading wider than the hollow, the great dragon's head lifting toward the northern ridge and the vast still thing watching from it.

And above them all, coming down out of the sky where he'd been circling the hills since the city, Kaisel. Landing behind Mau with a sound like a falling building. Lowering his head.

The kings look at them.

* * *

There's one more.

He hasn't called it in a long time. Not since the gate where he got it, the one where he had to be faster than the levels changing, where he thought he'd killed Makima and found out afterward he hadn't. He's kept it folded in the dark, the way you keep a thing you don't quite know what to do with.

He calls it now.

It rises out of his shadow slowly. Tall. Thin. Perfectly upright. Every line of it elegant, the way a blade is elegant, or a winter morning. Frost spreads from its feet across the grass. Its face is long and pale and beautiful, and there is nothing kind in it at all.

He named it Rime.

It's a copy. A pattern. A shape the gate built out of something it had seen, and he raised it, and it's his. It isn't the real thing. It never was.

But it looks exactly like the real thing.

* * *

The Frost Monarch sees it.

Mau watches it happen. Across the hollow, among the kings, the Monarch of Frost, who was getting up, who was gathering the cold to come at him, stops. Completely. As if someone has cut every string.

It's looking at Rime.

At itself. At its own face, its own height, its own elegance, its own frost spreading from its own feet. Standing behind Mau. In Mau's shadow-army. Among Mau's *soldiers*.

Its face goes through something Mau has never seen a face go through. Not fear. Not rage. Something underneath both. Something like a man looking in a mirror and finding his reflection belongs to somebody else.

And around it, one by one, the other kings turn and look too.

At Rime. At the Frost Monarch. At Rime again.

Mau feels it go through the hollow. A change in the air. Something the eyes can read even if he couldn't put a name to it. The seven of them, the eight, all at once, understanding something.

That the thing standing in front of them can do that. Can take one of them and make it *his*.

Even a copy. Even a shadow of a shadow. It doesn't matter. They're looking at one of their own in the army of the man they came to kill.

On the northern ridge, very far, the vast still thing that hasn't moved since he arrived, moves.

Just its head. Just slightly. Turning toward him.

* * *

“Mau,” Jin-Woo says, very quietly, beside him. ᴶʷ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

“That's the Frost Monarch.” ᴶʷ

“It's a copy.” ᴹ

“I know it's a copy.” ᴶʷ

Jin-Woo is quiet for a moment. He's still angry. Mau can feel it coming off him like heat off a stone in the sun. But there's something else under it now.

“Where,” Jin-Woo says, “did you *get* that.” ᴶʷ

“Long story.” ᴹ

“Tell me later.” ᴶʷ

“If there's a later.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo turns his head and looks at him for the first time since he stepped up beside him.

“There's a later,” he says. ᴶʷ

And the wolf-thing comes, howling, and the hollow explodes again.
