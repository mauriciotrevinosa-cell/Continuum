# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 26 — The First One Down

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 26
**Source episode:** S13E52
**Continuity state:** B+350, morning, the hollow. Transfiguration, having seen Rime, leaves at once without fighting. The Monarch of Beasts comes for Mau. Out here nobody is underneath for miles, so Mau can finally open Shrine. With Jin-Woo pinning the Monarch and the Shrine cutting faster than it can stop to heal, it dies. Mau raises it with the extraction Noise designed for Monarchs and never tested: the shadow comes up weak, half-formed, barely holding its shape, but it rises, and it kneels. Something not even Ashborn did. Plagues, Iron Body, the giant and White Flames begin to flee. The Frost Monarch does not. Destruction comes down off the ridge.

*When: B+350. The same morning. Perhaps a minute after the ninth breath.*

The thin one leaves first.

Mau sees it go. At the edge of the hollow, the shape with the face that won't stay still: it's looking at Rime. It looks for exactly one breath. Then it unfolds its hands from its sleeves, turns, and walks away into the haze to the west, unhurried, as though it has just remembered somewhere else it needs to be.

Nobody stops it. Nobody tries.

“Transfiguration,” Jin-Woo says beside him, very low. “He's seen what he came to see.” ᴶʷ

“He's not going to fight?” ᴹ

“He never fights,” Jin-Woo says. “He watches. And then he leaves, and then he plans.” ᴶʷ

Mau files it. Somewhere he keeps things he'll need later.

* * *

The wolf comes.

It doesn't plan. It doesn't watch. It's been fighting Jin-Woo's hundred for two minutes and it's bleeding from the long line Jin-Woo opened across its ribs, and it has seen Rime, and something in it has decided that the answer to what it saw is to tear out the throat of the thing that made it.

It comes across the hollow on all fours, howling, faster than anything Mau has fought since the duel.

The eyes read it. Low. Fast. Coming for his right side, because his right side is where his arm was broken twice in a room with a bed, and something in the wolf can smell an old wound the way a dog can smell fear.

He isn't there.

He's turned, the shoulder roll Jin-Woo taught him on the first morning, and the claws go past where he was. And Jin-Woo is already there, on the wolf's other side, and the weight comes down out of the air, Jin-Woo's own authority, nothing like Mau's, a crushing hand that pins the wolf to the earth.

It snarls. It heals. Mau can see it healing: the line across its ribs knitting from the inside, the way the Frost Monarch's wounds knit. It has to stop to do it. Even pinned, it stops.

*Like the other one*, Mau thinks. *They all heal. They all have to stop.*

*So don't let it stop long enough.*

* * *

He looks at the hollow.

Really looks. With the eyes all the way open. North, south, east, west, as far as they'll reach. Grass. Rock. Wind. Hills, going on and on. Not a house. Not a road. Not a single heartbeat for miles in any direction except the ones in this hollow.

Nobody underneath.

He's been carrying it for days. Since the city, since the sky, since he hung over a white city full of people and couldn't let himself do anything bigger than a finger-gun. Holding it closed. Holding it small.

He doesn't have to hold it any more.

“Jin-Woo,” he says. ᴹ

“What?” ᴶʷ

“Keep it there. Don't step in.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him. Then at the wolf, pinned under the weight. Then back at him.

He doesn't ask. He just nods, and steps back, out of the space between Mau and the wolf, and holds the weight down with both hands.

* * *

Mau opens the Shrine.

Not all of it. He isn't stupid. Makima is holding the sea, and he can feel her hold it, steady and absolute, but he can feel too how much the form is pulling, and the wheel, and everything he's spent since dawn. Not the whole domain. Not the thing he built in the duel. Just enough. A small one. A shrine the size of the hollow, with its edges drawn exactly where the eyes tell him they need to be, with Jin-Woo outside it and the wolf inside it and nothing else.

It goes up around the wolf like a held breath.

And then it cuts.

* * *

It doesn't stop.

That's what the Shrine is. That's what it's always been. Not one cut, not ten. *All* of them. Dismantle and Cleave over and over, from every direction at once, faster than anything can turn inward to heal, faster than anything can stop. The wolf heals the first cut and the second is already there. It heals the second and the tenth is already there. It can't stop. It can't turn inward. There's nothing left to turn inward *to*.

It screams.

Then it doesn't.

* * *

He closes the Shrine.

It takes more than he expected. He feels it come out of him as the walls come down: a bright hard ache behind his eyes, a hot wet line under his nose that he wipes away without looking at it. Inside him, very deep, he feels Makima's hand tighten on the sea.

*Steady*, she says, from in there. *It's holding. Go on.* ᴹᵏ

He looks at what's left on the ground.

* * *

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“I know what you want,” Noise says. ᴺ

“Can you?” ᴹ

A pause. A long one.

“I've never tried it,” Noise says. “I built it a long time ago. The extraction. For things like this. Kings. Things from other worlds. I built the plans and I put them away because you never needed them.” ᴺ

“I need them now.” ᴹ

“It might not work. It's never been tested. It might come out wrong. Or weak. Or not at all.” ᴺ

“Try,” Mau says. ᴹ

* * *

He holds out his hand over what's left of the Monarch of Beasts.

He's done this six thousand times. More. He knows how it feels: the reaching down into the dark for what's left, the pulling, the shape coming up out of nothing and standing and kneeling. He knows the word.

It doesn't feel like that.

It feels like pulling a cart up a hill with a broken axle. Like the word is too big for his mouth. He reaches down and what's there is *heavy*, so heavy, heavier than anything he's ever raised, and it doesn't want to come, and the plans Noise built are straining at every joint like a bridge with too much on it.

“Arise,” he says. ᴹ

His voice cracks on it.

* * *

It rises.

Slowly. Badly. Not the way his soldiers rise, clean and whole and ready. It comes up out of the ground like something being dragged: a shape of darkness, roughly a wolf, roughly a man, the edges of it smoking and unclear, flickering as if it might come apart any second. It's barely half the size the living thing was. It's barely there at all.

But it rises.

And it turns its smoking, unclear head toward Mau, and looks at him with two dim sparks where its eyes should be.

And it kneels.

* * *

The hollow goes completely still.

He feels it. Every king left standing. The queen in her humming cloud. The cliff that walks. The giant with his head in the clouds. The thing made of fire.

They're looking at the shape kneeling at his feet. At one of their own. At a king, killed and raised and kneeling in front of the man they came to kill, in less time than it takes to say his name.

Mau doesn't know what they know. He doesn't know their history, their wars, the long dark ages before Jin-Woo. He only knows what he reads on them through the eyes: a single shared understanding, going through all of them at once, like a cold wind through grass.

Not even Ashborn did this.

The queen breaks first.

Her swarm comes apart. Not attacking. *Fleeing*. Pouring away north and west in a great humming river, and her inside it, gone before Mau can turn his head. Then the cliff, turning, grinding, walking away east with the earth falling off its shoulders. Then the fire, guttering, rising, going. Then the giant, folding, sinking back into the eastern ridge the way it came, as if it had never been there.

Gone. All of them. In the space of three breaths.

All but one.

* * *

The Frost Monarch hasn't moved.

It's standing in the middle of the hollow where Mau threw it. Frost spreading from its feet. Its beautiful face very still. It's looking at Rime, behind Mau. At its own face in another man's army.

It doesn't run.

Mau sees the decision go through it. He's seen it before, in a mirror, on the worst days. *I will not leave this to anyone else. I will not let anyone see me run.* Pride. Pure and cold and absolute, the thing Jin-Woo said it was made of.

It's going to stay.

“That's its mistake,” Jin-Woo says beside him, very quietly. ᴶʷ

Mau doesn't answer.

Because on the northern ridge, the vast still thing that has done nothing at all since he arrived, that has watched and watched and not moved except to turn its head, has stood up.

And is coming down.
