# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 27 — Destruction

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S13E53
**Continuity state:** B+350, morning, the hollow. The Monarch of Destruction comes down off the northern ridge, not hurrying. Jin-Woo goes very still. Destruction doesn't attack Jin-Woo; it looks at Mau. One exchange each, a show more than a fight: Destruction's breath against Mau's Purple, meeting in the open air. Mau's left arm takes what his neutral can't hold: burned and broken from wrist to elbow, his left side seared. Destruction sees Kamish and Kaisel among Mau's shadows and calls them traitors. Mau: "I treat them better." Destruction says it will want to see him fight properly, one day, and leaves. The Frost Monarch is still standing.

*When: B+350. The same morning, a few breaths after the others fled.*

It doesn't hurry.

That's the first thing. Everything else on this hill has been fast: the wolf, the swarm, the fire, Jin-Woo, him. This doesn't hurry. It comes down off the northern ridge at a walk. Each step lands on the hillside with a weight the eyes can read from here, a pressure in the ground, as if the hill is bending a little under it and deciding not to complain.

It looks like a man. Mostly. Tall. Broad. Old in a way that has nothing to do with age. Dressed in something dark that moves like smoke. And behind it, folded, a shape the eyes keep trying to read and keep failing: something enormous, wings maybe, or a body, a dragon's body vaster than the hollow, there and not there, as if the man walking down the hill is only the part of it that fits.

Beside him, Jin-Woo has gone very still.

Not the stillness before a fight. The other kind. The kind Mau has only seen on him once before, at a fire, when he said *the one I'm afraid of*.

* * *

“That's him,” Mau says. ᴹ

“That's him,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“Can we—” ᴹ

“No,” Jin-Woo says. “Not like this. Not with what I have. Not with what *you* have, after the Shrine.” ᴶʷ

He doesn't look away from the figure on the hill.

“Don't step in front of me,” he says. “Mau. I mean it. Not this one.” ᴶʷ

Mau doesn't answer.

He doesn't step in front. He doesn't step back either. He stays exactly where he is, shoulder to shoulder, while the thing walks the last of the way down into the hollow and stops twenty paces away.

* * *

It doesn't look at Jin-Woo.

That's the strangest part. Mau expected it to. Jin-Woo is the one who killed it. Jin-Woo is the one they came for. But the Monarch of Destruction stops in the hollow and looks past Jin-Woo, past the hundred, past the Frost Monarch standing frozen in its own pride, past the smoking half-made shape still kneeling in the grass.

It looks at Mau.

The eyes try to read it. They can't. Not properly. It's like trying to read the sun. There's too much there, layered too deep, folded back into itself so many times that the eyes come back with nothing but *more*. He keeps them open anyway. He makes himself.

The Monarch looks at his red hair and his burning eyes and the wheel turning slowly over his head.

“So,” it says. “You're the one he chose.” ᴬⁿᵗ

Its voice is very quiet. It doesn't need to be loud.

“Ashborn,” it says. “A second time. In a second world.” ᴬⁿᵗ

Mau doesn't answer.

“I wanted to see,” it says. “What he'd pick. When he picked again.” ᴬⁿᵗ

It looks at him a moment longer.

Then it opens its mouth.

* * *

The breath comes out like the end of a world.

He's never seen anything like it. Not Sukuna's fire. Not Milim. Not anything. It isn't flame, exactly. It's destruction itself, the thing the Monarch is named for, poured out of its mouth in a single column that the eyes can't read at all: the light of it is wrong, the heat of it is wrong, it eats the air as it comes. The grass in its path doesn't burn. It *stops being*.

It comes at him.

And Jin-Woo is already moving, the weight already coming down, and Mau thinks, very clearly, *no, not you, not this one*, and puts his hands up.

Blue in the left. Red in the right.

He's done it a thousand times. In the yard. In a room with a bed. In a ring of standing stones with a warden coming apart around him. He brings them together.

Purple meets the breath in the open air between them.

* * *

The world goes white.

Not light. *White*. Sound and heat and pressure all at once, a wall of it, coming back at him from the place where the two things meet. He feels the neutral take it. The finger's width of space that never closes, holding.

And then not holding.

Not all the way. Just at the edge. Just on the left, where the breath is strongest, where something that eats the air eats into the space that never closes too. He feels it go. A small place on his left side where the nothing becomes something.

The breath comes through.

* * *

His arm.

His left arm, from the wrist to the elbow. He feels the bones go first. Not break. *Go*. Then the heat, which isn't heat, a wrongness that runs up through the skin like water up a cloth. And his side, the left side, where the old burns from the Trial already are, where the skin is already tight: he feels it sear, and keep searing, and keep going.

He doesn't make a sound. He doesn't have one.

The white goes away.

He's on one knee. He doesn't remember kneeling. His left arm is hanging at his side in a way arms shouldn't hang, and his left side is a single bright continuous scream, and there's something warm running down from his nose and from the corners of his eyes, and the wheel above his head is turning.

*Click.* *Click.* *Click.*

Three times. Fast. At something it's never seen before.

* * *

The Monarch of Destruction hasn't moved.

It's standing where it stood. Its breath is gone. And between them, in the open air, where the breath met the Purple, there's a hole. Not in the ground. In the *air*. A round dark place hanging in the middle of the hollow that the eyes can't look at, slowly, slowly closing.

The Monarch looks at the hole.

Then at him.

Something in its face changes. Not much. The way a face changes when it reaches for something on a shelf and finds it heavier than it expected.

“Hm,” it says. ᴬⁿᵗ

* * *

Mau gets up.

He doesn't know how. His left arm is useless. His side is screaming. Inside him, very deep, Makima's hand is tight on the sea, so tight he can feel it trembling. *Holding*, she says. *It's holding. Don't do that again.* ᴹᵏ

He gets up anyway.

RCT. Not to fix it; there isn't time to fix it, and he isn't sure he can. Just enough to stop the bleeding. Just enough to stand. He stands.

Jin-Woo's hand is on his good shoulder. Hard. Jin-Woo doesn't say anything. Mau can feel him shaking.

The Monarch looks at the two of them.

Then it looks past them. At the shadows behind Mau. At Beru, at Igris, at Bellion and Tusk and Rime. At the two great shapes behind all of them: Kamish, wings half-spread, his head lowered; Kaisel, coiled and silent.

Its eyes stop on the dragons.

* * *

“*Ah*,” it says. ᴬⁿᵗ

Very soft.

“I know those,” it says. “Those were mine.” ᴬⁿᵗ

Kamish's head comes up. Slowly. The great dragon looks across the hollow at the Monarch of Destruction, and something passes between them that Mau can't read, old and deep and complicated.

“Traitors,” says the Monarch. Not angry. Almost interested. “In your shadow. Both of them. Dragons of my blood, kneeling to the host of Ashborn.” ᴬⁿᵗ

Mau looks at Kamish. At Kaisel. At the two of them behind him, huge and dark and silent, neither of them moving away.

“They're not traitors,” he says. ᴹ

The Monarch's eyes come back to him.

“They're mine,” Mau says. “And I treat them better.” ᴹ

* * *

There's a silence.

Long. The hole in the air finishes closing. The wheel over Mau's head turns once more and goes still. Somewhere far off, the last of the queen's swarm is still humming away to the west.

The Monarch of Destruction looks at him for a long time.

Then it does something Mau doesn't expect.

It laughs.

Not loud. A short low sound, almost surprised, like something that hasn't laughed in a very long time and has forgotten how it goes.

“Better,” it says. ᴬⁿᵗ

It turns.

* * *

It doesn't attack again. It doesn't threaten. It just turns its back on the two of them, on Jin-Woo with his hand on Mau's shoulder, on the hundred and the named and the half-made king still kneeling in the grass, and starts walking back up the northern ridge.

At the top it stops. It looks back over its shoulder.

“Not today,” it says. “Today you're tired. And so is he.” ᴬⁿᵗ

A nod toward Jin-Woo.

“But one day,” it says, “I'll want to see you fight. Properly. When you've had time to become whatever he picked you to be.” ᴬⁿᵗ

It looks at him a moment longer.

“Keep my dragons well,” it says. ᴬⁿᵗ

And goes over the crest of the ridge, and is gone.

* * *

Mau stands there.

He stands there for a long time. His arm hanging. His side screaming. Jin-Woo's hand on his shoulder. The wheel still and silent overhead.

“You idiot,” Jin-Woo says finally. His voice is very low and not steady at all. “You absolute idiot. I told you not to step in front.” ᴶʷ

“I didn't step in front,” Mau says. “I stayed where I was.” ᴹ

“You put *Purple* in front of *his breath*.” ᴶʷ

“It was coming at you.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo's hand tightens on his shoulder until it hurts.

He doesn't say anything else.

* * *

In the middle of the hollow, frost spreading from its feet across the scorched and empty grass, the Monarch of Frost is still standing.

It hasn't moved through any of it. Not when the others fled. Not when Destruction came down. Not when Destruction left. It's standing exactly where Mau threw it, looking at Rime.

At itself. In his army.

And now it turns, very slowly, and looks at him.
