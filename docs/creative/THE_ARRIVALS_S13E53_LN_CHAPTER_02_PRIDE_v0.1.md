# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 28 — Pride

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 28
**Source episode:** S13E53
**Continuity state:** B+350, late morning, the hollow. The Frost Monarch stays because it cannot bear to be seen running, and because it cannot bear Rime. It comes for Mau. Rime meets it: copy against original, the same elegance on both sides. Jin-Woo pins; Mau, one-armed, cuts its shoulder again before it moves, and its pride does the rest. Mau finishes it with Bang through the core of the cold. He raises it: the second untested extraction, harder than the first; the shadow comes up weak and flickering, and kneels beside Rime, which looks exactly like it. Then Makima says enough, the form drops, and Mau goes down on the grass. Jin-Woo catches him.

*When: B+350. The same morning. The hollow is quiet now, except for the frost spreading.*

It comes for him.

He knew it would. He saw it decide, minutes ago, when the others ran. *I will not be seen running. I will not let anyone else finish this.* He knows that decision. He's made it a thousand times.

It doesn't come fast. It comes the way it does everything: elegantly. Each step a perfect line. The frost spreading ahead of it across the scorched grass in long white fans. Its face very calm and very beautiful and absolutely without mercy.

It isn't looking at him any more.

It's looking at Rime.

* * *

“It wants the copy,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“It wants me,” Mau says. “For making the copy.” ᴹ

“Same thing.” ᴶʷ

Mau's left arm is hanging uselessly at his side. His left side is a single sheet of pain. There's blood drying on his upper lip and under his eyes. Inside him, Makima's hand is shaking on the sea.

*You can't do much more*, she says. *I mean it. I can feel how much is left. It isn't much.* ᴹᵏ

*I know.* ᴹ

*Then don't.* ᴹᵏ

*One more.* ᴹ

A pause.

*One*, she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

Rime steps forward.

He doesn't order it to. He doesn't have to. It moves on its own, the way his named shadows move when they know what he wants before he does: out from behind him, past his shoulder, into the open ground between him and the king coming across the grass.

And stops.

And the two of them face each other.

* * *

He'll never forget it.

Two of the same thing, in a scorched hollow under a grey sky. The same height. The same long pale beautiful face. The same perfect upright stillness. The same frost spreading from the same feet, two fans of white reaching toward each other across the grass and meeting in the middle with a sound like ice cracking on a lake.

One of them is real. One of them is a pattern, a copy, a shape a gate built out of something it once saw.

He can't tell which is which.

Neither, he thinks, can the Monarch.

* * *

It strikes.

Fast. Faster than it struck at him in the city. Its hand comes up in that perfect line and the cold comes off it in a blade, a single sheet of white aimed at Rime's face.

Rime's hand comes up in the same line.

The same blade. The same white. The same instant.

They meet in the air between them and shatter, and the shards go everywhere, glittering, and for one breath the whole hollow is full of falling light.

The Monarch stares at its copy.

And Mau sees it. The thing Jin-Woo told him about at the fire. The thing that kept Jin-Woo alive the first time. The perfect calm cracking. The elegance starting to come apart at the edges. Because it isn't just being matched. It's being matched *by itself*. By its own face, its own hand, its own frost, in another man's army.

It can't bear it.

* * *

“Now,” Mau says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo's weight comes down.

The crushing hand out of the air, on the Monarch's shoulders, pinning it. It fights. It's strong; it's a king. But it's fighting two things at once, Jin-Woo's weight and its own face in front of it, and it can't give its whole self to either.

Mau moves.

One arm. His right. The dagger Jin-Woo taught him to hold, the grip corrected over weeks on his bad fingers. He crosses the open ground behind Rime, past Rime's shoulder, low, the way Jin-Woo showed him, and the Monarch sees him coming a quarter of a breath too late, and its hand starts to come up.

He cuts its shoulder.

The same shoulder. The same line. Before it's finished lifting its hand.

* * *

It screams.

Not pain. Mau's heard pain. This is something else. This is the sound of something being reached first, *again*, by the same man, in front of its own reflection, and having no more room inside it to hold that.

It stops fighting well.

He sees it happen. Exactly the way Jin-Woo said. The elegance goes. The perfect lines go. It turns on him, wild, all its cold at once, everything it has thrown at the one thing in the world it cannot stand, and in doing that it forgets Jin-Woo's weight, and it forgets Rime, and it forgets to protect anything at all.

Rime's hand comes up.

The Monarch's chest goes white.

* * *

Mau lifts two fingers.

He doesn't have much left. He can feel how little. The form is wavering on him now, the way it did in the warehouse yard; his hair, whatever colour it is, is starting to lose the wind that was in it. His eyes are hot and wet and blurring at the edges. Makima's hand is tight on the sea and he can feel her saying *one*.

He finds it with the eyes. Through the frost, through the white on its chest, through its cold, to the place at the centre of the cold where all the rest of it comes from. Small. Bright. Like a coal at the heart of a glacier.

“Bang,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

The Monarch of Frost goes still.

It stands there for a moment. Its face, very calm again now, very beautiful. Looking at Rime. At itself.

Then the frost under its feet stops spreading.

And it falls.

* * *

He's on his knees beside it. He doesn't remember kneeling.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“It'll be worse than the first,” Noise says. “You're nearly empty. I'm not sure the plans will hold.” ᴺ

“Try.” ᴹ

“Mau—” ᴺ

“*Try.*” ᴹ

He puts his good hand on what's left of the Monarch's chest.

* * *

It's worse than the first.

The first was pulling a cart up a hill with a broken axle. This is pulling it with a broken arm. What's down there is heavy, so heavy, heavier than the wolf, cold and proud and refusing, and the plans Noise built are groaning at every joint, and he can feel them start to give.

“Arise,” he says. ᴹ

It comes out a whisper.

* * *

It rises.

Barely. A shape of darkness, roughly tall, roughly thin, its edges flickering and unsteady as a candle in a draught. Half the size the living king was. Less. Its frost doesn't spread; there's just a faint cold around its feet that fades as soon as it reaches the grass. Two dim sparks where its eyes should be.

It looks at him.

And it kneels. Beside the smoking wolf-shape already kneeling in the grass.

Beside Rime.

Mau looks at the two of them. The copy, whole and clean and perfect, standing. The original, barely there, flickering, kneeling at its feet.

He doesn't know what to feel about that. He doesn't have anything left to feel it with.

* * *

*Enough*, Makima says. ᴹᵏ

He feels her say it. Not a request. The first time she's ever spoken to him like that, from in there, in a voice that isn't calm at all.

*Enough, Mau. Let it go. Now. Or I can't hold it.* ᴹᵏ

He lets it go.

* * *

The form goes out of him like water out of a cracked jar.

All at once. He feels his hair go still on his scalp. Feels the heat go out of his eyes and the world go back to ordinary grey, flat and soft and far away. Feels the wheel overhead stop, and fold, and sink back into the dark behind him. Feels his named shadows, one by one, Beru and Igris and Bellion and Tusk and Kamish and Kaisel and Rime and the two new ones that aren't really ones yet, sink back into the ground.

And then there's nothing holding him up at all.

He goes down.

He doesn't feel the grass. Jin-Woo catches him first. He feels that: Jin-Woo's arms, hard and shaking, getting under his shoulders before he hits the ground. Jin-Woo's voice, very close, saying something over and over that he can't make out.

He looks up at the sky. Grey. Flat. Soft.

*Yuka*, he thinks.

He can't see her from here. She's three days south. In a white city, on a terrace, with her hands on the stone.

He promised. As soon as he can.

*As soon as I can*, he thinks. *Not yet. Soon.*

And the grey goes dark.
