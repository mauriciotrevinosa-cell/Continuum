# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 29 — Someone Watching

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 29
**Source episode:** S13E54
**Continuity state:** B+350, midday, beyond the northern ridge. The War Devil's point of view (the observer who saw Shrine and the wheel in S12, V74 #19–20). She watched the whole hollow from far away. She finds the Monarch of Destruction sitting on a rock above the clouds and sits down beside him. He knows what she is. She tells him she saw the boy before, at the warehouse; that he got stronger; that the house underestimated is a mistake she won't make. He says he made it once. She suggests they might be useful to each other. He doesn't say yes. He doesn't say no.

*When: B+350. Midday. Three ridges north of the hollow, where the land rises above the cloud.*

She's been watching all morning.

That's what she does. She's good at it; better than anyone. She doesn't fight unless she has to. She doesn't lead. She watches, and she remembers, and then, when a war is ready to happen, she's already standing where it will start.

She watched the hollow from three ridges away, lying on her stomach in the long grass with her chin on her folded arms, like a girl watching a festival from a hill. She watched the kings come out of the ground. She watched the Shadow Monarch's hundred rise. She watched a tall man with red hair come out of the dark holding a king by the throat, and step out in front of seven more.

She watched the Shrine go up.

She's seen it before. Once. In a yard behind a warehouse, months ago, from a rooftop: the same man, the same Shrine, a smaller one, opened over a stretch of ground he'd checked was empty first. She remembered thinking then: *that one is careful*. She remembered thinking: *that one is going to be a problem for somebody*.

This morning he was less careful. This morning there was nobody for miles.

This morning he raised a king.

* * *

She finds the Monarch of Destruction above the clouds.

He's sitting on a rock at the very top of the highest hill in the north, where the land goes up out of the grey and into clear cold sky. Alone. His hands on his knees. Looking south, toward a valley he can't possibly see from here, as if he can.

She walks up the last of the slope and sits down on the rock beside him.

He doesn't turn his head.

“I know what you are,” he says. ᴬⁿᵗ

“Most people do, eventually,” she says. ʷᵈ

“War.” ᴬⁿᵗ

“War,” she agrees. ʷᵈ

He's quiet for a while. So is she. Below them the clouds go on forever, white and slow, and somewhere under them is a hollow full of scorched grass and a man lying in another man's arms.

* * *

“You watched,” he says. ᴬⁿᵗ

“I always watch.” ʷᵈ

“What did you see?” ᴬⁿᵗ

She thinks about it. She likes to be accurate.

“I saw him before,” she says. “In a yard. A few months ago. He opened the Shrine then, too. Smaller. Over ground he'd checked first.” ʷᵈ

“And now?” ᴬⁿᵗ

“Now he's stronger,” she says. “Not a little. A lot. He put Purple in front of your breath and it held for a moment. Nothing holds in front of your breath.” ʷᵈ

The Monarch doesn't say anything.

“And he raised two of your kind,” she says. “Badly. They came up half-made. But they came up. And they knelt.” ʷᵈ

She looks at him sideways.

“Not even Ashborn did that,” she says. ʷᵈ

* * *

He turns his head and looks at her for the first time.

His eyes are very old. Older than anything she's met in this world, except maybe the elf, and the elf hides it better.

“No,” he says. “He didn't.” ᴬⁿᵗ

“You're not angry.” ʷᵈ

“I'm interested,” he says. ᴬⁿᵗ

He looks south again.

“I came here to kill the one who killed me,” he says. “I found him weaker than I remembered. Softer. With a family. I could have done it this morning. He knew it. I knew it.” ᴬⁿᵗ

“But you didn't.” ʷᵈ

“Because of the other one,” he says. ᴬⁿᵗ

* * *

She waits.

“The house,” she says, after a while. “The one in the valley. With the elf's friend and the Shadow Monarch's family and the slime and the boy with the blindfold and that man.” ʷᵈ

“What about it?” ᴬⁿᵗ

“Everyone underestimates it,” she says. “Every time. The parliament did. The men with the forms did. You did, this morning.” ʷᵈ

He doesn't deny it.

“I won't,” she says. ʷᵈ

He looks at her.

“I've been watching that house for a long time,” she says. “Longer than you've been in this world. I know who's in it. I know what they can do and what they think they can't. I know who sleeps where and who eats with whom. I know which of them would die for which.” ʷᵈ

She pauses.

“I know what he does when someone he loves is afraid,” she says. “He steps in front. Every time. Without thinking. It's the only predictable thing about him.” ʷᵈ

“Transfiguration said the same,” the Monarch says. ᴬⁿᵗ

“Transfiguration was right,” she says. “He just didn't know what to do with it.” ʷᵈ

* * *

The Monarch of Destruction is quiet for a long time.

The clouds move below them. The wind comes up off the hills, cold and clean.

“And you do,” he says finally. ᴬⁿᵗ

“I might,” she says. “One day. With help.” ʷᵈ

She doesn't look at him when she says it. She looks at the clouds.

“We could be useful to each other,” she says. “You want to see him fight. Properly. I heard you say so. I want—” ʷᵈ

She stops. Considers.

“I want a war worth watching,” she says. “That's all I've ever wanted.” ʷᵈ

* * *

He doesn't say yes.

He doesn't say no either. He just sits on the rock above the clouds with his hands on his knees and looks south for a long time, toward a valley he can't see, where a man is lying in the grass with one arm burned and broken and a king kneeling over him in the shape of smoke.

“I'll think about it,” he says. ᴬⁿᵗ

She smiles.

That's all she needed. She's very good at waiting.

She stands up, and brushes the grass off her knees, and goes back down the hill the way she came, into the clouds, where nobody will see her until she wants them to.
