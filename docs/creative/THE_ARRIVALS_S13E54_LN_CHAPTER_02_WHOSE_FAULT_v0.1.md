# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 30 — Whose Fault

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 30
**Source episode:** S13E54
**Continuity state:** B+350, afternoon, the hollow. Mau wakes with his head on Jin-Woo's coat and Makima's empty coat folded under his burned arm. Jin-Woo has splinted the arm with a spear-shaft from his own shadows and is sitting beside him not speaking. When he does, it comes out all at once: not anger that Mau took the fight, but terror that if Mau had died it would have been his fault; he asked him to come. Mau tells him it was his choice. Then Jin-Woo thanks him: for coming, for not letting him go into another war alone. Mau says he has to go to Yuka. Jin-Woo goes home to his family.

*When: B+350. Mid-afternoon. The sun has come out over the hills for the first time all day.*

He wakes up because something is cold against his arm.

Not cold the way the Monarch was cold. Ordinary cold. Wet cloth. Someone has wrapped his left arm in a strip of something soaked in stream water, from the wrist to the elbow, and bound it to a straight length of dark wood. The wood is smooth and very old and faintly humming. A spear-shaft, he thinks. One of Jin-Woo's.

His head is on something folded. A coat. Not his.

He turns his head.

Jin-Woo is sitting beside him in the grass with his knees drawn up and his arms round them, looking at the hills. He's been there a long time. Mau can tell from the way the grass is flattened around him, and from the way he's sitting, very still, like someone who's been holding still on purpose so he doesn't do something else.

He doesn't turn round.

* * *

“How long?” Mau asks. ᴹ

His voice comes out rough. His throat is dry.

“Three hours,” Jin-Woo says. To the hills. ᴶʷ

Mau tries to sit up. His left side tells him not to, very clearly, with a sheet of pain that whites out the edges of the world. He lies back down.

“Don't,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“I'm fine.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo turns his head and looks at him.

Mau stops.

“No,” he says. “I'm not fine. My arm's broken. My side feels like it's still on fire. I think I'm about as empty as I've been since the long one. It's ugly.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him a moment longer. Then he turns back to the hills.

“Better,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

There's something under his burned arm. Something soft, folded, keeping the arm off the ground.

He looks. A coat. Long, dark, plain. Makima's. The one that fell empty to the grass at dawn when she stepped back inside him.

Jin-Woo folded it and put it under his arm.

Mau doesn't say anything about that. He doesn't know what he'd say.

Inside him, very deep, he can feel her. Holding. Tired, he thinks; if she gets tired. Her hand on the sea is steady again, but there's something different in the way she's holding it. Something careful. Like a hand that's been gripping too hard for too long and is letting itself loosen a finger at a time.

*Thank you*, he thinks at her. ᴹ

A long pause.

*You didn't listen*, she says. *I said one.* ᴹᵏ

*I did one.* ᴹ

*You did two. The Shrine and the Bang.* ᴹᵏ

*The Shrine was before you said one.* ᴹ

Another pause.

*That's a child's argument*, she says. ᴹᵏ

He almost laughs. It hurts too much.

* * *

“You should have stayed in the city,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

Mau doesn't answer.

“You should have stayed,” Jin-Woo says again. “With her. With the elf. Where you were needed. I told you. I *thought* at you. Don't come. Stay there. Stay with her.” ᴶʷ

His voice is very even. Too even. The way it was in the clinic.

“You were alone,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I was *fine*.” ᴶʷ

“You were alone against seven.” ᴹ

“I've been alone against more.” ᴶʷ

“I know,” Mau says. “That's why.” ᴹ

* * *

Jin-Woo is quiet for a long time.

Then it comes out of him.

All at once. The way Yuka's crying came out on the washroom floor: as though something that's been holding it for hours has simply given way.

“I *asked* you,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

He turns round. His face is wrong. Mau has never seen it like this. Not calm. Not angry, exactly. Something under angry.

“I asked you to come,” Jin-Woo says. “In the clinic. I asked. You were going to the city with her. To see the gardens. To stay a few days. And I asked you to come north instead. With me. Into this.” ᴶʷ

He gestures at the hollow. At the scorched grass. At the place where the breath met the Purple and the air was a hole.

“And you came,” he says. “And you stepped out in front of seven of them. And you put your hands up in front of *his* breath. And you went down in the grass with your arm like that and your side like that and blood coming out of your eyes, and for three hours I sat here, and I thought—” ᴶʷ

He stops.

“I thought,” he says, “if he dies, it's mine. If he dies here, on this hill, it's because I asked. Because I couldn't do it alone any more. Because I've got a family now and I'm not what I was, and I knew it, and I asked anyway. And she's in that city waiting for you, and she'd never know it was me. She'd just know you didn't come.” ᴶʷ

His voice breaks on the last word.

* * *

Mau doesn't say anything for a while.

He looks at Jin-Woo. At the man who has fought more wars than anyone he knows, alone at the front of every one of them. Who has never once, in all the time Mau has known him, asked anyone for anything. Who asked him.

“It's not yours,” Mau says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

“It's not yours,” Mau says again. “You asked. I said yes. I could have said no. Yuka would have let me say no. I *chose*.” ᴹ

He moves his good hand, slowly, through the grass, until it finds Jin-Woo's wrist.

“If I'd died,” he says, “it would have been mine. Not yours. I'd have died because I wanted to be here. Not because you asked.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo doesn't say anything.

“And I didn't die,” Mau says. “So it doesn't matter.” ᴹ

“It *matters*,” Jin-Woo says, very low. ᴶʷ

“Okay,” Mau says. “It matters. But it's not yours.” ᴹ

* * *

They sit there a long time.

The sun is warm on the grass. Somewhere off to the east, a bird is singing; the first sound the hollow has made all day that isn't a war.

“Thank you,” Jin-Woo says finally. ᴶʷ

Very quietly. Not looking at him.

“For coming,” he says. “For not letting me go into another one alone. I've been alone in all of them. Every war. Every king. Every time. I thought that was just—how it was. How it had to be.” ᴶʷ

He's quiet for a moment.

“You came out of the ground holding a king by the throat,” he says. “And you stood beside me.” ᴶʷ

“I stood in front of you,” Mau says. ᴹ

“You stood *beside* me,” Jin-Woo says. “I made you.” ᴶʷ

Mau laughs. It hurts. He does it anyway.

* * *

“I have to go,” Mau says, after a while. ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

“To Yuka,” Mau says. “I promised. If something happens, I tell her. As soon as I can. And then I go to her. Not back. To her.” ᴹ

“You can barely sit up.” ᴶʷ

“I don't have to sit up. I just have to find her soldier.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him for a long moment. Then he nods.

“Your Exchange is ready?” he asks. ᴶʷ

Mau checks. Nine breaths came and went hours ago. The door is there. And far to the south and west, at the end of it, a quiet soldier in a shadow on a terrace. Still now. Waiting.

“It's ready,” he says. ᴹ

“Then go,” Jin-Woo says. “And tell her I'm sorry.” ᴶʷ

“You don't have anything to be sorry for.” ᴹ

“Tell her anyway,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

* * *

He goes home himself, Jin-Woo. Mau watches him start to. He stands up in the hollow and lets his own shadow rise around him, and Mau knows where he's going without asking: back over the ridges, through the fields, past the orchard, down the path to the house where the ground slopes toward the stream. To a porch with a boy on it waiting for his father, and a woman with her hand on the boy's shoulder, and the Goddess's quiet line around all of it that nothing with harm in its heart can cross.

At the last moment, before the shadow closes over him, Jin-Woo looks back.

“Mau,” he says. ᴶʷ

“Yeah?” ᴹ

“Next time,” Jin-Woo says, “you stand beside me from the *start*.” ᴶʷ

And he's gone.

Mau lies alone in the grass with Makima's coat under his arm and the sun on his face.

He puts Makima's coat in the inventory first, with his good hand, because she'll want it back.

Then he reaches for Yuka's soldier, and goes.
