# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 31 — The Red Doesn't Suit You

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 31
**Source episode:** S13E55

*When: B+350. Sunset. Yuka hasn't left the balcony since Elmesia's healers finished with the terrace.*

She knows the moment before it happens.

She's on the balcony of the room Elmesia kept for them, the one that looks out over the whole city, with his jacket round her shoulders and her hands on the stone. She's been there since the healers finished with Elmesia's cuts and Elmesia sent her away from the terrace with *you're not helping by standing there, go and stand somewhere else*. So she came here and stood somewhere else. For hours.

The sun is going down over the rings of roofs. The light is long and gold.

And her shadow moves.

She feels it before she sees it. The soldier in it, the quiet one from the north wall, the one that's been perfectly still all day: something changes in it. Like a held breath being let go.

She turns round.

* * *

The shadow on the balcony floor opens.

It doesn't tear, the way the one under the table tore this morning, with a wheel coming up through it like a blade. It just *opens*. Soft. Like water when something surfaces. And something comes up out of it slowly, the way a person climbs out of a pool when they're too tired to climb.

He's on his knees.

That's the first thing she sees. Him, on his knees on the balcony floor, one hand flat on the stone to hold himself up. His left arm is bound to something long and dark, a spear-shaft, from the wrist past the elbow, wrapped in strips of wet cloth. The left side of his shirt is gone, burned away, and under it the skin is—she doesn't look at it. She'll look at it later. Not now.

And his hair is red.

Not all of it. Streaks of it, through the brown and the white, dark and wet-looking, like something seen through a wound. And his eyes, when he lifts his head and finds her: blue at the centre, too bright, with rings of orange round them, and the whites gone dark at the edges.

She saw him like this this morning, from the terrace, hanging in the sky over the city with a king held in his fist.

It was terrifying this morning.

It's worse now. Now he's on his knees on her balcony looking up at her with his arm on a stick.

* * *

“Hi,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is wrecked.

She doesn't move. She can't. She's standing at the parapet with his jacket round her shoulders and her hands behind her on the stone, and every part of her wants to go to him and every part of her is frozen, because she's been afraid since dawn, since the pull and the terrace and the king and the sky and *Jin-Woo, it's a trap, I'm going*, and her body hasn't caught up yet with the fact that it can stop.

He looks at her.

“Makima helped me through,” he says. “The Exchange. I didn't have enough left. Some of the form came back with me when she did. I'll—it'll go. In a minute.” ᴹ

She still doesn't move.

“I told you as soon as I could,” he says. “I'm sorry it was so late.” ᴹ

* * *

That's what does it.

Not the arm. Not the burns. Not the red in his hair or the wrong colours in his eyes. *I'm sorry it was so late.* As if he'd missed dinner.

She comes off the parapet. She crosses the balcony. She goes down on her knees on the stone in front of him so that their faces are level, and she looks at him from very close. At the streaks of red. At the blue and the orange and the dark.

She takes his face in both hands.

“You look ugly,” she says. ʸ

He blinks.

“You look *ugly*,” she says again. Her voice is shaking. “The red. In your hair. It doesn't suit you at all. And your eyes are all wrong. And your arm is on a *stick*.” ʸ

He stares at her.

Then he starts to laugh.

* * *

It isn't much of a laugh. It hurts him; she can see it hurt him, the way his breath catches on the left side. But he laughs anyway, on his knees on Elmesia's balcony with his face between her hands, red-haired and wrong-eyed and broken, laughing at her.

And as he laughs, it goes.

She watches it go. The red, first. Draining out of the streaks in his hair like dye out of cloth in water, until it's brown again, and white on the left side where it's always been white. Then the eyes. The orange rings fading, folding inward, gone. The dark at the edges going back to white. Until there's only the blue at the centre, ordinary blue, too bright from crying or laughing or both, looking at her.

“Better?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks at him for a long time.

“You looked good in both,” she says. ʸ

And he laughs again, harder, and has to stop because it hurts, and puts his good hand over hers on his face, and holds on.

* * *

She gets him onto the bed.

It takes both of them and DIO, who appears in the doorway in her hat at exactly the right moment, as if she's been waiting in the next room listening, which she has. DIO takes his good side. Yuka takes his bad side, very carefully, under the arm that isn't on a stick. Between them they get him across the room and onto the edge of the bed meant for two, and he sits there swaying slightly, looking at the balcony door with the last of the sunset in it.

“You can see the sea from here,” he says. “Elmesia said. On a clear day.” ᴹ

“It's not a clear day,” Yuka says. ʸ

“No,” he agrees. ᴹ

DIO looks at the two of them. Then she goes out, quietly, and closes the door behind her, and Yuka hears her hat bump the frame.

* * *

She sits on the bed beside him.

Close. As close as she can get without touching the burned side. Her shoulder against his good shoulder. Her hand on his knee.

“Tell me,” she says. ʸ

He tells her.

All of it. He doesn't make it smaller. She's waiting for him to; she's spent three years waiting for him to make things smaller, and she can see him start to, once, and stop. And then he just tells her.

The hollow. The seven of them. Jin-Woo alone in the middle. Stepping out in front without thinking, and Jin-Woo coming up beside him and saying *he is not going to die alone* in a voice Mau had never heard. Rime. The wolf, and the Shrine, and pulling the wolf up out of the ground with something Noise had never tested, and it coming up wrong and half-made and kneeling anyway. The thing on the northern ridge coming down. Its breath. Purple. The white. His arm.

“It got through,” he says. “The neutral. On the left. I didn't know anything could get through the neutral.” ᴹ

She doesn't say anything. She just holds his knee harder.

The Frost Monarch. The shoulder. The copy and the original, side by side. Raising it, worse than the first. Makima saying *enough*, in a voice that wasn't calm. The grey going dark. Three hours in the grass. Waking up with Makima's coat under his arm.

Jin-Woo. *If he dies, it's mine.*

“He says he's sorry,” Mau says. “I told him he didn't have anything to be sorry for. He said tell you anyway.” ᴹ

* * *

She's quiet for a long time.

“How is it,” she says finally. “Your arm. Your side. Really.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“Ugly,” he says. “The arm's broken in a lot of places. Worse than the Trial, I think. I closed the bleeding but I couldn't do anything else. The side feels like it's still on fire. Shoko's going to have to fix all of it. I'm emptier than I've been since the long one. My head hurts so much I can't see straight.” ᴹ

He pauses.

“And I was so scared,” he says. “When I saw them. All of them. Around him.” ᴹ

She closes her eyes.

“Thank you,” she says. ʸ

“For what?” ᴹ

“For not saying fine.” ʸ

* * *

She makes him lie down.

On his back, on his good side half turned, with pillows under the broken arm the way Shoko would want, the way she's seen Shoko do a hundred times. She takes his boots off. She pulls the blanket up. She doesn't let him help with any of it.

Then she lies down beside him.

Carefully. On his right, his good side. She fits herself along him without touching anything that hurts, her head on his good shoulder, her hand flat on his chest over his heart.

She feels him let go. Not the way he did in her lap that night, all at once. Slower. A long breath out, and then another, and his whole body going a little heavier against the bed, a little heavier against her.

“You came to me,” she says, very quietly. ʸ

“I said I would.” ᴹ

“Not back. To me.” ʸ

“To you,” he says. ᴹ

She puts her face in his shoulder.

Outside the balcony doors, the sun has gone down. The city is lighting up below, ring on ring of small warm lights going down the hill to the dark, and somewhere in it a fountain is still frozen solid, spraying very slightly to the left.

He's asleep before the lights reach the bottom of the hill.

She stays awake a long time after, with her hand on his heart, counting it.
