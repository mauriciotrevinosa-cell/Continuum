## Chapter 32 — What the City Owes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1

*When: B+351. The morning after. The fountain in the third ring is still frozen; Elmesia has asked the city not to thaw it yet.*

He wakes up because she's standing in the light.

It's coming in through the balcony doors, early and slanting and clean, and she's standing in it at the foot of the bed with her back to the window, waiting for him to open his eyes. He doesn't know how long she's been standing there. Long enough. She has the look of someone who's been holding very still on purpose.

She's wearing the blue thing.

He knows it's the blue thing. He'd know anyway; it's the one he carried in the inventory for three days, the one she put in his hands on the bed and said *that one, for when you get there*. It's a dress. Simple. Long. It moves when she breathes.

And it's a colour.

He can see it. On her. Where it touches her, where the light lands on it and on her at the same time, it has a colour, the way the sunset on the cape had colours where it fell on her skin. He's never seen this colour before. He doesn't have a word for it. It's deep and cool and clear, like looking down into very still water, and where it's close to her throat it's lighter, and where the light catches the fold at her hip it's almost silver.

He stares at it.

“Is that blue?” he asks. ᴹ

“That's blue,” she says. ʸ

“That's what blue looks like.” ᴹ

“On me,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't say anything for a long time. He just looks.

“I brought it,” she says, “so that when you saw the city for the first time, there'd be one thing in it you could see properly.” ʸ

* * *

Elmesia comes after breakfast.

She knocks, which she doesn't have to; it's her palace. She comes in with her hand bandaged and a fine line of healing along her cheek, in a plain dress, carrying a tray that she sets down on the table by the balcony herself instead of letting the servant behind her do it.

She looks at him propped up in the bed with his arm on its stick. She looks at Yuka beside him in the blue dress. She looks at the two of them for a long time.

“You took it up,” she says. ᴱˡ

He doesn't understand at first.

“The king,” she says. “You asked me where in the city we were. I said the centre. Everything below. And you took it up into the sky, where there was nothing underneath.” ᴱˡ

She sits down in the chair by the window.

“Nobody died,” she says. “In the city. Not one person below the citadel. The guards on the terrace, some of them are hurt, and the fountain in the third ring is frozen solid, and my terrace will need a new parapet and a new table and I'm told every cup we owned. But nobody in my city died.” ᴱˡ

She looks out of the window at the rings of roofs going down the hill.

“Because you asked where we were,” she says. ᴱˡ

* * *

“The city owes you,” she says. ᴱˡ

“It doesn't,” Mau says. ᴹ

“It does. I'm the city. I'm telling you.” ᴱˡ

“I'd hate it if you paid me.” ᴹ

“I know,” Elmesia says. “That's why I'm not going to. Not in gold. Not in land. Not in titles. You'd look at a title the way you looked at that crooked fountain.” ᴱˡ

Yuka laughs.

“I'll pay it the way I pay my friends,” Elmesia says. “Which is badly, and constantly, and without asking.” ᴱˡ

She folds her bandaged hands in her lap.

“The island,” she says. “All of you. Everyone. The word *some* in your letter is now officially *everyone*, and I'm not discussing it. And not the room I kept for you last time. A better one. There's a house on the far side of the island, on the cape where you watched the sunset. It's mine. It's always been mine. Nobody's stayed in it in four hundred years. It's yours, when you come.” ᴱˡ

Yuka has gone very still beside him.

“And this city,” Elmesia says, “whenever you want it. Every room. Every garden. Every crooked fountain. You don't write. You don't ask. You come.” ᴱˡ

She looks at Mau.

“And next time,” she says, “come through the floor *on purpose*. To visit. With her, and with DIO. I meant what I said on the terrace. I'll get cheaper china.” ᴱˡ

“I'll remind him,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I know you will,” says Elmesia, and smiles. ᴱˡ

* * *

She stays a while. She tells them the other news, too, because it's the kind of news that doesn't wait: the delegations from the sea finally signed, two days ago, before any of this. The city under the water, off the far side of the island, that has refused to speak to anyone on land for longer than Thalion has had walls.

“They'll want to meet you,” she says. “Eventually. Everyone does, eventually. Be careful. They're very polite and very wet.” ᴱˡ

Mau files that away. Somewhere he keeps things for later.

* * *

Rimuru comes through the wall at mid-morning.

Not the door. The wall. A ripple in the air beside the balcony doors, a fold in space that opens like a curtain, and Rimuru steps through it with his hand out behind him, holding someone else's.

Mau asked him. At dawn, over the link, the only line that runs between him and anyone in the house: *I'm in Thalion. I'm hurt. Not dying. The arm's bad. Can you bring Shoko?*

And Rimuru had sent back one word, *coming*, and then nothing for three hours while he did whatever it is Rimuru does to fold the distance between a valley and a white city into something you can step across.

Shoko comes through the fold behind him.

She's carrying her bag. She's wearing her coat. She stops just inside the room and looks at Mau in the bed.

At his arm, on its stick.

At the burned side.

At the dried blood still at the corners of his eyes.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't say *you idiot* or *what did you do* or *I told you*. Her face just goes very still, and then very white, and then she puts her bag down on the floor without looking where she's putting it.

“Shoko,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Don't,” she says. ˢʰ

She crosses the room.
