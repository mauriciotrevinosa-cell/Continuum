# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 79
## Chapter 33 — Home Through the Wall

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 79
**Reading order:** 33

*When: B+351. Midday. Elmesia has had everyone else sent out of the room except Yuka, Rimuru and DIO, who refused to go.*

Shoko doesn't talk while she works.

She never does, on the bad ones. He's learned that. On the small things — a cut, a cracked rib, a finger — she talks the whole time, dry and sharp, telling him what an idiot he is. On the bad ones she goes quiet. Completely. Her face goes still and her hands go steady and she works, and the only sounds in the room are her breathing and his.

This is a bad one.

She unwraps the arm first. The strips of wet cloth, the spear-shaft Jin-Woo bound it to. She looks at what's underneath for a long time without touching it. Then she puts her hands on it, very lightly, the way she does, feeling for something under the skin.

“Ciel,” she says. ˢʰ

Rimuru answers for her; Ciel speaks through him in rooms like this. Numbers. Places. A list of where the bones have gone. Mau stops listening after the fourth one. He looks at Yuka instead, sitting on his other side on the bed in the blue dress with her hand in his good one, and watches her listen to all of it.

“Some of these have started to knit wrong,” Shoko says. “Three hours in the grass. I have to set them. Some of them I have to break again first.” ˢʰ

“Okay,” Mau says. ᴹ

“It's going to hurt more than the gate.” ˢʰ

“Okay.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“Rimuru,” she says. “Hold his shoulder. Yuka, hold his hand. Don't let him pull away.” ˢʰ

* * *

He doesn't remember much of the next hour.

He remembers Rimuru's hand on his shoulder, cool and steady and immovable. He remembers Yuka's hand in his, her fingers so tight he can feel his own bones. He remembers the sound when Shoko breaks the first one again, which isn't loud, which is a small dry snap like a twig, and which he will hear in his sleep for weeks. He remembers not screaming, and then screaming, and Yuka saying something very close to his ear over and over that he can't make out.

He remembers DIO, in the corner, in her hat, not saying anything at all, and through the place in him where her attention lives, a steady unwavering warmth, like a hand on his back.

He remembers Makima. Deep inside, holding the sea. Not saying anything either. Just holding it, very carefully, so that whatever else happened, that wouldn't.

Then it's done.

* * *

The arm is straight.

He can feel it. Not good. Not healed. But straight, the bones where they should be, held in place by something Shoko built around it with her own cursed energy and then with a real splint over that, wood and cloth, from his knuckles to above his elbow. The side is cleaned and closed, in stages, the worst of it covered.

Shoko sits back.

Then she sits down on the floor beside the bed. Not on the chair. On the floor, with her back against the bed frame, and her hands in her lap.

They're shaking.

He watches them shake. Shoko's hands. The steadiest hands he's ever known. He's seen them in his chest, in his skull, in a pool of his own blood on a clinic floor, and they've never once shaken.

They're shaking now.

* * *

“Shoko,” he says. ᴹ

She doesn't look up.

“How was it?” she says. To her hands. “The breath. Whatever did that. How did it feel.” ˢʰ

He thinks about saying *fine*. He doesn't.

“Like the end of something,” he says. “It got through the neutral. Nothing gets through the neutral. It came through on the left and I felt my arm stop being an arm. I thought—for a second, I thought that was it. That I'd put my hands up in front of the wrong thing.” ᴹ

Shoko closes her eyes.

“And then the other one,” he says. “The Frost one. After. Makima told me to stop and I didn't. I raised it anyway. I couldn't see straight by then. And then I couldn't see at all.” ᴹ

“Three hours,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

“Jin-Woo said.” ᴹ

“In the grass. Three hours. With that arm. And your eyes bleeding. And nobody there but him.” ˢʰ

She's quiet for a long time.

* * *

“I'm tired,” she says. ˢʰ

He doesn't say anything.

“I'm so tired of this,” she says. “Of patching you up. Of you coming in through a door, or a wall, or out of the floor, with something new broken. Every time. Every *time*, Mau. The duel. The long one. The clinic. The red gate. This. I'm so *tired*.” ˢʰ

Her voice cracks.

“And I'll do it every time anyway,” she says. ˢʰ

She finally looks up at him. Her eyes are wet.

“Every time,” she says. “Do you understand? You come in broken, I put you back. I'll be tired, and I'll be angry, and I'll do it. Every time. As long as you keep coming back to be put back.” ˢʰ

He reaches down with his good hand. She looks at it for a moment. Then she takes it.

“I'll keep coming back,” he says. ᴹ

“You'd better,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

* * *

Yuka, on his other side, hasn't said anything for a long time.

She's still holding his good hand. Shoko's holding it too, now, from the floor. And Yuka looks down at Shoko, at the two of them holding the same hand from two sides, and something happens in her face.

She leans down. She puts her other hand on Shoko's head. Just for a moment. On her hair.

Shoko goes very still.

Then she laughs, a little, wetly, and lets go of his hand, and wipes her eyes on the back of her sleeve.

“Don't,” she says. “You'll make me cry in an elf's palace. I'll never live it down.” ˢʰ

“Elmesia made me cry in her garden,” Yuka says. “Yesterday. You'd be in good company.” ʸ

* * *

Rimuru folds them home at sunset.

All of them. Mau and Yuka and Shoko and DIO, one after another through the curtain of space Rimuru opens beside the balcony doors. Elmesia stands at the door of the room and watches them go. She doesn't say goodbye. She says *come through the floor on purpose next time*, and Yuka says *I'll remind him*, and Elmesia laughs.

Kaisel flies home on his own. Mau feels him go, a great dark shape lifting off the citadel's top terrace and turning north, alone, unhurried, the way he flies when nobody's on his back.

The fold closes behind them.

And they're in the yard.

* * *

Ori is on the porch.

She's sitting on the top step with her knees drawn up and the Pillow lying across the bottom three steps like a shaggy wall, and Lantern's head resting on her foot, and she's been there, Mau can tell, for a long time. Since morning. Since Rimuru left.

She sees them come out of the air in the middle of the yard.

She sees Yuka, in a dress Ori's never seen, the colour Yuka told her about once and Ori has been trying to imagine ever since.

She sees Mau, with his arm in a splint from his knuckles to his elbow, and the side of his shirt gone, and Yuka's arm round his waist holding him up.

She doesn't run. She doesn't shout. She stands up very slowly, the way Frieren taught her to stand up when something is too big to run at, and comes down the steps, past the Pillow, across the yard, and stops in front of him.

She looks at the splint.

Then she looks up at him.

“Did you win?” she asks. ᴼ

He thinks about it.

“Yeah,” he says. “Mostly.” ᴹ

Ori nods, very seriously.

Then she puts her arms round his waist, on the good side, very carefully, the way someone's taught her to hug people who are hurt. And holds on.

* * *

Jin-Woo's family comes up the orchard path at dusk.

All of them. Hae-In in front. Jin-Ah with her notebook. Their mother with a basket. Suho running ahead and being caught by Igris, Jin-Woo's Igris, and set back on the path.

And Jin-Woo, last, walking slowly.

He stops at the bottom of the porch steps. Mau is sitting on the top one, with Yuka beside him and Ori on his other side and the Pillow's nose on his boot.

Jin-Woo looks at the splint for a long time.

He doesn't say anything. His mother does. She comes up the steps with her basket and puts it down beside Mau and looks at him the way she looked at him the day she asked whether the soldiers hurt him.

“You brought him home,” she says. ᴶᵐ

“He brought himself home,” Mau says. ᴹ

“You stood with him,” she says. “He told us. He's never told us anything about any of it. About any war. He told us about this one.” ᴶᵐ

She puts her hand on his good shoulder.

“Thank you,” she says, “for not letting him go to another one alone.” ᴶᵐ

* * *

That night they sleep in their own bed.

Him on his back with the splinted arm on a pillow, the way Shoko wants it. Her on his right side, his good side, fitted against him without touching anything that hurts. She's still in the blue dress. She won't take it off. She says it's because she's too tired. He knows it isn't.

The house is quiet around them. The wind in the orchard. Beru in the hall, both hats. Shuna in the kitchen, very late, setting the bread for the morning. The third stair, once, as Ori comes up to check, and goes back down without knocking.

Inside him, very deep, Makima is holding the sea. Steady. Tired. And somewhere near her, in the dark under him where his shadows sleep, two new shapes he doesn't know what to do with yet: a wolf made of smoke, and a king made of frost, both kneeling, both half-there. And beside them, perfectly whole, a king made of frost who isn't one at all.

He'll think about them tomorrow.

“Tomorrow,” Yuka says, half asleep, into his shoulder. ʸ

“Tomorrow what?” ᴹ

“Tomorrow you tell me about the copy. Rime. Properly. Where you got it.” ʸ

“It's a long story.” ᴹ

“We have time,” she says. ʸ

He lies there in the dark with his wife in a blue dress asleep against him, and the house quiet, and his arm straight, and nobody, for once, standing in front of anything.

They have time.
