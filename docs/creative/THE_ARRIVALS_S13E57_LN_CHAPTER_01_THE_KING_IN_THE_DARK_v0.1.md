# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 1 — The King in the Dark

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1 — new volume opening; creator review pending
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E57
**Continuity state:** B+352, morning after the return from Thalion. Mau's left arm remains splinted from knuckles to above the elbow; his left side is still healing. Yuka remains beside him. She asks the question she saved at the end of V79: where Rime came from. Rime is a raised false Frost Monarch pattern from the old Red Gate, not the original. The newly raised true Monarch of Frost and Monarch of Beasts are weak, incomplete shadows; no successful repair or new power is established. Shoko checks Mau and bars training. Ori and the house get a quieter morning. No intimate scene.

*When: B+352. Dawn. Shuna has not yet lit the kitchen stove. Neither of them has any reason to get up.*

He wakes because she's moving.

Not away. She's moving closer, which would seem impossible if he hadn't watched her manage it every morning since the island. Her cheek is against his good shoulder, her arm across his waist where it won't touch the burns, and one of her legs is trapped in the blanket. She's trying to free it without waking him.

The splint rests on the pillow Shoko put under his left arm. It has been there all night. Shoko told him it would have to stay there even if the rest of him objected.

Most of him objects.

Yuka feels him open his eyes.

“Morning,” she says. ʸ

“Morning.” ᴹ

“You moved.” ʸ

“I woke up.” ᴹ

“You moved before that.” ʸ

He considers arguing. It isn't worth it.

“Sorry.” ᴹ

She lifts her face enough to look at him.

“You don't have to apologise for being hurt,” she says. ʸ

“Shoko's going to tell me I do.” ᴹ

“Shoko's going to tell you much worse things.” ʸ

That, he thinks, is certain.

* * *

For a while they listen to the house.

It isn't quiet in the way it was when Yuka spent a week in bed. That quiet had been work: people choosing where to step, what not to say, when to put down a cup. This is ordinary early-morning quiet. A door below. Water in the pipes. Beru, somewhere in the hall, attempting to move something large without making any noise at all.

The large thing makes an enormous scraping sound.

Yuka doesn't look away from Mau.

“Rime,” she says. ʸ

He was hoping she'd forgotten.

He should have known better.

“I told you it was a long story.” ᴹ

“Good. You can't go anywhere. I have time.” ʸ

She reaches up with two fingers and smooths the hair away from his eyes. She waits.

* * *

“It was a Red Gate,” he says. “Not the quarry. Much earlier. The one where the levels changed faster than I could keep up with them.” ᴹ

“I know that one.” ʸ

“You know the part I told you. Not all the things I kept when it was over.” ᴹ

“Then tell me the other part.” ʸ

He breathes in carefully. The burn pulls at his left ribs. He lets the breath go before it hurts more.

“The gate kept changing what was strong enough. The numbers would move and by the time you understood them the next thing was already different. I was trying to stay ahead of it. I thought I'd killed Makima.” ᴹ

Yuka's hand stops in his hair.

He doesn't soften the sentence. He told her last night that the fight with Destruction had been ugly; he meant it. He isn't going back to protecting her from the parts of his life she asks to know.

“I hadn't,” he says. “I found that out afterward. But for a while I thought I had. And there was a shape in there, a thing the gate had built using the pattern of a king made of frost.” ᴹ

“The king from yesterday.” ʸ

“Not him. A copy of him. A pattern the gate made. I killed that pattern and raised what was left.” ᴹ

“You called it Rime.” ʸ

“Noise helped with the name.” ᴹ

“Noise usually helps with names by making a list of forty.” ʸ

“Noise has been very quiet about the baby-name list.” ᴹ

“I know,” she says, and for a moment the room goes still around them. ʸ

He rests his good hand over hers.

Then she draws a breath and gives the morning back its shape.

“So Rime isn't the king you fought.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“But now you have that king too.” ʸ

“Part of him. I don't know how much of him survived the extraction.” ᴹ

“And the wolf.” ʸ

“Same problem.” ᴹ

“Do you know whether they can think?” ʸ

“Not yet.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸ

“Noise?” ʸ

“Then I don't have to repeat myself.” ʸ

“No,” Noise says. “You don't.” ᴺ

“Rime's different,” he tells her. “The shape is old. It's been with me a long time. I know what it does when I call it. The other two were never meant to be easy.” ᴹ

“You didn't know how difficult it would be until you tried.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“I don't know yet,” he says. ᴹ

When Ori goes downstairs, she takes Beru and the tray back with her. The Pillow refuses to move from the threshold until Yuka promises they are staying in bed.
