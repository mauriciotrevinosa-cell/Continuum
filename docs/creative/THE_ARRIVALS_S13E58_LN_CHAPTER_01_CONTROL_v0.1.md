# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 2 — Control

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S13E58
**Continuity state:** B+354, afternoon, the porch. Mau, splinted, with Yuka asleep against his good side, asks Makima to sit and asks her the question he's been carrying since the pact: whether all of it (the hug in the hall, the coat, the name on the list, "I don't want you to stop") is her trying to control him, since that is what she is and he is chaos. She answers honestly. She came to control; the contract doesn't let her; and binding herself to his body cost her the thing devils keep, the coming back. When his body ends, so does she. She chose a life with an end, near him. Mau realises he was wrong to suspect it was a strategy, and tells her why he never judged her: he saw himself in her, from his own trauma, and from what he has done, even in a Trial. Noise is listening and doesn't say anything rude.

*When: B+354. Mid-afternoon. Shoko has allowed the porch, the chair and nothing else.*

He's been carrying the question since the night of the contract.

He didn't know he was carrying it until today. It was just there, underneath everything: under the clinic, under the three days without names, under the hall at the top of the stairs where he held her and she went stiff, under a coat folded beneath his broken arm in the grass. A small hard thing at the bottom of his pocket that he kept touching without taking out.

Today he takes it out.

* * *

She comes to the bottom of the porch steps the way she always does, and stops, and looks at the chair beside his.

Yuka is asleep against his good side. She fell asleep an hour ago in the middle of telling him something about Mai's new dress pattern, with her head on his shoulder and her hand flat on his chest, and he hasn't moved since. He doesn't intend to.

Makima looks at Yuka. Then at him.

“May I?” she asks, about the chair. ᴹᵏ

“Sit,” he says quietly. ᴹ

She sits. She folds her hands. She looks at the yard, where the Pillow is lying in the grass and Ori is trying to teach it to fetch, and the Pillow is declining.

He lets them be quiet for a while.

Then he says it.

* * *

“Why are you here?” ᴹ

She doesn't turn her head.

“I'm bound to your body,” she says. “I can't be far.” ᴹᵏ

“That's where you are. I asked why.” ᴹ

A pause.

“You know what I am,” he says. “Mostly. A mess. Things I took from other people, things I made by accident, things that came out of a Trial nobody else saw. I don't stay in one shape. I don't do what I'm supposed to. I'm—” ᴹ

He looks for the word.

“Chaos,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“Chaos,” he agrees. ᴹ

“And I'm control,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

“So I keep thinking,” he says, “about what that means. You and me. In the same body.” ᴹ

He keeps his voice low. Yuka stirs against his shoulder and settles again.

“The hall,” he says. “When I hugged you. The coat in the grass. *I don't want you to stop.* The name on Noise's list.” ᴹ

He makes himself look at her.

“Is that you trying to hold something?” he asks. “The way you hold everything. Is it control? Getting close, so it's easier to put your hand on me later?” ᴹ

He doesn't say it angrily. He's not angry. He just needs to know.

Makima is quiet for a long time.

* * *

“When I came here,” she says finally, “to this world, I came to control. That's what I am. It's what I've always been. I find the thing that matters most and I put my hand on it, and then everything else follows.” ᴹᵏ

She looks at the yard. At the Pillow, lying very still while Ori throws a stick past its nose for the fifth time.

“You were the thing that mattered most,” she says. “From the first day I understood what you were. So I tried. You know what I tried. The devil I sent. The distance I made. The visits.” ᴹᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“And then you were dying,” she says, “and I had a minute, and I made a contract.” ᴹᵏ

She turns her head and looks at him for the first time.

“You set the terms,” she says. “Do you remember them?” ᴹᵏ

He does. *You don't hurt anyone in this house. You don't control me. Yuka, above everything.*

“*You don't control me*,” she says. “That was the second thing you said. With blood coming out of your eyes. You said it before you said anything about yourself.” ᴹᵏ

She looks back at the yard.

“I can't,” she says. “Even if I wanted to. The contract holds it. Every time I've reached for you since, out of habit, my hand stops. It just stops. Like reaching for a door and finding a wall.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

“Then why,” he says. ᴹ

Makima doesn't answer right away.

“Do you know what happens to devils,” she says, “when they die?” ᴹᵏ

He thinks about it. He knows a little. Things Denji said once, at a table. Things Power said less carefully.

“They come back,” he says. ᴹ

“They come back,” she agrees. “Somewhere. In some form. As long as people are afraid of what we are, we come back. Over and over. Forever. I have never once in my existence had an end.” ᴹᵏ

She looks at her hands, folded in her lap.

“I have one now,” she says. ᴹᵏ

He goes very still.

“Bound to your body,” she says. “For as long as it lives. That's what the contract says. I wrote it. I knew what it meant when I wrote it. When your body ends, I end. Not *come back*. End.” ᴹᵏ

She's quiet a moment.

“I traded forever,” she says, “for whatever you have left. Near you. That's all.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

He doesn't say anything for a long time.

He thinks about the hall. Her standing at the top of the stairs, not coming in, saying *I can't put it down*. The coat. *I don't want you to stop.* He thinks about the name she offered, *Asa*, morning, and how she said it without looking at him, as if it might be taken away.

He thinks about how he's been touching that small hard question at the bottom of his pocket for weeks, and how it never once occurred to him to ask what it had cost her to be there at all.

“I was wrong,” he says. ᴹ

She doesn't move.

“I thought—” He stops. Starts again. “I thought it might be a strategy. All of it. Because that's what you are. Because I didn't think you'd do anything without a reason that went somewhere.” ᴹ

“It does go somewhere,” Makima says. “It goes to you.” ᴹᵏ

“That's not a strategy.” ᴹ

“No,” she agrees. “I don't know what it is. I've been trying to find a word for it for weeks. I don't have one.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

“Can I tell you something?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks at him.

“Why I never judged you,” he says. “When Denji and Reze and Power—when everyone wanted me to. When you'd done everything you'd done. Why I took your hand in the clinic instead.” ᴹ

She waits.

“Because I saw myself,” he says. ᴹ

He doesn't look away from her.

“I don't remember anything from before the clearing,” he says. “But I remember the Trial. Four hundred and thirty-seven times. And there were things I did in there, Makima. To get through. To keep going. Things I don't tell people. A world, once. A whole world. It wasn't real; it was a Trial. It doesn't matter that it wasn't real. I still did it. I still know what I'm capable of when I decide something matters more than everything else.” ᴹ

Makima is perfectly still.

“And this house,” he says. “Look at it. Rimuru became a demon lord the way demon lords are made. Gojo, Maki, Yuta, nearly everyone from their world has killed. Frieren's killed more things than anyone here has ever met. We're not clean. None of us. We never were.” ᴹ

He looks down at Yuka, asleep against him.

“What you did,” he says, “manipulating people, sending things after people, *her*—that's its own thing. That's yours to carry. I'm not saying it's the same. But I'm not standing anywhere clean enough to throw it at you. And when I looked at you in the clinic, I didn't see a monster. I saw someone who'd been hurt into a shape and couldn't get out of it. I saw something I recognised.” ᴹ

He's quiet a moment.

“Or I wanted to,” he admits. “Maybe I just wanted to see that. Maybe I needed to believe someone could be that and still change. Because if they can't, then neither can I.” ᴹ

* * *

Makima doesn't say anything for a very long time.

In the yard, the Pillow finally gets up, ambles over to the stick, looks at it, lies down on it, and goes to sleep. Ori throws her hands up in despair.

“You've never told me that,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“I've never told anyone that,” he says. “Not like that.” ᴹ

She looks at him. And something happens to her face that he's seen a few times now, since the contract. The calm lifting, just a little, off whatever's underneath. Not all the way. Just enough.

“Thank you,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“For what?” ᴹ

“For asking,” she says. “Instead of deciding.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Inside him, somewhere, Noise has been listening.

He can feel it. That vast, precise attention, turned toward the porch, toward the woman in the chair beside him. Noise doesn't like her. Noise has said so, more than once. Noise built her a body because he asked it to and has been politely furious about it ever since.

Noise doesn't say anything now.

That's how he knows it heard all of it.

* * *

Yuka wakes up a little later.

She lifts her head off his shoulder and blinks at the yard, at the Pillow asleep on the stick, at the light going long. Then she sees Makima in the chair.

She doesn't say anything. She looks at Makima for a moment, and then at Mau, and he can see her reading something on his face.

“You talked,” she says. ʸ

“We talked,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks at Makima again. Makima looks back. Neither of them says anything.

Then Yuka puts her head back down on his shoulder.

“Tell me later,” she says. ʸ

“I'll tell you later,” he says. ᴹ

Makima stands up, very quietly. She inclines her head to both of them, and goes down the porch steps, and across the yard, past Ori and the sleeping Pillow, toward the village.

At the gate she stops and looks back.

He doesn't know what her face is doing from here. But she lifts one hand, just slightly, just once, the way you'd wave to someone from a train.

He lifts his good one back.
