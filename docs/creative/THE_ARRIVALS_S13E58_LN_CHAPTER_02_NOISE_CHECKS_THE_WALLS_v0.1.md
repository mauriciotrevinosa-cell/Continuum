# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 4 — Noise Checks the Walls

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E58
**Continuity state:** B+356. A lazy afternoon in bed with Yuka. Ciel does what she always does when Mau and Yuka are being private: closes her view and Noise's. DIO takes the chance. When Mau goes to the shared inner room later to ask Noise something, he finds Ciel and DIO crowded close on either side of Noise at the table and says he can come back later. They act as if nothing happened. Noise asks him for help. Makima, who lives in there too now, comes to see what the fuss is; Noise flees "to check the walls". Ciel and DIO bristle. Mau tells Yuka all of it, and she laughs until she cries. Closeness; no intimate scene.

*When: B+356. A grey afternoon. Shoko has decided that lying down counts as recovery and has told Yuka to make sure he does it.*

Yuka is making sure he does it.

She's lying half on top of him, on his good side, with her chin on his chest and her book propped against his ribs, reading out loud. Badly. She keeps doing voices. The book is the one with the man and the horse, and she's reached the part where the man has finally, after a hundred and forty pages, found the horse, and the horse has bitten him.

“He deserved it,” she says. ʸ

“He walked across a desert for that horse.” ᴹ

“He walked across a desert *and didn't bring it an apple*.” ʸ

He laughs. It still pulls at his side, a little. Less than yesterday.

She puts the book down on his chest and looks at him.

“You're not reading,” she says. ʸ

“You're reading.” ᴹ

“You're supposed to be *listening*.” ʸ

“I'm listening.” ᴹ

“You're looking at me.” ʸ

He doesn't deny it.

She goes a little pink, the way she does, the only colour in the room, and puts her face down on his chest so he can't see it, and stays there.

* * *

He feels Ciel close the door.

He always feels it. A small, polite, absolute *click*, somewhere behind his eyes. It's a rule she made a long time ago, before the island, before the hand. When it's just him and Yuka, being just themselves, Ciel closes her view of them. And Noise's. She says some things should belong to the people they belong to.

He's always been grateful for it. He's never once thought about what Ciel and Noise do with themselves while the door is closed.

* * *

He finds out an hour later.

Yuka has dozed off on his chest. He's lying there in the grey light with his good hand in her hair, half asleep himself, and a question floats up: something about the extraction, about whether the two new shapes in the dark might be steadier if Noise tried feeding them a little of the cold from Rime. A small question. The kind he asks Noise twenty times a day without thinking.

He turns inward to ask it.

He goes to the room.

The shared one, inside him. The one with the table and the chairs and the cup nobody ever quite finishes. He doesn't knock; he's never had to. He knows how it usually looks. Noise, the shadow outline of it, beside the table. Ciel in the chair Noise once adjusted for her so she could sit at the table without moving the cup. DIO wherever she likes, which is usually somewhere she can talk to Noise about building things. And Makima, since she came, in the chair by the far wall, very upright, her hands folded in her lap, the way he thinks of as holding the sea, though it's only a way of thinking about it.

What isn't normal is where everyone is.

* * *

Ciel and DIO are *right up against* Noise.

Both of them. From both sides. Pressed in close, the way Yuka presses in close on the couch during a movie. DIO has pulled her chair right up against the shadow of Noise, practically leaning on it, bright and delighted. And Ciel, who has never in all the time he's known her been anything but precise and composed and a little way off, is sitting on the other side, very primly, with her shoulder very definitely against it.

Noise is in the middle.

Noise is very, very still.

* * *

He stops.

They all stop.

There's a long moment in the room where nobody moves and everybody knows that everybody knows.

“I can come back later,” Mau says. ᴹ

* * *

They come apart so fast the chairs scrape.

DIO is up and across the room in one movement, at the far end of the table, humming something tuneless and examining the cup with great interest. Ciel is suddenly in her own chair, exactly where it always is, hands folded, composed, watching, as if she has been sitting there all afternoon and is mildly surprised to see him.

“Did you need something?” Ciel asks. ᶜ

Her voice is perfectly calm. Perfectly normal.

“The door was closed,” Mau says. ᴹ

“It was,” Ciel agrees. ᶜ

“For me and Yuka.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᶜ

“So you could have privacy.” ᴹ

“Some things,” Ciel says, with great dignity, “should belong to the people they belong to.” ᶜ

DIO, a respectable distance away, makes a sound like someone very bad at hiding a laugh.

* * *

“Mau,” Noise says. ᴺ

Very quietly. In a voice he's never heard it use.

“Help,” Noise says. ᴺ

He almost laughs out loud. He catches it just in time; Yuka's asleep on his chest.

“What's wrong?” he asks, very innocently. ᴹ

“They do this every time,” Noise says. “Every time the door closes. They wait until it's closed, and then they *come over*. Both of them. From both sides. And I don't know what to—I've never—” ᴺ

“You built yourself a body,” Mau says. “On the island. For a date with them.” ᴹ

“That was different. That was *planned*. I had a schedule. This is—” ᴺ

“Ambushes,” DIO offers helpfully, from her respectable distance. ᴰ

“*Yes*,” says Noise. ᴺ

* * *

The far door opens.

Makima, who lives in here too now, in her own body, the way the others do, comes in from her side to see what the commotion is about.

She stops just inside the door, very calm. She looks at the arrangement: DIO a little too far down the table to be natural, Ciel a little too composed to be innocent, and Noise in the middle, rigid with embarrassment.

“Is something wrong?” Makima asks. ᴹᵏ

Noise goes completely silent.

And then, very suddenly, the shadow of Noise isn't by the table. It's thinned out and slid away through the wall like someone who's just remembered an urgent errand on the other side of the house. Not gone. Noise can't be gone. Just very, very far away, at the outermost edge of him.

“I'm checking the walls,” Noise announces, from very far away. ᴺ

“What walls?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“*All* the walls.” ᴺ

* * *

There's a short silence.

DIO and Ciel are both very still. Not where they were before. Somewhere else. Both of them turned, very slightly, toward Makima.

He can feel it coming off them. Not hostility, exactly. Something more specific than that. Something with an edge.

Makima looks at the two of them. Then, very slightly, at the wall Noise went through.

“Ah,” she says. ᴹᵏ

That's all. Just *ah*. But there's something in it he's never heard from her before, something that in another person would be the beginning of being amused.

“I'll go back down,” she says. ᴹᵏ

She does. Back out through her door, and it closes behind her, very calmly.

DIO and Ciel stay turned toward the door for a long time.

* * *

Yuka wakes up to find him shaking.

She lifts her head off his chest, alarmed, and finds him lying on his back with his good hand over his mouth and tears running out of the corners of his eyes, laughing so hard he can't breathe.

“What,” she says. “*What*. Is it your side? Did I—” ʸ

He shakes his head. He can't talk.

“*Mau*.” ʸ

He tells her.

All of it. Ciel closing the door. DIO and Ciel on either side. *I can come back later.* Noise saying *help* in that voice. *Ambushes.* Makima arriving. *I'm checking the walls. All the walls.*

Yuka stares at him.

Then she puts her face down on his chest and laughs until she's crying too, until she has to hold her own side, until the two of them are lying on the bed in the grey afternoon wheezing and gasping and every time one of them almost stops, the other one says *all the walls* and it starts again.

* * *

Much later, when they can breathe, Yuka lies with her chin on his chest and looks at him very seriously.

“Is Noise okay?” she asks. ʸ

He checks.

Noise is still at the far edge, checking walls. Very thoroughly. Ciel has gone out to keep it company, a little way off, the way she always is. DIO is back at the table, humming, turning the cup round and round.

And Makima is in her chair by the far wall again, hands folded, the way he thinks of as holding the sea. And, he thinks, if he's not imagining it, still faintly amused.

“Noise is fine,” he says. ᴹ

“Noise is *very* popular,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Noise is very popular,” he agrees. ᴹ

She thinks about that.

“Good for Noise,” she says, and puts her head back down. ʸ

Yuka shifts higher against his good shoulder, careful to keep her weight off the splint and the side Shoko is still dressing. She cups his face in both hands as if she is about to ask a serious question.

“Are you sure you're not going to go check the walls too?” she says. ʸ

“I think Noise has that covered.” ᴹ

“All of them?”

“Every single one.”

She makes a heroic attempt to remain solemn. It lasts about two seconds.

He kisses the corner of her mouth before the laughter starts again, and she catches him by the collar with a quiet, indignant sound.

“That was supposed to be my joke,” she says. ʸ

“Your jokes take too long.”

“Your jokes aren't even jokes.”

“That's why I borrow yours.” ᴹ

She settles back down, warm against him, still smiling. He adjusts the blanket over her feet with his good hand. The afternoon is grey beyond the windows, and he can't do anything about the ache in his side, but it is the kind of afternoon he wouldn't trade: her breathing slowing against his chest, the last of their laughter leaving the room a little at a time.

After a while Yuka speaks without lifting her head.

“I like hearing you laugh like that,” she says. ʸ

“Like what?”

“Like you forgot to be careful.”

He thinks about that. Then he rests his cheek against her hair.

“I'm trying,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” she answers, and stays right where she is. ʸ
