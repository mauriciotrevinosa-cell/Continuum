# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 5 — Off the Stick

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S13E59
**Continuity state:** B+359. Shoko takes the splint off in the clinic with Yuka holding his hand. The arm is straight and whole, the skin new and tender, the grip a little weak; she and his own RCT did the rest over eight days. The burned side has closed into a tight new patch beside the old Trial burns. She clears him for ordinary life: walking, the stairs, the yard. No training, no extraction tests, no gates. Jin-Woo comes by and shakes the healed hand very carefully. That night Yuka says tomorrow they finish the inventory. No intimate scene.

*When: B+359. Eight days after Thalion. Mau has asked Shoko every morning whether today is the day. Today she said yes before he asked.*

Shoko cuts the splint off with a pair of scissors that Senku would recognise.

“Those are Senku's,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Mai borrowed them,” Shoko says. “I borrowed them from Mai. It's a chain of custody.” ˢʰ

She cuts the last of the cloth. She lifts the wood away. She sets it on the table, the long dark shaft of Jin-Woo's spear that held his arm together in the grass, and then her own splint on top of it.

He looks at his arm.

It's an arm. That's the first thing. It looks like an arm again. Straight. The right length. The skin over the forearm is new: thin and tender and smoother than the rest of him, like the inside of a wrist. There's a long faint line where the worst of the breaks were, from just above the wrist to just below the elbow. He can see it in the grey of everything, a slightly paler seam.

“Make a fist,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

He makes a fist.

It's weak. He can feel how weak. The fingers close but they don't close *hard*, and when he squeezes, the forearm trembles.

“Good,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

“It's weak.” ᴹ

“It was in eleven pieces eight days ago. It's allowed to be weak.” ˢʰ

* * *

Yuka is holding his other hand.

She's been holding it since they came into the clinic, sitting on the stool beside the bed, watching every snip of the scissors as if the arm under the splint might have changed shape while nobody was looking. Now she looks at the bare arm for a long time.

She reaches out. Stops.

“Can I?” she asks Shoko. ʸ

“It's his arm,” Shoko says. “Ask him.” ˢʰ

Yuka looks at him.

He holds it out.

She puts her fingers on the new skin. Very lightly. Just above the wrist, where the long pale line starts. She runs them along it, slowly, all the way up to the elbow, the way you'd trace a road on a map.

He watches her do it.

“It's soft,” she says. ʸ

“It's new.” ᴹ

“It's softer than the rest of you.” ʸ

“Everything's softer than the rest of me,” he says, and she laughs, and doesn't take her fingers away. ᴹ

* * *

Shoko checks the side next.

The burn has closed. Where Destruction's breath came through the neutral and ran up his ribs, there's a patch of tight new skin now, palm-wide, sitting just beside the old burns from the Trial. The new one is smoother. The old ones are ridged and pulled. Side by side they look like two maps of the same country drawn a year apart.

Shoko presses around the edges with her fingers, the way she does, feeling for something under the skin.

“It's going to pull,” she says. “For a while. When you stretch. When you lift your arm over your head. That's normal.” ˢʰ

“How long?” ᴹ

“A while.” ˢʰ

“That's not a date.” ᴹ

“It's the only date you get.” ˢʰ

* * *

“Ordinary life,” Shoko says, when she's done. ˢʰ

She ticks it off on her fingers.

“Walking. The stairs. The yard. The kitchen. Carrying things that weigh less than Ori. Sleeping on whatever side you want.” ˢʰ

“And?” ᴹ

“And nothing else.” ˢʰ

“No training.” ᴹ

“No training. No extraction tests on those two half-made things in the dark. No gates. No flying. No *Bang*.” ˢʰ

She looks at him.

“You've had the arm off the stick for five minutes,” she says. “Don't make me put it back on.” ˢʰ

* * *

Jin-Woo comes by in the afternoon.

He doesn't come to the clinic. He waits on the porch, the way he's waited on the porch every afternoon since Thalion, and when Mau comes out with Yuka at his side and his bare arm hanging at his side for the first time in eight days, Jin-Woo stands up.

He looks at the arm.

Then he holds out his hand.

Mau takes it. With the new arm. The weak one.

Jin-Woo doesn't squeeze. He just holds it, very carefully, the way you'd hold something you broke once and fixed and aren't sure yet will hold.

“Good,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“It's weak.” ᴹ

“I know. I can feel.” ᴶʷ

He doesn't let go right away.

“When Shoko says,” he says, “we start again. From the beginning. The grip. The roll. All of it.” ᴶʷ

“From the beginning?” ᴹ

“It's a new arm,” Jin-Woo says. “It doesn't know anything yet.” ᴶʷ

Then he lets go, and nods once to Yuka, and goes back down the orchard path.

* * *

That night, in bed, Yuka keeps finding the arm.

She doesn't mean to. Or she does. He can't tell. She's lying on his left side for the first time in eight days, carefully, and every few minutes her hand finds its way to his forearm and traces the long pale line from wrist to elbow, slowly, and then stops, and then a while later does it again.

“You keep doing that,” he says. ᴹ

“I like it.” ʸ

“It's a scar.” ᴹ

“It's a road,” she says. “From where it broke to where it's fixed.” ʸ

He doesn't have an answer to that. He puts his other arm round her.

“Tomorrow,” she says, after a while. ʸ

“Tomorrow what?” ᴹ

“The inventory. The rest of it. We only got through the first part.” ʸ

He remembers. The four piles. The notebook. *Tomorrow we go through the rest.* That was a week ago. Shoko made him stay in bed after that, and then the porch, and then the door that Ciel closes, and then the walls.

“There's a lot of it,” he warns. ᴹ

“I know. I have a new notebook.” ʸ

“You filled the old one?” ᴹ

“I filled the old one with things you forgot,” she says. “This one's for the things you didn't know you had.” ʸ

She settles her head on his shoulder. Her fingers go back to the line on his arm.

“And candy,” she adds. ʸ

“That's going to be the expensive part,” he says. ᴹ

She laughs into his shoulder, and holds on.
