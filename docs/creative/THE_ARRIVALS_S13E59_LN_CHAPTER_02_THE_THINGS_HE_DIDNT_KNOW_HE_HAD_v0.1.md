## Chapter 6 — The Things He Didn't Know He Had

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 6

**Continuity state:** B+360. The rest of the inventory, with Mau healed. Noise and Ciel have finished comparing records: the index gaps are storage that was sealed during the Final Trial and never reopened in his view. Opened, they hold things he'd stopped seeing: the castle pieces from S10, raw scales and chitin from what Beru, Kaisel and Kamish killed, old gate materials. Mau proposes that since nobody ever came for the castle, the house can hold the dragon war, part two. Mai and Senku sort what could go into the katana. * * *

Noise and Ciel finished in the night.

He felt them do it. Somewhere around two in the morning, while Yuka slept on his left side for the second night in a row, the two of them stopped arguing about methodology and simply *did* it: laid the old manifest from before the Trial against the list he sees now, line by line, entry by entry, until the gaps from last week's afternoon had shapes.

“Sealed by what?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“And when you came out—” ʸ

Yuka stares at him.

“You've been walking around for months,” she says slowly, “with a whole storeroom you couldn't see.” ʸ

“What did you write?” ᴹ

* * *

He opens it.

Noise does the work. He just has to want it. He reaches for the inventory and Noise, who has been waiting all morning for exactly this, *pulls*, and something that was folded flat somewhere at the back of him unfolds.

The list in front of his eyes gets longer.

Much longer.

He sits there and looks at it. The common room has gone quiet; people have drifted in from the kitchen, from the yard, from the stairs, the way they always drift in when something's about to happen. Ori on the arm of the couch with the Pillow's nose in her lap. Milim on the back of a chair. Senku in the doorway with his notebook already out.

“Well?” Yuka says. ʸ

“The castle,” Mau says. ᴹ

* * *

He'd forgotten the castle.

Not forgotten. He knew he'd taken it apart, a long time ago, with the shadows doing most of the lifting. Knew they'd carried it out in pieces. Knew the pieces were *somewhere*. He just hadn't thought about where in a very long time.

They're in the storeroom. All of them. Towers and walls and gates and a great hall, every stone numbered by Bellion in the neat way Bellion numbers things, folded flat and waiting.

He takes out one stone, just to look at it. It's the size of a cart. It lands on the common-room floor with a thump that rattles every cup on the table.

“Mau,” says Shuna from the kitchen door, with enormous restraint. ˢʰᵘ

“Sorry.” ᴹ

“Nobody ever came for it,” he says. ᴹ

“The castle,” he says. “When we took it. I thought someone would come. Ask for it back. Fight about it. Nobody ever did. It's just been sitting in there for months. Whole. In pieces. Waiting.” ᴹ

Milim, on the back of her chair, has gone very still. Her eyes are getting very large.

“So I was thinking,” Mau says. ᴹ

* * *

The rest of the storeroom takes all morning.

There's so much. Old gate materials he'd stopped seeing: crystals and plates and ore Fern registered once and then never saw again. Bolts of cloth from markets he's forgotten. Rations from the very first box he ever bought in the Shop, which Rem refuses to let anyone eat on principle and which Senku immediately wants to study.

And the remains.

Everything Beru, Kaisel and Kamish ever killed and didn't eat, in the long years since the clearing, stored the way he stores things: in parts, labelled, waiting. Scales. Twelve great plates of them, each as big as a door, from something Kaisel brought down over a mountain. Chitin, eighteen pieces, with a sheen that shifts when you tilt them, from things Beru fought in tunnels Mau never went into. Horn. Claw. A single tooth as long as his arm, from something Kamish killed before Mau even knew him.

Mai comes in from the workshop. She sees the pile growing on the common-room floor.

She sits down very slowly on the nearest chair.

“Oh,” she says. ᴹᵃ

They sort it together. Mai and Senku and Mau, and Yuka on the table with the notebook writing everything down, and Ori making a smaller, more important pile of things that are “pretty”.

Some of it is just material: good for building, good for Mai's workshop, good for the house. Some of it hums when he touches it, faintly, the way the shard from the red gate hummed, and Noise looks at those very carefully.

“That one,” Noise says, about a long dark scale from the pile Kaisel brought down. “And that.” A curved piece of chitin, very thin. “And the tooth.” ᴺ

He tells Mai.

All of it. The rings. Noise's pattern. A ring for the katana, built with his version of her Construction.

Mai listens with her arms crossed.

When he's done, she stands up. She walks round the pile of scales and chitin and the tooth, very calmly. She stops in front of him.

She reaches up and smacks him on the back of the head.

* * *

He finds the page by accident.

It's the afternoon. The common room has emptied out again; everyone has gone off with their share of the storeroom. Mai and Senku to the workshop with the scales and the tooth. Milim to the yard to practise being most of the dragons. Ori to her room with the pretty pile.

He's sitting on the bench with Yuka in his lap, idly going through the system menus, looking for whatever the faint box was trying to tell him about the remains. There are a lot of menus. He's never been through all of them. He's never needed to.

He opens one he doesn't recognise.

It says *LEVEL*.

* * *

He stares at it.

He knew about the levels. Of course he knew. In the old days, before the Trial, before Noise broke the ceiling, there were levels; he climbed them all the way to two hundred. Last week's afternoon found the old record. Two hundred. He'd thought that was the end of it. That when the ceiling broke, the numbers broke with it.

They didn't.

The page is different. Simpler. A new scale. And at the top, a short line of text in the flat clear voice the system uses:

> **[Level: 4.]**
>
> **[Note: Level 2 on the current scale corresponds to Level 200 on the former scale.]**

Underneath, a bar, nearly full.

> **[Progress to Level 5: 91%.]**
>
> **[Point allocation: locked until Level 5.]**

He reads it three times.

Yuka, in his lap, has gone very still.

“What,” she says. ʸ

“I'm level four,” he says. ᴹ

“You're—” ʸ

“On a new scale. Two is what two hundred used to be. I'm four. Almost five. And I can't use any points until five.” ᴹ

“You had a *level page*,” she says. ʸ

“I didn't know.” ᴹ

“You had a level page this *whole time*.” ʸ

“I never opened it.” ᴹ

“Since *when*?” ʸ

“Since Noise broke the ceiling,” he says. “Around the Sukuna fight.” ᴹ

Then she puts her face in his chest and screams into it. Not loud. Muffled. A long, heartfelt scream of pure disbelief.

“What else did you forget?” she says. ʸ

* * *

She doesn't stop all day.

Every time he does anything, she asks if he forgot something. When he gets up to get water, she asks if he forgot he has a water skill. When Shuna calls them to supper, she asks if he forgot he has a stomach. When he reaches for the bread at the table, she puts her hand over his and asks, very sweetly, whether he's *sure* he remembers how bread works, because she can write it down for him.

“I'm going to divorce you,” he tells her, at supper. ᴹ

“You said *never*,” she says. ʸ

“I'm reconsidering.” ᴹ

“You *can't*. It's in my notebook. *Forever*. You said it. I wrote it down.” ʸ

And she shows him. There, on a page, in her round careful handwriting, with the date: *He said forever.*

He stops arguing.

And she makes him buy her a sweet from every page of the Shop's snack section, one at a time, between supper and bed, as a fine for the level page. There are a lot of pages. By the end of it there's a small mountain of paper wrappers on the common-room table and Yuka is lying on the couch with her head in his lap, announcing that she can never eat again, and eating another one.

* * *

That night he's getting ready for bed.

He's at the wardrobe, with his back to the room. Pulling his shirt off over his head, carefully, because the new skin on his side pulls when he lifts his arms. He gets it off. He drops it on the chair. He turns round.

She's sitting up in bed.

Watching him.

Not reading. Not pretending to read. Just sitting there with the blanket round her knees and her chin on them, looking at him, with an expression on her face that he's seen before. On his own face. In a mirror. A long time ago, on the night they came home from the island, when he stood by this same wardrobe and couldn't stop looking at her.

He stops.

She doesn't look away. She doesn't even pretend.

“What are you looking at?” he asks. ᴹ

It's her line. He knows it's her line. She said it to him once, in this room, by this wardrobe, and he told her the truth.

She knows it too. He watches her know it. Watches her go pink, slowly, from her collarbones up, the only colour in the room, and still not look away.

“You,” she says. ʸ

Much later, she's asleep on his chest.

He's lying awake in the dark, with his new arm round her and the long pale line on it under her hand, where she fell asleep still tracing it. The house is quiet. Beru in the hall. The wind in the orchard. Somewhere at the far edge of him, Noise, very carefully not looking at anything, and Ciel, who closed the door hours ago, keeping it closed.

He thinks about the page.

Level four. Ninety-one percent. Five, and then points, whatever points mean on a scale where two is what two hundred used to be.

He doesn't know how he's going to get the last nine percent. Shoko said no gates. Shoko said no training.

He'll ask her. Later. In a few days. When the side stops pulling.

Yuka stirs against him. Her hand moves on his arm, along the line, from the wrist toward the elbow, and stops.

“You forgot to sleep,” she mumbles. ʸ

He laughs, very quietly, so as not to wake her more than he already has.

“I'm going,” he says. ᴹ

“Don't forget,” she says, and is asleep again before he can answer. ʸ
