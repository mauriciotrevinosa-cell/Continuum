# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 7 — The Pattern

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E60
**Continuity state:** B+362, Mai's workshop. As promised, Mau shows Mai the pattern Noise drew for the ring before anything is built: he lays it out with Authority in fine lines of chalk dust in the air over her bench, the way Noise describes it to him. Three rings' worth of understanding in one shape. Mai studies it for an hour without speaking, then corrects one curve. Noise, outraged, checks, and admits she's right. Yuta comes to look; he recognises the shape of his own ring in it and goes quiet. Yuka sits on the bench beside Mau with her feet in his lap the whole time. They set the build for after his birthday, which he has forgotten is in three days. Everyone else hasn't.

*When: B+362. Two days after the storeroom. Mai has had the stove lit since dawn, which she never does, and has cleared her entire bench.*

Mai has cleared her bench.

He's never seen it empty. In all the time he's been coming into the workshop, the bench has been covered: cloth and thread and steel, half-made things and finished ones, tools in an order only Mai understands. This morning it's bare. Scrubbed. A single sheet of dark slate laid in the middle of it, and nothing else.

She's standing beside it with her arms crossed.

“Show me,” she says. ᴹᵃ

* * *

He doesn't know how to show her.

The pattern isn't a drawing. It's something Noise holds, somewhere in the place where Noise holds things, and describes to him the way it describes a gate or a technique: shapes and relations and pressures, how this curve leans on that one, where the weight sits. He understands it when Noise tells him. He couldn't draw it to save his life.

So he doesn't draw it.

He takes a handful of the fine pale chalk dust Mai uses for marking cloth, and opens his hand over the slate, and lets Authority take the dust out of his palm grain by grain.

And Noise talks, and he listens, and the dust goes where Noise says.

* * *

It takes a long time.

The dust rises off his palm in a thin stream and hangs in the warm air over the bench, and slowly, slowly, it starts to make lines. A circle, first. Then another circle inside it, at an angle, as if the first were a ring lying flat and the second were a ring standing up through it. Then a third, crossing both. Then the fine work: threads of dust running between the three circles, curving, knotting, doubling back, a lattice so intricate that after a while he stops trying to follow it and just lets Noise steer his hand.

Mai doesn't move.

She stands at the end of the bench with her arms crossed and watches the pattern build itself in the air, line by line, and her face doesn't change at all.

Yuka is on the stool beside him. She came in with him and climbed up onto the end of the bench and put her feet in his lap, and she hasn't said a word since the dust started rising. She's watching it too. Every so often she looks from the pattern to his face and back.

When it's done, it hangs over the slate like a frost-flower: three rings and the web between them, pale against the dark, perfectly still.

“That's it,” Mau says. ᴹ

His arm is aching. The new one. He didn't notice until he stopped.

* * *

Mai looks at it for an hour.

She doesn't say anything. She walks round the bench, slowly, looking at it from every side. She crouches to look at it from underneath. She goes and gets a lamp and holds it at different angles so the light runs along the threads. Once she reaches out a finger, very carefully, and stops a hair's breadth from a knot where five lines cross, and looks at it for a long time, and takes her finger away.

Yuka, at some point, goes and makes tea. She brings three cups. Mai doesn't touch hers.

“Is she okay?” Yuka whispers, back on the stool, feet back in his lap. ʸ

“She's thinking,” Mau whispers back. ᴹ

“For an *hour*?” ʸ

“Noise thinks for longer.” ᴹ

“Noise isn't a person.” ʸ

“Don't tell Noise that.” ᴹ

* * *

“This one's wrong,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

She's pointing. Not touching. At a curve near the base of the third ring, where a thread bends back on itself before joining the lattice.

Inside him, Noise goes very still.

“It bends too early,” Mai says. “Here. If the weight's meant to sit where the second ring crosses, and it has to be, or the whole thing tips, then this curve has to carry it further before it turns. Like a seam that takes the strain. If it bends here, it'll hold for a while. And then one day it won't. And whatever's inside will come out all at once.” ᴹᵃ

She looks at him.

“Ask Noise,” she says. ᴹᵃ

He doesn't have to ask. He can feel Noise already looking. That vast precise attention turned entirely on one curve near the base of the third ring, checking, rechecking, running the weight through it a hundred times in the space of a breath.

There's a long silence.

“She's right,” Noise says. ᴺ

It sounds personally wounded.

“She's right,” Mau tells Mai. “Noise says you're right.” ᴹ

“I know I'm right,” Mai says. “Tell Noise I said it was good work. All the rest of it. That's the best pattern I've ever seen. I just have to keep it from tipping.” ᴹᵃ

Mau tells Noise.

Noise doesn't say anything. But he feels it, very slightly, go warm.

* * *

Yuta comes in in the afternoon.

Somebody told him. Probably Mai; probably Mai went and found him, which she would never admit. He comes in quietly and stands at the end of the bench beside her and looks at the pattern hanging over the slate, still holding its shape where Mau has kept it all day, a thin thread of Authority he's barely noticing any more.

Yuta looks at it for a long time.

Then he reaches up and touches the ring on his own hand. The one he's worn longer than Mau has known him.

“That's mine,” he says quietly. ʸᵗ

He's looking at the first circle. The flat one.

“That's how mine holds her,” he says. “I always knew it did something. I didn't know it looked like *that*.” ʸᵗ

He stands there with his hand on his ring and doesn't say anything else for a while.

“When you build it,” he says finally, “can I be there?” ʸᵗ

“Of course,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuta nods. He keeps looking at the pattern.

* * *

“When,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

It isn't really a question.

Mau thinks. “Tomorrow?” ᴹ

“No,” say Mai and Yuka at exactly the same moment. ᴹᵃ

He looks at them.

“Not tomorrow,” Yuka says quickly. ʸ

“Not this week,” Mai says, just as quickly. ᴹᵃ

“Why not this week?” ᴹ

The two of them look at each other.

“Shoko,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Shoko,” Mai agrees. “Your arm. It needs to be stronger. You've been holding that up for six hours and you're shaking.” ᴹᵃ

He looks at his arm. He is shaking. He hadn't noticed.

“After,” Mai says. “Next week. After—” ᴹᵃ

She stops.

“After Shoko says,” Yuka finishes smoothly. ʸ

Mai nods very firmly. “After Shoko says.” ᴹᵃ

He looks from one of them to the other. Something is happening that he doesn't understand. Mai is never smooth. Yuka is never this quick to agree with anyone about Shoko.

“Okay,” he says slowly. ᴹ

“*Okay*,” says Yuka, and takes her feet out of his lap so she can climb into it instead. ʸ

* * *

He lets the pattern go at sunset.

He doesn't want to. He'd keep it up all night if Mai let him. But his arm is shaking badly now, and Yuka has her hand on his wrist, and Mai has taken a long sheet of thin paper and laid it carefully under the pattern and is waiting.

He opens his hand. Authority lets go.

The dust falls. All at once, straight down, the way snow falls from a branch when the wind takes it. It lands on Mai's paper in a thin pale drift, and for a moment it's just dust.

Then Mai lifts the paper to the lamp.

The pattern is still there. Pressed into the dust as it fell. Faint, blurred at the edges, but there: three rings and the web between them, and the one curve near the base of the third ring that bends further now than it did this morning, the way Mai said.

She folds the paper very carefully. She puts it in a box. She puts the box on the top shelf of the workshop, where she keeps the things she doesn't let anyone touch.

“Next week,” she says. ᴹᵃ

* * *

That night, Yuka is strange.

Not strange. *Busy*. She keeps getting up. She goes downstairs twice after they've gone to bed and comes back both times saying she was thirsty. She whispers with Ori in the hallway. When he asks what about, she says *nothing* in a voice so innocent that Beru, outside the door, makes a small sound that might be a cough.

“Is something happening?” he asks, when she finally lies down. ᴹ

“No,” she says. ʸ

“Something's happening.” ᴹ

“Nothing's happening.” ʸ

“Mai said *after*. And then she stopped.” ᴹ

“Mai always stops,” says Yuka. “She's very busy. She has a lot of things to think about.” ʸ

She settles on his chest. Her hand finds the line on his arm.

“Go to sleep,” she says. ʸ

He lies there in the dark for a long time, trying to work out what he's missing.

He doesn't think to look at the calendar.
