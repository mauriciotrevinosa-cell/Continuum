# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 8 — Do Whatever You Want

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E60
**Continuity state:** B+365, morning. Mau wakes alone. On Yuka's pillow, a note: she won't be around for a while, he should do whatever he wants. He has no idea it's his birthday. The house is strange: everyone busy, everyone shooing him out of rooms. Shoko, on the porch, clears him for one gate, today only, and won't say why today. Beru asks to do a dungeon with him. At the household gate, Mau uses his authority as operator to set it hard and without restrictions, for him and Beru alone. He asks Beru to leave both hats behind, because if they get dirty Ori will kill them both. Beru runs to put them away and comes back. "Ciel, ready?" "Always."

*When: B+365. A little after dawn. The calendar on Fern's desk has a small careful circle drawn round today's square. Mau hasn't looked at it.*

He wakes up because she isn't there.

That's unusual enough to wake him. He's used to waking with her on him: her chin on his chest, her hair in his mouth, her hand on the line on his arm. He reaches for her before he's fully awake and finds the sheet cool.

He opens his eyes.

Her side of the bed is empty. Her pillow has been plumped and laid flat, the way she never leaves it. And on the pillow, folded once, a piece of paper.

* * *

He picks it up.

It's her handwriting. Round and careful, the same hand that wrote *He said forever* in her notebook.

*Won't be around for a while this morning. Don't come looking. Do whatever you want. — Y.*

And underneath, smaller, as if added at the last second:

*Seriously. WHATEVER you want.*

He reads it three times.

“Ciel,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes?” ᶜ

“Where's Yuka?” ᴹ

A pause. A very slightly too long pause.

“I closed my view,” Ciel says. “Earlier. She asked me to. She said it was private.” ᶜ

“Private what?” ᴹ

“She didn't say,” Ciel says, in a voice so smooth he could skate on it. ᶜ

* * *

The house is strange.

He notices it on the stairs. The third one creaks, the way it always does, and somewhere below him three different doors close at once.

He notices it in the kitchen. Shuna is there, the way she always is, with flour on her hands. But when he comes in, she turns round very fast and stands in front of something on the counter, and smiles at him with a smile he's never seen on her, bright and fixed and slightly desperate.

“Good morning!” she says. ˢʰᵘ

“Good morning,” he says. “What's that?” ᴹ

“Bread,” says Shuna. ˢʰᵘ

“Behind you.” ᴹ

“Also bread.” ˢʰᵘ

“It's covered with a cloth.” ᴹ

“It's very *shy* bread,” says Shuna, and turns him round by the shoulders and walks him back out of the kitchen before he can argue. ˢʰᵘ

* * *

It's like that everywhere.

He goes to the common room. Umaru is there with Rem and Taihei and the Fort kids, all clustered round the table, and the moment he walks in they all straighten up and spread out with their arms behind their backs and look at him with enormous innocence.

“Hi, Mau,” says Ori. ᴼ

“What are you doing?” ᴹ

“Nothing,” says Ori. ᴼ

“There's paint on your face.” ᴹ

Ori touches her cheek. Looks at her fingers. Looks at Chloe.

“That's—” Ori says. ᴼ

“Breakfast,” says Chloe firmly. ᶜʰ

“Breakfast was paint?” ᴹ

“Go *away*, Mau,” says Umaru, with great love, and Rem gets up and turns him round by the shoulders and walks him out of that room too. ᵁ

* * *

He goes to the workshop. The door is locked. Mai has never in her life locked the workshop door.

“Mai?” he says through it. ᴹ

“*Busy*,” says Mai's voice. ᴹᵃ

“Is Yuka in there?” ᴹ

A very long pause.

“No,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

He stands there looking at the locked door.

“Yuka,” he says. ᴹ

Another very long pause.

“She's *not here*,” says Mai, very firmly. “Go *away*. Go do whatever you want.” ᴹᵃ

He hears, very faintly, someone on the other side of the door trying very hard not to laugh.

* * *

He ends up on the porch.

He doesn't know what else to do. Everyone's busy. Everyone's turning him round by the shoulders. The note in his pocket says *whatever you want* and he can't think of a single thing he wants that the house will let him do.

Shoko is on the porch.

She's sitting in her chair at the end with a cup of tea and a book, and she looks up when he comes out, and something happens to her face that he can't read.

“Morning,” she says. ˢʰ

“Everyone's acting strange,” he says. ᴹ

“Are they?” ˢʰ

“Shuna has shy bread.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at her book very hard.

“Sit,” she says. ˢʰ

He sits.

* * *

“I'm clearing you,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

He looks at her.

“For a gate,” she says. “One. Today only.” ˢʰ

“You said no gates.” ᴹ

“I said no gates until I said. I'm saying.” ˢʰ

“Why today?” ᴹ

Shoko turns a page of her book. She doesn't read it.

“Because the arm's strong enough,” she says. “Because the side's closed. Because you've been sitting on this porch for a week vibrating like a bee in a jar. Because it's a nice day.” ˢʰ

She pauses.

“Because it's a very nice day,” she says. ˢʰ

There's something in her voice. He doesn't know what.

“One gate,” she says. “Not the one with the dragon war. Not anything with a castle. Something you can come home from by—” ˢʰ

She glances at the sky. At the angle of the sun.

“By late afternoon,” she says. “Not later. *Not later*, Mau.” ˢʰ

* * *

Beru finds him ten minutes later.

He comes up out of the shadow under the porch steps, both hats on, and stands at the bottom of the stairs, very straight, the way he stands when he has something to ask and has rehearsed it.

“My king,” he says. ᴮᵉ

“Beru.” ᴹ

“I would like to do a dungeon with you.” ᴮᵉ

Mau looks at him.

“Today?” he says. ᴹ

“Today,” says Beru. “Just the two of us. As it was in the beginning. Before the house. Before the queen. When it was only my king and his ant, and the dark, and whatever was in it.” ᴮᵉ

He pauses.

“I have missed it,” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

Mau doesn't say anything for a moment.

Behind him, on the porch, Shoko has put her book down and is looking at the two of them.

“Shoko just cleared me,” Mau says slowly. ᴹ

“A fortunate coincidence,” says Beru. ᴮᵉ

He says it in a voice Mau has heard him use exactly once before: when he told Ori that he certainly hadn't eaten the last biscuit, while there were biscuit crumbs on his mandibles.

* * *

The household gate is behind the orchard.

It's the one the house uses every week: the one Yuka set, on the morning at the table, *hard enough that I have to try, not so hard anybody comes home without skin on their hand, something warm in it somewhere*. A tall faint oval of light between two old trees, humming quietly to itself.

He stands in front of it.

He reaches for the permission. It's there, at the edge of him, the way it's been since the red gate. *Operator.* A box of light opens in front of his eyes.

> **[Gate Authority: household weekly gate.]**
>
> **[Current setting: household default.]**
>
> **[Adjust?]**

He thinks about it.

Yuka's setting is for the house. For Maki and Yuta to push themselves. For Ori to go in with Lantern and come out laughing. For the warm place at the bottom where anyone can rest.

It isn't for him and Beru.

“Hard,” he says. ᴹ

The box waits.

“As hard as it goes,” he says. “No restrictions. For us two only. Put it back the way she had it when we come out.” ᴹ

> **[Adjustment accepted: one entry, two participants, no restrictions. Household default restored on exit.]**

The light in the oval changes. He can't see how. But he can *feel* it: a weight coming off the gate that wasn't there before, deep and old and patient, like a breath held in a very large chest.

Beru, beside him, makes a sound of pure joy.

* * *

“Beru,” Mau says. ᴹ

“My king?” ᴮᵉ

“The hats.” ᴹ

Beru goes very still.

“Both of them,” Mau says. “Take them off. Leave them here.” ᴹ

“My king, the first hat is—” ᴮᵉ

“The first hat is from Ori,” Mau says. “She made it with Mai. With room for your antennae. And the second one is from Mai, for the rain. And if we go in there, and come out, and there is so much as a *spot* on either of them—” ᴹ

He doesn't finish.

Beru looks at him with all his eyes.

“She would kill us,” Beru says slowly. ᴮᵉ

“She would kill us both,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

Beru doesn't move for a moment. Then, with enormous care, he reaches up and takes off the rain hat. Then the cloth hat underneath. He holds them both in his claws and looks at them.

Then he's gone.

Mau doesn't see him go. There's just a gust of wind and a blur and the leaves on the orchard path whirling up in a spiral, and then, very faintly, from the direction of the house, a door, and Beru's voice saying something urgent to someone, and Shuna's voice saying *yes, yes, I'll keep them safe*, and then another gust and another blur.

Beru is standing beside him again. Hatless. Slightly out of breath.

“They are with Shuna,” Beru says. “In the pantry. On the high shelf. Away from the bread.” ᴮᵉ

“Good.” ᴹ

“The bread was very shy,” Beru adds. ᴮᵉ

Mau looks at him.

Beru looks at the gate, very innocently.

* * *

He rolls his shoulders.

The new arm doesn't hurt. It's weaker than the other; he can feel the difference when he makes a fist. But it doesn't hurt. The side pulls when he lifts both arms over his head, the way Shoko said it would. He does it anyway. Lets it pull. Lets it remind him.

Eight days of a splint. A week of porch. A month, almost, since the hills.

The itch is back. He can feel it in his hands.

“Ciel,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes?” ᶜ

“Ready?” ᴹ

And Ciel, who has closed her view of his wife for some private reason all morning, who has been smooth enough to skate on, who has said nothing at all about why the whole house is turning him round by the shoulders, says, with something in her voice that sounds very much like a smile:

“Always.” ᶜ

He walks into the gate. Beru beside him, hatless, humming.
