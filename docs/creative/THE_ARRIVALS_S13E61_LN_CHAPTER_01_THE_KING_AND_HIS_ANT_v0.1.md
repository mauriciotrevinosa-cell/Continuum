# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 9 — The King and His Ant

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S13E61
**Continuity state:** B+365, late morning to afternoon, inside the household gate at its hardest setting. Mau and Beru alone, the way it was before the house. Mau, healed but with the new arm still weaker, fights with daggers, Authority, Blue and Red, Shrine in small cuts, and Bang, which Shoko's clearance for the gate allows. Beru fights the way he hasn't been able to in months. They clear floor after floor without stopping. The guardian is a many-legged thing of stone and old hunger; Beru holds it and Mau finishes it. The system: level 5. Point allocation unlocked. Mau doesn't spend any; he wants to look at it with Yuka. Beru, on the way out, says it was the best morning he's had since the beginning. They come out in the late afternoon, as Shoko said.

*When: B+365. Late morning. Inside the gate, the light is the flat grey of a cave lit from nowhere. Outside, the house is very busy.*

The first room tries to kill them before they've finished stepping into it.

That's how he knows the setting took.

The floor drops. Not a trap: the whole floor, the instant their weight is on it, tipping away into a dark that has teeth at the bottom. He feels Sky go under his feet before he's thought about it. Beru's wings snap open beside him. And the walls come alive, long and many-jointed and grey as old stone, reaching for them both from every side at once.

Beru laughs.

It's a sound Mau hasn't heard in months. Not the polite rumble Beru makes at the dinner table. Not the soft *my king* in the hallway at night. A real laugh. Wild. Delighted. The laugh of something that was made to do exactly this and has been wearing a hat on a porch for far too long.

“*My king*,” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

“Go,” says Mau. ᴹ

Beru goes.

* * *

It's like the beginning.

He doesn't remember the beginning, not really. Not the way Beru does. He knows it happened: a tower, floors and floors of it, Beru newly raised at his side and the two of them going up through the dark together because there wasn't anyone else. He knows it the way you know something you were told. But his body remembers. His body knows how to stand with Beru on his left. How far ahead Beru will go. Which side Beru will leave open for him to take.

The walls come at them and Beru goes through them like a scythe through grass.

Mau goes after him.

* * *

The new arm is weaker.

He feels it on the first dagger stroke. The grip Jin-Woo spent weeks correcting on his bad fingers; the arm that was in eleven pieces nine days ago. The dagger goes in where he means it to go, but it doesn't go in as *far*, and when the thing he's cutting twists, his wrist twists with it a little more than it should.

He adjusts.

That's all. He adjusts, the way he's always adjusted. He lets the right hand lead and the left follow. He lets Authority take some of the weight the left wrist can't: a small grip on the hilt, steadying it, the way he steadied his own broken arm in a room with a bed. It isn't perfect. It's enough.

“You favour the left,” Ciel observes. ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

“It's fine. I'm just telling you I see it.” ᶜ

“Tell Shoko and I'll—” ᴹ

“I'm not telling Shoko anything,” Ciel says. “Today.” ᶜ

* * *

They go down.

The gate at its hardest isn't a tower. It's a well. A spiral of rooms going down and down into the earth, each one darker and older than the last, each one full of something that has been waiting a very long time for someone to come down and be eaten. Stone things with too many legs. Things that live in the walls and come out of them. A long low hall full of something like water that isn't water and burns where it touches.

They don't stop.

That's the thing he notices, after a while. He doesn't want to stop. The itch that's been in his hands since the hills, since the splint, since a week of sitting on the porch with Yuka's head in his lap: it's going. Every room it goes a little more. He uses Blue to pull a whole wall of the many-legged things off their feet and Beru goes through the gap before they hit the ground. He uses Red to blow the burning water back up the hall, and they run along the dry stone it leaves before it can fall again. He cuts with Shrine, small, careful, a single line here and there where a single line is enough. He doesn't open anything bigger. He doesn't need to.

And when a stone thing the size of a cart comes at him out of a side tunnel faster than he can turn, he lifts two fingers without thinking.

“Bang,” he says. ᴹ

It stops having a middle.

Beru turns round and looks at him.

“My king,” he says. “When did you learn *that*?” ᴮᵉ

“Makima,” Mau says. “Sort of. It's a long story.” ᴹ

“I would like to hear it,” says Beru, “after we have killed everything.” ᴮᵉ

* * *

He doesn't count the floors.

Ciel does. Ciel always does. Somewhere past the twentieth, she tells him, quietly, without being asked, the number, and how long they've been down, and the angle of the sun outside, which she can't see but can work out.

“Afternoon,” she says. “Early. Shoko said late afternoon. You have time.” ᶜ

“How much?” ᴹ

“Enough. If you don't stop to talk to the ant about his feelings.” ᶜ

Beside him, Beru, who can't hear Ciel, is telling him about a tunnel in the tower where they once fought something with a hundred mouths, which Mau doesn't remember at all and Beru remembers perfectly.

“It tried to eat your boot,” Beru says. “You were very upset. It was a new boot.” ᴮᵉ

“I don't remember that.” ᴹ

“You do not remember most things, my king,” says Beru. “That is why you have me.” ᴮᵉ

Mau stops. Just for a moment. In the middle of a dark stone room with something dead at his feet, he stops and looks at Beru.

Beru looks back at him with all his eyes.

“That is what I am for,” Beru says. “Partly. To remember for you. All of it. Every floor. Every boot.” ᴮᵉ

Mau doesn't say anything. He doesn't have anything to say.

He puts his hand on Beru's armoured shoulder, briefly. Then they go on down.

* * *

The guardian is at the bottom.

It's waiting in a round chamber with a floor of black stone polished smooth by something moving over it for a very long time. It's huge. Many-legged, the way the stone things were, but more: dozens of legs, hundreds, a great domed body of grey stone cracked all over like dry earth, and in the cracks, something glowing faintly that the eyes read as *hunger*.

It's been waiting here longer than the house has existed.

It turns toward them. Slowly. All its legs moving at once, like a tide.

“Beru,” Mau says. ᴹ

“My king.” ᴮᵉ

“Hold it.” ᴹ

Beru goes.

* * *

He holds it.

That's what Beru does. Mau has seen him do it a hundred times and still isn't used to it: the way Beru meets something ten times his size head-on, wings spread, claws out, and just *stops* it. Plants himself on the polished stone and takes the full weight of the thing on his armour and doesn't move. The legs come down on him like rain. He catches them. Breaks them. Holds.

“*Now*, my king—” ᴮᵉ

Mau is already there.

He goes up the side of the thing on Sky, through the forest of legs, onto the dome of its back. He finds the crack where the hunger is brightest, where the eyes say everything comes from. He puts his hand flat on the stone.

Blue in the right. He doesn't trust the left yet. Just Blue. A small one. Pressed straight down into the crack, into the place where the hunger lives.

It pulls.

The dome caves in around his hand like a rotten fruit.

* * *

The guardian goes still.

All its legs at once. They stop, and stand there, and then fold, one by one, slowly, like a building settling, until the great cracked dome is lying on the polished floor and the glow in the cracks has gone out.

Mau slides down the side of it. His legs are shaking. The new arm is shaking. His side is pulling hard enough now that he presses his hand over it.

Beru is standing at the bottom, wings half folded, armour scratched and dented in a dozen places, breathing hard.

They look at each other.

Then Beru starts to laugh again, and Mau does too, and they stand in the middle of the guardian's chamber laughing like idiots, because it's been months, because the itch is gone, because it was *good*.

* * *

The box comes while they're still laughing.

> **[Gate cleared.]**
>
> **[Guardian defeated.]**

And then, a moment later, a different box. One he's only seen once before, five days ago, on a page he never knew he had.

> **[Level 5 reached.]**
>
> **[Point allocation unlocked.]**
>
> **[Unallocated points: 10.]**

He stops laughing.

He reads it. Three times. Beru, beside him, can't see the boxes, but he can see Mau's face, and goes quiet.

“My king?” ᴮᵉ

“Five,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Five what?” ᴮᵉ

“Level five. I've got points. Ten of them. I can—” ᴹ

He stops.

He doesn't know what he can. He's never spent points on this scale. He spent them on the old one, a long time ago, in a place nobody else saw, the numbers going up one by one. He doesn't know what ten points means when two is what two hundred used to be.

“Noise?” he says. ᴹ

“I'm looking,” Noise says. “I'll need time. Don't spend them yet.” ᴺ

“I wasn't going to.” ᴹ

He wasn't. He looks at the box, at *10*, and thinks about the notebook. *Things you didn't know you had.* About sitting on the bench in the common room with her in his lap going through every page of the Shop, one sweet at a time.

He closes the box.

“Later,” he says. “With Yuka. She'll want to write it down.” ᴹ

* * *

They come out in the late afternoon.

The orchard is exactly as they left it. The two old trees. The faint oval of light between them, humming quietly to itself, already settling back into the shape she gave it. *Something warm in it, somewhere.* He feels the setting go back, the way he asked.

The sun is low. Shoko said not later. It isn't later.

Beru stands beside him on the orchard path. Dented. Scratched. Hatless. Looking toward the house.

“My king,” he says. ᴮᵉ

“Yeah?” ᴹ

“That was the best morning I have had,” Beru says, “since the beginning.” ᴮᵉ

Mau looks at him.

“Me too,” he says. ᴹ

He means it.

And then he notices the house.

* * *

It's very quiet.

That's what he notices first. The house is never quiet in the late afternoon. There's always someone in the yard, someone on the porch, someone shouting in the kitchen. Right now there's nobody. The yard is empty. The porch is empty. The windows are dark, all of them, though the sun is going down and someone should have lit a lamp.

“Beru,” he says slowly. ᴹ

“Yes, my king?” ᴮᵉ

“Where is everyone?” ᴹ

Beru looks at the dark windows. Then at the sky. Then very carefully at a tree.

“I could not say,” says Beru. ᴮᵉ

And Ciel, inside, very quietly, says: “You should go inside.” ᶜ
