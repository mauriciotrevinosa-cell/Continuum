# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 10 — The Day They Picked

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E61
**Continuity state:** B+365, evening and night. Mau walks into the dark common room and the whole house shouts. It's his birthday, the day the house picked for him back in S3; he had forgotten. Ori's painted banner. Shuna's shy bread was the cake. The Sungs, Makima at the edge, DIO bouncing, Gojo's speech cut short by Shoko. Elmesia's bird arrives with a letter: happy birthday, and her gift is waiting on the island. Frieren gives him her favourite spell, the one that makes flowers bloom, to make for Yuka on the island. He tells the house about level five; Yuka writes it down. At night, alone, Yuka gives him what she and Mai made in the locked workshop: his own enormous hat, to match hers, for the island. They're comfortable; she puts it on him; a kiss turns into a longer one. Intimate scene in a note for Work.

*When: B+365. Sunset. The house has been dark for exactly as long as it took Beru to walk Mau back from the orchard very, very slowly.*

He opens the door of the common room and it's dark.

Not just dim. *Dark*. Every lamp out. The curtains drawn. He stands in the doorway with Beru behind him and the last of the sunset at his back, and his eyes, without being asked, open a little and start to read the room.

There are a lot of heartbeats in it.

All of them. Every one. Packed into the common room in the dark, holding their breath, perfectly still. Thirty, forty, more. He can feel them the way he can feel his shadows. Ori's, quick and small, very close to the door. Milim's, much too fast. Gojo's, slow and amused. Shoko's. Rimuru's. The Sungs, all of them, at the back.

And one more. Near the middle. Racing.

Hers.

He stands there in the doorway with the eyes open and every heartbeat in the house in front of him, and has absolutely no idea why.

* * *

“*NOW*,” Ori screams. ᴼ

The lamps come on. All at once. Somebody, Rimuru probably, does something that lights every one of them in the same instant, and the room is suddenly full of light and people and noise, so much noise, everyone shouting the same two words at once at the top of their lungs:

“*HAPPY BIRTHDAY!*”

He stands there.

* * *

There's a banner.

It's across the far wall, from the window to the fireplace, a long strip of cloth with letters painted on it. Big, uneven, enthusiastic letters, some of them upside down and corrected, one of them painted twice because the first one was wrong. HAPPY BIRTHDAY MAU. And underneath, smaller, in a different hand: *(AGAIN)*.

There's paint on Ori's face. There's paint on Chloe's hands. There's paint on the Pillow, who is lying in front of the fireplace with a long smear across his shaggy flank and an expression of profound dignity.

There's a cake. On the table. Under the cloth that was in the kitchen this morning. The shy bread. It isn't bread. It's enormous and it's tiered and Shuna is standing beside it with her hands clasped and her eyes very bright.

And there's Yuka. In the middle of the room. In the blue dress. Looking at him.

* * *

“It's your birthday,” she says. ʸ

He stares at her.

“It's—” ᴹ

“The day we picked,” says Rimuru, from somewhere to his left. “You didn't have one, so the house picked one for you. And you forgot it. Again.” ᴿ

He did. He absolutely did. He hasn't thought about it once. He thought about the inventory and the ring and the level page and Shoko's *not later* and the dark windows, and not once, not for a second, did it occur to him that today was anything.

“You forgot,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I forgot,” he admits. ᴹ

“I *knew* you'd forget.” ʸ

“How did you know?” ᴹ

She holds up her notebook.

“It's in here,” she says. “*Things he forgets.* Page one. Line one. *His birthday*.” ʸ

The room laughs.

* * *

He doesn't remember most of the next hour.

Not because anything goes wrong. Because there's too much of it. People come at him from every side: hugging him, shaking his good hand and then his new one very carefully, shouting things over each other. Milim jumps on his back from a chair and has to be removed by Shoko. Gojo stands on the table and begins a speech that starts with *on the day this idiot walked into our lives* and gets about three sentences in before Shoko reaches up and pulls him off it by the back of his shirt.

The Sungs are there. All of them. Jin-Woo at the back, smiling the small real smile Mau has only seen on him a few times. Suho comes running and wraps himself round Mau's legs and announces that Sir Shadow helped make the banner, which Mau is fairly sure isn't true, and which he decides to believe.

DIO is bouncing. He can feel it through the place where her attention lives: a steady joyful drumming, like rain on a roof.

Makima is at the edge of the room, by the window. Alone. She doesn't come close. But when he looks at her, she inclines her head, and holds up a cup, just slightly, the way you'd toast someone across a room you weren't sure you were allowed to be in.

He lifts his own back.

* * *

The bird comes during the cake.

He's sitting at the table with Yuka in his lap and a plate in front of him that Shuna keeps refilling, and there's a tap at the window. A big dark bird with a band on its leg, sitting on the sill outside, looking at him with an expression of deep personal grievance.

“It's *that* bird,” Yuka says. ʸ

“It's that bird,” he agrees. ᴹ

He opens the window. The bird holds out its leg. There's a letter. He takes it, and then, because he's learned, he holds out a piece of cake. The bird looks at the cake. Looks at him. Takes the cake. Doesn't leave.

He opens the letter. Yuka reads it out loud over his shoulder.

“*Dear Mau. I'm told it's your birthday. Your wife told me. She tells me a great many things. Happy birthday. I have a gift for you. It's waiting on the island. You'll have it when you come. Don't ask what it is; I won't tell you, and neither will she, because she doesn't know either. Bring everyone. Come through the floor on purpose. — E.*” ʸ

“She knows about the floor,” Mau says. ᴹ

“*Everyone* knows about the floor,” says Yuka. ʸ

The bird finishes its cake. It looks at Mau's plate. He gives it another piece. It takes that too, and then, finally, flies away, heavier than when it came.

* * *

Frieren gives him something at the end.

She doesn't make a fuss about it. She waits until most people have moved away from the table, back toward the fire and the cake and Milim, who is trying to convince the Pillow to wear a paper hat. Then she sits down across from him and holds out her hand, palm up, over the table.

A small flower opens in her palm.

Then another. Then a third, spilling over her fingers onto the wood, and then a fourth on the table itself, growing up out of the grain, small and perfect and grey to him, all of them grey, a little field of them blooming between his plate and hers.

He's seen her do it before. Not often. It's the spell she likes best. The one she learned a long time ago, for no reason except that someone she travelled with liked flowers.

“I'll teach it to you,” she says. “Tomorrow. It's not useful. It doesn't do anything except this.” ᶠ

She looks at the little field on the table.

“You can make them for her,” she says. “On the island. She'll tell you what colour they are.” ᶠ

He can't say anything. He just looks at the flowers.

“Happy birthday,” says Frieren, and gets up, and goes back to her book by the fire, and leaves the flowers on the table. ᶠ

Yuka, in his lap, has gone very still. She reaches out and touches one of them with one finger.

“They're yellow,” she says, very quietly. “And some of them are white. And that one's blue.” ʸ

He looks at the blue one. He can't see it. He keeps looking anyway.

* * *

He tells them about the level near the end of the night.

He doesn't mean to make an announcement. He just says it to Yuka, quietly, at the table, while the room is loud: *I hit five today. In the gate. With Beru. I've got ten points.* And Yuka stops with a piece of cake halfway to her mouth, and stares at him, and then stands up on the bench and shouts it to the whole room.

“*HE'S LEVEL FIVE*.” ʸ

The room stops.

“*On his BIRTHDAY*,” Yuka shouts. “*With BERU*. *And he didn't spend ANY of the points because he wanted to do it WITH ME*.” ʸ

The room explodes again.

Beru, by the door, stands up very straight with both hats back on, which Shuna returned to him from the high shelf in the pantry the moment he walked in, and looks enormously proud.

Yuka sits back down. She takes out her notebook. She writes, very carefully, on a fresh page: *Level 5. B+365. With Beru. 10 points. NOT SPENT. (waiting for me)*

She underlines *waiting for me* three times.

* * *

They go upstairs late.

The house is still loud below them; Milim has found the music and Bocchi has been persuaded to play, and someone is dancing badly. But they're done. He's tired in the good way, the way the gate left him, the itch gone and the new arm aching pleasantly and his side pulling and his stomach full of cake.

Yuka closes the door behind them.

She doesn't go to the bed. She goes to the wardrobe and opens it and reaches up to the top shelf, behind his folded shirts, and takes something down.

“One more,” she says. ʸ

* * *

It's a hat.

An enormous hat. Wide-brimmed and slightly lopsided and absurd, woven out of something light, with a band round it in a fabric he can feel is good cloth, the kind Mai keeps for special things.

He looks at it.

He knows this hat. Or he knows its sister. He bought its sister for her on the island, in the village, with Elmesia's pass, and she wore it all day on principle and it blew off twice and he caught it both times.

“Mai and I made it,” Yuka says. “In the workshop. That's why the door was locked. I wanted it to match.” ʸ

“Match.” ᴹ

“Mine,” she says. “For the island. When we go back. I'm going to wear mine. And you're going to wear yours. And everyone on that whole island is going to know we came together.” ʸ

She holds it out.

“It's for the island,” she says. “Not a toy. Not a thing. Something to wear when we go back. So we match.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't take it.

He can't, for a moment. He just stands there looking at it, and at her holding it, in the blue dress, with her face doing something soft and nervous, like she's not sure he'll like it.

Then she gets tired of waiting, and steps forward, and goes up on her toes, and puts it on his head herself.

It's too big. It comes down over his eyebrows. She laughs and pushes it back with both hands, and her hands stay there, on either side of his face, under the brim.

“There,” she says. ʸ

“How do I look?” ᴹ

She considers him very seriously. The enormous hat. The paint someone smeared on his collar during the party. His face.

“Ridiculous,” she says. ʸ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“*Very* ridiculous.” ʸ

“You made it.” ᴹ

“I know. I'm very proud.” ʸ

They're standing very close. Her hands are still on his face. The brim of the hat is shading both of them.

He leans down.

* * *

Much later, the hat is on the floor.

It's lying upside down by the bed where it fell, enormous and lopsided and absurd, the brim crushed a little on one side. Neither of them has picked it up.

Yuka is asleep on his chest. Her hand is on his arm, on the line, where she fell asleep tracing it.

Downstairs the music has stopped. The house has gone quiet, the way it does at the very end of a long day: a door, a laugh, the third stair creaking once and then nothing.

On the bedside table, beside Ori's *welcome home* note and the chocolate wrapper from the red gate, there's one of Frieren's small flowers, which Yuka carried up in her hand from the common room and put in a cup of water.

He looks at it for a long time.

He thinks about the day the house picked a date and said *this one, this is yours*.

He didn't have one. Now he has one. He forgets it, and they remember.

“Happy birthday,” Yuka mumbles into his chest, half asleep. ʸ

“You already said.” ᴹ

“I'm saying it again. In case you forget.” ʸ

He puts his hand in her hair.

“I won't,” he says. ᴹ

He's fairly sure he will. He's fairly sure she'll remind him. He's fairly sure that's the point.
