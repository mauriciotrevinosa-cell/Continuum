# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 10 — The Day They Picked

**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E61
**Continuity state:** B+365, evening and night. Mau walks into the dark common room and the whole house shouts. It's his birthday, the day the house picked for him back in S3; he had forgotten. Ori's painted banner. Shuna's shy bread was the cake. The Sungs, Makima at the edge, DIO bouncing, Gojo's speech cut short by Shoko. Elmesia's bird arrives with a letter: happy birthday, and her gift is waiting on the island. Umaru and Jin-Woo offer to help with the points (level 5 = old 500; the unspent points equal 300 old levels, from 2 to 5). He tells the house about level five; Yuka writes it down. At night, alone, Yuka gives him what she and Mai made in the locked workshop: his own enormous hat, to match hers, for the island. They're comfortable; she puts it on him; a kiss turns into a longer one. Intimate scene in a note for Work.

*When: B+365. Sunset. The house has been dark for exactly as long as it took Beru to walk Mau back from the orchard very, very slowly.*

He opens the door of the common room and it's dark.

Not just dim. *Dark*. Every lamp out. The curtains drawn. He stands in the doorway with Beru behind him and the last of the sunset at his back, and his eyes, without being asked, open a little and start to read the room.

There are a lot of heartbeats in it.

All of them. Every one. Packed into the common room in the dark, holding their breath, perfectly still. Thirty, forty, more. He can feel them the way he can feel his shadows. Ori's, quick and small, very close to the door. Milim's, much too fast. Gojo's, slow and amused. Shoko's. Rimuru's. The Sungs, all of them, at the back.

And one more. Near the middle. Racing.

Hers.

He stands there in the doorway with the eyes open and every heartbeat in the house in front of him, and has absolutely no idea why.

* * *

“*NOW*,” Ori screams. ᴼ

The lamps come on. All at once. Somebody, Rimuru probably, does something that lights every one of them in the same instant, and the room is suddenly full of light and people and noise, so much noise, everyone shouting the same two words at once at the top of their lungs:

“*HAPPY BIRTHDAY!*”

He stands there.

* * *

There's a banner.

It's across the far wall, from the window to the fireplace, a long strip of cloth with letters painted on it. Big, uneven, enthusiastic letters, some of them upside down and corrected, one of them painted twice because the first one was wrong. HAPPY BIRTHDAY MAU. And underneath, smaller, in a different hand: *(AGAIN)*.

There's paint on Ori's face. There's paint on Chloe's hands. There's paint on the Pillow, who is lying in front of the fireplace with a long smear across his shaggy flank and an expression of profound dignity.

There's a cake. On the table. Under the cloth that was in the kitchen this morning. The shy bread. It isn't bread. It's enormous and it's tiered and Shuna is standing beside it with her hands clasped and her eyes very bright.

And there's Yuka. In the middle of the room. In the blue dress. Looking at him.

* * *

“It's your birthday,” she says. ʸ

He stares at her.

“It's—” ᴹ

“The day we picked,” says Rimuru, from somewhere to his left. “You didn't have one, so the house picked one for you. And you forgot it. Again.” ᴿ

He did. He absolutely did. He hasn't thought about it once. He thought about the inventory and the ring and the level page and Shoko's *not later* and the dark windows, and not once, not for a second, did it occur to him that today was anything.

“You forgot,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I forgot,” he admits. ᴹ

“I *knew* you'd forget.” ʸ

“How did you know?” ᴹ

She holds up her notebook.

“It's in here,” she says. “*Things he forgets.* Page one. Line one. *His birthday*.” ʸ

The room laughs.

* * *

He doesn't remember most of the next hour.

Not because anything goes wrong. Because there's too much of it. People come at him from every side: hugging him, shaking his good hand and then his new one very carefully, shouting things over each other. Milim jumps on his back from a chair and has to be removed by Shoko. Gojo stands on the table and begins a speech that starts with *on the day this idiot walked into our lives* and gets about three sentences in before Shoko reaches up and pulls him off it by the back of his shirt.

The Sungs are there. All of them. Jin-Woo at the back, smiling the small real smile Mau has only seen on him a few times. Suho comes running and wraps himself round Mau's legs and announces that Sir Shadow helped make the banner, which Mau is fairly sure isn't true, and which he decides to believe.

DIO is bouncing. He can feel it through the place where her attention lives: a steady joyful drumming, like rain on a roof.

Makima is at the edge of the room, by the window. Alone. She doesn't come close. But when he looks at her, she inclines her head, and holds up a cup, just slightly, the way you'd toast someone across a room you weren't sure you were allowed to be in.

He lifts his own back.

* * *

The bird comes during the cake.

He's sitting at the table with Yuka in his lap and a plate in front of him that Shuna keeps refilling, and there's a tap at the window. A big dark bird with a band on its leg, sitting on the sill outside, looking at him with an expression of deep personal grievance.

“It's *that* bird,” Yuka says. ʸ

“It's that bird,” he agrees. ᴹ

He opens the window. The bird holds out its leg. There's a letter. He takes it, and then, because he's learned, he holds out a piece of cake. The bird looks at the cake. Looks at him. Takes the cake. Doesn't leave.

He opens the letter. Yuka reads it out loud over his shoulder.

“*Dear Mau. I'm told it's your birthday. Your wife told me. She tells me a great many things. Happy birthday. I have a gift for you. It's waiting on the island. You'll have it when you come. Don't ask what it is; I won't tell you, and neither will she, because she doesn't know either. Bring everyone. Come through the floor on purpose. — E.*” ʸ

“She knows about the floor,” Mau says. ᴹ

“*Everyone* knows about the floor,” says Yuka. ʸ

The bird finishes its cake. It looks at Mau's plate. He gives it another piece. It takes that too, and then, finally, flies away, heavier than when it came.

* * *

Frieren gives him something at the end.

She waits until most people have moved away from the table, back toward the fire and the cake and Milim, who is trying to convince the Pillow to wear a paper hat. Then she sits down across from him with something wrapped in an old cloth, and puts it on the table between them, and doesn't let go of it.

He knows what it is before she unwraps it. He can tell by the way she's holding it.

A book. Old. The cover worn soft at the corners, the spine cracked and mended and cracked again. A grimoire. One of hers.

She unwraps it the rest of the way. She pushes it across the table toward him, an inch. Then she stops. Her hand is still on it.

“For you,” she says. ᶠ

Her hand doesn't move.

* * *

Everyone sees it.

That's the thing. Everyone at that end of the room. Fern, at the next table, puts her cup down very slowly. Stark, beside her, stops chewing. Even Gojo, who was halfway into a story about a curse that turned out to be a very large goat, goes quiet and turns his blindfolded face toward the table.

Because Frieren's face is doing something none of them have ever seen it do.

She's trying to look calm. She's trying very hard. She's pushing a grimoire across a table to someone she loves on his birthday, the way you're supposed to, and her face is perfectly composed, and her hand on the cover is *shaking*. Just slightly. Her fingers flat on the old leather, not quite able to lift. Her ears have gone down. Her eyes keep going to the book and away and back to the book, the way a child's eyes go to a toy she's been told to share.

She has been collecting grimoires for a thousand years. She has crossed continents for spells that do nothing but polish bronze. Mau has watched her trade a week of work for a single page about making grapes sour.

And she is giving one away. To him. And it is *killing* her.

Fern has put her hand over her mouth.

* * *

He looks at the book. He looks at her hand on it. He looks at her face.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

She nods. Her hand still doesn't move.

“I'll take it,” he says. ᴹ

Her fingers tighten on the cover, very slightly, and then, with visible effort, start to lift.

“But,” he says. ᴹ

She stops.

“I don't have anywhere to keep it,” he says. “Not properly. The inventory isn't the same. Things go in there and I forget them. I just found out I forgot a whole castle.” ᴹ

Somebody at the next table laughs and then stops.

“And I don't know how to look after something like this,” he says. “The pages. The binding. The way you keep them dry in winter. I'd ruin it. I'd leave it somewhere and Milim would use it as a step.” ᴹ

He looks at her.

“Would you keep it for me?” he asks. “In your room. With the others. Where it's safe. And when I want to read it, I'll come and read it there. With you.” ᴹ

* * *

Frieren's ears come up.

He watches it happen. Slowly, and then all at once: the long ears lifting from where they'd drooped, rising and rising until they're higher than he's seen them in months. Her hand on the book goes still. Her face does something complicated, and then something very simple.

“Yes,” she says. ᶠ

Very quickly. Before he can change his mind.

“Yes. I can do that. I'll keep it. You can come and read it. Whenever you want. I'll make room on the shelf. By the window. Where the light is good.” ᶠ

She's already pulling the book back toward her. Wrapping it in the cloth. Tucking the corners in with enormous care.

“It's still yours,” she says, holding it against her chest. “I'm just keeping it.” ᶠ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“It's *yours*.” ᶠ

“I know.” ᴹ

She stands up, holding it. She looks at him a moment longer. Her ears are still up.

“Happy birthday,” she says, and goes back to her chair by the fire, and sits down with the wrapped book in her lap and both hands on it, and doesn't open her other book for the rest of the night. ᶠ

* * *

Fern is looking at him across the tables.

She doesn't say anything. She just looks at him for a long moment, the way you'd look at someone who has just done something very kind very quietly and doesn't want to be thanked for it. Then she picks her cup back up.

Stark leans over to her.

“Did he just,” Stark starts. ˢᵗ

“Yes,” says Fern. ᶠᵉ

“So she still—” ˢᵗ

“Yes.” ᶠᵉ

“And he—” ˢᵗ

“He knew she would,” Fern says. “He's known her longer than any of us. He knew exactly what that book cost her.” ᶠᵉ

Yuka, in Mau's lap, has turned her face into his shoulder. He can feel her smiling.

“You're very good at her,” she says, so quietly only he can hear. ʸ

He doesn't say anything. He watches Frieren by the fire, with the book in her lap and her ears up. They aren't what they were. They haven't been for a long time. But she's his family, and he knows exactly what to do with a gift she can't bear to give.

* * *

He tells them about the level near the end of the night.

He doesn't mean to make an announcement. He just says it to Yuka, quietly, at the table, while the room is loud: *I hit five today. In the gate. With Beru. I've got points. Three hundred levels of them.* And Yuka stops with a piece of cake halfway to her mouth, and stares at him, and then stands up on the bench and shouts it to the whole room.

“*HE'S LEVEL FIVE*.” ʸ

The room stops.

“*On his BIRTHDAY*,” Yuka shouts. “*With BERU*. *And he didn't spend ANY of the points because he wanted to do it WITH ME*.” ʸ

The room explodes again.

Beru, by the door, stands up very straight with both hats back on, which Shuna returned to him from the high shelf in the pantry the moment he walked in, and looks enormously proud.

Umaru is on her feet before the noise has died down.

“How many points?” she demands. ᵁ

“Three hundred levels' worth.” ᴹ

Umaru's mouth opens. Nothing comes out.

“Everything from two to five,” Mau says. “On the old scale, that's two hundred to five hundred. They were locked until five. So they all piled up.” ᴹ

Umaru stares at him with an expression of pure professional hunger.

“Don't spend them,” she says. “Not one. Not until I've seen the stat sheet. I've done this a *thousand* times. You'll put them all in strength like an idiot.” ᵁ

“I wasn't going to—” ᴹ

“You *were*.” ᵁ

“If he wants help,” says Jin-Woo, quietly, from the back of the room, “I've done this too.” ᴶʷ

The room goes quiet for a second.

Jin-Woo shrugs.

“Not a game,” he says. “Mine was real. I know what happens when you put them in the wrong place. I'd be happy to look. If he wants.” ᴶʷ

Mau looks at him. At Umaru, vibrating with the need to see a stat sheet. At Yuka, in his lap, holding her notebook against her chest like a shield.

He can feel it in his hands. The itch. Not the old one, from the porch. A new one. Three hundred levels of points. Five hundred. He doesn't know what any of it means yet, and he wants to know so badly his fingers are twitching.

“Later,” he says. “All of you. With Yuka first.” ᴹ

Yuka sits back down. She takes out her notebook. She writes, very carefully, on a fresh page: *Level 5. B+365. With Beru. 300 LEVELS of points. NOT SPENT. (waiting for me)*

She underlines *waiting for me* three times.

* * *

They go upstairs late.

The house is still loud below them; Milim has found the music and Bocchi has been persuaded to play, and someone is dancing badly. But they're done. He's tired in the good way, the way the gate left him, the itch gone and the new arm aching pleasantly and his side pulling and his stomach full of cake.

Yuka closes the door behind them.

She doesn't go to the bed. She goes to the wardrobe and opens it and reaches up to the top shelf, behind his folded shirts, and takes something down.

“One more,” she says. ʸ

* * *

It's a hat.

An enormous hat. Wide-brimmed and slightly lopsided and absurd, woven out of something light, with a band round it in a fabric he can feel is good cloth, the kind Mai keeps for special things.

He looks at it.

He knows this hat. Or he knows its sister. He bought its sister for her on the island, in the village, with Elmesia's pass, and she wore it all day on principle and it blew off twice and he caught it both times.

“Mai and I made it,” Yuka says. “In the workshop. That's why the door was locked. I wanted it to match.” ʸ

“Match.” ᴹ

“Mine,” she says. “For the island. When we go back. I'm going to wear mine. And you're going to wear yours. And everyone on that whole island is going to know we came together.” ʸ

She holds it out.

“It's for the island,” she says. “Not a toy. Not a thing. Something to wear when we go back. So we match.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't take it.

He can't, for a moment. He just stands there looking at it, and at her holding it, in the blue dress, with her face doing something soft and nervous, like she's not sure he'll like it.

Then she gets tired of waiting, and steps forward, and goes up on her toes, and puts it on his head herself.

It's too big. It comes down over his eyebrows. She laughs and pushes it back with both hands, and her hands stay there, on either side of his face, under the brim.

“There,” she says. ʸ

“How do I look?” ᴹ

She considers him very seriously. The enormous hat. The paint someone smeared on his collar during the party. His face.

“Ridiculous,” she says. ʸ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“*Very* ridiculous.” ʸ

“You made it.” ᴹ

“I know. I'm very proud.” ʸ

They're standing very close. Her hands are still on his face. The brim of the hat is shading both of them.

He leans down.

He kisses her under the brim.

The hat immediately tries to help. The front edge drops across his eyes and strikes Yuka's forehead with a soft, ridiculous *thump*.

She starts laughing against his mouth.

“It's attacking me,” she says. ʸ

“You made it.”

“I know. I didn't make it for this.”

“What did you make it for?”

“So that when we get to the island people will look at the two of us and think, *those two have terrible taste in hats*.” ʸ

“They already think that.”

“Then we should be consistent.” ʸ

She takes the brim in both hands, tips it back, and makes a very deliberate inspection of his face. He's sure she's about to say something insulting. Instead she looks almost shy.

“Does it fit?”

“Eventually.”

“Mau.”

He lifts the hat and settles it properly, keeping it tipped back so he can see her.

“It fits,” he says. “I love it.” ᴹ

“Really?”

“Really.” ᴹ

She lets go of the brim. Her hands remain on his cheeks, thumbs moving once over the skin near his eyes.

“I wanted to get you something nobody could make you use for work,” she says. ʸ

“It's not good for work?”

“It's awful for work. That's the point.”

He thinks of the sword downstairs, the system, the numbers he used to think he had to keep chasing, the little mountain of things the whole house has given him because he always needs something fixed. A hat, apparently, is the first present he's received in a long time that doesn't ask him to do anything with it.

“You hid in the workshop for that,” he says. ᴹ

“Mai helped.”

“I know.”

“And nobody told you.”

“I noticed.”

“You didn't notice anything.” ʸ

“That's what I noticed.”

She laughs, triumphant, and takes his hand.

“Come sit down,” she says. ʸ

He lets her lead him to the bed, still wearing the hat. She sits beside him and turns to admire it from the side. The brim brushes the curtain when he looks toward the window; she pulls him back by the sleeve before he knocks anything over.

“You're worse than Beru,” she says. ʸ

“Beru has two hats and excellent balance.”

“You have one hat and a wife who'll stop you walking into things.”

“I like my chances.” ᴹ

She rests her head against his right shoulder. Downstairs someone starts another song, and they can hear Ori calling for someone to dance. Mau waits for the small ache to pass through his left side. It does, quickly. Yuka feels him take the deeper breath.

“Does it hurt?” she asks. ʸ

“A little. Not enough to stop.”

“That's not what I asked.”

He turns toward her, carefully.

“It's tired,” he says. “So am I. The good kind, like I told you.” ᴹ

“Then we can sit.”

“We can.”

She seems satisfied. She puts one hand on his left forearm and runs her thumb once along the healed line. He watches the movement under the shade of the brim.

He thinks about the other things she made happen tonight. The surprise. The whole house waiting in the dark. Ori's painted banner. Frieren handing him a grimoire with an expression that made the room feel suddenly very small. Shoko watching him read the letter from Elmesia and pretending she wasn't watching.

“Did you plan all of it?” he asks. ᴹ

“Not all. I was in charge of keeping you from remembering.”

“You did a very good job.”

“That part was easy.” ʸ

“Thank you.”

She turns her face toward him.

“For the hat?”

“For the whole day.” ᴹ

Her expression changes at once. She tries to look casual and fails.

“It's your birthday.”

“I know.”

“You didn't know this morning.” ʸ

“I know now.”

He takes her hand off his arm and folds it in his own. Her fingers are small enough to disappear between his when he closes his hand.

“I didn't know what to do when everyone shouted,” he says. “I'm used to being the one who shows up. The one who gets called when something's wrong. I don't always know what to do when everybody shows up for me instead.” ᴹ

“You don't have to do anything.”

“That's what you said about the hat.”

“It's a very good rule.” ʸ

He bends his head. She leans up at the same time, and they kiss again. The hat shifts sideways, but neither stops until she begins to laugh.

“Take it off before it gives me a bruise,” she whispers. ʸ

“You said I had to wear it.”

“On the island. Not in bed.” ʸ

He takes it off and sets it on the chair. She considers the chair, the hat, and the distance between them.

“Actually,” she says, “bring it back.”

He looks at her.

“Why?”

“Because I made it. I can make you wear it for five more minutes.” ʸ

“You're going to be unbearable on the island.”

“I'm going to be wonderful on the island.”

She reaches out. He lifts the hat from the chair and places it on her head instead. It is somehow even more enormous on her, covering one eye and most of her nose.

“Perfect,” he says. ᴹ

“You can't see me.”

“I can see the important part.”

“What important part?”

“The colour.”

She goes pink below the brim. He can see it plainly.

“That's cheating,” she says. ʸ

“It's my birthday.”

“That excuse expires at midnight.”

“We have a few minutes.”

She tips her head back far enough to uncover her face. She's still smiling, but quieter now, and she looks at him for a long time before she reaches up and touches his cheek.

“I like you like this,” she says. “Not because you won anything. Just because you're here.” ʸ

He thinks of the dungeon that morning and Beru's fierce pleasure at the end of it, and then of coming back through the door to find everyone waiting for him.

“I like being here,” he says. ᴹ

She kisses him then, still wearing the hat. It slides between them; she catches it with one hand, drops it onto the coverlet and laughs into his shoulder. He holds her until she can stop.

After a while she looks at the hat, lying upside down between the pillows.

“We're really going to wear these,” she says. ʸ

“Of course.”

“In public.”

“In front of everyone.” ᴹ

She nods, satisfied. They settle together against the headboard, trading stories from the party in low voices while the music carries through the floor. Whenever the song changes, she squeezes his hand and tells him who she thinks has taken control of the room downstairs.

Eventually she grows quiet, with her cheek against his chest and his hand in her hair. He draws the blanket across her shoulders. The hat slips from the coverlet when he reaches for the corner, landing softly beside the bed.

Neither of them bothers to pick it up.

* * *

Much later, the hat is on the floor.

It's lying upside down by the bed where it fell, enormous and lopsided and absurd, the brim crushed a little on one side. Neither of them has picked it up.

Yuka is asleep on his chest. Her hand is on his arm, on the line, where she fell asleep tracing it.

Downstairs the music has stopped. The house has gone quiet, the way it does at the very end of a long day: a door, a laugh, the third stair creaking once and then nothing.

On the bedside table there's Ori's *welcome home* note and the chocolate wrapper from the red gate. The grimoire is downstairs, on a shelf by Frieren's window, where the light is good. He'll go and read it tomorrow. With her.

He thinks about the day the house picked a date and said *this one, this is yours*.

He didn't have one. Now he has one. He forgets it, and they remember.

“Happy birthday,” Yuka mumbles into his chest, half asleep. ʸ

“You already said.” ᴹ

“I'm saying it again. In case you forget.” ʸ

He puts his hand in her hair.

“I won't,” he says. ᴹ

He's fairly sure he will. He's fairly sure she'll remind him. He's fairly sure that's the point.
