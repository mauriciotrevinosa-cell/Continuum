*When: B+365. Sunset. The house has been dark for exactly as long as it took Beru to walk Mau back from the orchard very, very slowly.*

He opens the door of the common room and it's dark.

Not just dim. *Dark*. Every lamp out. The curtains drawn. He stands in the doorway with Beru behind him and the last of the sunset at his back, and his eyes, without being asked, open a little and start to read the room.

There are a lot of heartbeats in it.

All of them. Every one. Packed into the common room in the dark, holding their breath, perfectly still. Thirty, forty, more. He can feel them the way he can feel his shadows. Ori's, quick and small, very close to the door. Milim's, much too fast. Gojo's, slow and amused. Shoko's. Rimuru's. The Sungs, all of them, at the back.

And one more. Near the middle. Racing.

Hers.

He stands there in the doorway with the eyes open and every heartbeat in the house in front of him, and has absolutely no idea why.

* * *

“*NOW*,” Ori screams. ᴼ

The lamps come on. All at once. Somebody, Rimuru probably, does something that lights every one of them in the same instant, and the room is suddenly full of light and people and noise, so much noise, everyone shouting the same two words at once at the top of their lungs:

“*HAPPY BIRTHDAY!*”

He stands there.

* * *

There's a banner.

It's across the far wall, from the window to the fireplace, a long strip of cloth with letters painted on it. Big, uneven, enthusiastic letters, some of them upside down and corrected, one of them painted twice because the first one was wrong. HAPPY BIRTHDAY MAU. And underneath, smaller, in a different hand: *(AGAIN)*.

There's paint on Ori's face. There's paint on Chloe's hands. There's paint on the Pillow, who is lying in front of the fireplace with a long smear across his shaggy flank and an expression of profound dignity.

There's a cake. On the table. Under the cloth that was in the kitchen this morning. The shy bread. It isn't bread. It's enormous and it's tiered and Shuna is standing beside it with her hands clasped and her eyes very bright.

And there's Yuka. In the middle of the room. In the blue dress. Looking at him.

* * *

“It's your birthday,” she says. ʸ

He stares at her.

“It's—” ᴹ

“The day we picked,” says Rimuru, from somewhere to his left. “You didn't have one, so the house picked one for you. And you forgot it. Again.” ᴿ

“You forgot,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I forgot,” he admits. ᴹ

“I *knew* you'd forget.” ʸ

“How did you know?” ᴹ

“It's in here,” she says. “*Things he forgets.* Page one. Line one. *His birthday*.” ʸ

* * *

He doesn't remember most of the next hour.

Not because anything goes wrong. Because there's too much of it. People come at him from every side: hugging him, shaking his good hand and then his new one very carefully, shouting things over each other. Milim jumps on his back from a chair and has to be removed by Shoko. Gojo stands on the table and begins a speech that starts with *on the day this idiot walked into our lives* and gets about three sentences in before Shoko reaches up and pulls him off it by the back of his shirt.

The Sungs are there. All of them. Jin-Woo at the back, smiling the small real smile Mau has only seen on him a few times. Makima is at the edge of the room, by the window. Alone. She doesn't come close. But when he looks at her, she inclines her head, and holds up a cup, just slightly, the way you'd toast someone across a room you weren't sure you were allowed to be in.

He lifts his own back.

* * *

The bird comes during the cake.

“It's *that* bird,” Yuka says. ʸ

“It's that bird,” he agrees. ᴹ

“*Dear Mau. I'm told it's your birthday. Your wife told me. She tells me a great many things. Happy birthday. I have a gift for you. It's waiting on the island. You'll have it when you come. “She knows about the floor,” Mau says. ᴹ

“*Everyone* knows about the floor,” says Yuka. ʸ

* * *

Frieren gives him something at the end.

She waits until most people have moved away from the table, back toward the fire and the cake and Milim, who is trying to convince the Pillow to wear a paper hat. Then she sits down across from him with something wrapped in an old cloth, and puts it on the table between them, and doesn't let go of it.

He knows what it is before she unwraps it. He can tell by the way she's holding it.

A book. Old. The cover worn soft at the corners, the spine cracked and mended and cracked again. A grimoire. One of hers.

She unwraps it the rest of the way. She pushes it across the table toward him, an inch. Then she stops. Her hand is still on it.

“For you,” she says. ᶠ

Her hand doesn't move.

* * *

Everyone sees it.

That's the thing. Everyone at that end of the room. Fern, at the next table, puts her cup down very slowly. Stark, beside her, stops chewing. Even Gojo, who was halfway into a story about a curse that turned out to be a very large goat, goes quiet and turns his blindfolded face toward the table.

Because Frieren's face is doing something none of them have ever seen it do.

She's trying to look calm. She's trying very hard. She's pushing a grimoire across a table to someone she loves on his birthday, the way you're supposed to, and her face is perfectly composed, and her hand on the cover is *shaking*. Just slightly. Her fingers flat on the old leather, not quite able to lift. Her ears have gone down. Her eyes keep going to the book and away and back to the book, the way a child's eyes go to a toy she's been told to share.

She has been collecting grimoires for a thousand years. She has crossed continents for spells that do nothing but polish bronze. Mau has watched her trade a week of work for a single page about making grapes sour.

And she is giving one away. To him. And it is *killing* her.

Fern has put her hand over her mouth.

* * *

He looks at the book. He looks at her hand on it. He looks at her face.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

“I'll take it,” he says. ᴹ

“But,” he says. ᴹ

“I don't have anywhere to keep it,” he says. “Not properly. The inventory isn't the same. Things go in there and I forget them. I just found out I forgot a whole castle.” ᴹ

He looks at her.

“Would you keep it for me?” he asks. “In your room. With the others. Where it's safe. And when I want to read it, I'll come and read it there. With you.” ᴹ

Frieren's ears come up.

He watches it happen. Slowly, and then all at once: the long ears lifting from where they'd drooped, rising and rising until they're higher than he's seen them in months. Her hand on the book goes still. Her face does something complicated, and then something very simple.

“Yes,” she says. ᶠ

“Yes. I can do that. I'll keep it. You can come and read it. Whenever you want. I'll make room on the shelf. By the window. Where the light is good.” ᶠ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“It's *yours*.” ᶠ

“I know.” ᴹ

They go upstairs late.

The house is still loud below them; Milim has found the music and Bocchi has been persuaded to play, and someone is dancing badly. But they're done. He's tired in the good way, the way the gate left him, the itch gone and the new arm aching pleasantly and his side pulling and his stomach full of cake.

Downstairs somebody cheers. They both pause, listening for a name. Then a door shuts, and Beru's voice follows, stern enough to send them into another brief fit of laughter.

“Your birthday party is going to be here until breakfast,” Yuka whispers. ʸ

“Probably.”

“Do you want to go back down?”

“No.” ᴹ

She studies him.

“Good. I don't either.” ʸ

She lets the smile fade, but doesn't move away.

“I wanted this part too,” she says. “The party was for everyone. This is just for us.” ʸ

He takes a moment to answer. There has been a great deal of noise today, much of it made on his behalf. Now that she says it, he realises he's been waiting to be alone with her as well, and he hadn't thought to tell her.

“I want that,” he says. ᴹ

She runs her thumb across his knuckles.

“Your side?”

“Pulls if I twist too far. Not if I stay like this.”

“Your arm?”

“Still weaker than I want, but it doesn't hurt when you hold it.” ᴹ

“Then tell me if either of those changes.”

“I will.”

She brings her forehead against his. The dress rustles as she shifts. He has been watching that dress since he first saw her in it, and now he's near enough to feel the soft fold at her shoulder move beneath his hand.

She lifts her head and looks at him for a long moment. Then she picks up the enormous hat from the edge of the coverlet and puts it on his head, where it comes down over his eyes, and kisses him under the brim.

They take their time settling farther back on the bed. Mau turns on his right side, testing the scar before he lets his weight rest against the pillow. Yuka slides the blanket clear of his elbow and draws the extra cushion closer in case he needs it. He catches her hand before she can start rearranging everything else.

“I'm comfortable,” he says. ᴹ

“Really?”

“Really. Come here.” ᴹ

She does. She lies down against him with her head on his shoulder and her hand on the line on his arm, and the hat slides off his head somewhere and neither of them goes after it. The faint sound of the party remains below them, but it is no longer something they need to listen to.

He kisses her hair. For the first time all day there is no one waiting to hand him something, ask for an answer or tell him what he's supposed to do next. He doesn't have to be useful. He only has to be here with her, and he wants to be.

The rest of the night is theirs, the way it is.

* * *

Much later, the hat is on the floor.

It's lying upside down by the bed where it fell, enormous and lopsided and absurd, the brim crushed a little on one side. Neither of them has picked it up.

Yuka is asleep on his chest. Her hand is on his arm, on the line, where she fell asleep tracing it.

Downstairs the music has stopped. The house has gone quiet, the way it does at the very end of a long day: a door, a laugh, the third stair creaking once and then nothing.

On the bedside table there's Ori's *welcome home* note and the chocolate wrapper from the red gate. The grimoire is downstairs, on a shelf by Frieren's window, where the light is good. He'll go and read it tomorrow. With her.

He thinks about the day the house picked a date and said *this one, this is yours*.

He didn't have one. Now he has one. He forgets it, and they remember.

“Happy birthday,” Yuka mumbles into his chest, half asleep. ʸ

“You already said.” ᴹ

“I'm saying it again. In case you forget.” ʸ

He puts his hand in her hair.

“I won't,” he says. ᴹ

He's fairly sure he will. He's fairly sure she'll remind him. He's fairly sure that's the point.
