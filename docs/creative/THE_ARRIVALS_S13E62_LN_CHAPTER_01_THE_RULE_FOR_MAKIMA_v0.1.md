# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 11 — The Rule for Makima

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E62
**Continuity state:** B+367. Expanding Yuka's contract. What already exists: Yuka can use any of Mau's techniques on his reserves with the weight falling on her; the second Limitless he built inside himself for her; and in an emergency he can trigger a technique in her. New today: the Exchange, which Yuka can now start herself. She tests it at once, pulls him across the yard to her, and Mau's face when he understands what he's agreed to makes the whole porch laugh. And a link in her head like his with Rimuru, so Ciel, Noise, DIO and Makima can reach her. Before anything, Makima asks what the rule is. Yuka: if it's important, talk to me; if not, I'll come to you; you can ask if you can speak, and I'll decide. A playful day; Ciel closes the door at night. The contract-night romance and the mutual link-privacy consent have been written in full, without a sex scene.

*When: B+367. Two days after the birthday. Yuka has written CONTRACT at the top of a new page and underlined it twice.*

“What I have now,” Yuka says, reading from the notebook. “So we don't forget.” ʸ

They're on the porch. She's in his lap, facing out, with the notebook on her knees. Noise and Ciel are listening; he can feel them leaning in. Half the house is listening too, from the yard, from the window, pretending not to.

“One,” Yuka reads. “I can use any of your techniques. Your reserves. The weight's on me.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Two. The second Limitless. The one you built inside you so I'd have my own.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Three. If something's about to kill me and I can't ask, you can turn one of your techniques on in me.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She taps the page with her pencil.

“And today,” she says, “two more.” ʸ

* * *

The Exchange was Noise's idea, and Ciel's arithmetic.

Mau's shadows are doors; he steps through them. Yuka has one of his soldiers in hers. The only thing that's ever been missing is a handle on her side. Today Noise builds it: a small, plain thing in the shape of her contract, so that the door can be opened from where she stands as well as from where he does.

“So I can call you,” she says. “Not just tell you. *Call* you. Pull you to me.” ʸ

“If you need to,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Or pull myself to you.” ʸ

“If you need to.” ᴹ

“Nine breaths between?” ʸ

“Nine breaths between,” Ciel confirms, through him. ᶜ

Yuka nods. She writes it down.

Then she stands up out of his lap, walks down the porch steps, across the yard, all the way to the training posts at the far end. Turns round. Looks at him.

“Yuka,” he says. ᴹ

She closes her eyes.

* * *

He's on the porch.

Then he isn't.

Then he's at the far end of the yard beside the training posts, standing exactly where her shadow was, with his chair still on the porch behind him and his tea still in his hand, which he did not intend to bring, and which has slopped down his shirt.

Yuka opens her eyes. She looks at him. At the tea.

She starts to laugh.

And he understands, standing there wet with tea at the far end of the yard, what he has done. He has given his wife, who once told him he was badly arranged and made him turn a couch round with Authority, who keeps a notebook titled *Things He Forgets*, who once made him buy her a sweet from every page of the Shop as a fine, the ability to *summon him*. Whenever she likes. From anywhere. Every nine breaths.

His face does something.

The porch, which has been watching, sees it do it.

Gojo falls off the railing.

* * *

“That's not what it's *for*,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know what it's for,” says Yuka, wiping her eyes. ʸ

“It's for *emergencies*.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“Like if you're in a gate and something's—” ᴹ

“I *know*, Mau.” ʸ

She's still laughing. She comes over and takes the cup out of his hand and drinks what's left.

“I just wanted to see your face,” she says. ʸ

“I'm going to regret this.” ᴹ

“You're going to *love* this,” she says, and kisses his cheek, and walks back to the porch. ʸ

He stands at the training posts, wet, and watches her go.

Nine breaths later, he's on the porch again.

* * *

The link takes longer.

It's the same kind of thing as the line between him and Rimuru: not a voice in the ear, not words exactly, a channel that runs through Ciel. Until now it's only ever run between the two of them. Today Ciel opens a second one. To Yuka. And through it, if Yuka allows, the others who live in him.

Noise. Ciel herself. DIO, who lives in him the way Ciel does and comes out in her own body whenever she likes, who has a guest room in the mini Inn with a small plant by the window that she looks after. When she's out, everyone can see her and hear her. When she's in, until today, only Mau could. She has been bouncing about this since breakfast.

And Makima.

“Before anything,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

She's at the bottom of the porch steps, where she always is. She came when Ciel started building. She hasn't come up.

“Before it's open,” she says. “What's the rule?” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her.

“For me,” Makima says. “With this. In your head. I'd like to know the rule before I'm in a position to break it.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka doesn't answer right away.

He feels her think about it, sitting in his lap with the notebook on her knees. He doesn't say anything. This is hers.

“If it's important,” Yuka says finally, “you talk to me. Don't wait. Don't ask. If something's wrong with him, or with the house, or you can feel something coming, you tell me.” ʸ

Makima nods.

“If it isn't important,” Yuka says, “I'll come to you. If I want to.” ʸ

Makima nods again.

“And if you want to talk to me,” Yuka says, “about something that isn't important, you can ask. Just ask. *Can I speak?* And I'll say yes or no. And if I say no, you don't ask why.” ʸ

“Yes,” says Makima. ᴹᵏ

“That's the rule.” ʸ

“That's the rule,” Makima agrees. ᴹᵏ

She inclines her head. Then, after a moment, very quietly:

“Thank you for making one,” she says. “Instead of just leaving me out.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her for a long moment.

“You live in my husband,” she says. “Leaving you out wasn't really an option.” ʸ

Something happens at the corner of Makima's mouth.

* * *

Ciel opens it at noon.

Yuka sits very still in his lap with her eyes closed. He feels the channel go in: a thin bright line from him, through Ciel, to her. And then, one by one, the others finding it.

Noise first, careful and enormous. *Hello*, it says, very formally, in a way that makes Yuka jump. ᴺ

Ciel, crisp. *Testing. Can you hear me?* ᶜ

And DIO, who comes out for her first word.

She steps out of him onto the porch in her own body, the way she does, and sits on the railing swinging her feet, and leans down to Yuka's ear.

“Hello,” she says, out loud. ᴰ

Then she goes back in. Just like that, between one breath and the next. And from inside him, down the new line, for the first time in Yuka's head instead of only in his:

*Hello*, she says. *Which one's better?* ᴰ

Yuka laughs out loud with her eyes still closed.

“Both,” she says. ʸ

Mau hears both, too. He hears all of it: every word that goes down the line to Yuka and every word she sends back. That was the point. It isn't a second room he's shut out of. It's the same room, with one more person in it.

And then, very faintly, a question.

*Can I speak?* ᴹᵏ

Yuka opens her eyes. She looks at Mau. He doesn't say anything.

“Yes,” she says, out loud. ʸ

A pause.

*Happy belated birthday to your husband*, Makima says. *I didn't say it at the party. I didn't know if I was allowed.* ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at the bottom of the porch steps, where Makima is standing with her hands folded, looking at the yard.

“You're allowed,” Yuka says. ʸ

* * *

The rest of the day is ridiculous.

Yuka tests everything. She asks Noise the time, every hour, until Noise starts answering before she asks. She asks Ciel to tell her what Mau is thinking, and Ciel says *no*, very primly, and Yuka says *please*, and Ciel says *absolutely not*, and Yuka laughs so hard she has to sit down. DIO sits beside her on the steps half the afternoon, out in her own body, and describes the island from very high up, the way she saw it once from the air: the bay, the roofs, the long curve of the beach where they walked. Yuka goes quiet for a long time. Mau, in the yard, hears every word of it and pretends not to.

She summons him four more times. Once from the kitchen, because she wants him to taste something. Once from the workshop, because Mai wants to see. Once from the bath, which he does not discuss with anyone. Once from absolutely nowhere, in the middle of the orchard, for no reason at all, and when he lands beside her she just puts her arms round him and says *I wanted to see if it worked from here*.

It worked from there.

* * *

That night, he's sitting on the edge of the bed.

She's lying behind him, the way she does, drawing shapes on his back with one finger. She's quiet. She's been quiet since the orchard.

“You're thinking,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm thinking,” she agrees. ʸ

“About what?” ᴹ

Her finger stops.

“That I can find you now,” she says. “Wherever you are. If you're in a gate, or a room with a bed, or somewhere like the hills. I don't have to wait for you to tell me. I can *find* you.” ʸ

He doesn't say anything.

“And pull you back,” she says. “Or go to you. If you get stuck. If the door closes. I said I'd come with you so you wouldn't be alone. Now I can come even if you went without me.” ʸ

He turns round.

She's lying on her side looking up at him, with her hand flat on the bed where his back was. Her face is very soft.

“I'm glad,” she says. ʸ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

He feels Ciel close the door. The small, polite *click*. And somewhere at the far edge of him, Noise, very carefully looking elsewhere.

She reaches up and pulls him down by the front of his shirt.

He leans toward her. She catches the front of his shirt in her fist and pulls him the last few inches.

The first kiss makes him laugh, not because it's funny but because he's spent the entire day appearing wherever she asks him to, and apparently that isn't quite close enough.

Yuka draws back until she can see his face.

“That's five,” she says. ʸ

“Five what?” ᴹ

“Times I brought you to me today.”

“This one doesn't count.”

“Why not?”

“You didn't use Exchange.”

“I used this.” ʸ

She gives his shirt another small tug. The cotton bunches inside her fist, pulling gently across his shoulder. He lets her bring him down until his forehead touches hers. She can feel his breath against the bridge of her nose, warm and slightly uneven from laughing. His hand stays open on the mattress beside her, not gripping or preparing for anything.

“That's a different technique,” he says. ᴹ

“It's an excellent one.”

“It's been in use for a long time.”

“And it still works.” ʸ

She looks pleased enough to try it again. He catches himself with one hand on the mattress beside her shoulder and the other against the headboard. The edge of the bed gives under his weight. Yuka shifts toward its middle, keeping a grip on his shirt so he can follow without having to reach over her.

“Wait,” he says. ᴹ

She stops immediately.

“Your foot's caught in the blanket.”

She looks down. One bare foot is tangled in the edge of the coverlet.

“That wasn't part of the plan,” she admits. ʸ

“You had a plan?”

“I had five successful tests.”

He lifts the corner of the blanket. She frees her foot and pulls the covers across her knees. When she looks back at him she's smiling again.

“You can come here now,” she says. ʸ

“Thank you.”

He moves toward the pillows, puts his right knee on the mattress and sits beside her. She turns to face him, one leg folded beneath her, her hand now resting lightly on his sleeve. There is no pull on his side when he settles, which he counts as progress. Yuka seems to be counting something else.

“Are you thinking about how it works?” he asks. ᴹ

“A little.”

“Noise has a very long explanation if you want it.”

“I heard enough explanations today.” ʸ

“Then what?”

She is quiet for a moment, looking at his hand on the blanket.

“If you're somewhere bad,” she says, “I can go to you now. Not wait for you to send something out. Not ask Rimuru to find a way in. I can go myself.” ʸ

He remembers her outside the gates before she could come with him. He remembers how often he treated that distance as something he was making safe for her, instead of something he was asking her to endure.

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“And I can call you to me.”

“Within the rules.”

“I know about the nine breaths. Noise explained it three times.” ʸ

“He does that.”

“I'm going to ask him a fourth time tomorrow.”

Mau smiles. She reaches out and turns his left hand palm up, tracing a short line across it with her thumb.

“I'm glad,” she says. “Not because I want to summon you from every room in the house. I mean, I do. But that's not why.” ʸ

“You're going to do it anyway.”

“Obviously.”

He rests his other hand over hers.

“Yuka, I'm glad too.”

“Even though you spent the whole afternoon looking surprised?”

“Especially because of that.” ᴹ

She moves closer. He kisses her, and this time he doesn't have to catch his weight. He's sitting with his back against the pillows, and she has one hand against his shoulder, the other around his left hand. The new scar is smooth where her thumb rests, but the skin beside it is warmer and less even. She notices how his fingers loosen when he realises she isn't going to let go.

The pillows make a soft sound against the headboard when he settles back. Yuka follows the movement with her own shoulder, close enough that she feels him release a breath he has been holding through the day. She stays for another moment, feeling the steady warmth of his hand between hers.

When she draws back, she remains close, studying his face.

“I don't want to use it just because I'm scared,” she says. ʸ

“Then don't.”

“I want to use it because I choose to be there. The way you choose to be with me.” ʸ

He thinks of the orchard. The fifth test was the one where she called him from nowhere and wrapped both arms around him for no reason except to see him arrive. He'd landed in the shadow of the apple tree and found her laughing at his expression.

“That's the one I liked best,” he says. ᴹ

“Which one?”

“The orchard.”

Her smile is immediate.

“I didn't need anything,” she says. ʸ

“I know.”

“I just wanted you there.”

“I know that too.” ᴹ

She leans against him. He puts his right arm around her shoulders and lets her settle with her cheek against his chest. He can feel her breathing slow. After the long day of testing the new contract she seems almost sleepy, until she lifts her face and asks another question.

“Can Ciel still hear me?” she says. ʸ

He checks the quiet behind his own thoughts. The small door has closed on his side. He has felt it close before, every time Ciel decides that the two of them deserve to be left alone. But Yuka has her own link now, and he doesn't know whether privacy on one side means privacy on both.

“I don't know,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka sits up.

“Ciel?”

The answer comes through her link, and Mau hears it too.

*Yes?* ᶜ

“Did you close the door on my side?” ʸ

*Not yet.*

Yuka makes a face.

“That's embarrassing.”

*I have not been watching.*

“That's not the same as a door.” ʸ

Mau bites back a laugh. Yuka catches him at it and points a finger at his chest.

“This is your fault.”

“It's your contract.”

“You built it.”

“Together.” ᴹ

She lowers her finger.

“Together,” she agrees. ʸ

Then she looks toward the place where she feels the link.

“Could you close my side too, when we ask? Not just his?” ʸ

*With your permission, yes.* ᶜ

“Then please. And if I call you, I need you to answer.”

*Of course.*

“And if something really matters, you still tell us.”

*We have already agreed on that rule.* ᶜ

“I'm repeating it.”

*I've noticed.* ᶜ

The small sensation of the link closing is different from a voice fading away. Yuka had expected silence, but what she notices first is the sudden absence of expectation. All afternoon a part of her attention has been angled toward the other voices, ready for a question, an answer, a joke from DIO. Now that part of her can rest. The room's ordinary sounds come forward: the soft drag of Mau's sleeve against the blanket, the faint creak of the bed, a distant knock from the kitchen pipes.

For a second the quiet is almost too complete. She looks at Mau, uncertain, and he takes her hand. His palm is warm. His thumb moves once across her knuckles, and the familiar pressure gives her something simple to attend to that belongs only to them.

“Is it all right?” he asks. ᴹ

She nods slowly.

“It feels like closing a door in a house that still has everybody in it.”

“That sounds about right.”

“Except one of the people in the house is Noise.”

“Noise is probably listening to Ciel explain how to check the walls again.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs. The uncertainty disappears.

“I like it,” she says. “I like choosing when the door is open.” ʸ

“Me too.”

She sits beside him again, close enough that her knee touches his. He slides his arm around her, and she tucks her shoulder under it.

For a while neither of them speaks. The little noises of the house are still there. Someone goes past their door and says good night to Beru. A pipe ticks as the kitchen water cools. Nothing in those sounds demands an answer.

Yuka turns her head toward him.

“Can I tell you something without putting it in the notebook?” she asks. ʸ

“You can tell me anything.”

“I was scared today.” ʸ

He waits.

“Not of the contract. Of how happy it made me. It felt like if I laughed too much, you'd think I didn't understand what it was for.”

He looks at her. He'd been watching her appear in doorways and gardens and laugh when he stepped out of her shadow. He hadn't realised there was another feeling underneath it.

“I knew you understood,” he says. ᴹ

“How?”

“You asked what would happen if you were hurt. And if I was. And if we were on opposite sides of a gate. You didn't stop asking until we knew what we could say for sure.”

She looks pleased, but not quite satisfied.

“And?”

“And you asked me to come to the orchard because you wanted a hug. That's important too.” ᴹ

She brings both arms around him.

“Like this?”

“Exactly like this.”

She squeezes. He holds her, the familiar weight of her against his right side, and lets the silence stay quiet.

“I'm still afraid sometimes,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.”

“That you'll get hurt because I made something wrong.”

“Then tell me if you find something wrong.”

“I will.”

“And I'll tell you if it hurts.” ʸ

He looks down at her.

“Even if you think I'll blame myself?”

“Especially then.” ʸ

It's his own answer, returned to him. He laughs softly, and she looks delighted that he noticed.

She draws back to touch his face with both hands. His hair has fallen over one eye; she brushes it away with her thumb, feeling the fine strands slip between her fingers. Then she kisses him. This time she doesn't pull his shirt or his wrist. He comes toward her on his own, shifting close enough that her arms fold naturally around his shoulders. She feels the change before she can think of a joke: the way his weight settles beside hers without any need for her to draw him nearer.

When they part, the place her hands have been holding his face is warm. She keeps one thumb there for a moment longer, watching him smile.

When they part, she reaches across to the bedside table for the notebook. He watches her take it, expects another line of rules or dates, and is surprised when she closes it without writing anything.

“No notes?” he asks. ᴹ

“Not for this.”

“That's unlike you.”

“I heard what you said. I'll remember.” ʸ

She puts the book back, turns toward him again and takes his hand beneath the blanket. The sheets are cool where they haven't been lying. He moves down from the pillows carefully; she follows, gathering the edge of the coverlet so it doesn't twist around his ankle. When she settles, the mattress gives slightly beneath both of them, drawing her knee against his. She shifts the pillow under his left elbow until the arm is supported and neither of them has to hold it up.

Her cheek finds his shoulder. Through the thin fabric of his shirt she feels the slow expansion of his ribs with each breath, and her own breathing falls into an easier rhythm beside it.

“Found you,” she whispers. ʸ

“You didn't lose me.”

“I know. I like finding you.” ʸ

He kisses her forehead. She rests there, then lifts her face for another kiss, then settles down again without having to ask him to move. The door inside them stays closed. The house stays a house. He feels her fingers curl around his and doesn't reach for anything else.

When she falls asleep, her hand is still in his.
