# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 12 — The Ring

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S13E62
**Continuity state:** B+369, Mai's workshop, the "next week" Mai set. Noise builds the ring from the pattern with Mai's correction, using Mau's version of Construction, with Mai's permission and Mai watching every curve. Mau implants it in the katana. He feeds it cursed energy and, for the first time, mana as well, so it holds both. The upgrades from the inventory go in with Mai and Senku: the long dark scale from Kaisel's mountain kill along the spine of the blade, the thin chitin in the guard, Kamish's tooth ground down into the edge. Rika's forms, finished at last, settle inside. Yuta is there when she comes out whole for the first time; he doesn't speak for a long time. Yuka beside Mau the whole build, feet in his lap. Evening: the points (300 old levels' worth, everything from level 2 to 5) with Yuka, Umaru and Jin-Woo. Umaru wants to spend; Jin-Woo says keep some back until he knows what he's facing. Mau doesn't spend any yet.

*When: B+369. A week after the chalk dust. Mai takes the box down from the top shelf at sunrise and won't let anyone talk until she's opened it.*

Mai opens the box.

The paper is exactly as she left it: the pale drift of dust, the three rings, the web between them, the one curve near the base of the third ring that bends further than it did before she touched it.

She lays it flat on the bench. She weighs the corners with four small tools. She looks at Mau.

“Ask,” she says. ᴹᵃ

He blinks.

“You asked last week,” Mai says. “You asked to show me the pattern. Today you're going to use my technique to build it. Ask.” ᴹᵃ

The workshop is full. Senku at the second bench with his sleeves rolled up and the scales laid out in a row. Yuta in the doorway, not quite in, the way he's stood in every doorway since he saw the shape of his own ring in the dust. Ori on a stool in the corner, because she was promised. Yuka on the bench beside Mau, where she was last week, with her feet in his lap.

Mau looks at Mai.

“Mai,” he says. “Can I use your technique to build the ring?” ᴹ

“Yes,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

She sits down across the bench from him and folds her arms.

“And if you get one curve wrong,” she says, “I'll know.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

He doesn't build it. Noise does.

That's the thing he has to keep remembering, sitting with his hands open over the bench and the pattern under them. His hands, his reserves, his version of her Construction. But the shape is Noise's. Every line of it. Noise holds the pattern the way you'd hold a breath, and tells his cursed energy where to go, and the cursed energy goes.

It starts as nothing. A faint pressure in the air above the paper. Then a thread. Then three threads, bending toward each other, the way the dust bent. He feels Construction take: the strange cold pull of making something that wasn't there, out of what's in him.

The first ring closes. A thin band, dull grey, no wider than a finger.

“Slower,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“I'm not doing anything,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Then tell *it* slower.” ᴹᵃ

“She's right,” Noise mutters, inside. “I'm rushing the second one.” ᴺ

It slows.

* * *

The second ring takes an hour.

Nobody talks. Senku stops sorting. Ori, in the corner, holds her breath for so long that Yuka has to reach out with her foot and nudge her. Yuta has come one step into the room.

The second ring closes inside the first. Not touching. Turning very slightly, the way a coin turns when you spin it on a table and it's nearly stopped.

The third ring is the one with Mai's curve.

He feels Noise reach it. Hesitate. The old line, the one Noise drew first, is still there somewhere in Noise's memory, and for a moment the cursed energy starts to follow it—

Mai's hand comes down flat on the bench.

“*No*,” she says. ᴹᵃ

The energy stops. Noise stops.

Then, very carefully, it bends. Further. The way Mai said. The way the dust says, pressed into the paper. The curve near the base of the third ring goes round and round and closes.

Three rings. One inside the next. And the web between them, catching the lamplight, very fine, like frost on a window.

Mai lets out a breath.

“Good,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“Thank you,” says Noise, through Mau, stiffly. ᴺ

Mai almost smiles.

* * *

Then the katana.

He takes it out of the shadow and lays it on the bench, and the room changes, the way rooms change when the katana comes out. He doesn't know what it is about it. It's only a sword. But Yuta, in the doorway, goes very still.

Senku comes over with the pieces.

“Scale,” he says, laying it down. ˢᵉ

The long dark one from Kaisel's mountain. Thin as paper at the edges, heavy as stone in the middle, and when Mau puts his hand on it, it hums.

“Chitin.” ˢᵉ

The curved piece from Beru's tunnels. It shifts when you tilt it, and the eyes tell him what the shifting is: it moves things. Slides them off. Whatever hits it doesn't stay where it hit.

“And the tooth.” ˢᵉ

Senku has to use both hands. It's as long as Mau's arm, from something Kamish killed before Mau knew him, and it's been lying in the dark for longer than the house has had a name.

Senku looks at it. Then at Mai.

“I've done the arithmetic,” he says. “Three times. It's ten billion per cent better than anything in that sword now. But you're going to have to grind it.” ˢᵉ

“I know,” says Mai. ᴹᵃ

She rolls up her sleeves.

* * *

It takes the rest of the morning.

Mai grinds the tooth. Senku tells her the angle. Mau holds the sword still with Authority, a light grip all along the blade, and feeds it what Mai asks for when she asks for it. The scale goes into the spine. He feels it go in: not pressed on, *in*, Construction making room for it inside the steel and closing round it. The blade gets heavier and then, strangely, lighter, as if it's remembered how to fly.

The chitin goes into the guard. When it's in, Mai taps the guard with a little hammer and the hammer slides off sideways and hits the bench.

Ori laughs out loud.

The tooth goes last. Ground down to a powder finer than the chalk dust, and then Mai lays it along the edge with a brush, one stroke, the whole length, and Mau puts his hand flat on the blade and Construction drinks it.

The edge doesn't look any different.

Mai picks up a piece of scrap steel as thick as her wrist. She lays it on the bench. She lays the katana on top of it, flat, just its own weight, no hand on it at all.

It goes through.

The scrap falls in two halves on either side, and the katana settles on the bench with a small *click*, and nobody in the workshop says anything for a very long time.

“Okay,” says Senku eventually. ˢᵉ

* * *

Then the ring.

He picks it up off the paper. It's lighter than it looks. Three rings and the web between them, cold in his palm, turning very slightly on itself.

“Where?” he asks. ᴹ

“The base of the blade,” says Noise. “Under the guard. Where the tang goes into the handle. Where she lives.” ᴺ

He knows where she lives. He's felt her there since the day the katana first held her: part of her, the shapes he'd managed to copy, never all.

He sets the ring against the steel.

Construction opens the sword like a mouth, and the ring goes in, and the steel closes.

* * *

“Now,” says Noise. “Feed it.” ᴺ

He feeds it cursed energy. The ring takes it the way the old rings did, the way Yuta's does: a slow deep pull, steady as a tide.

“And the other,” says Noise. ᴺ

He hesitates.

He's never done this. Not in a weapon. Mana and cursed energy live in him side by side and don't touch; they come from different places and go to different places, and he has never, once, asked them to share.

Yuka's foot presses gently into his thigh.

He opens the mana.

It goes in beside the cursed energy. Not mixed: *beside*. Two currents in the same channel, the way two rivers run side by side for a mile after they meet, before they become one water. He feels the ring take them both. Hold them both. The web between the three rings lights up from inside, faintly, like breath on cold glass.

“Both,” he says. ᴹ

“Both,” says Noise, and for once sounds a little shaken. ᴺ

* * *

And then she comes out.

He doesn't call her. He doesn't have to. The katana is lying on the bench under his hand and the ring is full, and something in it that has been waiting a very long time to be whole finally *is*, and stands up.

She fills the workshop.

Not the part of her he had before. Not the half-formed shape at his shoulder that came when he called and went when he let go. All of her. The great pale body. The arms. The face, which is a terrible face and also, somehow, a gentle one. And behind her, in the light the ring gives off, the other shapes she can take: the ones Yuta's ring held and the katana couldn't, folded into her like pages in a book, every one of them there.

Ori has her hands over her mouth.

Mai has stood up without meaning to.

And in the doorway, Yuta has stopped breathing.

* * *

Rika turns round.

Slowly. The whole enormous shape of her turning in the narrow workshop, careful of the benches, careful of the lamp. She looks at Mau. She looks at the katana. She looks at Yuka, on the bench, and Yuka looks back up at her and doesn't flinch.

Then she looks at the doorway.

Yuta doesn't move.

He stands there with one hand on the frame. He's seen her before. Not her: the copy, the half, the thing at Mau's shoulder. He's always looked away from it. Mau has watched him do it a hundred times.

He doesn't look away now.

Rika leans down.

Not far. Just enough. The great pale face coming down level with his, the way you'd lean down to look at someone smaller than you who you've known a long time.

Yuta's hand tightens on the frame.

He doesn't say anything. For a long time, he doesn't say anything at all. And Mau, at the bench with his hand on the katana, doesn't say anything either, and neither does anyone else. It isn't theirs.

Finally Yuta lets go of the doorframe.

“Hi,” he says, very quietly. ʸᵗ

That's all.

Rika stays there, leaning down, for a long moment more. Then she straightens, and turns, and settles back toward the katana like smoke going home.

Yuta stands in the doorway with his hand at his side.

Then he turns and walks away down the corridor, not fast, and Mau hears the door to the yard open, and close.

* * *

Nobody follows him.

Mai sits back down. Senku puts the scrap steel away. Ori lowers her hands from her mouth and says, in a very small voice, “Was that okay?” ᴼ

“I don't know,” Mau says honestly. ᴹ

Yuka has her hand on his arm.

“It was okay,” she says. “He said hi. He's never said hi to her before.” ʸ

She leans her head on his shoulder.

“Give him the afternoon,” she says. “Then go and sit on the wall with him. Don't say anything. Just sit.” ʸ

He does.

* * *

That evening they look at the points.

Yuka in his lap, with the notebook. Umaru on the floor in front of them, cross-legged, so close to the system box she'd climb into it if she could. Jin-Woo on the arm of the couch, with his tea.

Mau opens the page.

> **[Level 5.]**
>
> **[Unallocated points: accumulated from Level 2 to Level 5.]**
>
> **[Equivalent on the former scale: 300 levels.]**

Umaru reads the stat sheet out loud, every line of it, twice. Then she starts talking very fast about curves and caps and breakpoints and where three hundred levels of points would put him if he put them all in one place, which she *very strongly* recommends against, and then in another place, which she very strongly recommends *for*, and then she stops and says, “No, wait,” and starts again. ᵁ

Yuka writes all of it down.

Jin-Woo doesn't say anything until Umaru runs out of breath.

“Keep some back,” he says. ᴶʷ

Umaru opens her mouth.

“Not all,” Jin-Woo says. “Some. You don't know what you're going to face next. I didn't, either, most of the time. The best thing I ever did was have points left the day I needed them and nothing to put them in before.” ᴶʷ

He drinks his tea.

“You'll know when,” he says. ᴶʷ

Mau looks at the box. *300 levels*. Then at Yuka, who has stopped writing and is looking up at him.

“Not yet,” he says. ᴹ

Umaru groans and lies down on the floor.

Yuka writes *NOT YET — J.W. SAYS YOU'LL KNOW WHEN* and underlines it twice, and then, underneath, in smaller letters, *(Umaru disagrees)*.

* * *

That night Yuta is sitting on the wall at the bottom of the yard.

Mau goes out and sits next to him. He doesn't say anything. Yuta doesn't say anything. The stars come out, one by one, over the hills.

After a long time, Yuta says, “She looked well.” ʸᵗ

“Yes,” says Mau. ᴹ

That's all.

When he goes back in, Yuka is awake, waiting for him with the lamp turned down low. She doesn't ask. She just holds the blanket open, and he gets in, and she puts her head on his chest and her hand on the line on his arm and goes to sleep.
