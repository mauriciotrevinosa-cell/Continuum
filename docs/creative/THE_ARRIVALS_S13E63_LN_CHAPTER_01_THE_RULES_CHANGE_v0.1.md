# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 13 — The Rules Change

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S13E63
**Continuity state:** B+370, morning. Yuka keeps the promise she made at the quarry ("From now on, I'm coming with you"): she asks to go into the household gate with Mau to try the new contract fighting as one. Mau sets it to a middle setting. Inside, on the third floor, the game changes its rules without breaking: administrator override, the Rulers' trial of the Stabilizer. Condition: protect the Contracted. Modifier: any direct hit on Yuka is lethal; Mau is not multiplied. The Shop is open. Exit only when the Warden dies. The entrance closes. Outside, the orchard sees the oval change. The Exchange still works between the two of them, but not out of the gate. Mau doesn't lie to her about what it means.

*When: B+370. Morning. Three days after the contract, one day after the ring. Outside, Shoko is doing the inventory of the clinic; inside the house, nobody is worried yet.*

“I'm coming,” Yuka says. ʸ

She's standing in the bedroom doorway with her boots already on.

He's sitting on the edge of the bed, lacing his. He looks up at her.

“It's the household gate,” he says. “It's Thursday. I go every Thursday.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“It's boring. Middle setting. You've seen it.” ᴹ

“I've seen you come out of it.” ʸ

She comes in. She sits on the bed beside him and starts lacing the other boot for him, because his left hand is still slower than his right, and she knows it, and she's stopped pretending she doesn't.

“At the quarry,” she says, not looking up from the laces, “I told you. From now on I'm coming with you.” ʸ

He remembers. Her standing in front of the gate. *Even if I do nothing.*

“And now I've got the contract,” she says. “I can use your things. You can use them in me. We're supposed to fight like one. I want to see if we do. Somewhere boring. Before it matters.” ʸ

She ties the knot. She pulls it tight. She looks at him.

“Middle setting,” she says. “Boring. Thursday.” ʸ

He looks at her for a long moment.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

The house sees them off like it's nothing.

Because it is nothing. It's the weekly gate. It's Thursday. Beru is in Mau's shadow, the way he always is; Kaisel and Kamish are somewhere over the hills; Shoko waves at them from the clinic window without looking up from her lists. Gojo, on the porch, says *have fun storming the castle* in a voice so bored that Ori, beside him, throws a pinecone at him.

Mai is in the workshop doorway. She looks at the katana in its shadow-sheath at Mau's hip, the first time it's left the house since the ring went in.

“Don't break it,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“It's a middle setting,” he says. ᴹ

“Don't break it.” ᴹᵃ

The orchard is quiet. The two old trees. The oval of light between them, humming, the shape Yuka gave it a long time ago. *Something warm in it, somewhere.*

He puts his hand on the edge. He feels the setting. He sets it to the middle: hard enough that it's a real fight, soft enough that it isn't one that matters. He feels it take.

Yuka takes his hand.

They go in.

* * *

The first floor is a forest.

Not a real one. The household gate never makes real things; it makes the *idea* of a place, and lets you fill in the rest. Trees like pillars. A floor of something soft. Light from nowhere, flat and even. And things in the trees, many-legged and patient, which the eyes read at once: middle. Fast, a little venomous, nothing that matters.

“Ready?” he asks. ᴹ

She's already drawn her shadow up around her feet. Her dog is there, the white one, low and quiet at her heel. He can't see that it's white; he knows it the way he knows most colours, because people have told him. He sees Yuka, and her colours, and around her the grey of everything else.

“Ready,” she says. ʸ

The first thing comes out of the trees at her.

And he doesn't move.

He has to stop himself. Every part of him wants to step in front. Instead he stands where he is and lets her take it, because she asked, because that's what today is for, and he watches her reach into him, through the contract, without even looking at him.

Blue.

His Blue. Small, tight, in her palm. She's never used it before; he can feel her fumbling with it, the weight of it landing on her and not on him. It goes out of her hand a little crooked. But it goes. The thing in the air folds in on itself and drops.

She looks at her hand.

She looks at him.

“*Oh*,” she says. ʸ

* * *

It's good.

That's the thing. The first two floors are good. Better than good. She uses his Blue and he uses her dogs, the white one and the black one, through the contract, the other way; they've never done that either, and the dogs don't seem to mind which of them is asking. When a whole tree of the patient things comes down at once, she says *Mau* and he puts Infinity over her without thinking and she laughs out loud inside it. When three of them come at him from behind, she says nothing, just sends the black dog, and it's there before he's turned.

They don't talk much. They don't need to.

On the second floor, a thing like a great grey spider drops on them from a ceiling of branches. He's still turning when he feels her reach for Red. His Red. In her. He feels her hesitate, because Red is harder, because she's never even tried—

He opens it in her. The emergency way, the third thing on her list. Just a nudge. *Here*.

She throws it.

The spider goes back up into the branches a great deal faster than it came down.

She stands there breathing hard with her hand still out.

“Did you do that?” she says. ʸ

“I helped.” ᴹ

“I *felt* you help.” ʸ

“Was it bad?” ᴹ

She turns round. Her face is flushed and fierce and alight.

“It was *amazing*,” she says. ʸ

* * *

The third floor is where it happens.

They come down the stair into a long stone hall, and the hall is wrong.

He knows it before he knows why. The eyes go out ahead of him the way they always do, reading, and what they read is *wrong*: not the household gate. Not its idea of a hall. Something underneath it, older and colder, like a hand inside a glove.

He stops on the bottom stair.

“Mau?” ʸ

The box opens before he can answer.

It isn't the usual box. He can't see the colour, he never can. But Ciel, inside, goes very still, and then says, quietly:

“It's red.” ᶜ

> **[ADMINISTRATOR OVERRIDE.]**
>
> **[The current instance has been reassigned.]**
>
> **[Trial: THE STABILIZER.]**

He reads it. His hand goes out without his deciding and finds her arm.

> **[Participants: the Operator. The Contracted.]**
>
> **[Condition: Protect the Contracted.]**

“Mau,” Yuka says again. Her voice has changed. ʸ

> **[Modifier: Any direct hit on the Contracted is lethal.]**
>
> **[The Operator is not modified.]**

He stops breathing.

> **[Shop: available.]**
>
> **[Exit: on the death of the Warden.]**

Behind them, at the top of the stair, there's a sound like a door being shut in a very large, very quiet house.

* * *

He's up the stair before he's thought, and he hasn't let go of her arm. She comes up with him, two steps at a time, her dogs scrambling after.

The stair goes up to the second floor. The second floor is there: the forest, the branches, the spider lying where Red put it. But the way out, the long climb back to the first floor and the forest and the oval of light and the orchard: it isn't there anymore. Where the stair to the first floor was, there's stone. Flat. Seamless. The eyes go into it and find nothing. Not a wall. Not a seal. Just *the end of the gate*, as if there had never been anything above.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm looking.” ᴺ

“*Noise*.” ᴹ

“It's the Rulers,” Noise says. “Not the system: them. The same hand as the quarry. They've taken the household gate and put something else inside it. It's not broken. It's not a fault. It's working exactly the way they mean it to.” ᴺ

“Can I break it?” ᴹ

A pause.

“Not from here,” Noise says. “Not without the Warden. The exit's tied to it. I can feel the knot. If I cut it, the gate doesn't open. It just stops. With you inside.” ᴺ

* * *

They go back down together.

At the bottom she stands with her dogs pressed against her legs. She's very pale. It's the one face in the world he sees in colour, and all of it has drained out. She's reading the box again; he can see her lips moving.

*Any direct hit on the Contracted is lethal.*

“Is it true?” she says. ʸ

He could lie.

A year ago he would have. Six months ago. He would have said *it's probably exaggerating* or *the system does that* or *I'll handle it*, and smiled, and kept the rest of it behind his teeth where she couldn't see. He's very good at that. He's had a lot of practice.

He doesn't.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

She nods slowly.

“Ciel?” he says. ᴹ

“It's true,” Ciel says, through the link, so Yuka can hear her too. “Anything that hits you directly, Yuka. Anything. A claw. A stone. A fall. It doesn't matter how small. The gate will make it enough.” ᶜ

“And you?” Yuka says, to him. ʸ

“I'm not modified,” Mau says. “Things hit me as hard as they hit me.” ᴹ

“So it's the same as always for you.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And for me, one thing gets through, and I'm dead.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She stands very still.

DIO comes out.

She steps out of him onto the stone beside them in her own body, the way she does at home on the porch, and looks up the stair at the seamless place where the way out used to be, and then at Yuka.

“I'm here too,” she says. “For what it's worth. I came in with him. I'm staying in with him.” ᴰ

Yuka looks at her. Something in her shoulders comes down half an inch.

“It's worth something,” she says. ʸ

* * *

Then she does the thing he'll remember.

She doesn't cry. She doesn't shake. She looks at the box one more time. Then she closes it. She reaches out and takes his hand, the left one, the slower one, and holds it hard.

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

“Yuka—” ᴹ

“Okay,” she says. “So we kill the Warden. And nothing touches me. And you don't do anything stupid.” ʸ

She looks up at him.

“And you *tell* me,” she says. “Everything. When you're hurt. When it's bad. You don't say *it's fine*. You say what it is.” ʸ

“I will.” ᴹ

“Promise.” ʸ

“I promise.” ᴹ

“And the Exchange?” she says, to Ciel. “Can I get him out? Or him me?” ʸ

“Between the two of you, yes,” says Ciel. “Inside the gate. If he's somewhere and you need him, you can pull him to you. Nine breaths. But not out. Nothing goes out.” ᶜ

Yuka nods. She takes it in. He can see her taking it in: putting it on a shelf in her head where she'll be able to find it in a hurry.

“And the Shop?” she says. ʸ

* * *

He opens it.

His hands aren't steady. He notices that, distantly. He opens the Shop the way he's opened it a thousand times on the porch with her in his lap, going through every page for sweets, and he waits for the box that says *[Unavailable.]* The way it said at the quarry. The way it said, over and over, for a year, in the place he doesn't remember and his body does.

The Shop opens.

All of it. Every page. Food. Water. Tents. Clothes. Medicine. The sweets she likes, on the page she likes them on.

He stares at it.

And then the eyes, still out ahead of him, still reading, catch something else: the thing on the second floor. The spider Red put up into the branches. It's dead. And where it fell, in the flat grey light, there's a small heap of something that the system marks as *[Currency]*.

The monsters give money.

He sits down on the bottom stair.

He doesn't mean to. His legs just stop. He sits on the cold stone with the Shop open in front of him, every page, everything he could possibly need, and the money lying on the floor upstairs, and something in his chest that has been held very tight since he read the box lets go so suddenly that he has to put his hand over his face.

* * *

Yuka sits down next to him.

She doesn't say anything at first. She's looking at his face; at whatever it's doing behind his hand. He can feel her looking.

“That's relief,” she says, very quietly. ʸ

He doesn't answer.

“That's what you look like,” she says, “when you're *relieved*. I've never seen it. Not like that.” ʸ

She puts her hand on his back.

“You thought it'd be like the Trial,” she says. “Nothing. No food. No Shop. Just you and whatever you could find.” ʸ

“Yes,” he says, into his hand. ᴹ

“And it isn't.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“So we eat,” Yuka says. “We sleep. We buy a tent and blankets and the stupid sweets. And we kill the Warden. And it's not like that. Not even a little.” ʸ

She leans her head against his shoulder.

“I'm here this time,” she says. “Remember? That's the whole point. I'm here.” ʸ

He takes his hand away from his face.

He looks at her. Pale and fierce and very close, with her dogs at her feet, and her hand on his back, in a gate that wants to kill her.

“You're here,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Outside, in the orchard, Ori is the first to notice.

She's come down with a basket to pick the last of the small hard pears, the ones Shuna wants for the jam. She stops on the path. The oval between the two old trees has changed. It isn't humming anymore. It's gone very still, and very bright, and the light it gives off is a colour Ori has never seen it be.

She drops the basket.

She runs.
