# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 14 — What She Had Only Heard

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S13E63

In the middle floors they fight as one, using Yuka's already-familiar borrowed techniques and her own Ten Shadows, with the newly opened mana route supplying a limited Zoltraak cast at Mau's cost. On day two a stone-thing's tail comes for Yuka from her blind side; Mau takes it with his body and it opens his side and back. *When: Inside the trial, days one to three. Outside, the same B+370 morning; the household has begun gathering in the orchard.*

The tent is the first thing he buys.

Then blankets. Then water, two big skins of it, and bread, and a little iron stove with a chimney that folds, and a pot. Then, while she's looking the other way, three of the sweets she likes, from the page she likes them on.

He lays it all out on the third-floor stone in a neat row.

Yuka stands looking at it.

“You bought a *stove*,” she says. ʸ

“It's cold down here.” ᴹ

“You bought a stove and a pot and,” she nudges the little bag with her boot, “those.” ʸ

“They're for later.” ᴹ

“You bought my sweets in a gate that wants to kill me.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She looks at him. Something happens to her face.

Then she kneels down and starts putting up the tent, very fast, with her back to him, so he won't see it.

* * *

They make a rule the first night.

They sleep in turns. That's his rule. One of them is always awake, and Beru is always out, standing at the mouth of the alcove where they've pitched the tent with his wings half folded, and Kamish lies across the tent door like a wall of scales with one eye open.

Her rule is that she doesn't sleep while he's awake.

“That's not how turns work,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm not sleeping while you sit there watching the door.” ʸ

“That's the *point* of watching the door.” ᴹ

“Then I'll watch you watch the door.” ʸ

She does. For about an hour. Sitting up in the blankets beside him with her knees drawn up, eyes wide open, glaring at the tent flap as if it's personally offended her.

Then her head drops onto his shoulder.

Then the rest of her slides down into his lap.

He sits there all night with her asleep on him and his hand in her hair, watching the door, and doesn't wake her for her turn.

In the morning, she's furious.

“You didn't *wake* me.” ʸ

“You were asleep.” ᴹ

“That's what *waking* is *for*.” ʸ

He hands her a cup of something hot from the little stove. She drinks it, still glaring at him over the rim.

Then she drinks the whole thing and holds the cup out for more.

* * *

The middle floors are a city.

The idea of one. Streets, buildings, all of it stone, all of it empty, the flat grey light everywhere and no sky. Things live in the buildings. Big things. Things that come down off the roofs and out of the doorways, and the eyes read all of them before they've moved, and every reading has a line at the bottom that the household gate never had:

*[Lethal to the Contracted.]*

All of them. Every one.

It changes how he moves. He feels it change. In the quarry, in the hills, in every fight he's ever had, he moved toward the danger. Now he moves toward *her*. He always knows where she is. Two steps behind him and to the left; that's the place they agree on. He fights from there. Everything he does, he does from there.

And it's good.

That's the strange part. It's *good*. The borrowed cursed techniques aren't new to Yuka. She has had time to make Blue answer where she aims it, to use Red without losing her footing, and to direct her own dogs without asking Mau to do it for her. This is the first place where all of that has to work at once, with his reserves paying for her borrowed techniques and his attention kept on the cost.

When something big comes off a roof, she calls *Mau*, and he wraps his second Infinity around her long enough for the black dog to pass through the opening he leaves. Yuka moves the dog herself. He turns Sky across the creature's path, and she catches the changed angle with a short Blue, placing it directly under her dog's jaws.

On the next street three things come out of a broken shopfront with stone plates over their faces. She could move them with Blue. Instead she uses the other channel—the mana route they added three days before this gate.

“Clear the lane,” she says. ʸ

Mau steps to the left; she has a narrow line between his shoulder and the stone wall. He feels her take a carefully measured amount of mana. This isn't his cursed energy and isn't a second unlimited reserve magically appearing in her. She builds the spell herself, using a shape she's practised since the day she grew the little flowers between the training posts.

Zoltraak.

The shot goes through the gap beneath the front creature's jaw plate. Its head snaps back. The second one trips over the first, and Yuka's white dog takes it before it can regain its footing. She closes the mana draw, checks Mau's face and waits for his answer.

“Fine,” he says. “Small cast. My mana, my cost. We're still good.” ᴹ

“Then save the next one,” she says. “My dogs are free to use.” ʸ

Not unlimited. Nothing is. But they're hers, not another draft against his reserves.

When four things come up the next street at once, she calls Mau to her with Exchange. Nine breaths of distance vanish in an instant. He arrives at her side already turning, already cutting. The four things don't get close enough to touch her.

“That was on purpose,” he says, afterward. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“You didn't tell me.” ᴹ

“There wasn't time to tell you.” ʸ

She's breathing hard. Her cheeks are flushed. She looks at him and grins, fierce and bright, the way she used to look at Tsurugi across a training yard.

“Was it good?” she says. ʸ

“It was good,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

On the second day, something gets past.

Not past *him*. That's the point. Nothing gets past him. He's very careful about that.

It's a big thing. Stone-skinned, long, low, with a tail like a column of rubble. It comes up out of a broken street in front of them and he's already moving, already reading. Yuka's dogs are on the front; she has the mana channel closed, deliberately saving his reserves. Exchange is still inside its nine-breath interval from the last pull. The eyes find the tail half a second too late, because the tail isn't attached to the front of it. It's somewhere else. It's behind the wall to her left, coming round. Her blind side.

*[Lethal to the Contracted.]*

He doesn't decide anything.

He's just there. Between the tail and her. His back to it. His arms round her, pulling her down.

The tail hits him.

* * *

He hears it before he feels it.

A sound like a wet branch snapping. That's his side. Then his back, from the shoulder blade down. He feels the whole left half of him come open the way a sack comes open when you cut it: a long, wide, hot line from under his arm across his ribs, and then the tail dragging back the other way, and the stone of it taking things with it as it goes.

He doesn't let go of her.

He's on his knees on the stone, over her, and he doesn't let go.

“*Mau*—” ʸ

“Don't move,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is wrong. He knows it's wrong. He says it anyway.

“Beru,” he says. ᴹ

Beru is already there.

* * *

This is the part she's never seen.

He'll think about that later. That she's heard about it. Gojo told her about the clearing. Shoko told her about the clinic after the duel. Jin-Woo told her, carefully, about the walk out of the Trial. She's read the notebook. She's held his hand while he slept off things she only knew about from what other people said.

She's never watched it happen.

She watches it now.

Beru goes past them both like a wall falling and takes the stone thing's head off before it's finished turning. He doesn't stop to see it drop. He comes straight back and kneels on Mau's other side and puts his great clawed hands flat on Mau's back, either side of the long open line, and *holds it shut*. The way you hold a door against a wind.

“My king,” Beru says. Calm. Very calm. ᴮᵉ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Ciel?” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

“Ribs, four of them,” says Ciel, crisp, and Yuka can hear her too now; that's new. “Left lung. It's nicked, not open. Muscle down to the hip. The shoulder blade's cracked. Noise, the bleeding—” ᶜ

“Got it,” says Noise. “Pressure on the left. RCT on the lung first. Then the ribs. Don't touch the skin yet.” ᴺ

“I know,” Mau says. “I know.” ᴹ

DIO, inside, doesn't say anything at all. That's how he knows how bad it looks.

He breathes. It hurts. He does it anyway. He finds the place inside himself where the RCT lives, the way he's found it a hundred times, and opens it on the lung first, because Noise said so and Noise is right.

Yuka is still on the ground under him.

She's looking up at him. At his face. At Beru's claws on his back and whatever is coming out between them and dripping off the sides of him onto her.

She doesn't make a sound.

* * *

It takes a long time.

Not long the way the Trial was long. Long the way a bad hour is long. Beru holds. Ciel counts. Noise tells him where, and he goes there, and the RCT does what it does. The lung first. Then the ribs, one at a time, the bone pulling itself back together under the muscle with a feeling like teeth grinding. Then the shoulder blade. Then the muscle, layer by layer, from the inside out, the way Shoko taught him, so it doesn't close over something it shouldn't.

At some point Yuka gets out from under him.

He doesn't notice when. He just notices that she isn't under him anymore. She's beside him. On her knees on the stone, next to Beru, with her hands pressed flat on his back too, beside Beru's claws, because Beru moved one hand over to make room.

She's not crying.

She's watching him heal. Every second of it. Her face is completely still.

* * *

Most of it closes.

He feels it close. The ribs, the lung, the long tear in the muscle, the cracked bone. The skin, last of all, knitting over the top in a long raw seam that will be a scar by tomorrow and a line by next week.

Except one place.

Low on his back. Just above the hip. Where the last of the tail dragged across him as it went. A cut no longer than his hand.

It doesn't close.

He pushes the RCT into it. Again. Harder. The edges come together and then, as soon as he stops pushing, come apart again, slowly, like something being gently pulled.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

A long pause.

“It's the rule,” Noise says. “The trial's. Something that hits you while you're protecting her: it marks it. It doesn't stop the RCT. It just undoes it. Slowly. As long as the gate's alive.” ᴺ

“So it won't close.” ᴹ

“Not until the Warden's dead. You can keep it from getting worse. You can't make it better.” ᴺ

He breathes out.

Yuka's hands are still on his back.

“Tell me,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He could say *it's fine*.

He can feel the words in his mouth. They're very old. They fit very well. *It's fine. It's small. It's nothing. Don't worry about it.* He has said them a thousand times, to everyone, about everything, and every time they've been a door he could close between whatever was happening to him and whoever was looking.

He promised.

He turns his head. He looks at her.

“It's ugly,” he says. ᴹ

She doesn't look away.

“It's on my back, above the hip,” he says. “About this long.” He holds up his hand. “It's deep. It's not going to close. Not while we're in here. It's going to bleed a little all the time, and it's going to hurt every time I turn, and if I do something stupid it's going to open further. Noise says I can keep it from getting worse. I can't make it better.” ᴹ

He swallows.

“It's ugly,” he says again. “I'm sorry.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long time.

“Thank you,” she says. ʸ

That's all. *Thank you.* As if he's given her something.

Maybe he has.

* * *

That night she holds the lamp.

He sits on the blankets in the tent with his shirt off and his back to her, and she kneels behind him with the little lamp from the Shop held up in one hand and a cloth in the other. He tells her what to do. She does it. She cleans the edges the way Shoko would, slowly, with water from the stove, and then she sits back and looks at it in the lamplight and doesn't say anything for a long time.

He can feel her looking.

Not just at the cut. At all of it. At the new seam across his ribs, still raw. At the old lines under it, the ones from the clearing and the quarry and the Trial and the duel. At the place where his back healed crooked, a long time ago, before anyone was there to make it heal straight.

She's seen his back before. Hundreds of times. In the bath. In bed. With her fingers drawing shapes on it.

She's never seen it *happen*.

“Does it always look like that?” she asks quietly. ʸ

“Like what?” ᴹ

“Today. With Beru. And Ciel counting. And Noise telling you where. You didn't even—” ʸ

She stops.

“You didn't even scream,” she says. ʸ

He thinks about it.

“I used to,” he says. “At the start. Then I stopped.” ᴹ

“Why?” ʸ

“It used the breath I needed for the RCT.” ᴹ

He hears her put the lamp down.

Then her forehead comes to rest against the back of his neck, very gently, above all of it, and stays there.

She doesn't cry. He waits for her to, and she doesn't. She just stays there with her forehead against him, breathing, and her hand flat on his shoulder, not on any of the cuts, on the one place that doesn't hurt.

“You're going to tell me,” she says, into his neck. “Every time. Even the small ones.” ʸ

“Every time,” he says. ᴹ

“Even if it's ugly.” ʸ

“*Especially* if it's ugly.” ᴹ

She laughs. Very small. Into his neck.

* * *

Later, she's asleep.

She fell asleep sitting up, again, against his good side, with the lamp still burning. He's on watch. Beru is outside; Kamish is across the door. The cut above his hip is bleeding a little, the way Noise said it would, and he's keeping it from getting worse, the way Noise said he could.

He looks down at her.

*Any direct hit on the Contracted is lethal.*

It didn't hit her. It hit him. He was there.

He'll be there tomorrow, too.

He reaches over with his good hand and turns the lamp down, and sits in the dark with her asleep against him, watching the door.
