# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 16 — The Bell

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S13E64
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day four. The centre of the city floors: a square around a thin tower with a great bell that wakes everything in the city each time it rings. The floor's guardian lives in the bell. Yuka plans the fight in the notebook the night before, the first time she's planned one for both of them: they go up the inside stair together, quietly, so the bell never rings. They never split up. On the stair a flat thing comes out of the wall at Mau (aimed at him, not at her) and the cuts close clean, which is how they learn the trial only marks what he takes by getting between Yuka and something aimed at her. At the top, Yuka puts his second Limitless over the open side of the frame and Mau crushes the bell from inside with Blue before it can ring. A shard falls toward her; Mau is a stride too far, and Yuka uses her own Exchange to go to him: she saves herself, the way the contract was built for. DIO, stepping out at the foot of the tower afterward: from inside, "it felt like one person." No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day four. Outside, still B+370 morning; Shoko has reached the orchard with her bag and is standing in front of the oval, not touching it.*

She plans it the night before.

He wakes up for his turn on watch and she's sitting cross-legged on the new mattress with the lamp turned low and the notebook open on her knee, drawing.

A square. A tower in the middle. A spiral inside the tower that's the stair. Two little circles on the stair, side by side, and arrows.

“What's that?” ᴹ

“Tomorrow,” she says, without looking up. ʸ

He sits down beside her. He looks.

They saw the square at the end of the day, from the top of a broken wall: the middle of the city, wide and empty and paved, and in the middle of it a tower, very tall, very thin, and at the top, in an open frame of stone, a bell. A great one. Bigger than the tent. Every so often it moved, just slightly, and every time it moved, every building in the city that still had something in it shifted, the way a sleeper shifts at a sound.

“The eyes said the guardian's in the bell,” Yuka says. “Not under it. *In* it. When it rings, it's awake, and so's everything else in the city. And if everything else wakes up, it all comes for whoever's in the square.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“So it can't ring.” ʸ

She turns the notebook toward him.

“Here's how,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He reads it.

It's good. That's the first thing he thinks. Better than what he'd have done, which is take her up the outside of the tower on Sky as fast as he could and hit the bell with Blue before it could swing, and hope nothing woke while they were hanging in the open.

Her plan is the inside stair. Both of them. Slowly, on foot, him one step ahead, her one step behind, nothing loud. No Blue, no Red, nothing that makes the tower shake and the bell move. The dogs stay in her shadow, because the stair's too narrow for them and they'd scrape. And at the top—

“The bell's in a frame,” she says, finger on the drawing. “Stone on three sides. One side open. If I put Infinity over the open side, yours, the second one, it can't swing out. It can only swing *in*. Toward you. And you're already inside the frame, and you hit it with Blue where it can't get any room, and it crumples before it rings.” ʸ

He looks at the little circles on the stair. Side by side.

“And you're behind my Infinity,” he says. ᴹ

“And I'm behind your Infinity, and you're between me and the bell, and nothing gets at me.” ʸ

She looks up.

“Together,” she says. “The whole way. I'm not standing in a square full of things that wake up.” ʸ

He looks at the drawing for a long time.

“Ciel?” he says. ᴹ

“It works,” says Ciel. “One thing. The stair narrows near the top. Two floors from the top, she should be beside him, not behind. If something comes from above there's no room to turn.” ᶜ

Yuka draws an arrow and writes *2 floors: BESIDE*.

“Noise?” ᴹ

“It works,” says Noise, gruffly. “I'd have thought of it.” ᴺ

“You didn't,” says Yuka. ʸ

“I'd have thought of it *eventually*.” ᴺ

From inside him, DIO laughs out loud, and Yuka can hear that too now, and grins.

* * *

In the morning, they cross the square.

It's wider than it looked from the wall. Paved in great grey slabs, cracked, with something like weeds pushing up through the cracks. Buildings all round it, their windows dark. And in the windows, things. He can feel them. Hundreds of them, sleeping, shifting every time the bell up there moves a hair.

They don't run. Running is loud. They walk, side by side, across the open stone, with his Infinity over both of them and her hand in his, and every window they pass, something behind it shifts and settles and doesn't wake.

The tower door is open. It always was.

“Ready?” he says. ᴹ

“Ready,” she says. ʸ

They go in.

* * *

The stair is tight.

It goes round and round the inside of the tower, stone steps worn smooth in the middle, and things live on it. Not many. Not big. Grey, flat, many-legged things that cling to the walls and drop. The eyes read every one of them before it moves.

He goes one step ahead. She goes one step behind. He can feel her there the whole time, through the contract: her breathing, her hand on the wall, her shadow full of dogs that want to come out and can't.

The first thing that drops, he cuts in half on the way down. The second, he catches with Authority and puts out through a slit window very quietly. Nothing loud. Nothing that makes the tower shake.

Halfway up, something comes out of the wall faster than the eyes can follow. It isn't going for her. The eyes see that much: it's going for *him*, for the warm thing in front, and its legs go across his ribs before he's turned. His right side. Four long shallow lines, hot.

He kills it.

“Turned,” he says. ᴹ

“I saw,” she says, from one step behind. “How bad?” ʸ

“Ribs. Shallow. Four lines.” ᴹ

He opens the RCT on it. He waits for the edges to pull apart again, the way the one on his hip does.

They don't.

The lines close. Clean. All four, from the inside out, until there's nothing there but four thin seams that will be gone by tonight.

He stands very still on the stair.

“It closed,” he says. ᴹ

“It *closed*?” ʸ

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“It went for you,” says Noise slowly. “Not her. You didn't step in front of anything. The rule only marks what you take getting between her and something that's going for her.” ᴺ

Yuka is quiet behind him.

He hears what she doesn't say. *So every mark you've got that won't close, you got for me.*

She takes the notebook out, there on the stair, and writes it down, and puts it away.

“Keep going,” she says. ʸ

* * *

Two floors from the top, the stair narrows, the way Ciel said. She comes up beside him. Their shoulders touch on every turn.

Above them, he can feel the bell. Huge. Heavy. Moving, very slightly, on its beam, the way a sleeping animal's chest moves. And in it, the thing. Not a shape. The eyes can't find a shape. Just a weight and a hunger and a patience older than the tower.

On the last landing, a slit window looks out over the whole grey city. She stops and looks through it.

“That's a lot of windows,” she says, very quietly. ʸ

“They're asleep.” ᴹ

“Let's keep them asleep.” ʸ

She looks at him. Her face is flushed from the climb and very serious.

“On three,” she says. ʸ

* * *

It goes exactly the way she drew it.

They come out together onto the stone platform at the top of the tower, and the bell is there, enormous, hanging in its open frame with the dark all around it and the whole city spread out below. The thing inside it feels them come out and *wakes*. The bell starts to swing.

Yuka puts her hand out.

His second Limitless goes up over the open side of the frame like a pane of glass sliding into place. The bell swings out and *stops*, an inch from the edge, against nothing.

It swings back.

At him.

He's already inside the frame. He's already got his hand up. And the bell comes at him, the whole weight of it, with the guardian inside it awake and furious—

Blue.

Not small. Not quiet. Not anymore. Right into the mouth of the bell, at the place where the eyes say the hunger is.

The bell folds.

It folds the way the dome folded in the household gate on his birthday: inward, around his hand, metal crumpling like paper. And it doesn't ring. It crumples with a sound like a great door being slowly closed, low and long and *dull*, and nothing in the city below stirs.

* * *

Except the shock.

Neither of them thought about the shock. The bell folds and the frame cracks all the way round, and a wave of force comes off it, flat and wide, the way heat comes off a fire. It comes off the bell in every direction, and the only thing in its way on the open side is her Infinity, and on the inside, him.

It throws him back against the stone pillar hard enough that he hears his shoulder go.

“*Turned*,” he says, from the floor. ᴹ

“I saw.” ʸ

She's on her knees beside him.

“Shoulder. The left. It's out.” ᴹ

He puts it back himself, against the pillar, one hard push. She makes a small sound when it goes in and he doesn't.

The RCT, on the shoulder. He waits.

It closes. Clean. The shock wasn't going for her. It was just *going*, and he was where it went.

She lets out a breath she seems to have been holding since the stair.

* * *

The near-miss comes after.

They're both standing, at the edge of the platform, getting their breath. The guardian is dead; the box says so. And the crumpled bell groans on its beam above them.

She's gone to the far corner, three long strides away, to look down at the square and make sure nothing woke. He's at the pillar, still flexing his shoulder.

A piece of the bell comes away.

A shard. Long as a spear, edged like a knife. It drops off the crumpled lip and falls, turning, toward the corner where she's standing.

He sees it the instant the eyes do. He's moving. He knows, moving, that he isn't going to be in time: one stride too far, with the shard already halfway down, and if he gets there it'll be by getting in front of it, and it's going for her—

She doesn't wait for him.

She closes her eyes, and she's *beside him*. At the pillar. Pulled through her own shadow to his, the way Noise built it, the handle on her side, nine breaths' worth of distance in no time at all.

The shard goes into the stone of the far corner where she was standing, and stands there, quivering, buried a hand's depth.

* * *

She doesn't say anything for a long time.

She's shaking. He can feel it, all through her. He's got his arms round her and her face is in his chest and her hands are fisted in his shirt, and the shard stands in the stone at the other end of the platform, and below them, the city sleeps.

Then she starts to laugh.

Not a happy laugh. A shaky one. The kind that comes out of you when you were about to die and then didn't.

“I did it,” she says. “I didn't wait. I just—went.” ʸ

“You did it.” ᴹ

“You were going to get in front of it.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And it was going for me. So it'd have been another one. Another mark that doesn't close.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She pulls back. She looks at the shard. She looks at him.

“That's what it's for,” she says. “The Exchange. Not just you coming to me. Me getting myself out. So you don't have to.” ʸ

She takes the notebook out of her jacket and the pencil, and holds them out to him.

“Write it,” she says. “Under the plan.” ʸ

He writes, in his crooked letters, under her drawing of the tower:

*It worked.*

“And the rest,” she says. ʸ

*Mau: ribs (closed), shoulder (closed). Yuka: nothing.*

She takes the pencil back and adds, in her own hand:

*Nothing. Because I got out myself.*

She looks at it. Then she adds, smaller:

*(He was coming anyway.)*

* * *

At the foot of the tower, DIO comes out.

She steps out of him onto the square in her own body, the way she does, now that there's nothing awake to see her, and turns round to look up at the top of the tower, at the crumpled bell, at the shard standing in the corner.

“From inside,” she says, “it felt like one person.” ᴰ

Yuka stops.

“Up there. With the bell. I was in him and I could feel you both, through the contract, and it felt like one person with four hands,” says DIO. “And then you went by yourself, and it still felt like the same person.” ᴰ

She looks at Yuka.

“I thought you'd want to know,” she says. “Since you can't feel it from where you are.” ᴰ

And she goes back into him, between one step and the next, and Yuka stands at the foot of the tower holding his hand and doesn't say anything at all.
