# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 16 — The Bell

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S13E64
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day four. The centre of the city floors: a square around a tower with a great bell that wakes everything in the city each time it rings. The floor's guardian lives in the bell. Yuka plans the fight on the notebook the night before, the first time she's planned one for both of them. It works: they fight as one, using the Exchange both ways on purpose. Mau takes two hits that aren't for her (one on the tower stair; one from the bell's shockwave, aimed at him because her plan made him the bell's only target), and both close clean with RCT: the rule marks only hits he takes by getting between Yuka and something aimed at her. Yuka writes the difference down. One near-miss: a falling shard of bronze comes for Yuka and Mau, too far to reach in time, pulls her to him with the Exchange from his side for the first time. Afterward she shakes, then laughs, then makes him write the plan's result in the notebook. DIO watches from the square and says it out loud: they looked like one person. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day four. Outside, still B+370 morning; Shoko has reached the orchard with her bag and is standing in front of the oval, not touching it.*

She plans it the night before.

He wakes up for his turn on watch and she's sitting cross-legged on the new mattress with the lamp turned low and the notebook open on her knee, drawing.

A square. A tower in the middle. Lines coming off it. Little circles that are him and a little circle that's her, and arrows.

“What's that?” ᴹ

“Tomorrow,” she says, without looking up. ʸ

He sits down beside her. He looks.

They saw the square at the end of the day, from the top of a broken wall: the middle of the city, wide and empty and paved, and in the middle of it a tower, very tall, very thin, and at the top of the tower, in an open frame of stone, a bell. A great one. Bigger than the tent. Every so often it moved, just slightly, and every time it moved, every building in the city that still had something in it shifted, the way a sleeper shifts at a sound.

“The eyes said the guardian's in the bell,” Yuka says. “Not under it. *In* it. So when it rings, it's awake, and so's everything else. And if it rings while we're climbing, everything in the city comes for the tower.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“So it can't ring.” ʸ

She turns the notebook toward him.

“Here's how,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He reads it.

It's good. That's the first thing he thinks. It's *good*. Better than what he'd have done, which is go up the outside of the tower on Sky as fast as he could and hit the bell with Blue before it could swing, and hope.

Her plan has him going up the stair inside the tower, slowly, on foot. Her down in the square with the dogs. Him clearing the stair. Her keeping the square empty. And then, when he's three floors from the top, her calling the Exchange.

“You pull me up?” he says. ᴹ

“No,” she says. “You pull *me* up. From your side. You've never done it. Neither have I. If I do it, you land where I am. I don't want you in the square. I want *me* at the top.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“At the top,” he says. “Next to the bell. With the guardian in it.” ᴹ

“With *you*.” ʸ

“Yuka—” ᴹ

“Listen,” she says. She puts her finger on the drawing, on the top of the tower. “The bell's a wall. One side's open. If I'm there, I put Infinity over the open side. Yours. The second one. Then it can't swing out. It can only swing at *you*, and you're already there, and you hit it with Blue from inside the frame, where it can't get any room.” ʸ

She looks up.

“And nothing hits me,” she says, “because I'm behind your Infinity, and you're between me and the bell.” ʸ

He looks at the drawing for a long time.

“Ciel?” he says. ᴹ

“It works,” says Ciel. “I'd change one thing. He shouldn't wait three floors. Two. The stair narrows near the top; he'll want room to turn.” ᶜ

Yuka rubs out the *3* and writes *2*.

“Noise?” ᴹ

“It works,” says Noise, gruffly. “I'd have thought of it.” ᴺ

“You didn't,” says Yuka. ʸ

“I'd have thought of it *eventually*.” ᴺ

From inside him, DIO laughs out loud, and Yuka can hear that too now, and grins.

* * *

In the morning, they do it.

The square is wider than it looked from the wall. Paved in great grey slabs, cracked, with weeds that aren't weeds pushing up through the cracks. Buildings all round it, their windows dark. And in the windows, things. He can feel them. Hundreds of them, sleeping, shifting every time the bell up there moves a hair.

He stops at the foot of the tower.

Yuka stops beside him. Her dogs come up out of her shadow, one either side, low and quiet.

“Two steps,” he says. ᴹ

“Not today,” she says. “Today I'm down here and you're up there. That's the plan.” ʸ

He doesn't move.

She reaches out and straightens the collar of his shirt. The middle jacket is buttoned to her chin.

“Go,” she says. “I'll be up in a bit.” ʸ

* * *

The stair is tight.

It goes round and round the inside of the tower, stone steps worn smooth in the middle, and things live on it. Not many. Not big. Grey, flat, many-legged things that cling to the walls and drop. The eyes read them all: none of them is lethal to *him*. Nothing on this stair is lethal to him. That's the whole point. That's why she sent him up here alone.

He goes slowly. Like she said.

The first thing that drops on him, he cuts in half on the way down. The second, he catches with Authority and puts back through a window very quietly. He doesn't use Blue. He doesn't use Red. Nothing loud. Nothing that would make the tower shake and the bell move.

Halfway up, something comes out of the wall beside him faster than the eyes can follow, and its legs go across his ribs. His good side. Four long shallow lines, hot.

He kills it. He stands on the stair breathing for a second.

“Turned,” he says, into the link. ᴹ

“I heard,” says Yuka, from the square below, very small and very clear inside his head. “How bad?” ʸ

“Ribs. The right side. Shallow. Four lines.” ᴹ

“Is it closing?” ʸ

He opens the RCT on it. He waits for the edges to pull apart again, the way the one on his hip does.

They don't.

The lines close. Clean. All four, from the inside out, the way RCT is supposed to work, until there's nothing there but four thin pale seams that will be gone by tonight.

He stands very still on the stair.

“It closed,” he says. ᴹ

A pause.

“It *closed*?” ʸ

“All of it. Clean.” ᴹ

“Noise,” says Yuka. ʸ

“It wasn't for her,” says Noise slowly. “It went for him. He was alone. He wasn't protecting anyone. The rule only marks what hits him *for* her.” ᴺ

Down in the square, Yuka is quiet for a long moment.

“Okay,” she says. “Good. Keep going.” ʸ

He hears what she doesn't say. He hears it anyway. *So every mark you've got that won't close, you got for me.*

* * *

Down in the square, the things in the windows are waking.

Not because of the bell. Because of her. She can feel them, the way he feels things through the eyes, only rougher: the dogs telling her, low, with their bodies. Something at the window over there. Something at the door over there. They come out slowly, sleepy, many-legged, toward the foot of the tower where she's standing, because she's warm and she's alive and she's the only thing in the square.

*[Lethal to the Contracted.]*

She doesn't wait for them.

She sends the black dog to the left and the white one to the right, and she calls his Blue into her palm, small, the way she's practised, and puts it into the middle of the first three before they've finished crawling out of their doorway. It folds them up. It doesn't make a sound. That's important. Nothing loud.

He feels her do it. Through the contract. Her hand. His Blue. The weight of it landing on her.

“You okay?” he says. ᴹ

“Climb,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He climbs.

Two floors from the top, the stair narrows. Ciel was right. He stops in the last wide place, a landing with a slit window looking out over the city, and puts his back to the wall, and breathes.

Above him, he can feel the bell. Huge. Heavy. Moving, very slightly, on its beam, the way a sleeping animal's chest moves. And in it, the thing. Not a shape. The eyes can't find a shape. Just a weight, and a hunger, and a patience older than the tower.

“I'm here,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” says Yuka. “I can feel you.” ʸ

“Ready?” ᴹ

A pause. Down in the square, the dogs have pulled back to her. Something big is coming out of the largest building on the far side, slowly, dragging itself.

“Ready,” she says. ʸ

He closes his eyes. He finds her: not through the eyes, through the contract, the second Limitless he built in himself, the shadow of his soldier in hers. The handle Noise put on her side.

He's never pulled from his side.

He pulls.

* * *

She's there.

Beside him on the landing, between one breath and the next, with her hair across her face and her hand still up from throwing something. She stumbles. He catches her. For a moment they're just standing there on the narrow stone with his arm round her and her face in his chest and both of them breathing hard.

“That's what it feels like,” she says, muffled. “From the other side.” ʸ

“What does it feel like?” ᴹ

“Like falling *up*.” ʸ

She pushes away from him. She pushes her hair back.

“Two floors,” she says. “Go.” ʸ

* * *

It goes exactly the way she drew it.

He goes up the last of the stair and out onto the stone platform at the top of the tower, and the bell is there, enormous, hanging in its open frame with the dark all around it and the whole grey city spread out below. The thing inside it feels him come out and *wakes*. The bell starts to swing.

Yuka is right behind him.

She puts her hand out and his second Limitless goes up over the open side of the frame like a pane of glass sliding into place. The bell swings out and *stops*, an inch from the edge, against nothing at all.

It swings back.

At him.

He's already inside the frame. He's already got his hand up. And the bell comes at him, the whole huge weight of it, with the guardian inside it awake and furious and hungry—

Blue.

Not small. Not quiet. Not anymore. Right into the mouth of the bell, at the place where the eyes say the hunger is.

The bell folds.

It folds the way the dome folded in the household gate, on his birthday: inward, around his hand, metal crumpling like paper. And it doesn't ring. That's the thing. It crumples with a sound like a great door being slowly closed, low and long and *dull*, and nothing in the city below stirs.

* * *

Except the shock.

He didn't think about the shock. Neither of them did. The bell folds and the frame it hangs in cracks all the way round, and a wave of force comes off it, flat and wide, the way heat comes off a fire.

It hits him full in the chest.

It doesn't hit her. She's behind his Infinity, the way she planned. It hits him, because he's between, and he's in the frame, and it throws him back against the stone pillar of the frame hard enough that he hears something in his shoulder go.

“*Turned*,” he says, from the floor. ᴹ

“I saw,” she says. ʸ

She's on her knees beside him already.

“Shoulder,” he says. “The left. It's out.” ᴹ

He puts it back himself, the way he's done before. Against the pillar. One hard push. She makes a small sound when it goes in and he doesn't.

Then the RCT, on the shoulder. He waits.

It closes. Clean.

She lets out a breath she seems to have been holding since the stair.

“Why?” she says. “You were between me and it.” ʸ

“Because of your plan,” says Noise, through the link, and for once doesn't sound gruff at all. “The bell wasn't coming for you. You'd shut its side. It came for *him*. He didn't step in front of anything. He was already the thing it was aiming at.” ᴺ

Yuka stares at the crumpled bell.

“So if it's aiming at him,” she says slowly, “it closes. And if he gets in the way of something aiming at me—” ʸ

“It doesn't,” says Noise. ᴺ

She takes the notebook out. She writes it down. She underlines it twice.

* * *

The near-miss comes after.

They're standing up, both of them, at the edge of the platform, looking down at the square. The guardian is dead; the box says so. And the bell, what's left of it, groans on its beam above them.

And a piece of it comes away.

A shard. Bronze, if bronze were grey. Long as a spear, edged like a knife. It drops off the crumpled lip of the bell and falls, turning, straight down toward the place where Yuka is standing, at the far end of the platform where she went to look at the square.

He's at the other end.

Too far. He knows it in the same instant the eyes do. Too far to step in front. Too far to get Infinity there with the shard already falling. Too far for anything.

Except the handle.

He pulls.

She's in his arms. The shard goes into the stone where she was standing, and stands there, quivering, buried a hand's depth in the platform.

* * *

She doesn't say anything for a long time.

She's shaking. He can feel it, all through her. Her face in his chest. Her hands fisted in his shirt. He holds her and doesn't say anything either, and the shard stands in the stone at the other end of the platform, and below them, the grey city sleeps.

Then she starts to laugh.

Not a happy laugh. A shaky one. The kind that comes out of you when you were about to die and then didn't.

“You did it from your side,” she says. “Twice. In one day.” ʸ

“You told me to practise.” ᴹ

“I didn't tell you to practise *that*.” ʸ

She pulls back. She looks at the shard. She looks at him.

Then she takes the notebook out of her jacket, and the pencil, and holds them out.

“Write it,” she says. “Under the plan. What happened.” ʸ

He takes the pencil. He writes, in his crooked letters, under her neat drawing of the tower:

*It worked.*

She looks at it.

“And the rest,” she says. ʸ

He writes:

*Mau: ribs (closed), shoulder (closed). Yuka: nothing.*

She reads it twice. Then she takes the pencil back and adds, underneath, in her own hand:

*Nothing. Because of him. Again.*

She closes the notebook and puts it away, and takes his hand, and they go down the tower stair together, slowly, past the dead flat things on the walls, back to the square.

* * *

DIO is waiting at the foot of the tower.

She came out while they were up there, he realises. She's sitting on a broken slab with her chin in her hand and the two dogs lying at her feet as if they've known her all their lives.

She watches them come out of the tower door.

“From down here,” she says, “you looked like one person.” ᴰ

Yuka stops.

“Up there. At the top. With the bell. You moved like one person with four hands.” ᴰ

DIO stands up.

“I thought you'd want to know,” she says. “Since you can't see it from inside.” ᴰ

And she goes back into him, between one step and the next, and Yuka stands at the foot of the tower holding his hand, with the dogs at her feet, and doesn't say anything at all.
