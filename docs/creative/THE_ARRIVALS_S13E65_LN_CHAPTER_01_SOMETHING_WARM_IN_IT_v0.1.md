# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 17 — Something Warm in It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E65
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day five. Past the square, at the edge of the city floors, they find a small house with a lit hearth that nothing will go near. The eyes and Noise agree: it's the last line of the setting Yuka gave the household gate at the table (V78, S13E27): "something warm in it, somewhere, a place to rest, so nobody has to sleep on the cold floor", and the Rulers' override built over the gate without erasing it. They take a rest day there. Mau heats water and she bathes in the basin; then she washes his hair, careful of the hip. DIO comes out and sits by the fire with them. In the evening Yuka asks why the old precautions return even though Mau remembers the Architect's confinement. He distinguishes the original Trial, self-sealed lives and stolen memories from the tower, the Rulers' assessment, the Architect's field, other Red Gates and injuries or losses in ordinary life. DIO, Noise, Ciel and Makima remain present; no one diagnoses him with amnesia or treats every trauma as a single event. She doesn't cry; she listens. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day five. Outside, still B+370 morning; Gojo has arrived in the orchard and has taken his blindfold off.*

The house is at the edge of the city.

They find it at noon, on the far side of the square, where the streets start to thin and the buildings get lower and the flat grey light gets, if anything, flatter. A small house. One storey. A door, a window, a chimney.

There's smoke coming out of the chimney.

He stops in the street.

“Mau?” ʸ

“There's a fire,” he says. ᴹ

She looks. She sees it too. A thin thread of smoke going straight up from the chimney into the dark where the sky isn't, perfectly still, the way smoke goes up on a day with no wind.

The eyes go into the house.

Nothing in it. Nothing alive. Nothing waiting. A room. A hearth. A fire burning in the hearth with nobody tending it. And all round the house, in a ring about twenty paces wide, the eyes find something else: a line on the ground that isn't drawn, a boundary that isn't a ward. And on the other side of it, in every street and doorway he's walked past since the square, things that have stopped exactly at the line and gone no further.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“I see it.” ᴺ

“What is it?” ᴹ

A long pause.

“It's hers,” Noise says. ᴺ

* * *

He doesn't understand at first.

Then he does.

The table, after the quarry gate gave him the right to set the household one. Everybody watching. Him asking her what it should be, and her thinking about it for so long that Gojo started miming a snack bar. *Hard enough that I have to try. Not so hard anybody comes home without skin on their hand. Cores for Mai.* And then the last thing, the one she hesitated over.

*And something warm in it. Somewhere. A place to rest. So nobody has to sleep on the cold floor.*

“The old setting is still there,” Ciel says. “The Rulers overlaid their trial on the household gate: the floors, the rules, the Warden. They never removed the condition Yuka chose. This safe room is what remains of that instruction.” ᶜ

“Under the root,” Noise confirms. “They missed it.” ᴺ

He looks at the little house. The smoke.

“And the line says warm,” he says. ᴹ

“Exactly,” Ciel says. “And it is still being honoured.” ᶜ

He turns round.

Yuka is standing in the street behind him with her hand over her mouth.

* * *

“I forgot,” she says. ʸ

She's staring at the smoke.

“I forgot I said that. I said it at the table, and you said it was a good setting, and I never thought about it again. I just didn't want—” ʸ

She stops.

“I didn't want anybody sleeping on the cold floor,” she says. “Not you. Not anyone. If they were in there a long time.” ʸ

She takes her hand away from her mouth. She looks at him.

“And now we're in here,” she says. “A long time. And there's somewhere to go.” ʸ

He doesn't say anything. He can't.

He takes her hand, and they walk across the line together, and nothing follows them.

* * *

They stay all day.

They don't decide to. They just don't leave. The house is one room: a hearth, a table, two chairs, a bed against the wall with a straw mattress that's worse than theirs, so they put theirs on top of it. The fire burns without wood. It doesn't go out when nobody feeds it, and it doesn't get bigger when you put the pan on it. It's just warm. The whole room is warm, the first warm room in five days, and the window looks out on a grey street where nothing is moving, and nothing is going to move, not past that line.

He takes off his boots.

He doesn't remember the last time he took off his boots in a gate. He sits on the edge of the bed holding one of them and looking at it.

“Mau,” says Yuka, from across the room. ʸ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

“Put your boot down.” ʸ

He puts his boot down.

* * *

He heats water for her.

The basin from the Shop, and the two big water skins, and the pan on the strange fire, again and again, until the basin is full and steaming. He sets it in front of the hearth. He hangs the thick blanket from a nail over the window, though nothing is out there to look in.

Then he goes to the table and sits with his back to her.

“You don't have to do that,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You don't have to face the wall the whole time,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You can turn around if I ask you to.” ʸ

“I know that too.” ᴹ

She is quiet for a moment. During the difficult days after the false alarm, when she hadn't wanted to bathe at all, he had washed her because she needed the help; he had bathed on his own. This is different. She asked for time to recover, and he heard her. He isn't treating a basin of warm water as permission to assume anything else.

“I'm just going to wash,” she says. “I'm tired of the dust.” ʸ

“Take as long as you want.” ᴹ

He stays at the table with his back to her. He hears the water move and, a little later, the long breath she lets out when she finally gets warm. He doesn't turn unless she asks.

“DIO,” Yuka says, after a while. ʸ

“Yes?” says DIO, inside him, down the link. ᴰ

“Are you looking?” ʸ

“I'm *inside* him,” says DIO. “I see what he sees. He's looking at a very boring table.” ᴰ

“Good,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Very, very boring,” says DIO. “He's counting the knots in the wood. There are eleven.” ᴰ

* * *

Then he washes her hair.

He asks first. Yuka has changed into clean clothes; the bath is hers, and finished. Now she sits on the floor in front of the hearth, a blanket around her shoulders, with her back to the basin. Mau kneels behind her, keeping the wound above his hip from pulling as he lifts the pot.

“The hip,” she says. ʸ

“I know. I'll tell you if it hurts.” ᴹ

He pours warm water slowly through her hair. The grey dust of five days goes into the basin. He works in the soap without tugging, then rinses it away, careful not to let it run into her eyes. It is ordinary help, offered and accepted. Neither of them needs it to mean anything more.

Yuka closes her eyes.

The fire is warm on her face. His fingers move gently through her hair. The cut above his hip is still bleeding a little, steady and slow, the way Noise said, but he can keep his hands steady and let her rest for a moment.

“You're getting tired,” he says. ᴹ

“No.” ʸ

“Your head's going.” ᴹ

“It's very warm.” ʸ

“It's supposed to be,” he says softly. “You put it there.” ᴹ

* * *

DIO comes out in the afternoon.

She sits on the second chair by the fire in her own body with her feet tucked up, and holds her hands out to the hearth, and doesn't say anything for a long time.

Yuka, on the bed with her back against the wall and Mau's head in her lap, watches her.

“Do you get cold?” Yuka asks. “In there?” ʸ

DIO thinks about it.

“Not cold,” she says. “But I know what a fire is. I knew what one was before. In the other place. And I like being next to one.” ᴰ

She turns her hands over, warming the backs.

“I have a plant,” she says. “At home. In my room at the Inn. By the window.” ᴰ

“I know,” says Yuka. “I've seen it.” ʸ

“I hope someone's watering it,” says DIO. ᴰ

She says it lightly. She doesn't look at either of them.

“Someone will be,” says Yuka. “Ori will. She'll have noticed by now that we're gone. She notices everything. She'll have done it by lunch.” ʸ

DIO is quiet for a moment.

“Good,” she says. ᴰ

* * *

In the evening, Yuka asks him.

The hearth has settled into a steady light. DIO is sitting in the chair nearest it, her small hat in her lap. Mau's head is in Yuka's lap; she has been running her fingers through his hair while they listen to the fire.

“Can I ask about what happened before?” she says. ʸ

He opens his eyes.

“Which part?” ᴹ

It's a real question. They both know it.

“The way you went straight to the cheapest tent. And the way you sat down when the Shop opened.” ʸ

DIO looks over from the fire. She doesn't interrupt.

“Beru told me about the tower. I know some of what the Rulers put you through. I know what the Architect did, at least the parts you've chosen to tell me.” Yuka's hand slows against his hair. “But you keep moving as though the last one is the only one you survived.” ʸ

Mau lies still for a moment.

“It's not,” he says. ᴹ

“I know. I want to hear how *you* put them together.”

He rolls a little onto his back to see her face. The shift pulls at the mark over his hip, so he stops and gets comfortable before answering.

“I remember the Architect's field,” he says. “I remember the places he kept moving me through. I remember what happened to my back and what I did to keep breathing. I remember the room where Noise asked me to stay. I remember thinking I'd have to live there longer than anyone outside could bear.” ᴹ

*I was there,* Noise says. *You don't have to prove it to me.* ᴺ

“I know.” Mau's eyes remain on Yuka. “Ciel wasn't in that field with us. I couldn't summon a single shadow—not Beru, not Kamish, not any of the others. Inside, it was Noise and me. That was different from the tower.” ᴹ

DIO nods once. She was elsewhere; she doesn't claim to have shared that particular confinement.

“The tower wasn't a year of being locked in without a way home,” Mau continues. “We climbed, fought, came back out. Shoko repaired what she could. I went back. Sometimes I went back too soon. Beru knows that part, and so do the others. I nearly died more than once, but it wasn't the same kind of lonely.” ᴹ

Yuka's fingers stop completely.

“And before that?” she asks. ʸ

He breathes out.

“The first Trial is its own thing. So are the lives I put away afterward. I hid parts of those from myself because I couldn't keep living if I carried every version of every loss in front of me all day. That's a choice I made to survive, not proof I never knew what happened.” ᴹ

The room stays quiet.

“Then there was what was *taken* from my memory,” he says. “Someone else did that. It took time and help to get the truth back. You know the difference. You were there.” ᴹ

“I was.” ʸ

“And the Rulers' assessment. And the Red Gates. And Frieren nearly dying for real, where there was no rule promising she'd be restored when a scene ended. And the fights that happened in our actual world, with actual people left to look after afterward.” ᴹ

He lowers his eyes to their joined hands.

“They don't add up into one thing called *the Trial*. Sometimes a sound brings back one of them. Sometimes I know exactly which memory it is and still don't like what my body does with it.” ᴹ

DIO sets her hat on the chair.

“I was involved in what the Rulers did,” she says quietly. “I've had to learn what that cost. I don't want to call all of these the same thing either.” ᴰ

Mau looks at her.

“I know.” ᴹ

*May I speak?* Makima asks through the shared link. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at Mau, then answers for herself.

“Yes.” ʸ

*Remembering something doesn't mean you have agreed to feel safe when a similar thing happens again,* Makima says. *And you shouldn't have to pretend I don't have a history in this. If my presence makes a part of this room harder, tell me. I won't decide for you that it ought to be easy.* ᴹᵏ

DIO keeps her eyes on the fire. Yuka holds Mau's hand a little more firmly.

“Thank you,” Mau says. It's not an absolution. Makima doesn't treat it as one. ᴹ

Ciel speaks after a short pause.

*There isn't a missing year in your record,* she says. *There are memories you chose to keep private, experiences you chose not to revisit, and the effects they still have on you. Those are different questions.* ᶜ

“Exactly,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuka looks down at him.

“Then why the cheapest tent?”

“Because I remember what it felt like when I couldn't afford the next thing I needed.” ᴹ

“And the money?”

“Because this gate gives us another chance to earn it. I saw that and realised I wasn't going to have to count down a balance alone.” ᴹ

“You aren't alone now,” she says. ʸ

“I know. And I *remember* you telling me that. Knowing and settling down aren't the same action.” ᴹ

For a while she says nothing. Then she resumes moving her fingers through his hair.

“I think I understand better,” she says. “I was afraid you'd been walking around with whole pieces missing and hadn't told me.” ʸ

“I've kept some things behind a door,” he says. “I won't pretend I haven't. But if I close one, I'll tell you that it's closed. I won't say I can't remember when the truth is I don't want to go back inside it right then.” ᴹ

“That's what I want,” Yuka says. “You don't owe me every terrible moment on demand. Just don't leave me guessing whether you're hiding it to protect me.” ʸ

“I'll try.” ᴹ

She gives him a look.

“That's a real promise.”

“It's the one I can make.” ᴹ

“Then I'll take it.” ʸ

She bends toward him until their foreheads meet. His hip is supported by the folded blanket and doesn't pull. He can hear the fire behind DIO, Noise making some tiny adjustment to the sleeping watch, and Kamish shifting his weight on the far side of the threshold. Ordinary sounds, each belonging to the place they are actually in.

“Put your boot down,” Yuka whispers. ʸ

He glances at the boots by the hearth. Both are already on the floor.

“I did.” ᴹ

“I know. I'm practising.”

He laughs. She smiles against his forehead.

* * *

* * *

That night he doesn't keep watch.

She makes him. *Nothing's coming past the line*, she says, and Noise agrees, and Ciel agrees, and Beru, standing outside the door anyway with his wings folded, says *I will watch, my king, it is what I am for*, and Kamish lies across the threshold with one eye open.

So he sleeps.

In a gate, in a warm room, on a mattress that cost eighty, with his boots off and her hair clean and her asleep against his chest, he sleeps all night. The whole night. Without waking once.

It's the first uninterrupted night he's slept in this Red Gate. Ciel confirms it in the morning. She does not claim he hasn't rested once since some earlier Trial; there have been beds, houses and people who helped him rest between those events. This night matters because he chose to sleep here.

He doesn't tell Yuka.

Then he remembers his promise, and tells her.

She doesn't say anything. She just holds his hand very hard for a long time, and then gets up and puts the pan on the fire.
