# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 17 — Something Warm in It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E65
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day five. Past the square, at the edge of the city floors, they find a small house with a lit hearth that nothing will go near. The eyes and Noise agree: it's the last line of the setting Yuka gave the household gate at the table (V78, S13E27): "something warm in it, somewhere, a place to rest, so nobody has to sleep on the cold floor", and the Rulers' override built over the gate without erasing it. They take a rest day there. Mau heats water and she bathes in the basin; then she washes his hair, careful of the hip. DIO comes out and sits by the fire with them. In the evening Yuka asks what his body remembers of the Trial, and Mau tells her the truth as far as he has it: very little in his head, a great deal in his hands. She doesn't cry; she listens. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day five. Outside, still B+370 morning; Gojo has arrived in the orchard and has taken his blindfold off.*

The house is at the edge of the city.

They find it at noon, on the far side of the square, where the streets start to thin and the buildings get lower and the flat grey light gets, if anything, flatter. A small house. One storey. A door, a window, a chimney.

There's smoke coming out of the chimney.

He stops in the street.

“Mau?” ʸ

“There's a fire,” he says. ᴹ

She looks. She sees it too. A thin thread of smoke going straight up from the chimney into the dark where the sky isn't, perfectly still, the way smoke goes up on a day with no wind.

The eyes go into the house.

Nothing in it. Nothing alive. Nothing waiting. A room. A hearth. A fire burning in the hearth with nobody tending it. And all round the house, in a ring about twenty paces wide, the eyes find something else: a line on the ground that isn't drawn, a boundary that isn't a ward. And on the other side of it, in every street and doorway he's walked past since the square, things that have stopped exactly at the line and gone no further.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“I see it.” ᴺ

“What is it?” ᴹ

A long pause.

“It's hers,” Noise says. ᴺ

* * *

He doesn't understand at first.

Then he does.

The table, after the quarry gate gave him the right to set the household one. Everybody watching. Him asking her what it should be, and her thinking about it for so long that Gojo started miming a snack bar. *Hard enough that I have to try. Not so hard anybody comes home without skin on their hand. Cores for Mai.* And then the last thing, the one she hesitated over.

*And something warm in it. Somewhere. A place to rest. So nobody has to sleep on the cold floor.*

“The Rulers took the gate,” Noise says. “They built the trial over it. Floors, rules, the Warden, all of it. But they built *over*. They didn't take out what was underneath. They didn't think they needed to. It's just a line in the settings.” ᴺ

He looks at the little house. The smoke.

“And the line says warm,” he says. ᴹ

“The line says warm,” Noise agrees. ᴺ

He turns round.

Yuka is standing in the street behind him with her hand over her mouth.

* * *

“I forgot,” she says. ʸ

She's staring at the smoke.

“I forgot I said that. I said it at the table, and you said it was a good setting, and I never thought about it again. I just didn't want—” ʸ

She stops.

“I didn't want anybody sleeping on the cold floor,” she says. “Not you. Not anyone. If they were in there a long time.” ʸ

She takes her hand away from her mouth. She looks at him.

“And now we're in here,” she says. “A long time. And there's somewhere to go.” ʸ

He doesn't say anything. He can't.

He takes her hand, and they walk across the line together, and nothing follows them.

* * *

They stay all day.

They don't decide to. They just don't leave. The house is one room: a hearth, a table, two chairs, a bed against the wall with a straw mattress that's worse than theirs, so they put theirs on top of it. The fire burns without wood. It doesn't go out when nobody feeds it, and it doesn't get bigger when you put the pan on it. It's just warm. The whole room is warm, the first warm room in five days, and the window looks out on a grey street where nothing is moving, and nothing is going to move, not past that line.

He takes off his boots.

He doesn't remember the last time he took off his boots in a gate. He sits on the edge of the bed holding one of them and looking at it.

“Mau,” says Yuka, from across the room. ʸ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

“Put your boot down.” ʸ

He puts his boot down.

* * *

He heats water for her.

The basin from the Shop, and the two big water skins, and the pan on the strange fire, again and again, until the basin is full and steaming. He sets it in front of the hearth. He hangs the thick blanket from a nail over the window, though nothing is out there to look in.

Then he goes to the table and sits with his back to her.

“You don't have to do that,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“We're married.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You've seen me in the bath a hundred times.” ʸ

“Not in a *gate*.” ᴹ

She laughs. He hears her undress, and the water move, and her let out a long breath that seems to come from the bottom of her feet. He sits at the table with his back to her and listens to her hum. He's never heard her hum in a gate either.

“DIO,” Yuka says, after a while. ʸ

“Yes?” says DIO, inside him, down the link. ᴰ

“Are you looking?” ʸ

“I'm *inside* him,” says DIO. “I see what he sees. He's looking at a very boring table.” ᴰ

“Good,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Very, very boring,” says DIO. “He's counting the knots in the wood. There are eleven.” ᴰ

* * *

Then she washes his hair.

She makes him. He sits on the floor in front of the hearth with his back to the basin, which she's refilled, and she kneels behind him with her sleeves rolled up and pours warm water over his head from the pot.

“The hip,” he says. ᴹ

“I know. I'm not touching it.” ʸ

She isn't. She's very careful. She washes the grey dust of five days out of his hair, and the blood from the tower stair out from behind his ear where he didn't know it was, and she works the soap through slowly with her fingers, the way she does at home on a Sunday when he's too tired to do it himself.

He closes his eyes.

The fire is warm on his face. Her fingers are warm in his hair. The cut above his hip is still bleeding a little, steady and slow, the way Noise said, and he's still keeping it from getting worse, and he's somewhere else as well. Somewhere he hasn't been in five days.

“You're falling asleep,” she says. ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“You are. Your head's going.” ʸ

“It's very warm.” ᴹ

“It's supposed to be,” she says, very softly. “I put it there.” ʸ

* * *

DIO comes out in the afternoon.

She sits on the second chair by the fire in her own body with her feet tucked up, and holds her hands out to the hearth, and doesn't say anything for a long time.

Yuka, on the bed with her back against the wall and Mau's head in her lap, watches her.

“Do you get cold?” Yuka asks. “In there?” ʸ

DIO thinks about it.

“Not cold,” she says. “But I know what a fire is. I knew what one was before. In the other place. And I like being next to one.” ᴰ

She turns her hands over, warming the backs.

“I have a plant,” she says. “At home. In my room at the Inn. By the window.” ᴰ

“I know,” says Yuka. “I've seen it.” ʸ

“I hope someone's watering it,” says DIO. ᴰ

She says it lightly. She doesn't look at either of them.

“Someone will be,” says Yuka. “Ori will. She'll have noticed by now that we're gone. She notices everything. She'll have done it by lunch.” ʸ

DIO is quiet for a moment.

“Good,” she says. ᴰ

* * *

In the evening, Yuka asks him.

The fire is low; the light from it is the only light, and the room has gone soft and close around them. He's lying with his head in her lap still. She's been running her fingers through his clean hair for an hour.

“Can I ask about the Trial?” she says. ʸ

He opens his eyes.

“You can always ask,” he says. ᴹ

“I know. But this one.” ʸ

Her fingers stop.

“The year,” she says. “The one you don't remember. Beru said you bought the cheapest of everything for a long time after. And on the stair, when you said it closed, you sounded—” ʸ

She doesn't finish.

“What do you remember?” she says. ʸ

* * *

He thinks about it for a long time.

Not because he doesn't want to tell her. Because he wants to tell her *right*. The way he promised. Not *it's fine*. Not *it was a long time ago*. What it is.

“Almost nothing,” he says. ᴹ

She waits.

“In my head, almost nothing,” he says. “Pieces. A wall. A sound. Rain that wasn't rain. I know it was a year because Ciel counted and my hair grew. I know I was hungry because I came out thin. I know I was hurt because Shoko had to break my arm again to set it.” ᴹ

He swallows.

“But my hands remember,” he says. ᴹ

Her fingers are still in his hair. She doesn't move them.

“When I bought the tent,” he says, “my hand went to the cheapest one before I'd read the prices. When I saw the money on the floor upstairs, I sat down because my legs did it. Not me. When you said *put your boot down*, I didn't know I was holding it like that. Like I might have to run.” ᴹ

He looks up at her. At her face, upside down, in the firelight. The only face in the world he sees in colour.

“I don't remember the year,” he says. “But I've got it. All of it. In here.” He touches his chest. “Somewhere. And it comes out in my hands when I'm not looking. And I don't know what's in there. I don't know what else it's going to do.” ᴹ

He's quiet for a moment.

“That's the truth,” he says. “That's all of it. It's not very much. It's ugly anyway.” ᴹ

* * *

She doesn't cry.

He waits for her to. He's seen her cry about less. He's seen her cry about a letter, about a hat, about a birthday banner that said *(AGAIN)*.

She doesn't.

She looks down at him with her fingers still in his hair, and her face is very still, and very fierce, and very gentle all at once.

“Then we'll find out,” she says. ʸ

“Find out what?” ᴹ

“What it does. What else is in there. Together. When it comes out of your hands, I'll be watching, and I'll tell you, and you'll know.” ʸ

She bends down and presses her forehead to his.

“Like the boot,” she says. “I'll say *put your boot down*. Every time. And you'll put it down. And one day you won't be holding it.” ʸ

He closes his eyes.

The fire is warm. Her forehead is warm. Outside the window, past the blanket, past the line she drew without knowing she was drawing it, the grey city waits, and none of it comes in.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

That night he doesn't keep watch.

She makes him. *Nothing's coming past the line*, she says, and Noise agrees, and Ciel agrees, and Beru, standing outside the door anyway with his wings folded, says *I will watch, my king, it is what I am for*, and Kamish lies across the threshold with one eye open.

So he sleeps.

In a gate, in a warm room, on a mattress that cost eighty, with his boots off and his hair clean and her asleep against his chest, he sleeps all night. The whole night. Without waking once.

It's the first time since the Trial. Ciel tells him so in the morning, very quietly, so only he can hear.

He doesn't tell Yuka.

Then he remembers his promise, and tells her.

She doesn't say anything. She just holds his hand very hard for a long time, and then gets up and puts the pan on the fire.
