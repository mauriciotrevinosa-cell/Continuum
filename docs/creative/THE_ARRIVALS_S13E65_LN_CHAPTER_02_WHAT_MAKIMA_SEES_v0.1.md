# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 18 — What Makima Sees

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S13E65
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day six. Leaving the warm house is hard; Yuka takes the blanket off the window last. The city ends at a great stair going down. On the stair, while Mau and Beru clear a landing with her at the edge of it, Yuka uses her rule for the first time the other way: it isn't important, so she goes to Makima, through the link, and asks what Makima sees when Mau fights. Makima answers honestly: he fights with one hand, because the other is always kept free for Yuka; he has not opened anything large since they came in; and the thing in him that wants to open everything is held shut by him, not by the contract. Makima tells her she could hold that weight if it ever had to be opened, and that she'd tell Yuka first if she could. Noise listens and, for once, says Makima is right. Mau hears every word, as the link means he will, and doesn't interrupt. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day six. Outside, still B+370, late morning; Maki has arrived in the orchard and is standing next to Gojo without saying anything.*

Leaving is hard.

They both feel it. He packs the tent and the stove and the pan and the basin, slowly, more slowly than he needs to. She folds the mattress. Neither of them says anything about it. Then she goes to the window and stands there with her hand on the thick blanket he nailed up the day before.

“We could stay another day,” she says. ʸ

“We could.” ᴹ

“Nothing would come in.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

She stands there.

“But the Warden's still down there,” she says. “And your hip's still open.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She takes the blanket down. She folds it into a square and puts it in the bag on top of everything else, where it'll be the first thing out tonight.

At the door, she turns round and looks at the room one more time. The hearth. The fire that doesn't need wood. The two chairs. The worse mattress on the bed.

“Thank you,” she says, to nobody. To the room. To a line in the settings she said once at a table and forgot. ʸ

Then she takes his hand and they cross the line, and the grey city closes behind them.

* * *

The city ends at a stair.

It's the widest thing he's seen in the gate. A stair cut into the floor of the world, going down, broad as the square was, step after step after step into a dark the eyes can only follow so far. The steps are cracked. Things live in the cracks. And at intervals, every hundred steps or so, there's a landing, flat and wide, and on every landing, something waiting.

“How far?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Noise?” ᴹ

“Far,” says Noise. “Two days, maybe. The Warden's at the bottom of whatever's at the bottom of this. I can feel the knot. It's a long way down.” ᴺ

Yuka looks down the stair.

“Two steps,” she says. ʸ

“Two steps,” he says. ᴹ

They go down.

* * *

It's on the fourth landing that she does it.

He and Beru have gone ahead. There's something on the landing below, big and slow and many-armed, wrapped round a pillar in the middle of it like a vine round a post, and the eyes say it's lethal to her and not much to him. So he goes down the last steps onto the landing with Beru to deal with it, and she comes down right behind them to the bottom step, at the edge of the landing, inside his Infinity, with the dogs, close enough that he could touch her if he reached back.

He hears her in his head. Not to him.

*Makima.* ʸ

He doesn't stop. He's halfway through the many-armed thing's reach, cutting, and Beru is holding three of its arms at once, and he keeps going. But he hears.

*It's not important*, Yuka says, down the link. *So I'm coming to you. That's the rule.* ʸ

A pause. And then, from the back of him, the voice he's learned to listen for.

*Yes*, says Makima. *I'm here.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

He doesn't interrupt.

He thinks about it. Afterward, he'll think about it a lot. That he could have. That the link runs through him, through Ciel, and every word goes past him on its way, and he could have said *not now* or *later* or *what are you talking about*.

He doesn't. It isn't his. That's the rule too. Yuka made it, and Makima agreed to it, and it isn't his.

He cuts the last arm off the thing round the pillar and Beru takes its head.

*What do you see?* Yuka asks. *When he fights. You're inside. You see more than I do.* ʸ

A long pause.

*Do you want the honest answer?* says Makima. ᴹᵏ

*Yes.* ʸ

*He fights with one hand*, says Makima. ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuka doesn't say anything.

*Not literally*, says Makima. *He uses both. But he keeps one free. Always. Even now, on that landing, with nothing near you. Part of him is turned toward you every second. Not watching: holding. The way you'd hold a door you might have to slam. Every technique he opens, he opens with what's left over after that.* ᴹᵏ

He's standing over the dead thing at the foot of the pillar. He doesn't move.

*Is that bad?* says Yuka. ʸ

*It's what he chose*, says Makima. *I don't think it's bad. I think it's expensive. He hasn't opened anything large since you came in here. Blue, small. Red, with you. Shrine in lines. The few mana spells you cast through the new channel came out of his supply too, and he watched every cost without asking you to stop. He's very strong, Yuka. Stronger than he lets anyone see, including you. And he's been fighting this Red Gate with what he can spare.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

Beru has turned round. He's looking at Mau. He can't hear the link, but he can see Mau's face.

“My king?” ᴮᵉ

“It's fine,” Mau says. Then, because he promised, even if it's not to Beru: “No. It's not fine. It's just true. Give me a second.” ᴹ

Beru folds his wings and waits.

* * *

*Why doesn't he open the big things?* Yuka asks. *Is it the contract? Is it you?* ʸ

*No*, says Makima. *It's him.* ᴹᵏ

She's quiet for a moment.

*There's a part of him*, she says, *that wants to open everything. All of it. At once. You've felt the edge of it, in the north. That part is always there. It's very large. And he keeps it shut. Not the contract: him. With his own hands. Every day.* ᴹᵏ

*Because of me?* ʸ

*Because if he opens it in here, he doesn't know where it ends*, says Makima. *And you're in here. Two steps behind him. And he can't open everything and keep one hand free for you at the same time. So he chooses you. Every time.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

He hears Yuka breathe.

On the bottom step, at the edge of the landing. Inside his Infinity. With her dogs. He can feel her through the contract, and she's very still.

*Can it be opened?* she says. *Ever? In here? Safely?* ʸ

Another pause. Longer.

*Not by him alone*, says Makima. ᴹᵏ

*Then by who?* ʸ

*If he opened all of it*, says Makima, *something would have to hold the weight. Not the power. The weight. The pull of everything at once on a body that's still healing. The part that tears. Ciel can count it. Noise can steer it. Neither of them can hold it. They're part of him. They'd be torn too.* ᴹᵏ

*And you?* ʸ

*I'm bound to his body*, says Makima. *I gave up coming back for it. So I'm not part of him, quite. And I'm not outside him, either. If he opened everything, I could stand where the tearing is and hold it.* ᴹᵏ

She stops.

*For a little while*, she says. *Not long. But for a little while.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

Noise speaks.

Nobody asked it to. It's been quiet the whole time, listening, the way it listens to everything Makima says, with its whole enormous attention, ready to find the trick.

*She's right*, Noise says. ᴺ

Yuka makes a small surprised sound.

*I've checked*, says Noise. *I check everything she says. She's right. I can't hold it. Ciel can't. She could. For a little while.* ᴺ

A pause.

*I don't like saying it*, Noise adds. ᴺ

*I know*, says Makima, and there's something in her voice that almost sounds like warmth. *Thank you for saying it anyway.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

*Would you tell me?* Yuka says. ʸ

*Tell you what?* ᴹᵏ

*If it happened. If he had to open everything. If you were holding it. Would you tell me first?* ʸ

Makima doesn't answer right away.

*If I could*, she says. *If there was time. It's important, so I wouldn't ask. I'd just say it.* ᴹᵏ

*Okay*, says Yuka. ʸ

A pause.

*Thank you for coming to me*, says Makima. *With something that isn't important.* ᴹᵏ

*It was important*, says Yuka. *I just didn't know it yet.* ʸ

* * *

He turns round.

She's on the bottom step, an arm's length behind him, inside his Infinity, with her dogs. She looks at him as he comes up. She knows he heard. She doesn't pretend he didn't.

“You heard,” she says. ʸ

“I heard.” ᴹ

“All of it.” ʸ

“All of it.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment.

“Is it true?” she says. “One hand?” ʸ

He could lie.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

She nods. She looks down the endless stair, at the landings, at the dark.

“Okay,” she says. “Then I'll get stronger. So you need less of the hand.” ʸ

She steps down off the last step. She stands beside him. Not behind. Beside.

“Two steps,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” she says, and goes back to two steps, and they go down.

* * *

That night, on the seventh landing, she makes the tent with the thick blanket on top, the way she said. She doesn't say anything about Makima. Neither does he.

But before she sleeps, lying against him, she says, very quietly, down the link, not to him:

*Good night, Makima.* ʸ

And from the back of him, after a moment, so quiet he almost misses it:

*Good night, Yuka.* ᴹᵏ
