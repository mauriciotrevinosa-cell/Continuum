# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 19 — The Flooded Floors

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S13E66
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day seven. The great stair ends in water: a drowned level, waist-deep on Mau, chest-deep on Yuka, cold, with things moving under the surface that the eyes read as lethal to her. Mau carries her on his back the whole level; she keeps her arms round his neck and fights from there. Her dogs can't swim it and stay in her shadow. Yuka uses Mau's Shrine **for the first time** through their long-established technique contract. The borrowed cursed-energy cost falls on Mau; she must learn to shape and stop its cutting edge while aiming from his back through opaque water. Her first line goes too far and cuts a pillar, waking the level. Ciel guides their range calibration without taking over Yuka's execution. Kamish lies flat on the surface as a raft for the bags; Beru wades ahead. Nothing gets past to her. The hip cut gets wet and stings; he says so. At the far side they climb onto dry stone soaked and shaking and laughing, and Mau buys dry clothes for both of them, in the right size this time. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day seven. Outside, still B+370, late morning; Rimuru and Shizue have reached the orchard, and Rimuru has asked Ciel, through the link, whether Mau can hear him. He can't.*

The stair ends in water.

They come round the last landing and the next hundred steps are gone. Not broken: *under*. The stair goes down into a flat dark surface that fills the whole level from wall to wall, very still, and the steps go on underneath it, the eyes can see them, down and down, until they level out into a floor somewhere below.

He stops on the last dry step.

Yuka stops beside him.

“How deep?” she asks. ʸ

“Ciel?” ᴹ

“Waist on you,” says Ciel. “Chest on her. Most of the way. There are deeper places. I'll tell you where.” ᶜ

“How far?” ʸ

“A long way. The whole level. The stair to the next one is on the other side.” ᶜ

Yuka looks at the water.

Things are moving in it. He can see them through the eyes, long and slow and many of them, under the surface, nowhere near yet. Every reading has the same line at the bottom.

*[Lethal to the Contracted.]*

“Up,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at him.

“On my back,” he says. “The whole way. I'm not having you chest-deep in that.” ᴹ

* * *

She argues. Of course she does.

“Your hip,” she says. ʸ

“It's on the side. You'll be on the middle.” ᴹ

“You'll be carrying me *and* fighting.” ʸ

“You'll be fighting too. From up there. You'll be higher than me. You'll see more.” ᴹ

“The dogs can't swim that.” ʸ

“No. So they stay in. And you use mine.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment.

“Yours,” she says. “Which?” ʸ

“Whatever you need.” ᴹ

“Shrine?” ʸ

He hesitates. Just for a second. She sees it.

“I haven't used that one,” she says. “Not once.” ʸ

“I know,” he says. “The contract lets you. It doesn't make the first cut easy.” ᴹ

“I've watched you do it. I know what it looks like.” ʸ

“Knowing what it looks like isn't the same as feeling where it ends.” ᴹ

“Then help me find the end.” ʸ

He meets her eyes. She's asking to try a tool she has been allowed to borrow for a long time, not asking him to move her hand or give her permission she didn't have.

“I'll carry the cost,” he says. “You choose the cut. Start shorter than you think you need, and tell me if it feels wrong.” ᴹ

“I will.” ʸ

He turns round and crouches on the step with his back to her.

“Then it's yours,” he says. “Get on.” ᴹ

* * *

She climbs on.

She's ridden on his back before, at home, for nothing, for fun.

Never like this. Never in a gate with her arms round his neck and her knees tight at his sides and her breath on his ear, and the water in front of them full of things that could kill her with a touch.

He stands up. She's not heavy. She's never been heavy.

“Kamish,” he says. ᴹ

The dragon comes up out of his shadow and onto the water. Not into it. *Onto* it. He lies flat on the surface, his great wings spread out on either side, and the water takes his weight the way it takes a fallen tree. Mau puts the bags on his back: the tent, the stove, the mattress rolled tight, the thick blanket on top. Kamish doesn't seem to mind. He looks, if anything, pleased to be useful.

Beru goes into the water first. It comes up to his chest and he doesn't seem to notice.

“My king,” he says. “I will go ahead.” ᴮᵉ

“Not far.” ᴹ

“Not far,” Beru agrees. ᴮᵉ

Mau steps down off the last dry step.

The water is cold. Colder than he expected. It closes round his legs, his hips, and then the cut above his hip goes under, and he hisses through his teeth before he can stop himself.

“*Turned*?” she says, in his ear. ʸ

“Wet,” he says. “The hip. It stings.” ᴹ

“Bad?” ʸ

“Just stings. Like salt.” ᴹ

She tightens her arms round his neck. Not too tight. Just enough.

“Tell me if it's more,” she says. ʸ

“I'll tell you.” ᴹ

He wades out.

* * *

It's a long way.

The level is a hall, he thinks, under the water: pillars rising out of the surface at intervals, holding up a ceiling he can't see. The flat grey light comes off the water from below as well as from above, so that everything has no shadow, and Beru ahead of them pushes a long slow ripple out in front of him like the bow of a boat.

The first thing comes at them before they're a hundred steps out.

He sees it under the water before she does. Long. Flat. Coming fast from the left, low, under the surface, toward the place where her legs hang at his sides.

“Left,” he says. ᴹ

And he feels her reach for Shrine.

* * *

It goes wrong.

He feels the line go wrong as she releases it. For all the times Yuka has watched his Shrine, this is the first time the cutting edge has answered *her*. Blue pulls; Red pushes. Shrine is neither. The edge has length and width and a stopping point that she has to decide before she lets it go. The technique comes through their existing contract, and the cursed-energy cost falls on Mau, exactly as it should. The control is hers.

Below them the creature is moving under opaque water. The floor is too deep to judge by eye, and the pillar beyond her target rises out of the same grey surface. From Mau's back she is aiming down past his shoulder, with no horizon to measure against. She thinks she has ended the line behind the creature. She hasn't. The unfamiliar response of the technique and the distorted distance make the same mistake worse.

The line goes out from her hand across the water. It takes the long flat thing in half. And then it keeps going, wide and hungry, and it takes the pillar behind it too, clean through, a little above the surface.

The pillar stands there for a second, cut.

Then the top half of it slides off sideways and goes into the water with a sound like the world falling over.

* * *

Everything in the level hears it.

He feels them turn. All the long slow shapes, everywhere, under the surface. They stop drifting and turn toward the sound, and then toward the warm thing standing in the water with someone on his back.

“*Sorry*,” she says, in his ear. “Sorry, sorry, I—” ʸ

“It's fine.” ᴹ

“It's *not* fine, they're all—” ʸ

“It's not fine,” he agrees. “It's just what happened. Do it again. Shorter.” ᴹ

She goes very still on his back.

“Shorter,” he says. “Half the length. Think of a knife, not a sword. Stop it where you want it to stop. Ciel?” ᴹ

“Here,” says Ciel. “I'll give you lengths. When I say a number, that's how far. In steps.” ᶜ

“Okay,” Yuka says. Her voice is small. Then it isn't. “Okay.” ʸ

* * *

They come from everywhere.

That's the next part. The long slow shapes come from every side under the water, and he stands in the middle of the drowned hall with her on his back and does what he does. Infinity over both of them, low, so it sits just under the surface like ice. Authority grabbing one by the tail and throwing it back the way it came. The katana, out of its shadow, in his right hand, cutting along the top of the water where the shapes come up to look.

And from his back, over his shoulder, Shrine.

“Four,” says Ciel. ᶜ

A line goes out across the water. Four steps long. It stops where she means it to stop. The thing it was for comes apart.

“Two. Left.” ᶜ

Two steps. Left. Clean.

“Six, ahead, past Beru.” ᶜ

She hesitates. Six steps, past Beru. If she gets it wrong—

She doesn't get it wrong.

He feels her learn. Line by line, number by number, Yuka begins to recognise where Shrine wants to continue past the end she intended—and to stop it there. She's an experienced fighter, and Blue and Red already answer her, but this particular edge is new in her hands. The submerged steps give her reference points; Ciel's distances let her check each release. Mau pays the cursed-energy cost of every cut and calls out when the range is greater than they need. By the twentieth, Ciel has stopped giving her numbers because Yuka is giving them to herself, under her breath, in his ear.

“Three. Five. *Two*—” ʸ

And the things in the water stop coming.

* * *

He doesn't know how long it takes.

Long enough that his legs have gone numb in the cold. Long enough that the sting in his hip has gone dull and deep. He wades, and Beru wades ahead, and Kamish drifts beside them with the bags, and every so often something comes, and every time, between his katana and her Shrine, it stops coming.

Nothing touches her. He's very careful about that.

Halfway across, she puts her chin on his shoulder.

“Mau,” she says. ʸ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

“It's strange,” she says. “Using your Shrine from here.” ʸ

“From my back?” ᴹ

“In the water. When I can't see where the line ends.” ʸ

He hears her swallow.

“The one that—” She stops. ʸ

He knows what she's going to say. The duel. The quarry. The cuts he took and the cuts he gave, the technique he took from the one who tried to kill him, and the price of learning it.

“The one from Sukuna,” he says. ᴹ

“I've watched you use it so many times,” she says. “This is the first time I've felt it from my side of the contract. I didn't expect it to feel like that.” ʸ

“Neither did I, the first time.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a while.

“It's heavy,” she says. “Not the weight. Heavier than that. It feels like it *wants* to be longer. Every time.” ʸ

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

“Is it like that for you?” ʸ

He thinks about lying. He doesn't.

“Every time,” he says. ᴹ

She puts her cheek against the side of his head and doesn't say anything else for a long way.

* * *

The far side is a beach.

Not a real one. A long slope of grey stone coming up out of the water, and at the top of it, dry, a landing, and a stair going down into the next level. He wades up the slope with her on his back until the water's at his knees, his ankles, gone.

He kneels on the dry stone.

She slides off. She stands up. She's soaked to the waist, and he's soaked to the chest, and they're both shaking with cold so hard their teeth are knocking together.

She looks at him.

He looks at her.

She starts to laugh.

He doesn't know why. Neither does she. They're standing on a fake beach at the bottom of a gate that wants to kill her, wet through and frozen and exhausted, with the hip cut stinging and her arms aching from gripping Mau's shoulders for the whole crossing, and she's laughing so hard she has to sit down on the stone, and then he's laughing too, kneeling beside her, both of them shaking and laughing and unable to stop.

Beru comes up out of the water behind them. He looks at them both.

“My king,” he says. “My queen. Are you well?” ᴮᵉ

“*No*,” says Yuka, and laughs harder. ʸ

* * *

He buys dry clothes.

He opens the Shop with his hands still shaking and buys a shirt and trousers for himself, and then, for her, without thinking, without three of anything:

*Middle.*

He puts it in her lap.

She looks at it. She looks at him. She stops laughing.

“You remembered,” she says. ʸ

“You wrote it down,” he says. “I wrote it down. *Yuka: middle*.” ᴹ

She holds the dry shirt against her chest for a moment.

Then she changes, there on the stone, behind the thick blanket that Kamish holds up in his teeth like a curtain without being asked, and comes out in clothes that fit, and sits down beside him, and puts her head on his shoulder.

“I'm going to get better at Shrine,” she says. ʸ

“You already did.” ᴹ

“I cut a pillar.” ʸ

“Once,” he says. “And then you didn't.” ᴹ

She closes her eyes.

“Shorter,” she says, sleepily. “Think of a knife.” ʸ

He puts his arm round her, and they sit on the dry stone at the top of the fake beach and let the cold go out of them slowly, until the shaking stops.
