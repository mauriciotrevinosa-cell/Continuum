# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 20 — Three Sweets

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S13E66
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, night of day seven, on the dry landing above the drowned level. The three sweets Mau bought on day one, still uneaten: Yuka has been saving them, the way he saves everything, and catches herself doing it. They eat one each and split the third. They talk about the island: the matching enormous hats, Elmesia's secret gift, what they'll do on the first morning. DIO comes out to join in and asks to borrow the hat idea. Mau asks Yuka to read him the notebook page she's been hiding. She does: every mark, every "turned", every day. Then she writes a new line at the bottom and lets him read it. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, night of day seven. Outside, still B+370, around noon; Ori has watered DIO's plant at the Inn without being asked.*

The sweets are still in the bag.

He finds them when he's unpacking for the night: the little paper bag from the first day, three of them inside, from the page she likes. He bought them while she wasn't looking. *They're for later*, he said.

It's later.

He holds the bag up.

Yuka, sitting on the mattress with the thick blanket round her shoulders, still not quite warm from the water, looks at it. Then she looks away.

“Those are for later,” she says. ʸ

“It's day seven.” ᴹ

“There's still the Warden.” ʸ

“And?” ᴹ

“And we might need them more after,” she says. “If it's bad. If you're—” ʸ

She stops.

He watches her hear herself.

* * *

“Oh,” she says. ʸ

“Yeah,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm saving them.” ʸ

“You're saving them.” ᴹ

“In *case*.” ʸ

She puts both hands over her face.

“I made you buy the mattress,” she says through her fingers. “I told you to spend it on us. I said it's not like there. And I've been carrying three *sweets* around for seven days in case.” ʸ

He sits down beside her.

“It's catching,” he says. ᴹ

“It's not funny.” ʸ

“It's a little funny.” ᴹ

She takes her hands away. She looks at the bag.

“One each,” she says. “Now. And we split the third.” ʸ

* * *

They eat them slowly.

That's her rule. Slowly. You don't eat a sweet in a gate the way you'd eat one on the porch, in one bite, talking with your mouth full. You eat it like it's the only one. He doesn't argue.

It's a small hard thing with something soft in the middle. He doesn't know what flavour. He never does; he knows the words for flavours the way he knows the words for colours, from other people. But he knows it's the one she likes, because her eyes close when she bites it.

“That's the good one,” she says, with her eyes closed. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You always buy the good one.” ʸ

“You always point at it.” ᴹ

She opens her eyes and looks at him.

Then she breaks the third one in half, carefully, with her thumbs, and gives him the bigger half.

“You carried me across a lake,” she says. ʸ

* * *

They talk about the island.

He doesn't know how it starts. One of them says something. The bay, maybe. The roofs. The long beach where they walked the first time. And then they're lying on the mattress under the thick blanket with the lamp turned low, talking about it the way people talk about home when they're a long way from it.

“The hats,” she says. “We're wearing the hats.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Both of us. The enormous ones. Matching. The whole time.” ʸ

“The *whole* time?” ᴹ

“The whole time. Even in the water.” ʸ

“You can't swim in a hat.” ᴹ

“*I* can,” she says. “I'll learn.” ʸ

He laughs.

“And Elmesia's thing,” she says. “The gift. What do you think it is?” ʸ

“I don't know.” ᴹ

“Guess.” ʸ

“I don't want to guess. She said it's a surprise.” ᴹ

“*Guess*, Mau.” ʸ

He thinks about it.

“A boat,” he says. ᴹ

She stares at him.

“A *boat*?” ʸ

“She likes building things. She'd build a boat.” ᴹ

“Why would Elmesia give you a *boat*?” ʸ

“I don't know. You told me to guess.” ᴹ

She laughs so hard she has to put her face in the pillow.

* * *

DIO comes out in the middle of it.

She sits on the end of the mattress in her own body, in her own lopsided hat, and folds her legs under her.

“Can I have one?” she says. ᴰ

“A sweet?” says Yuka, guiltily. “We ate them. Sorry. I'll buy you one—” ʸ

“A hat,” says DIO. “For the island. An enormous one. I want to match.” ᴰ

Yuka looks at her.

“You've got a hat,” she says. ʸ

“I've got *a* hat,” says DIO. “I want *the* hat. The one you're both wearing. So when we're on the beach, there's three.” ᴰ

Yuka looks at Mau. Mau looks at Yuka.

“Three,” says Yuka slowly. ʸ

“Three,” DIO agrees. ᴰ

“Okay,” says Yuka. “Three.” ʸ

DIO beams. She takes her lopsided hat off and puts it in her lap and looks at it fondly, as if it's going to have to get used to being the second-best hat.

“And a boat,” she says, “if Elmesia gives him a boat. I want to go on the boat.” ᴰ

Yuka puts her face back in the pillow.

* * *

Later, DIO goes back in. The lamp is very low. Yuka is lying on her side facing him with her hand flat on his chest.

“Can I see the page?” he says. ᴹ

She goes still.

“Which page?” ʸ

“You know which page.” ᴹ

She doesn't answer for a long time.

“It's not nice,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“It's just a list.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Why do you want to see it?” ʸ

He thinks about it.

“Because you've been carrying it,” he says. “By yourself. For seven days. And you made me promise to tell you everything. And I have. And you've been writing it all down and not showing me. And I think it's heavy.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“Like the sweets,” he says. “You don't have to carry it in case.” ᴹ

* * *

She sits up.

She takes the notebook out of the middle jacket. She opens it to a page near the back, past the prices, past *Yuka: middle*, past the drawing of the tower. She doesn't give it to him. She holds it in her lap and reads it out loud, quietly, in the low lamplight.

“Day one,” she reads. “The box. You sat down on the stair.” ʸ

“Day two. The tail. Four ribs. Lung. Shoulder blade. Muscle down to the hip. Beru held it. The hip didn't close. You said *it's ugly*.” ʸ

“Day three. *Turned*, six times. The bandage, morning and night. Same. Edges hot.” ʸ

“Day four. The stair: ribs, closed. The shock: shoulder, out, closed. You put it back yourself. I made a noise and you didn't.” ʸ

“Day five. Nothing. The warm house. You slept all night.” ʸ

She stops on that one. Then goes on.

“Day six. *Turned*, four times. The stair. Makima said one hand.” ʸ

“Day seven. The water. The hip, stinging. *Turned*, nine times. You carried me the whole way. Nothing touched me.” ʸ

She closes the notebook.

* * *

He doesn't say anything.

He's lying on his back looking up at the roof of the tent. He's listening to her breathe.

He's heard all of it before. He was there. He said every *turned* himself. But he's never heard it like that, one after the other, in her voice. Seven days. The way someone who loves you writes down what happens to you.

“That's a lot of *turned*,” he says eventually. ᴹ

“That's a lot of *turned*,” she agrees. ʸ

“Is that why you didn't want me to see?” ᴹ

“No,” she says. ʸ

She opens the notebook again. To the same page. She takes the pencil.

“I didn't want you to see,” she says, “because I didn't know what to write at the bottom.” ʸ

She writes something. He hears the pencil move. Then she turns the notebook round and holds it so he can read it in the lamplight.

Under *Day seven*, in her neat small letters:

*He's still here. So am I.*

* * *

He reads it twice.

Then he takes the pencil from her, and the notebook, and under her line, in his own crooked letters, he writes:

*Both.*

She looks at it for a long time.

Then she closes the notebook and puts it back in the middle jacket, against her chest, and lies down, and puts her head on his shoulder and her hand on the line on his arm.

“Go to sleep,” she says. “It's my turn to watch.” ʸ

“You'll fall asleep.” ᴹ

“I won't.” ʸ

She does. Ten minutes later. He watches the door the rest of the night with her asleep on him and the empty sweet bag folded flat on the mattress beside them, and doesn't wake her for her turn, and in the morning, she's furious.
