# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 21 — The Garden

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S13E67
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day eight. Below the drowned level, a vast cavern where the floor is a nest: hundreds of small fast things that wake all at once. Too many directions for Mau to cover her from every side with one hand free. Yuka opens her Domain, Chimera Shadow Garden, around both of them: a closed barrier, a dark ground that takes their feet, her Ten Shadows and their duplicates placed through it. From outside nothing can see in. Inside, for the first time in the gate, Mau is the one being protected: he fights in her ground, on her terms, and nothing reaches either of them. When it's done and she closes it, the burnout is real: her technique won't answer and she's shaking and grey-faced. His techniques would still come through the contract, with the weight on him as always, but she won't add to what he's carrying. Mau carries her out of the cavern. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day eight. Outside, still B+370, early afternoon; Yuta has come down to the orchard and is sitting on the wall beside Maki.*

The cavern is quiet when they come into it.

That's what's wrong with it.

The stair from the beach goes down a long way, and at the bottom it opens out into a space so big the eyes can't find the far wall. A cavern. The floor is uneven, lumpy, grey, and it goes on and on into the flat light, and there's nothing on it. Nothing moving. Nothing in the air.

He stops at the bottom of the stair.

“Mau?” ʸ

“Wait.” ᴹ

The eyes go out across the floor. They read stone, and more stone, and—

The floor isn't stone.

* * *

“Nest,” says Noise, very quietly. ᴺ

The lumps. All of them. Every lump on the floor of the cavern, as far as the eyes can see. Each one a shell, curled tight, and in each one, something small and folded and asleep. Hundreds. Thousands. Packed so close together that the floor is made of them.

He reads one.

*[Lethal to the Contracted.]*

He reads the next.

*[Lethal to the Contracted.]*

All of them.

“Can we go round?” Yuka whispers. ʸ

“There isn't a round,” says Ciel. “The stair down is in the middle. The whole floor is like this.” ᶜ

“Can we go *over*?” ʸ

“Kaisel can't fly in here,” Ciel says. “The ceiling's too low at the edges and there's no room to turn. If he lands, he lands on them.” ᶜ

Yuka is quiet.

“Then we walk,” she says. “Quietly.” ʸ

They walk.

* * *

They get about a third of the way.

Step by step, between the shells, in the narrow places where the lumps don't quite touch. Beru goes first, wings folded tight, placing each foot with enormous care. Then Mau. Then Yuka, one step behind, her hand on the back of his shirt. Kamish stays in the shadow; he's too big.

Then one of them wakes.

He doesn't know why. Nobody stepped on it. Nobody touched it. It just uncurls, beside Beru's foot, a thing like a bundle of thin grey legs with a mouth in the middle, and it makes a sound.

A tiny sound. A click.

And every shell on the floor of the cavern clicks back.

* * *

They come up all at once.

It's like watching a field of grass stand up in a wind. The whole floor, as far as the eyes can see, uncurling, rising, legs unfolding, mouths opening. And every one of them turning toward the warm things in the middle.

Toward *her*.

He moves. Infinity over both of them. The katana out. Blue in his left hand, Shrine ready in the right. He knows how to do this. He's done it a hundred times: hold a line, cut what comes, let Beru take what gets close.

He reads the cavern and his stomach drops.

There's no line. They're coming from everywhere. Front, back, both sides, above, where some of them have run up the walls and across the low ceiling and are dropping. Hundreds of directions at once. And he's got one hand free for her, the way Makima said, and one hand isn't enough. Not for this. Not for every side at once.

Something drops from above, onto the edge of his Infinity, and the eyes say the next one will come through at the place he isn't looking.

“Mau,” says Yuka. ʸ

Her voice is very calm.

“Let me,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He knows what she means.

He's seen it before. Her hands coming together. Her shadow going *out*.

“It'll burn you out,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“After. You won't be able to—” ᴹ

“I know, Mau.” ʸ

Another thing hits the top of his Infinity. And another. They're stacking up on it, crawling over each other on the invisible curve over their heads like water on glass.

“You've carried me for eight days,” she says. “Let me carry you for ten minutes.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“Okay,” he says. ᴹ

She puts her hands together.

* * *

“*Domain Expansion*.” ʸ

Her shadow goes out.

Not *spreads*. Goes. All at once, from her feet, the way water goes out from a dropped stone, and where it goes the floor of the cavern stops being the floor of the cavern. It goes dark. Soft. It takes his feet, the way mud takes your feet, only it isn't mud; it's hers, and it *knows* him, and it holds him up.

“*Chimera Shadow Garden*.” ʸ

The barrier closes.

He feels it close over them like a lid. A dome. And on the other side of it, the cavern, and the thousands of things, and the walls and the low ceiling: gone. Not gone. *Outside*. He can feel them out there, crawling on the barrier, scratching at it. But they can't see in. Nothing out there can see in.

In here, it's hers.

* * *

He's never been inside it when it mattered.

He stands in the dark ground with the katana in his hand and watches her work.

The things that were already inside the barrier when it closed, a few dozen of them, caught between her shadow and the lid, turn toward her. And her shadows come up to meet them.

Not two dogs. Many. The white one and the black one, and then another white one and another black one, and another, duplicates rising out of the dark ground everywhere at once. The toad, huge, its tongue going out. Things he's seen her call one at a time, all of them, all at once, placed exactly where she wants them in the dark, as if she's laying out cups on a table.

He doesn't have to do anything.

That's the strange part. He keeps waiting to have to do something. To step in front. To cut. And every time something turns toward her, there's already a shadow there. And every time something turns toward *him*, there's a shadow there too.

She's protecting him.

He realises it the third time. A thing comes out of the dark at his back, from his blind side, and before he's turned a black dog has it by the middle and is gone with it into the ground.

She's not just fighting. She's covering *him*. The way he's covered her for eight days.

He stands there in her garden with the katana half-raised and feels something in his chest that he doesn't have a name for.

* * *

“Mau,” she says. “Help me with the ones on the lid.” ʸ

He looks up.

The barrier is thick with them, crawling on the outside, their weight pressing down. She's holding it. He can feel her holding it, through the contract: the strain of it, like holding a door shut against a crowd.

“Your Blue,” she says. “Through my ground. I'll open a hole. Just for a second. Put it through and pull them in, and I'll do the rest.” ʸ

“Your ground?” ᴹ

“It's mine,” she says. “It'll take yours if I tell it to.” ʸ

So he does it.

She opens a hole in the top of the dome, small, for one breath. He puts Blue through it, not small, not quiet, and it pulls. Everything on the outside of the lid near the hole goes in toward his fist like water down a drain, through the hole, into the garden.

Into her ground.

And her shadows are waiting.

* * *

They do it six times.

Every time, she opens a hole and he pulls, and the things come in, and her shadows take them. And every time, the weight on the lid gets less, and the clicking outside gets less, until on the sixth time, when he puts Blue through, almost nothing comes.

The cavern outside is quiet.

“Ciel?” he says. ᴹ

“A few,” says Ciel. “Scattered. Far away. They've stopped coming. They don't know where you went.” ᶜ

Yuka is standing in the middle of her garden with her hands still together. She's breathing very hard.

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

“Yuka—” ᴹ

“Okay,” she says again. “Closing.” ʸ

* * *

The garden goes.

The dark ground drains out from under his feet and the lid lifts and the cavern is there again, grey and lumpy and wide, and full of dead things. Thousands of them. A great ring of them all round the place where the barrier was, and inside the ring, nothing. Clean stone. Him. Her.

She sways.

He catches her before she's started falling.

* * *

The burnout is real.

He knew it would be. He's read about it, in the notes Ciel keeps; he's seen her after, white and shaking. But he's never held her through it. She's grey-faced in his arms, and cold, and shaking so hard her teeth are knocking, and when she tries to call her dog, nothing comes.

“It won't—” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“My shadow won't—” ʸ

“I know. It's the burnout. It'll come back.” ᴹ

“How *long*?” ʸ

“Ciel?” ᴹ

“A day,” says Ciel. “Maybe more. Her technique is closed until it settles. She can't open it. Not even a little.” ᶜ

Yuka closes her eyes.

“Your things,” she says. “Through the contract. I can still use yours.” ʸ

She reaches for his Blue, the smallest one, the one she threw on the first floor. It's there. It comes the way it always comes, and the weight of it lands where it always lands: on him.

She feels him take it.

The hip. The four lines of the tower stair, healed but still pulling. Eight days of one hand kept free. Everything he's already carrying, and now this on top, because she asked for it.

She lets go before it's finished forming.

“No,” she says. ʸ

“You can,” he says. “It's what it's for.” ᴹ

“Not today.” Her voice is very small. “You're carrying enough. I'm not putting more on you because I can't stand doing nothing.” ʸ

* * *

He picks her up.

Not on his back. In his arms. The way you carry someone who's asleep. She doesn't argue. She puts her face against his neck and her hand fisted in his shirt and shakes.

“I'm useless,” she says. ʸ

“You just killed a cavern.” ᴹ

“And now I'm *useless*. For a day. In a gate where one thing—” ʸ

“Nothing's touching you.” ᴹ

“You've only got one hand.” ʸ

He walks. Across the floor of the cavern, past the ring of dead things, toward the stair in the middle. Beru falls in on one side of him. Kamish rises out of his shadow on the other, too big for here and coming anyway, wings half-spread as a wall.

“I've got two,” he says. “Today. You're in both of them.” ᴹ

She doesn't say anything.

“You carried me for ten minutes,” he says. “I'll carry you for a day.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a long time. Then he feels her breathe out, slowly, against his neck, and the shaking eases a little.

“You were in my garden,” she says. ʸ

“I was.” ᴹ

“Did I do it right?” ʸ

He thinks about the black dog at his back. The shadows that were always there before he turned. The thing in his chest that he didn't have a name for.

“You covered me,” he says. “The whole time. Nothing got near me.” ᴹ

He feels her smile, against his neck. Very tired. Very small.

“Good,” she says. ʸ

And she falls asleep in his arms, halfway across the cavern, and he carries her down the stair into the next level without stopping.
