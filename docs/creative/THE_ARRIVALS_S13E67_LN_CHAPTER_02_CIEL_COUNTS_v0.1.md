# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 22 — Ciel Counts

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S13E67
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, night of day eight, in the tent on the first landing of the next level. Yuka wakes with Ten Shadows still in burnout; she can borrow Mau's cursed techniques and use the new mana channel, but both cost him, so she refuses to exhaust his reserves while his wound remains open. She's frightened of having lost her own reliable way to protect them for the next day. Mau makes the soup from the dried bag she bought on day three and feeds her when her hands shake too much. Then Ciel speaks to Yuka directly, through the link, for the first time about something other than numbers: she tells Yuka what counting is for. In the Trial she counted because counting was the only thing that proved time was passing and he was still in it; tonight she's counting Yuka's breaths for the same reason. Mau hears it all. Yuka asks Ciel to keep counting. DIO comes out and sits by the tent door so Beru can rest one wing. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, night of day eight. Outside, still B+370, mid-afternoon; Shoko has sat down on the grass in front of the oval, which she never does.*

She wakes up in the tent.

He sees her come back. Her eyes open, and for a moment she doesn't know where she is. Then she does. And then, very fast, she reaches for her shadow.

Nothing.

He watches it land on her. Watches her try again, harder, the way you'd try a door you know is locked because you can't believe it's locked. The dogs don't answer. That part of her technique is still burnt out.

She can feel the other routes: the familiar borrowed cursed techniques and the separate mana channel. She used that channel for one small spell on the way out of the cavern yesterday. Both still work. Both have a cost, and Mau is already carrying an injury that won't close.

She tests the smallest Blue just to be sure, feels the weight begin to settle on him, and lets it go before it forms. She doesn't even start a spell. It's a decision, not a failure of the contract.

She lies very still.

“How long was I asleep?” she says. ʸ

“The rest of the day.” ᴹ

“Where are we?” ʸ

“The first landing below the cavern. Nothing's near. Beru's outside. Kamish is across the door.” ᴹ

She doesn't say anything for a while.

“It's still closed,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

* * *

He makes the soup.

The bag of dried things she bought on the third day, the one she said was for soup, that he didn't understand. He understands now. He puts the pan on the little stove and the water in the pan and the dried things in the water, and they swell and soften and the tent fills with a smell that's almost like the kitchen at home.

She watches him from the mattress.

“You don't know how to make soup,” she says. ʸ

“I know how to put things in water.” ᴹ

“That's not soup.” ʸ

“It smells like soup.” ᴹ

She almost smiles.

He brings it to her in the cup. She sits up, slowly, and takes it in both hands, and her hands are shaking so badly the soup slops over the rim.

She looks at her hands.

He takes the cup back. He sits down beside her on the mattress. He holds the cup to her mouth.

She looks at him over the rim. Her face does something complicated.

“I can do it,” she says. ʸ

“I know you can.” ᴹ

“I'm not a *baby*.” ʸ

“After the Trial I couldn't hold a cup either,” he says. “I wasn't a baby.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long moment.

Then she drinks.

* * *

She's frightened.

She doesn't say it. She doesn't have to. He can feel it through the contract: not the sharp fear of the cavern, when everything woke at once. Something lower. Slower. Lying under everything like cold water.

“Tomorrow,” she says, when the cup is empty. ʸ

“Tomorrow you rest.” ᴹ

“We can't stay here.” ʸ

“We can stay a day.” ᴹ

“The *Warden*, Mau. And your hip. Every day we stay is another day you're—” ʸ

“Another day it's not getting worse,” he says. “Noise?” ᴹ

“It's not getting worse,” says Noise. “He's holding it. He can hold it a long time.” ᴺ

“And if something comes?” ʸ

“Then I'm here,” he says. “Both hands. And if we need a small spell, we'll choose it together. You aren't helpless just because we're being careful with what it costs.” ᴹ

She looks down at her own hands in her lap. Still shaking.

“I hate this,” she says, very quietly. “I know I can still cast your spells. I know I can borrow your techniques. I hate that my own dogs won't answer, and everything else I reach for makes you pay. I don't want to be something you have to stand in front of.” ʸ

He doesn't say anything.

“Is this what it's like for you?” she says. “When you're hurt. When Shoko won't let you do anything. When you're lying in the clinic and everyone's—” ʸ

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

She closes her eyes.

“It's horrible,” she says. ʸ

“It's horrible,” he agrees. ᴹ

* * *

Then Ciel speaks.

Not to him. To her. Through the link, the way Ciel has spoken to her every day since the contract: lengths in steps, readings, *it's true*, *nine breaths*, *it works*. Numbers. Ciel has only ever given her numbers.

This isn't a number.

“Yuka,” Ciel says. ᶜ

Yuka opens her eyes.

“Can I tell you something?” says Ciel. “About counting.” ᶜ

Mau goes still.

Yuka looks at him. He doesn't say anything. It isn't his.

“Yes,” she says. ʸ

* * *

“In the Trial,” Ciel says, “I counted.” ᶜ

Her voice is the same as always. Crisp. Even. But slower.

“Days. Hours, when I could. His breaths, when there wasn't anything else. You know that. Everyone knows that. *Ciel counted*. It's in the notes.” ᶜ

“Yes,” says Yuka. ʸ

“Nobody ever asked why,” says Ciel. ᶜ

Yuka waits.

“There wasn't a reason,” Ciel says. “Not a practical one. Knowing it was day forty didn't help him. Knowing it was the nine-thousandth breath that afternoon didn't feed him or close anything or open a door. I didn't count because it was useful.” ᶜ

A pause.

“I counted because it was the only thing that proved time was still passing,” Ciel says. “That we were still in it. That he was. Every number was one more than the one before. And as long as there was a next number, he was still there to have it.” ᶜ

* * *

Mau is looking at the tent wall.

He didn't know. He's never asked either. He's heard *Ciel counted* a hundred times, from Shoko, from Jin-Woo, from the notes, and he's never once thought to ask her why.

He feels Yuka's hand find his on the mattress.

“Ciel,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm talking to her,” says Ciel. “You can listen. That's the rule.” ᶜ

He shuts his mouth.

* * *

“I'm counting now,” Ciel says to Yuka. ᶜ

Yuka's hand tightens on his.

“Your breaths,” says Ciel. “Since you closed the garden. I started when you fell. I haven't stopped.” ᶜ

“Why?” says Yuka. Her voice is very small. ʸ

“The same reason,” says Ciel. “It's not useful. It doesn't open your technique. It doesn't make the burnout go faster. It just proves you're still here. One more than the one before.” ᶜ

A pause.

“It's a habit,” Ciel says. “I'm sorry if it's strange.” ᶜ

Yuka doesn't say anything for a long time.

Then she lies back down on the mattress, slowly, with her hand still in his. She looks up at the roof of the tent.

“What number am I on?” she says. ʸ

Ciel tells her.

It's a big number. Bigger than he expected. All those breaths, since the cavern, through the walk across the floor and down the stair and the long sleep and the soup. Ciel has been there for every one.

“Keep going,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I will,” says Ciel. ᶜ

“Don't stop. Even when it comes back. Even when I can do things again. Just—keep going. For a bit.” ʸ

“For as long as you like,” says Ciel. ᶜ

* * *

DIO comes out later.

She steps out of him in her own body and pushes the tent flap aside and goes and sits in the opening, at the edge, where Beru is standing. She looks up at him.

“You've been up for eight days,” she says. ᴰ

“I do not need to sleep,” says Beru. ᴮᵉ

“Fold one wing,” says DIO. “Just one. I'll watch that side.” ᴰ

Beru looks down at her. He looks at Mau, through the flap.

Mau nods.

Beru folds one wing. Slowly. The great shape of it settling against his side. And DIO sits on the edge of the tent with her lopsided hat in her lap and watches the dark on that side of the landing, very seriously, as if she's been doing it all her life.

* * *

Yuka falls asleep again near midnight.

He's sitting up against the tent pole with her head in his lap. Her hands have stopped shaking. Her shadow is still closed; he can feel the place where it should be, quiet and dark, like a room with the lamp out.

Somewhere at the back of him, very softly, so softly that only he can hear it, Ciel is still counting.

He listens for a while.

“Ciel,” he says, under his breath. ᴹ

The counting pauses.

“Thank you,” he says. “For then. I never said.” ᴹ

A long silence.

“You didn't have to,” says Ciel. ᶜ

“I'm saying it now.” ᴹ

Another silence. Longer.

“You're welcome,” says Ciel. ᶜ

And then, very quietly, the next number. And the one after that. And he sits in the dark with Yuka asleep in his lap and Ciel counting her breaths, one more than the one before, and lets it put him to sleep.
