# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 23 — Both Hands

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S13E68
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, day nine. A rest day on the landing while Yuka's technique is closed. Things come up the stair twice; Mau fights with both hands for the first time in the gate, Yuka on the mattress behind his Infinity, watching what Makima described. Nothing touches her and nothing he takes is for her, so it all closes. She sees what he looks like when he doesn't have to hold one hand back, and it frightens her a little and she says so. In the afternoon her shadow comes back: the white dog first, nosing out of the dark against her hand. She doesn't cry; she laughs and holds the dog. Mau buys her a light jacket of something the Shop calls warded hide; she refuses the heavy armour, they compromise. Evening: Noise feels the knot below get closer. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, day nine. Outside, still B+370, late afternoon; Gojo has started pacing, which Yuta says he has never seen him do.*

The first ones come up the stair an hour after she wakes.

He hears them before the eyes find them. A dry scraping on stone, a long way down. Many legs. Not the nest things: bigger, slower, three of them, climbing.

He stands up.

Yuka is sitting on the mattress with the thick blanket round her and the empty soup cup in her hands. She looks up at him. She reaches for her shadow, out of habit. Nothing.

“Stay there,” he says. ᴹ

“I wasn't going anywhere,” she says. “I *can't* go anywhere.” ʸ

He puts Infinity over the tent. Over her. Over the whole back half of the landing, wide and solid, the way he'd put it over the house if something came for it in the night.

Then he turns round.

* * *

He doesn't keep a hand back.

He notices it the way you notice you've put down something heavy you've been carrying so long you forgot it was there. There's nothing to keep a hand back *for*. She's behind the Infinity. She's not two steps behind him, moving, needing to be covered from a direction he hasn't thought of. She's sitting still in the one place nothing can reach.

So both hands.

The first thing comes over the lip of the stair, long and grey and armoured, and he doesn't cut it in a careful line. He opens Shrine the way it wants to be opened. Long. The way she said it feels: like it wants to be longer. He lets it be longer. The thing comes apart, and so does the stair under it for ten steps down.

The second one, he takes with Blue. Not small. The size Blue is when he isn't thinking about how big it is. It folds the second thing and the third one behind it into one shape and drops the shape off the side of the landing into the dark.

It takes about four seconds.

He stands at the top of the stair breathing evenly.

Then he turns round.

* * *

She's staring at him.

Through the Infinity. Sitting on the mattress with the blanket round her and the cup in her hands, staring.

“That was both,” she says. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“That's what it looks like. When you don't—” ʸ

She doesn't finish.

He comes back across the landing. He takes the Infinity down off the tent and sits on the edge of the mattress beside her.

“Makima said,” she says slowly. “*He's been fighting this whole gate with what he can spare.* I thought I understood. I didn't.” ʸ

She looks at the stair. At the ten steps that aren't there anymore.

“It frightened me a bit,” she says. “Not you. Just—seeing how much you've been holding back. For me. The whole time.” ʸ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“Is that okay to say?” ʸ

“You said I have to tell you when it's ugly,” he says. “You have to tell me too.” ᴹ

She thinks about that.

Then she leans against his shoulder.

“It frightened me a bit,” she says again, “and I'm very glad it's ours.” ʸ

* * *

The second time is around noon.

More of them. Six, and something bigger behind them that the eyes read as almost the size of the bell. They come up the broken stair and over the lip, and he puts Infinity over her and turns round and does it again. Both hands.

This time one of them gets a claw into his thigh before it dies. A clean deep gash, going for *him*, the warm thing in front.

“Turned,” he says, over his shoulder. ᴹ

“I saw,” she says. “Leg. The right. Is it—” ʸ

He opens the RCT. It closes. Clean. The way the stair cuts closed, and the shoulder, and the ribs.

“Closed,” he says. ᴹ

She lets out a breath.

“Write it down,” he says. ᴹ

She writes it down.

*Day nine. Leg (closed). He used both hands. Nothing touched me.*

And under it, smaller:

*I watched all of it this time.*

* * *

The dog comes back in the afternoon.

He doesn't see it happen. He's at the stove, heating water. He hears her make a sound behind him. Not a hurt sound. A sound like breath catching.

He turns round.

She's sitting very still on the mattress with her hand flat on the blanket beside her. And under her hand, coming up out of her shadow, slowly, nose first, the way a dog comes out from under a bed when it isn't sure it's allowed: the white one.

It noses at her palm.

She doesn't move. She doesn't breathe. The dog comes the rest of the way out, all of it, and stands on the mattress beside her, and pushes its head under her hand, and leans its whole weight against her side.

She puts both arms round it.

He waits for her to cry. He's ready for it. He's put the water down.

She laughs.

She laughs into the dog's neck, holding it, rocking a little, and the dog lets her, the way dogs do. Then the black one comes up too, out of the other side of her shadow, impatient, as if it's been waiting behind a door and someone finally opened it, and shoves its way in between her and the white one, and she laughs harder.

“It's back,” she says. ʸ

“It's back.” ᴹ

“Ciel, it's *back*—” ʸ

“I can see that,” says Ciel. And then, after a moment, a little softer: “I'm still counting.” ᶜ

Yuka goes quiet.

“Good,” she says, into the dog. “Keep going.” ʸ

* * *

He buys her armour in the evening.

He's been thinking about it since the cavern. He opens the Shop and goes to a page he's never visited, one she wouldn't have pointed at. *[Protection.]* Plates. Mail. Helmets. Things that cover you from head to foot.

He shows her.

She looks at the page. Then at him.

“No,” she says. ʸ

“Yuka.” ᴹ

“I can't move in that. I can't call the dogs in that. I'll fall over in the first room and something will step on me. No.” ʸ

“It's lethal, any hit, the box said—” ᴹ

“The box said *direct*,” she says. “If I'm in a tin can and something hits the tin can, is that direct?” ʸ

“Ciel?” he says. ᴹ

A pause.

“Yes,” says Ciel reluctantly. “It's direct. The rule doesn't care what's in the way. Armour won't stop it.” ᶜ

Yuka looks at him. He looks at the page.

“Something light, then,” he says. “Not for the rule. For everything else. For the stone chips and the falling things and the things that aren't trying.” ᴹ

She thinks about it.

“Light,” she says. “Something I can move in.” ʸ

He finds it. Near the bottom of the page. A jacket of something the Shop calls *[warded hide]*: thin, soft, the weight of cloth, and the eyes say it turns small things aside. He shows her.

“Middle?” she says. ʸ

“Middle,” he says. ᴹ

She puts it on over the middle jacket. It fits. She moves her arms. She calls the black dog out and in again to check.

“Okay,” she says. “That one.” ʸ

She looks at him.

“Thank you,” she says. “For trying to put me in a tin can.” ʸ

* * *

Late that night, Noise speaks.

They're lying on the mattress. She's asleep, almost, with the white dog curled against her back and her head on his shoulder. He's on watch.

“Mau,” says Noise. ᴺ

“Yeah.” ᴹ

“The knot,” says Noise. “The one the exit's tied to. I've been able to feel it the whole way down. Far off. Like something at the bottom of a well.” ᴺ

He waits.

“It's closer,” says Noise. “Today. A lot closer. We're near the bottom. A day. Maybe two.” ᴺ

He lies there in the dark.

Yuka isn't asleep. He feels her go still against him.

“The Warden,” she says. ʸ

“The Warden,” says Noise. ᴺ

She doesn't say anything else. She just finds his hand under the blanket and holds it, and the white dog shifts against her back, and somewhere at the back of him, very softly, Ciel goes on counting.
