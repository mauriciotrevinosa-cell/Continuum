# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 24 — The Last Night Above

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 24
**Source episode:** S13E68
**Continuity state:** Inside the Rulers' trial, still the night of day nine, after Noise says the knot is a day or two below. Neither of them sleeps. They talk in the dark: what they'll do if it's bad. Yuka asks Mau, straight, whether he'll open everything if he has to; he says yes, and that Makima would hold the weight. Yuka asks him to promise to come back from it, not to come back "in a way"; he promises what he can and tells her honestly what he can't. She tells him she'll stay two steps behind until she can't. DIO asks for a promise of her own: to go home so she can see her plant. Makima says good night through the link without asking; since the stair, it's something that doesn't need asking. Beru tells Yuka he has done this before, at the bottom of the tower, and his king came back. Toward morning, they sleep a little, both of them, with Beru and Kamish and DIO on watch. No intimate scene inside the gate.

*When: Inside the trial, the last hours of night on day nine. Outside, B+370, toward evening; nobody in the orchard has gone in for dinner.*

Neither of them sleeps.

They try. She lies with her head on his shoulder and the white dog against her back and her hand in his under the blanket, and he lies with his eyes on the roof of the tent, and after an hour she says, “You're not asleep.” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“Me neither.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

She shifts. She props herself up on one elbow so she can see his face in what's left of the lamplight.

“Can we talk about it?” she says. “Tomorrow. The bottom. If it's bad.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

“If it's bad,” she says, “what do you do?” ʸ

He thinks about it. Properly. The way he promised.

“I keep you behind me,” he says. “Two steps. Like always. I keep one hand free for you. I fight with the other.” ᴹ

“And if that's not enough?” ʸ

He doesn't answer right away.

“Makima said,” Yuka says. “There's a part of you that wants to open everything. And you keep it shut. With your own hands. For me.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“If one hand isn't enough,” she says, “will you open it?” ʸ

He looks at the roof of the tent.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

She doesn't flinch. He thought she might.

“And Makima holds it,” she says. ʸ

“Makima holds it,” he says. “For a little while. She said.” ᴹ

“And then?” ʸ

“And then I close it again.” ᴹ

“Can you?” ʸ

He could say *yes*. It would be easy. It's the kind of thing he would have said a year ago, without thinking, the way he'd have said *it's fine*.

“I don't know,” he says. ᴹ

She's quiet.

“I've never opened all of it,” he says. “Not all. Not at once. Not the way Makima means. In the duel, I opened a lot. In the north, I opened a lot. But I always kept something. A door. A hand on the door. I don't know what happens if I take the hand off.” ᴹ

He turns his head and looks at her.

“That's the truth,” he says. “That's all of it.” ᴹ

* * *

She looks at him for a long time.

“Then promise me something,” she says. ʸ

“If I can.” ᴹ

“If you open it,” she says, “come back. Not *back*. Not in a way. Not *mostly*. Come *back*. All of you. The one who buys the cheapest tent and sleeps sitting up and makes soup that's just things in water.” ʸ

He doesn't say anything.

“Promise,” she says. ʸ

He thinks about it. The truth. All of it.

“I'll promise to try,” he says. “With everything. I'll promise that if there's a way back, I'll find it. I'll promise not to go any further than I have to.” ᴹ

He swallows.

“I can't promise it works,” he says. “I don't lie to you anymore. So I can't promise that.” ᴹ

Her eyes are very bright in the low light.

“That's a better promise,” she says. “That's the real one.” ʸ

She lies back down with her head on his shoulder.

“I'll take the real one,” she says. ʸ

* * *

“And me,” she says, after a while. “Here's mine.” ʸ

“You don't have to—” ᴹ

“Two steps,” she says. “I'll stay two steps behind. The whole time. I'll do what you say. I'll get out of the way. I'll use the Exchange to get myself out instead of making you get in front.” ʸ

She's quiet for a moment.

“Until I can't,” she says. ʸ

He goes still.

“Yuka.” ᴹ

“Until I can't,” she says again. “That's *my* real one. I'm not going to lie to you either. If it's going to kill you, and I can stop it, I'm not going to stand two steps behind and watch. I'll try everything else first. I promise that. Everything. But if it's that or you—” ʸ

She doesn't finish.

He lies there in the dark with his hand in hers and doesn't say anything, because there isn't anything he can say to that. Not anything true. She's said the same thing he would say. Exactly the same thing.

“Okay,” he says finally. ᴹ

“Okay,” she says. ʸ

* * *

DIO comes out.

She sits on the end of the mattress in her own body, in the dark, with her lopsided hat in her lap. She's been listening; she's inside him, she can't help it. She doesn't pretend she wasn't.

“I want a promise too,” she says. ᴰ

Mau looks at her.

“Go home,” says DIO. “After. Both of you. With me. I want to see my plant.” ᴰ

She turns the hat over in her hands.

“I don't know if Ori's watering it right,” she says. “She probably is. But I want to see.” ᴰ

Yuka sits up.

“We're going home,” she says. “All of us. You'll see your plant.” ʸ

“Promise?” ᴰ

Yuka looks at Mau.

“The real one,” Mau says. “We'll try with everything.” ᴹ

DIO thinks about that.

“That's fine,” she says. “That's a good one.” ᴰ

She puts the hat on. She goes to sit in the tent opening beside Beru, with her back to them, watching the dark.

* * *

Makima says good night.

She doesn't ask. Two nights ago, on the stair, Yuka said good night to her first, and she said it back, and that seems to have made it something that doesn't need asking. Very quietly, down the link, from the back of him:

*Good night, Yuka. Good night, Mau.* ᴹᵏ

A pause.

*I'll be ready*, she says. *Whatever you open.* ᴹᵏ

Yuka closes her eyes.

“Thank you,” she says, out loud. ʸ

* * *

Beru is the last one.

Near morning, Yuka gets up. She pulls the thick blanket round her shoulders and goes and stands in the tent opening, beside DIO, beside Beru, looking down the broken stair into the dark where the knot is.

“Beru,” she says. ʸ

“My queen.” ᴮᵉ

“Have you done this before?” she says. “The night before the bottom?” ʸ

Beru is quiet for a moment.

“Yes,” he says. “In the tower. Many times. Every floor had a bottom. Every bottom had something waiting.” ᴮᵉ

“And?” ʸ

“And my king came back,” says Beru. “Every time. Sometimes in pieces. Sometimes I carried him. Sometimes he did not know my name for a day after. But he came back.” ᴮᵉ

He turns his great head to look down at her.

“And he did not have you then,” Beru says. “He had only me. I am very strong. But I am not you.” ᴮᵉ

Yuka looks up at him.

She doesn't say anything. She just reaches up and puts her hand flat on his armoured arm, the way she put her hand on Mau's back in the tent on the second night, on the one place that didn't hurt.

Beru goes very still.

“Thank you, my queen,” he says. ᴮᵉ

* * *

They sleep a little, after that.

Both of them. Not long. She comes back to the mattress and lies down against him with the white dog at her back, and he closes his eyes, and somewhere outside Beru and Kamish and DIO are watching the stair, and at the back of him Ciel is counting, and Noise is listening to the knot, and Makima is waiting.

He sleeps.

When he wakes, it's day ten, and she's already up, lacing his boots.
