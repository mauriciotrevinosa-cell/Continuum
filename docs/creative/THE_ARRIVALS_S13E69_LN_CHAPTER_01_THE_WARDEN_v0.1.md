# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 25 — The Warden

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 25
**Source episode:** S13E69

*When: Inside the trial, days ten and eleven. Outside, still B+370: the whole house is in the orchard now, and Shoko has had her bag packed for an hour.*

“Turned,” he says. ᴹ

They're walking down a long ramp below the landing where they spent the last night. He turned to look at something on the wall and the cut above his hip pulled.

“How bad?” ʸ

“It opened a bit. It's bleeding through.” ᴹ

She stops. She takes the roll of bandage out of the bag from the Shop. She doesn't make a face. She kneels behind him and lifts his shirt and puts a fresh pad over the old one and winds the bandage round twice, tight.

“Better?” ʸ

“Better.” ᴹ

She stands up and writes something in the notebook.

He doesn't ask what. He knows. She's been writing it since the second day. Ten days now. Every time he says *turned* or *it opened* or *that one hit*. A line for each. She read the whole page to him on the seventh night, and wrote *He's still here. So am I.* at the bottom, and he wrote *Both.* under it. Every line since goes under that.

* * *

The deep floors aren't a city anymore.

They're a throat. The gate narrows as it goes down: long sloping halls, round rooms with no corners, everything smooth and grey and faintly warm, like the inside of something alive. The things that live down here are fewer and worse. The eyes read them coming from three rooms away. Every reading says the same thing at the bottom.

*[Lethal to the Contracted.]*

He takes two more hits on the tenth day.

The first is a claw, raking across his forearm when a thing comes through a wall beside her; he gets his arm there and it goes in to the bone. The RCT closes it. Then, slowly, a line along the top opens again, thin, like a seam coming unstitched.

The second is a spine, shot from the far side of a round room at the place where she was standing a moment before he pulled her down. It goes through his thigh. The RCT pushes it out and closes the hole. Then, as he stands up, a small round mark on the front of his leg opens and stays open.

“Two more,” he tells her, that night. “The arm. The leg. Same as the back.” ᴹ

She writes them down.

She writes them under *Both*.

* * *

She's changed.

He notices it on the eleventh morning. She's still two steps behind and to the left. She still calls his Blue and sends her dogs and pulls him to her with the Exchange when something comes at her too fast. They still fight like one.

But she's watching him differently.

Not the way she watched him in the tent, holding the lamp. Not frightened. Something harder than that. Every time he steps in front of her, she watches where the thing hits him. Every time he says *turned*, she looks at the bandage before she looks at his face. She's started standing a little closer. A little less behind and a little more beside.

“Two steps,” he says, on the ramp down to the bottom level. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“You're at one.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

She doesn't move back.

* * *

The bottom floor is a stage.

That's what it looks like. A huge round hall, its floor flat and smooth as a lake, and around the edges, rising in rings, something like seats. Empty. All of them. Hundreds of rings of empty seats going up into a dark where the ceiling should be and isn't. No sky. Just *up*, forever, and nothing in it.

He stops at the edge of the floor.

He knows what this is. He felt it at the quarry. He felt it in the Trial, the long one, at the very end, in a room where something was watching that wasn't the Architect.

*Somebody's watching.*

“Noise,” he says quietly. ᴹ

“Yes,” Noise says. “Them. Not here. But looking.” ᴺ

Yuka's hand finds his.

In the middle of the floor, something stands up.

* * *

It was there all along.

That's the first wrong thing. The eyes swept the hall the second they came in, and found nothing, and now there's something in the middle of the floor, and the eyes are telling him it was always there. As if it's made of the hall. As if the floor stood up.

It's tall. Twice his height. Thin. A shape like a person drawn by someone who'd only had a person described to them: long arms, long legs, a head with no face on it at all. Smooth and grey, the same grey as the walls. In its right hand, a blade. Not a sword. Something longer, straighter, with an edge so fine the eyes can't find where it stops.

> **[The Warden.]**

That's all the box says. No level. No name. No line about what it is.

The eyes go into it, and for the first time since Gojo's eyes became his, they don't go all the way.

He can see its outside. He can see the blade, and the long limbs, and the way the weight sits in it. But underneath, where everything else he's ever looked at has had a shape, a flow, a *how*, there's nothing. Just more of the gate. Grey all the way down.

“I can't read it,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka's hand tightens.

“Ciel?” ʸ

“Neither can I,” Ciel says. “It's not hidden. It's just not *there* to read. It's the trial. It's the rule, standing up.” ᶜ

The Warden turns its faceless head.

Not toward him.

Toward her.

* * *

It moves.

He's never seen anything move like that. Not Sukuna. Not the Frost Monarch. It doesn't cross the floor so much as stop being where it was and start being where she is, and the blade is already coming down.

He's there.

He doesn't know how. He's just there, between, with the katana out of its shadow and up, and the blade comes down on the katana and the katana *holds*. The scale in the spine, the chitin in the guard: the blow slides off sideways the way Mai's hammer slid off, and goes into the floor beside them, and the floor splits in a long clean line as far as the first ring of seats.

He feels the shock of it all the way to his shoulder. His left shoulder. The new arm.

The cut above his hip tears open another inch.

“*Turned*,” he says, through his teeth. ᴹ

“I know,” she says. “I saw.” ʸ

* * *

It doesn't stop.

That's the shape of the next hour. It doesn't stop and it doesn't tire and it doesn't once look at him. Every stroke goes for her. Over his shoulder. Under his arm. Round his side. Low, high, from behind, from a direction there isn't any direction for. And every time, he's there. The katana up, or Infinity, or Blue pulling the blade off its line, or Authority grabbing the long arm and holding it for the half-second it takes him to move her.

And every time, it learns.

He can feel it learn. The first time he blocks with Infinity, the next stroke comes in a way that Infinity has to bend to stop. The first time he grabs its arm with Authority, the next stroke comes from the arm he isn't holding. It doesn't get faster. It gets *closer*. Every block teaches it a little more about how he blocks, and the next stroke uses it.

Yuka fights.

She does everything. She sends the dogs, both of them, and the Warden doesn't even look at them; they go through it like smoke through smoke. She calls his Blue and throws it and the Warden steps round it without looking. She calls the toad, the great one, to pull her out of the way, and the Warden's next stroke comes for the toad.

She calls Mahoraga.

He feels the wheel turn. He feels her reach for it, the great slow thing that adapts to everything, and he feels it come, and stand up behind her, huge, the wheel on its head turning once.

The Warden doesn't adapt. The Warden doesn't need to. It's not a technique. It's a rule.

The blade takes Mahoraga across the chest before the wheel can turn a second time, and the great shape is thrown back into her shadow like a coat flung through a door, and Yuka cries out as if she's been hit herself. She can still feel it in there. Whole. Hurt. Not coming back out today.

* * *

The third mark opens on his back.

Not the cut above his hip. A new one. The Warden's blade came round his shoulder for her throat, and he got his back there, and the edge went across his shoulder blades from one side to the other. The RCT closes it. And then, slowly, gently, the whole long line begins to open again.

He goes down on one knee.

He doesn't mean to. His legs just stop. Everything is open: the arm, the leg, the hip, the long line across his back. Not much. A little. Each of them a little. But all of them at once, all the time, bleeding the way Noise said they'd bleed, steady and slow, and every time he moves, every one of them pulls.

“Mau.” ʸ

He breathes.

He can still do this. He knows he can. He's done worse. He's done *much* worse. He's walked out of a Trial with his forearm broken twice over and two teeth in his pocket. He can stand up. He just needs a second. Just one.

The Warden turns toward her.

* * *

And Yuka steps in front of him.

He sees it happen. He's on one knee on the smooth grey floor with the katana across his thighs and his blood on the stone, and she steps past him. Not two steps behind. Not one. In front. Between him and the Warden, with her dogs on either side of her and his Infinity, the second one, the one he built inside himself for her, going up around her like glass.

She's shaking.

He can see her shaking. Her shoulders. Her hands. She knows what the box says. She knows what one hit means. She's read it every morning. She's counted his marks in the notebook, every one, under *Both*.

She doesn't move.

“No,” she says. To it. Out loud. Her voice is steady and very thin. “You don't get to keep doing that to him.” ʸ

The Warden lifts its blade.

* * *

“*Yuka*—” ᴹ

“You've done it for eleven days,” she says. She doesn't look back at him. “Every time. Every time it came for me, you were there, and it hit *you*. I counted. I've got them all written down.” ʸ

Her voice cracks.

“I'm not letting it do it again,” she says. “I'm *not*.” ʸ

The blade comes up above its faceless head. Higher. Higher than it's ever lifted it.

And somewhere at the back of him, very quietly, a voice he's learned to listen to says:

*Can I—* ᴹᵏ

It stops. Starts again, without the question.

It's important. And the rule is, if it's important, you don't ask.

*I'm holding the weight*, Makima says. *All of it. Whatever you open. Go.* ᴹᵏ
