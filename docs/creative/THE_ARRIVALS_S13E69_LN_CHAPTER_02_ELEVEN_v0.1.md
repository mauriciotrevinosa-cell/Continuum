*When: Inside the trial, day eleven. Outside, B+370, just before dusk; the oval in the orchard has started to shake.*

He takes his hand off the door.

That's what it feels like. He'll try to explain it afterward, to Yuka, to Shoko, to Gojo, and it's the only way he'll ever find to say it. There's a door in him. There always has been. Behind it is everything: all of it, everything he's taken and built and copied and been given and grown, everything Noise broke the ceiling to make room for. And he has always, every day, every fight, kept a hand on that door. Even in the duel. Even in the north.

He takes his hand off.

And the door doesn't open.

It *goes*.

* * *

The first thing he feels is Makima.

Not her voice. *Her*. At the back of him, where she lives, bound to his body by a contract she chose: she moves. She steps into the place where the door was, the place where everything is coming through all at once, and she stands in it, and she *holds*.

He feels the weight land on her.

It's enormous. He's never felt anything like it. Everything coming out of him at once, and the pull of it on a body that's still open in four places, the tearing that should be happening in his chest, in his spine, in the back of his skull where the eyes live: it isn't happening. It's going into her. She's taking it. All of it. Standing where the tearing is with her hands open and taking it.

*Go*, she says again, through her teeth. ᴹᵏ

He goes.

* * *

“Ten,” says Ciel. ᶜ

He's standing up. He doesn't remember deciding to. The cuts are open, all four, bleeding, and he's standing up anyway, with the katana in his hand and Yuka in front of him with her back to him and her hands up.

“That's ten,” says Ciel. “That's the duel. That's the most you've ever—” ᶜ

She stops.

“Eleven,” she says. ᶜ

Her voice has changed. He's never heard Ciel sound like that. Not frightened. Something past frightened.

“There isn't an eleven,” she says. “I built the scale. It stops at ten. *Mau*—” ᶜ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

The eyes go.

Not *out*. *Open*. The filter Ciel put on them a long time ago, so he could see without the seeing drowning him: it burns away. He feels it go, like paper in a fire, and everything comes in at once. The hall. The floor. The rings of empty seats going up forever. The dark where the ceiling isn't. The blood on the stone. Yuka's breath. The Warden's blade, still lifting.

And the Warden.

All the way down.

He can *read* it. For the first time. Not just its outside. Underneath, where there was nothing, where it was grey all the way down: there's a shape now. Not a body. A rule. A long, thin, perfectly straight line of intent running from the place where it stands up through the dark into the rings of seats, into the watching, into whoever is up there looking. *Protect the Contracted.* It's not a monster. It's a sentence, standing up. And sentences can be cut.

“Noise,” he says. ᴹ

“Eyes,” Noise says. “No drain.” ᴺ

“What?” ᴹ

Ciel answers, her voice tight with the effort of keeping up. “Your eyes always drew a little cursed energy. My filter managed both the flood of information and the cost. The filter has burned away. You are seeing more than ever, but the energy draw is zero. They are sustaining themselves.” ᶜ

Noise doesn't add an explanation. Mau feels it checking the reading a second time.

He doesn't have time to understand that.

The Warden's blade reaches the top of its arc.

* * *

It comes down.

It comes down the way nothing has come down at him in eleven days. Not fast. Not slow. *Absolute*. The highest stroke it's ever made, the one it's been learning toward since the first floor, every block he's made teaching it the shape of the one he can't make. And the eyes, wide open, read it all the way, and he knows exactly where it's going.

Her.

Through his Infinity, the second one, the one around her: it's learned that. Through her dogs: it's learned them. Through Mahoraga's shadow, still hurt, still in her: it's learned that too. A single line from the top of the dark to the top of her head.

She doesn't move. She's shaking and she doesn't move.

He goes past her.

* * *

He doesn't remember the step. He'll never remember it. One moment she's in front of him and the next she isn't, and he's in front of her, and his free hand, the one he kept free for eleven days, is on her shoulder pushing her down and back, and the blade—

The blade goes into him.

At the shoulder. The left one. The new arm, the weak one. It goes in at the shoulder and down through him at an angle, across his chest, across his ribs, across the place where the cut above his hip has been bleeding for nine days, and out the other side at the waist.

He feels it go all the way through.

He feels himself start to come apart.

* * *

And the RCT is already there.

He doesn't call it. He doesn't have to. It's been running in him for eleven days, holding four cuts from getting worse, and the eyes have stopped costing, and the door is open, and Makima is holding the weight, and for once there is *more than enough*. It doesn't wait for the blade to finish. It follows the blade *in*. Right behind the edge, a hair's width behind it, the whole length of the cut, putting him back together as fast as he's being taken apart.

The top half of him starts to slide.

It doesn't get anywhere.

By the time the blade comes out at his waist, the place it went in at his shoulder is already closed. By the time his body has understood that it was cut in two, it isn't anymore. He stands on the smooth grey floor with the Warden's blade sunk in the stone beside him and a long raw seam running from his left shoulder to his right hip, already pale, already a scar, already a line, and he's in one piece.

Behind him, on the floor where he pushed her, Yuka screams his name.

* * *

The Warden turns its faceless head.

It looks at him. For the first time in eleven days, it looks at *him*. Not past him. Not through him at her. At him.

He looks back. He can see all the way down it now. The long thin line of the rule, running up into the dark, into the watching.

He lifts his hands.

Blue in the left. Not small. Not quiet. Not the size of a careful fist on a careful floor with something to protect. The size Blue is when there's nothing holding it.

Red in the right.

He's made Purple before. Carefully, with a hand on the door, with Ciel counting and Noise steering and the whole of him braced for what it costs.

He doesn't brace.

He brings his hands together.

* * *

It doesn't make a sound.

That's what Yuka will tell him afterward. She was on the floor behind him, on her knees, with her hands over her mouth, and she watched it, and it didn't make a sound. Just a light, if you can call it light: the colour that isn't a colour, coming out of his hands in a line. Not at the Warden. *Through* it. Through the faceless head and the long thin body and the rule standing up inside it, and on, behind it, through the floor and the rings of empty seats and up, up, into the dark where the ceiling isn't. Into the watching.

The Warden doesn't fall.

It just stops being there. The way it started being there: as if the floor had stood up, and now the floor lies down again.

And the gate starts to come apart.

* * *

He feels the rule die.

It goes out of everything at once. The line at the bottom of every reading, *lethal to the Contracted*: gone. The knot Noise has been feeling for eleven days, the thing the exit was tied to: cut. And on his body, one by one, the four places that wouldn't close.

The arm.

The leg.

The long line across his back.

The cut above his hip, the first one, open since the second day: he feels the edges come together and stay together. For the first time in nine days, they stay.

* * *

The hall goes.

The rings of seats crumble upward into the dark. The smooth floor cracks in long lines like ice in spring. The dark overhead, where the ceiling wasn't, where the watching was, *opens*, and through it, far up, impossibly far, there's light. Not flat grey gate light. Something warm. Something going down. Late. Gold, if he could see gold. The orchard, at the end of the day.

And the box opens in front of him.

> **[Trial: THE STABILIZER.]**
>
> **[Complete.]**

That's all.

* * *

He closes the door.

It's the hardest thing he's ever done. Harder than opening it. Everything is still pouring out of him, through the place where the door was, into Makima's hands, and he has to reach back into all of it and find the door, wherever it went, and put his hand on it, and *push*.

It doesn't want to close.

He pushes.

He thinks of a tent with a hole in it. A mattress that cost eighty. Soup that's just things in water. A notebook page: *He's still here. So am I.* And under it, in his own crooked letters: *Both.*

The door closes.

He feels it latch.

* * *

Makima is barely there.

He reaches for her at once, at the back of himself, and for a terrible moment there's almost nothing: a thinness, a quiet, like a lamp turned all the way down. He's heard her say it, on the porch: when his body ends, she ends. He didn't think about what happens if she stands where everything comes through and holds it.

“Makima,” he says. ᴹ

Nothing.

“*Makima*.” ᴹ

And then, very faint, very tired, from a long way back:

*I held it.* ᴹᵏ

A breath.

*I said I would. I held it.* ᴹᵏ

He puts his hand over his chest, where she is, for a moment.

“You held it,” he says. ᴹ

*Tell Yuka*, she says. *I told her I would tell her first. There wasn't time. Tell her I'm sorry I didn't ask.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

He turns round.

Yuka is on her knees on the cracking floor where he pushed her, a few steps behind him. Two steps, almost. Her hands are over her mouth. Her dogs are pressed against her sides. Her face is white, and wet, and doesn't know what to do, and she's staring at him: at the long pale line running from his shoulder to his hip across the front of him, through the cut-open shirt. At him standing. In one piece.

He kneels down in front of her.

She doesn't say anything. She can't. She takes her hands away from her mouth and puts them on his face, both of them, very hard, as if she's checking he's solid.

“I'm here,” he says. ᴹ

Her hands don't move.

“All of me,” he says. “I came back. The real one.” ᴹ

She still doesn't say anything.

Above them, the dark opens all the way. The warm light comes down. And the floor of the trial, the last of it, gives way under them like a held breath let go—

And they're in the orchard.
