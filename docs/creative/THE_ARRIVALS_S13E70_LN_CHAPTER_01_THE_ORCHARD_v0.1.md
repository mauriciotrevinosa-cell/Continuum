# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 27 — The Orchard

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S13E70
**Continuity state:** B+370, dusk. Eleven days inside; less than one day outside. They come out on the orchard grass between the two old trees, the oval gone dark behind them. Mau's first thought and first words are about Yuka; he checks her before anyone can reach him. Yuka can't speak. As he walks up from the orchard toward the house, what's left of the raw seam across his body pales into a scar under everyone's eyes. Shoko gets to him first and sees the shoulder-to-hip line through the cut shirt, and has to sit down on the grass. In the clinic she checks everything twice and keeps her hand flat on his chest longer than any check needs, and he lets her; he tells her he needed her today, not to fix anything, to see he was okay. Gojo comes in and sits on the end of the bed, and Mau doesn't tell him to go away. Mau gives Yuka Makima's message. That night Yuka finally cries, in their bed, and Mau holds her until she stops; nothing else happens and nothing else is wanted.

*When: B+370, dusk. The same day they went in, a little before dinner. Eleven days, for them.*

The grass is wet.

That's the first thing. He's lying on his back on wet grass, and it's cold, and it smells of the orchard: the small hard pears, the old trees, the earth. Above him, the sky. A real one. Going dark at the edges.

The second thing is that his hand is empty.

He's up before he's thought about it. On his knees. Turning.

She's there. A step away, on the grass, on her hands and knees, with her dogs on either side of her and her hair across her face. The oval of light between the two old trees is behind her, and it's dark. Not humming. Just two trees and a space between them.

“Yuka,” he says. ᴹ

She lifts her head.

He reaches her. He doesn't remember crossing the step. He has her face in his hands, and he's looking at it, all of it, the only face in the world he sees in colour, and it's whole. No mark. No cut. Her hands, whole. Her arms. He runs his hands down them, both, all the way to the fingers, and turns them over, and looks at the palms, and there's nothing. Not a scratch. Not one.

“Are you hurt?” he says. “Anywhere. Anything. Did anything—” ᴹ

She stares at him.

“*Yuka*. Did anything touch you?” ᴹ

She shakes her head. Once.

He lets out a breath that comes from the bottom of his feet.

* * *

She can't speak.

He sees her try. Her mouth opens, and nothing comes. She's looking at him: at his face, at his chest, at the long line through the cut shirt from his left shoulder down across him to his right hip. Raw still. Pink, people will say later; he won't see it. Her eyes go along it and back and along it again, and her hands come up and stop an inch from it, not touching, as if touching it might open it.

“It's closed,” he says. “It's closed, Yuka. Look. It's not going to open.” ᴹ

She looks.

She still can't speak.

He stands up. He pulls her up with him. She comes up and stands against him with her face in his chest, and her hands fisted in what's left of his shirt, and doesn't make a sound.

And then he hears the house.

* * *

They're all there.

Not *all*. Most. He'll find out later who was where, how long, who wouldn't go in for lunch. But now, in the dusk, on the orchard path, they're just there. A crowd of shapes in the grey, all of them very still, all of them staring at the space between the two old trees and the two people standing in it.

Ori is at the front.

She's holding the basket she dropped this morning. Someone must have picked it up and given it back to her. She's been holding it all day.

“Dad,” she says. ᴼ

It isn't loud. It goes across the orchard like a stone across water.

He lifts his hand.

And everything moves at once.

* * *

He walks.

That's the thing he'll remember about it. He doesn't get carried. Nobody makes him sit. He walks up the orchard path toward the house with Yuka under his arm and her face in his side, and the house parts in front of him and closes behind him, and everyone is talking at once and he can't hear any of it.

And as he walks, he feels it finish.

The RCT. Still running. It hasn't stopped since the bottom of the gate. And now, out here, with the door closed and the eyes still costing nothing and nothing left open to hold, it goes over everything one last time. The long line across his front, going from raw to pale under the eyes of everyone on the path. The four places that wouldn't close, closed now, going the same way. The old ache in the left shoulder where the bell threw him. The tiredness in his legs from the water.

By the time he reaches the porch steps, there's nothing left to heal.

Just scars. Lines. A long one across his front, from shoulder to hip, that will never go.

* * *

Shoko gets to him at the steps.

She comes down off the porch the way she never does: fast, her bag in her hand, and stops in front of him.

She looks at the line.

Through the cut shirt. From the left shoulder, down, across the ribs, across the stomach, to the right hip. She looks at it the way she looks at things in the clinic: very still, all of her going into her eyes. He watches her understand what it is. What made it. What it means that it goes all the way across.

Her bag drops on the step.

She sits down.

Not on the porch. On the grass, at the foot of the steps, all at once, as if her legs have simply stopped. She sits on the grass in the dusk with her hand pressed flat over her own heart and doesn't say anything.

“Shoko,” he says. ᴹ

She holds up her other hand. *Wait.*

He waits.

Somebody, Gojo maybe, says something. Shoko doesn't look round. She sits there with her hand on her heart, breathing, slowly, the way she tells people to breathe when they've just been told something.

“Through,” she says finally. ˢʰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“All the way through.” ˢʰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And you walked up the path.” ˢʰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She closes her eyes.

“I'm going to need a minute,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

* * *

She takes him to the clinic anyway.

She checks everything. Twice. The line across his front, every inch of it, with her hands and her eyes and her own technique, slowly, the way she'd read a letter she couldn't believe. The four closed places. The shoulder. His eyes, for a long time, with a small light, and she frowns at whatever she sees there and doesn't say what it is.

Yuka is in the chair by the bed. She hasn't let go of his hand. She still hasn't said a word.

When Shoko is done, she doesn't step back.

She stands beside the bed with her hand flat on his chest, on the line, over his heart. She's finished checking. There's nothing there to check. She just leaves her hand there.

He doesn't move.

He looks up at her. At her face, which is very tired, and very still, and has the look on it he's seen once or twice: in the clinic after the duel. After the Trial. The look of someone who sat by a bed for a long time.

“You're not hurt,” she says. ˢʰ

It isn't a question. It's almost a complaint.

“No.” ᴹ

“You healed it yourself. All of it. Walking.” ˢʰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She nods slowly. Her hand doesn't move.

“Good,” she says. “That's good. That's very—” ˢʰ

She stops.

He sees it. He sees it the way he sees things now, with the eyes that don't cost anything, everything in, nothing filtered. Under the relief. Something else. Something small and sad and quiet that she'd never say.

*You didn't need me.*

He puts his hand over hers. On his chest.

“Shoko,” he says. ᴹ

She looks down at him.

“I needed you,” he says. “Today. When I came out. I didn't need you to fix anything. I needed you to see I was okay.” ᴹ

Shoko looks down at him.

Her face does something he's never seen it do.

“You absolute idiot,” she says. ˢʰ

Her voice cracks in the middle.

She doesn't take her hand away. She leans down instead, and puts her forehead against the top of his head, briefly, the way you'd do it to a child who's come home late, and stays there for a moment.

Then she straightens up and wipes her eyes with the back of her wrist, fiercely, and picks up her clipboard.

“You're on rest,” she says. “Three days. Minimum. I don't care if you can heal yourself. I don't care if you can heal *Gojo*. You're on rest. In bed. And she's on rest too.” ˢʰ

She points the clipboard at Yuka.

“Both of you,” she says. ˢʰ

* * *

Gojo comes in after.

He doesn't knock. He never knocks. He comes in and sits down on the end of the bed, uninvited, the way he's done a hundred times, and puts his chin in his hand, and looks at Mau through the blindfold.

Mau waits for himself to say *go away*.

He doesn't.

Gojo looks at him for a long time. At the line. At his eyes.

“Huh,” he says. ᴳ

That's all. *Huh.*

“What?” says Mau. ᴹ

“Your eyes,” says Gojo. ᴳ

He pulls the blindfold down, just a little, so one of his own shows over the top. It's very bright. It's looking at Mau's.

“They're not costing you anything,” Gojo says. “Are they.” ᴳ

“No.” ᴹ

“Since when?” ᴳ

“Today. The bottom of the gate. Noise says they're paying for themselves.” ᴹ

Gojo is quiet for a moment.

“Took me a while to get there,” he says. “Years.” ᴳ

He pulls the blindfold back up.

“We're going to have a talk,” he says. “When Shoko lets you out of bed. About what you can leave on all the time now. You've been switching things on and off like a kid with a light. You don't have to anymore.” ᴳ

He stands up.

At the door, he stops.

“Good job,” he says, without turning round. “Getting her out.” ᴳ

And he goes, and Mau lies there and realises he didn't once tell Gojo to go away, and doesn't mind.

* * *

He gives Yuka the message on the way upstairs.

She's walking beside him, under his arm, still silent. Shoko let them go at dark, on the condition that they go straight to bed and stay there. Beru is behind them on the stair. DIO went to the Inn the moment they came out of the clinic, to see her plant.

“Makima asked me to tell you something,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks up at him.

“She held it,” he says. “Everything. The whole time. She nearly didn't come back from holding it. And she said—” ᴹ

He stops on the stair.

“She said to tell you she's sorry she didn't ask,” he says. “She told you she'd tell you first, if there was time. And there wasn't. And she's sorry.” ᴹ

Yuka stands very still on the stair.

Then she turns her head, toward the back of him, where Makima is, and says, out loud, in a voice that's barely there, the first words she's said since the gate:

“Thank you.” ʸ

From the back of him, very faint: *You're welcome.* ᴹᵏ

* * *

She cries that night.

He knew she would. He's been waiting for it since the orchard. Since before: since the bottom of the gate, since the blade, since she screamed his name on the cracking floor. She didn't cry in the gate. Not once. Not for eleven days. Not when he was opened on the second day, not in the cavern, not when the Warden cut Mahoraga, not on the last night.

She cries now.

They're in their own bed. In their own room. Ori's *welcome home* note is still on the bedside table, and the chocolate wrapper from the red gate, and there's a real roof, and a real window, and outside it, the real sky, completely dark. She's lying against him with her face in his chest and her hand on the long line across his front, flat, and she starts to shake.

It isn't quiet. It's eleven days. All of it at once.

He holds her.

He doesn't say anything. There isn't anything to say. He holds her with both arms, both hands, nothing kept back, and lets her cry into the line across his chest until his shirt is soaked through, and her breath is coming in long broken pieces, and then slower, and then slower.

At some point she says, into his chest, “I watched it go through you.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I *watched* it.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“All the way through, Mau, I watched you come apart and I couldn't—” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

He holds her tighter.

“I came back,” he says. “The real one. I'm here.” ᴹ

She cries harder.

And then, a long time later, less. And then she's just breathing against him, slow and uneven, with her hand still on the line.

“Both,” she says, very quietly, into his chest. ʸ

“Both,” he says. ᴹ

She falls asleep like that. He doesn't. He lies awake in the dark with her asleep on him and her hand on his scar, listening to the house: someone on the stairs, someone in the kitchen, Beru's soft *good night, my king, good night, my queen* outside the door.

He doesn't need to watch anything.

He watches her anyway, until morning.
