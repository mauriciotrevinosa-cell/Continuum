# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 29 — A Long List

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 29
**Source episode:** S13E71
**Continuity state:** B+374, the first morning off rest. Mau keeps his promise and goes to the clinic with nothing wrong. Yuka comes; Ori follows. Ieiri is formalised: Shoko's clinic chart for Mau has only ever said "Mau", and she hands him the pen. He writes "Mau Ieiri" on the line, the thing Shoko once said would look "a little less lonely on the page". Yuka tries hers out loud ("Yuka Okkotsu Ieiri… Yuka Ieiri Okkotsu"), laughs, and says the decision is still pending; she wants it to be something they both pick, as she said before, and it's near the top. Ori asks if she can have it too. Mau looks at Shoko and tells her she has a granddaughter; they both laugh. Shoko asks him to explain who's who, then, if she's a grandmother now. Mau says the house is a very long list. They start it anyway, on the back of the chart.

*When: B+374, morning. Shoko's three days are over. Mau walks to the clinic before breakfast, the way she asked: with nothing wrong.*

He knocks on the clinic door.

He's never knocked on the clinic door. He's been carried through it, and walked through it bleeding. He's never stood outside it with nothing wrong and knocked.

“It's open,” Shoko calls. ˢʰ

He goes in.

She's at the desk by the window with her tea and her cigarette, not lit. She looks up. She looks at him: whole, upright, the shirt buttoned over the line across his chest, nothing in his hands.

“What's wrong?” she says. ˢʰ

“Nothing.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a moment longer.

“You said to come when there's nothing wrong,” he says. “Especially then.” ᴹ

Shoko puts her cup down.

“So I did,” she says. ˢʰ

* * *

Yuka comes in behind him. She didn't ask. She just came. And behind Yuka, a moment later, because she noticed them leave the house and notices everything, Ori.

“Is he hurt?” Ori says from the doorway, at once. ᴼ

“No,” say Mau and Shoko and Yuka together. ᴹ

Ori looks at the three of them.

“Then why is everyone in the clinic?” she says. ᴼ

Nobody has a good answer. Shoko starts to laugh.

* * *

He sits on the edge of the bed by the window. The one he's sat on more than any other bed in the house. Yuka sits beside him. Ori sits on the floor with her back against Yuka's legs.

Shoko gets up from the desk. She goes to the cabinet in the corner. She takes something out.

His chart.

He's seen it before. A thick sheaf of paper in a card folder, held together with string, years of it. Everything that's ever happened to him in this house, written down in her neat hard hand. The duel. The Trial. The arm she broke twice. The back that healed crooked before anyone was there to make it heal straight.

She opens it to the front page.

At the top, there's a line. A label printed beside it, from the old forms she brought from her world, the ones from a school with a long name: *NAME*.

And on the line, in her handwriting, one word.

*Mau.*

* * *

She holds it out to him. And a pen.

He looks at it. At the one word, alone on the line.

He remembers the clinic, before the island. Shoko at the window, with her reflection in the glass. *What would it be like to see it written down? Same as on the forms at school, I suppose. It'd look a little less lonely on the page.*

He takes the pen.

He looks at Yuka. She doesn't say anything. She nods.

He writes it. On the line. After the word that's always been there by itself. In his own crooked letters, which are nothing like Shoko's, which look a little ridiculous beside hers.

*Ieiri.*

* * *

Shoko doesn't say anything.

She takes the chart back. She looks at the line. *Mau Ieiri*, half in her handwriting and half in his.

She looks at it for a long time.

Then she turns round and goes to the window, the way she did before the island, and stands with her back to the room, very interested in the window. He can see her reflection in the glass. He can see it trying to get its face under control, and not managing, and trying again.

“It looks less lonely,” she says to the glass. ˢʰ

Her voice isn't quite steady.

“Your handwriting is appalling,” she adds. ˢʰ

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm going to have to look at that every time you get yourself half killed.” ˢʰ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Which is constantly.” ˢʰ

“I know.” ᴹ

She doesn't turn round. But he sees her reflection smile.

* * *

“Yuka Okkotsu Ieiri,” says Yuka. ʸ

They all look at her.

She's sitting on the bed with her chin in her hand, looking at the ceiling, trying it out.

“Yuka Ieiri Okkotsu,” she says. ʸ

She frowns.

“Yuka Okkotsu Ieiri,” she says again, slower. “Yuka Ieiri Okkotsu. Okkotsu Ieiri. Ieiri Okkotsu.” ʸ

She starts to laugh.

“They both sound like a *law firm*,” she says. ʸ

Shoko, at the window, makes a sound.

“So which?” says Mau. ᴹ

“I don't know,” says Yuka, still laughing. “Pending. The decision is pending.” ʸ

She leans against his shoulder.

“I said I wanted it to be something we both picked,” she says. “Remember? It's near the top. It's *very* near the top. It's the top.” ʸ

“But pending.” ᴹ

“But pending,” she agrees. “I'm not going to pick my name in a clinic at seven in the morning in a jacket I've been wearing for three days.” ʸ

“That's fair,” says Shoko, to the window. ˢʰ

* * *

Ori has been very quiet.

She's sitting on the floor with her back against Yuka's legs, looking at the chart in Shoko's hand. At the line. At the two words.

“Can I have it too?” she says. ᴼ

The room goes still.

Shoko turns round from the window.

Ori looks up at Mau. Not at Shoko. At him.

“If you're Ieiri,” she says, “and I'm yours. Then can I be Ieiri too? Ori Ieiri. If Shoko says.” ᴼ

Mau doesn't say anything. He can't.

He looks at Shoko.

Shoko is standing by the window with the chart in her hands and a face like someone who's just been hit very gently with something enormous.

“Shoko,” Mau says. ᴹ

She looks at him.

“You've got a granddaughter,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

For a second, nobody moves.

Then Shoko laughs.

Not the laugh from before the island, with her head tipped back. Something else. Something that starts as a laugh and goes somewhere in the middle and comes back out as a laugh again, and she puts her hand over her mouth, and her eyes are wet, and she's laughing.

And Mau's laughing too. He doesn't know why. It's not funny. It's the least funny thing that's ever happened in this clinic. He's laughing anyway, and so is Shoko, both of them, helplessly, and Yuka has her face in his shoulder, and Ori is looking from one of them to the other with enormous seriousness.

“Is that a yes?” Ori says. ᴼ

“*Yes*,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

She crouches down. Right there, on the clinic floor, in front of Ori, so their faces are level.

“Yes,” she says again, more quietly. “Ori Ieiri. If you want it. It's yours.” ˢʰ

Ori thinks about that very carefully.

“Okay,” she says. ᴼ

And she puts her arms round Shoko's neck, briefly, the way she does to people she's decided about, and lets go.

* * *

Shoko stays crouching for a moment after.

Then she stands up, slowly, and sits down in her chair at the desk, and puts the chart in front of her, and looks at Mau across it.

“Right,” she says. “If I'm a *grandmother* now—” ˢʰ

She says the word as if it might bite her.

“—then you're going to explain it to me,” she says. “All of it. Who's who. Who's what to whom. Because I've been patching this house together for as long as there's been a house, and I have *never* known who's related to who, and if I'm in it now, I want to know.” ˢʰ

Mau looks at her.

“It's a very long list,” he says. ᴹ

“I've got time,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“Shoko. It's a *very* long list.” ᴹ

“I've got all morning.” ˢʰ

* * *

So they start.

On the back of the chart. Shoko turns it over and takes the pen and says *go*, and Mau starts.

*Mau Ieiri.* That's him.

*Yuka.* Wife. Name pending.

*Ori Ieiri.* Daughter. Granddaughter of Shoko.

Shoko writes it down. Then stops.

“Yuka's grandparents,” she says, looking up. “Are Maki and Yuta.” ˢʰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Who live in this house.” ˢʰ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And Gojo thinks he's your father.” ˢʰ

“He *decided* he's my father,” says Mau. “Nobody asked him.” ᴹ

Shoko puts the pen down. She looks at the chart.

“So if I'm your mother,” she says slowly, “and Gojo's decided he's your father—” ˢʰ

She stops.

Yuka, beside him, starts to laugh.

“Oh no,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“Oh no,” Yuka agrees, delighted. ʸ

“I'm not writing that down,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“You have to write it down,” says Yuka. “It's the list.” ʸ

“I am *not*—” ˢʰ

“Frieren,” says Mau. “Write Frieren next. She's family.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him with deep gratitude and writes *Frieren*.

“And Fern,” he says. “And Stark. And Jin-Woo, and his whole family, and Rimuru, and Shizue, and Milim, and Beru—” ᴹ

“Is Beru *family*?” ˢʰ

“Beru is very family,” says Yuka. ʸ

Shoko writes *Beru*.

* * *

They run out of room on the back of the chart before they're halfway.

Shoko gets another sheet. Then another. Gojo comes past the window at some point, sees them all in the clinic, and comes in, and sees the list, and reads it upside down from the doorway, and his whole face lights up.

“Am I on it?” he says. ᴳ

“No,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

“I'm on it,” says Gojo, coming round the desk to look. “There I am. Next to you. Very close to you.” ᴳ

“That's *Maki*.” ˢʰ

“Next to Maki, then,” says Gojo, undeterred. “Under you. Write me under you.” ᴳ

“Get out of my clinic,” says Shoko. ˢʰ

He doesn't. He pulls up a chair.

* * *

By lunch there are five sheets.

Shoko has stopped trying to draw lines between the names. There are too many lines. Everyone is something to someone, and some people are three things to three people, and Rimuru turns out to be connected to almost everyone by some route or other, and Ori has insisted on being put next to Frieren *and* next to Shoko, which can't be done on a flat page, so Shoko has drawn an arrow.

Shoko sits back and looks at it.

“You weren't joking,” she says. ˢʰ

“I told you,” says Mau. “It's a very long list.” ᴹ

She looks at the top of the first sheet. At the chart, underneath, where the front page says *Mau Ieiri* in two kinds of handwriting.

Then at the five sheets of names on top of it.

“It used to be one word,” she says quietly. ˢʰ

He doesn't say anything.

She squares the sheets. She puts them inside the chart, in front of everything else, in front of the duel and the Trial and every broken thing. She ties the string.

“There,” she says. “Now it's not lonely at all.” ˢʰ
