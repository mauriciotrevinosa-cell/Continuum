# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 30 — Do Not Bother Us

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 30
**Source episode:** S13E71
**Continuity state:** B+376–377. The final island list, finished on the porch by Mau and Yuka (the names are not shown here: the creator will confirm who goes). Mau asks Elmesia first, by letter, whether he can bring it to her himself; she answers "come". He uses the Exchange to the soldier in her shadow, and Yuka comes through after him with her own. Elmesia reads the list, approves it, and confirms that she has arranged hospitality for the household. The separate gift she promised Mau remains a secret until the island. She gives them a sealed itinerary for the house and tells them not to open it. That evening Yuka reads the house her list of things it must not do on the island; every item is a version of "do not bother us", ending with: if you bother us I'll tell Elmesia not to let you come back. Elmesia's note, read aloud after it: "It's true." The house protests and agrees. The itinerary stays a mystery to Mau and Yuka. A playful, affectionate evening kiss and the couple's quiet afterward are complete.

*When: B+376, morning to B+377, evening. Six days after the orchard. Mai's workshop has been loud since sunrise every day.*

The list is on the porch table.

It's been on the porch table, in one form or another, since before the north. Since Elmesia's first letter said *bring the whole family next time if you must*, and the second asked for names, and Yuka flew it to her on Kaisel. It's been crossed out and written again. It's had names added in Ori's handwriting and taken out in Yuka's. It's had a coffee ring on it from Gojo, who says the ring is not his.

This morning, it's finished.

Yuka writes the last name. She puts the pencil down. She sits back in his lap and looks at it.

“That's it,” she says. ʸ

He looks at it over her shoulder.

“That's it,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't send it the way they sent the others.

He could. He could fold it into a letter and give it to the bird. But he's been thinking, since the gate, about the soldier in Elmesia's shadow, and the nine breaths it takes to get there, and the fact that he's never once used it for anything but an emergency.

“I could take it to her,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka turns round in his lap.

“Now?” ʸ

“If she says yes. I'll ask first.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“You're going to *ask*,” she says. “Before you appear in her shadow.” ʸ

“She's a queen. It's her city. I'm not just going to come out of her floor.” ᴹ

Yuka starts to smile.

“Who are you,” she says, “and what have you done with my husband?” ʸ

* * *

He writes the note himself.

It takes him three tries. Yuka watches, and doesn't help, and laughs at the second one. In the end it's short:

*Elmesia —*

*The list is finished. May I bring it to you myself, through the soldier in your shadow? Yuka would come with me. If now isn't a good time, the bird can bring it instead.*

*Mau.*

He gives it to the bird. The bird looks at him with one eye, as if it's never been asked to carry anything so polite, and goes.

* * *

The answer comes back before lunch.

One word, in Elmesia's long elegant hand, in the middle of an otherwise empty sheet:

*Come.*

And under it, smaller:

*And next time, don't ask. You saved my city. You may come out of my floor whenever you like.*

Yuka reads it over his shoulder and laughs out loud.

* * *

He goes first.

He stands in the yard with the list folded in his pocket and his hand in hers, and closes his eyes, and finds the soldier, very far away, in a shadow in a city he's only seen once, with a wall around it and gardens inside.

“Nine breaths,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” she says. “Go. I'll be right behind you.” ʸ

He goes.

* * *

He comes out in a room full of light.

Tall windows. A long table. Papers. Plants on every sill. And Elmesia, at the far end of the table, with a pen in her hand, looking up.

She doesn't startle. She just puts the pen down.

“You asked,” she says. ᴱˡ

“You said I could come.” ᴹ

“I said *come*,” says Elmesia. “I didn't expect you to *ask* first. Gojo told me you'd just appear.” ᴱˡ

“Gojo doesn't know everything.” ᴹ

“Gojo,” says Elmesia, “would be devastated to hear that.” ᴱˡ

Then Yuka is there beside him.

Pulled through her own shadow to his, the handle on her side, the way it's built: one moment the floor beside him is empty, and the next she's standing on it, a little breathless, her hair across her face, still holding his hand as if she never let go.

Elmesia looks at her. Then at him. Then at their hands.

Something happens to her face. He's not sure what.

“Oh,” she says softly. “*That's* new.” ᴱˡ

* * *

She reads the list at the long table.

She reads it slowly. All of it. Every name. Once or twice she stops and looks up at them, and then down again, and once she smiles at something and doesn't say what.

He waits. Yuka waits, sitting beside him, with her hand on his knee under the table.

When she's done, Elmesia folds the list in half. Then in half again. She puts it in a drawer in the table and closes the drawer.

“It's good,” she says. ᴱˡ

Yuka lets out a breath.

“It's a very good list,” says Elmesia. “It's the right people. And it's the right *number* of people, which I was worried about, because I've seen your house, and I wasn't sure you'd be able to stop.” ᴱˡ

“We stopped,” says Yuka. ʸ

“You stopped,” Elmesia agrees. ᴱˡ

* * *

Then Elmesia looks at them both, and her expression changes.

“The arrangements for everyone are made,” she says. “They'll have somewhere to sleep, things to eat, somewhere to argue over who gets the best view. Don't worry about that part.” ᴱˡ

Mau starts to answer. Elmesia raises a finger.

“And before you tell me you're imposing: you aren't. You came when my city needed you. I invited you long before that, and the invitation is still mine to give.” ᴱˡ

Yuka squeezes his knee under the table. He gives her the smallest nod.

“Now,” Elmesia says, turning to Mau. “Do you remember what I promised you when you left?” ᴱˡ

He does. He has thought of it at odd moments, between Shoko's appointments and the gate and the quiet mornings after. Something she wanted to give him on the island, with no explanation.

“I remember.” ᴹ

“Good. I'm keeping that promise.” ᴱˡ

“What is it?” Yuka asks immediately. ʸ

“I see your patience hasn't improved.”

“Not at all.” ʸ

“Then you will have to suffer until you arrive,” Elmesia says, far too pleased with herself. “I'm not telling either of you what it is. Not here. Not in a letter. Not if you use your new little shadow trick to appear in my sitting room a dozen more times.” ᴱˡ

Mau looks at Yuka. Her expression tells him she's already considered precisely that.

“We won't,” he says. ᴹ

“You're speaking for both of us?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“I'm hoping.”

Elmesia laughs. She doesn't reach for a box, an envelope or a key. Whatever she's decided, she leaves no clue on the table.

“On the island,” she says. “You'll know then.” ᴱˡ

For now, they let that be enough.

* * *

She gives them an envelope before they go.

Thick. Sealed with a blot of wax and her mark pressed into it.

“For the house,” she says. “The plan. Where everyone goes, what they do, when they come back. Give it to whoever in your house is most sensible.” ᴱˡ

“That's Shoko,” say Mau and Yuka together. ᴹ

“Then give it to Shoko,” says Elmesia. ᴱˡ

Mau reaches for it. Elmesia doesn't let go.

“And *you*,” she says, “don't open it.” ᴱˡ

“Why not?” ᴹ

“Because it's not for you. You two have a different plan.” ᴱˡ

“What plan?” ʸ

Elmesia smiles at them both. It's a very particular smile. He's seen it on Gojo, when Gojo knows something nobody else does.

“You'll see,” she says. ᴱˡ

She lets go of the envelope.

* * *

That evening, Yuka reads the house the list.

Not *the* list. The other one. The one she's been writing on the back pages of the notebook since the gate, and hiding, and adding to, and laughing at by herself under the blanket at night when she thinks he's asleep.

She stands up at dinner.

She taps her glass with her spoon. The whole table goes quiet. Everyone looks at her.

“Island rules,” she says. ʸ

She opens the notebook.

* * *

“One,” she reads. “Do not knock on our door.” ʸ

Gojo opens his mouth.

“Two,” Yuka reads, louder. “Do not knock on our door to ask if you can knock on our door.” ʸ

Gojo closes his mouth.

“Three. Do not stand outside our door and talk loudly about how you're definitely not going to knock on our door.” ʸ

Someone at the far end of the table laughs. Yuka doesn't look up.

“Four. If you see us on the beach, wave. Do not come over. Waving is enough. We will wave back. Probably.” ʸ

“*Probably*?” says Gojo. ᴳ

“Five,” says Yuka. “Do not ask us what we did today.” ʸ

“Six. Do not ask us what we're *going* to do today.” ʸ

“Seven. Do not *guess* what we did today. Out loud. At dinner.” ʸ

She looks up, directly at Gojo.

“That one's for you,” she says. ʸ

“I haven't *said* anything—” ᴳ

“You were going to.” ʸ

* * *

The list goes on.

Twenty-three items. He counts. Every single one of them is a version of the same thing. Do not follow us. Do not bring us anything unless we ask. Do not organise anything that requires us. Do not tell Elmesia anything she doesn't already know. Do not come to the room. Do not come near the room. Do not *look* at the room in a meaningful way.

Rimuru, halfway down the table, has his face in his hands. Milim is taking notes. Ori is nodding seriously at every item, as if they're all very reasonable. Shoko is drinking her tea and not hiding her smile at all.

“Twenty-three,” Yuka reads. ʸ

She closes the notebook.

“If anyone bothers us,” she says, “I'll tell Elmesia not to let you come back.” ʸ

* * *

The table erupts.

“She wouldn't—” ᴳ

“That's not *fair*—” ᴹⁱ

“She can't just—” ᴿ

Yuka holds up one hand. The table goes quiet again.

She reaches into her jacket. She takes out a small folded note, the one Elmesia slipped into the envelope for her alone, and unfolds it, and reads it out loud.

Four words, in the long elegant hand:

“*It's true. — Elmesia.*” ʸ

There's a long silence.

Then Gojo puts his head down on the table and groans.

And the whole house is laughing, all of it, every seat, and Yuka sits back down in Mau's lap with the notebook against her chest and her face pink and pleased, and he puts his arms round her from behind and puts his chin on her shoulder.

“Twenty-three,” he says in her ear. ᴹ

“I had more,” she says. “I cut some.” ʸ

“How many more?” ᴹ

“Eleven.” ʸ

He laughs into her hair.

Shoko has the envelope.

She opened it after dinner, in the clinic, alone. She came out looking pleased with herself and has refused to say anything since. When Mau asked, she said *it's not for you*. When Yuka asked, she said *Elmesia said you'd ask*.

“Is it good?” Yuka tries, at the bottom of the stairs. ʸ

“It's very good,” she says. “It's very, very good.” ˢʰ

That night, in bed, Yuka is still laughing.

Little bits of it. Coming up out of her at odd moments. She's lying on her front with her chin on his chest and the notebook beside her, and every so often she looks at it and starts again.

“Gojo's *face*,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“When I read *it's true*.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

She looks up at him. Her eyes are bright and her cheeks are still pink and she's never looked less like someone who walked into a gate built to kill her. For the house that was a week ago. For her, more than two.

“We're going,” she says. “The day after tomorrow. We're actually going.” ʸ

“We're actually going.” ᴹ

“And we don't know what we're doing.” ʸ

“Elmesia knows.” ᴹ

“And Shoko knows.” ʸ

“And Shoko knows.” ᴹ

“I don't mind not knowing,” she says. “For once. I don't mind at all.” ʸ

“I don't mind not knowing,” she repeats, as though she's testing how it feels to say it. ʸ

“Even though you asked Elmesia about the gift four times?”

“Three.”

“Four.”

“One of those was a perfectly normal question about luggage.” ʸ

“Of course.”

She narrows her eyes at him. He raises his eyebrows, which makes her laugh again. Her face is still warm and pink from reading the list aloud; he can see every bit of it, the colour against the ordinary grey of the room.

“You're making fun of me,” she says. ʸ

“A little.”

“I have twenty-three rules about that too.”

“I thought they were all for Gojo.”

“Gojo is an example. A very extensive example.” ʸ

He reaches up and brushes her hair behind her ear. She stops smiling quite so broadly, but she doesn't move away. Her hands come to rest on either side of his face.

She kisses him first, laughing against his mouth when he tries to say something and can't. The second kiss is quieter. After that she stays where she is, forehead against his, letting the laughter and the day's excitement settle.

“You're really not worried about the itinerary?” he asks. ᴹ

“No.” ʸ

“Not even a little?”

“I spent eleven days knowing exactly what could happen tomorrow. I would like one trip where someone else worries about breakfast.” ʸ

He holds her closer. Nothing about the sealed envelope can hurt them tonight. Shoko has it. Elmesia has a plan. The house is arguing somewhere downstairs about an itinerary they aren't allowed to read.

“Then we'll go,” he says. “And find out.” ᴹ

“Together.”

“Together.”

She gives him one more brief kiss, then lays her cheek over his heart as though she has decided this is where the rest of the evening belongs. He strokes her hair. Neither reaches for the list again.
