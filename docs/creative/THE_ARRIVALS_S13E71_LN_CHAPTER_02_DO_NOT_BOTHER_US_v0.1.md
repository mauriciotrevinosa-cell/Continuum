## Chapter 30 — Do Not Bother Us

**Continuity state:** B+376–377. The final island list, finished on the porch by Mau and Yuka (the names are not shown here: the creator will confirm who goes). Mau asks Elmesia first, by letter, whether he can bring it to her himself; she answers "come". He uses the Exchange to the soldier in her shadow, and Yuka comes through after him with her own. Elmesia reads the list, approves it, and confirms that she has arranged hospitality for the household. The separate gift she promised Mau remains a secret until the island. She gives them a sealed itinerary for the house and tells them not to open it. That evening Yuka reads the house her list of things it must not do on the island; every item is a version of "do not bother us", ending with: if you bother us I'll tell Elmesia not to let you come back. Elmesia's note, read aloud after it: "It's true." The house protests and agrees. The itinerary stays a mystery to Mau and Yuka. On the next afternoon, Mai witnesses a bounded training test with Mau's gradually CE-imbued daggers, katana, and six marked stones, using Momo Nishimiya's Tool Manipulation as a route distinct from Ruler's Authority and the inherited psychic hands of Momo Ayase. Gojo consents to sharing one narrow battlefield memory; the experiment proves only local control, not field-wide mastery. A playful, affectionate evening kiss and the couple's quiet afterward are complete.

*When: B+376, morning to B+377, evening. Six days after the orchard. Mai's workshop has been loud since sunrise every day.*

The list is on the porch table.

It's been on the porch table, in one form or another, since before the north. Since Elmesia's first letter said *bring the whole family next time if you must*, and the second asked for names, and Yuka flew it to her on Kaisel. It's been crossed out and written again. It's had names added in Ori's handwriting and taken out in Yuka's. It's had a coffee ring on it from Gojo, who says the ring is not his.

This morning, it's finished.

Yuka writes the last name. She puts the pencil down. She sits back in his lap and looks at it.

“That's it,” she says. ʸ

He looks at it over her shoulder.

“That's it,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

He doesn't send it the way they sent the others.

He could. He could fold it into a letter and give it to the bird. But he's been thinking, since the gate, about the soldier in Elmesia's shadow, and the nine breaths it takes to get there, and the fact that he's never once used it for anything but an emergency.

“I could take it to her,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka turns round in his lap.

“Now?” ʸ

“If she says yes. I'll ask first.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“You're going to *ask*,” she says. “Before you appear in her shadow.” ʸ

“She's a queen. It's her city. I'm not just going to come out of her floor.” ᴹ

Yuka starts to smile.

“Who are you,” she says, “and what have you done with my husband?” ʸ

* * *

He writes the note himself.

It takes him three tries. Yuka watches, and doesn't help, and laughs at the second one. In the end it's short:

*Elmesia —*

*The list is finished. May I bring it to you myself, through the soldier in your shadow? Yuka would come with me. If now isn't a good time, the bird can bring it instead.*

*Mau.*

He gives it to the bird. The bird looks at him with one eye, as if it's never been asked to carry anything so polite, and goes.

* * *

The answer comes back before lunch.

One word, in Elmesia's long elegant hand, in the middle of an otherwise empty sheet:

*Come.*

And under it, smaller:

*And next time, don't ask. You saved my city. You may come out of my floor whenever you like.*

Yuka reads it over his shoulder and laughs out loud.

* * *

He goes first.

He stands in the yard with the list folded in his pocket and his hand in hers, and closes his eyes, and finds the soldier, very far away, in a shadow in a city he's only seen once, with a wall around it and gardens inside.

“Nine breaths,” he says. ᴹ

“I know,” she says. “Go. I'll be right behind you.” ʸ

He goes.

* * *

He comes out in a room full of light.

Tall windows. A long table. Papers. Plants on every sill. And Elmesia, at the far end of the table, with a pen in her hand, looking up.

She doesn't startle. She just puts the pen down.

“You asked,” she says. ᴱˡ

“You said I could come.” ᴹ

“I said *come*,” says Elmesia. “I didn't expect you to *ask* first. Gojo told me you'd just appear.” ᴱˡ

“Gojo doesn't know everything.” ᴹ

“Gojo,” says Elmesia, “would be devastated to hear that.” ᴱˡ

Then Yuka is there beside him.

Pulled through her own shadow to his, the handle on her side, the way it's built: one moment the floor beside him is empty, and the next she's standing on it, a little breathless, her hair across her face, still holding his hand as if she never let go.

Elmesia looks at her. Then at him. Then at their hands.

Something happens to her face. He's not sure what.

“Oh,” she says softly. “*That's* new.” ᴱˡ

* * *

She reads the list at the long table.

She reads it slowly. All of it. Every name. Once or twice she stops and looks up at them, and then down again, and once she smiles at something and doesn't say what.

He waits. Yuka waits, sitting beside him, with her hand on his knee under the table.

When she's done, Elmesia folds the list in half. Then in half again. She puts it in a drawer in the table and closes the drawer.

“It's good,” she says. ᴱˡ

Yuka lets out a breath.

“It's a very good list,” says Elmesia. “It's the right people. And it's the right *number* of people, which I was worried about, because I've seen your house, and I wasn't sure you'd be able to stop.” ᴱˡ

“We stopped,” says Yuka. ʸ

“You stopped,” Elmesia agrees. ᴱˡ

Then Elmesia looks at them both, and her expression changes.

“The arrangements for everyone are made,” she says. “They'll have somewhere to sleep, things to eat, somewhere to argue over who gets the best view. Don't worry about that part.” ᴱˡ

Mau starts to answer. Elmesia raises a finger.

“And before you tell me you're imposing: you aren't. You came when my city needed you. I invited you long before that, and the invitation is still mine to give.” ᴱˡ

Yuka squeezes his knee under the table. He gives her the smallest nod.

“Now,” Elmesia says, turning to Mau. “Do you remember what I promised you when you left?” ᴱˡ

“I remember.” ᴹ

“Good. I'm keeping that promise.” ᴱˡ

“What is it?” Yuka asks immediately. ʸ

“I see your patience hasn't improved.”

“Then you will have to suffer until you arrive,” Elmesia says, far too pleased with herself. “I'm not telling either of you what it is. Not here. Not in a letter. Not if you use your new little shadow trick to appear in my sitting room a dozen more times.” ᴱˡ

“We won't,” he says. ᴹ

She gives them an envelope before they go.

“For the house,” she says. “The plan. Where everyone goes, what they do, when they come back. Give it to whoever in your house is most sensible.” ᴱˡ

“Then give it to Shoko,” says Elmesia. ᴱˡ

“Because it's not for you. You two have a different plan.” ᴱˡ

“What plan?” ʸ

“You'll see,” she says. ᴱˡ

The next afternoon, Mai comes out of the workshop because something keeps tapping against the door.

Three knocks. A pause. Three more.

She opens it expecting Gojo.

Instead, a dagger hangs in the air at her eye level. It wobbles, turns its point down and drops onto the packed dirt.

Mau is standing in the yard with the other dagger in one hand and the katana, still sheathed, on a bench behind him. Gojo sits on the fence. Yuka is beside him, holding the notebook.

“Don't tell me you came to practice throwing things at my door,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“I didn't throw it,” Mau says. ᴹ

“That's not better.”

“No,” says Gojo. “It's different.” ᴳ

* * *

Mau picks up the dagger.

He doesn't reach for it with Authority. Mai has watched him do that before. With Authority he can move an object without preparing it at all: a stone, a weapon, even apply pressure where there's no object to grab. It works through the spatial grip, with mana rather than a cursed-energy tether. This isn't that.

Nor is it the inherited psychic-hand pattern Mau learned from Momo Ayase. Those hands extend his own spiritual aura to seize something with substance—a person, a spirit, a cup. They cannot simply push on empty space, and Mau has never been able to hold them indefinitely without concentration and strain.

This is the third route.

“Momo Nishimiya's broom,” he says. ᴹ

Mai looks at him.

“Her Tool Manipulation?”

“Her broom holds her cursed energy. She moves the thing she's prepared, not anything she happens to look at.” ᴹ

“Which is why she can't just fly the school building away,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

“Exactly,” says Ciel through Mau. “The technique requires a compatible object imbued with cursed energy. The spatial grip and the psychic hands do not share that prerequisite.” ᶜ

Mau starts with the first dagger. A little cursed energy, allowed to settle into the metal. Then another measured layer. He's done this with weapons before to strengthen them, but he's never tried to make the charge into a handle for this technique. He stops, checks the pattern, adds a little more.

The dagger lifts, slowly. A finger's width. Then the length of his hand. It turns across the yard and lands flat on a wooden practice stand.

He lets out his breath.

“Again,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

The second dagger takes longer. Mau puts the first down, measures the new charge and repeats the same little route. Two blades rise this time. One takes a clean curve; the other starts to wobble, and he lowers it before it reaches the stand.

“Not Authority?” Gojo asks. ᴳ

“Off,” Noise says. “Confirmed.” ᴺ

“Not the psychic hands either,” Ciel adds. “Those would be drawing on spiritual aura. This movement follows the CE embedded in the blades.” ᶜ

Mau looks at the katana on the bench. Its ring already holds CE and mana in separate paths, and Rika has her own place in it. He doesn't disturb any of that. He feeds a small, separate measure of his cursed energy into the blade itself, slowly, without touching the ring or pretending to give Rika an order.

The sheathed katana rises an inch. He holds it there for two breaths and returns it to the bench.

“It isn't finished,” he says. ᴹ

“Nothing useful ever is,” Mai replies. ᴹᵃ

Gojo hops down from the fence.

“There are easier ways to throw a rock,” he says. ᴳ

“I know,” Mau says. “But I don't want to throw one rock.” ᴹ

“Can I ask you something?” ᴹ

“You've been doing that all afternoon.”

“A memory. One. From the fight with Sukuna. The battlefield with cursed energy spread across the debris. I want to see the pattern as you remember it.” ᴹ

Gojo goes quiet.

Mau doesn't reach with his eyes. Reading the shape of a cursed technique inscribed in someone's brain is not the same as permission to take their memories. He can read Gojo's Limitless pattern; he has been able to do that for a long time. A memory is somebody else's.

“Only that part?” Gojo asks. ᴳ

“Only the ground and the energy on it. Not the rest.” ᴹ

“That part. Nothing after.” ᴳ

Mau takes it. Ciel keeps the scope narrow, following only the recollection Gojo deliberately offers. It is brief: fractured paving, scattered stone, cursed-energy traces laid across the terrain. Not one perfect blanket and not proof that every pebble was controllable. The important thing is how many separate places could be prepared before anybody needed to move them.

Then Gojo takes his wrist back, and the memory is over.

“You didn't get my whole life out of that, did you?” Gojo says. ᴳ

“No.”

“Good. Most of it's embarrassing.” ᴳ

“Most?” Mai asks. ᴹᵃ

“I'm being generous.” ᴳ

Mau kneels in the gravel.

He could use Authority and lift a whole patch of stones at once. That wouldn't prove anything. Instead he touches one stone with CE, then another, then another, feeding the imprint into each without asking them to move yet. The new efficiency of his eyes keeps the activation of the technique almost free, but the cursed energy he deposits into an object is still an actual charge. It doesn't appear there for nothing. The information from every prepared stone also asks for its own place in his attention.

At the sixth stone, he stops.

“Six,” Ciel says. “The imprints are distinct. Try only three.” ᶜ

“Three,” Mau repeats. ᴹ

Three pebbles lift. They pass one another, not quickly, and settle at the far end of the stand. He tries a fourth; it jerks toward the wrong target, and he puts it down before it can strike the fence.

“Control first,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“I'm aware.” ᴹ

“Then why are you grinning?” ᴳ

Because beyond the yard there are roads, broken walls, fields of stone. Because given time, each piece could have its own small handle. Because the daggers and the katana could move without his hands being on them, while Authority and the inherited psychic hands remained separate ways of acting on something else.

“Everything,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“Not everything,” Ciel corrects, gently. “Not without enough CE to imbue the objects, a reliable range, and the mental capacity to direct them. We're testing one route, not declaring an entire battlefield mastered.” ᶜ

“Fine,” Mai says. “Then start with six. And learn how to keep them from putting holes in my workshop.” ᴹᵃ

*PREPARE THE FIELD.*

Mau puts the daggers away, lowers the katana without touching its ring, and leaves the stones where they are. The next test can happen another day. Tomorrow they're supposed to be packing for the island.

Gojo watches the gravel for a moment longer. Then he smiles, the same interested smile he wore when he asked for another round.
