# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 80
## Chapter 31 — The Night Before

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 80
**Reading order:** 31 (end of volume)
**Source episode:** S13E72
**Continuity state:** B+378, the day and night before the island; the volume ends the next morning as they leave. Packing: Yuka packs for both of them and makes him carry it all in the inventory; the two enormous matching hats and the third one Mai made for DIO; DIO takes her plant to Ori with written instructions. Makima, who can't be far from his body, asks quietly whether she's going too; Yuka answers before Mau can: yes, and on one day of it, if Yuka is in a good mood, maybe more than the bottom of the steps. That night, the let go: in the gate Mau closed the door again himself, and learned what it is to keep a hand on it; tonight, with her, he takes his hand off something for the first time without anything to fight. The intimate emotional let-go scene is written in full as non-graphic close romance; no unresolved placeholder remains. Morning: the house in the yard; the sealed itinerary in Shoko's pocket; the hats; they step out. Who goes is not named.

*When: B+378. The day before the island, and the night, and the first light of the morning after.*

She packs for both of them.

He offers to help. She says *no*. He offers again, an hour later, and she says *you'll pack the cheapest of everything*, and he can't argue with that, so he sits on the end of the bed and watches.

She's very serious about it. She lays everything out on the floor in rows, the way he laid out the tent and the stove on the third-floor stone. Shirts. Trousers. The middle jacket from the gate, washed. The blue dress from Elmesia's city, which he's been told is blue. A swimming thing he hasn't seen before and is told not to look at yet. Sandals, the bad ones she likes. Two of everything for him, in sizes she knows without asking.

Then she stands back and looks at the rows.

“In,” she says. ʸ

He puts them in the inventory. One row at a time. She watches every piece go, and writes it down in the notebook, on a new page headed *ISLAND*, so that when they get there and he forgets what he has, she'll know.

* * *

The hats come last.

Mai brings them up. She's barely slept in a week; she's been building things in the workshop that nobody's allowed to see, with the cursed energy that doesn't have to be saved for anything now. But she made the hats first. She said they were a warm-up.

Three of them.

Enormous. Wide as a cartwheel. Woven out of something light and stiff that holds its shape in a wind. Two of them exactly alike. The third the same, but with a small sea-glass pin stuck in the band, the one from DIO's old lopsided hat.

Yuka puts one on.

It comes down over her eyes. She pushes it up. It comes down again. She leaves it.

“Perfect,” she says, from under it. ʸ

He puts the other one on. He can't see anything at all.

“Perfect,” he agrees. ᴹ

Mai looks at the two of them standing in the middle of the bedroom with their faces gone under the brims, and makes a sound she tries to turn into a cough.

“They're *structurally* perfect,” she says. “I can't help how you look in them.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

DIO comes out for hers.

She steps out of him in her own body and takes the third hat from Mai with both hands, very carefully, and puts it on. It comes down over her eyes too. She pushes it up, the way Yuka did, and holds it there with one hand, and looks at herself in the mirror on the wardrobe door for a long time.

“Three,” she says. ᴰ

“Three,” says Yuka. ʸ

DIO turns round. Under the enormous brim, her face is doing something very happy and trying not to.

“I have to take my plant to Ori,” she says. ᴰ

* * *

He goes with her. Yuka comes too.

The Inn is quiet in the afternoon. DIO's room is at the end of the hall, small and plain, with the window looking out over the patio. The plant is on the sill where it's always been. It's bigger than it was. It's put out two new leaves since the gate.

DIO picks it up in its pot. She holds it against her chest.

She's written instructions. Two pages. In very careful letters. How much water, and when, and which side of the sill the light comes from in the morning, and what the leaves look like when it's thirsty, and what they look like when it's had too much.

She gives both pages and the plant to Ori, in the hall outside Ori's room.

Ori reads both pages. All the way through. Then she looks up.

“I watered it while you were in the gate,” she says. ᴼ

“I know,” says DIO. “I saw. It's got two new leaves.” ᴰ

Ori looks at the plant.

“I'll do it exactly like this,” she says, holding up the pages. ᴼ

“I know you will,” says DIO. ᴰ

She doesn't let go of the pot right away. Then she does.

* * *

Makima asks at dusk.

She's at the bottom of the porch steps, where she always is. She hasn't come up since the gate; she's been quieter since the gate, thinner somehow, as if holding everything took something out of her that's taking a while to come back. Mau has felt her at the back of him every day, steady, recovering.

She doesn't ask Mau. She looks past him, at Yuka, sitting in his lap on the porch with the notebook.

“Can I speak?” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Yes,” says Yuka. ʸ

Makima hesitates.

“The island,” she says. “I'm bound to his body. I can't be far from it. So I'm going whether anyone wants me to or not.” ᴹᵏ

She stops.

“I wanted to ask anyway,” she says. “If it's all right. That I'm there.” ᴹᵏ

Mau opens his mouth.

Yuka gets there first.

“Yes,” she says. ʸ

Makima looks at her.

“You held it,” Yuka says. “At the bottom. You held all of it, and you nearly didn't come back, and you said sorry you didn't ask first.” ʸ

She's quiet for a moment.

“You're coming,” she says. “And one day, if I'm in a very good mood, which I might be, because it's the island—” ʸ

She stops. She looks at Mau. He doesn't say anything. It's hers.

“—maybe more than the bottom of the steps,” Yuka says. “One day. We'll see.” ʸ

Makima doesn't move for a long moment.

Then something happens at the corner of her mouth. More than last time. Almost all the way.

“Thank you,” she says. ᴹᵏ

* * *

That night, she's quiet.

The bags are in the inventory. The hats are on the chair by the window, stacked one on top of the other, enormous. The notebook is on the bedside table, closed, next to Ori's *welcome home* note and the chocolate wrapper from the red gate. Outside, the house is going to bed: doors closing, someone laughing on the stairs, Beru's soft *good night, my king, good night, my queen* outside the door.

She's lying on her side, facing him, with her hand flat on the line across his chest.

“You're thinking,” he says. ᴹ

“I'm thinking,” she agrees. ʸ

“About what?” ᴹ

She's quiet for a while.

“The door,” she says. ʸ

He goes still.

“At the bottom,” she says. “You told me. On the third day of rest, when you couldn't sleep. You said there's a door in you, and you've always kept a hand on it. Even in the duel. Even in the north. Every day. And at the bottom you took your hand off, and then you had to reach back into everything and find it and close it again yourself.” ʸ

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

“And you did.” ʸ

“I did.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“Do you keep a hand on it with me?” she says. ʸ

* * *

He thinks about it.

He thinks about it the way he promised. Properly. The truth.

He thinks about the porch, before the island the first time, checking that she was still there every time she came close. About the bed after the Trial, waking up and reaching for her. About the gate: eleven days of one hand always kept free, always turned toward her, holding. About the tent, and the lamp, and *it's ugly*, and *both*.

“I used to,” he says. ᴹ

She waits.

“Not the same door,” he says. “A smaller one. Not the one with everything behind it. The one with—” ᴹ

He doesn't know how to say it.

“The one with *me* behind it,” he says. “The part that's afraid you'll go. That checks. That keeps a hand free. Even here. Even in bed. Even when there's nothing to fight.” ᴹ

She doesn't say anything. Her hand is very still on his chest.

“I still do,” he says. “A bit. I didn't know until you asked.” ᴹ

* * *

She sits up.

She looks down at him in the dark. Her face is very soft, and very fierce, and very sure, all at once, the way it was in the warm house with her fingers in his hair, the way it was on the stair when she said *then I'll get stronger*.

“There's nothing to fight,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“There's no gate. No Warden. No box. Nothing's coming through the door. Beru's outside. Ciel's counting, probably. The whole house is asleep. And we're going to the island tomorrow, and we don't even know what we're doing there, and I don't mind.” ʸ

She takes his hand. The left one. The one he kept free for eleven days.

She puts it on her own heart.

“You don't have to keep this one free,” she says. “Not tonight. There's nothing it needs to be ready for.” ʸ

He feels her heart under his palm. Steady. Fast. Hers.

“Let go,” she says. ʸ

* * *

He feels Ciel close the door. The small, polite *click*. And somewhere at the far edge of him, Noise, very carefully looking elsewhere. And DIO, inside, gone very quiet. And Makima, at the very back, turning away.

And he lets go.

Not of her. Of the other thing. The small door, with him behind it. The hand he didn't know he still kept on it.

He takes his hand off.

And nothing happens. Nothing comes through. Nothing breaks. There's no weight to hold and no one standing in the gap holding it. There's only her, in the dark, with his hand on her heart, and him, all of him, with nothing kept back and nothing held ready and nothing turned toward anything else.

He didn't know it would feel like that.

She sees it on his face. He doesn't know what his face is doing. She sees it anyway.

“*There* you are,” she says, very softly. ʸ

She doesn't take her hand away from his.

He looks down at where his left palm rests over her heart. The line down the back of his hand is pale, a different shape from the one across his chest. Both are old enough to be part of him now. Yuka has her fingers spread over his, holding it in place, not because she thinks he will pull away, but because she wants him to know he doesn't have to.

“Say something,” she whispers. ʸ

He tries.

There is a peculiar amount of room inside his chest. He had thought the absence of that small, constant readiness would feel like a hole. It doesn't. It feels like someone has moved an enormous piece of furniture out of a room where he's lived for years, and he can finally see the floor.

“I don't know what to say,” he admits. ᴹ

“That's okay.”

“I keep expecting—”

He stops. The old habit is to say *nothing*. To tell her he's fine. To turn the sentence into something she can laugh at.

Instead he finishes it.

“I keep expecting to have to check that you're still here,” he says. “Even though I can feel your hand.” ᴹ

Yuka doesn't tell him that he shouldn't. She doesn't say he's being silly. Her fingers tighten gently around his.

“Then look,” she says. ʸ

He looks at her.

“I'm here.”

“I know.” ᴹ

“Look again if you need to.”

He does. Her face is close enough that he can see the soft pink appearing beneath her eyes, the only colour in the room. He can see the outline of the smile she's trying not to make, and the way the smile wins anyway.

“There,” she says. “Did I go anywhere?” ʸ

“No.”

“Did anything happen?”

“No.”

“Good. Now you can stop checking for one minute.” ʸ

He looks at her again.

“You're very demanding.”

“I'm your wife. I have rights.”

“That's not in the contract.”

“It doesn't have to be.” ʸ

He laughs. It comes out much less steady than he expects. She hears it, of course. She hears everything he tries to hide in his voice.

“Mau,” she says softly. ʸ

“Yeah.”

“Come here.”

He's already lying beside her. She knows that. He knows she knows it. She asks anyway, and this time he doesn't try to make a joke about distance. He moves closer until their foreheads meet.

She lifts her free hand and cups his cheek. Her thumb passes over the place under his eye where he hadn't realised there was moisture.

He blinks.

“You're crying,” she says. ʸ

“Apparently.”

“Is it bad?”

“No.” ᴹ

She waits.

He searches for a better answer, one that doesn't pretend it isn't happening.

“It's a lot,” he says. “I thought I was done being afraid of losing you. After the island. After you came back to me in the red gate. I thought I'd put that away. But I was still checking.” ᴹ

“I knew.”

“I didn't.”

“I know,” she says. “That's why I'm telling you now.” ʸ

His hand is still over her heart. He can feel its rhythm under his palm. He's seen the terrible things a body can survive and the ones it can't. He's spent eleven days standing between her and rules designed to end her life. But her heart beneath his hand is not a countdown or a problem to solve. It's hers. Alive and steady and here.

“I'm sorry,” he begins. ᴹ

“No.”

He stops.

“You can tell me what hurts,” she says. “You don't have to apologise for having a place that hurts.” ʸ

He lets his forehead rest against hers. His shoulders have started to shake. He hadn't known they would. He keeps trying to hold them still, and each time he does she brings him closer, until he stops.

They stay there for a while. He hears the orchard moving beyond the window, one branch brushing another in the night wind. Somewhere below them a door opens and closes. The house is home, doing all the things it does when nobody's trying to save it.

When he breathes again without catching on the inhale, Yuka shifts back enough to look at him.

“Can I ask something?” she says. ʸ

“Anything.”

“When you say you didn't keep anything back, does that include all the terrible jokes?” ʸ

He stares at her.

“All of them?” he asks. ᴹ

“Every last one.”

“Then I'm afraid you're in trouble.”

She bursts out laughing, and he does too, and the last of the tightness in his chest loosens with it. She catches his face between her hands before he can turn away from the sound.

“I love you,” she says. ʸ

“I love you too.”

“More?”

“More than what?” ᴹ

“Than you knew how to say this morning.”

He thinks about it. He could give her a bigger word, a grander promise. He knows she'd hear the feeling underneath it. But she asked for the truth, and the truth is smaller and much harder to turn away from.

“I think I knew,” he says. “I was just afraid to show you how much.” ᴹ

Her expression changes. Not into sadness. Into something so open that he nearly looks away out of habit.

He doesn't.

She kisses him. Her hands are still on his cheeks, and he feels her smile before she draws back.

“There,” she whispers. ʸ

“Where?”

“Right there. That's you.”

She kisses him again. This time he reaches for her first, his left hand moving from her heart to the back of her shoulder. It is a familiar, ordinary movement, but he feels the difference. The hand isn't held in reserve. It isn't pointed at a door. It is simply holding his wife.

Yuka notices.

“Both hands,” she says. ʸ

He looks at her, and she laughs at the expression he must be making.

“You're hugging me with both hands.”

“I've done that before.”

“I know. But you're not thinking about doing something else with one of them.” ʸ

He is surprised to discover that she's right.

“You can tell?”

“I can always tell.” ʸ

She moves closer, then gives a small, indignant noise when her knee gets caught in the blanket.

“Wait,” she says. ʸ

He lifts the edge so she can free it. She tries to pull the blanket up, tangles it around his ankle instead, and both of them end up laughing at the absurd amount of work it takes to settle beside someone they've been sleeping next to for so long.

“Very badly arranged,” she says, triumphant. ʸ

“You've said that about every bed we've ever had.”

“And I'm always right.” ʸ

He smooths the blanket over her knees. She draws his left arm across her shoulders and puts her own arm around his waist, careful only because she remembers where the scar crosses him.

“Here?” she asks. ʸ

“Here.”

“Does anything pull?”

“No.”

“Are you sure?”

“Yes.” ᴹ

She considers him with mock suspicion.

“I'll believe you this time.”

“Generous.”

“I am a generous wife.” ʸ

She rests her cheek against his chest. He can feel her breathing change as she settles. The pace is slow, unhurried, and he catches himself matching it. He doesn't need Ciel to count. He doesn't need the eyes to measure the room. He doesn't need to know where everyone is in the house before letting his own body grow heavy against the pillows.

After a while she lifts her head.

“What?” he asks. ᴹ

“I don't want you to think letting go means you have to stop protecting me,” she says. ʸ

“I don't.”

“Or that I'm going to stop wanting to protect you.”

“I know.” ᴹ

She studies him. He keeps looking at her, waiting for the rest.

“I want us to get better at both,” she says. “At fighting together. At telling each other when something's ugly. And at coming home and being stupid about hats and sweets.” ʸ

“We're already excellent at that last part.”

“Only because I do most of the work.” ʸ

“Of course.”

“And when you're scared again, because you will be, I want you to tell me.”

“I will.”

“And if I get scared, I tell you.”

“Every time.” ᴹ

“Even if it sounds ridiculous.”

“Especially then.”

She smiles at him.

“Good.”

They kiss again, no longer because there's anything to prove. She curls against him afterward, then draws back because she wants to see his face, then leans close again. He doesn't have to decide whether to move first or wait. They know each other well enough to ask without fear of getting the answer wrong.

At one point she notices the room has gone completely quiet inside the link.

“Ciel?” she whispers. ʸ

There is no response. A door has been closed on both sides, exactly as they agreed. Yuka smiles to herself.

“She's very good at that,” she says. ʸ

“At privacy?”

“At letting us have it.” ʸ

She settles again, satisfied. He strokes the hair back from her forehead and kisses the place he has just uncovered.

“Tomorrow,” she says after a while. ʸ

“The island.”

“The hats.”

“The mystery.”

“And Elmesia refusing to tell me anything.” ʸ

“She'll enjoy that.”

“I know. I might enjoy it too.” ʸ

He looks toward the chair where the hats are stacked like badly built roofs. The closed notebook is beside Ori's note on the table. Downstairs the house has finally gone quiet, and in the morning some of them will be leaving and some will be staying, and Shoko will be holding an envelope she won't let either of them read.

He feels no urge to find out before it's time.

“Will you really be all right not knowing?” he asks. ᴹ

Yuka nods.

“I know who I'm going with.” ʸ

He smiles.

“Me?”

“Don't be ridiculous. The hat.” ʸ

He laughs, loud enough that she puts a finger over his mouth and tells him Ori will hear. Then she takes the finger away and kisses him once more.

When she draws back, she doesn't move far. She puts her hand over his left one, where it rests against her shoulder, and keeps it there until his breathing slows.

“Let go,” she murmurs, not as a command this time but as something they already understand. ʸ

“I am.”

And he is.

He falls asleep with both arms around her, with the last thing he sees the colour of her face beside his. For once he doesn't check the window, or the door, or the shadows. He trusts the quiet to remain quiet, and trusts her to wake him if she needs anything.

She stays awake a little longer, just long enough to hear his breathing settle. Then she closes her eyes too.

* * *

In the morning, the house is in the yard.

Not all of it is going. He knows that much. Shoko has the envelope in her pocket and an expression that says she knows exactly who is going where, and when, and is not going to tell him. Gojo is on the porch railing, being told by three people at once to get down. Ori is holding DIO's plant, and the two pages, and won't put either of them down even to wave.

Mau comes out onto the porch in the enormous hat.

He can't see anything. He tips it back. It comes down again.

Yuka comes out beside him in hers. She takes his hand. Not the right one. The left one. And holds it, and doesn't need it free for anything, and neither does he.

DIO steps out of him onto the porch in the third hat, with the sea-glass pin catching the morning light.

The house sees the three of them standing there, three enormous hats in a row, and goes completely silent.

Then Gojo falls off the railing.

* * *

“Ready?” Yuka says. ʸ

He looks at the yard. At the house. At Ori with the plant, and Shoko with the envelope, and Mai in the workshop door with something behind her back that she isn't showing anyone yet. At the hills beyond the wall, grey, the way everything is grey. At Yuka, under the brim of her hat, in colour, the only thing in the world in colour.

He doesn't know what they're doing on the island. He doesn't know where they're going first, or who'll be there, or what Elmesia's different plan is, or what the gift is.

He doesn't mind.

“Ready,” he says. ᴹ

They go down the porch steps together, into the morning.
