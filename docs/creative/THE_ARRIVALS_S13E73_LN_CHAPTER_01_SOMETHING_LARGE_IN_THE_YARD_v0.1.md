# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 81
## Chapter 1 — Something Large in the Yard

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 81
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E73
**Continuity state:** Noise reveals completed Ten Shadows draconic shikigami. Mai, Mau, Senku and Byakuya design a passenger platform. Unmanned weighted first test fails at a torsion joint; no passenger hurt, construction correction still pending. House departure delayed naturally. No new Arise dragon or limitless transport capability.

*When: B+379, morning; immediately after the porch departure.*

Gojo gets up before anyone offers to help him.

He makes a reasonable attempt at making it look intentional, brushing one sleeve and looking back at the railing as if he has just finished inspecting it. Shoko stands over him with two cups of coffee.

“The second step's loose,” he says.

“You didn't use the steps.”

“Then I've identified a problem with the railing.”

Yuka leans against Mau's arm. Her hat knocks his hat sideways, and he has to catch both brims before she can finish laughing. Across the path, Ori is trying to tell Chloe what happened. Chloe saw it happen. This does not appear to reduce the importance of the account.

“It was very fast,” Ori explains.

“Most falls are,” says Shoko.

Mau looks at the coffee she hasn't handed over.

“Is that for him?”

“Depends how much more he has to say.”

Gojo gets his cup. He accepts the condition with a dignified silence that lasts until he takes the first sip.

The yard has spread itself into several small departures. Rem is holding a cardigan against Umaru, who insists that the island has weather and therefore will provide its own warmth. Taihei has the other sleeve. Neither of them appears persuaded. Near the workshop, Momo Nishimiya sits on her broom with both feet dangling, listening to Mai explain why a parcel must remain flat. Mai has said it three times. Momo is beginning to look personally accused by gravity.

Yuta crouches beside a case that Maki has already closed. He gets it open, checks the contents and closes it again.

“It's still in there,” Maki says.

“I know.”

“Then stop.”

He puts his hands on his knees and stands up.

Yuka watches him with the sympathetic expression of someone who has checked their own notebook twice since breakfast. When Mau glances at it, she puts it behind her back.

“Mine has other people's things.”

“So does his.”

“You're supposed to be on my side.”

“I am. I'm criticizing him silently.”

She considers this, then gives him the notebook to hold while she tightens her sandal. He takes it in the hand with the two fingers that never lie quite flat. The paper settles crookedly against his palm. Yuka finishes with the buckle and turns his hand over to retrieve the book without bending them.

Behind them DIO comes down the steps very carefully, holding her enormous brim with both hands.

“Are we ready?” she asks.

“Almost,” says Mau.

Inside him, Noise makes a small sound.

Mau stops.

Yuka looks up. He knows the expression she is about to give him, and tries to answer it before she can.

“I didn't schedule anything.”

*Ready,* says Noise.

Mau waits for the rest. There isn't any.

*Ready what?*

A shape unfolds behind his eyes. He sees the familiar organization of the Ten Shadows, the space that already belongs to each shikigami, and a pattern he remembers watching Noise work on. It has changed since he last looked. The incomplete lines have joined. There is a weight to the finished thing, a suggestion of shoulders opening under a sky too small for them.

His mouth comes open.

“What?” Yuka asks.

He looks at the patch of yard between the workshop and the orchard. Too small. He looks over the roofs toward the far field.

“I need everybody to stay here for a second.”

Maki raises her head.

“Why?”

“Noise finished something.”

Several people stop what they're doing.

Gojo puts his coffee behind Shoko's shoulder. She moves it away from his hand before he can leave it with her.

“On the table,” she says.

* * *

Mau goes to the field with Yuka, Mai and Yuta. Maki follows after telling Ori to stay by the path. Ori goes to the path and stands at its very edge, where Chloe joins her. They both move their toes back when Maki looks over.

“How big?” Mai asks.

Mau points at the orchard, hesitates and points farther out.

Mai looks at the space he's indicated.

“Oh.”

He takes off his hat and gives it to Yuka. She puts it over her own. She is still adjusting the two brims when the shadow beneath him runs forward across the grass.

He feels the response in the familiar place where he calls the others. This is the technique's answer: a shikigami rising into a body, the ground darkening beneath it, a huge head lifting before the shoulders can follow.

The grass bends.

For a moment he sees one eye open at the level of the lower branches. Then the head rises beyond them. A long neck clears the orchard and the rest of the creature takes shape across the field, immense folded wings drawing the morning light into stripes. Its tail lies in a slow curve that makes the workshop look closer than it was a moment ago.

Yuta takes a step to one side, watching the distance between the nearest wing and the trees.

“That's enough,” he says.

Mau stops the unfolding there. The shikigami stays low, its claws pressing into empty earth beyond the last bed of vegetables.

From the path comes a noise that is mostly Milim.

Then several other noises join it.

Yuka tips the hats back far enough to see the entire head.

“That's the one?”

“The one from Elmesia's dragon. The pattern Noise was building.”

*Finished,* Noise says, with unmistakable satisfaction.

Mau smiles so hard that the edge of the old scar near his eye pulls.

*You did it.*

*Yes.*

He puts his palm against the side of the shikigami's neck. It lowers its head a little more. The patterned surface under his hand catches the light differently from Kaisel's scales; farther along the body, the shapes belong to the technique that made it. He can feel it waiting for direction.

“Elmesia wanted to see it when you were done,” Yuka says.

“She's going to see it.”

Mai turns slowly, taking in the expanse of its back.

“And you want us to sit on that?”

“I haven't asked you to make anything.”

“You looked at me.”

“I like looking at you.”

“Try that on your wife.”

“It works on her.”

Yuka holds out one hand. He kisses the back of it because he knows what she wants. She hands him his hat.

Mai makes an impatient sound and walks closer to the shikigami. She stops before touching it.

“Will it stay still?”

Mau asks it to. Its chest moves once beneath the folded wing, then settles.

She puts her hand down, follows the slope with her eyes and looks back toward the workshop.

“Get Senku.”

* * *

Byakuya brings chalk. Senku brings a string, a short length of wood and the expression he wears when someone has presented him with an exceptionally expensive mistake.

“Who designed the passenger structure?” he asks.

“Nobody yet,” says Mai.

“Excellent. That's the most promising thing I've heard.”

The first drawing goes on a board from the workshop. Mai sketches a platform that can rest across the broad part of the back. Byakuya asks where people are supposed to climb on. She adds a stair. Senku asks where their weight goes when the dragon turns. She erases the stair, moves it and glares at him before he can ask the next question.

Mau stands on the opposite side of the board, holding it steady. Yuka leans over his shoulder. The brim of her hat touches Mai's pencil.

“Hat,” Mai says.

Yuka bends farther forward.

“The rest of the hat.”

Mau takes it off her and hooks both hats over a fence post. Yuka stays where she is, her chin almost on his shoulder.

The design changes while the yard watches at a sensible distance. Fern arrives with the list Anko has been using and asks how much space they intend to leave between seats. Jinshi asks whether anyone has considered the people who will want to stand up halfway through the journey.

“They won't,” says Mai.

Behind Jinshi, Gojo raises his hand.

Mai looks at him and adds another line to the drawing.

“They can't.”

It takes most of an hour to reach the first version they are willing to test. Mau asks before calling up the version of Construction he learned from her. Mai makes him wait while she corrects the base of the forward rail.

“Here,” she says, tapping the board. “And no improving it in the middle because you suddenly get an idea.”

“If I get a good idea?”

“Tell me.”

He tells her the first one. She listens, changes the fastening and lets him begin.

The structure forms over cleared ground. Mau keeps his attention on the drawing and the shape arriving beneath his hands. He has the energy for it. What takes time is following the decisions in the right order, holding a junction long enough to check that the next part meets it properly. Noise presents the pattern without speaking. Ciel catches a measurement before he closes a brace.

*Lower support. Three fingers to the left.*

He changes it. Mai crouches to look underneath and makes him wait again.

Yuka brings her a cloth for her knees. Mai takes it without looking up.

When the platform is finished, Byakuya walks around it twice. Senku sets a foot on the boarding step and watches its supports. Yuta lifts one side just enough to let Mai inspect a joint she couldn't reach.

“Now,” Senku says, “we put something on it that won't complain if we get this wrong.”

They load sacks from the workshop. Stones. Empty containers filled with water. A bundle of spare timber whose weight Byakuya has written on the side. Mau places each piece where Senku points, then draws the platform up onto the shikigami's back with Authority while Mai watches the joining points.

Nobody boards it.

At the path, Ori stands on tiptoe.

“Does it get a name?” she calls.

“When we know what it does,” Mau calls back.

“It carries people.”

“We're checking that part.”

The shikigami rises a little under his direction. Earth falls from one claw. The platform lifts with it, the rear supports taking the change in angle as intended.

The front support doesn't.

There is a short, ugly crack. One of the water containers slides. A rail bows out of line and Mai throws up her hand.

“Down.”

Mau lowers the shikigami immediately. He catches the container before it goes over, holds the rest of the load where it is and feels the platform shift beneath it.

Another fastening gives way.

The entire forward corner sags toward the neck.

For a few seconds everyone is quiet. Water ticks out of the split container onto the grass.

Mai walks over, looks at the failed support and then at the drawing. Senku crouches beside her.

“Well,” says Gojo from the path. “That seat had an excellent view.”

Shoko takes his coffee away.

Mau holds the broken corner up while Mai reaches beneath it. Her face has lost the irritation. She runs a thumb over the clean break, presses the surviving joint and studies the direction in which it bent.

“We put the weight through this,” she says.

“And asked it to twist here,” Senku answers.

She nods once.

Yuka retrieves the board and holds it for them. Byakuya brings the chalk. Mau keeps the load suspended until Yuta and Maki have cleared it, one piece at a time.

“We're going to be late,” he says quietly to Yuka.

She looks at the damaged platform, then past it to the enormous creature waiting patiently in the field.

“We're going to get there sitting down.”

She puts the chalk into Mai's open hand.
