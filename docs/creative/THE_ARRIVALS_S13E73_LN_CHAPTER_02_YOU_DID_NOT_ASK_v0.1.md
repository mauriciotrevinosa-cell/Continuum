# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 81
## Chapter 2 — You Did Not Ask

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 81
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S13E73
**Continuity state:** Transport rebuilt and unmanned low test passed after repeated checks. Mini Ciel remains damaged, Ciel repairs; blindfold voluntarily moderates sensory input. Mai clarifies specific collaborative-pattern permission rather than universal ban. Ori, Momo Nishimiya and Maki receive enormous hats. House shadow tasks and privacy delegated; plant stays home with instructions.

*When: B+379, late morning into lunch.*

The first thing Mai does is take away Gojo's suggestion that they reinforce the whole platform with something that cannot break.

“Then the next thing breaks,” she says. ᴹᵃ

“Make that unbreakable too.” ᴳ

“Keep going. Eventually you'll discover the ground.” ᴹᵃ

Senku smiles at his chalkboard. Byakuya writes down the angle at which the support failed. Gojo looks for his coffee and finds Shoko drinking it.

“That's mine.” ᴳ

“It's cooled down. You don't like it that way.” ˢʰ

He opens his mouth, then closes it as she takes another sip.

The platform comes apart at the front. Mau lowers the pieces onto the grass and kneels beside Mai while she shows him where the curved support needs to sit. Her first drawing had assumed a surface that held its shape. The dragon's back moves when it raises its head. They put a little space into the joining, change the angle and give the front somewhere to shift before it has to carry the weight.

“Like a shoulder,” Yuka says, watching Mai turn the small model in her fingers. ʸ

“Like a shoulder that doesn't listen when I tell it to stay still,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Mau glances at Yuka.

“Don't,” she tells him. ʸ

“I didn't say anything.” ᴹ

“I was there when you thought it.” ʸ

He looks genuinely interested for half a second. She taps his forehead with one finger.

“Your face. I'm talking about your face.” ʸ

---

They test the replacement on the ground first. Byakuya pushes down while Maki pulls the far end sideways. The joint moves within its new housing. Mai makes them do it again, watches it from below and finally calls Mau over.

“This version. Exactly this version.” ᴹᵃ

“Can I use the pattern?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵃ

He waits until she's put her thumb over the part she's corrected, then builds it the way she asks. There is satisfaction in getting to the end and having her turn it over, inspect it and put it beside the first one without a word of complaint.

Two supports later the platform sits on the shikigami again.

This time it rises with the sacks distributed differently. The foreleg bends. The load shifts toward the side they are watching and settles against a brace that has room to receive it. Nothing cracks.

Mau waits for Mai. Mai waits for Senku. Senku looks at Byakuya, who is standing far enough away to watch the whole assembly.

“Once more,” Byakuya says. ᵇʸᵃᵏᵘʸᵃ

They do it once more. Then on the opposite side. Then with the water containers farther forward.

At the fence, Milim sits down beside Ori and puts her chin on the top rail.

“It's going to take forever.” ᴹⁱ

“It has to be safe,” Ori says. ᴼ

“I know.” ᴹⁱ

“You can help watch.” ᴼ

Milim straightens. She starts watching extremely hard.

The final test is short and low over an empty part of the field. No passengers. Mau keeps the shikigami close enough to put it down the instant Mai raises a hand. A shadow waits beside each of the load points, under orders to signal a loose fastening. The soldiers make no sound; one points when a cord pulls tight, and Senku has Mau land so they can adjust it.

By the time the platform comes down for the last time, the yard smells of warm grass and lunch.

Mai holds up the board for Mau.

“Now you can ask people to sit on it.” ᴹᵃ

He takes the board, looks at the finished structure and then at her.

“You made a flying bus.” ᴹ

“We made a seat that might let you stop complaining about carrying everyone.” ᴹᵃ

“I've never complained.” ᴹ

“Good. Keep that up.” ᴹᵃ

She takes the board back before he can get chalk on his shirt.

Lunch moves outside because nobody wants to stop looking at the dragon.

Shuna has decided on something people can hold while standing. Shion hands out plates. Mau takes two, brings them to Yuka and discovers that her hands are occupied with Anko's list.

“Hold it there,” she says. ʸ

He holds the plate under her chin. She eats a mouthful, turns a page and points with the empty fork.

“That's Momo's. The other Momo. Write the family name.” ʸ

Anko adds it. Beside her, Ori has a pencil tucked behind one ear and several small labels stuck to the front of her dress.

“You have to put the names on before he takes them,” she tells Ryo. ᴼ

Ryo looks at the case at her feet.

“If I don't, is it still mine?” ᴿʸ

“Yes.” ᴼ

“Then how will he know?” ᴿʸ

Ori gives her a label. Ryo writes on it immediately. Kita crouches beside her to make sure it is a name rather than a price.

Ori stays beside the bags after Kita goes. She peels another label from the sheet, turns it over and sticks it to her thumb instead.

Yuka notices when the child does it a second time.

“Have we missed someone?” ʸ

Ori looks toward Mau. He is on the other side of the platform with Yuta, testing the boarding rail. Her mouth settles into a small, determined line.

“Can I still come to you?” ᴼ

Yuka puts the list down.

“On the trip?” ʸ

Ori nods. She has made it sound like a question about where to put another bag. It takes Yuka a moment to understand why the girl is holding the labels so tightly.

“Because of the rules,” Ori adds. “For your holiday.” ᴼ

Yuka crouches. The enormous brim is not on Ori yet; there is nothing between her and the tears she is trying very hard to keep still.

“Come here.” ʸ

Ori goes into her arms carefully, as though she might still have misunderstood.

“Yes, you can come to us. To me, to Mau. You don't have to wait until we get home.” ʸ

“Even if you're together?” ᴼ

“Even then. If we need a minute, we'll tell you. You don't have to work it out from across the room.” ʸ

Ori presses her face against Yuka's shoulder. Yuka rubs her back, feeling the little breath she has been keeping in.

“Most of those rules are for Gojo,” she adds. ʸ

The child draws back enough to look at her.

“He has his own?” ᴼ

“He keeps earning more.” ʸ

Ori laughs through her nose. Yuka wipes beneath one eye with her thumb before the girl can do it with the label-covered hand.

“Mau and I do want some time together. That doesn't mean we don't want you. We can want both.” ʸ

“Mama too?” ᴼ

“Mama too. I was hoping you three would take me to the water tomorrow. I don't know how to find all the things you find.” ʸ

Ori's hands loosen.

“You have to look in the little pools.” ᴼ

“Then you'll have to show me.” ʸ

“Early. Before people step in them.” ᴼ

“Early,” Yuka agrees. ʸ

Ori hugs her again, much less carefully this time. Then she remembers something urgent about telling Mama and runs across the yard with the labels still on her fingers.

Mau watches her arrive beside Frieren, talking so quickly that her mother has to close the book to listen. He saw the expression she brought to Yuka before he saw the hug. For a moment it had been the same fiercely contained disappointment Frieren wore over the grimoire: determined not to ask for the thing she wanted too much.

He finds Yuka as she gets up.

“What did she ask you?” ᴹ

Yuka puts her palm against his chest, stopping him before he can decide something needs fixing.

“Whether she could still come to us on holiday. I told her yes.” ʸ

“I didn't mean—” ᴹ

“I know. Neither of us did.” ʸ

“You want to go diving with Ori?” ᴹ

“There. Now we won't leave you behind either.” ʸ

The guard posts already have their instructions; the new sheet records which parts of the domestic work are allowed while people are away. Muster stands at the gate, looking down the road. A pair of ordinary soldiers wait beside the tools for the gardens, empty-handed until Jinshi identifies what needs moving.

DIO arrives with her plant's two pages and finds Ori checking a bag.

“I made another copy,” she says. ᴰ

“Did something change?” ᴼ

“No. I remembered you're coming.” ᴰ
