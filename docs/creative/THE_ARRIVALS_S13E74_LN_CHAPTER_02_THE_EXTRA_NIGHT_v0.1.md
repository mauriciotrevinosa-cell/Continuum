# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 81
## Chapter 4 — The Extra Night

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 81
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S13E74
**Continuity state:** Advance four fly together on Kaisel with checked travelling straps. Mau leaves verified home anchor and will return for shared-house transport. Elmesia confirms existing pass and neighbouring Frieren/Ori room. DIO and Makima physically manifest near Mau by consent; Makima invited to dinner with couple from start, no handholding grant yet. First independent meal choice rather than ritual dedication. Minor physical scar pull, no reserve exhaustion.

*When: B+379, afternoon flight and sunset arrival.*

Ori insists on saying goodbye to the transport.

It is still standing in the field when they go to Kaisel. The platform has been unloaded, the test marks left in place. Mai walks under it with a piece of chalk between her teeth while Byakuya holds the edge of the diagram up for her.

“I'll be back tomorrow,” Ori tells the shikigami.

Its head follows Mau's small direction and lowers toward her. Ori pats the broad curve above its nostril. She puts both hands on it when one seems too little.

“Chloe's coming with you.”

Chloe raises a hand from the path. Milim has promised to help watch the next test and is already looking at the sky, trying to decide whether it will be windy. Maki has made her promise to watch from the ground.

Mau leaves a soldier at the agreed home anchor and checks the returning route before they mount. Bellion acknowledges the next day's arrangement. The long test will be on the empty land outside the town; Mau will return before it begins. Nobody flies until the builders are satisfied.

“If it takes longer, send word,” Shoko says.

“I will.”

“And eat dinner before you start doing things for the hotel.”

“I'm going to the hotel to eat dinner.”

“Good. Stay with that plan.”

She adjusts the edge of his blindfold where the hat has pushed it up, her touch brief and matter-of-fact. He stands still for her.

“Bring me back something ridiculous,” Gojo says.

Mau turns his head toward him.

“Would you settle for a mirror?”

Shoko leaves before either of them can look at her for a decision.

* * *

Kaisel carries the four of them with the travelling straps checked and the loose baggage already stored. Ori sits in front of Mau, Yuka close behind him, Frieren at the rear of the saddle where she can see over the folded brim of Ori's hat. They begin low over the road. Only when everybody has settled does Mau ask Kaisel to rise.

The town gets smaller beneath them.

Ori waves for a long time. Yuka catches her hat just as a gust begins working at its edge, passes it back and helps her tighten the tie. Frieren holds the end until Ori has both hands on the strap again.

“Now?” Ori asks.

“Now,” Mau says.

Kaisel climbs.

The wind grows steady against Mau's face. The blindfold takes away the worst of the detail, and what remains reaches him in shapes he can choose to attend to: the line of Kaisel's neck, the road passing behind them, Yuka's arm coming around his side to hold the forward strap. Her sleeve is a clear patch of colour against everything else. He knows where to look when the rest becomes too much.

Ori asks whether the island is already visible.

“Not yet.”

She waits a little longer.

“Now?”

Frieren leans forward enough to see her face.

“You'll know when he knows.”

“I know.”

She keeps watching.

Halfway through the flight, Yuka taps Mau's shoulder and asks him for the packet he packed within reach. He brings it out of the inventory into her hand. She peels the paper away while holding it against his back and feeds him the first piece before giving one to Ori.

“What is it?” Frieren asks.

“The biscuits we were supposed to save.”

“For what?”

Yuka considers the packet.

“Later.”

Frieren takes one.

* * *

They see the sea long before the island.

Ori stops asking questions. She leans forward, her hand spread across the strap at Mau's waist, and watches the moving surface reach farther than she can turn her head. He loosens the edge of the blindfold briefly, enough to see what she is pointing at: a vessel running south, its wake opening behind it.

“Will we go on one?”

“If you want.”

She looks back to Frieren.

“Can we?”

Frieren has been watching the water too.

“We can ask which ones come back before dark.”

“Some don't?”

“Some go very far.”

Ori turns toward the boat again. She stays with it until it is too small to pick out, then rests back against Mau's chest. He puts a hand over hers to tell her she can let the strap take some of the work.

Yuka slips her cheek against his shoulder.

“You told her she could choose a boat.”

“I did.”

“I'm choosing one too.”

“I suspected.”

“One with lunch.”

“That narrows it down.”

“And something soft to sit on.”

He turns his head enough to kiss her hair. The brim gets between them. She laughs, takes the hat off his head and sets it in her lap until he has done it properly.

Inside, DIO asks whether the small bright stretch near the coast is a beach or a roof. Mau lifts the cloth a little and gives her the view through the channel he agrees to open. He waits while she looks. Noise adds a brief shape to the edge of his attention, a suggestion of where the new shikigami could land tomorrow. Ciel holds the practical part of that thought until he asks for it.

For a while the three of them have nothing to tell him.

* * *

Elmesia is on the landing when they arrive.

The last of the sun lies across the water behind her. Two attendants wait by a trolley, which remains empty when Mau puts the saddlebags straight into his inventory. One looks at the trolley, then at the other attendant. Elmesia smiles without explaining.

“Welcome back.”

Yuka is already trying to look past the buildings toward the pool. Mau gets down first and helps Ori, who immediately turns around to watch Frieren descend. Yuka steps into his waiting hands as if he has been standing there for precisely that purpose.

“The bad sandals survived?” Elmesia asks.

Yuka looks down, pleased to have them recognized.

“They're comfortable.”

“Of course.”

Elmesia turns to Ori.

“And you must be the one who needs the early tide.”

Ori nods, then remembers to greet her. Frieren does the same with rather less urgency.

“There are shallow pools past the west steps,” Elmesia tells them. “Go after breakfast. I'll have someone show you which paths stay clear when the water rises.”

“Thank you,” Ori says.

She is trying not to look too excited while someone important is speaking. Mau has seen the attempt before. It usually lasts until she can find Chloe.

Elmesia walks them to the desk herself. The attendant recognizes the seal on Yuka's pass and writes their rooms down without presenting a bill. Elmesia adds Frieren and Ori to the neighbouring room for tonight and tells him the rest of the household will arrive tomorrow, after Mau has fetched them.

“There's no charge for the extra night,” she says before Mau can begin calculating anything. “Nor for deciding you want another after that. You've been told.”

“Several times,” Yuka says happily.

Mau looks at her.

“You sound like you believe it.”

“I read the card.”

“So did I.”

“Then stop looking for the small writing.”

Elmesia laughs and leaves them with their keys.

* * *

The room is familiar in the ways Yuka wanted it to be.

She finds the balcony first. The water is visible between the rail and the brim of her hat. Below, the path leads toward the pool where they ate breakfast on the last trip. She points out the turn to Mau, then steps aside so he can bring their things out one by one.

His shirts go where she tells him. Her dress stays in its cover. The notebook goes by the bed, open at the packing page until she has checked the last sandal.

“We brought too much,” he says.

“You aren't carrying it.”

“It's inside me.”

“Then it's very close. You should be happy.”

He looks at her for a moment, then reaches for her waist and pulls her back between his knees where he has sat on the edge of the bed.

“I can be happier.”

She lets the notebook fall onto the blanket and rests her arms around his shoulders. The room is warm from the afternoon. His shoulder seam tugs when he lifts one elbow too far; he changes its position, and her hand follows the adjustment without making him explain it.

“Dinner,” she says after a while.

“Shoko told you?”

“I'm hungry.”

“That's much more serious.”

She kisses his forehead, then lifts the brim off his head so she can kiss him properly.

* * *

They eat on the sheltered terrace.

DIO comes out near Mau after they have chosen a table. Elmesia brings her a chair without asking whether she will use it. Ori tells them about the boat. Frieren has ordered something with a name she would like explained before it arrives.

Makima waits until Mau looks toward the place beside the terrace door.

“May I?” she asks.

Yuka turns. Makima's attention moves to her before she makes any attempt to come farther.

“We're eating,” Yuka says. “You can sit here.”

She indicates the empty chair on Mau's other side of the table, with Elmesia between it and her own. Makima sits. An attendant brings a glass; she watches him pour, then studies the menu that Elmesia passes her.

Mau asks what she would like.

“What are you having?”

He tells her. She considers it with more concentration than the dish appears to require.

“Something else,” Elmesia suggests. “Then you can see which you prefer.”

Makima looks at the choices again. She picks one and closes the menu.

When the food arrives, Ori forgets all her earlier restraint and asks what everything is. She has a little taste from Frieren's plate, accepts a different piece from Yuka and declares that she would like to try both again before she decides.

Mau reaches for his bread. Yuka has already taken half.

“We haven't been here an hour,” he says.

“I'm making memories.”

“With my dinner.”

She holds the remaining half out to him. He eats it from her hand while Ori laughs.

Makima looks from Yuka's hand to his face. Then she takes the first bite of what she chose for herself.
