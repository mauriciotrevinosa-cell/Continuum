# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 81
## Chapter 7 — Yours and Mine

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 81
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E75
**Continuity state:** Ori morning POV transitions at breakfast. All travelers arrived together prior night; four-person outing while others choose own plans. Known bird caught harmlessly with learned Frieren spell, released by separate fruit bowl, still steals. No new familiar or injury. Existing island pass covers meal. Cloth remains voluntary visual moderation. First west-beach outing begins.

*When: B+380, first shared-holiday morning.*

Ori wakes before her mother.

At least she thinks she does. Frieren is lying with her eyes closed, one hand on the blanket, and the book beside the bed has not moved since Ori last checked it. The room is different enough that she has to look at the window before remembering where they are.

There is light around the curtain.

She slides one foot out, finds her sandal and changes her mind about putting it on in bed. It will be much quieter to carry both. She gets to the end of the mattress without making the frame creak and turns to see whether Frieren noticed.

“Your hat's on the other chair,” Frieren says. ᶠ

Ori stops.

“I wasn't leaving.” ᴼ

“All right.” ᶠ

“I was looking at the weather.” ᴼ

“How is it?” ᶠ

Ori opens the curtain just far enough to see the path. Beyond it is a piece of sea, very bright and apparently ready.

“Good.” ᴼ

Frieren sits up slowly. Ori brings her the hat she hasn't asked for yet, then her own. They check the little bag together: the cup she can see through, the cloth for shells, the string she wants to use if she finds something with a hole. Frieren adds water.

“We have to eat first,” she says. ᶠ

“I know.” ᴼ

Ori waits beside the door while she gets ready. She thinks about the shallows Elmesia described. She thinks about Chloe's shell and decides it should fit in Chloe's hand without being so little that she loses it.

When Frieren takes her hand, Ori is ready enough for both of them.

---

Mau and Yuka are already outside.

Ori sees his hat first. It projects from the side of the path at a height that means he is sitting down. Yuka is perched across his lap with her sandals dangling; she has one hand tucked into his shirt collar and is telling him something that makes him smile.

Then she sees Ori and lifts her head.

“We were coming to find you.” ʸ

Ori tries to go faster without pulling her mother. Frieren allows the change of pace. When they reach the bench, Mau sets Yuka carefully on her feet and crouches so Ori can show him the cup.

“You look through this.” ᴼ

“I see.” ᴹ

“At things in the water.” ᴼ

“That explains why you're showing me an empty cup.” ᴹ

Ori considers whether he is teasing. His mouth gives him away. She puts the cup in his hands so he can inspect it properly.

Yuka takes the little bag from her shoulder while they look. She checks the water, asks Frieren which path they intend to take and puts the bag on her own shoulder.

“I'll carry it until we get there.” ʸ

“It's mine.” ᴼ

“You can have it back when we need it.” ʸ

Ori thinks about that, then lets go of the strap.

---

The poolside tables are mostly empty.

A few guests eat under the shade. Someone swims slowly from one side of the pool to the other. The water reaches the edge with a small regular sound, and there is already a table set where Yuka wanted it.

There is also a bird.

Mau stops walking.

Yuka follows his attention and begins laughing before he says anything.

“You.” ᴹ

The bird turns its head.

Ori looks up at him, then at Yuka.

“Is that the one?” ᴼ

“That's the one,” Yuka says. ʸ

Frieren puts her bag down and examines the bird with interest.

“You said you were prepared.” ᶠ

“I am.” ᴹ

Mau lets the edge of the cloth lift for a moment. The bird has taken up a position on the back of his chair, one foot tucked beneath it, looking at the empty place where breakfast should be. He sets his hat down and raises one hand.

The spell catches the bird neatly before it can hop to the next chair.

Ori inhales.

“It's all right,” Frieren tells her. “It won't hurt him.” ᶠ

Mau holds the small creature still in the air, well clear of anything it can strike with a wing. He looks satisfied in a way Ori usually associates with finding something very useful in the shop.

“I would like,” he tells the attendant approaching the table, “a small bowl of fruit.” ᴹ

Yuka leans her elbows on the table.

“For breakfast?” ʸ

“For negotiations.” ᴹ

The attendant has worked here long enough to accept the request without commenting on the suspended bird. He asks what they would like for themselves. Yuka answers with the breakfast she described on the flight, remembering the changes she wants made to it and the drink Mau forgot to ask for last time.

Ori listens, impressed.

“Have you practised?” ᴼ

“I've been thinking about it.” ʸ

Mau looks at the bird.

“So have I.” ᴹ

---

He releases it beside the fruit bowl.

It takes one short, indignant hop before noticing what he has provided. The four of them watch while it examines a piece, pecks it and picks it up.

Mau points at the bowl.

“Yours.” ᴹ

The bird swallows.

He points at the place in front of his chair.

“Mine.” ᴹ

Yuka hides her face behind her cup. Ori waits to see whether the bird understands. Frieren has already opened the napkin on her lap.

Breakfast arrives in stages. Ori gets something she can break apart with her fingers. Frieren asks what the pale sauce is and accepts a little on the side. Yuka's plate has the changes she requested; she looks so pleased when she sees it that Mau stops watching the bird to watch her instead.

“Right?” she asks. ʸ

“Right.” ᴹ

She takes the first bite, closes her eyes for a second and pushes the plate toward him so he can try it too.

He leans forward.

The bird takes a piece of fruit from his plate.

It is gone before he gets his hand back to the table.

Yuka makes a noise into her cup. Frieren puts a hand over Ori's plate before the girl turns far enough to leave it unguarded.

Mau looks at the little bowl. It is still mostly full.

“We had an agreement.” ᴹ

The bird settles on the low wall with the stolen piece.

“You didn't get it in writing,” Frieren says. ᶠ

That finishes Yuka. She bends against Mau's shoulder, laughing so hard that he has to rescue the cup from her hand. Ori laughs too, partly because of the bird and partly because he looks so personally disappointed.

Mau puts the cup down, puts an arm around his wife and waits until she can breathe.

“I caught it,” he says. ᴹ

“You did,” she manages. ʸ

“Perfectly.” ᴹ

“Perfectly.” ʸ

“I gave it breakfast.” ᴹ

She nods against him.

“You're a very good host.” ʸ

The bird finishes its piece and begins looking at his plate again.

---

After a while Mau gives up dividing the fruit.

He moves his plate closer, keeps the things he wants within reach and lets the bird decide how much diplomatic progress it has made. Yuka eats some of her own breakfast, some of his and a little of the portion Frieren doesn't want. Ori tells them that this makes three breakfasts, which Yuka accepts as an accurate count.

“He's going to tell everybody,” Mau says of the bird. ᴹ

“That you feed people?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“That I lost.” ᴹ

Ori watches it hop along the wall. She looks at the full bowl and then at Mau.

“But it did get what you wanted it to have.” ᴼ

He looks at her.

“You think I won?” ᴹ

She considers it carefully.

“A little.” ᴼ

He reaches across and taps one finger gently against the brim of her hat.

“I'll take a little.” ᴹ

---

The path to the west steps is still quiet when they leave breakfast.

Frieren walks beside Ori. Mau and Yuka follow at a pace that lets the girl stop without anyone walking into her. She finds a smooth stone, decides it might be better than a shell and puts it back when Frieren tells her to see what the beach has first.

An attendant at the steps shows them where the water will reach and which dark strip of rock to keep away from. Mau listens. He asks about the return path, then lets Ori repeat the important part to him.

“We stay on this side.” ᴼ

“And when the water reaches that?” ᴹ

“We move.” ᴼ

“Good.” ᴹ

Yuka sits on the low step to take off her sandals. He crouches beside her and undoes the buckle she has made too tight. She puts a hand in his hair while he works.

“I can do the other one,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

She lets him do both.

Ori has taken hers off already and is standing at the edge of the sand, waiting with enormous effort. Frieren is folding the cloth they brought so it won't drag when Ori carries it.

Mau gets up with Yuka's sandals in one hand. Yuka takes the hats, stacks his above hers and balances both against her hip.

“Ready?” she asks. ʸ

Ori looks at Frieren. Frieren nods.

The girl runs the last few steps to the wet sand and stops just before the water touches her toes. She waits for it to come back, bends over the cup and calls them to see what she has found.

All three go to look.
