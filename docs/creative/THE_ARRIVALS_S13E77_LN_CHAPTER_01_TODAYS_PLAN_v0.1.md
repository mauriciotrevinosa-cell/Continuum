# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 81
## Chapter 10 — Today's Plan

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 81
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E77
**Continuity state:** Whole quiet couple day; established Authority pool use; physical scars retained, no reserve exhaustion. Affection nonsexual and pause intact.

*When: B+381, morning through early evening.*

Yuka opens one eye, looks at the bright line beneath the curtains and closes it again.

Mau is awake. She knows because the hand resting at her back moves when she presses closer. He has been lying still for long enough that she is fairly certain he has decided to wait for her.

She approves of the decision.

“Good morning,” he says. ᴹ

“Too early.” ʸ

“You haven't seen the clock.” ᴹ

“Then don't show me.” ʸ

She finds his wrist under the blanket and draws his arm farther around her. He follows willingly, shifting until she has the pillow the way she wants it. The large seam across his upper chest is pale beneath her hand. Farther down, the older marks cross and interrupt it in ways she knows by touch. A few of the little scratches he once brought home are hard to find now. The deep ones remain.

She runs a fingertip along the edge nearest his shoulder and stops when she reaches the part that still tightens if he stretches too quickly.

“Does this bother you today?” ʸ

He moves his shoulder a little, considering it.

“Only if I do that.” ᴹ

“Then don't do that.” ʸ

“Excellent medical advice.” ᴹ

She puts her hand back flat and rests her cheek beside it.

“I learned from Shoko.” ʸ

His laugh is quiet under her ear. She closes her eyes again.

---

They last another half hour before hunger becomes an opinion neither can ignore.

Mau suggests breakfast. Yuka agrees that breakfast should happen. Their disagreement is about the amount of walking it ought to involve.

“The pool's not far.” ᴹ

“I know where the pool is.” ʸ

“We can ask for food there.” ᴹ

She lifts her head.

“The little table in the water?” ʸ

“If you want.” ᴹ

Her expression changes at once.

“The eggs on toast. The potatoes with the little peppers, but the peppers beside them. And the fruit with the thick yogurt.” ʸ

Mau has not finished moving the blanket.

“Honey?” ᴹ

“Separate. I want to try it first. And the coffee you had last time, with milk, but not in the coffee. Beside it.” ʸ

“We're going to need a second table for all the things beside your breakfast.” ᴹ

“You can hold them.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“You're very awake suddenly.” ᴹ

“I was awake before.” ʸ

“You told me it was too early.” ᴹ

“It was.” ʸ

He gets up. She follows him as far as sitting on the edge of the bed, then holds out the dress she has selected from the chair.

“Help.” ʸ

He takes it.

“Can you describe the emergency?” ᴹ

“My husband is standing up and I'm not.” ʸ

“Serious.” ᴹ

“Very.” ʸ

He helps with the fastening she can't easily reach, hands her the rest and goes to dress himself while she finishes. When he comes back she has the enormous hat in place and both sandals in her hands.

“Breakfast,” she announces. ʸ

He looks at the sandals, then at her.

She raises her arms.

“Breakfast,” she repeats. ʸ

---

They pass Shoko on the way down.

She is reading a sheet with Gojo's handwriting on it and has the expression she generally reserves for things he is about to explain. She looks up as Mau approaches carrying Yuka, the hat brim occupying most of the space between their faces.

“Everything all right?” ˢʰ

“Perfect,” Yuka says. ʸ

Shoko looks at Mau.

“I was given instructions.” ᴹ

“I see.” ˢʰ

Yuka lifts the brim so she can look at her properly.

“What did he do?” ʸ

Shoko turns the sheet around. It appears to be a proposal for an activity that requires every condition Elmesia has advised guests to avoid.

“I'm hoping it's a joke,” she says. ˢʰ

“Will you ask him?” Mau says. ᴹ

“I'm going to take him somewhere with witnesses.” ˢʰ

Yuka laughs against his shoulder. Shoko steps aside to let them pass, then calls after them to eat something that has more substance than fruit.

“She means you,” Mau tells his wife. ᴹ

“She looked at you.” ʸ

“You were holding the hat in front of me.” ᴹ

“Then she made an informed decision.” ʸ

---

The table in the shallow part of the pool is available.

Yuka puts her sandals on the dry edge and slips into the water with a little intake of breath. Mau follows after setting their hats well back from the splash. She holds out her hands to him. He comes close enough that she can guide him onto the seat beside her and put one knee against his.

The attendant remembers her breakfast.

Yuka looks pleased until he asks whether she wants it exactly the same.

“Could the toast come dry? The eggs can stay on a little plate. I want to put them together myself.” ʸ

“Another beside,” Mau murmurs. ᴹ

She puts a hand on his knee beneath the water.

“And the potatoes can have the peppers. I've reconsidered.” ʸ

The attendant makes the correction without betraying them by laughing.

“And for you?” ᵃᵗᵗ

“The hot sandwiches. Two. Whatever pickles come with them, please leave them on my plate until she's looked at her own.” ᴹ

Yuka waits until the man has gone before turning to him.

“I wasn't going to take your pickles.” ʸ

“I admire your certainty.” ᴹ

“Half your breakfast is already mine.” ᴹ

“You're welcome.” ʸ

She splashes water over her own forearm, looks toward the quiet end of the pool and begins planning where they will sit afterward. There are canopied daybeds beneath a line of palms, spaced far enough apart that she can hear the water between the voices.

“That one,” she says. ʸ

Mau follows the line of her hand.

“Shade?” ᴹ

“And you.” ʸ

“I come with the other ones too.” ᴹ

“Then you can pick the next one.” ʸ

He puts an arm along the back of her seat. She leans into it without looking, exactly where she expects him to be.

They spend a while discussing a boat they haven't chosen.

Yuka wants the one with lunch and a place to lie down. Mau wants to know whether it actually goes anywhere or simply moves the dining room into the sea. She says both sound good. He says they might be able to achieve the same result by ordering more food right here.

She points at him with her fork.

“That's today's plan.” ʸ

“You had a plan?” ᴹ

“I told you when I woke up.” ʸ

“You told me not to show you the clock.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸ

Breakfast takes a long time. Partway through, Anko and Hinata pass the pool with Luminous. Anko is wearing a light overshirt and holding two different maps. She stops to ask whether Mau knows which stairs lead to the shorter walk.

“The ones on the other side,” Hinata answers. ᴴⁱ

“I wanted a second opinion.” ᴬⁿ

“He hasn't done the walk.” ᴴⁱ

“I liked his expression.” ᴬⁿ

Mau holds both hands up out of the water.

“I support whatever gets you to lunch.” ᴹ

Anko points at him as if he has resolved the disagreement. Luminous keeps walking. Hinata takes Anko's hand and brings her along before she can ask anyone else.

Yuka watches them go.

“She looks happy.” ʸ

“She is.” ᴹ

“You should sit with her later.” ʸ

“If she comes back from the stairs.” ᴹ

“I'll make room.” ʸ

She says it while settling farther against him. He kisses the top of her head and finishes the breakfast she has graciously left on his plate.

---

The daybed is better than Yuka expected.

She tests the pillow, the edge nearest the sun and the angle from which she can see the water. Mau stands beside it holding their things until she decides where he belongs. Then he puts the bag down, sits and is immediately required to lie back so she can use him instead of the pillow.

He arranges the blindfold, lets the shaded light remain at the edge of his perception and rests a hand over her waist.

“You chose a very specific piece of furniture.” ᴹ

“You said you came with it.” ʸ

She turns onto her side so she can look at him. Her fingers find the back of his neck. For a while she traces the hair there, the uneven shorter pieces near the old scar, and he lets the little touch occupy his attention.

Beyond the curtains, someone is discussing whether the water will be warmer farther out. A child at another daybed has decided it already is. The sound of footsteps goes past and fades.

Yuka kisses his cheek, settles back down and asks him to tell her which part of the trip he has been looking forward to.

He thinks about it properly.

“Going out with you and not having to come back because something's waiting.” ᴹ

She moves her head so she can see his face.

“Something's always waiting.” ʸ

“It can wait.” ᴹ

Her smile comes slowly, then stays.

He watches it for a moment before drawing her closer. Yuka shifts over him, one hand settling beside his head, and meets him halfway. The kiss lasts long enough that she has to move her elbow to keep from losing her balance. He laughs against her mouth and holds her more securely.

“You distracted me,” she says when she can look at him again. ʸ

“From what?” ᴹ

She considers, then bends to kiss him once more instead of answering.

“I like that answer.” ʸ

“I thought you might.” ᴹ

She reaches across him for the drink on the small table and discovers that it is just beyond her fingers. He brings it closer. She takes a sip, gives him one and puts it where she can reach next time.

---

At midday they go into the water again.

Mau supports himself with the quiet application of Authority he already knows, checking the movement before he lets Yuka settle across him. Her head rests against his stomach. Her legs trail in the water, and one hand stays loosely over his wrist while they drift within the shallow, quiet part of the pool.

“Don't let me sink,” she says. ʸ

“You're holding my wrist.” ᴹ

“Then you know where I am.” ʸ

He moves his thumb over her fingers. She closes her eyes.

They stay like that until she decides she wants lunch. He is hungry too, which she appears to find highly suspicious after all the breakfast he has had.

“I had a portion of breakfast,” he tells her. ᴹ

“A good portion.” ʸ

“Very carefully selected.” ᴹ

She opens one eye, looks up at him and smiles.

“I'll let you order something else.” ʸ

He brings them nearer the steps before either of them gets tempted to negotiate the entire meal while floating.

---

In the late afternoon they are still at the daybed.

Yuka has borrowed a slim novel from the shelf near the desk. Its title, The House with the Locked Garden, made her expect considerably more gardening than the first chapter contains.

She gets to the bottom of a page and lowers it onto Mau's chest.

“Why doesn't she ask for the key?” ʸ

He reads the place she indicates. The woman in the story has climbed the garden wall three times to visit the music shop. This time she has lost a shoe on the other side.

“Her father said she couldn't go.” ᴹ

“He said she couldn't go through the front gate.” ʸ

“I suspect the wall was included in the spirit of the arrangement.” ᴹ

Yuka turns the book around to check.

“She's twenty-six.” ʸ

“Then perhaps he shouldn't have the key.” ᴹ

“That's much better.” ʸ

He takes the book from her and reads the next paragraph aloud. The woman retrieves her shoe by throwing the other one after it, hoping the shopkeeper will notice the pair.

Mau stops.

“I withdraw my support. She's going to kill someone with footwear.” ᴹ

Yuka starts laughing. She reaches for the book, misses because he has kept it above her shoulder and moves across him to get it properly. He lifts his free arm around her back, steadies her and lets her win.

“Keep reading,” she tells him, putting it back in his hand. ʸ

“From the scene of the crime?” ᴹ

“From where you stopped.” ʸ

She lies with her cheek against his upper arm while he finds the place. The shopkeeper has indeed noticed the shoes. Unfortunately, he thinks they have fallen from the laundry above him and spends the next page trying to return them to a stranger.

They are still laughing when Yuka declares that the woman ought to have thrown a note.

Yuka sits up to rescue the book from beneath the towel. Mau follows her, but only as far as putting his head in her lap.

She looks down at him. He takes her free hand and places it in his hair.

“Oh,” she says. ʸ

“Is this place taken?” ᴹ

“Very.” ʸ

He begins to lift his head. She pushes gently against his shoulder until he lies back down.

“By you. Don't be annoying.” ʸ

He closes his eyes, smiling. She finds a comfortable place for her feet, bends to kiss him and returns to the paragraph they abandoned. Her fingers stay in his hair. When a longer piece catches at the knot of his blindfold, she frees it without disturbing the cloth.

“She's going to throw something else,” he murmurs. ᴹ

“If you keep interrupting, so will I.” ʸ

He laughs once, then grows quiet. Yuka reads another page aloud before realizing that he has missed the shoe being returned to its actual owner. His hand rests against her knee, loose enough that she can turn it over and fit her fingers through his without waking him.

She tries to reach her drink. It is too far away. After considering the distance and the man asleep across her legs, she asks the attendant passing the curtain to move it closer.

“Anything else?” he asks softly. ᵃᵗᵗ

She looks at the empty little plate.

“The same thing again, please.” ʸ

Mau sleeps through its arrival. Yuka eats, reads two more chapters and occasionally gives his hair the attention he claimed before going to sleep. She has an objection ready for him when his eyes finally open beneath the cloth.

“You missed the best part.” ʸ

He turns his face toward her stomach and holds her waist for a moment before looking up.

“I disagree.” ᴹ

She puts the book down, caught smiling at him.

“You'll have to read it yourself now.” ʸ

“Will you stay here while I do?” ᴹ

“You could try getting rid of me.” ʸ

He sits up just enough to kiss her. She keeps a hand behind his head until he has stopped looking so pleased with his own answer.

By the time the attendant comes to ask about dinner, Mau has settled beside her again and opened the book. Yuka takes the offered menu, lays it across the page and begins explaining what she wants him to order.
