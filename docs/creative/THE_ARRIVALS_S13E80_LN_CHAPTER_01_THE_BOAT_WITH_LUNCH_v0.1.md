# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 81
## Chapter 15 — The Boat with Lunch

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 81
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S13E80
**Continuity state:** Existing Elmesia seal covers boat and food; selected ordinary itinerary, no universal teleportation. Couple relaxed exploration and playful mutual affection. Mau vision remains grey except Yuka and moderated by cloth; no reserve depletion. Physical scars/fingers retained; Yuka pause remains. Genuine own wishes and gentle activities, no new attack.

*When: B+384, couple boat day.*

The boat has lunch.

Yuka establishes this before she asks where it goes. The man at the pier shows her the shaded seats, explains the route around the sheltered side of the island and points to the place where they will stop if the water stays calm. She listens with one hand tucked into Mau's elbow.

“And we come back before dinner?” she asks. ʸ

“Plenty of time before,” he answers. ᵃᵗᵗ

She looks up at Mau.

“This one.” ʸ

He asks about the water, the return route and whether the bench on the far side stays in shade. The man answers all three without appearing to think them unusual questions. When Mau takes out the pass, he recognizes the seal and writes their names on the page for the day.

Yuka watches the pen stop after Mau's first name.

“Ieiri,” she supplies. ʸ

Mau turns toward her.

She keeps her face perfectly composed while the man finishes writing.

“I've been wanting to say it,” she tells him when they step aboard. ʸ

He takes her hand to help her over the gap.

“You can.” ᴹ

* * *

They leave the pier slowly enough to watch the island rearrange itself.

Buildings that looked close from the path separate into different levels. The wall they climbed yesterday becomes a line above the roofs. Yuka picks out their turn, then the wrong one, and points at the little patch of shade where they sat.

“We could have waved to the boat,” she says. ʸ

“We hadn't chosen it.” ᴹ

“It would have been polite.” ʸ

He lets her keep narrating until the view changes. The wind makes a serious attempt at her hat, which they anticipated well enough to keep it in the bag. Her hair is less cooperative. She catches a strand in her mouth, makes an annoyed sound and turns so he can help.

He gathers it back with both hands and waits while she finds the tie she put somewhere convenient.

It is not in the first pocket. Or the second.

She looks at the little bag suspiciously.

“I put it in here.” ʸ

“I'm in no hurry.” ᴹ

“You say that because you aren't looking.” ʸ

“Would you like me to let go?” ᴹ

She puts a hand over his wrist immediately.

“Absolutely not.” ʸ

He laughs and keeps holding her hair until she finds the tie around her own wrist.

Neither comments on this discovery. The restraint costs him something; she can see it at the corner of his mouth.

* * *

The shaded seat is wide enough for Yuka to sit facing him with one knee tucked beside his leg.

She takes advantage of the space as soon as the boat has settled into its course. He moves the cushion behind her back, puts the bag where neither can kick it over and accepts the hand she lays against his cheek.

“Can you see enough?” she asks. ʸ

“Enough of what I want.” ᴹ

She narrows her eyes at him.

“That was very prepared.” ʸ

“I've been waiting for you to ask.” ᴹ

“Terrible.” ʸ

He kisses the inside of her wrist. She loses the expression she was trying to hold and leans forward to rest against him.

They spend the first part of the route watching the shore. Mau keeps the cloth in place and attends to broad shapes, the slope of the island against the water and the patch of clear colour that is Yuka beside him. When she points at something smaller, he lets her tell him about it instead of forcing the detail into focus.

She is very good at telling him exactly what she means. Occasionally she changes her mind halfway through, corrects the whole description and insists the original thing was interesting too.

“I believe you,” he says. ᴹ

“You sound entertained.” ʸ

“I'm listening.” ᴹ

“You can do both.” ʸ

He puts his arm around her and does.

* * *

Lunch arrives while she is telling him where she would put the bed if she owned a room facing this side of the water.

The crew member sets the dishes on the little table and shows them the catch that was prepared for today. Yuka asks which part he recommends trying first. Mau waits until she has made her selection, then takes a different piece so she can compare them.

He is halfway through his second bite when he notices her watching him.

“What?” ᴹ

“You haven't asked.” ʸ

“What would I be asking?” ᴹ

“For mine.” ʸ

He looks at her plate. She has already set aside a little portion for him.

“I was being patient.” ᴹ

“You've been looking at it.” ʸ

“You chose well.” ᴹ

She puts the portion on his plate, takes the one he offers and sits back against the cushion to consider the difference. He waits for her conclusion. It changes after she tries the second sauce.

“Both,” she says at last. ʸ

“A diplomatic answer.” ᴹ

“An accurate one.” ʸ

He agrees. They spend the rest of lunch comparing things rather than keeping track of whose plate each came from.

* * *

At the stop they go ashore with a basket and their sandals in hand.

The crew points out the clear place to walk and the uneven stones farther along. Yuka thanks them and leads Mau toward the strip of sand where they can sit with their feet near the water. The boat stays within sight, turning gently against its line.

“We could stay here for a while,” she says. ʸ

“We have a while.” ᴹ

She sits first. He joins her, sets the basket behind them and lets her put her legs across his lap. She examines the mark the sandal has made against one ankle and tells him the other buckle is better. He listens as seriously as she intends him to.

After a few minutes she grows quiet.

Mau waits without filling the space. She has both hands around one of his, her thumb moving slowly across the damaged fingers. The boat creaks against its rope. Water comes close enough to wet the lower edge of their footprints and goes away again.

“I liked yesterday,” she says. ʸ

“Even the gate?” ᴹ

“Especially when you admitted the map was right.” ʸ

He leans his head against hers.

“You waited until I checked.” ᴹ

“I wanted you to have the full experience.” ʸ

She laughs softly, then moves so her forehead rests against the side of his face.

“I like this too.” ʸ

He puts his free hand over the two she has around his.

“So do I.” ᴹ

* * *

They walk before going back aboard.

Yuka finds a flat stone that skims once when she throws it and promptly decides that it should have gone farther. Mau finds another and hands it to her. She tries again. This time it skips twice.

She looks toward him with immediate suspicion.

He lifts both hands.

“That was yours.” ᴹ

“You didn't help?” ʸ

“I found the stone.” ᴹ

She makes him try one himself. He chooses badly and sends a perfectly ordinary splash into the water.

Yuka stares at the place it vanished, then turns toward him with such delight that he knows he has lost the next several minutes.

“I can teach you,” she offers. ʸ

“Please do.” ᴹ

She finds him a flatter stone and demonstrates the wrist movement twice. He follows the instruction, gets one small skip and receives extravagant praise for the improvement.

“You're very kind,” he tells her. ᴹ

“I know. You're lucky.” ʸ

He pulls her close enough to kiss her. She keeps laughing against his mouth until he has to stop laughing too.

* * *

On the way back she sits with her head against his chest.

The afternoon has left her pleasantly heavy. Her hand rests beneath his on the cushion, and when the boat changes direction she presses her cheek a little closer rather than sitting up.

Mau watches the shape of the shore return. He thinks of the people already on it: Ori with a plan he will hear about later, Shoko with coffee, the conversations they can walk into when they feel like it.

Yuka's breathing changes. He looks down.

She opens one eye before he can decide she has fallen asleep.

“I'm awake.” ʸ

“Of course.” ᴹ

“Don't put my hat on me if I sleep.” ʸ

“Why would I do that?” ᴹ

“Because it would be funny.” ʸ

He considers it for just long enough to make her lift her head.

“Mau.” ʸ

He kisses her forehead.

“No hat.” ᴹ

She settles back down, satisfied with the agreement. He keeps his arm around her until the man at the pier comes into view with the rope, and only then does he tell her they are almost home for the evening.
