Shuna asks the cook about a dish, and the conversation takes longer than lunch.

Mau notices when the last plates have gone and she is still standing beside the open service doorway, asking how the vegetables were cut before they reached the pan. The cook shows her with his hands. She asks another question. He looks around for something to demonstrate with.

Elmesia comes up beside Mau.

“She's welcome to look inside,” she says. ᴱˡ

“You should tell her.” ᴹ

“I just did.” ᴱˡ

Shuna has heard. She turns, asks whether it would interrupt the work and receives an answer that sends her through the doorway with immediate interest. Shion follows as far as the threshold before the cook gives her something to carry and shows her where to set it down.

Yuka watches them disappear.

“We lost them.” ʸ

“Temporarily,” Elmesia says. ᴱˡ

Mau looks into the kitchen, where Shuna has already found the surface she wants to examine.

“That's what we said about the card game.” ᴹ

They spend the afternoon near enough that Shuna can come out and tell them what she has learned.

It happens in several visits. The first concerns the way the stove holds heat. The second is about an ingredient she wants to find before she returns home. By the third, Yuka has pulled up another chair and Elmesia has asked for a little plate so they can understand the part Shuna is describing.

Mau tries it. He waits, tastes another little piece and looks at the kitchen doorway.

“That would go well with the one you make at home.” ᴹ

Shuna's face changes.

“Yes,” she says. ˢʰᵘ

The answer comes with such certainty that Yuka turns toward her.

“You miss it.” ʸ

Shuna rests her hands around the empty edge of the plate.

“I like this kitchen. I like talking to someone who has tried different things. But I keep thinking of where I'd put them when I get back to mine.” ˢʰᵘ

Elmesia smiles.

“That's a good reason to travel.” ᴱˡ

“It is.” ˢʰᵘ

Shuna looks toward the room she has been visiting, then at the path leading back to their own rooms.

“I'd like to go home tomorrow.” ˢʰᵘ

She tells Rimuru before dinner.

He is sitting with Shizue and the students near the shaded courtyard, listening to Kenya explain why one of the games has been scored incorrectly. Shizue is letting him make the complete case before she points out that the rule he is citing was added afterward.

Shuna waits until they finish. Rimuru looks up and asks if she found something good in the kitchen.

She tells him. Then she tells him the rest.

“I can arrange the return,” Mau says when she looks toward him. ᴹ

Shion has come up behind her, carrying the little package the cook gave them.

“I'll go with you.” ˢʰⁱ

“You can stay.” ˢʰᵘ

“I know.” ˢʰⁱ

“Someone has to make sure she brings the ingredients inside before she starts trying things.” ˢʰⁱ

“You mean before you try them,” Shuna says. ˢʰᵘ

Shion considers whether a denial is worth the effort. She decides it is not and opens the package so Rimuru can see.

Mau checks the return arrangements with Bellion that evening.

The report reaches him through the established connection, concise where it concerns the posts and a little more detailed where a particular part of the domestic work needs explaining. Nobody has entered a private room without authorization. The plant has received the measured water. Something left beneath the shared table has been put aside rather than treated as rubbish.

Mau tells Bellion where Shuna and Shion will arrive and what to have clear. Bellion acknowledges the orders. There is no need to reopen the guard arrangement every time a member of the house comes home.

Beru stands beside Mau while he listens, wearing the hat he already has from Ori's earlier gift. He has taken it off for the report and holds it with both hands.

“My king,” he says when Mau's attention returns, “the princess asked whether Lantern could join the game tomorrow.” ᴮᵉ

“Which game?” ᴹ

“There are several under consideration.” ᴮᵉ

“That sounds like her.” ᴹ

“I told her you would like to know the rules first.” ᴮᵉ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“She said that was why she was writing them.” ᴮᵉ

The next morning Shuna spends more time wrapping the cook's parting gift than packing her clothes.

The ingredients have labels and instructions. She has written back with a recipe he asked about, and the last exchange at the kitchen door takes long enough that Shion puts their bag down and leans against the wall.

“You can write,” Elmesia reminds them. ᴱˡ

The cook looks relieved. Shuna laughs and promises she will.

At the cleared return place, Yuka gives Shuna a packet they found in the market and explains why she thinks it might go with something from home. Shuna listens, reads the label and thanks her with such pleasure that Yuka looks at Mau for a moment afterward.

He squeezes her hand.

Shion tells Milim that somebody will have to report what happens in the next game. Milim promises a version that will be completely accurate as long as she wins.

Rimuru comes down with Shizue. He thanks Shuna for the part of the trip she spent feeding people who were already being fed. She tells him that he should try the new thing before discussing it, and he agrees much too quickly to suggest that this will be a hardship.

Mau brings Shuna and Shion back with their things when everyone has finished. Yuka remains with the exchanged soldier until the interval has passed and he returns. There is already a new packet in his hand: Shuna found something in her kitchen that the island cook ought to try.

“Home?” she asks. ʸ

“Home.” ᴹ

Lunch is quiet for about thirty seconds.

Then somebody asks what Shuna would have made of the dish, and the conversation becomes an argument between people who have eaten enough of her cooking to possess very confident opinions. Mau offers one. Yuka disagrees with him. Rimuru joins in and receives a look from Shizue that suggests she is deciding whether to warn him.

“We can ask her when we get back,” Yuka says. ʸ

“We can,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

“And you'll admit if I was right.” ʸ

“I'll ask for evidence.” ᴹ

She takes a little piece from his plate and tastes it with exaggerated seriousness.

“I have evidence.” ʸ

He laughs and moves the plate into the middle so she can examine the rest.
