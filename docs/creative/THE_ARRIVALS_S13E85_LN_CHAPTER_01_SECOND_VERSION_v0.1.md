## Chapter 26 — Second Version

*When: B+389, afternoon kite workshop through evening.*

The paper is too thin for what Milim intends to do with it.

Mau knows this before she has explained the design because she is holding two sticks farther apart than the table. Ori is looking from one to the other with the expression she wears when an idea is becoming bigger in front of her.

“We can make a very big one,” Milim says. ᴹⁱ

“Or,” says Byakuya, “we can make one that goes up.” ᵇʸᵃᵏᵘʸᵃ

She looks offended by the distinction.

Yuka comes around the table carrying the packet the workshop gave them. Elmesia has arranged the use of a clear stretch of beach, and the woman who teaches the activity has laid out paper, cloth, light frames and enough string to suggest that she has met ambitious guests before.

“Start with these,” she tells Milim, handing her two smaller sticks. ᵃᵗᵗ

Milim takes them. She looks at Ori.

“First version.” ᴹⁱ

Ori nods with renewed purpose.

---

The kites divide into styles as soon as people begin drawing.

Coco sketches a shape she wants to try before choosing where to put the colour. Her mother holds the paper while Tetia suggests an addition and Agott tells them how much edge they will need to leave. Richeh begins with a much smaller design and does not explain why until the woman comes to look at its balance.

Marin has brought a pattern she wants Wakana to help her fit to the cloth. He asks what happens to it when the wind pulls the corners. She considers this, changes the angle and gives him the pencil.

Mau and Yuka have been given a plain frame.

“What do you want on it?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks at the sheet, then at the sea.

“Something we can recognize from far away.” ʸ

“Your hat?” ᴹ

She turns toward him.

“My hat flies perfectly well on its own.” ʸ

He laughs and holds the frame steady while she decides.

She draws a bird.

Mau closes his eyes beneath the cloth for a moment.

“You knew what you were doing.” ᴹ

“It's a very recognizable bird.” ʸ

---

He is permitted to make one alteration.

Yuka studies the result, looks at him and starts laughing. He has given the bird a little bowl of fruit and a smaller, more suspicious object under its wing.

“Is that yours?” she asks. ʸ

“Evidence.” ᴹ

She adds a second piece of stolen fruit.

“Be accurate.” ʸ

He leaves the rest of the drawing to her. His part is to tie the frame, keep the tension even and listen while the woman explains how the line will attach. He asks her to show him the knot again. She does. Yuka holds the other end so he can work around the fingers that do not bend as easily.

“There,” she says when it lies flat. ʸ

He runs a thumb over the knot and gives it a gentle pull. It holds.

Across the table, Maki has already made a practical little kite that looks better able to withstand a disagreement than most of the others. Yuta is adding a small mark where she told him it wouldn't affect anything important. Gojo watches long enough to receive an instruction to help with his own.

Shoko has selected a very simple pattern.

“Simple,” she reminds him. ˢʰ

“I was going to improve the tail.” ᴳ

“Tie it first.” ˢʰ

He ties it.

---

DIO comes with Elmesia after they have finished at the glass workshop.

They have acquired the completed tile, which DIO carries wrapped in both hands until Mau finds a place in his inventory where it will lie safely beside the vessel. She watches him store it, satisfied that the two will stay together.

Elmesia takes one look at the tables and asks whether there is enough material left for another attempt.

“There is for a small one,” the woman says. ᵃᵗᵗ

“That sounds like a warning.” ᴱˡ

“Experience.” ᵃᵗᵗ

DIO sits beside Elmesia. They compare the sample frame with the empty cloth and begin choosing something neither has any obligation to make well.

Makima takes the place at the end of Mau's table when he gestures to it. She has been watching the preparations from the shaded path, close enough to join if she chooses.

“Would you like one?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks at the remaining materials.

“Are you making it with me?” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks up from the bird's particularly criminal expression. Mau keeps one hand on their frame.

“I'm finishing this one with Yuka. We can help you get yours started.” ᴹ

Makima takes in the distinction. It would be easy to ask again in a different form. She looks at the smaller frame the woman offers and puts both hands on it instead.

“All right.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka moves the spare pencil closer to her without making a speech about it.

---

The first launch belongs to Ori and Milim.

They have produced something with a broad face, a carefully repaired corner and a tail that Seika has persuaded them to shorten. Milim holds it up while Ori takes the line. The woman shows them where to stand, waits for a change in the breeze and gives the signal.

Milim lets go.

The kite rises, tilts and buries its nose in the sand.

Ori looks at it.

Milim looks at Ori.

“Second version,” she says. ᴹⁱ

Byakuya is laughing before he can pretend he is examining the line. He comes over, shows them where the pull has been sitting and helps Ori move it a little.

Mau waits behind the marked place with Yuka. She has both hands around the rolled line while he holds their kite above his shoulder.

“If yours goes up,” Milim tells him, “we're inspecting it.” ᴹⁱ

“You helped write the conditions,” he answers. ᴹ

“That's why.” ᴹⁱ

Yuka nods at him. He releases the kite when the wind catches it, and she takes two quick steps backward before finding the pull.

The bird goes up.

Mau gets a hand beneath hers when the line tightens. She turns toward him, delighted, while the little drawing climbs above the palms with its stolen fruit.

“Look,” she says, as if he could have missed what they are doing. ʸ

He looks at her first, then at the shape she is pointing to.

---

More kites join it.

Some rise easily. Some have to be repaired. One of Marin's decorative pieces comes loose and becomes briefly more successful at flying than the kite it was attached to. Wakana retrieves it before the next gust can take it out of reach.

Yuta discovers that Maki has made something that pulls much harder than its modest size suggests. She takes the line from him, tests it and looks pleased. Gojo manages to launch Shoko's kite on the first attempt and is still celebrating when it turns and dives past his shoulder.

Shoko catches the line near the reel, waits for the turn and sends it up again.

“Like that,” she tells him. ˢʰ

Mau almost loses his grip from laughing.

Yuka nudges him.

“Our bird.” ʸ

He puts his attention back where it belongs.

---

Makima's first attempt goes sideways.

She brings it down before it can reach the people standing near the path. She examines the frame, then the line. Mau sees her considering the result and comes over when Yuka tells him she has their bird for a moment.

“Want another hand?” he asks. ᴹ

Makima looks at him.

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

He holds the kite while the woman changes the point of the pull. Makima watches closely. The adjustment is small enough that she would have been tempted to dismiss it if she had not just seen the consequence herself.

The next attempt rises.

She holds the line, feeling the little changes arrive from something too far away for her to put a hand on. Mau stays beside her until she has found the angle, then steps back toward Yuka when she calls his name.

Makima watches him go for a second. Then she looks up at what she made and keeps it in the air.

---

DIO and Elmesia have made a crooked little shape that requires both of them to discuss which side is wrong.

Their first launch lasts longer than Ori's and shorter than they would like. DIO apologizes to the kite when it hits the sand. Elmesia tells her the kite is being unreasonable and lifts it by the frame to inspect it.

They try again.

This time the line catches. DIO holds it while Elmesia steadies her wrist from the side, both of them looking upward. The little shape moves above them, awkward and unmistakably flying.

DIO laughs.

Elmesia looks at her instead of the kite for half a second too long. DIO's hand shifts, the line slackens, and they both have to pay attention again.

Mau sees Yuka notice it from the corner of her eye.

“Don't,” he murmurs. ᴹ

“I haven't said anything.” ʸ

He knows exactly what her smile means and stays beside her while she enjoys not saying it.

---

Ori and Milim succeed on the fourth attempt.

They count the first repair as its own version, so there is a brief disagreement about which attempt this makes. It ends when the kite stays up long enough for Ori to forget the count entirely.

She looks toward Frieren, who is sitting just beyond the line of shade with her book open but unread.

“Mama!” ᴼ

Frieren raises her face.

“I see.” ᶠ

“It's staying.” ᴼ

“It is.” ᶠ

Ori turns toward Mau and Yuka next, demanding witnesses from every important direction. Milim holds the line with her, making her own hands very still so the girl can feel the pull.

Lantern lies farther along the beach, head lifted to watch. The tails move above him without anyone needing him to do anything.

---

They take the kites down before the evening wind grows stronger.

Mau winds the line while Yuka brings the bird back to him. It has survived with one crumpled edge and all of its evidence intact. She puts it against his chest and holds it there while he finishes.

“We're keeping it.” ʸ

“Where?” ᴹ

“I'll decide at home.” ʸ

He lets her fold it the way the woman showed them, keeping the frame supported while the kite is still in their hands. Then he stores it. She has tied a little ribbon around the folded frame so she can recognize hers when all the kites come out together.

Yuka brushes sand from his new shirt and finds that she has put more on it than she removed. He looks down. She looks at her hands.

They both start laughing.

Mau catches her hands before she can try to improve the shirt again. She looks up, still laughing, and he kisses her with the kite line looped loosely around his wrist. Her fingers close over his. When he draws back, she follows him for another kiss, rising onto her toes until he bends farther to meet her.

“Your shirt is still dirty,” she tells him. ʸ

“It has suffered enough.” ᴹ

She leaves it alone and stays beneath his arm while they finish putting the things away.

Behind them, Milim is already asking whether the larger sticks are still available for tomorrow.
