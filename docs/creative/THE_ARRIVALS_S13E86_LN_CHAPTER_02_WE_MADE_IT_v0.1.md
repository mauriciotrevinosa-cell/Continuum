## Chapter 29 — We Made It

*When: B+391, cooking lesson dinner and walk home.*

The large parcel takes exactly as long as the cook said it would.

Ori and Chloe spend most of that time standing where they can see the tray without being in his way. Each checks on the other's patience. Neither intends to be the one who suggests taking it out early.

Mau sets the dishes from his own table down beside Yuka's and looks across at them.

“Do you need somewhere to sit?” he asks. ᴹ

Ori shakes her head.

“We're waiting.” ᴼ

“I noticed.” ᴹ

Chloe looks at the tray, then toward the cook.

“It's almost ready.” ᶜʰ

Mau leaves them the place they have chosen. When he turns, Yuka is putting their extra portion into the space Umaru has already made on the table.

“We were successful,” Yuka tells him. ʸ

“We've supplied a second household.” ᴹ

Umaru looks offended by the implication that this might be a problem.

---

They begin eating before anyone has organized an official comparison.

This lasts until Marin asks whether somebody tried the version she and Wakana made. She is certain the shape has improved it. Wakana says the shape changed how long it took to cook, which the cook confirms when asked. Marin accepts the explanation as support for her original argument.

Maki gives Yuta the first piece of theirs. He tastes it, thinks and makes a suggestion so careful that she asks him to stop preparing the sentence and say the actual thing.

“A little more spring onion next time,” he says, pointing. ʸᵗ

She tastes it too.

“Yes.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He looks relieved, which makes her smile into her plate.

At the next table Gojo says he was responsible for ensuring an even distribution of filling. Shoko points out that he achieved this by eating a sample from every bowl.

“Quality control,” he says. ᴳ

“Then explain why ours is the only tray with one empty space.” ˢʰ

He looks at the tray as though the missing pie is news to him. Yuta starts laughing before Gojo has finished examining the evidence.

Yuka rests a hand on Mau's forearm while they listen. He gives her a little piece from the dish they made and waits while she tastes it.

“Good?” he asks. ᴹ

She nods with immediate relief.

“Good.” ʸ

“Even after?” ᴹ

“Especially after.” ʸ

He takes his own bite. The repaired seasoning works. She watches his face closely enough to see that he means it when he says so.

---

Ori and Chloe bring their parcel to the table together.

It has been given a dish of its own. They carry it slowly, their hands arranged the way the cook showed them, and set it in the space Frieren clears between the plates.

Milim leans forward.

“It's huge.” ᴹⁱ

“It has to be,” Ori says. ᴼ

Chloe explains how much potato they needed to make the fish filling stay in their enormous pie. The account includes the first failed closing, the extra outside and the reason they chose not to make it smaller. Mau listens as carefully as he listened to Mai explain the platform, keeping the smile off his face until Ori has reached the important conclusion.

“There's enough for people to try.” ᴼ

Frieren takes the first small portion when offered. She tastes it without hurrying, looks at both girls and nods.

“You did well.” ᶠ

Ori has apparently been waiting for those particular words. She turns toward Chloe with a grin so bright that Chloe begins smiling before she has even finished cutting the next portion.

Yuka accepts a piece, then holds the plate where Mau can reach it too. He eats while Ori watches him for the answer.

“Worth waiting for,” he says. ᴹ

The girl takes a breath and begins finding the next person who should be allowed to try it.

Elmesia's and DIO's version looks a little different from the demonstration.

DIO wants to explain why. Elmesia tastes it first and tells her that the explanation can wait until she has decided whether she likes it.

DIO tries a careful piece.

She pauses.

“I do.” ᴰ

“So do I.” ᴱˡ

They look at the rest of the dish with new confidence. Testarossa asks to try it and offers some of theirs in exchange. Makima watches DIO take the first offered portion, then puts a little of the one she helped make onto a clean plate for Mau and Yuka.

She brings it herself.

“We made this,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Mau clears a space. Yuka thanks her, looking at the dish before reaching for a fork.

Makima stays while they try it. There are things she could say to lead the response: how closely they followed the instructions, which part she did, what she thought of the balance. She waits instead.

Yuka looks up first.

“I like the edge,” she says. ʸ

Makima looks at the piece she means.

“That part was mine.” ᴹᵏ

“You should show me how you did it.” ʸ

It is a practical request, made while Yuka is still chewing. Makima takes a moment to answer.

“I can.” ᴹᵏ

Mau passes her the spare chair so she can sit while she explains.

The evening becomes a series of exchanges.

People bring portions, take advice, defend accidents and discover that the thing they thought they ruined is somebody else's favourite. Jinshi tastes what Maomao has made and asks a question that gets him a longer explanation than he expected. He listens to all of it. She gives him another portion at the end, as if his attention has made him eligible to continue.

Momo Ayase insists that Okarun try the part she has been most uncertain about. He does, says what he likes and then has to say it again because she thinks he answered too quickly. Jiji offers his assessment from the other side and receives a look that makes him raise both hands before he has finished.

Across the table, Ram passes something to Seika without asking. Seika takes it, tastes and gives her back the plate with a little more left than she would have kept for anyone else. Ram notices. She does not point it out.

Mau watches these small arrangements while Yuka leans against him, warm from the cooking space and pleasantly tired.

“We should send Shuna the instructions,” she says. ʸ

“She'll ask what happened to the measure.” ᴹ

“We can tell her.” ʸ

He turns toward her.

“We?” ᴹ

“We made it.” ʸ

She takes his hand beneath the table and squeezes it once, smiling before he can answer.

---

Mai and Momo Nishimiya stay behind to help put the unused things away.

They are still at the preparation table when Mau and Yuka bring back their clean bowls. Mai takes the larger one, asks where it belongs and turns away to stack it.

Momo waits until she has put it down.

“Do you want to go back to that place tomorrow?” she asks. ᴹⁿ

Mai looks at her.

“The shop?” ᴹᵃ

“After the shop.” ᴹⁿ

“The path?” ᴹᵃ

Momo is beginning to look as if she regrets choosing a phrase that can be interpreted so many ways.

“With me,” she says. ᴹⁿ

Mai stops making her work for it.

“Yes.” ᴹᵃ

Momo's face changes before she can put the careful expression back on it. Mai takes the cloth from her hand, folds it once and sets it beside the bowls.

“Just us?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

Momo nods.

“Just us.” ᴹⁿ

Mau catches Yuka's wrist before she can stand there smiling at them. He brings her toward the path with the empty bag, giving them the rest of the conversation.

---

They walk back by the longer way.

Yuka asks if he liked the lesson. He says he liked making something with her. She tells him that is a suspiciously safe answer and makes him describe his favourite part.

He thinks about it, then answers honestly.

“When you tasted it at dinner,” he says. ᴹ

“Because it was good?” ʸ

“Because you stopped looking at it like it was the wrong bowl and started wanting everyone to try.” ᴹ

She looks down at their hands.

“You helped.” ʸ

“You let me.” ᴹ

She laughs softly.

“Don't make that sound noble. I wanted dinner.” ʸ

“So did I.” ᴹ

She bumps her shoulder into his arm. He stops at the turn, drawing her around to face him, and brushes his thumb across the little crease between her eyebrows.

“What?” she asks. ʸ

“I liked dinner.” ᴹ

“You said.” ʸ

“I'm saying it again.” ᴹ

He kisses her before she can tell him she heard the first time. Her hand settles against his chest, then slides up to his shoulder as she returns it. When they part, she stays close enough to touch his nose with hers.

“We can make it again,” she says. ʸ

“I'd like that.” ᴹ

She kisses him once more, briefly, and pulls him toward their room. Behind them the outdoor kitchen is still bright, full of people putting things away and finishing stories that began over the bowls.
