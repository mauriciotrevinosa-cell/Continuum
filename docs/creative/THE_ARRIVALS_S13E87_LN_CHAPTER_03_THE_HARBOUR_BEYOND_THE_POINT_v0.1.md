# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 81
## Chapter 32 — The Harbour Beyond the Point

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 81
**Reading order:** 32
**Source episode:** S13E87
**Continuity state:** Mai and Momo Nishimiya choose private date after cooking invitation. Previously postponed Kamish ride paid, checked harness, ordinary landing and waiting dragon. Overlaps Mau filter tests; no new relationship label, novice flying or public inner voices.

*When: B+392, beginning before Mau filter tests and continuing through the afternoon.*

Before the afternoon's filter tests, Momo finds Mau outside his room trying to retie the cloth without catching the knot in his hair.

Yuka is doing most of the useful work. He holds still while she frees a strand, then turns when Momo says his name.

“Kamish,” she begins. ᴹⁿ

Yuka's hands stop.

Momo looks at her, then back at Mau.

“The flight. Mai and I wanted to know if we could still ask him.” ᴹⁿ

Mau lowers the hand which was about to make his wife's work harder.

“Of course. Have you finished the harness?” ᴹ

“Mai checked it. She wants you to look before we use it.” ᴹⁿ

“Then let's look.” ᴹ

He gets as far as the turn before noticing Momo has not quite followed.

She is looking at her hands.

“There's something else?” he asks. ᴹ

She shakes her head, then changes the answer before he can make anything of it.

“I thought there might be. But I asked her myself.” ᴹⁿ

Mau waits.

“To come with me. Just us.” ᴹⁿ

His smile arrives slowly enough that she can see him deciding not to make it a joke.

“And?” ᴹ

“She said yes.” ᴹⁿ

Yuka takes Mau's hand and begins leading him toward the place where Mai is waiting. Momo follows, grateful to both of them for the small amount of conversation they allow the answer to need.

---

Kamish lowers his head when they come to the clear strip beyond the landing.

Mai has the finished fastening laid out beside her. She shows Mau where the straps sit, which one carries the load and which releases it. He checks the version she has made, has Kamish move the shoulder beneath it and waits until she has tested the altered angle herself.

“That one stays clear,” she says. ᴹᵃ

Kamish regards her.

“I am relieved.” ᴷ

Momo puts a hand over her mouth. Mai looks at the dragon, then starts laughing too.

Mau leaves them the short boarding step when they have finished. Yuka tells them they will see them later. Neither asks what the two women intend to do when the flight is over.

Mai offers her hand first.

Momo takes it to climb, keeps it while she settles into the place they have made and discovers that releasing it requires a decision she has no interest in making.

Mai checks the fastening across both of them. She leaves her shoulder against Momo's when she has finished.

“Still not frightened?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

Momo turns to look at her.

“Not of the flight.” ᴹⁿ

Mai's expression changes. She does not improve the answer for her or pretend it means something more convenient.

“Okay,” she says softly. ᴹᵃ

Kamish waits until they tell him they are ready.

---

The first rise is nothing like the broom.

Momo knows how it feels to lift herself. She knows the little adjustments that make balance something she does before thinking about it. This begins beneath her, a gathering of movement through a body so large that the first wingbeat reaches her as a change in the air rather than a thing she has commanded.

She catches the strap.

Mai looks at her hand and says nothing about fear.

The landing falls away. The people become separate figures again, Mau and Yuka still standing together until the turn hides them. Momo looks toward the water, finds the horizon and lets her grip ease.

“Better?” Mai asks. ᴹᵃ

“Different.” ᴹⁿ

“Good different?” ᴹᵃ

Momo looks at the roofs passing below them. Beyond the pale line of the promenade, the sea opens into a distance she usually has to attend to herself.

“Yes.” ᴹⁿ

Kamish follows the coast at an unhurried height. When Mai asks whether he can show them the little harbour beyond the point, he turns without making her feel she has requested a favour which will have to be paid back later.

“On your left,” he tells them. ᴷ

The harbour comes into view in pieces: the breakwater, the boats inside it, the narrow road which follows the shore and disappears beneath an awning of vines. Mai points to a white building at the bend.

“There. That's the one.” ᴹᵃ

“The shop?” ᴹⁿ

“You remembered.” ᴹᵃ

Momo has remembered considerably more than the shop. She has been carrying the description since Mai mentioned it, turning it into something she might ask without sounding as though she has planned every word beforehand.

She nods.

Mai's thumb moves against hers.

For a while they watch the harbour without deciding which part of the quiet needs an explanation.

---

They land at the broad clear place the harbour attendant indicates after Kamish circles far enough out to be seen approaching.

The dragon waits while Mai releases the fastening. Momo climbs down first, then turns to give her a hand even though Mai has already found the step.

“We'll come and find you when we're ready,” Mai tells Kamish. ᴹᵃ

“I shall be where the boats are not.” ᴷ

He moves to the empty outer stretch beyond the harbour wall. Momo watches him settle before following Mai onto the road.

The white building is not quite a shop. There is a workroom behind the shelves, and the woman at the counter is finishing a little metal clasp before she looks up. Mai recognizes one of the tools and forgets the question she was going to ask.

Momo stays beside her while the conversation becomes about how a hinge can close without catching the cloth beneath it. She does not understand all of the answer. She likes watching Mai understand it.

After a while Mai looks back.

“I'm doing the thing.” ᴹᵃ

“Which thing?” ᴹⁿ

“Stopping for one question and making you wait.” ᴹᵃ

Momo looks at the hinge still in the woman's hand.

“I wanted to know how it works too.” ᴹⁿ

Mai studies her face, then smiles and turns back to ask the last question for both of them.

---

They get food from the little counter facing the harbour.

Mai chooses the rolls filled with cheese and softened onion. Momo wants the folded orange-peel cakes beside them and has prepared a sentence about sharing before Mai orders one of each.

They take the food outside. There is a low wall with enough space to sit facing the boats, and shade from the vines she saw from above. Momo sets her broom within reach, sits and immediately discovers that Mai has chosen the place close enough for their knees to touch.

This is becoming a pattern. She is beginning to prefer it to figuring out what to do with the space between them.

Mai breaks the roll, gives her the larger half and waits until she has tried it before reaching for the cake.

“Is this what you meant?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

Momo looks up.

“When you asked me yesterday.” ᴹᵃ

The answer is easy until she has to give it aloud.

“Yes.” ᴹⁿ

Mai keeps looking at her, gentle enough that Momo finds the next part herself.

“I wanted to go somewhere with you. Without having to wait until everyone else decided to go too.” ᴹⁿ

“You can ask me.” ᴹᵃ

“I did.” ᴹⁿ

“I noticed.” ᴹᵃ

Momo begins laughing. It loosens the last of the careful posture she brought from the workroom. She leans against Mai's shoulder and feels Mai adjust at once to make the position easier.

The harbour keeps moving in front of them. A man drops the end of a line, retrieves it with a long pole and receives advice from three people who made no attempt to help. Mai watches until the man has secured it, then looks down at Momo.

“Better than the straps?” she asks. ᴹᵃ

Momo lifts her head.

“Holding your hand?” ᴹⁿ

Mai's face colours this time.

“For the flight,” she says, smiling. ᴹᵃ

Momo takes the hand resting beside her on the wall.

“Yes,” she answers, and keeps it through the rest of the view. ᴹⁿ

---

Kamish brings them back before dinner.

The fastening goes into its bag. Mai thanks him for the flight, and Momo does too, finding it easier now to look up at the great head without forgetting the rest of her sentence.

“A satisfactory first journey?” he asks. ᴷ

Mai looks at Momo.

“Very.” ᴹᵃ

They walk back along the guest path with the bag between them and their hands together on the other side. At the turn Mai remembers the little clasp she bought and begins explaining the difference between its two halves.

Momo listens all the way back.
