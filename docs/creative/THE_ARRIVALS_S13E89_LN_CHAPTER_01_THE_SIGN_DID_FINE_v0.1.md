# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 1 — The Sign Did Fine

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E89
**Continuity state:** The household is home; Mau and Yuka extend their island stay. Sexual pause and physical limits remain unchanged.

*When: B+395, morning through afternoon; Island III, day seventeen.*

Yuka wakes when Mau tries to retrieve his arm.

She does not open her eyes. Her hand travels across his chest, finds his wrist and puts it back where it was.

“I was going to get water.” ᴹ

“You have a shop.” ʸ

“You said we should use what we already have.” ᴹ

“I already have you here.” ʸ

He laughs into her hair. The bottle appears in his free hand, and she lifts her head just far enough to let him drink. When he offers it to her, she takes several unhurried swallows before settling against him again.

The curtains have not quite met. Beyond the narrow opening, a strip of sea moves in the morning light. There is no line of people waiting to use the bathroom, no flight to prepare, nobody asking whether an awkwardly shaped parcel will fit on the platform. He listens out of habit and hears a cart pass outside their door.

“Did you sleep?” she asks. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“After you stopped laughing at me?” ʸ

“You put your foot on my face.” ᴹ

“You were in the way.” ʸ

She turns enough to look at him. He is smiling, the uninjured side of his face crumpling more easily. She smooths a little of his hair away from his forehead and leaves her fingers there.

“We don't have to find something to do today.” ʸ

“I found something yesterday.” ᴹ

Her expression changes.

“What?” ʸ

“Breakfast.” ᴹ

Yuka hits his shoulder with an open palm. He catches her hand afterward and kisses it, which does very little to improve her opinion of the joke.

“I also found somewhere we haven't gone.” ᴹ

“Where?” ʸ

“If I tell you, you'll make a plan.” ᴹ

“I already have a plan.” ʸ

“What is it?” ᴹ

She adjusts herself across him and closes her eyes again.

“This.” ʸ

He gives the room another quiet examination, then puts the water within reach and accepts the amendment.

---

They leave much later, after an argument about whether carrying Yuka also requires carrying the hat.

She has chosen the enormous one. With it in place, she has to turn carefully to avoid striking the doorframe. Mau watches her try twice before offering to store it until they get downstairs.

“Then how will anybody know I'm on vacation?” ʸ

“You could tell them.” ᴹ

“Carry me.” ʸ

He bends to pick her up. She immediately takes the hat off and holds it away from his face.

“That feels like a problem you could have solved earlier.” ᴹ

“You looked capable.” ʸ

He carries her down the path to breakfast, stopping once so that she can point out a bird she insists is their bird. It is much larger, standing beside a drain with a fish in its beak.

“He has diversified,” she says. ʸ

“That one has a job.” ᴹ

At the terrace, the attendant recognizes them and leads them to a table farther from the main path. Yuka waits until Mau has put her down before fitting the hat back on her head. Its brim covers the edge of the neighbouring chair.

“Will anyone else be joining you?” the attendant asks. ᵃᵗᵗ

Yuka looks up at Mau. He looks at the empty chairs, then back at her.

“Just us this morning,” he says. ᴹ

It takes Yuka a moment to order. She knows what she wants, but there are two versions of the dish and she is deciding whether the one with the sauce is worth risking the thing she liked before. Mau orders the other version.

“We can trade.” ᴹ

“You want the other one.” ʸ

“I can want both.” ᴹ

She agrees with that reasoning and steals a piece of his first when the food arrives.

Their conversation wanders through the things they brought back yesterday. Ori's shell door. The kite they will need room to fly at home. Shoko telling Gojo that carrying her bag did not entitle him to choose where it went. Yuka asks whether the others arrived while Mau was still there, and he tells her about Shuna coming out to inspect the covered dishes before anybody could mistake them for luggage.

“Did you tell her we cooked?” ʸ

“I told her you saved mine.” ᴹ

“I did not save it.” ʸ

“You prevented a very large mistake.” ᴹ

“By making a different one.” ʸ

“We work well together.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs with her mouth full and has to take the napkin he holds out. He waits for her to finish before asking whether she wants to go to the place he found.

“You still haven't told me what it is.” ʸ

“A path. There was a sign for a lookout.” ᴹ

“You found a sign.” ʸ

“An important discovery.” ᴹ

“Did Anko draw it for you?” ʸ

“Her map doesn't go that far.” ᴹ

Yuka glances down at her shoes, then moves the hat to the empty chair so that she can finish eating without it catching the edge of the table.

“We take something to drink.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And if it's terrible, you admit it.” ʸ

“I will blame the sign.” ᴹ

---

The path goes inland before it climbs. That is the first thing the sign failed to mention.

The second is that the last stretch has steps.

Yuka stops at the bottom of them. Mau looks from her to the worn stone and begins to smile.

“Don't,” she says. ʸ

“I haven't said anything.” ᴹ

“You're thinking it.” ʸ

“You can't prove that.” ᴹ

She starts up before he can offer to carry her again. He follows, holding the hat she has finally conceded is poorly suited to a narrow path with low branches. Halfway up, she holds out her hand without looking back. He takes it, and the remainder of the climb becomes a conversation conducted between breaths: whether the steps were meant for people with longer legs, whether he has been here before, whether being able to fly means he is forbidden from having an opinion.

At the top, the trees open onto a low wall.

Yuka reaches it first. The wind moves her hair across her face. She pushes it away and looks down at the hotel roofs, the bay, the small boats arranged beyond the swimming area. From here the place where they ate breakfast is hidden by a line of trees.

Mau rests beside her, hat pinned carefully beneath one arm.

“Well?” he asks. ᴹ

“The sign did fine.” ʸ

“And me?” ᴹ

She leans over and kisses his cheek.

“You can have some water.” ʸ

They share the bottle on a bench farther along the wall. Mau studies the routes below, following the little breaks between roofs. Yuka studies him until he turns toward her.

“What?” ᴹ

“Nothing.” ʸ

“That's a suspicious nothing.” ᴹ

“You look pleased with yourself.” ʸ

“I found a good sign.” ᴹ

She laughs, then slides closer. His arm goes around her before she has finished moving.

For a while they identify places badly. Yuka is certain one building is the shop where she bought the cord for her bag. Mau thinks it is a storage building. Neither of them can see the entrance. They agree to inspect it on the way down, which will require going in the wrong direction, and decide that this is a reasonable use of their afternoon.

After a silence, Yuka says, “I'm glad we stayed.” ʸ

“Me too.” ᴹ

“I liked everybody being here.” ʸ

“Me too.” ᴹ

She turns her head on his shoulder.

“You can give a different answer.” ʸ

“I liked taking them home. Ori wanted to show me where the shell goes. Shuna had already changed something in the kitchen. It was good.” ᴹ

“And now?” ʸ

He looks at the view, then at her.

“Now I want to find out which of us is right about that building.” ᴹ

“Me.” ʸ

“You haven't seen it.” ᴹ

“I don't need to.” ʸ

He is still laughing when she takes the hat from him and puts it back on. They stay on the bench until the bottle is empty, making increasingly unreasonable claims about the distant roofs.

On the way down, the building turns out to be a laundry.

Yuka looks at its open door for a long moment.

“There could be a shop in the back.” ʸ

Mau stops walking. She gets several steps ahead before realizing he is no longer beside her.

When she turns around, he is bent slightly over, laughing so hard he cannot answer her.
