# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 1 — The Sign Did Fine

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S13E89
**Continuity state:** The household is home; Mau and Yuka extend their island stay. Sexual pause and physical limits remain unchanged.

*When: B+395, morning through afternoon; Island III, day seventeen.*

Yuka wakes when Mau tries to retrieve his arm.

She does not open her eyes. Her hand travels across his chest, finds his wrist and puts it back where it was.

“I was going to get water.” ᴹ

“You have a shop.” ʸ

“You said we should use what we already have.” ᴹ

“I already have you here.” ʸ

He laughs into her hair. The bottle appears in his free hand, and she lifts her head just far enough to let him drink. When he offers it to her, she takes several unhurried swallows before settling against him again.

The curtains have not quite met. Beyond the narrow opening, a strip of sea moves in the morning light. There is no line of people waiting to use the bathroom, no flight to prepare, nobody asking whether an awkwardly shaped parcel will fit on the platform. He listens out of habit and hears a cart pass outside their door.

“Did you sleep?” she asks. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“After you stopped laughing at me?” ʸ

“You put your foot on my face.” ᴹ

“You were in the way.” ʸ

She turns enough to look at him. He is smiling, the uninjured side of his face crumpling more easily. She smooths a little of his hair away from his forehead and leaves her fingers there.

“We don't have to find something to do today.” ʸ

“I found something yesterday.” ᴹ

Her expression changes.

“What?” ʸ

“Breakfast.” ᴹ

Yuka hits his shoulder with an open palm. He catches her hand afterward and kisses it, which does very little to improve her opinion of the joke.

“I also found somewhere we haven't gone.” ᴹ

“Where?” ʸ

“If I tell you, you'll make a plan.” ᴹ

“I already have a plan.” ʸ

“What is it?” ᴹ

She adjusts herself across him and closes her eyes again.

“This.” ʸ

He gives the room another quiet examination, then puts the water within reach and accepts the amendment.

---

They leave much later, after an argument about whether carrying Yuka also requires carrying the hat.

She has chosen the enormous one. With it in place, she has to turn carefully to avoid striking the doorframe. Mau watches her try twice before offering to store it until they get downstairs.

“Then how will anybody know I'm on vacation?” ʸ

“You could tell them.” ᴹ

“Carry me.” ʸ

He bends to pick her up. She immediately takes the hat off and holds it away from his face.

“That feels like a problem you could have solved earlier.” ᴹ

“You looked capable.” ʸ

He carries her down the path to breakfast, stopping once so that she can point out a bird she insists is their bird. It is much larger, standing beside a drain with a fish in its beak.

“He has diversified,” she says. ʸ

“That one has a job.” ᴹ

At the terrace, the attendant recognizes them and leads them to a table farther from the main path. Yuka waits until Mau has put her down before fitting the hat back on her head. Its brim covers the edge of the neighbouring chair.

“Will anyone else be joining you?” the attendant asks. ᵃᵗᵗ

Yuka looks up at Mau. He looks at the empty chairs, then back at her.

“Just us this morning,” he says. ᴹ

It takes Yuka a moment to order. She knows what she wants, but there are two versions of the dish and she is deciding whether the one with the sauce is worth risking the thing she liked before. Mau orders the other version.

“We can trade.” ᴹ

“You want the other one.” ʸ

“I can want both.” ᴹ

She agrees with that reasoning and steals a piece of his first when the food arrives.

Their conversation wanders through the things they brought back yesterday. Ori's shell door. The kite they will need room to fly at home. Shoko telling Gojo that carrying her bag did not entitle him to choose where it went. Yuka asks whether the others arrived while Mau was still there, and he tells her about Shuna coming out to inspect the covered dishes before anybody could mistake them for luggage.

“Did you tell her we cooked?” ʸ

“I told her how much dip we made.” ᴹ

“We made plenty.” ʸ

“The amount was impressive.” ᴹ

“By making a different one.” ʸ

“We work well together.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs with her mouth full and has to take the napkin he holds out. He waits for her to finish before asking whether she wants to go to the place he found.

“You still haven't told me what it is.” ʸ

“A little trail along the promenade. Five carved fish, and one of them has no tail. There's a sign near the desk.” ᴹ

Yuka considers him.

“You're inviting me to find fish made for children.” ʸ

“I thought we'd practice before Ori examines us.” ᴹ

“We take something to drink. And you don't use the eyes to find all five from breakfast.” ʸ

“I was planning to walk with my wife.” ᴹ

She smiles and takes the last bite of his toast.

---

The first fish is beneath a bench.

Yuka finds it while Mau is studying a very convincing fish-shaped mark on the wall. It has a tail, a disproportionately large eye and a little notch where someone has worn the stone smooth by touching it.

“One,” she says. ʸ

He looks down at it, then at the mark on the wall.

“A strong start for the team.” ᴹ

“For me.” ʸ

“I was providing a comparison.” ᴹ

She takes his hand and pulls him along before he can improve the explanation.

The route stays beside the water, through the shade of the low buildings behind the promenade. They pass a woman repairing a net who tells them she has seen people miss the second fish while leaning on it to tie a shoe. Mau finds that one in the paving. Yuka gives him generous praise, crouches to check its tail and tells him not to become overconfident.

At the third marker, the instructions tell them to look where a fisherman leaves his dinner.

There is a restaurant immediately opposite.

“Too obvious,” Yuka says. ʸ

Mau looks at the menu hanging by the door.

“I think we should investigate.” ᴹ

“We just ate.” ʸ

“He may have left dessert.” ᴹ

She is still telling him that they have to solve the clue honestly when the restaurant's cat climbs onto the empty crate beside them. It settles with one paw hanging over the edge and begins cleaning its face.

Yuka looks at the crate. There is a fish carved into the wood beneath the cat's paw.

“Dinner,” she says. ʸ

Mau leans closer. The cat opens one eye.

“I can see why he leaves it here. Very secure.” ᴹ

They wait until the animal moves enough to show the tail. It makes them earn this by remaining in exactly the wrong position while they discuss whether a treat would be bribery.

Yuka ends up sitting on the low wall beside it. Mau sits next to her. Neither has produced a treat when the cat decides to relocate onto her lap.

“Effective strategy,” he tells her. ᴹ

“Learn from it.” ʸ

She strokes the narrow head. Her free hand finds his wrist and turns it until she can rest their joined hands on her knee without disturbing the animal.

“I'm glad we stayed,” she says after a while. ʸ

“Me too.” ᴹ

“I liked everybody being here.” ʸ

“Me too.” ᴹ

She turns toward him.

“You can give a different answer.” ʸ

He looks at the sleeping cat, then at her.

“I liked taking them home. Ori wanted to show me where the shell goes. Shuna had already changed something in the kitchen. And then I got to come back because I wanted to be here with you.” ᴹ

Yuka's expression softens. She leans over to kiss him, carefully enough not to dislodge the cat.

“You can have another team point for that.” ʸ

“I'm going to become impossible.” ᴹ

“I'm preparing.” ʸ

---

They find four fish.

The fourth has no tail because it has been carved to disappear beneath a painted wave. Yuka regards this as cheating by the person who wrote the sign. Mau tells her he would like the objection recorded alongside all the complaints made against his own perfectly reasonable comparisons.

The woman with the net points toward the far end of the lane when they return to ask about the fifth.

“Look up,” she says. ᵃᵗᵗ

There is a row of fabric fish hanging above an open door. One has been repaired with a patch at the nose. Yuka points at it, delighted.

“That's a shop. I knew there'd be one.” ʸ

Mau looks at the steam escaping past the curtain inside.

“I'm not sure they're selling anything.” ᴹ

“They might make the fish.” ʸ

He studies the clothespins, then the small sign above them.

“The sign did fine,” he says. “We've added a commercial interpretation.” ᴹ

She goes as far as the doorway before seeing the sheets folded on the long table, the tubs and the basket of towels waiting to be washed.

A laundry.

Yuka looks at it for a long moment.

“There could be a shop in the back.” ʸ

Mau stops walking. She gets several steps ahead before realizing he is no longer beside her.

When she turns, he is bent slightly over, laughing so hard he cannot answer her.
