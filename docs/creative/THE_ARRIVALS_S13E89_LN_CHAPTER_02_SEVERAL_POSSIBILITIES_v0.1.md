# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 2 — Several Possibilities

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S13E89
**Continuity state:** Remaining island company spends the evening together; DIO and Elmesia explore their interest without relabeling other bonds.

*When: B+395, evening; continuous after the promenade fish trail.*

By the time they find the others, Elmesia has won two games and DIO is arguing about the rules of the third.

The board sits between them on a low table. Testarossa has a book open on her lap and an expression suggesting that it has taken her several minutes to read the same page. Antonio stands behind her chair, broad head tilted toward the pieces.

“You can move it,” Elmesia says. “You just have to accept what happens afterward.” ᴱˡ

“That was not how you explained it.” ᴰ

“You hadn't tried it yet.” ᴱˡ

Mau stops beside the table.

“Should we come back?” ᴹ

“No,” DIO says immediately. “You can tell her.” ᴰ

He looks at the board. He looks at Yuka.

“Do you know this game?” ᴹ

“No.” ʸ

“Then we're in excellent condition to judge.” ᴹ

Testarossa closes the book around a finger. Her shoulders move once.

“I told you they would be useful,” she says. ᵀᵉ

Makima sits a little farther along the terrace, where the light falls across the page she is reading. She looks up when Mau takes the remaining chair. Yuka sits on the arm of it long enough to remove her hat, then lets herself slide into the place beside him.

“You went walking,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“We made a discovery,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

Yuka puts her hand over his mouth.

“We went looking for the carved fish.” ʸ

He waits until she removes it.

“And a laundry.” ᴹ

Yuka turns her face into his shoulder. DIO looks between them, then at Elmesia, as though a rule she has not been told is operating elsewhere on the terrace.

“There isn't a shop in the laundry, is there?” Yuka asks Elmesia. ʸ

“Not unless somebody has started a business without telling me.” ᴱˡ

Mau begins laughing again. Yuka presses an elbow into him and asks for something to drink.

---

Elmesia teaches them the game after dinner.

It should be easy. Each player has a small set of carved counters and is trying to reach the far side of the board. The difficulty lies in the middle, where a counter can turn, block somebody else or help the person least likely to thank it.

Mau gets his first piece trapped almost immediately.

“Why did you put it there?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“I was going somewhere.” ᴹ

“Not anymore.” ʸ

Makima studies the board from the other side. When Elmesia invites her to take the fourth place, she marks the page in her book and sets it down.

DIO stays beside Elmesia to observe. She intends to watch closely enough that nobody can surprise her with another rule.

Mau moves a second counter into trouble. Makima looks at it, then at the route Yuka has left open.

“You can stop her there,” she says to him. ᴹᵏ

“She has more pieces behind it.” ᴹ

“They will have to wait.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka leans over the board.

“You could also let me win.” ʸ

“I did hear that option.” ᴹ

“You liked it.” ʸ

“I'm considering several possibilities.” ᴹ

He blocks her. She stares at the counter he has put in her way, then slowly turns to him.

“The marriage is being reviewed,” she says. ʸ

“We can settle out of court.” ᴹ

“Dessert.” ʸ

“That's an expensive review.” ᴹ

Makima moves while they are talking. She has seen the opening Elmesia left, and closes it before it can become the move that wins the game.

Elmesia regards her for a moment.

“That was unkind.” ᴱˡ

“You said I could.” ᴹᵏ

“You certainly can.” ᴱˡ

The smile that follows is small and delighted. Makima looks down at the board again, less certain of that response than she was of the move.

She wins by a single counter. Yuka asks for another game before Mau can congratulate her, and Makima says yes so promptly that she has to move her hand away from the pieces to let Elmesia reset them.

This time Mau notices the route too late to prevent Yuka from using it.

She lays her last counter down and holds out her hand.

“Dessert.” ʸ

“You won.” ᴹ

“I'm very upset about what happened earlier.” ʸ

He gets up to find an attendant. Makima watches him go, then asks Elmesia if they have time for a third.

---

Later, when the board has been put away and Yuka has taken Mau down the path to inspect the dessert cart herself, DIO remains beside Elmesia.

Testarossa has gone to answer a message. Makima has chosen to go back inside for a while; Mau asked whether she wanted to stay, and she said she wanted to finish the chapter she had begun before dinner. DIO had heard the answer and noticed that he had not asked her to account for the choice.

“Will you teach me properly tomorrow?” DIO asks. ᴰ

Elmesia turns toward her.

“I thought I had.” ᴱˡ

“You were winning while you did it.” ᴰ

“Would you prefer a teacher who loses?” ᴱˡ

DIO considers.

“No.” ᴰ

“Then tomorrow.” ᴱˡ

There is a little space between their chairs. DIO puts her hand on the arm of hers, thinking about moving it closer, and realizes Elmesia is watching the movement.

She stops.

Elmesia does not fill the silence for her. Below the terrace, somebody is rolling the dinner tables back beneath the roof. DIO watches until the last one has gone out of sight.

“I like when you notice things,” she says. ᴰ

“Which things?” ᴱˡ

“That I wanted to ask. Before I had asked.” ᴰ

Elmesia rests an elbow on the arm of her chair.

“Sometimes I'm wrong.” ᴱˡ

“I know.” ᴰ

The answer makes Elmesia laugh. DIO smiles before understanding that she has not offended her.

“You do tell me,” Elmesia says. ᴱˡ

“You don't seem to mind.” ᴰ

“I mind several things. That isn't one of them.” ᴱˡ

DIO shifts her hand again. This time she sets it on the little table between them.

“I don't always know what I'm asking.” ᴰ

Elmesia looks at it, then back at her face.

“You can still ask.” ᴱˡ

“And if I change my mind?” ᴰ

“You can tell me that too.” ᴱˡ

DIO studies her with the attention she gives an answer she expects to need again. Elmesia lets her, but after a while she reaches for her drink.

“You are allowed to look away.” ᴱˡ

“I know.” ᴰ

“Do you?” ᴱˡ

“Yes.” ᴰ

Neither woman looks away first. At last DIO begins to laugh, and Elmesia puts down the glass she has not managed to drink from.

Mau finds them there on the way back.

Yuka carries a paper parcel. He has a second one tucked under his arm, and she is explaining why the two things in it are different enough to justify keeping both.

“Are we interrupting?” he asks. ᴹ

DIO turns toward him, still smiling.

“No.” ᴰ

Elmesia looks at her. DIO notices and adds, with unexpected firmness, “Not anymore.” ᴰ

Mau looks at Elmesia. Her expression gives him no help at all.

“I can walk very slowly,” he offers. ᴹ

Yuka catches the back of his shirt before he can demonstrate.

“You said you wanted to sit down.” ʸ

“I can sit very far away.” ᴹ

Elmesia gestures toward the unoccupied chair. He accepts the defeat, and Yuka sits across his legs with the parcel between them. DIO shifts her chair toward Elmesia's by the small amount she had been considering earlier.

This time, when Elmesia notices, DIO leaves it where it is.

Yuka opens the paper and offers the first piece across the table. Elmesia takes it. DIO asks what it is, tries the piece she is given and then asks whether the other one really is different.

“That's what I said,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

“You said I was buying the same thing twice.” ʸ

“An early version of the argument.” ᴹ

“You lost that version.” ʸ

He leans back, defeated, while she feeds him the evidence.
