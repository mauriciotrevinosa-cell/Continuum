# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 3 — Local Authorities

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S13E90
**Continuity state:** Letters keep household ties active; Yuka uses their chosen parental relationship naturally. No arrival, power change or resumed attempt for a child.

*When: B+396, late morning through evening; Island III, day eighteen.*

The first letter is addressed to Yuka.

The second is addressed to Mau, with VENDING CITIZEN written beneath his name in larger letters than the address.

He puts that one facedown.

Yuka sees him do it and holds out her hand.

“Evidence.” ʸ

“There hasn't been a crime.” ᴹ

“Then you have nothing to hide.” ʸ

He gives her the envelope. She opens it with the same care she used for her own, but begins laughing before she reaches the end of the first page.

Umaru has enclosed an itemized complaint. The supply of one particular kind of snack has become uneven, which appears to mean that Ryo discovered where it was kept. There has been a hearing. Ryo has offered to pay later. Umaru has found this assurance unsatisfactory.

At the bottom, Rem has written that they are fine and that Mau does not need to return to settle the matter.

“Very reassuring,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka unfolds the second sheet.

“There's a proposed sentence.” ʸ

“For her?” ᴹ

“For you.” ʸ

He takes it from her.

It is a list of things he is requested to bring home, divided into essentials and emergencies. The two columns appear to contain the same things in a different order.

He looks up to find Yuka watching him with the pleased expression she gets when something has become his problem.

“You married into this,” he reminds her. ᴹ

“I was here first.” ʸ

“That's worse.” ᴹ

She laughs and goes back to her letter.

They have brought the post to the shaded table outside their room. It is late enough that breakfast has become a distant event, but neither of them is hungry yet. Yuka has her legs stretched across the empty chair. Mau reads with his elbow beside her knee, occasionally shifting a page to keep it out of the wind.

Maki's letter asks how they are, tells Yuka which things have been put away and then becomes less orderly as other people add their own messages. Mai has drawn an arrow to a sentence she says Yuka should ignore. Yuta's addition is shorter. He wants them to enjoy the rest of their stay and says they can let him know when they have decided about coming home.

“He hasn't asked when,” Yuka says. ʸ

“He asked me when I brought everyone home.” ᴹ

“What did you tell him?” ʸ

“That we hadn't decided.” ᴹ

“And?” ʸ

“He said that was what he thought you'd say.” ᴹ

Yuka smiles down at the page.

They read the additions together. Mau points out the place where Gojo has tried to insert something and Shoko has crossed it out. Yuka insists she can still decipher it. They spend several minutes tilting the paper before she gives up and tells him they can ask when they return.

Ori's page is folded inside Frieren's.

It contains a drawing of the place where the shell door now stands, a question about whether Lantern can understand being drawn too small, and a description of a disagreement over who gets to keep the string from the kite workshop. Chloe has signed the bottom beside her. Frieren adds that Ori ate well, slept well and would like to know whether the bird is still stealing.

Mau reads that part aloud.

“We're being watched,” Yuka says. ʸ

“We should send a report.” ᴹ

“We need reliable information.” ʸ

She swings her legs off the other chair.

“Bring the paper.” ʸ

---

They find the bird near the breakfast terrace, pecking at something beneath a table.

Yuka stops Mau with a hand across his stomach. He looks down at it, then at her face.

“Don't frighten the witness.” ʸ

“He robbed me.” ᴹ

“Allegedly.” ʸ

The bird hops onto a chair. Mau pulls out the one opposite it and sits down.

An attendant notices them and asks whether they would like anything. Yuka orders something small enough, she says, to leave room for lunch. By the time she has finished naming the things she considers small, Mau has lowered his head onto one hand.

“You can help,” she tells him. ʸ

“I'm honoured.” ᴹ

When the food arrives, the bird has moved to the wall. It watches while Yuka organizes the plates, then looks away with a show of indifference that Mau finds personally insulting.

He writes to Ori while Yuka eats.

The bird remains at large. Its demands have become unreasonable. Local authorities have failed to act.

Yuka leans over his shoulder to read.

“Don't tell her that. She'll come back to help.” ʸ

He crosses out the last sentence and writes that they are having a good time.

Yuka watches his hand. The altered fingers make him reposition the page when he reaches the bottom of it. She moves her cup before his wrist can knock it, then passes him the second sheet without interrupting what he is doing.

He tells Ori where they walked yesterday. He draws a very bad roof to explain the laundry, and Yuka takes the pen from him to add what she says is necessary context.

“You can't tell our daughter I thought it was a shop without telling her why.” ʸ

Mau looks at her.

She has bent over the paper. The words came easily enough that she appears not to have noticed them.

He rests his hand lightly on her back and lets her finish.

The bird drops onto the empty chair beside him. Mau turns just as it takes a piece of fruit from his plate.

Yuka's laugh makes the pen jerk across the page.

“We have our answer,” she says. ʸ

---

They post the letters after lunch.

Mau has added a separate response to Bellion's practical report. The home watch remains where he ordered it. The routine adjustments are small, the ones he can make without disturbing the people whose rooms and paths the soldiers protect. Yuka stands beside him while he checks the arrangements, interested enough to ask why he is leaving one post where it is.

“The view from the other side is blocked when the door opens.” ᴹ

“Could he stand farther back?” ʸ

“He'd lose the turn.” ᴹ

She studies the little sketch he has made.

“Then leave it.” ʸ

He does, and puts the completed instructions away. Bellion acknowledges the completed instructions. Mau puts the pen down and turns back to Yuka.

Yuka takes the pen to finish the address on the last envelope.

“Do we owe anybody else a letter?” ʸ

“Anko.” ᴹ

“You wrote to her.” ʸ

“She'll object to the laundry not being on her map.” ᴹ

“You can draw it.” ʸ

“You saw the roof.” ᴹ

“I'll draw it.” ʸ

They sit back down. Mau describes the turn while Yuka draws the path, and the resulting map becomes less useful as she adds an exaggerated picture of him standing in front of the door. He reaches for the pen to correct it. She moves the sheet out of his reach and adds the hat.

By the time they finally hand the letters over, she is almost as pleased with her drawing as with the walk itself.

---

That evening they take their food to the balcony.

There is no reason for it beyond Yuka wanting to see the water and Mau not wanting to put his shoes on again. He pulls the table closer to the wall. She brings out the last plate and steals his chair while he is adjusting the other one.

He stops behind her.

“That's mine.” ᴹ

“I thought we were sharing.” ʸ

He puts his arms around her shoulders. She tilts her head against him, waiting for his answer.

“Then move over.” ᴹ

“There's another chair.” ʸ

“That's yours.” ᴹ

She begins laughing, but keeps the chair until he bends to kiss her. Then she stands, lets him sit down and immediately settles across his lap.

“A satisfactory arrangement,” she says. ʸ

He brings the table within reach and puts his arm around her.

“I was trying to have dinner with my wife.” ᴹ

“You found her.” ʸ

She offers him the first bite from her plate. He accepts, gives his opinion of it and has to try hers again when she disagrees. By the time they have settled that, the extra chair holds the things neither wants on the table.

They discuss what to do tomorrow while she eats from both plates. She wants another swim. He wants to find the place Elmesia mentioned when she was teaching them the game. They settle on swimming first and deciding the rest afterward.

Halfway through the meal, Yuka puts her fork down, turns in his lap and kisses him.

He catches the back of her head gently before she can draw away.

When she does, she stays close.

“What was that for?” he asks. ᴹ

“You were here.” ʸ

He smiles, looking at the little bit of sauce she has left beside his plate.

“Convenient.” ᴹ

She kisses him again, then takes the piece he was about to eat.
