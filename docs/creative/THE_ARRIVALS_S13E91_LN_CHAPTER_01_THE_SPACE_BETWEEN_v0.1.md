## Chapter 5 — The Space Between

“What did you notice?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Yoru regards him over the table.

“You have more choices than you use.” ʷᵈ

“Most people do.” ᴹ

“Most people can't make a king kneel.” ʷᵈ

“Is that why you're here?” ᴹ

“Part of it.” ʷᵈ

She does not look away when she answers. Mau has met enough people who came to tell him what he is to notice the pleasure she takes in leaving the rest unsaid.

“Then tell me the other part.” ᴹ

Yoru's eyes shift to Makima.

“I wanted to know what changed.” ʷᵈ

Makima sits with her hands together, saying nothing.

“You could ask her.” ᴹ

Yoru turns to her.

“What happened to you?” ʷᵈ

Makima keeps her hands together.

“You've seen him protect me. Which part requires explaining?” ᴹᵏ

“The part where you let him decide when you sit down.” ʷᵈ

“I decided to come.” ᴹᵏ

Yoru looks from her to Mau.

“That isn't an answer.” ʷᵈ

“It's the one she gave you,” he says. ᴹ

Yoru smiles.

“You really do speak to her like that.” ʷᵈ

Yuka's hand moves on his knee. Mau turns his own palm up beneath the table, and she gives him her fingers.

“How should I speak to her?” he asks. ᴹ

“Carefully.” ʷᵈ

“I try.” ᴹ

“Not the way you mean.” ʷᵈ

“Then we're both disappointed.” ᴹ

Yoru leans back. The wind catches the edge of her hair, and she pushes it away impatiently.

“I thought you were making a mistake at the warehouse,” she says. “Keeping them alive. Taking time to let them learn what you could do.” ʷᵈ

“And now?” ᴹ

“Now I think they made the mistake. They were still fighting the person they expected.” ʷᵈ

Makima looks at her.

“You watched all of it.” ᴹᵏ

“Enough.” ʷᵈ

“You didn't intervene.” ᴹᵏ

“You survived.” ʷᵈ

Mau glances at the place in the distance where the hotel roof rises above the trees. It is still an ordinary afternoon over there. He would like it to remain one.

“If you want a demonstration, the answer is no.” ᴹ

“You haven't heard what I'm offering.” ʷᵈ

“Then offer it.” ᴹ

“You don't make this enjoyable.” ʷᵈ

Yuka lifts an eyebrow.

“We were having a nice day.” ʸ

Yoru looks at her, then at Mau. Her expression changes with a thought he does not like having to guess at.

She stands.

He stands with her. There is enough space between the chairs for her to come around the table, and he allows her to do that much because he wants to know whether she intends to leave. When she stops in front of him, he moves his arm slightly away from Yuka's chair.

Yoru reaches toward his face.

“You let her close,” she says. ʷᵈ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She leans in.

The space stops her before she reaches him.

For a moment she does not understand that she has stopped. Her hand is still extended, her face close enough that he can see the annoyance forming before the rest of her expression catches up.

Mau looks past her at Yuka.

His wife has not moved from her chair. Her eyes travel from Yoru's outstretched hand to his face with a clarity that requires no message at all.

*I haven't moved,* he tells her. ᴹ

*Good.* ʸ

Yoru tries to close the remaining distance. Nothing changes.

“You haven't taken it off,” she says. ʷᵈ

Mau brings his attention back to her.

“I don't think it likes you.” ᴹ

Behind her, Yuka makes a short sound that could become a laugh under better circumstances.

Yoru's mouth tightens.

“Do you think—” ʷᵈ

Her free hand has gone to the pin holding part of her hair back. She draws it loose. The little piece of metal begins to change between her fingers, lengthening past the shape it had a moment ago.

*Weapon,* Noise says through the channel Mau has kept open. ᴺ

Mau stops smiling.

He does not raise his voice. He does not have to. His attention settles on the movement in her hand, and Yoru sees him decide exactly which distance matters now.

The metal stops changing.

For one long second, she holds it between them.

Then she steps back.

“It wasn't an attack,” she says. ʷᵈ

“Then put it away.” ᴹ

She looks at Makima as though she expects the other woman to say something. Makima's expression has not changed.

“You came to talk,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yoru's fingers close around the unfinished weapon.

There are people beyond the trees. There is the woman behind Mau, the island beneath them and the thing she has travelled here to understand. He has shown her the instant before a fight begins; she knows enough now to recognize that he will not spend it waiting to be impressed.

She lowers her hand.

“Fine.” ʷᵈ

Mau waits until she has returned to her chair before he sits down.

Yuka keeps her gaze on Yoru a moment longer. Then she puts her hand back on his knee, and he covers it with his own.

“Don't do that again,” Yuka says. ʸ

Yoru is examining the piece of metal in her palm.

“He stopped me.” ʷᵈ

“I noticed.” ʸ

Yoru looks up. Whatever answer she was preparing does not seem worth the expression waiting for it.

“I wanted to see,” she says instead. ʷᵈ

“You saw,” Mau tells her. “Now we either talk or you leave.” ᴹ

“You're very particular about this.” ʷᵈ

“Yes.” ᴹ

The plain answer leaves her nothing useful to push against. She sets the changed pin on the table, well within her own reach, and folds her arms.

“I want to see you fight,” she says. ʷᵈ

“You already have.” ᴹ

“When I choose. From close enough to know what I'm seeing.” ʷᵈ

“No.” ᴹ

“I can make it worth your time.” ʷᵈ

“You can start by telling me what that means.” ᴹ

She looks at the joined hands beneath the edge of the table. This time she does not approach.

“There are things I can choose not to do.” ʷᵈ

“For how long?” ᴹ

The question pleases her. He sees it before she has decided whether to let him.

“Then you do want something.” ʷᵈ

“I want you to finish a sentence.” ᴹ

Yuka's grip tightens once, briefly. He cannot tell whether she is warning him or trying not to laugh, and decides both are reasonable.

The change interrupts Yoru in the middle of her next answer.

She has begun to say what she could promise about the house when her posture falters. The marks are gone. Her hands come down to the table, and the girl looking across it appears appalled by where she has found herself.

“I told you not to do that,” she says. ᴬˢ

“Who am I speaking to?” ᴹ

“Asa. Mitaka.” ᴬˢ

“The name on the note.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴬˢ

“It's water,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I know what water is.” ᴬˢ

Asa looks down.

“Sorry.” ᴬˢ

“It's been an awkward afternoon,” Yuka says. ʸ

Asa lets out a breath that almost becomes a laugh. She catches it and reaches for the glass instead.

Mau does not look away from her to ask Makima a question about what she is. He waits until she has taken a drink, then asks Asa directly.

“How much of that did you hear?” ᴹ

“All of it.” ᴬˢ

“And can she hear us now?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴬˢ

The unfinished metal lies beside Asa's glass. She moves it away with the same hand that began making it.
