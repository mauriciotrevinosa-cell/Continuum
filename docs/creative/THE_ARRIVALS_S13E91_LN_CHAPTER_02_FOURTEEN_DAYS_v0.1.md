# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 6 — Fourteen Days

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S13E91
**Continuity state:** Asa authorizes investigation only, not separation. Provisional fourteen-day terms are NOT yet signed. Mau considers old inn Trial memory for ninety days and tells Yuka the risk before committing.

*When: B+397, afternoon; continuous negotiation at the old landing.*

Asa holds the glass with both hands.

She has the uncomfortable feeling that the others are waiting for an explanation she should have prepared before getting on the boat. Yoru had prepared several. None of them included having to sit here after what she just tried to do.

“I wasn't trying to kiss you,” Asa says. ᴬˢ

Mau's expression changes slightly.

“I understood that.” ᴹ

“I just wanted to be clear.” ᴬˢ

“You are.” ᴹ

Yuka reaches for her own glass. Asa cannot tell whether she is being polite or giving her somewhere else to look. She takes the opportunity anyway.

“How long have you shared a body?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Asa hesitates. Yoru is standing beside him now, where only she can see her, looking profoundly offended by the question.

“A while.” ᴬˢ

“Did you choose it?” ᴹ

Yoru's expression hardens.

Asa looks down at her hands.

“I was dying.” ᴬˢ

Nobody answers immediately. It is worse, in some ways, than having to explain more. She can hear the sea beyond the wall and a loose corner of paper moving on the table. The people in front of her are giving the sentence time to mean what it means.

“I said I wanted to live,” she adds. ᴬˢ

Mau nods.

“I can understand that.” ᴹ

She looks up too quickly, suspicious of being understood so easily.

He does not try to prove it. He glances at Makima, then brings his attention back to Asa.

“Do you want somebody to look into whether there's a safe way to change the arrangement?” ᴹ

Yoru is suddenly beside Asa's chair.

Asa puts the glass down before she can spill it.

“What do you mean, change?” ᴬˢ

“Separate you. Give you more control over what happens. I don't know what's possible yet.” ᴹ

“You can't just take her out.” ᴬˢ

“I haven't said I can.” ᴹ

“Part of me—” ᴬˢ

She stops. The explanation feels unpleasantly physical when she tries to put it in front of strangers.

“I don't know what would be left,” she says. ᴬˢ

“Then we don't try anything before we know.” ᴹ

Yuka leans forward.

“You don't have to decide today.” ʸ

Asa looks at her, then at Makima, who has listened with an attention she cannot make comfortable.

“And you wouldn't decide for me?” ᴬˢ

“No,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yoru speaks beside her, furious now. Asa hears every word. She keeps looking at Mau until she has found an answer which belongs to her.

“You can find out,” she says. “That doesn't mean you can do it.” ᴬˢ

“I understand.” ᴹ

“And you tell me what you find.” ᴬˢ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Asa nods once. She has not agreed to lose Yoru, keep her forever or be grateful for everything that has happened since the night she wanted to live. For now, the difference matters enough that she can draw a breath without it catching.

Then Yoru takes the chair back.

---

“You want to get rid of me,” Yoru says. ʷᵈ

Mau meets her gaze.

“I asked her what she wanted.” ᴹ

“And Makima gets to stay.” ʷᵈ

Makima watches her without moving.

“She made her own arrangement,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yoru looks toward Yuka, enjoying the opening she thinks she has found.

“Does your wife know how much time you spend finding ways to keep her?” ʷᵈ

“Yes,” Yuka says. ʸ

The answer costs Yoru a moment.

“You could have offered her the same thing.” ʷᵈ

“I hadn't thought of it that way,” Mau says. ᴹ

*I had,* he tells Yuka. *About whether it could be done safely. I haven't promised either of them anything beyond finding out.* ᴹ

Her hand presses once against his leg.

*I know what you meant.* ʸ

Yoru's eyes narrow at the little change in his face. She cannot hear the words. Mau lets the silence remain hers to interpret.

Makima finally speaks.

“You were going to offer something.” ᴹᵏ

“I won't attack his house,” Yoru says. ʷᵈ

“For how long?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“We can discuss that.” ʷᵈ

“And you won't ask anybody else to do it?” ᴹ

Yoru smiles.

“I didn't say that.” ʷᵈ

“That sounds very vague.” ᴹ

“It is.” ʷᵈ

He leans back. Her willingness to admit it is almost refreshing. It does not make the offer better.

“Then it's not worth very much.” ᴹ

“To you.” ʷᵈ

“I'm the one you're asking.” ᴹ

The sea wind reaches the table again. Yoru moves the changed pin away from the paper before it can roll over the edge. Mau notices the practical little movement and gives her time to make the next one.

“You want nobody to die,” she says. ʷᵈ

“I want you to stop causing it.” ᴹ

“For a while.” ʷᵈ

“For a while.” ᴹ

“And I get what?” ʷᵈ

“What are you asking for?” ᴹ

She names the first battle. The yard. Then the hollow. She wants to see what he saw, with enough of the part between attacks to understand why he chose them. No view from three ridges away, no guessing what the smoke hid.

Mau considers before answering.

“Selected parts. Nothing inside the people who live with me. Nothing that belongs to somebody else.” ᴹ

“You choose what matters?” ʷᵈ

“I choose what I give you.” ᴹ

“Then you'll give me the version you want me to have.” ʷᵈ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Yoru laughs, once. It is the first sound she has made which does not seem intended to push somebody somewhere.

“At least you know.” ʷᵈ

She offers fourteen days.

---

They spend the next part of the afternoon disagreeing about fourteen days.

Mau wants the promise to cover deaths she causes through other people, including instructions already given which she can still withdraw. Yoru wants him to admit that she cannot control everybody who decides to start a fight while she is sitting still. Makima points out where the two statements differ. Yuka asks what happens if Yoru calls giving somebody a weapon a different act from using it.

Yoru looks at her with renewed interest.

“You listen.” ʷᵈ

“When somebody is offering not to kill people, yes.” ʸ

The terms become shorter as they become more exact. She will not kill anyone, arrange their death, provide a means for a killing she intends to occur during the covered period, or use another person to do what she has agreed not to do. Existing plans under her direction which would cause such a death during that time must be stopped. She cannot be held responsible for an independent stranger merely because war pleases her.

The house and its people will not be attacked by her or through her instructions while the agreement holds. Preparing a later plan is not the same as carrying one out now. She can watch a future fight if Mau separately agrees to the place and occasion; the contract gives her no address, summons or right to put somebody else in danger to produce one.

“You're asking me to be bored,” she says. ʷᵈ

“You found plenty to watch without killing anybody at the landing.” ᴹ

“You don't know what I did on the way here.” ʷᵈ

His expression does not change.

“I know what I'm asking you not to do after you agree.” ᴹ

Yoru studies him. The answer she was looking for has failed to arrive again.

She taps one finger on the table.

“Fourteen days,” she repeats. ʷᵈ

Mau looks down at the paper between them.

There are many things he could show her. She has already seen enough of the recent battles to know where she would want to look more closely. Each piece he offers teaches her something, and each piece he withholds leaves her guessing. He can feel the safe edge of the bargain.

Then he thinks about what fourteen days buys the people at home.

“Ninety,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka turns toward him.

Yoru's fingers stop moving.

“For what?” ʷᵈ

Mau does not answer immediately. He looks at Yuka first.

She knows the change in his face before he says the words.

“There's another one.” ᴹ

---

They move away from the table to speak.

Yoru objects. Mau tells her she can wait or leave. For once, she chooses waiting without using the decision to make another speech.

Yuka stops near the wall, facing him.

“Which one?” ʸ

“The inn.” ᴹ

Her face changes.

He lets her have the time he wishes he had taken before saying it.

“You don't have to pay with that,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Do you?” ʸ

He looks down at their hands.

“I want more time than she's offering.” ᴹ

“And after you show her?” ʸ

“I don't know.” ᴹ

The answer hurts him to give. He gives it anyway.

“I can choose the memory. Ciel can keep it from opening into anything else. That doesn't mean I'll be fine after it.” ᴹ

Yuka takes a breath, looking past him toward the table. Makima remains seated. Yoru is looking out at the water, but the angle of her head tells Yuka she is counting how long this conversation lasts.

“She'll know where it hurts,” Yuka says. ʸ

“She already knows who I go back for.” ᴹ

“Knowing isn't the same as seeing it.” ʸ

He nods.

There is no clever answer that will make the choice harmless. Yuka sees him look for one and stop.

“I don't like it,” she says. ʸ

“I don't either.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long time.

“If you decide to do it, I stay. We tell Ciel exactly what you're opening, and when it's over you don't go somewhere by yourself to pretend it wasn't hard.” ʸ

“I won't.” ᴹ

“And the house hears what you agreed to.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She folds his hand between both of hers. Her anger is not at being asked to understand; it is at the thing he is considering, the use another person can make of what has already hurt him. He can see that much. He stays until she is ready to walk back with him.

When they do, he has still not promised Yoru anything.
