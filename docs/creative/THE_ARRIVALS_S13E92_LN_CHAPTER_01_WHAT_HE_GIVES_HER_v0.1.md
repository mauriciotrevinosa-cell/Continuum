# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 7 — What He Gives Her

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S13E92
**Continuity state:** Ninety-day contract finalized until same hour B+487. Asa consents to selected viewing; no access to other archives. Household notified. Memory is the old constructed inn Trial on the OG Frieren line, not current history.

*When: B+397, late afternoon; selected memory begins, remembered date undisclosed to Yoru.*

When they return to the table, Yoru has put the metal away.

She watches Mau sit. Her eyes go to Yuka next, measuring the distance between them, and stay there until Yuka looks back.

“Ninety days,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I heard you the first time.” ʷᵈ

“All the conditions we just discussed. From when we agree until this time on B plus four hundred and eighty-seven.” ᴹ

Yoru's attention returns to him.

“And I see what?” ʷᵈ

“One memory. The part I choose to open, from the beginning to its end. You don't go looking for anything else.” ᴹ

“Which fight?” ʷᵈ

“You wanted to know what happens when I don't stop.” ᴹ

She is still for a moment.

“You said that. I didn't.” ʷᵈ

“Do you want to see it?” ᴹ

“Where?” ʷᵈ

“I'm not giving you where. Or when.” ᴹ

“Then how do I know it's useful?” ʷᵈ

“You don't know how you'll use it. You know why you came here.” ᴹ

Yoru's eyes narrow.

“People were afraid of the house before the armies arrived,” he continues. “You watched those armies fail. Now you want to know what would make the next war different.” ᴹ

“You don't know what I want.” ʷᵈ

“Then ninety days is too much for something you've already seen.” ᴹ

He leaves his hand beside the paper. She glances at it, then at his wife.

“A fight ends,” Yoru says. “What it makes out of people can last much longer.” ʷᵈ

“This one didn't end when they stopped hitting each other.” ᴹ

For the first time she stops looking for the shape of a demonstration. He can see her considering what a man like him might make of the people around him after a war has taken away the thing he fought for.

“And I see that part.” ʷᵈ

“In the memory I choose. No more.” ᴹ

Yoru studies his face, looking for the part of the offer which will tell her how to value the rest. He can feel himself wanting to explain. He lets the silence do its work instead.

Finally she leans forward.

“Why this one?” ʷᵈ

“Because I know what I'm asking for it.” ᴹ

The answer settles between them. Makima looks at Mau, then at the hand Yuka has kept beneath the table.

“No access to the rest of him,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yoru turns toward her.

“Are you negotiating for him now?” ʷᵈ

“I'm listening to what he offered.” ᴹᵏ

“I can hear.” ʷᵈ

“Then you won't mind hearing it twice.” ᴹᵏ

Mau almost smiles. The expression does not quite form.

He waits while Ciel checks the final wording with him. She does it privately, asking about the selected record and the boundary around it before she speaks about anything else.

*I can limit what is transmitted. I cannot promise what remembering it will do to you.* ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*You may stop participating in the replay. The bounded record can remain available to complete the exchange. You do not have to remain inside it for her to receive what was agreed.* ᶜ

Mau looks at Yuka. He tells her that part aloud.

She nods, but does not loosen her grip.

---

Asa is given the chair again before anything is signed.

Yoru resents the delay. Asa's first words are a sharp answer to something nobody else has heard.

“Then you shouldn't have made it my problem.” ᴬˢ

She looks at the others and goes red.

Mau gives her time to recover.

“She wants to see a memory,” he says. “You'll be there too. You can say no.” ᴹ

“What kind of memory?” ᴬˢ

“People die in it.” ᴹ

Asa looks at Yuka.

“Is that what she came for?” ᴬˢ

“It's what she's asking for now,” Yuka says. ʸ

Asa's eyes move toward Yoru's invisible place. Her mouth tightens as she listens, and Mau waits while the argument he cannot hear travels across her face.

“Will I think I'm there?” she asks at last. ᴬˢ

“You'll know you're watching. It's my memory. If you want to stop, say it.” ᴹ

“And she can't make me keep watching?” ᴬˢ

“Not through what I'm sharing.” ᴹ

Asa looks down at the paper.

“Ninety days?” ᴬˢ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She reads the conditions herself. When she gets to the part about providing weapons for someone else to use, she looks toward the empty space again.

“You heard that part?” ᴬˢ

Her answer arrives without words anyone else can hear. She takes a breath.

“All right,” she says. “I'll watch. If I tell you to stop, stop.” ᴬˢ

“I will.” ᴹ

She gives the chair back reluctantly, keeping her eyes on him until the marks return.

Yoru's first expression is one of irritation. Her second is much harder to read.

“From now,” she says, touching the date on the page. ʷᵈ

They finish the agreement while the sun is still above the roof of the landing. Yoru gives the promise in the exact terms they have settled. Mau gives the limited access he has offered. The familiar weight of a contract settles into the words; Ciel keeps the completed record of what each has accepted.

Ninety days. B+397 to B+487, at the same hour. The conditions do not depend on whether either of them likes what follows.

Yuka reads the final page before it is put away.

Then she moves her chair beside Mau's.

---

They tell Elmesia before opening the memory.

Mau gives her the part concerning the island first: no fight, no demonstration directed into the world around them. The visitor has accepted a promise against killing and attacking the household. He is sharing a selected recollection in exchange.

Elmesia asks whether he needs a quieter room. He looks at the little terrace, the bare stone, the water which will still be there when he looks up.

“I'd rather stay here.” ᴹ

She studies him and then Yuka.

“Then I'll keep the path clear.” ᴱˡ

Testarossa stays with her. Makima asks Mau whether she should go in. He looks at her, considering the question instead of answering from habit.

“Stay nearby. Don't follow the memory.” ᴹ

“I won't.” ᴹᵏ

He sends a short account home through the channel Ciel can carry to Rimuru: who came, the conditions, the time limit, what he has chosen to give. No softened version to be corrected after somebody else notices he is unwell.

The answer is not immediate. He does not treat the lack of one as permission or a reason to conceal the rest.

Yuka turns his chair a little so that she can sit beside him without the edge of the table between their knees. He watches her do it.

“What do you need me to say?” she asks. ʸ

He thinks about the end of that place. The quiet after it.

“My name,” he says. ᴹ

“Okay.” ʸ

She takes his hand where he can feel it against the side of his leg.

Across the table, Yoru waits without pretending she has lost interest.

Mau closes his eyes.

---

The inn is warm.

That is the first thing he has kept without knowing he kept it: the heat gathering beneath the long table, the uncomfortable place near the hearth where nobody wants to sit until the evening turns cold. A chair scrapes. Somebody tells somebody else to move their elbow.

Mau stands in the entrance with a cup in his hand.

He knows he has been here before. That knowledge has followed him into too many mornings to help him decide what this one is going to do.

Frieren sits beside the window. She has a book open, but one finger rests across the page where she stopped reading to listen to Fern. Fern is holding a tablecloth which has been folded into a thick, unsteady square. The discussion appears to concern whether putting it down counts as putting it away.

Milim calls him from the table.

“You're in the way.” ᴹⁱ

He moves. She gets past him carrying a tureen by its lid, with the base balanced against her other wrist.

“That's going to spill,” he says. ᴹ

“Then help.” ᴹⁱ

He gets both hands beneath the tureen. They get it to the table without spilling very much broth, and Rimuru tells them he is impressed by the improvement.

It is an ordinary conversation.

Mau listens to it with an attention which makes him late answering the next question.

Frieren has looked up from the book.

“Are you staying there?” she asks. ᶠ

He looks at the chair beside her. It is empty. The things which happened in other places do not show on her face. She is annoyed only because he is letting the drink cool while he decides whether to sit down.

“For a minute,” he says. ᴹ

She moves the book far enough to make room for his cup.

He sits. Her shoulder rests against his for a moment before she goes back to the page.

He has known versions of this quiet which ended badly. He has tried refusing them before they could begin. That did not save anyone either.

So he drinks what is in the cup and listens while Fern finishes explaining about the folded thing. When Frieren answers, he laughs in the place where he would have laughed before he learned to count mornings by what they might take away.

Her hand finds his beneath the edge of the table.

He lets her have it.
