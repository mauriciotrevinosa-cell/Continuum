# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 8 — While They Were There

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S13E92
**Continuity state:** Constructed old household, OG Frieren romance. Mau leaves to seek a rule or door after earlier loops failed. No current Yuka-wife role, modern kit or new external opponent identity.

*When: Selected old inn Trial memory, before the solitary journey; viewing remains B+397.*

By afternoon, Mau has decided to leave.

He does not do it immediately. There are things he can still check, and checking gives him a reason to move through the inn without having to explain the decision before he has found words for it.

The stairs creak in the same place. The little window above the landing opens onto the yard. Somebody has left a cloth over the rail to dry. He moves it so that the wet edge will stop dripping on the step, and stands there afterward with it in his hand.

He has seen a beam fall through a room. He has held somebody who was still speaking when the next thing happened. He has learned that running toward a sound can be wrong and that remaining beside the person making it can be wrong too.

The cloth is an absurd thing to notice.

He hangs it properly anyway.

Downstairs, Maki and Yuta are arguing about a repair to the frame beside the entrance. Maki believes the frame can survive one more person leaning on it if that person stops doing it on purpose. Yuta points out that this is an optimistic method of maintenance.

“He's right,” Mau says. ᴹ

Maki looks at him.

“Then fix it.” ᴹᵃᵏ

He sets his hands where she indicates. The repair is small enough to do without thinking very much. Maki checks the join when he has finished, gives it an experimental shove and tells Yuta that the problem has been solved.

Yuta thanks him. Mau nods too late.

“You all right?” Yuta asks. ʸᵗ

“Thinking.” ᴹ

“About?” ʸᵗ

Mau looks past him at the open yard.

“A way out.” ᴹ

Maki's expression changes a little. Yuta turns enough to follow his gaze, finding nothing there which explains the answer.

“Out of what?” ʸᵗ

Mau almost tells him.

The words are crowded together, so numerous that he cannot find the first one without hearing the last. Every time he puts somebody inside the explanation, they become another person he has to watch when the room changes. He does not know whether the act of telling them helps or merely gives the place something else to use.

“I need to look farther,” he says. ᴹ

Maki takes the tool from the table and puts it away herself.

“Then tell somebody where you're going.” ᴹᵃᵏ

It is a reasonable request. He has no reason not to follow it except that he does not know the answer yet.

---

He finds Rimuru outside with Milim.

Milim has been drawing something in the dirt with a stick. Rimuru appears to have become responsible for identifying it. Mau arrives in time to hear him make a guess which she finds insulting.

“It has wings,” she says. ᴹⁱ

“So does the thing I said.” ᴿ

“Not like those.” ᴹⁱ

Mau looks at the drawing.

“A dragon?” ᴹ

Milim points at him triumphantly.

“He knows.” ᴹⁱ

Rimuru studies the uneven shape again.

“Of course.” ᴿ

Mau sits on the step. For a few minutes, while Milim improves the drawing in ways which make it harder to identify, he lets them pull him into the argument. He knows enough about the thing she is trying to show them to ask whether it should have another leg. She looks at the picture, declares it unfinished and adds one.

Rimuru waits until she is occupied before turning to him.

“You had something to ask.” ᴿ

Mau looks toward the trees.

“If I went away for a while, would you keep everybody here?” ᴹ

“Keep them here how?” ᴿ

“Safe.” ᴹ

Milim's stick stops moving.

Rimuru looks at him for long enough that Mau begins preparing another version of the question. He does not get to use it.

“What happened?” ᴿ

“Nothing here.” ᴹ

“That's an unusual answer.” ᴿ

Mau presses his palms together between his knees. There are things he could say about them being strong, about the things which will not be able to reach the inn while Rimuru and Milim remain beside it. All of those things have been true before. None of them has been enough.

“I need to find something,” he says. “And I don't think I can find it from here.” ᴹ

“Then we go with you,” Milim says. ᴹⁱ

“No.” ᴹ

She looks hurt before she looks angry. He sees the order of it and almost changes his mind.

“I need you here,” he tells her. ᴹ

“You can say that without sounding like you're leaving forever.” ᴹⁱ

Mau stares at the drawing until he can answer.

“I'm not trying to.” ᴹ

Rimuru shifts on the step. His shoulder touches Mau's briefly.

“Talk to Frieren before you go.” ᴿ

“I will.” ᴹ

“And if you don't find it, come back anyway.” ᴿ

Mau nods.

Milim puts the stick down. She has decided she is angry now, and the decision gives her something useful to do with the worry. She tells him that if he finds anything interesting he has to describe it properly when he returns. A dragon, especially. Not the version Rimuru thinks she drew.

“I heard that,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“You were supposed to.” ᴹⁱ

Mau laughs. For a second the sound surprises him.

---

Frieren knows he has come to say something before he reaches her.

She puts the book down on the bench. There is room beside her, but he remains standing, and her eyes go to the little collection of supplies he is carrying.

“How far?” she asks. ᶠ

“I don't know.” ᴹ

“That's not very useful.” ᶠ

“I know.” ᴹ

He sits because she is still looking at the space beside her. She moves the book out of his way and waits while he puts the supplies down.

“I think I've been looking in the wrong place,” he says. ᴹ

“For what?” ᶠ

“The thing that makes it end.” ᴹ

Frieren studies him. He can see the moment she decides that asking the obvious question will not get her an answer he can give cleanly.

“And you think it's far away?” ᶠ

“I think it might not be here.” ᴹ

“That isn't the same thing.” ᶠ

He looks at her hands, folded loosely over the book.

“No.” ᴹ

She touches his face. The movement is so familiar that he closes his eyes before he can stop himself.

“Mau.” ᶠ

“I'm here.” ᴹ

“For now.” ᶠ

He opens his eyes. Her expression hurts more than if she had tried to forbid it.

“I want to come back,” he says. ᴹ

She keeps her hand against his cheek.

“Then do that.” ᶠ

He rests his forehead against hers. For a while neither of them moves. The yard remains audible around them: Milim has begun explaining the drawing to somebody new, and the person listening has made the same mistake Rimuru made.

Mau almost stays.

Then he remembers all the times he did.

---

He leaves while the inn is still busy.

Fern has added wool socks and another cloth-wrapped loaf to his supplies and objects when he calls them unnecessary. He takes it without further argument. Bocchi moves out of the doorway with a hurried little apology, although he is the one who has stopped in front of her. Eren stands farther along the path, watching the line of trees.

“Alone?” Eren asks. ᴱʳ

“This time.” ᴹ

Eren looks at him, uncertain what to do with that.

Mau waits for the question. It does not come.

“Come back,” Eren says instead. ᴱʳ

Mau nods once and walks past him.

He does not look back until he reaches the bend where the building will disappear behind the trees. Frieren is still beside the bench. Milim has followed him partway across the yard and stopped with her arms folded. Rimuru stands near her, one hand lifted in a small farewell.

All of them are alive.

Mau holds that picture while he turns away.

He has tried being the one in front. He has tried leaving space for somebody else to move. He has asked the right question too late and the wrong one early enough to make everything worse.

The thought he cannot stop following has become simple.

Perhaps the thing which keeps finding them is following him.

He walks faster once the inn is out of sight.
