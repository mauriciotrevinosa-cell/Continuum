# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 10 — The Frame Holds

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S13E93
**Continuity state:** Most old constructed household dead in yard/common room/stairs. Anonymous accusing voice remains unidentified. Frieren dead; no deathbed speech. Wheel and Construction begin autonomously; surviving Rimuru and Milim arrive to intervene.

*When: Selected inn Trial memory, return after the long search; viewing remains B+397.*

He recognizes the bend before he recognizes the trees.

For a few steps, that is enough to make him hurry. The road has changed under the weather, but the angle is the same. Another turn, the ground rising a little, and then he will be able to see the inn.

He has rehearsed what he will say so many times that none of it remains useful now.

Sorry is too small. I couldn't find it invites the question he does not know how to answer. I thought you would be safer sounds like something a person says after making a decision everybody else has had to live with.

He will let them be angry.

He will sit through the questions. He will tell Frieren how long it felt, and if she tells him it was not nearly that long, he will try to believe her. He can apologize to Rimuru for not finding a better way to explain before he left. He can tell Milim about the things he saw even if none of them was a dragon.

The inn comes into view.

He stops.

For a moment, he cannot say what is wrong with it. The roof is where it should be. The yard has not moved. The entrance stands in the same place, beneath the frame he repaired.

Then he understands that he has been listening for people.

He begins running.

---

The first person is in the yard.

He kneels before he has decided to stop. His supplies strike the ground beside him. There is dirt on the face, a little of it caught against the mouth, and he wipes it away with the ridiculous care of somebody who thinks discomfort is still the problem.

“Hey.” ᴹ

He touches the neck. The wrist. He looks at the place where the body should be moving and makes his own breath slow down so that he will not mistake it for something he has imagined.

Nothing.

He tries anyway.

The response he knows how to call is there. It moves where he sends it. The body beneath his hands remains a body.

He changes what he is doing, thinking that he has missed something obvious because he came too fast. He has been wrong before. Being wrong is still a thing he can survive if it means there is something left to do.

The face does not change.

Mau looks toward the door.

There is somebody else near the step.

He gets up without taking the supplies.

---

The frame holds when he catches it.

That is the detail he will not be able to put anywhere afterward. The part of the inn he touched before leaving has remained strong enough to bear his weight.

Inside, a chair lies on its side beside the long table. One cup has rolled beneath the edge. Another is upright, exactly where somebody put it before there was no longer anybody to pick it up.

Mau says a name.

Then another.

There is a shape beyond the table which he knows before his mind agrees to know it. He goes toward it, stopping halfway because something near the stairs moves in the corner of his vision.

It has not moved. His own shadow crossed it when he entered.

“Fern?” ᴹ

The name comes out with the rising end of an ordinary question.

He reaches the stairs and knows he has used it too late.

He kneels again. He has begun repeating the same few checks on each person, unable to stop because stopping would mean he has accepted the answer. He searches for a sound, a little warmth with a source other than the room. Something to make the next thing he tries more than the last thing he tried.

Behind him, somebody begins screaming.

He turns so fast his hand strikes the step.

“Where were you?” ᵛ

The voice comes apart in the room. He cannot find the face belonging to it. He looks toward the entrance, the table, the open space beyond the fallen chair.

“Where the hell were you?” ᵛ

“I'm here.” ᴹ

He gets to his feet.

“Tell me what happened.” ᴹ

The voice does not answer the question. It gives him his own absence again, louder this time, as though he has failed to understand the only fact which matters.

“I came back.” ᴹ

He hears how useless that sounds.

He moves toward the next person anyway.

---

Frieren is not beside the bench.

He has looked there twice before he admits that he is avoiding another place in the room. The book is near the window. He can see the cover from where he stands. It is closed, but he remembers the page she was reading and the way she moved it to make space for his cup.

He goes to her.

His knees hit the floor hard enough that he should feel it. He gathers her carefully, one hand behind her head, and begins talking before he has decided what to say.

“I'm back.” ᴹ

There is a little dirt on her sleeve. He brushes it away. He straightens the part caught beneath her shoulder so that it will not pull when he lifts her.

“I couldn't find it.” ᴹ

His hand trembles against her face. He puts the other beneath it, as though the problem is that he has not held her steadily enough to do what needs doing.

“I looked.” ᴹ

He does not know who he is telling anymore.

He tries to heal her.

He tries until he has lost the distinction between the things which might have helped when she was alive and the things he is doing because he cannot put her down. There is no practical question left in it. He wants to be wrong about what he knows, and wanting has become something his hands keep obeying after the answer has stopped changing.

The voice finds him again.

“Where were you?” ᵛ

He looks toward it without turning his head all the way. The room has narrowed around the place on the floor where he is sitting.

He remembers the bridge. The villages. The empty place at the woman's table.

He remembers deciding that another road might contain the thing which would let him come back properly.

He looks down at Frieren.

“I thought—” ᴹ

The sentence stops there.

He has made versions of it all the way home. None of them can be spoken while she is like this.

---

He does not know when he begins hearing the wheel.

At first the sound becomes part of the room, another thing he cannot locate. He thinks the voice has changed. He thinks something outside has moved. He looks toward the door and sees the piece of the yard which was visible when he left.

The wheel turns again.

Something inside him answers it before he has asked for anything.

There is a pressure beneath his skin, a sequence beginning too quickly for him to follow. A response forms and is overtaken by another. He tries to hold one thing still long enough to understand what it is building, and feels the next thing move through the place where it should have waited.

“Stop.” ᴹ

He does not know whether he has said it aloud.

The wheel turns.

He thinks of the place outside where all the roads continued. Of how much room the world had found for him to walk away in. Of how small the space beside her has become.

He puts his hand against the floor.

There should be an instruction he can give. Something which begins here and ends where he chooses. His thoughts keep reaching the first part after the thing inside him has already passed it.

He is adapting to something. Building something. He cannot name the hurt precisely enough to know what the answer is meant to do.

The voice screams again.

Mau closes his eyes.

For a moment he sees the yard as it was when he turned at the bend: Milim with her arms folded, Rimuru beside her, Frieren looking back at him from the bench.

He has held that picture for what felt like years.

It breaks when he opens his eyes.

---

Rimuru reaches him after the first sound outside.

Mau knows the voice. It is his name, spoken from the entrance, and the recognition is so fierce that he nearly gets up to answer it.

Then something moves through the space between them.

Wood tears away from the frame. The repaired join holds a moment longer than the things around it, and then the whole entrance opens into the yard.

“Mau!” Rimuru calls again. ᴿ

Milim is behind him.

Alive.

Mau tries to hold onto that. He looks at them and wants the fact to become large enough to stop whatever is happening. Rimuru takes another step. Milim sees what Mau is holding, and the anger she was bringing into the room disappears from her face.

The wheel turns faster.

Mau hears somebody else approaching outside. A familiar movement. A shadow across the broken entrance.

He opens his mouth to tell them not to come closer.

He cannot remember, afterward, whether the warning ever becomes a word.
