# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 11 — The Hands He Knows

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S13E94
**Continuity state:** Rimuru, Milim and Eren try to stop him. Fragmented memory preserves uncertainty about individual outcomes, mechanism and fidelity. Destruction is an old constructed-world event, not a current power benchmark. No newly copied skill.

*When: Selected inn Trial memory; intervention and collapse, exact duration unverified.*

Rimuru gets close enough to touch his shoulder.

For a moment, Mau feels the hand. He knows it. He tries to turn toward it with an answer which has nothing to do with the things forming around him.

Then the contact is gone.

Rimuru lands beyond the broken entrance. Something has carried him there faster than Mau can follow. He is already moving again when Milim comes through the opening, one arm in front of her face against the dust.

“Look at me,” she says. ᴹⁱ

Mau does.

He sees the frightened part of her before she hides it. He has seen that expression on her in other places, over other bodies. He wants to tell her he knows. He wants her to understand that he did not mean to leave them alone with this.

“I'm trying,” he says. ᴹ

Something strikes the space beside her.

The wall behind it breaks. Milim throws herself forward, reaches him and catches his wrist before his hand can finish moving.

“Then stop.” ᴹⁱ

He tries.

The effort is real. He can feel himself making it: the thought of holding still, the wish to keep the room around them intact long enough for her to say something else. His fingers open. Her grip loosens because she feels them do it.

The next change does not begin in his hand.

Milim sees it before he does. She lets go and moves, so quickly that the space she occupied has not finished breaking when she reaches the yard.

Mau stares at the place where she was.

“No.” ᴹ

The wheel turns over the word.

---

He puts Frieren down carefully.

Later, that detail will exist without the path to it. One moment he has her in his arms. Another, she is beside the wall, sleeve straightened, and he is looking at his empty hands.

There is a mark on his palm from the edge of the step.

He presses it with his thumb. The small hurt almost makes sense. It begins in a place he can point to. It has a size.

Then somebody calls his name outside, and the building around that small place ceases to be something he can keep.

He is in the yard.

Rimuru stands between him and the road. Milim has moved to the other side. They have stopped approaching from the same direction. He knows why without being able to remember when they agreed to it.

“We're here,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then stay with us.” ᴿ

Mau looks back at the broken entrance.

He can see part of the table through it. The cup is no longer upright. He has changed that. The little thing which survived whatever happened before he returned has been broken by what he did after.

Milim says something. He cannot separate the words from the sound beginning behind him.

Rimuru raises a hand.

A boundary forms across the yard. Mau recognizes the intent before the shape: contain this, keep it from reaching the rest. He wants it to work so badly that for a moment he mistakes wanting for agreement.

The construction inside him meets the boundary.

He feels it learn.

The wheel turns.

Rimuru's expression changes.

---

Eren reaches them before the next part has finished.

Mau knows the voice before he sees him. He hears his own name, then an instruction to the others. Eren has understood enough from the broken yard to stop asking what happened before deciding where to stand.

Mau turns toward him.

He remembers the path. The way Eren looked at him before he left. The simple thing he asked because he had not known what question would produce a better answer.

Come back.

“I did,” Mau says. ᴹ

Eren is too far away to hear.

Light breaks through the space between them. A shape rises beyond the yard, too large for the trees which stood there a moment ago. The ground shifts under Mau's feet. He should look away from the pressure gathering in front of him. Instead he looks up, recognizing the decision rather than the face.

They are trying to stop him.

The realization is clear enough to hold for one breath.

He is the thing which has to be stopped.

He opens his mouth to agree.

His body moves first.

---

There are pieces he cannot put in order.

Milim's hands on his shoulders, pushing him back from something he does not remember approaching. Rimuru's voice close to his ear. The ground coming up toward him and then becoming a place he is no longer standing.

A grip he breaks without intending to hurt the person holding him.

The next grip broken before he has finished recognizing whose it is.

He knows pain. He feels it arrive, followed by a response which is already changing before the pain has become a useful warning. His sight clears and clouds and clears again. He cannot follow the work long enough to stop it in one place without losing the next.

There is no single weapon he can put down.

He searches for one anyway. A hand to close. A thought to refuse. Some first action which will make the things after it unnecessary.

The first action has happened before he finds it.

Rimuru is in front of him. Then he is above him. Then Mau hears the impact somewhere he cannot see.

“Don't come back,” he tries to say. ᴹ

Milim comes back.

She hits him hard enough to move him away from the inn, and for a few instants the distance is something he can understand. He sees the building behind her. The broken wall. The small place near the window.

He reaches toward it.

She catches his hand.

“Mau.” ᴹⁱ

He looks at her.

There is dirt on her face now. One side of her mouth is hurt. He notices it with the old reflex which should make him ask whether she is all right.

The wheel turns again.

She sees his face change.

For a moment he knows she is afraid of him.

That becomes another thing the construction tries to answer.

---

He has spent so long trying to learn the correct response that the place inside him which responds no longer waits for a question.

Distance becomes something to overcome. Containment becomes something to break. An attack teaches the next response how not to be hurt the same way. A hand reaching for him becomes pressure; pressure becomes an obstacle; an obstacle becomes something which can be changed.

He knows the hands.

He cannot make knowing fast enough.

There is a moment when all three of them are moving. He can see enough to understand the shape of it: Rimuru giving the others a space to work, Milim taking the risk because she can reach him, Eren closing the direction he keeps trying to take.

They know one another. They have chosen this together.

Mau tries to hold that thought.

Something inside him finds the gap.

The moment ends.

Afterward, he cannot remember the move. He remembers the sound disappearing from one side of the world.

He turns toward it.

Then the other side changes too.

---

He is no longer sure where the inn stood.

There is a shape which might be part of it. There are pieces of wood, earth opened where it should be flat and a place where the road once curved away beneath the trees. He goes toward that because it is the last direction he knew how to find without thinking.

The wheel has not stopped.

He looks for Rimuru.

He looks for Milim.

He says Eren's name and hears his own voice with no answer large enough to reach him.

Something farther away moves, and his attention catches on it. He has stopped understanding what he is reacting to. Every new sound enters the same place. Everything which reaches him becomes part of the thing he cannot bear to have happened.

He wants it to end.

The construction has learned too many ways to obey.

---

The land beyond the yard disappears from his memory in sections.

He remembers reaching a height from which the place where he lived should have been easy to find. He remembers searching for the road he followed home and finding a change too large to belong to the map he carried.

There is light. There is the absence after it.

He cannot tell how far the damage goes. Every time he looks for an edge, the looking leads him to something else which is gone.

No voice explains what he has achieved. Nothing tells him that this was the answer. There is no person left near enough to tell him to stop, and the part of him which wanted somebody to make the decision for him has nowhere else to turn.

He stands in the quiet.

The wheel finally slows.

He feels each turn separately again. The space between them becomes long enough to think a thought all the way through.

He thinks of the repaired frame.

Of Milim's drawing.

Of the person at the roadside asking where he was trying to go.

He turns, looking for one thing he can still reach without hurting it.

There is nothing.
