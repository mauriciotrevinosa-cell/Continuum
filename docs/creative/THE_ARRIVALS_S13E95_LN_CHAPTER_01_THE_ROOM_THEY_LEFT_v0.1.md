# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 13 — The Room They Left

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S13E95
**Continuity state:** No new physical damage or public internal voices. Ciel relays home response privately through Rimuru; Mau verbally relays Shoko. Makima uses existing intertwined access with explicit consent, no Control or memory invasion; Yuka feels gratitude and hurt together.

*When: B+397, evening into night, after Yoru departs.*

They leave the landing slowly.

Yuka does not ask Mau to stand until he has looked at her long enough to understand the question. When he does get up, his hand tightens around hers with a force he corrects almost at once.

“You're okay,” she says. ʸ

He looks at their hands, worried.

“You didn't hurt me.” ʸ

He nods, but keeps looking until she turns his palm up and puts her fingers back against it herself.

Makima walks on his other side. DIO has gone in before they leave the terrace. Elmesia stays ahead of them, clearing the small obstacles people ordinarily step around without thinking: a tray left beside a chair, the edge of a cart, somebody about to ask whether the table should be prepared for dinner.

“Not yet,” she tells the attendant. ᴱˡ

Mau stops when he hears it.

Yuka turns with him.

“What's wrong?” ʸ

He looks toward the cart, then beyond it. She waits for him to make the distance between what he has heard and where they are.

“Nothing,” he says at last. ᴹ

The word makes her throat tighten.

He sees her face and tries again.

“I thought—there was a sound.” ᴹ

“I heard it too. The cart.” ʸ

He looks at it. The attendant has stopped moving it, one hand still on the handle.

“Yes.” ᴹ

They continue only after he has taken the first step.

---

In the room, he sits where Yuka guides him and looks at the clothes he left beside the bed.

His shirt is on top of hers. They were going to change after swimming, then decide where to eat. She remembers him complaining about the damp towel on the chair and moving it so that it would dry properly.

Now he looks at the chair as though it has become something he needs to place in a much longer explanation.

Yuka takes the towel away.

“We can deal with those later.” ʸ

She opens the curtain enough to let him see the balcony, then stops when he turns his head toward the light too quickly.

“Like this?” ʸ

He looks at her, then at the opening.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She leaves it. There is water on the table. He takes the glass when she puts it in his hand, but holds it without drinking until she lifts her own.

“I'm thirsty,” she says. ʸ

He watches her drink. Then he takes a mouthful himself.

It is such an ordinary thing that she nearly cries over it.

She sets her glass down carefully and sits beside him.

“I don't know what to ask you right now,” she admits. ʸ

His eyes move to her face.

“You don't have to.” ᴹ

It is the longest answer he has given since they left the landing. She has to swallow before answering.

“Okay.” ʸ

She puts her head against his shoulder. His arm begins to move around her, stops, then completes the movement with obvious attention to where his hand lands.

Yuka stays there. She lets him feel the weight he knows, the familiar place where her hair touches his neck.

After a while he whispers, “I know it's you.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“I'm not—” ᴹ

He cannot finish.

She lifts her head enough to look at him.

“You don't have to prove it to me tonight.” ʸ

His face changes, and she puts her arms around him before he can try to apologize for that too.

---

Makima waits outside the bedroom.

When Yuka comes out to ask about the food, the other woman is standing near the little table where they read their letters. Elmesia has put a covered tray there. Testarossa has arranged for the staff to leave the neighbouring part of the path quiet without turning the whole place into something people will gather to look at.

“He drank,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima nods.

Yuka lifts the cover from the tray, looks at the food and puts it down again. She cannot remember what she thought she would find under it.

“Did you know it would be like this?” she asks. ʸ

Makima is quiet long enough that Yuka looks up.

“I knew he was frightened of remembering it. I didn't know the shape it would take afterward.” ᴹᵏ

“You can feel it?” ʸ

“Some of it. That isn't the same as knowing what he is seeing.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka rests both hands against the table.

“He's answering me.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“And then he goes away again.” ʸ

Makima looks toward the closed door. She does not step closer to it.

“He keeps finding the end of it,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka closes her eyes.

For a moment she is angry at the precision. At Makima being able to name something she has been trying to understand by watching his face. She wants the precision to help. It does help. That makes it harder to know what to do with the ache it leaves behind.

She opens her eyes again.

“Can you help?” ʸ

“I may be able to help him keep track of what his body is doing here. Ciel is already staying with him. I would ask him before trying.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at the tray.

“Let me take this in first.” ʸ

Makima moves out of her way.

---

He eats a little.

Yuka tells him what each thing is before asking whether he wants it. He refuses one, accepts another and looks embarrassed when she wipes a drop from his hand.

“I can do it.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

She gives him the cloth. He uses it, folds it and sets it beside the plate with the careful concentration he has given everything since the memory ended.

Inside, Ciel passes him the response from home. Rimuru has received the terms and the account of what happened afterward. Shoko has asked what he can manage, whether he wants her there and whether he knows he can come home without waiting to feel better first.

Mau looks at Yuka.

“Shoko,” he says. ᴹ

She waits.

“She asked if I want to go home.” ᴹ

“Do you?” ʸ

He looks around the room. The balcony. Their clothes. The second glass she put on the table beside his.

“I want to stay with you.” ᴹ

“I'm not going anywhere.” ʸ

He nods slowly.

“Tell her. Please.” ᴹ

Ciel carries the answer to Rimuru. The next message includes no demand that he make a better decision. Shoko wants him to know that the offer remains.

He closes his eyes for a moment and holds Yuka's hand against his knee.

---

They ask Makima in before he tries to sleep.

She stops where he can see her and explains what she is offering. She does not ask to watch the memory. She does not tell him to stop feeling what has happened. The connection which already joins them gives her a second way to follow the present body, and she wants to use it to help him keep that sensation distinct from what he remembers.

“May I?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

Mau looks at Yuka. She nods, then waits until he has made his own small motion of agreement.

Makima sits at the foot of the bed, far enough away that Yuka can remain against his side. She rests two fingers at his wrist after he turns it toward her.

For a while nothing visible happens.

Yuka hears him breathe. The first few breaths remain shallow. The next is longer, interrupted by a little movement of his mouth as though he has begun to speak and changed his mind.

“Where?” he whispers. ᴹ

“Here,” Yuka says. ʸ

He looks at her.

This time he keeps looking.

His hand moves to the place behind her shoulder, and he draws her closer with enough certainty that she feels the difference at once.

“There,” Makima says quietly, to him. ᴹᵏ

Yuka holds him while he begins to cry.

She is grateful. The gratitude comes first, with a force which makes every other feeling seem too small to matter.

Then, while his face is against her neck, she looks over his shoulder at Makima and finds that the other feeling has arrived too.

She does not ask her to stop.
