# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 14 — The Day He Has

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S13E95
**Continuity state:** Mau slowly reconnects but is not cured; no new physical injury, amnesia or fixed-hour relapse. Yuka receives help from remaining guests while protecting him from demands for emotional reassurance.

*When: B+398, morning through early afternoon; day after the memory.*

In the morning, Yuka wakes with Mau's hand at the back of her head.

He is asleep. She checks before moving, watching the little changes in his face until she is certain enough to let herself breathe properly. His grip has loosened during the night. The other arm remains around her waist, holding her close without the terrible care he used when they first came into the room.

She does not remember deciding to sleep.

She remembers crying with him. Drinking water afterward. The awkward business of moving the plate because neither of them wanted to let go long enough to put it somewhere safe. Makima leaving when Mau said he wanted to lie down, asking whether she should stay within reach and accepting the answer without making him thank her again.

Yuka remembers the relief when his breathing changed.

Now there is light on the curtain. Somewhere beyond it, the ordinary work of the hotel has begun.

Mau's fingers move in her hair.

She looks up. His eyes have opened, but for a moment he has not found her.

“Good morning,” she says. ʸ

He looks down.

She waits.

His hand moves from her hair to her cheek, finding the edge of her face with a familiarity which makes her eyes sting.

“Morning.” ᴹ

“I was going to complain that you were keeping me here.” ʸ

He looks at the arm around her waist.

It begins to loosen.

She catches his wrist.

“It wasn't a serious complaint.” ʸ

He looks at her again. This time the beginning of a smile reaches his mouth, faint and tired and recognizably his.

“Okay.” ᴹ

She puts her head back down before she can turn the smile into something he has to keep giving her.

---

Breakfast is late and quiet.

They eat in the room because Mau cannot decide about going out and Yuka is hungry enough to decide that part for herself. He helps move the tray. When she asks which chair he wants, he looks between them until she takes one and leaves the other beside it.

After a few minutes, he asks for the plate she has set nearer her.

She passes it without remarking on the request.

He eats more than he did last night. The sight of it makes her want to keep offering things, and she has to stop herself from filling every pause with another question about whether he wants something else.

He puts the fork down at last.

“What?” he asks. ᴹ

Yuka looks at him.

“Nothing.” ʸ

His eyebrows move a little.

She lets out a breath.

“I'm trying not to ask you ten things at once.” ʸ

He looks at the table, considering.

“I might be slow.” ᴹ

“I noticed.” ʸ

The small answer comes out more tenderly than she intended. She reaches across and wipes a crumb from the corner of his mouth with her thumb.

He turns his head toward the touch.

“I'm here,” he says. ᴹ

“I know. You have breakfast on your face.” ʸ

He looks down at the plate as though it might explain how that happened. She laughs, and he looks back at her before the sound has finished.

For a little while, they manage to talk about the food.

---

He goes quiet again when they move to the balcony.

Yuka has brought the book she meant to finish before they went home. She reads a page twice without taking any of it in. Beside her, Mau looks toward the water. His hand rests open on the arm of the chair, where hers can find it without asking.

“Do you want me to read?” she asks. ʸ

He turns his head.

“You don't have to.” ᴹ

“I'm having trouble doing it in my head.” ʸ

He looks at the book, then back at her.

“Okay.” ᴹ

She begins. The first paragraph contains a name she is not certain how to say. She tries it, dislikes the result and tries another version.

Mau moves his fingers against hers.

“Which one?” she asks. ʸ

He takes a moment to answer.

“The first.” ᴹ

She goes back and reads the sentence again with the first version. This time it sounds even worse, but he is looking at her rather than the water, and she decides the character will have to live with it.

She gets several pages farther before Mau asks her to stop.

She closes the book at once.

“Too much?” ʸ

He nods, ashamed of the answer before she has done anything to give him a reason.

Yuka marks the page and sets the book down.

“I wanted to know what happened,” he says. ᴹ

“It'll still be there.” ʸ

He looks at the closed cover.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She puts her legs across his lap. His hand settles over her ankle, and they stay there until the patch of sun reaches the edge of his chair.

---

Elmesia brings lunch herself.

It is not an elaborate arrival. She has one part of the tray, an attendant has the other, and she tells Mau to remain seated when he begins to get up. The little table is put where both of them can reach it.

“I was offered three opinions about what you might want,” she says. ᴱˡ

Yuka looks at the covered dishes.

“Whose?” ʸ

“Mine, the kitchen's and somebody who believes she is being subtle.” ᴱˡ

From behind her, DIO makes an offended sound.

Mau looks toward it. DIO stands at the open door, plainly waiting to be asked whether she may come farther in.

“You can come,” he says. ᴹ

She does. Elmesia moves the spare chair out of her way and takes the place farther from Mau, leaving him enough room not to be surrounded.

They talk while Yuka serves the food. The boat left as arranged. The visitor did not threaten the staff on her way out. Testarossa has sent the necessary notice home and remains available if they need anything else.

Elmesia gives the information without asking Mau to produce a political conclusion from it.

He nods at the parts he can follow.

When his attention drifts, she lets the sentence finish and begins telling Yuka about the ridiculous request made by another guest that morning. It concerns a table which has already been moved twice because the view did not agree with the person looking at it.

Yuka listens, then glances at Mau.

“We should be very embarrassed about the hat.” ʸ

He looks at her.

She gestures toward the hat, occupying most of a chair inside.

“At least I moved the chair myself.” ʸ

He follows her gaze. A little breath leaves him, almost a laugh.

Elmesia continues the story without marking the improvement as an event.

---

Yuka walks Elmesia to the door afterward.

DIO has stayed beside Mau for a moment, not speaking. Yuka can see them through the opening: his hands folded loosely, her attention caught on the place where the injured fingers rest against the others.

Elmesia looks back once.

“Do you want us to come again later?” she asks. ᴱˡ

“Yes. I think so.” ʸ

“Then we'll ask later.” ᴱˡ

Yuka nods.

Elmesia waits. She has not asked how much of the memory she is entitled to know, and the restraint makes it easier for Yuka to say the little she can manage.

“He thought being away might save them.” ʸ

The words leave her with an ache which has been growing since she understood what he offered Yoru.

“And now he's back in the part where it didn't.” ʸ

Elmesia's expression changes. She looks toward the room again, then at Yuka.

“You don't have to make him ready for anybody today.” ᴱˡ

Yuka presses her lips together.

“I want him to have the day.” ʸ

“Then that's what we'll give him.” ᴱˡ

Inside, DIO has begun to speak. Yuka hears the first word and turns before the rest can become something Mau is asked to carry.

“Later,” she says, gently enough that DIO looks up instead of recoiling. ʸ

DIO looks at Mau. He is watching both of them, still trying to work out what has been interrupted.

She closes her mouth, nods and rises.

At the door, she touches Yuka's arm for a moment after Yuka allows it.

“Tell me if there's something,” DIO says. ᴰ

“I will.” ʸ

Elmesia leaves with her. Yuka closes the door and goes back to the balcony.

Mau moves his hand from the chair as she approaches, making room before she asks for it. She sits sideways across his lap and lays her head against his shoulder.

This time she does not bring the book.
