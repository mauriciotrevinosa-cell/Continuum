# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 15 — Not Without Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S13E96
**Continuity state:** First renewed initiative follows a full day of support; memory residue persists. Bird remark prompts voluntary embrace, not a cure. Remaining island company dines with present Mau. No broad waiver of Makima contact restrictions.

*When: B+398, afternoon through dinner, continuous after #14.*

In the afternoon, Mau asks what time it is.

Yuka tells him. He looks toward the balcony, then back at her.

“We were supposed to swim.” ᴹ

“That was yesterday.” ʸ

He goes still.

She sees what the answer has done and touches his shoulder.

“We can do it another day.” ʸ

He looks down at his hands. The skin across one knuckle is different from the hand he keeps remembering. He turns it, watching the altered fingers move.

“I know,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka sits closer.

“What are you looking at?” ʸ

“Mine.” ᴹ

She looks at his hand too. He lets her take it, and she traces the old mark without trying to smooth it away.

“Here?” she asks. ʸ

He nods.

“It was different.” ᴹ

“In the memory.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She puts his palm against her cheek. He watches her do it. After a moment his thumb moves, carefully, along the place beside her mouth.

“This one?” she asks. ʸ

His eyes fill.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She holds it there until he is ready to move it himself.

---

Makima comes when they ask her.

This time Mau is able to tell her where he keeps losing the room. He does it badly, stopping in the middle of one sentence to begin another. She listens without completing either for him.

“I know there's water,” he says. “I can hear it. Then I look at something and it's after. The part after.” ᴹ

“After the memory's fighting?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

He nods.

“And you find the room again when she speaks?” ᴹᵏ

Mau looks at Yuka.

“Sometimes. Not all the way.” ᴹ

Yuka keeps her face still enough for him to continue looking at her.

Makima asks whether he wants the same help as last night. He turns his wrist toward her before remembering to answer, then says yes aloud.

She sits where he indicates. Yuka remains on his other side.

There is no command. Makima follows the present contact through the connection already joining them, giving him the same small sensation from both directions. He can feel her fingers. He can also feel the living body receiving them, the place Ciel has been helping him name when the remembered quiet becomes too large.

He takes a breath.

Yuka says his name.

His eyes find her more quickly this time.

“I hear you,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“I heard you before too.” ᴹ

She nods. The effort not to cry makes the movement smaller than she intended.

Makima withdraws when he asks. She does not keep the contact because it worked. She waits until his hand has settled in Yuka's before getting up.

At the door, Yuka stops her.

“Thank you.” ʸ

Makima looks toward the bed, then back at her.

“You can ask again.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at the other woman's hand, the one which was on his wrist. The hurt is still there. Understanding where it comes from has not made it disappear.

She raises her eyes.

“I will if we need it.” ʸ

Makima nods and leaves.

---

For a while they do very little.

Mau asks for water and finishes it without needing to watch Yuka drink first. She opens The House with the Locked Garden where they stopped on the balcony. The shopkeeper has returned both shoes to the wrong woman, who has decided the heroine ought to come and retrieve them herself.

“She's making it worse,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuka looks up from the page.

“The woman with the shoes?” ʸ

“The one still throwing things over the wall.” ᴹ

She laughs quietly.

“She's thrown a letter this time.” ʸ

“An improvement.” ᴹ

He loses the next paragraph and asks her to read it again. She does, finding the sentence with her finger. When he points out that the letter has landed in the wet gutter, she turns back to check instead of asking how he feels.

Yuka leaves the book open on the table.

Afterward he looks tired in a more ordinary way. She knows better than to ask whether that means everything is all right now.

She stands, stretching one arm above her head.

“I'm going outside for a minute.” ʸ

Mau looks up.

“Where?” ᴹ

“Just down the path. I want some fruit.” ʸ

He nods, then looks at the open door.

She finds her sandals. One has slipped beneath the edge of the chair, and she bends to retrieve it while he watches with the concentration he has been giving the ordinary things she does.

“I might talk to the bird,” she adds. ʸ

His gaze changes.

She straightens, sandal in hand.

“He'll think he's won if we disappear.” ʸ

She begins to step past him.

Mau catches her hand.

It is gentle enough that she could keep walking. She stops before he has finished the movement, and he draws her back toward him, one arm going around her waist.

For a moment he holds her without speaking.

Then he rests his forehead against her side.

“You're not going to that bird without me.” ᴹ

Yuka's laugh breaks in the middle.

She puts the sandal down. Her hands find the back of his head, the shoulder beneath them, and she stands there while he holds her with a certainty she has not felt from him since before the landing.

He lifts his face.

“What?” ᴹ

She shakes her head, unable to make the answer small enough to say without crying.

“You can come,” she manages. ʸ

He looks at the sandals, then at his own bare feet.

“I need a minute.” ᴹ

“You can have one.” ʸ

He does not let go immediately. Neither does she.

---

They walk slowly.

Yuka keeps beside him without choosing every turn. At the first fork, she waits until he looks toward the path he remembers. He picks it, then pauses to make sure she is following.

She catches up and puts her arm around his.

The bird is not where they expect it to be.

They find an empty chair, a recently cleared table and an attendant who remembers which fruit Yuka likes. Mau orders a little bowl for himself. When the attendant asks whether they would like another, he considers the wall beside them.

“One more,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka puts her head against his shoulder.

They wait together. The bird does not arrive before the food, which Mau regards as poor manners, and then does not arrive before they have eaten most of it.

“He's busy,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Doing what?” ᴹ

“He has other people to rob.” ʸ

Mau looks toward the far side of the terrace.

“Disloyal.” ᴹ

She laughs. He looks back at her, the sound holding his attention until her smile has settled.

They finish their bowls. Yuka insists on leaving the extra one where they can see it, and Mau agrees, although he says the bird should not mistake this for an apology.

On the way back, he stops once beside the low wall.

Yuka waits.

He looks at the water, then down at his hand on the stone.

“It's still there,” he says. ᴹ

She understands which place he means without having to ask.

“I know.” ʸ

He takes a breath. When he moves again, he takes her hand himself.

---

They join the others for dinner after sunset.

Elmesia has left the choice open until they arrive. The smaller table has room for all of them without making Mau sit at the centre. He chooses the place beside Yuka. Makima takes the chair across from them, DIO sits near Elmesia, and Testarossa moves the extra setting so that Antonio has room behind her.

Nobody asks Mau to tell the story.

He asks Testarossa whether the visitor left by the boat she came on. She answers. He asks Elmesia whether anybody at the hotel was frightened. She tells him what the staff saw and what they were told, giving him enough to understand without claiming that nothing unusual happened.

“I'm sorry it came here,” he says. ᴹ

“You asked before using the terrace. You kept your agreement with me,” she answers. “We can discuss the rest when you have more of an appetite.” ᴱˡ

He looks down at the food.

“That's an unusual condition.” ᴹ

“I'm told you respond well to them.” ᴱˡ

Yuka's hand tightens around his beneath the table.

He looks at Elmesia long enough to find the invitation in her expression. Then he laughs, softly, and asks her to pass the dish on her left.

The conversation moves on. DIO complains that Elmesia promised a proper lesson and then spent most of it winning again. Testarossa says she has finally finished the chapter she began before their first game. Makima has an opinion about the move DIO should not have made, and DIO immediately asks why she did not mention it at the time.

“You were very certain,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

Mau looks up.

“That'll do it,” he says. ᴹ

DIO turns toward him in outrage. Elmesia covers her mouth with the edge of her napkin, and Yuka laughs against his shoulder.

He is tired before the meal ends. When Yuka asks whether he wants to go, he says yes without arranging a better version of the answer first.

He says good night to each of them. At Makima's chair he pauses, meeting her eyes.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

She looks surprised for a fraction of a second.

“Good night, Mau.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka waits beside him. He takes her hand, and they walk back past the empty breakfast terrace.

On the wall, beside the abandoned bowl, a small bird is eating the last piece of fruit.

Mau stops.

Yuka looks at the bird, then at him.

“Don't,” she whispers. ʸ

He looks personally betrayed.

She is still laughing when they reach their door.
