# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 16 — Tomorrow With Us

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S13E96
**Continuity state:** Yuka voluntarily invites Makima to spend B+400 with the couple. Obsessed first-friend uncertainty does not remove Makima competence/control nature. DIO/Ciel/Noise conversation stays inside the refuge. Sexual pause persists.

*When: B+399, afternoon through evening; one day after the recovery walk.*

Makima sees them before they see her.

They have stopped near the turn beyond the market, where Yuka is trying to make Mau look at something she has found in a basket. He bends toward it. She holds it up beside his face, considers the result and begins laughing.

He takes it from her and puts it beside hers.

The laugh becomes louder.

Makima pauses beneath the awning long enough to understand that neither has noticed the people waiting to get past. Then Yuka sees the blocked path, moves against Mau's side and apologizes. He moves with her, still holding the thing she chose.

It is the day after the dinner. She has seen him briefly since breakfast, when he told her he and Yuka were going out and asked whether she had plans with the others. She had said she would find something to read. He had asked whether she wanted him to bring anything back.

Nothing in the exchange had been difficult to understand.

She has spent an unreasonable amount of the afternoon thinking about it.

Now Elmesia notices them from the stall beside her and lifts a hand.

Yuka turns first. She says something to Mau which Makima cannot hear, and he looks across the path. He smiles when he sees them.

Makima does not have to decide whether approaching him will interrupt. He is already coming toward her.

---

The thing from the basket is a small carved bird.

Mau puts it in Makima's hand when she asks what they bought. It has a large, offended-looking head and a beak which occupies more of its face than seems useful.

“It's him,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima turns it once.

“It has a piece of fruit.” ᴹᵏ

“Evidence,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

She looks up at him.

“Are you going to give it to Ori?” ᴹᵏ

“I might need it for the hearing.” ᴹ

Yuka leans against his arm, laughing. Makima gives the little bird back before she can spend too long deciding which part of the answer was offered for her benefit.

Elmesia wants to know where they found it. Yuka points to the stall, then begins describing the other things in the basket. They go back to look, leaving Mau beside Makima in the shade.

“What did you choose?” he asks. ᴹ

She shows him the book. The Third Berth has a woman standing on a ship's rail on the cover, one foot planted on a trunk which appears much too large for the boat.

“Do you like it?” ᴹ

“I haven't started.” ᴹᵏ

“Then you'll have to tell me.” ᴹ

He says it easily. Not a request for information he needs before making a decision. Something he expects there will be time to hear later.

Makima looks down at the cover.

“I can do that.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka returns with Elmesia before the silence becomes uncomfortable. She has found another bird, smaller than the first and carrying something which might be a seed. Mau looks at it with an expression Makima cannot immediately interpret.

“We can't let them multiply,” he says. ᴹ

“It would be lonely.” ʸ

“It has my fruit.” ᴹ

“A terrible substitute for companionship.” ʸ

He looks at her. She smiles at him with the certainty of somebody who knows she has already won.

He takes the second bird and puts it beside the first in his hand.

“Organized crime,” he says. ᴹ

---

They sit together before going back.

Elmesia has messages waiting and leaves them after making Yuka promise to show her what Ori thinks of the carving. Mau remains between the two women, his hand resting against Yuka's back while she unwraps the little parcel of food they bought on the way.

He is more present than he was yesterday. Makima can feel the difference without reaching for the part of him which made last night so hard to observe. The remembered hurt remains. It is no longer the only thing arriving when he turns toward a sound.

Yuka offers her a piece from the parcel.

Makima accepts it.

“Are you doing anything tomorrow?” Yuka asks. ʸ

The question is ordinary enough that Makima nearly gives the answer which would be useful in any other conversation.

She stops before saying she is available for whatever they require.

“Nothing arranged.” ᴹᵏ

“Would you like to come with us?” ʸ

Makima looks at Mau. He is watching her with an expression which gives her very little to prepare against.

“Where?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

“We thought we'd take a boat along the shore. There's a place we saw from the water and haven't stopped at.” ʸ

“And afterward?” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at Mau.

“Food?” she suggests. ʸ

“Probably food.” ᴹ

“And then we can decide.” ʸ

Makima waits for the rest. When it does not arrive, she realizes there may not be one.

“The three of us?” ᴹᵏ

“If you want to,” Mau says. ᴹ

She looks back at Yuka.

There are reasons for the invitation. Makima can see enough of them to know she should not mistake it for an erasure of what came before. The woman across from her has had to decide whether she wants to offer it anyway.

For once, Makima finds that understanding the reason does not tell her what to do with receiving the thing itself.

“Yes,” she says. ᴹᵏ

It comes out quickly. She notices and does not correct it.

“What time?” ᴹᵏ

Yuka tells her when they intend to eat breakfast. Makima nods, commits the time to memory and then asks whether they will need anything for the boat.

Mau glances at the parcel on Yuka's lap.

“We should probably get more of that.” ᴹ

Yuka looks down. Most of the food is gone.

“You had some.” ʸ

“I remember it fondly.” ᴹ

Makima laughs before deciding whether the sound belongs in the conversation.

Mau turns toward her at once, pleased, and she finds that this is considerably more difficult to manage than being watched for a mistake.

---

Inside later, she asks DIO whether they have been to the little cove before.

DIO says no. She asks why Makima wants to know, and Makima explains the invitation in fewer words than the amount of attention she has given it deserves.

Ciel listens. Noise remains occupied until DIO calls him into the question about boats.

“Mau knows boats?” DIO asks. ᴰ

“Enough,” Noise says. ᴺ

“That's reassuring.” ᴰ

“It is.” ᴺ

Makima waits until they have finished.

“She asked,” she says. ᴹᵏ

DIO looks at her.

“Yuka?” ᴰ

Makima nods.

For a moment DIO appears to consider several answers. She chooses the one which does not tell Makima how much progress somebody else thinks she has made.

“Then you should go.” ᴰ

“I said I would.” ᴹᵏ

“Good.” ᴰ

Makima looks toward the place where Mau's attention remains outside with his wife. She does not follow their private conversation. The boundary is familiar now, and she has no need to test whether wanting to be closer has changed it.

There had been a time when the thing above her was an absence she wanted to reach: a power which could remove her, a figure to be held beyond the people who could not matter the same way.

Mau had survived the thing she thought final. More troublingly, he had continued speaking to her afterward. He had let her stay when he knew enough to refuse. He could tell her no without making the answer an end to every future conversation.

She still wanted too much from him. She could name that without becoming willing to want less.

Tomorrow, his wife had offered her a day in which he had already agreed to be there.

Makima thinks about the breakfast time again.

She has attended meetings with considerably more at stake and spent less effort deciding how early to arrive.
