# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 17 — The Man in the Book

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S13E97
**Continuity state:** Three-person day begins by Yuka invitation. Couple kisses freely without ending sexual pause; Makima respects ordinary space. No boat skill acquired or boat ownership yet; existing pass covers outing.

*When: B+400, breakfast through late morning at the cove.*

Makima is at the breakfast table before they arrive.

She has not ordered for them. Yuka notices that first, although she has not decided what she would have thought if Makima had. There is water in the glasses and a small plate of bread within reach of every chair. The other woman gets up when she sees them, then sits again after Mau tells her she does not have to.

“Did you eat?” he asks. ᴹ

“Not yet.” ᴹᵏ

“Then let's fix that.” ᴹ

Yuka takes the place beside him. She is still sleepy enough to lean her head against his shoulder while they look at the menu, and he angles it so that she can read without moving.

“You know what you want,” he says. ᴹ

“I like checking.” ʸ

“In case it escaped?” ᴹ

“In case they added something.” ʸ

Makima looks down at her own menu.

“They have,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka lifts her head.

Mau closes his eyes for a moment.

“Don't encourage her.” ᴹ

“You were wrong,” Yuka tells him, already looking for the addition. ʸ

Makima points it out. It is something small at the bottom of the page, available with the breakfast Yuka intended to order anyway. She considers it with a seriousness Mau finds entirely predictable, then asks whether he wants to share.

“Do I?” he asks. ᴹ

She turns toward him, smiling.

He kisses her before she can answer. It is quick enough not to become a performance, but she catches his chin and keeps him for a second one.

“Yes,” she says when she lets go. ʸ

He passes the menu to the attendant and orders the extra dish.

Across the table, Makima has lowered her gaze to the bread. Yuka sees the little pause, the decision about where to put her attention, and takes a piece herself before the silence can become a question somebody has to answer.

“Which one do you want?” she asks. ʸ

Makima looks up.

Yuka moves the plate between them.

“You can take the last one of those. He'll eat the other.” ʸ

Mau looks at the plate.

“I have been consulted.” ᴹ

“You love being consulted.” ʸ

He takes the piece she has assigned him. Makima chooses the one she actually wanted and begins telling them what she noticed on the walk over.

---

The man at the pier remembers them.

He asks whether they want the same route. Yuka points to the little cove they saw last time and asks whether they can stop there instead of continuing around the point.

He checks the water, tells them where they can land and explains which part of the beach will be in shade by midday. Mau asks about the return. Makima listens to the answer without trying to make the man shorten it.

When he has finished, she asks whether the path beyond the beach reaches the little buildings farther inland.

“It does. Not all of them are places you can visit.” ᵃᵗᵗ

“Then we'll ask when we get there.” ᴹᵏ

The man nods, satisfied. He takes down their names and recognizes the seal on the pass before Mau has finished showing it to him.

Yuka steps aboard first. Mau helps her over the gap, then turns toward Makima. She takes the offered hand for the crossing and releases it once she has both feet on the deck.

The small distinction is deliberate. Yuka notices because she is trying to notice what Makima does rather than only what she fears she might do next.

They choose seats beneath the shade. There is room for Makima opposite them, where she can see the shore without leaning past anybody. She sits with the book she mentioned yesterday beside her.

“You brought it,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I started.” ᴹᵏ

“And?” ᴹ

“I dislike the man who explains everything.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at the cover.

“Is he meant to be disliked?” ʸ

“I don't think the writer knows.” ᴹᵏ

Mau laughs. Makima looks at him, pleased enough by the response that she nearly misses Yuka asking which man she means.

She finds the page. Yuka reads the paragraph and hands the book to Mau, who reads it twice before offering an interpretation neither woman accepts.

By the time the boat leaves the pier, they are already arguing about a person who has no idea how much trouble he has caused.

---

The cove has more steps than it appeared to have from the water.

Yuka informs Mau that this is becoming a pattern. He says they have not reached them yet and should avoid judging too quickly. Makima points out that the steps appear to lead away from the beach rather than toward it.

Yuka stops complaining immediately.

“Then we don't need them.” ʸ

“I admire your process,” Mau says. ᴹ

She takes his hand and leads him toward the sand. Makima follows with the food they collected before boarding, declining his offer to store it because they have almost reached the place where they intend to sit.

The beach is small enough that they can see both ends. There are a few people near the landing and nobody at the farther line of shade. Yuka chooses that part after checking that the ground is comfortable enough for the cloth they brought.

Mau spreads it. Makima sets the food down and asks where Yuka wants the bag. Yuka points without thinking, then looks up as the other woman puts it there exactly as asked.

They sit.

For a little while, the boatman is still visible at the landing, checking a line before he takes the boat farther out to wait. Makima watches the practical business with interest.

“Would you want one?” she asks Mau. ᴹᵏ

“A boat?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

He looks toward it.

“I don't know enough to own that one.” ᴹ

Yuka lifts her head from the bag she is opening.

“You could learn.” ʸ

“That's how most of our problems begin.” ᴹ

“Some of them are good problems.” ʸ

He looks at her, considering, and she reaches over to push the cloth away from a place where it has folded beneath his knee.

“We could learn,” she adds. ʸ

He turns back toward the water.

“Maybe.” ᴹ

Makima listens to the answer without trying to improve it into a plan.

---

They walk along the water before eating.

Mau leaves his shoes beside the cloth. Yuka gives him hers, watches them disappear into the inventory and then objects when he asks whether she intends to charge him for carrying them home.

“You offered.” ʸ

“I offered to take the shoes.” ᴹ

“And I am being very cooperative.” ʸ

She puts both arms around him from behind while he is still trying to organize the things they have left. He stops, one hand on hers. She rests her cheek between his shoulders.

Makima turns toward the water, giving them the little moment without moving so far away that it becomes an announcement.

“Are we going anywhere?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Eventually.” ʸ

He turns within her arms. She lifts her face to him, and he bends to kiss her, taking his time until she is smiling against his mouth.

When they separate, he leaves his forehead against hers briefly.

“Eventually?” ᴹ

“Now,” she says, and takes his hand. ʸ

They catch up with Makima near the wet sand. She has found a little trail left by something moving beneath the surface and is trying to decide where it ends. Mau kneels to look without disturbing it. Yuka stands beside him, holding the back of his shirt so that he does not put it in the water when he leans farther.

They do not find the creature. They find another trail and follow that one until the first wave reaches Yuka's ankles.

She makes a noise and steps back against Mau. He steadies her, laughing, and then gets the next wave himself.

Makima watches the water take their neat little investigation apart.

“We may have been outmaneuvered,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her.

Then she laughs, and this time it is not because Mau has already begun.
