# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 18 — For a Little While

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S13E97
**Continuity state:** Makima asks to hold Mau hand; Yuka temporarily permits and Mau consents. Voluntary Yuka touch grants only that contact, not broad restriction waiver. Yuka shares mixed gratitude/hurt; link does not give Makima complete insight. No romantic reciprocity from Mau.

*When: B+400, cove picnic and unhurried afternoon; continuous after #17.*

They have packed too much food.

Mau establishes this when Yuka takes the third wrapped dish out of the bag. She gives him a look which suggests he has mistaken an achievement for a problem.

“We don't have to eat it all.” ʸ

“You've said that before.” ᴹ

“And you survived.” ʸ

Makima accepts the plate Yuka passes her. The three of them sit facing the water, with the dishes between them and a folded corner of the cloth beneath the thing most likely to tip over.

Mau has put the boat's return time where he will remember it. Until then, the only immediate decision concerns which of the parcels should be opened first. Yuka chooses the flatbreads wrapped around grilled chicken. Makima asks about the little pickled plums. He gives them both what they ask for and keeps the roasted potatoes with lime within reach of his own knee.

Yuka notices.

“Protective.” ʸ

“I've learned.” ᴹ

“Not enough,” Makima says, looking at the piece Yuka has already taken. ᴹᵏ

Mau turns.

His wife is chewing with an expression of perfect innocence.

“You were distracted,” she tells him. ʸ

He takes the plate into his lap. Makima laughs, and Yuka points at her as though she has found a witness who will finally be useful.

“She understands.” ʸ

“I understand the sequence,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

“Very neutral,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

“I thought so.” ᴹᵏ

They talk about the book while they eat.

Makima has read farther than the passage she showed them on the boat. Yuka wants to know whether that improves her opinion of the man. It does not.

Mau offers to defend Iven on the basis that at least Nera will not have to sleep in the rain.

“It's a covered deck,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Then he's solved a smaller problem than I thought.” ᴹ

“She already solved it,” Makima points out. “He has complicated her solution.” ᴹᵏ

“You're making it difficult to help.” ᴹ

“He would say that too,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka laughs hard enough to have to set her cup down. Mau watches her, smiling in spite of himself, and lets the argument go somewhere he did not intend.

The breeze moves the edge of Makima's sleeve. She presses it down with the hand not holding her plate, following Yuka's explanation of why the other character should have left the room three chapters earlier. She disagrees about the leaving. Some rooms, she thinks, become easier to understand if you remain long enough to hear what people say after they believe they have won.

Mau looks at her over his cup.

“You're giving him a lot of credit.” ᴹ

“I didn't say he used the opportunity well.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka leans over, laughing, and puts her hand on Makima's arm.

The other woman moves at once.

It is a small movement, but all three of them notice it. Makima's plate tilts. Mau reaches to steady the edge before anything falls.

“I'm sorry,” Makima says. “The restriction—” ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her own hand.

“I touched you.” ʸ

Makima stops.

“I meant to,” Yuka adds. “That was okay. I'm telling you that one was okay.” ʸ

The precision arrives only after she sees the care with which Makima is listening. She does not offer a rule she cannot mean tomorrow. She leaves her hand there for another moment, making the permission something the other woman can see as well as hear.

Then she takes it back to rescue her cup from the sand.

Mau steadies Makima's plate until she has it properly again. She thanks him with the same composure she had before, but she looks at the place Yuka touched for a little longer than the contact lasted.

---

After they eat, Yuka puts the covered dishes together and decides the rest can wait.

Mau stores what should not sit out. He keeps the water, the book and a little packet Yuka says she is not finished with. Then she settles against his side, her head on his shoulder and her feet stretched toward the edge of the cloth.

Makima remains seated on the other side of him.

For a while they watch the people farther along the beach. Somebody at the landing has dropped a hat and is arguing with the person who offered to retrieve it. Neither seems willing to be the first one to get wet.

Mau is about to offer a prediction when Makima says his name.

He turns.

“May I hold your hand too?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

The question is quiet. She looks at him first, then at Yuka, giving both of them time to understand what she has asked instead of reaching before an answer can form.

Yuka lifts her head from his shoulder.

Mau waits. His hand remains on the cloth beside him.

“For a little while,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima's eyes return to him.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

He offers the open hand. She takes it carefully, adjusting her fingers so that she does not put pressure on the ones which move less easily.

Yuka settles back against his other side. Her hand finds the front of his shirt and stays there, holding the fold beneath his collar.

Mau looks toward the landing again. The hat has been retrieved by a third person, who appears distinctly unimpressed by the argument he interrupted.

“I had the wrong one,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka follows his gaze.

“Which did you choose?” ʸ

“The one with the stick.” ᴹ

“I liked the other one.” ʸ

Makima listens to them disagree about the usefulness of the stick. Her hand remains in his through the entire unimportant conversation.

---

When Yuka sits up again, she looks at Makima rather than at Mau.

“There's something I wanted to tell you.” ʸ

Makima turns toward her.

Mau feels the change in Yuka's fingers against his shirt. He moves his shoulder enough to let her sit comfortably without making the other woman release him before she has chosen to.

“I was glad you helped him,” Yuka says. ʸ

Makima looks at him once.

“I could reach the present sensation because we're already joined. I didn't know something you had failed to know.” ᴹᵏ

“I know.” ʸ

Yuka takes a breath.

“I knew when I asked you to come today. That's part of why I asked.” ʸ

Makima waits.

“And it still hurt,” Yuka says. “I was there, and I was talking to him, and I couldn't get him to look at me like he did when you helped.” ʸ

Mau turns toward her fully.

She looks at him before he can speak.

“I'm not asking you to apologize.” ʸ

He closes his mouth. After a moment he nods.

Yuka looks back at Makima.

“I didn't like feeling that while you were doing something I wanted you to keep doing.” ʸ

The sea moves beyond their feet. Makima's hand has become very still inside his.

“Would you rather I hadn't?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

“No.” ʸ

The answer is immediate enough to change Makima's expression.

“No,” Yuka repeats, more quietly. “I wanted you to help him. I wanted him back here. I still do.” ʸ

Makima looks down at their joined hands, then at the woman beside him.

“I don't know what would make that stop hurting,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka lets out a small breath.

“I don't either. I just wanted you to know why I said thank you.” ʸ

For a while nobody tries to improve the answer.

Then Mau reaches with his free hand and touches the one Yuka has kept against his chest.

“I heard you,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at him.

“When I couldn't answer. I kept hearing you.” ᴹ

Her mouth trembles. She leans forward until her forehead rests against his, and he meets her there without letting the space become another thing she has to cross alone.

Makima releases his hand while they are close. Not abruptly, not as a demand to be noticed. She sets hers on the cloth beside her and waits until Yuka has sat back again.

“I would like to stay for the rest of the day,” she says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looks at her.

Then she reaches for the packet she said she had not finished.

“We still haven't decided what we're doing after this.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹᵏ

Mau looks from one to the other.

“We could start by deciding whether there's any of that left for me.” ᴹ

Yuka opens the packet, examines its contents and looks back at him with an expression he has learned to fear.

Makima laughs first this time.
