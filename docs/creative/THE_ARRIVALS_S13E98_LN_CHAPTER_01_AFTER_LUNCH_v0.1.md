# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 19 — After Lunch

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S13E98
**Continuity state:** Three-person day remains actual leisure, with book disagreement and ordinary shopping. Makima keeps obsession and boundaries; Yuka can like her without forgiveness mandate. Couple affection stays active and reciprocal.

*When: B+400, afternoon through return from the cove.*

They do not get up when they finish the last of the food.

Mau makes sure of this before putting the dishes away. Yuka has already stretched out again. Makima has opened the book, but she holds it with a finger between the pages while she watches the little boat beyond the landing.

“We have time,” he tells her. ᴹ

“I remember.” ᴹᵏ

“You're looking at it like it's about to escape.” ᴹ

She turns toward him.

“I was wondering what makes this the right time to do nothing.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka opens one eye.

“Lunch.” ʸ

Mau laughs. Makima looks at the place the food occupied, then back at Yuka.

“A useful rule.” ᴹᵏ

“He's still learning.” ʸ

“I've made considerable progress.” ᴹ

Yuka pats his leg and closes her eye again.

Makima opens the book properly. After a few minutes, her expression changes. Mau notices because it is the first time she has looked offended by somebody who is not there.

“What happened?” he asks. ᴹ

“He sent another letter.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka sits up.

“To her?” ʸ

“To the person arranging the journey.” ᴹᵏ

“Did he ask her first?” ʸ

Makima turns the book so that she can see the paragraph.

Yuka reads it. Then she takes the book and gives it to Mau.

“Defend that.” ʸ

He looks at the page. The man in the story appears to have mistaken making an arrangement for having a conversation. The woman he is trying to impress has said something very clear in the previous paragraph, which he has chosen to interpret as a problem the next letter will solve.

Mau reads to the end.

“He should probably stop writing.” ᴹ

“An excellent development,” Makima says. ᴹᵏ

Yuka settles against Mau again and asks her to read the next part aloud.

---

They listen until the shade has shifted enough to make Yuka move the edge of the cloth.

Makima reads without performing the conversation for them. Her voice changes slightly when she reaches a sentence she dislikes, and by the third time Mau can predict it from the pause before the words. Yuka begins predicting it too. Their glances become distracting enough that Makima stops in the middle of a paragraph.

“What?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

“Nothing,” Yuka says. ʸ

Mau looks away toward the water.

Makima waits.

Yuka tries to explain and begins laughing halfway through. Mau makes it worse by asking whether the next letter will require a larger boat.

Makima looks down at the page.

Then she reads the man's next sentence in exactly the tone they have been waiting for.

Yuka covers her face. Mau bends forward, laughing, and Makima stops pretending the interruption has inconvenienced her.

They do not finish the chapter. When the boatman comes closer to the landing at the agreed time, Makima puts a marker where they stopped and closes the book around it.

“We should find out what she does,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Tomorrow?” Makima asks. ᴹᵏ

It is the smallest question she has asked all day.

Yuka looks at Mau. He is folding the cloth, making sure none of the corners has caught sand inside.

“We can read a little after breakfast,” she says. ʸ

Makima nods. She puts the book in the bag herself.

---

They stop at the little shop above the landing before boarding.

The steps turn out to be worth the complaint. Yuka reaches the top and announces that whoever built them has not considered people who have already had lunch. Mau offers her water, then stops her from leaning back against a wall still warm from the afternoon sun.

Makima goes inside first. When they join her, she is looking at a shelf of small wrapped sweets and trying to determine which are made nearby.

The woman behind the counter explains. Yuka asks about the filling of one she has not seen before. Mau asks whether it will survive the trip home, because he already knows where the question will lead.

Makima chooses a packet before either of them has finished.

“For you?” Yuka asks. ʸ

She pauses.

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

“Tell me if it's good.” ʸ

The woman offers a little piece from the opened sample. Makima tries it. She considers the taste before answering rather than looking for the response most likely to keep the conversation agreeable.

“It is.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka buys another packet. Mau looks between them with a helpless resignation both women appear to find entertaining.

“I was saving room for dinner,” he says. ᴹ

“You can keep saving it,” Yuka tells him. ʸ

He takes the things which need to be stored and leaves her with the sample she has decided belongs to the walk back down.

---

On the return, Makima sits where she can see both of them and the shore.

Yuka has her feet tucked beside her on the shaded bench. Mau sits close enough for her to lean against him, one arm along the seat behind her. When the boat turns, he steadies the cup in her hand instead of asking her to move.

Makima watches the practical little exchange.

She had wanted to know what being allowed to stay would feel like. She knows what it can feel like to be wanted for a purpose. This is more difficult to hold still long enough to examine. There have been several hours in which she could have left without ruining anything and was not asked to.

The things she wants from Mau have not become small. She does not pretend they have. She has touched his hand, watched him laugh, been given time beside him without having to earn the next minute by producing something useful.

Yuka looks across at her.

“You're quiet.” ʸ

Makima turns from the water.

“I'm thinking about the day.” ᴹᵏ

“Was it all right?” Mau asks. ᴹ

She looks at him, then at his wife.

“Yes.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka smiles, small and unforced, and goes back to looking at the shore. The simplicity of it occupies Makima for the rest of the crossing.

---

Back in their room, Yuka puts the empty bag on the table and catches Mau before he can go to the balcony.

Both arms go around his waist. She presses her face into his back and stays there until he lays his hands over hers.

“Hello,” he says. ᴹ

“Hello.” ʸ

“Did you lose something?” ᴹ

“I'm checking.” ʸ

He turns carefully within the embrace. She rises toward him, and the kiss she gives him is familiar, happy and unhurried enough that he forgets what he intended to do outside.

When she draws back, she keeps her arms around him.

“I liked today,” she says. ʸ

“Me too.” ᴹ

“I liked her today.” ʸ

He looks at her properly. She lets him, watching the care with which he decides what not to make of the answer.

“You don't have to decide anything else because of that.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

She rests her forehead against his chest.

“I wanted you to know.” ʸ

He kisses her hair, then waits until she lifts her face to kiss her mouth again.

“Thank you for inviting her,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka studies him.

“You're welcome.” ʸ

Then she catches his collar, pulls him a little closer and says, “And now I want you to help me decide what I'm wearing to dinner.” ʸ

He looks toward the open wardrobe.

“Do I get an opinion?” ᴹ

“Several. I'll tell you which one I like.” ʸ

He is still smiling when she takes his hand and leads him over.
