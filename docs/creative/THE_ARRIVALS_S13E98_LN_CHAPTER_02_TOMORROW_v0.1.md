# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 20 — Tomorrow

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S13E98
**Continuity state:** Creator delegated gift choice: Elmesia gives couple an ordinary refurbished small sailboat, Tomorrow. Personal property, no political obligation or magical upgrade; may moor here or store. Both require supervised practice, no instant sailing mastery. DIO provided bird bowl.

*When: B+401, afternoon; after the promised shared reading at breakfast.*

Elmesia asks them to come to the other landing after lunch.

Yuka wants to know whether it involves steps. Elmesia considers the question seriously enough that Mau begins to suspect she has heard about yesterday.

“A few,” she says. ᴱˡ

Yuka looks at him.

“I didn't build them.” ᴹ

“You're very defensive.” ʸ

“I've had a difficult week with stairs.” ᴹ

Makima closes the book they have been reading. They have finally reached the woman's answer to the third letter, and Yuka has declared it satisfactory enough to leave the next chapter until tomorrow.

“Should I bring this?” Makima asks. ᴹᵏ

Elmesia smiles.

“You can leave it here.” ᴱˡ

DIO looks from her to the two of them. She knows something. Mau can see the pleasure she is taking in not being the one required to explain it.

“What did you do?” he asks. ᴹ

“I was very helpful,” DIO says. ᴰ

“That's an alarming answer.” ᴹ

She refuses to improve it.

---

The boat is tied at the quiet end of the landing.

Mau sees the man who took them to the cove first. He is standing beside a smaller craft with a single mast, checking something near the side before he steps back to let them pass.

Yuka stops beside Mau.

The hull has been cleaned and refinished. There is room to sit under a little shade, a low cabin beneath it and a folded sail waiting along the boom. Somebody has left a small bowl on the table inside the covered part.

Elmesia stands on the landing beside it.

“I promised you something when you came,” she says. ᴱˡ

Mau looks at her.

Then he looks at the boat again.

“Elmesia.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴱˡ

“That's a boat.” ᴹ

“I'm relieved we agree.” ᴱˡ

Yuka puts a hand on his arm. He cannot tell whether she is holding him still or keeping herself from moving before somebody has explained what she thinks she has understood.

“For us?” she asks. ʸ

“For you.” ᴱˡ

Mau looks at the man beside the mast, then back at Elmesia.

“We don't know how to sail.” ᴹ

“You can learn.” ᴱˡ

Yuka turns toward him so quickly he begins to smile before she has spoken.

“That's what I said.” ʸ

“I remember.” ᴹ

“I chose this one before you arrived,” Elmesia says. “It needed work before I was willing to give it to you. I would have preferred the work to finish sooner, but I refused to have it rushed.” ᴱˡ

She gestures toward the deck.

“Come and look before you decide how unreasonable I'm being.” ᴱˡ

---

The cabin is small and unexpectedly comfortable.

Yuka goes down first. Mau follows after checking where to put his hand, and finds her already testing whether she can sit with her legs stretched along the bench.

She can.

Her face changes.

“Oh,” she says. ʸ

There are places for the things a day on the water needs: food kept out of the spray, dry clothes, cups which will not immediately slide across the table. The bench can be made into a place to lie down, although Mau has to duck when he tries to stand too close to the lower end of the roof.

Yuka catches his sleeve before he can do it again.

“Somebody has considered us,” she says. ʸ

He looks around the little space. It does have the unsettling quality of an answer to a question he has never put together well enough to ask.

Elmesia looks down through the opening.

“You can take it with you if that is what you prefer. If you leave it here, it will be looked after. You can tell me what you decide once you've learned which end you intend to point at the sea.” ᴱˡ

Mau looks up.

“There are several?” ᴹ

“We begin lessons this afternoon,” the boatman says from above. ᵃᵗᵗ

Yuka laughs. Mau sits beside her, taking the hand she has been holding out since she found the bench.

“It's too much,” he says when Elmesia comes close enough to hear without leaning over them. ᴹ

She steps down carefully.

“You can tell me that while I show you the storage.” ᴱˡ

“You know that's not an answer.” ᴹ

“I know you're about to ask whether accepting it gives me the right to ask for something you wouldn't otherwise agree to do.” ᴱˡ

He goes quiet.

Elmesia rests her hand on the edge of the table.

“It gives me the pleasure of giving you a boat. I'd like you to let me have that.” ᴱˡ

Yuka squeezes his fingers.

He looks at her, then at the little cabin again.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

Elmesia smiles.

“You're welcome.” ᴱˡ

---

The bowl is DIO's contribution.

She admits this when Yuka asks, watching from the landing as they come back up. It is broad enough to hold fruit, heavy enough not to fall over easily and unmistakably meant for one particular guest.

“He still won't respect it,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

“He may be more polite at sea.” ᴰ

“You have a generous view of him.” ᴹ

“He hasn't stolen my food.” ᴰ

“Yet.” ʸ

Elmesia gives DIO a hand aboard. DIO takes it, steps across and keeps it a moment longer than the crossing requires.

The boatman begins by showing them where things belong.

Mau listens. Yuka asks the first question, then makes him repeat the answer so that she can try the knot herself. He does, patiently, waiting while she gets the loop the wrong way around and works out why it will not hold.

Mau is quicker at seeing the shape. He is not immediately good at doing it with a line under tension. The difference interests him enough that he asks to try again rather than let somebody tell him he has understood.

Yuka watches his second attempt.

“Show me that part.” ʸ

He loosens it and begins again, slower, until she can follow the turn with her own hands.

Makima stays beside DIO near the landing, listening when invited and leaving them the space to make their own mistakes. Before the lines are released, both return inside Mau by agreement; neither is left outside the reach they have arranged with him. Elmesia takes the place the boatman gives her once they are ready to move a short distance under his direction.

They remain close to shore.

There is enough wind to make the lesson real and enough supervision to prevent understanding the explanation from being mistaken for knowing how to manage every condition. Mau has to correct a movement twice. Yuka tells him when she sees the turn begin too early. He listens and changes what he is doing.

When the boat settles into the direction they wanted, she looks at him with a delight which makes the mistake worth having made in front of her.

He leans across to kiss her.

“Eyes here,” the boatman reminds him. ᵃᵗᵗ

Mau turns back immediately. Yuka begins laughing so hard that Elmesia has to move the loose line away from her feet.

---

They have not chosen a name by the time they return to the landing.

Elmesia has deliberately left the place for it clear. DIO and Makima come out again once the boat is secured at the landing. Yuka inspects the little blank area and tells Mau that he is not allowed to call it anything insulting about the bird.

“That removes several good choices.” ᴹ

“We'll survive.” ʸ

They remain aboard after the others have stepped off. Elmesia has gone to speak to the man about the arrangements for looking after it. DIO waits with Makima farther along the landing, holding the book they left at breakfast.

Yuka sits beside Mau beneath the shade.

“What do you want to call it?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks toward the water. The answer takes her long enough that he stops trying to think of a joke to improve his earlier suggestion.

“Tomorrow,” she says at last. ʸ

He looks at her.

“We can decide later if you don't like it.” ʸ

“I like it.” ᴹ

She turns back to him. He takes her hand and brings it to his mouth, then draws her close enough to kiss her properly this time, with the boat securely tied and nobody waiting for him to watch where he is going.

She is smiling when they part.

“Tomorrow,” she says again, testing it. ʸ

He looks at the water beyond the little roof.

“Tomorrow.” ᴹ
