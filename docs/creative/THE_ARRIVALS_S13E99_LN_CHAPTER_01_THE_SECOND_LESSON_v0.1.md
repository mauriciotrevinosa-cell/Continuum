# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 21 — The Second Lesson

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S13E99
**Continuity state:** Yuka practices sailing under supervision; Tomorrow stored empty with equipment. Memory residue remains manageable and disclosed. DIO/Elmesia voluntarily hold hands without relabeling other bonds; all outside bodies stay near Mau. Sexual pause remains.

*When: B+402, last full island day; return planned B+403.*

On their last full morning, Yuka wakes first.

She knows because Mau does not move when she lifts her head. He is on his back, one arm beneath the pillow and the other resting across the place where she had been. His face is turned toward her side of the bed.

She watches him for a while.

The quiet has become familiar enough that she no longer has to use it immediately. She can lie here, warm against him, thinking about breakfast and the boat and whether she has left something in the drawer she did not intend to use. He can sleep through her making three plans and abandoning two.

She reaches up to touch his face.

His eyes open before her fingers arrive.

“Caught,” he says. ᴹ

“You were awake.” ʸ

“Recently.” ᴹ

“How recently?” ʸ

He considers.

“Enough.” ᴹ

She pushes his shoulder. He catches her hand, brings it down and draws her across him until she has to put her other palm beside his head to keep her balance.

“You could have said good morning,” she tells him. ʸ

“You looked busy.” ᴹ

“Doing what?” ʸ

“Investigating.” ᴹ

She bends to kiss him. He returns it, his hand settling behind her shoulder, and she takes her time drawing away because there is still nobody waiting to use the morning before they do.

“Good morning,” she says. ʸ

“Much better.” ᴹ

She kisses him again for that answer, then puts her head against his chest.

“Tomorrow?” she asks. ʸ

“The boat or going home?” ᴹ

She begins laughing.

“We have made this difficult.” ʸ

He holds her while she recovers enough to tell him which one she meant.

---

The second lesson belongs mostly to Yuka.

She has asked for that before they arrive. Mau sits where the boatman puts him, helps when instructed and otherwise watches his wife discover how much of yesterday she remembers once she has to do it herself.

Some of it goes well.

Some of it produces a look toward Mau which he has enough sense not to answer with a smile.

“You can say it,” she tells him after the second correction. ʸ

“You're doing the turn earlier than yesterday.” ᴹ

“I know that part.” ʸ

“Then I have nothing useful.” ᴹ

The boatman shows her the place to watch before she begins. She tries again. Mau holds the line where she asked him to, waiting for her signal rather than deciding she is about to need help.

This time the turn comes more cleanly.

Yuka looks at him at once.

“I saw,” he says. ᴹ

She smiles so openly that the boatman gives them both another reminder about attending to the boat while it is moving.

Mau does not even try to defend himself.

---

They put it away after lunch.

The boatman goes through the things which need to be dried, secured or left loose before the vessel is stored. Mau follows him through the little cabin, asking questions rather than assuming that because he can make the boat disappear the things inside it will cease to require care.

Yuka takes the fruit bowl out.

“That comes with us,” she says. ʸ

“It was going to.” ᴹ

“I want to hold it.” ʸ

He lets her. When the last line has been released from the landing and the boat is empty, he stores Tomorrow with the equipment belonging to it. The water closes over the place where the hull rested.

Yuka looks at the empty berth.

“We actually have a boat.” ʸ

“I was there.” ᴹ

She turns toward him.

“You can sound more excited.” ʸ

He takes her hand.

“We have a boat.” ᴹ

She studies his expression, finds the delight he is no longer making much effort to hide and rises to kiss him beside the empty mooring.

The boatman turns away with a discretion they both notice too late.

---

They spend the afternoon at the pool.

Yuka insists this is the correct use of a last afternoon. Mau agrees so readily that she becomes suspicious, but he has already put the things they need beneath the shade and offered her the place beside him.

They eat. They swim. For a while he floats in the quiet part of the water with the familiar, measured use of Authority, letting her rest against him while she describes the things they should tell Ori about learning to sail.

“Not the first turn,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Of course not.” ᴹ

“Or the second.” ʸ

“You're making the story very short.” ᴹ

She lifts her head and gives him a look.

He laughs, keeping them steady until she decides where to settle again.

There are moments when the old memory reaches him. Yuka feels the small change in his hand before he says anything. He looks toward the edge of the pool, finds the people moving there, then looks back at her.

“A minute,” he says once. ᴹ

She nods and keeps her hand over his. He takes the minute. When he speaks again, he tells her which part of the story she has forbidden him from describing.

She answers without making him explain the interruption.

---

They walk with the others before dinner.

Testarossa is returning to Tempest tomorrow. Elmesia has arrangements waiting beyond the island and a growing collection of messages which she has allowed to remain arrangements rather than emergencies. They agree about the future tea at the house without pretending to know exactly which afternoon all of them will be free.

DIO walks beside Elmesia. Mau remains a little ahead with Yuka and Makima, close enough that the outside bodies stay within the reach agreed with him. Nobody has to shorten the walk to preserve the little bit of privacy between the two women.

At the turn above the beach, Elmesia offers DIO her hand.

DIO looks down at it.

“For the steps?” she asks. ᴰ

“If you like.” ᴱˡ

DIO takes it. There are only a few steps, and then a level path, and Elmesia does not reclaim her hand when the reason she offered has ceased to be useful.

Neither does DIO.

Mau catches Yuka looking back. She catches him catching her and kisses the corner of his mouth before he can tell her not to say anything.

“Very subtle,” he murmurs. ᴹ

“I'm improving.” ʸ

Makima looks toward the two women and then, with a restraint which costs her very little for once, asks Yuka about the second sailing lesson.

Mau gives his wife a chance to tell the version she prefers.

He adds only one detail.

She steps on his foot.

---

They say good night later than intended.

Elmesia embraces Yuka first. When Mau thanks her again, she tells him that he is allowed to show her the boat after he has used it, instead of apologizing for owning it.

“I wasn't apologizing.” ᴹ

“You're preparing.” ᴱˡ

He looks at Yuka. She has declined to help him.

“We'll come back,” he says. ᴹ

“Let me know when you do.” ᴱˡ

“We will.” ʸ

DIO stays out until they are ready to return to the room. She and Elmesia speak a little apart, then come back without announcing what the conversation has become. Testarossa has Antonio's luggage arrangements complete and a final joke about the difficulty of finding souvenirs which will fit the person carrying them.

Makima leaves the unfinished book with Yuka overnight. They have agreed to take it home together, with the next chapter still waiting.

When Mau and Yuka finally close their door, most of the packing is already done. The inventory holds the things which would otherwise have filled the room. Her hat remains on the chair, too large to be forgotten.

She turns toward him.

“Come here.” ʸ

He does. She puts her arms around his neck, and he bends far enough that she does not have to pull him down to kiss him.

“I had a good time,” she says afterward. ʸ

“So did I.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a moment, including the tiredness he has not hidden from her.

“Even the stairs?” ʸ

He begins to laugh.

“Especially your opinion of them.” ᴹ

She draws him toward the bed, still disputing the fairness of that answer. He follows, puts the water within reach before they lie down, and lets her take as much of his pillow as she wants.
