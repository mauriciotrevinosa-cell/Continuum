# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 82
## Chapter 22 — The Things Waiting at Home

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 82
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S13E99
**Continuity state:** Couple return through verified home anchor with DIO/Makima inside and Tomorrow stored. Testarossa returns Tempest; Elmesia has own departure. Household ties active; Shoko maternal support, Kamish talks (ᴷ). Ori withheld request still not revealed.

*When: B+403, morning homecoming through afternoon.*

They come home with less luggage than Yuka's last inspection of the room suggests they ought to have.

Mau has stored the things she found in the drawer, the things beneath them and the packet she insists she remembers putting somewhere else. She has watched the last one disappear into the inventory with visible suspicion, as though the room may produce another as soon as they turn their backs.

It does not.

They say their last goodbyes at the landing. Testarossa's return to Tempest is arranged. Elmesia has her own journey waiting and makes them promise to tell her when they take Tomorrow out again. Antonio inclines his great head toward Mau, then lowers a claw for Yuka to tap in farewell.

DIO and Makima go inside before the exchange.

Yuka takes Mau's hand.

He checks the familiar home anchor, feels the soldier waiting where he expects and brings them back together.

The air changes.

Somebody in the kitchen is objecting to a lid being opened too soon.

Yuka looks at him.

“Home,” she says. ʸ

He smiles before they have taken the first step toward the sound.

---

Umaru sees them through the open doorway.

She stops with a cup halfway to her mouth. Then she puts it down with the care of somebody who wants to be free to make an important demand.

“Did you bring it?” ᵁ

Mau looks at Yuka.

“We could go back.” ᴹ

“Too late,” Umaru says. ᵁ

Rem comes around the table before Umaru has reached him. She embraces Yuka, then Mau, asking how the journey was and whether they have eaten. He has begun to answer when Umaru puts the list from her letter in front of him.

There are additions.

“You amended it after sending it,” he says. ᴹ

“New evidence.” ᵁ

Yuka takes the page from her.

“We brought most of these.” ʸ

“Most?” ᵁ

“The international situation is delicate,” Mau says. ᴹ

Rem laughs. Umaru refuses to be distracted until he begins putting the packets on the table, at which point she becomes intensely interested in the practical outcome of the negotiation.

He has not finished when Ori arrives.

She comes quickly enough that he turns at the sound of her steps and kneels before she has to stop herself. She goes into his arms with both hands around his neck.

“You're back.” ᴼ

“I'm back.” ᴹ

Yuka bends beside them, and Ori turns far enough to include her without releasing him. Mau holds them both until the girl begins asking questions too quickly to choose which one should be answered first.

The bird. The fruit. The carving. Whether the laundry was really a laundry. Whether they brought something for Mama to put the shell door beside.

“One at a time,” Yuka says, laughing. ʸ

Ori looks at her.

“The bird?” ᴼ

“Unrepentant,” Mau tells her. ᴹ

Ori appears to approve of this answer.

---

Frieren reaches them with Chloe.

She lets Ori finish the question she is asking before telling Mau that he has returned just in time to hear the end of a disagreement about where the kite belongs. He says he has been warned. Chloe tells him that he has only heard one side.

“That's how most warnings work,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka shows them the little carved birds. Ori takes the larger one, studies the fruit in its beak and immediately asks whether they can put it beside the shell door.

“We'll find a place,” Frieren says. ᶠ

Mau watches her take the second carving from Yuka.

The movement catches him for a moment. The old picture comes close enough that he feels the space between the two rooms before he can name it.

Frieren looks up.

He takes a breath.

“It's good to see you,” he says. ᴹ

She studies him, understanding enough from the account sent home not to mistake the words for a greeting he has already finished giving.

“You too,” she answers. ᶠ

Ori tugs gently at his sleeve, asking whether he wants to see the shell now. He looks down, finds the question waiting for an ordinary answer and says yes.

They go together.

---

Shoko finds them after he has been shown the shell, the kite string and three things which have changed at home since he left.

She does not begin with a question. She opens her arms, waits for him to come close and holds him long enough that he stops trying to make the embrace a brief reassurance for her benefit.

“Hi,” he says against her shoulder. ᴹ

“Hi.” ˢʰ

He takes a breath. She lets him have it before drawing back to look at his face.

“How is it?” she asks. ˢʰ

“Some of the time it's close.” ᴹ

“The memory.” ˢʰ

He nods.

“And now?” ˢʰ

He looks toward Yuka. She is helping Ori decide which carving should stand nearest the shell. Frieren is refusing to let the arrangement occupy the place where she puts her cup.

“Now I'm glad to be here.” ᴹ

Shoko rests her hand briefly against his cheek.

“Then sit down when you need to. You'll still be here.” ˢʰ

Gojo arrives in time to tell him that his return has created several urgent household questions, none of which should be answered before he has eaten.

“Very considerate,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I am known for it.” ᴳ

Shoko looks at him.

“By whom?” ˢʰ

Mau begins laughing before Gojo has chosen his defence.

---

The boat comes out in conversation over the meal.

Yuka tells Maki, who asks one practical question, then looks at Mau with an expression which suggests she is waiting for the unnecessary complication. He tells her it has sails. She says that qualifies.

Mai asks whether they know how to use it. Yuka describes the lessons before Mau can describe the first turn, and he receives a quiet squeeze beneath the table for his restraint.

Ori has stopped eating.

“A real boat?” ᴼ

“A small one,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Can we see?” ᴼ

“When we have somewhere suitable to put it. There's more to it than putting it on the grass.” ᴹ

She looks toward the yard anyway, considering.

Umaru wants to know how many snacks it can carry. Rem tells her that this does not have to be the first question about every new form of transport.

“It wasn't the first,” Umaru says. ᵁ

Across the room, Makima has come out by agreement and chosen a seat where she is included without displacing anybody. She has the book beside her. Yuka tells Mai they have a chapter to finish later, then turns back to ask Mau what he is going to eat before the dish is empty.

He takes a portion. She leans across to kiss him after he puts it on his plate.

Gojo looks delighted by the opportunity to comment.

Shoko puts something in his mouth before he can.

---

Later, Mau goes out to check the yard.

Yuka comes with him. Bellion gives the concise account he expects, responds to the few changes Mau orders and returns to the watch without making the household wait for a ceremony. The ordinary soldiers keep their places. Some are visible at the edge of the grounds; others remain where they can warn him without standing in anybody's way.

Kamish lifts his head when Mau calls him out near the open field.

“Monarch.” ᴷ

“How are things?” ᴹ

“The princess has explained several matters of jurisdiction.” ᴷ

Yuka looks up at the enormous head.

“Already?” ʸ

“She is thorough.” ᴷ

Mau smiles.

“I appreciate the warning.” ᴹ

Kamish's eyes move to Yuka.

“Was the journey satisfactory?” ᴷ

“Very,” she says. ʸ

“Then I shall await the consequences.” ᴷ

Mau laughs. Yuka leans against him, watching the dragon settle, and tells him she thinks Kamish knows them too well.

Behind them, Ori calls from the door. She has found the other thing she wanted to show them.

Mau offers Yuka his hand, and they go back inside before the list grows longer.
