<!-- scene-id: s1e10-ln02-walking -->

The beam had been visible from far away.

Far enough that following it required actual travel.

Fern set the pace at first.

Then forgot the others did not move like she did.

Anko noticed the distance growing.

"You can move faster than this."

Fern looked back.

"Yes."

"Thanks."

Fern slowed.

No apology.

No complaint.

That worked.

The terrain shifted between winter forest and rough ground.

They crossed a stream.

Maomao crouched and checked the water before drinking.

Momo watched with suspicion.

"Are you checking if it's safe?"

"Yes."

"For once?"

Maomao looked offended.

Fern almost smiled.

The expression disappeared before Momo could be certain she had seen it.

Conversation came in fragments.

Momo talked more than the others because silence gave her too much time to imagine what had happened.

Spirits.

Aliens.

Some other category nobody had named yet.

Anko listened with the professional patience of someone who could take an impossible claim seriously without surrendering skepticism.

Maomao cared more about local plants and medicine than what metaphysical label described their relocation.

Fern mostly listened.

"What were you doing before this?" Momo asked eventually.

"Fighting."

Momo waited.

Fern did not continue.

Anko did.

"With the person you're looking for?"

"Yes."

A few steps later Fern added, "I think the attack threw me away from her."

It still felt like the simplest explanation available.

The separation in Fern's mind was short.

One impossible interruption.

One route back.

She had no idea that Frieren's experience of it would be measured differently.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e10-ln02-camp -->

Dark arrived before the settlement did.

Fern could have continued.

The others should not.

They found a place where terrain sheltered them from the worst wind.

Fern handled the fire with efficient magic.

Momo watched the flame settle.

"Okay. That's magic."

"Yes."

"Just checking."

Anko sat with her back to a tree and reconstructed their direction from memory.

Maomao examined the plant sample in the firelight.

Momo saw.

"You're still carrying that?"

"Of course."

Fern looked over.

"Don't eat it."

"I wasn't going to."

Momo pointed.

"She means taste it too."

Maomao looked between them.

Outnumbered.

She put the sample away.

Food was shared as well as four strangers could manage.

No one declared a watch schedule.

Anko stayed awake first.

When she shifted later, Fern took over without discussion.

That was not friendship.

It was enough to get through one night.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e10-ln02-first-light -->

They moved again at first light.

Their spacing had changed.

Fern no longer walked far ahead.

Momo had learned to notice when Maomao drifted toward anything botanically suspicious.

Anko no longer asked whether Fern could use magic around an obstacle. She pointed. Fern responded.

Small efficiencies.

Nothing that turned strangers into family overnight.

At a rise, Anko stopped.

Old cut stump.

Faint track.

Then smoke.

Momo exhaled.

"Please be normal people."

Maomao looked at her.

"What counts as normal now?"

"People who know where they are."

Anko glanced toward the smoke.

"Low standard."

They continued.

Fern still sensed nothing familiar.

She did not say how much that worried her.
