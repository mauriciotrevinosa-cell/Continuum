Back at the settlement, the beam became a household argument almost immediately.

Rimuru had run outside too late to see the beginning.

Bocchi complained nobody had warned her before using the sky as a flashbang.

Yuta apologized.

Frieren remained pleased with her comparison.

"You know it's cursed energy."

"Yes."

"Then stop calling it Zoltraak."

"It looks like Zoltraak."

"It doesn't."

Mau ate another bite.

"It did from where I was sitting."

Yuta pointed at him.

"You're not helping."

The day continued.

None of them knew the beam had given four strangers a direction.

<!-- scene-id: s1e10-ln03-approach -->

By the time G2 found the first clear evidence of habitation, the morning had advanced.

Track.

Old walls.

Smoke.

Then roofs through trees.

Fern's pace increased again.

Momo noticed.

Again, she said nothing.

The settlement looked abandoned from the wrong angle.

Then a repaired window appeared.

A fence.

Something hung to dry.

Recent footprints.

People.

Fern reached outward once more.

At first—nothing.

Then something small.

So small anyone else might have dismissed it.

A familiar mana signature habitually suppressed until it barely seemed present at all.

Fern stopped.

Her breath caught.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e10-ln03-ordinary-afternoon -->

Behind the inn, Frieren and Mau argued about a container.

"It's sealed," Mau said.

"It's crooked."

"Those can both be true."

Frieren took it.

Straightened the lid.

Handed it back.

Mau pressed down.

"Still sealed."

Frieren ignored him.

Then she stopped.

Completely.

Mau noticed the silence before the expression.

"Frieren?"

She turned toward the forest.

Not caution.

Recognition.

Her hand rose toward Fern's ornament without touching it.

A tiny mana signature.

Close.

Known.

Impossible and familiar at once.

Four figures appeared between the trees.

Fern first.

She saw the white hair.

Her shoulders loosened.

To Fern, this was the end of an inconvenient, frightening separation.

She stepped forward.

"Mistress Frieren."

Frieren did not move yet.

"There you are."

Mau looked from Fern to Frieren.

Understanding arrived immediately.

This was Fern.

The person whose absence had lived in the inn before almost anything else did.

Frieren took one step.

The moment stopped there.
