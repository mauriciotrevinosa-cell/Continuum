<!-- scene-id: s1e15-ln03-cake -->

Mau had no known experience with birthday candles.

Momo explained.

Bocchi added a rule about wishes.

Then corrected herself.

Then added a second rule that contradicted the first.

Anko told her to stop modifying birthday law in real time.

Maomao asked whether inhaling candle smoke counted as a medicinal concern.

Momo told her no.

Mau looked around the table.

Closed his eyes.

Made a wish he did not share.

Blew.

Everyone cheered too loudly.

Mau laughed.

The cake was uneven.

He called it excellent.

"You have no basis for comparison," Momo said.

"Then you can't prove I'm wrong."

Momo opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Rimuru laughed.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e15-ln03-gifts -->

The gifts arrived one by one.

Fern's was practical.

Something useful for reading and travel, selected because she had watched what Mau actually used instead of guessing at a personality he could not remember.

Yuta's connected to their lessons.

Bocchi's was awkwardly wrapped and linked to music in a way she tried to explain too much before Mau stopped her by simply liking it.

Anko's included a short note.

Mau read it twice.

Maomao's was useful and only mildly alarming.

Momo's was meant for daily life.

Rimuru chose something simple because abundance would have missed the point.

The pile grew.

Mau touched each object again after opening it.

"These are all mine?"

"Yes," Momo said.

"Why?"

Several people answered.

The responses overlapped.

Because you use this.

Because I thought you'd like it.

Because yours was bad.

Because you helped me.

Because it's your birthday.

No single sentence mattered.

The room did.

Mau laughed more easily as the pile grew.

Not because the gifts were expensive.

Because each existed only because someone had thought about him while he was somewhere else.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e15-ln03-frieren-gift -->

Frieren waited until the louder part ended.

"Come upstairs."

Mau followed.

Their room was dim except for a lamp and the window.

Frieren went to the side.

Then returned carrying an old bucket.

Mau stopped.

He knew it.

The bucket from the first day.

The one he had been sent to fill with water.

Inside it was the plant he had brought back instead.

Alive.

Healthy.

Around it, several flowers had been grown and maintained.

Mau stared.

"You kept it."

"Yes."

He crouched.

Touched one leaf carefully.

Frieren watched.

"You said you went to check on your plant."

Mau looked up.

"So I brought it home."

Home.

The word did not refer to the clearing.

It referred to this room.

The inn.

Everyone downstairs.

Mau looked back at the bucket.

The object had begun as proof he did not know how to follow a simple survival task.

Now it carried living evidence that the first day had not disappeared.

"Frieren."

"Hm?"

"Thank you."

She nodded.

No speech.

No explanation of what the plant represented.

The plant did not need one.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e15-ln03-birthday-now -->

Later, gifts gathered near Mau's side of the room.

The real flower from E9 remained near the window.

The old bucket and original plant had joined it.

Mau looked between the objects.

"Frieren."

"Hm?"

"Is today really my birthday now?"

"If you want it to be."

He considered.

No memory arrived to challenge the date.

No forgotten past demanded priority.

"Okay."

A small smile.

"I like it."

Frieren looked toward him.

"Me too."

The day did not tell Mau when he had been born.

It told him that the people here were glad he existed.

For now, that was more useful.
