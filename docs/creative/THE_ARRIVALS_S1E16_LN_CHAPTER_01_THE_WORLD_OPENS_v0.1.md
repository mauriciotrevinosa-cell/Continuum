<!-- scene-id: s1e16-ln01-found-people -->

Yuta returned later than expected.

Dusty.

Alert.

Not injured.

Momo noticed first.

"I found people."

She stopped cutting vegetables.

"...People people?"

"A lot of them."

Rimuru looked up.

"How many?"

"A town."

Bocchi blinked.

"...With people?"

Yuta looked at her.

"That's usually how towns work."

The room came alive.

For the first time, the world beyond their settlement stopped being an abstraction.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e16-ln01-plan -->

Yuta sketched what he had seen.

Road.

Carts.

Commercial walls rather than military fortification.

Smoke.

Animals.

Market movement.

Rimuru asked about guards.

Anko about road conditions.

Maomao about herb stalls.

Momo about food that had not been improvised from the same supplies.

Bocchi asked whether there were crowds and regretted the answer immediately.

Frieren studied the route.

Mau watched everyone.

They decided to go together.

Not because nine people were needed.

Because this was the first native society any of them had found.

Everyone had a reason to understand it.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e16-ln01-road-to-society -->

The route changed gradually.

Game trail.

Cart track.

Maintained road.

The first native traveler passed before the group was emotionally prepared for normality.

A farmer with a wagon looked at their clothing.

Looked at their faces.

Continued.

No prophecy.

No alarm.

No recognition.

Strangers.

That was almost more disorienting.

A loaded cart came from the opposite direction.

Mau drifted too close while studying the wheel.

Frieren grabbed the back of his clothes and pulled him away.

"I was looking."

"You were standing in the road."

"I can do both."

"Apparently not."

The cart continued.

The world did not stop because they had arrived in it.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e16-ln01-town -->

The mercantile town was busy rather than grand.

Timber.

Stone.

Narrow streets.

Carts.

Guild signs.

Work animals.

Metalwork.

Cloth awnings.

Food stalls.

Each person saw a different town first.

Maomao saw herbs.

Bocchi saw a music stall and then the crowd around it.

Momo saw people.

Anko saw signs, exits and behavior.

Rimuru saw prices.

Yuta saw guards and routes.

Frieren noticed magic.

Mau tried to see everything and nearly walked into another cart.

Frieren corrected his direction without looking.

Maomao stopped so abruptly at a herbalist that Yuta nearly hit her.

"Wait."

Momo turned back.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to."

Maomao was already reading labels.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e16-ln01-money -->

The first problem was money.

Local coins.

Local values.

Nothing they had in their pockets meant anything here.

Rimuru exchanged a small useful material item.

Mau took one of the coins and turned it over.

Weight.

Marking.

Metal.

"So this is money."

"Local money," Anko said.

"Right."

He asked what a meal cost.

Then cloth.

Then lamp oil.

Not because he wanted any of them.

Because value needed scale before the coin meant anything.

By the time Rimuru took it back, Mau understood enough to know that even a small personal share later would represent real work.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e16-ln01-map -->

They found a map seller.

The regional map changed everything.

Their abandoned settlement did not appear.

The mercantile town was one node among roads and smaller communities.

Coastline farther out.

Islands.

Mountains.

Rivers.

Ruins and dungeons marked with varying confidence.

Momo stared at the map longer than anyone.

Searching without naming what for.

Frieren recognized that and left her alone.

Mau pointed toward the sea.

"That's all water?"

"Yes," Momo said.

"That's a lot of water."

Rimuru bought the map.

For the first time, the unknown world had edges on paper.
