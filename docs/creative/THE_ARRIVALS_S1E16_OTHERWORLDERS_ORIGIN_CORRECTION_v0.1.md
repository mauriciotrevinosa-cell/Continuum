## Correction

In E16, the native world does **not** already have a known historical category of prior Otherworlders.

Frieren and Mau are intended to be among the first known arrivals / first known cases, and the main household is not entering an already-established administrative category.

The term **Otherworlders** should therefore emerge in E16 as an improvised descriptive label after a guild clerk / local notices that the newcomers keep naming places, countries, histories, or concepts that do not exist anywhere in the known world.

Representative rhythm:

- The clerk asks where they are from.
- Different members give incompatible answers: regions or countries absent from local maps, multiple versions of Japan, and Mau simply not knowing.
- The clerk realizes the issue is larger than ordinary foreign travel.
- The clerk says something in the spirit of:

> “Otherworlders, then.”

Someone asks what that means.

The clerk answers simply that they keep naming places that **are not from this world / do not exist in this world**.

There should be no line implying:

> “People from other worlds have appeared here before.”

There should be no established Otherworlder registry, precedent, or historical bureaucracy at this point unless later canon explicitly introduces one.

The term is initially neutral and descriptive. Its later social weight can change after Sukuna’s catastrophe, but that later evolution works better precisely because the label starts here as a fresh description rather than an old feared category.
