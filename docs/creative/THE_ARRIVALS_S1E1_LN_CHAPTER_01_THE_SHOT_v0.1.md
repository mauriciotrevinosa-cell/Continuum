Fern moved before Frieren could stop her.

There was nothing reckless about the decision at first.

The opening was there.

A gap had formed through the fading edge of the enemy's last spell, narrow but clean, exactly the kind Fern had learned to recognize after years of fighting beside Frieren. She did not waste movement. She never had. One step, then another, her staff already aligned with the line she needed.

Frieren saw the same opening.

Then she saw what sat behind it.

The enemy was giving ground too easily.

Not retreating.

Making space.

"Fern, wait—"

Fern had already committed.

The enemy turned into the attack instead of away from it.

For a fraction of a second, Frieren saw the entire mistake at once: Fern's path closing, the counter forming, the distance between them becoming too large to cross in time.

There was no room left for warning.

Frieren fired.

The clearing vanished into white.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln01-the-shot -->

Sound returned slowly.

First a high ringing in Frieren's ears.

Then wind.

Then pieces of earth falling back to the ground.

The enemy was down.

Frieren did not look at it.

"Fern?"

Smoke dragged itself between the trees.

The place where Fern had been was empty.

Frieren took several steps forward. Her staff lowered without her noticing.

"Fern."

A small crater scarred the ground.

She reached for mana.

Nothing.

Not *nothing* in the sense of an empty world. The clearing was full of the fading residue of battle, her own magic, disturbed earth, dying traces that would normally have told her exactly what had happened.

Nothing that was Fern.

Frieren crouched beside the crater.

There had to be something.

A trail. A residue. Blood. Cloth. A sign that the blast had thrown her somewhere beyond the clearing.

Her eyes moved across broken soil too quickly.

Then they stopped.

Something pale lay half-buried near the edge.

Frieren reached down.

Fern's hair ornament rested in her palm.

"No."

The word came out before thought did.

She looked around again.

"No..."

Magic lifted debris from the crater.

Stones rolled aside. Broken roots tore free. Dirt rose in clumps and fell elsewhere.

Nothing.

Frieren moved more.

The pattern stopped being efficient.

At some point she was using her hands.

"No. No, no..."

There was no body.

That should have helped.

It did not.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln01-search -->

Stark found her before sunset.

He stopped at the edge of the clearing.

Frieren heard him arrive. She did not turn.

For several seconds he said nothing.

His gaze moved from the crater to the ornament clenched in Frieren's hand.

He did not ask where Fern was.

Frieren stood.

"If the blast threw her clear, she would've landed this way."

She started down the slope.

Stark followed.

They searched until dark.

The next morning, they searched again.

And the next.

Frieren checked broken branches, disturbed soil, shallow impressions in mud, residual mana, the angle of the crater, the direction of every spell she could remember casting.

One morning Stark found her kneeling over a mark in the dirt.

He stared at it.

"That's from yesterday."

Frieren looked down.

She remembered making it.

She stood anyway.

Another day.

Stark set food beside her while she worked over a rough map covered in notes and crossed areas.

"Eat."

"Later."

Hours passed.

The food remained untouched.

Stark moved it directly in front of her.

"You said that yesterday."

Frieren finally looked up.

He did not move the plate.

She ate.

Stark looked away while she did.

By the fifth night the map had become a record of places where Fern was not.

By the sixth morning, Stark recognized the route Frieren was preparing before she reached the door.

"We already searched there."

"I know."

"Then why—"

"I might've missed something."

Stark looked at her.

She had slept. Technically.

Not enough.

There was dirt at the edge of her sleeve from the day before. Her hands moved through the packing routine without the rest of her seeming fully present.

"Frieren."

She kept going.

"It's been six days."

Her hand paused over the travel case.

"If Fern was hurt..." Stark swallowed. "She would've answered us."

"Not necessarily."

"We searched everywhere."

"Then we search again."

Stark's eyes lowered.

When he spoke again, his voice had lost all force.

"She's probably dead."

Frieren stopped.

He regretted it immediately.

"No."

"Frieren—"

"Fern isn't dead."

There was no anger in her voice.

That was worse.

Frieren walked out.

The door closed behind her.

Stark stood in the inn for a long time.

Then his shoulders shook once.

He covered his face.

Outside, Frieren walked alone down the road.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln01-last-search -->

On the seventh day, Frieren returned to the crater.

Alone.

She checked the ground.

She searched for mana.

She walked the perimeter.

She repeated the angle of the shot in her head until the geometry became meaningless.

Nothing new appeared.

No hidden trail.

No teleportation residue she could identify.

No body.

No answer.

Frieren knelt where she had found the ornament.

"Fern?"

The forest gave her wind.

That was all.

She stayed until the light began to change.

Then, for the first time in seven days, Frieren stood and left without choosing another place to search.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln01-im-sorry -->

Stark was awake when she returned.

He looked up from the common room.

Frieren walked past him.

She looked exhausted beyond speech. The dirt from the search was still on her clothes. She did not clean up or properly change; she removed only what she had to before sleep and sat on the bed.

He did not stop her.

No notes for tomorrow.

No map.

No list.

Fern's hair ornament lay in her hand.

Frieren curled her fingers around it and brought it against her chest.

"I'm sorry, Fern."

The room remained still.

"I'm sorry, Heiter."

She did not decide to sleep.

Her body made the decision for her.

Darkness came before she could think of another place to search.
