Wind moved through leaves.

Frieren opened her eyes.

Sky.

For half a second, that was all.

Then she felt grass beneath her instead of a mattress.

She sat up.

The air was wrong.

Not dangerous. Not immediately.

Different.

The trees around the clearing were unfamiliar in small ways first—the shape of a leaf, the color of bark, the call of a bird she had no name for. Beyond them, the horizon refused to place itself anywhere in her memory.

Frieren reached outward with her senses.

Mana.

So the world had magic.

That was almost reassuring.

Nothing else was.

No teleportation residue she recognized.

No spell structure around the clearing.

No reason for her to have fallen asleep in an inn and awakened outside beneath another sky.

She looked down.

Her normal travel clothes.

Her staff lay in the grass nearby.

Her travel case was there too.

Frieren's eyes narrowed.

She had not gone to sleep like this.

Something pressed against her palm.

The ornament.

Frieren opened her hand.

For a moment the clearing disappeared behind a memory of a crater.

She placed Fern's ornament in her own hair.

Then she looked around again.

That was when she saw the man.

He lay only a few feet away.

Frieren went still.

She had not sensed him.

He was young—adult, but not much older in appearance than the humans she had spent most of her recent life around. Taller than Frieren by enough to be obvious even lying down. Dark hair. Breathing.

Alive.

Frieren moved closer and crouched beside him.

His mana was...

She looked again.

Almost nothing.

Not hidden, as far as she could tell.

Not absent in a way that made sense either.

Just wrong enough to be interesting.

His eyelids moved.

Frieren waited.

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln02-mau-wakes -->

Mau woke to white hair.

That was the first thing the world gave him.

Then green eyes.

Long ears.

A face close enough that he knew the stranger had been watching him wake.

"Can you hear me?"

He understood the words.

Mau blinked.

The trees behind her sharpened.

So did the sky.

He gave a small nod.

"What's your name?"

The question should have been easy.

Mau opened his mouth.

Nothing came.

A chill moved through him that had nothing to do with the air.

What was his name?

He searched for it.

Not in the clearing.

Inside.

For one horrible moment there was only empty space.

Then—

"...Mau."

The stranger noticed the hesitation.

"Where are you from?"

Mau searched again.

Nothing.

His eyes moved to the trees as though geography might solve the problem for him.

"...I don't know."

"Do you remember how you got here?"

"No."

"Your home?"

He tried.

No room.

No street.

No landscape.

Nothing attached to the idea.

Mau shook his head.

The stranger waited.

"Family?"

He looked for a face.

Any face.

There should have been one.

There wasn't.

"...No."

She stopped asking.

That helped.

Mau looked down at his hands.

They were familiar.

They had to be.

He knew how fingers worked. He knew what grass was. He knew the person in front of him was a woman, that her ears were unusual, that he was sitting outdoors beneath trees.

He knew language.

He knew his name.

Maybe.

Everything around those facts was missing.

When he looked up again, the white-haired stranger was still there.

For the moment, she was the only person in the world he could place anywhere at all.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln02-noise -->

Something scraped through his thoughts.

Mau flinched.

Not pain.

Not sound exactly.

A piece of something.

`...unk...`

His hand rose toward his temple.

The stranger noticed immediately.

"What's wrong?"

Another fragment.

`...known...`

Mau turned his head.

Nothing in the clearing had moved.

"I... heard something."

The woman's attention shifted outward.

"What?"

Mau listened.

Leaves.

Wind.

His own breathing.

"Didn't you hear it?"

She shook her head.

Mau stared at the trees.

"...I don't know."

The phrase was becoming familiar far too quickly.

The woman listened to the clearing one more time.

Whatever Mau had heard, she could not find it.

Then she sat near him.

"I didn't hear anything."

Mau lowered his hand.

A glow gathered above her fingers.

His attention snapped to it.

One flower appeared in the grass.

Then another.

Then more.

The clearing changed around them.

Not all at once. The flowers spread in small clusters, color pushing through cold green until the space between them no longer looked like the place Mau had woken frightened and empty.

He reached toward one.

Soft petals.

A real stem.

Mau looked at her.

She had done it casually.

As if making flowers from nothing was less unusual than not remembering where you came from.

"I don't know what's happening to you," she said.

Mau waited.

"But I'll help."

He looked back at the flowers.

Something in his chest loosened.

Not because he understood anything.

Because, for the first time since waking, understanding did not feel like something he had to find alone.

A petal drifted onto his sleeve.

Neither of them noticed.
