<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln03-bucket -->

Frieren handed Mau a bucket.

"We need water."

He took it with both hands.

"Help me."

Mau looked at the bucket with the seriousness of someone who had just been assigned something important.

Frieren let him go.

She spent the next several minutes clearing a space beneath the tree, checking what little food had arrived with her, and deciding which part of the clearing was least inconvenient for a fire.

Footsteps returned.

Mau appeared between the trees.

He had the bucket.

It was empty.

In his other hand was a small plant.

"I found this."

He looked genuinely pleased.

Frieren looked at the plant.

Then the bucket.

Then Mau.

A smile escaped before she could stop it.

The expression on Mau's face changed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Frieren took the bucket.

"Come on."

They walked together.

Mau kept the plant.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln03-learning -->

Water showed between the trees ahead, a river widening through a slow bend.

Frieren filled the bucket.

Mau watched closely.

She noticed.

On the way back, Frieren stopped beside a fallen branch.

Dry.

Good.

Mau followed her attention.

"We'll need firewood."

He gathered what he could find.

The first branch he brought her was damp and beginning to rot.

Frieren took it from him.

"Not that one."

She picked up another.

"This one."

Mau studied the difference.

Later, without being told, he returned with a dry branch.

Frieren noticed.

She did not praise him.

He did not need praise for finding a stick.

A small animal moved through brush nearby.

Frieren turned.

Mau turned at exactly the same time.

The animal froze.

Both of them tilted their heads slightly.

The animal fled.

Frieren looked back toward the shelter.

Mau did too.

Neither noticed what the other had done.

By evening, the clearing had become a camp.

Not a good one.

A camp.

The bucket sat near the tree. The plant Mau had found rested beside it as if it had always been part of the plan.

Frieren opened her travel case.

For the first time all day, her expression carried something close to pride.

"My secret stash."

Mau leaned over.

Bread.

Very old bread.

Very hard bread.

Frieren broke off a piece and bit into it.

Her face barely changed.

Barely.

Mau tried his.

They chewed in silence.

A long time.

"This is bad," Mau said.

"Yes."

"You saved it?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Frieren looked at the bread.

"I don't remember."

Mau nodded.

For once, he understood the answer perfectly.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln03-fire -->

Night settled over the clearing.

The fire took longer than Frieren wanted.

Mau watched every step.

He had spent most of the day doing that—watching the things Frieren assumed people already knew.

Sometimes he did know.

Sometimes he knew the name of something but not the experience attached to it.

Sometimes there was only a hole.

When the fire finally held, they sat beside it without facing each other directly.

Mau watched the flames.

"Do you remember where you came from?"

"Yes."

His silence after that answer lasted long enough for Frieren to look toward him.

"Do you remember your family?"

Frieren returned her eyes to the fire.

"I did."

Mau heard the tense.

"Did they die?"

Her fingers moved toward the ornament in her hair.

Fern.

Heiter.

People separated from her by more than one kind of distance.

"...Yes."

"I'm sorry."

Frieren watched the fire.

"Me too."

Another quiet stretch.

"What are you going to do?"

"Figure out where we are."

That answer was easier.

Then she looked at him.

"And what happened to you."

Mau met her eyes.

He did not say thank you.

He did not need to.

When he looked back at the fire, his shoulders had lowered.

Frieren noticed a pale petal caught near the seam of his sleeve.

One of the flowers from earlier.

She reached over and brushed it away.

Mau looked down.

"What?"

"Nothing."

He looked at her.

Frieren looked away first.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln03-blanket -->

The temperature dropped as the fire burned lower.

Frieren settled beneath the shelter with the blanket.

Mau chose a place farther away.

He lay down.

Within a minute, he was shivering.

"You'll be cold over there."

"I'm okay."

"You're shivering."

Mau looked down at himself as though his body had betrayed him in public.

A pause.

Frieren lifted one side of the blanket.

That was all.

Mau moved closer.

He left more space between them than the blanket reasonably allowed.

Frieren did not comment.

He stared at the dying fire.

Then at the sky.

Then, once, at her.

His eyes closed.

His breathing evened out almost immediately.

Frieren looked over.

Already?

The thought came with faint disbelief.

Then she understood.

He had known her for hours.

He had no memory of family.

No home.

No idea where he was.

And something in his body had still decided that sleeping beside her was safe.

The blanket had slipped from his shoulder.

Frieren adjusted it.

Mau did not wake.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e1-ln03-night-watch -->

Later, Frieren was still awake.

The fire had nearly gone out.

The forest had become a collection of dark shapes and small sounds.

Mau moved beside her.

His eyes opened.

For a moment he looked disoriented.

Then he saw Frieren.

"You didn't sleep?"

"Not yet."

Mau pushed himself up.

Frieren expected another question.

Instead he shifted closer.

Not against her.

Just close enough that the gap under the blanket disappeared.

He sat there for less than a minute.

Then his eyes closed again.

His head dipped.

Mau fell asleep a second time as if the matter had been settled.

Frieren stared at him.

Then she pulled the blanket higher over his shoulder.

Her fingers brushed the ornament in her hair.

A memory came with it.

Fern much younger.

At the beginning.

Not the crater.

Not the flash.

Fern.

Frieren looked at Mau again.

He slept without fear beside someone he had met that morning.

*Not this time.*

The thought was quiet.

Frieren closed her eyes.

The world beyond the clearing remained unknown.

For that night, they were alive.

And neither of them was alone.
