## Creator decision

Mau no longer retrieves `Mau` from memory when Frieren asks his name.

Instead, he arrives with a backpack among the belongings reconstructed / transferred with him. When Frieren realizes he cannot answer even the name question, she directs his attention to the backpack. Inside, Mau finds a personalized jersey whose readable name field says **`MAU`**.

Frieren treats the clue honestly rather than as proof:

> **Frieren:** `Maybe your name is Mau.`

Mau looks at the jersey and the name for a beat, then accepts the possibility with simple, sincere approval:

> **Mau:** `I like it.`

This becomes the first identity anchor Mau chooses in the new world. It is materially discovered, not recovered from memory.

The exact jersey team / brand / colors / number remain pending the creator wardrobe-reference pass. The only story-critical visual requirement at this stage is that the personalization `MAU` is clearly readable when the reveal lands.

---

## Why this revision improves E1

The beat keeps Mau's amnesia cleaner: at awakening he does not possess one suspiciously convenient autobiographical fact while everything else is blank.

It also gives the backpack immediate narrative purpose, turns a real piece of Mau's wardrobe into identity evidence, and lets Frieren participate in his first act of self-definition without pretending she knows more than she does.

`I like it.` should remain small and literal rather than played as a joke or a grand identity declaration.

### Local prose interpretation correction

Any E1 prose describing Mau as *remembering*, *retrieving*, or definitively *knowing* his own name is superseded by this addendum.

The intended state is now:

> Mau cannot access his autobiographical identity. `Mau` is the first plausible identity clue he finds and the first one he voluntarily accepts.

Scene 10's emotional transition should therefore read as Mau processing that even the name he is using came from an external clue, not from accessible memory.
