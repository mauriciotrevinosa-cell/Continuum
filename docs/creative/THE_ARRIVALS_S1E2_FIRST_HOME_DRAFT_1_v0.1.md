This is the first continuous detailed draft for S1E2. It follows S1E1 `Not This Time` as the dramatic-writing reference: visual behavior carries as much information as possible, dialogue stays compact, and neither Frieren nor Mau over-explains emotion.

E2 is intentionally a small episode. Its function is not to escalate the mythology. It turns the temporary camp of E1 into the first chosen shelter of the series and gives Frieren and Mau one quiet stretch in which trust becomes routine before another person arrives.

# S1E2 — `First Home`

## Scene 1 — Morning After

Morning comes cold and gray.

The fire from the night before has collapsed into a bed of coals. The blanket Frieren shared with Mau is still around both of them, but only barely: during the night Mau has shifted enough that most of it is over him.

Frieren wakes first.

She looks down at the blanket arrangement, then at Mau.

He is still asleep.

For several seconds she does nothing.

Then she pulls the blanket back just enough to cover herself again without waking him.

Mau opens his eyes anyway.

He looks at Frieren, then at the blanket, then at the dead fire.

**MAU**  
Morning.

**FRIEREN**  
Morning.

Mau sits up slowly. The unfamiliar world is still there.

Frieren is already checking their supplies.

Mau watches her put away the things they used the night before.

**MAU**  
Are we leaving?

**FRIEREN**  
Yes.

**MAU**  
Where are we going?

Frieren closes the travel case.

**FRIEREN**  
I don't know.

Mau waits for more.

There is no more.

**MAU**  
Then how do you know we're going the right way?

Frieren looks toward the slope beyond the clearing.

**FRIEREN**  
That way is less steep.

Mau follows her gaze.

He accepts this.

They pack.

Before leaving, Mau looks once toward the flowers Frieren made the previous day. A few remain visible in the cold grass.

He does not pick one.

He simply remembers where they are.

Then he follows Frieren into the trees.

---

## Scene 2 — No Destination

The day becomes a long walk through unfamiliar forest.

Frieren chooses direction by terrain rather than destination. She studies waterways, slopes, animal tracks, edible plants, and the position of the sun. Mau watches everything she notices.

He does not ask about each thing.

He has already learned that if he stays close long enough, some answers appear on their own.

They cross a shallow stream.

Mau steps where Frieren stepped and still manages to get one boot wet.

He looks down at it.

Frieren looks back.

**MAU**  
You made that look easier.

**FRIEREN**  
It was easy.

Mau looks at her.

Frieren keeps walking.

A while later, the forest opens into rough ground where old cut stumps have begun to rot back into the soil.

Frieren stops.

Mau nearly walks into her.

He looks around.

**MAU**  
What?

Frieren touches one of the stumps.

The cut is old, but too clean to be natural.

Farther ahead, partly hidden behind bare branches, is the corner of a roof.

Mau sees it.

**MAU**  
People?

Frieren starts toward it.

**FRIEREN**  
Maybe.

---

## Scene 3 — The Settlement

The roof belongs to a small house.

Or what is left of one.

The wall facing the forest has partially collapsed. The door hangs open. Weeds grow through the step.

Mau slows before entering the clearing beyond it.

More structures come into view.

A narrow lane.

Several damaged houses.

A shed with half its roof missing.

A low stone wall around an overgrown field.

A larger timber-and-stone building near the center.

No smoke.

No voices.

No animals tied outside.

No footprints that look recent.

Mau takes it in.

**MAU**  
There were people here.

**FRIEREN**  
Yes.

**MAU**  
Where did they go?

Frieren looks down the empty lane.

**FRIEREN**  
I don't know.

Mau looks at her.

**MAU**  
You say that a lot.

Frieren glances at him.

**FRIEREN**  
There's a lot I don't know.

Mau considers that, then nods.

They move deeper into the settlement.

Frieren checks doors and windows without barging into every room. She looks for signs of recent use: warm ash, stored food, repaired hinges, fresh waste, tracks.

Nothing.

Mau stands beside an abandoned handcart whose wheel has split.

He brushes snow-dry dust from the handle.

**MAU**  
How do you know nobody lives here?

Frieren points toward the nearest chimney.

Cold.

Then the road.

No new tracks.

Then a rain barrel with leaves rotting in the bottom.

**FRIEREN**  
Nobody's been taking care of it.

Mau looks at the buildings again.

That answer makes more sense to him than a declaration that the place is abandoned.

---

## Scene 4 — The Inn

The largest building was once an inn.

A faded hanging sign still swings from one chain above the entrance. The painted symbol is too worn to read clearly.

Mau pushes the door.

It sticks.

He pushes harder.

The lower hinge gives a little with a tired wooden groan.

Frieren steps past him and uses a small spell to shift the warped frame enough for the door to open.

Mau looks at her.

**MAU**  
You could've done that first.

**FRIEREN**  
You were already pushing.

Inside, the common room is dirty but recognizable.

A long counter.

Several tables.

A fireplace.

Stairs.

Broken glass beneath one window.

A section of ceiling has water damage, but most of the main roof appears intact.

Mau walks to the center of the room and looks up.

**MAU**  
This is bad.

Frieren checks the fireplace.

**FRIEREN**  
Mostly dry.

Mau turns toward her.

**MAU**  
That's your standard?

**FRIEREN**  
For now.

She goes upstairs.

Mau follows.

The upper floor has a corridor with roughly ten small guest rooms. Some doors are gone. Two rooms have ceiling damage. Another has a cracked window but a usable bed frame. Most are dusty rather than destroyed.

Frieren opens one room, checks the walls, checks the roof line, then moves to the next.

Mau watches her work through the building like it is a problem with several acceptable answers.

**MAU**  
Are we staying here?

Frieren looks out through the cracked window at the empty settlement.

The first tiny flakes of snow drift through the air.

**FRIEREN**  
Tonight.

Mau looks at the snow.

Then at the room.

That is enough of an answer for now.

---

## Scene 5 — Making One Room Usable

They do not repair the inn.

They make one part of it survivable.

Frieren clears the fireplace and checks the chimney while Mau drags broken furniture into a pile.

He tries lifting a table with one hand, realizes immediately that he misjudged the weight, and changes grip before Frieren can comment.

She comments anyway.

**FRIEREN**  
Use both hands.

**MAU**  
I am.

He is now.

They choose one intact upstairs room to store their travel things and one corner of the common room for sleeping near the fireplace until the building warms.

Frieren uses a basic cleaning spell on the worst of the soot and dust, but the spell does not rebuild wood, replace glass, fix hinges, or restore the roof.

The room becomes cleaner.

It does not become new.

Mau carries pieces of broken glass outside.

Frieren stops him before he puts them in a loose pile near the path.

**FRIEREN**  
Not there.

Mau looks at the glass.

**MAU**  
Why?

Frieren points to the ground where someone would naturally walk.

Mau understands.

He moves the pile against a wall instead.

Later, Frieren wedges old boards over the broken window from inside while Mau holds them in place.

**MAU**  
Higher?

**FRIEREN**  
A little.

Mau moves it too far.

**FRIEREN**  
Less.

He moves it back.

Frieren nails it into the old frame using salvaged hardware.

The wind drops from a draft to a thin whistle.

Mau puts his hand near the edge.

**MAU**  
Still cold.

**FRIEREN**  
Less cold.

He looks at her.

**MAU**  
That's also your standard?

Frieren considers it.

**FRIEREN**  
For now.

---

## Scene 6 — The Field

By afternoon the snow has not settled yet, but the air has changed enough that Frieren decides to inspect the rest of the settlement before visibility worsens.

They walk through the lane again.

Behind the inn is a patch of land bordered by stones and old fencing.

The field is overgrown, but the soil beneath the dead weeds is dark and workable.

Frieren kneels and breaks a little between her fingers.

Mau copies her.

**MAU**  
Is it good?

**FRIEREN**  
Probably.

**MAU**  
Probably?

**FRIEREN**  
Ask me in spring.

Mau looks around at the dead grass, the cold air, and the sky.

**MAU**  
When is spring?

Frieren pauses.

It is the kind of question that reminds her his missing past is not always easy to predict. He knows words. He knows how to walk, speak, carry things, and recognize food. But personal context appears unevenly.

**FRIEREN**  
After winter.

Mau waits.

Frieren points at the first snow beginning to collect on the top of the stone wall.

**FRIEREN**  
This is winter.

Mau looks at the snowflake melting on his sleeve.

**MAU**  
I know what snow is.

**FRIEREN**  
Good.

**MAU**  
I don't know if I've seen it before.

Frieren has no answer for that.

They continue around the settlement.

There is a well with a damaged cover but usable water below. A small shed may be salvageable. Several houses are too compromised to sleep in safely. One has collapsed completely and could later provide wood if necessary.

Frieren remembers each useful thing.

Mau remembers where she looks.

---

## Scene 7 — First Snow

The snow begins properly on the walk back to the inn.

Large soft flakes drift between the empty buildings.

The settlement changes under them.

Broken roofs and rotting fences become quiet shapes beneath white edges.

Mau slows.

Frieren keeps walking for a few steps before realizing he is no longer beside her.

She looks back.

Mau is standing in the lane, looking up.

**FRIEREN**  
Mau.

He lowers his eyes.

**MAU**  
Sorry.

He catches up.

At the inn entrance, he looks back at the lane one more time.

The silence does not feel empty in quite the same way now.

Inside, the common room is warmer than before.

Not warm.

Warmer.

Mau puts wood beside the fireplace.

Frieren removes wet snow from her hair and checks the roof for new leaks.

A few drops hit an old bucket they positioned earlier.

Mau listens to them.

**MAU**  
Is this our home now?

Frieren stops for a beat.

The question lands harder than Mau intends.

She looks around the repaired corner of a ruined inn.

The boarded window.

The old tables.

Their travel things in one room upstairs.

The two bedrolls beside the same fire.

**FRIEREN**  
It's somewhere to stay.

Mau thinks about the distinction.

**MAU**  
What's the difference?

Frieren takes off her cloak and hangs it near the fire.

**FRIEREN**  
You stop planning to leave.

Mau looks toward the door.

**MAU**  
Are we planning to leave?

Frieren looks at the snow outside.

**FRIEREN**  
Not tonight.

Mau nods.

A few moments later, while placing another log on the fire:

**MAU**  
Do people always leave eventually?

Frieren does not answer immediately.

Her hand brushes, almost unconsciously, near Fern's ornament in her hair.

**FRIEREN**  
...Not always.

Mau does not notice what the answer cost her.

He only accepts it.

---

## Scene 8 — Dinner for Two

Their first meal in the inn is unimpressive.

Simple food from what they carried and gathered.

Two bowls on an old table they cleaned enough to use.

The room around them is still mostly dark because they have only one fire and one small light source.

Mau eats with the concentration of someone who still treats every ordinary experience as data.

Frieren eats faster.

Mau notices.

**MAU**  
Do you like it?

Frieren looks at the food.

**FRIEREN**  
It's fine.

**MAU**  
You ate yours faster.

**FRIEREN**  
I was hungry.

Mau looks back at his bowl and speeds up slightly.

Frieren notices.

**FRIEREN**  
You don't have to race me.

Mau slows again.

**MAU**  
I wasn't.

Frieren says nothing.

The quiet between them is no longer the uneasy quiet of two strangers waking in a field.

It is simply quiet.

Wind presses against the boarded window.

The board holds.

Mau looks toward it.

**MAU**  
We fixed that.

Frieren follows his gaze.

**FRIEREN**  
Mostly.

Mau almost smiles.

---

## Scene 9 — Upstairs

After dinner they inspect the usable rooms again.

Mau chooses one without understanding that he is choosing.

It is simply the room where he sets down the small things he has carried since E1.

There are not many.

Frieren's travel case goes into the room across the hall at first.

Then she pauses.

The corridor is colder than the common room and the roof above that room has a faint damp mark.

She moves her things into Mau's room instead.

Mau watches her.

**MAU**  
Are you sleeping here too?

**FRIEREN**  
It's warmer.

Mau looks at the single usable bed frame.

Then at Frieren.

Frieren looks at the frame.

**FRIEREN**  
We'll sleep downstairs.

Mau nods.

Neither of them treats the shared room as meaningful yet. It is storage, dry space, and practical proximity.

That practicality will matter later.

They return to the common room with blankets.

---

## Scene 10 — Waiting for Someone

Night settles completely.

The snow outside muffles the settlement.

The fire is lower now.

Mau lies on one bedroll near it, not asleep yet.

Frieren sits against the wall with a grimoire open, though she has been on the same page for several minutes.

Mau looks at her.

Then at the ornament in her hair.

He has seen it since he woke, but he has never asked about it.

Tonight he asks a different question.

**MAU**  
Are you waiting for someone?

Frieren's eyes lift from the book.

Mau gestures vaguely toward the world outside rather than the ornament itself.

**MAU**  
You keep looking when we hear things.

Frieren closes the book halfway.

There is no reason to lie to him.

**FRIEREN**  
Maybe.

Mau waits.

**MAU**  
Who?

A small pause.

**FRIEREN**  
Fern.

It is the first time Mau hears the name clearly in this world.

**MAU**  
The person from before?

Frieren looks at him.

He means the person she mentioned at the campfire, the one she believed she failed.

**FRIEREN**  
Yes.

Mau thinks about the empty settlement around them.

**MAU**  
Do you think she'll come here?

Frieren looks toward the boarded window.

The world beyond it is invisible now.

**FRIEREN**  
I don't know.

Mau gives the smallest nod.

**MAU**  
You say that a lot.

Frieren looks back at him.

Mau is not teasing her cruelly. He is repeating an observation from earlier.

Frieren's mouth changes by almost nothing.

**FRIEREN**  
I do.

Mau settles back down.

A few seconds pass.

**MAU**  
If she comes, can she stay here too?

Frieren looks around the enormous mostly empty inn.

**FRIEREN**  
There's room.

Mau looks satisfied with this practical answer.

He closes his eyes.

Frieren opens the grimoire again.

She still does not turn the page.

---

## Scene 11 — The Scream

The inn is almost completely quiet.

Fire.

Wind.

The occasional drip into the bucket upstairs.

Mau is nearly asleep.

Frieren finally begins reading again.

Then, from somewhere outside the inn—close enough to be unmistakable—

A scream tears through the night.

Not a monster roar.

A human scream.

High, panicked, sudden.

**BOCCHI (OFF)**  
AAAAAAAH—!

Mau's eyes open instantly.

Frieren is already standing before the sound finishes.

Her grimoire drops closed against the floor.

For one beat, they look at each other.

Mau does not ask whether she heard it.

This time she did.

Frieren reaches for her staff.

Mau gets up.

Outside, the snow keeps falling over the empty settlement.

**CUT.**

## Stage 3 continuity notes

- E2 remains only Frieren + Mau on-screen until Bocchi's off-screen scream in the final beat.
- No new power-system explanation is introduced.
- Frieren's hope that Fern may arrive remains restrained and uncertain; she does not resume the desperate E1 search pattern.
- Mau remains curious and literal, not unintelligent or child-coded.
- The inn is still a damaged ruin: one usable common-room zone, one practical shared storage room, multiple damaged rooms, broken infrastructure, salvageable field/well/buildings.
- The first snow establishes cold-season atmosphere and motivates practical shelter without turning the settlement into a winter wonderland.
- `home` is deliberately not emotionally resolved here. The episode establishes shelter; later episodes earn home.
- The shared-room continuity is seeded practically rather than romantically: Frieren moves her things into the drier/warmer room, but both still sleep near the common-room fire.
- Bocchi is not visually revealed in E2; E3 retains her POV introduction and bird-triggered scream explanation.
