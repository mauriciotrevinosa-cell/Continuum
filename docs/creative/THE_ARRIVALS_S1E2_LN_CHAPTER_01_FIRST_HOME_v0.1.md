Morning arrived gray and cold.

The fire had collapsed into coals.

Frieren woke first and discovered that sometime during the night Mau had migrated across the blanket until most of it covered him.

She looked at him.

Then at the blanket.

Then pulled enough of it back to cover herself without waking him.

Mau opened his eyes anyway.

His gaze moved from Frieren to the dead fire.

"Morning."

"Morning."

He sat up.

The world had not repaired itself while he slept.

The same trees surrounded them. The same river moved somewhere beyond them. His past remained exactly as absent as it had been the day before.

Frieren was already packing.

"Are we leaving?"

"Yes."

"Where are we going?"

"I don't know."

Mau waited.

There was no second half to the answer.

"Then how do you know we're going the right way?"

Frieren closed the travel case and looked toward the slope.

"That way is less steep."

Mau followed her eyes.

"...Okay."

They packed.

Before leaving, Mau looked once toward the flowers Frieren had made the day before.

A few still survived in the cold grass.

He did not pick one.

He simply looked long enough to remember where they had been.

Then he followed Frieren into the trees.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln01-no-destination -->

Frieren chose their direction by terrain.

Water.

Slope.

Animal tracks.

The sun when it appeared between clouds.

Mau watched.

He had learned something useful in less than a day: Frieren did not explain every action, but if he stayed close enough, the reason usually revealed itself.

At a shallow stream, he stepped exactly where she stepped and still managed to sink one boot deeper than hers.

Mau looked down.

Frieren looked back.

"You made that look easier."

"It was easy."

She kept walking.

Mau pulled his foot free and followed.

Later, the forest opened onto rougher ground.

Old tree stumps rose from the earth, edges softened by years of rot but still too clean to be natural.

Frieren stopped.

Mau nearly walked into her.

"What?"

She touched one of the cut surfaces.

Before she answered, Mau saw it.

A roof.

Only a corner at first, behind bare branches.

"People?"

"Maybe."

They moved toward it.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln01-settlement -->

The first house had once been small.

Now one wall leaned outward and the door hung open beneath a roof that had given up on one side.

Mau slowed.

Beyond it, the rest of the settlement appeared piece by piece.

A narrow lane.

Ruined houses.

A shed with half its roof missing.

A low stone border around what had once been cultivated land.

A larger timber-and-stone building near the center.

No smoke.

No voices.

No recent tracks.

"There were people here."

"Yes."

"Where did they go?"

Frieren looked down the empty lane.

"I don't know."

Mau looked at her.

"You say that a lot."

"There's a lot I don't know."

He considered that.

It sounded less like failure when she said it.

They inspected the nearest buildings without entering everything blindly.

Frieren checked cold chimneys, old ash, rain barrels full of leaves, doors that had not been repaired in years.

Mau watched her gather conclusions from things he would have passed without noticing.

At an abandoned cart, he wiped dust from the handle.

"How do you know nobody lives here?"

Frieren pointed.

Cold chimney.

No tracks.

Rotting water in the barrel.

"Nobody's been taking care of it."

Mau looked down the lane again.

That answer he understood.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln01-inn -->

The largest building had been an inn.

A faded sign still hung above the entrance by one chain.

Mau pushed the door.

It stuck.

He pushed harder.

The hinge groaned.

Frieren stepped beside him and used a small spell to shift the warped frame.

The door opened.

Mau looked at her.

"You could've done that first."

"You were already pushing."

Inside, dust covered most surfaces.

Broken glass glittered beneath one window.

A long counter divided part of the common room. Several tables still stood. A fireplace occupied one wall. Stairs led upward.

The ceiling showed water damage, but the roof above the central section held.

Mau looked around.

"This is bad."

Frieren checked the fireplace.

"Mostly dry."

"That's your standard?"

"For now."

Upstairs, a corridor connected roughly ten guest rooms.

Some doors were missing.

Two rooms had obvious roof damage.

One had a cracked window but a bed frame that looked usable.

Most were simply abandoned.

Frieren checked them one by one.

At first Mau followed.

By the fourth room, he opened the next door before she reached it and looked up at the ceiling.

Frieren noticed.

By the sixth, he checked the window while she inspected the floor.

No instruction.

They were not good at this.

They were becoming faster.

At the end of the corridor, Frieren looked through a cracked pane.

Snow had begun to fall.

Only a few flakes.

"Are we staying?"

"Tonight."

Mau looked at the room.

Then the snow.

Tonight was enough.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln01-perimeter -->

Before the weather worsened, Frieren wanted the shape of the place.

Not every room.

The settlement.

They walked its perimeter together.

Behind the inn stood the neglected cultivation patch, bordered by stones and dead weeds.

A damaged well sat nearby.

Five houses close enough to the inn to matter had roofs in varying states of failure. Most were safer as sources of future material than as shelter.

Behind the inn, the land rose into a low hill with a tree visible above the roofline.

A narrow trail disappeared toward the lake.

On the opposite side, an old route left the settlement between weeds and broken stone.

Mau stood in it.

"Road?"

"Maybe."

It had almost vanished.

Nothing about it suggested an active town waited nearby.

Frieren memorized it anyway.

Mau looked back toward the inn.

Light snow settled along the roof.

One building among several ruins.

The only one they had chosen.

For tonight.
