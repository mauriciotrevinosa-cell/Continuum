By the time Mau cleaned the common-room window, they had been in the settlement long enough for the inn to contain habits.

Not comfort.

Habits.

The broom leaned in the same place.

Dry wood stayed beneath the stairs.

The least damaged upstairs room held their travel things.

They still slept near the hearth on the coldest nights.

Mau had learned which ruined house had the safest boards to salvage from.

Frieren had learned that if she could not find a tool, Mau had probably put it somewhere that made more sense than where she had left it.

The window took most of an afternoon.

Dust.

Old residue.

A stubborn film along the inside of the glass.

When Mau finally stepped back, late sunlight reached through the cleaned section and crossed the common room in a warm rectangle.

He stared at it.

Frieren stopped on the stairs.

The light made the inn look different.

Not fixed.

Not new.

Occupied.

Mau looked around.

"Is this our home now?"

Frieren's hand rested on the stair rail.

The question still carried more weight than he knew.

"It's somewhere to stay."

"What's the difference?"

Frieren looked at the repaired corner.

The table they used.

The boarded window that still let in a thin whistle.

Their things upstairs.

"You stop planning to leave."

Mau thought about that.

"Are we planning to leave?"

Frieren looked toward the clean window.

Snow beyond it.

"Not tonight."

Mau nodded.

A moment later, while moving another piece of wood toward the hearth, he asked:

"Do people always leave eventually?"

Frieren touched the ornament in her hair.

"...Not always."

Mau accepted the answer.

He did not know what it had cost.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln03-dinner-for-two -->

Dinner was simple.

Two bowls.

One repaired table.

Wind outside.

Mau ate slowly, still capable of treating ordinary food like information.

Frieren finished first.

"Do you like it?" he asked.

"It's fine."

"You ate faster."

"I was hungry."

Mau immediately started eating faster.

Frieren looked at him.

"You don't have to race me."

He slowed.

"I wasn't."

The quiet that followed was not the quiet of the first campfire anymore.

It was simply dinner.

Mau glanced toward the boarded window.

"We fixed that."

"Mostly."

He smiled.

Frieren pretended not to notice.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln03-upstairs -->

Their belongings had settled into one upstairs room almost by accident.

At first Frieren had chosen the room opposite Mau's.

Then she found damp spreading along the ceiling.

She moved her case across the corridor.

"It's warmer," she said.

Mau looked at the single bed frame.

"Are you sleeping here too?"

Frieren looked at it.

"We're sleeping downstairs."

"Right."

Neither treated the shared room as important.

It was dry.

Their things fit.

That was enough.

Later, it would matter that it had become normal before either of them thought to define it.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln03-waiting -->

One night, Mau lay near the fire while Frieren read.

Or appeared to.

He looked toward the window when something shifted outside.

Frieren looked too.

Mau noticed.

"You keep doing that."

Frieren's eyes returned to the page.

"Doing what?"

"Looking when we hear things."

She did not answer.

"Are you waiting for someone?"

The question found her anyway.

"Maybe."

"Who?"

A pause.

"Fern."

It was the first time Mau heard the name clearly in this world.

"The person you told me about?"

"Yes."

"Do you think she'll come here?"

Frieren looked at the black window.

Beyond it, the settlement had disappeared into night.

"I don't know."

Mau nodded.

"You say that a lot."

Frieren looked at him.

He was not mocking her.

"I do."

"If she comes, can she stay here too?"

Frieren looked around the enormous half-ruined inn.

"There's room."

That answer satisfied him.

Mau closed his eyes.

Frieren reopened the book.

Several minutes passed before she turned a page.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e2-ln03-final-night -->

The scream came on an otherwise ordinary night.

That was what made it work.

Mau was nearly asleep.

Frieren had finally started reading for real.

The fire popped once.

Wind moved against the building.

Then, from somewhere outside—

"AAAAAAAH—!"

Mau's eyes opened.

Frieren was standing before the scream finished.

Her book hit the floor closed.

Human.

Close.

Terrified.

They looked at each other.

Mau did not ask whether she had heard it.

This time, she had.

Frieren reached for her staff.

Mau was already moving.

Outside, snow fell over the empty settlement.

Someone else was there.
