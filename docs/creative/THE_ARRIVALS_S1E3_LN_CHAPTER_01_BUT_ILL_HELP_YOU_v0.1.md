Cold.

That was the first thing Hitori Gotoh noticed.

The second was the weight on her chest.

Her eyes opened to a gray sky framed by bare branches.

Something small moved above her.

Bocchi lowered her gaze.

A bird stood on her coat.

It tilted its head.

Bocchi stopped breathing.

The bird hopped closer.

Every possible response vanished except one.

"AAAAAAAH—!"

The bird launched away.

Bocchi jerked upright, caught one arm in the strap of her guitar case, twisted, and fell sideways into the thin snow.

She stayed there for one second.

Two.

Her face burned even though nobody had seen it.

Then she noticed the trees.

The road.

Ruined buildings beyond them.

None of it belonged anywhere near Tokyo.

Bocchi sat up again.

"...Where am I?"

No answer.

She reached for her phone.

Still there.

No signal.

She stared at the empty bars, moved the phone slightly, stood, raised it higher, then stopped because she realized what she was doing.

Nothing.

Her fingers started shaking.

The settlement.

The forest.

The road.

A thought arrived at full speed.

*I've been kidnapped into the mountains and I'm going to die before I can explain why I missed rehearsal.*

Footsteps crunched through snow.

Bocchi's head snapped toward the sound.

Somehow, this was worse.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e3-ln01-two-strangers -->

The first person she saw had white hair, pointed ears, a staff, and clothing Bocchi could not place in any normal category.

The second at least looked human.

That did not help enough.

They stopped when they saw her.

Bocchi stopped too, even though she was already sitting.

The white-haired woman lowered her staff slightly.

"Are you hurt?"

Bocchi opened her mouth.

"I— um— I— no— maybe—"

She looked down at herself.

Was she hurt?

She had not checked.

The man watched this process without interrupting.

"Can you stand?"

A direct question.

Better.

Bocchi pushed herself up.

Her foot caught the guitar strap.

She almost fell again.

The man caught her by the forearm.

Bocchi froze.

He released her immediately once she was balanced.

The white-haired woman picked up the bag Bocchi had dropped.

Then the guitar case.

Bocchi's voice returned with surprising efficiency.

"Ah—that's—that's mine."

The case was handed back.

"I assumed."

Bocchi hugged it to herself.

The man looked at her.

"What's your name?"

A question she had answered her entire life.

Her brain treated it like a surprise exam.

"Hitori Gotoh."

Pause.

"People call me Bocchi."

Another pause.

"Not everyone. Some people. It's complicated."

The man nodded as if this had been useful.

"I'm Mau."

"Frieren," the woman said.

Bocchi looked at Frieren's ears.

Frieren noticed.

Bocchi looked away so quickly her neck hurt.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e3-ln01-not-from-here -->

They took her toward the inn because standing outside discussing whether reality had broken was apparently not useful.

Bocchi walked between them, slightly behind.

She kept looking back.

The road did not become familiar.

No train tracks appeared.

No convenience store.

No other person.

"Is there... a station nearby?"

Mau looked at Frieren.

Frieren looked at Mau.

"What's a station?"

Bocchi stopped.

"...Oh no."

The inn looked worse from outside than the phrase *we have somewhere warm* had suggested.

Boarded windows.

Old sign.

Damage.

Inside, however, there was a fire.

Blankets.

Food.

Tools.

Two bowls drying beside a third place that had not existed yet.

It looked less like a murder location and more like two people had been losing an argument with a ruined building for a while.

That helped.

Slightly.

"Where is this?" Bocchi asked.

"We don't know," Frieren said.

Bocchi stared.

Mau sat near the table.

"We're not from here either."

"You're from Japan?"

"I don't know."

Bocchi's expression fell.

Frieren gave her the shortest explanation possible.

She had woken here.

Mau had too.

Neither knew the world.

They had found the settlement and stayed because the inn was usable enough.

No route home.

No authority coming.

No map that answered anything.

Bocchi lowered herself into a chair.

Her guitar case stayed between her knees.

"So... what am I supposed to do now?"

Mau answered first.

"I don't know."

Bocchi looked at him.

He continued.

"But I'll help you."

The sentence was simple.

Too simple to argue with.

Bocchi looked toward Frieren.

Frieren was watching Mau.

There was something in the look that Bocchi could not read yet.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

Then Frieren turned toward the fire.

Bocchi held the guitar case a little less tightly.

For the moment, help was enough.
