<!-- scene-id: s1e3-ln02-room -->

Frieren showed Bocchi the upstairs corridor.

Mau carried one of her bags because Bocchi had not let go of the guitar.

The inn became less reassuring when it changed from *the place those two people live* to *the place I may have to sleep.*

One room had no door.

Another smelled damp.

A third contained enough dark corners for Bocchi's imagination to populate it with several species of things that probably did not exist.

Probably.

Frieren opened one of the better rooms.

Bed frame.

Small table.

Mostly intact window.

Dust.

"This one is usable."

Bocchi stared at it.

"Usable."

"Mostly."

From behind them, Mau said, "That's her standard."

Frieren looked back.

Mau did not look guilty.

A small sound escaped Bocchi.

Almost a laugh.

That surprised her enough to make her stop.

Mau put the bag against the wall.

"If you want another room, there are more."

Bocchi looked down the corridor.

Dark doors.

Unknown spaces.

"This one is good."

Too fast.

"I mean—fine. It's fine."

Frieren nodded.

No speech about bravery.

No expectation that Bocchi act grateful.

That helped.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e3-ln02-first-dinner -->

Dinner introduced a new problem.

Three people.

A table.

Conversation.

Bocchi had spent years becoming nervous in situations with fewer variables.

Mau looked toward the guitar case.

"What is the big case?"

Bocchi almost said *nothing.*

She stopped herself.

"A guitar."

Mau waited.

"What's a guitar?"

Bocchi looked at Frieren.

Frieren kept eating.

No rescue.

"It's an instrument."

"What does it do?"

Bocchi stared.

How did you explain what an instrument did?

"It... makes music."

"Can you show me?"

Bocchi nearly inhaled dinner.

"Now?"

"If you want."

That last part saved her.

"Maybe later."

"Okay."

Mau returned to eating.

No disappointed look.

No insistence.

No social debt apparently created by refusing.

Bocchi watched him for a moment.

Frieren noticed.

She said nothing.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e3-ln02-alone-upstairs -->

The room became colder after dark.

That was not the real problem.

Every sound was.

Wood shifted.

Wind touched the glass.

Something moved outside through snow.

The building settled around her with a vocabulary of creaks Bocchi had not learned yet.

She lay beneath the blanket with the guitar case beside the bed.

Her phone still had no signal.

The battery had dropped.

The screen contained pieces of a life that now seemed impossibly far away.

Messages.

Photos.

Names.

She turned it off.

Darkness.

Worse.

A faint orange line showed beneath the door.

The common-room fire.

Bocchi sat up.

She spent several minutes debating whether going downstairs would make her look needy.

A louder creak ended the debate.

She wrapped the blanket around her shoulders and left.

Halfway down the stairs, she heard Frieren.

Bocchi stopped.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e3-ln02-established-pattern -->

Frieren was crying.

Not loudly.

That somehow made it more private.

From the stairwell, Bocchi could see the fire and part of the common room.

Mau sat near it.

Frieren sat beside him, face lowered.

Bocchi had expected the people who found her to know what they were doing.

They had food.

A shelter.

Answers to practical questions.

Frieren had magic.

Mau had somehow looked calm while admitting he did not know who he had been.

They had seemed stable.

Now Bocchi saw the part underneath that.

Frieren wiped her face once.

More tears came.

"I should've seen it sooner."

Mau did not answer immediately.

He knew this face.

Bocchi could tell from the way he stayed still.

Not frozen.

Waiting.

"Fern would've listened if I'd been faster."

Mau looked at her.

"You tried to save her."

"It wasn't enough."

Silence.

No *everything happens for a reason.*

No argument.

Frieren leaned closer.

Mau let her.

His arm came around her shoulders with the slight awkwardness of someone who had learned the action before ever becoming good at it.

This had happened before.

That realization changed the scene.

Bocchi was not witnessing an emergency.

She was accidentally seeing a routine built around pain.

Frieren's breathing slowed.

Eventually she fell asleep against Mau.

Only then did Mau notice Bocchi.

Their eyes met.

Bocchi froze.

Mau lifted one finger to his lips.

She nodded so fast the blanket nearly fell.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e3-ln02-question -->

Bocchi descended carefully.

Mau could not stand without moving Frieren.

He pointed toward a folded blanket near the fire.

Bocchi took it and sat on the other side of the hearth.

For a while they listened to the fire.

She kept looking at Frieren.

"Does she... um..."

Bocchi stopped.

Mau waited.

"Does she do that a lot?"

"Sometimes."

"Because of Fern?"

"Yes."

"Right. Yeah. Fern."

Silence again.

Bocchi thought the conversation had ended.

Then she looked at Mau.

He looked tired.

Not dramatic.

Just tired.

The question escaped before she could stop it.

"Who's helping you?"

Mau almost answered.

Bocchi's eyes widened.

"I mean—not that you need— I didn't mean that you don't—"

She stopped.

Both hands disappeared farther into the blanket.

"Sorry."

Mau frowned.

"Why are you sorry?"

"That sounded weird."

"What part?"

Bocchi stared at him.

"All of it?"

Mau looked down at Frieren.

She had found him.

Fed him.

Taught him.

Stayed.

That should have been an answer.

But Bocchi was asking something different.

He adjusted the blanket over Frieren's shoulder.

Then looked back at Bocchi.

"...I don't know."

Bocchi immediately looked horrified.

"No, no, I shouldn't have—forget I asked. Actually, please forget I asked."

"Why?"

"I made it sad."

"You asked a question."

"It became sad."

Mau considered this.

He was not sure questions could become sad.

This one apparently had.

Bocchi sank deeper into her blankets.

The silence returned.

Several minutes later:

"Thank you," she whispered.

Mau looked over.

"For the blanket?"

"Y-yes."

Pause.

"No."

Another pause.

"I mean, yes, for the blanket too."

Mau waited.

Bocchi opened her mouth.

Closed it.

"Just... thank you."

It was clearly about more than the blanket.

Mau could have asked.

He didn't.

Bocchi curled closer to the fire.

Maybe not asking was another way people helped.
