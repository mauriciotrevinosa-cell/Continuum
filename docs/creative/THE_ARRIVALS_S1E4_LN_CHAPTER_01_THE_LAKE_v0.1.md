A few days after Bocchi arrived, the inn had not transformed.

It had habits.

Bocchi's guitar case lived against the same wall.

Three bowls dried near the kitchen area.

A second window had been patched badly enough to be temporary and well enough to matter.

Mau sat at the table sorting salvaged hardware into categories that made sense until Frieren walked past and moved two pieces.

He looked up.

"I was going to do that."

Frieren did not stop.

Bocchi swept near the hearth.

The snow outside had thinned.

Frieren looked through the window.

"We should go to the lake."

Mau stopped sorting.

"Why?"

"Fish."

Bocchi's broom slowed.

"All of us?"

"If you want."

Bocchi looked at the door.

Then at Frieren.

Then back at the door.

"...Okay."

Mau stood and accidentally put the corrected nails back into the wrong pile.

Frieren fixed them while passing.

<!-- scene-id: s1e4-ln01-lake-arrival -->

The lake was farther than Bocchi expected and closer than she had feared.

Forest opened without warning onto broad winter water.

Bocchi stopped.

Mau kept walking until he noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She took out her phone.

No signal.

No one to send the picture to.

She took it anyway.

Mau leaned slightly closer.

"What does that do?"

"It saves pictures."

He moved a little nearer to see the screen.

Bocchi nearly dropped the phone.

Frieren had already turned toward the water.

Her expression changed.

Mau noticed because he was watching her.

"What?"

"Stay here."

The ease had left her voice.

Bocchi went still.

Mau looked across the lake.

Nothing obvious.

Frieren felt something else.

Dense.

Structured.

Not mana.

Power with a logic she did not recognize.

Across the water, someone had noticed her too.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e4-ln01-yuta -->

Yuta Okkotsu had already been having a difficult day.

He had woken in a body decades younger than the one he remembered dying in.

The landscape meant nothing.

The rules meant less.

Now a mana presence large enough to demand attention was moving toward him.

His hand rested on his sword.

He did not draw.

Not yet.

The white-haired mage came around the lake.

Staff.

Pointed ears.

Power that made pretending she was harmless impossible.

She stopped at a distance.

Yuta did too.

"Who are you?"

"I was going to ask you the same thing."

Neither voice was hostile.

Neither was relaxed.

Frieren looked at his sword.

Yuta looked at her staff.

Wind moved through the trees.

Yuta felt her mana shift.

His sword came free.

Frieren reacted to the draw.

Her staff rose.

The misunderstanding completed itself in less than a second.

Zoltraak flashed across the space between them.

Compact by Frieren's standards.

Serious by almost anyone else's.

Yuta reinforced and moved.

The edge of the attack tore past him, cutting ground behind.

He landed.

Prepared to answer.

Frieren was already prepared to receive it.

Then a third voice arrived, slightly out of breath.

"You're not from here either."

Yuta paused.

Frieren did too.

Mau stood at the edge of the damaged ground.

He looked at Yuta.

Then Frieren.

Then the trench.

"Neither are we."

Yuta's grip changed.

"...What?"

The possibility was ridiculous.

So was waking young after remembering death.

His sword lowered.

Frieren's staff followed a moment later.

Bocchi arrived several seconds after Mau, having made the sensible choice not to sprint toward supernatural combat.

She stared at the damaged earth.

Then Yuta.

"I think I missed something."

No one answered immediately.

That was probably answer enough.
