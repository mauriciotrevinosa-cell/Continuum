<!-- scene-id: s1e4-ln02-four-by-water -->

Nobody suggested becoming friends.

They sat near the lake because walking home while still uncertain whether the new stranger might attack again seemed inefficient.

Yuta kept his sword sheathed but close.

Frieren's staff rested beside her.

Bocchi positioned herself slightly behind Mau and appeared to hope nobody would notice.

Mau began with the question that had worked on everyone so far.

"What's your name?"

"Yuta Okkotsu."

"I'm Mau."

Bocchi lifted one hand.

"Hitori Gotoh."

"Frieren."

Yuta looked at Frieren.

"Sorry about the sword."

Frieren glanced toward the cut ground.

"I fired first."

"I drew first."

Mau looked between them.

"So you're both sorry."

A short laugh escaped Yuta.

Frieren did not respond.

That was close enough.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e4-ln02-reflection -->

Before they left the lake, Yuta crouched near the water.

For a moment he appeared to be checking something along the shore.

Mau noticed him looking at his reflection instead.

Young face.

Young hands.

Yuta lifted one hand and turned it once, studying the fingers.

The last hands he remembered had looked different.

Older.

He did not stare long.

Long enough.

When he stood, Mau did not ask.

That restraint registered.

They started back.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e4-ln02-another-world -->

Conversation came while walking.

Japan meant something to Bocchi.

Nothing to Frieren.

Mau could not tell whether the word should mean something to him.

Yuta asked where they were.

"We don't know," Frieren said.

"Okay."

Bocchi looked at him.

Yuta noticed.

"I've had stranger days."

She believed him immediately.

That might not have been rational.

Yuta learned that Frieren and Bocchi came from different worlds.

Mau had no accessible origin at all.

The first time Mau said "I don't know" about his past, Yuta did not follow with another question.

Mau noticed.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e4-ln02-meal -->

At the inn, Yuta stopped in the doorway.

"You live here?"

"Mostly," Mau said.

Frieren looked at him.

Mau nodded to himself.

"That's our standard now."

Bocchi hid a smile.

Food came before explanations.

That mattered more than Yuta expected.

He sat at the old table with a bowl in front of him and watched steam rise.

An ordinary meal.

His hands looked young around the bowl.

The body accepted hunger differently.

No stiffness he remembered.

No old ache.

No small caution in lifting something hot.

For several seconds Yuta forgot to eat.

Bocchi noticed.

"Is it bad?"

Yuta looked up.

"No."

He took a bite.

Warm.

Simple.

Real.

Something about that was harder than the fight had been.

He kept eating.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e4-ln02-body-problem -->

Later, when enough trust existed to make questions possible, Yuta touched his own face.

"I remember getting old."

Bocchi looked up.

"A lot older than this."

Mau studied him.

"How old?"

Yuta paused.

"Old enough that this isn't the body I died in."

Silence.

Bocchi stopped moving.

"You died?"

"I think so."

He did not dramatize it.

The drama belonged to a life none of them had seen.

"I remember the end. Then I woke up here like this."

Mau looked at his own hands.

"So this place changed you."

"Or rebuilt something." Yuta looked down at the table. "I don't know."

Frieren's attention moved toward her travel case.

Her staff.

Fern's ornament.

Objects that should not have been with her when she fell asleep.

A pattern.

Incomplete.

But a pattern.

"Then maybe Fern can—"

The words stopped.

Nobody finished them for her.

Frieren lowered her eyes.

Seven days of searching sat too close behind the possibility.

She stood.

"We need more wood."

Bocchi looked toward the existing pile.

Enough for the night.

She said nothing.

Yuta understood.

Mau watched Frieren reach for what she needed.

"I'll go."

Outside, he followed her toward the salvage pile.

Neither spoke for a while.

Then Frieren said, "I shouldn't assume anything."

Mau picked up a piece of dry wood.

"Okay."

Frieren looked at him.

No reassurance.

No *but maybe.*

Just an answer that let the possibility stay unfinished.

It was enough.
