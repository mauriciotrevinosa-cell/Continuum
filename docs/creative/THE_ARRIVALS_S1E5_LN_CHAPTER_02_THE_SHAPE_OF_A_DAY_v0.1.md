<!-- scene-id: s1e5-ln02-repairs -->

The next day was mostly repair work.

A loose upper-floor railing.

A table with one leg shorter than the others.

A door that only closed if lifted first.

Problems small enough to solve.

Yuta showed Mau how to brace a board before driving a nail.

Mau held it wrong.

Yuta moved his hand.

"Here."

"Why?"

Yuta began explaining force and angle.

Then stopped.

He demonstrated instead.

Mau tried again.

Better.

Frieren crossed the corridor carrying salvaged cloth.

She saw Mau working without her.

He did not notice her.

Yuta said something.

Mau laughed.

Frieren paused for half a second.

Not hurt.

Not jealous.

Simply registering that Mau's world had become larger than one person.

Then Bocchi called from downstairs.

"Um—I think the table is worse."

Frieren continued down.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e5-ln02-table -->

It was worse.

Bocchi had folded cloth beneath the short leg.

The table now leaned in a new direction.

Frieren pressed one side.

It rocked.

"I can put it back," Bocchi said quickly.

"Leave it."

Bocchi's face fell.

Frieren crouched and inspected the leg.

"We'll cut a piece to fit."

"Oh."

Frieren looked up.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Frieren accepted that answer.

Later, Mau pressed both hands against the repaired table.

It stayed level.

"Better."

Bocchi nodded.

"Mostly."

Mau looked betrayed.

The word was everybody's now.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e5-ln02-object-chain -->

A hammer left Mau's hand.

Bocchi took it to secure a loose board near the table.

Yuta borrowed it when he needed the same tool upstairs.

Frieren used it to knock a stubborn peg free from a damaged frame.

Hours later, Mau found the hammer beside his original pile of materials.

He picked it up.

"Did anyone use this?"

Three voices answered from different parts of the inn.

"Yes."

Mau looked at the hammer.

Then toward the ceiling.

"Okay."

The tool had crossed the entire household and returned.

No one had planned that either.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e5-ln02-lunch -->

They ate lunch near the entrance because the sky was clear.

Bocchi sat on the step.

Then, while the others talked, shifted down onto the ground without noticing she had moved farther outside than usual.

Yuta described a trail farther north.

Mau interrupted.

"The deer tracks were there."

"We're not talking about the deer."

"The trail had deer tracks."

Yuta sighed.

"That is technically true."

Frieren ate another bite.

"Maybe it followed you home."

Mau turned toward the forest.

Bocchi laughed.

This time, when everyone looked at her, she did not disappear completely into herself.

"Sorry."

"Don't be," Yuta said. "He deserved that one."

Mau looked at Frieren.

She continued eating as if she had said nothing unusual.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e5-ln02-plants -->

That afternoon Mau helped Frieren gather plants.

He identified one before she pointed it out.

"This one."

"Yes."

A little later he picked another.

Wrong.

Frieren removed it from the basket.

"Not that one."

"It looks the same."

"It doesn't."

Frieren held the two beside each other and pointed to a small difference in the leaf.

Mau leaned closer.

"That's unfair."

"To who?"

He had no answer.

They kept working.

Nothing about the exchange was important.

That was why it mattered.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e5-ln02-guitar-dinner -->

At night, Bocchi played in the common room.

Not hidden upstairs.

Not loudly.

Openly.

Yuta maintained his sword.

Mau tried the pot handle again with a better method.

Frieren read.

Bocchi hit a wrong note and stopped.

Mau looked up.

"Was that part of it?"

"No."

"It sounded fine."

Bocchi was not sure whether that helped.

Yuta did not look up.

"Keep going."

She did.

Frieren had not turned a page in a while.

Later, at dinner, Mau and Yuta reached for the same piece of food.

Yuta let go.

Mau took it.

Then noticed.

"You wanted this."

"It's fine."

Mau split it and put half on Yuta's plate.

Conversation continued around the movement.

Bocchi asked Frieren about the field.

Yuta asked Mau about going out tomorrow.

Two conversations overlapped.

"If the deer is there," Mau said.

Yuta closed his eyes.

Bocchi looked at Mau.

"I think you scared it more than it scared you."

"That's what it wants you to think."

Frieren looked up.

"You're learning."

Mau looked pleased.

Then realized.

Yuta laughed into his food.

Bocchi joined him.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e5-ln02-ordinary-morning -->

Another morning.

No arrival.

No fight.

No mystery opening beneath the floor.

Yuta left to check the perimeter.

Frieren took a basket toward the field.

Bocchi brought her broom outside to beat dust from it and remained there longer than the task required.

Mau stood in the common room.

He looked toward Yuta's route.

Then Frieren.

Then Bocchi.

Frieren saw him deciding.

"Go with Yuta."

"You don't need help?"

Frieren lifted the basket.

"I'll survive."

"Probably?"

She looked at him.

Mau smiled and ran after Yuta.

Frieren watched him for one extra beat.

Then turned toward the field.

From the inn, Bocchi's guitar began again.

The settlement was still broken.

The day was completely ordinary.
