<!-- scene-id: s1e6-ln02-passive-evaluation -->

Raphael's ordinary evaluative functions had been working in the background since Rimuru entered the inn.

Nothing unusual about that.

Frieren did not fit Tempest's world, but she was coherent.

Mana.

Body.

Spell structure.

Different did not mean impossible.

Yuta was stranger.

The energy around him did not map cleanly to magic, but it behaved consistently enough to possess structure.

Bocchi was, by comparison, wonderfully uncomplicated.

Then there was Mau.

No vast hidden reading.

No secret result waiting behind an error message.

The problem was more basic.

Expected categories refused to settle cleanly.

Passive comparisons failed to resolve into a useful answer.

Raphael registered a classification problem.

That was all.

Rimuru had already decided to ask about it later.

Then Mau had spoken her name.

"Raphael."

Only Rimuru heard the word clearly.

Raphael registered the external utterance.

She did not know why he knew it.

Frieren saw Mau's lips move from nearby.

Yuta heard that he had said something.

Bocchi looked up from the guitar.

Rimuru stared.

"How did you—"

Mau's hand rose toward his head.

His breath caught.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e6-ln02-overload -->

Inside Mau, something broke across thought.

Not a voice Rimuru could hear.

Not a signal Raphael could perceive.

No interface opened.

No warning appeared in the air.

Fragments without enough shape to become language pressed through him faster than he could separate them.

Mau's knees gave out.

Frieren moved first.

"Mau."

She caught him before his head struck the floor.

No flash.

No wave of power.

No force left his body.

Nothing traveled from Rimuru to Mau.

One moment Mau had been standing.

The next he was unconscious.

Frieren checked his breathing.

Pulse.

Obvious injury.

Alive.

Yuta was beside her.

Bocchi stood with both hands half-raised, uncertain where to put herself.

Rimuru had not moved.

"I didn't—"

Frieren looked at him.

The sentence stopped.

Yuta's voice stayed even.

"Did you do something?"

"No."

Rimuru meant it completely.

Frieren had known him for minutes.

That did not make the answer useful.

She lifted Mau with Yuta's help.

"Move."

Rimuru moved.

Bocchi grabbed blankets and followed.

Within seconds the common room had emptied.

Rimuru remained beside the table.

A few minutes ago they had laughed with him.

Now every person in the house was upstairs because Mau had collapsed while looking at him.

Whether that made sense or not did not change how it felt.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e6-ln02-first-night -->

Mau did not wake that night.

Near midnight, Rimuru sat alone downstairs.

"Raphael."

`Yes.`

"How did he know your name?"

A pause.

`Unknown.`

Rimuru shut his eyes.

"Did you do something to him?"

`No deliberate action was initiated.`

Raphael could describe only what had actually occurred.

Automatic observation.

Comparison.

Failure to resolve Mau cleanly into expected categories.

No intentional attack.

No successful classification.

No mechanism connecting that process to his collapse.

"Could trying again tell us?"

This pause was shorter.

`Not recommended.`

"Because it might happen again."

`Yes.`

Rimuru leaned back against the wall.

Unknown usually meant interesting.

This time someone else had paid for it.

"Then we don't."

`Agreed.`

Neither of them considered The Noise.

Neither knew it existed.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e6-ln02-two-days -->

Morning light crossed Mau's room.

Frieren was there.

Midday light changed the angle.

Frieren was still there.

Yuta came and went.

Food.

Water.

Perimeter.

Mau.

Bocchi brought her guitar upstairs and played quietly enough that the sound stayed near the room.

Rimuru repaired things downstairs.

When he offered to help closer to the bed, Frieren said no.

Not cruelly.

Simply no.

That night, a lamp replaced daylight.

Frieren remained nearby.

The next morning, Yuta convinced her to eat.

She did.

Then returned.

Bocchi sat against the wall and repeated the same progression on the guitar until her fingers no longer needed attention.

Rimuru passed the doorway once.

Frieren looked up.

He stopped.

Then kept walking.

Two days was long enough for the inn to develop a new routine around one person not moving.
