<!-- scene-id: s1e7-ln01-probation -->

Rimuru still lived at the inn.

That did not mean Frieren trusted him.

He had learned the difference.

Morning rain ticked against repaired glass while Rimuru studied a water-stained section of ceiling.

"I can do that."

Frieren took the ladder from him.

"I know."

She climbed.

Rimuru stared upward.

"That's somehow worse."

Yuta passed carrying wood.

"Give it time."

"I am," Frieren said from the ladder.

Nobody argued.

Across the room, Mau helped Bocchi separate salvaged wire from a pile of nails.

He looked normal again.

Frieren checked him anyway.

Not obviously.

Often.

Rimuru noticed.

He also noticed everything else he had entered late.

Yuta knew where the tools belonged.

Bocchi stepped around a squeaky floorboard without looking down.

Mau moved between people as if the room had always expected him.

Frieren's things and Mau's things had stopped respecting clean ownership upstairs.

Rimuru had a chair.

A bowl.

A place to sleep.

He did not yet have a place in the rhythm.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e7-ln01-normal-day -->

The day required work whether anyone trusted anyone or not.

Yuta and Mau checked snares and gathered food.

Bocchi repaired a torn sack just outside the doorway, close enough to retreat and far enough that she had chosen outside.

Frieren headed toward the field.

Rimuru picked up a basket.

"I can come."

"No."

Rimuru stopped.

"Do you want me to stay here or just not come with you?"

Frieren thought.

"Either is fine."

Rimuru looked toward Bocchi.

Bocchi looked away with the speed of someone refusing appointment as mediator.

Rimuru stayed.

He repaired something else.

By evening everyone was too hungry to sustain tension at full strength.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e7-ln01-warm-table -->

Dinner did not begin as story night.

Rimuru tasted the stew and smiled.

"This reminds me of when my city was basically still a village."

Yuta looked up.

"You had a city?"

"Eventually."

Mau stopped eating.

"How do you get a city?"

Rimuru laughed.

"That's a long answer."

Bocchi leaned forward despite herself.

"Did you build it?"

The question opened the wrong door if anyone had intended dinner to remain simple.

Rimuru did not recite a biography.

He remembered problems.

People.

Friends who had turned one problem into five.

Small decisions that became cities because enough people kept choosing tomorrow.

Yuta answered one detail with a memory of his own.

Bocchi tried to stay quiet.

Then someone asked about the guitar.

"I was in a band."

Mau looked at her.

"The guitar people."

"That's... one way to describe them."

Yuta smiled.

"What were they called?"

"Kessoku Band."

The name changed Bocchi's face before the rest of her sentence arrived.

Nijika.

Ryo.

Kita.

Rehearsal.

Food.

Live houses.

Embarrassing moments she now would have traded almost anything to repeat.

Nobody said *you miss them.*

Nobody needed to.

A comment about time turned toward Frieren.

"How long did you travel with the hero party?" Rimuru asked.

"Ten years."

Rimuru waited.

"That's it?"

"It didn't feel like it."

The answer belonged to Frieren in a way nobody else at the table could reproduce.

Himmel entered one story because he was necessary to it.

Heiter entered another.

Fern appeared in several.

Not as names on a memorial.

As people who had eaten bad food, argued, walked too slowly or too quickly, liked particular things.

Mau listened.

At first with interest.

Then more quietly.

Every story produced another person.

Another place.

Another memory.

Even grief had shape.

He had facts.

Nothing behind them.

Rimuru noticed the silence too late.

"What about you, Mau?"

The room stilled in pieces.

Bocchi first.

Yuta second.

Frieren had already been watching him.

Mau looked down at his bowl.

There was no story waiting.

"I'm going outside."

The chair moved.

He left.

Frieren stood immediately.

Yuta stepped into her path.

Not forcefully.

"Move."

"I know where he's going."

Frieren's eyes narrowed.

Unknown to her, Rika was already following at a distance.

"Let me go first."

"Why?"

Yuta looked toward the door.

"Because he always comes to you."

Frieren said nothing.

"Maybe he shouldn't have to this time."

That was not an accusation.

Frieren sat.

She hated doing it.

She did it anyway.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e7-ln01-outside-laughter -->

Outside, Mau walked far enough that the inn's voices became muffled.

Then laughter broke through the walls.

Warm.

Several people at once.

Mau stopped.

He smiled.

They were happy.

He was glad.

The laughter came again.

This time it carried the same reminder as the dinner table.

They had things to remember together.

He did not.

Mau kept walking toward the lake.
