## Locked voice / scene principles

- Conversations should now feel increasingly fluid and relational rather than question-answer interviews.
- Rimuru remains socially warm and well-intentioned; the Mau trigger is accidental because he is trying to include him.
- The dinner story-sharing sequence should emerge organically through associative memories, not turn-taking exposition.
- Frieren's time scale should remain understated and in-character; brief lines such as `Ten years.` / `It didn't feel like it.` can carry more than explanation.
- Mau's emotional rupture should be quiet. He realizes everyone else has a remembered past, even if painful, while he does not.
- Mau's departure from dinner should carry weight precisely because he rarely makes dramatic exits.
- Yuta should stop Frieren from immediately following, not by authority but by recognizing that Mau needs another person to reach him first.
- Yuta must not pretend to understand Mau's amnesia. His support works because he honestly admits he does not know what that feels like.
- The lake conversation should avoid therapy-speech and instead use silence, plain acknowledgement, and Yuta's lived experience.
- Bocchi's `We're here to help you too.` is the direct payoff to Mau's earlier `I'll help you.` promise in E3.
- Mau's simple `Okay.` should carry the emotional weight; do not overwrite the payoff.
- Frieren's growth in E7 is partly visual: she learns to stay a few steps back and let Bocchi/Yuta reach Mau without feeling she is abandoning him.
- Closing exchange remains:
  - Frieren: `Do you feel better?`
  - Mau: `Do you?`
  - Frieren: `I'm getting there.`
  - Mau: `Okay.`
- E7 does not cure Mau. It expands his support network beyond Frieren and advances Frieren's ability to share responsibility for him.
