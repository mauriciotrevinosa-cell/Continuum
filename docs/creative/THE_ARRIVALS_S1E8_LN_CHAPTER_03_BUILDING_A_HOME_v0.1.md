<!-- scene-id: s1e8-ln03-chain-of-hands -->

Rimuru did not build a city around them.

He organized a mess.

Yuta handled heavy work.

Frieren used magic when magic genuinely saved labor.

Bocchi cleared and insulated the future music room.

Mau measured a board wrong once and learned what being stared at by three people simultaneously felt like.

Materials moved through hands.

Yuta to Rimuru.

Rimuru to Bocchi.

Bocchi to Mau.

Mau across a gap to Frieren.

Their fingers briefly met on the same board.

Neither looked up.

The material kept moving.

That was the work.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e8-ln03-music-room -->

The music room was not impressive.

Reinforced walls.

Improvised sound damping.

A door that closed.

Bocchi stood in it with her guitar.

"Try it," Rimuru said.

She closed the door.

One loud chord.

Outside, everyone heard it.

Much less.

The door opened.

Bocchi's eyes were wide.

"I can be louder."

Yuta looked at her.

"That's what you're excited about?"

"You don't understand."

Mau nodded.

"I don't."

Bocchi closed the door in their faces and played louder.

The hallway filled with a muffled suggestion of guitar.

Rimuru smiled.

This improvement did nothing to keep anyone alive.

That was why it mattered.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e8-ln03-clothes-stop-being-static -->

Fixing the building made smaller problems easier to notice.

One evening Rimuru found a pile of clothing on the table.

Not laundry.

Repairs.

A seam on one of Yuta's everyday layers had opened from work.

The inside of Bocchi's coat had started pulling loose near the cuff.

Frieren's cold-weather clothing had a worn section she had apparently decided did not count as a problem because it still functioned.

Mau's McLaren hoodie had begun to fray where one cuff kept catching on rough timber.

Rimuru looked at the pile.

Then at everyone.

"You know clothes can also be repaired before they fall apart."

Frieren looked up from her book.

"They're not falling apart."

Rimuru held up her worn layer.

"This is the argument you used about the roof."

"The roof leaked."

"This is how we get there."

Mau turned his wrist and inspected the frayed cuff.

"I thought that was supposed to look like that."

"No."

"Oh."

They did not gain a wardrobe overnight.

They gained a repair pile.

Salvaged cloth stopped being only insulation.

Some became patches.

Strong thread became worth saving.

Pieces that did not fit anyone properly could be cut down, lined, reinforced or reused instead of discarded.

Rimuru was better at making those decisions efficiently.

Bocchi cared about whether repairs were visible.

Yuta did not.

Frieren cared only after someone else pointed out the damage.

Mau discovered he liked keeping the original pieces even when the repaired sections made them look slightly different.

The house had already taught them to reuse wood and stone.

Clothing became another version of the same lesson.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e8-ln03-common-space-object -->

During the work, Mau left one of the few personal things that had arrived with him on the common-room table.

Rimuru noticed first.

Yuta noticed Rimuru noticing.

Neither touched it.

Hours later Mau returned, picked it up, and carried it upstairs without checking whether anyone had moved it.

The action lasted seconds.

The trust inside it had taken longer.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e8-ln03-trust-without-speech -->

Rimuru worked near the side entrance, tying a simple warning line into place.

He needed a small metal part held at a precise height.

Frieren passed with a book.

"Can you hold this?"

She took it.

Rimuru tied the line.

Several seconds passed.

Then he looked at her hand.

Frieren followed his gaze.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She looked suspicious.

She kept holding the part until he finished.

No one announced that trust had improved.

It had.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e8-ln03-mau-learns -->

Mau and Rimuru fought with a hinge.

"How did you know the angle was wrong?"

Rimuru showed him.

Frieren passed.

Mau asked another question.

In E5, she would have paused at the sight of him learning from someone else.

This time she left the tool they would need next beside them and kept walking.

"Thanks," Mau called.

"Mm."

Rimuru looked between them.

"You two do that a lot."

"Do what?"

Rimuru reconsidered.

"Nothing."

Mau frowned.

"Everyone keeps saying that."

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e8-ln03-home -->

The inn remained modest.

The roof stopped leaking into the common room.

The kitchen had a real work surface.

Storage stayed dry.

The bath worked.

Bocchi had a room for music.

Warnings protected the easiest approaches.

Everything looked repaired, reused or chosen.

At dinner, Rimuru sat where the empty distance around him used to be.

Nobody had assigned the chair.

It was simply where he sat.

One of the new bells sounded outside.

Mau stood.

Yuta started to.

Frieren listened.

A small animal crossed the window.

"Not dangerous."

Mau sat again.

Bocchi looked toward the glass.

"The deer?"

Yuta groaned.

Rimuru looked around.

"What deer?"

That made it worse.

Five people had built things here because they expected to use them tomorrow.

The inn had once been somewhere to stay.

Nobody needed to define the difference now.
