## Approval

S1E8 is GREEN-LIT after the revised voice-check pass.

The approved interpretation is that multiple members of G1 can already infer that Rimuru did not intentionally hurt Mau, but Frieren needs a higher standard of certainty because she has become especially protective of Mau. The episode therefore uses the Frieren–Rimuru conversation to earn the beginning of trust rather than to reveal guilt.

## Locked conversation logic

- Frieren did **not** hear Mau say `Raphael` before his E6 collapse.
- In E8 she asks Rimuru what Mau said.
- Rimuru initially hesitates because answering exposes something deeply private about himself.
- Raphael privately recommends that Rimuru answer truthfully.
- Rimuru tells Frieren that Mau said `Raphael`.
- Frieren then asks who Raphael is.
- Rimuru chooses to disclose that Raphael is an internal intelligence / presence that talks and thinks with him and is not audible to other people.
- This voluntary disclosure is an important trust-building act.
- When Frieren says Raphael was doing something to Mau, Raphael privately corrects the framing: no hostile action was performed; Mau was being processed by ordinary passive analytical functions.
- Rimuru relays the correction rather than hiding it.
- Raphael cannot properly analyze Mau, cannot explain why Mau knew her name, and **cannot detect Mau's internal `The Noise` at all**.
- Neither Rimuru nor Raphael claims to understand the E6 event.
- Rimuru and Raphael stop further attempts because they will not risk hurting Mau again merely to satisfy curiosity.
- Frieren's approved trust line remains: `I don't know if I believe you. But I'll try.`

## Building-a-home half

The second half keeps the approved lighter ensemble construction energy:

- Rimuru notices how improvised the inn still is and pushes for real improvements.
- Bath, kitchen, storage, heating, music space, defenses and general repairs can expand naturally.
- Bocchi's soundproof-room reaction is approved.
- Frieren and Mau retain their own working dynamic; Frieren begins accepting that Mau can learn useful things from other people too.
- Rimuru gradually stops feeling like the new outsider through shared work rather than a formal declaration.
- Humor remains character-driven and must not distort established voices.
- The episode closes on the inn visibly becoming a home they chose to build, paying off E2's earlier `somewhere to stay` distinction without restating it.
