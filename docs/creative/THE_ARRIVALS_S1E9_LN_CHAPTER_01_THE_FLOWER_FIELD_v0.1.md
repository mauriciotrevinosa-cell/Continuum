<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln01-shelves -->

Improving the inn created a new problem.

They had places to put things.

Which meant they had enough things to run out of places to put them.

Mau stood in the common room holding three grimoires and staring at a shelf that had been half-empty the day before.

"There was space yesterday."

Frieren sat at the table with a book open.

"There was."

Mau looked at the shelf.

Then at her.

"What happened?"

"Books."

Yuta passed behind him carrying supplies.

"You walked into that one."

Mau followed him with the grimoires.

"I need another shelf."

From the other side of the room Rimuru answered, "Everyone needs another shelf."

Bocchi opened the music-room door.

"I don't."

Rimuru pointed toward the cables, cloth, spare strings and small objects already accumulating behind her.

Bocchi looked back.

Then quietly closed the door.

"You do," Rimuru said to the wood.

<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln01-rooms -->

The rooms had begun to reveal their owners without anyone formally decorating them.

Yuta's was almost aggressively sparse.

Bed.

Sword.

A few practical things.

Mau stood in the doorway.

"You don't have anything."

"I have things."

"Where?"

Yuta pointed at the sword.

Mau waited.

Yuta looked around.

"...I see your point."

Bocchi's music room had become the opposite. Not messy exactly. Organized in the way only its owner understood. Guitar. Case. Chair. Notes. Cloth. Small objects placed where her hands expected them to be.

Rimuru's room had surrendered almost immediately and become half sleeping space, half planning station.

Frieren's room was different.

It did not look shared because two people had made a decision.

It looked shared because nobody had noticed when it stopped belonging to only one of them.

Her books.

Her travel case.

Her clothes.

Mau's everyday things between them.

A cup he used more often than she did.

Several of his books stacked near hers.

His black-and-orange McLaren hoodie draped over a chair because Frieren had taken it earlier when the corridor was cold and never returned it to his side of the room.

Mau had asked once.

"Is that mine?"

Frieren had looked down at herself.

"Yes."

"Okay."

That had been the entire dispute.

Another morning, Frieren came downstairs with his McLaren Triple Crown cap.

Bocchi noticed.

Yuta noticed Bocchi noticing.

Rimuru noticed all three of them.

Mau looked up from breakfast.

"Did you need that?"

Frieren touched the brim.

"For now."

Mau returned to eating.

Nobody explained anything.

A few days later laundry created a different problem.

Frieren's usual day clothes were still damp.

She came downstairs wearing Mau's red Ferrari team shirt instead.

The sleeves were a little long on her. She had rolled them once and apparently decided that was enough.

Mau looked up.

"That's mine."

"I know."

"Do you know what Ferrari is?"

"No."

He considered this.

"Okay."

Bocchi looked down at breakfast before her face could give her away.

Rimuru did not bother.

"You two know other clothes exist, right?"

Frieren sat.

"These were dry."

That ended the discussion as far as she was concerned.

The Ferrari shirt went back upstairs later.

Not to Mau's nominal room.

To the chair beside Frieren's books where shared things had begun accumulating without permission from either of them.

Mau technically had a room of his own down the corridor.

It held books.

Grimoires.

Study material.

Things that had no better home.

It was not where he ended his day.

Nobody called Frieren's room theirs.

Nobody needed to.

Not yet.

<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln01-plant -->

Late one morning Mau fastened his cloak and headed toward the door.

Frieren looked up.

"Where are you going?"

"To check on my plant."

Her expression softened by almost nothing.

Yuta looked between them.

"Your plant?"

Bocchi looked up too.

"You have a plant?"

Mau had already opened the door.

"Yes."

Both looked at Frieren.

She returned to her book.

"Nothing."

Mau stepped outside.

Frieren looked up before the door closed.

"Before dark."

"Okay."

She let him go alone.

That would have been impossible at the beginning.

Now it was ordinary enough to be easy.

Mostly.

<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln01-clearing -->

The route returned to him more quickly than he expected.

Stream.

Slope.

Trees.

The clearing where his life, as far as he could remember it, had begun.

Most of Frieren's flowers were gone.

That should not have surprised him.

It did anyway.

A few remained near the edge of the clearing. Some survivors. Some perhaps ordinary descendants. Mau could not tell.

He crouched where he remembered touching the first one.

There had been light.

Color spreading through grass.

Frieren saying she did not know what was happening to him.

Then promising to help.

Mau raised one hand.

Nothing happened.

He held it there.

Still nothing.

He tried to remember the shape of Frieren's movement.

Then stopped.

The gesture had not been the important part.

He had watched enough magic now to know that.

He tried to remember the intention instead.

Flower.

Form.

Create.

Nothing.

A pressure scraped at the edge of thought.

Mau's hand lowered slightly.

`...form...`

He winced.

The fragment disappeared before it could become anything useful.

"That isn't helpful."

No one answered.

Mau tried again.

No light.

No mana he could recognize.

No flower.

Only cold grass beneath his hand.

He changed the position of his fingers.

Nothing.

Again.

Nothing.

Embarrassment arrived even though nobody was there to see him fail.

"...Okay."

He sat back.

The clearing remained peaceful.

That might have been why he kept returning.

The first beautiful thing he remembered had happened here.

He still did not understand why reproducing it mattered.

Before leaving, Mau noticed one real flower growing near the edge.

He crouched.

This time he did not try to make anything.

He carefully took the flower with enough stem to keep it alive during the walk.

That was the only flower he succeeded in bringing home.
