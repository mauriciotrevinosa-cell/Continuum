<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln02-watching-magic -->

Mau came home before dark.

He did not mention the clearing.

The flower stayed protected until he could find somewhere safe for it.

Later, Frieren used a small drying spell near the kitchen.

Mau watched.

Not the result.

Her hand.

The brief pattern of mana before the effect settled into damp cloth.

Frieren noticed without turning completely.

"What?"

Mau looked away.

"Nothing."

"You were staring."

"I was looking."

Frieren finally faced him.

Mau realized the distinction had not helped.

"It was interesting."

"Why?"

He did not know.

That was the problem.

"I don't know."

Frieren studied him for a few seconds.

Then let it go.

She knew enough about his silences now to recognize when pushing would only produce another version of the same answer.

Mau watched the cloth finish drying.

More carefully than he meant to.

<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln02-soundproof -->

Bocchi's room worked too well.

Rimuru discovered this when he knocked.

Nothing.

He knocked harder.

Nothing.

Yuta came down the corridor.

"She's playing."

"I know."

"Then she can't hear you."

Rimuru looked at the wall he had personally helped soundproof.

"I know."

Mau arrived carrying a board.

"Why are you standing here?"

Yuta answered first.

"Rimuru succeeded."

"Too much," Rimuru said.

The guitar stopped.

The door opened.

Bocchi almost walked into all three of them.

She froze.

"...Were you waiting for me?"

Rimuru pointed at the door.

"We need a bell."

Bocchi slowly closed it again.

They tested several options over the next hour.

Knocking.

A louder knock.

A string connected to a small bell inside.

That worked.

Too well.

The first pull sent Bocchi nearly out of her chair.

The second version was quieter.

The third survived her opening the door.

"Good?" Rimuru asked.

Bocchi stared at the bell.

"...Mostly."

Mau looked offended on principle.

<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln02-nominal-room -->

Mau finally got another shelf.

It went into his nominal room because Frieren's had no sensible wall space left.

Rimuru helped level it.

"You actually use this room?"

"Yes."

Rimuru waited.

Mau pointed at the books.

"For these."

Rimuru looked at the unused bed frame.

No blanket.

No everyday clothes.

No cup.

No evidence Mau had slept there recently.

He understood enough not to say anything.

"Another shelf on that wall later."

"Good."

They kept working.

The room was real.

It simply was not home in the same way.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln02-dinner -->

Dinner happened without a plot.

Bocchi complained about the new bell.

Rimuru explained the alternative was people opening the door while she played.

Bocchi accepted the bell immediately.

Yuta looked at Mau.

"Where did you go?"

"My plant."

"Right. The secret plant."

"It's not secret," Frieren said.

Yuta looked at her.

"Then what is it?"

Frieren took another bite.

"A plant."

Mau nodded, satisfied.

Bocchi stared at both of them.

"You two are impossible."

Rimuru sighed.

"I'm starting to understand that."

Conversation moved on.

Nobody noticed Mau had not answered what he actually did there.

* * *

<!-- scene-id: s1e9-ln02-flower -->

Upstairs, Mau took out the flower.

Frieren watched him set it in a small container of water near the window.

"You brought one back."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Mau considered telling her.

The failed attempts.

The fragment.

The feeling that he should be able to understand what she had done.

He did not.

"I liked it."

Frieren accepted that.

Mau adjusted the container.

The next morning, it had moved slightly.

He noticed.

Frieren had shifted it away from a draft.

Mau moved it closer to the light.

That afternoon Frieren moved it again because the sun had become too direct.

Mau found a better angle.

For several days the flower traveled a few inches at a time around the window.

No conversation.

Eventually neither of them moved it again.

The place had become correct by mutual accident.

One evening Frieren set a book beside it.

Mau left his small repair tool on the other side.

The flower had become part of the room.

His first deliberately chosen object.

Not something that arrived with him.

Not something someone assigned.

Something real he had carried home.

No magic.

No success.

That was enough.
