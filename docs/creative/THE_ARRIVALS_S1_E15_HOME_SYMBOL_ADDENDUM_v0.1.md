## Frieren's birthday gift to Mau

Frieren's gift is not a newly fabricated object.

She brings back **the same bucket from S1E1** that Mau returned with when he had been asked to get water but instead found a plant.

Inside it is Mau's original plant, now healthy and accompanied by several flowers grown / maintained by Frieren.

The gift directly links Mau's first day in the new world to the family he has now.

Possible simple presentation:

> "You said you went to check on your plant."  
> "So I brought it home."

The exact dialogue remains rough.

After the birthday, the bucket / plant / flowers stay in Frieren and Mau's room, preferably near the window, and can recur visually in later scenes without requiring dialogue.

## Symbolic progression

```text
S1E1
Mau is sent for water
→ returns with a plant in the bucket

S1
Mau repeatedly returns to the flower site
→ OBSERVE
→ tries to understand Frieren's flower magic
→ cannot reproduce it yet

S1E15
Frieren gives him the original plant in the original bucket
→ now accompanied by flowers
→ the object returns to their shared room

S2 favored payoff
Mau eventually succeeds in reconstructing / creating the flowers himself
```

This does **not** move the successful Mau flower-field payoff back into S1. The successful reconstruction remains favored for S2 after more system growth is earned.

## First explicit use of "home"

S1E15 is favored as the first emotionally explicit moment where the settlement is called **home** rather than merely the inn, base, village, shelter, or place they are staying.

This should not require a speech. The word can appear naturally while Frieren explains the birthday / gift or when someone refers to returning there.

The importance is retrospective:

- E2 begins with an abandoned shelter;
- the group repairs it repeatedly;
- each person gains a room / specialized or communal space;
- they eat, sleep, argue, celebrate, recover, and wait for one another there;
- only after all of that does somebody finally call it **home**.

That makes the word earned rather than declared.

## Why this matters for the later story

The first settlement should become emotionally expensive to leave in S2.

By the time relocation becomes necessary, the audience should not think they are abandoning a damaged inn. They are abandoning:

- the room where Frieren and Mau learned to rely on each other;
- Bocchi's music space;
- the communal table;
- Maomao's work area;
- Momo's shared-home / skywatch ideas;
- the bathhouse and later additions;
- the places where G1 and G2 became family;
- Mau's plant and the first place he was ever told was home.

This is an emotional continuity object, not merely set decoration.
