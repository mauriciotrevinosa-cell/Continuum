# The Arrivals — S1 E16+ World / G3 Rough Roadmap v0.1

**Status:** APPROVED ROUGH / HIGH-CONFIDENCE DIRECTION — not locked at S1E1 level  
**Date:** 2026-09-13  
**Project:** `The Arrivals`  
**Branch:** `creative/s1-season-board-v0.1`

This document continues the current approved-rough S1 roadmap after E15 and records the remaining season-board decisions around world opening, G3, power/source-snapshot planning, and the S1→S2 bridge.

Important: this world is **medieval / fantasy**, not modern. Civilization should read through roads, carts/wagons, pack animals, timber/stone buildings, guilds, inns, markets, blacksmiths, merchants, docks/boats, farms, caravans, watch posts, and similar infrastructure — not modern cars or contemporary city architecture.

Episode numbers from E16 onward remain rough and may shift once the final S1 audit is complete.

## E16 — The World Opens

### Purpose

Move G1+G2 from an isolated first-home bubble into the functioning native world for the first time. Establish the nearby mercantile settlement, native economy, guild structure, coast/islands geography, and the first public use of the `Otherworlder` label without immediately turning it into prejudice.

### Discovery

Yuta (or a small scouting party led by Yuta) travels farther than the group has previously explored. From elevated ground / a ridge / road approach he finds signs of real civilization:

- maintained road;
- wagon / cart traffic;
- smoke from chimneys;
- timber and stone buildings;
- market activity;
- travelers / merchants;
- a small but functioning mercantile town.

He returns to the inn with the simple revelation:

> "I found people."

This is emotionally large because the group's known world until now has largely been forest, lake, ruins, and themselves.

### First group trip to the mercantile town

The nine travel to the settlement together or in a large enough group that it feels like their first formal entry into native society.

They learn naturally, through maps, merchants, guild staff, signs, travelers, and trade, that:

- they are relatively near the sea;
- the mercantile town is part of a broader regional road/trade network;
- the large lake / forest area where their inn sits is only a small piece of the region;
- a cluster / chain of islands lies off the coast, roughly on the far side of the lake / regional geography;
- boats and coastal trade exist;
- there are other villages, towns, routes, guild posts, monster zones, and dungeons.

The island information is **foreshadowing for the future secure-city / autonomous-territory direction**, not an immediate relocation plot. The group does not yet understand its future importance.

### Guild / work integration

The group discovers an adventurer / explorer / hunter-style guild or equivalent local institution.

They register as a party / working group so they can earn legitimate local money and take work such as:

- monster hunting;
- escort / route clearing;
- resource gathering;
- mapping / exploration;
- officially known dungeon work.

They deliberately **do not advertise the dungeon / structure near their own lake / future-island direction yet**. It is still poorly understood, close to their private home, and they have not explored enough of the region to know its importance.

This is not framed as sinister secrecy; it is caution around the only home and local anomaly they personally know.

### `Otherworlder` as a neutral term first

Native people naturally ask where this extremely strange party comes from.

Their answers do not fit the world's geography or one another. A local term equivalent to **Otherworlder** can emerge here as a descriptive category.

At this stage it is mostly curiosity, not hatred:

> strange newcomers from somewhere beyond the known world.

This is important because the same word can later acquire fear after Sukuna's catastrophe.

### Material progression at home

Their first earnings / trade purchases create a different kind of progress from Rimuru's salvage engineering.

They can now buy refined goods they could not easily produce themselves:

- a rug;
- better mattresses / bedding;
- glass panes;
- nails / hinges / quality tools;
- pots / kitchenware;
- lamps;
- spices;
- books / maps;
- cloth;
- storage hardware;
- small decorative objects.

The inn changes from `functional repaired ruin` into a place where people are beginning to spend money simply because they want to live comfortably there.

This should visually reinforce the E15 milestone that the place is now becoming **home**.

## E17 — G3 / Three Roads

### Purpose

Introduce G3 as a parallel arrival thread without merging them into the main household yet. Let the audience understand that they are moving toward the same mercantile region while the characters remain unaware of one another.

Current G3 roster:

```text
Mikasa
Okarun
Umaru Doma / Himouto Umaru
Kita
Marin
Wakana Gojo
Qifrey
Coco
```

Current rough grouping direction:

### Group A

```text
Okarun
Umaru
Kita
```

They can be found / picked up along the same trade road or by a merchant / caravan after appearing at different nearby points.

This grouping deliberately combines very different personalities and gives Okarun / Kita / Umaru room to exist before meeting Momo / Bocchi / the main cast.

### Group B

```text
Coco
Wakana Gojo
Marin
Qifrey
```

A second traveler / merchant / caravan thread can collect or encounter them elsewhere along the broader route.

Qifrey and Coco provide a fantasy-native-looking contrast to Marin / Wakana despite all four being Otherworlders in this world.

### Group C

```text
Mikasa — alone
```

Mikasa remains separate initially. This gives her arrival weight and avoids instantly giving every vulnerable / confused arrival a ready-made support group.

She may later intersect one of the road groups naturally.

### Distance from the mercantile town

Do not place G3 immediately outside the gates.

The favored direction is that they are still **several days of medieval travel** from the mercantile town when introduced. Exact distance remains open and should be set during world-calendar work.

A merchant can give a line in the spirit of:

> "A few more days if the road stays clear."

Avoid fixing `weeks` unless the final world map requires it.

### End-state

By the end of the episode, the audience understands that these separate people / groups are all being funneled toward the same mercantile network that G1+G2 have just discovered.

They do **not** reach the main group in S1.

## E18 candidate — Campfire / Road Days

### Purpose

Avoid a blunt multi-week time jump while still allowing meaningful calendar time to pass before the S1 finale and later S2 information cascade.

This can be a low-plot, character-heavy road episode following one or more G3 groups over several travel days.

Possible material:

- making camp;
- cooking with limited supplies;
- wagon repairs;
- rain / bad road;
- taking watches;
- awkward sleeping arrangements;
- small monster / animal problem;
- Okarun, Kita, and Umaru gradually learning each other's rhythms;
- Marin and Wakana falling into familiar conversational habits while Qifrey / Coco interpret them through a different cultural lens;
- Mikasa learning the local road signs / travel behavior alone;
- merchants discussing regional rumors without yet having reliable Sukuna information;
- changing weather / repeated camps / road landmarks to communicate days passing rather than a title-card time skip.

The episode should feel like real travel, not filler disguised as travel.

Current favored ending: G3 is still not merged with G1+G2, but the mercantile region is becoming visibly closer.

This road buffer gives S2 room for the social situation to evolve before the later Bocchi incident.

## S1→S2 information-travel logic — rough

The native world has medieval-speed communication.

Sukuna's end-of-S1 catastrophe happens in a **major native city far from the protagonists' current mercantile region**.

Information does not propagate instantly.

A believable rough chain is:

```text
catastrophe
→ nearby survivors / travelers / merchants witness aftermath
→ rumors spread through roads and trade nodes
→ more consistent accounts emerge
→ the term Otherworlder becomes attached to the event
→ fear propagates outward
→ news eventually reaches the protagonists' mercantile town
```

The exact elapsed time is OPEN. A span of **weeks to perhaps one or two months** is plausible for the story if the world map / route network supports it, but this should not be locked until the geography/calendar is defined.

Prefer showing passage through ordinary episodes, travel, work, guild jobs, rebuilding, and road/camp sequences rather than relying on a single large explicit time skip.

S1 finale still occurs before the main group receives the full public-news consequences.

S2 can begin with Yuta briefly reacting in terror because he recognizes Sukuna's cursed energy, followed by Sukuna suppressing it. Yuta therefore knows **Sukuna has arrived** before the wider group knows what Sukuna has done.

## Point 7 — World scale — current resolved direction

At S1 rough level, the following is sufficient to move forward:

- medieval/fantasy infrastructure;
- first home = abandoned small settlement / inn near forest + lake;
- mercantile town reachable by an extended but practical scouting/travel route;
- coast not extremely distant from the mercantile region;
- island chain offshore / across the broader lake-coast geography;
- islands are future secure-territory foreshadowing, not current destination;
- roads, caravans, guild work, monster zones, dungeons, trade, money, and local government / merchants exist;
- information propagates at road / ship / merchant speed, not instantly;
- world is large enough that a catastrophic city event can take weeks to materially alter social behavior elsewhere.

Exact map scale, miles/km, political borders, island size, and city populations remain author-bible work rather than blockers for the S1 rough roadmap.

## Point 8 — Power / source snapshots — author knowledge vs character disclosure

The story should **not** force a full power-disclosure meeting in S1 merely because the writers need the information.

Separate:

```text
AUTHOR KNOWLEDGE
= exact source snapshot + arrival capability + current project capability

CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE
= what each person has actually revealed / observed in-story
```

The large mutual power / weakness disclosure pact remains favored for **S2 after the Bocchi social attack**, when the group has a concrete reason to stop hiding dangerous unknowns from one another.

For the author bible, build multidimensional cross-system comparisons rather than one DBZ-style score.

Required dimensions remain:

- Output
- Range
- Precision
- Speed / Reaction
- Defense
- Durability
- Mobility
- Perception / Information
- Hax / Special Authority
- Stamina / Resource Economy
- Recovery
- Collateral Risk
- Matchup Dependency

For each major character track:

```text
SOURCE CAPABILITY
ARRIVAL CAPABILITY
CURRENT PROJECT CAPABILITY
```

Cross-check representative strongest / weakest / specialist cases to calibrate the scale.

Examples like `Hollow Purple vs Frieren's black-hole-like / high-end destructive spell` are **comparison exercises, not equivalence laws**. Compare threat class, mechanics, speed, range, cost, counters, precision, survivability, and collateral rather than declaring two attacks equal because they look similarly destructive.

This author-bible cross-check can be done after the S1 rough story architecture is sealed and before detailed scripting of scenes that depend on exact combat outcomes.

## Point 10 — Sukuna finale — preserved

Favored S1 ending remains extremely short, approximately one final minute if pacing supports it:

- Sukuna awakens in / near a major native city far from the protagonists;
- he detects Yuta's cursed energy;
- reaction in the spirit of: **"So that brat is here."**
- he learns / hears enough to recognize the local `Otherworlder` framing;
- smiles;
- **Domain Expansion: Malevolent Shrine**;
- season cut.

Do **not** lock an exact destruction radius into the story bible until JJK source-canon verification is completed for the chosen Sukuna snapshot.

S2 opening bridge:
- Yuta briefly reacts with genuine terror to Sukuna's CE;
- the signal vanishes / is suppressed;
- Yuta knows Sukuna is here;
- the rest of the group does not yet know the public catastrophe or its coming social consequences.
