## 1. Guild work should be a multi-day lived-in montage, not one checkbox mission

After E16 opens the mercantile town / guild / economy, show G1+G2 actually working across several days.

The tone can borrow the useful rhythm of a comedy-adventure party montage: different jobs, different pairings, small failures, small victories, fatigue, money, food, and the sense that this strange household is becoming functional inside the native world.

Possible jobs / fragments:

- clearing a low-level monster nest;
- gathering herbs / monster materials;
- escorting a merchant wagon for part of a route;
- helping inspect or clear a small known dungeon floor;
- recovering materials from a dangerous area;
- route scouting / mapping;
- a physically annoying but low-danger guild request;
- one task that goes comically worse than expected without becoming a major crisis.

The point is not to make every fragment an action set piece. The montage should communicate **days passing and the group learning how to work in this world's economy**.

End-state:

- they return tired but successful;
- earnings from multiple jobs are pooled;
- Rimuru, because of his engineering / adult-life practicality and comfort with numbers, works out a sensible budget;
- the household divides money among food, repairs, reserves, tools, guild costs, and a small amount of comfort / discretionary spending;
- this directly supports the visual upgrade of the inn/home with better bedding, cookware, glass, rugs, tools, spices, lamps, books, and other refined goods.

This should make the first stable economic phase feel earned rather than magically solved.

## 2. Frieren / Fern overprotection should heal in visible stages during the same work period

Use the guild-work stretch to pay Frieren's post-reunion overprotectiveness gradually rather than with one sudden character change.

Suggested progression:

```text
first mission opportunity
→ Fern wants to go
→ Frieren refuses / does not let her leave

later mission
→ Frieren goes with Fern

later mission
→ Frieren lets Fern go with Yuta, Rimuru, or another trusted capable companion

later still
→ Fern leaves on a normal low-risk task alone
→ Frieren notices
→ consciously lets her go
```

No speech is required.

The final beat should be small. Frieren may visibly want to stop Fern, pause, then choose not to. Fern may notice or may simply leave. The important fact is that Frieren has moved from `I cannot let you out of my sight` toward `I am still afraid, but I trust you to come back.`

This is not full healing and does not erase the trauma of believing Fern dead. It is the correct S1 payoff-sized step.

## 3. Okarun's Momo-specific memory gap remains required in late-S1 G3 material

During G3's late-S1 arrival / merchant-road material, seed that Okarun has a meaningful hole in his personal memory.

Do **not** reveal the full Momo answer yet.

Possible light indications:

- another character asks whether there is anyone he is trying to find and he hesitates because he feels that there should be someone but cannot name them;
- a familiar concept / phrase / object creates discomfort or a near-memory that does not resolve;
- he can remember surrounding parts of his life but an emotionally important connection is inaccessible.

This must remain subtle enough that S2E6 still lands as a reveal.

The later S2 payoff remains:

> **Momo:** "Okarun!"  
> **Okarun:** "Who are you?"

Momo remembers him after her Arrival; Okarun does not remember her.

---

## 4. Why this is useful for the calendar

This late-S1 work period can naturally cover several days without a blunt time card.

It simultaneously shows:

- real world integration;
- economic progress;
- group competence;
- household upgrades;
- changing team pairings;
- Frieren slowly letting Fern regain independence;
- enough ordinary stability for Sukuna's finale to feel like a genuine tonal rupture.

Therefore these beats should be treated as **late-S1 texture / enrichment carrying required continuity payoffs**, not as a separate mandatory macro arc.
