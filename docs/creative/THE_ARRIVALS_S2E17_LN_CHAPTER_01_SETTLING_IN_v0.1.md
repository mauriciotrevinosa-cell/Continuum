The four girls stopped being new in uneven ways.

Hakari became dangerous at dinner.

Karane learned where everyone hid from Hakari.

Shizuka and Bocchi developed conversations made mostly of books, guitar and silence.

Nano began helping Rimuru standardize inventories and discovered the household had three names for the same container.

She fixed this.

Momo resisted.

Progress continued.

Maki and Frieren had reached the stage of friendship where they could insult each other's methods without explanation.

Stark learned the breakfast theft pattern and now guarded his plate.

G3 explained rules to G4.

That was perhaps the cleanest sign of time.

<!-- scene-id: s2e17-ln01-dream2 -->
Mau dreamed it would rain during an outing.

The sky in the dream had been clear until noon.

He remembered Yuta complaining about wet sleeves.

He said nothing that morning because one coincidence was still a coincidence.

At noon it rained.

Yuta returned wet and irritated.

Mau stopped smiling.

Frieren noticed.

"What?"

"I dreamed that."

"Before?"

"Yes."

"Tell Rimuru."

Mau hesitated.

Then nodded.

He did.

No secrecy.

No conclusion.

Rimuru wrote it down.

One dream was coincidence.

Two were data.

<!-- scene-id: s2e17-ln01-maki-frieren -->
Frieren and Maki walked back from gathering kindling.

Maki carried more.

Frieren carried one branch and a book.

"You could help."

"I am."

"You're holding one branch."

"It counts."

Maki sighed.

Then noticed Frieren looking toward the inn.

Toward Mau.

Mau was helping Coco hang a line.

"You do that a lot."

"Do what?"

"Check where he is."

Frieren looked at Maki.

Maki kept walking.

No diagnosis.

No conclusion.

Just observation.

Frieren followed.


<!-- scene-id: s2e17-ln01-choice -->
Later that week, Maki found Yuta and Mau sorting damaged training straps at the same table.

Mau was not sorting.

He was staring at one buckle as though it had asked him a philosophical question.

Maki sat down.

"What?"

Mau looked up.

"If this world can change what people remember. Or what they can do. Or even what state they arrive in."

"Yes?"

"How do you know a feeling is still yours?"

Yuta stopped working.

Maki did not answer immediately.

"Did someone tell you it wasn't?"

"No."

"Then why are you trying to disqualify it?"

Mau frowned.

"That's not what I'm doing."

"It is a little."

Yuta smiled.

Mau looked betrayed.

Maki continued.

"Changed circumstances don't make every choice after them fake. Yuta and I aren't the same people we were at fifteen."

Yuta said, "Thankfully."

Maki ignored him.

"That doesn't mean what we choose now belongs to somebody else."

Mau looked at the buckle again.

"What if you don't know what kind of feeling it is?"

"Then you don't know yet."

"Useful."

"You asked."

Yuta leaned back.

"Family. Romantic. Protective. Attraction. Attachment. They can overlap."

Mau looked at him.

"How do you separate them?"

Yuta opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Maki laughed once.

"Great expert."

"I was going to say it takes time."

"That's because you're stealing my answer."

Mau looked between them.

Maki's expression softened.

"You don't need the right category before you're allowed to care about someone."

"And when I do need the category?"

"Ask what you want to choose with them."

Mau was quiet.

Not solved.

But the question had changed shape.

That was enough.
