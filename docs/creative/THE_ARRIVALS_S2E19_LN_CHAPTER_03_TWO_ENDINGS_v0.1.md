# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 7
## Chapter 3 — Two Endings

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT
**Volume:** 7
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S2E19 — `Ghosts and Choices`

The dreams repeated.

Not identically.

Reliably.

In one branch Mau stayed outside the predicted geometry.

Frieren fell.

In another he moved.

Frieren survived.

Mau did not see what came after for himself.

The details shifted. The choice did not.

He tested smaller predictions.

A knife left in the wrong place.

A phrase Momo would say.

A merchant arriving early.

He changed his own behavior.

The small events bent around him or reached the same result by another route.

Then the battlefield returned.

The more the harmless dreams proved themselves, the less rational it felt to dismiss the dangerous ones.

Frieren's questions did not disappear after one conversation with Maki.

A few days later they were folding dry cloth because Wakana had learned that assigning Frieren a task was easier than asking whether she wanted one.

Frieren said, "I think I wanted more time with Himmel."

Maki folded a sheet.

"That seems obvious."

"I mean when he was alive."

"Still obvious."

"I wanted to understand him."

Maki waited.

Frieren folded one sleeve badly.

Maki fixed it.

"With Mau," Frieren said, "I keep thinking about things that haven't happened yet."

Maki stopped.

Frieren continued.

"The next trip. The next winter. Whether he'll still leave cups in the wrong place if we move."

"Does he leave them in the wrong place?"

"Yes."

"According to who?"

"Me."

Maki almost smiled.

Frieren looked annoyed.

"You're doing the thing."

"What thing?"

"Not answering."

"You're getting closer without me."

Frieren looked down at the cloth.

Past and future.

Understanding what had been lost.

Wanting something that had not happened yet.

Not the same.

Not smaller.

Different.

The problem was that her magic had started reacting to the difference before her language did.

Once, while Mau was outside with Yuta, Frieren felt a violent wrongness near the east line.

She stood.

Staff in hand.

Fern followed.

There was nothing there.

A deer had crossed the perimeter.

Another evening Frieren felt Mau's absence like approaching danger even though he was five minutes away helping Rimuru with storage.

The feeling vanished when he came back.

That was worse.

Her mana sense was not broken.

Her interpretation of it was noisy.

Fear, attachment and actual external information had become too difficult to separate.

Frieren hated unreliable tools.

So she made a deliberate choice.

She stopped actively reaching for that deeper danger-reading layer.

Not forever.

Until she understood what part of the signal was the world and what part was her.

Fern noticed.

"You're trusting us."

Frieren looked at her.

"Temporarily."

Fern smiled.

"Of course."

Ordinary life became cruel because it remained ordinary.

Bocchi and Kita argued about a song.

Momo and Okarun recovered habits faster than complete memories.

Wakana adjusted Stark's clothes.

Coco dragged Agott into work with Qifrey.

Yuta and Maki sat together after training.

Himmel helped repair a roof.

Frieren read with her feet against Mau's leg.

Every person around him had someone.

Under pressure, Mau turned that into arithmetic.

Bocchi had Kita and Fern.

Coco had Qifrey and Agott.

Yuta had Maki.

Frieren had Fern, Stark, Himmel.

Everyone would hurt.

Everyone would continue.

He hated himself for thinking that.

Then thought it again.

One night, Frieren reached for his hand under the table without looking.

Mau held it.

The choice became harder.

It did not change.
