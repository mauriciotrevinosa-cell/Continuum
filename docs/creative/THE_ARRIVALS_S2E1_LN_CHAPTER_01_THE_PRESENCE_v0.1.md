<!-- scene-id: s2e1-ln01-midday -->

At the inn, midday was ordinary.

That was the first problem.

Mau had one of the kitchen windows open because somebody had burned something.

Momo insisted it had not been her.

Maomao said that was technically true.

Anko asked what that meant.

Maomao did not answer.

Across the common room, Frieren had claimed a chair, the end of a bench, and enough of Mau's black-and-orange hoodie to make it difficult to determine whether he still owned it.

He was wearing the red Ferrari shirt instead.

Bocchi was tuning her guitar near the stairs.

Fern was sorting dried herbs Maomao had labeled in handwriting no one else wanted to interpret.

Rimuru had become a slime because, according to Rimuru, sitting normally was "unnecessary right now."

Yuta came through the front door carrying two lengths of salvaged timber.

Then stopped.

One board hit the floor.

The other stayed in his hand because his fingers had locked around it.

Mau looked up first.

"Yuta?"

Yuta did not answer.

For less than a second, the world contained something he knew.

Not a face.

Not a location.

A presence.

Ancient in the way a nightmare could be ancient even if you had only lived through it once.

Malice did not begin to describe it.

Neither did strength.

The cursed energy touched the edge of Yuta's perception from impossibly far away and every part of him that remembered Shinjuku reacted before thought did.

His breath stopped.

Rika stirred.

Not manifested.

Not visible.

Present.

Then the sensation vanished.

Completely.

Yuta stared at nothing.

Mau had already crossed half the room.

"Yuta."

This time the name reached him.

Yuta looked at Mau.

Then at the others.

The room had changed without anyone moving.

Fern's hands were still over the herbs.

Bocchi's fingers had stopped on the strings.

Frieren had closed her book.

Rimuru was human again.

"What happened?" Rimuru asked.

Yuta set the remaining board down carefully.

"Sukuna."

No one spoke.

Mau knew the name.

Not the memory behind it.

The name had lived in Yuta's stories as something that required context around it. A shape large enough that Yuta usually simplified before explaining.

Frieren stood.

"Where?"

"I don't know."

"How far?"

"I don't know."

"You sensed him."

Yuta nodded once.

"For a moment."

"Then?"

"Nothing."

Rimuru's expression had gone still.

"Nothing as in he moved out of range?"

"No."

Yuta hated how certain that answer felt.

"He disappeared."

Momo frowned.

"You mean—"

"His cursed energy."

Yuta looked toward the open window as if distance could become visible through it.

"I felt him. Then he hid it."

Mau watched Yuta's hand.

It was shaking.

Very slightly.

Yuta noticed him noticing and closed it.

That did not help.

Mau had seen Yuta injured.

Had seen him tired.

Had watched him speak about things from his old world with the calm of someone sorting objects after a fire.

This was different.

"What does that mean?" Mau asked.

Yuta took longer than usual.

"It means he's here."

Outside, wind moved through the settlement.

Somewhere beyond the stripped houses, a loose board knocked twice against a wall.

Nobody in the room mistook the sound for anything important.

That almost made it worse.

<!-- scene-id: s2e1-ln01-warning -->

They did not call it a meeting.

Everyone simply ended up at the table.

Maomao came in from the side room because silence in this house was apparently now a warning system.

Anko brought her notebook.

Momo brought food and then forgot to eat it.

Bocchi sat close enough to Fern that their sleeves touched.

Frieren took the seat beside Mau.

Yuta remained standing.

"There are things I should've explained more clearly before," he said.

Mau tilted his head.

"Because you thought they wouldn't matter here."

Yuta looked at him.

"Yeah."

No one made him apologize for that.

Yuta spoke.

Not everything.

Enough.

Sukuna.

Cursed energy.

What it meant that Yuta recognized the presence instantly.

What it meant that Sukuna had once inhabited bodies that were not simply his own.

What he could do when he stopped treating a place as something that should continue existing.

He did not describe Shinjuku in detail.

He did not need to.

"The important part," Yuta said, "is that if I sensed him, there's a chance he sensed me first."

Rimuru leaned forward.

"Could he locate you?"

"I don't know."

"Would he come here?"

"I don't know."

"Can you find him?"

"No."

Rimuru's mouth tightened.

Three answers.

All useless.

Frieren looked at Yuta.

"If he is suppressing his energy, can you detect him at all?"

"Not like this."

"Can Rika?"

Yuta shook his head.

"Not if there's nothing to follow."

Mau looked at the map on the table.

It was the regional map they had brought back after the forgotten road became usable again.

The inn.

The mercantile town.

Roads.

Settlements.

A world that had recently become larger.

Now there was a name somewhere inside it that made Yuta afraid.

"Then what do we do?" Bocchi asked.

The question was quiet.

No one pretended not to hear it.

Yuta looked at her.

"For now?"

He hated the answer before he gave it.

"We don't know where he is."

Rimuru nodded slowly.

"So we cannot chase."

"No."

"We cannot evacuate from a location we don't know he is approaching."

"No."

"We can increase watches."

"Yes."

"We can make sure people don't go alone."

Yuta started to answer.

Frieren did first.

"No."

Everyone looked at her.

She rested one hand against the table.

"If we change everything because something might be anywhere, then it is already controlling the house."

Yuta's expression tightened.

Frieren continued.

"We pay attention."

Her eyes moved to him.

"You tell us if you feel it again."

"I will."

"We do not pretend it isn't real."

"No."

"And we don't make Bocchi sleep in armor."

Bocchi looked down at herself as if checking whether this had been proposed.

Momo raised a hand.

"I wasn't going to make her sleep in armor."

"No one said you were."

"Okay."

Mau looked at Yuta.

"You'll tell us if there's anything else you didn't explain because you thought it wouldn't matter here."

Yuta exhaled.

"Yeah."

"Good."

The meeting ended because there was nothing else to decide.

That was the second problem.

There was a monster somewhere in the world.

They knew his name.

They knew almost nothing else.

And the afternoon still needed dinner.

<!-- scene-id: s2e1-ln01-ordinary -->

Mau picked the fallen timber up.

Yuta reached for the other end.

"You don't have to."

"I know."

They carried it outside together.

Neither mentioned that Yuta kept looking toward the road.

By evening, the open kitchen window had done its job.

The burned smell faded.

Momo finally admitted the pan had been hers.

Maomao corrected her.

"It was your pan. It was my mistake."

Momo put a hand to her chest.

"You're taking responsibility?"

"No. I'm clarifying ownership."

Anko laughed.

Bocchi played for a while after dinner.

Frieren read.

Yuta sat where he could see the door.

Mau noticed.

He did not ask him to stop.

That night, for the first time since arriving in Continuum, Yuta slept with his sword within reach.

Nothing happened.
