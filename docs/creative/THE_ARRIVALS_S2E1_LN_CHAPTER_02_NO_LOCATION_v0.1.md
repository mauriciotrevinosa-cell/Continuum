<!-- scene-id: s2e1-ln02-next-day -->

The next morning proved that terror could become part of breakfast.

Yuta checked the horizon before sitting down.

Nobody commented.

Mau's watch lay beside his cup because he had taken it off during washing and not put it back yet.

The gray face displayed a time with perfect confidence.

Continuum had never agreed to care.

Frieren reached across him for bread.

The sleeve of his hoodie slid down her wrist.

"That's still mine," Mau said.

"I know."

"You've had it for three days."

"It's cold."

"You have cold-weather clothes."

Frieren took the bread.

"These are closer."

Fern did not look up from her tea.

"Your clothing stopped being yours months ago."

Mau considered that.

"Some of it."

"Most of it."

Frieren looked at him.

"Mostly."

Mau stared at her.

She returned to the bread.

Yuta laughed once.

It surprised him enough that he stopped.

Mau looked over.

"Better?"

"No."

"Okay."

It was still better than silence.

<!-- scene-id: s2e1-ln02-search -->

Yuta spent the morning trying to prove that yesterday had happened.

Rimuru helped.

They tried distance.

Direction.

Residual cursed energy.

Rika's perception.

Rimuru's analysis.

Raphael produced careful statements about insufficient data.

The result was the same every time.

Nothing.

Not zero.

Nothing to measure.

Yuta hated that distinction.

By noon, he had walked the settlement perimeter twice.

Mau found him near the stripped shell of what had once been a house.

"You know he's not hiding behind that wall."

Yuta looked at the wall.

Half of it was missing.

"Probably."

"Good."

Mau leaned against a beam.

Yuta looked at him.

"Are you checking on me?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"You dropped wood yesterday."

Yuta waited.

"That's the threshold?"

"It was unusual."

Yuta looked toward the forest.

"He knows my cursed energy."

"You said that."

"If he wants me, he could come here."

"He could."

"If he comes here—"

"We'll deal with that when he comes here."

Yuta frowned.

"You sound like Frieren."

"That's concerning."

"It should be."

Mau looked at the road.

"Do you think he's coming?"

"I don't know."

Mau nodded.

Yuta's patience broke a little.

"That doesn't bother you?"

"It bothers me."

"You don't look bothered."

"I also don't know what direction to look."

Yuta stared at him.

Then laughed despite himself.

Mau watched.

"Better?"

"A little."

"Okay."

They went back inside.

<!-- scene-id: s2e1-ln02-g3-road -->

Far from the inn, the road had become the whole world for eight people who did not yet know they were traveling toward it.

The merchant's wagon moved badly over a section of old stone.

Umaru had decided that the bumping was a personal attack.

"This vehicle is hostile."

"It's a wagon," Kita said.

"Exactly."

Okarun sat across from them, quiet.

He had been quiet more often since arriving.

Not frightened in the same way Kita was frightened.

Not homesick in the same way Umaru was pretending not to be.

There was simply a blank place inside him he kept discovering from different angles.

Kita held a cup with both hands.

"Do you think there are really other people like us?"

The merchant had used the word.

Otherworlders.

Not with fear.

Not yet.

"There are stories," he had said.

Stories were better than nothing.

Umaru pulled her borrowed blanket higher.

"I would settle for someone with electricity."

Kita smiled.

Okarun looked out at the road.

A different wagon traveled farther ahead.

Marin's voice carried from it even when the distance between wheels widened.

Wakana answered more quietly.

Coco leaned out until Qifrey pulled her back by the shoulder.

Mikasa walked part of the route rather than ride.

She said it helped her understand the terrain.

Umaru said that sounded exhausting.

No one knew where home was.

So, for now, they followed the person who knew the road.

<!-- scene-id: s2e1-ln02-night -->

At the inn, the second night passed.

Then a third.

Yuta sensed nothing.

He began sleeping slightly farther from his sword.

Not far.

Slightly.

Ordinary work resumed because ordinary work had never stopped.

Frieren and Mau took the east route to check an old marker.

Fern helped Maomao.

Bocchi played while Anko wrote.

Momo accused Rimuru of hiding secrets because he had been a slime for forty minutes.

Rimuru pointed out that he was asleep.

"Suspicious," Momo said.

Yuta carried timber.

This time he did not drop it.

Somewhere in the same world, Sukuna remained impossible to locate.

The threat had not gone away.

It had simply stopped giving them anything to do about it.

That was how normal life returned.
