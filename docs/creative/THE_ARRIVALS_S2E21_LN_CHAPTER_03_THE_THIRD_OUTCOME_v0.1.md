# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 7
## Chapter 10 — The Third Outcome

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT
**Volume:** 7
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S2E21 — `One Week`

On the fifth day, Mau formed Blue long enough for everyone involved to stop breathing.

It appeared around his left hand like space had forgotten how to stay where it was.

Unstable.

Hungry.

Wrong.

Mau released it before it consumed more than the air around him.

His hand shook.

Yuta smiled despite everything.

"Again tomorrow."

Mau looked at him.

"Not now?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because I'm trying to keep you alive."

The sentence landed between them.

Mau looked away first.

Red remained theoretical until evening.

Raphael had helped Rimuru isolate a pattern Mau could understand.

Not reproduce.

Understand.

Sometimes that was enough for Construction to notice.

The requirement list grew.

Knowledge.

Output.

Opposing vectors.

A moment of convergence.

An external target large enough to justify the cost.

One use.

Maybe.

"Plan C," Rimuru said.

Yuta hated the name.

Mau liked that it sounded ordinary.

They still did not know whether it would save him.

That was why Yuta kept training RCT.

That evening Rimuru put the draft Contract on the table.

No one touched it.

Yuta read it first.

Then again.

"I hate this."

"So do I," Rimuru said.

Mau did not say anything.

The Contract was narrow on purpose.

If Qifrey's prepared field trigger activated at the predicted decisive geometry, Rimuru would not physically interfere with Mau's attempt to reach Frieren.

Instead Rimuru would commit to the barrier and casualty-containment role they had already designed.

It did not prevent Rimuru from arguing before the moment.

It did not prevent him from searching for another answer.

It did not require him to accept Mau's death.

It existed only for the instant in which everyone might panic and destroy the only plan they had.

Yuta pushed the page away.

"No."

Mau looked at him.

"We need Rimuru to hold the barrier."

"We need you alive."

"Both can be true."

"Not if this exists."

Rimuru had been staring at Mau.

"Why are you asking me?"

Mau's answer came too quickly.

"Because you'll stop me."

"Correct."

Mau looked down.

For several seconds nothing moved.

Then he said, "Frieren is the first person I remember."

Rimuru's expression changed.

Mau continued because stopping would make starting again impossible.

"I don't remember parents. A home. School. Friends. Anything before her."

Yuta went still.

"She was just there when I opened my eyes. She gave me somewhere to stand. Then a name. Then a room stopped feeling temporary."

His voice tightened.

"I know surviving matters."

Nobody interrupted.

"But if the branch is me standing there afterward and she's gone—"

Mau stopped.

Tried again.

"I don't know what that version of surviving means."

Rimuru closed his eyes.

That was the weakness.

Not stupidity.

Not coercion.

Understanding.

He understood that Mau was not saying he wanted to die.

Mau was saying the life he knew how to continue had been built around the first person who made it real.

Rimuru hated that he understood.

"You're asking me to let you choose the hit."

"If the exact moment comes."

"And then trust Yuta."

"Yes."

"And do my job instead of grabbing you."

"Yes."

Rimuru looked at Yuta.

Yuta's face said no.

His preparation said he already knew why the question existed.

Rimuru looked back at Mau.

"I am still trying to find another answer until the last possible second."

"So am I."

"If the trigger is wrong, this does not activate."

"Agreed."

"If circumstances change enough that the predicted geometry is no longer the same, this does not activate."

"Agreed."

"If it activates, I hold the barrier."

Mau swallowed.

"Yes."

"And I hate you for asking."

Mau almost smiled.

"Fair."

Rimuru placed his hand over the Contract.

Voluntary.

Understood.

Specific.

Mau did the same.

The agreement settled.

Raphael's assessment arrived immediately.

**Forced violation would trigger Contract-specific enforcement. Severe loss of function is plausible. Barrier continuity could be compromised.**

Rimuru opened his eyes.

"Great."

Yuta looked at him.

"What?"

"If I try to break it at the worst moment, I may take myself out and drop the barrier."

Yuta swore.

Mau said nothing.

There was nothing useful to say.

Rimuru folded the page.

"We are going to make sure this never matters."

That was why the festival happening two nights later suddenly mattered too much.

Mau returned inside.

Frieren was asleep in the common room with a book open on her chest.

He took the book away and covered her with his hoodie.

Halfway upstairs, Frieren woke and looked down.

"This is yours."

Mau smiled.

"Currently."

Frieren stared at him.

Then smiled too.

The joke had traveled far enough to come home.
