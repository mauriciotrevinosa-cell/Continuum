# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 9
## Chapter 6 — Different Shapes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT
**Volume:** 9
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S2E29 — `Trying to Understand Love`

Mau learned love by watching other people.

He had been doing that since he arrived.

Bocchi and Kita made the relationship explicit after already caring.

Yuta and Maki had lived an entire lifetime and still chose each other again.

Marin and Wakana carried affection through work.

Momo and Okarun proved memory loss could injure a relationship without proving the relationship had been imaginary.

Himmel loved Frieren without demanding that she build her future around him.

Ori complicated all of it.

Mau loved being around her.

He worried when she disappeared from a room.

He wanted her safe.

He liked seeing her laugh.

If she left the household, the absence would hurt.

Those statements sounded familiar.

That was the problem.

He found Yuta and Maki outside.

"Can you love two people?"

Maki looked at Yuta.

Yuta looked at Maki.

Mau added, "Not necessarily the same way."

Maki relaxed.

"Obviously."

Mau waited.

Yuta understood.

"You're asking how you tell the difference."

"Yes."

Maki sat on the low wall.

"You don't classify feelings by intensity."

Mau frowned.

"If I would risk my life for someone—"

"That proves you're you," Maki said. "Not that you're romantically in love with them."

Yuta almost laughed.

Mau stared.

Maki continued.

"Family can be enormous. Friendship can be enormous. Grief can be enormous. Protectiveness can be unhealthy and still enormous."

"So what's romance?"

"Choice plus desire plus the kind of future you want with that person."

"That's vague."

"Welcome to being human."

Mau looked toward the inn.

Frieren was visible through the window.

Ori sat somewhere deeper in the room with Coco.

Two people he loved.

Different futures formed around them without effort.

With Ori, Mau wanted her to become secure enough not to need him.

With Frieren, the thought of a future where she no longer needed him did not make him want to leave.

He wanted to remain.

Not as necessary.

As chosen.

The distinction arrived quietly.

Mau did not say it aloud yet.

But for the first time, he knew where the missing words were pointing.
