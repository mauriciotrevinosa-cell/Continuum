The first story said the city had been attacked by a dragon.

The second said there had been an earthquake.

The third said the city had offended a god.

The fourth used the word Otherworlder.

That one stayed.

Rimuru heard it from a merchant who had once sold them lamp oil without caring where they came from.

The man still sold them the oil.

He simply did not invite Rimuru to stay for tea.

That was new.

"What happened?" Rimuru asked.

The merchant looked toward the road before answering.

"Depends who you ask."

"I am asking you."

"I wasn't there."

"Then tell me what reached you."

A city to the south and east.

The nearest large regional city connected to the same trade network.

Buildings cut apart.

Streets gone.

People missing.

Survivors who could not agree on what they had seen because there had not been enough time to see it.

One traveler swore the attacker had appeared from nowhere.

Another claimed soldiers had brought an Otherworlder into the city.

Another said the word itself was nonsense used by frightened people who needed a category.

The merchant rubbed one thumb against the edge of the counter.

"They're saying one of you did it."

Rimuru's expression did not change.

"One of us meaning?"

"You know."

"No. Say it."

The man looked uncomfortable.

"An Otherworlder."

"Do they know that?"

"No."

"Do you?"

"No."

"Good."

Rimuru paid for the lamp oil.

The merchant counted the coins twice.

Not because Rimuru had shorted him.

Because neither of them knew what else to do with the silence.

Before leaving, Rimuru said, "If you hear anything more specific, send word."

The merchant nodded.

Then, after Rimuru had turned away:

"Be careful."

Rimuru looked back.

The man was already pretending to reorganize a shelf.

Fear had not erased every relationship.

It had changed the shape of them.

<!-- scene-id: s2e2-ln01-home -->

Back at the inn, the story split into six versions before dinner.

Momo preferred the dragon version.

"That one is least likely to become our problem."

Yuta looked at her.

"It is our problem."

"You know what I mean."

"I do."

Bocchi sat near the end of the table, listening.

Anko had written the known facts in one column and rumors in another.

The rumor column was winning.

Frieren read it upside down from across the table.

"God is unlikely."

Mau looked at her.

"Why?"

"Gods usually want people to know."

No one knew what to do with that.

Maomao tapped one line.

"People cut apart without understanding how."

Yuta did not answer.

Mau did.

"That sounds like what Yuta described."

"Yes."

The table got quieter.

Yuta stared at the paper.

He had not sensed Sukuna again.

That fact no longer felt reassuring.

Fern folded her arms.

"If the city was destroyed when Yuta sensed him, then the timing matches."

"Probably," Yuta said.

"Probably is enough to be careful."

"It isn't enough to find him."

"No one said it was."

Frieren's hand rested near Mau's cup.

Not on him.

Near enough that the distance looked practiced.

"What matters here," she said, "is what people believe."

Rimuru nodded.

"The word is spreading faster than the facts."

"Words usually do."

Momo raised a hand.

"Can we replace Otherworlder with something cuter?"

"No," three people said.

"Okay."

<!-- scene-id: s2e2-ln01-road-rumors -->

G3 heard the catastrophe as a campfire story.

The merchant driving them had stopped beside another small caravan for the night.

Food became information because roads worked that way.

A trader with a split lip said half a city had vanished.

A woman beside him said that was exaggeration.

A third person said the attacker had been an Otherworlder.

Coco looked toward Qifrey.

Marin stopped talking.

Kita's expression tightened.

Umaru pulled her hood farther forward.

Okarun asked, "What's an Otherworlder?"

The trader looked at him.

Then at the rest of the wagon.

The silence answered before the merchant did.

"People who came from somewhere else," their merchant said evenly.

"Like us?" Coco asked.

The trader's face changed.

Qifrey noticed.

So did Mikasa.

Their merchant sighed.

"Like you."

Nobody moved for several seconds.

Then Marin asked, "Did an Otherworlder actually do it?"

"No one here knows."

That answer mattered.

It did not remove the fear.

It put a boundary around it.

Later, when the other caravan had gone quiet, Kita whispered:

"If they're scared of people like us, where are we going?"

The merchant looked into the fire.

"Somewhere I heard people like you are already living."

"Is that safe?"

He smiled without humor.

"Safer than walking into the city I'm taking you around."

For the first time, the road ahead had a destination.

Not a place any of them knew.

A rumor about a ruined settlement near a lake.

A house full of strangers from other worlds.

It was not much.

It was more than they had yesterday.
