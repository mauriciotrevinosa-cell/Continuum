The word had not changed.

People had.

Otherworlder.

Two weeks earlier it had meant a strange traveler with impossible clothing.

Someone who asked the wrong questions about money.

Someone who did not know a road everyone else knew.

Someone who might have a useful story.

Now, sometimes, it meant danger.

Not always.

That was worse in a different way.

Mau noticed because a baker who normally complained about his order being too large packed it without conversation.

An older woman who sold dried fruit still asked how Frieren was doing.

A guild clerk stared at Yuta's sword longer than usual.

A child pointed at Rimuru and was immediately pulled away by his father.

The father apologized.

To the child.

Not to Rimuru.

Mau remembered all of it.

He did not know what to do with any of it.

On the walk back, Fern said, "You're counting."

Mau looked at her.

"Counting what?"

"People."

"I wasn't."

"You were."

He thought about it.

"Maybe."

Fern adjusted the strap of the bag she carried.

"Stop."

"Why?"

"Because then you'll decide six people looked at us badly and four didn't, and you'll think that means something useful."

Mau considered this.

"That's exactly what I was doing."

"I know."

"Is it not useful?"

"No."

"Okay."

He stopped counting.

He still noticed.

<!-- scene-id: s2e2-ln02-small-kindness -->

At the edge of town, someone called after them.

Mau turned.

The lamp-oil merchant's apprentice jogged across the road carrying a wrapped package.

"You forgot this."

Rimuru frowned.

"I didn't buy anything else."

"I know."

The apprentice pushed it toward him.

Inside was lamp wick.

More than they had paid for.

"My boss said the last batch was bad."

"It wasn't."

"Yeah."

Rimuru looked toward the shop.

The merchant was visible through the window.

He did not wave.

Rimuru closed the package.

"Tell him thank you."

The apprentice nodded and ran back.

Mau watched him go.

Fern said nothing.

This did not cancel the father who pulled his child away.

It did not cancel the guild clerk.

It did not prove coexistence would be fine.

It was simply another true thing.

The world had not chosen one feeling.

<!-- scene-id: s2e2-ln02-home-talk -->

That evening, the inn sounded normal enough to make the conversation feel misplaced.

Bocchi's guitar drifted from upstairs.

Maomao was crushing something medicinal.

Momo and Anko were arguing over whether one could investigate a rumor without leaving the common room.

Frieren wore Mau's Ferrari shirt because it had ended up in the clean pile and, according to her, the distinction between piles had become inefficient.

Mau had decided not to contest this.

Rimuru put the extra lamp wick on the table.

"We need to talk about the town."

Frieren looked up from her book.

"We already did."

"We need to do it again."

"That's less efficient."

"Yes."

Mau sat beside her.

Rimuru spread the regional map.

"Nothing is immediately broken. We can still buy supplies. The guild isn't refusing us work. We still have people there who trust us."

"Some," Fern said.

"Some."

Yuta leaned against the wall.

"And some don't."

"Yes."

Rimuru tapped the road between the inn and town.

"I'm not saying we stop going."

Frieren turned a page.

"I'm saying we should."

Everyone looked at her.

She noticed eventually.

"What?"

Rimuru rested both hands on the table.

"If we vanish the moment people become afraid, we confirm every story that says we're dangerous."

"If we keep sending people somewhere they aren't wanted, we create friction for no reason."

"Those are not the only options."

"They usually are."

"No, Frieren."

Her eyes lifted.

Rimuru's voice stayed calm.

"We have relationships there."

"So?"

"So people are allowed to be afraid and still know us."

Frieren considered him.

That was not the answer she expected.

Rimuru continued.

"We reduce unnecessary trips. We don't pretend nothing changed. We also don't abandon every person who hasn't abandoned us."

Mau looked at the map.

"What if being connected to us puts them in danger?"

No one answered immediately.

That question had been sitting underneath the others.

The lamp-oil merchant.

The guild clerk.

The people who still spoke to them.

If something followed the household, proximity could become a cost.

Rimuru exhaled.

"Then we may eventually need a place farther from other people."

The room went quiet.

The inn had been a solution for so long that nobody liked hearing it described as temporary.

Frieren looked around the common room.

Repaired beams.

Bocchi's music overhead.

Mau's cap on a peg where it had no reason to be.

Fern's cup.

Maomao's labels.

Momo's notes.

Rimuru's ridiculous stack of ledgers.

Home.

"We're not leaving now," Mau said.

"No," Rimuru said.

"But we start looking."

"Maybe."

Frieren closed her book.

"For what?"

Rimuru looked down at the map.

"Somewhere that can hold more people."

Momo leaned over the table.

"How many more?"

No one knew.

That was part of the problem.

Outside, the settlement remained quiet.

The inn had begun as a place two people could survive.

Now nine people lived there.

The world had already shown them that eight was not necessarily the final number.
