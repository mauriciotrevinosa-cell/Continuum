<!-- scene-id: s2e31-ln02-map -->

The future home was chosen without anyone saying the word chosen.

That was how most important things happened in the house.

The latest site report arrived after dinner.

Not from a dramatic expedition.

Not with a discovery.

Just paper.

Water remained reliable.

The soil was workable.

The north route needed clearing but not rebuilding.

The second approach was slower, but usable.

The slope above the stream could hold the first permanent structures without fighting floodwater.

Winter light was better than the failed valley.

No new problem had appeared large enough to disqualify it.

Rimuru spread the notes across the table.

Everyone who cared came over.

Which meant everyone.

Momo read one line upside down.

Marin leaned over Wakana.

Coco tried to see through Qifrey.

Stark stood behind Fern.

Himmel moved a chair for Frieren without being asked.

Mau sat at the end of the table.

Ori sat two seats away.

The distance was intentional.

It no longer felt like exile.

Rimuru tapped the map.

"We can keep looking."

Nobody answered.

He waited.

"We should keep verifying routes either way."

Still nothing.

Mau looked around.

"Is there a reason not to use this one?"

Maomao pointed at one note.

"Medical plants are less convenient."

"Solvable?" Rimuru asked.

"Annoying."

"That isn't what I asked."

Maomao looked at him.

"Solvable."

Wakana pointed at another.

"Weather exposure on the west side."

"Solvable."

Mikasa said, "The northern ridge needs a better watch point."

Yuta looked at it.

"Solvable."

One by one, the problems stopped being reasons to leave.

They became work.

That was the difference.

<!-- scene-id: s2e31-ln02-ori -->

Ori had added a note days earlier.

A question about a room.

She saw it now.

Her handwriting in the margin.

She almost covered it.

Frieren noticed.

"Leave it."

Ori looked at her.

"It's embarrassing."

"Why?"

"I wrote it before—"

She stopped.

Before Mau rejected her.

Before she knew whether staying would feel possible.

Frieren looked at the note.

Then at Ori.

"Do you still want a window there?"

Ori hesitated.

"Yes."

"Then leave it."

Ori did.

Across the table, Mau heard none of this.

He was arguing with Rimuru about whether "enough room" counted as a measurable construction requirement.

It did not.

Mau believed it should.

Ori watched for a moment.

The hurt existed.

So did the room she wanted.

Both could be true.

<!-- scene-id: s2e31-ln02-first-things -->

The conversation changed from whether to build there to what had to exist first.

Water.

Shelter.

Kitchen.

Bathing.

Storage.

Workshop space.

A proper medical room.

Defensive visibility.

A music room eventually.

Bocchi looked up.

"Eventually?"

Rimuru looked at the list.

Bocchi stared.

Rimuru sighed and moved it higher.

Kita smiled.

Wakana asked about a workshop.

Marin asked about windows.

Maomao asked whether anyone had learned anything from the current storage disaster.

Everyone avoided eye contact.

Mau wrote:

`arrival room?`

Rimuru read it.

"What does that mean?"

"If someone new comes."

The table quieted.

Not fear.

Recognition.

There would be someone new eventually.

Maybe one.

Maybe ten.

Maybe someone they knew.

Maybe someone nobody knew.

Rimuru added it to the list.

"Temporary housing first."

Mau nodded.

Frieren looked at the map.

"We're building another inn."

Rimuru frowned.

"No."

Frieren looked around the table.

"That is what this became."

Nobody had a good counterargument.

<!-- scene-id: s2e31-ln02-not-yet -->

Nothing moved that night.

No boxes were packed.

No one chose a departure date.

The inn remained home.

The future remained paper.

After everyone left the table, Mau stayed behind.

Frieren came back for her book.

The map was still open.

Mau looked at the chosen site.

"Do you want to live there?"

Frieren picked up the book.

"With everyone?"

"Yes."

"Then yes."

Mau looked at her.

"That was easy."

Frieren opened the book.

"It wasn't the difficult part."

"What was?"

Frieren looked around the common room.

The repaired walls.

The chair she always used.

The table that carried too many marks.

The stairs.

Everything.

"Leaving this."

Mau followed her gaze.

"Me too."

They did not solve that.

Not tonight.

Frieren walked upstairs.

Mau folded the map carefully.

The next home had finally become real enough to make leaving the first one hurt.
