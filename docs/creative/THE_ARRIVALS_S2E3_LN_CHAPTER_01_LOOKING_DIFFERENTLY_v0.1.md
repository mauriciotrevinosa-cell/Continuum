<!-- scene-id: s2e3-ln01-guild -->

The guild did not refuse them work.

It simply stopped giving them the work that involved staying inside town longer than necessary.

Yuta noticed on the third assignment.

Anko noticed on the second.

Mau noticed when the clerk stopped asking whether they wanted anything else.

Rimuru noticed before any of them and said nothing until they were back outside.

"They're routing us outward."

Yuta looked at the paper.

"Monster trail. East ridge."

"Yesterday was timber escort."

"Also outside."

"The day before that was road debris."

Anko folded her copy of the assignment.

"Outside."

Mau looked back through the guild window.

The clerk was helping someone else.

No staring.

No argument.

No sign that anything had happened.

"Maybe they think we're better at outside jobs."

Anko looked at him.

"That's adorable."

"Is it wrong?"

"Probably."

Yuta read the assignment again.

"They're still paying us."

Rimuru nodded.

"Which matters."

Nobody wanted the easy answer where the town had become an enemy.

Reality was more irritating.

The guild still needed them.

Some people still trusted them.

Some were afraid.

Some were afraid and trusted them anyway.

Some had decided distance was safer than deciding anything.

Mau folded the paper.

"So we take the job."

"Yes," Rimuru said.

"We don't make this a fight."

"No."

"We also notice."

"Exactly."

Anko smiled.

"He's learning politics."

Mau frowned.

"I don't like it."

"That's also politics."

<!-- scene-id: s2e3-ln01-house -->

At home, the future began appearing on scraps of paper.

Not plans.

Questions.

Water.

Distance from roads.

Distance from towns.

Defensibility.

Space.

Soil.

Travel time.

Trade access.

A place for Maomao to grow things without threatening to poison the entire household.

Maomao objected to the wording.

Momo added it back after she left.

The map lived on one end of the table.

Nobody treated it as urgent.

That was how serious things entered the inn.

Quietly.

Frieren would move a cup and expose a route line.

Fern would add a note after a work trip.

Rimuru would complain that nobody wrote scale.

Mau would draw something, then realize he had no idea how large a settlement for ten people should be.

"Eight," Bocchi corrected once.

Mau looked around.

"Right."

Momo leaned over his shoulder.

"You were planning for ten?"

"I forgot how many of us there are."

"That's concerning."

"Two more seems plausible."

Everyone stopped for half a second.

Then resumed what they were doing.

The possibility of more arrivals was no longer abstract.

It was arithmetic.

<!-- scene-id: s2e3-ln01-ordinary -->

The house continued.

That mattered.

Frieren finished one grimoire and began another.

Mau repaired the same hinge twice because the wood around it was the actual problem.

Fern and Bocchi developed a system where Bocchi could practice in the music room while Fern studied in the next room without either pretending they could not hear the other.

Momo and Maomao turned one shelf into a territorial dispute.

Anko started leaving her notebook on the same chair every night.

Yuta went three full days without checking the horizon during breakfast.

Rimuru stopped pretending the storage problem would solve itself.

One evening it rained.

Then it kept raining.

The road softened.

The lake darkened.

The whole settlement seemed to pull inward toward the inn.

Mau stood at the doorway watching water come off the roof.

Frieren appeared beside him wearing her own cold-weather layer this time.

He noticed.

She noticed him noticing.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You were looking at my clothes."

"You're wearing yours."

"Yes."

"Okay."

Frieren looked down at herself.

Then at him.

"You can have the hoodie tomorrow."

"I wasn't asking."

"Mostly."

Mau smiled.

The rain got harder.

Behind them, Momo shouted that the ceiling was leaking.

Rimuru shouted back that it was not the ceiling, it was a window.

Maomao shouted that this distinction did not improve the floor.

Mau closed the door.

For one night, the problem outside was only weather.
