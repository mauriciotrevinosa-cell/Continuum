<!-- scene-id: s2e3-ln02-space -->

The inn had more rooms than people.

That did not mean it had enough room.

Rimuru proved this with a measuring cord.

Momo disputed the cord.

"The building is huge."

"The building is old."

"Same thing."

"No."

Rimuru walked them through the second floor.

One room had a wall that still needed reinforcement.

Another had become storage because the floor was reliable and storage had to exist somewhere.

The music room was not available because Bocchi looked physically ill when Momo suggested it.

Maomao had claimed enough workspace that everyone had collectively stopped asking which room technically belonged to her.

Fern's room functioned as Fern's room.

Anko's room functioned as Anko's room and, increasingly, the place Momo entered without knocking when she wanted to ask a question.

Mau's nominal room had long since become more study than bedroom.

His actual life remained mixed into Frieren's room.

Rimuru stopped in the hallway.

"If four more people arrived tomorrow, where would they sleep?"

Momo pointed at the common room.

"On the floor."

"If eight arrived?"

Momo stopped pointing.

Mau leaned against the wall.

"Common room plus floor."

Rimuru looked at him.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

"That wasn't praise."

"I know."

Frieren walked past them carrying a book.

"We can repair another house."

Rimuru shook his head.

"We've taken too much usable material from the nearest ones."

Frieren stopped.

That was true.

The settlement around the inn had been shrinking all season without anyone naming it that way.

Doors.

Boards.

Stone.

Roof pieces.

Hardware.

Anything salvageable had migrated inward.

They had made the inn stronger by making the surrounding ruins less recoverable.

Frieren looked through the hallway window at the stripped structures outside.

"We could go farther."

"We can," Rimuru said. "But then we're maintaining multiple buildings across the settlement. Heating them. Watching them. Repairing routes between them."

Mau looked at the wall.

"So the inn worked."

Rimuru blinked.

"What?"

"It worked too well."

The others looked at him.

Mau shrugged.

"We keep fixing it. People keep fitting. Then more people might come."

Frieren's eyes moved toward him.

"The problem isn't that this is a bad home."

"No."

"It's that it may become too small."

"Yes."

She looked out the window again.

The distinction mattered.

<!-- scene-id: s2e3-ln02-map -->

That night, the map gained another page.

Not because anyone had decided to leave.

Because the question had become real.

Rimuru wrote criteria.

Fern corrected his handwriting.

Yuta marked routes that had defensible terrain.

Anko added places they had heard rumors about but not verified.

Maomao drew a circle around water sources.

Momo drew a larger circle around "places with snacks."

Rimuru crossed it out.

Momo redrew it.

Bocchi sat nearby with her guitar across her knees.

She had not said much.

Mau noticed.

"Do you want to leave?"

Everyone looked at him.

Bocchi froze.

Mau realized too late that asking Bocchi a question in front of everyone remained one of the fastest ways to convert her into furniture.

He tried again.

"Not now."

Bocchi breathed.

"Later."

She looked at the room.

The repaired beams.

The fire.

Fern's books.

Frieren's chair.

The corner where Mau usually left his cap.

Her music upstairs.

"...I don't know."

"Okay."

"I like it here."

"So do I."

"Then why—"

She stopped.

Rimuru answered gently.

"Because liking somewhere doesn't make it safe forever."

Bocchi looked at the map.

"Would we all go?"

There it was.

Not the building.

The question underneath it.

Frieren answered.

"Yes."

Bocchi looked at her.

"If we go."

Fern added, "We're not splitting because the house got crowded."

Momo leaned across the table.

"Unless Maomao poisons us."

"I can hear you."

"See? Threat confirmed."

The tension broke.

Bocchi's shoulders lowered.

The map stayed on the table after everyone went to bed.

A mug rested on one corner.

Mau's watch sat beside it.

Nothing had been chosen.

The possibility was now part of the room.
