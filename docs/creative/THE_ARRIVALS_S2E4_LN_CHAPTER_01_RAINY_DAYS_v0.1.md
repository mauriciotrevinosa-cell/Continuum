<!-- scene-id: s2e4-ln01-rain-one -->

Rain removed choice from the schedule.

No scouting.

No long guild route.

No lake trip.

No argument about whether the east path was muddy because the east path had become mud.

Everyone stayed home.

On the first day, this was pleasant.

Frieren read in the common room with her feet tucked under Mau's leg because he had made the mistake of sitting first.

Mau read too.

Not a grimoire.

One of Yuta's copied notes on local trade routes.

He had made it through the same paragraph four times.

"You're heavy," he said.

Frieren turned a page.

"No."

"That's not a response."

"It is."

Across the room, Bocchi practiced quietly.

Fern worked through Maomao's labels.

Anko wrote.

Momo tried to teach Rimuru a card game and changed the rules twice.

Rimuru accused her of cheating.

Momo accused him of being unable to handle innovation.

Maomao fell asleep at the table.

Yuta covered her notes before a leak could reach them.

Nobody noticed until Maomao woke and accused him of rearranging her work.

He had moved it eight centimeters.

The argument lasted ten minutes.

<!-- scene-id: s2e4-ln01-rain-two -->

On the second day, everyone became worse.

Mau repaired a stool that did not need repairing.

Fern reorganized a shelf and discovered Momo had been storing snacks behind medical reference material.

Momo insisted this was strategic.

Bocchi attempted to spend six consecutive hours in the music room.

Frieren found her after four.

"You need food."

Bocchi looked at the door.

Frieren held out a plate.

Bocchi took it.

"Thank you."

Frieren stayed.

Bocchi waited.

Frieren looked at the guitar.

"Play something."

Bocchi nearly dropped the plate.

Downstairs, Mau heard the first notes and looked toward the ceiling.

Anko looked too.

"That's new."

"What?"

"Frieren asking."

Mau smiled.

"Mostly."

Anko frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"Nothing."

"That means something."

"It used to."

"That's worse."

<!-- scene-id: s2e4-ln01-clothes -->

Laundry became a geopolitical crisis.

The rain refused to let anything dry properly.

Mau found Frieren wearing the navy knitted polo from his backpack.

It hung differently on her than the Ferrari shirt.

She had rolled the sleeves.

"You found another one."

"My clothes are wet."

"You have more clothes."

"They're also wet."

"All of them?"

Frieren looked at him.

Mau stopped.

"Right."

Rimuru passed carrying a bundle.

"I am begging both of you to stop making this sound like a mystery. We have twelve square meters of drying space and eight people."

Mau looked at the line of damp clothing near the hearth.

"Can we make more drying space?"

"Yes."

"Then why are we arguing?"

Rimuru stared at him.

"Because I have been asking for help for an hour."

"Oh."

Mau stood.

Frieren continued reading.

Rimuru pointed at her.

"You too."

Frieren looked at the book.

Then at the wet clothes.

"I'll supervise."

"No."

She sighed and stood.

By evening, a new indoor drying rack existed.

It was ugly.

It worked.

Mau's Ferrari shirt was on it.

Frieren's winter layer was beside it.

Fern's socks occupied an amount of space Fern considered reasonable and everyone else considered militarily precise.

The house smelled like damp wool, food and wood smoke.

It was not romantic.

It was home.

<!-- scene-id: s2e4-ln01-night -->

That night, thunder rolled over the lake.

Bocchi came downstairs carrying her blanket.

No one asked why.

Fern moved over.

Momo moved over.

Rimuru, in slime form, was moved without consent.

Mau ended up on the floor because all available furniture had been claimed.

Frieren looked down at him from the bench.

"You can sit here."

"There is no space."

Frieren shifted two centimeters.

"There."

Mau stared.

Bocchi hid a smile in her blanket.

Yuta turned away so nobody could prove he had seen anything.

Mau sat.

It was not enough space.

Frieren leaned against him anyway.

Outside, rain hit the windows.

Inside, the world became very small.

For a while, that was enough.
