<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln01-road -->

Bocchi almost changed her mind three times.

The first was at the door.

The second was at the edge of the settlement.

The third was when the mercantile town became visible through the trees.

Mau noticed all three.

He said nothing.

That helped.

The McLaren cap Momo had placed on Bocchi the night before was still on her head.

Bocchi had tried to return it at breakfast.

Mau had told her it was useful because the sky was bright after several days of rain.

This was not technically an argument.

Bocchi had still lost.

She walked beside him carrying one empty bag.

Mau carried the others.

"You said I was helping."

"You are."

"I'm carrying one."

"Yes."

"You have four."

"Yes."

Bocchi looked at him.

Mau looked back.

She reached for another bag.

He gave it to her.

No speech.

No praise.

That helped too.

<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln01-market -->

The town smelled different after rain.

Wet stone.

Wood.

Bread.

Mud.

The market had returned to ordinary noise.

For ten minutes, Bocchi almost believed the last weeks had been exaggerated by everyone else's anxiety.

The baker sold them two loaves and complained about how quickly eight people consumed bread.

Mau told him this was not new information.

The baker told him to stop bringing facts into a complaint.

Bocchi smiled.

At the stationery stall, Anko's ink cost more than expected.

At the apothecary, Maomao's third ingredient had a name that became less pronounceable when the seller corrected Mau.

At the music shop, Bocchi stared at strings for so long that the owner asked whether she was comparing gauge.

Bocchi stopped functioning.

Mau answered that she was.

The owner looked at him.

"Are you the musician?"

"No."

"Then let her answer."

Mau looked at Bocchi.

Bocchi looked at the strings.

"...These."

The owner nodded.

"Good choice."

Bocchi carried the packet outside like evidence she had survived a trial.

"You answered," Mau said.

"Please don't."

"Okay."

They continued.

<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln01-grimoire -->

Frieren's grimoire was last.

This was deliberate.

If they bought it first, Mau suspected the budget would become spiritually compromised.

The bookseller knew Frieren by description.

"Small elf. White hair. Looks bored even when she's stealing my afternoon."

"Yes."

"Tell her the second title isn't worth the price."

Mau looked at the list.

"She'll be unhappy."

"She'll survive."

Bocchi leaned toward one shelf.

A customer nearby noticed the cap first.

Then Mau's shirt.

Modern branding meant little here.

The faces meant more.

"You're from the settlement."

Mau looked over.

"Yes."

The man's expression changed.

Not hatred.

Recognition first.

Then uncertainty.

"Otherworlders."

Bocchi's shoulders rose.

Mau answered before the silence could sharpen.

"Yes."

The bookseller kept wrapping the grimoire.

The other customer stared.

"That city—"

"We weren't there," Mau said.

"You people keep saying that."

Mau's voice stayed even.

"I said we weren't there."

The bookseller put the wrapped book on the counter harder than necessary.

"He's buying a book, Deren."

The man looked at him.

"I'm asking."

"You're not."

A second person had turned to listen.

Then a third.

Bocchi moved closer to Mau.

Mau noticed.

He did not touch her.

He did not want to make her feel like she was being handled.

"We should go," he said.

Bocchi nodded.

The bookseller pushed the grimoire across.

"No charge for the wrapping."

Mau paid anyway.

Outside, the market still sounded normal.

That was the last moment the trip felt ordinary.
