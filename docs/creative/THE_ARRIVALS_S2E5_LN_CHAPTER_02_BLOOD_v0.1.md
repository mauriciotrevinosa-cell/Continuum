<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln02-street -->

They were almost out of the market when someone said:

"How many of you are there now?"

Mau kept walking.

Bocchi did too.

The question followed.

"How many?"

Mau stopped.

That was his mistake.

Not because stopping was wrong.

Because it gave the street time to choose whether this was a conversation.

A man near a cart had been watching them.

Mau did not recognize him.

"Eight," Mau said.

The man laughed once.

"Eight."

Someone else muttered something Mau did not catch.

The man did.

"City had thousands."

Mau looked at him.

"What does that have to do with us?"

"You tell me."

"I don't know."

"Convenient."

Bocchi's fingers tightened around the bags.

Mau saw it.

"We're leaving."

The man stepped into the road.

Not close enough to be an attack.

Close enough to make leaving require a choice.

"People died."

"I know."

"You don't look like you care."

Mau's face changed by almost nothing.

Bocchi knew by then that this did not mean nothing was happening.

"He cares," she said.

The words came out before she could stop them.

Everyone looked at her.

Bocchi stopped breathing.

The man looked at the pink hair under Mau's cap.

"Then why are you here?"

Bocchi's mouth opened.

Nothing came.

Mau stepped slightly in front of her.

"That's enough."

Someone farther back said, "Leave them alone."

Another voice answered, "They always say that."

The street became too many people with too many opinions.

Most did not move closer.

That mattered.

One did.

Mau put a hand up.

Not threatening.

A boundary.

"Don't."

The man slapped the hand away.

Mau caught his wrist on reflex.

Everything changed at once.

"Let go!"

"I will if you step back."

Someone shouted.

A basket fell.

Bocchi moved because a body bumped her shoulder.

Mau released the wrist.

Then something hard came from the side.

Small.

Fast.

Thrown badly.

Mau saw it too late.

The stone struck Bocchi high on the forehead.

Not hard enough to knock her down.

Hard enough to split skin.

Bocchi made a sound that was more surprise than pain.

Then blood ran down her face.

The entire street stopped.

<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln02-panic -->

Mau did not remember crossing the distance.

One moment Bocchi was standing.

The next his hands were on her shoulders.

"Bocchi."

She blinked.

Blood reached her eyebrow.

"Bocchi."

"I'm—"

Her voice shook.

Mau looked at the cut.

Too much blood.

Head wounds did that.

He knew that.

Knowledge arrived cleanly.

Calm did not.

"We're going home."

"I'm okay."

"We're going home."

The bookseller had come outside.

A woman Mau did not know pushed clean cloth into his hand.

"Press this."

Mau took it.

"Thank you."

The man whose wrist he had caught had gone pale.

"I didn't throw that."

Mau looked at him.

"I know."

That was true.

He did not know who had.

Someone in the street did.

Nobody pointed.

Mau pressed the cloth gently against Bocchi's forehead.

She flinched.

"Sorry."

"It's okay."

"No."

"Mau."

Her knees weakened.

He caught her.

That was enough.

Mau lifted her.

Bocchi made a small noise of protest.

"I can walk."

"No."

"I really can."

"Later."

The woman with the cloth picked up Bocchi's dropped bag.

"I'll send this with someone."

Mau looked at her.

"Thank you."

The crowd opened.

Not everyone looked angry.

Some looked ashamed.

Some afraid.

Some simply wanted the scene to end.

Mau carried Bocchi out of town.

He did not look back.

<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln02-road-home -->

Halfway home, Bocchi said, "The cap."

Mau looked down.

"What?"

"Your cap."

It was gone.

Probably in the street.

Mau had not noticed.

"I don't care."

Bocchi stared at his shirt.

Blood had reached the red fabric near his shoulder where her head rested.

"It's yours."

"I have another."

"No, you don't."

"I have a bucket hat."

Bocchi made a sound.

For one second Mau thought she was crying.

Then he realized she had laughed.

Barely.

He held her more carefully.

"Does your head hurt?"

"Yes."

"Are you dizzy?"

"A little."

"Nauseous?"

"No."

"Vision?"

"Normal."

"Do you know where we are?"

Bocchi looked at the road.

"Going home."

Mau swallowed.

"Good."

The word came out wrong.

Bocchi heard it.

"I'm okay."

Mau kept walking.

He did not say he knew.

He did not.

Not yet.

The inn appeared through the trees.

Mau shouted before they reached the door.

"Maomao."

The house answered all at once.
