<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln04-morning -->

Bocchi came downstairs wearing a bandage and Mau's bucket hat.

Nobody asked where she had found it.

Mau stared.

Bocchi stopped halfway down the stairs.

"I can take it off."

"No."

He looked at the hat.

Then at her.

"It works."

Bocchi touched the brim.

Momo whispered, "Recovery fashion."

Fern looked at her.

Momo stopped.

Breakfast happened.

That was important.

Bocchi ate.

Maomao checked her.

Yuta complained that Mau had slept three hours.

Mau pointed out Yuta had slept four.

Rimuru threatened to remove both of them from the conversation.

Frieren wore the now-clean Ferrari shirt.

Mau noticed the shirt.

Then Bocchi's hat.

Then his watch on the table.

His belongings had become distributed across the household.

This was apparently normal.

He found that comforting.

<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln04-meeting -->

The meeting happened after breakfast.

This one had to be called a meeting.

Rimuru stood at the head of the table.

"We're not deciding whether we were wrong to go to town."

Bocchi looked down.

Rimuru saw it.

"Especially you."

Bocchi looked up.

"You did nothing wrong."

The words landed hard enough that she had to look away again.

Rimuru continued.

"We're deciding what changes now."

No one argued with the premise.

They stayed together.

That came first.

No one proposed sending weaker residents somewhere else.

No one proposed splitting fighters from nonfighters.

The house was not a security arrangement.

It was a household.

Second:

fewer unnecessary town trips.

Trusted merchants where possible.

Indirect purchases.

More supplies stored at home.

Different routes.

Third:

basic alarms.

Not walls.

Not a fortress.

Enough warning that someone approaching the settlement would no longer appear at the door by surprise.

Fourth:

scouting.

Not only for threats.

For water.

Routes.

Resources.

Possible future homes.

Fifth:

information.

That one took longer.

Yuta looked around the table.

"If we're making decisions based on what can happen to each other, we need to know more."

Maomao raised an eyebrow.

"About?"

"What each of us can do. What we can't. What can hurt us. What follows us."

Silence.

Anko said, "That sounds dangerously close to trust."

Momo put a hand over her mouth.

"Disgusting."

Bocchi laughed and immediately touched her forehead.

Maomao pointed at her.

"No laughing aggressively."

"I don't know how to control that."

"Learn."

The meeting went on.

Not confessions.

Not biographies.

Boundaries.

Yuta explained more about cursed energy.

Frieren explained enough about mana suppression that Yuta realized he had misunderstood several things about her.

Rimuru described resistances and limits without turning himself into a manual.

Maomao explained why some poisons were not universally useful.

Momo and Anko argued over whether psychic phenomena counted as powers or personal information.

Fern clarified what she could and could not reliably detect.

Bocchi said she could play guitar.

Everyone looked at her.

She panicked.

"I know that's not—"

Mau said, "It counts."

Bocchi stopped.

Rimuru nodded.

"It does."

The point was not combat.

The point was understanding what the people beside you carried.

When the conversation reached Mau, the room changed.

He had the least to explain and somehow the most.

Knowledge without memory.

The Noise.

Construction.

The internal fragments that sometimes behaved like a system and sometimes like a wound.

Things he could not reproduce.

Things no one understood.

Mau looked at his hands.

"I don't know what my limits are."

Frieren sat beside him.

"Neither do we."

That did not feel better.

It felt more accurate.

<!-- scene-id: s2e5-ln04-construction -->

Later, Mau went outside alone.

Frieren followed because of course she did.

He did not ask why.

They walked beyond the stripped houses to a patch of ground no one used for anything.

Mau held one of Frieren's simple magical diagrams in memory.

Not the spell itself.

The shape of it.

"What are you trying?" Frieren asked.

"I don't know."

"Useful."

"I want to see if knowing more changed anything."

Frieren stood several steps away.

Mau focused.

Nothing.

Then something.

Not light.

Not exactly mana.

The air in front of his hand seemed to decide there should be structure where there was none.

Lines appeared for less than a second.

Too thin to be light.

Too precise to be imagination.

Frieren's eyes widened.

Mau stopped breathing.

The geometry folded inward.

Not exploded.

Collapsed.

For a fraction of a moment, something existed between them that neither recognized.

Then it was gone.

The grass beneath it had not burned.

The air did not smell different.

Mau's hand hurt.

Not badly.

Enough.

Frieren crossed the distance.

"What was that?"

"I don't know."

"Do it again."

Mau looked at her.

"That sounded like you."

Frieren ignored him.

Mau tried.

Nothing happened.

Again.

Nothing.

A third time.

Pain sharpened behind his eyes.

Frieren put a hand on his wrist.

"Stop."

Mau stopped.

They stared at the empty air.

"Tell Rimuru?" Mau asked.

Frieren considered it.

"We don't know what we're telling him."

"That's true."

"Tell him something happened."

Mau looked at her.

"But not pretend we know what."

"Yes."

That was different from secrecy.

They walked back together.

At the door, Frieren still had hold of his wrist.

Neither noticed until Fern looked at it.

Fern looked at them.

Then at the bandage on Bocchi's forehead.

Then at the map on the table.

The house had changed in twenty-four hours.

No one knew yet how much.
