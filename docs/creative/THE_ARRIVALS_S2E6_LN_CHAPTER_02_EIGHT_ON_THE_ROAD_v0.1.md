<!-- scene-id: s2e6-ln02-merchant -->

The merchant changed the route before anyone understood why.

The wagon turned away from the wider road toward the mercantile town.

Kita noticed first.

"Isn't the town that way?"

"Yes."

"Then why are we going this way?"

"Because you're not going to the town."

Umaru sat up.

"Was that optional?"

The merchant kept his eyes on the road.

"Today, yes."

Okarun leaned forward.

"Why?"

The merchant was quiet long enough that everyone understood the answer would be unpleasant.

"People are tense."

Marin frowned.

"Because of the city?"

"Because people like simple explanations."

Coco looked toward Qifrey.

Mikasa, walking beside the wagon, shifted closer.

The merchant continued.

"I heard there's an old settlement near the lake. Other people like you live there."

"Otherworlders," Wakana said.

"Yes."

"Do you know them?"

"No."

"Then how do you know it's safe?"

"I don't."

Marin stared.

The merchant sighed.

"I know the town is a bad idea. I know the people at the settlement still trade. I know one of the guild runners said they shelter strangers."

Kita's hands tightened in her lap.

"Do you know their names?"

"No."

The answer should have disappointed her.

Instead it created space for hope.

Names could still be there.

<!-- scene-id: s2e6-ln02-road-groups -->

The eight had become one traveling group without becoming one social group.

Okarun, Umaru and Kita had learned each other's rhythms first.

Umaru complained enough to make silence less frightening.

Kita kept track of practical things everyone else forgot.

Okarun fixed small problems with the wagon because doing something with his hands gave him fewer opportunities to discover missing memories.

Coco, Qifrey, Wakana and Marin occupied a different orbit.

Marin could talk to anyone.

Coco tried.

Qifrey made sure neither walked off the road while asking questions.

Wakana repaired what the road damaged.

Mikasa remained slightly outside every cluster by choice.

She watched tree lines.

Counted exits.

Noticed how the merchant kept looking back at her as though trying to decide whether walking beside the wagon for six hours was normal.

It was normal to her.

That was enough.

The road narrowed.

The forest grew denser.

Then the merchant slowed.

A tiny bell sounded somewhere ahead.

Everyone looked around.

"What was that?" Coco asked.

The merchant smiled.

"Probably a good sign."

Mikasa did not agree yet.

<!-- scene-id: s2e6-ln02-alarm -->

At the inn, the bell rang.

Once.

Then stopped.

Mau looked up from the table.

Rimuru was already standing.

Yuta reached the window.

Frieren closed her book.

Bocchi froze halfway through tuning.

"Which line?" Fern asked.

"Southwest approach," Rimuru said.

Mau moved toward the door.

Fern caught his sleeve.

He looked back.

"Don't run out alone."

Mau nodded.

Yuta came with him.

So did Frieren.

Rimuru followed after telling the others to stay near the house until they understood what was approaching.

Momo ignored the spirit of this instruction by moving to the largest window.

Anko joined her.

Bocchi stayed near the door.

Outside, the afternoon was bright.

Mau saw the wagon first.

Then the people.

Too many for a normal merchant stop.

Yuta's posture eased slightly.

"No cursed energy I recognize."

Frieren's gaze moved across them.

"Several are wrong in the same way."

Mau knew what she meant.

Arrivals.

Rimuru exhaled.

"Eight?"

"Looks like it."

Mau stared.

The future-home map flashed through his mind.

Momo's question.

How many more?

Eight, apparently.

<!-- scene-id: s2e6-ln02-pink-hair -->

The wagon stopped before the inn.

People began climbing down.

The merchant raised both hands.

"I'm told this is where the Otherworlders live."

Rimuru looked at the building.

Then at the crowd behind him.

"Some of them."

Kita stepped down from the wagon.

She was listening to the adults.

Then she wasn't.

A flash of pink hair had moved behind Mau near the doorway.

Kita stopped breathing.

No face.

No bandage.

Just the shape.

The hair.

The way the person stood half-hidden because too many strangers had arrived at once.

"Bocchi-chan?"

At the door, Bocchi went completely still.

Everyone turned toward her.

Bocchi turned.

Kita's face broke open with relief.

Then she saw the bandage.

The relief changed.

"Bocchi-chan?"

Same words.

Different question.

Bocchi touched her forehead.

"Kita-chan."

That was as far as either of them got before Kita started moving.

Volume 4 ended with the front yard no longer large enough for everyone standing in it.
