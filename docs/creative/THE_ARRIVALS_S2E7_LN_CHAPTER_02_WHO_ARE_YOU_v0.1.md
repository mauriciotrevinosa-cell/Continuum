Momo had missed most of the doorway chaos.

Not because she had been told to stay away.

Once the wagon stopped, she had gone outside to help the merchant unload the last boxes and blankets while everyone else tried to fit eight new people through one front door.

By the time she came back inside carrying a stack of blankets, Kita had already found Bocchi.

The room was loud.

Okarun was standing near the fireplace.

His back was to her.

Momo stopped.

The blankets fell.

Nobody reacted at first.

The house was too loud.

Okarun turned at the sound.

Momo forgot how to breathe.

There were things the mind did before permission.

Recognized posture.

Recognized shoulders.

Recognized the stupid way he stood when he was trying to take up less room.

"Okarun!"

His face changed.

Not recognition.

Attention.

Momo crossed half the room.

Okarun stepped back.

That stopped her harder than a wall.

His eyes searched her face.

Nothing arrived.

"I'm sorry," he said.

Momo's smile stayed because it did not yet understand the situation.

"What?"

"Do I know you?"

The room became quiet in pieces.

Mau looked up.

Anko stopped writing.

Yuta understood before most of them.

Momo laughed once.

Wrong sound.

"Okarun."

He looked uncomfortable.

"I've been told that's what people call me."

Momo's face lost color.

"What do you mean you've been told?"

Okarun looked at the others as if one of them might explain his own brain better.

"I don't remember some things."

Momo stared.

"Some things."

"People."

Her expression broke.

Okarun flinched.

"I know my name. I know—things. I know what I can do. I remember parts of my life."

"But not me."

He could not lie.

"No."

Momo looked like he had hit her.

That was worse because he had not.

<!-- scene-id: s2e7-ln03-boundary -->

Mau moved first.

Not toward Momo.

Toward everyone else.

"Give them space."

It was the simplest useful thing.

Kita took Coco upstairs.

Marin took Umaru because Umaru had begun asking questions with her face.

Rimuru redirected Wakana.

Frieren picked up her book and left without needing to be told.

Fern stayed near the stairs.

Anko stayed because Momo reached blindly and found her sleeve.

Okarun saw that.

Stored it.

Momo's hand tightened.

"Do you remember Ayase?"

Okarun frowned.

"That's your name?"

Momo closed her eyes.

"Momo Ayase."

He repeated it carefully.

"Momo Ayase."

Wrong.

Too formal.

Too new.

Her mouth trembled.

Okarun looked miserable.

"I'm sorry."

"Stop apologizing."

"Okay."

"That sounded worse."

"I'm sorry."

Momo laughed and cried at the same time.

Okarun looked like he wanted instructions.

There were none.

Anko sat beside Momo.

Mau moved away.

That mattered too.

<!-- scene-id: s2e7-ln03-later -->

Hours later, Okarun found Mau outside.

Mau was hanging wet towels because seventeen people had created laundry at a scale the inn considered hostile.

"Is she okay?"

Mau looked at him.

"No."

Okarun swallowed.

"Do you know what I forgot?"

"No."

"She does."

"Yes."

"Should I ask?"

Mau clipped a towel to the line.

"I don't know."

Okarun looked frustrated.

"That's your answer to a lot of things."

"Yes."

"Does it help?"

"Sometimes."

Okarun looked toward the inn.

"I feel like everyone knows something about me I don't."

Mau understood that sentence too well.

He held out a second towel.

Okarun took it.

"When I arrived, I couldn't remember my name."

Okarun looked at him.

Mau continued.

"Frieren found something in my backpack. She guessed."

"You still don't know?"

"No."

"But you use it."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Mau thought.

"Because it's mine now."

Okarun looked at the towel.

Then the house.

"Momo says I called her Momo."

"Then maybe you did."

"I don't remember."

"That doesn't make her memory fake."

Okarun was quiet.

"Doesn't make mine fake either."

"No."

They hung laundry.

Nothing was solved.

That was better than pretending.
