The problem with seventeen people was that everyone knew something someone else wanted to know.

Umaru wanted to know why Rimuru could become a slime.

Coco wanted to know almost everything.

Kita wanted to know what had happened to Bocchi before she arrived.

Bocchi wanted to tell her.

Momo wanted to tell Okarun who he had been to her.

Okarun wanted to know.

That should have made it simple.

It did not.

"Can I tell him?" Momo asked.

The room went quiet.

Okarun sat across from her.

Anko sat beside Momo.

Mau was repairing a drawer nearby because the household had apparently decided privacy conversations needed carpentry.

"Tell me what?" Okarun asked.

Momo looked at him.

Too much lived behind the answer.

"Us."

Okarun's hands tightened.

"What about us?"

Momo almost said it.

Stopped.

The thing that stopped her was not fear.

It was the fact that the memory belonged to both of them.

And only one of them currently had it.

Rimuru leaned against the table.

"This is the problem."

Momo looked at him.

"No kidding."

"No. I mean this is the actual problem."

He gestured around the room.

"We built trust among nine people because we learned each other's limits over time. Now there are seventeen."

Coco raised a hand.

"We can learn."

Qifrey looked at her.

"Not every truth is ours to request."

Coco lowered her hand.

Mau stopped working on the drawer.

That sentence mattered.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln01-bocchikita -->

Kita learned about the town from Bocchi.

Not everything.

Enough.

They sat in the music room.

Bocchi held the guitar but did not play.

Kita watched the faint line above her forehead where the injury was healing.

"Did they attack you because you're an Otherworlder?"

Bocchi's fingers tightened.

"...Because they were scared."

"That's not an answer."

"It is."

Kita looked at her.

Bocchi swallowed.

"Most people didn't do anything."

"Someone did."

"Yes."

"And Mau?"

Bocchi looked down.

"He got me home."

Kita heard the way she said it.

Not hero worship.

Certainty.

"He takes care of you."

Bocchi nodded.

"Fern too."

"Frieren?"

Bocchi nodded again.

"Kita-chan..."

"What?"

Bocchi's face became red.

"They're not replacing anyone."

Kita blinked.

Bocchi stared at the guitar.

"You. Nijika. Ryo. My family. They're not—"

Kita's expression softened.

"I know."

Bocchi looked up.

Kita touched the healing edge of the bandage gently.

"I just need to learn who these people are too."

Bocchi's shoulders dropped.

That was fair.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln01-qifrey -->

Qifrey found Mau outside trying to reproduce the Construction event.

Nothing happened.

Again.

Nothing.

Qifrey watched from a distance.

"May I?"

Mau lowered his hand.

"Watch?"

"Ask."

Mau thought.

"Yes."

"What are you expecting to happen?"

"I don't know."

Qifrey smiled slightly.

"That is an unusually difficult starting point."

"I know."

"Do you understand what you did last time?"

"No."

"What triggered it?"

"I knew more than before."

"About magic?"

"About everyone."

Qifrey's expression changed.

"That may be important."

"Or not."

"Also true."

Mau looked at his hand.

Qifrey did not ask to see inside whatever system Mau described.

Did not ask what the Noise was.

Did not ask for more than permission allowed.

That made Mau trust him faster.

Not fully.

Enough.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln01-right-to-tell -->

That night, the question returned.

Who has the right to tell someone else's story?

No one had a perfect answer.

So they wrote smaller rules.

You could explain your own abilities.

Your own history.

Your own risks.

You could not disclose another person's weakness, secret or source-world trauma simply because you knew it.

If a practical safety issue affected the household, you brought it to the person first where possible.

If someone was unconscious or unavailable, immediate safety came before privacy.

Momo stared at the page.

"So I can't tell Okarun about us."

Okarun looked at her.

Rimuru answered carefully.

"You can tell him your memories."

Momo frowned.

"What's the difference?"

"Your feelings are yours. Your experiences are yours. But deciding what his missing memories mean for him—that has to involve him."

Okarun looked at Momo.

Momo looked back.

It hurt.

It also felt more respectful than dumping a life into his lap and demanding he wear it.

"Fine," she said.

Okarun exhaled.

"Thank you."

Momo immediately became angry.

"Don't thank me like a stranger."

Okarun flinched.

Momo covered her face.

"This is impossible."

Anko patted her shoulder.

"Good news. We have rules now."

Momo looked at her through her fingers.

"I hate rules."

"We know."

The rules were not enough.

That was why the Contract came next.
