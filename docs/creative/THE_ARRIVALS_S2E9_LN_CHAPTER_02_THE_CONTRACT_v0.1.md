<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln02-why -->

Rimuru did not propose magic first.

That mattered.

They spent two days trying ordinary rules.

Ordinary rules worked for ordinary mistakes.

The problem was that the household contained information that could get someone killed.

Weaknesses.

Abilities.

Names of people still outside.

Things from source worlds that enemies could exploit.

Mikasa understood this immediately.

Yuta understood too.

Qifrey understood for different reasons.

Okarun understood once Momo explained only enough of her own experience for him to realize how much she was choosing not to say.

The Contract came from that pressure.

Not suspicion of G3.

The opposite.

Trust was expanding faster than privacy could safely follow.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln02-terms -->

Rimuru explained the terms three times.

Then Qifrey explained them differently.

Then Fern asked questions until the explanation stopped sounding elegant and started sounding practical.

The Contract did not prevent speech in general.

It did not forbid leaving.

It did not make people obedient.

It did not turn every secret into state property.

Protected information had to be designated through the household's agreed process.

People had to enter voluntarily.

They could still refuse to receive protected information.

If they accepted it under the Contract, deliberate disclosure outside the allowed circle would meet resistance.

"How much resistance?" Okarun asked.

Rimuru looked at him.

"Do not test that."

Okarun nodded.

Everyone signed or agreed according to the mechanism Rimuru could support.

Mau watched.

His own earlier experience with Contracts had taught him enough to distrust any sentence containing the words it should probably be fine.

He asked questions.

"What if someone needs to warn an outsider to save them?"

"Emergency exceptions."

"What if they forget something is protected?"

"The mechanism distinguishes deliberate disclosure."

"What if they want out later?"

"We remove future access and release where the terms permit. Existing protected knowledge remains covered."

"What if the Contract is wrong?"

Rimuru was quiet.

"Then we change it together."

Mau nodded.

That answer mattered more than the others.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln02-okarun-test -->

Okarun tested it the next afternoon.

Not because he wanted to betray anyone.

Because he needed to know what lived inside his own mouth now.

He stood outside with Mau and Yuta.

The road was empty.

Okarun said, "Rimuru can—"

The word stopped.

Not forgotten.

Blocked.

His throat closed around the protected detail.

He coughed.

Mau looked at him.

"Enough."

Okarun put a hand up.

"Wait."

Yuta's expression changed.

"Don't push it."

Okarun tried again.

This time he forced.

Pain flashed through him like lightning under the skin.

He dropped to one knee.

Yuta caught his shoulder.

Mau crouched.

"That was stupid."

Okarun breathed through clenched teeth.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I needed to know."

"You knew."

"I needed to feel the boundary."

Mau understood that more than he wanted to.

Yuta did too.

"Well?" Yuta asked.

Okarun straightened slowly.

"Boundary works."

Mau stared.

Okarun winced.

"I won't do it again."

"Good."

They went inside.

Rimuru saw Okarun's face and knew immediately.

"I specifically told you—"

"I know."

"What happened?"

"Lightning."

Rimuru closed his eyes.

Momo appeared.

"What did you do?"

Okarun looked at her.

"Science."

Momo hit him with a cushion.

The Contract became real to everyone else after that.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln02-momo -->

That night, Momo sat with Okarun near the fire.

Not alone.

Nothing in this house was reliably alone anymore.

But private enough.

"I can tell you what I remember," she said.

Okarun looked at her.

"About me?"

"About me."

He understood the distinction.

Momo swallowed.

"I remember caring about you."

Okarun's face changed.

She kept going before courage failed.

"I remember being angry with you. I remember you making me angry. A lot."

"That sounds plausible."

Momo laughed.

"Shut up."

Okarun smiled.

The expression hurt because she knew it.

"I remember calling you Okarun."

He looked down.

"I know."

"I remember you calling me Momo."

He was quiet.

Momo did not ask him to say it.

Not yet.

The Contract could protect secrets.

It could not return a missing relationship.

That part would have to grow differently.
