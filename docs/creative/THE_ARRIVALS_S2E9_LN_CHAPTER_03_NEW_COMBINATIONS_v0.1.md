<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln03-anko-mikasa -->

Anko and Mikasa became friends because neither required conversation to prove a conversation had happened.

They shared a patrol once.

Then twice.

On the third, Anko brought a notebook.

Mikasa asked, "Why?"

"I think while walking."

"You write while walking?"

"Sometimes."

"That seems inefficient."

"So does being traumatized."

Mikasa looked at her.

Anko looked back.

"Too much?"

Mikasa considered.

"No."

That was enough.

Later, Mikasa found Anko in the common room and sat beside her without checking the exits first.

Anko noticed.

Did not mention it.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln03-okarun-mau -->

Okarun and Mau became friends by fixing things.

Neither called it friendship.

They repaired a shelf.

Then a section of perimeter line.

Then a wheelbarrow that should have been replaced.

Okarun asked about memory.

Mau answered.

Mau asked about knowing how to do things without knowing where the knowledge came from.

Okarun understood more than most.

One afternoon, Okarun said:

"I think Momo is waiting for me to become someone."

Mau tightened a bolt.

"Who?"

"Me."

"That's complicated."

"Thanks."

"I don't think she expects you to fake it."

Okarun looked at him.

"How do you know?"

"I don't."

Okarun sighed.

Mau added, "But she looks angry when you apologize."

"She looks angry a lot."

"Maybe that's normal."

Okarun laughed.

The wheelbarrow survived.

Mostly.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln03-frieren-coco -->

Coco stopped being afraid of Frieren after discovering Frieren could be bribed with questions that were interesting enough.

"Why does your mana disappear?"

"Practice."

"How much?"

"A lot."

"Can anyone learn?"

"Probably."

"Can I?"

"Maybe."

Qifrey sat nearby trying not to intervene.

Frieren looked at him.

"You're making a face."

"I'm practicing restraint."

"It's bad."

Coco laughed.

Mau passed through carrying firewood.

Coco called him.

"Can you suppress whatever you have?"

Mau stopped.

"I don't know what I have."

Frieren answered without looking up.

"He is very consistent."

Mau stared at her.

Coco laughed harder.

Qifrey watched the exchange.

Mau and Frieren did not perform closeness.

They simply assumed the other person belonged in the conversation.

That told him more than explanation would have.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln03-umarubridge -->

Umaru's second game night did not include Mau.

This surprised her.

Not because he had excluded her.

Because she had forgotten to look for him.

She sat with Momo, Coco, Bocchi and Marin around a table.

The rules changed twice.

Momo cheated.

Marin accused her.

Coco misunderstood the objective and nearly won.

Bocchi said something funny too quietly, then had to repeat it because everyone wanted to hear.

Umaru laughed until she fell sideways.

Halfway through, Mau came downstairs.

Umaru looked up.

"You're late."

"To what?"

She pointed at the table.

Mau looked at the game.

"There are no seats."

"Exactly."

He kept walking.

Umaru returned to the game.

Later, she realized that had been the point.

<!-- scene-id: s2e9-ln03-dinner -->

At dinner, the old groupings no longer explained the table.

Kita sat beside Fern.

Mikasa sat near Anko.

Coco argued with Maomao about whether something counted as magic.

Wakana talked with Rimuru about cloth and insulation.

Marin talked to Momo with the frightening speed of two people who had discovered each other.

Okarun sat close enough to Momo to hear her without pretending the distance meant anything yet.

Bocchi sat between Kita and Yuta.

Mau moved through the room helping with plates.

Frieren read until Fern took the book away.

"You'll spill."

Frieren looked offended.

"You spill."

"Less."

Mau put food in front of both of them.

Seventeen people ate.

Nobody gave a speech about becoming one family.

The table did not need one.
