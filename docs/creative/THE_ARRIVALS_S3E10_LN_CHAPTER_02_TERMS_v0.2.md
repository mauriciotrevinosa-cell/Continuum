# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 21 — Terms

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 21  
**Continuity state:** Day 3 — afternoon | Witch bargain recorded | Old inn remains destination

They wrote the agreement down.

Anko would have been proud.

She was not there.

So Rimuru did it.

Not a magical Contract.

Not yet.

Paper.

Names where names were offered.

Conditions.

The witches could accompany or visit under notice.

They could ask Mau questions after medical stabilization.

Mau could stop a conversation at any time.

No dream induction.

No perception alteration.

No memory probing without separate consent.

No one in the household would attack them merely for approaching under the agreed terms.

No witch would prevent Mau from leaving once travel was safe.

Milim read the page upside down.

"Why not just make a Contract?"

Rimuru answered:

"Because not every agreement needs supernatural punishment."

That surprised Diablo.

Good.

Qifrey added:

"And because we don't fully understand how their systems interact with ours."

Also good.

The witches accepted paper with varying degrees of amusement.

One signed.

One drew something that may have been a name.

Another refused to sign but verbally accepted.

Rimuru wrote that too.

The agreement was imperfect.

Real agreements often were.

Yuta checked Mau again.

"Can move in an hour if the fever doesn't spike."

Frieren sat beside him.

Mau woke briefly.

"Where are we going?"

"Home."

He looked at her.

"The house?"

"The inn."

Specific.

His shoulders loosened.

Good.

He remembered the inn.

Probably.

Frieren caught herself thinking the word.

Probably.

Mau saw it.

"Do you remember it too?"

The question was not yet a habit.

It would become one.

"Yes."

He closed his eyes.

Outside, G5 remained at the old inn.

They needed dinner.

The future site remained dark.

Construction had lost another day.

The missing-person board had gained no new search because nearly everyone capable of verifying rumors was occupied.

The cost did not disappear because Mau had been found.

Now they had to bring him back.

The difference mattered.

Finding a body in a location was not the same as returning a person to his life.

The witches understood that better than anyone wanted.

When the hour passed, they prepared to move.

No victory.

No solved mystery.

Just a stretcher, blankets, route lights and a road home.
