# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 27 — The Deal Comes Due

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 27  
**Continuity state:** Day 4 — late morning | Mau stable enough for first witch questions

The witches waited until Maomao said Mau could sit at a table for an hour.

That was more restraint than anyone expected.

The first session happened downstairs because Mau refused to make his bedroom feel like an interrogation room.

Frieren sat near him.

Not beside him.

His choice.

Rimuru attended.

Yuta.

Qifrey.

One witch asked:

"Do you want them here?"

Mau looked around.

"Yes."

"All?"

Mau looked at Rimuru.

Then Yuta.

Qifrey.

Frieren.

"Yes."

Good.

Question one:

"What is your first memory?"

Mau answered without hesitation.

"Frieren."

The witch waited.

Mau clarified.

"Waking up. Seeing her."

Frieren looked at him.

The witch asked:

"Anything before?"

"No."

"Dreams?"

"Not memories."

"Concepts?"

"I knew language. Objects. Things."

"But no autobiography."

"No."

Question two:

"When did the false memories begin?"

Mau told them about the drawer.

The impossible mill.

The altered lake conversation.

The Frieren/Himmel memory.

The witch asked details.

Mau answered.

Then corrected himself.

Then corrected the correction.

The frustration built.

Frieren started to intervene.

Mau raised a hand.

"Let me."

She stopped.

Agency in practice.

Question three:

"Did any false memory feel weaker than a real one?"

Mau laughed.

"No."

The witch did not.

"That is important."

Rimuru asked:

"Why?"

"Because whatever is happening is not merely placing images in his mind."

She chose words carefully.

"It is attaching ownership."

Yuta frowned.

"Wrong ownership."

"Possibly."

Qifrey asked whether magic could do that.

The witch said yes.

Then no.

Then:

"Not like this."

Useful uncertainty.

Mau shifted.

The witch noticed.

"Stop?"

He considered.

"No."

Frieren looked at him.

Mau added:

"Ten more minutes."

The witch accepted.

No one praised him for enduring.

No one treated stopping as failure.

At the end, she asked one final question.

"Why did you go north?"

Mau looked at the table.

"Because I remembered being told."

"By whom?"

His throat tightened.

"Everyone."

That answer changed the room.

Not literally everyone.

Different versions.

Different trusted people.

The manipulation—or process—was not only inventing events.

It was borrowing authority.

The witch ended the session herself.

"Enough."

Mau was surprised.

"So that's it?"

"For today."

Bargain honored.

Questions asked.

Mystery larger.
