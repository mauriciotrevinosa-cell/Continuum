# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 30 — It Didn't Match Itself

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 30  
**Continuity state:** Day 4 — night | Diablo clue enters investigation | Rimuru/Raphael still lack internal cause

After dinner, five people became nine.

Then twelve.

Then Fern sent half of them away.

Correctly.

Mau stayed.

His problem.

His choice.

Diablo drew no diagram.

He hated diagrams.

Senku was not here yet to fix that personality defect.

Instead Diablo described sensation.

At one point on Mau's path, the emotional residue implied fear of Frieren.

Physical movement implied urgency toward something else.

At another, Diablo perceived a soul-pattern consistent with Mau while the attached emotional direction felt as if it belonged to a different remembered event.

Qifrey translated cautiously.

"Same person. Wrong context."

"Approximately."

The witch who remained nodded.

"Ownership."

Yuta hated the word.

Mau did too.

Rimuru asked:

"Could you tell what caused it?"

"No."

"Could you tell when it started?"

"No."

"Could you tell whether it's still happening?"

Diablo looked at Mau.

"Not reliably."

No miracle demon.

Good.

Rimuru turned inward.

"Raphael."

Mau flinched at the name.

Everyone noticed.

Raphael answered Rimuru privately.

Her passive observations remained what they had always been around Mau:

classification instability.

No identifiable internal actor.

No detected mechanism producing the mismatch.

No hidden diagnostic she had somehow forgotten to mention.

Rimuru said it aloud.

"She can't see the cause."

Mau looked at him.

"Same as before?"

Rimuru nodded.

"S1?"

Mau did not remember saying Raphael's name then.

He knew the story from others.

"Same basic limitation."

That mattered.

The group had:
- witches seeing memory seams;
- Diablo seeing mismatched residue/context;
- Frieren identifying false shared history;
- Yuta tracking physical state;
- Qifrey recognizing rule interactions;
- Raphael unable to resolve the internal cause.

Partial perspectives.

No universal truth.

Mau looked at the notes.

"So what do we do?"

Frieren answered:

"Tomorrow, nothing dramatic."

He looked at her.

"Rest."

"That's dramatic."

"No."

"To me."

One witch smiled.

Frieren did not.

The plan became boring.

Sleep.

Food.

No solo travel.

Limited questioning.

Ordinary work only if Maomao allowed it.

Record mismatches when they happened.

Do not hunt a source based on a memory telling Mau where to go.

That last rule was written twice.

Mau looked at it.

"Fair."

The group dispersed.

Diablo remained with Rimuru.

At last, they had ten minutes to be reunited.

Rimuru looked at him.

"Hi."

Diablo smiled.

"Hello, Rimuru-sama."

Then both laughed because the timing was terrible.

Outside, the worksite waited for morning.

Inside, Mau went upstairs with Frieren.

For the next few days, he would appear better.

That would be the dangerous part.
