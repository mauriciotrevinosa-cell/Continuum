# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 31 — The Second Witch Session

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 31  
**Continuity state:** Day 6 — morning | Mau voluntarily resumes witch questioning | No universal diagnosis

The second session was Mau's idea.

That surprised everyone.

Not because he had become fearless.

Because the first session had taught him something important:

refusing all questions would not make the wrong memories stop existing.

Choosing the conditions might let him remain a person inside the investigation.

The witch arrived after breakfast.

Only one.

Mau appreciated that.

He chose Ori and Frieren to remain.

Rimuru stayed outside.

Question:

"What do you remember about leaving?"

Mau closed his eyes.

The cabin.

Except there had been no cabin where he was found.

He remembered a cabin anyway.

He remembered opening a door.

He remembered a woman saying:

"You took your time."

That had happened in one version of the story inside his head.

In reality, the witches had found him collapsed by a stream.

Mau opened his eyes.

"I remember arriving awake."

Ori went still.

Frieren too.

The witch did not.

"Good."

Mau stared.

"Good?"

"Useful."

She asked him to describe the room.

He did.

Then she described the actual shelter where they had carried him after finding him.

Some details matched.

Others did not.

A window was on the wrong wall.

One table existed in both.

A phrase spoken after he briefly woke had migrated earlier into his memory.

Dream and waking had folded together.

The witch said:

"This is not one false memory replacing one real memory."

Frieren asked:

"Then what?"

"Reconstruction happening without his permission."

Mau's hand tightened.

Ori put her hand near his.

Not on it.

Choice.

Mau took it.

The witch noticed.

"Your memories involving her?"

Mau looked at Ori.

"Mostly consistent."

"Mostly?"

Ori said:

"He remembers one breakfast wrong."

Mau frowned.

"You salted it."

"I did not."

Frieren said:

"He did."

Mau looked betrayed.

Normal disagreement.

The witch smiled slightly.

"Keep that."

"What?"

"Proof that consistency does not require perfection."

At minute thirty-seven Mau stopped answering.

Not dramatically.

He held up one hand.

"Break."

The witch stopped immediately.

Frieren looked at the clock.

Ori poured water.

Nobody asked him to justify why.

Three minutes later Mau said:

"I can keep going."

The witch shook her head.

"Next time."

Mau looked surprised.

The consent rule from the road bargain had survived contact with curiosity.

Good.

The session ended after forty minutes.

No spell.

No answer.

But one useful distinction:

wrong memory was not always foreign memory.

Sometimes it was Mau's own mind rebuilding around foreign pressure.

That made removal more dangerous.

They could not simply cut out what looked wrong.

Some wrongness used real material.

Some reality looked wrong because the structure around it had changed.
