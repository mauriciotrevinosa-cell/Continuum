# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 34 — Ori Is the Same

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 34  
**Continuity state:** Day 7 — morning | Ori consistency pattern becomes explicit | Exact metaphysics open

Mau tested Ori without telling her.

That lasted three questions.

Then Ori noticed.

He had disguised the first two as conversation.

Badly.

Ori knew his "casual" voice now.

It sounded like a person trying to hide a clipboard behind his back.

"You're testing me."

Mau looked guilty.

"Yes."

"Ask properly."

He did.

The first time she wore one of his shirts.

Same.

The confession.

Same enough that both looked away.

The rejection.

Same.

Frieren sitting with Ori afterward.

Same.

A small argument over whether a cup belonged upstairs.

Same.

The breakfast salt incident.

Different.

Ori insisted Mau had added it.

Mau insisted she had.

Frieren, passing through, said:

"Mau did."

Mau pointed.

"Unreliable witness."

Frieren kept walking.

Ori laughed.

Mau wrote:

ORI-RELATED RECALL: HIGHER CONSISTENCY, NOT PERFECT.

Rimuru underlined NOT PERFECT.

Then crossed out Mau's next proposed heading:

ORI ANCHOR.

Mau looked at him.

"What?"

"She's a person."

Mau stared.

Right.

He rewrote:

ORI-RELATED MEMORIES CURRENTLY SHOW HIGHER CONSISTENCY.

Longer.

Less dangerous.

Ori approved.

No one wrote:

goddess immunity.

No one knew.

The pattern mattered anyway.

Ori looked at the page.

Once, she had worried she was less real because she had no ordinary past.

Now Mau's memory treated her as one of the most stable people in his life.

"I don't like being useful because your brain is broken."

Mau looked at her.

"Good."

Ori frowned.

"Good?"

"I don't want you to think you need a function to stay."

The sentence hit both of them.

Mau realized he should probably apply it inward.

Did not.

Not yet.

Ori sat beside him.

"Do you remember what you told me after I confessed?"

Mau looked at her.

"That I loved you."

Ori's throat tightened.

"And?"

"Not the way I love Frieren."

She nodded.

Pain still existed.

Memory correct.

Both survived.

Mau looked at her.

"Do you remember it that way too?"

Ori smiled.

"Yes."

Then:

"And if I remembered it differently?"

Mau's smile disappeared.

Ori waited.

He understood the test.

"I'd want to know."

"Even if mine didn't match yours?"

"Yes."

"You wouldn't decide mine was fake?"

Mau looked at the notebook.

"No."

Good.

Memory consistency was useful.

It was not ownership of truth.

That question—do you remember it too?—was becoming something other than panic.

A request for another person's version.

Not a demand that one of them disappear.
