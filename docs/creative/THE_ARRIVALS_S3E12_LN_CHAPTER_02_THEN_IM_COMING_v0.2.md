# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 12
## Chapter 2 — Then I'm Coming

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 12  
**Reading order:** 2  
**Continuity state:** Day 6 — before dawn | Mau/Ori preparing second departure

"If you go," Ori said, "I'm coming."

"No."

"Good. Then we agree you're not going."

Mau looked at her.

Ori folded her arms.

He had no clean route out of the logic.

"You could get hurt."

"So could you."

"That's different."

"Why?"

Mau opened his mouth.

Ori pointed.

"Don't say because you're you."

He closed it.

She knew him too well.

That was another reason he trusted her memories.

Ori packed food.

More than Mau had taken the first time.

Two blankets.

Water.

Basic medicine.

A rope.

A small repair kit.

The evidence notebook.

Mau removed the notebook.

Ori put it back.

Mau removed it again.

Ori stared.

"Do you want evidence or not?"

"I want the evidence to survive."

"Then copy the important parts."

Right.

No carrying the only record into another disappearance.

Mau objected.

"No."

"Why?"

"If we lose it—"

"We copy the important pages."

She did.

Not all of them.

Only categories and route notes.

The original stayed at the inn.

Better continuity.

Ori wrote the letter.

Mau tried.

His version became apology instead of information.

Ori took the page.

"We need them to know we're together."

Mau looked at her.

"They'll follow."

"Yes."

"I don't want them to."

"I don't care."

She wrote:

Mau is with me.

We are going south.

He believes something there connects to the memory problem.

We have food and water.

Do not assume we are safe.

Frieren, I'm sorry.

Mau stared at the last line.

Ori did not erase it.

"You're telling them direction."

"Yes."

"That defeats the point."

"The point is you're not alone. Not that everyone else becomes blind."

Mau hated how reasonable she was.

Before leaving, Ori stood outside Frieren's door.

Hand raised.

She almost knocked.

Inside, a floorboard moved.

Frieren turning in sleep.

Ori froze.

One knock.

That was all.

Wake her.

Let the argument happen.

Maybe Mau stays.

Maybe he leaves immediately and alone.

Maybe Ori loses the only leverage she has:
if you go, I'm coming.

Ori hated every version.

She lowered her hand.

Not because Frieren mattered less.

Because Ori had decided the least dangerous available choice was refusing to let Mau disappear alone.

"I'm sorry," she whispered.

Then she went downstairs.

Mau waited by the door.

Ori looked at him.

"This is not me choosing you over her."

"I know."

"If you say otherwise later, I'll hit you."

"Fair."

They left together.

At the edge of the yard Mau stopped.

Ori almost asked why.

He looked back.

Old inn.

One upstairs window.

He could still turn around.

That mattered.

He did not.

Ori did not pretend his choice had become correct because she accompanied him.

She adjusted the pack.

"South."

Mau nodded.

"South."

The old inn disappeared behind trees.

Neither called the road freedom.

Behind them, the letter waited to turn fear into a direction.

Ahead, Mau still believed finding one thing without anyone else's testimony might return ownership of himself.

Ori already suspected the opposite.

She went anyway.
