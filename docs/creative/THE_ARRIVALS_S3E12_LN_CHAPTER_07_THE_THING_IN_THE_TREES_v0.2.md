# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 12
## Chapter 7 — The Thing in the Trees

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 12  
**Reading order:** 7  
**Continuity state:** Day 9 — morning | Third travel day | Monster encounter begins

The forest had become quiet before Mau woke.

Ori hated that.

Not because quiet always meant danger.

Because she had finally learned enough ordinary forest noise to notice its absence.

She touched Mau's shoulder.

His eyes opened immediately.

Too immediately.

"What?"

Ori held one finger up.

Listen.

Mau did.

Water.

Leaves.

Nothing alive advertising itself.

No insects.

No birds.

He sat slowly.

"Do you remember a monster here?"

Ori regretted the question as soon as she asked it.

Mau looked at her.

"No."

"Good?"

"No."

Correct.

Absence from memory meant nothing now.

They packed without breakfast.

Quiet movements.

No running.

The pressure Mau had been following south had changed overnight.

Not stronger.

Broader.

As if the entire area now felt relevant.

He did not tell Ori immediately.

Then remembered the rule:

say changes early.

"The feeling's different."

Ori tied the pack.

"How?"

"Every direction feels wrong."

"Excellent."

Mau looked at her.

"You've developed a disturbing relationship with bad information."

"It keeps being honest."

They moved toward open ground near the river.

Bad choice?

Maybe.

Trees provided cover.

They also hid large things.

Open ground reduced surprise.

Mau wanted to choose based on terrain rather than certainty.

Good.

The first sign was not the creature.

It was a tree shaking against the wind.

Then another.

Low.

Too low for branches.

Ori stopped.

Mau looked left.

The first impact broke through brush and clipped a trunk hard enough to crack it.

Not aimed at them.

Close enough.

The creature moved low despite its size.

Armor-like plating covered one side.

The other side looked softer and badly scarred.

Too many limbs for the body plan Mau expected.

One eye damaged.

Not magical in any clean way he could identify.

Hungry was sufficient.

Ori stepped back.

Mau stepped in front of her.

She grabbed his coat.

"No."

"It's coming."

"That doesn't mean you become the wall."

The sentence hit him harder than expected.

Sukuna.

Frieren.

Promise.

No unilateral sacrifice.

Mau moved sideways instead.

"Together."

Ori let go.

Good.

The monster lunged.

Mau pushed Ori across rather than taking the impact straight.

Claws caught his shoulder.

Pain.

He stayed upright.

Barely.

Ori attacked with what she had.

The strike did not seriously hurt it.

It did make it turn.

Useful.

Mau's mind began its familiar dangerous sequence.

Observe.

Armor favors right side.

Analyze.

Damaged eye.

Understand.

Rear leg slips near mud.

Construct—

No.

Mau stopped that one.

The knowledge arriving too quickly was exactly part of why they were here.

No experimental power under stress.

Use what exists.

"River side!"

Ori trusted the call.

They moved.

The creature corrected faster than Mau expected.

He had been wrong about the blind angle.

Not fully blind.

Reduced.

Important distinction learned badly.

Its next sweep clipped a stone where Mau's knee had been.

Ori shouted:

"Plan?"

"Working on one."

"Bad time."

"Agreed."

They retreated toward fallen timber.

The monster followed.

Mau saw mud near its rear limbs.

Not enough.

He needed it committed.

Ori saw where he was looking.

"What?"

"Make it turn."

"How?"

Mau looked at her.

Ori hated the answer before he gave it.

"Be annoying."

She stared.

"I'm excellent at that."

She moved left.

Threw a stone.

Shouted.

The creature reacted.

Too much.

It lunged.

Mau moved to intercept its line without standing directly between.

Better.

Not enough.

The rear limb slipped.

Half-second.

Opening.

Mau went low.

Knife.

Too small.

He knew immediately.

The creature twisted.

Faster than expected.

A forelimb caught the side of his head.

The world became white sound.

Ground arrived before pain.

Ori screamed his name.

Mau did not hear the first one.

He heard the second from very far away.

Then nothing.

The fight had not ended.

Only Mau's clean memory of it had.
