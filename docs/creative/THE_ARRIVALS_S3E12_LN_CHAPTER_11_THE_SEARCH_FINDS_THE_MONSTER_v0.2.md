# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 12
## Chapter 11 — The Search Finds the Monster

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 12  
**Reading order:** 11  
**Continuity state:** Day 10 — morning | Main search roughly one day behind | Fight evidence becomes medical urgency

Frieren found blood first.

Not much.

Enough.

She stopped so quickly Yuta nearly walked into her.

A dark smear on bark at shoulder height.

Another on leaves.

Not old enough.

Yuta crouched.

"Don't touch."

Frieren's hand stopped centimeters away.

She hated him for being right.

Diablo moved ahead.

Then stopped.

The smell reached Yuta before the sight.

Dead creature.

Large.

Plated.

Partly collapsed into river mud.

Frieren saw it.

Everything in her face disappeared.

"Where are they?"

No answer.

Ori's marker was on the nearest tree.

Strip of cloth.

Two knots.

Deliberate.

Frieren touched that instead.

"She wanted us to find it."

Yuta surveyed ground.

Two human track patterns before the fight.

Chaos during.

One pattern unstable afterward.

A place someone had been on the ground.

Then another.

Mau.

Probably.

No.

Evidence.

Not certainty.

Yuta forced language to remain precise.

"One person took a hard impact here."

Frieren looked at him.

"How hard?"

"I don't know."

"Guess."

"No."

Frieren's mana shifted.

Yuta looked up.

"Guessing doesn't make him safer."

The anger left as quickly as it arrived.

Frieren closed her eyes.

"Sorry."

"No."

He returned to ground.

Vomit near the bank.

Head blood on stone.

The monster's injuries told another story.

Knife marks at close range.

Broken blade tip.

Improvised wood driven beneath plated tissue.

Diablo studied the carcass.

"The killing strike was not elegant."

Yuta looked at him.

"Meaning?"

"Desperate."

Frieren stared.

Mau had killed it.

Not Frieren.

Not Yuta.

Not Rimuru.

Not Diablo.

Mau with Ori.

That frightened her more than finding the body alive would have.

Himmel arrived from the secondary route a few minutes later.

He saw Frieren's face.

Did not ask what she felt.

Asked:

"Direction?"

Yuta pointed.

"Southwest after the fight."

"Can they travel?"

"Someone did."

Not answer.

Correct.

Frieren found another marker fifty meters away.

Then another.

Ori had built a chain.

Not continuous.

Enough.

At the second marker, the tracks showed Mau leaning on something.

At the third, two sets moved closer together.

Support.

Frieren's hand tightened around the cloth.

Ori had not simply gone with him.

She was keeping him moving.

The thought produced gratitude and anger in equal measure.

Later.

Yuta found where they had stopped.

Flattened grass.

Blood less active.

No sign of catastrophic bleeding.

Good.

Then one print dragged.

Bad.

He stood.

"This changes the search."

Frieren looked at him.

"It was already a search."

"Now it's medical."

Meaning:

pace changed.

Supplies changed.

Who joined changed.

Rimuru received the signal.

At the old inn, Maomao began packing before the message finished.

Water.

Clean cloth.

Simple medications.

Things for fever.

Things for nausea.

Things she could actually use without knowing exact cause.

She told Fern:

"Do not send everyone."

Fern:

"Wasn't planning to."

Good.

G5 took another worksite shift.

One route shipment still needed escort.

Another arrival had shown up at the inn overnight.

Search did not cancel population.

Population did not cancel search.

Milim received instructions to look farther ahead but not touch unknown biological anomalies alone.

She objected.

Rimuru repeated:

"Report."

Milim crossed her arms.

"Fine."

Diablo watched Frieren.

"You will not like the pace Yuta recommends."

Frieren did not look at him.

"I know."

"If we find him unstable—"

"I know."

"We may not carry him immediately."

Frieren turned then.

That possibility had not fully existed in her mind.

Find Mau.

Take Mau home.

Two actions she had treated as one.

Diablo saw the realization.

Said nothing else.

They moved south.

Not recklessly.

Fast enough.

Every marker Ori left changed the forest from uncertainty into chronology.

Monster.

Impact.

Rest.

Movement.

Deterioration.

Mau was alive after the fight.

At least for some distance.

Frieren used that as hope.

Not proof.

By noon, the searchers were no longer asking where Mau had gone.

They were asking what condition they would find him in when they caught up.
