# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 12
## Chapter 22 — The Message

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 12  
**Reading order:** 22  
**Continuity state:** Second disappearance, Day 12 — night | Old inn receives the search signal | Medical relief group leaves for the field camp

Okarun hit the porch at a speed that rattled the door in its frame.

He was breathing like someone who had run a very long way very fast.

He had.

The common room went silent the way it had learned to go silent.

Momo was already halfway across the floor.

"Okarun—"

"Found them."

Two words.

Nobody moved.

Then he said the rest the way Rimuru had taught all of them to say things now.

Only what was verified.

"Both alive. Ori's okay. Tired. Mau's hurt. His head. And something else. He's not awake much. They can't move him yet."

He stopped to breathe.

"Somebody found them first. Three people. Before us."

Nobody knew who.

Nobody cared yet.

Alive.

Both.

Marin sat down on the floor exactly where she was standing.

Wakana sat down beside her without a word, and Marin leaned into his shoulder, and for once in her life she did not say anything at all.

Neither did he.

Kita put both arms around Bocchi from behind, and Bocchi let her, and then turned around and held on.

Umaru set down the lamp she was carrying very carefully, because her hands had begun to shake, and went upstairs without a word.

Stark looked at Fern.

Fern was holding the edge of the table.

Hard.

For two seconds she did not move at all.

Then she let go and said "Maomao" in a perfectly ordinary voice, and Stark knew she would be fine, and would not be fine until she saw them.

Maomao was already gone from the room.

When she came back a minute later she had a pack over each shoulder and Kusuri behind her carrying a third.

"Head injury, fever, convulsions?" Maomao said to Okarun.

"How did you—"

"You said 'something else.' Something else is usually convulsions."

Okarun nodded.

Maomao turned.

"Kusuri. You're coming."

Kusuri did not argue.

She had stopped arguing with Maomao about medicine somewhere around the third day.

"Can you take us back?" Maomao asked.

Okarun had not sat down.

He did not sit down now.

"Yes."

Momo caught his sleeve.

He looked at her.

She looked at his face, his legs, his breathing, checking the way she always checked whether or not she admitted to it.

"Eat something first."

"There isn't time—"

She pushed a wrapped bundle into his hands.

He looked down at it.

His name was on the knot.

A small face drawn beside it.

Marin, still on the floor, raised one hand without getting up.

"Made it this morning," she said. "Just in case."

Okarun ate it in four bites standing in the doorway.

Then they were gone.



Upstairs, Umaru was in Mau's room.

She had not touched anything.

She sat on the floor beside the bed with her blanket around her shoulders, the way she had sat against the front door, crying in the quiet, furious way of someone who had promised herself she would not.

Coco found her.

Coco did not say anything clever.

She sat down on the other side of the bed, so that the two of them were on either side of the place he slept.

After a while Coco said:

"He's going to be so annoying about this."

Umaru laughed, wetly.

"So annoying."

"He'll apologize like forty times."

"Fifty."

"We should make him do something embarrassing."

"Karaoke."

"Karaoke."

They stayed there planning their revenge in detail until both of them could breathe properly again.



Downstairs, Seiko found Momo in the kitchen.

Momo was setting the table.

Every place.

Two at the corner.

"They won't be back tonight," Seiko said.

"I know."

Momo put the cups down anyway.

Seiko watched her.

Then she fetched two more cups.

This time she did not have to ask which places they were for.



That night the inn did something it had not done in six days.

It slept.

Not well.

Not all of it.

Enough.

Somewhere far to the south, people were gathering around a fire and a body that would not stay still.

Here, the lamps went out one by one, and the house that had been holding its breath for six days finally let some of it go.
