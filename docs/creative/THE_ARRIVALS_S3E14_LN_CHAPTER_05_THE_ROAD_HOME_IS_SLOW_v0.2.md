# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 5 — The Road Home Is Slow

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 5  
**Continuity state:** Day 13–14 | Mau stable enough to attempt transport | Return remains conditional

They did not move at sunrise.

They did not move when Mau opened his eyes.

They moved when several small things stayed true at the same time.

His fever had fallen enough.

He could drink without immediately losing it.

He had gone hours without another severe episode.

When Maomao woke him, he could answer three simple questions without the answers changing before she finished asking them.

Name.

Mau.

Place.

Field camp.

Who is beside you?

Frieren.

Maomao waited.

Mau looked annoyed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"That's unsettling."

"Good."

Frieren almost smiled.

Then Maomao said:

"We can try."

Try.

The word followed them onto the road.

The litter was rebuilt properly.

Not comfortable.

Stable.

Rimuru and Yuta took the first shift carrying.

Diablo cleared difficult sections without turning the forest into a road.

Mikasa ranged ahead.

Aira stayed near the rear.

Rem stayed close enough to see Mau's face.

Kaneki stayed where he could watch for the physical change without staring at him like an object.

Ori walked until Rem finally stopped.

"Get on the wagon."

"I'm fine."

"You almost walked into a tree."

"I saw it."

"After it hit your shoulder."

Ori got on the wagon.

She fell asleep before she could stay angry.

The first hour went well.

That made everyone nervous.

Mau slept.

No sudden movement.

No strange memory.

No dark structure.

Then the road tilted downward.

The litter shifted.

Mau woke.

"Stop."

Rimuru stopped immediately.

Not because the word sounded panicked.

Because they had agreed beforehand that if Mau said stop while awake enough to say it clearly, they stopped.

Frieren came into view.

"What is it?"

Mau looked at the trees.

"This isn't the road."

"It is."

"No."

Frieren waited.

Mau's face tightened.

"I remember a stone marker."

Mikasa looked back.

"There isn't one."

Mau closed his eyes.

"Okay."

Nobody said good.

Nobody praised him for doubting himself.

They waited until his breathing settled.

Then continued.

At midday, Mau woke and knew everyone.

At dusk, he looked at Rimuru and asked whether they had met.

Rimuru answered:

"Yes."

Mau nodded.

"Sorry."

"You don't have to apologize for not remembering me."

Mau looked unconvinced.

Night forced a second camp.

Frieren hated that.

The inn was less than a full day's travel away.

Close enough to imagine.

Too far to risk pushing through darkness with Mau still changing hour to hour.

They camped.

Mau woke near midnight.

Ori was asleep nearby.

Frieren sat beside him.

He looked at her.

Recognition.

"Are you mad?"

Frieren answered honestly.

"Yes."

Relief crossed his face.

She noticed.

"Why does that make you feel better?"

"Because that's not hate."

Frieren remembered he had said something similar before.

Or maybe this was the first time.

For one terrible second Memory Arc infected her too.

Had he said it already?

She looked at Ori's notebook.

No.

Different wording.

Similar feeling.

Frieren understood a little more of what Mau had been living inside.

She held his hand.

"You are not required to remember every sentence exactly."

Mau looked at her.

"That's a dangerous thing to tell me right now."

"Yes."

He laughed weakly.

Then slept.

The next morning they moved again.

Slower.

No one complained.

When the roof of the inn finally appeared through the trees on the second afternoon, Mau woke without anyone calling him.

He stared.

Tears filled his eyes.

"Home."

Frieren's hand tightened around his.

"Yes."

This time the word matched the place.

That did not mean everything inside him had returned with it.
