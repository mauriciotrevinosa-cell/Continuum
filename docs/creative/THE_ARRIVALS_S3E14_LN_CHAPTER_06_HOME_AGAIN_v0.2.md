# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 6 — Home Again

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 6  
**Continuity state:** Day 14 — evening | Mau physically returns to old inn | Lucidity still fluctuates

The second return was quieter.

That did not mean the house was empty.

Fern controlled the doorway.

"No crowd."

Behind her, the common room was full of people pretending not to be a crowd.

Momo had food ready that nobody expected Mau to eat yet.

Anko had the route notes from the search folded under one arm and had not made a joke in twenty minutes.

Marin had reorganized blankets twice because doing something with her hands was easier than waiting.

Wakana had repaired a torn litter strap nobody needed anymore.

Coco sat close to Qifrey with both hands wrapped around a cup gone cold.

Umaru had taken back her place against the front door the moment the lookout called the litter in sight, and had to be physically moved so that anyone could open it.

Maki stood near the wall where she could see Yuta the moment he came through.

Hakari, Karane, Shizuka and Nano had been told not to fill the doorway and, for once, had obeyed.

Himmel and Stark were outside because someone still had to watch the road.

Milim had been threatened with physical removal by Rimuru if she shouted.

Diablo had taken that threat much too seriously.

No crowd did not mean nobody came.

It meant the family had learned that love could wait five meters away.

Momo opened her mouth.

Fern looked at her.

Momo closed it.

The litter came inside.

Mau's eyes were open.

He looked at the stairs.

At the common room.

At Bocchi.

Nothing.

Bocchi stopped.

Mau frowned.

He knew he was supposed to know her.

Then:

"Bocchi."

Her shoulders dropped.

"Yes."

"Sorry."

"No."

No hug.

No demand.

She moved out of the way.

They took him upstairs.

Rem entered the inn for the first time with the McLaren cap still in her pack.

Kaneki came behind her.

Aira looked at the hallway.

"This place has too many shoes."

Momo shouted from downstairs:

"THANK YOU."

First bond.

Upstairs, Maomao had simplified the room.

Low light.

Water.

Clean bedding.

Space.

Mau saw the bed.

His expression changed.

"What?"

Frieren asked.

"I remember being here yesterday."

Silence.

Mau closed his eyes.

Then corrected himself.

"No."

"We weren't."

Frieren sat beside him.

"No."

Mau looked around again.

Same room as the false memory.

Real now.

"How do I know this time?"

Nobody had a perfect answer.

Fern, from the doorway:

"Because I am actually here."

Mau looked at her.

Fern added:

"And Stark did put blankets in the wrong place."

Stark objected from the hallway.

"I did not."

Mau laughed.

Small.

But real.

They settled him into bed.

For the first hour he knew exactly where he was.

Then he woke from a short sleep and asked:

"River?"

Frieren answered:

"Not here."

"The camp?"

"No."

"Where?"

"The inn."

Mau turned toward her.

Blank.

"Who are you?"

Frieren had heard it before.

It still hurt.

"Frieren."

He studied her.

No immediate recognition.

Then his eyes dropped to the McLaren hoodie she was wearing.

A pause.

"You steal my clothes."

Frieren's eyes filled.

"Yes."

Something clicked.

"That's you."

"Yes."

Habit reached him before biography.

That mattered.

Downstairs, the room stayed quieter than it had any right to.

People looked up whenever the floor creaked overhead.

Then looked away again.

Nobody demanded updates every five minutes.

Maki took Yuta's empty cup and replaced it with water when he finally came down.

Marin handed Frieren a clean shirt without commenting on the one she had been wearing for too long.

Anko asked Rem exactly one route question, wrote the answer, and stopped.

That restraint was its own kind of care.

Rem finally put the cap on the table.

Bocchi saw it.

Stopped.

"Where did you find that?"

"Near the river."

Bocchi touched the worn brim.

The object Mau had abandoned during her own crisis had returned during his.

She cried.

Momo started telling Rem the story.

Bocchi corrected her twice.

For once, the argument about memory happened downstairs over an object several people remembered differently in tiny harmless ways.

Upstairs, Mau slept.

Home again.

Not recovered.

Not fully lucid.

Only back inside the place where more people knew how to wait with him.
