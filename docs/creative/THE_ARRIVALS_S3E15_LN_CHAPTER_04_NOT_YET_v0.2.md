# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 10 — Not Yet

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 10  
**Continuity state:** Day 16 — evening | Rimuru finally proposes containment after observation | No authorization yet

Rimuru waited until Mau was asleep.

Then asked Frieren to step into the hallway.

She already knew the conversation would be bad.

"What?"

Rimuru leaned against the wall.

"I think I have a way to reduce the number of systems interacting with him."

Frieren's expression changed.

"What way?"

"Containment."

No recognition.

Rimuru forced himself to use the ugly word.

"Absorption. Temporarily."

Frieren's mana rose.

"No."

Expected.

Rimuru nodded.

"Okay."

That made her angrier.

"Okay?"

"I'm not doing it without him agreeing."

"Then why are you asking me?"

"Because you're going to hear it from him if I ask, and I don't want this to sound safer than it is."

That stopped her.

Rimuru explained slowly.

Not cure.

Not diagnosis.

Not memory repair.

Inside his containment space, Raphael could observe interactions under fewer external variables.

Maybe stabilize.

Maybe isolate some cascading effects.

Maybe do nothing.

Unknown compatibility.

Unknown effect on Mau's changing body.

Unknown effect on memory.

Frieren listened with her arms crossed.

"What happens if he wants out?"

"If he's stable, I release him."

"And if he isn't?"

Rimuru did not lie.

"Then we have a problem."

"No."

"Okay."

"For now."

Rimuru nodded.

"For now."

Frieren looked through the half-open door.

Mau slept badly.

One hand near the edge of the blanket.

She had spent days trying to reach him through rooms he sometimes could not remember.

The idea of putting him somewhere she could not follow felt impossible.

"Ask him when he's clear."

"I will."

"Not when he's frightened."

"Agreed."

"Not after he forgets what you explained."

"Agreed."

Frieren looked at him.

"And if we can't tell whether he's clear?"

Rimuru had no answer.

That was the real problem.

They went back inside.

Mau's eyes were open.

He had heard enough.

"You said no."

Frieren sat beside him.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I don't want you inside Rimuru."

Mau almost smiled.

"That sentence is terrible."

"Don't."

"Sorry."

He looked at Rimuru.

"Is it dangerous?"

"Unknown."

"Could it help?"

"Maybe."

"Could Raphael read my memories?"

"Not through anything I'm proposing on purpose."

"On accident?"

Rimuru hesitated.

"Unknown."

Mau looked at Frieren.

She saw the answer forming.

"No."

"I didn't say yes."

"You were going to."

Mau closed his eyes.

Then opened them again.

"If I say yes right now, do you trust that answer?"

Frieren stopped.

Rimuru did too.

Mau looked tired rather than dramatic.

"I've spent two days forgetting things while sounding normal."

He swallowed.

"I don't know if I trust me."

There.

The containment problem became a consent problem.

Frieren took his hand.

"Then not yet."

Mau nodded.

"Not yet."

He thought for a moment.

"If it gets worse, ask again."

"How do we know when you're clear?"

"I don't know."

Mau looked toward the door.

"Ask Ori."

Frieren's fingers tightened slightly.

He continued.

"Yuta. Maomao. Kaneki if it's body stuff."

Rimuru listened.

"Not one person."

Mau looked at Frieren.

"Especially not just you."

Pain crossed her face.

Then understanding.

Love did not make her sole owner of his clarity.

"Okay."

Rimuru stood.

No containment that night.

No solution.

Only a question everyone now knew they might have to answer later.
