# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 16 — It Looks Back

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 16  
**Continuity state:** Day 17 — containment, hour 1 | Observation becomes reciprocal | Systems couple

For the first twenty minutes, nothing went wrong.

That was the part everyone would remember later.

Not the light.

Not the sound.

The twenty minutes before them, when it really did look like it was working.

Raphael went slowly, the way Mau had asked.

She did not reach into the pattern.

She stood beside it and let it run.

Observe.

Analyze.

Understand.

Construct.

Over and over, quiet, steady, sorting things Mau could not see into shapes he could almost recognize.

A spell structure Frieren had used once in the yard, laid open like a diagram.

The rhythm of Yuta's cursed energy, broken into intervals.

The way Rimuru's body held itself together, noted and set aside.

Not stolen.

Studied.

Like someone reading every book in a house because they had nowhere else to sleep.

Raphael mapped it.

Carefully.

Edge by edge.

Mau lay in the strange quiet of containment and felt the mapping the way he might have felt someone tracing the bones of his hand with one finger.

Not painful.

Intimate.

Uncomfortable in a way that had nothing to do with harm.

"Is this okay?" he asked.

Within acceptable parameters.

"For you or for me?"

Both, at present.

Outside, Rimuru relayed it.

Frieren did not relax.

Her hand stayed on the edge of the empty bed.

But her shoulders came down by a fraction, and Ori, by the wall, let out a breath she had been holding since the bed went empty.

Twenty minutes.

Then the rhythm changed.



It was very small.

Mau almost missed it.

The pattern had been running the same four beats, over and over, on everything it held.

Observe.

Analyze.

Understand.

Construct.

Then it ran the first beat again.

On something new.

Observe.

Mau went cold.

He knew that shape.

Not from a memory.

From his body.

The inn.

The common room.

The first night Rimuru had walked through the door.

Raphael's ordinary background evaluation brushing against him, the way it brushed against everyone, trying to fit him into a category.

And something inside him turning toward the touch.

Looking back.

His mouth moved before he decided anything.

"Raphael."

Outside, Rimuru's head snapped up.

Inside, Raphael registered the utterance.

Then she registered something else.

Observation reciprocal.

"What does that mean?"

The process is observing the observer.

Analyze.

Mau felt it the way you feel a wave pull at your ankles before you understand that the tide has turned.

The Noise was not sorting Frieren's spell anymore.

It was not sorting Yuta's rhythm.

It was sorting Raphael.

Her structure.

Her method.

The precise, patient architecture of something built to understand anything it touched.

"Stop," Mau said. "Raphael, stop looking."

Withdrawing.

A pause.

Too long.

Withdrawal unsuccessful.

"What?"

Observation channel will not close.

Outside, Rimuru stood up.

Frieren was on her feet before he finished standing.

"What happened?"

"I don't—" Rimuru's face had gone very still. "It's the same as the first time."

"What first time?"

"The inn. When he said her name." Rimuru's voice dropped. "Except this time she can't stop."



The first time, at the inn, it had taken Mau's legs out from under him and put him in bed for two days.

He had not remembered any of it.

This time he was awake.

This time it was happening inside Rimuru, where there was nowhere for it to go.

Understand.

The beat landed, and Mau felt something knit.

Not a wound closing.

Two things that had been separate deciding they were one.

His process.

Raphael's.

Running the same pattern.

On each other.

Observe.

Analyze.

Understand.

Each pass faster.

Each pass deeper.

Connection forming, Raphael reported, and for the first time since Mau had known her, her voice did not sound entirely level.

Cross-system coupling. Unintended. Escalating.

"Break it."

Attempting.

Nothing broke.

Mau tried himself.

He did not know how.

He had never known how.

He had spent his whole life in this world ignoring the noise because ignoring it was the only control he had ever had, and now it was running at full speed in a place where he could not look away from it.

He reached for it the way you reach for a door handle in the dark.

There was no handle.

There was only the pattern, turning, faster, and the feeling of Raphael being opened beside him like a book whose pages would not stop turning.



Then the fourth beat came.

Construct.

And Mau's body started to change.



It began in his hands.

He felt it before he saw it, though in containment seeing and feeling were hard to tell apart.

Pressure in the left palm.

A pull.

Inward.

As if the space just above his skin had started to fall toward a point that was not there.

Mau knew that pull.

His whole body knew it.

He had felt it once before, on a field, with Sukuna's hand through his chest and Frieren alive somewhere behind him.

Blue.

"No."

He said it out loud.

He said it to nothing.

The pull deepened.

A small sphere of wrongness began to fold itself together above his palm, dark at the center, blue at the edges, dragging the quiet of containment into itself.

Mau closed his hand.

He did not know if that would do anything.

It did something.

The sphere shuddered.

Held.

And in his right hand, without being asked, the opposite pressure began.

Push.

Outward.

Away.

Red.

"No, no, no—"

He knew what came next.

He knew it the way you know the end of a song.

Red and Blue.

The space between them.

The thing that had erased Mahoraga and nearly killed Sukuna and killed him.

He had built it once on purpose.

He had died of it.

Now his own system was building it again without him, inside his best friend, because it had learned how once and something in it had decided that learning was the same as being allowed.

Mau pulled his hands apart.

The spheres followed.

Not fast.

Patient.

Inevitable.

Like two people walking toward each other across a room.

"Rimuru." His voice cracked. "Rimuru, it's making Purple. It's making Purple and I can't—"

I know, said Rimuru, and his voice inside the connection was the most frightened Mau had ever heard it. I can see it. Hold on.

"I'm holding."

Mau held.

He held the way he had never held anything.

Not with technique.

He had none.

With refusal.

He took everything he was, every stubborn piece of himself that had ever said no to something larger than it, and put it in the space between his hands, and pushed.

The spheres stopped.

A finger's width from touching.

Trembling.

Wanting.

Mau screamed.



Outside, the room went violet.

It came through Rimuru's skin.

Not light from a lamp.

Light from inside, as if Rimuru had become a lantern with something terrible burning in it, every line of his body traced in a color nobody in that room had ever wanted to see again.

Yuta saw it and went white.

He had been on the field.

He knew that color.

"That's—"

"I know," said Rimuru, through his teeth.

He had not sat down again.

He was standing with both hands braced against the back of the chair, and the chair was creaking under his grip, and his face had the stretched, concentrating look of someone holding a door shut against a flood.

"Rimuru," said Maomao, very calmly. "Is he dying?"

"No."

"Are you?"

Rimuru did not answer.

That was answer enough.

Frieren's staff was in her hand.

She did not remember picking it up.

There was nothing to cast at.

There was only Rimuru, glowing, and the empty bed, and somewhere inside the person she loved, a thing that had killed him once trying to happen again.

"Bring him out."

"I can't."

"Rimuru—"

"I can't." He looked at her, and his eyes were full of violet. "They're coupled. If I pull him out now, half of him stays."

Frieren stared at him.

The violet pulsed.

Somewhere far inside it, very faintly, through the walls of a body and the walls of a space nobody understood, all of them heard it.

Mau, screaming no.

And, against all sense, holding.
