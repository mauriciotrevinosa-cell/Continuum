# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 17 — From the Inside

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 17  
**Continuity state:** Day 17 — containment, hour 1–2 | Constructs form inside Rimuru | Household arrives

Blue in his left hand.

Red in his right.

A finger's width of nothing between them.

Mau held.

He did not know how long he had been holding. A minute. Ten. The spheres did not get tired. He did. Every few seconds they leaned toward each other, gently, the way two magnets lean, and every few seconds he pulled them apart again, and every time it cost him something he could not name and did not have much of.

And while he held, the pattern did not stop.

It had finished with Raphael's surface.

It went looking for what she was attached to.

The Noise did not mean to hurt Rimuru.

That was the worst part.

Rimuru would say so afterward, many times, to many people, until they believed him.

It did not hate him.

It did not want anything from him.

It was doing the only thing it had ever done.

It had found something new, and it was trying to understand it.

And the new thing was Rimuru.



He felt it as a hand on the inside of his body.

Then as many hands.

Not grasping.

Reading.

Every structure he was made of, opened and laid flat and looked at.

The part of him that could take things in and keep them.

The part that broke them down and learned their shape.

The way his body could forget it was a body and remember again.

Every skill he had ever used. Every skill he had ever watched someone else use, in a world none of these people had ever seen. Friends. Enemies. A goblin village. A forest. A cave where a dragon had once talked to him for so long that he had stopped being afraid.

All of it, opened.

All of it, studied.

And then, one by one, quietly, faithfully, with the terrible innocence of something that did not know it was doing anything wrong:

Constructed.



Inside containment, Mau felt the copies form.

He could not stop them.

He was still holding Purple apart with everything he had, and there was nothing left over to hold anything else.

When the first copy formed, the spheres jerked toward each other, as if the pattern had remembered them.

Half a finger's width.

Mau dragged them back.

His arms were shaking now. In containment there were no arms, not really, but he felt them shake anyway.

They came out of the pattern like reflections stepping out of a mirror.

Rimuru's hunger, the one that could swallow a lake.

Rimuru's patience, the analytical cold that took a problem apart into pieces small enough to carry.

Skills Mau had no names for. Skills Rimuru had only ever seen once, in someone else's hands, half a world away and a lifetime ago.

Each one built, inside Mau, from the reading.

Each one not quite right.

Each one heavy.

Mau's body tried to hold them.

His body could not hold them.

He felt his back split, the dark structure Kaneki had seen at the field camp tearing loose and folding back and tearing loose again. He felt his ears change shape and change back. He felt his skin go soft and loose and slime-strange, then harden, then knit with the cursed-energy repair Yuta had taught it, then come apart again.

Every survival route his body had ever observed, all at once.

Trying to build a body that could carry what was being built inside it.

Failing.

Trying again.

"Raphael," he gasped. "What's happening to me?"

Concurrent adaptation. Unbounded. Your body is attempting to construct a vessel adequate to the constructs.

"Can it?"

A pause.

No.

"Then why is it trying?"

It does not know how to stop.

Neither did Mau.



Outside, the pressure arrived.

It did not come in a wave.

It came in a weight.

The air in the room thickened, and then the air in the hallway, and then the air in the house, until breathing felt like lifting something.

Downstairs, Fern's pen stopped moving.

In the common room, Bocchi's guitar strings all hummed at once, though nobody was touching them.

On the porch, Okarun stood up so fast his chair went over.

"Momo."

"I feel it."

Momo had gone pale. Her hand was pressed flat against the wall of the inn as if she could feel it through the wood.

"Something up there is looking at everything," she said. "Everything. All at once. It's not—it isn't evil, it's just—"

Seiko, beside her, had closed her eyes.

She opened them.

"That's not a spirit," she said, and for the first time in a very long time, the old woman sounded frightened.

Rimuru's voice came through the floor.

Not words.

A sound.

The sound of someone being hurt by something they had agreed to.

Diablo was on the stairs before the sound ended.



He knew the pressure.

That was what Rimuru would understand afterward, and what made his hands shake when he remembered it.

He had felt it before.

In a cave.

In the presence of the being who had been his first friend in the world where he had been reborn, sealed in light for three hundred years, so vast that standing near him had felt like standing in the open mouth of weather.

A True Dragon.

That was what the Noise was building now.

Not the dragon.

The scale.

Mau's pattern had read something in Rimuru's memory of that cave, and understood it, and was constructing the size of it, inside a body that was coming apart trying to contain a fraction of what it had already made.

Rimuru's knees hit the floor.

"Rimuru!"

Frieren was beside him.

So was Yuta.

So was Maomao, already reaching for his pulse and finding, to her visible horror, that she did not know where a slime kept one.

"Stop it," Rimuru said, to nobody. "Mau, please, if you can hear me—"

I'm trying, said Mau, inside him, and he sounded like someone being torn in half. I'm trying, Rimuru, I can't, there's too much, it keeps—

Then the wheel turned.



Everyone in that room who had been on the field heard it.

Yuta.

Frieren.

Rimuru.

A sound like something enormous and old shifting a single notch on an axle nobody could see.

Yuta's face went grey.

He had heard that sound above Mahoraga's head, and every time it had meant that something they had used against it would never work again.

Inside containment, Mau felt it as a click.

Something in his pattern settling.

Adapting.

Whatever had been hurting his body, his body hurt a little less.

Whatever Rimuru had been doing to hold the pressure back, it worked a little less.

Rimuru gasped.

The wheel turned again.



Diablo came through the door.

Milim came through it behind him.

Behind Milim, half the house: Fern, Bocchi with Kita gripping her sleeve, Umaru and Coco, Okarun and Momo, Anko, Marin with her hands over her mouth.

They stopped in the doorway.

They saw Rimuru on his knees, lit violet from inside.

They saw the empty bed.

They saw Frieren with her staff raised at nothing.

None of them understood.

None of them could have.

"Rimuru-sama."

Diablo's voice was very quiet.

Very calm.

The calmest anyone had ever heard it.

"What is inside you."

"Mau."

"What is hurting you."

Rimuru looked up at him.

He saw exactly what Diablo was thinking.

"Diablo. No."

"Rimuru-sama, if I were permitted—"

"No." Rimuru's voice cracked across the room. "He's not doing it. He's fighting it. Do you hear me? He is fighting it. If anyone touches him, I will never forgive you."

Diablo did not move.

Something in his face looked as if it were being held in place by a very thin thread.

He did not move.

Milim was trembling.

"I can hit it," she said. "Rimuru, let me hit it, I can hit anything—"

"There's nothing to hit."

"There's always something to hit!"

"Not this time."

Frieren did not look away from Rimuru.

"Tell me what to do."

"I don't know."

"Tell me anyway."

Rimuru opened his mouth.

Then his left arm was gone.



It did not fall off.

It did not burst.

It simply stopped being there.

From the shoulder down, his sleeve hung empty, and there was no blood, because slimes do not keep blood, and there was no wound, because there was nothing left to be wounded.

Maomao made a sound Mau had never heard her make.

Coco screamed.

Bocchi sat down on the floor of the doorway as if her legs had been cut.

Rimuru looked at the empty sleeve.

He understood.

Raphael understood a fraction of a second before him and told him, and her voice was strained in a way it had never been in all the years he had known her.

Foreign-structure rejection. The Noise has classified part of your body as external to the coupled system and is removing it.

"It's attacking me?"

Not attacking. Excluding. It is trying to protect the vessel from something it does not recognize.

Something it did not recognize.

Rimuru.

Who had swallowed Mau into himself to keep him safe.



Separate, Rimuru told her. Raphael. Separate us. Now.

Attempting.

He felt her pull.

He felt the coupling hold.

He felt her pull harder, the most precise mind he had ever known throwing everything it had against a knot it could not see the end of.

Unsuccessful.

Then I'll push him out—

If you expel him now, the coupled portion of his system will remain bound to me. He will not survive the separation intact.

"Then what do I do?"

For the first time, Raphael did not have an answer.

Rimuru knelt on the floor of a borrowed room with one arm, glowing, while the people who loved the person inside him stood in the doorway and did not understand, and the wheel turned a third time, and somewhere very deep inside him, a man who had died once for all of them screamed and screamed and did not let go of the two spheres he was holding apart.
