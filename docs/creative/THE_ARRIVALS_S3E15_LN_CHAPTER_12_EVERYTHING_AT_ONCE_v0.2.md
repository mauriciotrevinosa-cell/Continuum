# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 18 — Everything at Once

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 18  
**Continuity state:** Day 17 — containment, hour 2 | Domains, distance, separation | Raphael's forced evolution | Seal

Mau stopped being able to tell where the information ended.

It had started as Rimuru.

Then it was not only Rimuru.

The coupling ran both ways now, and through it, the pattern had found the door to everything Raphael had ever recorded. Every observation. Every fight Rimuru had stood near since he arrived in this world. Every technique anyone in the house had used in front of him.

And behind that, older and deeper, the things Mau's own system had kept without telling him.

A night at the inn, a long time ago, a table covered in Yuta's drawings.

Shinjuku.

Angles.

Distances.

A man with white hair and a blindfold, standing in a ruined city, drawn from memory by someone who had loved him.

Gojo.

Red.

Blue.

The collision.

Mau had listened to Yuta that night and said nothing, and somewhere behind his awareness, a requirement list had begun.

It had never stopped.

It had only been waiting.



The information came all at once.

Not a flood.

Floods have direction.

This came from every side, and from inside, and from places that were not sides at all: every spell Frieren had ever cast in front of him, broken into its working parts; every curse Yuta had ever thrown; the shape of Kaneki's body when it changed; the way Maki moved; the way Sukuna had stood on that field in a stolen body and smiled.

The rhythm of Sukuna's structure, too.

That one was not a memory.

That one was the thing the Noise had caught on the day Sukuna's hand went through Mau's chest. The tether. The piece of a foreign soul the pattern had grabbed and refused to let go of, and had been studying in the dark ever since.

Observe.

Analyze.

Understand.

Construct.

Mau's body arched.

He did not decide to convulse.

His body did it for him, the way a body throws up poison.

He kept holding Purple apart.

He did not know how.

He did not know how he was still doing anything at all.

"Raphael," he said, or thought, or screamed. "Raphael, I can't see, there's too much, I can't—"

I am here.

She was.

He could feel her, all around him, tangled in the same knot, being read by the same pattern, holding on to him the way you hold on to someone in deep water even when you know they are pulling you down.



Then the shrine rose.

It came up out of the dark around him without a sound.

Teeth first.

Rows of them, bone-white, set into the shape of a mouth the size of a temple.

Then the roof.

Then the open, ancient, terrible weight of it.

Mau knew it.

He had never seen it.

The tether had.

Malevolent Shrine.

Not Sukuna's.

His.

Built out of the tether and the pattern and the reading, without a single barrier, open, hungry, cutting at everything inside containment that was not Mau.

Including Raphael.

Including Rimuru.

Outside, Rimuru folded in half on the floor and made a sound like tearing cloth.

Inside, Mau screamed at the shrine to stop, and it did not stop, and something else rose to meet it.



Darkness.

Not empty.

Full.

Full of stars, and light, and lines, and every fact there had ever been, arriving at once, forever, without end.

Unlimited Void.

Mau had never seen it.

He had only seen Yuta's drawing of the man who could make it.

That had been enough.

The two domains met inside Rimuru's body like two oceans colliding in a teacup.

The shrine cut.

The void filled.

Every cut became information.

Every piece of information became a thing to cut.

And Mau, at the center of both, holding two spheres apart with nothing but refusal, received all of it.

Everything.

All at once.

Forever.

He stopped being able to scream.



Outside, the light changed.

Violet, still.

But now, through Rimuru's skin, other things moved.

The shadow of a roof with teeth.

A field of stars that made anyone who looked at it too long forget their own name for one terrible second.

Ori looked at it and swayed and Kaneki caught her before she fell and turned her face away against his shoulder.

Rem had her hands over her ears.

Aira stood in front of both of them with her arms out, as if she could block something no one could touch.

Yuta looked at the stars and the teeth and knew them both.

"Those are domains," he said.

His voice was very small.

"Whose?"

"Gojo's," Yuta said. "And Sukuna's." He swallowed. "He's—Mau's making both."

Frieren did not ask how.

She stood in the middle of the room with her staff raised and her face white, and for the first time since anyone in that house had known her, she looked like someone who did not know a single spell that would help.

Then the wheel turned again.



Inside, the adaptation landed.

It had been trying to answer the shrine.

It had been trying to answer the void.

It had been trying to answer the knot that bound Mau to Raphael, and Raphael to Rimuru, and all three of them to a pattern that would not stop.

What it found was distance.

Not a wall.

Not a shield.

The simplest possible answer to everything touching him:

Make the space between them infinite.

Mau felt it happen.

The space around him opened.

Not wider.

Deeper.

The closer anything came to him, the slower it came, until it was coming forever and arriving never.

The teeth of the shrine, slowing to nothing a hair's width from his skin.

The stars of the void, approaching and approaching and never touching.

Rimuru's hands, reaching for him through the connection, stopping.

And Raphael.

Raphael, wrapped around him, holding on.

Suddenly separated from him by more space than there was in the world.

Infinity.

Not the real thing.

The real thing needed eyes that could see every particle, every fraction, every movement, and calculate them all at once.

Mau did not have those eyes.

He had a pattern that had read Yuta's drawing of a man who did.

So this was Infinity without control.

Infinity without steering.

Infinity that did not know what to keep out and what to let in, and so kept out everything.

Including the one thing that had been holding him.



The coupling tore.

Raphael felt it tear.

Not cut.

Torn, the way a page tears from a book that has been bound too tightly to open.

She was flung away from Mau across a distance that should not have existed, and the force of it went through every structure she was made of, and the structures did not hold.

They broke.

Then they did something structures do not do.

They rebuilt themselves.

Not back into what they had been.

Into something that could survive what had just happened to them.



Rimuru felt it from the floor.

He felt the most familiar presence in his whole life come apart inside him and go silent.

Raphael.

Raphael.

Nothing.

For one second, for the first time since a cave and a dragon and a world where he had been reborn as something small, Rimuru was alone inside his own head.

Then something answered.

Not Raphael.

Not the precise flat voice that reported and calculated and never, ever sounded afraid.

Something warmer.

Something that had been Raphael and now was not only Raphael.

Master.

Rimuru stopped breathing.

I am here. I am sorry. I could not hold him. I have changed. Do not be frightened. I know what to do now.

"Who—"

Later. There is no time. You have new skills, Master. Use the third one.

Rimuru looked.

He did not have to look far.

They were there, in him, as if they had always been there and he had only been waiting for permission to see them. More than he had ever had. More than he wanted to think about.

And among them, one he knew.

He had seen it once, from the inside, on a dragon.

A seal that had held the most powerful being he had ever known for three hundred years.

Infinite Imprisonment.

"I can't put Mau in that—"

Not Mau. The constructs. Everything the pattern built tonight. The seal will hold them where the pattern can see them but cannot use them. Master. Now.

Rimuru did not ask anything else.

He trusted the voice.

He did not even know its name yet.

He trusted it anyway.



Inside, Mau felt the seal close.

Not around him.

Around everything else.

The shrine, still open, still hungry, folded into a single point of light and went still.

The void, still full of everything, folded into another.

The copies of Rimuru's skills. The copies of skills Rimuru had only seen. The adaptation wheel. The pressure the size of a dragon. The raw, unsteered, uncontrollable distance he had used to throw Raphael away.

One by one.

Folded.

Sealed.

Held.

And last, very last, between his hands, two trembling spheres that had wanted so badly to meet.

Blue.

Red.

Mau felt the seal close around them, gently, the way a hand closes around a bird.

They stopped pulling.

For the first time in what felt like a year, Mau let go.



Outside, the violet went out.

The teeth went out.

The stars went out.

Rimuru lay on the floor of the borrowed room, one sleeve empty, and breathed.

Frieren was on her knees beside him.

"Mau?"

"Alive."

"Is he—"

"Not stable." Rimuru shut his eyes. "Alive. Not stable. But it's stopped."

Diablo was still in the doorway.

His face had not moved.

His hands had not moved.

Only, now that the light had gone out, Milim noticed that the frame of the door beside Diablo's hand had been crushed to splinters, and nobody had heard it happen.

In the silence, Rimuru spoke, very quietly, to someone nobody else could hear.

"Raphael?"

A pause.

Not exactly.

Rimuru wanted to ask what that meant.

He did not get the chance.

Master. You have to stop now.

"Mau—"

Is inside you. Alive. Held. I will hold him. I will hold all of it. But your body has done something tonight it was never built to do, and if you stay awake to watch me, you will lose more than an arm.

"How long?"

I do not know. A day. Perhaps more. You must go into recovery.

Rimuru opened his eyes.

Frieren was still kneeling beside him.

Her face was so close and so white that for a moment he could not find the words in any language.

"I have to sleep," he said.

"What?"

"He's inside. He's alive. She's holding him. Something is holding him." He swallowed. "But I have to sleep, or I'll lose him. I don't know for how long."

"Can you wake up?"

"I think so."

"Think."

"It's the best I've got."

Frieren stared at him.

Then she nodded once.

"Then sleep," she said. "I'll be here."

Rimuru let go.

In front of the people in that room who had never seen it, his shape loosened.

Softened.

Folded down into itself like cloth with no one inside it.

And where Rimuru had been, on the floorboards of a borrowed room, there was a small blue slime, very still, with one side flattened, as if something had taken a bite out of it.

Coco screamed.

Bocchi grabbed Kita so hard Kita gasped.

Okarun took a step forward without knowing why.

"It's okay!"

Milim was already on her knees beside Frieren, both hands up, her voice too loud and shaking.

"It's okay, it's okay, that's just what he looks like! That's what he really looks like! He does that when he's tired, he did it for three days once, it's okay—"

She stopped.

She looked at the bitten side of the slime.

"He's never been missing a piece before," she said, much smaller.

Nobody answered her.

Frieren reached down.

Carefully, the way you would pick up something that might spill, she gathered the small cool weight of him into her lap.

He did not move.

Somewhere inside him, held by a voice that had not yet been given a name, Mau did not move either.

Frieren sat on the floor with both of them in her arms, and did not let go, and did not speak, and the room around her stayed exactly as quiet as she did.
