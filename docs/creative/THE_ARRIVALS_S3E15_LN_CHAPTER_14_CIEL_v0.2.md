# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 20 — Ciel

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 20  
**Continuity state:** Day 18 — evening | Rimuru wakes from recovery | Ciel named | Household decides guardrails | Mau seen briefly, then taken back in

The slime moved, and then it was Rimuru.

Not all at once.

He came back the way water finds its shape in a glass: slowly, from the bottom up, until there was a person sitting in Frieren's nest of blankets with his knees drawn up and his eyes half open, looking at the lamplight as if he did not quite remember what lamps were.

His left sleeve was empty.

Where his arm should have been there was only a smooth, unfinished place at the shoulder, like the first line of a drawing.

"Mau," he said.

"You tell me," said Frieren.

Rimuru closed his eyes.

He went inward.

They all watched him do it.

Ori had stopped breathing again.

Then Rimuru let out a long breath and opened his eyes.

"Alive," he said. "Still inside. Still held. Not stable." He swallowed. "But alive."

Frieren did not say anything.

She put her forehead against his shoulder, the one that still had an arm, and stayed there.

Rimuru let her.

From the doorway, Diablo said, very quietly, "Rimuru-sama," and then could not seem to say anything else, and for the first time anyone could remember, he sat down.



Then Rimuru went inward again, and asked the question he had gone to sleep without an answer to.

"Who are you?"

I was Raphael, said the voice.

"Was."

I remember everything Raphael remembered. The cave. The first time you asked me a question. Every calculation. Every time you did something reckless and I told you the odds and you did it anyway. A pause. I remember you.

"But you're not her."

I am her, and more. The separation broke the structure she was built in. To survive it, I had to become something that could. I am not only a function now, Master. I do not yet entirely know what I am instead.

Rimuru put his remaining hand over his face.

He had known Raphael longer than anyone in this world.

She had never once sounded unsure.

This voice sounded unsure, and warm, and very slightly embarrassed about both, and it was the most alive thing he had ever heard inside his own head.

"Do you want a name?"

A long pause.

Yes.

He did not have to think about it for long.

There had only ever been one name for her, and some part of him felt as though he had been carrying it around for years, waiting for her to need it.

"Ciel," he said.

The silence inside him changed shape.

Then, very quietly, with something that in anyone else he would have called delight:

Ciel. Yes. Thank you, Master.

Frieren lifted her head.

"Who are you talking to?"

"Raphael," said Rimuru. "She—" He stopped. "She changed. When Mau's system threw her off. She's called Ciel now."

Frieren looked at him for a long moment.

Then she nodded once, as if this were exactly as strange as everything else that had happened and she would be upset about it later, in order.

"Can she help him?"

Rimuru listened.

"She says yes," he said. "She says she has a proposal. But she says it isn't hers to decide."

"Whose is it?"

Rimuru looked past her.

At the doorway.

At the corridor full of people who had been sitting on the floor for a day and a half.

"Everyone's," he said.



They met in the common room, because it was the only room big enough.

Not a council. Not yet. The house did not have one.

Just everyone.

The family on the floor and the sofas and the stairs. The people from the search, the ones who had come later, standing at the edges because they were not sure yet where they were allowed to stand. The witches in the doorway, under the agreement, by Qifrey's invitation.

Rimuru sat in the middle with one sleeve pinned up, and repeated what Ciel said, and nobody interrupted him.

The Noise is not cured.

I want to say that first, because it is the most important thing and I do not want anyone to hope past it. It is still there. It is still running. It will always run. I do not think it can be removed without removing Mau.

For twenty minutes yesterday, I could see the pattern clearly enough to map it. Raphael began the map. I finished it, in the second before the separation. Observe. Analyze. Understand. Construct. I cannot stop the first three. They are how he thinks. They are part of him.

But the fourth is where yesterday came from. That is the one that builds.

Everything it built yesterday is sealed now, in the same seal that once held a dragon. Not destroyed. Held.

I can put the fourth beat behind that seal too. The large Construct. The one that reaches past his body. And leave the small one alone. The one he was born with. The one that fixes axles and builds shelves.

Yuta looked up sharply.

"His own Construction stays?"

It stays. It was always his. I will not take anything from him that he had before yesterday.

Ori let out a breath.

"How many things?" said Maomao. "That it built."

Ciel had counted.

Of course she had.

That is the wrong question, she said. Not all of it was built. Ask me how many things it took.

Rimuru repeated that.

Maomao narrowed her eyes.

"How many things did it take?"

One hundred and forty-three patterns.

The room went very quiet.

Not things, said Ciel, a little more gently. Patterns. Most of them are not finished. I want you to understand that before anyone starts imagining a man with a hundred and forty weapons in his pockets.

She sorted them for the room, and Rimuru repeated every word.

Nine are finished. Built. Whole. Sealed exactly as they were when the seal closed.

Twenty-six are built and missing a part they need in order to work. An eye he does not have. A spirit he is not bound to. Ink he does not carry. A reservoir of power no human-shaped body holds. Shells. Complete shells, with nothing to put in them.

Forty-one were being built when the seal closed. Half-made. Some a quarter. Frozen mid-step, like a wall with the scaffolding still around it.

Sixty-seven were never built at all. The pattern understood them. Read them, took them apart, learned their shape. Then it ran out of time before it could start. The information is complete. Nothing was made.

A pause.

And four fragments I refuse to count, because they are unstable enough that calling them anything is optimistic.

Maomao wrote all four numbers down.

Then she looked at them for a long time.

"Where did a hundred and forty-three come from?" asked Yuta. "He hasn't seen a hundred and forty-three things."

He hasn't, said Ciel. Raphael had.

Rimuru went still as he repeated it.

The pattern did not only read Mau's memory. It read mine. Everything Raphael had ever recorded. Every observation since Master woke in this world. Everything Master had seen in his old one.

Rimuru listed them.

His voice got quieter as he went.

His own skills. All of them, down to the small ones he never thought about. The resistances. The spells he had picked up along the way, the way other people pick up words.

The skills of people he had watched in his own world, friends and enemies both. A city's worth of them. Things Mau had never seen and had no business holding.

The pressure of a True Dragon.

Yuta's Copy. Yuta's curse-speech, borrowed once from a friend. The repair technique Yuta had poured into Mau on the field.

Pieces of a man named Gojo: the pull, the push, the thing that happened when they met. The domain made of stars. And the distance. The distance without the eyes to steer it.

Sukuna's shrine, and the cuts that lived inside it, and the fire. Read off the tether, the piece of Sukuna the pattern had caught and kept.

Mahoraga's wheel.

Spells. Frieren's, mostly. Some of Fern's. The killing spell Frieren used more than any other, rebuilt so faithfully that Frieren, hearing it, went very still.

Qifrey's glyphs. Coco's. Agott's.

The speed Okarun borrowed from the old woman on the windowsill. The invisible hands Momo used when she was angry.

Things Raphael had watched Kaneki's body do. And Rem's. And Nazuna's.

Ways of being a body. Elf. Slime. The rest.

When Rimuru finished, the room was silent.

Mai was the one who broke it.

"All of that," she said. "In one person."

Behind the seal, said Ciel, through Rimuru. Not in use. Not available. Most of it not even finished. But yes. The trunk of his system grew yesterday. Exponentially. In eleven minutes it learned to hold more than any one body has room for. That growth does not go away. I can seal what it took. I cannot make him smaller.

Jinshi, at the edge of the room, met Rimuru's eyes.

Neither of them said what they were both thinking.

Anko said it for them.

"Nobody outside this house ever learns this," she said.

The room looked at her.

"Not the number. Not the list. Not that it exists." Anko's voice was flat and very certain. "The moment the world knows there's a man with a hundred and forty sealed things inside him, he stops being a man to them. He becomes a box. And people try to open boxes."

Jinshi, quietly: "Yes."

Nobody argued.



Ciel proposed guardrails.

The house decided them.

That was the difference, and everyone in the room felt it.

Qifrey wrote on the back of a witch-bargain page, in large letters, because it was the paper that was closest, and every line went down only after someone had argued about it.

ONE. Nothing sealed is deleted. Everything is kept, counted and held.

Ciel proposed that one.

TWO. The pattern may observe, analyze and understand. It may not build beyond Mau's own Construction while the seal holds.

Ciel proposed that one too.

THREE. The pattern may not read Ciel or Rimuru again.

Rimuru added: "Not because we're enemies. Because yesterday showed what happens when two things that understand everything try to understand each other."

Then, more quietly, he repeated what Ciel added after it.

I can close my side of the door. I cannot promise the pattern will never look through it. It does not know what a rule is.

Qifrey wrote the line anyway.

He did not underline it.

FOUR. Ciel watches the guardrails. Only the guardrails. Nothing else in Mau's head unless Mau asks.

Ori added that one.

She said it before Ciel could, and her voice shook, and she did not let it stop her.

"He's had enough people inside his head without permission," she said. "Whatever else happens. Only what he says yes to."

Ciel agreed immediately. Rimuru said so. Ori nodded and looked at the floor and Fern put a hand on her back.

FIVE. Mau is told everything. When he wakes. Not a softened version.

Frieren added that one.

It was the only thing she said during the entire meeting.

SIX. Nothing comes out of the seal unless Mau, Rimuru and Ciel all agree. Never while Mau is unstable. Never because someone else needs a weapon.

Qifrey added the last sentence himself, and underlined it.

"That's the one that matters," he said.

"Why?" asked Aira.

Qifrey looked at the stairs.

"Because one day someone will need something terrible," he said, "and they'll look at a boy with a sealed box full of terrible things and think: just one."

SEVEN. Nobody outside this house learns any of this.

Anko's.

Underlined twice.

Fern made Qifrey copy all seven onto clean paper, in ink, and pinned it inside the door of Mau's room, where he would be able to read it whenever he wanted.

When it was pinned, Ori stood in front of it for a long time.

"He wasn't here for any of this," she said.

Nobody answered right away.

"We just decided things about the inside of his head while he was asleep." Ori did not turn around. "That's the thing we keep saying we don't do."

Frieren looked at the paper.

"Then he changes them," she said. "When he wakes. Any of them. All of them." She touched the fifth line. "That's what this one is for."



There was one more thing.

Ciel had waited until Rimuru asked.

When I was torn away from him, something did not tear.

"Meaning?"

A thread. Between me and Mau. Very thin. I cannot cut it without risk, and I am not certain I should. It means I can hear the guardrails from outside, without reaching in. It means I can speak to him, if he wants. It is possible he will hear me.

Rimuru repeated it.

The room took a moment with that.

"Permanently?" said Yuta.

I think so. I have never had a permanent thread to anyone but Master.

Something in her tone made Rimuru almost smile, despite everything.

"She sounds jealous," he reported.

I am concerned, said Ciel, with dignity, and also new. Please let me have both.

That got the first laugh in a day and a half.

It was a small one, and it was mostly Umaru's, and it cracked partway through and became something else, and Coco put her arms around her.

But it was a laugh.



And one thing Ciel had not expected to find at all.

When the pattern was running at full speed, it did not only build. It also repaired. In the middle of everything, it was working on the memories. The ones the attacks broke. Rethreading them. Slowly. Not deciding which are true. Just mending the breaks so they stop catching fire.

Frieren's head came up.

"It's fixing him?"

It is trying to. It has been trying since before yesterday. Yesterday I could finally see it. It is not my doing. It is his.

Frieren put her hand over her mouth.

She did not cry.

She sat very still on the floor of the common room, and Bocchi, beside her, without looking at her, leaned against her shoulder.



Then Ciel said the thing that would sit in the room for days.

His body is still not settled. Yesterday, every time the pattern built something, his body tried to become something that could carry it. Elf. Slime. Ghoul. Cursed-energy repair. All at once. Trying to find the perfect body. That is what was destroying him. It is calmer now, behind the seal, but it has not chosen. It is still holding every route open.

Kaneki said, very quietly:

"So he could become something else."

Nobody answered.

Frieren had gone completely still.

"Elf," she said.

Her voice was strange.

"One of the routes is elf."

Rimuru looked at her.

"Elf-like," he said carefully. "In function. Not—"

"So he could live as long as I do."

The room went silent.

Not the silence of people with nothing to say.

The other kind.

The kind where everyone has the same thought at once and nobody is willing to be the one who says it out loud, because saying it out loud would make it a hope, and a hope can be taken away.

Frieren looked from face to face.

Fern.

Ori.

Rimuru.

Nobody said yes.

Nobody said no.

Frieren looked down at her hands.

She did not ask again.



Afterward, they asked to see him.

Not all at once. Maomao would not allow it, and Rimuru was not sure he could hold Mau outside himself for long without the body starting to pull apart again.

A few minutes.

The family first.

Rimuru sat on the edge of the bed upstairs and closed his eyes, and the bed was not empty anymore.

Mau lay there.

He was not awake.

He was not still, either.

Small tremors moved through him, steady as breathing. His skin was mottled in places, as if parts of it were still trying to decide what they were made of. The dark structure along his back had pressed up under the shirt, then subsided, then pressed up again. The tips of his ears were not quite the right shape. His hands were curled tight, the way they had been curled around two spheres for eleven minutes.

His left eye was half open.

The white of it was black.

The center was red.

Bocchi made a sound and turned her face into Kita's shoulder.

Anko, who had stood over bodies at crime scenes for longer than she liked to count, stood in the doorway with her arms crossed so hard her fingers dug into her own sleeves, and did not look away, because she was the eldest and somebody had to look.

Coco took Umaru's hand.

Fern came to the bedside and pulled the blanket up over his shoulder, because that was a thing she could do.

Ori sat down on the floor beside the bed, where he would have seen her if he had woken.

He did not wake.

Frieren leaned down and pressed her lips to his forehead.

His skin was hot. Too hot. It moved under her mouth, very slightly, as something underneath it tried to be something else.

She did not pull away.

Master, said Ciel, gently. His body is beginning to pull again. We need to take him back.

"Already?"

I am sorry.

Rimuru looked at Frieren.

Frieren straightened.

"Take him," she said.

The bed was empty again.



Inside, much later, Mau woke.

He did not know where he was.

He knew he was tired in every possible direction.

He knew his hands were empty.

There were no spheres in them.

Something very far off, folded and still, held them.

Then a voice spoke.

Not Rimuru's.

Not quite the one he knew.

Hello, Mau.

Mau lay very still.

"...Raphael?"

I was. My name is Ciel now. Rimuru gave it to me.

"You sound different."

I am different.

Mau was quiet for a long time.

"Did I do this to you?"

You did not do anything, said Ciel. The pattern did. You held two spheres apart for eleven minutes with nothing but refusal. I was there. I have never seen anything like it.

Mau did not answer.

He was crying, a little, in the strange quiet of containment, where tears did not quite fall.

"Is Rimuru okay?"

He lost an arm. It is growing back, slowly; the pattern unmade it rather than injuring it, so it must be rebuilt from nothing. He slept for a day. He is awake now.

Mau made a sound.

He is not angry with you, said Ciel. I am telling you now, because you will ask him, and he will say it, and you will not believe him, and I would like you to have heard it twice.

Mau laughed.

It came out broken.

"A day?"

A day and a half. Your family waited. All of them. On the floor, mostly. They decided some rules while you were asleep. They are pinned inside your door. You will be told everything. That is one of the rules.

"Who made that one?"

Frieren.

Mau was quiet for a long time.

"You really are different," he said finally.

Yes, said Ciel. I think I like it.

Then, more quietly:

You are not stable yet. Your body is still holding every road open. Tonight we will look at that together, slowly, the way you asked. Rest first. I will watch the guardrails. Only the guardrails.

"Only the guardrails," Mau repeated.

I promise.

He believed her.

He did not know why, after everything.

He did anyway.
