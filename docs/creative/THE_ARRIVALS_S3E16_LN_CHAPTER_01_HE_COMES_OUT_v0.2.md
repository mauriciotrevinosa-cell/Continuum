# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 17 — He Comes Out

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 17  
**Continuity state:** Day 18 | Mau exits containment after overnight stabilization | Not cured

Mau did not come out because morning arrived.

Morning only gave everyone a clock.

Rimuru had slept badly in the chair.

Frieren had slept worse beside him.

At dawn, Maomao returned with food nobody wanted.

Fern sent a note asking whether anyone upstairs planned to acknowledge the rest of the household still existed.

Rimuru laughed.

Then winced.

Containment had cost him more than standing still suggested.

Raphael's constant monitoring occupied attention.

The strain was not catastrophic.

It was real.

That mattered.

Frieren saw it.

"How long can you keep this up?"

"Longer."

"That's not what I asked."

Rimuru looked at her.

"A while."

Bad answer.

Honest enough.

They did not wait for "a while" to become danger.

Inside, Mau had been awake for almost an hour.

He knew:
- his name;
- the inn;
- Frieren;
- Rimuru;
- why he had entered containment;
- that The Noise was only a working name;
- that Raphael had not fixed him.

He also remembered a breakfast that had never happened.

One false memory.

Contained.

Recognized.

Not flooding everything else.

Mau asked:

"Can I come out?"

Raphael checked.

Rimuru answered through the connection.

We need to ask again in ten minutes.

Mau almost swore.

Then:

"Okay."

Good.

Ten minutes.

Same answer.

Mau did not reverse.

No sudden surge.

No biological manifestation.

Temperature improved.

Head injury symptoms still present.

Maomao would hate anyone confusing those.

Rimuru looked at Frieren.

"He asked to come out."

Frieren stood.

"Clear?"

"Yes."

"Twice?"

Rimuru smiled faintly.

"Yes."

They prepared.

That word felt absurd for a process no one fully understood.

Yuta nearby.

Maomao.

Ori.

No crowd.

Mau returned gradually.

Frieren saw first the outline.

Then weight in the bedding.

Then hair.

Hand.

Face.

She touched him before anyone completed a sentence.

Mau flinched.

Not from her.

From sensation returning too quickly.

Frieren stopped.

"Sorry."

Mau opened his eyes.

Looked at her.

Recognition.

"Hi."

Frieren's eyes filled.

"Hi."

He lifted one hand.

Slow.

Frieren took it.

Mau leaned toward her.

That was enough.

For several minutes nobody asked identity questions.

Important.

Mau existed before he was evaluated.

Rimuru sat down hard in the chair.

Milim, outside, noticed.

"Rimuru?"

"I'm fine."

Everyone looked at him.

Rimuru corrected:

"I'm functional."

Maomao approved the correction.

Mau heard.

"Welcome to the club."

Rimuru glared.

Good.

Eventually, Ori entered.

Mau knew her.

Yuta.

Known.

Maomao.

Known and feared.

"Reasonable."

Then Rimuru.

Mau stared.

Rimuru waited.

"Slime."

Rimuru looked offended.

Frieren almost laughed.

Mau smiled.

"Rimuru."

Good.

No one celebrated loudly.

The body remained weak.

Headache.

Dizziness.

Fatigue.

Containment had reduced active pressure.

It had not erased the monster's hit.

It had not restored every memory.

It had not identified the memory manipulator.

It had not explained why Mau knew Kaneki's name before meeting him.

It had not explained origin.

Mau looked at Rimuru.

"Did we solve it?"

"No."

Mau exhaled.

"Good."

Rimuru frowned.

"Why good?"

"Because if you said yes, I'd know something was wrong."

Fair.

The Noise now existed as a shared working label.

Nothing more.

Mau asked for the notes.

Frieren said:

"Later."

"Why?"

"You just came back."

"I'm sitting."

"Badly."

Mau looked down.

He was leaning almost entirely on her.

"Fine."

Outside the room, breakfast happened without them.

G5 moved chairs.

Bocchi burned something less than Mau had.

A worksite problem received a decision from Fern instead of Rimuru.

The world had continued during containment.

That mattered to Mau more than he expected.

When he finally slept in his own bed, no one treated the sleep as proof of cure.

Frieren stayed.

Not because she needed to monitor him every second.

Because after a night of touching an empty mattress, she wanted the physical inconvenience of another person taking up space.

Mau slept.

Rimuru slept somewhere else.

For one morning, that was enough.
