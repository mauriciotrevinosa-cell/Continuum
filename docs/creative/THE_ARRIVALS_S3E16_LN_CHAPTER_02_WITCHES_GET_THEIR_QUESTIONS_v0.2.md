# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 18 — Witches Get Their Questions

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 18  
**Continuity state:** Day 19 | Mau recovering | Witch bargain resumes under Mau's control

The witches came back exactly when the agreement said they could.

Mau almost respected that.

Almost.

Fern met them downstairs.

"No wandering."

One witch looked offended.

"We don't wander."

Another looked at her.

"You absolutely wander."

Fern pointed upstairs.

"Thirty minutes if Mau agrees."

They did.

Mau chose thirty.

Not because thirty had medical meaning.

Because twenty felt too short and an hour felt like giving his entire morning away.

Frieren stood near the door.

"I'll stay."

Mau looked at her.

Then changed his mind.

"No."

Frieren's expression sharpened.

"No?"

"I want to do one of these without you in the room."

She hated the sentence.

Mau saw.

"This isn't me pushing you away."

"I know."

"I need to know I can answer questions without checking your face for whether the answer is safe."

That landed.

Frieren looked at Ori.

Ori did not volunteer automatically.

Mau asked:

"Stay?"

Ori nodded.

Qifrey stayed too.

Different reasons.

Witness.

System knowledge.

Not emotional ownership.

Frieren left.

Closed the door.

Then remained close enough in the hallway to hear if Mau called.

Compromise.

The first witch sat.

"No dreams."

"Correct."

"No touching memories."

"Correct."

"Questions."

"Yes."

She looked at Mau.

"Do you remember being inside Rimuru?"

"Yes."

"Clearly?"

Mau thought.

"Clearer than parts of the last week."

"Do you trust that clarity?"

Mau paused.

Good question.

"More than some things. Not absolutely."

The witch smiled.

"Better."

Another:

"Does the name The Noise feel old?"

Mau frowned.

"The phrase? No."

"The sensation?"

"Yes."

"How old?"

"I don't know."

"Before Continuum?"

"I don't know."

"Before the memory problem?"

"Yes."

That answer changed the room.

Qifrey wrote.

Ori looked at Mau.

Mau continued quickly.

"That doesn't mean the current crisis is unrelated."

The witch nodded.

"No one said it did."

"Good."

Question:

"Did the memory manipulation ever present as Observe, Analyze, Understand, Construct?"

Mau thought.

"No."

"Did The Noise tell you to leave the inn?"

"No."

"Did it tell you Frieren lied?"

"No."

"Did it produce the false bridge?"

Mau's mouth tightened.

"I don't know."

Important distinction.

The memories and certainties had driven the escape.

The Noise, as far as Mau could support, had not issued commands.

Qifrey wrote in large letters:

DO NOT CONFLATE MEMORY MANIPULATOR WITH THE NOISE.

One witch reached over and underlined it.

Mau stared.

"I hate agreeing with you."

"Mutual."

Another witch asked:

"When Raphael observed it, did it react?"

Mau closed his eyes.

"I couldn't tell."

"Did it hide?"

"I couldn't tell."

"Did it speak?"

"No."

"Did you feel watched?"

Mau opened his eyes.

"By Raphael."

"Not what I asked."

He looked at her.

Longer pause.

"No."

Good.

Then:

"Do you think The Noise is you?"

The room changed.

Ori looked at Mau.

Qifrey stopped writing.

Mau stared at his own hands.

Could a process be him without being identity?

Could it be part of him?

A tool?

A scar?

A system?

A remnant from a life he could not remember?

"I don't know."

The witch did not fill the silence.

Mau appreciated that.

Then she asked:

"If it were part of you, would you want it removed?"

Mau looked up.

"No."

Immediate enough to surprise him.

"Why?"

"Because we don't know what removing it means."

The witch waited.

Mau added:

"And because I don't want everyone solving frightening parts of me by cutting them out."

Ori's eyes filled.

Qifrey wrote that one too.

Thirty minutes ended.

Mau looked at the clock.

"Done."

The witch stopped mid-question.

No argument.

Agreement honored.

That mattered almost as much as anything they had learned.

Outside, Frieren stood when the door opened.

She looked at Mau.

Not at the witches.

"Okay?"

"Tired."

"Do you want to tell me?"

Mau thought.

"Some."

Frieren nodded.

They walked downstairs slowly.

Mau told her about the non-conflation.

About the old sensation.

About not knowing whether The Noise was part of him.

He did not tell her every question.

Frieren did not ask for the rest.

That was new too.

Agency repeated until it stopped feeling ceremonial and started becoming ordinary.
