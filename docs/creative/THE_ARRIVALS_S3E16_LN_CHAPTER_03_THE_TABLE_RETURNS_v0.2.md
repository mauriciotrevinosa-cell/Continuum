# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 26 — The Table Returns

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 26  
**Continuity state:** Day 21–22 | Collaborative reconstruction begins | Old inn table becomes evidence space

The dining table became evidence again.

Nobody announced it.

Fern cleared breakfast.

Mau brought down the notebook.

Frieren brought old notes.

Rimuru brought dated records, carrying them in two hands again. The left one was new enough that he kept flexing it when he thought nobody was looking.

Then the table stopped being a table.

Cards.

Paper.

Objects.

Questions.

This time nobody expected the table to save Mau.

That helped.

The first attempt at categories failed in eleven minutes.

TRUE.

FALSE.

UNKNOWN.

Mau stared at the three labels.

"No."

Rimuru looked up.

"Why?"

"Because false is doing too much."

Frieren understood first.

A memory could be:
- an invented event;
- a real event with altered dialogue;
- a dream later remembered as lived;
- someone else's information placed in Mau's ownership;
- an authentic event with emotional weight changed afterward.

All of those felt different once named.

So they started again.

PROVABLY FALSE.

REAL / ALTERED.

DREAM-DERIVED.

WRONG OWNERSHIP / IMPLANTED POSSIBILITY.

AUTHENTIC / EMOTIONAL WEIGHT SUSPECT.

RECOVERED.

UNCERTAIN.

The last category was written largest.

Not intentionally.

It simply needed more space.

Bocchi arrived with tea and stopped.

"Oh."

Mau looked at the table.

"Welcome to my personality."

Bocchi's face panicked immediately.

"No."

"Joke."

Frieren, without looking up:

"Bad."

Fair.

They started with easy things.

Dates.

Travel routes.

Meals that had receipts or witnesses.

Small enough to verify without hurting.

Mau remembered a supply run with Yuta.

Yuta remembered it.

Route log matched.

REAL.

Mau hated how relieved that made him.

Then a memory of Momo stealing fruit.

Momo objected.

"I did not."

Three people looked at her.

Momo corrected:

"I do not remember."

Different.

Good.

No forced verdict.

UNCERTAIN.

Coco brought drawings.

Not proof of Mau's internal experience.

Proof that certain places, clothes and arrangements existed.

One sketch showed Mau sitting with Frieren beside an unfinished storage frame.

Mau remembered the scene.

Frieren remembered the scene.

Coco had drawn it that day.

REAL, probably.

Then Mau remembered Frieren saying:

"I need you here."

Frieren frowned.

"I don't remember saying that."

Mau's chest tightened.

The table stopped.

Coco checked the drawing.

No dialogue.

No proof.

Mau looked at Frieren.

"Would you have?"

"Yes."

Not enough.

Could be real.

Could be a later emotional rewrite.

They placed it:

AUTHENTIC / EMOTIONAL WEIGHT SUSPECT.

Mau hated that category most.

By lunch, everyone was tired.

Good.

They stopped.

That mattered.

No marathon until Mau collapsed.

After eating, the group changed.

Bocchi came.

Kita.

Ori.

Aira.

Rem.

Kaneki.

Not all at once.

People contributed the memories they actually owned.

Rem:
river route.

Cap.

Field timing.

Nothing before meeting Mau.

Kaneki:
biological sequence.

What Mau called him.

Nothing about Frieren.

Aira:
movement.

Shelter.

What Mau said while half awake.

No theories.

Diablo:
the mismatch in the field signal during the first disappearance.

Not internal truth.

The witches:
seam observations.

No final verdicts.

Ciel:
only what containment allowed her to observe, and the guardrails she now kept.

No TRUE/FALSE scanner.

That rule got written in capital letters after someone asked for the third time.

RAPHAEL DOES NOT CERTIFY AUTOBIOGRAPHY.

Mau approved.

Late afternoon, the table looked worse than morning.

More cards.

More uncertainty.

Fewer simple stories.

Mau stood over it.

"So my identity is a committee."

Bocchi began:

"No—"

He raised both hands.

"I know. Bad joke."

Frieren looked at the table.

"Your identity is not here."

Mau stopped.

She pointed at the cards.

"Evidence is here."

Different.

Mau looked around.

People he loved.

People he barely knew.

People who did not love him but had seen something.

Records.

Objects.

Contradictions.

No one owned him.

They only helped prevent uncertainty from becoming a cliff.

Mau sat.

"Tomorrow?"

Frieren looked at him.

"Tomorrow."

The table remained messy overnight.

Nobody cleaned it into certainty.
