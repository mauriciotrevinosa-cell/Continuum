# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 30 — Visiting Hours

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 30  
**Continuity state:** Day 23 | Mau's mind clearer than his body | Visitors one at a time | Old inn

On the twenty-first day Maomao nailed a sheet of paper to Mau's door.

VISITORS.

One at a time.

Ten minutes.

No questions about memory.

No crying on the patient.

Below that, in different handwriting, someone had added:

No karaoke.

Below that, in a third hand, someone had crossed it out.

Mau read the sheet through the open door from his bed.

"Who's that for?"

Maomao did not look up from the tray she was preparing.

"In case."

"In case of what?"

"In case people come."

Mau looked out of the window.

The sky was clear for the first time in days.

Good weather for the worksite.

"It's a workday," he said. "Nobody's going to come."

Frieren, sitting by the window with a notebook open on her knee, said nothing.

She turned to a clean page.



Okarun came first.

Too early.

Maomao let him in anyway, because he had spent most of the last two days sitting on the porch and she was tired of stepping over him.

He stood beside the bed with his hands in his pockets.

"Hey."

"Hey."

They looked at each other.

Neither of them was good at this part.

"So," Okarun said. "You owe me a run."

Mau winced.

"I heard."

"Maomao and Kusuri. At night. Through a forest. Maomao told me where to put my feet the whole way."

"Sorry."

"Don't."

Okarun shifted his weight.

"Next time you need to go check something real in the middle of nowhere," he said, "take somebody who can run."

Mau looked at him.

"Okay."

"I mean it."

"I know you do."

Okarun nodded once, satisfied, and then sat on the end of the bed and spent the rest of his ten minutes explaining a theory about the monster's teeth that Mau did not follow at all and enjoyed completely.

Frieren wrote something down.



Anko came at the exact moment Okarun's ten minutes ended, as if she had been timing it from the hall.

She had been.

She sat down with a folder.

"I've brought you something to help you rest."

"What is it?"

"The full route report from both searches." She opened it. "Read aloud. In order."

"That sounds like torture."

"It's medicinal."

She began.

Her voice could have flattened a landscape.

By the second page Mau was laughing hard enough that Maomao looked in to check whether something was wrong.

By the fourth page Anko had stopped reading the report and started reading him a list instead.

Every stupid thing he had done since the first morning he went missing.

Numbered.

With commentary.

"Item nine. Did not bring a proper pack."

"I brought a flask."

"Item ten. Brought a flask."

Mau put his face in his hands.

At ten minutes exactly Anko closed the folder and stood up.

At the door she stopped.

Leaned back in.

"I'm keeping a file on you," she said. "It's very long."

Then she was gone.

Mau stared at the door.

Frieren wrote something down.



Umaru and Coco came in together.

Maomao objected.

"One at a time."

"We're small," Coco said. "We count as one."

Maomao looked at Mau.

Mau looked at Maomao.

"They count as one," Mau said.

Maomao left without a word, which they all understood to be the highest available form of permission.

Coco sat on the chair.

Umaru did not sit on the chair.

Umaru climbed onto the foot of the bed with her blanket and curled up there as if she had always meant to, which made Mau feel something he could not name.

Coco looked at the hair Mau had pulled down over the left side of his face.

She had heard. Everyone had heard.

"Can we see?"

Mau hesitated.

Then, because it was Coco and Umaru and they had sat on the bottom stair for eight hours, he pushed his hair back.

His eye was his own. Brown, tired, a little bloodshot.

Then, under two people looking at it, the edges darkened, and the red came up through the middle, and it was not his own anymore.

Coco stared.

Umaru sat up inside her blanket.

"That," said Umaru, with deep, sincere admiration, "is the coolest thing that has ever happened in this house."

Mau stared at her.

"It's a medical valve."

"It's a cool medical valve."

Coco nodded seriously.

"Very cool," she agreed. "Does it hurt?"

"No."

"Then it's just cool."

The red faded. The black drew back. Mau let his hair fall forward again, slower this time, and found that something in his chest had loosened that he had not noticed was tight.

Coco produced a list.

"We've decided on your penalty."

"My what?"

"For making everyone worry. Twice."

Mau looked at Umaru for help.

Umaru shrugged inside the blanket.

"It was unanimous."

"Who voted?"

"Us."

"That's two people."

"Unanimously."

Coco cleared her throat.

"You will sing. In front of everyone. Something embarrassing. When Maomao says you're allowed out of bed."

"No."

"Yes."

"The door says no karaoke."

"Someone crossed that out," Umaru said, and did not explain who.

Mau tried to negotiate.

He failed at every stage.

By the end he had agreed to one song, of their choosing, with dancing optional and also mandatory.

Coco wrote it down with great ceremony.

Umaru stayed curled at the end of the bed for the rest of their ten minutes, and then for the next visitor's ten minutes, and nobody made her move.

Frieren wrote something down.



Bocchi stood in the doorway for a long time before she came in.

Kita stood behind her.

Bocchi was holding her guitar.

"...Um."

Mau sat up a little.

"Hi, Bocchi."

"I—"

She stopped.

Kita put one hand on the small of her back.

Not pushing.

Just there.

"I learned something," Bocchi said, very fast. "While you were gone. I practiced. Shizuka said you would want to hear it. So. Um."

"I want to hear it," Mau said.

Bocchi sat on the floor.

Not the chair.

The floor.

She played.

It was not a song Mau knew.

It might not have been a song anyone knew yet.

It started very quietly and then forgot to be quiet, and somewhere in the middle Bocchi stopped looking at her hands and closed her eyes, and the room did not feel like a sickroom for a while.

When she finished nobody said anything.

Then Mau said:

"When did you learn that?"

Bocchi went scarlet.

"...While you were gone."

"She practiced every night," Kita said. "Every single night. I timed it."

"Kita-chan—"

"Seven nights."

Bocchi tried to disappear into the guitar.

Mau was quiet for a moment.

"Thank you," he said.

Bocchi nodded without looking up.

Kita helped her stand.

At the door Kita glanced back at Mau, and then at her girlfriend, and smiled the kind of smile that meant she had known exactly how this would go.

Frieren wrote something down.



Himmel came after lunch.

He sat in the chair as though he had been invited to a long dinner and intended to enjoy it.

"I hear you've been making Frieren walk," he said.

Mau winced.

"Sixteen hours, the first time."

"I've seen her refuse to walk sixteen minutes for an entire kingdom," Himmel said comfortably. "She once spent a whole afternoon arguing that a demon lord's castle could wait until she found a spell for removing rust from statues."

From the window, without looking up:

"That isn't how it happened."

"It's exactly how it happened."

"It was a spell for cleaning statues. Rust was secondary."

Himmel spread his hands at Mau as if to say: you see.

Mau laughed until his head hurt and he had to stop.

Himmel waited, patient.

Then, more quietly:

"She walked sixteen hours for you," he said. "In all the years I knew her, I never once saw her do that for anyone."

He did not look at Frieren while he said it.

Frieren did not look up.

Her pen had stopped moving.

After a moment, it started again.
