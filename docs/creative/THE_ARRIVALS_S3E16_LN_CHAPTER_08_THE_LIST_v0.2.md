# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 24 — The List

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 24  
**Continuity state:** Day 21 — afternoon into evening | Visitors continue | Old inn

Stark did not know what to say.

He came in, stood beside the bed, opened his mouth, closed it, and looked around the room for something else to do.

The window latch rattled whenever the wind moved.

It had been rattling for three days.

Stark took a small knife out of his pocket and fixed it.

It took him most of his ten minutes.

When he was done he tested it twice, nodded, and turned to leave.

At the door he stopped.

"Fern didn't sleep either," he said to the doorframe. "Both times."

He paused.

"Don't tell her I said."

Then he left.

Mau looked at the silent latch for a long time.

Frieren wrote something down.



Seiko did not ask Maomao's permission.

She arrived with a covered bowl, pulled the chair closer to the bed than anyone else had, sat down, and took the lid off.

"Eat."

"I ate lunch."

"You ate half of lunch. I asked."

Mau ate.

Seiko watched him the way old women had been watching young men eat since before there were inns.

"Too thin," she said. "Your face was better in the stories."

"People keep telling me that."

"Then people keep being right."

He finished the bowl.

She took it back and did not leave.

"My granddaughter set a place for you at dinner," she said. "Every night you were gone."

Mau stopped.

"She what?"

"Your place. And the girl's. Every night. Cup and everything."

"She never—"

"No. She wouldn't." Seiko folded her hands over the empty bowl. "So somebody had better tell you, and I'm old enough not to care about being thanked."

Mau did not know what to do with his face.

Seiko watched him not know.

Then she reached over and patted his hand twice, briskly, the way one pats a dog that has done something foolish and survived it.

"Eat dinner tonight," she said. "All of it."

"Yes ma'am."

"Hm," said Seiko, who had clearly decided something.

She came back two hours later to make sure he had.

Frieren wrote that down too.



Marin did not visit so much as redecorate.

She came in with an armful of things and Wakana behind her with another armful, and within three minutes the medicine table had a cloth over it, there were wildflowers in a cup on the windowsill, and the blanket on the bed was a better blanket.

"It looked like a hospital," Marin said.

"It is a hospital."

"It doesn't have to look like one. You'll get better faster if you're not staring at bottles."

"Is that medicine?"

"It's common sense."

Mau looked at Frieren for support.

Frieren was looking at the flowers.

She said nothing.

Marin took that as a victory and was correct.

Wakana did not redecorate.

He set a folded coat on the end of the bed.

Mau's coat.

The one he had worn south.

The last time Mau had seen it, it had been torn open along the back.

Now the seam ran clean and straight.

"It was ruined," Mau said.

"It was torn," Wakana said. "That's different."

He turned a little red when Mau looked at him too long.

Marin put her arm through his and steered him out before he could die of it.

"Ten minutes!" she announced from the hall. "We were exactly ten minutes!"

They had been nineteen.



Mikasa came in, checked the window, checked the latch Stark had fixed, checked the door, and looked at Mau.

"You're safe here," she said.

Then she left.

The entire visit lasted under a minute.

Mau found that it had helped more than he could explain.



Maki leaned in the doorway instead of entering.

"Mai asked how you were," she said.

"Mai did?"

"Twice." Maki pushed off the frame. "She'll deny it."

Then she was gone too.



By evening the light had turned gold and Mau was tired in the good way, the way that came from being with people instead of from fighting his own head.

Frieren closed the notebook.

He had been watching her write all day.

"What is that?"

She looked at it.

Then crossed the room and put it in his hands.

It was a list.

Names.

Times.

Small notes beside some of them, in her careful, exact handwriting.

Okarun. Early. Stayed past ten minutes. Explained teeth.

Anko. Report. Then not report. Keeping a file.

Umaru and Coco. "Count as one." Karaoke, one song, unavoidable.

Bocchi and Kita. Bocchi played. Seven nights of practice.

Himmel. Lies about rust.

Stark. Fixed the latch.

Seiko. Twice.

Marin and Wakana. Flowers. Coat.

Mikasa.

Maki. On behalf of Mai.

It went on.

Mau read it to the end.

Then read it again.

"You wrote down everyone who came."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Frieren sat on the edge of the bed.

She took a moment to find the true answer rather than the easy one.

"This morning you said it was a workday," she said. "That nobody would come."

Mau said nothing.

"So I counted," Frieren said. "In case tomorrow you decide the same thing again."

He looked down at the list.

The workday names.

The people who had left the site, or the kitchen, or the porch, or the roof, to stand beside his bed for ten minutes because they wanted to.

None of them had needed anything from him.

He did not know what to do with that.

He had never known what to do with that.

Frieren did not ask him to know.

She took the pen back from where it had rolled against the blanket, turned to the bottom of the page, and added one more line.

Frieren. All day.

Then she gave the notebook back to him.

Mau kept it.
