# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 26 — No. But You Can Stay.

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 26  
**Continuity state:** Day 23 | Mau allowed limited ordinary activity at worksite

Mau's first return to the worksite lasted two hours.

Maomao gave him three rules.

No lifting.

No Construction.

No wandering off alone.

Mau repeated them back with the tone of a prisoner receiving terms of release.

Maomao stared.

"Do you want a fourth?"

"No."

Good.

The road from the inn felt longer than before.

Not because distance had changed.

Because Mau now knew exactly how easily a road could become uncertain.

He caught himself checking landmarks.

Tree with split trunk.

Stone bend.

Stream crossing.

Frieren noticed.

Did not comment.

When the future site came into view, Mau stopped.

More frames.

Covered storage.

A better path around the mud.

One water line marked for later.

Three things built while he was missing.

Seven while he was recovering.

People had kept moving.

That should have been reassuring.

Part of him heard:

They do not need you.

Memory Arc had trained the sentence too well.

Mau stood there long enough that Frieren asked:

"Head?"

"No."

"Then?"

Mau looked at the site.

"They kept building."

"Yes."

He waited.

Frieren did not supply interpretation.

Good.

Mau said:

"Good."

The word took effort.

Then Wakana walked past carrying folded cloth.

Mau's body moved before thought.

"Need help?"

Wakana glanced at the bundle.

"No."

The answer hit harder than it should have.

No task.

No need.

Mau looked away.

Wakana kept walking two steps.

Stopped.

Turned.

"But you can stay."

Mau stared.

Wakana looked confused by the reaction.

"I only mean I don't need help carrying this."

"I know."

Mau's voice came out wrong.

Wakana's expression softened.

Mau sat on an overturned crate.

No invented job.

No courtesy assignment.

Presence without usefulness.

For ten minutes, nobody asked him to do anything.

This was apparently therapy designed by sadists.

Coco arrived.

"What are you doing?"

"Nothing."

"Why?"

"Doctor's orders."

"Maomao told you to do nothing?"

"No. Society did."

Coco sat beside him.

Then Agott arrived because Coco had stopped working.

"What are you doing?"

Coco pointed at Mau.

"Nothing."

Agott looked at Mau.

"Finally."

Mau stared.

Coco laughed.

Qifrey came looking for both students.

Found all three sitting.

Productivity declined further.

Nobody died.

That fact became funnier to Mau than it should have.

Later, Fern arrived with route records.

Mau asked:

"Need help?"

"No."

He nodded.

No emotional collapse.

Progress.

Five minutes later Maomao passed with a box.

"Need—"

"No."

Mau raised both hands.

"Didn't finish."

"Still no."

Bocchi came by carrying coiled cord with Kita.

Mau opened his mouth.

Bocchi pointed.

"No."

Mau looked personally betrayed.

"I hate all of you."

Umaru, from somewhere behind:

"Nobody needs you! Sit!"

Several people laughed.

Mau did too.

The joke only worked because the lesson had stopped being abstract.

He could be present without becoming function.

At some point, a child from one of the temporary G5 groups sat near him and started arranging stones.

No request.

No recognition of importance.

Mau helped move one stone because the child handed it to him.

That was different.

Not need.

Invitation.

By the time Frieren said they should return to the inn, Mau had done almost nothing.

He felt strangely exhausted.

"You okay?"

Frieren asked.

Mau looked at the worksite.

"Yeah."

He meant it.

At the inn, Umaru pointed at a chair as soon as he entered.

"Nobody needs you. Sit."

Mau groaned.

Frieren looked up from his hoodie.

"Stay."

Different word.

Same lesson.

Mau sat.

For once, being unnecessary did not feel exactly like disappearing.
