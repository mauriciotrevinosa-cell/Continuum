# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 26 — Kaneki Doesn't Flinch

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 26  
**Continuity state:** Day 24–25 | Kaneki/Mau relationship moves from rescue witness toward older-brother-like family trust

Mau found Kaneki in the kitchen after midnight.

That sentence sounded more dramatic than the reality.

Kaneki was staring at a bowl.

Mau was staring at the stairs because he had underestimated how much energy stairs required.

Kaneki looked up.

"Need help?"

Mau immediately:

"No."

Kaneki nodded.

No argument.

Good.

Mau made it to the chair.

Sat.

Waited for his body to stop acting like sitting down had been a strategic operation.

Kaneki looked back at the bowl.

Mau looked at it too.

"Bad?"

"Manageable."

The food arrangement Maomao had found was not elegant.

Continuum had adapted some things.

Not enough.

Kaneki could eat without immediate crisis.

That did not make eating ordinary.

Mau knew better than to say:
at least.

They sat in silence.

Then pressure crawled under Mau's shoulder blade.

He froze.

Kaneki noticed before Mau moved.

"Back?"

"Maybe."

"Pain?"

"Pressure."

"Sharp?"

"No."

"Moving?"

Mau waited.

"No."

Kaneki did not stand.

Did not inspect.

Did not turn Mau into a patient because one symptom appeared.

"Want me to look?"

Mau thought.

"Not yet."

"Okay."

Silence again.

The pressure faded.

Mau exhaled.

Kaneki kept eating.

That bothered Mau.

"What?"

Kaneki looked up.

"You didn't react."

"You said it faded."

"No, I mean..."

Mau touched his back.

"The thing."

Kaneki understood.

The manifestation.

The dark structure.

The body changing.

The field camp.

Mau looking at someone he knew without knowing them.

"You've seen worse," Mau said.

Kaneki's expression shifted.

"That's not why."

"Then why?"

Kaneki set the spoon down.

"Because you were scared."

Mau waited.

"So?"

"So I was watching you."

Not the structure.

Mau looked away.

Kaneki continued.

"In the forest, everyone kept asking whether what your body was doing meant something about what you were."

"Reasonable question."

"Medical question. Maybe."

Mau looked back.

Kaneki's eyes were steady.

"It wasn't an identity answer."

There.

The same thing Kaneki had understood when Mau looked less human and more dangerous.

Monstrous was not the same as no longer Mau.

Mau rubbed his thumb against the edge of the table.

"You said that before."

"I know."

"What if you were wrong?"

Kaneki did not answer quickly.

Good.

"I can be wrong about what your body is."

Mau's chest tightened.

Kaneki continued.

"I'm not using your body as proof of who you are."

Mau laughed once.

"Very healthy sentence for someone with your biography."

"Experience."

"Trauma."

"Also experience."

Fair.

Mau looked at the bowl.

Then:

"Thank you."

Kaneki's face changed.

"For tonight?"

"The forest."

"No."

Mau frowned.

"No?"

"Don't turn it into debt."

Kaneki had already told him that.

Mau remembered.

Real memory.

Probably.

He almost asked.

Stopped.

Kaneki saw.

"You're doing it."

"Doing what?"

"Checking whether I said it before."

Mau looked betrayed.

"Everyone knows my face now."

"Your face is loud."

"Frieren says that too."

"She's right."

Terrible.

Mau rested his head against the chair.

"Yuta does this."

"What?"

"Older-brother lecture disguised as normal conversation."

Kaneki looked genuinely uncomfortable.

"I am not competing with Yuta."

"Didn't say you were."

"Good."

Mau smiled.

There it was.

Not replacement.

Not duplicate role.

Yuta had known Mau from nearly the beginning.

Kaneki knew a different edge of him.

One did not reduce the other.

A sound came from the hall.

Small.

Someone trying not to cry.

Both men looked.

A new Arrival stood near the kitchen entrance.

Young.

Had been sleeping in the common room.

One hand over his mouth.

"Sorry."

Kaneki stood first.

Not Mau.

Good.

"Nightmare?"

The boy nodded.

Kaneki asked:

"Want water?"

Another nod.

No questions about source world.

No:
what happened?

Water first.

Mau watched Kaneki hand over the cup.

No performance.

No special insight.

Just someone who knew what it meant to wake afraid of your own mind and body.

The boy sat.

Kaneki sat too.

Mau stayed.

Three people awake after midnight.

No one saved anyone.

Eventually the boy went back to bed.

Kaneki returned to the bowl.

Mau said:

"You're staying."

Kaneki looked at him.

It had not been a question.

Mau realized.

"Sorry. That sounded like I was deciding."

Kaneki thought.

"For now."

"Okay."

"Maybe longer."

Mau looked up.

Kaneki added:

"Don't make it a thing."

Mau raised both hands.

"I've been told."

The pressure under Mau's shoulder did not return.

Before leaving, Kaneki said:

"If it does tonight, wake me."

Mau started:

"You don't have to—"

Kaneki looked at him.

Mau stopped.

Right.

Accepting help was also letting someone choose.

"Okay."

Kaneki nodded.

Older brother was not a title either of them needed to say.

The behavior was already there.
