# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 27 — Looks Fine Is Not Fine

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 27  
**Continuity state:** Day 26+ | Dominant Memory crisis de-escalated | Long-term protocol replaces emergency mode

Mau looked fine.

Nobody used that as evidence anymore.

That sentence became the first line of the new protocol.

Fern hated the word protocol.

"Sounds medical."

"It partly is," Maomao said.

Fern changed the title to:

WHEN MAU SAYS SOMETHING FEELS WRONG.

Mau hated that more.

Good enough.

They listed what actually helped.

Sleep.

Headaches.

Memory mismatches.

Physical pressure along his back.

New Noise fragments.

Too many simultaneous questions.

Whether he wanted company.

Whether he wanted none.

The last two mattered enough to get separate lines.

Care could become surveillance.

They had nearly done that.

During the worst days, someone had always been watching.

Necessary then.

Dangerous if permanent.

Mau read the draft.

"Raphael monitoring?"

Rimuru answered:

"Only what you already agreed to."

"Emergency override?"

They defined it.

Specific.

Narrow.

Loss of consciousness.

Immediate physical danger.

Severe uncontrolled manifestation.

Not:
Mau seems sad.

Good.

Maomao tracked physical recovery.

Kaneki biological changes when Mau asked or symptoms returned.

Ori continuity when requested.

Not automatic reality oracle.

Frieren refused a formal role.

Mau looked surprised.

"Why?"

She looked at him.

"I'm your girlfriend."

"That is a role."

"Not a monitoring role."

Important.

She lived with him.

She could notice.

She could ask.

She did not become official keeper of whether Mau was real.

The witches received scheduled access.

Not surprise visits.

Their agreement gained one additional line:

No questioning during obvious cognitive instability.

Even they accepted.

Diablo's observations stayed field-specific.

No one turned him into supernatural truth detector.

Raphael remained containment-limited for deeper observation.

No casual internal scans because the technology now existed.

The evidence table stayed available.

Not permanent.

Fern demanded half the dining table back.

Mau protested.

Fern won.

Cards moved into boxes.

Uncertain.

False.

Recovered.

Not erased.

Stored.

That act mattered.

The crisis stopped occupying every meal.

At the worksite, progress continued.

Slow.

One cluster had roofs.

Water planning.

Covered storage.

Basic latrine locations.

Enough wall to begin imagining sleep.

Not enough to move everyone.

Good.

The old inn remained home.

No one confused that.

Rimuru brought the latest plan to dinner.

Real dinner.

Food on the table where evidence had lived.

"If we finish water access, latrines, covered food storage and two more enclosed rooms, we could move a first group."

Conversation stopped.

Move.

The word felt bigger than it should have.

Momo recovered first.

"Finally."

Fern:

"We are not moving tomorrow."

Momo deflated.

Rimuru:

"Not everyone at once."

Bocchi looked relieved.

Then embarrassed by relief.

Wakana looked toward the window as if he could see his unfinished workshop through trees.

Maomao asked:

"Sanitation first?"

"Yes."

"Then maybe."

Kaneki asked about food storage.

Rem said nothing.

Her status was still guest.

Aira asked about routes.

G5 voices joined.

Population made the question messy.

Good.

Mau looked around the old inn.

Too many shoes.

Temporary bedding.

Bocchi's guitar.

Ori's cup.

Frieren wearing his clothes.

The recovered McLaren cap hanging where someone had put it after cleaning.

Home.

Then the map.

Future.

No one said:
we already live there.

No one called it home.

Not yet.

Rimuru asked:

"Do we start planning the move?"

Mau felt every eye almost turn toward him.

Almost.

Old habit.

This time he did not answer first.

He looked at Fern.

Fern had already been thinking.

"Yes."

The conversation moved.

Dates.

First wave.

What had to exist before anyone slept there.

Who wanted to remain longer at the inn.

Mau listened.

No one needed his permission.

He was part of the choice.

Different.

Volume Thirteen ended with the household deciding to prepare to leave the old inn.

Not because the old home had failed.

Because it had done its job well enough for them to grow beyond it.
