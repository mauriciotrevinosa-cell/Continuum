# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 33 — Rem Keeps Coming Back

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 33  
**Continuity state:** Post-Memory recovery | Rem still calls herself a guest

Rem had left twice.

She had also come back twice.

Nobody pointed this out.

The third return happened before breakfast.

Momo opened the door.

Rem stood there holding a cloth-wrapped bundle.

"Good morning."

Momo looked at the bundle.

"Food?"

"Tea."

"Also acceptable."

She let Rem in.

No guest ceremony.

No:
welcome back.

Rem knew where the cups were.

That was new.

Mau came downstairs slowly.

Not weak enough to need help.

Not steady enough for Fern to stop noticing.

Rem noticed too.

She did not move toward him.

That was also new.

"Morning," Mau said.

"Morning."

He sat.

Rem placed the tea near Momo.

Then took the chair across from Mau.

For several seconds neither spoke.

Mau liked that she could do that.

Some people saw recovery and filled silence because they were afraid of what silence meant.

Rem could leave it empty.

Ori arrived next.

Hair wrong from sleep.

She saw Rem.

Smiled.

Not surprise.

"You're back."

Rem looked at her.

"I said I might be."

"You always say might."

"That is because I mean might."

Ori considered this.

"Okay."

She sat beside Rem instead of Mau.

Mau noticed.

Again:
did not turn it into a developmental milestone.

Progress.

Frieren entered wearing Mau's hoodie.

Rem's eyes went to it.

Then to Mau.

Mau:

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

Frieren sat next to Mau and took his cup.

Rem blinked.

"Was that his?"

"Yes."

"She does that," Ori said.

"I have gathered."

Mau looked around the table.

"Everyone is very comfortable discussing my oppression."

Fern entered.

"Because it is not oppression."

"Thank you for the legal review."

Breakfast became breakfast.

That was the point.

Rem helped Momo because Momo asked.

Not because Rem had arrived pre-assigned to service.

She cut fruit badly once.

Momo stared.

Rem stared back.

"I can fight."

"That wasn't the question."

Rem tried again.

Better.

Later, Mau found her outside washing the cup she had used.

His recovered cap was on his head.

Rem looked at it.

Mau touched the brim automatically.

"You still remember the river?"

"Yes."

"All of it?"

"Enough."

He nodded.

That answer would have terrified him weeks ago.

Now it felt honest.

Rem dried the cup.

Mau asked:

"Why do you keep coming back?"

Too direct.

He regretted it immediately.

Rem did not.

"I like it here."

Mau waited.

She added:

"Sometimes."

He laughed.

"Devastating endorsement."

"I like the inn when everyone is too loud."

"That sentence contradicts itself."

"No. The loudness is predictable."

Mau understood more than he expected.

Rem looked toward the door.

"I like Ori asking if I came back."

Inside, Ori was arguing with Coco about whether a page counted as upside down if the spell still worked.

"I like that Frieren does not look at me like I am something she failed to kill."

Mau stopped smiling.

Rem continued before he could make it heavy.

"And I like Momo's breakfast."

"Strongest reason so far."

"Obviously."

Mau leaned against the wall.

"Do you want a room at the new place?"

Rem's shoulders changed.

Small.

There.

Mau saw it.

Old Mau might have rushed to fix the question.

Newer Mau tried something else.

"You don't have to answer."

"I know."

"No, I mean—"

"I know."

She looked at him.

"I don't know."

Mau nodded.

Rem frowned.

"You're not going to explain that uncertainty is valid?"

"I was told I over-narrate."

"By whom?"

From inside:

"EVERYONE," Anko shouted.

Mau looked offended.

Rem laughed.

Real laugh.

Brief.

Enough.

She picked up the cup.

Then paused.

"This one can stay here."

Mau looked at it.

Blue rim.

Nothing special.

Rem placed it on the shelf beside the others.

Not luggage.

Not a declaration.

One cup.

Mau did not call it a sign.

He had thanked her for not turning his cap into one.

He could return the favor.

That evening Rem left again.

The cup stayed.
