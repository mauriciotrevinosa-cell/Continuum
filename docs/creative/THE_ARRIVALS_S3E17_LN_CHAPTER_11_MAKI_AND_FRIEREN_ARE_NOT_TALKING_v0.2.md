# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 13
## Chapter 33 — Maki and Frieren Are Not Talking

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 13  
**Reading order:** 33  
**Continuity state:** Post-Memory recovery | Maki/Frieren relationship resumes outside crisis

Maki and Frieren were not talking.

They were sitting outside with tea.

Different.

Mau knew better than to interrupt.

He learned this because Maki threw a pebble at him without looking.

It missed on purpose.

Probably.

He went back inside.

Frieren watched him disappear.

"He is learning."

"Slowly," Maki said.

"Yes."

They drank.

For several minutes the conversation remained nonexistent.

The old inn yard was quieter than the common room.

Someone inside shouted about cards.

Milim.

Then Rimuru.

Then Karane.

Nobody died.

Probably.

Maki looked toward the noise.

"This place is ridiculous."

Frieren:

"Yes."

"You like it."

"Yes."

Another minute.

Maki asked:

"How bad?"

Frieren knew what she meant.

"Which part?"

"Exactly."

Frieren looked at her cup.

She could say:
he forgot me.

Too simple.

He had known her and not known her.

Loved her and looked through her.

Remembered things that had never happened.

Asked whether reality was real.

Trusted people while being unable to trust his own mind.

Maki waited.

Frieren said:

"I hated not being useful."

Maki nodded.

Not:
I understand.

Not immediately.

Frieren continued.

"I could stay. I could answer. I could remind him. None of those fixed it."

"That's usually the part people like us hate."

"People like us?"

"The ones who think standing between something and the people we love should count."

Frieren looked at her.

Maki looked back.

"Sometimes it doesn't."

Frieren disliked that.

Maki knew.

Good.

Frieren asked:

"Did Yuta ever forget you?"

Maki's face changed.

Not pain exactly.

Age.

A life longer than the bodies they wore.

"No."

Frieren waited.

Maki said:

"But we had plenty of things strength couldn't fix."

Old age.

Time.

Children growing.

Loss.

An ending that had once been natural.

Now undone in the strangest possible way.

Frieren looked away.

Maki added:

"Doesn't mean you stop trying."

"Obviously."

"Just means trying isn't the same as controlling the result."

Frieren's eyes narrowed.

"That sounds like something you should tell Mau."

"I did."

"When?"

"Before you did."

Frieren looked offended.

Maki smiled.

Small victory.

Inside, Mau sneezed.

Neither believed in the superstition.

Still funny.

Frieren said:

"He keeps trying to give people space."

"Good."

"Too much."

Maki turned.

Frieren continued:

"He asks instead of assuming. Then sometimes he assumes asking means he should leave."

Maki thought about that.

"That's stupid."

"Yes."

"You tell him?"

"No."

"Why?"

Frieren took another sip.

"He has to notice some things."

Maki laughed.

"There you are."

Frieren stared.

"What?"

"Nothing."

They sat.

This was what had gone missing during the crisis.

Not affection.

Time.

Maki and Frieren had built a friendship before everything became triage.

Then Mau broke.

Yuta broke differently.

Everyone became useful.

Useful was dangerous.

It made relationships disappear behind functions.

Maki put her cup down.

"Tomorrow I'm taking Yuta out."

"Where?"

"Doesn't matter."

Frieren nodded.

Maki glanced at her.

"You and Mau?"

"He is not allowed to make tomorrow productive."

"Good."

"Marin decided."

"Terrifying."

They both understood that Marin's authority in this area was effectively absolute.

A shadow crossed the doorway.

Mau.

He did not come outside.

Progress.

Maki called:

"You can stop pretending you're not listening."

Mau leaned around the frame.

"I was checking whether you needed anything."

Frieren:

"No."

Mau:

"Okay."

He vanished.

Maki stared.

"That was healthier."

"Annoying."

"You wanted him to come out?"

Frieren did not answer.

Maki smiled.

Five minutes later Mau returned with a blanket.

He placed it over the back of Frieren's chair without speaking.

Then left again.

Maki looked at the blanket.

Frieren pulled it around herself.

Neither commented.

They were not talking.

For another hour.
