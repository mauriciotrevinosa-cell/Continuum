# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 4 — Ceiling

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 4  
**Continuity state:** Pre-Move, old inn | Night | Flight leak while asleep

Frieren woke in the middle of the night because the bed was too big.

She did not open her eyes at first.

She reached across, the way she always did, for the warm shape that was always there.

Her hand found blanket.

Flat blanket.

Cold sheet.

She opened her eyes.

The other side of the bed was empty.

For one second, every night of the Memory crisis came back at once. The empty side. The coat gone. The boots gone. Gone before breakfast. Her heart did something it had not done in weeks, a hard ugly lurch, and she was already sitting up, already reaching for her staff, already halfway to his name—

Then she noticed the blanket was moving.

Not on the bed.

Above it.

It hung in the air over her like a sheet on a line, sagging in the middle, one corner trailing down almost to her face.

Frieren looked up.

Mau was asleep on the ceiling.

Not stuck to it.

Floating against it, gently, the way a fallen leaf rests on still water, one arm flung out, his hair spread against the boards, his mouth slightly open, completely and peacefully unconscious.

The blanket had gone up with him and then given up halfway.

Frieren stared at him.

He snored, very slightly.

She stared at him for so long that Ciel, who had promised to watch only the guardrails and had kept that promise, said quietly into Mau's sleeping head, and therefore into nobody's:

Flight. Hers. It came through about an hour ago. He has been there since. He is perfectly fine.

Nobody heard her.

Frieren began to laugh.



It started as a breath.

Then she put her hand over her mouth, because it was the middle of the night and the walls of the old inn were thin.

Then she could not stop.

She laughed until her eyes ran, until she had to fold over on the bed with her face in the pillow, until she was making a sound that was very nearly a wail, and across the corridor a door opened and Fern's voice said, sharply:

"Mistress Frieren?"

Frieren could not answer.

Fern came in with a lamp.

She saw Frieren, doubled over on the bed, shaking.

She saw the empty pillow.

Her face went white.

Then Frieren pointed at the ceiling.

Fern lifted the lamp.

She looked up for a very long time.

"...Is he asleep?"

Frieren nodded, still unable to speak.

"On the ceiling."

Frieren nodded harder.

Fern lowered the lamp.

Then she sat down on the edge of the bed, very carefully, put the lamp on the floor, and began to laugh too.

They woke Stark, who came to the door with his axe, saw the ceiling, said "What," and went back to bed without another word.

They woke Ori, who stood in the doorway with her hand over her mouth and her shoulders shaking.

They did not, somehow, wake Mau.



"How do we get him down?" said Ori, eventually.

Frieren wiped her eyes.

She stood up on the bed. Reached up. Caught hold of his ankle.

She pulled.

Mau came down slowly, like a kite being reeled in, still asleep, until he was hovering a hand's width above the mattress, where he stopped, and refused to go any further.

Frieren let go.

He started drifting back up.

She caught the ankle again.

"Hm," said Frieren.

She looked around the room.

Then she took the ribbon out of her own hair, tied one end around his ankle and the other around the bedpost, and let go.

Mau drifted up exactly as far as the ribbon allowed and stopped there, a hand above the bed, bobbing very gently, like a boat at a mooring.

Fern put her face in her hands.

Ori had to leave the room.

Frieren lay back down underneath him, pulled what remained of the blanket over herself, and closed her eyes.

"You're going to sleep like that?" said Fern.

"He's tied," said Frieren. "He's not going anywhere."

She reached up, without opening her eyes, and laid one hand flat against his back, where he floated just above her.

He was warm.

He was there.

Sometime before dawn, the flight wore off, and he settled down onto the bed the way snow settles, so softly that neither of them woke.



"Why is there a ribbon on my ankle?" said Mau, at breakfast.

The whole table went silent.

Fern looked at her tea.

Ori looked at the ceiling.

Stark, who had been there, said nothing, with great effort.

Frieren took a piece of bread off his plate.

"Rule eight," she said.

Mau looked at her.

"What did I break?"

"Gravity," said Frieren. "For about four hours."

Mau stared at her.

Then at the ribbon.

Then, slowly, up at the ceiling of the common room, as if it might accuse him.

"Ciel?"

Flight, said Ciel. Frieren's. You were asleep. You were never in any danger. Your body handled it beautifully. A pause. You snore.

"I do not snore."

You do, said Ciel. Rule eight.

The table, which had been holding itself together through heroic effort, broke.

Mau sat there with a ribbon on his ankle and his face in his hands while the entire household laughed at him, and after a while, against his will, he laughed too.

He kept the ribbon.

He did not take it off for a week.

Frieren noticed.

She did not ask for it back.
