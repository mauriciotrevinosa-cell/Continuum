# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 5 — Finished Means What?

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 5  
**Continuity state:** Post-Memory recovery | Old inn = current home | Future site = first habitable cluster nearly ready

Rimuru made the mistake of asking whether the first cluster was finished.

Seventeen people answered.

None agreed.

"Defensible," Mikasa said.

"Washable," Maomao said at the same time.

"Usable," Wakana added.

Bocchi, after being asked twice:

"Quiet enough."

Umaru lifted one hand.

"Wi—"

Everyone looked at her.

She lowered it.

"Never mind."

Frieren, staring at the roof:

"It has one."

Mau looked at her.

"That's a low bar."

Frieren looked toward the direction of the old inn.

"Historically not always."

Fair.

They stood in the middle of the first cluster while wind moved through walls that were technically walls and doors that still smelled like fresh wood.

The place had crossed a line nobody had marked.

Yesterday:

construction site.

Today:

maybe someone could sleep here and not regret it.

That was not the same as finished.

Rimuru opened the list.

"One long sleeping shell."

Fern:

"With no inside walls."

Wakana, quietly:

"On purpose."

"One common room."

Momo:

"Echoes."

"Covered food storage."

Maomao:

"Not fully rodent-proof."

Momo looked at her.

"That sentence ruined my morning."

"Good."

"Basic latrines."

Maomao nodded.

"Actually acceptable."

Everyone looked at her.

"Don't celebrate."

They did not.

Water access existed.

Crude washing area.

One workshop shell.

One medical/storage room.

Drainage good enough for ordinary rain.

No one used the phrase ordinary rain confidently anymore.

Things still missing:
- finished floors in several rooms;
- enough privacy;
- bath facilities;
- permanent Arrival intake space;
- enough beds;
- enough lighting;
- enough storage;
- enough everything if another group arrived tomorrow.

Mau looked at the list.

"So no."

Fern shook her head.

"Not finished."

Rimuru sighed.

"That's what I asked."

Fern continued.

"Habitable."

The word changed the room.

Bocchi looked around again.

Not home.

But possible.

Wakana looked at the workshop shell.

Maomao looked at the drainage.

Mikasa looked at exits.

Mau looked at everyone looking.

No single definition.

That was good.

Rimuru erased FINISHED from the top of the board.

Wrote:

FIRST HABITABLE PHASE.

Then, underneath:

NOT THE FINAL HOUSE.

That second line mattered.

The enclosed rooms around them were the first safe cluster:
somewhere to sleep,
eat,
store medicine,
test water,
and learn what broke when real people used it.

Wakana explained the rest the way he explained everything: quietly, mostly to the floor, as if it were obvious.

"When you make a dress," he said, "you don't cut the good fabric first. You sew it in cheap cloth. Muslin. You put it on the person. You see where it pulls. Where it gapes. Where they can't lift their arms. You mark it, you fix it, and then you cut the real thing."

Senku:

"A prototype."

"A muslin," said Wakana.

From somewhere behind him, Marin:

"A toile!"

Wakana considered this.

"...A toile," he agreed, because arguing with Marin about sewing words was not a fight anyone won.

The name that stuck was the Muslin.

Everything on one floor, at ground level, because one floor was easy to change. Inside walls made of boards slotted between posts, which two people could lift out and move in an afternoon. The first long shell already roofed. A second section beside it still stakes and footing, waiting for whatever the plan became.

The real house, the permanent one with foundations nobody could argue with afterward, would come later. It would be cut from whatever the Muslin taught them.

Nobody's room choice in the Muslin was a deed to an eternal rectangle.

That was the point.

Walls could move.

Connections could change.

The next section could still be turned around before anyone raised it.

Momo complained that FIRST HABITABLE PHASE sounded boring.

"Good," Fern said.

Then came the real question.

Not:
Can we move?

Who moves first?

And what had to stay behind?

The old inn still held:
- core household;
- G5 guests;
- Rem as guest;
- Kaneki and Aira as temporary residents;
- one traveler waiting on route information;
- boxes no one admitted belonged to them;
- too many shoes.

The first cluster could not absorb all of that.

Not without becoming worse than the inn in different ways.

That was another reason nobody called the settlement finished.

They were not moving into a completed town.

They were moving into enough shelter to learn how to build the real home around actual life.

So the move would be staged.

Logistics forced what emotion might not have accepted yet.

Mau walked to the edge of the first sleeping building.

Touched the wall.

"Feels weird."

Frieren stood beside him.

"What?"

"Planning to sleep somewhere I don't remember meeting you."

Frieren looked at him.

A month ago, that sentence would have been heavy. Now it was mostly a joke. Mostly.

Mau continued:

"The inn has... layers."

"Yes."

"This doesn't."

"Not yet."

That helped.

No demand that new home feel like old home immediately.

Rimuru called everyone back.

"First-night volunteers?"

Silence.

Milim raised both hands.

Rimuru lowered one for her.

"One vote."

Mau laughed.

Then looked around.

Nobody wanted to be the first person to turn a construction site into somewhere people woke up.

That was when he understood:

The move had started before anyone packed.

The first thing being moved was the meaning of home.
