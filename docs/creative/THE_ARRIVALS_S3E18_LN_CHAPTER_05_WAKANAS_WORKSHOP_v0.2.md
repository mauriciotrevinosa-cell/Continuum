# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 5 — Wakana's Workshop

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 5  
**Continuity state:** Packing Day 2 | Resident-specific spaces start making settlement feel inhabited

Wakana's workshop existed before his bedroom felt finished.

He objected.

Everyone ignored him.

"This table is too high."

Wakana looked at the temporary worktable.

"It is acceptable."

Marin:

"It is too high."

Wakana:

"I can work."

Mau, sitting because Maomao had caught him carrying something again:

"That's not the standard anymore."

Wakana looked at him.

Traitor.

The workshop became a practical argument.

Light direction.

Table height.

Storage.

Clean and dirty work zones.

Ventilation.

Where fabric could hang without dust.

Where tools could exist without becoming trip hazards.

Marin cared about mirrors.

Wakana cared about cutting space.

Both right.

Different use.

At the inn, Wakana had adapted to whatever room existed.

Here, people were asking:

What does your work need?

That embarrassed him.

Mau recognized the expression.

"Feels selfish?"

Wakana looked over.

"Yes."

"Apparently we're banned from that word when describing a chair that fits."

Wakana smiled.

"Who banned it?"

"Everyone. Repeatedly."

Marin pointed at Mau.

"Correct."

Wakana adjusted the table measurement.

Not dramatically.

Enough.

The workshop took shape.

Fabric.

Thread.

Patterns.

Tools in places chosen instead of inherited.

A covered mannequin form.

Marin claimed one mirror.

Wakana reclaimed it.

No legal process.

The first boxes moved there before Wakana did.

That created a strange afternoon.

His scissors lived at the settlement.

He lived at the inn.

A half-finished piece existed in both places because part of it was packed and part remained on his old table.

Wakana hated this.

Mau loved it.

"Your life has split geographically."

"Please stop describing it."

"One foot in each era."

"Mau."

"Fine."

But that was exactly what the move was becoming.

Objects changed address before people.

Routines moved before emotions.

One night, Wakana needed a specific thread.

Reached automatically toward the old inn shelf.

Empty.

Remembered.

"It's at the workshop."

Marin looked up.

"Want to go get it?"

Too late.

Dark.

Wakana stared at the empty shelf.

The first time an object was "at the other place."

Not lost.

Not gone.

Elsewhere.

Home had acquired a second coordinate.

Wakana used a different thread.

Complained the whole time.

Progress.
