# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 12 — The Quiet Corner Loses

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 12  
**Continuity state:** Move planning Day 2 | Family chooses proximity without Mau assigning it

Mau and Frieren had chosen a corner.

Technically.

The plan was not final enough for the word chosen to mean much.

But there was a quieter side of the future residential footprint, away from the workshop shell and the most obvious route between kitchen and common room.

Mau had looked at it.

Frieren had looked at it.

"Here?"

"Fine."

That was the entire romantic process.

Then Coco put a marker three rooms away.

Umaru put one beside Coco.

Tetia put hers on top of Coco's. Same room.

Richeh did not put down a marker at all. She was still a guest, she said mildly, and guests did not choose rooms in other people's houses. Nobody argued with her. Tetia looked at her for a moment and did not move her own marker back.

Agott put hers at the far end of the shell, alone, in the smallest room on the plan.

Coco looked at it.

Richeh looked at it.

Tetia looked at it.

Then Coco and Tetia picked up Agott's marker together and set it on top of theirs.

"I want my own room," said Agott.

"We'll put you up a curtain," said Coco.

"If you want it that badly," said Richeh.

"A nice one," said Tetia.

Agott opened her mouth to argue, went slowly and thoroughly red, and did not move the marker back.

Ori looked for the room next to theirs first.

There wasn't one. Beside their corner, the plan had a narrow gap marked BOARDS — STORAGE, where the builders meant to stack whatever was left over.

So she put hers across the courtyard, at the closest point she could find, and measured it on the paper with two fingers.

Twelve steps.

She did not look happy about it. She did not say so.

Fern moved hers close enough to the common room to hear when somebody started doing something stupid.

Which, according to Fern, was a practical requirement.

Mau stared at the plan.

"Okay."

Bocchi's marker arrived next.

Not directly beside them.

Near.

Music room as buffer.
Family courtyard on the other side.

Mau stared longer.

Anko leaned over his shoulder.

"You look like someone stole your tax refund."

"I don't know what that means here."

"It means you're making a face."

"I am not."

"You are."

Across the table, Marin was helping Wakana compare two possible room shapes while also rejecting both wall colors despite there being no wall color yet.

Momo had marked a room with good access to the kitchen.

Okarun had chosen nearby.

Aira put hers near an exterior route.

Mikasa chose a room with two useful exits.

Qifrey placed his between the Witch Hat room and the turn in the corridor: close enough to hear his students, close enough to hear Mau. He explained this to nobody.

Yuta did not make a speech about it.

He put his marker directly across the corridor from theirs, looked at it once, and went back to measuring a doorway.

"Corridor's too narrow there," Rimuru said.

"Then widen it," Yuta said, without looking up.

Rimuru widened it on the plan.

Then, after a moment, he set his own marker on the far side of the same turn in the hall, so that the three rooms made a small, careful triangle around one corner.

Nobody commented on the shape.

Several people noticed it.

Maki did not put down a marker at all.

She picked up Yuta's, wrote her own name under his, and put it back.

Nobody commented on that either. Nobody who knew what those two had already been to each other, for a whole life, in another world, needed it explained.

Milim put hers on top of Rimuru's.

Rimuru moved it to the next room.

Milim moved it back.

"Where Rimuru sleeps, I sleep," she said, as if quoting law.

Diablo asked whether the corridor outside Rimuru's door could be considered a room.

Everyone said no.

He wrote his name on it anyway, in very small letters.

At the turn of the hall, between the triangle and the courtyard, there was one narrow room nobody had marked.

Mau did not mark it either.

When Nijika reached toward it, Mau said, "That one gets bad light," which was not true. Nijika, who was not stupid, took the next one along.

Ori saw.

She did not ask who it was for.

She already knew.

Stark put his beside Fern's.

Fern pretended not to notice, with great concentration.

Himmel asked for the room facing the road, so he could see who was coming.

Hakari, Karane, Shizuka and Nano claimed four rooms in a row in the same instant and then spent twenty minutes arguing about the order.

Different reasons.

Same growing problem.

The supposedly quiet corner was becoming the densest part of the plan.

Mau looked at Ori.

"You know there are rooms farther out."

"I know."

"Quieter."

"I know."

"More private."

Ori looked at him.

"I have a door."

"Right."

Umaru did not even let him start.

"I'm staying close."

"Okay."

"Don't make the face."

"What face?"

"The 'I need to make sure you're independent' face."

Mau looked at Frieren.

"Do I have that face?"

"Yes."

Betrayal everywhere.

Coco arrived with Qifrey.

She pointed at her marker.

"I want this one."

Mau:

"Because?"

Coco looked confused.

"Because it's near everyone."

"That's it?"

"Yes."

Qifrey watched Mau carefully.

Not correcting.

Letting the answer do the work.

Mau looked back at the plan.

He had spent weeks learning not to decide for people.

Somewhere inside that lesson he had added a rule nobody gave him:

If I love them correctly, I should never assume they want to stay close.

That sounded wise.

It was also, apparently, incomplete.

Bocchi approached the table.

Slow.

Kita with her.

Bocchi tapped her marker.

"Can it stay there?"

Mau's chest tightened.

"Of course."

She nodded.

Left.

No speech.

Better.

Frieren leaned against the table beside him.

The corner was gone.

Not physically.

Conceptually.

Mau whispered:

"I don't think we're winning this battle."

Frieren studied the markers.

Fern.
Ori.
Coco.
Umaru.
Bocchi nearby.
Momo not far.
People already arguing about routes between all of them.

"No."

"Should we move ours?"

Frieren looked at him.

"Do you want to?"

Mau thought.

The original reason had been quiet.

Recovery.
Sleep.
Less traffic.

Reasonable.

But there was another thing under it.

A fear that if he occupied the center, everyone else would be forced to orbit him.

Except nobody had been forced.

They had just put down markers.

One by one.

Mau looked at the crowded map.

"No."

Frieren nodded.

"Then don't."

Anko, still listening:

"Beautiful. Inspiring. Now move your marker six inches because you're blocking the corridor."

Mau moved it.

Romance survived.

The corner did not.
