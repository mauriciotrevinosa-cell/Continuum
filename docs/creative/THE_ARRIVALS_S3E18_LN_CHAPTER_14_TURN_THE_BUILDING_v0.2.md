# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 14 — Turn the Building

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 14  
**Continuity state:** Move planning Day 2 | Main family-house footprint still changeable

Frieren wanted an east-facing window.

This information arrived too late.

Rimuru stared at her.

"You said you didn't care."

"I don't."

Mau pointed.

"See? She does this."

Frieren ignored him.

Wakana looked at the plan.

The problem was small enough to sound stupid and large enough to affect six other rooms.

The current orientation gave Mau and Frieren morning light at an angle.

Not east.

Frieren had decided this mattered.

Nobody knew why.

Mau asked.

Frieren answered:

"I like morning light."

That was all.

Enough.

Senku crouched near the stakes outside.

He looked at the unfinished foundation lines.

Then at the drainage channel.

Then at Rimuru.

"We're not rotating a building."

Mau:

"Good."

"We're rotating a plan."

Mau:

"Less terrifying."

Senku pointed with a measuring stick.

"Permanent residential wing isn't there yet. You've got a habitable test cluster and partial footing. If you reorient the next section now, you pay in drainage adjustment and two route changes."

Maomao:

"How much drainage adjustment?"

Senku answered.

Maomao asked three worse questions.

Kusuri asked one question that made Maomao turn slowly toward her.

"No."

"I didn't even finish."

"No."

Qifrey crouched on the other side of the plan.

"If the covered connector bends here, the spell-maintenance line can remain accessible."

Agott:

"Unless someone builds storage over it later."

Everyone looked at Jinshi.

Jinshi looked offended.

"Why me?"

"Administration," Momo said.

"That is not what administration means."

Marin arrived halfway through the argument.

Listened.

Looked at the markers.

"So the problem is everybody wants to live near them."

Mau:

"That is an aggressive summary."

"It is also correct."

Frieren:

"Yes."

Mau looked at her.

No help.

Marin pointed at the footprint.

"And instead of moving people away, we're moving the house."

Senku:

"The future wing."

"Less dramatic."

"It's also what is actually happening."

Rimuru rubbed his forehead.

"We can do it."

Mau looked at the stakes.

"Really?"

"We haven't built the thing you're emotionally attached to."

"I'm not emotionally attached."

Frieren:

"He is."

Mau:

"Can everyone stop knowing me?"

No.

They shifted the line.

Not much.

Enough.

One stake moved.
Then another.
A string changed angle.
The covered route bent.
The drainage plan gained an annotation Maomao hated.
Wakana recalculated a wall.
Qifrey changed a maintenance access point.
Senku wrote down two future failure modes.

Nothing rose from the ground.

That was the point.

They were changing the plan while change was still cheap.

Frieren stood where the future window might be.

Looked east.

Morning was long gone.

Still.

She imagined it.

Mau stood beside her.

"Worth it?"

"Yes."

"Everybody is still going to be right next to us."

"Yes."

"So we lost twice."

Frieren looked at him.

"No."

Mau waited.

She pointed toward the room markers.

"They chose."

Then toward the east.

"So did I."

Mau smiled.

Right.

Architecture did not have to protect them from being wanted.

It had to make room for what people actually chose.

The next morning, Frieren returned before breakfast.

She stood behind the new line.

The sun rose through the gap where her window would eventually be.

Mau found her there.

Neither said anything.

The wall did not exist yet.

The view did.
