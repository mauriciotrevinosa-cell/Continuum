The mug was still on the map in the morning.

Mau noticed because Rimuru tried to move it.

"Don't."

Rimuru stopped.

Mau looked at the mug.

Then at the map under it.

Yesterday the map had been future.

Today it had measurements written in three different hands and a stain near the river line because Momo had set something wet on it.

"What?" Rimuru asked.

"Nothing."

Mau moved the mug himself.

That felt different enough.

Breakfast happened around the work.

People ate while standing.

Fern complained about people eating while standing.

Stark guarded his plate.

Himmel stole nothing because Stark watched him specifically now.

Ori read over Qifrey's shoulder.

Coco read over Ori's shoulder.

Agott told both of them they were blocking the light.

Frieren wore Mau's McLaren hoodie and had one hand on the map.

Official girlfriend status had not returned the hoodie.

Mau had accepted this.

Mostly.

Rimuru tapped the chosen site.

"Today is not moving day."

Momo looked disappointed.

"You said we start today."

"We start working today."

"Same thing."

"No."

"It has movement in it."

"That's not how language works."

Nano looked up from a list.

"Rimuru is correct."

Momo pointed at her.

"Traitor."

<!-- scene-id: s3e1-ln01-notmove -->

The distinction mattered.

Nobody packed bedrooms.

Nobody dismantled the old kitchen.

Nobody carried the bookshelves outside.

They packed:
- rope;
- stakes;
- chalk;
- measuring tools;
- food;
- water;
- first-aid supplies;
- Qifrey's carefully protected materials;
- Rimuru's notes;
- too many blankets because Coco insisted;
- one folding table because Anko said every bad decision looked more official on a table.

The old inn stayed behind them when they left.

Not abandoned.

Home.

The chosen region was several hours away by the route they currently trusted.

The route they currently trusted was not the route they expected to use forever.

That sentence described most of the project.

<!-- scene-id: s3e1-ln01-firststep -->

The land did not look like a city.

Good.

It looked like grass.

Stone.

Trees.

A lake wide enough to hold sky.

A river feeding away from it.

The waterfall farther north.

Mountains doing useful things without caring whether anyone thanked them.

There were no foundations.

No streets.

No gate.

No council hall.

No Arrival House.

Nothing waiting for them except terrain.

Mau stood near the place where one corner of the map claimed housing could begin.

Rimuru stood beside him.

Frieren joined them.

Then Ori.

Then everybody else until the quiet view became twenty conversations.

Mau looked down.

Someone had pushed the first wooden stake into the soil.

Not deep.

Just enough to stand.

He did not know who.

That felt right.

Season Three began with nobody certain where the second stake should go.
