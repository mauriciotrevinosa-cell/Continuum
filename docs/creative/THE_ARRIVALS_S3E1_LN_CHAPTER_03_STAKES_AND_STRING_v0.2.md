<!-- scene-id: s3e1-ln03-lines -->

String made everyone overconfident.

A line tied between two stakes looked official.

That was the problem.

Rimuru had three colors.

Housing.

Shared use.

Do not build yet.

Momo took one spool.

"What color is fun?"

"Give that back."

Marin took another.

"What color is aesthetically flexible?"

"Give that back too."

Nano rescued the system.

For twelve minutes.

Then Qifrey moved a stake because a future spell-maintenance corridor needed more space.

Mikasa moved another because the proposed sightline was terrible.

Maomao moved a third because putting food storage downwind of a sanitation area was apparently something everyone should have known before she had to say it.

Coco planted a small marker where she wanted a garden.

Agott moved it three meters.

Coco moved it back two.

They compromised by arguing for twenty minutes.

<!-- scene-id: s3e1-ln03-mau -->

A merchant from a nearby route stopped to look.

He had heard the site belonged to the Otherworlders.

His eyes found Mau.

"So you're deciding all this?"

Mau looked behind him.

Rimuru was arguing with Nano.

Qifrey was drawing.

Fern was making Stark move a crate.

Frieren was reading while somehow remaining part of the conversation.

"No."

The merchant waited.

Mau added, "I'm participating."

"Who decides, then?"

Mau looked at the strings.

"Apparently whoever notices the next problem."

The merchant laughed because he thought Mau was joking.

Mau did not correct him.

<!-- scene-id: s3e1-ln03-notgrid -->

By noon the first proposed street had become a curve.

Not artistic.

Necessary.

The ground dropped.

A stand of old trees created shade they wanted to keep.

A drainage channel might need the lower line.

Rimuru erased the rectangular block from the map.

Momo leaned over.

"Cities are supposed to have streets."

"This will have streets."

"Straight streets."

"Why?"

Momo opened her mouth.

Closed it.

"Because."

Rimuru smiled.

"Compelling."

The string changed.

The site became less like the map.

Better.

Worse.

More real.

Mau stood at the center of three provisional zones and realized that building a home meant making choices concrete enough to trip over.

He stepped over a red line.

Agott pointed.

"Don't."

"What?"

"That means don't build."

"I'm not building."

"You're standing."

Mau stepped back.

Agott nodded.

Civilization advanced.
