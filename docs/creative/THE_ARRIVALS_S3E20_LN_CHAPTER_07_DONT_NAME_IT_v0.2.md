# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 28 — Don't Name It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 28  
**Continuity state:** Move Day 4 — late night | First dangerous leak: the shadow medium tries to supply a missing requirement | Ciel closes it | Mau stays and asks for help

Mau woke a little after two, thirsty.

Frieren was asleep. Properly asleep this time, not the half-sleep of the first nights, when every new sound in the new building had brought her up on one elbow. She had one hand curled in the hem of his shirt. He unhooked it gently and she frowned and let him go.

The corridor was dark.

He knew it now. Three nights had been enough for that. Four steps to the turn. The board that dipped. Bocchi's door, quiet at last. The common room at the end, where somebody always left one lamp burning low on the table because Fern had decreed that nobody should have to find the water in the dark.

The lamp was burning.

The water barrel stood against the far wall.

Mau crossed the room, took the dipper off its hook, and drank, and stood there afterward with the dipper in his hand, half-asleep, looking at nothing.

The lamp was behind him.

His shadow lay long across the floorboards in front of him, all the way to the base of the barrel.

He noticed, slowly, the way you notice things at two in the morning, that it was too dark.

Not a shadow-colored dark.

A hole-colored dark.

The floorboards did not continue under it. The grain of the wood simply stopped at its edge, as if someone had cut the shape of a man out of the floor and left the space beneath empty all the way down.

Cold came up out of it.

Mau did not move.

Something in the hole moved instead.

It came up slowly, the way something comes up out of deep water. First a darkness that was more solid than the dark around it. Then a shape in that darkness. A long jaw. Black fur, or something drawn to look like fur by someone who had only heard it described. A white ear, folded back. The gleam of a single eye, low to the floor, fixed on him.

A muzzle broke the surface of his shadow and breathed out.

He felt the breath on his ankles.

It was cold.

Claws came up after it, four of them, and scraped once across the floorboards, slow and deliberate, with a sound like a nail drawn over slate.

Mau, said Ciel.

He had never heard her voice like that. Not loud. Absolutely flat.

Do not move.

He did not move.

Do not name it.

He did not know its name.

Some part of him did.

He could feel the word rising behind his teeth the way the jar of radish had risen to the roof, without being asked. A short word. Two syllables. A word that someone else had said, once, in a world he had never seen, with his hands shaped just so.

Do not, said Ciel. Do not shape your hands. Do not say anything. It is trying to finish itself. It needs a requirement it does not have. If you give it anything at all, a word, a gesture, it may take that instead.

Mau kept his hands at his sides.

He held the dipper so tightly the wood creaked.

The muzzle turned toward the sound.

Closing, said Ciel. Now.

The cold stopped.

The hole stopped being a hole.

It did not sink back. It did not withdraw. It simply was not there anymore, and his shadow lay across the floorboards the way a shadow should, thin and gray, with the grain of the wood showing through it.

The water barrel had frost on its rim.

Four long pale scratches crossed the boards at his feet.

Mau sat down on the floor.

He did not decide to. His legs decided.

His left eye was open. He could feel it, the heat and the pressure behind it, the valve doing what it was for. He did not cover it. There was no one to cover it from.



Yuta was in the doorway.

Mau had not heard him come. Yuta never made any sound when he did not want to.

He was not looking at Mau. He was looking at the four scratches in the floor.

Then he crossed the room, crouched, and laid his fingers along them, very lightly, the way you would touch something that might still be warm.

"That was a dog," said Yuta.

"Yes."

"A black one."

"Yes. One white ear."

Yuta closed his eyes.

"Megumi's," he said. "A friend. A student. That's his first one. The divine dogs." He opened his eyes again. "Ten Shadows."

Mau did not know what to say to that.

So he did the thing he had promised to do.

"Ciel. Tell Rimuru."

Master is already awake, said Ciel. Master is already coming.

Rimuru was there before she finished.

He did not use the door. He was simply in the room, the way he had been in the room at the worksite with a corner post in his hands, and he looked at Mau on the floor, and Yuta crouched beside him, and the frost on the barrel, and the scratches.

He did not ask if Mau was all right.

He sat down on the floor too.

"Tell me," he said.

So Mau told him. The hole. The cold. The muzzle. The claws. The word behind his teeth. Ciel said what she had seen, and Rimuru repeated it aloud for Yuta, flatly, without softening any of it.

The Ten Shadows patterns are in the second group, said Ciel. Finished, missing a requirement. The technique needs a medium and it needs the shikigami themselves, and those are things the seal could only read about, never take. Tonight the pattern did not leak a finished thing. It tried to make the missing piece.

"It tried to make a dog," said Rimuru.

It tried to make a substitute for the thing that makes the dog. It failed. A pause. The part I do not like is that it tried.

"Can it try again?"

Yes.

Nobody said anything for a while.

Then Yuta said, very quietly, "Ten Shadows has ten."

Mau looked at him.

"Ten shikigami," said Yuta. "You start with the dogs. You earn the rest one at a time." He did not look up from the scratches. "The one nobody has ever tamed is Mahoraga."

Mau felt it land.

The wheel.

The adaptation wheel sat in the seal, finished, one of the nine whole things. He had felt it turn, once, inside Rimuru, on the worst day of his life.

"The wheel's sealed," said Rimuru.

The wheel is finished, said Ciel. The shikigami it belongs to is not. Tonight, the pattern began at the first shadow. I do not know whether it intends to go any further. I do not know whether it intends anything at all.

"It won't," said Rimuru.

Master, said Ciel. Rule eight.

Rimuru shut his eyes.

He sat like that for a long moment, cross-legged on the floor of the new common room at two in the morning, a slime who had once carried a storm dragon inside himself, and Mau watched him decide not to say the comfortable thing.

"We don't know," said Rimuru.

"Okay," said Mau.

It was, strangely, much better than "it won't".



Frieren came in a little after that.

She had woken to an empty bed and she had heard voices, and she had not run, which Mau noticed. She stood in the doorway in his shirt and looked at the three of them on the floor, and at the frost, and at the scratches, and at his open eye.

She did not ask anything.

She came and sat down next to him on the boards, close enough that her shoulder pressed his, and took the dipper out of his hand, because he was still holding it, and put it back on its hook.

"I'm telling everyone at breakfast," said Mau.

"Yes," said Frieren.

"All of it."

"Yes."

He looked down at the four pale lines in the wood.

"And I'm asking Wakana not to sand these."

Rimuru looked at him.

"Why?"

Mau thought about it.

"Because it's not just spoons," he said. "I keep making it about spoons. Roofs, and jars, and floating, and everybody laughing. And that's real. That's mostly what it is." He touched the nearest scratch. "But it's not only that. I don't want anyone in this house forgetting it's not only that. Including me."

Nobody argued.

After a while, Yuta got up and turned the lamp higher, and then went and lit the second lamp on the other side of the room, and then the third, until every corner of the common room was full of light and nobody's shadow fell anywhere very long at all.

He did not explain why.

He did not have to.
