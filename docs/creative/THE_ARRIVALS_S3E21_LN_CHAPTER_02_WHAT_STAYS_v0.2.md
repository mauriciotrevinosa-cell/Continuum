# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 28 — What Stays

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 28  
**Continuity state:** Move Day 5 | Old inn preservation becomes practical policy

They made the list at the old table.

Not a memorial list.

Maintenance.

That distinction mattered.

WHAT STAYS.

Momo immediately wrote:

ME.

Fern crossed it out.

Momo objected.

"Temporarily."

Fern crossed it out again.

The table stayed.

No debate.

The old mug.

Several chairs.

One shelf of books.

Old route board.

Marks on the wall nobody could fully explain.

A repaired section of roof that represented more arguments than architecture deserved.

Some bedding.

Kitchen basics.

Enough that a traveler arriving tomorrow could still eat and sleep.

Bocchi looked toward the stairs.

"What about rooms?"

Not objects.

Spaces.

The room where she had first slept after one of the worst days.

The room Mau and Frieren shared.

Hallway.

Doorway where too many arguments happened.

Could a room stay without freezing it?

Mau looked at Fern.

"We keep using them."

There.

No plaque.

No glass.

No:
do not touch history.

Use.

Guest rooms.

Emergency overflow.

Quiet retreat from settlement noise.

Searcher staging if geography made sense.

A stop for people who did not want the main settlement.

That last one mattered.

The inn could become a softer edge.

Not everyone entering their orbit needed to enter the city.

Rimuru arrived late.

Read the list.

"Protected site?"

Mau made a face.

"Sounds official."

Fern:

"It needs maintenance responsibility."

Correct.

Roof does not care about symbolism.

They wrote:

Rotating inspection.

Stored water.

Fire check.

Food refresh.

Repair budget.

No stripping materials for construction without group approval.

No permanent conversion without consultation.

Keys.

Mau stared.

"We accidentally invented historic preservation."

Momo:

"Can we charge admission?"

"No."

"Gift shop?"

"No."

Frieren looked around.

"People should still live here sometimes."

Everyone turned.

She continued.

"If no one lives here, it becomes a thing we remember instead of a place."

Mau looked at her.

Exactly.

The old inn did not need to remain unchanged.

It needed to remain recognizable and useful.

Historical and alive.

Later residents could hear:

where the first family started.

But the building would not become sacred because nobody touched it.

It would remain sacred because people kept:
- sweeping;
- fixing the roof;
- replacing spoiled food;
- sleeping there when needed;
- arguing at the same table.

Mau added one final line.

DO NOT CALL IT ABANDONED.

Fern looked.

"That's not maintenance."

"It is emotionally."

She allowed it.

They left the list on the table.

Not framed.

Someone would probably spill tea on it.

Good.
