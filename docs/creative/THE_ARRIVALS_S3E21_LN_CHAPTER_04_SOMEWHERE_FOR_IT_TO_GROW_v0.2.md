# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 14
## Chapter 33 — Somewhere for It to Grow

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 14  
**Reading order:** 33  
**Continuity state:** End of Move Week 1 | Settlement is home in daily language | Witch Hat seed from S2E20 planted at Coco's first-day garden stake

Qifrey kept it in the last case he owned that still held any ink.

Coco knew which case.

She had always known.

She found him in the half-built room that would one day be the atelier, sitting on a crate with the case open on his knees, holding a folded cloth he had not unfolded.

"Is it time?" she asked.

Qifrey did not pretend not to understand her.

"The plan was: once there was a home for it."

Coco looked out of the doorway.

Stakes.

String.

Half a roof.

A common room that already would not empty.

People who had spent the entire week calling this place home without anyone deciding they would.

"There's one now," she said.

Qifrey looked at the cloth in his hands for a long moment.

Then he unfolded it.

The seed was small enough to hide in a closed fist.

It had come from the last of what he had carried out of his own world. Most of that was gone now, spent on a field drawn to stop a monster. What remained was this.

Not a supply.

A beginning.

"I marked a garden," Coco said. "The first day. Before anybody had a house."

"I remember."

"Can it go there?"

Qifrey folded the cloth back around the seed and stood up.

"Show me."



They had not gone ten steps before Rimuru saw them.

He took one look at the cloth in Qifrey's hand and fell into step beside them without asking.

"I helped make that," he said. "I want to see if it does anything."

"It's a seed," Coco said. "It's going to do seed things."

"That's what I want to see."

Coco grabbed Mau's sleeve as they passed the common room door.

Mau, who had been trying to read a delivery list upside down, looked up.

"What—"

"You're coming."

"Coming where?"

"It's a memory," Coco said, and towed him out.

Mau went, because Coco scheduling a memory had become a thing nobody refused.

Frieren followed Mau, because that was what Frieren did, and also because the folded cloth in Qifrey's hand was doing something to the air she had never felt before.



Coco's stake was still there.

Leaning a little.

A rag tied to the top, faded by weeks of weather.

Nobody had built on it.

Nobody had moved it.

Coco knelt beside it.

Qifrey knelt beside her and opened the cloth.

Frieren crouched down on the other side and held one hand above the seed, not touching.

She frowned.

"That isn't mana."

"No," Qifrey said.

"I don't know what it is."

"It's ours," Qifrey said. "The way we work. It never was mana."

Frieren kept her hand there a moment longer.

More than a thousand years of magic, and something new in a cloth no bigger than a handkerchief.

"I want to study it," she said.

"After it's in the ground."

Frieren looked faintly betrayed, the way she looked when a grimoire turned out to be someone's shopping list, and withdrew her hand.



It was at this point that Agott noticed Coco and Qifrey kneeling in the dirt and came over to tell them the soil was wrong.

"It's too wet," Agott said. "It'll rot."

"It's not too wet," Coco said.

"It's going to drain into that trench."

Richeh and Tetia had followed Agott, because they always eventually followed Agott, and now stood there inspecting the trench in question.

"It does drain into the trench," Tetia said, apologetically.

Then Senku walked past on his way to somewhere else, saw four people and a seed and a hole, and physically could not keep walking.

"Drainage isn't your problem," he said. "Your problem is that you haven't tested what's in that soil. If it's as acidic as the ground by the river—"

"It's not by the river," said Agott.

"You don't know that."

"I know where the river is."

They looked at each other.

Two people who had both been right too often to be comfortable around anyone else who was.

"Fine," said Senku. "Prove the drainage."

"Fine," said Agott. "Prove the acid."

They knelt down together on the same side of the hole and began to argue in a way that was, Richeh observed quietly to Tetia, almost exactly the way Agott argued with Coco.

Suika arrived because Senku was there.

Maomao arrived because Senku had said the word compost loudly enough to be heard from the medical shed, and she had opinions about compost.

Umaru and Milim arrived because there was now a crowd, and a crowd meant something was happening, and neither of them could bear the thought of something happening without them.

"What is it?" Milim said.

"A seed," Umaru told her.

Milim looked at it.

"It's very small."

"It's going to be a tree."

Milim considered this.

"How long?"

Frieren, still crouching, said: "A long time."

Milim looked offended on the seed's behalf.

Wakana arrived with a measuring cord and a stick of chalk, measured a careful circle around the stake, walked to the nearest drawn plan, and marked the circle there too.

Rimuru watched him do it.

"What's that for?"

"So nobody builds on it," Wakana said. "Later. When they forget."

Rimuru thought about the last three versions of the residential plan, and how many corners of it had been built on by people who forgot, and took the chalk and darkened the circle himself.



Momo came to call everyone for lunch and found fourteen people standing around a patch of dirt.

She opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Stayed.

Stark arrived carrying two buckets, because someone had said the word water and Stark had learned that when someone said water, he was going to be the one who carried it.

Himmel arrived last.

He took in the crowd, the stake, the faded rag, the seed in Qifrey's open cloth, and he said, with great feeling, that the best trees were always the ones planted by people who would not be the ones to sit under them.

Nobody answered him.

Then everyone did exactly what he said anyway, which was what usually happened with Himmel, and which he had long since made his peace with.



Coco planted it.

Qifrey let her.

He did not guide her hand.

He sat back on his heels and watched his student put the last of his world into the ground of a new one, and his face did something Coco did not see and everyone else pretended not to.

"Mau," Coco said.

He looked up.

She pointed at the loose earth beside the hole.

"First handful."

"Me? I didn't make it."

"You were gone twice," Coco said. "You should be here for something that stays."

Mau looked at her for a long moment.

Then knelt, his hair falling forward over his left eye the way he wore it now, took a handful of soil that Senku and Agott had finally agreed was acceptable, and covered the seed.

Coco covered it the rest of the way.

Stark watered it.

Too much.

Agott said so.

Senku agreed.

They both looked very unhappy about agreeing.



Then there was the question of who would water it.

Everyone volunteered.

Agott produced a schedule.

Senku produced a better schedule.

Maomao pointed out that both schedules would kill it.

Coco took both schedules, folded them in half, and threw them away.

Then she found a board, and a stick of charcoal, and wrote across the top:

WHO WATERS IT

And underneath, without asking anyone:

Everyone.

She hung it on Wakana's measuring stake.

Nobody argued.

It was a strange thing to agree on. A board that said everyone was technically the worst possible schedule.

It worked anyway.

It went on working for a long time.



They stood around the patch of dark wet earth for longer than there was any reason to.

Nothing was there yet.

There would be nothing there for weeks.

"Trees take a long time," Frieren said.

Mau looked at the circle Wakana had chalked onto the plan.

At the board.

At everyone who had come out to watch a seed go into the ground for no reason except that someone they loved had wanted them there.

"That's fine," he said. "We'll be around."

Coco smiled.

She did not say anything.

She did not need to.

Lunch was cold by the time they got back.

Momo had known it would be.

She had saved it anyway.
