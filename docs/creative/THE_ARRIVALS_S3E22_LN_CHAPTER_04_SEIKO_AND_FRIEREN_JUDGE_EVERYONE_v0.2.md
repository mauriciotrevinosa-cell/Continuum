# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 4 — Seiko and Frieren Judge Everyone

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 4  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary Week 2 | Seiko/Frieren friendship begins through mutual practical judgment

Seiko and Frieren became friends by denying it.

Mau did not interfere.

He valued his life.

They sat outside the unfinished main house while everyone else argued about a roof angle.

Neither participated.

Seiko drank tea.

Frieren read.

Five minutes passed.

Seiko:

"That boy's going to fall."

Frieren:

"Yes."

Wakana, on the roof frame, did not fall.

Marin yelled anyway.

Another five minutes.

Seiko:

"Your boyfriend eats like somebody has to remind him."

Frieren turned a page.

"Yes."

Mau, carrying boards nearby:

"I can hear you."

Neither responded.

Seiko looked at Frieren.

"You let him get away with too much."

Frieren:

"No."

Mau:

"Thank you."

Frieren:

"Fern doesn't."

Mau:

"I withdraw my gratitude."

Seiko laughed.

Not loud.

Enough.

Their similarities were mostly misleading.

Seiko was louder when annoyed.

Frieren was more dangerous while quiet.

Seiko's world treated spirits and weirdness as familiar categories.

Frieren's world had demons and magic with centuries of history behind them.

Both had lived long enough around danger to distrust anyone who mistook confidence for evidence.

Both had acquired younger people without apparently signing paperwork.

That last category expanded too quickly.

Suika ran past with Coco and Umaru.

Seiko watched.

"Those yours?"

Frieren:

"No."

A pause.

"Maybe."

Mau nearly dropped a board.

Seiko grinned.

"You've got it bad."

Frieren looked at her.

"What?"

"The grandma disease."

Frieren stared.

Mau stopped walking.

This was worth dying for.

Frieren:

"I'm older than you."

Seiko:

"Exactly. Advanced case."

Mau made a noise.

Frieren turned.

He resumed carrying boards immediately.

Smart.

Later, Seiko asked about demons.

Not interrogation.

Not Rem specifically at first.

Just:
what Frieren meant when she said demon.

Frieren explained enough.

Seiko listened.

Then:

"And Rem?"

Frieren's hand stopped on the page.

"Different."

"Because?"

"Because she is."

Seiko waited.

Frieren did not offer philosophy.

She offered evidence.

Rem worried.
Rem chose.
Rem hesitated.
Rem cared for Mau when he could not understand what was happening.
Rem could be wrong.
Rem could be afraid.
Rem could change her mind.

Person before category.

Seiko nodded.

"Good."

Frieren:

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You are judging me."

"Obviously."

Frieren accepted this.

In return, Frieren asked about Vamola.

Seiko's answer was equally practical.

Not:
alien.

Vamola.

Person first.

By the time Mau returned, the two women had moved on to criticizing the current route markers.

Mikasa approached.

Heard them.

Immediately regretted it.

Seiko:

"This one is too low."

Frieren:

"Yes."

Mikasa:

"It is temporary."

Both:

"Still too low."

Mikasa looked at Mau.

Mau raised both hands.

"I am not involved."

Cowardice was sometimes wisdom.

That evening Seiko called Mau over.

He expected a route complaint.

Instead she fixed the collar of his shirt.

"You're wearing this wrong."

"I am twenty—"

Nobody actually knew.

Bad argument.

Seiko smacked his hand away when he tried to help.

Frieren watched.

Mau looked at her.

"Are you going to save me?"

"No."

"You're enjoying this."

"Yes."

Seiko finished.

Stepped back.

"There."

Mau looked between them.

Something warm and deeply threatening had formed.

He did not have a word for it.

Grandmother was close.

Friendship was close.

Family was closer.

Seiko returned to her tea.

Frieren returned to her book.

Neither acknowledged anything had changed.

Which was how Mau knew it had.
