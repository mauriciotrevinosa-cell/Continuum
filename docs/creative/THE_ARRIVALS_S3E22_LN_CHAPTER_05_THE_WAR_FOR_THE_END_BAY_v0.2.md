# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 5 — The War for the End Bay

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 5  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary Week 1–2 | Four-day war, one duel | Umaru wins and gives the room to Milim on conditions | Umaru's Door

The war lasted four days.

Rimuru had laid down exactly one rule before it started, standing in the middle of the common room with both hands raised like a man trying to stop two carts.

"Milim. No strength. None. Not a finger. Not a breath. If I see a wall move, the war is over and you both sleep in the stable."

Milim had agreed so fast that it was obvious she had not been planning to use it. Milim did not need strength to enjoy a war. Milim simply needed a war.

Umaru, it turned out, had been preparing for one her whole life.



Day one was propaganda.

Posters appeared overnight in every corridor of the Muslin, drawn in Coco's best ink-free charcoal, because Umaru had recruited Coco within the hour. They showed a small, noble figure in a hooded cloak being driven from her rightful home by a pink-haired tyrant. One of them had a tiny crying hamster in the corner. Nobody knew what the hamster meant. Umaru refused to explain the hamster.

Milim loved the posters.

She went around the corridors looking at each one, very seriously, and then took one down and pinned it on her own wall, above her pillow.

"That's me," she said proudly, pointing at the tyrant.

Umaru, watching from the turn of the corridor, felt that the propaganda had not landed as intended.



Day two was blockade.

The end bay could only be reached by going down the corridor past Mau and Frieren's door. Umaru built a fort across the corridor out of every cushion in the common room, two crates, a folding screen from Marin's workroom and Suika, who had been told she was a sentry and took it extremely seriously.

To pass, you had to pay a toll.

The toll was a snack.

Milim paid it cheerfully every time, with snacks from her own enormous private hoard, which nobody had known she had. By evening the fort was so full of snacks that Umaru and Suika had to eat their way out of it.

Mau also had to pay the toll, to get to his own room.

"I live here," he said.

"Toll," said Suika, holding out her hand.

Mau paid the toll.



Day three was diplomacy, which was to say Fern.

Fern called both parties into the common room at breakfast. She sat at the head of the table with her ledger, and Umaru sat on one side with Coco as her second, and Milim sat on the other side with nobody, because nobody had volunteered and Milim had not noticed she needed anyone.

"This," said Fern, "is a house. Not a battlefield."

"It's a war," said Umaru.

"It's a corridor full of cushions."

"It's a fortified position."

Fern looked at her for a long time.

"How do you want this to end?"

Umaru opened her mouth.

She shut it again.

It occurred to her, possibly for the first time, that she did not know. She had wanted the room. She still wanted the room. But the war had been four days of posters and toll snacks and Milim laughing so hard at the fort that she fell over, and it had been, if Umaru was honest, the best four days she'd had since the Move.

"A duel," she said finally.

Milim lit up.

"A REAL duel?"

"A game," said Umaru. "My game. Best of seven. If I win, I get the room."

"And if I win?"

Umaru had not considered this possibility.

"You won't," she said.



Day four was the duel.

The whole household came. It was the most people the common room had held since the Move. Rimuru sat in the front row with his face in his hands. Diablo sat beside him explaining the rules of the game to Rimuru-sama in great and incorrect detail. Coco kept score on Wakana's board, erasing three hooks to make room.

The game was one of the ones Umaru had carried across from her world in a battered box, cards and counters and a board that folded in four. It needed cleverness, and patience, and a willingness to lose small to win big.

Milim had none of these things.

She lost the first game in nine minutes.

She lost the second in seven.

She lost the third so quickly that Seiko, who had been betting with Anko on the side, did not have time to finish her tea.

Then, somewhere in the fourth, something changed.

Milim stopped trying to win the board, and started watching Umaru.

She was not clever the way Umaru was clever. But she was very, very old, and she had watched a great many people decide things, and she could see, now that she was paying attention, exactly what Umaru looked like right before she did something sly.

She won the fourth.

She won the fifth.

The common room had gone completely silent.

Umaru stared at the board.

"How," she said.

"You bite your lip," said Milim, "before you do the sneaky thing."

Umaru stopped biting her lip.

Milim won the sixth anyway, by watching her hands instead.

Three games each.

The seventh went on for forty minutes. Nobody left. Momo forgot about lunch. Bocchi stopped tuning. Frieren, who had claimed to be reading in the corner, had stopped turning pages twenty minutes ago and nobody was polite enough to pretend otherwise.

Umaru won it by one move.

She sat back.

Milim looked at the board for a long time.

Then she grinned, the biggest grin Umaru had ever seen on anyone, and reached across and grabbed Umaru's hand and shook it so hard that Umaru's whole body shook with it.

"AGAIN," said Milim. "Tomorrow. Again."

"I won."

"You did! It was amazing! You're amazing! Again!"

The room started cheering.



Umaru went to claim her prize that evening.

She walked down the corridor, past the remains of the fort, past Mau and Frieren's door, and stopped at the end bay.

The door was open.

Milim was inside, sitting cross-legged on her pillow on the floor, packing.

She did not have much. A blanket. A second pillow. A bag of snacks she was very clearly leaving behind on purpose, on top of the folded blanket, in a neat pile that said *for Umaru*. And the propaganda poster, which she had taken off the wall and was rolling up very carefully so it would not crease.

She was looking out of the little window.

Straight up the corridor. At Rimuru's door.

Umaru stood in the doorway and watched her look.

She thought about what Milim had said at the start, about sleeping alone in her world for a very long time. She had not been listening properly then. She had been busy declaring war.

She thought about her own room, beside the Witch Hat girls'. Coco through one wall, laughing at night. Tetia's stars on the curtain. Agott pretending not to listen to their conversations and then correcting their facts. Her room was full of noise. Her room had never once been quiet enough to be lonely in.

"Milim," said Umaru.

Milim looked up, already smiling.

"It's yours! You won! I'm almost done, I just have to—"

"Keep it."

Milim stopped.

"I won," said Umaru, "so I get to decide. And I decide you keep it." She folded her arms, because her face was doing something embarrassing. "On conditions."

Milim stared at her.

"What conditions?"

"One. We play every week. Every single week. And you have to get better, because beating you three times in nine minutes is boring."

"Yes," said Milim instantly.

"Two. The snacks stay in the fort. The fort is permanent now. It's a monument."

"YES."

"Three." Umaru looked down the corridor. At Mau's door. At the long way round she'd have to walk, every time, from the Witch Hat side. "I want a door."

"A door?"

"A door. My own door. From my side straight across the courtyard to here. So I don't have to go past four rooms and a demon to see my brother."

Milim thought about it very seriously.

"I don't know how to make a door."

"Wakana does," said Umaru.



Wakana did.

He cut it the next morning: a small, low door in the wall of the Witch Hat corridor, just beside Umaru's room, opening straight onto the boardwalk across the courtyard. From it to the east end of the shell was eleven steps, one fewer than the boardwalk had ever been.

Umaru counted.

Someone painted UMARU'S DOOR across the top of it. Everybody assumed it was Umaru, and it was not. It was Milim, in enormous pink letters, and she had done it at dawn while Umaru was still asleep, and she spent the entire morning pretending she did not know who could possibly have done such a thing.

On Wakana's board, under the hook that said UMARU CANNOT REACH MAU. THIS IS WAR, somebody had written:

TREATY SIGNED.

And under that, in very large pink letters:

AGAIN TOMORROW.
