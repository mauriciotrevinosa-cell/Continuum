# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 7 — A Small Library

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 7  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary Week 2 | Mau and Frieren's room is let out east: library bay, closet, reading chair | Treaty boardwalk restored

"A library," said Frieren.

Mau looked up from the bed.

She was standing in the middle of their room with her arms full of books. Not a few books. All of them. Every grimoire she had carried out of the old inn, plus every grimoire that had followed her there over the months, plus a handful of things that were not grimoires but had been near grimoires long enough to count.

The shelf, the famous shelf, had given up two days ago. Books lay in stacks along the north wall, under the bed, on the windowsill, and in one tower beside the door that Ori had walked into twice.

"We have a shelf," said Mau.

"We have a shelf that has surrendered."

"We could get another shelf."

"Where?"

Mau looked around the room.

It was not a large room. It had been a large room on the plan. It had been a large room the first night, when there was nothing in it but a bed and two people and a cap on a temporary hook. Then the books had come, and the clothes, and his backpack, and Frieren's things from the old inn, and Ori's spare blanket because Ori's new room was cold, and a chair that nobody remembered bringing.

"A small library," said Mau.

Frieren considered.

"Define small."

"A shelf. A real one. Floor to ceiling. One wall."

"Two walls."

"One and a half."

"Two," said Frieren, "and a reading chair."

"There's no room for a reading chair."

Frieren looked at him with great patience.

"Then," she said, "we need more room."



The advantage of living in a muslin, Wakana explained, was that you could let it out.

He walked the outside of their room with his chalk. The walls on three sides were taken: Ori through one, Bocchi's buffer through another, Milim's end bay through the third. The corridor was the fourth. There was only one way to grow.

East.

Out into the courtyard, toward the morning.

"One bay," said Wakana. "Posts, a short roof, boards. Two days."

"One bay for the library," said Mau.

"And a closet," said Frieren.

Mau turned.

"Since when do we need a closet?"

"Since you started keeping your shirts on the chair."

"You wear my shirts."

"Yes," said Frieren. "From the chair. If they were in a closet, I could find them."

Mau opened his mouth to argue.

He found he did not have an argument.

"One bay," said Wakana, writing. "Library. Closet. Reading chair."

"I said no reading chair."

"You said there was no room for one," said Frieren. "Now there's room."

Mau sat down on the bed.

"I'm losing."

"Yes," said Frieren kindly.



The bay went up in two days.

It was perfect for exactly one morning.

Then Umaru came across the courtyard through Umaru's Door, along the boardwalk, eleven steps exactly as the treaty promised, and walked straight into the back wall of the new closet.

She stood there with her nose almost touching the boards.

Then she walked around it. The long way. Around the new bay, past the closet, past the library wall, through the mud the boardwalk had been built to avoid, to the east end of the shell.

Nineteen steps.

She came in through their door with mud on her socks and a face of pure constitutional outrage.

"TREATY VIOLATION."

Mau and Frieren were sitting on the bed. Frieren had a book. Mau had a shirt, which he had just taken out of the closet for the first time, with some ceremony.

"What?"

"Eleven steps. The treaty says eleven steps. You built a closet ON MY ROAD."

"It's not a road. It's a boardwalk."

"It's a TREATY BOARDWALK."

Frieren put her book down.

"She's right," she said.

Mau turned to look at her.

"You wanted the closet."

"I did. I want Umaru to get here in eleven steps more."

Umaru, who had been ready for a long fight, was so surprised by this that she forgot to be angry for almost three seconds.

They moved the closet.

It took a day. Wakana lifted the boards, turned the closet a quarter turn into the inner corner of the room, and shortened the new bay by half a step, so that the boardwalk could end where it had always ended: at a little landing just outside the library wall, with a low step up to the corridor door.

Umaru counted on the way back across.

Eleven.

She nodded with deep satisfaction, as if she had won something much larger than a closet, and went home through her own door.



That evening, Mau lay on the bed and looked at the room.

The library filled the new bay: two walls of shelves, floor to ceiling, mostly full already. A reading chair Mau had definitely said no to sat in the corner of it, and Frieren was in it, with her feet tucked up and a lamp beside her. The closet, turned a quarter, stood in the inner corner. The old shelf stood exactly where it always had, because neither of them would concede its placement. It now held Mau's books, which the library had politely declined. The bed was still on the north wall, under the east window, where the light would land in the morning.

On the other side of the west wall, Ori was moving around, arranging something, humming under her breath.

On the other side of the south wall, Bocchi was playing something, very softly, and the buffer swallowed most of it and let a little through.

On the other side of the east end, Milim was talking in her sleep. It sounded like she was winning an argument.

Across the corridor, Yuta laughed at something Maki said.

Down the corridor, faintly, the second bell rang. Somebody pulling it by accident, probably, on the way past.

"We lost the corner," said Mau.

"We lost the corner months ago," said Frieren, without looking up from her book.

"We lost the closet."

"It's still there."

"It's in a different place."

"It's in a better place."

"We lost the shelf."

"The shelf is exactly where it was."

"It's in the wrong place."

"That's a different argument."

"We lost," said Mau, "all of it. Every single argument. The room's twice the size I wanted and half of it is books."

Frieren turned a page.

"Do you mind?"

Mau thought about it honestly.

He listened to Ori through one wall and Bocchi through another and Milim through the third. He listened to Yuta and Maki across the corridor. He looked at the library, and the reading chair, and Frieren in it, in the lamplight, with her feet tucked up.

"No," he said.

"Good," said Frieren.

She went on reading.

After a while, so did he.
