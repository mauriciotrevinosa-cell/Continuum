# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 8 — Where Rimuru Sleeps

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 8  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary Week 2 | Rimuru sleep-travels to the one room nobody knocks on | Wakana builds a loft over the closet

The first time, Mau thought it was a dream.

He woke a little before dawn and something on the bed was wrong. Not frightening. Just wrong. A weight where there should not have been a weight, on Frieren's side, round and soft and faintly cool, rising and falling very slightly in the dark.

He lifted his head.

Rimuru was asleep on Frieren's stomach.

Not Rimuru the person. Rimuru the slime. A blue dome about the size of a loaf of bread, settled comfortably in the middle of Frieren's blanket like a cat that had chosen its spot, with his eyes closed into two contented lines.

Frieren was asleep too.

She had one hand resting on top of him.

Mau lay there and looked at this for a long time.

Then he went back to sleep, because it was too early to have an opinion about anything.

When he woke properly, Rimuru was gone.



The second time, Frieren noticed first.

"There's a slime on me," she said, at dawn, without opening her eyes.

Mau looked.

There was a slime on her.

"It's Rimuru."

"I know it's Rimuru," said Frieren. "I'm asking why."

Rimuru opened one eye. Then the other. Then he looked at where he was, and at Frieren's hand resting on top of him, and at Mau looking at him over the edge of the blanket, and he went very slightly paler blue, which Mau had not known was possible.

"I don't know," said Rimuru.

"You don't know?"

"I went to sleep in my room. I definitely went to sleep in my room. Diablo was outside. Somebody had knocked about the chimney at midnight." He looked around. "This is not my room."

"No," Frieren agreed.

"I'm so sorry—"

"It's fine," said Frieren. "You're warm."

Mau turned to look at her.

"He's cold. Slimes are cold."

"He's warm in the middle," said Frieren, and went back to sleep.



The third time, nobody was surprised.

The fourth time, Mau asked.

"Ciel," he said, very quietly, so as not to wake either of them. "Does Rimuru know how he's getting in here?"

No, said Ciel.

"Do you?"

Yes.

"How?"

There was a pause, and something in it that Mau had come to recognize as Ciel deciding whether a thing was funny or not.

Spatial motion, said Ciel. At approximately two in the morning. Every night. While asleep. He does not wake for it. He simply arrives.

Mau stared at the ceiling.

"He's leaking."

He is not leaking. He has full control of his skills. A pause. He is choosing a destination while asleep, without informing himself.

"That's leaking."

That is a slime who has not slept properly since the Move and has found the one room in this building where nobody looks for him.

Mau was quiet.

He thought about it. Rimuru's room, with Diablo outside it, with people knocking at midnight about chimneys. The common room, which never emptied. The workshop, where people found him to ask about boards. Every corner of the Muslin had a reason for someone to come looking for the builder.

Except this one.

Fern had made a rule, the first week. Nobody knocked on Mau and Frieren's door after dark unless something was on fire or someone was dying. She had made it for Mau, because of the leaks and the bad nights. Everyone obeyed it. Even Diablo obeyed it, out of some private principle about respecting a master's brother's sleep.

Nobody looked for anything in this room after dark.

So Rimuru's sleeping self had found it.

Do not tell him I said it like that, said Ciel. He will be embarrassed.

"You're going to tell him."

I am going to tell him the destination and the time. I am not going to tell him the reason. He knows the reason.



At breakfast, Rimuru apologized for the fourth time.

"I'll stop," he said. "I'll ask Ciel to wake me before I— I don't know. I'll sleep in the workshop. I'll put up a ward."

"Don't," said Frieren.

Rimuru looked at her.

"You need somewhere nobody looks," she said, buttering bread with great concentration. "We have somewhere nobody looks. It's not complicated."

"It's your room."

"It's a big room now," said Frieren. "Mau lost every argument."

"I did not lose every argument."

"Name one you won."

Mau thought about it.

He did not name one.

Rimuru looked between them, and then down at his plate, and his face did something private.

"I can't keep sleeping on your stomach," he said finally.

"Why not?"

"Because it's your stomach."

"Then sleep somewhere else in the room," said Frieren. "Somewhere that's yours."



Wakana built it in an afternoon.

He looked at the room, and the closet in its inner corner, and the space above it, which went all the way up to the roof beams and held nothing but air. He went away and came back with boards and a ladder, and Rimuru, watching, said, "Oh," in a voice Mau had never heard him use.

"What?"

"It's a loft," said Rimuru. "In my world. Small apartments had them. The bed goes up high, over the closet or the kitchen, and you climb up a ladder to sleep." He watched Wakana fit the first board. "I lived in one. Before I was a slime. It was the only part of that apartment I liked."

The loft was not large. It did not need to be. It was a platform over the closet with a low rail on the open side so that a sleeping slime would not roll off it. There was a short ladder that Rimuru, as a slime, did not need, and a folded blanket, and a pillow that was much too small for a person and exactly the right size for a dome of blue.

Rimuru climbed up the ladder anyway, in his human form, because Wakana had built it and it would have been rude not to.

He sat on the edge of the loft with his legs dangling over the closet and looked down at the room. The bed under the east window. The library bay. Frieren in her reading chair. Mau on the bed.

"It's good," he said.

"Do you want your room back?" said Mau. "For sleeping, I mean. We'd understand."

Rimuru shook his head.

"I'll keep it for the day. An office. People can knock on it all they want." He lay back on the loft, looking up at the roof beams very close above him. "This is for sleeping."

Diablo, when told, was briefly devastated. Then he decided that standing outside Rimuru-sama's office during the day was a far more significant duty than standing outside his bedroom at night, and that this was, in fact, a promotion.

Milim, when told, was outraged for about four seconds. Then she realized that her end bay shared a wall with Mau and Frieren's room, and that Rimuru was now sleeping on the other side of that wall, and she was so pleased that she went and woke Umaru to tell her.

Umaru, when woken, said something that could not be repeated.



That night, Mau lay in bed and listened to the room.

Frieren beside him, asleep, with most of the blanket.

Ori through one wall.

Bocchi, faintly, through another.

Milim through the third, talking in her sleep.

Yuta and Maki across the corridor.

And above them, over the closet, in the loft, a very soft sound he had never heard before. Slow and even and contented, like the sea heard from very far away.

Rimuru, snoring.

Mau had not known slimes could snore.

He lay and listened to it for a long time, and somewhere in the listening he understood that he had lost every argument about this room, every single one, and that he had never in his life been so glad to lose anything.

Above him, in the dark, the snoring stopped.

"Mau," said Rimuru, very quietly.

"Yeah?"

"Thank you."

"Go to sleep."

Rimuru went to sleep.

So, eventually, did he.
