# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 10 — Anko Wants One Too

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 10  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary Week 2 | Nazuna's dark loft over Anko's bed | Four rooms become one room with five beds

Anko saw the loft on a Tuesday and asked for one on Wednesday.

She did not ask Wakana. She asked Mau, because she had known Mau longest and because asking Mau was the fastest way to make something happen while pretending you had not asked anyone.

"It's not for me," she said.

They were in the common room. Anko was writing. She was always writing. She did not look up.

"Who's it for?"

"Nobody."

"Anko."

Anko sighed and put her pen down.

"Nazuna sleeps all day," she said. "You know that. Everyone knows that. She's a vampire. Light bothers her. Noise bothers her. And our room has a window on the courtyard side, and half the house goes past it from noon onward, and every afternoon she sleeps with her head under a pillow and wakes up in a terrible mood and tells me it's fine."

"Is it fine?"

"She missed an entire day last week," said Anko. "Slept through it. Woke up after dark and everyone had already had the day without her. She laughed about it." Anko picked her pen up again and looked at it. "She didn't think it was funny."

Mau thought about Nazuna, who was always awake when everyone else was asleep and always asleep when everyone else was awake, and who had turned up at the old inn following Anko and never quite explained why she stayed.

"So a loft," he said.

"High up. Dark. A curtain all the way round, heavy, so no light gets in. She can sleep up there all day and the house can go past underneath." Anko shrugged. "And I'll sleep below. At night. When she's up."

"You'll be in the same room at opposite times."

"Yes."

"You'll never be asleep together."

"No," said Anko. "But we'll always be in the same room."

Mau looked at her.

Anko looked back.

"Shut up," she said.

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."



Wakana built that one in an afternoon too.

It was taller than Rimuru's. Nazuna was a person-sized person and needed person-sized space, and she also, it turned out, liked to sit up there at night with her legs over the edge and look down at the room. So Wakana made it high, almost to the roof beams, with a ladder that folded flat against the wall. Marin made the curtain. It was black, triple-lined and heavy enough to stop a lantern, and it ran on a rail all the way round the loft, so that when it was closed the whole platform became a small dark box with a bed in it.

Nazuna watched the whole thing with her arms folded and an expression of deep, theatrical boredom.

"I don't need it," she said.

"Okay," said Anko.

"I sleep fine."

"Okay."

"I'm not going to use it."

"Okay," said Anko.

Nazuna climbed up the ladder to inspect it, purely to be polite. She drew the curtain all the way round, purely to test it. There was a long silence.

She did not come down for eleven hours.

When she did, it was after dark, and the house was eating dinner, and she walked into the common room yawning, in the best mood anyone had ever seen her in, and sat down next to Anko, and stole her bread.

"Slept well?" said Anko, not looking up.

"Didn't use it," said Nazuna.

Anko let her keep the bread.



The wall between Hakari and Karane came down the same week.

It had been coming down for a long time.

From the first night in the Muslin, the four of them had talked through it. Hakari, Karane, Shizuka and Nano, in their four small rooms in a row, had argued, laughed, complained and told each other increasingly unhinged stories through three board walls at a volume that carried to the far end of the corridor. Karane had announced, through a wall, that Hakari's snoring was structural. Hakari had announced back that Karane's complaining was load-bearing. Shizuka had participated by holding her book up to the gap at the top of the wall and reading the right line out of it at exactly the right moment. Nano had kept minutes.

Wakana's board had a hook on it about this that predated almost everything else.

Four rooms. One conversation.

"We should just take the walls out," said Hakari one morning.

"Absolutely not," said Karane. "I need my own space. I need my privacy. I need—"

She stopped.

The others looked at her.

"I need," said Karane, slowly, "to hear what you idiots are talking about, because every time I can't hear it I get out of bed and come and stand at the wall anyway."

"So yes," said Hakari.

"It's not because I like you."

"It's because you love us," said Hakari, and threw her arms around Karane, who made a noise like a kettle.

Nano, consulted, said that four rooms with one shared wall-gap was, on a calculated basis, less efficient than one room with four beds. Shizuka held up her book. The line it was open to said, in neat print, *At last, she thought, we are home.*

Wakana lifted out three walls in an hour.

Then he looked at the fourth wall, the one at the end, beyond which was the small room where Kusuri slept when she was not at Maomao's laboratory bench. He looked at the four of them.

Kusuri was standing in the corridor in her lab coat, with a cup of something steaming in one hand. She had come out to see what the noise was. Her face was trying very hard to look as if she had no opinion about anything.

"That one too?" said Wakana.

Hakari looked at Kusuri.

Kusuri looked at the floor.

"Only if she wants," said Hakari, suddenly very gentle.

Kusuri drank some of whatever was in the cup. It was not tea. It was steaming in a slightly unusual color.

"My work bench stays at Maomao's," she said. "Chemicals don't sleep where people sleep. Maomao's rule."

"Obviously," said Karane.

"But my bed—" Kusuri stopped. "My bed doesn't have to be by itself."

Wakana lifted out the fourth wall.



By the end of that week, there were eleven hooks left on Wakana's board.

He stood in front of it one evening with his chalk, reading them over. The kitchen bell: fixed. The courtyard: fixed. Ori: fixed. Umaru: treaty. Rimuru: loft. Nazuna: loft, and a hook that had never been written down, now crossed out anyway. The four rooms in a row that had always been one room: one room, now, with five beds.

Marin came and stood beside him.

"It fits better," she said.

"It fits better," Wakana agreed.

"Is it done?"

Wakana looked at the board for a long time.

"No," he said. "But now I know what the real one looks like."

He wrote that on the board too, in small letters, at the bottom:

The real one: everyone close. Somewhere dark for Nazuna. Somewhere nobody knocks for Rimuru. A door for Umaru. A wall that comes out for whoever needs it to.

Then, after a moment:

More bells.
