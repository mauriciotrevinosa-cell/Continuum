# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 16 — The Day We Picked

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 16  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary Week 3 | Mau's chosen birthday (S1E15) passed unnoticed during the Memory crisis | Household plans it late

Fern found it in the last box from the old inn kitchen.

The box nobody had wanted to unpack, because it held the things that had no obvious place in the new one.

A ladle with a cracked handle.

Three mismatched lids.

The calendar.

It had hung by the stove for over a year, gathering grease and small notes in five different hands. Deliveries. Market days. The day Milim was not allowed near the oven, underlined twice.

And one day, circled.

Fern had circled it herself.

She stood very still with the calendar in her hands, and counted.

Then counted again.

The circled day had come and gone weeks ago.

While they were all counting other things.

Days without Mau.

Hours of fever.

Minutes of lucidity.

Nobody had looked at a calendar.

Not even Fern, who looked at everything.

Momo came in for water and stopped in the doorway.

"What's wrong with your face?"

Fern held up the calendar.

Momo looked at the circle.

Looked at it longer.

Her face did the same thing Fern's had.

"...We missed it."

"Yes."

"His birthday."

"Yes."

Momo set down the water jug very carefully.

For a moment the two of them just stood there, in the kitchen of a house that was not finished yet, holding a calendar from a house they had left.

Then Momo said:

"No."

Fern looked at her.

"No, we didn't miss it," Momo said. "It's just late."



Coco heard about it within the hour, because Coco heard about everything within the hour.

She went very still.

Then she turned to Umaru, who was lying on the common room floor doing nothing with tremendous commitment.

"Umaru."

"Mm."

"The karaoke."

Umaru sat straight up.

They looked at each other.

Neither of them said anything.

Neither of them needed to.



By evening, half the settlement knew, and the half that knew had agreed on exactly one rule.

Mau could not find out.

This was harder than it sounded, because Mau lived in the middle of everything, and noticed when the middle of everything went quiet.

Frieren was assigned to keep him away.

She objected.

"I'm bad at secrets."

"You don't have to keep a secret," Fern said. "You just have to keep him."

Frieren considered this.

"That I can do."

The next morning she announced that she needed to walk to the river to look for a spell.

What spell?

She did not say.

Which spell, Mau asked, all the way to the river and halfway back, and Frieren said she would know it when she found it, and held his hand, and walked very slowly, and did not find it, and was not sorry.



Everyone else had a job.

Nobody had been assigned one.

They simply appeared.

Kessoku Band claimed the music before anyone could argue.

Nijika arranged it. Ryo said she would handle the bass if nobody asked her to talk about it. Kita said she would sing, then remembered it was supposed to be Mau's song, and said she would sing backup, loudly.

Bocchi said nothing.

Bocchi went into the unfinished music room and did not come out for six hours, and the sounds that came through the door were not quite any song anybody knew.

Marin took over decorations.

Wakana took over making the decorations survive Marin.

Senku looked at the music room, then at the empty common room, then at the music room again, and said that if anyone wanted the sound to carry past the third row of chairs they were going to need a horn.

"A what?" said Nijika.

"Paper. Cone. Acoustics." He was already measuring. "Ten billion percent."

Suika held the paper.

Seiko and Momo took the kitchen.

Maomao took the kitchen away from them every twenty minutes to inspect what they were putting in it, on the grounds that the guest of honor had a head injury and a digestive system that had been through enough.

"It's cake," Momo said.

"It's cake with how much of that?"

"The normal amount!"

"Show me the normal amount."

Jinshi watched this for some time from the doorway.

Then he quietly took a slate and began organizing who was bringing what, from where, and when, so that nothing arrived twice and nothing failed to arrive at all.

Maomao saw him doing it.

She said nothing.

She did not stop him either.

From Maomao, that was a speech.



Kaneki had found something close enough to coffee at the native market weeks ago, and had been improving it ever since without telling anyone.

He told them now.

Himmel tasted it, closed his eyes, and declared that a man who could make that out of whatever this was could do anything in this world.

Kaneki looked at the cup.

"It's just coffee."

"Nothing," said Himmel, "is just coffee."

Stark carried tables.

Nobody asked him to.

He had decided around the third table that this was his job, and around the fifth table Fern walked past and said, without stopping, "That one goes by the window," and Stark moved it by the window, and something about the exchange made Marin put her hand over her heart from across the room.



Qifrey found Coco at the long table drawing on a strip of paper with a pen.

Small marks.

Light.

Not much.

"How much ink is that?" Qifrey asked.

"Hardly any."

He looked at the strip.

Little stars, in a row, that would glow when the room got dark.

"Show me how you set the third one," he said.

Coco showed him.

He corrected her angle by the smallest fraction, then took out his own pen and drew a fourth star beside hers.

"Qifrey—that's yours—you don't have—"

"For this?" Qifrey said. "Yes."



Diablo offered to prepare decorations worthy of the occasion.

Rimuru said no.

Diablo offered to prepare decorations slightly less worthy of the occasion.

Rimuru said no.

Diablo asked what level of worthiness would be acceptable.

Rimuru looked at Marin's paper flowers.

"That one."

Diablo studied the paper flowers for a long time, with the expression of a man being asked to reconsider the fundamental nature of beauty.

Then he went and helped Wakana fold them.

He was very, very good at it.



Milim's job was to keep Mau away from the common room if Frieren failed.

She took it extremely seriously.

She recruited Umaru.

They practiced intercepting him.

They intercepted Okarun four times, Rimuru twice, and a goat once.



Yuta stood in the doorway of his room for most of an afternoon, looking at a small wrapped object on his bed.

Maki leaned on the frame.

"You've been staring at that for an hour."

"I don't know if it's right."

"It's a present."

"Last time I spent forever deciding what a normal birthday present was."

"And?"

"And I gave up on normal."

Maki looked at the object.

Then at him.

"So give up on normal again."

Yuta thought about that.

Then he picked it up and put it in his pocket.



Mai heard about the party from Maki and said it was absurd to celebrate a birthday on a day that was not the birthday.

Then she spent an hour helping Shizuka fold napkins into shapes neither of them could name.

Karane announced that the entire concept was absurd.

Nano said that in this house the date was a suggestion, and that the sooner Karane accepted this the sooner she would be happy.

Karane said she would never be happy.

She folded twelve napkins.



Ori did not tell anyone what she was making.

She worked on it in her room, at night, with the door closed.

When Rem asked, she said only:

"It's small."

Rem nodded and did not ask again.

Later that night Rem found herself at the long table, helping Hakari string paper stars for no reason except that the table was where everyone was, and she had a cup in her hand that she had known exactly where to find.

She did not notice when she had started thinking of the party as hers to help with.

Nobody else noticed either.

That was how it was supposed to work.



On the morning of the day they picked, Fern hung the calendar from the old inn on the new kitchen wall.

She did not take down the circle.

She drew a second one, a few weeks later, around today.

Then she went to wake everyone.
