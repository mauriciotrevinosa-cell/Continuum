# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 5 — The Table Gets a Roof

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 5  
**Continuity state:** Arrival House becomes provisional institution because weather and people demand it

Rain solved the naming argument.

The intake table got wet.

Not poetically.

Forms turned soft.

One map curled.

Ink from a route note bled enough that Okarun swore louder than Fern expected.

Fern looked at the sky.

Then at the table.

"Build a roof."

Momo smiled.

"Arrival House."

Fern:

"No."

Two days later, the table had canvas.

Second table.

Three mismatched chairs.

Crate for blankets.

Small privacy screen that fell twice in wind.

Momo stood beneath it.

"Arrival House."

Fern looked at the absence of walls.

Then at the people waiting.

"Fine."

The name survived architecture.

Functions accumulated because cases did.

A woman arrived asking only for shoes.

No room.

No search.

Shoes.

Wakana found a temporary pair.

Arrival complete.

A child arrived with an older sibling and asked:

"Can monsters get in?"

Mikasa did not say no.

She showed them:
alarm line;
safe room;
what sound meant move;
what sound meant wait.

The child slept.

A traveler asked:

"If I leave tomorrow, is that rude?"

Fern stared.

"No."

The traveler looked shocked.

"People said you want Otherworlders here."

"We want people safe."

"Same."

"No."

Useful distinction.

A man arrived afraid of Contracts because someone told him accepting food could bind him.

Rimuru nearly choked.

They added a sign:

FOOD IS NOT A CONTRACT.

Momo added beneath:

UNLESS THE FOOD IS SUSPICIOUS.

Fern removed it.

Medical check became available.

Not mandatory for everyone.

Required when symptoms could affect others.

Privacy explanation.

Maps.

Missing-person board.

Local-language help.

Status discussion.

Guest.

Temporary protection.

Temporary resident.

Resident.

Transit.

No one loved the categories.

People needed words to know what they had agreed to.

Arrival House became more real every time someone asked a question the settlement had never considered.

Can I sleep here and leave before sunrise?

Yes.

Can I use the map without giving my name?

Usually.

Can you hide me from someone?

Depends who and why.

Can my friend stay if I leave?

Their choice.

Can I refuse a Contract?

Yes.

Can I tell you my power later?

Usually.

Can I not talk about my old world?

Yes.

By the end of the week, everyone called the canvas structure Arrival House.

Not because it was a house.

Because arrival had become work.

Someone had to receive people without turning them into either threats or family before they had time to become themselves.
