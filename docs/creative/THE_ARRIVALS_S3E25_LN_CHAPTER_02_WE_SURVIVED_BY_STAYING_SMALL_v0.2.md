# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 14 — We Survived by Staying Small

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 14

The second community's argument had survived reality.

That mattered more than whether Rimuru liked it.

No giant power displays.

No famous resident walking the road under a recognizable name.

No public claim to territory.

No concentration large enough to become easy political shorthand.

Their spokesperson said:

"We survived because nobody cared enough to fear us."

The sentence hurt because it was not cowardice.

It was strategy.

Mikasa asked:

"What happens if someone disappears?"

The spokesperson looked at her.

"We look."

"How far?"

"As far as we can."

"How many people?"

"Depends who can leave work."

There.

The weakness.

Small meant less visible.

Also:
fewer medics;
fewer scouts;
fewer spare beds;
fewer people who could leave for two days without somebody else's work failing.

A child had been sick the previous winter.

They had relied on the native village's healer and luck.

A monster attack months earlier had damaged three homes.

Everyone rebuilt.

If the same attack had been twice as large, maybe not.

Rimuru did not hide the main settlement's weakness either.

"We're becoming a target."

The spokesperson nodded.

"Yes."

"We can absorb more people."

"Yes."

"We can search farther."

"Yes."

"You have relationships here we don't."

"Yes."

No winner.

The second community had avoided dangers the main settlement invited.

The main settlement had capacities the second could not match.

A younger resident interrupted.

"My sister wants to move to yours."

The spokesperson:

"She can."

Rimuru looked up.

No territorial ownership.

"Would that be a problem?"

"Why?"

Fair.

Another resident said:

"I'd never live there."

Also fine.

The two communities discussed what cooperation could exist without merger.

Warning signals.

Route reports.

Medical requests.

Temporary refuge during specific threats.

Trade.

Searcher coordination only when invited.

No shared command.

No automatic defense obligation.

Anko wrote.

The spokesperson watched.

"You're going to turn this into rules."

"Only the parts we agreed."

"Good."

At the end, Rimuru offered an emergency signal method.

Not a permanent surveillance system.

Something they could trigger.

The second community accepted.

Then offered a local contact network in return.

Their strength was not military.

It was embedded relationships.

They knew farmers.

Carters.

A village elder.

Which road patrol actually enforced which order.

Information the main settlement's power could not manufacture.

On the road home, Mikasa said:

"They need us more than they think."

Anko:

"Maybe."

Rimuru:

"And we need things they have."

Mikasa thought.

Then nodded.

No moral superiority.

Cooperation became possible only after everyone stopped treating scale as virtue.
