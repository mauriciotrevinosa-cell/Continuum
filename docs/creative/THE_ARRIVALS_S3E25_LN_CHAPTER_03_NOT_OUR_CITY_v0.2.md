# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 23 — Not Our City

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 23

Six residents from the second community visited.

Most did not.

That was part of the point.

They arrived without ceremony.

Arrival House offered guest cards.

One visitor looked at the card.

"Do I need this?"

Fern:

"No. It helps us know where you're sleeping."

"I'm not."

"Then no."

The visitor smiled.

Good start.

Tour became impossible almost immediately.

One older resident hated the common-room noise.

One teenager loved Bocchi's music room enough to ask three technical questions Bocchi barely survived answering.

Another visitor stood near the water system and said:

"This is why people think you're building a state."

Rimuru looked at the half-finished channel.

"It's plumbing."

"States have plumbing."

Momo:

"Checkmate."

Rimuru ignored her.

They saw Milim in the distance.

One visitor stopped.

"She lives here?"

Rimuru hesitated.

Milim shouted from far away:

"YES!"

The visitor looked at Rimuru.

"I couldn't live here."

Momo, offended:

"Why?"

"Too many powerful people."

Milim waved.

Valid.

Another disliked Arrival House.

"Looks like registration."

Fern's face changed.

"It is not mandatory."

"It still looks like somebody wants to know who comes in."

"Sometimes we do."

Honest.

The visitor appreciated the honesty more than reassurance.

At dinner, someone from the main settlement asked:

"Wouldn't you be safer if you moved here?"

The question quieted the table.

The second-community spokesperson was not present.

A younger visitor answered.

"Safer from what?"

Silence.

Monster attack?

Probably.

Holding site?

Maybe.

Political targeting because concentrated Otherworlders were visible?

Maybe not.

Being socially absorbed by a place with powerful founders and existing relationships?

Not if the settlement did its job correctly.

But fear was rational.

Mau sat two seats away.

He felt the urge to defend home.

Water works.

Searchers.

Arrival House.

Defense.

Rooms.

People.

All true.

He said nothing.

The visitor continued:

"We survived by not becoming a symbol."

Mau looked around.

The main settlement already was one.

Not automatically better.

Different cost.

The next morning, four visitors left.

Two stayed another day because they wanted to.

One teenager traded music notes with Bocchi.

The older resident gave Maomao a recipe for a tea nobody liked except Maomao.

They took route warnings.

Left information about a local official.

No conversion.

No dramatic unity.

Mau watched them go.

Frieren stood beside him.

"Do you want them to stay?"

"Some."

"Why?"

Mau thought.

"Because I like having people here."

Good.

Not:
because it's safer for them.

Not:
because our model is correct.

Frieren nodded.

Desire admitted without becoming authority.

The two communities became more real by remaining different.
