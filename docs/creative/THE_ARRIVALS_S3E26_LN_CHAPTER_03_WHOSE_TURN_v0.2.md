# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 35 — Whose Turn

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 35  
**Continuity state:** Refuge week | Care flows back toward Mau | Sprout appears

At the long table in Arrival House, Mau had three ledgers open and had not eaten since breakfast.

Beds.

Water.

Names.

Who had arrived.

Who was leaving.

Who had asked where the western road went and been told the truth, which was that right now it went to a checkpoint.

He did not remember deciding to take all three ledgers.

He did not remember fetching the third one from the storeroom, either. It had simply been in his hand. Ciel had told him so, an hour ago, under rule eight.

The first one in weeks.

Tired, she had said. You get careless with distance when you are tired.

He only remembered that they had needed someone, and he had been there.

That was the old problem.

It had not gone anywhere.

It had only learned to wait until he was tired.

Ori came in and sat down across from him.

She did not say anything.

She reached over and slid the middle ledger out from under his hand.

Mau looked up.

"I was using that."

"I know."

"I'm halfway through the—"

"Bed count. I know." Ori turned it toward herself and picked up a pen. "Fifty-one spaces. Fifty-five bodies, since this afternoon. Two of the second-community guests are sharing because they asked to, not because we made them. You wrote that down twice."

Mau looked at the page.

He had.

"You taught me how to do these," Ori said, already writing. "Remember? The first week. I kept following you into the kitchen, so you gave me the jars in the pantry to count. So I would know there was enough."

He did remember.

That one, he was sure of.

"That was different."

"Why?"

He did not have an answer.

Ori did not wait for one.

She finished the bed count in less time than it would have taken him, in handwriting neater than his, and slid it back, and took the third ledger as well.

Mau watched his own work disappear into her hands.

It made him uncomfortable in a way he could not name.

Ori saw it.

"You can still stay," she said. "You just don't have to carry it."



Fern was in the doorway.

She had been there for a little while.

She had been watching Ori take a ledger out from under someone's hand while they protested, and turn it toward herself, and do it better, and not ask permission.

She knew that motion.

She had been making it for years.

Maps taken off the table while someone ate.

Food put down in front of someone who said they were not hungry.

A spell book closed, gently, at two in the morning.

Later, when Mau had gone to wash his hands, Fern sat down beside Ori.

"Does he let you?" she asked.

Ori thought about it.

"No."

Fern nodded slowly.

"Neither does she," she said. "You do it anyway."

Ori looked at her.

Fern did not smile, exactly.

But something in her face said: I know. I know exactly what this is. You are not doing it wrong.

They sat there together for a while with the ledgers between them, two people who had each, separately, come to love someone who could not take care of themselves, and had decided to do it for them.

Neither of them said that out loud.

They did not need to.



"Come on," Ori said, when Mau came back.

"Where?"

"It's my turn."

"Turn for what?"

She pointed out of the window.

Across the yard, at Wakana's measuring stake, the board still hung where Coco had hung it.

WHO WATERS IT

Everyone.

"That's not a turn," said Mau. "That's everyone."

"Then it's my turn," said Ori, "and it's yours, and you're coming."



The circle Wakana had chalked onto the ground had faded to a pale ring.

Inside it, the earth was dark from someone's watering that morning.

And in the middle of the dark earth, so small Mau almost stepped past it, something green.

Two leaves.

Folded.

Barely open.

Mau stopped.

Ori stopped beside him.

Neither of them said anything for a while.

Then, from behind them:

"I saw it at dawn."

Coco.

She was sitting on an upturned bucket a short distance away, chin in her hands, as if she had been waiting there for some time.

"Why didn't you tell anyone?" Ori asked.

Coco looked at Mau.

"It was supposed to be a memory," she said. "So I scheduled it."



Qifrey came next, because Coco had been gone from the atelier for an hour and he had known where she would be.

He crouched beside the circle and looked at the two small leaves for a long, long time.

He did not say anything.

He did not need to.

Then Senku and Agott arrived together, still in the middle of an argument that had apparently been going on since lunch about whether the seed had died or was only slow.

Senku had said three weeks.

Agott had said five.

It had been longer than both.

They looked at the sprout.

They looked at each other.

"Late," said Senku.

"Late," said Agott, through her teeth.

Neither of them had predicted late.

Umaru and Milim arrived because there was a crowd.

Stark arrived with a bucket of water, because someone had said watering, and when someone said watering he was going to be the one carrying it. Ori took the bucket from him and poured very carefully, the way she had been taught to count jars.

Rem came across the yard with her blue-rimmed cup in one hand, saw everyone, and stopped at the edge of the group, and then, after a moment, did not stop at the edge.

Kaneki followed Rem.

Okarun followed the noise.

Yuta and Maki came last, from the direction of the training ground, both sweaty, both pretending they had not been running to see what everyone was looking at.

Frieren came because Mau was there.

She crouched down across the circle from Qifrey and held her hand above the two green leaves, not touching.

Still not mana.

Still something she did not know.

She did not frown at it this time.

She looked almost pleased.



Nobody did anything for an hour.

Fifty-five people needed fifty-one beds.

The western road went to a checkpoint.

Somewhere on that road, a rumor about them was still getting worse.

None of it went away.

All of it waited.

For one hour, a dozen people sat in the yard around a sprout the size of a fingernail, and Umaru fell asleep against Milim's shoulder, and Senku and Agott found something new to argue about, and Okarun told Kaneki a theory about whether the plant would have opinions, and Kaneki considered it seriously, and Rem drank her tea, and Mau sat on the ground with his back against Wakana's stake and did not reach for a single ledger.

Ori had them.



That night the bed count was finished, and correct, and in Ori's handwriting.

Mau looked at it for a long time before he went to sleep.

He did not take it back.
