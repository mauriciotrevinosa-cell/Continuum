# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 28 — Who Are We Speaking For?

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 28  
**Continuity state:** Refuge pressure high | Main settlement, second community and native partners consider public response

The idea of a public statement came from a native merchant.

Not Mau.

Not Rimuru.

That mattered.

The merchant had spent three days carrying copies of checkpoint rules that changed every time a town rewrote them.

He placed the papers on Arrival House table.

"If you don't say what you are, other people will."

The room disliked that.

The second community disliked it more.

Their spokesperson arrived before sunset after hearing the proposal.

"We are not part of your settlement."

Rimuru:

"Correct."

"We are not joining it by signing anything."

"Correct."

"Then don't write 'we' like you mean all of us."

Anko crossed out the first paragraph.

Good start.

The meeting grew larger than intended.

Main-settlement delegates.

Second-community delegates.

Two native partners.

Arrival House.

Searchers.

One trader who actually carried rumors.

No person claiming:
all Otherworlders.

Mau attended.

Spoke too much in the first twenty minutes.

Frieren kicked his ankle under the table.

He looked at her.

She did not apologize.

Helpful.

First draft:

"We, the displaced people of other worlds—"

Second-community representative:

"No."

Nano:

"Grammatically?"

"Politically."

Crossed out.

Another proposal:

"We ask the people of this world to understand—"

Native representative:

"Also no."

Momo whispered:

"Everyone hates language."

Anko:

"Good."

The native representative explained.

"You don't need to ask the entire world to understand you. Say what you object to."

Specific.

Better.

They tried again.

Collective punishment is wrong.

That survived.

Indefinite disappearance under registration is wrong.

Survived after wording changed twice.

A person's origin does not erase individual responsibility.

Native partner liked.

Second community liked.

Main settlement liked.

Then:

"Peaceful Otherworlders deserve protection."

A native representative crossed out peaceful.

Mau looked.

"Why?"

"Because rights shouldn't depend on somebody being harmless."

Silence.

Aira, standing at the wall because sitting through meetings was torture, said:

"Keep that."

Mikasa nodded.

Someone objected:

"What about dangerous people?"

Anko answered:

"Individual danger can justify individual measures. Category danger doesn't justify making people disappear."

There.

Specific.

The statement became boring.

Excellent.

Movement.

Detention.

Evidence.

Sanctuary.

Accountability.

No promise that every Otherworlder was safe.

No claim that natives had no reason for fear.

No claim that the main settlement was innocent of every future mistake.

No threat.

Then the hardest line:

Who are we speaking for?

They answered with signatures instead of abstraction.

Main settlement could speak for main settlement.

Second community for itself.

Native partners for their own communities.

Individuals for themselves.

No:
all people from other worlds.

Mau looked at the page.

"Less powerful."

Anko:

"More true."

Good.

One line survived every revision:

> No person should disappear because fear found a category easier than a name.

Mau liked it.

So did people who did not particularly like Mau.

Even better.

Near midnight, the meeting stopped.

Not finished.

Everyone hungry.

The merchant who started it looked at the pages.

"You know people will still simplify this."

Fern:

"Yes."

"Then why bother?"

The second-community spokesperson answered.

"Because when they lie about what we said, we need something real to point to."

The statement did not create unity.

It created a record of where different groups actually overlapped.

That was enough for one night.
