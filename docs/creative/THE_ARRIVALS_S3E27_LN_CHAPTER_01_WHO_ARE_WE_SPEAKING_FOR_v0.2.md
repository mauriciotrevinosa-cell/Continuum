The idea of a public statement came from a native merchant.

Not Mau.

The second community disliked it more.

"We are not part of your settlement."

"Correct."

"Then don't speak for us."

"Correct."

Good start.

A joint statement could only exist if each participant chose the words.

That made it slow.

Main settlement representatives.

Second-community representatives.

Two native partners willing to oppose collective punishment.

Arrival House.

Searchers.

Spoke too much.

Helpful.

The central question:

What could they say together without pretending agreement on everything?

Not:
we are one people.

Not:
trust all Otherworlders.

Not:
our settlement is innocent of everything.

They found narrower ground:

Collective punishment is wrong.

Disappearance without process is wrong.

A person's origin does not erase individual responsibility or individual rights.

People should be allowed to seek safety without being forced into one political identity.

That sounded boring.

Good.

Boring principles survive charisma.
