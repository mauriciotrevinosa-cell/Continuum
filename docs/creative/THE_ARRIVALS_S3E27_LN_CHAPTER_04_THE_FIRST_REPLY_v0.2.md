# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 15
## Chapter 33 — The First Reply

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 15  
**Reading order:** 33

The first reply arrived two days later.

Not support.

Warning.

A local authority declared the statement proof that Otherworlders were organizing politically.

Momo read the notice.

"So we said we're not one state and they heard we're one state."

Anko:

"Yes."

"Excellent literacy."

Fern:

"Don't."

The notice quoted three lines accurately.

One inaccurately.

Searchers pinned both copies side by side.

Original.

Reply.

Evidence.

Mau wanted an immediate rebuttal.

Anko stopped him.

"Read why."

The notice referenced:
concentration of armed Otherworlders;
shared communication;
coordinated routes;
joint statements.

Not invented facts.

Interpretation.

Dangerous precisely because it used reality.

Mau hated that.

The second reply came from the east village.

Short.

They would keep the road open to travelers carrying no active warrant for a named crime.

Not alliance.

Safe corridor.

Material consequence.

Everyone stared at the map.

A dangerous detour could become half a day shorter.

Small line on paper.

Real lives.

Third reply:

silence.

Also information.

Fourth came hidden inside a supply bundle.

One sentence.

> We heard you exist.

No signature.

Origin:
holding station.

Arrival House quieted.

Someone inside detention had received the message.

Not rescued.

Not free.

But now knew a destination existed.

That changed Searcher priorities immediately.

Could they verify sender?

Could they reply safely?

Would replying endanger whoever sent it?

No impulsive heroism.

Anko wrote questions.

Mau hated waiting.

Did anyway.

Fifth reply came from a native healer near the second community.

Not political.

Request for medical exchange.

Maomao read it twice.

"Useful."

That was her version of enthusiasm.

By evening, the message had produced:
hostility;
road access;
silence;
hope;
medical cooperation.

No unity outcome.

No grand shift.

Exactly the kind of contradictory response their world actually generated.

Mau stood before the board.

One statement.

Five reflections.

Rimuru joined him.

"You wanted the message to make things clearer?"

"Yes."

"Did it?"

"No."

"Good."

Mau looked at him.

Rimuru smiled.

"Means people are real."

Annoying.

Correct.

The paper had not united the world.

It had made the settlement legible.

To friends.

Enemies.

People in between.

And people trapped somewhere who needed to know where to point hope.
