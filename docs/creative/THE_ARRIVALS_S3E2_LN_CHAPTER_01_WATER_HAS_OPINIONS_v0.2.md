<!-- scene-id: s3e2-ln01-upstream -->

Maomao refused to discuss bedrooms until someone could answer where dirty water went.

Nobody could.

That ended the bedroom discussion.

They walked the river.

The lake fed one branch.

The waterfall fed another through rock.

The two met below the site.

Rimuru had assumed they could pull clean water from the higher branch and route waste far downstream.

Maomao asked:

"How far?"

Rimuru pointed.

"Far."

"Distance."

Rimuru stopped pointing.

Qifrey smiled.

"She does that."

"I've noticed."

<!-- scene-id: s3e2-ln01-magic -->

Coco proposed a circle that could redirect flow.

Qifrey asked how often it needed inspection.

Coco proposed another circle.

Agott asked what happened when mud covered part of it.

Coco frowned.

Qifrey did not rescue her.

That was teaching.

Frieren watched.

"Witch Hat magic makes infrastructure strange."

Qifrey looked at her.

"Your magic doesn't?"

"It makes other things strange."

Mau crouched near the bank.

"What if we filter before the water goes back?"

Maomao said, "Filter what?"

Mau looked at the river.

"All of it?"

"No."

"Right."

Specificity returned.

They listed:
- human waste;
- cooking waste;
- workshop runoff;
- medical contamination;
- ordinary wash water;
- storm water.

Different problems.

Different routes.

One pipe—or one spell—would not solve them.

<!-- scene-id: s3e2-ln01-visible -->

By evening the first water plan looked less elegant than the morning plan.

That was an improvement.

Qifrey wanted maintenance points physically accessible.

Maomao wanted dirty flow impossible to confuse with clean.

Rimuru wanted redundancy.

Agott wanted every public symbol legible enough that a trained maintainer could tell when someone had damaged it.

Coco wanted it not to look ugly.

Nobody dismissed that.

If people had to live around a system for years, ugly mattered too.

They tested the first assumption with buckets before drawing another line.

Clean water in one.

Wash water in another.

A handful of harmless crushed leaves in the second so everyone could see where it went.

Coco poured.

Agott watched the low channel.

The green-tinted water took the turn they expected—

then slowed in a depression nobody had marked.

Maomao pointed.

"There."

Rimuru crouched.

"What?"

"Standing water."

It was barely a puddle.

Enough for insects.

Enough for contamination if the wrong flow used it.

The map changed again.

No one complained this time.

The test had made the argument visible.

Mau looked at the sketched channels.

Some would remain visible.

"People will see the treatment system."

"Yes," Maomao said.

"Good."

She looked at him.

"Why?"

"So nobody forgets the water goes somewhere."

Maomao nodded once.

Approval from Maomao remained difficult enough to feel valuable.
