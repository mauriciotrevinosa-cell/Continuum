## Chapter 5 — The Wrong Road

<!-- scene-id: s3e2-ln02-easy -->

The easiest road was wrong.

They did not know that when they made it.

The route from the old inn to the site descended through a shallow basin, crossed packed ground, then reached the southern edge with almost no clearing required.

Perfect.

For three days.

Wagons used it.

Materials arrived faster.

Stark stopped complaining about one particular hill.

Momo declared the road a triumph of common sense.

Then it rained.

Not heavily.

Enough.

<!-- scene-id: s3e2-ln02-rain -->

The basin filled.

Not dramatically.

Worse.

Slowly.

Water spread across the packed track and stayed.

A wagon sank to the axle.

Himmel pushed.

Stark pushed.

Mikasa found firmer ground.

Rimuru stared at the water like it had betrayed him personally.

Mau stood beside him.

"Water has opinions."

Rimuru looked at him.

"Don't."

Mau smiled.

Qifrey arrived under a rain cover.

Maomao behind him.

Maomao looked once.

"We're moving the road."

Momo protested.

"We just made it."

"Yes."

"It took three days."

"Yes."

"That's terrible."

"Also yes."

<!-- scene-id: s3e2-ln02-cost -->

Nobody enjoyed undoing work.

That mattered.

The old route had taken effort.

People had cut brush.

Moved stone.

Packed soil.

Marked turns.

A better plan did not refund any of it.

Rimuru spent the afternoon measuring a higher contour route.

Mikasa checked whether the new approach created blind spots.

Qifrey asked whether a raised section could double as protected maintenance access for future water lines.

The answer was maybe.

Mau helped pull the sunk wagon free.

His boots disappeared into mud.

Frieren watched from dry ground.

"You're dirty."

"Thank you."

"You'll get it in the room."

"I'll take my boots off."

"You said that last time."

Mau remembered saying it.

Probably.

The thought passed so quickly he barely noticed.

They moved the road.

The mud stayed where the first plan had failed.

For weeks afterward, people called it Old Road.

It had been a road for three days.
