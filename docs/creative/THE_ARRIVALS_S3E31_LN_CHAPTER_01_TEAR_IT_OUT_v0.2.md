# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 7 — Tear It Out

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 7  
**Continuity state:** Inhabited settlement redesign begins | Existing work has emotional/material cost

The first road they tore out had been used for weeks.

Children knew it.

Carts knew it.

Momo had named one bend.

That made the first hammer strike feel personal.

Senku stood over the plan.

"Remove it."

Everyone hated him.

Reason:
runoff.

The road had been raised to solve mud.

That part worked.

It also redirected heavy rain toward two sleeping structures.

Under ordinary weather:
fine.

Under refuge load plus blocked drain plus storm:
bad.

Coco pointed at a proposed magical bypass.

"We can preserve the road."

Senku looked.

"Maintenance cost?"

Rimuru had number.

Too high.

Qifrey:

"Still possible."

Maomao:

"Should it be?"

There.

Different question.

The workaround would preserve what existed by making future residents maintain an avoidable vulnerability forever.

Mau looked at road.

People had already adapted.

One child had drawn a game grid near wall.

A merchant always cut same corner.

Bocchi used quieter route around it at night.

Infrastructure had become habit.

Tearing it out meant tearing habits.

Rimuru called residents nearest road.

Not only builders.

One older guest liked road because it was flatter.

A parent hated puddle near doorway.

Wakana cared about cart angle.

Aira cared about sightline.

Nobody agreed immediately.

They walked the road in rain simulation.

Buckets.

Temporary flow.

Water moved visibly wrong.

That changed argument.

The older guest sighed.

"Fine."

No one celebrated.

Removal began next morning.

Momo complained during every board she lifted.

"This bend had a name."

Senku:

"Name the new one."

"That's not the point."

"Correct."

He kept working.

Coco pried up one marker she had painted.

Angry.

Senku crouched near slope.

"Want explanation?"

"I know why."

"Different from liking it."

"Yes."

Good.

Agott asked:

"Can we reuse the boards?"

Senku looked.

"Most."

That helped.

Materials did not become punishment.

Some old road boards became edge reinforcement elsewhere.

One became temporary bench.

Momo sat on it dramatically.

"This is the corpse."

Fern told her to move.

A native builder corrected Senku's assumption about support spacing.

Local wood flexed differently when wet.

Senku changed design immediately.

No ego ritual.

Good data.

Coco noticed.

That mattered.

The redesign took three days.

People were inconvenienced.

Carts rerouted.

Someone complained at Arrival House because directions changed twice.

Good.

A real city caused transitional annoyance.

By sunset day three, the replacement path curved farther around water collection and created maintenance access no one had before.

Mikasa liked evacuation visibility.

Wakana liked cart approach.

Children immediately found a shortcut across unfinished dirt.

Senku stared.

Fern:

"People are people."

"People are data too."

"Don't say that to them."

Mau stood beside broken old boards.

"Feels terrible."

Coco:

"Yes."

Senku:

"Good."

Everyone turned.

He clarified.

"If ripping it out cost nothing emotionally or materially, the first thing hadn't become real enough to matter."

Still not comforting.

True.

Coco looked at new route.

Then old painted marker in hand.

She put marker at beginning of new path.

Not because road was same.

Because history could move without pretending mistakes never happened.

The settlement learned first redesign rule:

building wrong was not failure.

Refusing to change because people had already cared was.
