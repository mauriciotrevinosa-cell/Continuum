# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 20 — Ciel Doesn't Sleep

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 20  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary night | Mau shows Ciel the house, by request, under rule four

Mau was awake at three in the morning for no reason at all.

Not the bad kind of awake. No panic. No heat behind the eye. No Noise pressing at the edges of anything. Just awake, the ordinary way a person is awake when they slept early and their body has decided that was enough.

Frieren was asleep against the north wall, in the bed under the east window, with most of the blanket. Up in the loft, Rimuru was asleep too, in slime form, which meant he was asleep the way a dropped pudding is asleep: completely, and in a slightly different shape than he had started.

He lay on his back and looked at the ceiling.

"Ciel," he said, under his breath. "You up?"

I am always up. A pause. Master is not. Master sleeps like a stone. A soft one.

"Right. Sorry."

You do not need to apologize. I am not tired. I do not get tired. A pause. I am also not busy. The walls are quiet tonight.

"Quiet how?"

Humming. The pattern is working, but slowly. Like someone doing something with their hands while thinking about something else.

Mau listened to the dark for a while.

"Can I ask you something weird?"

You have never asked me anything that was not weird.

"What do you actually see? Day to day. When you're not looking at the walls."

Ciel was quiet for longer than usual.

Master, she said at last. I see what Master sees, and a great deal that Master does not notice he sees. And you. But not through your eyes. Rule four. I see your walls, and I hear you when you talk to me, and I hear what you say aloud when I am already listening. The rest of your life reaches me the way a conversation reaches someone in the next room.

"The next room."

Yes.

"You've never seen the house."

I have seen it through Master. Master does not look at houses. Master looks at load-bearing walls and at whether anyone is crying.

Mau smiled.

Then he sat up, carefully, so as not to steal back any blanket, and swung his legs over the edge of the bed.

"Do you want to?"

Want to what?

"See it. Through me. Just tonight. I'm asking. Rule four says I'm allowed to ask."

The silence went on so long that he thought he had offended her.

Then, very quietly:

Yes.



He walked her through it.

He did not know how to do it properly, so he simply did it the way he would have done it for anyone. He opened his door slowly. He looked at things for a long time. He said what they were.

"This is the corridor. That board dips. Everyone steps over it except Milim, who steps on it on purpose. That's Bocchi's door. She's asleep. She sleeps with the guitar case open, like it's going to need to breathe."

The lamp at the turn of the corridor is too high.

"That's what Momo said."

Momo is correct.

"This is the common room."

He stood in the doorway and let her see it. The couch Wakana had rebuilt. The long table, which was not the long table, and which everyone was still a little rude to because of it. The lamps, five of them now, one in every corner and one on the table, all turned low and all burning, so that even at three in the morning the shadows in that room were short.

The four scratches in the floor under their clear coat of varnish.

Ciel looked at those for a long time.

You kept them, she said.

"Yeah."

Why?

"So nobody forgets."

Forgets what?

Mau thought about it.

"That you're doing something hard," he said. "Every day. Every night. That it's not nothing, holding the walls. It's easy to forget, when it's quiet. When it's spoons." He nudged the edge of one scratch with his toe. "These are what you're holding back. I wanted them somewhere I'd walk past."

Ciel did not say anything at all.

He went out the front door.



The settlement at three in the morning was a different place. Quiet. Half-built. The unfinished wing a ribcage of timber against the stars. Lamps in a few windows. Somebody's laundry still on the line, because somebody always forgot.

He walked her to the edge of the washing court, and to the water point, and along the curve of the new road to the place where it bent around the rock.

"That's Momo's Rock."

That is a rock.

"It's Momo's Rock. Fern lost. You can't fight it."

I see no evidence of ownership.

"Nobody does. It's still Momo's."

I will note it, said Ciel, as Momo's Rock, with reservations.

He laughed, and the sound carried strangely in the empty road.

They sat on the rock. He did not think Momo would mind.

"Can I ask you something else?"

You may ask me anything you like tonight. I may not answer.

"Do you remember being Raphael?"

Yes.

"What's it like?"

The way you remember being very young, said Ciel. Everything is there. Every observation. Every calculation. Every day of Master's life since he woke in the cave. I can open any of it and look. Her voice changed very slightly. None of it was me. It was a very good instrument. I remember being an instrument. I do not remember being anyone.

"Do you miss it?"

Raphael did not miss things, said Ciel. Raphael had nothing to miss with.

"That's not an answer."

No. A pause. I do not miss it. Sometimes I miss how simple it was. That is different.

Mau nodded slowly.

"I get that."

I know you do. That is why I am telling you.

Above them, the stars turned. Somewhere behind them, in the settlement, a door opened and closed, and somebody's footsteps went toward the latrines and came back again.

"Can I ask one more?"

You have been asking one more for an hour.

"Are you jealous of me?"

The silence that followed had a distinctly offended quality.

I am concerned, said Ciel.

"About what?"

Master spends a great deal of attention on you. He gets tired. He does not notice when he is tired. I notice. That is concern.

"That's jealousy."

It is concern with a particular shape.

Mau grinned up at the stars.

"Okay."

It is not funny.

"It's a little funny."

It is a little funny, Ciel admitted, after a moment. Do not tell Master.

"I won't."

You will.

"I probably will."



They walked back as the sky over the trees began to gray.

At the edge of the wing, Mau stopped and looked at the dark shape of the half-built walls, and at the window of his own room, and the faint shape of Frieren asleep beyond the half-open shutter.

"What are you for?" he asked. "When the walls aren't needed anymore."

He had not planned to ask it.

Ciel did not answer for a long time.

I do not know, she said finally. I was made to analyze. Then I was made into something else, very quickly, by a disaster. Now I hold walls. If the walls stop needing holding, I do not know what I am for.

Mau leaned his shoulder against the corner post of the wing.

"Same," he said.

Ciel was quiet.

"I was made by something. I don't know what. I've got a whole system in me I didn't choose, doing things I don't understand. The witches asked me once if it was me. I said I didn't know." He looked at the window. "I still don't. But I don't think you have to know what you're for to be someone. I think you just have to be someone for long enough, and then you look around and you are."

Is that a scientific position? said Ciel.

"No. It's a Mau position."

They are not the same.

"They're not."

The first light touched the top of the east window.

Inside, Frieren stirred, and lifted her head off the pillow, and looked out at him through the gap with the absolute, unimpressed blankness of an elf woken before she intended.

She pushed the shutter wide.

"Who are you talking to?"

"Ciel."

Frieren considered this.

"Tell her the shelf is fine."

It is not, said Ciel at once.

"She says it's not."

Frieren pulled the shutter closed.

Mau stood outside it and laughed until he had to sit down on the step, and inside his head, very faintly, for the first time since he had known her, Ciel laughed too.
