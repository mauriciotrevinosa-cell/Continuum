# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 23 — Teach Someone Else

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 23

The maintenance rule became:

If only one person knows, it is not finished.

Qifrey hated how effective the sentence was.

Senku loved it.

Fern wrote it on wall.

Mau stared too long.

Nobody commented.

Every major system now needed:
primary maintainer;
secondary maintainer;
written or visual guide;
manual shutdown;
known failure signs;
one person outside original design team who could explain basics.

Coco taught two people water symbols.

First lesson went badly.

She moved too fast.

The learners copied shapes without understanding purpose.

Senku stopped.

"Why does this line exist?"

One learner guessed.

Wrong.

Coco realized she had taught drawing, not system.

Start again.

Function first.

Flow.

Isolation.

Warning.

Then geometry.

Better.

Agott taught inspection order.

She was annoyingly good at it.

Precise.

No extra words.

One learner complained she sounded judgmental.

Agott:

"I am."

Coco laughed.

Qifrey pretended not to.

Senku trained native builders in settlement measurement standards.

Native builders trained Senku in local material behavior.

One wood species warped opposite what he expected under repeated magical heating.

He wrote it down.

No embarrassment.

Knowledge belonged where it worked.

Maomao taught sanitation checks.

She intentionally gave trainees dirty mock room.

They missed one contaminated handle.

Maomao made them redo entire route.

Momo called cruel.

Maomao:

"Yes."

Wakana taught repair of protective cloth structures.

Not combat barriers.

Shade.

Privacy.

Weather.

Simple things that failed at worst times.

Rimuru documented capacity assumptions.

Normal load.

Refuge load.

Emergency load.

Unknown.

Mau was assigned nothing unique.

He noticed.

Meeting board had:
Qifrey.
Coco.
Agott.
Senku.
Maomao.
Wakana.
Rimuru.
native builders.

Mau:
documentation support.

No critical monopoly.

He stared.

The old sentence rose automatically.

Not necessary.

He let it arrive.

Then another thought:

Good.

A city should not become helpless if one resident disappears.

Mau helped write guides.

His job:
translate specialist explanation into something another person could use.

Surprisingly difficult.

Coco explained one circle.

Mau wrote.

Agott read.

"This sentence is wrong."

"It's simplified."

"It's wrong."

Mau sighed.

Rewrite.

Senku read another.

"Too vague."

Maomao:

"Too many words."

Mau:

"I hate literacy."

Frieren, passing:

"You read badly anyway."

Betrayal.

Weeks later, Qifrey left on short trip.

Water mark damaged.

No panic.

Secondary maintainer handled shutdown.

Called Agott.

Repair.

System returned.

Qifrey came back after.

Was told.

"Good."

No:
why didn't you wait for me?

That was success.

Mau watched.

The feeling of being less necessary still hurt sometimes.

But now he had physical proof:

redundancy was not rejection.

It was care made structural.

A city that loved its people should not require any one of them to be irreplaceable.
