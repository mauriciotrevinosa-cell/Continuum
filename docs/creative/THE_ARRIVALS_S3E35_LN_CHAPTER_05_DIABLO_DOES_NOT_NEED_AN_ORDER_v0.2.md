# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 26 — Diablo Does Not Need an Order

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 26  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary settlement life | Diablo begins serving the household by judgment, not only Rimuru's direct instruction

Diablo moved the lumber before anyone asked.

That was the problem.

Mau had been carrying one end.

Then he wasn't.

The beam rose out of his hands.

Mau looked up.

Diablo held the entire thing alone.

"Hey."

Diablo smiled.

"Yes?"

"I was using that."

"You were carrying it."

"Exactly."

"Slowly."

Mau stared.

Across the work area, Rimuru looked away.

Coward.

Mau pointed at the marked stack.

"Put it there."

Diablo did.

Perfectly.

Then turned back.

"Anything else?"

"No."

Diablo remained.

Mau narrowed his eyes.

"Why are you still here?"

"I am available."

"That is threatening when you say it."

Diablo seemed pleased.

The problem had started after the memory crisis.

At first Diablo's attention made sense.

Rimuru cared about Mau.

Therefore Diablo cared about the thing Rimuru cared about.

Simple.

Then the search happened.

Then the field camp.

Then Mau returned looking like a person who had survived being taken apart by his own mind.

Something shifted.

Diablo started doing things nobody had ordered.

Small things.

He took late watch when Frieren had not slept.

Not because Rimuru commanded it.

He carried supplies to Arrival House after Fern mentioned a shortage.

Not because Rimuru noticed.

He returned a lost child to Momo before anyone finished organizing a search.

Not because Mau was involved.

Anko found this suspicious.

Naturally.

She cornered him near the storage room.

"What are you doing?"

Diablo smiled.

"At present?"

"In general."

"A broad question."

"You know what I mean."

He did.

Rimuru was nearby, pretending to examine a route map.

Badly.

Diablo looked toward him.

Rimuru had changed in Continuum.

Not essence.

Expression.

The concern showed more.

Frustration.

Fear.

Attachment.

In his own world, Rimuru had carried enormous responsibility and still loved people openly.

Here, stripped of institutional scale and surrounded by people who did not automatically treat him as ruler, those emotions became harder to hide behind role.

Diablo had watched Rimuru sit awake while Mau was missing.

Watched him fail to solve something.

Watched him accept "I don't know."

That mattered.

Diablo answered:

"Lord Rimuru values these people."

Anko:

"That's the old answer."

Diablo's eyes sharpened.

Good.

She had seen it too.

He looked toward the common room.

Momo arguing with Okarun.

Bocchi carrying something with Nijika.

Seiko asleep? No. Nazuna asleep.

Seiko yelling at someone about tea.

Suika running after Coco.

Mau trying to carry a second beam because apparently learning was nonlinear.

Diablo's smile changed.

"They have become valuable independently."

Anko waited.

"To you?"

A pause.

Then:

"Yes."

There.

No oath.

No conversion.

Anko nodded.

"Good."

Diablo:

"You expected resistance?"

"I expected a speech about Rimuru."

"Lord Rimuru remains central."

"Obviously."

Rimuru finally turned around.

"Can both of you stop discussing me like I'm not here?"

Anko:

"No."

Diablo:

"Of course, Rimuru-sama."

Rimuru groaned.

Later, Mau attempted to carry the second beam.

Diablo took the other end.

Not the whole thing.

Mau looked suspicious.

"What?"

"I am assisting."

"Normal amount?"

"An imprecise unit."

"Half."

Diablo considered.

Then adjusted his grip so Mau still carried weight.

Progress.

They walked.

Mau asked:

"Did Rimuru tell you to help me?"

"No."

Mau nearly stopped.

Diablo continued walking.

"Should he have?"

"No."

"Then why are you surprised?"

Mau had no clean answer.

Because being helped without necessity still felt like debt.

Because Diablo had once been a terrifying stranger who arrived looking for someone else.

Because Mau knew exactly how ridiculous he looked next to beings who could move the beam alone.

Diablo read too much from his face.

Annoying.

"You misunderstand."

"Probably."

"I do not think you are useful because you can carry lumber."

Mau:

"That somehow sounds worse."

"It is not."

They set the beam down.

Diablo looked toward the settlement.

People crossing.

Calling names.

Asking questions.

Ignoring Mau entirely.

Then someone saw him.

"Mau! Do you know where Fern put the—"

Fern, from another doorway:

"Ask me."

The person turned.

Solved.

Diablo smiled.

"This exists in part because you kept making room for people."

Mau's shoulders tightened.

Diablo added:

"That does not mean it belongs to you."

Mau looked at him.

Surprisingly precise.

Diablo:

"It means destroying you would wound many people."

There it was.

More Diablo.

Mau:

"Please never phrase affection like a threat assessment again."

"No."

Of course not.

Then the overprotection got worse.

Mau climbed onto a low scaffold.

Diablo moved underneath it.

Mau looked down.

"What are you doing?"

"Nothing."

"You're spotting me."

"I am standing."

"Directly under me."

"Coincidence."

Rimuru walked past.

Saw.

Stopped.

"Diablo."

"Rimuru-sama."

"Let him climb."

"I am."

"Without looking like you'll murder gravity."

Diablo looked genuinely confused.

"Gravity has not yet offended me."

Mau laughed so hard he had to sit on the scaffold.

Diablo's head snapped up.

"Are you injured?"

"No."

"Headache?"

"No."

"Noise?"

"No."

"Then—"

"I'm laughing."

Diablo paused.

Rimuru smiled.

Mau saw it.

So did Diablo.

Something softened.

Not much.

Enough.

Diablo had started as Rimuru's searcher.

Then Mau's rescuer.

Now he was becoming the kind of person who moved lumber, took watches and terrified gravity because he cared about the people living here.

He still belonged to Rimuru in the way Diablo chose.

That relationship did not have to shrink for the circle around it to grow.
