# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 28 — Senku Gets Faster

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 28

Senku's test times improved.

Slowly.

Not magically.

He knew because notebook was merciless.

Week one:
clear drop under mixed local-system load.

Week two:
less.

Week three:
working memory recovered most.

Reaction to magic-plus-engineering problems still lagged source expectation.

Senku updated graph.

Mau looked over.

"Happy?"

"No."

"You're impossible."

"Accuracy is not happiness."

"Those can coexist."

"Not relevant."

Mau pointed.

"Line goes up."

"Performance line goes toward baseline."

"That is scientist happy."

Senku ignored.

The reduced capacity remained.

So did adaptation.

More important:
habits built around limitation remained too.

Written process.

Peer checking.

Delegation.

Local verification.

Senku sometimes hated those after feeling faster.

One afternoon he solved structural problem mentally.

Reached answer before others.

Started giving instruction.

Then stopped.

"Wait."

Everyone stared.

He wrote.

Checked.

Asked native builder.

One assumption wrong.

Not final answer, but enough to change.

Senku exhaled.

Coco:

"You caught it."

"Process caught it."

Difference.

Later Mau asked:

"Would old you have checked?"

Senku looked at him.

"Old me isn't here."

Mau nodded.

Fair.

Then:

"Do you hate it?"

Senku understood question.

Not procedures.

Limitation.

"Yes."

Immediate.

Mau stopped.

Good.

No silver lining forced.

Senku continued.

"I hate being slower."

"I hate losing capacity."

"I hate that Continuum can alter what I take for granted about my own mind."

Mau knew that one.

Senku looked at notebook.

"Adaptation producing useful habits does not make the limitation good."

Mau:

"Yeah."

"Don't romanticize damage."

"Yeah."

Pause.

Senku tapped graph.

"But if damage exists, wasting what you learn from adapting is also stupid."

Mau smiled.

"There."

"What?"

"Scientist therapy."

"Get out."

Mau did not.

They tested next run.

Suika timed him.

Not Senku timing himself.

That mattered because Senku had developed a suspicious habit of reclassifying pauses as "setup."

Suika did not allow this.

"Start."

Senku worked.

Stopped once.

Checked the written assumption.

Asked for local measurement.

Finished.

Suika looked at the time.

Senku looked at her face.

"How bad?"

She smiled.

"Better."

He took the notebook.

Actually better.

Not source peak.

Not miraculous.

Measured.

He wrote the result himself.

Then added one note:

PROCESS RETAINED EVEN AS THROUGHPUT RECOVERS.

Mau read over his shoulder.

"That sounds like growth."

Senku:

"It sounds like data."

"Same thing sometimes."

"No."

Better.

Still not source peak.

No cure arc.

No tragedy arc.

A person learning new constraints.

Settlement benefited from the systems Senku built around uncertainty.

Senku would still prefer not to need them.

Both truths stayed.

Before she left, Suika stopped at the survey map pinned above Senku's bench.

On its edge, circled twice, was a number nobody had asked about.

"What's this one?"

"Stride length."

Suika looked at the chain of five round ponds past the last fields. Then at the number. Then at the ponds again.

"Of what?"

Senku did not look up from the notebook.

"Exactly," he said.
