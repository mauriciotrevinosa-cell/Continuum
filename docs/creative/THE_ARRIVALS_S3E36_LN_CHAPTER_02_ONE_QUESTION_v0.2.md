# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 30 — One Question

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 30  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary evenings | Kita and Kessoku | Mau checks an old memory against someone else's

Kita found Mau on the bench by the water court, which was where people found Mau now when they wanted him without an audience.

She sat down beside him.

She did not say anything.

That was how Mau knew it was serious. Kita always said something.

"I need help," she said at last. "Again."

Mau looked at her.

"Again?"

"Like last time."

Something in him went careful.

It did that now.

It had done it ever since the weeks when his own head had handed him things that were not true and let him believe them. A road remembered wrong. A conversation that had not happened. He had learned, slowly, to stop trusting a memory just because it felt solid.

"Tell me what you remember about last time," he said. "I want to check mine against yours."

Kita did not look hurt.

She looked like someone who had been expecting the question and had decided, in advance, not to make it heavy.

"You were at the well," she said. "Cleaning mud off your shoes. I stood there forever before you looked up. Then I sat on the stone edge and asked how you ask Bocchi things without making her panic."

"What did I say?"

"That you make her panic."

Mau closed his eyes.

There.

The cold stone.

Water in the bucket going brown.

Kita laughing at an answer that had not been a joke.

"I had it as the back step," he said. "Behind the kitchen. Not the well."

"The well."

"But then I said something else."

"One question," Kita said. "Then stop."

Mau opened his eyes.

That part matched.

That part matched exactly.

He let out a breath he had not known he was holding.

"Okay," he said. "That one's real."

He had not asked Ciel.

She could not have told him. Which memories were true had never been what she was for. Only Kita could do this part.

Kita smiled at him.

It was a soft smile, for Kita. No performance in it.

"You helped me ask the most important question of my life," she said, "and you don't trust that you did it."

"I trust it now."

"Good." She reached into her jacket. "Because I've done it again."



It was a song.

Or it was trying to be one.

Six verses, front and back, in Kita's round handwriting, with arrows and crossings-out and one small drawing of a guitar in the margin that Mau suspected had not been intentional.

"Bocchi writes all our lyrics," Kita said. "She always has. I sing them. I love singing them. But I've never—" She stopped. "I've never given her any words back. Mine. Ones she didn't write. And I wanted to."

Mau read the first verse.

Then the second.

He read all six.

Kita watched his face the entire time, like someone watching a doctor read an X-ray.

"Well?"

"It's six verses," said Mau.

Kita's face fell.

"Is that bad?"

"It's six verses," Mau said again, gently. "What's the one thing?"

Kita stared at him.

"...You're doing it again."

"What's the one thing you want her to know?"

Kita looked down at the paper.

At all six verses.

At the arrows and the guitar.

She was quiet for a long time, and Mau let her be quiet, because that had been the hardest part last time too.

Then she put her finger on one line, halfway down the fourth verse, nearly lost among the others.

"That one," she said.

Mau read it.

He did not say anything.

He did not need to.

Kita read it again herself, and her eyes went wet, and she laughed at herself.

"Oh no," she said. "It's that one, isn't it."

"It's that one."

"Everything else is just me being nervous."

"Everything else is just you being nervous," Mau agreed.

Kita folded the paper so that only that one line showed.

"I can't write music," she said. "I can sing anything. I can't write a single bar."



Nijika was easy.

Nijika said yes before Kita had finished the sentence, and then asked what the song was about, and then said, "No, don't tell me, I'll find out," and then asked again two minutes later.

Ryo was harder.

Ryo listened to the whole explanation lying on the floor of the music room with her eyes shut.

Then she said:

"What do I get?"

"Ryo!" said Nijika.

"Composing is labor."

Ryo opened one eye and looked at Mau, who had come because Kita had made him.

"You could lend me money."

"Ryo."

"A small amount."

"Ryo."

Mau, who had very little money and knew exactly how much of it he would ever see again, started to reach into his pocket anyway.

Ryo watched him do it.

She looked at Mau's hand. At Kita's folded paper. At Nijika's face.

Then she closed the eye again.

"Fine," she said. "Free. For Bocchi."

Nijika stared at her.

"Did you just do something for free?"

"Don't tell anyone."

"I'm telling everyone."

"Then I'll deny it."



They worked on it for three evenings in the music room with the door shut.

Mau sat in the corner.

He had been told he was required, and he was not sure why, because he did not write a single word or a single note.

He just listened.

When Kita added a second line, he looked at her, and she took it back out.

When Ryo built a bridge that went somewhere clever and away from the point, he tilted his head, and Ryo swore at him and rebuilt it plainer.

When Nijika asked whether the song needed a chorus, he said, "Does it?" and they all looked at each other and agreed that it did not.

On the third evening, Nijika sat back from her drum pad and watched him.

She said, quietly, to Ryo:

"Oh. That's why."

"Why what?"

"Why Bocchi trusts him." Nijika nodded at the corner. "He didn't write any of it."

Ryo looked at Mau too, for a long moment.

"He wrote nothing," she agreed. "He just kept taking things out."

"Is that bad?"

Ryo considered it, with the seriousness she gave to almost nothing that was not music.

"No," she said. "That's arranging."



Bocchi, meanwhile, was falling apart.

Kita had disappeared three evenings in a row.

Kita had taken Nijika and Ryo with her.

Kita had taken Mau.

The music room door was shut, and the music room was half Bocchi's, and nobody had told her why it was shut.

By the third evening, Bocchi had concluded that she had done something terrible, that the band was being re-formed without her, that Kita had finally noticed she was strange, and that she would be living alone in the woods by the end of the week.

She did not tell anyone this.

She sat on the courtyard wall instead, curled into as small a shape as possible.

Maki found her there.

Maki had not been looking for her.

She had been walking back from the training ground, and she saw a pink tracksuit folded into the shape of a person on a wall, and stopped, because she was Maki, and some things you did not walk past.

"What happened?"

Bocchi made a sound.

Maki sat down on the wall beside her.

Maki and Bocchi did not, generally, talk.

Not because of anything.

They just lived at different volumes.

"Is it Kita?" said Maki.

Bocchi made a smaller sound.

"Did she say something?"

"She—" Bocchi managed. "Didn't. Say. Anything. She's in the music room with everyone and the door is shut and she took Mau and—"

"Did you ask her?"

Bocchi looked at Maki in pure horror.

"No!"

"Then you don't know." Maki leaned back on her hands. "Listen. I don't know anything about music. But I know Ryo. Nobody breaks up with someone by borrowing Ryo. Ryo costs money."

Bocchi stared at her.

Then, very slowly, the horror on her face turned into something more confused.

"...That's true," she said.

"It is true," said Maki. "Go practice something. Whatever it is, you'll find out."

Bocchi went.

Maki stayed on the wall a while longer.

Yuta found her there later and asked what she was smiling at.

"Nothing," said Maki. "I think I just helped."

"You think?"

"It's new," said Maki. "Give me time."



They asked her on the fourth evening.

Nijika went to get her.

"Bocchi-chan. Come to the music room. It's important."

Bocchi came with the expression of a woman walking to her own sentencing.

She opened the door.

Kita was standing in the middle of the room.

Ryo had her bass.

Nijika slid behind her drum pad.

Mau was in the corner, and Frieren was beside him now too, because Mau had asked her, and she had come.

Nobody else.

Bocchi stood in the doorway.

"What—"

"Sit down," said Kita. "Please."

Bocchi sat down on the floor, exactly where she was.

Kita took a breath.

She had six sentences ready.

She could feel all of them.

She did not say any of them.

Nijika counted in.

It was a short song.

Ryo's line under it was plain and steady.

Nijika barely touched the drums.

And Kita sang it, and the song was made almost entirely of one line, sung three different ways, softer each time:

I always know where your hand is.

That was it.

That was all of it.

When it was over, the music room was very quiet.

Bocchi did not move.

She had stopped breathing at some point during the second time through, and she had not, as far as anyone could tell, started again.

Kita stood in the middle of the floor and waited.

She kept not talking.

It was, she would say afterward, the second-longest silence of her life.

Then Bocchi got up.

She did not say anything.

She walked past Kita, picked up her own guitar from its stand, sat down on the amp, and played the line back.

Not Kita's tune.

Her own.

The same words, without the words, the way Bocchi said things she could not say.

Then she looked up.

Bright red.

Eyes wet.

And held out her hand, without looking, in exactly the right direction.

Kita took it.



Afterward, when Nijika had cried and denied it, and Ryo had pretended to be asleep, and Bocchi and Kita had gone out into the courtyard and not come back for some time, Mau sat on the back step of the music room with Frieren.

"You didn't write anything," Frieren said.

"No."

"You didn't play anything."

"No."

"Then what did you do?"

Mau thought about it.

"I said one question," he said. "Then I stopped."

Frieren looked at him for a while.

"You did that the first time too?"

"I think so," said Mau. "Kita says so. We checked."

Frieren nodded slowly.

Then she leaned her head on his shoulder.

That night, in his notes, under the date, Mau wrote:

Kita. The well, not the back step. One question, then stop.

And then, after a moment:

Checked. Real.

And underneath that, smaller, because it was the part he wanted to keep:

She knows where Bocchi's hand is.
