# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 34 — Let Me Out

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 34  
**Continuity state:** Seal breach, about nineteen minutes | Shrine and Void clash in the plaza | Wall reinstated | Blue collapses, Red bursts | A voice under the wall: 'let me out'

The refuge took eleven minutes to fill.

Mikasa counted. She always counted. Every name on Fern's list, every visitor, every child, every merchant, every one of the people who lived in the settlement and had never once been inside the house. Eleven minutes from the first stroke of the bell to the last person through the refuge door, and when she reached the end of the list she counted it again, out loud, because nobody in the room was breathing properly and somebody had to make a sound.

"All here," said Mikasa.

Rimuru looked at Yuta. Yuta looked at Milim.

Milim let go of Frieren's wrist for the first time in eleven minutes. There was a red mark on it in the exact shape of her hand.

"Now," said Milim.

Frieren was already at the door.



They went back four.

Rimuru first, because Ciel was talking to him and he needed to hear her. Then Yuta, with his sword out and his face very calm. Then Milim, who had stopped pretending this was anything but terrifying. And Frieren, who had kept her promise to the letter, eleven minutes and not one second more, and was not going to be stopped now by anyone in any world.

Ciel talked the whole way.

Rimuru repeated it aloud as they ran, in short pieces, for the others.

"Three patterns. All at once. They found the walls together. That's never happened. The wheel came up through the shadow medium. That's the Ten Shadows line, the same one as the dog. Blue and Red came out of the Gojo set. And two domains. Two. One from the Sukuna tether and one from the Void." He was breathing hard, which slimes did not need to do. "The wall's breached in three places. She's rebuilding it around them while he holds them apart. He's holding all of it. All of it at once."

"How long?" said Frieren.

Rimuru listened.

"Fourteen minutes so far."

Frieren made a sound that was not a word.



They came out of the north route into the plaza and stopped.

The plaza was gone.

Not destroyed. Gone, in the way a room is gone when you walk into it and it is somewhere else. The benches, the play boards, the tree, the shade cloth, the stones Wakana had laid, all of it was still there in some sense. But over all of it hung a vast invisible thing that cut. The air itself was full of lines. They appeared and vanished faster than the eye could follow, crossing and recrossing in a dome that filled the whole plaza from edge to edge, and wherever they crossed, the world came apart.

The tree had been cut into a hundred pieces that had not yet fallen.

Do not go closer, said Ciel, to Rimuru. Her voice was very calm, the way it was only when she was most afraid. If you go closer, you will die. All of you. Even Milim. Even you, Master.

Rimuru put his arm out, and all three of them stopped.

In the middle of the cutting dome, where the water point had been, there was a sphere.

Black. Perfectly black. Not the black of a shadow but the black of a place where light had decided not to bother. It was about the height of a house, and it sat in the heart of the slashing air as if the slashes could not see it, and the slashes broke against its skin, and the skin did not break.

And above it, turning, a ring of light.

Red and blue, spinning around each other so fast that they had stopped being two colors. It looked like two stars, two quasars, wheeling around a center that was not there, close enough now that at the edges of the ring the light had begun to bleed into a third color. Violet. Deep, terrible violet, flickering in and out at the points where they nearly touched.

They could not tell whether the two had already joined.

From down here it was all purple.

"He's inside that," said Yuta. It was not a question.

"He's inside his own domain," said Rimuru. "Ciel says it's the only place he can hold the wheel. Inside the Void." He swallowed. "She says the wheel is trying to adapt to it."

"Adapt to what?"

Rimuru listened for a long time.

"To not existing," he said.



Inside, there was no plaza.

There was everything.

That was what the Void was. Not darkness. Everything. Every fact, every sensation, every piece of information there had ever been, arriving all at once and forever and with no end. It was the thing Gojo had built to drown his enemies in, the thing Yuta had rebuilt once in Shinjuku and that the seal had read out of him. Mau had never been inside it from this side. He had only ever been the one drowning.

Now he was the one it belonged to, and it still nearly drowned him.

He held it anyway.

He held it around the wheel.

The wheel was not finished. That was the only reason he was still alive. It was the wheel and a shape beneath it, a great pale body only half drawn, legs and arms and something like a face. It was a shikigami that had never been summoned, built out of a pattern that had read a description of it and nothing else, and it did not know how to be all the way here. But it knew how to adapt. That was what it was. That was all it was.

It had been dragged into a place made of infinite information. So it was trying to adapt to infinite information.

It was trying to become the kind of thing that could survive the Void.

Every few seconds, the wheel strained toward another eighth of a turn, and every time it did, the rules around it shifted. The Void flickered. The weight of everything-at-once lightened, just for an instant, as if the thing at the center had begun to learn how to not be there for it.

Mau held the turn.

He held it with his hands. He held it with his body. He held it with every piece of whatever he was that he could reach, and he did not think about the wheel, because thinking about the wheel was how the wheel won.

He thought about two lights.

Two lights circling each other over a plaza. Red and blue. Around and around. Never touching. Like two people dancing who must never be allowed to hold hands. He thought about them so hard, and so precisely, that they never stopped spinning, even while he was drowning in everything there was.

Eighteen minutes, said Ciel. Very faint, from very far away.

He held.

Eighteen and a half.

The edges of him were starting to go. He could feel it, the same way he had felt it in the clearing: fingertips softening, losing their argument with being fingers. The left eye open and burning. Pressure along his back.

Nineteen, said Ciel. Mau. Mau. Hold. Almost.

"I can't," he said, or thought, into everything.

You can. Ten seconds. Hold.

He held.

*Wall reinstated*, said Ciel.



Outside, in the plaza, the cutting stopped.

It did not fade. It stopped, all at once, like a held breath let go. The lines vanished from the air, and the tree, which had been waiting to fall, finally fell, a hundred pieces at once, softly, onto stones that were themselves crossed with fine straight cuts.

Then the black sphere opened.

It opened like an eye: a seam down the middle, then a widening, the black peeling back from the inside, and the light of an ordinary noon coming in.

They saw him.

He was standing where the water point had been. His feet were planted in a hole in the world that was his shadow. His arms were above his head, and between his hands, enormous and pale and straining, was the wheel. It was still trying to turn. He was still holding it.

His eyes were closed.

His lips were moving.

Frieren would say later that she knew exactly what he was doing. That he was not fighting the wheel at all. That he was imagining something, hard, the way he had imagined a room once, and a stripe of light across a floor, and a chair that wobbled.

Above him, the ring of red and blue was slowing down.

They could see it separate again as it slowed. Red and blue, two lights, not one. Circling. Circling more slowly. Coming apart.

The wheel sank.

Slowly, and then all at once, like something heavy settling into deep water, it went down into his shadow and was gone, and the shadow drew back in around his feet until it was only a shadow, small and gray at noon, the way a shadow should be.

The blue light stopped.

It hung there for a moment over his head, very small and very deep, and then it folded. It turned in on itself, pulled at its own edges, and drew itself into a smaller and smaller point until there was no point left. It ate itself, quietly, and was gone.

The red light burst.

Not an explosion. Nothing so violent. It came apart into a thousand sparks that went up and out and over the plaza in a great slow bloom of red and gold, like a firework at a festival. In the refuge, through the high windows, children who did not understand what they were seeing pointed at it and said *pretty*.

Mau lowered his arms.



Before he fell, two people heard it.

Not Rimuru. Not Yuta. Not Milim. Not Frieren, who was already running across the cut stones toward him.

Only Mau, and Ciel.

A voice. Not Ciel's. Not any voice he knew. It came from very deep down, from somewhere under the place where the wall had just been put back. It was broken, and dry, and strange, as if it had never been used, or had been used once a very long time ago and then left in the dark.

*Let me out.*

Then nothing.

Mau's legs went.

Frieren caught him before he touched the stones, the way she had caught him in the clearing in a ring of flowers. She went down with him onto the broken plaza and held his head against her chest. Rimuru was there, and Yuta, and Milim, and the sparks were still falling, red and gold, all around them.

Inside, very quietly, to Rimuru, Ciel said:

Master. Did you hear that?

Hear what?

A long pause.

A voice, said Ciel. Under the wall. Mau heard it too. I do not know whose it was.

She did not know.

She went on not knowing for a long time.
