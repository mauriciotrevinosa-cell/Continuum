# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 36 — Okarun Comes Looking for Mau

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 36  
**Continuity state:** Post-infrastructure push | Okarun/Mau friendship exists independently of Momo

Okarun came looking for Mau.

Not Momo.

That was how Mau knew something had changed.

He was near the rough commons with a stack of route notes when Okarun appeared at the edge of the path.

"Mau."

Mau looked up.

"Everything okay?"

Okarun immediately looked nervous.

Bad opening.

Mau lowered the papers.

"Sorry. Normal question?"

"Yeah."

"Okay."

Okarun stood there.

Mau waited.

This was usually where Momo would arrive and translate an emotion nobody had asked her to translate.

She did not.

Good.

Okarun held up a small metal piece.

"I wanted to show you something."

Mau took it.

It looked like trash.

Important rule:
do not say that.

"What am I looking at?"

Okarun brightened.

Supernatural pattern.

Not exactly yokai.

Not exactly local magic.

A residue on a route marker that had moved overnight.

Probably harmless.

Maybe interesting.

Definitely Okarun.

He explained too fast.

Mau understood forty percent.

Asked questions anyway.

They walked to the marker.

No Momo.

No investigation team.

Just two guys looking at something weird because both liked weird things.

At the marker, Mau crouched.

"Could be the maintenance spell."

Okarun:

"I thought that too, but look."

He pointed.

The residue crossed the edge where Qifrey's pattern stopped.

Mau leaned closer.

"Okay."

"Right?"

"Still could be rain."

Okarun looked offended.

"Rain?"

"Nature's greatest conspiracy."

Okarun laughed.

Small.

Then they checked.

Water path.
Spell residue.
Scrape marks.

Suika had probably moved the marker while measuring route height and forgotten to mention it.

They found her note ten minutes later.

Okarun stared at the paper.

Mau:

"Rain defeated."

"I hate this."

"Welcome to investigation."

Okarun sat on the edge of the path.

Mau sat too.

They should have gone back.

Didn't.

Okarun asked:

"Do you ever miss a world you can't remember?"

Mau looked at him.

There.

Real question.

"I don't know."

"That's not useful."

"No."

Okarun picked at the edge of the metal piece.

"I remember mine."

"Some."

"Yeah."

Momo.

Source life.

Things still broken around memory.

Mau did not ask for details.

Okarun continued:

"Sometimes I feel bad because I have things here I like."

Mau waited.

"Like that's proof I stopped caring about what I lost."

Mau looked toward the settlement.

People moving.

Unfinished roofs.

Someone yelling.

Probably Karane.

"No."

Okarun looked at him.

Mau realized he had answered too quickly.

He tried again.

"I don't think loving a new thing cancels an old one."

Okarun stared.

"You're saying that like you rehearsed it."

Mau laughed.

"Apparently my entire life is becoming that sentence."

Old inn and new home.

Source world and Continuum.

People from before and people from now.

Frieren and Himmel without rivalry turning history into betrayal.

Momo and Okarun.

Everything.

Okarun looked down.

"I think Momo considers all of you family."

Mau smiled.

"She'll kill you if you say that."

"I know."

"Then yes."

Okarun smiled too.

"And I guess..."

He stopped.

Mau did not help.

Good.

Okarun tried again.

"If she's family with you, that made it easier at first."

Mau nodded.

"But now?"

Mau looked at him.

Okarun looked deeply offended that Mau had asked.

"Now I came looking for you."

There.

Mau's face changed.

Okarun immediately panicked.

"Not like—"

"I know."

"Don't make it weird."

"I wasn't."

"You were."

"I was going to say thanks."

"That's weird."

Mau laughed.

"Fine."

They sat.

Direct friendship had arrived without declaration.

Momo had been the bridge.

She did not remain the requirement.

Later, Okarun helped Mau carry the route notes back.

Halfway there Momo found them.

She looked between them.

"What are you doing?"

Okarun:

"Nothing."

Mau:

"Rain conspiracy."

Momo stared.

"I leave you alone for one hour."

"Exactly," Mau said.

She took one of the papers.

Walked with them anyway.

Not because Okarun needed supervision.

Because she belonged in both directions.

At dinner, Okarun sat near Mau for part of the meal.

Then Momo called him over.

He moved.

No emotional symbolism.

Good.

A relationship could be real without requiring permanent seating.
