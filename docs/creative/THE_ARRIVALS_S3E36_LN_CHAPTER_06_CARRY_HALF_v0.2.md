# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 37 — Carry Half

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 37  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary work day | Yuta/Mau older-brother bond reappears outside medical crisis

Yuta took half the box.

Mau objected.

"I can carry it."

"I know."

"Then why?"

"Because there are two of us."

Mau stared.

Yuta kept walking.

Annoying.

The box held nothing dramatic.

Fasteners.

Replacement fittings.

Two rolls of material Wakana had requested.

Heavy enough to be inconvenient.

Not heavy enough to justify anyone with supernatural strength.

Mau had chosen it for exactly that reason.

Normal work.

Then Yuta took half.

Not all.

That detail mattered.

After the memory crisis, too many people had moved from:

let Mau do everything

to:

Mau must do nothing.

Both felt bad.

Yuta, apparently, understood.

They carried together.

Mau looked sideways.

"Did Frieren tell you to do this?"

"No."

"Fern?"

"No."

"Maomao?"

"No."

"Maki?"

Yuta smiled.

"She would've taken the whole box and called you slow."

Fair.

They crossed the rough commons.

People moved around them.

Nobody asked Yuta to heal anyone.

Nobody asked Mau to decide anything.

For almost a minute they were just carrying a box.

Mau said:

"I miss this."

Yuta looked.

"Carrying things?"

"Being useless in a specific way."

Yuta laughed.

"I know what you mean."

Of course he did.

From the beginning, Yuta had been one of the people who could stand near Mau without needing Mau to perform.

Older-brother energy.

Not because Yuta always knew better.

Because he knew when to let Mau be younger.

Mau hated that thought enough not to say it.

Yuta probably knew anyway.

They reached the workshop.

Wakana took the materials.

"Thanks."

Done.

No plot.

On the way back, Yuta changed direction.

Mau noticed.

"Where are we going?"

"Food."

"I ate."

"When?"

Mau thought.

Yuta:

"Food."

Older brother.

Terrible.

They found Maki already there.

She looked at Mau.

Then Yuta.

"You kidnapped him?"

Yuta:

"Fed him."

Maki:

"Same."

Mau sat.

Maki put food in front of him.

"This is coordinated."

"Yes," Yuta said.

"At least admit it."

Mau ate.

Halfway through, Yuta asked:

"How's the representative thing?"

Mau slowed.

There.

Not crisis.

Still important.

"I hate that people call it that."

"Do you hate doing it?"

"Some of it."

Maki:

"He likes translating."

Mau looked at her.

"Frieren tells you too much."

"Frieren barely tells me anything."

Worse.

Maki had eyes.

Yuta smiled.

Mau sighed.

"I like when two groups are arguing because they mean different things with the same word and I can make them realize it."

"That's a skill," Yuta said.

"I don't want it to turn into authority."

"Then don't pretend the skill isn't real."

Mau looked at him.

Same lesson again.

Relational gravity.

Influence.

Chosen trust.

Not sin.

Yuta continued:

"You're allowed to be important."

Mau immediately disliked the sentence.

Maki saw.

"Look at his face."

"Shut up."

Yuta:

"That's the problem."

Mau put down his food.

"What?"

"You hear 'important' and translate it to 'responsible for everyone's outcome.'"

Mau's mouth opened.

Closed.

Yuta had watched him die once.

Literally.

He had watched Mau make himself the answer to impossible arithmetic.

Of course he knew the shape.

Yuta leaned back.

"If Bocchi thinks you're her brother, that doesn't make you responsible for every bad day she ever has."

Mau stared.

"If Umaru wants you around, you don't owe her a perfect life."

Mau looked away.

"If I call you family, you don't owe me repayment."

There.

Mau's throat tightened.

Maki spoke softer.

"People choosing you creates relationship. Not debt."

Mau looked at both of them.

Yuta and Maki had an entire completed life behind them.

Children.
Descendants.
Loss.
A world Mau had never seen.

And still they had made space for him here.

Exactly the thing Mau struggled to believe.

Yuta said:

"Carry half."

Mau frowned.

"What?"

"The box."

"That was not metaphorical when you did it."

"It can be now."

"That is cheating."

Maki:

"Still works."

Mau hated them.

Affectionately.

Later, the three walked back together.

Maki left first for another task.

Yuta and Mau reached the family courtyard.

Before separating, Mau said:

"Hey."

Yuta stopped.

Mau had no elegant sentence.

"You're still my brother."

Yuta's expression softened.

"Yeah."

"I know the world is bigger now."

"Yeah."

"Just..."

He did not know how to finish.

Yuta did.

"It didn't replace anything."

Mau exhaled.

"Yeah."

Then Yuta handed him a smaller box somebody had left by the door.

Mau looked at it.

"Seriously?"

"Carry your half."

Yuta walked away.

Mau laughed.

The family had grown.

The oldest bonds did not have to shrink to make room.
