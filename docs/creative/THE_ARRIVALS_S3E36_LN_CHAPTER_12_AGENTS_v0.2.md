# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 16
## Chapter 43 — Agents

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 16  
**Reading order:** 43  
**Continuity state:** Storm, day 3 | Agent coats | The Legendary Artifact (a kettle) | Frieren secretly doubles the dose | The bird has eaten; the bird is singing

The agents were issued their uniforms at dawn.

Wakana had made them overnight with Rimuru's help, the two of them in the workshop by lamplight with a bolt of black cloth that had been meant for winter coats. Nobody had told Wakana what they were for. He had been told *covert*, and *urgent*, and *Frieren*, and that had been enough.

They were long black coats with high collars. Each had a small square of white paper pinned to the lapel, on which Coco had written AGENT in her best hand, and then, underneath, smaller, a codename.

Umaru was AGENT HAMSTER. Milim was AGENT DRAGON, which she had chosen herself and would not discuss. Coco was AGENT INK. Bocchi was AGENT PINK, which she had not chosen and had not been able to object to in time. Suika was AGENT WATERMELON, and wore her coat over her helmet, which made her look like a very small, very determined lamp.

Rimuru had a coat too. It was slime-sized. Wakana had made it as a joke, and Rimuru put it on without a word, and Wakana had to leave the workshop for a while.

They stood in a row in the common room and looked at each other.

"We look," said Umaru, with enormous satisfaction, "incredible."

They looked like a children's theater production of a spy novel. Nobody said so. It was not the morning for it.



The sisters took Mau on a quest at nine.

It was raining as hard as it had rained all week. Umaru announced, at breakfast, that there was a Legendary Artifact somewhere in the old inn's attic, left there before the Move, and that she required her brother to retrieve it with her. Immediately. Coco and Bocchi agreed that it was Legendary. Nobody else at the table said anything at all.

"In this rain?" said Mau.

"Legends don't wait for weather," said Umaru.

Mau looked at the three of them. All three were wearing long black coats with AGENT pinned to the lapel.

He looked at the coats for a moment. Then he decided, visibly, that it was too early in the morning to ask.

"Okay," he said, and went to get his boots.

Frieren, at the end of the table, did not look up from her book. Her ears twitched once.



Maomao made the chocolate in the workroom with the door locked.

She did it the way she did everything: precisely, slowly, with the measures written down before she began and checked twice as she went. Cacao. Cream. The local root, ground fine. A dose half of what it had been two years ago, because two years ago there had not been a seal in anyone, and she had not liked the thought of what an uninhibited pattern-beat might decide to build.

She poured it into a small square mold. While it set, she went to the kitchen to find a dish worthy of a Legendary Artifact, because Umaru had insisted on presentation.

She was gone four minutes.

In those four minutes, the workroom door, which was locked, opened very quietly. Frieren, who had been opening locked doors for a thousand years and had never once considered whether she ought to, came in.

She looked at the chocolate setting in its mold.

She looked at the small labeled vial beside it, which said, in Maomao's handwriting, ROOT EXTRACT — DO NOT ADD MORE.

She considered this instruction carefully, as she considered all instructions.

Then she added more.

Not a lot. A little. She had read the measures upside down over Maomao's shoulder the night before. It had seemed to her, as a matter of simple reasoning, that a couple of hours was not enough time to find out what she needed to know. She stirred it in with the tip of one finger so the surface stayed smooth. She hesitated, thought about Himmel turning red to the ears, and added a little more.

Then she left, and closed the door, and locked it behind her.

Maomao came back with a small blue dish, looked at the chocolate, found it set, and noticed nothing.

She would notice later.

She would have a great deal to say about it.



The girls dressed Frieren at noon.

They did it in her room, with the door shut and Ori on guard outside it. They had argued at the meeting about whose clothes she should wear, and Marin, with surprising force, had won. Not theirs. Hers. Mau should see *her*. Not Marin's idea of her, or Fern's. Her.

So they opened the closet that had been turned a quarter into the inner corner of the room, and went through everything Frieren owned. It was not much. Traveling clothes. Mau's shirts. Mau's hoodie. More of Mau's shirts. And at the very back, folded small and wrapped in paper, a dark green dress with a high collar and silver at the cuffs. She had worn it once, a very long time ago, to a coronation she had found extremely boring.

Marin held it up.

The room went quiet.

"Oh," said Kita.

"Oh," said Marin.

Fern did her hair. She had not done Frieren's hair in a very long time. She did it the way she remembered from when she was small, when Frieren had still let her, twin coils pinned up and back with two of Frieren's own silver pins. When she was done, Fern stood back and looked, and for a moment did not say anything at all.

"You look like a queen," said Umaru, back from the old inn, soaking wet, carrying a Legendary Artifact that was, on inspection, an old kettle.

"I look like I'm going to a coronation," said Frieren, looking at herself in the bit of polished metal Marin was holding up. "Which I hated."

"You look," said Maki, from the doorway, "like a woman who knows exactly what she's doing."

"I don't," said Frieren.

"Nobody does," said Maki. "That's the trick."



Umaru delivered the chocolate at one.

She did it in the kitchen, where Mau was drying off from the quest with a towel over his head. She put the small blue dish down beside him with the square of chocolate on it, and stood back, and folded her arms inside her AGENT HAMSTER coat.

"Quest reward," she said.

Mau looked at the dish.

"For the kettle?"

"For the *Legendary Artifact*."

"It's a kettle, Umaru."

"Eat your reward."

Mau ate it.

Behind the kitchen door, four agents in black coats held their breath. Down the corridor, at the turn, someone whispered into a paper cup, and the whisper went along a waxed string across three ceilings and into the common room. There, Frieren, in green, with her hair up, was sitting on the couch with a book she was not reading.

*The bird has eaten.*

Frieren's ears twitched.



Nothing happened.

Mau finished drying his hair. He went and helped Momo with the lunch dishes. He helped Wakana move a bolt of cloth, which was apparently now short a few coats. He did not notice. He sat with Senku for twenty minutes going over drainage figures. He was entirely, completely, maddeningly normal.

In the common room, Frieren turned a page she had not read.

An hour went by.

Another.

"Maybe it didn't work," whispered Coco into a cup.

"It worked," whispered Maomao from the workroom. "It always works. Give it time."

She did not yet know how much time it was going to need to be given.



The first sign was small, and nobody but the agents noticed it.

Bocchi brought Mau a box of nails he had asked for, in her AGENT PINK coat. Mau said, "Thanks, Bocchi." Then, instead of going back to what he was doing, he reached out and put his hand on her head, gently, the way you would with a little sister, and left it there for a second.

Bocchi went completely rigid.

Mau went back to work as if nothing had happened.

Ten minutes later Coco brought him a lamp and got the same thing. Then Umaru, who came to check on him and had her hair ruffled so thoroughly that her hood fell off. Then Suika, whose helmet he patted, very solemnly, as if it were a head.

The agents retreated to the corridor and conferred in whispers.

"He's never done that," whispered Coco.

"He's always wanted to," whispered Umaru, with total certainty. "He just never let himself."

"Is it working?"

"It's starting," whispered Umaru.



It finished starting in the kitchen at half past three.

Mau was at the long table with Yuta, Rimuru and Rem, peeling vegetables for dinner because Momo had decreed that everyone who was not an agent had to be useful. Yuta was telling a story about a training exercise. Rem was listening. Rimuru, in his small black coat, was supposed to be listening, and was in fact watching Mau very closely.

"—so the whole thing collapses," Yuta was saying, "and Maki just stands there—"

"Frieren would have caught it," said Mau.

Yuta stopped.

"What?"

"The thing. The collapsing thing. Frieren would have caught it. She's very fast." Mau peeled a potato. "Not with her hands. She'd do a spell. She has a spell for everything. Do you know she has a spell for making bad tea less bad? It took her forty years to find. Forty years. For tea."

"I— yes," said Yuta. "I know about the tea spell."

"She found it for herself," said Mau. "Not for anyone. Just because she wanted better tea. I think that's the best thing I've ever heard."

Rem had stopped peeling.

"Mau," she said slowly.

"Her eyes are green," said Mau. "Did you know that? Of course you know that. Everyone knows that. But they're a specific green. In the morning, when the light comes in the east window—"

Rimuru set down his peeler.

Ciel, said Rimuru, inside.

Inhibition reduced, said Ciel. Steadily. Walls steady. Nothing in the seal has noticed anything. A pause. His heart rate rises every time he says her name. I am noting that. It is not a guardrail matter.

"—and she pretends she doesn't like the birds, at the old inn, the ones in the rafters, but she fed them every single morning—"

Rimuru turned to the paper cup that hung on its string from a hook above the kitchen door. Senku had put one in every room. He leaned in close and spoke into it, very quietly, in what he clearly believed was a professional voice.

"The bird," said Rimuru, "is singing."

Down a long waxed string, across three ceilings, in the common room, Frieren, in green, closed her book.



They brought him to the common room at four.

Two agents on either side of him, Milim and Umaru, in black coats, with expressions of tremendous seriousness, each holding one of his elbows. Mau let them, cheerfully, the way he would let them do anything. Behind them came Rimuru, and Yuta, and Rem, who had decided that whatever this was, they were not missing it.

Diablo opened the common room door for them with a bow.

Inside, every chair was full. Thirty people had found reasons to be in the common room on a rainy afternoon. Every one of them was pretending, badly, to be doing something else.

And on the couch, in the middle of all of it, in dark green with silver at her cuffs and her hair pinned up in two silver coils, sat Frieren. She was holding a book, and looking at the door, and waiting.

Mau stopped in the doorway.

Everyone in the room held their breath.
