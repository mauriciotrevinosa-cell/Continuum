## Chapter 46 — The Most Beautiful Doesn't Work

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2

Frieren came down to breakfast smiling.

That was the first thing. The second thing was that she did not stop.

She came down the corridor and into the common room still in Mau's hoodie, hair loose, and sat down at the long table with a book. The whole household had been waiting for her since dawn, in a state of tension that had made Momo burn the first batch of eggs. She was smiling. Not the small sideways smile that barely moved her mouth. A wide one. It went from ear to ear and did not appear to be under her control in any way.

Thirty people stared at her.

Umaru, still in her AGENT HAMSTER coat because she had slept in it, leaned so far across the table that she nearly fell into the porridge.

"*Well?*"

Frieren opened her book.

"He said," said Frieren, to the page, "that I'm the most beautiful."

The common room went silent.

"The most beautiful *what*?" said Kita.

"Person," said Frieren. "Or place. Or thing." She turned a page. "Anything. Since the first second."

Somebody made a noise like a kettle. It might have been Marin. It might have been several people.

"And my eyes," Frieren added, still to the book. "He said my eyes."

Kita burst into tears. Bocchi patted her on the back, also crying. Milim threw both arms in the air and shouted something in a language nobody recognized. Nazuna, who had stayed awake past dawn for this and would sleep until the following sunset, said "*Finally*," fell over sideways on the couch, and was asleep before she landed.

Frieren went on reading.

She went on reading through breakfast. She went on reading through the end of breakfast, when people got up to start the morning's work. She went on reading when the rain eased off and the worksite crews began pulling on their boots, and when Wakana came through with an armful of planks, and when the whole house was, around her, busy.

She did not get up.

Fern stood beside the table with a ledger.

"You're on the drying-rack crew today," said Fern.

"No," said Frieren.

"You signed up."

"I signed up yesterday," said Frieren. "Yesterday I didn't know I was the most beautiful."

Fern looked at her.

"The most beautiful," said Frieren calmly, and turned a page, "doesn't work."

Fern opened her mouth to say something.

Then she looked properly at Frieren's face. She had known that face all her life, since she was a child too small to see over a table. In all that time she had seen it do almost nothing. She had seen it grieve, sometimes, and been frightened by it. She had seen it pleased, rarely, and treasured it. She had never once seen it like this.

Frieren looked happy. Plainly, foolishly, entirely happy, like a person who had never been told no in her life and had just been told yes.

Fern closed her mouth.

She took Frieren's name off the drying-rack crew without a word and wrote her own name in its place.

"Thank you," said Frieren, without looking up.

"Don't," said Fern, and went to dry racks, and found, about halfway through, that she was smiling too.



Mau woke up a little after ten, which was very late for him.

The east window was full of grey, rain-washed light. The bed beside him was empty and still faintly warm. His head felt thick and slow and enormously well-rested, the way it did after a fever had broken. For a moment he lay there not thinking anything at all.

Then he started remembering.

It came back in pieces, the way dreams do, except it was not a dream. The kitchen. Potatoes. *Her eyes are a specific green.* Bocchi's head under his hand. The common room, thirty people, the green dress. *Frierennnnn*, out loud, in front of everyone. Lying across her lap on the couch like a dog. The deer. *I think it was you.* Telling her she was terrible at flirting.

And then, last of all, slowly and in perfect detail, the bed. Her in his hoodie. *You're the most beautiful person I've ever seen. Or place. Or thing.*

*Your eyes.*

Mau put both hands over his face.

He could feel it happening. He could feel the heat go up his neck and into his face and his ears and the top of his head, until he was certain he was glowing. He was certain anyone walking past the door would be able to see it through the wood.

Above him, in the loft, someone was laughing.

It was not loud. It was the helpless, wheezing, breathless laughter of someone who had been trying very hard to hold it in for a long time and had finally failed. Mau took his hands off his face and looked up. A round blue face was peering at him over the edge of the loft, shaking.

"You're awake," said Rimuru, wiping at his slime-eyes with a slime-hand. "Good. I didn't want to come down until you were awake. I wanted to see your face."

"Shut up."

"She's never going to let this go," said Rimuru. "You know that, right? Ever. She's going to bring it up in a hundred years."

"Shut *up*."

Mau, said Ciel, calmly, inside his head. Nothing happened. The walls held all night. Nothing in the seal noticed anything. A pause. Your heart rate is currently elevated.

"Shut up," said Mau, to the ceiling, to both of them, to the world.

Rimuru laughed so hard he slid off the edge of the loft and landed on the bed in a heap.

Mau lay there under the east window with his face on fire and his best friend dissolving with laughter on his legs.

Eventually, because there was nothing else to do, he said:

"...What *happened*?"

Rimuru, still on his legs, rolled over and looked up at him, very pleased with himself.

"Okay," he said. "So. There was a meeting."
