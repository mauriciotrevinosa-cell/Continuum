# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 1 — Same Words

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 1

The phrase appeared in three regions.

> uncontrolled concentration of foreign capability

Same order.

Same adjective.

Same bureaucratic rhythm.

Different authorities.

Anko laid the documents side by side.

Nobody said mastermind.

Not yet.

Momo leaned over.

"They copied."

Senku:

"Probably."

Anko:

"Probably isn't provenance."

Good.

Second repeated phrase:

> temporary relocation pending capability assessment

Five documents.

Four seals.

One office that could not be verified.

That was worse.

Not because fake office proved conspiracy.

Because nobody could tell whether office was:
real;
renamed;
copied;
invented;
clerical ghost.

Searchers began collecting source chain.

Who issued?

Who delivered?

What packet?

Which map?

Which rumor arrived first?

One document used river bend drawn wrong.

Senku compared another.

Same wrong angle.

Two independent offices could copy same base map.

That was plausible.

Three?

Still plausible.

Different dates?

Interesting.

No leap.

Anko wrote:

COMMON SOURCE MATERIAL LIKELY.

Not:
mastermind.

Mau watched board.

"I hate how slow evidence is."

Anko:

"Good evidence often is."

He knew that lesson too well.

No perfect scanner.

Outside, city defense proposal was already circulating.

Repeated hostile language made residents nervous.

Some wanted stronger perimeter checks.

Others worried every extra checkpoint would make propaganda look true.

Fern scheduled open discussion.

No emergency decree.

Good.

One refugee looked at documents and said:

"If they all say same thing, maybe they're right."

Silence.

Aira almost answered emotionally.

Stopped.

Rimuru asked:

"Which part?"

"That gathering us makes us dangerous."

Rimuru looked around.

Milim visible in distance.

Diablo somewhere nearby.

Frieren.

Mau.

Eren not yet.

"The concentration is real," Rimuru said.

The refugee waited.

"So is the danger?"

"Potentially."

No propaganda denial.

Then:

"That does not make collective punishment correct."

Different.

The city had to defend itself without pretending outsiders invented every concern.

Senku pointed to phrase.

"Words are being standardized faster than facts."

That became important conclusion.

Fear was learning to reproduce itself.

Maybe because someone taught it.

Maybe because institutions copy one another.

Maybe both.

Searchers added document provenance to route work.

Not glamorous.

A merchant brought packet.

A local clerk remembered who handed original.

A town had received language from regional office nobody could verify.

Threads.

At dinner, Mau asked:

"How many repeated phrases before we call it intentional?"

Anko:

"Intentional copying is already likely."

"I mean one actor."

"More."

He sighed.

No villain reveal.

Evidence network.

The city had grown enough that opponents—or merely frightened institutions—could describe it using shared language before it had named itself.

That made defense political before it became military.

And it meant next person who arrived carrying fear might not know where the fear's words came from.
