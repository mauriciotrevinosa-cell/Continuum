# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 2 — The Legendary Snack

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 2  
**Continuity state:** Ordinary day before Eren | Umaru's quest | Himmel and Mau

The rumor reached Umaru at breakfast, through a Searcher who had no idea what he was starting.

"They fry potatoes in the market town now," he said, to nobody in particular, over his porridge. "Slice them thin. Salt them. Sell them in paper cones. Strange thing. Half the stalls have started doing it."

Umaru's spoon stopped.

Very slowly, she turned her head.

"Thin," she said.

The Searcher looked at her.

"...Yes?"

"Salted."

"Yes."

"Crisp?"

The Searcher, who had now noticed that the entire end of the table had gone silent, said carefully, "I think so?"

Umaru stood up.

She stood up on the bench, which Fern had told her eleven times not to do.

"Everyone," she announced. "This is a quest."



Mau said yes before she had finished explaining.

Umaru narrowed her eyes at him.

"Can't you just do the thing? The poof thing? And take us?"

"I can't call it," said Mau. "It calls me."

"How often?"

Mau thought about it.

"Twice this month."

"That's useless," said Umaru, with deep disappointment, and went to find her shoes.

He always did.

That was the problem, according to Fern, and the entire point, according to Umaru.

"It's two hours each way," said Fern.

"Yes," said Umaru.

"The east road—"

"Searchers say it's quiet this week. Aira said so twice."

"For potatoes."

"For chips."

"They might be bad."

Umaru looked at her with the patient pity of a woman who had lived without chips for a very long time.

"Fern," she said. "That's why it's a quest."

Okarun came because Mau was going.

Turbo Granny came because Okarun was going, and because, as she put it, somebody had to watch a pack of idiots walk two hours for fried dirt.

Coco and Suika came because Umaru was going, and because a quest needed a map, and Suika had a map, and Coco had decided the map needed stars.

Milim came because she heard the word quest from the other side of the courtyard and arrived before anyone could say no.

"You're the hired muscle," Umaru told her.

Milim looked delighted.

"What do I hit?"

"Nothing."

Milim looked less delighted.

"Probably nothing," Umaru amended, and Milim cheered up.

And Himmel came because, as he explained to the gathering party, he had heard the word quest and the word party in the same sentence, and he could not in good conscience let either happen without a hero.

Umaru stared at him.

"You're a real hero."

"I am."

"Like an actual one. From a story."

"From several," said Himmel modestly.

Umaru grabbed both of Coco's hands and made a sound only dogs could hear.



Himmel took the quest very seriously.

He took everything about quests seriously.

That was what made it so funny.

He organized a marching order at the gate.

He insisted the party have a name.

He gave a short speech about courage, the dangers of the road, and the importance of looking out for one another, which would have been very moving if the road had been dangerous, and if Turbo Granny had not been heckling him the entire time.

"It's a two-hour walk to a vegetable stall, you idiot."

"Every great journey begins with a single step."

"It ends with a potato!"

"Many do," said Himmel serenely, and started walking.

Okarun, beside Mau, leaned over.

"Is he always like this?"

Mau watched Himmel stride out in front of the party, cloak swinging, chin up, as if they were marching on a demon king instead of a market.

"I think so," said Mau. "I think he's always exactly like this."

Okarun considered it.

"I kind of like it," he admitted.

"Yeah," said Mau. "Me too."



Frieren was waiting at the first bend in the road.

She was sitting on a rock with her staff across her knees, looking at nothing, as if she had happened to be there.

Mau stopped.

"I thought you said no."

"I did."

"So why—"

"There's a book stall in the market town," Frieren said. "Sometimes they have old grimoires."

"You've never been to the market town."

"I've heard."

"From who?"

Frieren stood up and fell into step beside him without answering.

Behind them, Himmel had gone very quiet.

Mau glanced back.

Himmel was looking at Frieren the way people look at a place they used to know and find has been rebuilt into something else.

Not sad.

Surprised.

Then he laughed, out loud, delighted, and caught up with the party.



Halfway there, they stopped to rest by a stream.

Coco and Suika updated the map.

The map now had a dragon on it.

There were no dragons in the region.

"It's for Milim," Coco explained. "So she feels included."

Milim looked at the dragon for a long time.

"It's very good," she said gruffly, and did not let anyone else hold the map for the rest of the day.

Turbo Granny sat on a rock and told Okarun everything he was doing wrong with his life.

Okarun listened to it all.

Then he said, "Granny, did you come because you were worried about me?"

Turbo Granny stared at him.

"I came," she said, "because if you get eaten by something on the road, I'll have to hear Momo cry about it, and her crying is ugly."

"So, yes."

"Shut up, idiot."

Okarun smiled into the stream.

Mau watched them and did not say anything, because some things were not his to say, and Okarun looked over and caught his eye, and grinned, and Mau grinned back.



The market town was louder than the settlement and smaller than Umaru had imagined.

Mau wore Marin's patch, the dark blue one with the star, because a town was full of strangers.

A small boy stared at it the whole way down the first street.

Himmel leaned down and told him, in a confidential whisper, that the man with the patch was a retired pirate who had sworn an oath never to speak of the sea, and the boy looked at Mau with such reverence that Mau had to walk the rest of the street with his face turned away so nobody would see him laughing.

She found the stall in four minutes.

She had smelled it from the gate.

The paper cones were stacked on a board. The potatoes were sliced thin. The salt was real.

Umaru paid with coins she had been saving for some time for exactly this kind of emergency, and took one cone, and held it up to the light like treasure.

Everyone watched.

She ate one.

Her face did something complicated.

She ate another one.

Her face did it again.

"Well?" said Coco.

Umaru lowered the cone.

"They're soft," she said.

Silence.

"They're thin, and salted, and soft. Like—like a potato that's tired." Her voice cracked. "They're not chips. They're sad potatoes."

Milim tried one.

"These are sad," she agreed.

Suika tried one, and nodded solemnly.

Okarun tried one, and said, with great diplomacy, that they were very good sad potatoes.

Umaru sat down on the edge of the stall's step and looked at the cone in her hands like someone who had walked very far for something that did not exist.

Mau sat down next to her.

He did not say they could try again some other time.

He did not say it had been a nice walk anyway.

He just sat there.

Himmel crouched down in front of her.

"Umaru," he said. "I have been on a great many quests."

Umaru sniffed.

"Most of them," Himmel went on, "did not end with the treasure we came for. One ended with nothing but a statue of me, which I insisted on, and which was the right decision. One ended with a sword nobody could pull out, which was fine, because the point turned out to be the walk. A quest is almost never about the treasure."

Umaru looked up at him.

"It's about the chips," she said thickly.

Himmel opened his mouth.

Closed it.

"Yes," he conceded. "In this case it's about the chips."



Mau bought a sack of raw potatoes.

Everyone looked at him.

"Momo," he said. "And Seiko. And Senku knows about oil."

Umaru stared at the sack.

Slowly, the despair on her face began to rearrange itself into something more dangerous.

"We make them ourselves," she whispered.

"We try," said Mau.

"Mau." Umaru grabbed his sleeve. "Mau, that's the real quest."

Himmel, behind her, put his hand over his heart, and Turbo Granny made a gagging noise so loud a stallholder dropped a basket.

Frieren came back from the book stall with a small battered grimoire and an expression of great satisfaction.

"What is it?" Mau asked.

Frieren showed him the page.

He read it.

Then he read it again.

"It's a spell," he said slowly, "for making fried things crisp."

"Yes."

"You found that today."

"Yes."

"At the one stall in the one town we came to, on the one day we came for chips."

Frieren looked at him.

"Sometimes," she said, "the grimoire is about the chips."



They got home after dark.

Momo took one look at the sack of potatoes, and one look at Umaru's face, and rolled up her sleeves without being asked.

Seiko supervised.

Senku, who had come into the kitchen to complain about the noise, stayed to measure the oil, because there was a correct temperature and he was not going to let anyone guess it.

The first batch was too thick.

The second batch burned.

The third batch was almost right, and Senku said so, and Momo said nobody had asked him, and Seiko said they were all doing it wrong and took over.

The fourth batch came out of the oil golden, and Frieren held one hand over the tray, and said three words very quietly.

Senku watched her.

"That's cheating."

"It's a spell," said Frieren.

Umaru took one.

She bit it.

It cracked.

The whole kitchen heard it crack.

Umaru stood very still with her eyes closed.

Then she started to cry.

Not sad crying.

The other kind.

Coco hugged her from one side and Suika hugged her from the other, and Milim hugged all three of them and knocked over a chair, and Momo said, "Okay, okay, there's more," and kept frying.



Later, on the back step, with the noise of the kitchen still spilling out behind them, Himmel sat down beside Mau with a paper cone of real chips in his hand.

He ate one thoughtfully.

"These are very good," he said.

"Momo's good."

"Momo's very good." Himmel turned the cone in his fingers. "When I knew her, you know, Frieren would never have come on a quest for chips."

Mau looked at him.

He felt something in his chest go careful, the old way.

But Himmel's face was open, and easy, and there was nothing underneath it but what he said.

"She came today," said Mau.

"Yes," said Himmel. "That's what I mean."

He ate another chip.

"She made up a book stall," he said. "Did you notice? She'd never heard of any book stall. She came because you were walking down the road, and she didn't want to be somewhere you weren't." He smiled at the dark. "And then the book stall turned out to be real, and had exactly the right book in it, which is the most Frieren thing that has ever happened."

Mau laughed.

He had not expected to.

Himmel laughed with him.

For a while they just sat there, two people who had both loved the same impossible person in different times and different ways, eating chips on a back step, while Umaru inside announced that the quest was complete and the party was disbanded and she would be founding a new party tomorrow.

"Himmel," said Mau.

"Mm?"

"Thank you for coming."

Himmel looked genuinely surprised.

"Why wouldn't I?" he said. "It was a quest."
