# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 11 — Not Here Yet

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 11  
**Continuity state:** Night after Stark's birthday | The adaptation wheel turns one eighth inside the seal | Target absent

The sound woke him.

It was not a loud sound. It was not, Mau would realize later, a sound anyone else could have heard. It was the kind of sound you hear with your teeth: a single heavy click, deep and round and final, like a great iron wheel turning one notch on its axle somewhere very far down.

He was awake before it finished.

The room was dark. Stark's birthday had gone on late, and the whole wing smelled faintly of smoke and sugar, and Frieren was asleep against his side with a paper crown still tangled in her hair because she had refused to take it off.

Up in the loft, a soft shape shifted, and a round blue face appeared over the edge of the boards.

"Mau?" said Rimuru, very quietly. "Ciel just woke me."

I looked, said Ciel, before Mau could ask. Rule four. I am telling you I looked.

"What was that?"

The wheel.

Mau went cold.

"The wheel turned?"

Inside the seal. Not through it. Nothing came out. A pause. One notch. One eighth of a full turn. Then it stopped.

Mau lay very still.

He had felt the wheel turn once before. Inside Rimuru, on the worst day of his life, when the pattern had built it whole out of everything it had read and set it spinning against whatever it could not survive. It had turned then because something was hitting him. It had turned to learn how not to be hit by that again.

"What's hitting me?" he whispered.

Nothing, said Ciel.

"Then what's it adapting to?"

I do not know. There is nothing here for it to adapt to. I have checked the settlement. I have checked the room. I have checked you. Ciel's voice was very level. It is adapting to something that is not here.

Mau stared at the ceiling.

"Yet?" he said.

The silence went on for a long time.

I would like you not to have said that, said Ciel.



Rimuru came down from the loft.

He did it the slime way, pouring over the edge and reforming on the floor as a person, rumpled and barefoot, with one cheek creased from the blanket. He sat on the end of the bed, carefully, so as not to wake Frieren, and Mau sat up beside him.

"Tell me what it felt like," said Rimuru.

Mau tried.

"Like when the weather changes," he said. "Before a storm. When the air goes heavy and your ears pop and nothing's happened yet but everything knows it's going to. Except it wasn't the air." He touched his own sternum. "It was here. And it wasn't a storm. It was more like..." He frowned. "A wall. Like something had drawn a line somewhere, and the wheel heard it."

"A line where?"

"I don't know. Not anywhere. That's what's strange. It didn't point anywhere."

Rimuru was quiet.

Ciel, he said, inside, and Mau heard it because Ciel let him. Can you read direction from the turn?

No, Master. It did not turn toward a place. Mau is correct. It turned toward a kind of thing.

"What kind?"

I don't know that either.

Rimuru rubbed his face with both hands.

"Mahoraga adapts after it's hit," he said slowly. "That's what Yuta says. It takes the hit, it turns, and next time that hit doesn't work. It doesn't turn first." He looked at Mau. "It's not supposed to turn first."

"It turned first."

"Yeah."

They sat there in the dark.

Frieren slept on, the paper crown slipping slowly off her head.



Yuta, in the morning, said the same thing Rimuru had, almost word for word. They had been up on the roof together two nights before, the three of them, the way they were every week now. Mau found that he was glad, absurdly glad, that it was Yuta he was telling this to on a step and not somebody he had to explain everything to from the beginning.

He was sitting on the step outside the common room with a cup of tea gone cold in his hands, and he listened to all of it without interrupting, and at the end he set the cup down on the step beside him and was quiet for a while.

"It adapts to what hits it," he said. "It turned before anything hit."

"So what does that mean?"

Yuta shook his head.

"I don't know. I've never heard of it happening." He looked out at the settlement waking up. "Maybe it means the pattern's not just a copy anymore. Maybe it learned something the real one never needed to." He paused. "Or maybe something is coming that it already recognizes."

"Recognizes from where?"

"From you," said Yuta simply. "It read everything in you. Everything in Rimuru. Everything in Ciel's archive. Maybe something in all that looks like what's coming."

Mau did not like that answer.

He noticed that he did not like it, and noticed also that he did not try to make Yuta say a different one.

That was new too.



Ciel wrote it down. She told him she was writing it down, because rule four, and because he asked.

Adaptation: one eighth. Target: absent.

Mau wrote it down too, in the notebook he still kept, though it had been a long time now since he had needed the notebook to tell him what was real. He wrote the date. He wrote what it had felt like. Weather. A line. A wall.

Then, underneath, after some thought, he wrote:

Not here yet.

He looked at it for a long time.

Then he closed the notebook and went to help clear up after Stark's birthday, because there were plates everywhere and Fern was already standing in the middle of the common room with the expression she wore when she was about to start assigning people to things, and the day was ordinary, and he wanted, very badly, for it to stay ordinary for as long as it could.

It stayed ordinary all day.

The wheel did not turn again.

He listened for it anyway.
