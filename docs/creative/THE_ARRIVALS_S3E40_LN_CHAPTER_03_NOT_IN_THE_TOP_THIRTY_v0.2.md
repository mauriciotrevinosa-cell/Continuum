# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 14 — Not in the Top Thirty

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 14

It happened at lunch, which was where most things in the house happened.

Eren had been in the settlement for nine days. In nine days he had attended four meetings, argued in all of them, and been right in two. In nine days the whole place had bent itself around him: hearings, drafts, whole evenings spent on what to do about a man who had not yet done anything. He had noticed. He had not decided yet whether he liked it.

Today it was the walls again.

He had come out of the morning's route meeting still arguing, carried the argument down the corridor, and set it on the long table between the bread and the soup. Half the household was trying to eat around it.

"You're building a cage and calling it a door," said Eren. "Every route you mark is a route someone else can close. Every warning system is a list of where your people are. You think you're making them safe. You're making them findable."

"We talked about this," said Mikasa quietly.

"You talked. I listened. Now I'm talking." He was standing now. He had not noticed himself stand. "None of you have lived through what happens when the world decides you're a threat. I have. And if nobody in this house is willing to do what actually has to be done—"

He pushed his chair back to leave.

It was a good exit. It had the shape of every exit he had ever made: chair scraping, sentence unfinished, everyone left to sit in the silence.

He did not get to make it.

Milim, at the far end of the table, had been eating her fourth bowl of soup. She did not get up. She did not look particularly interested. She simply reached across the corner of the table without putting down her spoon, hooked one finger through the back of Eren's collar, lifted him off the floor, held him there for a moment at arm's length like a cat that had climbed something it should not have, and set him back down in his chair.

Then she went on eating her soup.

The whole thing had taken about a second.

Eren sat in his chair.

He did not move.

At the head of the table, Diablo, who had been standing with a teapot because Diablo was always standing with a teapot, leaned over without a word and filled Eren's cup.

"Lunch," said Milim, through soup.

Nobody else said anything.

Nobody laughed. That was the strange part, Eren would think afterward. Nobody laughed at him. Momo passed the bread down the table. Okarun asked Aira for the salt. Bocchi, two seats down, went back to cutting her potatoes into very small, very precise pieces. Lunch simply went on around him, the way weather goes on around a rock.

Eren looked down at the cup of tea.

He picked it up.

He drank it.

It was very good tea.



Mau found him at the well that afternoon.

Eren was sitting on the edge of it, which Momo had told everyone not to do, looking at nothing. Mau had come for water. He nodded at Eren, said nothing, and started to wind the bucket up.

"Does she do that a lot?" said Eren, after a while.

"Milim?" Mau thought about it. "Not to people. Mostly to Rimuru. Sometimes to me. Once to Diablo, but he liked it."

"She didn't even put her spoon down."

"No," said Mau. "She doesn't usually."

The bucket came up. Mau unhooked it.

"She could kill everyone in this settlement before lunch," said Eren. "Couldn't she."

"Before the soup got cold," said Mau. "Yeah. Probably."

"And you just eat with her."

"Every day." Mau set the bucket down on the rim. "She cheats at cards. She's terrible at it. Umaru beats her every week. Milim makes her do it again until she wins one, and then she's so happy she cries." He shrugged. "That's Milim. The other thing's true too. Both things are true."

Eren was quiet for a moment.

"Rimuru's the same," he said slowly. "Isn't he. And Diablo. And Frieren."

"And Yuta. And Maki. And Mikasa, a bit. And—" Mau stopped counting. "A lot of people here, yeah."

"So I'm not—"

He stopped.

Mau waited.

"I've been arguing with you all for nine days," said Eren, "like I'm the most dangerous thing in this place."

"You're not," said Mau gently. "Not even close."

He picked up the bucket.

And the bucket was not in his hand.

He was not at the well.

He was at the far side of the yard, by the washing court, forty steps away, standing in exactly the same posture, hand closed on nothing, water splashing down onto his feet from the place where the bucket had just been. The bucket was still sitting on the rim of the well, rocking.

Eren had seen Titans come out of nowhere. He had seen men vanish into steam. He had never seen anyone simply be somewhere else between one breath and the next.

He stood up very fast.

Across the yard, Mau looked down at his wet feet, then back at the well, and sighed.

He walked back. Forty steps, at an ordinary pace. When he reached the well, he picked the bucket up again, and Eren saw his left eye.

It had opened. The white of it had gone black, all of it, and in the middle the iris burned red. Something not quite human looked out of half of Mau's face for a moment. Then Mau blinked hard, and breathed out slowly, and it began to fade.

"Sorry," said Mau. "That was a leak."

"A *what*?"

"There's a thing in me. I didn't choose it. It's held back, mostly. But some of it leaks. Little things. Distance, mostly. Sometimes floating." He rubbed his eye. "Sometimes the eye, when I get a fright. That one was the first in a while."

Eren stared at him.

"You're supposed to tell me when it happens," said Mau. "It's a rule. Rule eight. You didn't know. That's fine."

"You're—"

Eren could not find the word.

Mau found it for him.

"Broken?" He smiled a little. "Yeah. Everyone here is, a bit. Milim. Rimuru. Kaneki. Frieren. Me." He lifted the bucket. "It's kind of the entry requirement."

He walked back toward the kitchen with the water, at an ordinary pace, the whole way.



Milim found Eren that evening. She found him, rather than the other way around, which he was beginning to understand was how it went with Milim.

He was on the roof of the common room, on the eastern half, because someone had told him it was the good half. She came up the ladder in one jump, sat down next to him without asking, and swung her legs over the edge.

For a while she did not say anything, which was so unlike Milim that Eren kept glancing at her to check it was really her.

"Sorry about lunch," said Milim eventually.

"No, you're not."

"No," Milim agreed cheerfully. "But Rimuru says I should say it."

They sat.

The lamps were coming on across the settlement below them. Somewhere in the washing court, somebody was singing badly. The new tree in the plaza had put out two leaves.

"I had a friend," said Milim.

Eren looked at her.

She was not looking at him. She was looking out over the settlement, at the lamps, and her face was doing something it did not usually do. It was holding still.

"When I was very little," said Milim. "A long, long time ago. Longer than Frieren. He was a dragon. A little one. He was my best friend in the whole world." She swung her legs. "A kingdom killed him. I don't remember why. I don't think there was a good reason. People just decided."

Eren did not say anything.

"I was very young," said Milim. "And I was very strong. Stronger than anyone. So I went to the kingdom." She paused. "There isn't a kingdom there anymore. There isn't anything there anymore. Not a wall. Not a stone."

Eren had gone completely still.

"It didn't bring him back," said Milim.

She said it simply, the way she said everything, the way a child says a thing she worked out a long time ago and has never had to say out loud since.

"Then I was alone for a very long time," said Milim. "Thousands of years. Being the strongest thing in the world is very boring, did you know? Nobody plays with you. Everyone's scared. You win every game, and nobody wants to play again." She was quiet. "Time is the worst part. Not the kingdom. The time after."

She looked at him at last.

"Rimuru says you haven't done it yet," said Milim. "Your thing. The big thing. In your world."

Eren's throat moved.

"No," he said. "Not yet."

"Good," said Milim. She considered him for a long moment, and the stillness went out of her face, and she was Milim again. "Then don't. It's boring afterward. And you don't have to, here. Nobody here needs you to be the strongest. There's lots of us. You can just be a person who's really good at arguing about doors."

She stood up on the edge of the roof.

"Do you want to play cards?" she said. "Umaru's going to win. She always wins. It's very fun."

Eren looked up at her.

"...Yes," he said.



He lost every hand.

He lost to Umaru, and to Suika, and to Coco, and once, astonishingly, to Milim. When she won she threw her cards in the air and burst into tears of joy and had to be hugged by three people.

Later, when the game had broken up and the common room was emptying, he went out onto the step and sat down. After a while, Mikasa came and sat down beside him, the way she had been doing every night for nine days.

They sat in the dark.

"Milim picked me up," said Eren. "With one finger."

"I saw."

"Mau teleported. By accident. In the middle of a sentence. His eye went black."

"I know."

"Diablo poured me tea. Like nothing had happened."

"Diablo always pours tea," said Mikasa.

Eren was quiet for a long time.

"I'm not the most dangerous thing here," he said finally.

"No," said Mikasa.

"I'm not even close."

"You're not in the top thirty," said Mikasa.

She did not say it unkindly. She said it the way she said true things, plainly, looking out at the dark.

Then she turned her head and looked at him.

"Isn't that a relief?"

Eren opened his mouth to argue.

He found that he could not.

All his life, as far back as he could remember, everything had depended on him. The wall. The war. The world. Whether everyone he loved lived or died had come down, every time, to what he chose, and what he became, and whether he was strong enough. He had been the center of it all since he was a child. He had never once been allowed to be anything else.

Here, a girl with pink hair had picked him up with one finger at lunch and gone on eating her soup. Nobody had even laughed.

Here, he was not the most dangerous person at the table. He was not even in the top thirty.

Here, if something terrible came, there were thirty people who would stand in front of it before anyone even thought to ask him.

"Yes," said Eren, very quietly.

Mikasa waited.

"I hate that it is," said Eren.

"I know," said Mikasa. She looked back out at the dark. "That's how you know it's real."

They sat on the step until the lamps went out.
