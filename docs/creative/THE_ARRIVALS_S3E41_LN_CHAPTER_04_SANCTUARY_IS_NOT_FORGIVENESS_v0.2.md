# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 18 — Sanctuary Is Not Forgiveness

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 18

The hearing lasted two hours.

Nobody called it trial.

That distinction did not make it painless.

The harmed resident spoke first.

Not interrupted.

Not required to be calm.

They described:
sister found;
people taken;
message routes;
years of not knowing which informant.

The injured man listened.

No demand for eye contact.

Then he spoke.

Anko asked specific questions.

Not:
are you good now?

Facts.

He participated.

Not under total coercion.

Not fully free either.

People were harmed.

He later helped two escape.

His pursuers intended execution.

No fact erased another.

Rimuru asked:

"What are you asking from us?"

The injured man stared.

"Not to hand me over."

"Residence?"

"No."

"Trust?"

"No."

Good.

Only immediate sanctuary from summary death.

The harmed resident:

"And what about what he did?"

That was harder.

Anko:

"We don't erase it."

"Then punish him."

"What consequence would you consider legitimate?"

The resident had no answer ready.

Execution?

No.

Expulsion into execution?

No.

Permanent confinement?

Maybe.

Evidence incomplete.

Mau sat near back.

Did not chair.

His instinct wanted solution.

No.

Let process exist.

Eren attended quietly.

He watched people argue about someone guilty of real harm.

This was better test than future hypothetical.

Decision:

No handover for summary execution.

Temporary sanctuary.

Medical care.

Restricted access to sensitive areas.

No route records.

No missing-person files.

No unsupervised movement into certain zones until review.

Evidence gathering.

Second review date.

The harmed resident did not forgive.

Nobody asked.

They left furious.

At door, Fern said:

"You can still use Arrival House."

The resident looked.

"With him here?"

"We can schedule separation."

Not:
be mature.

Not:
share space.

Protection included victim too.

The injured man heard decision.

Relief.

Then:

"How long?"

Anko gave exact review date.

He nodded.

No gratitude performance.

Later Eren asked Mau:

"You okay with protecting him?"

Mau thought.

"No."

Eren looked surprised.

Mau continued.

"I think it's right not to hand him over to be killed."

"Not same."

"No."

Exactly.

Sanctuary was not trust.

Not forgiveness.

Not innocence.

Not citizenship.

It was refusal to let one threat erase process.

The settlement protected two people who hated each other.

That was the point.

If sanctuary only applied to people everyone liked, it was hospitality with better branding.
