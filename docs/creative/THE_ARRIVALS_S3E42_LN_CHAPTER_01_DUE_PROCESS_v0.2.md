# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 15 — Due Process

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 15

Due process sounded boring until someone hated the person receiving it.

Then it sounded offensive.

A later applicant made the problem harder than the collaborator.

Current harm.

Here.

Documented.

Not old-world ambiguity.

The applicant had injured two people during a fight after arriving in Continuum.

One badly.

Then fled west.

Now returned because another group wanted to execute him for different reasons.

Arrival House accepted medical stabilization and temporary confinement.

Not open sanctuary.

Not immediate expulsion.

Eren asked first:

"How long?"

Fern:

"Until review."

"When?"

Silence.

Bad.

Eren looked around.

"That's how temporary becomes permanent."

Mikasa's face changed.

He was right.

Anko wrote date immediately.

Forty-eight hours for first formal review.

Restrictions specific:
room confinement except medical;
no weapons;
supervised conversation;
no record access.

Evidence list.

Victim statements.

Witness.

Applicant response.

No secret file if safety allowed disclosure.

One victim objected:

"Why does he get to see what we said?"

Anko:

"So he can answer claims used to restrict him."

"He hurt us."

"Yes."

No denial.

The victim hated answer.

Valid.

Eren sat at back.

Future guilt discussion had been abstract.

This was not.

Current conduct justified restriction.

Still not blank check.

At review, evidence showed:
fight initiated by applicant?
Partly.

One victim had threatened him first.

Applicant escalated far beyond necessary.

Both.

No simple self-defense.

No simple attack.

Decision:
continued restriction;
broader movement allowed under supervision;
restitution work only if victims agree not to interact;
second review in one week;
no handover for unrelated summary execution.

Nobody satisfied.

Victim wanted stronger.

Applicant wanted free movement.

Some residents wanted expulsion.

Others wanted complete sanctuary.

Good sign.

The process did not manufacture consensus.

It made disagreement legible.

They formalized basics:

allegations written;
evidence disclosed where safe;
accused can respond;
victims can speak without forgiving;
restrictions tied to current risks;
reviews scheduled;
emergency confinement has clock;
external enemies do not decide internal guilt;
no indefinite "pending" without recorded reason.

Momo read.

"Boring."

Anko:

"Good."

Eren looked at review date.

Mikasa looked at him.

He noticed.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"You looked."

Mikasa almost smiled.

The system still had power.

People could be confined.

Restricted.

Watched.

That needed more scrutiny, not less.

Later Eren asked Anko:

"What if everyone agrees someone is dangerous?"

"Then evidence should be easy."

"What if evidence isn't?"

"Then agreement isn't enough."

Hard sentence.

Necessary.

The settlement was becoming a place where people could hate outcomes and still know:
who decided;
why;
for how long;
what could change decision.

Not justice perfected.

Only refusal to let fear become indefinite by default.
