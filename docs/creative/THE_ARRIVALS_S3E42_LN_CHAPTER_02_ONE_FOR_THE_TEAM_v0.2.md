# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 21 — One for the Team

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 21  
**Continuity state:** Day after the hearings | Rimuru's quiet day | 'Ciel said' (she did not) | Unc | Mau turns it into a family trip to the sea

Rimuru wanted one quiet day.

That was all. One. He had sat through four days of hearings about Eren and a week of drafting before that. He had a stack of letters on his office desk, all addressed to *the leaders of the settlement*, which he was pretending not to have noticed. He had a loft he loved and a slime-sized coat he had never taken off since the agents. And he wanted, very badly, to spend one entire day lying in that loft doing absolutely nothing at all.

He made it until breakfast.

"RIMURU."

Milim landed on the end of the long table in a crouch, scattering bread, and beamed at him.

"Today," said Milim, "we're going to—"

"Ciel told me," said Rimuru, very fast, "that Mau wants you with him today."

Milim stopped.

Inside Rimuru's head, there was a silence so cold it had texture.

*I said no such thing*, said Ciel.

"He does?" said Milim.

"He does," said Rimuru, not looking at anyone. "All day. Just you. Ciel said. Specifically."

*I have never in my existence said anything about Mau wanting Milim. I would like that recorded.*

Rimuru kept his face perfectly still and thought, as hard as he could, *take one for the team*.

There was a long, offended pause.

*You owe me one*, said Ciel.

*I owe you one.*

*A large one.*

*A large one.*

Milim was already off the table and gone down the corridor at a speed that made the lamps flicker, shouting Mau's name.

Rimuru put his face down on the table among the scattered bread and breathed out.

He had about four minutes.



Milim found Mau in the yard and told him, at enormous volume, that today it was just the two of them, all day, because Ciel had said so.

Mau, who had been about to fix a gate, looked at her for a long moment.

"Ciel said that?"

"Rimuru said Ciel said that."

"Ah," said Mau, who knew Rimuru. "Okay."

He did not argue. He had learned a long time ago that when Milim decided a day belonged to her, the day agreed.

Milim, overjoyed, went to tell somebody. The nearest somebody was Coco, crossing the yard with a basket of laundry. Milim stopped her and told her, beaming, that today it was just Mau and Milim, all day, nobody else, it was official, Ciel had said.

Coco stood very still with the laundry.

"Just you?"

"Just me!"

"All day?"

"All day!"

Coco put the laundry down.

She went to find Umaru.

Umaru was in the common room with Suika and Bocchi, teaching them a card game. Coco told them.

The three of them looked at each other.

"That's not fair," said Umaru.

"It's very not fair," said Suika.

Bocchi did not say anything, but she put her cards down with great finality.

"If we can't have Mau," said Umaru slowly, "then we'll have—"

All four of them turned and looked down the corridor toward the office door where, every morning, Diablo stood guard over Rimuru-sama's peace.



Rimuru heard them coming.

He heard Diablo say, "I'm afraid Rimuru-sama is not—" and then a pause. Then he heard Diablo say, in a completely different tone, "...ah. The sisters. Of course. Please, go in." Diablo had decided long ago that the sisters were a force of nature and that it would be impolite to stand in the way of weather.

The door opened.

Four girls came in. Five, counting Milim, who had come back because she heard there was a plan. They surrounded his desk.

"You lied," said Umaru.

"I didn't lie. Ciel—"

*I did not*, said Ciel.

"Ciel says she didn't," said Rimuru, defeated.

"We know," said Coco. "Mau told us you'd say that."

Rimuru looked at the five of them, and at the stack of unread letters, and at his quiet day disappearing out the window.

Then, through the open door, all five of them saw Yuta walking past in the corridor with a cup of tea.

They turned.

Yuta, who had been having a perfectly pleasant morning, felt it before he saw it. He stopped. He looked into the office. He saw five faces turned toward him with exactly the same expression, and he took one careful step back.

"Unc," said Umaru.

Yuta's whole face changed.

"*Unc?*"

"Unc," agreed Suika.

"Why am I *unc*? I'm not— I'm barely older than— why *unc*?"

"Because you've abandoned us," said Coco.

"I haven't—"

"You have," said Umaru, folding her arms inside her hamster hood. "You've been catching up. For weeks. With Maki. You haven't played a single game with us since the Move. You didn't come to the fort. You missed the war. You weren't even an agent."

"I was busy—"

"Unc behavior," said Suika.

Yuta opened his mouth. Then he closed it and looked over his shoulder, desperately, for help.

Maki was standing in the corridor behind him.

She had been there the whole time. She had a cup of tea too. She was looking at her husband from another life, and at five small determined faces, and she was not saying anything. Her shoulders were shaking very slightly, the way they did when she was laughing so hard on the inside that none of it was allowed to reach her face.

"Maki," said Yuta.

Maki took a slow sip of tea.

"He has been," she said, very seriously, "a very bad brother."

The sisters cheered.

"*Maki*—"

"But today," said Maki, "he's going to make up for it. A little."

She took his tea out of his hand, kissed his cheek, and walked away down the corridor with both cups, and did not look back.

Yuta stood in the office doorway, surrounded.

He looked at Rimuru.

Rimuru looked back at him with the deep, quiet sympathy of one prisoner for another.

"I give up," said Yuta.



It was Mau, in the end, who turned it into a trip.

He came looking for Milim, since it was apparently their day, and found her in Rimuru's office with Rimuru, Yuta, four sisters, and a stack of letters nobody was reading. He looked at all of them for a while.

"So nobody's getting a quiet day," he said.

"No," said Rimuru, into his hands.

"And Milim gets me, and you get the sisters, and Yuta gets—"

"Unc'd," said Yuta grimly.

Mau thought about it.

"The Searchers found Eren by the sea," he said. "It's a day's cart ride. Mikasa knows the road." He looked around the office. "What if nobody gets anybody? What if everybody just... goes?"

There was a short, stunned silence.

"A *trip*," breathed Milim.

"A family trip," said Coco.

"To the *sea*," said Umaru, who had never seen this world's sea and had only been told it was there.

Rimuru lifted his face out of his hands.

He thought about his quiet day. Then he thought about a beach, and lying in the shallows in slime form with the sun on him and nobody bringing him letters, for two whole days, as far from his desk as it was possible to get.

*You still owe me one*, said Ciel.

*I know*, thought Rimuru. *I'm about to pay it.*

"Okay," he said. "Yes. Let's go to the sea."



It took the household four hours to get ready to leave. Marin said this was a record.

Fern made a list. Momo packed enough food to feed an army and then packed the second-best pot as well. Senku packed instruments for measuring the tide, then unpacked them when Fern looked at him, then packed one, small, when she was not looking. Maomao packed a medical kit and a jar for collecting seaweed, in case there was a useful one. Seiko packed a folding chair and an umbrella and announced she would not be moving from either. Turbo Granny announced that she would be racing anybody who was foolish enough to race her, and Okarun went pale.

Mikasa went to find Eren.

He was in his small room, reading something he did not seem to be enjoying.

"We're going to the sea," said Mikasa. "All of us. Two days."

Eren looked up.

"I just came from the sea."

"You came from the sea alone," said Mikasa. "Going with people is different." She paused in the doorway. "You don't have to come."

Eren looked at her for a long moment.

Then he closed the book.

"Who's driving the carts?"

"Rimuru."

"Then I'll take the reins," said Eren, standing. "I've seen how he drives."

Down the corridor, Rimuru shouted that he had heard that. Eren, for the first time since he had arrived, almost smiled.



They left a little before noon. Four carts, a pot that rattled, and everyone.

As the first cart turned onto the coast road, a deer stepped out of the trees ahead of them. It stood in the middle of the road and looked at them.

Everyone in the lead cart went silent.

The deer looked at Mau.

Mau looked at the deer.

"...It followed us," said Mau.

"*Don't*," said Yuta.

The deer bounded off into the trees. The cart went on toward the sea. All the way there Mau would not stop talking about it, and Frieren, beside him, with her book open on her knees, did not turn a single page.
