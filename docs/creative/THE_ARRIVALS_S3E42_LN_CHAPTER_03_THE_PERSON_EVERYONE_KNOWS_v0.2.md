# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 20 — The Person Everyone Knows

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 20  
**Continuity state:** Council exists | Relational gravity is acknowledged without converting it into ownership

The first outside delegation asked for the leader.

Fern answered:

"We don't have one."

The delegation asked again.

Slower.

"As in, who speaks for you?"

Fern:

"On what?"

That broke the question.

Trade?

Water access?

Road security?

Arrival status?

Medical?

Defense?

The delegation had come to discuss a road inspection agreement.

So Fern sent for:
the Searcher route coordinator;
a native road partner;
Anko for records;
and Mau.

Mau stared when summoned.

"Why me?"

Fern looked at him.

"Because they asked who can explain how decisions cross groups."

"Rimuru."

"Also invited."

"Then why me?"

Fern waited.

Mau already knew.

He just disliked the answer.

People told him things.

Not everything.

Not always first.

But across more circles than almost anyone else.

Bocchi would tell him something she would never announce in council.

Senku would argue with him without needing social translation.

Momo would tell him when a rule was hurting ordinary life.

Maomao would tell him when everyone else was being medically stupid.

Mikasa would tell him when safety language was hiding fear.

Coco would drag him into Witch Hat problems.

Yuta would tell him when Mau himself was being an idiot.

Rimuru did much of the same from a different angle.

The delegation arrived expecting hierarchy.

They got six chairs.

This disappointed them.

Good.

The lead official looked at Mau first anyway.

"Are you the founder?"

Mau:

"No."

Fern:

"One of the earliest residents."

The official looked at Rimuru.

"Then you?"

Rimuru:

"No."

"Who authorized the settlement?"

Silence.

That question assumed an answer the world had never provided.

The old inn had not been authorized.

People had survived there.

Then more arrived.

Then they had needed water.

Then rooms.

Then roads.

Then somewhere safer.

The settlement had grown from necessity and choice, not charter.

Anko said:

"No one person did."

The official's eyes returned to Mau.

"Yet everyone here knows you."

Mau almost denied.

That would have been false modesty.

He looked at the people around the table.

"Most do."

"And they listen to you."

"Sometimes."

Fern:

"Too often."

Mau looked betrayed.

Rimuru laughed.

The official did not.

"If you told them to close the road?"

Mau answered carefully.

"Some would ask why. Some would ignore me. Some would tell me I'm being stupid. The people who actually have route authority would need evidence."

The official frowned.

"You have influence."

"Yes."

There.

No denial.

Important.

Mau had spent months learning that the answer to unwanted responsibility was not pretending he had no effect on people.

He did.

Frieren loved him.

Bocchi trusted him.

Umaru followed him into nonsense.

Coco pulled him into hers.

People asked his opinion because years of shared life had made it matter.

That was real.

It was not government.

The official asked:

"Then what are you?"

Mau looked at Fern.

Fern did not rescue him.

Good.

Mau said:

"A person a lot of people here know."

It sounded stupid.

It was also the cleanest answer.

Rimuru added:

"And one of several people who can translate between groups."

Better.

The road discussion began.

It was boring.

Excellent.

Inspection windows.

Neutral witnesses.

What records could be viewed.

No resident ability registry.

No surprise detention.

Shared bridge responsibility.

At one point the official asked Mau whether a condition was acceptable.

Mau looked at the Searcher coordinator.

"Not mine to answer."

The coordinator answered.

At another point they asked about refugee intake.

Mau looked at Fern.

Fern answered.

Then about defensive response.

Mikasa had sent written limits.

Read those.

By the end, the delegation was annoyed.

Not because nobody represented the settlement.

Because representation kept changing with the subject.

Before leaving, the official said:

"This would be simpler with one person."

Anko:

"For you."

Exactly.

Afterward, the council discussed external liaisons.

Not rulers.

Liaisons.

Mau was an obvious candidate.

So was Rimuru.

So was Fern.

Anko for evidence-heavy work.
Jinshi for administrative negotiation.
Native partners when native relations were involved.
Mikasa when defense terms required someone who actually understood them.

Momo:

"So Mau is the face sometimes."

Mau hated the phrase.

Frieren, from the wall:

"Yes."

He turned.

"You too?"

"You are."

"That's not helpful."

"It isn't supposed to be."

She closed her book.

"People knowing you is not the problem."

Mau waited.

"People deciding that means they own your answer is."

There.

Mau looked at the council table.

He had been trying so hard not to become a leader that sometimes he treated his own relational centrality like a flaw to hide.

It wasn't.

The family had chosen closeness.

Pretending otherwise would insult that choice.

The safeguard was not:
Mau must become peripheral.

The safeguard was:
closeness is not command.

The council adopted a simple external rule.

REPRESENTATIVES ARE ISSUE-SPECIFIC.

NO REPRESENTATIVE OWNS THE PEOPLE THEY SPEAK WITH OR FOR.

WHEN PRACTICAL, SEND MORE THAN ONE.

Mau read it.

"Can we add 'please stop asking for the king'?"

Fern:

"No."

Momo:

"Coward."

They did not.

The next delegation still asked for the leader.

This time Mau answered:

"Wrong question."

Then helped them find the right people.
