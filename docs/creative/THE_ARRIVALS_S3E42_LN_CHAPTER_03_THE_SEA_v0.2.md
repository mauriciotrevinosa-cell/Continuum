# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 22 — The Sea

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 22  
**Continuity state:** Family trip, day 1 | Milim versus the waves | Yuta abducted by the sisters | Rimuru finally drifts | Eren at the water: 'More people. Who probably like soup.'

They came over the last hill in the late afternoon, and there it was.

The sea.

Grey-green and enormous, rolling in under a pale sky, breaking white on a long curved beach of pale sand with dark rocks at either end. The wind came off it smelling of salt and distance. Every single person in the four carts went quiet at once.

Then Milim screamed with joy, leapt off the lead cart while it was still moving, and ran straight into the water fully clothed.

The spell broke.

Everyone ran.



It was chaos for an hour, and it was the best kind of chaos, the kind with no purpose at all.

Umaru and Suika started a sandcastle that within thirty minutes became a fortress, complete with a moat, two towers and a flag made of Umaru's hood. Umaru declared it a sovereign nation and a monument. Coco added decorative turrets. Agott added structural supports nobody had asked for, and then got so invested in the drainage that she stayed with it until dark.

Milim fought the waves.

There was no other word for it. She stood waist-deep in the surf and met each wave as it came in, fists up, and every time it knocked her over she came up laughing and challenged the next one. After half an hour, the sea was clearly winning on points and Milim had never been happier.

Senku, who had sworn to Fern that he had not brought any instruments, was found at the waterline with a stick and a piece of string, measuring the tide. He said he was not measuring the tide. He was observing it. It was different. Suika abandoned the fortress to come and help him observe it, and the two of them spent an hour arguing about where the high-water mark would be. Neither of them would admit they were enjoying it.

Turbo Granny raced Okarun along the length of the beach and won by an embarrassing margin. Then she raced him back and won by more. Then she challenged Mikasa, who considered it, took off her boots, and lost by only a little. That made Turbo Granny so delighted that she insulted Mikasa respectfully for the rest of the day.

Seiko set up her folding chair and her umbrella exactly where she had said she would, and did not move. She was occupied all afternoon by a rotating series of people who came to sit on the sand beside her and be told, frankly, what was wrong with them.

Maomao waded out to the rocks at the north end with her jar, came back an hour later with three kinds of seaweed and a small crab that had attached itself to her sleeve, and refused to discuss the crab.

Bocchi sat at the very edge of the water with her shoes off and her feet in the foam, very still, while Kita sat beside her with her head on Bocchi's shoulder. Neither of them talked. Once, Bocchi took out a little notebook and wrote something in it, and Kita did not ask what. She just watched Bocchi's face while she wrote, and smiled.

Kaneki sat on a rock a little apart from everyone, with a book he did not open. After a while Rem came and sat on the next rock. Neither of them said anything either. They stayed there for a long time, watching the household make fools of itself in the surf, and once, when Milim got knocked flat by a particularly large wave and came up spitting seaweed, they both laughed at exactly the same moment, and looked at each other, startled, and laughed again.

And Rimuru, at last, got his quiet day.

He turned into a slime at the waterline and let the tide take him. He drifted out a little way into the calm water past the breakers and lay there, a small blue dome rising and falling on the swell, with the sun on him and nothing to do and nobody, for the first time in weeks, bringing him a single letter.

Paying, he thought.

*Paid*, said Ciel, a long time later, grudgingly. A pause. *Partly.*



Yuta had been abducted by the sisters within ten minutes of arrival and did not get free until sunset.

He was buried in sand up to his neck, at Umaru's orders, as a punishment for abandonment. He was then appointed royal engineer of the fortress. He was then deposed in a coup led by Suika, and reinstated after the coup collapsed when Milim joined the wrong side. He was made to judge a shell competition with no rules, and a race between two crabs. He was asked to explain, in detail, why he had abandoned them, and answered with complete honesty that he had been catching up on a whole lifetime with his wife. The sisters found this romantic enough to make Coco cry, and boring enough that they sent him to fetch more water for the moat.

Maki watched all of it from a towel higher up the beach, with an expression of deep, private satisfaction.

When he finally escaped, at sunset, sandy to the ears, and sat down on the towel beside her, she handed him a cup of something warm without looking at him.

"Good brother," said Maki.

"I hate you," said Yuta, and leaned his head against her shoulder, and did not move for an hour.



Eren stood at the water's edge for a long time.

He did not go in. He stood just where the foam ran up the sand and then slid back, so that it covered his boots and left them again, over and over.

Mikasa came and stood beside him.

They had stood at the sea together before. Mikasa remembered. Eren remembered too. It had been a different sea, in a different world, and he had looked out at it and asked a question about freedom and what was on the other side, and the answer to that question had eventually cost everything.

He did not ask it now.

Behind them, the household was shouting. Milim had been knocked flat again. Umaru was declaring war on the tide. Somebody was singing badly, and somebody else was laughing at the singing, and the smell of Momo's cooking was coming down the beach from the fire.

"What's on the other side?" said Mikasa quietly, because she knew he was thinking it.

Eren looked out at the grey horizon for a long time.

"I don't know," he said. "More people, probably."

He paused.

"Who probably like soup," he added.

Mikasa turned and looked at him.

Eren was not smiling. Not quite. But something in his face had come loose that Mikasa had not seen loose since they were very small, on a hill, under a tree, a very long time ago.

She did not say anything.

She stood beside him at the edge of the water until Momo shouted that dinner was ready, and then they walked back up the beach together toward the fire. On the way, Milim came running past, soaking wet, grabbed both of their hands, and dragged them the rest of the way at a sprint.
