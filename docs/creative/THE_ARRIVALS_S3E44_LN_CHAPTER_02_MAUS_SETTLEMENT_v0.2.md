# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 22 — Mau's Settlement

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 22

A trader called it:

"Mau's settlement."

Mau corrected automatically.

"No."

The trader laughed.

"Whatever you call it."

"We don't."

"Exactly."

The trader gestured around.

Arrival House.

Water court.

Workshops.

Refuge bunks.

Defense markers.

People carrying things without asking Mau anything.

"Then everyone else names it for you."

That annoyed Mau more because it was true.

Outside names had accumulated.

Mau's settlement.

Otherworlder city.

Refuge.

Fort.

Monster town.

Rimuru's place.

Witch city.

Each description selected a different fear.

The naming meeting happened that afternoon.

Again.

Momo proposed:
Continuopolis.

Rejected.

Milim proposed:
Milim City.

Rejected faster.

Diablo proposed something honoring Rimuru.

Rimuru ended that branch personally.

Second-community observer said:

"Do not choose a name that sounds like you're claiming us."

Good.

Native partner:

"Do not choose one that sounds like you own the road."

Also good.

Senku:

"Can we use geographic descriptor until something sticks?"

Fern:

"Which geography?"

Silence.

Continuum hated easy naming.

No answer.

Meeting adjourned.

Then the political consequence arrived.

A regional notice used:

MAU-CONTROLLED SETTLEMENT.

Not:
Mau's settlement colloquially.

Controlled.

Anko placed document on table.

Mau stared.

"That's false."

"Yes."

"Can we correct it?"

"Yes."

"Will they care?"

"Unknown."

Worse.

The notice justified caution around "followers."

Bocchi read.

Looked genuinely offended.

"I'm not his follower."

Mau:

"Thank you?"

Bocchi:

"That's not—"

Kita laughed.

Rimuru read next.

The naming problem had become political classification.

If outside institutions saw one founder/leader:
they could negotiate with one person.
threaten one person.
blame one person.
demand one person.

Convenient.

Dangerous.

Mau finally understood why Anko kept refusing shorthand.

A trader saying "Mau's place" was harmless enough.

A government writing "Mau-controlled" changed legal logic.

Council issued correction:
no sole ruler.
no ownership by Mau.
function-based coordination.
independent second community not subordinate.
residents not "followers."

Momo:

"Should we include that Mau barely controls his laundry?"

Frieren:

"He doesn't."

Mau:

"Why are you here?"

Humor.

Then later, not humor.

Near evening Mau crossed plaza.

Two newer residents talking.

"If something goes wrong there, Mau handles it."

He slowed.

Not eavesdropping.

Then:

"That's why he's here, right?"

Mau kept walking.

Frieren had heard too.

At dinner he joked.

"Apparently job description expanded."

Frieren did not laugh.

Mau looked.

"What?"

"You heard what they said."

"So?"

"So you made it funny."

"They were wrong."

"And now they don't have to feel wrong."

Mau frowned.

"They were embarrassed."

"Good."

He stared.

Frieren:

"Embarrassment teaches."

Harsh.

Maybe.

Mau looked toward plaza.

The naming problem had shifted.

If the place carried his name long enough, every threat to settlement could become:
Mau's responsibility.

And every threat seeking Mau could become:
settlement's problem because of him.

Names made expectations.

Expectations made roles.

Roles could become sacrifice before anyone voted.

Mau hated the thought.

Then someone called from water court:

"Mau, can you help?"

He turned automatically.

Frieren watched.

Not every request was wrong.

That was what made boundary difficult.
