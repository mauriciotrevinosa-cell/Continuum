# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 23 — Give Them One Name

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 23  
**Continuity state:** Anti-Otherworlder classification grows more deliberate | No mastermind identity established

Anko found the form inside a packet that was supposed to be boring.

That was becoming a pattern.

The packet came from a town that had agreed to share the paperwork behind its new capability restrictions.

No raid.

No theft.

A clerk there disliked the policy and copied what he could before the office locked the rest.

Most pages were ordinary.

Inventory.
Road access.
Incident categories.
Definitions copied from somewhere else.

Then:

ORGANIZATIONAL ASSESSMENT.

Anko read it twice.

Called Senku.

Then Fern.

Then Mau.

Mau arrived last because he had been helping move lumber.

Anko pointed.

The form assumed every Otherworlder concentration had:

PRIMARY AUTHORITY  
SECONDARY COMMAND  
COMBAT ARM  
RECRUITMENT FUNCTION  
LOGISTICS ARM  
DEPENDENT POPULATION

Mau stared.

"That's not us."

Anko:

"I know."

Rimuru arrived.

Read.

"That's not most communities."

"Probably."

Second page:

> If no formal authority is declared, identify the individual with greatest cross-group influence and public recognition.

Silence.

Mau felt the room change.

Third line:

> Treat repeated denial of centralized authority as an unverified claim until command relationships are assessed.

Momo, reading over Anko's shoulder:

"So if we say there isn't a king, that proves the king is hiding."

Anko:

"Structurally, yes."

"That's bullshit."

"Yes."

Senku read the footer.

No author.

Only a distribution code.

Same family as codes on three earlier packets.

Not identical.

Related enough to matter.

He marked it.

Mau looked at the first field again.

PRIMARY AUTHORITY.

His name had been written there by hand.

Below it:

RIMURU — SECONDARY / SYSTEM SUPPORT.

FRIEREN — PERSONAL ENFORCEMENT / CLOSE PROTECTION.

Mau stopped breathing for half a second.

Frieren entered behind him.

Saw the page.

Her face did not change.

That was worse.

Fern read the next section.

Bocchi and Kessoku were listed under:
CULTURAL COHESION.

Witch Hat under:
MAGICAL TECHNICAL ARM.

Searchers:
RECRUITMENT / FIELD COLLECTION.

Arrival House:
INTAKE / RETENTION.

Momo:

"They turned the house into a military chart."

Anko:

"Yes."

Not because the writer had invented every observation.

That was the dangerous part.

Searchers did go out and find people.

Arrival House did receive them.

Witch Hat did maintain magic.

Bocchi did play music everyone knew.

Mau did connect groups.

Real facts.

Wrong frame.

Senku tapped the paper.

"This is a model forcing every input into command structure."

"Could be bureaucratic habit," Anko said.

"Could."

"Could be deliberate."

"Could."

No leap.

Diablo read the handwritten notes.

His smile disappeared.

"Whoever wrote this has not met Lord Rimuru."

Rimuru:

"That's your problem with it?"

"It is one of several."

Good enough.

Mau looked at Frieren's line.

PERSONAL ENFORCEMENT.

"That's disgusting."

Frieren:

"Yes."

Not because she rejected protecting him.

Because someone had turned love into assigned function.

Maki arrived with Yuta.

Read the page.

Found herself under:
COMBAT ASSOCIATES.

She laughed once.

No humor.

"They really can't imagine people just... liking each other."

That sentence stayed.

Anko copied it onto a separate note.

Not evidence.

Interpretive warning.

The hostile model needed the family to be a command network.

Because command networks had:
leaders;
chains;
assets;
recruiters;
dependents.

All things governments knew how to fear.

A family where a vampire slept through meetings, a hero washed dishes, a slime became a pillow, a musician refused eye contact, a genius got kidnapped into dinner, and half the residents would tell Mau to shut up was harder to categorize.

So the form categorized harder.

Mau asked:

"Can we prove this packet is behind the regional language?"

"No."

"Can we prove the same distributor touched the others?"

"Not yet."

"Can we prove whoever made it wants me treated as leader?"

Anko pointed at the line.

"We can prove this form tells assessors to pick somebody even when no formal leader exists."

That was enough.

Not identity.

Intent at the document level.

The council published the form.

Redacted names of the cooperating clerk.

Attached correction.

Arrival House is not retention.
Searchers are not recruitment.
Family ties are not command roles.
Issue-specific representatives are not rulers.

The hostile version spread faster.

Of course.

By the next week, another notice referred to:

> the Mau-aligned network.

Mau read it.

Then:

"They upgraded me from settlement to network."

Momo:

"Congratulations."

Frieren did not laugh.

Mau didn't either.

Anko pinned the notice beside the form.

The enemy—if there was one enemy—had not only learned how to describe them as dangerous.

It had learned how to turn the fact that people loved one another into evidence of organization.

That was a much better weapon.

And much harder to break with a sword.
