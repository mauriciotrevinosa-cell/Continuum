# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 36 — Who Speaks for Us

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 36  
**Continuity state:** Rings One and Two in council, night | The world already wrote 'Mau (?)' | Two faces, Mau and Rimuru; the council stays | Roles emerge

Fern called the meeting three days after the second copy of the document arrived.

She did not call it a meeting. She did not call it anything. She went door to door through the family wing after dinner, the way Frieren had once gone door to door for a very different reason, and said the same sentence at every door.

"Common room. After the children are asleep. Everyone."

Then, at the end of the corridor, she stopped at the door of the small room the house had given Eren, which was not in the family wing and not in Arrival House either, and knocked.

He opened it.

"Common room," said Fern. "After the children are asleep."

Eren looked at her.

"I'm not—"

"Mikasa asked for you," said Fern. "And so did I."

He came.



By ten the common room was full in the way it had not been full since the storm.

Not the house. The house was always full. This was something narrower. Everyone who had a room in the family wing of the Muslin, or would have had one if they had asked. Everyone who had come in through someone already here and stayed until they were no longer a guest. Everyone who had been at the old inn the night Mau disappeared the first time, and everyone who had come since, up to Rem and Kaneki and Aira, who had arrived out of the same terrible week.

And Eren, by the window, with his arms folded, because Mikasa had asked.

Diablo stood outside the door. Nobody had asked him to. He had simply taken up position, folded his hands behind his back, and smiled at the corridor. Two travelers on their way to the washing court had turned around without being told.

Coco's glyph glowed on the ceiling. Nobody's shadow fell very far.

Fern stood at the board on the wall, where Wakana's hooks had once been, with a piece of chalk.

She wrote four lines.

WHO SPEAKS FOR US.
WHAT ARE WE BECOMING.
WHO GETS TOLD THINGS.
MAU'S SHADOW.

Then she turned around.

"Nobody leaves," said Fern, "until we've talked about all four."

Marin, who usually said that sort of thing, looked deeply impressed.



Mau had tried to sit at the back.

He had a good spot picked out, on the floor by the shelf, where he could see everybody and nobody could see him very well. He had made it about halfway there when Frieren took him by the sleeve without looking up from the cushion she was choosing, steered him into the middle of the couch, and sat down beside him.

"I was going to sit—"

"I know," said Frieren.

On his other side, after a moment, Rimuru sat down too, in person form, with his elbows on his knees.

Yuta, across from them on the bench under the window with Maki, looked at the three of them. Something in his face eased. He did not say anything.



"The first one," said Fern, tapping the board. "Who speaks for us."

She put the chalk down.

"Three delegations this month have asked for our leader. I told all three we don't have one. All three left thinking we were lying." She picked up a sheet of paper from the table. It was a copy of the document Anko had pinned to the evidence wall: the form from the western authority, with its boxes and its standardized phrase. "This form has a box for the leader of an Otherworlder concentration. If the assessor can't find one, the form tells them to write in whoever the community seems to orbit."

She held it up. Everyone could see what had been written in the box on the copy that had come back from the river towns.

*Mau (?)*

The room was quiet.

"So," said Fern. "We can keep saying we don't have a leader. Or we can choose who speaks for us. If we don't, the world will choose, and it already has. It just used a question mark."



Everyone had something to say.

That was the point. Fern had known it would be, and she let them.

"A representative isn't a ruler," said Senku, from the long table, where he had brought a notebook out of habit. "It's a function. Someone has to be the address the letters go to. That's all. Don't make it a crown and it won't become one."

"It always becomes one," said Jinshi, quietly, from beside Maomao. Everyone turned to look at him. He had spent his whole life in a palace where a face was a target and a shield at the same time, and he did not often talk about it. "Not because the person wants it to. Because everyone else needs it to. Whoever you choose, they will be stood in front of every arrow. That is what a face is for."

"Then don't choose one," said Eren, from the window.

The room turned to him.

He did not unfold his arms.

"If you pick a face," said Eren, "they'll aim at the face. I've seen what happens when a whole people puts one person at the front. It doesn't end with the person. It ends with everyone behind them doing terrible things in that person's name."

Mikasa, beside him, did not say anything. But she did not look away from him either.

"Then we choose two," said Yuta.

Everyone looked at him.

"Two faces," said Yuta. "Never one." He was looking at the couch, at Mau and Rimuru side by side. "In my world, there was a man everyone called the strongest, and everyone let him stand in front alone. I'm not going to let that happen twice. If we do this, it's two people, and they stand next to each other, and the rest of us stand behind both of them. And if either one starts carrying something he shouldn't, the rest of us say so."

"You're stealing the roof rule," said Rimuru.

"I'm borrowing it," said Yuta.



Milim put her hand up.

"I'll do it," said Milim. "I'll be the face. I have a great face."

"No," said about fifteen people at once.

Milim looked wounded.

"Why not?"

"Because the last delegation you met," said Fern, "you challenged to arm-wrestling."

"He accepted!"

"He's still in a sling."

Milim considered this, then folded her arms and sulked magnificently for almost a minute. Then Umaru whispered something to her, and she brightened.

"Can I be the Head of Fun?"

Fern looked at her for a long time.

Then she picked up the chalk, wrote HEAD OF FUN: MILIM on the very bottom corner of the board, very small, and drew a box around it.

Milim was so happy she had to go and stand by the window for a while.



"It should be Mau," said Umaru.

She said it from the floor, cross-legged, in her hamster hood, with the absolute certainty she brought to games and nothing else.

"No," said Mau.

"It should be Mau," said Umaru again, "because he can't say no to anybody. And a person who can't say no to anybody is exactly who you want answering letters. Because he'll read all of them."

"That's the worst reason I've ever heard," said Mau.

"It's a great reason," said Seiko, from her armchair by the fire. "The boy listens. People can tell. That's half of governing. The other half is not being an idiot, and for that you have Rimuru."

"Thank you, Seiko-san," said Rimuru.

"I wasn't complimenting you. I was assigning you."

The room laughed.

Mau did not.

He was sitting very still between Frieren and Rimuru, looking at the copy of the form on the table. *Mau (?)*.

"I don't want it," he said.

The room went quiet.

"I know," said Fern.

"I'm not good at it. I don't know how cities work. I don't know how anything works. I wake up some days and have to check whether things happened. I can't be the person people write to."

"You already are," said Anko.

She had been taking notes the whole time, in the code nobody else could read, and she did not look up from them now.

"Twenty-two letters this month have your name on them," said Anko. "I counted. Some from refuge families. Some from the second community. Some from the river towns. Three from people who just wanted to know if you were real." She turned a page. "You're not being asked whether you want to be the person people write to. You're being asked whether you want to be the one who writes back, or the one they keep writing about."



Mau did not say anything for a long time.

Then he looked at Rimuru.

Rimuru had not said anything through all of it. He was sitting with his elbows on his knees, looking at the floor, with the face of someone who had sat through this exact meeting before, in another world, in another room, about another set of people.

"You've done this," said Mau.

"Yeah," said Rimuru.

"Was it worth it?"

Rimuru took a long time to answer.

"Some days," he said. "Not all of them. Most of them." He looked up. "I didn't choose it, either. The first time. A goblin village asked me to name them, and I did, and then I was responsible for every single one of them for the rest of my life. Nobody asked me. It just happened while I was being kind." He paused. "This time, at least, we get to choose it."

"Together," said Mau.

"Together," said Rimuru. "Or not at all. Yuta's right. It's two or nothing."

Mau looked around the room.

At Fern at the board. At Anko with her notes, and Senku with his notebook, and Jinshi, and Eren by the window. At Seiko in her armchair, and Umaru on the floor. At Yuta on the bench with Maki. At all of them, every person in the room who had come in through somebody else and stayed.

At Frieren, beside him, who had not said a single word.

"Frieren?"

Frieren did not look at him. She was looking at the board.

"You'll hate it," she said.

"Yes."

"Some days you'll hate it so much you'll want to walk out into the rain and not come back."

"Probably."

"You won't," said Frieren. "Because I'll be there. And when it starts to eat you, I'll stop it." She turned her head at last and looked at him. "Choose it if you want it. Not because they wrote your name in a box. If you choose it, I'm with you. If you don't, I'm with you."

Mau looked at her for a long moment.

Then he looked at the form.

*Mau (?)*.

"Okay," he said. "Both of us. Rimuru and me. We speak for everyone. We don't decide for everyone. And the council stays."

"The council stays," said Fern, and wrote it on the board, and underlined it.

"And if either one of us starts carrying something he shouldn't—"

"We say so," said about twenty people at once.

Mau laughed despite himself, and the room laughed with him.

Fern picked up the copy of the form, crossed out the question mark in the box, and in the space beside it, in her neat ledger hand, wrote:

*Mau. Rimuru.*



After that, the rest came quickly.

It was not that anyone decided it, exactly. It was that once two people had agreed to stand in front, everyone else in the room realized at the same moment that they were already standing somewhere. They simply said where.

Wakana, quietly, from beside Marin: the workshop. Cloth, repair, building, anything that needed hands and patience.

Mikasa: the Searchers, the scouts, the routes, the drills. She said it as if it had never been in question, and nobody questioned it.

Maomao: medicine. Kusuri beside her: anything that bubbled, smoked, or needed to be kept away from food.

Senku: water, drainage, engineering, the bridge, anything that had to be measured before it was built. "And anything that has to be measured after it's built," he added, "because people lie."

Qifrey: the atelier, the ink, the circles, the safety of anything magical. Coco and Agott beside him, and Tetia, and Richeh, who said nothing and did not leave.

Fern: the ledger. The administration. The board. Everything that had to be remembered and nobody else wanted to remember.

Anko: records, and the evidence room, and anything that needed investigating. Jinshi beside her: the registry, the protocol, the forms, and knowing what other cities meant when they said something politely.

Momo: food, and the stores, and anyone who forgot to eat.

Seiko: anything that was not quite alive, or not quite dead, or not quite either.

Rem: the old inn, and the travelers who came to it.

Yuta: training. Defense. "And the roof," he said, looking at Mau and Rimuru. "Once a week. That's my job too."

Marin, last, stood up and announced that someone had to be in charge of making sure all these people stopped working occasionally. Fern looked at her for a long moment, sighed, and wrote MINISTER OF NOT WORKING: MARIN beside Milim's box in the bottom corner.

Marin sat down glowing.

Frieren, when they got to her, said she would do whatever she wanted, which was what she had always done. Fern wrote FRIEREN: WHATEVER SHE WANTS on the board without hesitating. It was the most accurate thing anyone wrote all night.



It was nearly midnight when Fern tapped the first line on the board and drew a line through it.

WHO SPEAKS FOR US.

Mau looked at the line.

It was a very small line. A chalk mark. It did not feel small.

He thought about letters with his name on them, and forms with boxes, and a question mark crossed out. He thought about a man in Yuta's world who had stood in front alone. He thought about a boy in a clearing who had not known his own name, and who had just agreed, in a room full of people who loved him, to let the whole world learn it.

Under the table, Frieren's hand found his and held on.

On his other side, Rimuru bumped his shoulder very gently against Mau's.

"Two," said Rimuru quietly.

"Two," said Mau.

Fern tapped the second line on the board.

WHAT ARE WE BECOMING.

Nobody, it turned out, was going to bed for a long time.
