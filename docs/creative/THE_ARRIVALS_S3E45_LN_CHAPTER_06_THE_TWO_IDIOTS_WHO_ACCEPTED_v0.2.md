# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 17
## Chapter 39 — The Two Idiots Who Accepted

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 17  
**Reading order:** 39  
**Continuity state:** Four days after the council | the sisters' ceremony in the fort | Mau and Rimuru named 'the two idiots who accepted' (no royal word used) | the first letter to both

It took the sisters four days.

Four days was a long time for Umaru to keep a secret. It was the longest she had ever kept one that was not about snacks. But the council had ended at three in the morning with two names in a box on the common-room board. By the time Umaru woke up at noon, she had already decided three things: something had to be done about it, it had to be done properly, and it could not be done by grown-ups.

Grown-ups, in Umaru's experience, had already ruined it. They had made it a meeting.

"It needs a ceremony," she said.

She said it inside the fort. The fort from the war still stood in the corner of the common room, because nobody had ever had the heart to take it down: cushions, crates, a folding screen, and the toll booth with its sign still on it. Over the weeks it had become a monument, then a meeting place, and now the seat of government for an organization that did not officially exist. It had four founding members, and in practice as many as the founding members could drag inside.

Coco was there, with her hat off because the roof was low. Suika was there, lying on her stomach with her chin on her fists. Bocchi was there, mostly. Technically she was in the fort, but she had wedged herself into the farthest corner behind the toll booth, which was as close to not being in a meeting as it was possible to be while still being in one.

Ori was there, because Ori was always there now, sitting against Umaru's side. Tetia and Richeh were there because Coco had brought them. Agott was there because she had come to complain about Coco bringing them and had never left. Kita was there because Bocchi was, Nijika because Kita was, and Ryo because Nijika had mentioned snacks.

"A ceremony for what?" said Suika.

"For Mau and Rimuru," said Umaru. "For saying yes."

Coco frowned. "They didn't want to say yes. Mau said he didn't want it. Out loud. In front of everyone."

"That's why," said Umaru. "Nobody makes a ceremony when someone does something they wanted to do anyway. You make one when they do something hard."

Coco thought about it.

"Like an apprentice's first circle," she said slowly. "Qifrey says the first circle doesn't matter because it's good. It matters because you drew it even though you were scared you'd draw it wrong."

"Exactly like that," said Umaru. She had no idea what an apprentice's first circle was, but she recognized a supporter when she heard one.



The problem was Senku.

Not Senku personally. Senku had said one sentence at the council, from the long table, with his notebook open out of habit: *Don't make it a crown and it won't become one.* Umaru had been on the floor in her hood, and she had heard it. She had understood at once that he was right, and that he had ruined all of her first ideas.

She had a list anyway. She read it out for the record and crossed off each item as she went.

"No crowns." Cross. "No thrones." Cross. "Not even a chair that's taller than the other chairs." Cross. "No bowing. No *your* anything. No capes." She paused over the last one with real grief. "No capes."

"Not even small capes?" said Suika.

"Capes are how it starts," said Umaru darkly. She had read a great deal of manga. She knew.

Coco raised her hand. "What about a hat?"

Everyone looked at Coco's hat, which lay beside her on the cushion, tall and pointed and important.

"A hat," said Umaru, "is a crown that went to school."

Coco considered this, and put her hat behind her back.

From behind the toll booth, very quietly, Bocchi said something.

"What?"

"...what do we... call them?" said Bocchi. "If it's not... any of those."

Nobody said anything. It was a very good question.

They tried. They tried for most of an afternoon.

*Leader* was what the form said. Anko had pinned the form on the evidence wall, and nobody wanted anything that came off that wall.

*Representative* was Senku's word. Suika said it sounded like a job at a post office.

*President* was Tetia's, and Agott vetoed it before Tetia had finished the word. *Captain* was Nijika's. Ryo, without opening her eyes, suggested *Sponsor*, and was ignored.

*Face* was Yuta's word. Suika said it made it sound like they were going to get punched in it. Coco said quietly that that had sort of been Yuta's point, and then everybody felt bad for a while.

*Big brother* was Coco's. "He already is that," said Umaru. "That's not a title. That's a fact."

"Rimuru isn't our big brother," said Suika.

"Rimuru's everybody's big brother," said Umaru. "He just doesn't know it yet."

Then she remembered Seiko.

Seiko in her armchair, at the council, assigning Rimuru: *The boy listens. That's half of governing. The other half is not being an idiot.* And Anko with her twenty-two letters. And Rimuru saying *some days, not all of them*, about a city he had already lost people in. And Mau saying *I don't want it*, then saying yes anyway, with everyone watching, while Frieren told him he would hate it.

"Who says yes to that?" said Umaru slowly. "Knowing all of it. The letters, and the forms, and the people who think he's a monster. Rimuru's already done it once, and he said it was only worth it some days. And they both said yes anyway."

"Idiots," said Suika, with total confidence.

Umaru looked at her.

Then she began to smile, very slowly. It was the smile she got when she saw the winning move three turns ahead.

"Two idiots," said Umaru. "Who accepted."

Coco gasped. Behind the toll booth, Bocchi made a sound that might have been a laugh. Suika rolled onto her back and kicked her feet in the air.

It was not a vote. It did not need to be.



A ceremony needed staff.

Over the next three days the fort recruited, one person at a time, in the strictest secrecy. By the second evening, that meant everyone in the house knew except Mau and Rimuru.

Milim was first. Milim was the Head of Fun, and this, she said with both fists clenched, was her first official act. She had been waiting four whole days for something to be head of. She wanted fireworks. She was given confetti. She accepted the confetti the way a general accepts a sword.

Marin was second, because she was the Minister of Not Working, and somebody had to declare the evening a holiday so that nobody had an excuse. She drew a seal on a sheet of paper and pinned it to the board under the rings:

*BY ORDER OF THE MINISTRY: THE EVENING OF THE FOURTH DAY IS NOT FOR WORK. VIOLATORS WILL BE HUGGED.*

Wakana was third, because there had to be something to wear, and it could not be a crown, or a cape, or a hat. He listened to the entire list of things it could not be, nodded, and said, "Sashes."

"Is a sash a crown?" said Umaru suspiciously.

"A sash is a strip of cloth that tells people what you are," said Wakana. "That's all it is."

He made them in one evening, out of the offcuts from the refuge curtains, and hemmed them so neatly that Marin cried a little.

Coco did the lettering. She did not use glyph ink; that was rationed, and Qifrey would have fainted. She used ordinary black ink from Fern's ledger supply. Fern handed it over with the expression of someone who strongly suspected what it was for and had decided not to know.

Momo did the food. Nobody had to ask.

Hakari's group was not recruited so much as kidnapped. Suika walked into their room, said "Fort," and walked out again, and all five of them followed her. Nano timed the ceremony to the second. Shizuka wrote out the order of ceremony in her careful hand, so that Umaru would have something to read from. Karane complained that nobody had asked her opinion, then gave it at length, and most of it was used. Hakari volunteered for everything, and Kusuri made the confetti fall slower than confetti should, and would not say how.

Anko, who could find out anything that happened in the settlement, was put in charge of making sure Mau found out nothing. She took the job very seriously and enjoyed it more than she admitted. She brought Nazuna into the secret too, on the second evening, and Nazuna was awake for the whole ceremony, which was new.

Seiko said yes before they finished asking. A ceremony needed someone who had done ceremonies, she said, and she had conducted more of them than anyone alive. Most had been for things that were dead, so she was long overdue for one with living idiots.

They went to Senku last, because Umaru was afraid of him.

Senku took the sashes and inspected them, front and back. He measured them. He held one up to the light.

"Cloth," he said. "No metal. No height advantage. Worn across the body, not on the head." He handed them back. "Ten billion percent not a crown. Approved."

He went back to his work. Then, without turning around: "Spelling's right, too."

Bocchi was given the fanfare. She did not volunteer for it. Umaru told her, very gently, that there could not be a ceremony without music, and that Bocchi was the only person in any world Umaru trusted with it. Bocchi went pale. Then she went away to the music room for three days and composed something alone that nobody was allowed to hear.

Kita found out about the fanfare from Bocchi. That was how everyone learned Bocchi was actually doing it.



On the evening of the fourth day, Rimuru came out of his office and found Diablo waiting outside it.

"Rimuru-sama," said Diablo. "Your presence is required in the common room."

"By who?"

Diablo's face did something complicated.

"I have been asked not to say," he said. "I have also been asked," he added, with visible pain, "to tell you that you are not in trouble. I was not told whether this is true."

Mau was fetched from the washing court by Ori. She took him by the hand without explaining anything and did not let go all the way across the plaza.

They arrived at the same moment, from opposite ends of the house, and stopped in the doorway of the common room.

Everybody was there.

Rem stood near the kitchen door with a tray of the blue-rimmed cups, the way she stood at the edge of most things: close enough to help and far enough to leave. Umaru, stepping out of the fort, saw her there. She looked at her a moment longer than she needed to, and filed it away.

The furniture had been pushed back against the walls. The chairs were set out in rows, and in front of the rows, facing them, were two more chairs. They were exactly the same height. The fort had been decorated: a long strip of refuge-curtain cloth hung across its front like a stage curtain, and someone had tied ribbons to the toll booth. Milim stood beside it with both fists full of confetti, vibrating very slightly. Marin's decree was pinned to the board, under the four rings. Frieren sat on the couch with a book open in her lap, not reading it.

Umaru stepped out of the fort.

She was in her hamster hood, and over it she wore a sash of her own that said, in Coco's careful lettering, MASTER OF CEREMONIES.

"Please sit down," said Umaru, with immense dignity. "In the two chairs. They're the same height. Senku checked."

Mau and Rimuru looked at each other.

"What did we do?" said Mau.

"You know what you did," said Umaru.

They sat down.



Umaru nodded at the fort.

From behind the curtain came a guitar.

Bocchi's fanfare lasted about four seconds. Everyone agreed afterward that they were an extremely good four seconds: bright and sudden, the kind of thing you play when someone walks out onto a stage. Then it stopped, abruptly, as if the person playing had just remembered that people were listening. There was a small thump, which was Bocchi lying down.

Then, from inside the fort, Kita sang the rest of it. Nobody had known it had words. Kita had known. Kita had apparently known for three days.

When it was over, Umaru unfolded a sheet of paper.

"Order of ceremony," she read. "One. Fanfare." She looked at the fort. "Done. Two. The Questions." She looked up. "Seiko-san."

Seiko rose from her armchair.

For the occasion she had put on a dark formal jacket that nobody had ever seen before. She carried a stick with strips of white paper tied to one end, the kind, she said, that shrines in her world used to sweep bad luck off things. She waved it over the two of them, briskly, as if dusting furniture.

"That's for the bad luck," she said. "You two have had enough of it."

Then she unfolded her own sheet of paper, which the sisters had written, and read it out in her best shrine voice.

"Do you promise to read the letters? All of them. Even the boring ones. Even the mean ones."

"...Yes," said Mau.

"I'll read the boring ones," said Rimuru.

"*All* of them," said four voices from the fort.

"Yes," said Rimuru.

"Do you promise," read Seiko, "to stand next to each other? And never in front alone?"

Both of them looked, without meaning to, at Yuta. He was sitting on the bench under the window with Maki. He didn't say anything. He only nodded once.

"Yes," said Mau.

"Yes," said Rimuru.

"Do you promise to let us tell you when you're being stupid?"

"Do we have a choice?" said Rimuru.

"No," said about twenty people at once.

"Yes," said Rimuru. "Fine. Yes."

"Do you promise to stop working when the Minister says so?"

Marin stood up in the second row with her hands clasped and her eyes shining.

"Yes," they said.

"Do you promise to have fun when the Head of Fun says so?"

Milim stopped vibrating. She went perfectly still, which was much more frightening.

"...Within reason," said Rimuru carefully.

"What's reason?" said Milim.

"Yes," said Mau quickly. "Yes. We promise."

Seiko looked at the last question on her paper. She read it to herself, and then she did not read it out. She folded the paper and held it out to Umaru.

"This one's yours," said Seiko.

Umaru took it. She looked at it for a moment, then looked up at the two of them in their two chairs of exactly the same height.

"Do you promise," said Umaru, "that if it gets too heavy, you'll put it down? And let us carry it for a while?"

The room went quiet.

Rimuru looked at the floor.

Mau looked at Umaru in her hood and her sash, holding the paper with both hands. He saw that she was not joking, and that she had written this one herself, and that she had been thinking about it for four days.

"I'll try," he said.

"That's not one of the answers," said Umaru.

Mau breathed out.

"Yes," he said. "I promise."

"Yes," said Rimuru, very quietly.



"Three," said Umaru, and her voice was perfectly steady again. "The Sashes."

Wakana brought them in on a cushion, folded.

Umaru had meant to put them on herself. But before she could take the first one, Ori stood up from the front row and asked, very quietly, whether she could do Mau's. Umaru, who knew things, said yes.

Ori lifted the sash over Mau's head and settled it across his chest. She smoothed it flat, carefully, with both hands. Then she went back and sat down without looking at anybody.

Milim did Rimuru's. She did it with tremendous enthusiasm, and for a moment it looked as if she might strangle him with it.

Mau looked down at his chest. Rimuru looked down at his.

Mau's said, in Coco's careful black lettering:

IDIOT WHO ACCEPTED

Rimuru's said:

ALSO AN IDIOT WHO ACCEPTED

"Why am I *also*?" said Rimuru.

"Because Mau said *together* first," said Umaru.

"I said *this time we get to choose it*!"

"That," said Fern, from the long table, without looking up from the ledger, "was not an acceptance. That was an observation."

Fern had kept the minutes. Fern always kept the minutes.

Rimuru opened his mouth, then closed it again.

"Ciel says," he said heavily, "that Fern is correct."



Umaru climbed onto a crate in front of the fort.

"Four," she said. "The Naming."

She took a very deep breath.

"By the authority of the fort," said Umaru, "and the Head of Fun, and the Minister of Not Working, and Seiko-san, who has done this before, and the sisters, who thought of it—"

"I thought of the word," said Suika.

"—and Suika, who thought of the word—"

She took another breath, even deeper.

"—we hereby name Mau and Rimuru," said Umaru, loud enough to rattle the toll booth, "*THE TWO IDIOTS WHO ACCEPTED!*"

Milim threw the confetti.

She threw all of it, at once, directly at them, from about a foot away.

The room erupted. People clapped and stamped. Nijika kept time on Momo's soup pot with two spoons. Okarun whistled through his fingers. Inside the fort, Kita sang the fanfare again from the top, and this time Bocchi got up off the floor and played it with her.

Diablo had gone perfectly still at the word *idiot* applied to Rimuru-sama. He stood at the back of the room and watched Rimuru's face. Rimuru was laughing so hard that the edges of him had begun to go soft and blue. Diablo considered this for a long time. Then he bowed, deeply and formally, to the sash.

Senku leaned over to look at the sashes again and said, to nobody in particular, "Still not a crown."

By the window, Eren watched the whole thing with his arms folded. Mikasa stood beside him.

"Good," said Eren quietly.

Mikasa looked at him.

"Nobody ever went to war in the name of an idiot," said Eren.

Mikasa thought about it.

"Two idiots," she said.

Something happened at the corner of Eren's mouth. In a few more months, it might become a smile.

At the long table, Mau looked over at Fern through a cloud of confetti.

"Do we get a vote?" he said.

"You may object," said Fern.

"I object," said Mau.

"I also object," said Rimuru, from somewhere underneath Milim.

Fern wrote it in the ledger, in her neat hand, and turned the book around so they could see:

*Title conferred: The Two Idiots Who Accepted. Objections: two, both from idiots. Overruled.*

Umaru took her proclamation down off the crate and pinned it to the board under the four rings, beside Marin's decree. Nobody ever took it down.



"Five," said Umaru, when the confetti had more or less settled. "The First Letter."

That had not been on the program anyone else had seen.

Coco brought it out of the fort. It was a box, painted blue, with a slot in the lid; Wakana had built it and Coco had painted it. On the front, in Coco's careful lettering, it said: FOR THE TWO IDIOTS. LETTERS.

"Because you promised to read all of them," said Umaru. "So they need somewhere to go."

Mau took the box. It was not empty. Something slid inside it.

"There's one in there already," said Coco. "It's the first one. Don't open it now. Open it later. When it's quiet."

"Six," said Umaru, and climbed down off her crate. "Snacks."

Momo had made enough for the whole settlement, as usual. Nobody minded.

"Seven," said Milim, rising from the wreckage of Rimuru with her fists in the air. "*FUN!*"

Rimuru, flat on the floor in a pile of confetti, said, "Within reason," and nobody listened to him at all.



It was a long time before it got quiet.

Frieren had not moved from the couch through any of it. Eventually Mau came and sat down beside her. He had confetti in his hair, and his sash had gone crooked.

Frieren looked at the sash for a long time.

"I've been calling you that for a long time," she said. "It's nice that it's official."

"You helped them."

"I didn't."

Mau waited.

"I told Coco how to spell *accepted*," said Frieren. "Two c's. She had one."

She reached over and straightened the sash, carefully, until it lay flat across his chest. Then she left her hand there for a moment.

"*Idiot*," she said, very softly. It was the way she said it when nobody else was in the room.



Much later, Mau and Rimuru sat on the step outside the front door, still in their sashes, because Milim had said she would check. The plaza was dark. The lamps were lit along the bridge. They had the blue box between them.

Mau opened it.

There was one letter inside, folded twice. The handwriting changed more times than he could count on the way down the page.

*Dear Two Idiots,*

*This is the first letter to both of you.*

*We wanted the first one to be from us, so the first one isn't from someone who is scared of you.*

*We are not scared of you.*

*Please read all the other ones. If any of them are mean, tell us. We will write back.*

*From, the fort*
*(Umaru, Coco, Suika, Bocchi, and everyone else who was in it)*

*P.S. idiots — Suika*

*P.P.S. I did not write any of this. — Agott*

Mau read it twice. Then he passed it to Rimuru, and Rimuru read it twice.

Neither of them said anything for a while.

"In my world," said Rimuru at last, "they gave me titles. Long ones. You needed a breath in the middle."

"Do you miss them?"

"No." Rimuru looked down at his sash. ALSO AN IDIOT WHO ACCEPTED. "This is the first one anybody ever gave me as a joke." He was quiet for a moment. "It's the first one I'd keep."

Mau folded the letter and put it back in the box.

"We should answer it," he said.

"Now?"

"We promised. All of them."

Rimuru thought about it. Then he reached into himself, the way he did, and came out with a pencil and a scrap of paper from somewhere he kept such things.

They wrote it together on the step, by the light from the door, passing the pencil back and forth.

*Dear fort,*

*Thank you.*

*We accept.*

*— The Two Idiots*

Rimuru slid it under the curtain of the fort on the way to bed. In the morning it was gone, and nobody in the fort ever admitted to having it. But for weeks afterward, if you knew where to look behind the toll booth, there was a scrap of paper pinned there that said *We accept*, in two different handwritings.



Volume Seventeen ended with three things on the same table. A defense system designed not to become a cage. A crossed-out equation that had tried to turn Mau into a civic resource. And a board in the common room with four rings drawn on it, two names where a question mark had been, and one true sentence about a shadow that was not the whole truth.

Pinned beneath the rings were two more things. One was a decree from the Ministry of Not Working. The other was a page from the fort, which named the two people who had agreed to stand in front and did not use a single word that could ever be made into a crown.

The city had become worth protecting. It had chosen who would speak for it, what it wanted to be, and who would be told what.

Now it had to prove it could protect itself without consuming the two people it had just asked to stand in front.
