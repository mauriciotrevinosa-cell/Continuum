# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 1 — Say It Again

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 1  
**Continuity state:** A week after the council | a quiet night in the room with the east window | the pattern in Mau is still analyzing the walls

It was one of those nights when nothing was happening.

No meeting. No storm. No letters that could not wait until morning. The council had met a week ago and drawn its rings, and the board in the common room still had them on it in chalk, and nobody had needed to look at it since. Most of the house was home. The common room had emptied early. Somewhere down the corridor, Bocchi was playing something so quietly that you could only hear it if you were already listening for it.

The plaza had been months ago. The wheel in the night had been weeks. Long enough, again, that the quiet days had started to feel like a promise.

In the room with the east window, Frieren was being impossible.

She had been impossible for weeks, in private, ever since the afternoon in the rain when Mau had done the math on his fingers and Fern had ruled, without looking up from her mending, that emotionally, at least, Frieren was the younger one. In public, nothing had changed. She was still the elf who did not look up from her book. But once the door of their room was shut, something had come loose in her that had apparently been waiting a thousand years for permission. Tonight she was lying full length on top of him on the bed, chin on his chest, her hair everywhere, looking at him with an expression of enormous expectation.

"Say it again," said Frieren.

Mau, who had been very nearly asleep, opened one eye.

"Say what?"

"What you said in the clearing. When you asked."

"That was months ago."

"Yes," said Frieren. "Say it again."

Mau looked at the ceiling.

He knew what she meant. Of course he did. The clearing where he had woken up, with flowers she had made for him all around them, and the whole household badly hidden behind the trees. He had stood in front of her with nothing prepared, because every piece of advice he had been given had fallen out of his head the moment he looked at her.

"I said I love you," said Mau.

"And?"

"And that I love you romantically."

"*And?*"

"And that I wanted to be with you." He thought harder. "And then I asked if you'd be my girlfriend, and you said—"

"I said I thought I already was," said Frieren, with deep satisfaction. "And you said?"

"I said I know."

"And I said?"

"Why are you asking, then."

"And you said?"

"Because I wanted to ask you."

Frieren made a small sound into his chest that was very nearly a hum.

"Now the other one," she said.

Mau groaned.

"No."

"Yes."

"I wasn't— I'd eaten Maomao's chocolate. I don't remember it word for word."

"I do," said Frieren. "Try."

So he tried.

He did it badly. He got the order wrong. He said *most beautiful thing* before he said *person*, and Frieren corrected him without lifting her chin off his chest. *Person. Then place. Then thing.* He forgot the part about the first second, and she gave it back to him. He said *your eyes are green*, and she told him, with great precision, that he had not said *green* the first time. He had said *that green*, and it was different, and she was not going to accept the inferior version.

"*That* green," said Mau.

"Better."

"You've memorized it."

"Of course I have."

"You've been repeating it to yourself."

"No," said Frieren.

"You have."

"Only at night," said Frieren. "And in the mornings. And sometimes in the afternoon." She paused. "You're not finished. There was another thing. At the very end. When you were almost asleep."

Mau knew which thing.

He looked at her: a thousand years old, lying on his chest in his shirt, her ears already going pink in anticipation, waiting for him to get it wrong so she could correct him again.

He did not get it wrong.

Before she could say anything else, he lifted his head and kissed her, quick and soft, so that she lost whatever she had been about to say. Then he lay back down and said it, exactly as he had said it the first time, word for word, because it turned out he had not forgotten that part at all.

"You said you'd be there for the tree. In a hundred years. When it's big enough to sit under." He looked at her. "I'm going to find a way to be there too. With you. I promise."

Frieren stared at him.

Her face did nothing for a moment. Then it went red, all of it, the way it had that night, slowly and completely.

"*Idiot*," said Frieren.

She put her arms around his neck and her face in his shoulder and held on. She did not move again.

Mau closed his eyes.

They fell asleep like that. Her on top of him, his arm across her back, the lamp turned low and the east window dark, in the quiet of a night when nothing was happening.



Rimuru came in a little after midnight.

He had been in his office arguing with a letter. He came through the door in slime form, already halfway to the closet and the ladder up to the loft, and stopped.

Frieren was asleep on top of Mau like a cat on a warm stone. Her hair was spread across both of them, one hand fisted in his shirt. Mau was asleep underneath her with one arm across her back and his mouth slightly open. Both of them looked about twelve years old.

Rimuru put both slime-hands over his slime-mouth.

He stood there for a while, shaking very slightly, fighting with everything he had not to laugh out loud. Then he got himself under control and crept up the ladder into the loft. He settled onto the small pillow that was much too small for a person and exactly right for him, and lay looking down at the two of them over the edge of the boards.

*They're going to need more room*, he thought. *Ciel. Do you think they'd mind if I asked to extend the closet? I keep leaving my things in the office. It would be nice to have a shelf up here. Somewhere to put the sash.*

Ciel did not answer.

Rimuru waited.

*Ciel?*

Nothing.

*Don't be jealous*, Rimuru thought, amused. *It's just a shelf. I'm not moving in. Well, I already moved in. But it's a small shelf.*

Silence.

Rimuru's smile faded.

Ciel did not do silence. Ciel did offended silence, and dry silence, and pointed silence, and *I am choosing not to dignify that* silence. All of those had a texture to them, a sense of somebody in the room. This one did not.

This one sounded like someone listening very hard to something else.

*Ciel*, thought Rimuru. *What's happening?*

*Something is happening to Mau.*

Rimuru sat up.

*His heart rate has been elevated for an hour*, said Ciel. Her voice was very level. *I noted it. I did not report it. Frieren is lying on him, and they were talking, and I believed it was that.* A pause. *It is not that. It has stopped being steady. It is going up and down in a way I do not recognize. Very fast. Then very slow. Then very fast.*

*The walls?*

*Holding. Nothing has come out. Nothing is leaking.* Another pause, and when Ciel spoke again she sounded, for the first time since the containment, unsure. *Something is happening on the inside. Below the walls. I can feel the pattern moving. It is analyzing. It is analyzing very hard. I don't know what.*

Rimuru was already pouring himself down off the edge of the loft.

*Wake him.*

*I am trying.*

Rimuru reformed on the floor as a person, crouched beside the bed. Mau was still asleep, Frieren still asleep on top of him, and nothing looked wrong. The lamp was low. Nothing looked wrong at all.

*Mau*, said Ciel, inside both of them at once, very loud. *Mau. Wake up. Mau.*

Mau did not wake up.

His breathing changed. It went shallow, then deep, then shallow again, in a rhythm that was not the rhythm of sleep. His face did not move.

*Mau.*

Nothing.

Then Ciel's voice went sharp. Rimuru had heard it go sharp exactly once before, in an old inn kitchen with a jar of salt on the table.

*Master. Go and get Yuta.*

Rimuru did not ask why.

He did not say anything at all. He was simply not in the room any more. The door had not moved and he had not used it. Spatial motion, the way he had once arrived at a worksite with a corner post in his hands, the way he had arrived in a common room at two in the morning to sit on the floor beside a shadow.

As he went, Ciel's voice went with him, fast and flat.

*Tell him reverse cursed technique. Tell him Mau. Tell him now.*
