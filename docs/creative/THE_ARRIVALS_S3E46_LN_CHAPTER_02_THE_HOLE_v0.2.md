# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 2 — The Hole

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 2  
**Continuity state:** Same night | Analyze reverts Mau's body toward the moment after Sukuna | Yuta's reverse cursed technique | the bay is prepared

Frieren was dreaming. Or not dreaming exactly. She was in the place just under sleep, where thoughts come loose and drift without anyone steering them.

She was thinking about how she had been behaving.

For weeks now. In this room, with the door shut. Lying on him like a cat. Making him repeat things. Correcting him. Kicking her feet like a girl. She had never once in a thousand years behaved like that, with anyone, about anything. If someone had described it to her a year ago, she would have looked up from her book and said, quite sincerely, that they had the wrong elf.

She had thought, at first, that it was the chocolate. Or the rain. Or the shock of being told, in front of the whole common room, that by elf standards she was barely grown. A phase. Something that had happened *to* her and would wear off.

It had not worn off.

Drifting in the dark, lying on his chest with her face in his shoulder, Frieren found herself thinking a thought so new she did not have a shape for it yet.

*Maybe this is what I'm like.*

*Maybe this was always what I was like, underneath, and I just never had anyone to be like it with.*

She waited for the thought to bother her.

It didn't.

She found, turning it over, that she did not mind it at all. That she might even, a little, like it. Like being someone who lay on a person and made them say things twice. Like being the kind of person whose feet kicked.

*I don't dislike it*, thought Frieren, with something that felt almost like wonder.

She came up out of the thought slowly, the way you come up through warm water. She was smiling a little. She was going to wake him up. She was going to tell him, in the most annoying way she could think of, that she had just worked out something very important about herself, and refuse to say what it was.

She pressed her hand down on the bed beside him to lift herself.

The bed was wet.



She did not understand it at first.

Wet. Warm. Soaked through the blanket and into the mattress under her palm, spreading. Her first thought, absurdly, was that someone had spilled tea. Her second was that it was too warm to be tea.

She lifted her hand and looked at it in the low lamplight.

It was dark. All of it. Her palm and her fingers, to the wrist.

She did not understand that either.

She looked down at Mau.

He was lying exactly as he had fallen asleep, on his back, his arm still across where she had been. His face was very pale. His eyes were closed. And his shirt was dark, and the darkness was spreading outward from the middle of him across the cloth, across the blanket, into the bed.

Frieren put her hand on his chest to wake him.

Her hand went through him.

Not into a wound. Through. There was a space in the middle of him where there should not have been a space. It was the width of a hand. A fist. A hand driven straight through a body on a battlefield a long time ago, when he had stepped into a line on purpose and Sukuna had put his hand through Mau's torso as if it were paper.

She had seen that hole once before.

She had seen it from across a field. She had watched it happen, and tried to move, and been held back. She had walked home beside his body under Rimuru's coat. She had sat beside him in a room for a long time afterward, not knowing if he would wake.

It was here again. In their bed. In the middle of him. Under her hand.

Frieren did not scream.

She did not make any sound at all. Every thought she had ever had stopped at once. She knelt on the soaked bed over him, with her hand inside the hole in him and her own breath gone somewhere she could not find it, and did not move.

Then the room was full.



Rimuru came first, and Yuta was with him. Rimuru had not let go of him, so Yuta arrived the way Rimuru arrived, between one second and the next, barefoot, in the clothes he slept in, with his hair wild.

Yuta did not ask anything.

He saw. He was on the bed before Rimuru had finished reforming, both hands flat on Mau's chest on either side of the hole, and the air in the room changed. Something warm and fierce poured out of Yuta's palms into Mau. It was the same thing Yuta had poured into him once before, on a road, under a coat, refusing the meaning of the weight in his arms. Reverse cursed technique. Life, pushed back into a body faster than the body could lose it.

"Frieren," said Rimuru. "Frieren, move back. Let him work."

Frieren did not move.

Rimuru put his hands on her shoulders and lifted her bodily off the bed. She weighed almost nothing. She let him do it. Her hand came out of Mau with a sound she would hear for a long time afterward. Rimuru set her down on the floor beside the bed and knelt in front of her. Her hands, her arms, the front of Mau's shirt that she was wearing, all of it was dark.

Inside him, Ciel was talking very fast.

"It's not a new wound," said Rimuru. He said it aloud, for Frieren, for Yuta, for anybody, word by word as Ciel gave it to him. "It's the old one. Ciel says the pattern— it was analyzing the walls. It's been analyzing them for weeks, and tonight it decided they're—" He stopped, listening, his face going white. "It decided they're an injury. Something wrong with him. Something to be healed. So it tried to put him back. Back to before the walls existed. Before the seal. And it doesn't know how to choose a moment. It doesn't understand time. It just reached for the last time his body was rebuilt from nothing."

Yuta's hands did not move from Mau's chest.

"After Sukuna," said Yuta, through his teeth. "That's the last time."

"Yes," said Rimuru. "It put his body back to the second after the hand went through. Before your repair. Before anything." He swallowed. "It thinks it's healing him."

Frieren was staring at her hands.

She was not hearing him. Or she was hearing him and the words were sliding off something. Her lips were moving.

"I was on him," she said.

Rimuru looked at her.

"I was lying on him. All night. I was on top of him. I was— I was *pressing*—"

"No."

"I was on top of him and I didn't feel— I didn't notice— I was thinking about *myself*, I was lying there thinking about my *feet*—"

"Frieren. No." Rimuru took her face in both his hands and made her look at him. "Listen to me. You didn't do this. Nobody did this. Ciel saw it. It's Sukuna. It's the wound Sukuna gave him, the same one. His body went back to that moment. It was going to happen whether you were there or not. You being there is the only reason anyone knew in time." He held her face. "Do you hear me? It's Sukuna's wound. Not yours."

Frieren looked at him.

She heard him. He could see her hear him. It helped. He could see that it helped.

It was not enough. He could see that too.



The noise had woken the corridor.

Doors were opening up and down the family wing. Ori's first, then Bocchi's, then Milim's from the end bay. Feet on boards. Voices. Somebody asking what was happening. Somebody else, further away, asking the same thing louder.

Rimuru stood up and went to the doorway and, in a voice that went through the whole Muslin like a bell, shouted:

"*Maomao! Prepare the bay!*"

It reached the far end of the house, where Maomao's room was beside the medical store. She was out of bed before the second word. She did not ask a single question. Neither did Senku, two doors down, already awake because Senku was always already awake. Neither did Kusuri, who came out of the big room at the end of the corridor pulling a coat on over her nightclothes, took one look at Maomao's face going past, and followed her at a run.

They had drilled this. Not this exactly. Nobody had imagined this. But they had drilled the shape of it: someone hurt, the bay, the four of them, every second counting.

"Lift him," said Yuta. "I can carry. I can't stop. Rimuru, keep his head."

They lifted him.

Yuta carried him the way he had carried him once before. Arms full, hands never leaving him, RCT pouring out of his palms into the hole in the middle of Mau, refusing it with everything he had. Rimuru walked beside him with one hand under Mau's head and the other on his chest, and Ciel inside him, working.

They went down the corridor at a run.

Behind them, all the way from the door of the room with the east window to the door of the medical bay, they left a line of dark drops on the boards.
