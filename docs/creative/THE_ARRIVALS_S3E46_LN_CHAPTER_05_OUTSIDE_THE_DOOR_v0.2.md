# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 5 — Outside the Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 5  
**Continuity state:** Days 1-2 | Frieren waits outside the bay | Rika lends Yuta her reserve | Ciel teaches the pattern the walls are part of him | Rimuru cleans the room

It took two days.

Nobody would ever be able to say exactly when it turned. There was no moment when the door of the bay opened and someone came out smiling. There were only hours, one after another, and small changes that only the people inside could see, and one person outside the door waiting for every one of them.



Frieren sat on the floor of the corridor outside the bay with her back against the wall.

She was wearing his hoodie, with the sleeves pulled down over her hands. Her hair was clean and still a little damp at the ends, because Milim had washed it twice, the second time without being asked. Milim sat on the floor beside her, cross-legged, very still, the way she had sat on the roof with Eren, and did not leave.

Frieren did not go in.

She had been told not to, by Rimuru, who had promised, and by Maomao. Maomao had come to the door once in the first hour with her arms red to the elbow, looked at Frieren on the floor, and said, very calmly and very clearly: *Not until I say. If you come in, I'll have to look at you, and I can't afford to look at anything but him.* Then she had shut the door.

So Frieren did not go in. She sat outside it and listened to everything she could hear through the wood.

She could hear Maomao's voice, low and steady, naming things.

She could hear Yuta breathing. Long, slow, deliberate breaths, the breathing of someone doing something enormous for a very long time and rationing himself so he could keep doing it.

She could not hear Mau at all.



Rimuru kept his promise.

He came out of the bay every time anything changed. Good or bad. He crouched down in front of her in the corridor, every time, so their eyes were level, and told her.

The first time, an hour in: "He's holding. Yuta's holding him. The bleeding's stopped getting worse."

The second time, near dawn: "Ciel found what's doing it. She knows what it is now. She's talking to it." He made a face. "Not talking. You know what I mean."

The third time, mid-morning, when the sun had come in through the corridor's one high window and lay across the floor between them in a long stripe: "Senku's put Stark's blood in him. Then Mikasa's. Then Momo's." A pause. "His color's better. Maomao says his color's better."

Frieren did not say anything any of those times. She only nodded, and held her sleeves, and watched Rimuru go back through the door.



Others came and went.

Fern came with food, every few hours, and set it down beside Frieren. She sat with her for a while, then took it away untouched. Then came back with more. The fourth time, she did not set it down. She sat down in front of Frieren on the floor, picked up the spoon, and held it out, the way you would for someone very young or very sick. She held it there, patiently, without a word. Eventually Frieren opened her mouth.

Fern fed her the whole bowl like that. A spoonful at a time.

Neither of them said anything. A long time ago, Frieren had sat with a small girl named Fern who had been too sad to eat, and had simply stayed beside her, without hurrying her, until she did. Fern remembered. Frieren remembered. Neither of them needed to say it.

Rem came with tea, in her blue-rimmed cup, and sat on the other side of the corridor with her back against the opposite wall. She did not try to talk. She simply stayed, across from Frieren, for hours at a time. She was one more person in the corridor who was not going anywhere.

Ori came, and sat down on Frieren's other side, the side Milim was not on, and leaned her head against Frieren's shoulder. She stayed there through most of the first day. Frieren put her arm around her without seeming to notice she had done it.

Nobody else came down that corridor.

That was the house's doing. Nobody had decided it out loud. But somewhere in the first night, the whole household had understood that the corridor outside the bay was not a place for crowds. It was for the few people Frieren could bear. Everyone else kept the rest of the house going. They cooked, and fed the refuge, and answered the bridge, and held the fort in the common room in shifts. They walked past the turn of the corridor to the bay without looking down it, because looking was not what helped.

The fort did not empty for two days. Without anyone deciding it, it became the place the rest of the house reported to. Marin and Hakari's group ran the kitchen with Momo in shifts. Karane complained about every shift and worked every one of them. The Witch Hat girls kept every lamp in the family wing filled, and Agott checked each one twice as if the lamps had personally failed her. Kessoku carried water and messages. Once, when Bocchi could not stand the quiet any longer, they played something in the common room so softly that it might have been the house breathing. Anko kept the fort's log, which recorded nothing except who had eaten and who had not. And Rem went back and forth between the fort and the corridor with tea, all day and all night. She was one of the only people who belonged in both.



On the second day, Yuta began to run out.

Frieren heard it through the door before anyone told her. His long, slow, rationed breathing changed. It went ragged, and then raggeder. Once there was a sound like someone catching a table to keep from falling.

Maki was in the bay. She had gone in on the first night and not come out. She was the only one Maomao let stay who was not working on Mau, because she was working on Yuta. She held cups to his mouth and food to his lips. She held him up, literally, with her arm around his waist, when his knees began to go and his hands could not leave Mau's chest.

And when even that was not enough, something else came.

Frieren felt it through the wall. Everyone in that end of the house felt it. A cold, enormous presence, rising up in the bay behind Yuta like a shadow standing up. It was huge and old, and it loved him with a love that was terrifying to stand anywhere near.

Rika.

She did not speak. She did not do anything anyone could see. She simply stood behind Yuta, in whatever way a thing like Rika stood, and gave him what she had. She had been holding a reserve for him, inside herself, since before he could remember, for exactly this. The ragged breathing steadied. The sound of someone catching a table did not come again.

On the other side of the wall, Frieren closed her eyes.



Late on the second day, Rimuru came out of the bay and did not crouch down.

He just stood there in the corridor, swaying a little, with his human face grey and his hands shaking. For a moment Frieren's heart stopped. She thought—

"It stopped," said Rimuru.

Frieren stared at him.

"The pattern. It stopped going back. Ciel showed it everything. Every day since the walls went up. The flowers. The storm. The sea. Every single day he's been alive with them." Rimuru's voice cracked. "And it looked at all of that, and it analyzed all of that, and somewhere in the last hour it understood. The walls aren't a wound. They're part of him. They're what he's like now." He breathed out. "It stopped trying to fix him. It let him be what he is."

He sat down on the floor, suddenly, as if his legs had decided for him.

"And then his body helped," said Rimuru. "Yuta's been pouring into him for two days. And about an hour ago, Yuta said it was like the first time. After Sukuna. Like there was finally something on the other side, pushing back. Taking it. Doing it with him." He put his face in his hands. "The pathway. The one his body learned last time. It woke up. It helped."

Frieren could not speak.

"The hole's closed," said Rimuru, into his hands. "Not healed. Closed. He has to rest. For a long time. Maomao says weeks. But it's closed. Ciel says the walls are holding, and the pattern's quiet, and it's not trying to go back anymore. She says—" He stopped and listened, and something very like a laugh came out of him, wet and cracked. "She says she doesn't know how long it'll stay understood. She says she'll be watching it every day for the rest of his life. She says she'd like that noted."

Beside Frieren, Milim burst into tears.

She did it all at once, enormously, the way she did everything. She threw both arms around Frieren and Ori together and sobbed into both of them. Ori was crying too, quietly, with her face in Frieren's shoulder.

Frieren did not cry.

She sat very still on the floor of the corridor, with Milim and Ori wrapped around her, and looked at the door of the bay. She had been looking at it for two days.

She was not crying yet.



While they waited for Maomao to say Mau could be moved, Rimuru went back to the room with the east window alone.

He had asked Diablo, on the first night, to let no one in. No one had gone in. Not one person in two days. The room was exactly as they had left it, with the lamp burned out and the bed still dark and stiff from the middle outward.

Rimuru looked at it for a long moment.

Then he rolled up his sleeves and cleaned it.

He did not ask anyone to help him. He did not want anyone to have to. He stripped the bed down to the frame. He carried the blanket and the sheets and the stained mattress out the back of the wing, to the place past the washing court where they burned things that could not be saved, and burned them. He came back and scrubbed the frame and the floor beneath it and the boards of the corridor, all the way down to the bay, every single drop of the line. Then he went to the store, found a new mattress Wakana had been saving, and carried it back by himself.

He put new sheets on the bed. Clean ones. The good ones from the closet, the ones Frieren liked.

He found the folded grey shirt by the washroom door, where Maki had left it, and took it out to the fire as well.

Then he went up the ladder into the loft, and lay there for a while with his face in his small pillow, and did not make any noise at all.
