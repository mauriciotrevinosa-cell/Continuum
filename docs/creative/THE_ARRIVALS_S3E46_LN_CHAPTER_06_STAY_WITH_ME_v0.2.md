# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 6 — Stay With Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 6  
**Continuity state:** Evening of Day 2 | Mau back in the room, asleep | only Maomao, Rimuru, Rem and Frieren allowed in | Frieren sleeps in the chair holding Rimuru

They brought him back to the room at sunset on the second day.

Yuta carried him. He would not let anyone else. He walked down the corridor so slowly that Maki walked beside him with her hand flat against his back, ready. Maomao walked on the other side, carrying her kit, watching Mau's chest rise and fall the whole way. Rimuru went ahead to open the door.

Only four people were allowed in that room now. Maomao had decided it, and nobody argued. Maomao, because she was the doctor. Rimuru, because it was his room too, and because Ciel had to be close. Rem, because she was the one who had kept coming back, through the weeks after the memory crisis, with tea and nothing she needed to say, and knew how to be in a room with him when he was not himself. And Frieren.

Yuta laid him down on the clean bed under the east window, on the new mattress, on the good sheets. He stood back. He looked at Mau for a long moment, breathing. Then his knees finally went, and Maki caught him, and took him away to sleep for the first time in two days.

Maomao checked everything twice. Pulse. Breath. The dressing across the middle of him, under which the hole was closed but not healed: new skin, thin and shining, over something that would take weeks to be whole. She wrote it all down. Then she looked at Frieren in the doorway.

"You can come in now," said Maomao. "He's asleep. He's going to stay asleep for a while. His body's tired. Talk to him if you want. Don't wake him." She paused, and her voice changed very slightly. "He'll be all right. I don't say that unless it's true."

She went out. Rem went with her, to fetch water.

Frieren stood in the doorway.



She did not go to the bed.

She meant to. She had been waiting two days to go to the bed. She had sat on a corridor floor and listened through a door to every breath that was not his, and thought about nothing except the moment she would be allowed to go to him.

Now the moment was here, and she could not make her feet move.

The last time she had been on that bed, she had been lying on him. Thinking about herself. About her feet, and whether she was the kind of person whose feet kicked. Feeling something new and enormous and good, and liking it, while underneath her, the whole time, he had been coming apart.

She knew it was not her. Rimuru had told her. Ciel had told him. Sukuna's wound, Sukuna's hand, a pattern in Mau that did not understand time and had reached for the wrong moment. She knew. She had understood it, the way she understood spells, with the part of her that understood things.

The rest of her had not caught up.

The rest of her stood in the doorway and saw the bed, and saw her own hand going down onto it, and felt the wet.



Rimuru found her there an hour later, still in the doorway.

He did not say anything. He went past her into the room, crossed to her reading corner by the library bay, and picked up the chair. The reading chair, the one Mau had said no to and she had gotten anyway, the one she sat in every evening with her feet tucked up. He carried it across the room and set it down beside the bed. Close. Close enough to touch, if she wanted to. Not on the bed.

Then he went and stood by the closet, out of the way, and waited.

After a long time, Frieren came in.

She sat down in the chair.

She did not touch him. She sat with her knees drawn up and her arms around them and his sleeves over her hands, and looked at him.

He was very pale. His face was turned a little toward the east window, where the last of the light was going. His hair needed cutting. There was a small crease between his eyebrows, as if even asleep he was thinking hard about something.

He was breathing.

She watched him breathe.



She stopped being anything else, after that.

That was the only way Rimuru would ever find to describe it. Not to anyone else, because he never told anyone. Only to himself, sometimes, years later, on nights when he could not sleep.

The thousand-year-old mage who did not look up from her book. The elf who had walked out of a hero's funeral and kept walking for decades. The woman who had stood in front of demons without a flicker on her face. All of that went out of Frieren in that chair, the way light goes out of a room. What was left behind was something much smaller and much younger. Something that had finally been given the one thing it had never known it wanted, and had nearly had it torn away by something it did not understand. And had no idea at all what to do with itself.

She counted his breaths. Out loud, very quietly. Not the way Mikasa counted things, to be sure. The way a child counts sheep, to have something to hold on to.

She reached out, once, and pulled the blanket up over his shoulder where it had slipped. Then she pulled her hand back into her sleeve as if she had touched something hot.

She asked questions. Not to Rimuru exactly. To the room. Small ones, one at a time, with long silences between them.

"Will he wake up?"

"Yes," said Rimuru, from the closet. "Maomao said."

"Will he remember?"

"I don't know."

A long silence.

"Did it hurt him?"

Rimuru was quiet.

"Ciel says he was asleep the whole time," he said at last, gently. "She says he didn't feel it. He just slept."

Another long silence.

"What if it happens again," said Frieren, "while I'm asleep."

"Ciel's watching. Every minute. She says she'll never stop."

"What if she misses it."

"She won't."

"She missed it this time," said Frieren. Her voice did not rise. It just got smaller. "For an hour. She thought it was me."

Rimuru did not have an answer for that. There was no answer. So he didn't pretend.

"Yes," he said. "She did. She knows. She's never going to forgive herself for that hour." He paused. "She'd want you to know that."



It got dark.

Rem came back with water and set it down on the table by the bed, and looked at Frieren in the chair, and did not say anything. She went out again quietly and shut the door.

Frieren did not move from the chair. She did not get into the bed. The bed was where it had happened. She could not, yet. She sat in her reading chair beside it with her knees drawn up and watched him breathe in the dark.

Rimuru should have gone up the ladder to the loft. He had barely slept in two days either. He was so tired his human shape kept wanting to slump into something rounder.

He did not go up.

He came and stood beside the chair instead, and looked down at her.

"He's resting," said Rimuru softly. "The worst is over. You can sleep. I'll watch. Ciel will watch. You don't have to do it alone."

Frieren did not look up.

She was quiet for so long that he thought she was not going to answer at all.

Then, very quietly, without taking her eyes off Mau, the way a child asks for something it is not sure it is allowed to have, Frieren said:

"Tonight. Can you stay with me?"

Rimuru looked at her.

He did not say anything. He did not need to.

He let his human shape go, all at once, and was a slime. A small blue dome on the floor beside the chair. Then he climbed up, slowly and carefully, into her lap, and settled there. He was warm in the middle, the way she had once told Mau he was. He was something to hold.

Frieren put both her arms around him.

She held him the way you hold something in the dark when you are very young and very frightened. She held on.

They stayed like that. Frieren in the chair, Rimuru in her lap, Mau breathing on the bed beside them. The east window full of stars, and Ciel watching every minute, and the house beyond the door finally, for the first time in two days, beginning to sleep.

At some point, Frieren slept too.

She did not let go.
