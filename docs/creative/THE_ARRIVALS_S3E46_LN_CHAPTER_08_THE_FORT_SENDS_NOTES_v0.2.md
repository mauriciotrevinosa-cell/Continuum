# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 8 — The Fort Sends Notes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 8  
**Continuity state:** Days 4-14 after Mau wakes | the pack can't go in, so Rem becomes the fort's agent | notes, the window concert, Seiko: 'Then hit him' | Rem realizes she has joined: (SISTER)

The fort was not allowed in.

Maomao had said so, and when Maomao said something about a patient, it stayed said. Four people could go into the room with the east window until she decided otherwise. Maomao, because she was the doctor. Rimuru, because he lived there. Frieren, because nobody in any world was going to keep her out. And Rem.

That was the list. It was pinned to the door. The fort was not on it.

On the fourth morning after Mau woke, the fort convened in emergency session.

It was very full. It had been very full ever since the night of the bay, when it had gone from four people to nineteen and one teacher and had never quite gone back. Umaru presided from the cushion by the toll booth. Coco, Suika and Bocchi sat at her sides, as founders. Around them, in roughly the places they had ended up that night, sat everyone else: Ori against Umaru, the Witch Hat girls along the back, Kessoku in the corner, Hakari's group in a heap, Kusuri back among them now, Marin, Mai at the very edge with her arms folded, and Anko with her notebook.

Rem was there too. She had been there every evening since the night of the bay. Nobody had invited her again, because nobody had needed to.

"I tried Maomao," said Umaru. "She said no."

"What did you offer her?" said Hakari.

"Everything in the toll booth."

"And?"

"She said no and took a biscuit."

The fort considered this.

"Then we need someone on the inside," said Umaru.

Every head in the fort turned, slowly, toward Rem.

Rem was pouring tea. She stopped pouring tea.

"I am only allowed in to help," she said.

"This is helping," said Umaru.

"You're our agent now," said Suika.

"What's her codename?" said Hakari.

Rem looked around the fort at all of them. She looked at Ori, who was looking at her with enormous, hopeful eyes, and at Coco, who already had a pencil out. She looked at Agott, who was pretending not to care and was plainly waiting for the answer.

"My name is fine," said Rem.

Nano wrote it down.



The first basket went in that afternoon.

Rem carried it down the family corridor with both hands. Umaru had read every single thing in it first, very carefully, before it was allowed to leave the fort. She did not say why. Nobody asked. One of Suika's notes came back out with a line crossed through so hard that the pencil had torn the paper. Suika looked at it, and then at Umaru, and did not argue.

Maomao met Rem at the door. She went through the basket item by item, the way she went through anything that was going to be near a patient.

Coco had drawn the fort, in charcoal, because ink was for circles. In the middle of the fort was a bed, and in the bed was a very small Mau. Around him, everyone was crowded in at once, far more people than could possibly fit. Underneath, in her careful lettering, it said: WHEN YOU CAN WALK.

There was a slice of watermelon from Suika. Maomao confiscated it, inspected it, and then gave it back.

There was a note from Agott: *Recover efficiently. — A.* On the back, much smaller, as if written by a different and more embarrassed person: *Please.*

There was a sweet from Tetia, wrapped in paper, with a note that had more exclamation marks than words.

There was a corner torn from a page with a very small drawing of a slime on it, and nothing else. That was Richeh's. Rimuru took it before anyone else could and would not give it back.

There was a list from Hakari titled ONE HUNDRED REASONS TO GET BETTER. It reached forty-one, and then said *the rest are coming*.

There was a note from Karane: *I'm not worried. — K.* Underneath it, in Shizuka's neat hand: *She is.*

There was a schedule from Nano, calculated to the hour, covering everything from sleep to soup to *talking: permitted in moderation*. It included the line *laughing: allowed after day six*.

There was a drawing from Marin of the sash, redesigned, with a magnificent flowing cape. Someone, very obviously Umaru, had crossed out the cape and written NO CAPES across it in capitals.

There was a setlist from Kessoku, for something they called UNDER THE WINDOW. It had one song on it. Next to the song, Nijika had written its length: *4 sec.*

There was a folder from Anko, an actual folder, with a label on the front: THE CASE OF THE MAN WHO WILL NOT ADMIT IT HURTS. Inside was one page. It said: *Status: open.*

There was a note from Mai that said: *Maki says hi. I don't.*

And at the very bottom of the basket, with no note at all, was a small smooth stone from the tidepools.

Maomao looked at all of it for a long time.

"Fine," she said, and stood aside.



Mau was propped up on three pillows, which was two more than he wanted and exactly as many as Maomao had ordered. He looked better than he had on the first morning. He looked worse than he pretended to.

Frieren was in her reading chair by the bed with a book she was actually reading, which was new.

Rem set the basket on the blanket.

"From the fort," she said.

Mau looked at the basket. Then at Rem.

"The fort sends mail now?"

"The fort has an agent now," said Rem.

He went through it slowly, one thing at a time. He laughed at Agott's note, and at Karane's, and at Shizuka's line underneath it. When he got to Nano's schedule, he laughed properly for the first time since he had woken, then stopped very suddenly in the middle of it and went still.

He did not say anything.

Rem did not say anything either. She leaned over, moved the top pillow an inch to the left and lowered the second, so that the weight came off the middle of him. Then she sat back down.

Mau looked at her.

"How did you know?"

"You don't have to tell me," said Rem.

"Tell you what?"

"Anything," said Rem.

Mau looked at her for a while longer. Then he picked up Anko's folder, read the one page inside it, and put it face down on the blanket very carefully, as if it might be listening.

Frieren turned a page of her book and did not look up. She was smiling.

He held the stone from the tidepools the longest. He turned it over in his fingers and did not say anything about it, and when Rem left, it was on the windowsill, where he could see it from the bed.



He wrote back.

It took him most of the evening, because his hands were slower than they should have been, and because he insisted on answering every single thing. Rem carried the answers back to the fort in the empty basket. The fort read every one of them aloud, in a circle, with the solemnity of a court.

*Dear Agott: I will recover as efficiently as I can. Thank you for the second side.*

Agott said that she did not know what he was talking about and went red to the ears.

*Dear Karane and Shizuka: I believe Shizuka.*

*Dear Nano: I laughed on day four. I'm sorry. It was your fault.*

Nano amended the schedule.

*Dear Marin: I would have worn the cape.*

Umaru read that one out twice, in a voice of terrible betrayal.

*Dear Anko: The case is closed. There is nothing to investigate. Please stop investigating.*

Anko reopened the case.

To Ori there was no note. He had sent back a stone instead, a different one: a small grey one from the yard under the east window, which he had asked Frieren to fetch because he could not go himself. Ori held it in her hand all evening and slept with it.

After that, the basket went in every day.

Sometimes twice a day. Then, on the eighth day, when Hakari's list finally reached a hundred and the fort could not wait until afternoon, three times. Rem carried it every time. She went down the family corridor so often that Wakana said she was wearing a path in the boards, and Rem said that Wakana had built the boards and could fix them, and Wakana laughed so hard she had to sit down.

The fort started sending things for Rem too. They went in the basket on the way back, so that she would not notice until she unpacked it. A ribbon from Tetia. A small sketch from Richeh of a blue-rimmed cup. A second list from Hakari, titled TWELVE REASONS REM IS THE BEST AGENT, that reached twelve and stopped, because twelve was all she needed.

Rem did not say anything about them. She kept every one.



On the sixth night, Frieren slept in the bed again.

She had slept in the chair for five nights. She had sat beside the bed and held his hand across the gap, and slept with her head on her own arm, and woken up stiff and not complained. On the sixth night, she put her book down and stood up, stood looking at the bed for a long moment, and got in. She lay on the side away from the dressing, very carefully, with one hand on his arm and the rest of her not touching him at all.

Mau did not say anything. He just turned his hand over under hers.

Up in the loft, Rimuru pretended to be asleep. Nobody mentioned it the next day.

Frieren sent one note to the fort that week. It was the only one she sent. It said, in her angular hand:

*He complained about the food today. Tell Seiko.*

Seiko's reply came back in the next basket, in pencil, in three words.

*Then hit him.*



Nano's schedule said that laughing was allowed after day six. On day seven, the fort decided he should have something to laugh at.

Maomao gave them ten minutes. Nano timed it.

At sunset they gathered in the yard under the east window. Kessoku stood at the front, with Bocchi in the middle holding her guitar like a shield. Behind them stood everybody else, the whole fort, in a crowd that did not fit the yard any better than it had ever fitted the fort.

Inside, Rimuru opened the window. Frieren helped Mau sit up, slowly, until he could see out.

Bocchi played the fanfare.

It lasted four seconds, the same four seconds as at the ceremony: bright and sudden, the kind of thing you play when someone walks out onto a stage. Then, instead of stopping, Bocchi kept going. Kita came in singing, and Nijika came in on a drum made from Momo's soup pot, and Ryo came in on the bass she had not been seen to practice in two weeks and played perfectly. For eight minutes, Kessoku played something none of them had ever played for anyone before. Nobody in the yard knew it, because it was new. Bocchi had written it in the music room, alone, over the last week, and had not told anybody what it was for.

It was not sad. That was what Mau would remember. It was a song for someone getting better.

He laughed at the end, when Suika shouted *IDIOT!* up at the window in pure delight, and the whole yard shouted it after her. It hurt. He did not say so. Frieren, beside him, felt him flinch and said nothing either, and kept her hand flat on his back until it passed.

Nano called time at exactly ten minutes. Maomao closed the window herself.



Near the end of the second week, Rem carried the empty basket back to the fort one evening and stopped in the doorway of the common room.

Somebody had made a new sign for the toll booth. It said, in Coco's careful lettering:

TOLL: SNACKS
TOLL FOR REM: NONE

Beside the toll booth, on a hook that had not been there that morning, hung a blue-rimmed cup.

Rem stood looking at it.

"Coco made the sign," said Umaru from inside the fort. "Wakana did the hook. Suika picked the cup. It's not the cup from the kitchen. It's yours, for here."

Rem did not say anything.

"You know you're one of us now," said Umaru. "Right?"

Rem looked at the cup for a long time.

"I have a sister," she said at last, quietly. "Somewhere else. I was always the little one." She paused. "I have never been anyone's big one."

"We've got too many little ones," said Umaru.

"Hey," said Suika.

"You could be the big one," said Coco. "Mau's busy."

Rem laughed.

It surprised her. It came out of her before she knew it was coming. It was a real laugh, the kind she had not heard from herself in a long time, in this world or the other one. The fort heard it too. It went very quiet, the way people go quiet when a bird lands nearby and they don't want to frighten it off.

She went into the fort. She sat down in the place between Ori and Coco that had been hers since the night of the bay, and Ori leaned against her without a word.

It was only later, going up to bed, that she noticed she had not slept at the old inn in nine nights. She noticed it the way you notice you have stopped limping. She had not decided anything. She had only stopped going.

At the turn of the family corridor was the narrow room, with the slip on the door that said REM in pencil, in her own hand.

Under it, tonight, there was a second slip. It was smaller, in Coco's careful lettering:

*(SISTER)*

Rem stood in front of the door for a long time.

She did not take it down.
