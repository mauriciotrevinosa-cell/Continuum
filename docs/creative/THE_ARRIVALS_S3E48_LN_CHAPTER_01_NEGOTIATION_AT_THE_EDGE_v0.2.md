# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 20 — Negotiation at the Edge

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 20

Anko went west.

Himmel.

One native representative from the safe-corridor partnership.

No Diablo visible.

No Milim.

Deliberate.

The western group was not an army in any clean sense.

Local militia.

Hired fighters.

Officials.

Three wagons.

Frightened civilians behind them.

Some armed.

Some not.

A captain came forward holding the same demand.

Anko did not take it.

"We already answered."

"Your answer was rejected."

"By whom?"

"The regional office."

"Name."

The captain's mouth tightened.

Same problem.

Orders existed more clearly than authorship.

"Submit unregistered Otherworlders for temporary relocation and capability assessment."

Anko:

"Specific names?"

"No."

"Specific crimes?"

"Capability risk."

"That's not a crime."

The captain knew.

That was visible.

His people knew too.

Some looked away.

Himmel watched faces rather than weapons.

Fear below.

Orders above.

Not necessarily same motive.

The native representative asked:

"Why civilians?"

Captain looked behind.

"Evacuated from northwestern farms."

Anko's attention sharpened.

"Why?"

"Animal movement."

Same reports.

So the western column might not have manufactured that part.

Could still exploit it.

Could still be exploited.

No certainty.

Anko asked whether civilians could be moved through eastern corridor instead of held behind an armed demand.

The captain hesitated.

One official objected.

"They remain under regional protection."

Native representative:

"They are standing between two armed groups."

That landed.

Negotiation shifted.

Not one issue.

Civilians.

Demand.

Route.

Unknown northern threat.

Then a bell carried from settlement.

Northwest fallback.

Everyone heard.

Captain looked toward sound.

So did hired fighters.

Then treeline behind northern rise moved.

Not wind.

Animals broke across open ground.

Running.

One local militia fighter raised weapon.

Himmel pushed barrel down.

"They're fleeing."

The fighter stared.

"What from?"

No one knew.

Anko said:

"Move your civilians south of road."

Captain:

"That puts them closer to you."

"It puts them farther from whatever is north."

He looked at his officers.

Orders conflict with survival.

Good.

An official insisted negotiation continue.

Then first monster appeared beyond fleeing animals.

Large.

Wrong.

Not one they could ignore.

Another behind.

Then another.

The captain swore.

Anko:

"We can keep arguing after they don't eat your people."

No speech.

Decision.

Captain ordered civilians back.

One hired fighter refused to move from line facing settlement.

Still considered Otherworlders primary threat.

Fear held.

Then monster struck one of their rear wagons.

The western group's formation broke by function.

Some turned north.

Some kept weapons east.

Some froze.

No clean enemy.

Diablo appeared only when one fighter attempted to exploit chaos and rush the settlement boundary.

He did not kill.

He was simply there.

The fighter stopped.

Anko glared at Diablo.

"You were supposed to stay unseen."

"I remained unseen until useful."

Annoying.

True.

The captain saw him.

Fear spiked.

Anko immediately:

"He is not attacking if you do not."

Diablo smiled.

Wrong smile.

Rimuru would complain later.

The negotiation did not end with agreement.

It ended with a temporary fact:

everyone in that field had a larger immediate survival problem.

The captain shouted:

"After this, the demand remains."

Anko:

"After this, we'll still say no."

Good.

No alliance born from shared danger.

Only a temporary refusal to let bureaucracy get people killed before argument was finished.

Then the northwest line broke harder.

Anko's communication token lit.

EVACUATION PREP.

The city had run out of time.
