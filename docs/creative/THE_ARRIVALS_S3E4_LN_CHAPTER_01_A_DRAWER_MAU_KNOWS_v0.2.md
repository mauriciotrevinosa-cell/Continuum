## Chapter 10 — A Drawer Mau Knows

Mau opened the third drawer.

Bandages.

He stared.

Closed it.

Opened the second.

Twine.

First.

Tools.

He stood in the storage room long enough for Umaru to notice.

"What are you doing?"

"Looking for the nails."

"Top shelf."

"No."

Umaru pointed.

"Yes."

Mau looked.

A small wooden box sat exactly where she indicated.

He knew that box.

He had used it.

He also knew the nails lived in the third drawer.

They always had.

Except the third drawer held bandages.

"Who moved them?"

Umaru looked offended.

"Nobody."

"Someone did."

"I've been taking nails from that box for weeks."

Mau looked at her.

Umaru's expression changed.

Not joking now.

"Maybe before?"

Mau tried to remember.

His memory produced his own hand opening the third drawer.

Nails.

Morning light.

Frieren saying something from the doorway.

Clear.

Ordinary.

Impossible to distrust.

"Maybe."

Umaru did not let him leave yet.

"How sure are you?"

Mau looked at her.

"About the drawer?"

"Yes."

"Very."

"Like guessing?"

"No."

"Like remembering?"

Mau's answer took too long.

"Yes."

That changed the room.

Not much.

Enough.

Umaru opened the third drawer again.

Bandages.

Then the second.

Twine.

Then pointed to the shelf.

Nails.

No hidden second box.

No joke.

Mau took the box.

Before leaving, Umaru wrote the date on a scrap of paper.

Mau noticed.

"What are you doing?"

"Making sure tomorrow has something today can argue with."

He wanted to laugh.

Did not.

"Good idea."

The first record existed before anyone knew they needed a system.

<!-- scene-id: s3e4-ln01-noise -->

At the site, he forgot about it.

Almost.

Rimuru needed nails.

Mau handed him the box.

The movement triggered the memory again.

Third drawer.

Frieren.

Morning.

He could hear her voice.

`You're using the last ones.`

Mau frowned.

They had never run out.

Probably.

He hated the word.

That evening he checked the storage room again.

Third drawer:
bandages.

Second:
twine.

Shelf:
nails.

Same.

He wrote it down himself.

Not because writing could make memory true.

Because tomorrow's certainty might not deserve more authority than today's evidence.

He looked at the box.

Full enough.

"Everything okay?" Rimuru asked.

"Yeah."

The answer came easily.

That was the problem nobody knew existed yet.

Mau was not lying.

He believed it.
