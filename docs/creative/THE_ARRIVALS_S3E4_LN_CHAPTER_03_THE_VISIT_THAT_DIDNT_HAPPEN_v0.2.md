# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 10
## Chapter 12 — The Visit That Didn't Happen

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Version:** 0.2  
**Volume:** 10  
**Reading order:** 12  
**Source episode:** S3E4 — `Small Wrong Things`

<!-- scene-id: s3e4-ln03-mill -->

The abandoned mill was on the northern route.

Mau knew this because he had been there.

He remembered Okarun complaining about the roof.

Anko finding old ledgers.

A family of three sleeping in the rear room.

No Arrivals.

Just travelers.

They had shared food.

Mau remembered the youngest child asking whether Rimuru was a toy.

He laughed when Mikasa mentioned the mill.

"Did the roof finally fall?"

Mikasa looked at him.

"What roof?"

"The mill."

"We haven't reached it yet."

Mau's smile disappeared.

The room did not.

That would have been easier.

<!-- scene-id: s3e4-ln03-detail -->

He described it.

Too well.

Broken upper wheel.

Two doors.

Moss on the east wall.

A blue cloth tied around one interior support.

Anko's ledger.

The child.

The joke about Rimuru.

Anko listened.

Then opened her notebook.

"We found the bridge."

She turned pages.

"We heard about strangers near the mill."

Another page.

"We did not reach it. Light was going. We came back."

Mau looked at Okarun.

Okarun shook his head.

"I've never been there."

Mau could feel the cold floor under his boots.

Memory had texture.

That fact stopped being comforting.

<!-- scene-id: s3e4-ln03-verify -->

They did not go to the mill that night.

That mattered.

Mau wanted to leave immediately.

Not tomorrow.

Not with a team.

Now.

Because every minute between memory and verification felt like contamination.

Frieren stood in the doorway.

"Tomorrow."

"What if something changes?"

"Then it changes."

"What if the family leaves?"

"Mau."

He looked at her.

She did not say:
you're wrong.

She said:

"If you go tonight, you are using danger to answer a memory question."

That stopped him.

Barely.

They did not go to the mill that night.

That mattered.

Mau wanted to.

Rimuru said tomorrow.

Frieren said tomorrow.

Mau hated them both for twelve seconds.

Then hated that too.

The next day, Mau did not join the verification team.

He hated that too.

Anko went.

Okarun went.

Mikasa.

A native guide.

Mau stayed with Frieren and tried not to reconstruct the route from a memory nobody trusted.

Hours later Anko returned.

She placed the report face down.

Mau:

"Just tell me."

Anko turned it.

Broken upper wheel.

Two doors.

Moss on the east wall.

Exactly.

Mau's chest loosened for one dangerous second.

Then:

No blue cloth.

No ledger.

No family.

No bedding.

No fresh fire residue.

No evidence anyone had slept there recently.

The memory had not invented a random building.

It had taken a real place he had never visited and furnished it with a visit that had never happened.

Mau stared at Anko's report.

Half the place existed.

Half the memory did not.

That was worse than being completely wrong.
