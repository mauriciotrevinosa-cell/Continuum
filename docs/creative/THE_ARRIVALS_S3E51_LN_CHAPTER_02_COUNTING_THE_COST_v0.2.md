# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 18 — Counting the Cost

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 18  
**Continuity state:** Post-defense Day 1 | Damage accounting before blame | Recovery distributed across multiple communities

The first post-defense council meeting began with a rule.

No blame until numbers.

Anko wrote it at the top of the board.

Rimuru read it.

"Optimistic."

"Necessary."

The room smelled like damp paper.

Nobody had changed all the wet clothes.

Good.

The people making decisions looked like people who had been awake too long.

Fern started with beds.

"Forty-three temporarily unusable."

Mau's face changed.

Fern held up one finger.

"Unusable. Not destroyed."

He nodded.

Important distinction.

"Old inn can take fourteen without crowding beyond emergency level."

Jinshi:

"Second community offered twelve."

"Offered," Fern repeated.

Not assigned.

"East village households offered six."

Native partnership.

Not ownership.

Remaining people:
temporary common-room sleep.

Next:
water.

Senku stood.

"Clean reserve intact. Upper diversion damaged. Washing court offline. Full redundancy restoration: minimum twelve days if weather cooperates."

Rimuru:

"Twelve?"

Senku smiled.

"Physics does not negotiate."

Qifrey added:

"Magic can reduce labor."

Senku:

"Not cure materials."

Qifrey:

"I know."

Rimuru looked betrayed by both reality systems.

Next:
medicine.

Maomao.

"Low."

Kusuri beside her:

"Specific."

She listed:
bandages;
antiseptic compounds;
fever support;
two herbs;
one imported medication they could not replace locally.

No magic solved supply.

Yuta could heal many things.

He could not be the entire medical system.

That was one of the season's oldest lessons returning in another form.

Next:
roads.

Mikasa marked two blocked sections.

One passable on foot.

One not.

Searchers could route around.

Trade carts could not.

Next:
people.

The room got quieter.

No mass casualty collapse.

Not zero.

Names.

Not numbers first.

Names.

The council heard each.

One native volunteer.

Two residents.

One militia fighter who had died pulling civilians from a wagon after arriving as part of the hostile column.

Nobody knew where to place that fact emotionally.

So they did not force it.

The militia captain requested his body.

Granted.

One Searcher hospitalized.

Three serious injuries.

Dozens minor.

A pregnant resident and baby both stable.

Maomao said that before anyone asked because everyone had been trying not to.

Fern exhaled.

Then:
prisoners.

Not prisoners, Anko corrected.

Detainees pending review where active violence had occurred.

Surrendered people who had committed no identifiable settlement crime were not automatically detained forever.

Eren listened.

Exit rights under stress.

Good.

The sanctuary collaborator had been injured protecting a resident who hated him.

Someone said:

"Maybe he's changed."

The harmed resident, attending by choice, looked up.

"Maybe."

Everyone waited.

She continued.

"That doesn't erase what he did."

No argument.

"Also he helped."

Both facts remained.

The council recorded both.

Not moral transformation.

Not permanent villain.

Fact.

Then repair.

Senku projected weeks.

Rimuru wanted days.

"Can Milim—"

"No," Senku said.

Milim, present for exactly the wrong sentence:

"I can."

Senku pointed at the cracked western foundation.

"You can move mass. You cannot make cured mortar age faster."

Milim crossed her arms.

"I can intimidate it."

"No."

Momo whispered:

"I want to see."

No.

Kusuri raised a hand.

"Could we accelerate curing chemically?"

Senku looked at her.

"Possibly."

Maomao:

"What byproduct?"

Kusuri:

"Depends."

Maomao:

"Then no until tested."

Senku:

"Agreed."

Kusuri looked betrayed.

"Both of you are boring."

Good.

No emergency chemistry miracle.

The estimate remained weeks.

That mattered.

The city would not bounce back between chapters because the battle was over.

People moved again.

Not The Move.

Temporary recovery.

Some back to the inn.

Some to the second community.

Some hosted by native partners.

A family that had spent months insisting the second community was "not our city" accepted two injured residents for a week.

No symbolism.

Beds.

The network widened.

The main settlement was no longer the only safe node.

That reduced pressure.

It also increased politics.

Who paid for material?

Who supplied food?

Could the second community refuse later?

Yes.

Could native partners request labor in return?

Yes.

Could emergency support become permanent tax obligation accidentally?

Jinshi wrote that question down.

Anko underlined it.

Good.

No gratitude debt becoming governance by accident.

At the end, Mau had said almost nothing.

Rimuru noticed.

"You okay?"

Mau looked at the board.

Names.

Beds.

Days.

Water.

Road.

"I keep wanting to turn the whole thing into one problem."

Senku:

"It isn't."

"I know."

"Then don't."

Mau looked at him.

"Helpful."

"Correct."

Frieren sat against the back wall.

She saw the old pattern too.

Mau could carry one person.

One box.

One conversation.

He could not carry "recovery."

No one could.

The council ended after three hours.

Not with:
we rebuild.

With assignments.

Small enough to fail visibly.

Water.

Beds.

Road.

Medicine.

Detainee review.

Aid agreements.

Rumor correction.

Evidence chain.

The city had survived by distributed systems.

Recovery would require the same humility.

Outside, someone had already started repairing a roof.

Not because the council authorized hope.

Because rain was expected.
