# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 32 — Sukuna Hears They Survived

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — CREATOR REVISION 2026-10-04  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 32  
**Same time as:** V18 #19–#21 (elsewhere: Sukuna)  
**Continuity state:** Parallel antagonist interlude | Sukuna remains active and independent of the anti-Otherworlder network

Sukuna heard about the battle from a frightened man who did not know who he was speaking to.

That was funny.

The man had a cart.

Half broken.

Two wheels caked with mud.

He had spent twenty minutes complaining about roads before reaching the interesting part.

"The Otherworlder city fought the west."

Sukuna sat under the shade of a ruined waystone.

"Did it?"

"They say their leader refused assessment."

Leader.

Sukuna smiled.

"Who?"

"The worldless one."

That was new.

"Mau."

The man nodded quickly.

"You know him?"

Sukuna's smile widened.

"Enough."

The man did not like that answer.

Good instincts.

He continued anyway because fear and gossip were cousins.

"They say he commands demons. Monsters too. A slime king. An elf. Witches. Fighters from other worlds."

Sukuna laughed.

Not loudly.

Worse.

Mau commanding Frieren was funny enough.

Mau commanding Rimuru?

Milim?

Diablo?

Better.

The story had misunderstood the entire shape.

That made it useful.

"What else?"

"They say he keeps a monster in his shadow. A great pale wheel. People saw it come up in the middle of a market, in daylight."

Sukuna went very still.

Not laughing now.

He knew exactly one wheel like that. He had worn it.

"Did it turn?"

The man blinked.

"What?"

"The wheel. Did it turn?"

"They say he held it. With his hands. Until it went back down."

Sukuna was quiet for a moment.

Then he smiled, slowly, and it was the worst thing the man had seen all year.

"Of course he did," said Sukuna. "What else?"

"They built a fortress."

Sukuna looked at the road.

He had heard versions of that too.

Fortress.

City.

Refuge.

Camp.

One rumor claimed walls taller than a keep.

Another claimed no walls at all.

People were bad at scale when afraid.

"Did they survive?"

The man hesitated.

"Yes."

There.

Sukuna's expression changed by almost nothing.

Interesting.

He had not been idle.

He had learned this world punished careless displays differently depending on region.

Learned where cursed energy drew attention.

Learned how long it took rumors to outrun him.

Learned which monsters were worth killing and which were worth letting follow.

Learned that suppressing himself made frightened officials invent explanations anyway.

He had also learned that Mau was difficult to kill.

Not impossible.

Difficult.

More interesting.

The man pulled a folded notice from his coat.

"See?"

Sukuna took it.

The page described:

MAU-ALIGNED NETWORK.

Organized resistance.

Foreign capability concentration.

Recruitment.

Command.

Sukuna read the same kind of fear translated into bureaucratic language.

He had seen fear become religion.

Clan law.

Military structure.

Now paperwork.

Humans remained inventive in boring ways.

"Do you believe this?"

The man swallowed.

"They have monsters."

"So do you."

The man looked around.

Sukuna gestured at the wilderness.

"Your world."

The man had no answer.

Good.

Sukuna's finger stopped on Mau's name.

The paper wanted one center.

One leader.

One thing to remove.

Sukuna understood the temptation.

He also understood the mistake.

Mau was central.

That part was true.

But not because the others obeyed him.

That distinction would matter if someone tried to break the place by killing its supposed king.

Sukuna folded the notice.

Kept it.

The man stared.

"Are you going there?"

"No."

Relief.

Premature.

Sukuna stood.

"Not yet."

The man went pale.

Sukuna walked east for half a mile.

Then turned south.

Not toward the settlement.

Not away from it in any permanent sense.

He had his own interests.

He did not serve whoever was spreading the notices.

He did not care whether frightened governments wanted Mau contained.

If some hidden hand thought Sukuna could be pointed like a weapon, that hand would eventually learn what hands usually learned.

Still.

Chaos around the settlement had value.

Pressure revealed structures.

Fear made people show what they protected.

And Mau, apparently, now had much more to protect than the last time they met.

Sukuna smiled.

Good.

Growth was more interesting when both sides had something to lose.

Behind him, the trader remained at the waystone until Sukuna disappeared from sight.

Only then did he realize the notice was gone.

By the time he reported the encounter, the story changed again.

A horned demon.

A cursed king.

A man with impossible eyes.

Someone who knew Mau.

The rumor network absorbed Sukuna too.

It did not know yet that this was worse.
