# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 32 — Leadership After the Front

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 32  
**Continuity state:** Post-defense recovery | Emergency authority under review | Permanent personal rule rejected

After crisis, people wanted certainty.

The petition arrived on good paper.

That worried Jinshi before he read it.

Good paper meant somebody had planned.

He brought it to council.

"Fixed leadership structure for external security and emergency coordination."

Rimuru read the first proposed names.

Then sighed.

"Well. They got the names right."

Mau did not laugh.

Weeks ago, in the common room, Rings One and Two had already chosen the two of them to speak for the settlement. To speak, not to rule. This petition wanted more than that. It wanted the speaking to come with authority attached, and no end date.

The petition had signatures.

Enough to matter.

Not enough to be mandate.

Reasons repeated:

They connect groups.

They were present from the beginning.

People listen.

They can speak across power systems.

Outside authorities already treat them as representatives.

All true enough.

Dangerous logic.

Anko placed the paper down.

"Being convenient to outsiders is not a constitutional theory."

Senku:

"Neither is being popular."

Milim:

"I vote Rimuru."

Everyone:

"No."

Milim looked offended.

Mau read his own name again.

Something in him liked the practical part.

That frightened him more than hating it would have.

Rimuru noticed.

"What?"

Mau looked up.

"I know what problems I'd solve first."

Silence.

Frieren, against the wall:

"That's not an answer."

Mau exhaled.

No escape.

"I like coordination."

Jinshi:

"Meaning?"

"People tell me things. I translate. I notice when two groups are solving the same problem differently."

"And authority?"

Mau's mouth tightened.

"Terrifying."

Frieren:

"Good."

He looked at her.

"If you liked deciding everyone's lives, I'd worry."

Fair.

The council did not reject leadership.

That would have been another simplification.

Emergency authority had existed during the defense.

Mikasa could close routes.

Fern could redirect refuge flow.

Maomao could control medical space.

Rimuru could coordinate cross-system response.

Those powers worked because they were scoped.

Time-limited.

Reviewable.

People understood when they ended.

Jinshi wrote four words on the board.

TERM.

SCOPE.

REMOVAL.

REVIEW.

Anko added:

APPEAL.

Eren added:

EXIT.

Everyone looked at him.

He shrugged.

"Authority feels different when leaving becomes impossible."

No one argued.

The discussion became less romantic.

Good.

Could Rimuru hold a coordination role?

Yes.

For how long?

Defined term.

Could Mau?

Yes.

Same.

Could either command private lives?

No.

Could emergency authority override ordinary movement?

Only immediate safety and only while routes were actively dangerous.

Could residents remove them?

Process required.

Could outsiders demand "the leader"?

They could be given a liaison.

Not owner.

Not king.

Not founder-sovereign.

Mau liked the liaison part.

Of course he did.

"Translation between people," he said.

Frieren looked at him.

"That's the part you want."

"Yeah."

"And deciding?"

"Still terrifying."

"Good."

Rimuru leaned back.

"I'd rather build something that doesn't collapse when I leave."

Senku:

"Then stop being good at everything."

Rimuru stared.

"That is rude."

"Correct."

Kusuri whispered to Maomao:

"They do that a lot."

"Constantly."

The final structure remained provisional.

Coordination council.

Scoped roles.

Term review.

Emergency powers sunset automatically after crisis conditions ended unless renewed under public procedure.

No hereditary anything.

No personal city ownership.

No Mau exception because Continuum could not classify him.

Especially no Mau exception.

He insisted on that line himself.

Anko:

"You know writing 'no Mau exception' guarantees someone invents the phrase."

Mau:

"I hate governance."

Jinshi smiled.

"Good beginning."

"Stop saying that."

After the meeting, Frieren walked with Mau toward the repaired water route.

"Do you want the role?"

"Part of it."

"Which part?"

"Being useful."

There.

The dangerous core.

Frieren stopped.

Mau stopped too.

She said:

"You enjoy relieving people of pain."

He waited.

"That is not the same as being responsible for everyone."

Mau looked away.

"I know."

"Do you?"

Longer silence.

"More than before."

Enough for today.

The flaw remained possible precisely because Mau did not crave power.

He craved reducing burden.

People would hand authority to someone like that because it felt safe.

He might accept too much because refusal felt like abandoning them.

Leadership after the front did not solve that.

It made the risk visible enough to design around.

That was better than trust alone.
