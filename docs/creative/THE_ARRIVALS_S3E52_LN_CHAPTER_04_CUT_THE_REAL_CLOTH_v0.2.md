# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 32 — Cut the Real Cloth

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 32  
**Continuity state:** Rebuild weeks, after the memorial | The real house is drawn, not built: two floors, a cellar, two exits under every section, a room with no name

Wakana took the board down three weeks after the memorial.

Not the hooks. The hooks had been coming off it all season, one by one, as the Muslin was let out and taken in and turned and fixed. There were only four left now, and none of them mattered much. What he took down was the board itself: the whole thing, with every note anyone had ever chalked on it, from FOOD! CAN'T BE HEARD to TREATY SIGNED to The house goes around the tree. Not on it.

He laid it flat on the long table in the common room, under the shadowless light, and stood back.

"It's time," he said.

Everybody knew what he meant. Nobody had said it out loud since the Move.

Marin said it for him.

"The real one."

"Not build it," said Fern at once, from the head of the table, before anyone could get excited. "Not this year. There isn't the stone, there isn't the timber, and there isn't the money, and half the settlement is still fixing what the Last Defense broke."

"Not build it," Wakana agreed. "Draw it."

He took a clean sheet of paper the size of a door, and pinned it to the wall beside the board.

"Cut it," he said. "From everything the Muslin told us."



They argued for six evenings.

Not angrily. Not after the Last Defense. Everyone had come through the same three days, and everyone had stood at the same memorial for the same three people who had not. Something had gone out of the arguing that week and had not come back yet. What was left was careful.

Two floors. That came first, and fastest, because the Muslin had proved it was too long. Everything on one level had made sense when walls had to move every week. But a house that was always going to stay meant a house you could cross without walking forty-one steps around a puddle. Downstairs would be for everyone: the common room, the kitchen, the dining room, the music room with its buffer, the atelier, Wakana's workshop at the noisy end, and Maomao's medical room at the far end from the kitchen, because Maomao's rules about chemicals were not negotiable and never had been.

Upstairs would be for sleeping.

And upstairs would be drawn in sections.

"Like the Muslin," said Wakana. "Not one room each in a long row. Groups. With the walls between them made to come out."

"Why?"

"Because they always do," said Wakana simply. "Every group we put in separate rooms ended up wanting the walls gone. The four of them. The witches' girls. Anko and Nazuna. Mau and Frieren and Rimuru." He tapped the board, where the hooks had been. "So the walls between the people who want to be together come out on purpose, from the start. And the ones between the people who need quiet are thick."

The east section went in first, because nobody would have dared draw anything else first.

Mau and Frieren's room at the very east end, with the window facing the morning light, exactly where the light would land. The library bay, built in, two walls of shelves, with the reading chair Mau had said no to drawn into the plan in ink. The closet in the inner corner, with a loft above it. Rimuru was consulted and said "Yes. Please. The same," in a voice that made Frieren look up from her book.

Ori through one wall. Bocchi and the music room's buffer through the other, except that the music room was downstairs now and Bocchi would have to choose. She chose upstairs, next to Mau, with a thick wall, and a stair straight down to the music room so she could go there at night without waking anyone. Milim at the end, with a window that looked back down the corridor at Rimuru's office door. Yuta and Maki across the corridor, one room, one name on the plan written as two.

"The shelf," said Mau.

"What about it?"

"Where does the shelf go?"

Frieren looked at him for a long moment.

"We'll argue about it," she said, "when there's a wall to put it against."

Wakana did not draw the shelf.



The rest of the upstairs took four evenings.

The big room for Hakari, Karane, Shizuka, Nano and Kusuri. Five beds, no walls, and one thick wall between them and everyone else, "for the good of the house," said Fern. Kusuri's chemistry stayed downstairs at Maomao's bench.

The Witch Hat girls' room, with an alcove built into one corner. It had a real door, and the door had a curtain over it, dark green with silver stars, because Tetia said the curtain was coming whether there was a door or not. Agott, consulted, said the alcove was unnecessary. Nobody took it out of the plan. She did not ask them to. There was a fourth bed drawn by the wall, too, unlabelled. Tetia had insisted on it, for Richeh, in case she ever stopped being a guest. Richeh looked at it on the plan for a long time and said nothing, which for Richeh was a great deal.

Qifrey between his students and the east section. Exactly between them, measured.

Umaru and Suika in the room next to the Witch Hat girls'. Umaru had demanded, as a condition of the treaty's renewal, that the plan include *Umaru's Door, Mark Two*: a short covered bridge across the upstairs landing that went straight from her door to the east section in eleven steps. Wakana drew it. Umaru counted it on the paper with two fingers.

Anko and Nazuna's room on the north side, where the light came least. The high loft was drawn in with its black curtain all the way round, and Anko's bed below.

Fern's room near the top of the main stair, where she could hear everything. Stark's beside it. He asked for nothing else. Fern pretended not to notice, with great concentration, exactly as she had the first time.

Himmel's room on the road side, facing the way people would come.

Mikasa's room with two exits.

Senku's room next to his workroom downstairs, because Fern had said, months ago, that Senku would not sleep in the workshop, and Fern did not forget things. It had a door that Fern could lock from the outside. Senku objected to the lock in writing. Fern added a second lock.

And at the turn of the upstairs corridor, a narrow room. The plan said REM in pencil, because when Wakana went to ink it, Rem said "Pencil," and nobody argued.



The cellar was Senku's idea and Aira's insistence.

Before the Last Defense, nobody would have put one in. Cellars were for storage. Root vegetables. Wine nobody had.

Then a device had sat for five weeks under the central refuge, in the sublevel, inside a wall built from a pallet of stone that nobody had checked. It had sat there, quiet, through every drill, until the night it mattered.

"Nothing goes under this house," said Aira, "that we can't walk to."

So the plan got a cellar. Under the whole house. Not for vegetables. For everything that a house hides. Every drain pipe, every water line, every glyph Qifrey and his students ever drew into the floors, every beam that held up anything, all of it would run through a low, lit, walkable space where anyone could go down with a lamp and look at it. A maintenance floor. Boring. Ugly. Checkable.

"And exits," said Mikasa.

Everyone looked at her.

Mikasa was standing in front of the plan with her arms folded. She was looking at it the way she looked at everything, the way that had kept a great many people alive in a world that killed them easily.

"During the Last Defense, two of the evacuation routes failed," she said. "The ones everybody expected to work. They failed because everybody expected them to work, and so did whoever planned against us." She put her finger on the cellar. "If there's a floor under the whole house, then every section can have a stair down to it. And from the cellar, two ways out. One toward the refuge. One away from it. Under every group of rooms. So nobody upstairs is ever more than one stair and one tunnel from outside."

The common room was very quiet.

"That's a lot of digging," said Senku finally.

"Yes," said Mikasa.

"Years of it."

"Yes."

Senku looked at the plan for a long moment.

Then he took the pencil out of Wakana's hand and drew the first tunnel himself.



Eren had come to watch on the third evening. He did not say anything. He was not family, and he knew it, and he sat at the very end of the long table where the light did not quite reach and watched the household argue about bridges and alcoves and locks for Senku's door.

On the sixth evening, when Mikasa drew the exits, he spoke for the first time.

"You're building exits," he said, "before you've built rooms."

Mikasa did not turn around.

"Yes."

Eren looked at the plan. At the house drawn around a circle of small stakes for a tree that would not be tall enough to sit under for a hundred years. At the cellar, and the tunnels, and the two ways out under every section. At the place where, in another world, someone would have drawn a wall.

"Good," he said.

He did not say anything else. But he stayed until the end.



The last thing was Mau's.

He had not asked for anything all six evenings. Everybody had noticed. Several people had tried to make him, and he had said, "I've got everything," and meant it, and nobody could argue with that, though Frieren gave him a look.

On the sixth evening, when the plan was almost finished, he stood up and went to it. He took the pencil from Senku, who was still holding it, and found a space at the far end of the upstairs corridor, past Qifrey, past the bridge, where nobody had drawn anything.

He drew a room.

An ordinary room. Not large. One window.

He did not write a name on it.

"Who's that for?" said Fern.

"Nobody yet," said Mau.

Fern looked at him.

"Arrival House is for strangers," he said. "That's right. That's how it should be. Nobody should have to live with us just because they arrived." He looked at the empty room on the plan. "But sometimes somebody arrives and doesn't stay a stranger. And there should be a room for them already, before anyone has to ask. Like the one at the turn. Empty. Somewhere they can find for themselves."

Across the table, Ori smiled. She did not hide it.

Rem, who slept at the turn most nights now and at the old inn the rest, looked down at her blue-rimmed cup and did not look up for some time.

Wakana took the pencil back and drew one more line around the empty room. It was not a wall. It was the dotted line he used for things that could still change.

Then, at the very top of the plan, he drew one more thing: a line around the whole roof, dotted, a full storey above the second floor.

"What's that?"

"Someday," said Wakana.

Rimuru laughed out loud.

"Someday," Fern agreed. "Not yet."

"Not yet," said Wakana.

He pinned the plan to the wall of the common room, beside the empty space where the board had been, under the shadowless light. Everyone stood and looked at it for a long time: the house that would be cut from the Muslin someday, after stone, after money, after the settlement had finished mending. Two floors, a cellar, a tree, two ways out under every room, and one room with no name.

"Now we know where to cut," said Wakana.
