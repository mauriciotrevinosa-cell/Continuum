# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 33 — Threshold

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 33  
**Continuity state:** Weeks into recovery | Distinct Noise overload begins | Mau reports symptoms before collapse

The later Noise overload began with Mau saying:

"Something's wrong."

No disappearance.

No secret route.

No waking up miles away.

Just a sentence.

That alone proved learning.

He was in Senku's work room comparing pressure readings from three rebuilt infrastructure points.

Boring work.

Excellent.

Senku had designed the exercise partly to see whether Mau could distinguish:
measurement;
intuition;
Noise-provided pattern.

Mau hated that this was necessary.

He did it anyway.

"Point C is drifting."

Senku looked at the gauge.

"It isn't."

Mau stopped.

Good.

Did not insist.

He checked the page.

"Then I thought it was."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

A term arrived internally.

Not heard.

Not spoken.

Known.

INTEGRATION LOAD.

Mau's hand froze over the pencil.

Senku saw.

"What?"

Mau looked at him.

Old instinct:
test silently.

New instinct:
report.

"Integration load."

Senku did not ask what it meant.

He wrote the words.

"Source?"

"The Noise."

"Confidence?"

"That the term appeared? High. That it means anything? Low."

Senku looked almost proud.

Annoying.

"Symptoms?"

Mau checked.

Not performed for drama.

Body scan.

"Headache."

"Memory mismatch?"

He looked at the room.

Table.

Window.

Senku.

Wrong cup.

Wait.

His cup.

"None I can identify."

"Body?"

"Fine."

"Vision?"

"Too sharp."

Senku put the pencil down.

"Protocol."

Mau exhaled.

"Yeah."

He did not argue.

That was new too.

Rimuru arrived first.

Then Maomao.

Not sprinting.

Fast enough.

Kaneki.

Qifrey.

Frieren.

Ori remained outside until asked.

Important.

No automatic crowd because Mau had symptoms.

Maomao took temperature.

Pulse.

Pupils.

No fever.

Rimuru checked with Ciel against the old containment observations.

"Activity elevated."

"The seal?"

Rimuru listened.

"Holding. She says the pressure isn't coming from behind the seal. It's coming from the trunk around it."

Mau looked at him.

"The Noise?"

"Whatever pattern Ciel tracks under that working designation."

Good.

No accidental reification.

Qifrey checked mana behavior.

"Responsive, not unstable yet."

Kaneki:

"Back?"

"Fine."

Frieren watched Mau's hands.

Then his face.

His left eye had opened. Black and red. It did not close again.

Not his answers.

He noticed.

"What?"

"You rub your thumb when you're hiding pain."

Mau looked down.

He was.

"Headache worse."

Maomao wrote it.

Senku showed the term.

INTEGRATION LOAD.

Nobody treated it as diagnosis.

Mau lay down before anyone forced him.

The room noticed.

No collapse.

No argument.

He asked:

"Can Ori come in?"

Rimuru looked at Maomao.

She looked at Mau.

"Do you want another person?"

"Yes."

Ori entered.

Not because she was reality itself.

Because Mau chose one more familiar reference.

He looked at everyone.

"Too many."

Good.

Protocol adapted.

Senku left.

Qifrey left after baseline.

Kaneki stayed nearby, not inside.

Rimuru reduced active conversation.

Maomao stayed.

Frieren stayed.

Ori stayed.

Three people plus monitoring.

The Noise offered another term.

CROSS-SYSTEM CONTENTION.

Mau closed his eyes.

"Another."

Rimuru wrote it.

No belief.

No interpretation yet.

Mau's perception shifted.

Mana became flow.

Cursed energy became pressure.

Biology became geometry.

Not metaphor.

Too literal.

He opened his eyes and looked at Frieren.

For half a second her face was not face.

Systems.

Channels.

Structures.

Mau recoiled so hard he hit the headboard.

Frieren did not touch him.

"What did you see?"

"Too much."

"Me?"

"Not you."

Wrong.

He corrected.

"Yes. You. But not as you."

Frieren understood less than she wanted.

Stayed anyway.

This was not the memory crisis.

Mau knew his name.

Knew the room.

Knew Frieren.

Memory stable.

The repaired places are holding, said Ciel, because he asked. All of them. The scars too.

Perception failing differently.

Prepared people recognized the difference.

That mattered.

The old crisis had taught them procedures.

The new one tested whether they could use those procedures without forcing every new symptom into an old story.

Mau breathed.

"Something's wrong."

Again.

No shame.

No surprise.

The sentence had become a tool.
