# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 18
## Chapter 39 — Protocol

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 18  
**Reading order:** 39  
**Continuity state:** Noise overload active | Protocol adapted from Memory crisis without assuming same cause

Protocol meant sequence.

That was why it helped.

Not certainty.

Not cure.

Sequence.

Maomao read from the card Fern had insisted they write after the Memory crisis.

"Reduce stimuli."

One lamp off.

Curtain half closed.

Door shut.

"No Construction."

Mau nodded.

"No new system exposure."

Senku, outside:

"Rude."

Maomao ignored him.

"Hydration."

Frieren handed Mau the cup.

He stared at the water too long.

"What?"

she asked.

"It looks like a fluid model."

She waited.

Mau blinked.

"Still water."

Good.

He drank.

"Temperature."

Normal.

"Memory checks only when useful."

Not every five minutes.

No interrogation loop.

"One speaker."

Everyone looked at everyone else.

Rimuru raised one hand.

"I'll coordinate."

Maomao:

"Only if you stop answering medical questions."

"Fine."

"Document Noise terms without treating them as truth."

Rimuru looked at the page.

INTEGRATION LOAD.

CROSS-SYSTEM CONTENTION.

Labels.

Not revelations.

Ciel monitored the same internal activity she had mapped during containment months earlier, and the seal beside it, because Mau had asked her to.

The seal held.

The pattern pressed against the guardrails the way water presses against a dam: not attacking, only full.

Elevated.

Patterned.

Still not identified.

Kaneki remained outside until body symptoms appeared.

Qifrey left after confirming no magical instability required his presence.

Senku monitored the external room from the hall.

Ori stayed because Mau had asked.

Frieren stayed because Mau had asked.

Maomao stayed because she was Maomao.

Mau looked around.

"Still too many."

No one took offense.

Important.

Rimuru:

"Who leaves?"

Mau looked at Ori.

Stopped.

Old guilt.

Ori saw it.

"Say it."

Mau swallowed.

"You."

Frieren looked at him.

Ori did not.

She nodded.

"Okay."

Mau's face changed.

Ori noticed.

"I'm not being rejected."

"I know."

"Do you?"

He breathed.

"Trying."

"Good."

She stood.

At the door she added:

"If you need me, ask."

Then left.

No martyrdom.

No romantic triangle.

No guilt tax.

The room got quieter.

Mau's shoulders dropped.

Protocol had worked before anyone touched his biology.

That mattered.

The Noise produced another internal certainty.

ALIGNMENT FAILURE.

Mau said it.

Rimuru wrote it.

"Meaning?"

"I don't know."

Good.

No forced interpretation.

Then Mau's perception changed again.

Frieren's mana lines appeared as layered currents.

Not visible exactly.

Known visually.

The room's protective circle became geometry over geometry.

Maomao's pulse became rhythm he could predict before feeling.

Rimuru became impossible.

Too many systems at once.

Mau shut his eyes.

"Worse."

"Memory?"

"No."

"Identity?"

"I'm Mau."

"Where?"

"Medical room."

"Who stayed?"

"Frieren. You. Maomao."

Good.

Memory intact.

Perception not.

Different crisis.

Maomao moved one chair.

Tiny sound.

Mau flinched.

"Sorry."

"Don't apologize. Just stop moving things."

She stopped.

Outside, Senku sent a written question through the door instead of entering.

DO EXTERNAL READINGS CHANGE WHEN MAU REPORTS TERMS?

Rimuru checked.

Some.

Not all.

Correlation.

No cause.

He wrote back:

YES / INCONSISTENT.

Good.

Kusuri sent another note beneath it.

ANY CHEMICAL EXPOSURE TODAY?

Maomao answered:

NONE NEW.

Kusuri replied:

GOOD.

No surprise sedative.

No experiment.

No scientist deciding crisis granted access.

Mau's breathing sped.

Frieren saw.

"Look at me."

He opened his eyes.

Bad idea.

Her face became systems again.

He looked away.

"No."

Frieren adjusted immediately.

"Listen to me."

Better.

"Window."

Mau listened.

"Rain."

No rain.

Wind through leaves.

"Wind."

"Door."

Still.

"Maomao."

Pen scratch.

"Rimuru."

Too much.

He laughed once.

"Rimuru sounds complicated."

Rimuru:

"I am offended."

Mau laughed harder.

Pain.

Then calmer.

Protocol was not sterile.

Humor counted as regulation if it did not become avoidance.

Hours passed.

No collapse.

No disappearance.

No containment.

The Noise stayed loud.

Then changed.

Instead of terms, it began mapping relationships between systems.

Mana could be treated like flow.

Cursed energy like pressure.

Biology like adaptive geometry.

Construction like externalized model.

Mau felt the categories trying to merge.

Senku's phrase from outside came through the door when Rimuru summarized.

"Overfitting."

Mau opened one eye.

"What?"

"Your system may be trying to create one model that explains incompatible systems."

Rimuru:

"Maybe."

Senku:

"Obviously maybe."

No grand diagnosis.

Working hypothesis.

Mau closed his eyes again.

"Tell it to stop."

Nobody could.

That helplessness remained.

But now helpless did not mean unstructured.

The protocol gave them:
what to reduce;
what to watch;
what not to assume;
who had authority for which question;
when to escalate.

Not cure.

A floor.

Mau stayed on it.

That night, the Noise was still loud.

Memory still stable.

The difference mattered enough to keep the panic from becoming an old story wearing a new face.
