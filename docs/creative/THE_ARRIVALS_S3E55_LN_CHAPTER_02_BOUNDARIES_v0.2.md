# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 2 — Boundaries

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 2  
**Continuity state:** Three days after voice | Goddess demonstrates spatial definition without yet starting Trial

The bells were followed by lines.

Not walls.

Worse.

Lines of pale light appeared in the air around the settlement perimeter.

Thin.

Vertical only where terrain demanded.

Horizontal across open field.

They did not connect like a cage.

They implied one.

Somewhere far down inside Mau, a wheel turned.

One notch.

The same heavy click he had heard once before, on the night after Stark's birthday. He knew it the way you know a voice.

I looked, said Ciel, very quietly. The wheel. One eighth. Toward the same kind of thing as before.

"Target?"

Present.

Mau looked at the lines of light standing in the air over the fields.

Not here yet, he had written in his notebook.

It was here now.

The wheel did not turn further. One eighth of a turn was not adaptation. It was recognition.

Senku reached the nearest marker first with three instruments and no sense of self-preservation.

Mikasa stopped him two meters short.

"Wait."

"I am waiting."

"You are moving."

"Slowly."

"Stop."

He stopped.

Suika arrived behind him carrying the secondary notebook.

Good.

Independent observer.

Qifrey approached from another angle.

"No circle."

Senku:

"That was fast."

"There is nothing to read."

"That's different."

Good.

Rimuru reached the line.

"Ciel?"

No ordinary barrier characteristics.

No clear energy transfer.

No known local system signature.

Helpful only in excluding easy answers.

Rimuru extended one hand.

Frieren:

"Don't."

He looked at her.

"I can test."

"With your whole hand?"

Rimuru withdrew one finger.

"Better?"

"No."

Mau almost laughed.

Anko solved it.

"Rope."

They tied a cloth strip to a pole.

Extended through.

Nothing.

Pulled back.

Normal.

Then Rimuru crossed.

One step.

Two.

Outside.

Turned.

Walked back.

Normal.

Milim crossed because waiting had become emotionally impossible.

Normal.

Diablo.

Normal.

Mikasa sent a non-Otherworlder volunteer only after consent.

Normal.

Then the lines vanished.

No attack.

No trap.

No result.

Everyone hated it.

Senku wrote coordinates.

"Showing capability."

Anko looked at him.

"Interpretation."

"Yes."

Qifrey:

"Could be measurement."

"Also interpretation."

Mau looked toward Ori.

She was twenty meters away with Nano.

Everyone else looked too.

Ori saw.

Her face hardened.

"Stop."

Several people looked away.

Not enough.

Ori walked closer.

"I am not an instrument."

Nobody had said she was.

The meeting had still begun making her one.

Senku closed his notebook.

That mattered.

Ori looked at the map of boundary coordinates.

"You can study the lines."

She pointed at herself.

"You ask me before studying me."

Clear.

Rimuru nodded.

"Yes."

Mau stayed quiet.

Good.

Ori looked at him.

"You too."

"I know."

"Say it."

Mau breathed.

"I ask."

Better.

The group reconvened in Arrival House because Ori chose it.

Not the council room.

Reason:
she wanted a door she could leave through.

Jinshi noticed.

No comment.

They defined contact boundaries.

If stillness formed around Ori:
do not speak for her unless immediate physical safety required it.

Do not ask her to summon, provoke or test the Goddess.

Do not use her origin as public evidence without consent.

If the entity makes claims about Ori:
Ori's testimony about herself remains primary where the claim concerns her choice, experience or identity.

Senku asked:

"What if the claim is factual about construction?"

Ori thought.

"You can investigate the fact."

"And ownership?"

"No."

Easy.

Mau wrote the last line himself:

ORIGIN DOES NOT EQUAL AUTHORITY.

Frieren read it.

Said nothing.

Outside, workers repaired the west road.

The line demonstration had changed nothing practical.

That was part of its power.

The Goddess could interrupt reality without paying the cost of cleanup.

The settlement still had to finish mortar.

Before leaving, Ori stood at the threshold of Arrival House.

She looked back at Mau.

"If it comes through me again?"

Mau answered carefully.

"I wait for you."

"Even if you're scared?"

"Especially then."

Ori nodded.

Not comforted.

But heard.

The family had started preparing for divine contact by writing rules about themselves.

Not rules for the Goddess.

They had no power to enforce those yet.

But they could decide what they would refuse to become while afraid.
