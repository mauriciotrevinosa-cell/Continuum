# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 4 — Seven Options

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 4  
**Continuity state:** Trial domain sustained | External pressure approaching | Seven non-sacrificial options attempted before Mau agrees to participate

They tried seven options.

Not seven dramatic attacks.

Seven actual alternatives.

The distinction mattered.

First:
Rimuru.

Structural analysis.

Ciel mapped every detectable relation between interior space and boundary.

Then Rimuru attempted to separate the asserted rule from the space containing it.

Nothing.

Not resisted.

The operation simply produced no relevant change.

Rimuru stopped before brute force replaced analysis.

Good.

Second:
Qifrey.

Rule-writing.

He treated the boundary like unfamiliar magic.

Looked for stroke.

Anchor.

Intent.

Maintenance.

Nothing.

No circle.

No material carrier.

No place to put a correction.

He hated it.

Stopped anyway.

Third:
Diablo.

Soul-space logic.

He attempted movement through a layer ordinary geometry should not constrain.

Returned to origin.

His expression after was more frightening than the failed attempt.

"It did not defeat the path."

Anko:

"What then?"

"It defined the path as irrelevant."

Nobody liked that sentence.

Fourth:
Milim.

Force.

Everyone objected.

Milim objected louder.

They compromised:
controlled output toward empty boundary zone.

Power gathered.

For one heartbeat the domain displayed:

TRIAL-RELEVANT OUTPUT: 0.

Milim's power vanished from availability.

Not drained.

Not stolen.

Unavailable.

Then returned.

Milim went still.

That scared everyone.

She stepped back voluntarily.

Fifth:
Senku.

Physical discontinuity.

Lasers were unavailable.

Most precision tools primitive.

He used what existed:
line-of-sight markers;
weights;
water;
sound;
timing;
people at multiple points.

No measurable edge he could exploit.

Light crossed strangely without detectable refraction.

Thrown objects returned.

Sound did not reveal an outside.

"Physics is being told what answer to give."

Working description.

Not metaphysics.

Sixth:
Eren.

Language.

He stared at the refusal condition.

"Protection withdrawn does not mean attack."

Good.

"Maybe the choice is being framed to make participation feel necessary."

Also good.

They asked the Goddess to state:
what protection;
from what;
whether existing danger had been altered;
whether refusal caused new danger;
whether delay changed risk.

One answer.

EXISTING EXTERNAL CONDITIONS CONTINUE.

Worse.

The monsters were real.

Not necessarily created for coercion.

But the domain was suspending defenses while real danger advanced.

Mau's jaw tightened.

Seventh:
wait.

No test.

No power.

Do nothing and see whether assertion decayed.

It did not.

One hour.

Then another.

Outside observation degraded further.

A Searcher at the east limit could still see movement in the distance.

Closer.

Fern started relocating the most vulnerable toward Last Defense again.

The city had just survived using those systems.

Now the domain was making them less available.

Mau stood in the council room.

Nobody had asked him to.

He had spent every attempt hoping somebody smarter, stronger, older or stranger would find a door.

No one did.

Frieren stood across from him.

"Anything else?"

Mau asked the room.

Silence.

Rimuru hated silence.

He asked Ciel again.

Nothing new.

Qifrey stared at his hands.

Senku stared at a page with seven failures.

Milim looked ready to cry from uselessness and would kill anyone who said so.

Ori looked at Mau.

Not pleading.

That helped.

Mau inhaled.

"If I participate, does settlement protection restore?"

The Goddess answered.

"Yes."

"Immediately?"

"Upon entry."

"Does anyone else enter physically?"

"No."

Frieren:

"Constructs?"

No answer yet.

Mau looked at everyone.

"I participate."

Frieren's face broke.

Not because he had acted secretly.

Because even the right process could reach a terrible outcome.

Mau walked to her.

"I'm not doing this because I think my life matters less."

Frieren's eyes filled.

"Do you believe that?"

Mau thought.

Long enough to hurt.

"More than I used to."

Not enough.

Real.

Rimuru asked:

"Do you understand we are not asking you to?"

"Yes."

Ori:

"Do you understand I didn't ask?"

"Yes."

Fern:

"Do you understand if another option appears before entry, we take it?"

"Yes."

Consent checked under coercive conditions.

Imperfect.

Still better than lone sacrifice.

The boundary text changed.

ENTRY AVAILABLE.

Mau looked at Frieren.

"I'll come back."

She closed her eyes.

"Don't promise what you don't know."

Memory Arc had taught them that too.

Mau corrected.

"I'll try."

Frieren touched his face.

"That I believe."

The domain opened.

Not like a portal.

Like reality deciding Mau belonged somewhere else.

The settlement vanished.

The protections returned behind him.

He did not see that.

The others did.

The first thing Mau saw was a road.

The second was Frieren bleeding ahead.

The Trial had begun.
