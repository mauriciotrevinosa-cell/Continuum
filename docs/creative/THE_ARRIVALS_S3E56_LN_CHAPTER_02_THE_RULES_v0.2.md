# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 6 — The Rules

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 6  
**Continuity state:** Trial entry | Scenario ontology bounded publicly | Participant looping rule established | First scenario begins immediately

The rules appeared one line at a time.

That made them worse.

SCENARIOS ARE CONSTRUCTED WITHIN TRIAL DOMAIN.

Mau read it twice.

Frieren beside him.

Rimuru behind.

Everyone still present for the rules.

Good.

"Constructed means not physically real?"

The Goddess answered:

"Constructed does not mean consequence-free."

Not answer.

Anko:

"External residents?"

Next line appeared.

EXTERNAL RESIDENTS ARE NOT PHYSICALLY TRANSFERRED INTO SCENARIOS.

Mau exhaled.

Frieren did not.

"Then a Frieren inside isn't her?"

The Goddess:

"Correct."

Relief.

Then:

PARTICIPANT WILL NOT BE INFORMED DURING SCENARIO WHETHER A PERSON IS CONSTRUCTED OR EXTERNAL.

Mau stared.

Rimuru:

"You just said external residents aren't transferred."

"Correct."

"Then all scenario people are constructed."

"No."

Silence.

Anko understood.

"External effects can enter without physical transfer?"

No answer.

The wording was deliberately precise and deliberately insufficient.

Mau rubbed his thumb.

Frieren saw.

Next:

DEATH OF PARTICIPANT RETURNS PARTICIPANT TO TRIAL ANCHOR.

Mau's hand stopped.

MEMORY OF PRIOR LOOPS REMAINS.

Frieren:

"No."

The word did nothing.

CONSTRUCTS MAY RESET.

Mau asked:

"How many?"

No answer.

"How long can a scenario last?"

No answer.

"Can I end the Trial without completing it?"

No answer.

"What is completion?"

The Goddess replied:

"Understanding."

Senku, outside the participant boundary:

"Define understanding."

No answer.

Of course.

Next:

EACH SCENARIO CONTAINS LOSS.

Mau felt something inside him turn cold.

PARTICIPANT MAY ACCEPT PERSONAL LOSS OR ALLOW DESIGNATED OTHER LOSS.

There.

The trap in plain language.

Mau looked at Frieren.

She looked furious enough to burn a god she could not locate.

"This is not a choice," she said.

The Goddess answered:

"It is a choice."

"No. It is coercion between losses."

Silence.

Ori stepped forward.

"Why him?"

The Goddess:

"Because he asks the question."

Mau:

"What question?"

No answer.

Good.

The rule text remained.

Mau tried another angle.

"If I refuse an individual scenario?"

"Scenario persists."

"If I do nothing?"

"Loss occurs."

"If I find another solution?"

Pause.

"Permitted."

Mau looked at everyone.

There.

A door.

Maybe.

Not binary by absolute rule.

The scenarios could contain a third option.

The Trial was not promising one.

Mau latched onto that.

Of course he did.

Frieren knew.

"Don't make finding a third option the same as having to save everyone."

Mau looked at her.

"That's literally what—"

"No."

She stepped closer.

"If there is another way, find it. If there isn't, that doesn't make everything your fault."

Mau wanted to believe her.

The domain changed.

The plaza vanished.

No transition.

Road.

Rain.

Frieren ahead.

Bleeding.

Real enough that Mau's body forgot every rule immediately.

"Mau."

Her voice.

Exactly wrong.

Exactly right.

He ran.

Behind him, the Trial anchor existed somewhere he could no longer see.

The first scenario had begun before he had finished being afraid of the rules.
