# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 14 — Every Answer Costs Someone

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 14  
**Continuity state:** Mid-Trial | Mau has died dozens of times | Trial shifts from death-choice binaries to consequence patterns

The Trial stopped asking only:

Who dies?

That would have been easier.

One scenario placed Mau in a room with three exits.

Frieren behind one.

A child Mau did not know behind another.

Rimuru behind the third.

Only two could open.

Mau searched for a fourth mechanism.

There wasn't one.

He chose Frieren and the child.

Rimuru died.

Anchor.

Next loop, same room.

Different arrangement.

Mau chose Rimuru and the child.

Frieren died.

Anchor.

Next.

He chose Frieren and Rimuru.

The child died.

Anchor.

Mau came back furious.

"There's no right answer."

The Goddess did not respond.

That was the lesson.

Next scenario:

Nobody died.

Worse.

Mau chose who received the only route home.

Bocchi went.

The construct survived.

Years passed in minutes around Mau.

Bocchi returned older.

Not physically.

Emotionally.

She looked at him and said:

"I spent my whole life wondering whether I abandoned you."

Anchor.

Mau stared at empty white.

"That wasn't death."

No answer.

Next:

He made Bocchi stay.

Years again.

She loved people in Continuum.

She built a life.

Then one night she asked:

"What if my family needed me?"

Anchor.

Mau began to understand the new cruelty.

The Trial was no longer proving that Mau would die for people.

It was teaching him that **survival did not prevent regret**.

Next:

Ori chose herself.

She lived.

Mau lost access to her.

She was happy somewhere he could not reach.

He experienced years of not knowing.

Anchor.

Next:

Ori chose Mau.

She stayed.

Later she wondered whether the choice had been hers or whether attachment to Mau had made it for her.

Anchor.

Mau shouted:

"Stop."

The Goddess did not.

Next:

A stranger chose Return.

Their memories of Continuum faded into something dreamlike.

They were happier.

Anchor.

Next:

Same stranger.

Same choice.

This time they spent decades grieving people they could barely remember.

Anchor.

"Those are contradictory."

The Goddess answered for the first time in several loops.

"Yes."

Mau went still.

The Trial did not claim every choice produced the same result.

It produced different people.

Different contexts.

Different aftermaths.

No universal optimization.

That should have taught Mau humility.

Intellectually, it did.

Emotionally, something worse happened.

He began trying to memorize consequences.

Choice A.

Possible grief.

Choice B.

Possible guilt.

Choice C.

Possible death.

Choice D.

Unknown.

If he could see enough variations, maybe he could protect real people later.

That thought formed quietly.

No one outside heard it.

The Goddess did.

Next scenario:

Frieren chose to stay with Mau.

She was happy.

Years.

Real enough.

Then one evening construct-Frieren said:

"I still wonder."

Mau's chest tightened.

"About what?"

"Home."

Anchor.

Next:

Frieren returned.

Mau stayed.

The Trial made him live decades without her.

No death.

No reset until the end.

He learned what an empty chair could become over thirty years.

Anchor.

When he returned, Mau did not scream.

He asked:

"How many outcomes are there?"

The Goddess answered:

"More than you can carry."

Mau looked up.

Wrong challenge.

Because by then, carrying them was exactly what he had decided to try.
