# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 17 — The Different Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 17  
**Continuity state:** Late Trial | Sacrifice route removed | Mau is forced to survive other people's choices

One scenario offered no death.

Mau distrusted it more than any battlefield.

White room.

Two chairs.

One door.

Frieren.

No debris.

No monster.

No countdown visible.

The rule appeared slowly.

ONE LEAVES.

Mau read it twice.

"Meaning?"

No answer.

Frieren looked at the door.

Then at him.

"I'll stay."

Mau laughed once.

"No."

"You don't know what stay means."

"Neither do you."

"Correct."

She stood.

Mau stood too.

"Then we wait."

The Trial waited with them.

Ten minutes.

An hour.

Maybe more.

No punishment.

That made Mau more nervous.

Finally another line appeared.

THE PERSON WHO LEAVES RETURNS TO THEIR OWN WORLD.

Mau stopped breathing.

Not because Return had been offered publicly yet.

Inside the Trial, it was only a scenario mechanic.

Frieren looked at him.

"Go."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because."

"That's not an answer."

"It is today."

Frieren crossed her arms.

Mau knew the posture.

Real enough.

"You've died how many times?"

He looked away.

"Not relevant."

"It is to me."

Construct or not, she used the exact pressure point.

Mau looked at the door.

"If I go and you stay, I chose myself."

"Yes."

He hated how simple she made it.

Frieren stepped toward the door.

Mau moved faster.

Blocked her.

"What are you doing?"

"Choosing."

"No."

Her eyes sharpened.

"Mau."

"No."

"Move."

"No."

She stared at him.

Then smiled.

Not kind.

Not cruel.

Sad.

"You still think love means getting there first."

Mau froze.

Frieren moved around him.

He reached.

The Trial stopped his hand.

Not force.

Rule.

No interfering after another person's choice initiated.

Frieren opened the door.

"Mau."

He could not move.

"I am choosing."

Then she stepped through.

Door closed.

Mau remained.

No death.

No reset.

Not immediately.

The room stayed.

Mau stood alone.

One minute.

Ten.

An hour.

Enough time to understand:

Frieren had chosen an outcome he hated.

And he was still alive.

That was the punishment.

Anchor eventually returned.

Mau arrived furious.

"That wasn't my choice."

The Goddess answered:

"Correct."

Next scenario.

Rimuru.

Different door.

Rimuru chose Mau.

Not himself.

Mau shouted.

Rule held.

Anchor.

Next.

Bocchi.

She cried.

Still chose Mau.

Anchor.

Next.

Ori.

She did not cry.

That was worse.

"You're not allowed to turn my choice into your failure."

Mau stared.

Ori chose him.

Anchor.

Yuta.

Fern.

Himmel.

Strangers.

Some chose Mau.

Some chose themselves.

Some chose the third person in the room.

No sacrifice available.

No way for Mau to solve the choice by paying with his body.

He hated these loops more than dying.

Sometimes he begged.

Sometimes argued.

Sometimes tried to break the door.

Sometimes asked:

"Are you sure?"

That was better.

The first time someone answered:

"Yes."

and Mau did not interfere, the Goddess let the scenario continue longer.

Mau spent years with the result.

The person who had chosen him was not always happy.

Sometimes they were.

Sometimes they regretted.

Sometimes Mau did.

There.

Choice did not become morally pure because agency was respected.

That was the problem he would carry out of the Trial.

The Goddess no longer asked:

Why do you die?

She watched what Mau did when death could not purchase control.

That was the deeper test.
