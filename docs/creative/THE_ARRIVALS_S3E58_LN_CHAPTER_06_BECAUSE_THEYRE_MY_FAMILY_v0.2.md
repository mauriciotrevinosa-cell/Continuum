# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 18 — Because They're My Family

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 18  
**Continuity state:** End of sacrificial-loop phase | Hundreds of subjective outcomes accumulated

The scenarios stopped without warning.

Mau remained standing at anchor.

No door.

No fire.

No bridge.

No person asking him to choose.

Silence hurt.

His body waited for the next condition.

Nothing.

Mau looked down.

Hands intact.

Chest intact.

No blood.

He could remember pain in places that had never been injured in the same body twice.

That sentence no longer made sense.

He accepted it anyway.

The Goddess asked:

"Why do you continue to accept personal loss?"

Mau laughed.

The question felt almost childish now.

Hundreds of loops.

Hundreds of versions.

Dying had become the easiest category.

"Because I can come back here."

"No."

Mau looked up.

Right.

That had not been true in the first loops.

He had chosen himself before he knew reset was reliable.

The Goddess asked again.

"Why not allow them to die?"

Faces arrived faster than Mau wanted.

Frieren.

Bocchi.

Ori.

Rimuru.

Strangers.

Enemies.

People he had saved only to watch regret something later.

People who had chosen him and made him furious by surviving him.

Mau closed his eyes.

"Because they're my family."

The Goddess did not answer.

Mau opened his eyes.

"Some of them."

Pause.

"Some weren't."

The stranger.

The hostile fighter.

People whose names he never learned.

Mau's voice lowered.

"I don't know how to make somebody's life cheap enough that watching them die becomes easy."

That was closer.

The Goddess:

"And your own?"

Mau said nothing.

Outside, nobody moved.

Real Frieren's face broke.

The question had always been there.

Mau looked at his hands.

"I know."

The Goddess:

"Do you?"

Mau's mouth tightened.

The old words again.

He understood the sentence.

He did not live it consistently.

Mau whispered:

"I don't need a better reason to protect them."

"Correct."

Relief arrived too fast.

One breath.

Maybe two.

Mau's shoulders dropped.

Then the Goddess continued.

"That was not the question."

Mau looked up.

White space changed.

Not into another death trap.

Into the settlement.

People walking.

Water moving.

Arrival House.

Searchers leaving without Mau.

Bocchi playing.

Fern deciding something.

Frieren reading.

Ori laughing with Coco.

Life.

The Goddess asked:

"Why must protection require you to decide the cost?"

Mau went still.

The Trial had spent hundreds of loops removing every easy answer.

Now it asked the one he could not solve by dying.
