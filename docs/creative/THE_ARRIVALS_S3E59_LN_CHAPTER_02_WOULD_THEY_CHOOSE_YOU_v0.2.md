# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 20 — Would They Choose You?

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 20  
**Continuity state:** Final Trial question | Return/Stay becomes relevant | Mau answers uncertainty honestly rather than claiming others' choices

The settlement returned.

This time Mau stood in the plaza.

People everywhere.

Real?

Constructed?

The Trial had made the distinction emotionally useless.

Mau did not assume.

Good.

The Goddess asked:

"Would they choose this place if they could leave?"

Mau looked at the people.

Some had built rooms.

Some had never stopped calling another world home.

Some had arrived as children.

Some as fighters.

Some had relationships here they could not have imagined before.

Some still slept at the inn because the new settlement never became theirs.

"Some."

The Goddess:

"Would all?"

"No."

The answer hurt.

Also obvious.

A person in the crowd packed a bag.

Another unpacked one.

Images changed.

Not predictions.

Possibilities.

"Would Rimuru?"

Mau looked for him.

Rimuru stood near Milim and Diablo.

Source world.

People there.

Responsibilities Mau could not measure.

"I don't know."

"Would Bocchi?"

Bocchi near Kita.

Then image shifted.

Nijika.

Ryo.

Music.

A world where she had roots.

"I don't know."

"Would Ori?"

Mau's throat tightened.

Ori had no source world to return to in the ordinary sense.

Or maybe the Goddess counted something else.

"I don't know what her choice would even mean."

Good.

No claiming.

Then:

"Would Frieren?"

The plaza disappeared around her.

Only Frieren remained.

The Goddess showed Mau pieces.

Himmel.

Fern.

Her world.

Landscapes.

Magic.

Graves.

Centuries Mau had not shared.

People she had lost.

People she might find.

A life that had begun long before him and could continue long after.

Mau wanted yes.

The wanting was physical.

Stay.

Choose me.

Choose us.

Choose this room.

Choose stolen hoodies and bad bread and dates with time limits.

He hated how much he wanted it.

The Goddess asked:

"Would she choose you if her world were open?"

Mau looked at Frieren.

Construct.

Maybe.

He remembered the bridge.

Let me choose.

He remembered every version where he had overridden someone because uncertainty frightened him.

"I don't know."

The answer hurt more than dying.

The Goddess:

"Could you accept no?"

Mau's mouth opened.

Nothing.

Honesty.

"I don't know."

Better than false virtue.

"Could you accept yes?"

That surprised him.

"Why wouldn't I?"

The Goddess showed:
Frieren choosing Stay.

Then centuries later.

Regret.

Maybe.

Another:
Frieren choosing Stay.

Happiness.

Another:
Return.

Grief.

Another:
Return.

Peace.

Contradictory outcomes.

Same apparent choice.

Mau recoiled.

No universal result.

The Trial had already taught that.

The Goddess asked:

"If she chooses you and suffers, will you make her choice your crime?"

Mau went still.

There.

Another control route.

Not only preventing others from choosing.

Accepting their choice without owning every consequence.

Mau looked at Frieren.

"I don't know."

The answer became the only honest tool he had left.

Outside the Trial, the domain shifted.

The family saw the plaza image become translucent.

Mau's figure appeared at its center.

For the first time since entry, the Trial opened enough that their voices might carry.

Frieren stepped forward.

"Mau."

He heard.

His entire body reacted.

Not reset.

Real voice.

Maybe.

He did not assume.

"Frieren?"

"Yes."

The Goddess asked:

"Would they choose you?"

Mau looked around at people he loved.

Then at the person he most desperately wanted an answer from.

He could ask.

Do you stay?

Do you choose me?

Do you love me enough?

He did not.

"I don't know."

Frieren's face changed.

Not hurt.

Recognition.

The Goddess:

"Then let them answer."

The domain opened farther.

Air returned.

Real plaza under constructed plaza.

People outside hearing enough to understand.

The question stopped belonging to Mau.

That was the final turn.

The Trial had not produced a correct sacrifice.

It had removed Mau's claim to be the only person allowed to carry irreversible choice.

The next choice would belong to everyone individually.

The Goddess was about to make that literal.
