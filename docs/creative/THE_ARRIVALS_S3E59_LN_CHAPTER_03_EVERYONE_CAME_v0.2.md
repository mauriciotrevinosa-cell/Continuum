# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 21 — Everyone Came

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 21  
**Continuity state:** Final Trial scenario | The wedding Mau wants, on the elf route | Everyone dies; Frieren's last words blame his choice | The Goddess is heard, never seen | Domain opens

The white went on for a long time.

Through the wall, as at every anchor, Ciel said a number. He did not hear what it was. He had not heard one in a long time.

The white went on.

Long enough that Mau began to hope it was over.

Then there was frost.

Grass under his feet, cold through his shoes. Trees making a shape against a pale sky that he knew better than his own face. Mist lifting off the ground in the first light, the way it had lifted on the first morning of his life that he could remember.

The clearing.

He knew it was not real. He knew it the way he knew his own name. And the Trial had spent hundreds of deaths teaching him that knowing a thing was not real did nothing at all to stop it hurting.

He looked down at his hands.

They were his. Older, a little. A scar across the back of the left one that he did not have yet.

He touched his ear without meaning to.

It came to a point.

He stood very still in the frost with his fingers on the tip of his own ear, and understood what the Goddess was showing him. Not a death, this time. Not a door or a bridge or a fire.

A future.

The one he wanted.



Everyone came.

That was the first thing, and the thing that would stay with him longest: how many of them there were, and how happy, and how ordinary.

They came up through the trees in twos and threes and crowds. Fern was complaining about a dress Marin had put her in, and Stark was telling her it looked nice and being ignored. Bocchi had her guitar, and Kita was carrying the case for her, and Nijika and Ryo were arguing about whether a clearing counted as a venue. Momo had brought enough food for a city and was already threatening people about it, and Okarun was carrying most of it. Aira was criticizing the decorations, which she had helped put up.

Coco ran ahead and nearly fell. Agott caught her by the collar without looking. Tetia and Richeh followed with armfuls of something silver. Richeh, he saw, was not a guest anymore. She was walking with the others, at the same pace, and he did not know how he knew that, only that he did. Qifrey came last of the Witch Hat group, and smiled at Mau across the clearing in a way he had never once smiled in Mau's real life.

Yuta and Maki together, her hand through his arm. Milim on Rimuru's shoulders, shouting. Diablo behind them, radiant with some private triumph. Umaru and Suika, matching ribbons. Anko with a parasol held over Nazuna, who was awake in daylight for once and pretending not to enjoy it. Seiko with Turbo Granny, both of them already insulting the weather. Hakari and Karane and Shizuka and Nano and Kusuri in a single loud knot. Maomao, who had come to make sure nobody died of the food. Kaneki, standing a little apart, then not apart at all because Hakari dragged him in. Mikasa scanning the treeline out of pure habit, then making herself stop. Eren at the very edge of the trees, not quite family and here anyway. Senku with a measuring cord in his pocket, which he would deny. Rem, with her blue-rimmed cup, which she had brought to a wedding for no reason anyone could explain and everyone understood.

Himmel came alone, a little after the others.

He stood at the back of the clearing for a long time, looking at Frieren. Then he looked at Mau, and nodded once, and his face was entirely at peace.

And Rimuru came and stood beside Mau, the way a brother does.

"You look terrible," said Rimuru.

"Thanks."

"You look happy," said Rimuru. "On you it looks terrible."

Ori came last.

She was carrying Frieren's grimoire. She did not open it. She simply held it against her chest, very carefully, the way she had held it on a hundred ordinary mornings, and came and stood on Mau's other side.

"She's coming," said Ori.

He looked up.



Frieren came out of the trees in white.

Not a gown. Frieren would never have worn a gown. Something simple and pale that moved when she walked, which Wakana had made and Marin had argued about. A small blue flower was tucked into her hair above one ear.

She walked straight across the frost to him, the way she walked everywhere: unhurried, unbothered, as if the whole clearing had been arranged at her convenience and she was willing to accept it.

She stopped in front of him.

She looked at his ears.

"They suit you," she said.

He could not answer.

She reached up and touched the tip of one, very lightly, with one finger.

"It took long enough," she said.

"Sorry."

"Don't be. I had time."



Nobody officiated. Nobody needed to. They stood in the middle of the clearing where she had found him, with everyone they loved in a ring around them, and said what they had come to say.

Frieren went first, because she always did.

"I didn't know how long I'd have you," she said. "I used to count. I don't, anymore." She looked at him. "That's all. That's the whole thing."

Then it was his turn, and he had prepared something, and he forgot all of it.

"I'll be here," said Mau. "Long enough that you never have to go around asking anyone what I was like."

Frieren's face did nothing.

Then it did everything.

He held out his hand over the frost. No fever. No count. No Ciel in his head saying *fourteen minutes, stable*. Only his own mana, steady and warm, moving down his arm the way water moves downhill.

Flowers opened.

White. Blue. A ring of them, then a field, spreading out from under his hand across the whole clearing and up into the roots of the trees, until everyone was standing ankle-deep in small bright flowers.

Bocchi started to play.

Somebody was crying. A lot of people were crying.

Milim threw her arms in the air and whooped.

For one minute, perhaps two, Mau had everything.



The sky split.

Not with sound. With a line. A thin dark seam drawn across the pale morning from one side of the clearing to the other, very straight, the way a blade draws a line through paper.

Everyone looked up.

Then the line opened, and the slashes came out of it.

They came down into the clearing like rain. Invisible until they landed. Each one cut a clean line through whatever it touched: grass, earth, trees, people. Mau saw a tree fall in four pieces. He saw the flowers at his feet cut through, the white ones and the blue ones, and the frost under them, and the earth under that.

"It came for you," said a voice from everywhere.

He moved without thinking. Toward Frieren. Toward Ori. Toward all of them. To get in front of them, the way he had always gotten in front of them, the way he had learned to in every scenario the Trial had ever built.

And the seal opened.

He felt it go. The deep place where Ciel had held the walls, which had held for so long, which had leaked and bent and held. It did not break. It simply opened, one door, the way the elf route had opened, on purpose. But nobody had asked for this one.

Infinity came out.

He had felt the seal open once before, for real. In the plaza, in daylight, with visitors watching: three things at once, held for nineteen minutes. He had held all of those.

He could not hold this one for one.

It came out of him the way it had come out of him on the first day of the leaks, at the old inn, when he had run and Frieren had reached for him and her hand had stopped a finger's width from his back. It wrapped around him whole and perfect, without the Six Eyes and without control. Nothing could reach him. No slash. No falling tree.

No one.

He was perfectly safe.

He could not move toward them. Every step he took, the space between him and them stretched to fit it. He could not drop it. He tore at it with everything he had, and it did not notice.

He watched.

Milim went up into the sky at the seam and the sky cut her. Rimuru threw a barrier over the children, and it held for three seconds, and then it did not. Yuta and Maki went in back to back, the way they must have done a thousand times in another life, and they did not come out. Mikasa got everyone she could to the treeline and the treeline was cut too. Fern and Stark held the north edge until there was no north edge. Bocchi stopped playing.

He watched all of it from inside a silence so complete he could hear his own heart.

And Frieren.

Frieren was not fighting the sky.

Frieren was fighting him.

She was at the edge of his Infinity with her staff in both hands, driving spell after spell into the space around him, and every spell slowed and slowed and stopped a finger's width from his skin. She was trying to get in. To get to him. To pull him out. She did not look up at the seam even once. She did not look at anything but him.

A slash went through her, and she did not stop.

Another did, and she went down on one knee in the cut flowers and kept her hand up, flat against nothing, a finger's width from his chest.

Then the Infinity let go.

All at once, for no reason, the way it had come.

He caught her before she touched the ground.



The clearing was quiet.

There was nothing left to make a sound. The seam in the sky had closed. The slashes had stopped. The flowers he had made lay cut in clean lines all around them, white and blue, among everyone who had come.

Frieren was very light in his arms.

She looked up at him. Her face was calm. Her eyes were clear. She did not look like she was in pain. She looked like Frieren, considering something.

"You stood in front of the door," she said.

He did not understand.

Then he did. The white room. Two chairs. ONE LEAVES. She had stepped toward the door, and he had stepped in front of it, and she had looked at him with that sad smile.

"I could have gone home," said Frieren. "I could have gone back to my world. You stood in front of the door, and I let you."

"Frieren—"

"I wanted this so much I was angry about it." Her voice did not change. That was the worst part. It was perfectly gentle. "And look."

He looked.

He could not stop looking.

"If it weren't for you," said Frieren, "if you'd let me go back, I would be happy. I would be bored, somewhere, and happy. I would still be alive."

She closed her eyes.

She did not open them again.



He knelt in the cut flowers with her in his arms for a long time.

He did not count the time. He had stopped being able to count anything.

When the voice came, it did not come from anywhere. There was no figure in the trees, no light in the sky, no face. Only a voice, very calm, from all around the clearing at once, from the frost and the cut grass and the bodies of everyone he loved.

"Would they choose you?" said the Goddess.

Mau did not answer.

"They did," said the Goddess. "Look."

He looked.

He saw all of them. Every single one. Everyone who had come because it was his wedding, because they loved him, because he had asked. Everyone who had chosen to be here.

"They are here," said the Goddess, "because of you."



Outside the Trial, there was still only the white wall. There had been nothing else all day.

Frieren stood in front of it, where she had stood since the beginning. It was the hour. She said his name the way she had said it every hour, into nothing, never knowing whether anything got through.

"Mau."

He heard.

His entire body reacted.

Not reset.

Real voice.

He lifted his head. The clearing was still around him, and the dead were still around him, and she was still in his arms, and her voice was coming from beyond the trees. Alive. Close. Saying his name.

He did not know which one to believe.

"Frieren?"

No answer came. Only the one word, once, and then the silence again, as if a door had been opened a crack and closed.

The Goddess asked again, quietly:

"Would they choose you?"

Mau looked down at the woman in his arms. Then up, toward the voice beyond the trees.

The last time he had been asked, he had said *I don't know*. It had been the only honest thing he had left.

He found that he did not have it anymore.

"They shouldn't," he said.

Outside, nobody heard it.

"Then," said the Goddess, "let them answer."

The domain opened farther.

The clearing went out like a lamp. The frost, the flowers, the bodies, the weight in his arms, all at once. Air came back, real air, smelling of dust and smoke and the settlement. Under his knees was not frost but the packed dirt of the plaza that people kept calling a plaza even though one edge was still unfinished.

His arms were empty.

He was kneeling at the edge of what remained of the Trial. He was not fully released from it, close enough to touch the real plaza and not yet standing in it. On the other side of that last thin edge was every single person he had just watched die, and every single one of them was alive, and looking at him.

He could not look back.

The Trial had not proven that everyone would stay.

It had proven that they had already chosen him, many times, in ways he had never asked for and could not take back.

And then, last, as carefully as a gift, it had shown him what he believed choosing him would cost them.

Both lessons felt true.

Only one of them had been built for him.

The Goddess was about to make the choice literal.
