# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 23 — Stay

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 23  
**Continuity state:** Return/Stay offer fully stated | Stay carries bounded petition possibility | Neither path is morally privileged

The second word appeared beneath the first.

STAY.

For one moment it looked simpler.

Remain.

Continue.

The settlement already existed.

Rooms.

Work.

Relationships.

No threshold to imagine.

Then the Goddess spoke.

"A chooser who remains in Continuum may receive one bounded petition concerning a specific loved person."

The plaza exploded into questions.

"Bring them?"

"Find them?"

"Restore them?"

"Alive?"

"Dead?"

"Healthy?"

"Same age?"

"From when?"

"Do they consent?"

That last one cut through.

Ori had asked it.

The Goddess answered:

"A petition does not override the subject's relevant agency."

Ori exhaled.

Mau did too.

Good.

Then:

"Not unlimited."

Nobody had thought it would be.

Still hurt.

"Not guaranteed in all cases."

Coco's face changed.

Richeh noticed immediately.

Agott too.

Coco whispered a name.

Not loud enough for the narration to steal.

Possibility.

Not promise.

That alone changed the moral geometry.

Staying was no longer merely:
choose the life you built.

It could contain hope for someone absent.

Return was no longer merely:
go home.

It could contain memory loss, danger, restored obligations.

Neither became clean.

Senku asked:

"What bounds petition capability?"

"Personal terms."

"Of course."

Kusuri raised one hand.

"If somebody stays and petitions for a person who refuses to come?"

"The refusal stands."

Good.

Her shoulders relaxed.

Hakari looked at her.

Noted.

No conversation yet.

Milim asked:

"Can I petition for food?"

Rimuru:

"Specific loved person."

"I love food."

"No."

The absurdity helped.

For ten seconds.

Then Rem asked whether a petition could concern someone dead.

Silence.

The Goddess:

"Some."

Not all.

No guarantee.

Rem looked down.

Nobody touched the answer.

Kaneki asked nothing.

His silence had weight too.

Mau watched everyone.

Trial scenarios multiplied in him.

Stay.

Petition succeeds.

Petition fails.

Loved person refuses.

Relationship changes.

Return.

Memory fades.

Memory stays.

People regret.

People do not.

Hundreds of lives trying to attach themselves to real faces.

Mau's breathing changed.

Frieren saw.

She moved near.

Not asking what she would choose.

Not asking him.

Just near.

Jinshi asked the procedural question everyone needed.

"Can a person request terms for both Return and Stay before choosing?"

"Yes."

"Privately?"

"Yes."

"With a witness?"

"If chooser consents."

"Can the chooser delay?"

The sky changed.

TEN DAYS.

Not yet explained fully.

Enough.

Mau looked at the number.

His body understood countdown faster than his mind.

Trial.

Five seconds.

Ten.

Choose.

He flinched.

Frieren noticed.

The Goddess continued.

"Individual terms may differ."

That killed any hope of a universal comparison chart.

Good.

No council could rank options for everyone.

No Mau could.

No Senku.

No Rimuru.

No one.

The choice was individual because the conditions were.

Stay did not mean loyalty.

Return did not mean abandonment.

A petition did not make Stay morally superior.

A source world did not make Return morally pure.

If the story made one correct answer, everything the Trial had done would collapse into manipulation.

Mau understood that.

For now.

The domain pressure began to loosen.

The Goddess had given them choices.

That did not mean she had given them peace.
