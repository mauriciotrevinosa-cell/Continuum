# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 26 — One Life Each

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 26  
**Continuity state:** Day 1 after Trial | Return/Stay exists publicly | Mau begins connecting real choice to Trial consequence

By noon, people were already asking questions.

Not choosing.

Questions.

That should have reassured Mau.

It did not.

At Arrival House, someone asked:

"If I return and Continuum becomes dreamlike, will I know I chose?"

Personal terms.

Another:

"If I stay, can the person I petition for refuse?"

Personal terms.

Another:

"What if I choose Stay because my partner does and then we break up?"

No divine answer required.

Only human fear.

Mau stood outside long enough to hear too much.

He left.

Not because he wanted privacy.

Because every question generated scenarios automatically.

Partner stays.

You stay.

Ten years later resentment.

Partner leaves.

You stay.

Regret.

Both leave.

Memory fades unevenly.

One remembers.

One doesn't.

The Trial had shown versions of all of it.

Not these exact people.

Close enough.

Mau walked toward the water court.

Senku was there measuring flow.

Normality.

Good.

Senku looked at him.

"You look terrible."

"Thank you."

"Sleep?"

"Technically."

"That's not a quantity."

Mau sat.

For a while they discussed water.

Numbers Mau liked because numbers stayed put.

Flow.

Volume.

Capacity.

Then Senku said:

"People are going to need time."

Mau looked at him.

"To choose."

"Yeah."

The word hit.

Mau's body reacted before thought.

Choice.

Room.

Door.

Countdown.

Senku saw the change.

"What?"

Mau looked at the water.

"In there, if I chose wrong, I got another scenario."

Senku stayed quiet.

Mau continued.

"Not the same one. But I got another chance to learn something."

"Sometimes."

"Sometimes I died."

"Yes."

"Then came back."

Senku understood where this was going.

Mau looked toward Arrival House.

"They don't."

There.

The real difference.

The Trial could afford cruelty because Mau reset.

Real people did not.

"They get one Return."

Maybe.

One Stay.

Maybe.

One relationship at that moment.

One version of what memory becomes.

One chance to decide what home means.

No loop to compare.

No hundred outcomes.

Mau felt sick.

Senku said:

"That doesn't mean you can solve it for them."

Mau looked at him sharply.

Too fast.

Senku noticed.

"I didn't say you were planning to."

Mau looked away.

Good.

Not planning.

Not yet.

But the premise had appeared.

Real people have one life each.

One decision.

No reset.

Mau had hundreds of failures in his head.

Information nobody else could possess.

Not reliable information about their futures.

He knew that intellectually.

Emotion did not care.

He had seen:
love become regret.
return become grief.
stay become guilt.
sacrifice become resentment.
freedom become loneliness.
protection become control.

Hundreds of times.

What kind of person watches that and then casually says:

Choose whatever feels right?

Senku returned to measurement.

"Uncertainty isn't a defect you can engineer out of being alive."

Mau laughed without humor.

"That's a very bad scientist sentence."

"It's an excellent scientist sentence."

Mau looked at him.

Senku continued:

"You don't have the sample that tells you what happens to these people."

"I know."

"Do you?"

Mau did.

He understood the sentence.

Again.

The problem was not knowledge.

It was tolerance.

Could Mau tolerate watching people he loved make an irreversible choice without protecting them from the pain of choosing?

The Goddess had asked whether they would choose him.

Mau had answered:

I don't know.

Now the world asked him to live beside that not-knowing.

He watched someone leave Arrival House without requesting terms.

No choice yet.

Mau's shoulders lowered slightly.

Then another person entered.

His mind generated six futures before the door closed.

The Trial was over.

The loops were not.
