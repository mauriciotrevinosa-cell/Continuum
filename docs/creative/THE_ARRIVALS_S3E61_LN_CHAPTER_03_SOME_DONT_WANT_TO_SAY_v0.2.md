# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 30 — Some Don't Want to Say

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 30  
**Continuity state:** Day 2 after Trial | First major privacy conflict | Mau successfully respects a smaller version of the agency problem he will later fail at scale

The first privacy conflict came from love.

That was why nobody could dismiss it as selfishness.

One partner requested terms.

Returned.

The other waited until the Arrival House door closed.

"What did they say?"

The first person shook their head.

"I'm not ready."

"What did they say?"

"Not now."

The second person's face changed.

Fear.

"If we're deciding a future together—"

"We are not deciding tonight."

"That's not what I mean."

"I know."

"No, you don't."

The argument grew.

Not loud enough for spectacle.

Sharp enough for everyone nearby to hear.

Arrival House staff moved them into a private room.

Not punishment.

Privacy for conflict about privacy.

The mediator did not solve it.

Good.

She made sentences specific.

"You want the terms because your choice depends partly on theirs."

"Yes."

"You do not want to share because speaking them out loud feels like committing."

The first partner exhaled.

"Yes."

Both facts real.

Painfully compatible.

"You are allowed private information."

The second person flinched.

"Even if it affects me?"

The mediator:

"Privacy can affect you."

Hard answer.

"You are also allowed to decide what you need in order to continue the relationship."

There.

Boundary both ways.

No one forced disclosure.

No one forced the other person to pretend non-disclosure had no consequence.

Mau heard about the dispute later.

His first instinct arrived fully formed.

I can help.

He knew both people.

He had seen analogous Trial futures.

One where secrecy destroyed trust.

One where forced disclosure did.

One where waiting saved the relationship.

One where waiting made it worse.

Hundreds of outcomes offered themselves as advice.

Mau stood.

Then sat back down.

Frieren noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Lie."

Mau looked toward Arrival House.

"Not my relationship."

Frieren waited.

"That's it?"

"That's it."

Hard.

Correct.

He stayed out.

That success mattered.

Because later he would fail on the larger version of exactly the same principle.

That afternoon Frieren said:

"I'm requesting terms."

Mau stopped breathing for one beat.

Not visibly.

Frieren saw anyway.

"Okay."

No:
which?

No:
can I come?

No:
why?

Progress.

Frieren looked at him.

"You can ask if I'm okay with you coming."

Mau almost did.

Then:

"Do you want me there?"

"No."

Pain.

Small.

Immediate.

Mau nodded.

"Okay."

Frieren left.

The hour became unbearable.

Mau cleaned something that did not need cleaning.

Started reading.

Did not read.

Walked past the door once.

Turned around before anyone could call it orbiting.

Good.

He imagined the terms anyway.

Return:
Himmel.
Fern?
Her world.
Centuries.

Stay:
him.
Petition.
Who?

No.

He stopped.

Tried again.

Stopped.

At fifty-eight minutes Frieren returned.

Mau was in the common room.

He stood.

She looked calm.

Not exactly.

"Are you okay?"

Frieren considered.

"Not exactly."

Mau's heart dropped.

He kept the next question inside long enough to change it.

"Do you want to tell me?"

"No."

The answer hurt more than he expected.

Mau nodded.

"Okay."

Frieren watched him.

No anger?

Plenty.

Fear?

More.

Still okay.

She sat beside him.

Not telling.

Still choosing closeness.

That was almost harder.

Mau stared ahead.

Every Trial-trained part of him wanted the data.

If he knew the terms, maybe he could prepare.

Maybe protect.

Maybe anticipate regret.

Instead he sat beside a person he loved while she held information that could change both their lives.

He did not ask.

One of the hardest things he did all season.

Frieren reached for his hand after several minutes.

Mau let her.

No answer.

Contact anyway.

Privacy had not become distance.

That distinction would matter when fear got worse.
