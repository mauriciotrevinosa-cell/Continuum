# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 19
## Chapter 42 — What We Don't Know

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 19  
**Reading order:** 42  
**Continuity state:** Day 3 afternoon, Mau asleep | Yuta's Gojo comparison | Ciel refuses Rimuru the number | The household drafts the condition and decides to ask, not pin

By the middle of the afternoon, Mau was asleep and staying asleep, and Frieren had not left the chair beside the bed, and the ribbon was still tied to his ankle.

Everyone else ended up in the common room.

Nobody called a meeting. Nobody needed to. They simply drifted there, one and two at a time, the way the household had always drifted toward the same room when something was wrong. Fern with her ledger, not writing in it. Rimuru by the cold hearth. Yuta and Maki on the bench under the window. Ori in the doorway, where she could see down the corridor to Mau's door. Bocchi and Kita. Momo, Okarun, Aira. Seiko. Anko, with her notebook closed for once. Umaru, still holding the empty prison-meal tray because she had forgotten to put it down. Coco. Qifrey. Maomao. Senku. Milim, sitting on the floor with her knees under her chin. Diablo, standing, because Diablo did not know how to be in a room like this sitting down.

For a long time, nobody said anything.

Then Momo said, too loudly, the thing she had clearly been holding since the road:

"We don't expect anything from him."

A few people looked up.

"We don't," said Momo. "We never asked him to fix anything. We never asked him to save anybody. We never asked him to go to the Goddess and— and—" She could not finish it. "Nobody here expects anything from him. He decided all that by himself."

Several people nodded. It was a comfortable thing to believe.

Yuta did not nod.

He sat on the bench under the window for a moment, looking at his hands. Then he looked up.

"Seriously?" he said. "Nobody?"

Momo opened her mouth.

"There was a man in my world," said Yuta, before she could. He did not raise his voice. He never did. "My teacher. Everyone called him the strongest. He carried everything. The students, the war, the whole world on his shoulders. Nobody ever asked him to. That was the thing. Nobody ever said *carry this for us*. We just stood behind him and expected him to be there. And he always was." He paused. "Until he wasn't."

Maki's hand found his on the bench between them.

"Nobody here asked Mau for anything," said Yuta. "That's true. And every single one of us expects him to be our center anyway. He's the reason any of us is even thinking about staying. You know it. I know it." He looked around the room. "And he knows it. He knows that if we stay, we might never get this chance again. Never get to go home. He knows that some of us are only staying because of him. He knows exactly how much we're handing him without asking."

Nobody said anything.

"And we don't know what happened in there," said Yuta, more quietly. "That's the part I can't stop thinking about. We don't know what he saw. What he did. What it showed him. What it made him choose. He told us on the road that he died hundreds of times, and we heard the number, and we think that means we know. It doesn't. We know he came out. And two days later he was walking down a road in the dark to make a pact that would send us all home without asking us."

He let that sit.

"That's not someone nobody expected anything from," said Yuta. "That's someone who tried to carry the whole world back to where it came from by himself, because he thought it was his to carry. Gojo-sensei would have understood him perfectly." His voice went rough at last. "I don't want Mau to understand him that well."

Momo's eyes had filled.

"I didn't mean—"

"I know," said Yuta gently. "None of us did. That's how it happens."



Rimuru had not said anything through all of it.

He was standing by the cold hearth with his arms folded, looking at nothing, and Mau would have recognized the look. It was the look of someone talking to someone else inside his own head.

"Ciel," he said aloud, so the room could hear his half. "What happened in there?"

The room went still.

Rimuru listened. His face did not change.

"She doesn't know," he said, for the others. "She couldn't see anything inside the domain. She could only reach him at the start of each... attempt. That's her word. She says she knows how many attempts there were. That's all she knows."

He waited.

Everyone waited with him.

Nothing.

Rimuru's eyebrows drew together very slightly. In all the years since he had woken in a cave with a voice in his head, that voice had never once let a question from him hang in the air.

"Ciel," he said. "The number, please."

A pause.

Then Rimuru's face did change. Not much. Just enough that Milim, on the floor, sat up straight.

"She says she can't," said Rimuru slowly.

"Why?" said Milim.

Rimuru was quiet for a moment, listening.

"She says she promised Mau she'd give him the chance to tell me himself."

The common room was very quiet.

Diablo, by the wall, had gone utterly motionless, the way a cat goes motionless before something it does not understand.

"She's never refused you," said Diablo softly. It was not a question.

"No," said Rimuru. "She's never refused me."

He did not look angry. He looked, for a moment, like a man who has reached for something with his left hand and found it a little further away than he expected.

Then he unfolded his arms.

"Okay," he said, to the room and to the voice inside him. "Okay. Then we give him the chance."



Fern opened her ledger.

She did it the way she did everything that frightened her: by making it into something that could be written down. She took out her pencil and turned to a clean page and sat there with the pencil over the paper.

"He doesn't go to the Goddess," she said. "That's the first thing."

"Not alone," said Mikasa.

"Not with anyone," said Fern. "Not for anyone."

She wrote it.

"Until what?" said Anko. "There has to be an until. If there's no until, it's just a cage, and he'll find a way out of a cage. He always does."

Fern looked at Yuta.

Yuta thought about it.

"Until he tells us what happened in there," he said. "Properly. Not on a road at four in the morning, shouting. Properly."

Fern wrote: *1. He tells us what happened.*

"And until we've talked about it," said Rimuru from the hearth. "All of us who want to. Not just him explaining and us nodding. Talking."

Fern wrote: *2. We talk about it.*

She looked at the page.

Then Ori said, from the doorway, very quietly:

"We're doing it again."

Everyone turned.

"Writing rules for him while he's asleep," said Ori. "Pinning them to his door. That's what we did after the containment. That was the thing we kept saying we don't do."

The room went silent.

Fern looked down at the ledger in her hands.

"Then we don't pin it," she said at last. "We don't put it anywhere. When he wakes up, we show it to him and we ask him. And if he says no, we start again."

Ori nodded once.

Fern closed the ledger over the page and held it in her lap, and nobody said anything else for a long time.

Down the corridor, in the room with the east window, Mau slept on, with a ribbon around his ankle and Frieren beside him. He did not know that the whole house had just spent an afternoon talking about a number he was carrying, and a promise he had made, and what it might cost him to keep it.
