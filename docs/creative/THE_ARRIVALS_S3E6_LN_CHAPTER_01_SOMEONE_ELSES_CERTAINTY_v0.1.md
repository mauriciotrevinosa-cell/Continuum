<!-- scene-id: s3e6-ln01-himmel -->

Himmel verified the place.

That was all.

Mau described another memory from the weeks before Sukuna.

Himmel and Frieren behind the old inn.

Late afternoon.

A stack of repaired roof tiles.

Mau passing close enough to hear them.

Frieren saying:

"If you had come before Mau, maybe I would have tried."

Mau remembered stopping.

Himmel looking at Frieren.

Frieren looking tired.

Then Mau walking away before they saw him.

Himmel's face went still.

"We had a conversation there."

Mau's stomach dropped.

Frieren stood beside him.

Himmel continued.

"About us."

"Did she say that?"

"No."

Mau looked at Frieren.

"No," she said.

<!-- scene-id: s3e6-ln01-real -->

Himmel did not soften the truth into something useless.

"She told me she loved me."

Mau knew that.

"She told me she did not think she loved me romantically."

Mau knew that too.

"She told me she still wanted me in her life."

Frieren nodded.

Himmel looked at Mau.

"She did not say she would have chosen me if I arrived earlier."

The false memory was cruel because it had not invented the conversation.

Himmel supplied details Mau had not offered.

The west-side tiles had already been stacked.

Frieren had carried a book she never opened.

Fern had called from inside once.

Mau remembered all three.

Physical scaffolding: real.

It had rewritten one sentence inside a real one.

The setting.

People.

Weather.

Roof tiles.

All correct.

Meaning altered.

<!-- scene-id: s3e6-ln01-notjealousy -->

Mau sat.

He felt stupid for the emotion that arrived.

Not jealousy exactly.

Something lower.

If the memory could make him believe Frieren's choice depended on timing—

What else could it make him believe?

That someone resented him?

That a promise had been made?

That a warning existed?

That a place had been designated safe?

A false cup was frightening.

A false instruction could move his body.

Frieren crouched in front of him.

"This isn't about Himmel."

"I know."

Himmel stayed.

That mattered too.

No defensive joke.

No heroic speech.

He simply refused to let a false version of himself become more important than the real one.

Mau looked at both of them.

For one second he remembered this exact moment happening yesterday.

It had not.

He stood too fast.

The room tilted.

Frieren caught him.

Mau heard himself say:

"I need air."

Himmel moved first.

Then stopped when Mau said:

"Please."

Space.

Frieren loosened her hand.

Mau walked only to the yard.

He stood beside the rain barrel until the dizziness passed.

Frieren remained visible through the doorway.

Six minutes later he came back inside.

Everyone relaxed.

That small return became evidence against the danger they did not yet understand.

Nobody stopped him.

Later they would remember that choice too harshly.

The mistake was not giving Mau air.

The mistake was not yet knowing that certainty itself had become a route something could exploit.
