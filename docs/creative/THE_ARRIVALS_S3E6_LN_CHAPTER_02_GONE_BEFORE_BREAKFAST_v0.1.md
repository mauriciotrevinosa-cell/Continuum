<!-- scene-id: s3e6-ln02-night -->

Mau came back inside.

That was why nobody treated the walk as a disappearance.

He ate.

Barely.

Sat with everyone.

Answered questions.

Went upstairs with Frieren.

Lay down.

Frieren stayed awake longer than he did.

At least Mau remembered that.

Sometime before dawn, he opened his eyes.

The room was empty.

No.

Frieren was there.

Sleeping.

Mau knew that.

He could see her.

He also remembered her leaving two hours earlier after saying:

`I need space from you.`

The two realities existed at once.

Frieren breathing beside him.

Frieren gone.

Blanket warm.

Sentence remembered.

His chest hurt.

Mau tried the notebook method.

Observed: Frieren is physically here.

Remembered: Frieren left.

Interpretation: blank.

He hated the blank most at night.

He touched the blanket near her hand.

Warm.

Present.

The memory insisted anyway.

<!-- scene-id: s3e6-ln02-direction -->

Then another certainty arrived.

A place north of the site.

Trees.

A cabin.

He had been told to go there if this happened.

By whom?

Rimuru.

No.

Qifrey.

Maybe.

A woman he could not see clearly.

The instruction felt older than the question.

He checked the evidence notebook.

No cabin.

No instruction.

That should have weakened the certainty.

Instead his mind supplied another explanation:

You did not write this one because it was private.

The false memory defended itself against verification.

Mau sat on the edge of the bed.

If he woke Frieren, she would stop him.

Because she loved him.

That thought should have been comforting.

Instead his mind supplied a second interpretation:

`She stops you because everyone has learned how much trouble you create.`

Mau closed his eyes.

Wrong.

Probably wrong.

He needed one thing he could verify without asking someone else who he was.

The place.

If the place existed, something was real.

That logic was terrible.

He knew it was terrible.

But uncertainty had become intolerable and the false route offered a test he could perform without asking someone else who he was.

If the place existed, something.

If it did not, something else.

A binary.

Manageable.

At five in the morning it felt clean.

<!-- scene-id: s3e6-ln02-leave -->

He dressed quietly.

Took water.

A coat.

Not enough food.

His watch.

No weapon worth calling a weapon.

He looked at Frieren.

She was still there.

Sleeping.

He remembered her not being there.

His hand reached toward Frieren's shoulder.

Wake her.

Say it.

Then the remembered sentence returned:

I need space from you.

Mau's body reacted as if waking her would violate a boundary she had already set.

That was the cruelty.

The false memory contained its own defense against correction.

He lowered his hand.

Mau left before one of those facts could win.

<!-- scene-id: s3e6-ln02-breakfast -->

Frieren woke to an empty side of the bed.

That was not immediately wrong.

Mau woke early sometimes.

She went downstairs.

No Mau.

Kitchen.

No Mau.

Storage.

No Mau.

Outside.

No Mau.

His boots were gone.

The water flask too.

His coat.

Watch.

No proper pack.

Not enough food for a planned trip.

Frieren checked the evidence notebook.

Still there.

He had left the one thing they had agreed to use for checking reality.

Frieren stood in the doorway.

Behind her, breakfast continued for six more seconds.

Then Ori looked up.

"Where's Mau?"

Frieren did not answer.

Rimuru saw her face.

"What did he take?"

"Water. Coat. Watch."

"Food?"

"Not enough."

"Direction?"

"No note."

Mikasa entered from outside.

Heard enough.

"Tracks?"

Frieren was gone before the word finished.

The future city stopped mattering for exactly one breath.

Then everything moved.

Not every person.

Not yet.

First:
yard.
Road.
Tracks.
Questions.

A missing person was not yet a season-scale mystery.

It was Mau gone before breakfast.

That was enough.
