# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 5 — The People Who Stayed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 5  
**Continuity state:** First disappearance, Night 1 into Day 2 dawn | Old inn = search headquarters | The household that stayed | Mau: missing

The inn did not sleep.

Not all of it went searching.

Somebody had to keep it a place worth coming back to.

That was its own kind of work.

Nobody would ever call it the search.

It was.



Umaru was on the floor by the front door.

Blanket around her shoulders.

Hood up.

Back against the wood.

The door had opened once, after midnight.

Frieren.

Yuta.

Ori.

Not him.

Umaru had felt the whole room look up, and then she had felt it stop hoping, and then she had moved her blanket to the door so that next time she would know first.

Fern found her there after Frieren went upstairs.

"Go to bed."

"No."

"Umaru."

"If it opens, I want to feel it."

Fern looked at the door.

Then at the girl using herself as a doorstop.

Umaru pulled the blanket tighter.

"He always notices when I haven't eaten."

Quiet.

"Nobody asks him to. He just notices."

Fern did not answer.

She went to the kitchen.

Came back with two bowls.

Sat down on the floor beside her.

Umaru stared.

"You're supposed to make me go to bed."

"I'm supposed to make you eat."

Fern handed her a bowl.

"Bed is negotiable."

They ate against the door.

It did not open.

They stayed anyway.



Upstairs, the music room had one candle.

Bocchi sat on the floor holding her guitar.

Not playing.

Holding.

Kita sat beside her, shoulders touching.

Kita had tried talking for the first hour.

Then learned.

Bocchi did not need words tonight.

She needed someone to stay exactly where her hand could find them.

So Kita stayed.

At some point Bocchi's fingers closed around Kita's and held on too hard.

Kita did not mention it.

That, she had decided months ago, was most of what being someone's girlfriend meant.

Being where the hand could find you.

"What if he doesn't remember us?"

Bocchi's voice was very small.

Kita squeezed back.

"Then we remind him."

"How?"

"Loudly. Repeatedly. With visual aids."

Bocchi made a sound that would have been a laugh if it had survived the trip.

"I'm very memorable," Kita added.

"...You are."

The door opened a crack.

Shizuka.

Book held against her chest.

She did not ask to come in.

She sat in the corner, opened the book, and read.

Books, guitar and silence.

The three of them had been fluent in that for a long time.

After a while Shizuka wrote something in the margin and turned the page toward Bocchi.

*When he comes back he'll want to hear what you've been practicing.*

Bocchi read it twice.

She did not play.

She started tuning.

Kita watched the peg turn.

Small.

Enough.



In the workroom, Wakana was cutting cloth.

Rimuru had asked for more markers at dusk.

Anything bright enough to tie to a branch.

Wakana had handed over every strip he owned.

Then he had started making more.

Marin found him an hour after midnight, needle moving under the lamp.

"They don't need hems, Wakana."

"They do."

"They're for trees."

"It's going to rain."

He bit the thread.

"Raw edges fray in rain. Then the cloth looks old. Then somebody standing in a forest at noon can't tell whether it's the one they tied this morning."

Marin looked at the pile.

Forty strips.

Every one finished.

"You haven't slept."

"Neither have you."

Fair.

Marin sat beside him and threaded a second needle.

On another night she would have talked the entire time.

About colors.

About how forty identical strips was a crime against the forest.

About Mau's terrible taste in hoodies.

Tonight she sewed.

Wakana noticed the silence the way he noticed a crooked seam.

He did not fix it.

He just moved the lamp so it lit her side of the table too.



Maki was on the porch roof.

She had wanted to be in the forest.

Rimuru had looked at the list of everyone leaving and said one sentence.

"Somebody has to be here if something else comes."

Agott had said the same thing at dinner in a crueler voice.

Both had been right.

Maki hated it.

From the roof she could see search lanterns moving along the ridge and the eastern cut.

She counted them every few minutes.

Three.

Still three.

Below, Maomao came out with a crate and set it beside the door.

Maki looked down.

"What's that?"

"For when they find him."

Exposure.

Dehydration.

Exhaustion.

Whatever had been done to his head that nothing in the crate could reach.

Every jar labeled twice.

"You're assuming they'll find him."

Maomao did not look up.

"I'm assuming that if they do, I won't have time to pack."

Not hope.

Logistics.

Maomao's version of hope.

Maki understood it better than comfort.

Not long after half past one, Maki climbed down for water.

Frieren was asleep on the couch.

Boots still on.

One hand closed around nothing.

Yuta sat in the armchair across from her, awake, watching the front door over Umaru's head.

Maki stood there a moment.

She did not wake Frieren.

She took the blanket from the back of the couch and put it over her.

Then went back up to the roof.

Yuta saw.

Neither of them said anything.

Twenty minutes later Frieren sat up and the blanket slid off and she never asked who had put it there.

Maki never said.

Some things between the two of them had stopped needing to be said a long time ago.



Before dawn the kitchen filled with people who had not slept and were pretending they had.

Marin had been in it since three.

She could not track.

Could not fly.

Could not read a broken branch the way Mikasa could, or feel mana the way Frieren did, or run the roads the way Okarun could.

So she did the one thing she could do with her whole heart.

Food.

Rice shaped by hand.

Bread split and filled.

Each one wrapped in cloth and tied, and on every knot a name in ink.

YUTA.

ORI.

MIKASA.

HIMMEL — YOU FORGOT LUNCH YESTERDAY.

FRIEREN — EAT THIS ONE.

Beside every name she drew a small face.

Karane watched from the doorway with a blanket around her shoulders.

"Is this really the time?"

"It's exactly the time."

Marin tied another knot.

"When they open it on some cold ridge, they should feel like somebody was thinking about them."

Karane opened her mouth to argue.

Closed it.

Picked up a cloth and started folding.

Badly.

Marin let her.

Stark burned the first pan of eggs.

Then the second.

He put the third in front of Fern without a word.

Fern looked at the plate.

At him.

"I'm not hungry."

"You told Frieren that doesn't count."

Fern's mouth tightened.

She ate.

Stark sat across from her and did not point out that he had won.

That was how she knew he was frightened too.

Hakari loaded Marin's bundles into two baskets.

"We're running these out to the search lines."

Karane stared at her.

"We are not trackers."

"We're couriers."

"Since when?"

"Since about four minutes ago."

Karane complained the entire way to the door.

She carried both baskets.



On the hook by the stove hung the old mug.

Someone had washed it the night before and put it away with the others.

Marin took it down.

Turned it once in her hands.

Then carried it into the common room and set it back on the corner of the settlement map.

Where Mau always left it.

Nobody had asked her to.

When the searchers came down for the second day, the inn was ready for them.

Food with their names on it.

Markers that would survive the rain.

Water already hot.

The map on the table, and the mug in its corner.

Frieren looked at the mug one second longer than at anything else.

Then took the bundle with her name on it.

FRIEREN — EAT THIS ONE.

She almost said something.

Didn't.

Put it inside her coat.

Marin saw.

That was enough.

The door opened.

Cold air.

Umaru felt it against her back and lifted her head.

Not him.

Not yet.

She stayed by the door.

The people who stayed did not find Mau.

That was never their job.

Their job was to make sure that whoever did would still have a home to carry him back to.

They did it all night.

They would do it again tonight.
