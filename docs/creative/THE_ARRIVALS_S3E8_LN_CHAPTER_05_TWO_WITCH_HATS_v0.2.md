# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 10 — Two Witch Hats

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 10  
**Continuity state:** First disappearance, after midnight | Mau still missing | G5 relationship wave continues

Coco recognized the hat before the face.

That was embarrassing later.

In the moment it was everything.

Diablo entered with two girls behind him.

One looked annoyed.

The other looked delighted by the fact that an inn could contain this many impossible people at once.

Coco stood so fast her chair fell.

"Richeh?"

Richeh stopped.

Tetia looked from Coco to Agott to Qifrey.

Then smiled wide enough to become dangerous.

"Coco!"

Coco crossed the room.

Richeh did not brace in time.

Coco hit her hard enough to move both of them.

Richeh made a sound of protest.

Then held on.

Tetia joined from the side and converted the reunion into a structural problem.

Agott stayed where she was.

For three seconds.

Four.

Richeh saw her over Coco's shoulder.

"Agott."

Agott's face betrayed her.

Tetia noticed.

"Agott!"

"No."

Tetia opened her arms.

"No."

She kept them open.

Agott walked into the hug anyway.

Qifrey remained behind them.

Not distant.

Waiting.

Richeh disentangled first.

She looked at him.

Teacher.

Home.

Complication.

Qifrey smiled.

"Hello, Richeh."

Her mouth trembled once.

She looked furious about it.

"Hello."

Tetia reached him faster.

Qifrey accepted the second collision of the night.

No one asked who Mau was for several minutes.

That mattered too.

Some arrivals should be allowed to arrive into relationships that existed before him.

When the first shock passed, Fern did the same intake she had done for everyone else.

Hurt?

No.

Followed?

Unknown.

Need food?

Yes.

Need privacy?

Richeh: yes.

Tetia: "Eventually."

Agott:

"Now."

Tetia laughed.

The worksite notes caught Richeh's attention before the food did.

Not because she was a plot device.

Because Coco had ink on her hands from them.

Richeh pointed.

"You drew that?"

Coco brightened.

"Part of it."

"That's not how you used to—"

"I know."

The sentence held time.

Richeh looked again.

Coco had learned things without her.

Agott too.

Qifrey had built routines in a world Richeh had never seen.

For one small second, reunion hurt.

Coco saw.

Did not know what to say.

Richeh saved her.

"Show me later."

"Okay."

Not now.

Now was food.

Tetia sat beside Agott specifically because Agott had said not to.

Richeh chose the seat with a clear view of both exits.

Qifrey noticed.

Did not comment.

At the other end of the table, Senku was testing how many unfamiliar symbols he could hold in working memory while doing arithmetic.

Richeh watched for twenty seconds.

"Why?"

Senku looked up.

"Measurement."

"Of yourself?"

"Yes."

Richeh considered.

"That's awful."

"Correct."

Good first interaction.

Then Coco tried to explain the search.

Mau had left.

Memory trouble.

No reliable signature.

Diablo kept finding other people.

Tetia raised her hand.

"So we're mistakes?"

Diablo's expression tightened.

"False positives."

"Worse."

Richeh looked at Coco.

"Do you know him?"

"Yes."

"Do you trust him?"

Coco looked toward the empty chair where Mau usually sat.

"Yes."

Agott, from beside her:

"That is not the same as saying all this is reasonable."

Coco looked at her.

Agott continued.

"We are spending people, sleep and supplies on one search."

Richeh waited for argument.

Instead Qifrey said:

"Yes."

Agott blinked.

"Yes?"

"That cost should be spoken aloud."

Coco nodded slowly.

Richeh understood something about this new home.

They were not required to pretend love made logistics disappear.

Later, at the worksite, Coco showed Richeh one circle.

Only one.

A temporary drainage assist.

Richeh crouched.

"Too dependent on the second line."

Agott:

"I said that."

Coco:

"You say that about everything."

"Because you're reckless."

Richeh smiled.

The old rhythm returned with new stakes.

Tetia carried covered material with Suika and managed to make even that a conversation.

No one asked the new witches to solve Mau.

Good.

Their magic was not a universal key.

They helped keep the place standing while others searched.

On the walk back, Richeh asked Coco:

"Where is his room?"

Coco pointed up.

"That one."

Light off.

Empty.

Richeh looked at the window.

She had never met Mau.

Still, she now knew which dark square everyone checked when they came through the yard.

That was how absence introduced him first.
