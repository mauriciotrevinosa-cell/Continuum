# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 13 — Jinshi Gets the Wrong Welcome

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 13  
**Continuity state:** First disappearance, pre-dawn | Mau still missing | G5 social/logistical wave

Maomao was asleep at the table.

This was rare enough that nobody woke her.

Her head rested on one arm beside notes about:
food,
fever,
two newcomers with incompatible diets,
one vampire blood-supply problem that did not exist yet but would if ignored,
and Mau.

The door opened.

Everyone looked.

Diablo entered.

Not Mau.

Behind him stood Jinshi.

Maomao woke because the room changed.

She lifted her head.

Saw him.

Put her head back down.

Jinshi stared.

"Maomao."

No response.

"Maomao."

She raised one hand without looking.

"No."

The entire room watched.

Jinshi took one step.

Maomao sat up.

"You."

"Yes."

"Why."

Jinshi looked genuinely wounded.

"That's your first question?"

"It is the important one."

He looked at Diablo.

"I was brought here by a demon who insisted I might be a missing man."

Maomao closed her eyes.

"Of course."

Diablo:

"The signature—"

"Stop helping."

Rimuru covered his mouth.

He was laughing.

Jinshi finally looked around.

Overcrowded inn.

Weapons.

Maps.

A slime.

An elf on the edge of exhaustion.

Several reunited groups still not done processing.

A pink-haired vampire asleep in a chair despite insisting she did not sleep at night.

"Where are we?"

Maomao:

"Bad place for court manners."

"That is not a location."

"Continuum."

"That is not—"

"It is the best answer."

Jinshi accepted the insult before the explanation.

That was growth.

Fern ran intake.

He answered cleanly.

No entourage.

No known pursuer.

No immediate injury.

Disorientation moderate.

Political status:

Fern did not ask.

Jinshi noticed.

Interesting.

No title field.

No rank field.

No assumption that source-world authority transferred.

Maomao noticed him noticing.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

His face became neutral.

Worse.

At breakfast-that-wasn't-breakfast, Jinshi asked who Mau was.

Maomao pointed at the search board.

"Missing resident."

Rimuru added:

"Friend."

Frieren, quietly:

"Family."

Jinshi looked at her.

He understood waiting for someone with too much political machinery around the waiting.

Then he looked at the inn.

No court.

No servants assigned by rank.

People taking turns with water because there were too many bodies.

One toilet argument in progress.

A worksite still receiving materials despite the search.

"You need an intake system," he said.

Fern looked at him.

"We have one."

"A larger one."

"Yes."

"You also need someone tracking who intends to stay, who needs escort, who is waiting for someone, and who is dangerous only because nobody understands their needs."

Maomao's eyes narrowed.

"Useful."

Jinshi brightened.

Then she added:

"Don't get excited."

Too late.

At the worksite, Jinshi did not design roads.

Good.

He noticed people.

A builder asked three different people for the same approval because responsibilities were unclear.

A food delivery waited because nobody knew whether temporary arrivals counted in the headcount.

Two guests avoided the inn because they did not want to be mistaken for residents.

Jinshi wrote none of this as:
I should rule.

He wrote:
unclear responsibility creates coercion by accident.

Mau would have liked that sentence.

Jinshi did not know him.

Yet.

Before Diablo left again, Jinshi stopped him.

"You are searching for one person and returning with everyone else."

"An oversimplification."

"Is it inaccurate?"

Diablo paused.

"No."

"Then bring back information next time."

Diablo smiled.

"That is the intention."

Jinshi looked at the crowded room.

"Apparently intention needs assistance."

Maomao laughed before she could stop herself.

Jinshi looked delighted.

She looked furious.

For one brief minute, reunion became ordinary irritation.

Then Frieren stood because Diablo had opened the door again.

The whole room followed her with their eyes.

Jinshi saw the movement.

The missing man had not spoken to him.

Had not met him.

Had no political office.

And still the room rotated around the fact that one chair remained empty.

Jinshi understood influence.

This was not that.

It was relationship.

Different system.

Harder to administer.
