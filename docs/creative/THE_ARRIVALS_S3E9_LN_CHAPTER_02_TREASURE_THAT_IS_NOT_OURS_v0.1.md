# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 8 — Treasure That Is Not Ours

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 8  
**Source episode:** S3E9 — `Witches First`

The witches disagreed about Mau.

That helped.

One treated him like a puzzle.

One treated him like an injured animal that might bite.

One treated the fact that something had touched his memories as a personal insult despite having met him less than an hour earlier.

Their motives did not combine into friendship.

Mau trusted that more.

"You called me treasure."

"I almost did."

"Why?"

"Because you have no before."

Mau's hand tightened around the cup.

The witch tilted her head.

"Most people arrive carrying a world behind them."

He said nothing.

"You arrived carrying a blank."

Another witch cut in.

"Stop."

"What?"

"You're making the blank louder."

The first actually looked ashamed.

Mau hated noticing that.

They could feel something.

Not truth.

Not source.

Pressure.

One described the contaminated memories as impressions with seams where there should not be seams.

Another disagreed with the metaphor.

A third said the problem was not that the memories were fake.

"Some are."

"Some are not."

"Some are real things wearing the wrong meaning."

Mau looked up.

The Frieren/Himmel conversation.

Real place.

Real people.

Wrong sentence.

"What did you do to me?"

The room became still.

The answer came from the witch who had nearly called him treasure.

"Nothing."

Mau did not believe her.

She looked offended.

"Not this."

That distinction landed differently.

"Something used material that belongs to you," another said.

"Then the memories are mine."

"No."

Immediate.

"Material can be yours. Arrangement can be foreign."

The witch looked toward the door.

At the signal still hanging outside.

"And whoever arranged it made you walk to us."

That was when fascination became anger.

Not because they cared for Mau the way Frieren did.

Because something else had reached into a thing they considered interesting before they could understand it themselves.

Possessive.

Uncomfortable.

Still useful.

"Treasure," one said softly.

Another corrected her.

"Not ours."

For the first time that day, Mau believed them.
