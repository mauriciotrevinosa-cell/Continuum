# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 9 — The Wrong Suspect

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 9  
**Source episode:** S3E9 — `Witches First`

Frieren reached the cabin first.

Diablo was two seconds behind her.

Ori arrived with Yuta.

Rimuru arrived as slime because it was faster and became human before anyone could comment.

Milim landed outside hard enough that one witch opened the door and said:

"Could you not?"

Milim stared.

"No."

The witch closed the door again.

Frieren did not laugh.

She saw Mau.

Everything else stopped mattering.

He was sitting.

Alive.

No visible wound.

Terrified.

"Mau."

He looked at her.

Relief happened first.

Then doubt.

Frieren saw both.

That hurt more.

She crossed the room slowly.

One witch moved between them.

Frieren's mana rose.

Diablo's presence sharpened.

Rimuru said:

"Nobody starts."

The witch looked at Mau.

"He decides."

Mau looked at Frieren.

She stopped.

That mattered.

He held out his hand.

Frieren took it.

Only then did the room breathe.

The accusation came anyway.

Rimuru asked what the witches had done.

They answered badly.

Diablo asked differently.

Not "what did you do?"

"What did you detect?"

That shifted the conversation.

The witches described seams.

Wrong ownership.

Dream-material hardened into autobiography.

Real memories with altered emotional weight.

Diablo listened.

Raphael listened.

Qifrey arrived late and listened.

Nobody agreed on vocabulary.

Good.

Diablo examined Mau without touching him.

"There is foreign structure."

Mau's face changed.

Diablo added immediately:

"That does not mean foreign content."

One witch nodded.

"Exactly."

Rimuru looked irritated that the demon and witch had found common ground so quickly.

Frieren did not care.

"Can you remove it?"

Silence.

The most honest answer came from three systems at once.

"No."

Not safely.

Not completely.

Not without knowing what belonged to Mau.

Frieren tightened her grip on his hand.

The witches were no longer the obvious enemy.

That did not make them safe.

It made the real problem larger.
