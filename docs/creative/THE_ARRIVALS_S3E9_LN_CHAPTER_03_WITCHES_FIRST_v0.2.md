# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 11
## Chapter 17 — Witches First

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — REBUILD v0.2  
**Volume:** 11  
**Reading order:** 17  
**Continuity state:** Earlier — Day 2 morning | Mau: unconscious from exhaustion | Witches find him before the household has a reliable lead

The witches had found Mau the previous morning, while the inn still had no real lead.\n\nHe was face-down beside a stream.

Not dramatically.

No ritual.

No glowing symbol.

A body where a body should not have been.

One of them noticed he was breathing.

Another noticed his shoes first.

Mud.

Blood at one ankle from a shallow cut.

Hands scratched.

No major wound.

Exhaustion was doing the killing work.

They turned him carefully.

Mau did not wake.

A witch touched his forehead.

Cold from the ground.

Too warm underneath.

"Human?"

"Mostly."

"That's rude."

"I meant structurally."

"Still rude."

They carried him away from the water.

He weighed more than curiosity suggested.

The nearest shelter was not theirs exactly.

Nothing around them belonged to one person in a way outsiders would understand.

Mau remained unconscious through water on his lips.

Through a blanket.

Through voices.

At one point his eyes moved beneath the lids.

He said:

"Frieren."

That changed the room.

Not because they knew the name.

Because the emotional shape around it was unmistakable.

One witch leaned closer.

"Who is Frieren?"

Another pulled her back.

"He's unconscious."

"He answered."

"He made a sound."

Mau's breathing tightened.

Dream.

Memory.

Something crossed his face.

Then:

"No. That's not what you said."

The witches looked at one another.

Interest sharpened.

A dream specialist would have called one part familiar.

A perception specialist another.

None liked the seams.

The memory pressure around Mau did not behave like one person remembering badly.

Nor like one clean external spell.

Pieces matched.

Pieces contradicted.

Emotional certainty was attached to structures that did not align with the rest.

One witch murmured:

"Someone has been rearranging a room without moving the walls."

Another disagreed.

"The walls moved too."

A third:

"Not all of them."

Mau's hand twitched.

The first witch watched him.

"Treasure."

"Don't."

"I didn't mean possession."

"You never do until you do."

Mau did not wake to object.

That made the word worse.

The witches stayed.

Not because they had become his friends.

Because whatever had happened to him offended several different kinds of curiosity at once.

Hours earlier, Mau had followed a certainty toward this place.

He had walked until the certainty stopped giving directions.

Then kept going because going back required trusting the memory that told him where home was.

By the time the witches found him, his body had solved the argument.

It shut down.

The family was still coming.

The witches were first.
