# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 4 — Rules About Him

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 4  
**Continuity state:** Night of Day 5 | Rings One and Two arrange the next six days around him | Mau sees his fourth draft from the other side | Frieren's hand, two seconds | Rimuru: 'So. The number.'

They were all there.

Rings One and Two, the way they had been at the council: the long table and the benches and the floor, the cold hearth with Rimuru beside it, the board with its four rings and its three new rules and SLEEP. Fern at the head of the table with the ledger. Anko with her notebook. Yuta and Maki on the bench under the window. Eren by the window itself, standing, the way he always stood, as if he might have to leave. Mikasa beside him. Milim on the floor, cross-legged, with her back to the room. The fort had emptied into the common room and sat in a cluster at the north end in front of its own curtained doorway, as if guarding it. Ori was among them, at the back, with her knees up.

Frieren was on the couch. There was a space beside her.

Mau sat in it.

Nobody said anything about the fact that he had come.



They did not start with him.

They started, the way the house always started, with the work, because the work was what held them up. Fern opened the ledger and read the day's entries aloud in her clear even voice, and they went through them one by one.

Arrival House had seen forty-one people for terms that day. The line had gone around the corner twice. Jinshi and the staff had added a second witness room, and a rule against announcing decisions inside the building, after a man had come out of his terms shaking and tried to tell the whole waiting room what he had chosen.

The second community's bridge crossing had reported eleven people crossing *in* that week. That was fewer than any week since Last Defense. Fourteen had crossed *out*. The woman who ran the crossing had told Mikasa privately that some of them had asked, at the bridge, whether the *monster town* would let them leave if they wanted to.

Anko's count of rumors stood at six. The newest one, from the river towns, said that the man with the summon in his shadow had tried to send everyone in the settlement away to a place they could not come back from, so that he could have it to himself.

"That one's almost true," said Anko, without inflection, "which makes it the dangerous one."

Nobody looked at Mau.



Then they got to him. Not *to* him. *About* him.

"Supervision," said Fern. "We need a rotation for tomorrow. Yuta had today. Mikasa had the afternoon."

"I'll take the morning," said Maki.

"He has to go to the washing court," said Momo. "Every day. Maomao's rule, from the council. He has to be seen."

"Not with the crowd the way it is," said Mikasa. "There were three people in the plaza today who wanted him to know they'd seen him. They made sure he knew."

"Then the bridge-side court. It's quieter."

"Then he looks like he's hiding."

"He *is* hiding," said Maki, "from forty people who think he tried to send them into the dark."

"He should talk to them," said Anko. "To Arrival House. Not to explain himself. Just to be seen asking nobody for anything."

"He shouldn't go near Arrival House," said Jinshi at once. "Not during the window. Not with the terms rooms open. If one person who's come out of their terms shaking sees him in the corridor, we'll have a riot."

"What about at night," said Senku. "If he floats again. Frieren can't sleep tied to a bedpost for six more nights."

"I can," said Frieren.

"You shouldn't have to."

"He could sleep in the fort," said Hakari. "There's room now. There's six spare—"

"No," said Umaru, from the north end, very quietly. "Not those."

"What about Ciel," said Yuta, turning to Rimuru. "Can she watch him at night? Not the walls. Him."

"Not without breaking rule four," said Rimuru. "She watches the guardrails, nothing else, and she tells him every time she looks. That's the rule. *His* addition."

"Then we change it."

"We don't change his rules while he's—"

"He's *sitting right there*," said Maki.

There was a short silence.

Everyone looked, briefly, at Mau. Then away.

And they went on.

They went on arranging the next six days around him. Who would walk with him, and where. What would be said in the plaza and what would not. What the house would tell Ring Three, what Ring Three would tell Ring Four, and what Anko would put in the evidence room against the six rumors. How the house would keep him from the Goddess's sites, and from the first clearing, and from the road south, and from himself. They were careful and kind and furious and thorough. Every word of it was about him. And not one sentence was said to him.

Mau sat on the couch and listened.

Somewhere around the middle of it, he understood what he was listening to.

He had done this. On the night of the fourth draft, at the little table in the room with the east window, at three in the morning, with Frieren asleep behind him. He had sat and arranged the futures of everyone in this room, carefully and kindly and thoroughly, in a notebook, while they slept. He had decided what would be best for them. He had decided it would be easier for them not to have to choose. He had never once asked.

This was what it looked like from the other side.

It was very quiet, from the other side. It felt like being a piece of furniture in a room where people were deciding where the furniture should go.

He opened his mouth.

"I could talk to Arrival House," he said. "If it would help. I could—"

"No," said three people at once.

He closed his mouth.

Frieren, beside him, did not look at him. But under the edge of the couch, where nobody else could see, the back of her hand came to rest against the back of his. Not holding it, only touching it. It was the first time she had touched him since the road.

It lasted perhaps two seconds. Then her hand went back to her own knee.



It was Rimuru who stopped it.

He had been sitting by the cold hearth through all of it, with his elbows on his knees and his face very still, saying nothing except the thing about rule four. Then, in the middle of a sentence of Jinshi's about which corridor of Arrival House could be closed off, he stood up.

"Enough," said Rimuru.

The room went quiet.

"Enough rules," said Rimuru. "We've been making rules for two days. Rules about where he walks, and who he sees, and where he sleeps, and what he's allowed to say in the plaza. We've written so many rules about him that we could paper the walls with them." He looked down the long table, at all of them. "And we still don't know the one thing we actually need to know. We don't know what happened in there."

Nobody answered.

Rimuru turned.

He looked straight at Mau. Not *about* him. *At* him. It was the first time anyone in the room had done that all evening.

"You made a promise to someone," said Rimuru. "You know who." His voice was very level. "You promised you'd tell me a number. Yourself. Not through her. You made her promise to wait for you, and she's waited. She told me *not forever*." He paused. "It's been forever, Mau."

In two heads in that room, and only two, very faintly, a voice said one word.

*Master.*

Rimuru did not look away.

"So," he said. "The number."
