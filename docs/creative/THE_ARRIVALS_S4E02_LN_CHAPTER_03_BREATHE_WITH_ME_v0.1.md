# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 7 — Breathe With Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 7  
**Continuity state:** Same night | Mau's panic attack: Maki's loop, the white-haired man, the knife | Maki talks him down without touching: 'Look at me. Breathe with me.' | Frieren recognizes the words from her own night

It came all at once, the way it had come to Frieren on the floor beside the bed on the night of the bay. He did not know that. He had not been awake for it. Nobody had told him, because that night was not spoken of.

His breath went short and high, and then shorter, and then it stopped going anywhere at all. It sat in the top of his chest and fluttered there like something trapped. The room tipped. The lamps went bright and far away at the same time.

And the scenarios came.

Not one at a time. All of them. Every room the Trial had ever built rushed into the common room of the Muslin and stood there, laid over it, the way the clearing had been laid over the plaza at the end.

A field of broken stone, and Maki.

Maki, beside him, back to back, the way she fought beside Yuta. Something came at them out of the dust, and they fought it together for a time he could not measure. Hours. Days. He did not know. The light never changed in that one. They fought until her blade broke, and then until his arms would not lift. Then neither of them was standing, and he could not remember which of them had fallen first. Only that neither of them had made it out. Only that she had looked at him at the end and said *well, that's annoying*, the way the real Maki would have, and then had not said anything again.

A street of white light, and a man.

He did not know the man. White hair, and something over his eyes, and a smile like a door left open on purpose. The man had not even seemed to be trying. Mau had thrown everything he had at him, every pattern, every shape the Trial would let him build, and every one of them had stopped in the air a finger's width short. The man had looked at him, almost kindly, and said something he could not remember. And then it was over, and he was back at the start, and he never found out who the man was, or why he had been there, or whose side he was on.

A room with no doors, and a voice.

*We have her.* Frieren, somewhere he could not see. Frieren, whom he could hear, saying his name. *She lives if you do it yourself.* And a knife, placed very neatly on a table in front of him, and his own hand reaching for it, and—

"Mau."

He did not know where the voice was.

"Mau. Look at me."

It was not Frieren's. It was lower, flatter, rougher.

"Not at her. Not at them. At *me*."

He looked.

Maki was kneeling on the floor in front of him. He did not remember getting down onto the floor himself, but he was on it, on his knees, at the end of the couch, with his hands pressed flat against the boards. She was close, very close, close enough that he could see the small scar at the edge of her eyebrow. But she was not touching him. Her hands were on her own knees. She had put them there on purpose, where he could see them.

"There you are," said Maki. "Good. Now breathe with me."

He tried. It caught halfway.

"In," said Maki. "Slowly. Like this." She breathed in, slow and deep, and held it. "Out."

He tried again. It caught.

"Again," said Maki. "In."

"I can't—"

"You can. You've done it your whole life. Both your lives. However many lives you've got now." Her voice did not change at all. "You're just doing it too fast. In."

He breathed in.

"Out."

Out.

"Again."



On the couch, Frieren went completely still.

She knew those words.

She knew them the way you know a song you heard once in a fever, and did not know you remembered until someone hummed it. *Look at me. Not at your hands. At me. Breathe with me.* A floor beside a bed. Her own hands held out in front of her, dark to the wrist. A voice, low and flat and rough, counting. *You've done it for a thousand years. You're just doing it too fast.*

She had never told anyone how she had come back that night. She had not known how to describe it, and then it had been written in the ledger that the night was not spoken of. She had never asked Maki what she had done, or why it had worked, or where she had learned it.

She was watching it now.

She was watching Maki kneel in front of Mau, close but not touching, with her hands on her own knees where he could see them, and count his breaths back into him one at a time. It was exactly the way she had counted Frieren's.

Frieren pressed her open hand flat against her own chest. She held it there.



It took a long time.

Nobody else moved. Nobody else spoke. The whole house sat in the common room and watched Maki bring him back. Nobody tried to help, because everybody understood, without anyone saying it, that helping was what Maki was doing, and that more hands would only be more things coming at him.

Yuta, on the bench, had both his hands over his mouth. His eyes did not leave Maki's back.

At last Mau's breathing slowed. It was not right, not yet, but it was going somewhere again. In, and out. In, and out, at Maki's count. His hands came up off the floor. He sat back on his heels. The rooms that had been laid over the common room went thin, and then thinner, and then they were only the common room again. The lamps were the right brightness and the right distance away. The pot in the kitchen had stopped ticking.

"There," said Maki. "There you are."

Mau looked at her.

"You died," he said. His voice was wrecked. "In one of them. We fought something together. For a long time. I don't know how long. And we both—" He stopped. "You said *well, that's annoying*. At the end."

Maki looked at him for a moment.

"Sounds like me," she said.

Then, slowly, telegraphing every inch of it so that he could see it coming and say no, she reached out and put one hand on his shoulder.

He did not pull away.

She left it there for a breath, and then two, and then took it back to her own knee.

"Good," said Maki quietly. "That's enough for now. That's plenty."

She stood up, went back to the bench under the window, and sat down next to Yuta. And Yuta, without a word, took her hand in both of his and held on to it so hard that his knuckles went white, and did not let go for the rest of the night.
