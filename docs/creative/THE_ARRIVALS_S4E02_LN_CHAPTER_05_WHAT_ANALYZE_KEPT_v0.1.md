# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 9 — What Analyze Kept

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 9  
**Continuity state:** Same night | Ciel confesses she looked (rule four); Ori: 'Tell him first. Before.' | Analyze read every scenario; people who haven't arrived yet; it is building, slowly | Rika understood (begun on the night of the bay) | limits

When it was quiet again, Rimuru spoke.

He had come away from the cold hearth at some point during the apologies, and now he was sitting on the floor too, a little way from Mau, with his back against the long table. He looked very tired. His eyes kept going somewhere else and coming back.

"Ciel wants to say something," he said. "Out loud. Through me. She's asked me to say it exactly as she says it." He paused. "She's never asked that before."

Nobody objected.

Rimuru closed his eyes, the way he did when he wanted to get the words right. When he spoke again it was in his own voice, but with her rhythm in it, plain and level, each sentence set down like a stone on a table.

"*First,*" said Rimuru, "*I have broken a rule.*"



"*While I was silent,*" he said, "*in the days after the Trial, I looked.*

"*Not at the walls. Not at the guardrails. At him. At his memories. I wanted to understand what I had counted. I had counted four hundred and thirty-seven of something, and I did not know what it was, and I could not bear it. I am not built to count without knowing.*

"*So I looked. I told myself it was an emergency. It was. It is also a breach of rule four. Ciel watches the guardrails, and nothing else in Mau's head, unless Mau asks. Mau did not ask.*

"*Rule four has an addition. His. Ciel tells Mau every time she looks.*

"*I am telling him now. I am telling all of you. I am sorry.*"

Rimuru opened his eyes.

At the north end of the room, in front of the fort's curtain, someone stood up.

It was Ori.

She had not said anything all night. She had not said anything to Mau, or near him, or about him, since the road. Now she stood with her hands in fists at her sides and her face very white, and looked at Rimuru, not at Mau.

"That's my rule," said Ori.

"*Yes,*" said Rimuru, in Ciel's rhythm.

"You weren't supposed to look. Not without asking. That was the whole *point*." Her voice shook. "After the containment, everyone was in his head. You were in his head. The pattern was in his head. The false memories were in his head. I made the rule so that something in there would be his. Only his."

"*Yes.*"

Ori looked at the floor for a long time.

"Did it help?" she said finally. "When you looked. Did you find out what you counted?"

"*Yes.*"

"Then—" Ori stopped. Her fists unclenched, very slowly. "Then next time, tell him first. Before. Not after. Ask him before you look." She lifted her chin. "That's how you keep it. You tell him first."

There was a pause.

"*Yes,*" said Rimuru. "*I will tell him first.*"

Ori sat down again, at the back, with her knees up. She still had not looked at Mau.

But Mau had heard. *Tell him first.* It was her word for it. It had been her word since the tidepools at dawn, on the morning after the sea, when she had made him promise: *when something happens to you, tell me first, before you go.* He had broken it, on the road, with a note.

She had just given that promise to someone else, for him.



"*Second,*" said Rimuru.

He opened his eyes and looked at Mau.

"*Analyze kept things.*

"*In the Trial, every scenario was built of something. Of people, and places, and powers, and things. I do not know where the Goddess took them from. Some of them were you, all of you, as you are. Some of them were things from your worlds. Some of them were neither.*

"*Analyze was in there with him the whole time. It does what it does. Observe. Analyze. Understand. It did not stop because the world around it was false. It read everything. Four hundred and thirty-seven times.*

"*It does not know what it read. I have looked, and I cannot tell. It only understands that in every one of those memories there were people who should not have been there, or who have not arrived here yet. And things he did not know existed. It understands that it has the shapes of them now.*

"*And it is building them.*"

Nobody moved.

"*Slowly,*" said Rimuru. "*I want to be precise. Slowly.*

"*He will not have only a hundred and forty-three patterns behind the walls anymore. I cannot give you a final number. I do not believe Analyze knows the final number. I will count them as they appear, and I will tell him each one. First.*"

Senku had put down his notebook.

"How many so far?" he said.

"*I do not know how to count something that is still deciding what it is.*"



"*Third,*" said Rimuru. And then, in his own voice, more quietly: "Yuta. This one's for you."

Yuta looked up.

"*It understood Rika,*" said Rimuru.

The air behind Yuta changed. Very faintly. Something cold and enormous and fond turned its attention to the room. Several people felt it, and did not move.

"*It began on the night of the bay,*" said Rimuru. "*For two days, Rika gave you her reserve, and you poured it into him. Analyze was there. It reads everything it touches. Senku would not let Eren's blood near it, because of that.*" A small, strange pause. "*Rika went in anyway. There was no other way.*

"*It did not understand her then. It was busy. It was learning that the walls were not a wound.*

"*But Rika was in the Trial too. In many of the scenarios. Always behind you. Always the same. And in there, over and over, it read her again. And at last it understood how she works. How she holds what she holds. The infinity of it.*"

Yuta's face had gone very still.

"*One day,*" said Rimuru, "*he may be able to copy. As you do. I do not know what he will be able to copy, or how, or when. I do not think it will be soon. But the shape of it is in him now, and it was not before.*"

The cold presence behind Yuta lingered for a moment longer, turned toward Mau. Then it settled, the way a cat settles.

"Rika says," said Yuta slowly, in an odd voice, "she doesn't mind."



"*Last,*" said Rimuru. "*The limits. Because you will all want to know the limits.*

"*Analyze is slow. It does not build things in an hour, or a day. It took weeks to decide the walls were a wound, and two days for me to teach it otherwise.*

"*Everything it builds costs him. Mana. Cursed energy. Strength. If it builds something large, he will be tired, or ill, or worse, and Maomao will be very angry.*

"*I am writing new rules. They will go into the guardrails, beside the old ones, if the household agrees. He will read every one of them first.*

"*And the walls hold. I check them every day. They held through the Trial. They held through the road. They are holding now.*"

Rimuru opened his eyes, and his voice was his own again.

"That's what she wanted to say," he said. "That's all of it." He hesitated. "Except, she says—"

He stopped and listened.

"She says she's sorry she looked," said Rimuru. "And she isn't sorry she knows."
