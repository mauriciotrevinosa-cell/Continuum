# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 12 — Somewhere I Never Died

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 12  
**Continuity state:** Night of Day 5 to dawn of Day 6 | north, upriver, past the stepping stones | the Trial used every place he loved | he falls and stops a finger's width above the stones | the falls where Senku found no clay | 'Here.'

He walked north in the dark with the lamp swinging, and did not look back.

He knew he would want to. He had decided at the door that he would not. If he looked back and saw the windows of the Muslin lit, and the shape of the fort's flag on its broom handle against the common-room glass, he would turn around. He would walk back in, and they would all look at him, and nobody would be able to touch him, and he would have to watch them try.

So he did not look back. He walked.

Past the yard. Past the ink tree in its ring of stakes, which had nine leaves now, maybe ten, silver-green in the lamplight. Past the edge of the plaza, where Arrival House still had one window lit at this hour, someone in there reading their terms for the seventh time. Past the washing court, empty and cold, the stone channels running quietly in the dark. Down to the river path.

The river was loud at night. He had never noticed that before. In the daytime it was only background, a sound under the sound of the settlement. At night, with nothing else to hear, it filled everything.

He turned upriver.



He had chosen it on the floor of the room with the east window, in the fifteen minutes. He had sat with the backpack open in front of him and thought: *where?*

Not the first clearing. That was the first thing he thought, and he thought it so hard it felt like a hand on his chest. The first clearing was where the wedding had been. The first clearing was where he had planned to go to the Goddess. It was on the board in Fern's hand, NO RESIDUAL TRIAL / GODDESS CONTACT SITE ALONE, and he had looked at that rule on the board every morning and known it was there because of him.

Not the road south. Not the old inn. Not the plaza, or the bridge, or the washing court, or the sea, or the room with the east window, or the common room, or any of the places he loved. Because the Trial had used them. All of them. That was what he had understood, sitting on the floor with the backpack. The Trial had built its rooms out of the places he loved, so that he would know them when he died in them. He had died in the plaza. He had died at the old inn. He had died on the bridge, and at the sea, and in the room with the east window, more than once. There was nowhere in the settlement that had not, somewhere in four hundred and thirty-seven, been the last place he saw.

So he had thought: *somewhere it never used.*

Somewhere ordinary. Somewhere nobody went, because there was no reason to. Somewhere the Goddess would not have bothered to build, because there was nothing there he loved.

Upriver, past the stepping stones, the river came down out of the hills in a long broken stair of pools and falls. He had been there exactly once, months ago, with Senku and Suika, looking for a better clay bank for the kilns. They had not found one. Senku had said *useless* about the whole place and turned around. Mau had stood on a flat rock above the highest pool for a minute, looking at the water, and then followed him.

That was all. He had never thought of it again. He had never loved it, or been afraid there, or been anything there at all.

It had never been in the Trial. He was almost sure.

That was where he was going.



It took most of the night.

The path ran out past the stepping stones, and after that he followed the river itself, over stones and through brush, with the lamp held out in front of him. Twice he slipped. The first time his foot went into the water up to the ankle, and he stood there for a moment feeling the cold come through his boot.

He could feel it. The cold. The water. That was good. He had wondered whether he would.

The second time, he fell.

He went down hard on a slope of wet stones, sideways, the lamp flying out of his hand. He threw out his arm to catch himself, and his hand did not reach the stone. It stopped a finger's width above it. All of him did. He hung there for an instant on nothing, a finger's width above the ground, the way he had drifted at the end of the ribbon in his sleep. Then, slowly, as if the world had remembered him, he settled the last finger's width onto the stones. He felt nothing at all.

He lay there in the dark for a while, on his side, with the lamp rolled away and guttering a little way off.

Infinity did not care whether something was a hand reaching for him or the ground coming up to hit him. It kept everything out. It had kept her out. It was keeping the ground out.

He laughed, once, into the dark. It did not sound like a laugh.

Then he got up, retrieved the lamp, and kept going.



He reached the falls a little before dawn.

They were smaller than he remembered. A long stair of pools stepping down out of the hills, each one spilling into the next over a lip of grey stone, the water white where it fell and black where it lay. Above the highest pool was the flat rock. Pines on the slope behind, with their branches heavy and dark. No path. No building. No sign that anyone had ever stopped here for any reason except to look for clay, find none, and leave.

He climbed up to the flat rock and set the lamp down on it, and the backpack beside it, and stood there.

He looked around for a long time.

Nothing.

No memory came. No room laid itself over the pools. Nobody he loved lay at the bottom of any of them. There was no smoke, no frost, no cut flowers. There was only water, falling, and pines, and the sky going grey in the east over the hills.

He had never died here.

He sat down on the flat rock very slowly, with his knees up and his arms around them, and watched the light come.

Some time in the first hour of the morning, without deciding to, he fell asleep sitting up. He slept the way you sleep after carrying something heavy a very long way: completely, with nothing in it. When he woke it was full day, and the sun was on the rock, and he had not floated, and nobody had tied him down, and nothing had reset.

He sat and looked at the water for a long time.

*Here,* he thought.

It was the first day.
