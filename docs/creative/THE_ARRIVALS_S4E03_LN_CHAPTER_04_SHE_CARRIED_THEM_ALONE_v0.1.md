# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 15 — She Carried Them Alone

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 15  
**Continuity state:** Night of Day 6, the roof | Rimuru tells Yuta about Chloe, and the far-end 'possibility' | 'Now it's not alone' | Yuta: Gojo never got to walk back | Ciel: 'He is dry. He would rather not be.'

That night, Yuta found Rimuru on the roof.

It was not the roof night. The roof was once a week, by Yuta's rule, written on the board at the council in his own hand: *the roof, once a week, that's my job too*. Three of them, him and Rimuru and Mau, on the flat part of the Muslin's roof above the common room, where you could see the whole settlement and the river and the line of the hills. It was not the night for it. But Rimuru was up there anyway, alone, in the cold, sitting with his knees up and his human shape held together by what looked like a great deal of effort.

Yuta climbed up and sat beside him. He did not say anything.

Below them, the settlement had gone to bed. Arrival House's window was dark, for once. The lamps along the bridge burned. To the north, the river path ran away into the dark toward the hills, and somewhere up there, beyond where they could see, a single small light that they also could not see was burning on a rock.

"He said it," said Rimuru at last. "*Here.* She sent it."

"I heard."

"I keep thinking it's not enough. One word. I keep thinking I should have asked for more. A sentence. A report. Where he is, whether he's eaten, whether he's—" He stopped. "And then I think, no. One word is right. One word is exactly right. Because that's what she sent him."

Yuta waited.

"In the Trial," said Rimuru. "One word each time. At the start. That's all she could get through. The count." He looked at his hands. "He got four hundred and thirty-seven words from her. Numbers. One at a time. Each one meant he'd died again, and she didn't know it, and he did." He let out a breath that went white in the cold. "And now we get one word a day from him. *Here.* Each one means he hasn't."

Yuta did not say anything for a long time.



"I have to tell someone something," said Rimuru. "And I want it to be you. Because of the roof. Because you made the roof rule, and the roof rule says that if either of us starts carrying something he shouldn't, the others say so."

"Okay."

"I'm carrying something I shouldn't."

"Okay."

Rimuru looked out at the dark hills.

"There was a girl," he said. "In my world. In the city. A long time ago. Her name was Chloe."

He told Yuta about Chloe.

He had never told anyone here the whole of it. He had told them about Tempest at the council: the twenty thousand, the city, the friend who had smiled at him for years and been working with clowns the whole time. He had not told them about Chloe. It had seemed like the kind of thing that belonged to another world. It was too large to carry across.

He told it now. About a girl who had loved him the way children love the people who save them. About the power she had found: to go back through time, the whole way, two thousand years at once, and live forward to the moment she had left. And about how, in that time, again and again, something had gone wrong, and he had died. Every time, she went back. Every time, she lived the two thousand years again, alone, carrying everything she knew, trying to change one thing that would let him live.

"I don't know how many times," said Rimuru. "Nobody does. Not even Raphael could count. Hundreds, maybe. Thousands. Two thousand years each time. She did that for me. Alone. And I didn't know. I was living my life, building my city, sleeping in my bed, and all that time she was carrying it. I found out at the very end." He was quiet for a moment. "And I swore. After. With everything I am. That I would never let anyone carry something like that alone for me again. Never."

Yuta said nothing.

"And he did," said Rimuru. "For us. Four hundred and thirty-seven times. And I was standing three steps away from him on that road, and I didn't know." His voice did not break. It went very thin instead, like ice over deep water. "Ciel gave me two numbers last night. I said one of them out loud. The small one. A hundred years older than Frieren."

"And the other?"

Rimuru closed his eyes.

"She said it was a possibility. Only a possibility. The far edge of three measurements that don't agree. She doesn't think it's likely." He swallowed. "She said it's possible that he's older than all of Chloe's resets put together."

Yuta did not move.

"I didn't say it," said Rimuru. "I folded it up and put it somewhere and told myself it was only an example. The horizon. Something you point at without meaning anyone will walk there." He opened his eyes. "But I can't stop looking at it. All day. It's all I can see. That's what I'm carrying. That's what I shouldn't be carrying alone."

The cold came off the river. Somewhere below them a door closed. Somebody's lamp went out.

"Now it's not alone," said Yuta.

Rimuru looked at him.

"You told me," said Yuta. "So now there are two of us carrying it. That's the rule. That's the whole rule." He paused. "We don't tell him. Not yet. Ciel said possible. We don't put the horizon on him when he's sitting on a rock up there trying to learn how to get wet. But you don't carry it by yourself either. Not on my roof."

Rimuru did not answer for a long moment.

Then something in his shoulders came down, a very little way. His human shape went slightly rounder at the edges, the way it did only when he was so tired he had stopped holding it.

"Okay," he said.



They sat on the roof for a long time.

"In my world," said Yuta eventually, "there was a man everybody called the strongest. You know about him. I've told you."

"Gojo."

"He stood in front of everyone. Alone. Because he was the strongest, and nobody else could. And we let him." Yuta looked out at the hills. "I let him. I was strong enough to stand next to him, almost, and I didn't. I let him go in front, because that's where he always went, and because I thought he'd be all right, because he always was." He was quiet. "He wasn't."

Rimuru said nothing.

"On the road," said Yuta, "when we caught Mau. Before the sun came up. I kept thinking, *this is different*. This time we went after him. This time we didn't let him stand in front alone. We ran down a road in the dark and caught him and dragged him back, and he hated it, and we did it anyway." His hands tightened on his knees. "I kept thinking, that's different from my world. That's the thing I didn't do."

"Is it enough?" said Rimuru.

Yuta thought about it.

"No," he said honestly. "But it's a start. And tomorrow night, or the morning after, he's going to walk back down that river path on his own. By himself. Because he said he would." He looked at Rimuru. "Gojo never got to walk back. Not once. Mau's going to walk back. That's different too."

Rimuru looked out at the dark north, where the river went.

"Ciel," he said, out loud.

*Yes, Master.*

"Is he all right?"

A pause.

*He is asleep,* said Ciel. *On a rock. In the rain. He is dry.* Another pause. *He would rather not be.*

Rimuru made a sound that was almost a laugh.

"Of course he would," he said.
