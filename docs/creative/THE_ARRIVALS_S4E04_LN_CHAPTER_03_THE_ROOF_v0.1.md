# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 21 — The Roof

**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 21

That night was the roof.

It was not the night for it. The roof was once a week. But Yuta came to the room with the east window after dinner and stood in the doorway and said *roof*, and Rimuru dropped off the loft ladder in slime form and said *roof*, and Frieren, in the reading chair, without looking up from her book, said *go*.

So they went.

The flat part of the Muslin's roof, above the common room. The whole settlement below them, lamps and dark roofs, and the river, and the line of the hills to the north against the stars. It was very cold. Marin had made sure all three of them were wearing their winter coats, with their names still pinned to them, before she would let them up the ladder.

They sat in a row. Yuta, Mau, Rimuru. Nobody said anything for a long time.



"I need to tell you about someone," said Rimuru finally.

He told Mau about Chloe.

Not all of it. Not the far end, the possibility. He had folded that up and given half of it to Yuta on this roof two nights before, and he and Yuta had agreed: not yet. Not on someone who had just learned how to get wet again.

But the rest. A girl in another world. A power to go back. Two thousand years each time, alone, carrying everything, again and again, so that he would live. And him, not knowing. Living his life, building his city, sleeping in his bed. Finding out at the very end.

"I swore," said Rimuru. "After. That I'd never let anyone carry something like that alone for me again. Never."

He looked at Mau.

"And you did," he said. "For us. And I was three steps away from you on that road. And I didn't know."

Mau did not say anything.

"I'm not angry at you," said Rimuru. "I want you to know that. I was. On the road. I'm not now. I'm angry at me." He looked out at the hills. "I've been here before. I should have known what it looked like. When someone comes out of something like that and doesn't say anything. When someone stops sleeping and starts writing things down at three in the morning. I knew what it looked like. I've seen it. I should have known."

"You couldn't have," said Mau. "I made Ciel promise."

"I know. And she kept it. And I'm proud of her for keeping it, and I hate that she had to." Rimuru's mouth twisted. "That's the thing about Chloe. She didn't tell me either. Not because she didn't trust me. Because she was the only one who could carry it, and telling me wouldn't have made it lighter. It would only have made two of us who couldn't put it down." He paused. "I think that's what you thought too."

Mau looked at his hands.

"Yes," he said.

"It's not true," said Rimuru. "It wasn't true for her, either. I only found out after." He leaned back on his hands and looked up at the stars. "It would have been lighter. It's lighter now. Just from you saying the number out loud on the floor of the common room. Just from that." He was quiet for a moment. "I'm not going to let you carry it alone again. I know I can't make you. I'm telling you anyway. That's all."



Yuta had not said anything. He was looking at the hills.

"Gojo never walked back," he said.

Mau turned his head.

"I've told you about him," said Yuta. "The strongest. The one in front. Everybody let him stand there alone, because he was the strongest, because he always came back. I let him." His breath went white. "One day he didn't come back. And all of us who'd let him stand in front alone had to stand there afterward and look at the place where he'd been."

He was quiet for a long time.

"On the road," he said, "when we caught you, I kept thinking, *this is different*. Because we went after you. Because we didn't let you go in front alone." He shook his head. "But that's not the different thing. Anyone can run down a road. The different thing is yesterday. You walked back. By yourself. Down the river path, at dusk, with your hair wet, because you said you would." He looked at Mau. "Gojo never got to do that. Not once. You did."

"I'm not him," said Mau.

"No," said Yuta. "You're not. You're the one who walked back. That's the whole difference." He paused. "Keep being that one."



They sat on the roof for a long time after that, not talking.

Below them, the settlement slept. One window was lit at Arrival House: someone reading their terms. Two days left in the window.

"Roof every night," said Yuta eventually. "For a while. Not once a week. Every night. Until I say."

"Is that a rule?" said Mau.

"It's my rule. The roof rule. If one of us starts carrying something he shouldn't, the others say so." Yuta stood up and stretched. "I'm saying so."

Rimuru let his human shape go, all at once, and was a slime on the cold roof. He climbed up onto Mau's knee and settled there like a small warm stone.

"Seconded," he said.

Mau put his hand on him, without thinking.

He could feel him. Warm in the middle, the way Frieren had once told him a slime was warm in the middle. He kept his hand there.
