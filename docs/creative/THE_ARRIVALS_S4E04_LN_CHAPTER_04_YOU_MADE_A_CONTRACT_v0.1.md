# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 22 — You Made a Contract

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 22  
**Continuity state:** Day 9, morning, after the roof night | Mau asks to go for his own terms; 'step two is we talk', and the answer is no; he accepts | 'Stop protecting me... let me be that idiot' | the ledger: 'Mau is not protected from his own life' | the petition-vetting agreement; his corner

On the ninth morning, Mau asked.

He did it properly. He went to the common room after breakfast, when everyone was still at the long table, and stood at the end of it, and waited until they were looking at him.

"The condition," he said. "Fern's page. *Mau does not go to the Goddess until he tells us what happened, and we talk about it.*"

Fern did not open the ledger. She did not need to.

"I told you," said Mau. "On the floor, two nights ago. Everything I could. And we talked. Some of it on the floor, and some of it on the roof, and some of it in the fort." He glanced at Umaru. "So I'd like to go. Not alone. Not before dawn. Not to ask for anything for anyone. I'd like to go with whoever wants to come, in daylight, like everyone else, and ask for my own terms. Just mine. Like a person."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Fern opened the ledger after all, and turned it around on the table so that he could see the page.

"Read the bottom," she said.

He read it.

*MAU DOES NOT GO TO THE GODDESS.*
*Not alone. Not with anyone. Not for anyone.*
*Until:*
*1. He tells us what happened.*
*2. We talk about it.*

"Yes," said Mau. "That's—"

"We talked about it," said Fern. "All of us. Last night, while you were on the roof." She folded her hands on the table. "Step two isn't *we talk, and then he does what he wants*. Step two is *we talk*. And this is what we decided, at the end of talking." She looked at him steadily. "You don't go."

Mau looked at her.

"Not this time," said Fern. "Not in this window. Not for terms, not with witnesses, not in daylight. You don't go to the Goddess at all."

"Why?"

"Because you made a contract," said Rimuru, from the cold hearth. "With us. You signed it. *Not tonight*, you said, and then you signed it anyway. And a contract you sign with your family means you accept what your family decides when the talking's done." He did not say it unkindly. "Even when you don't like it."

"*Especially* when you don't like it," said Maki.

Mau stood at the end of the table for a long moment.

He thought about arguing. He had the arguments. He could feel them lining up: *it's my terms, it affects only me, you said the choice was individual, the rules say*. They were good arguments. Some of them were even true.

He thought about a notebook at three in the morning, and four drafts, and the word *intend*.

"Okay," said Mau.

Nobody had expected that. He could see it go round the table.

"Okay," he said again. "I won't go. I made a contract. I'll accept what you decided."

He meant it. He was surprised how much.



That would have been the end of it. Fern was already closing the ledger. People were already turning back to their bowls.

"But," said Mau.

The bowls stopped.

"I want to say something," said Mau. "Not about the Goddess. I'm not going. That's done." He swallowed. "About after. About everything after."

Fern opened the ledger again and picked up her pen.

He stood at the end of the long table with his hands at his sides. He did not know how to say it. He had been thinking about it since the rock, since the leaf, since the roof, and he still did not know how to say it. So he said it the only way he could, which was all at once.

"Stop protecting me," said Mau.

The room went still.

"Not from the Goddess," he said quickly. "That's fair. That's what I signed. I mean everything else. For two days before the talk you made rules about me. Where I walk, where I sleep, who I talk to. You talked about me at this table as if I weren't sitting at it. And I understood. I did it to you first. On the fourth draft. So I understood." He took a breath. "But I can't live like that. I can't be the thing everyone arranges the furniture around."

Nobody said anything.

"And there's another thing," said Mau. "You keep telling me you don't need me. That's what all of it is. *We don't need you to save us. We don't need you to stand in front. We'll be fine.* And I know why you say it. You're trying to take it off me. You're trying to make it so I don't have to carry anything." His voice wavered. "But when you tell me you don't need me, I don't hear *we love you*. I hear *you can go*. Do you understand? That's what it sounds like, from in here. It sounds like the door."

Bocchi, at the far end of the table, put her hand over her mouth.

"So don't," said Mau. "Don't protect me by telling me I'm not needed. It hurts more. Let me go. Let me *do* things. Let me help. Let me carry my corner, and let me fail, and let me be bad at it, and tell me when I'm being stupid. That's the roof rule. That's the rule you already made." He looked down the table at all of them. "And I'm going to get stronger. You know that. Ciel told you. Analyze is building things in me nobody understands yet. In a few years, maybe less, I'm going to be something nobody can stop. Rimuru said it, once, about a dragon. A true dragon." He swallowed. "You can't keep me in a fort forever. I'll outgrow the fort."

At the north end, Umaru made a small offended sound.

"Sorry," said Mau. "It's a good fort." He took a breath. "So trust me. Not to be safe. I'm not going to be safe. Trust me to come back. I came back down the river path. I'll keep coming back down it. Every time." His voice dropped. "I'm one of the idiots who accepted. You made the sash. You said it yourselves. So let me be that idiot."

The silence went on for a long time.



Seiko broke it.

She was in her armchair by the fire, as she always was, with her hands folded on her paper fan. She had not said anything all morning. She did not say anything now. She held up the fan, closed, and pointed it at him across the room. He understood that this was not a threat. It was the opposite of a threat.

Then Rimuru stood up from the cold hearth.

"Fern," he said. "Write it down."

Fern looked up.

"*Mau is not protected from his own life,*" said Rimuru. "*He is told what we decide about the Goddess, and he accepts it, because he signed. Everything else, he's in. Not managed. In.*" He looked at Mau. "Is that what you mean?"

"Yes."

"And you." Rimuru turned to the table. "Is anyone against it?"

Nobody was against it. Maki looked as if she might be, for a second, and then looked at Yuta, and was not. Fern wrote it down in her neat hand, word for word, and drew a line under it, and turned the ledger around so that he could see.

It was the first rule anyone in that house had made *with* him in a long time, instead of about him.

He read it twice.



"Then you're in," said Fern, turning the ledger back. "Starting now. You can help with the petitions."

Mau blinked. "The petitions?"

"Stay," said Fern. "The Goddess said anyone who stays may make one petition. For one loved person. You heard it." She turned a page. "People are already asking. In Arrival House, in the plaza, in the fort. *Who would you ask for? Could I ask for my mother? My brother? Someone I haven't seen in ten years? Someone the house has never met?*" She looked up. "We talked about that too, last night. Before you got off the roof."

"And?"

"We agreed," said Fern, "that we'll help each other with it. Nobody has to. A petition is yours. But if anyone in the house wants to ask for someone the house doesn't know, someone with no tie to the first ring or the second, then before they close it, we look at it together. At least one person from the third ring has to know who it is and say they're safe to bring." She paused. "Not to stop anyone. To make sure nobody brings a stranger into a house where Sukuna knows about the wheel, and the river towns call us monsters, without somebody having looked them in the eye first."

Mau thought about it.

"That's good," he said. "That's careful. That's—"

"It's your job," said Fern. "Yours and Rimuru's. You're the two who speak for us. So you sit on it. With me, and the ledger." She held out a pen. "That's helping. That's your corner."

Mau looked at the pen.

He took it.
