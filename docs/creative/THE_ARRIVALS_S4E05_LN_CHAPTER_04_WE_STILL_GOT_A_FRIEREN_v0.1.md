# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 20
## Chapter 27 — We Still Got a Frieren

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 20  
**Reading order:** 27  
**Continuity state:** Day 11, morning | he wakes alone; every door open; the rules wiped; 'THE DAY AFTER IS NOT FOR WORK' | the plaza breakfast | Karane: 'All the people from the Arrival House left! But we still got a Frieren!' | he laughs | two coats; the coin in his pocket

On the morning of the eleventh day, Mau woke up alone.

He knew it before he opened his eyes. The bed was too cold on one side. There was no weight of hair across his arm, no breathing beside him, no cold foot tucked against his shin. He lay still for a moment with his eyes closed and listened.

Nothing.

He opened his eyes.

The room with the east window was full of morning. The stone was on the windowsill. The reading chair was in its corner, empty. Frieren's side of the bed had been made, neatly, the blanket pulled up and smoothed. It looked as if nobody had slept in it at all.

He sat up.

"Frieren?"

Nothing.

He got up. He did not stop to put on his coat. He went to the door and opened it, and the family corridor was empty, and it was very quiet.

Too quiet.

Every door down the corridor was standing open. Ori's, next door, open, the room empty, the bed made. Bocchi's, open, her guitar gone from its stand. Milim's, at the end bay, open, the bed made, which Milim never did. The narrow room at the turn, with REM in pencil on the door and *(SISTER)* under it, open and empty, the blanket folded on its shelf.

He went down the corridor faster.

The common room was empty.

The long table, bare. The benches, empty. The cold hearth, with nobody sitting by it. The fort at the north end, with its curtain tied back and nobody inside: the toll booth empty, the stage empty, the annex curtain open, and six cushions with NEXT in front of them, and twenty-two more with nobody on them at all. The Milim Beam. The hamster flag on its broom handle, hanging limp.

Nobody.

The kitchen was empty. The stove was cold. There was no smell of bread.

His heart was going very fast.

*Window's closed*, he told himself. *It closed yesterday. At sunset. You were there. You watched the bridge empty. Everybody else stayed. You saw them. They were holding their coins. They came home. You ate dinner with all of them. Frieren slept next to you. You felt her.*

The common room was empty.

He thought: *was that real?*

He stood in the middle of the empty common room with his heart going and his hands going cold. The old thing came up in him, the thing from the white, the thing that had taught him four hundred and thirty-seven times that waking up did not mean anything. A room that looked right could be a scenario. Everyone you loved could be gone between one breath and the next. You could open your eyes and be at the beginning again, alone.

He looked at the board.

The rules were gone.

All of them. NO RESIDUAL TRIAL / GODDESS CONTACT SITE ALONE. NO CONTRACT AFFECTING ABSENT PEOPLE. NO SECRET COLLECTIVE TERMS. SLEEP. Gone, wiped off, the board clean except for the four rings and the fort's proclamation, and a single new line in the middle, in big round cheerful letters he knew:

*BY ORDER OF THE MINISTRY: THE DAY AFTER IS NOT FOR WORK.*

He stared at it.

And then, from outside, from the direction of the plaza, he heard noise.



He went out the front door without his coat.

The cold hit him. The sky was clear and hard and very blue, the first truly cold morning of winter. Frost on the yard. His breath went white in front of him. He did not notice any of it. He was listening.

Voices. A great many. Laughter. Somebody shouting. Somebody playing a guitar, badly, and then well. The clatter of plates. A pot banged with a spoon.

He went across the yard toward it, past the ink tree in its ring of stakes, with its ten silver-green leaves rimed with frost. He was half running. Around the corner of the Muslin. Out into the plaza.

And stopped.

They were all there.

All of them. The whole house, and more than the house. The long tables from the common room had been carried out into the middle of the plaza and set end to end, with every bench in the Muslin around them and half the chairs from the old inn. They were covered in food: bread, a great deal of bread, Momo's second-batch bread, and soup steaming in the cold, and something of Maomao's in a jar, and Seiko's pickles, and a mountain of dried pears that could only have come from one stall. Somebody had strung paper stars between the lamp posts. Kessoku was on a crate at one end with their instruments, Ryo asleep against the amplifier that was not an amplifier. Marin was going up and down the tables with an armful of winter coats, putting them on people whether they liked it or not.

And all around the tables, eating, arguing, shouting, laughing, was everyone.

Rimuru, with Milim on his shoulders. Yuta and Maki at the end of a bench, her head on his shoulder. Fern, very upright, next to Stark, who was trying to feed her a dried pear and being refused and being allowed. Senku explaining something to Suika with a spoon. Eren and Mikasa and Kaneki, the three of them together at the corner of a table, not saying much. Anko with a parasol, and Nazuna asleep across her lap in its shade, in broad daylight, which Nazuna never did. Coco and Agott and Tetia and Richeh. Bocchi on the crate with her guitar. Umaru in her sash, presiding over nothing at all. Ori. Rem with the teapot. The cooper from the river towns, at a side table, with his hat off. Lise, next to Rem, holding a cup in both hands. Seiko in her armchair, which somebody had carried all the way out from the common room, with her paper fan.

And at the end of the nearest table, with a plate in her hand and a dried pear halfway to her mouth, Frieren. She looked up and saw him standing at the edge of the plaza, without his coat, with his face doing whatever it was doing.

She put down the pear.



It was Hakari who saw him first, after Frieren.

She stood up on the bench, so that she was taller than everyone, and pointed at him with a piece of bread, and shouted to the whole plaza in a voice of total delight:

"HE'S UP!"

The whole plaza turned.

"We let you sleep!" Hakari shouted. "Maomao's orders! You looked so peaceful! Marin said the day after isn't for work! We carried the tables out! We didn't wake you up because you were *finally sleeping*!"

Mau stood at the edge of the plaza and could not say anything.

Karane, beside Hakari, pulled her down off the bench by the back of her coat, and then, red in the face, stood up on the bench herself.

"All the people from the Arrival House left!" she shouted at him, as if it were his fault, which was how Karane said most things. "Every single one! The whole place is *empty*!" She flung out an arm at the long tables, at the whole crowd of them, everyone. "But we still got a Frieren!"

And Shizuka held up her notebook, on which she had already written it in large neat letters: *WE STILL GOT A FRIEREN.*

And Nano, flat and precise, from the end of the bench: "Statistically, we still got everyone."

Mau laughed.

It came out of him all at once, from somewhere very deep, the kind of laugh you cannot stop once it starts. It bent him over, there at the edge of the plaza in the frost, without his coat, with his hands on his knees. He laughed until he could not breathe, and then laughed some more, and somewhere in the middle of it he realized he was also crying. He did not care. He laughed and cried in the cold, and the whole plaza watched him, and some of them started laughing too, because it is very hard not to.

Frieren came across the plaza.

She did not hurry. She walked straight to him across the frosted stones with her winter coat open and her plate still in one hand, and stopped in front of him, and waited until he could stand up straight again.

Then she put the plate down on the nearest table, took off her coat, and put it around his shoulders. It was too small for him. It did not matter.

"You woke up alone," she said.

He nodded. He could not speak.

"I'm sorry," said Frieren. "We should have left someone. Marin said it would be a surprise." She looked at his face, all of it. "It was a bad surprise."

"No," said Mau. His voice was wrecked. "No. It was the best one."

"You thought—"

"I know what I thought," said Mau. "I thought you'd all gone. I woke up and every door was open and the whole house was empty and I thought—" He stopped. He looked past her at the long tables, the paper stars, the whole crowd of them in the cold. "And then I heard you. All of you. Out here. Making noise."

Frieren took his hand.

"We're very loud," she said.

"You're the loudest thing I've ever heard," said Mau.



He ate breakfast in the plaza with all of them, on the first cold morning of winter, in Frieren's coat, which was too small. Then in his own, which Marin found and put on him over hers, so that he was wearing two coats. Nobody thought that was strange.

Kessoku played. The slow circling thing Bocchi had been playing all week, with the ending Kita had found for it. It was not sad.

Milim came down off Rimuru's shoulders and sat next to him on the bench and leaned on him, very carefully, at the speed of a normal person.

Momo put a third bowl of soup in front of him and dared him with her eyes to complain about it. He did not complain.

Seiko pointed her fan at him from her armchair, closed, across the whole length of the table. He understood that this, too, was the opposite of a threat.

And at some point in the morning, without thinking, he put his hand in the pocket of his coat and found the coin there, small and warm.

He did not take it out. He did not need to. He knew exactly what it was.

It was not for nothing. None of it had been for nothing. All around him, at the long tables in the plaza, on the first cold morning after the window closed, everyone who had stood at the door and looked through it and turned around was eating breakfast. They were loud. They had stayed. It was the thing he had bought, and they had chosen it, both at once.

He held the coin in his pocket, and listened to them, and did not let go.
