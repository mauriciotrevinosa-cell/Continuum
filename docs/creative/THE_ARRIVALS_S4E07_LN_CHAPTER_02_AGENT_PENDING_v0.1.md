# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 21
## Chapter 6 — Agent (Pending)

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 21  
**Reading order:** 6  
**Continuity state:** Day 20 | the fort's first codename session of the winter | AGENT TURBO (not DEATH); AGENT TEACUP for Rem, unanimous; AGENT TINK for Momo; Seiko is codenamed SEIKO | AGENT BREAD ROLL ties 14-14 (Ryo: NAP) | Fern's card stays pending; a burnt corner: AGEN

The fort held its first codename session of the winter on the twentieth day, in the afternoon, behind the curtain, with the toll booth closed.

Mau knew this because he was not allowed in. Nobody who was not a citizen was allowed in during a session, and Rimuru, who was an honorary citizen, was allowed in only as far as his bowl. So Mau sat at the long table outside the curtain with Stark and a pot of tea, and they listened.

It was impossible not to listen. The curtain was a curtain.

"The session is open," said Umaru's voice. "The President is presiding. The Opposition is present."

"Under protest," said Agott.

"Noted. First item. Applications for codenames." A rustle of paper. "We have three. Four, if you count the one that keeps being postponed."

A silence behind the curtain, of a very particular quality.

Stark, across the table, looked into his tea.



The first application was Turbo Granny's.

She had made it herself, on a piece of bark, in charcoal, in letters that went uphill. Mau heard it unrolled. He heard Coco read it aloud, carefully, the way she read glyphs.

"*I demand a codename,*" read Coco. "*Something with dignity. Something that strikes fear. I have been a vengeful spirit for longer than any of you brats have been alive, and I sit on the roof of your stupid booth every day, and I deserve*—" Coco stopped. "The rest is just swearing."

"Read the swearing," said Turbo Granny, from the booth roof.

"I'm not reading the swearing."

"Then I'll say it."

"The President rules the swearing as read," said Umaru hastily. "Proposals for Turbo Granny's codename."

"AGENT GRANNY," said Hakari, at once.

The booth roof made a sound like a kettle exploding.

"AGENT TURBO," said Suika.

"AGENT DEATH," said Turbo Granny. "I want AGENT DEATH."

"We can't have AGENT DEATH," said Kita. "There are children in the settlement."

"There are children in this fort!"

"That's why!"

The argument went on for some time. Mau and Stark, outside the curtain, refilled their tea. In the end there was a vote, which Ryo spoiled by writing *AGENT NAP* on her slip and then falling asleep, and the result was eleven for TURBO, nine for GRANNY, one for DEATH, and one for NAP.

"AGENT TURBO," declared Umaru. "Passed. Card to be issued."

"Fine," said Turbo Granny, from the roof, in a tone that meant it was the best thing that had happened to her in a hundred years, and that if anyone mentioned this she would curse them. "Fine. It's acceptable. It's barely acceptable."

"Who voted DEATH?" said Agott.

There was a silence.

"It was a secret ballot," said Mikasa, flatly, and nobody pursued it.



The second application was Rem's.

Rem had not applied. That was the point. The application had been made on her behalf by Ori and Umaru together, the night before, in crayon, after the toll booth. It was very short.

*Rem serves tea at the booth every day. She has a cup on a hook. She is an agent. She has been an agent since the night of the bay. She does not have a codename. This is wrong.*

"Proposals," said Umaru.

"AGENT TEACUP," said Ori.

Nobody proposed anything else.

There was a pause behind the curtain. Mau could not see it, but he could feel it: the whole fort looking at the girl with the teapot, who had sat between Ori and Coco on the night of the window and said *not this time*, and who had, the evening before, said her sister's name out loud for the first time in this house.

"All in favour," said Umaru.

The sound of thirty hands going up at once is very distinctive.

Behind the curtain, someone made a small sound. Then Rem's voice, not quite steady:

"Thank you."

"Card to be issued," said Umaru, and her voice was not quite steady either. "In ink."



The third application was Momo's, and Momo had made it herself, and it was entirely about the ladle.

"*On the fourteenth,*" read Coco, "*I dropped a pot and scared him half to death. Then I dropped a ladle on purpose, quietly, so it went* tink, *and he laughed. I think that should count for something.*"

"AGENT LADLE," said Hakari.

"AGENT TINK," said Karane.

"AGENT POT," said Ryo, who had woken up, and went back to sleep.

There was a short debate about whether a codename could be a sound. Nano ruled that onomatopoeia was permitted under Article Four, which nobody remembered the contents of, and nobody wanted to check, in case she was wrong. AGENT TINK passed by a narrow margin over AGENT LADLE. Momo said that it was stupid, and that she loved it, in the same sentence. From outside the curtain Mau heard Seiko, in her high seat, make the sound she made instead of laughing.

"And yours, Grandmother?" said Momo. "You never applied."

"I don't need a codename," said Seiko. "A codename is for people who need to hide." A pause. "I have never needed to hide in my life."

The fort considered this. Nobody felt qualified to argue.

"Seiko," said Umaru, at last, "is codenamed SEIKO."

"Passed," said Seiko, before anyone could vote, and that was that.



Then the fourth.

The silence came back. The particular one.

"The fourth item," said Umaru, very carefully, the way someone walks across a frozen pond, "is the matter of Fern's codename."

"There is no matter," said Fern's voice. "I don't want one."

"Your card says *pending*."

"My card can say *pending* forever."

"It's been pending since summer."

"Then it's had practice."

Outside the curtain, Stark put his cup down very slowly.

"Proposals were made in the autumn," said Anko's voice, reading from her notebook. "AGENT LEDGER. AGENT STERN. AGENT PUDDING."

"I reject all three."

"New proposals," said Umaru.

There was a pause.

"AGENT BREAD ROLL," said Hakari, in a small voice.

The silence became total.

Mau, outside, remembered: the sixth evening of the plan, a dotted wall, two hooks moved on a Saturday, nobody saying anything at breakfast so loudly that Fern had thrown a bread roll across the common room at Umaru, who had not said anything either, but had been about to. The whole house had seen it. The whole house had been very careful never to mention it again.

"No," said Fern.

"It's accurate," said Agott, who had been waiting all session to vote for something.

"It is not accurate. It was one roll."

"It was a very good throw," said Mikasa, who knew about throws.

"All in favour of AGENT BREAD ROLL," said Umaru, quickly, before anyone could stop her.

Hands went up. Mau could hear them. A lot of hands. Then, from behind the curtain, a second sound: Fern standing up.

"All opposed," said Umaru, faster.

More hands. Fern's, presumably, and Rem's, out of loyalty, and Ori's, out of fairness, and Seiko's, out of a principle nobody understood. Anko counted. Anko counted again.

"Fourteen for," said Anko. "Fourteen against. One spoiled." A pause. "Ryo wrote *AGENT NAP* again."

"Ryo!"

"Zzz," said Ryo, distinctly, which was not the sound of someone asleep.

"A tie," said Umaru, with deep regret, "means the matter remains pending. The card stays as it is." A rustle. "The session is closed."



The curtain opened. The fort came out in a crowd, arguing, carrying cards. Turbo Granny's was nailed to the booth roof within the minute. Rem held hers in both hands and did not seem able to put it down. Momo had already written TINK on the handle of her ladle.

Fern came out last.

She walked straight past the long table, without looking at either of them, toward the kitchen, with her chin up and her face perfectly composed and her ears very slightly red.

Stark watched her go.

"She wanted one," he said quietly.

"Yes," said Mau.

"She'll never say."

"No."

Stark picked up his cup, and looked into it, and put it down again.

That night, coming back late from the roof, Mau passed the kitchen and saw a light under the door. He did not go in. But through the gap he could see Fern at the kitchen table, alone, in her nightgown, with a candle and a scrap of paper and a pencil. She was writing a word, looking at it, crossing it out, and writing another.

He did not see what the words were.

He went to bed. He did not tell anyone. In the morning, in the ashes of the kitchen stove, there was a small curl of burnt paper. Mau thought he could make out, on the one unburnt corner, the letters *AGEN*, and then nothing.

Fern's card still said *pending*.

It was going to say that for a while.
