Every night, Eren put his boots by the door with the toes pointing out.

Mikasa had noticed on the first night. That was the night, in the autumn, when she had written his name on the top bunk of her room in the Muslin without asking him, and he had stood in the doorway looking at it for a long time, and then had not argued. He had climbed up. Before he climbed up, he had taken off his boots and set them side by side by the door, neatly, with the toes toward the corridor, the way you set your boots when you might have to put them on in the dark and run.

She had lain in the bottom bunk and looked at the boots.

When she was sure he was asleep, she got up and turned them round, so that the toes pointed into the room.

In the morning, they were pointing out again.

She had not said anything. Neither had he. That was how it had gone, every night since: he set them toward the door, she turned them toward the room, and every morning they were toward the door again. It was the longest conversation they had ever had.



On the twenty-fifth, it snowed.

It began after dark, the first real snow of the winter, after a fortnight of hard frost. Mikasa woke without knowing what had woken her, and lay still, the way she always lay still when she woke, listening for the thing that had done it. There was nothing. Only a change in the quality of the silence, a thickening, the sound the world makes when snow is falling on it.

And Eren was awake.

She could tell from his breathing, above her. She had shared rooms with him in another life, in barracks and in ruins and in the hold of a ship, and she knew the difference between Eren asleep and Eren pretending to be. He was lying on his back on the top bunk, very still, with his face turned toward the small window, where the snow was going past in the dark.

She did not say anything.

After a while he said, without moving, "It's snowing."

"Yes."

"I didn't know it snowed here."

"It snowed last winter. Before you came."

"I wasn't here last winter." A pause. "I wasn't anywhere last winter."

She did not answer that. She did not know what the answer was. She knew what *she* had been doing last winter, and the winter before, and the one before that, in a life that had gone on for a long time after his. She knew things about winters he had not had yet. She carried them the way she carried the scarf, all the time, without looking at them.

Above her, in the dark, he started counting.

He did it on his fingers. She could hear it: the tiny sound of a thumb touching each fingertip in turn, the way a child counts. He had done it in the other life too, near the end, when he thought nobody could hear. She had never asked him what he was counting.

She had found out, later. Afterward. When it no longer mattered.

She did not ask now either.



"Why bunk beds?" said Eren.

"Because you'd have slept outside the door."

"I'd have slept in the yard."

"Yes," said Mikasa. "That's why."

The snow went past the window.

"You wrote my name on it," said Eren. "Without asking."

"You'd have said no."

"I would have said no."

"That's why I didn't ask."

There was a long pause, and then a sound from the top bunk that she did not recognize at first. It took her a moment. It was Eren, very quietly, laughing. Not much. A breath through the nose. She had not heard it in so long that she had forgotten what it sounded like.

She lay in the bottom bunk with her eyes open in the dark and did not move, in case moving made it stop.

"Kaneki's going to do it someday," said Eren.

"Do what?"

"Spend his." A pause. "He told me who. On the bridge. I'm not going to tell you who. But he's going to." Another pause, longer. "He said *someday*. Like it was nothing. Like you just say *someday* and there's a someday there, waiting."

Mikasa did not say anything.

"Some people get to say *someday*," said Eren.

His fingers had stopped counting. She did not know whether he had reached the end of the number or simply given up.

"You have one," she said. "A coin."

"I know."

"You could spend it."

"On who?" said Eren. He did not sound bitter. He sounded tired, the way he had sounded on the bridge when he said *I'd rather be the one who got in line*. "Everyone I'd bring is—" He stopped. "Armin," he said, after a moment. "Maybe. Someday." He heard himself say it. "Someday," he said again, differently, as if he were trying the word out to see whether it held his weight.

Mikasa's hand had closed on the edge of the scarf.

She knew that he thought he did not have a someday. She knew how many years he thought he had. She had found out, afterward. She had counted them herself, afterward, on her own fingers, alone, in a house on a hill, for a long time. She knew how the counting ended.

But that had been another world.

She did not know how many years he had here. Nobody did. Nobody had asked. Not Maomao, not Rimuru, not Ciel. Nobody had thought to look, because nobody but her knew there was anything to look for.

She lay in the dark and thought about that, and did not say it, because if she said it and she was wrong, it would be the cruellest thing she had ever done.

"Mikasa," said Eren.

"What?"

There was a silence. A long one. She heard him breathe in, the way you breathe in before you say something you cannot take back.

Then she heard him let it out.

"Nothing," said Eren. "Goodnight."

"Goodnight," said Mikasa.

She knew what he had been going to say. Or she knew what the Eren she remembered had never said, and had carried instead, all the way to the end, until it was too late to say anything. She had heard it, once, at the very end, in a place that was not a place. She had heard it after there was nothing left that either of them could do with it.

She did not know if this Eren would ever say it.

She knew why he didn't.



She waited until his breathing changed. It took a long time. The snow kept falling. When she was sure, she got up, very quietly, in the dark, and went to the door, and knelt down by his boots.

The toes were pointing toward the corridor.

She turned them round so that they pointed into the room, toward the bunk, toward her. She lined them up neatly, side by side. Then she got back into bed and lay with her eyes open for a while, listening to the snow and to him breathing above her, and at some point she slept.



In the morning there was snow on everything. The yard was white. The ink tree was a small dark shape in its ring of stakes with ten leaves held up out of the snow like hands. The hamster flag on its broom handle had a cap of snow and looked very surprised.

Mikasa got up before Eren. She always did.

She went to the door to put on her own boots.

His were pointing into the room.

She stood there for a moment and looked at them.

He had not moved them. For the first time since the autumn, he had got up in the night, or had not got up, or had known and had not changed it. She did not know which. She would never ask.

Above her, in the top bunk, Eren was asleep. Really asleep, this time. She could tell the difference. His hand was hanging over the edge of the bunk, open, fingers loose, not counting anything at all.

She put on her boots and went out into the snow.
