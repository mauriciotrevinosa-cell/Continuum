*When: The night of Day 27, after the song in the snow. The first snow had come two days earlier, the roof was now once a week, and in the next room Nijika was saying a name to the ceiling.*

Kita lasted an hour.

She lay in bed in the dark with her hands folded on her stomach and smiled at the ceiling, the way she had been smiling since she came in from the music room. She thought about a page on the floor, upside down. *Every morning, someone looks.* She thought about the yard, and the snow, and the children, and eleven times through, and Bocchi's hands on the strings going red with the cold and not stopping.

Then she thought about Bocchi's hands going red with the cold. And not stopping. And how, when Kita had come in to say goodnight, Bocchi had covered the page with one of them very fast, and that hand had still been red. In a warm room. Two hours after the snow.

Kita sat up.

"Oh, for—" she said, to nobody, and got out of bed.



The music room was dark except for the stove behind the buffer wall, which glowed through the grate and threw a dim red square across the floor. Bocchi was in it.

She was not playing. She was sitting on the floor in the red square with her back against the warm wall and her knees up and her guitar lying across her feet, asleep. Or nearly. Her head had tipped sideways against the wall. Her hair was over her face. The page with the first line on it was on the floor beside her, face down now, with her pencil on top of it like a paperweight.

Her hands were in her sleeves. Both of them. Pulled all the way up inside the cuffs of her pink tracksuit, so that only the very tips of her fingers showed.

Kita stood in the doorway and looked at her for a long moment.

Then she came across the room, very quietly, and sat down on the floor beside her in the red square, against the warm wall, and reached for her hand.

She found it on the first try. She always did. That was a thing Bocchi had told her once, in the old inn, very red, in a voice so small Kita had had to ask her to say it again. *You always know where my hand is. Even in my sleeve. Even when I don't.*

Kita slid her fingers into the cuff of the pink sleeve and found Bocchi's fingers inside it and closed her hand round them.

They were ice.

"*Hitori-chan,*" said Kita.

Bocchi's eyes came open. She looked at Kita, very close, in the red light, without understanding for a moment where she was. Then she understood, and went the colour of the stove grate, and tried to pull her hand back up her sleeve.

Kita did not let go.

"Your hands are frozen," said Kita.

"They're fine."

"They're *ice*. It's been two hours. You've been sitting next to a stove for two hours." Kita held the hand in both of hers now, rubbing it. "Why didn't you come to bed? Why didn't you put them in the warm water? Why didn't you tell anyone?"

"Because," said Bocchi.

She did not finish. She looked at the page on the floor, face down, with the pencil on it.

Kita waited. She had learned to wait, with Bocchi. You waited, and the thing came out eventually, sideways, in pieces, like something crawling out from under a bed.

"Because everyone was looking," said Bocchi, very small. "In the yard. The kids. And Kita-chan. And Ryo-san and Nijika-chan. Everyone was looking at me, and I played anyway, and it was okay." She stared at the page. "And then I came in here and I couldn't stop shaking. Not from the cold. From the—" She stopped. "From the everyone-looking. It came after. Like it waited until I was alone." She pressed her face into her knees. "So I sat here until it stopped. And I didn't want anyone to see. Because it was *okay*. In the yard it was okay. It's stupid to shake after it's okay."

Kita did not say it was not stupid. Bocchi would not have believed her.

She lifted Bocchi's frozen hand instead, still in both of hers, and held it against her own cheek, which was warm from bed.

Bocchi made a sound like a kettle.

"There," said Kita. "Now it's not a secret. I can see it. I'm looking at it." She held the cold hand against her face. "It's shaking. And it was okay in the yard. Both things. At the same time." She looked at Bocchi over their hands. "Mau says that's allowed. Maomao says it's *allowed*. She says it at breakfast. Ugly days are allowed, hiding them isn't."

"I'm not Mau," said Bocchi, into her knees.

"No," Kita agreed. "You're my girlfriend. So it's *more* allowed."



They sat there in the red square for a while. Kita held one cold hand against her cheek and then the other, swapping them over when the first one had warmed up. Bocchi let her. She had stopped trying to pull them back up her sleeves. After a while she stopped being the colour of the grate, and was only pink.

"You were amazing," said Kita.

"It was eleven times."

"I counted." Kita turned the hand over and looked at the fingertips, which had little dents in them from the strings. "Eleven times, in the snow, with your fingers going red, with a whole yard of kids watching. And the eleventh was the best." She pressed the dented fingertips with her thumb. "And then you came in here and wrote the first line of a song about it."

Bocchi went red again.

"You saw."

"Upside down. Only the first line." Kita smiled. "I'm not going to read the rest until you say. I just wanted you to know I saw it. And I lay in bed for an hour smiling at the ceiling like an idiot about one line." She paused. "Mau told me once, before I asked you, that if I felt something about you I should say it properly. Out loud. Not just think it and hope you'd know." She looked at Bocchi. "So: you were amazing. And I'm really proud of you. And I'm not going back to bed without you, because your hands are ice and it's stupid."

Bocchi stared at her.

For a long moment, she did not say anything at all.

Then, very slowly, she unfolded herself from her knees, and leaned sideways, and put her head on Kita's shoulder. Just that. In the red light, against the warm wall, with her guitar across her feet and her hand still in Kita's.

"Okay," said Bocchi, into Kita's shoulder.

"Okay?"

"Okay, I'll come to bed." A pause. "In a minute."

"In a minute," Kita agreed.

It was a lot more than a minute.



Nijika found them in the morning.

She came into the music room at first light to get her drumsticks, which she had left on the stool by the soup pot, and stopped in the doorway.

They were asleep on the floor in front of the stove, which had burned down to grey ash in the night. Both of them, on the rug, under Kita's blanket, which Kita must have gone back for at some point, and Bocchi's coat on top of that. Bocchi had her face in Kita's shoulder. Kita had one arm over her. Their hands were together on top of the blanket, in plain sight, fingers laced, not in any sleeve at all.

Nijika stood in the doorway and looked at them for a long time with her drumsticks forgotten.

Then Ryo came up behind her, yawning, and looked over her shoulder.

"Gross," said Ryo.

Nijika elbowed her in the ribs without looking round. Hard.

Ryo looked at the two of them on the floor a moment longer, under the blankets, with their hands in plain sight. Her face did not change. But she reached past Nijika, very quietly, and took the cushion off the stool by the soup pot, and leaned into the room, and dropped it gently on the rug beside Bocchi's head, where Bocchi's head would hit the floor if she rolled over.

Then she went to breakfast.

Nijika went after her, grinning at the floor, without her drumsticks. She did not go back for them until noon.

At breakfast, Kita came in late, with Bocchi a step behind her. At the door of the common room, in front of the whole long table, Kita reached back without looking and found Bocchi's hand on the first try, and held it, all the way across the room to the bench.

Bocchi went red to the ears. But she did not let go. And she did not put the hand in her sleeve, not even when Umaru, from the fort, saw it and made a sound like a kettle of her own.

Frieren, beside Mau, watched them cross the room.

"They're holding hands," she said.

"They do that," said Mau.

"In front of everyone."

"Yeah."

Frieren considered this for a moment, over her tea. Then, under the long table, where nobody could see, she found his hand on the first try, and held it.
