## Chapter 12 — Five Beds, No Walls

The twenty-eighth was the coldest day of the winter so far, and Nano had a schedule for it.

She posted it on the inside of the door of the big room at six in the morning, in her precise, square, slightly too-small handwriting, on a sheet of paper ruled into fifteen-minute intervals. It began at 06:00 with *WAKE (ALL)* and ended at 22:00 with *SLEEP (ALL)*, and in between it allocated, among other things, three meals, two periods of mandatory hydration, one walk to the washing court in pairs, and a block from 13:00 to 17:00 labelled, mysteriously, *PROJECT K*.

Karane read it with her arms folded.

"What's Project K?"

"Classified," said Nano.

"You can't classify things in our own room."

"I have classified it," said Nano. "Therefore it is classified. Hydration in eleven minutes."

Shizuka, from her bed, held up her book. It said: *I love it when she does this.*

"Don't encourage her," said Karane, and went to get dressed, and was ready for hydration in ten.



The big room had five beds and no walls.

It had been four rooms once, at the start of the Muslin, one for each of them, because that was what you did when people moved into a house: one room each, in a row. It had lasted a week. By the end of the week all four of them had been sleeping in Hakari's room every night, on the floor, on the bed, wherever, because it turned out that none of them could sleep alone after everything, and then Kusuri had arrived and made five, and Wakana had taken the walls out without being asked. Now there were five beds pushed into a rough circle, heads together, like the spokes of a wheel, and one thick wall between them and the rest of the house, *for the good of the house*, as Fern had said, because the five of them together at night made a great deal of noise.

They did not make much noise on the twenty-eighth. It was too cold. Even Hakari, who could make noise in any weather, was quiet that morning, sitting on her bed with three blankets round her and her coin turning over and over in her fingers.

"Hakari," said Kusuri.

"Mm."

"You're doing it again."

Hakari looked down at the coin as if she had not known it was there. She put it in her pocket. A moment later she took it out again.

Nobody said anything. They had all seen it. They all had coins too.



Project K turned out to be a table.

Senku came at one o'clock, exactly on Nano's schedule, which Nano had arranged with him the day before in strictest secrecy, carrying a low square table from the workshop with the legs cut short. Wakana came behind him with a quilt so large it took both arms. Momo came behind Wakana with a covered iron pan, held out in front of her with a thick cloth, full of stones heated in the kitchen oven until they glowed.

"I don't do charcoal under tables," said Senku, before anyone could ask. "Charcoal under a quilt in a closed room is how you get a very peaceful nap that nobody wakes up from. Stones are ten billion percent safer. They hold heat for hours, they don't make anything you can't breathe, and when they cool down you swap them." He set the table in the middle of the five beds, in the hub of the wheel. "Momo's agreed to keep a second pan in the oven. Swap every three hours."

"What is it?" said Hakari.

Nano had the expression she wore on the rare occasions when something had gone exactly according to plan.

"It is a kotatsu," said Nano.

Shizuka dropped her book.

Karane said, "*Oh*," in a voice that was not a Karane voice at all, and then went red, and said, "I mean. Fine. It's fine."

Momo slid the pan of hot stones onto the iron tray under the table. Wakana threw the great quilt over the top, so that it fell to the floor on every side. Senku laid a square board over the quilt, for a tabletop, and stood back.

"Feet under," said Senku. "Not hands. Hands are for the table. Feet under. That's the whole design."

Nobody needed telling twice.



By two o'clock all five of them were under it.

Five people round a table built for four, with their legs tangled together under the quilt in the dark warm cave where the stones glowed, and their bodies sticking out like the spokes of a wheel, and their heads pillowed on their arms on the tabletop. Nano had brought tea, at the scheduled interval. Hakari had brought a bag of dried pears from the stall in the plaza. Kusuri had a notebook out and was making observations about the rate at which the stones lost heat. Shizuka had her book open on the table and was writing in it slowly, now and then turning it round so that the others could read.

*This is the warmest I have been since I came here.*

*This is the warmest I have been since I was small.*

*I don't want to get up ever.*

"Get up at seventeen hundred," said Nano. "For the pan swap."

*I will get up at seventeen hundred,* wrote Shizuka. *Under protest.*

It was very quiet. The snow came down outside the one small window. The stones ticked under the table as they cooled. Somewhere in the house Bocchi was playing the song from the yard, slowly, through the buffer wall, and it came to them very faint, like something from another room in a dream.

"We used to do this," said Karane suddenly.

They looked at her.

"Not this. Not a kotatsu. But—" Karane was looking at the tabletop, not at any of them. "All of us. Together, like this. In one place. Before." She stopped. "I didn't think we'd get to do it again. When I came here, and I didn't know if any of you were coming. I thought—" Her voice went thin. "I thought that was over. All of it. The five of us in one place."

Nobody said anything.

Under the quilt, in the dark, Hakari's foot found Karane's and pressed against it.

"It's not over," said Hakari.

"I *know* it's not over, idiot, I'm *in* it, that's my *point*—"

"I know," said Hakari. "I know that's your point. I'm just saying it anyway."

Karane put her face down on her arms on the tabletop. Her shoulders shook a little. Nobody said anything about that either.

Shizuka turned her book round. It said: *Five beds. No walls.*

Kusuri reached across the table, without looking up from her notebook, and put her hand on top of Karane's head, the way you would put a hand on a cat.

Nano checked the time.

"Fourteen forty," she said. "No scheduled activity until fifteen hundred." She put her own head down on her arms. "I am therefore scheduling nothing."



It did not stay secret for long.

By three o'clock Milim had found it. Milim found anything warm in the house within an hour, the way water finds the lowest point. She appeared in the doorway of the big room, stared at the kotatsu with the expression of a dragon discovering a hoard, and said, "What is *that*?"

"Ours," said five people.

By three-thirty Umaru had found it, in her sash, and made a formal application on behalf of the fort for diplomatic access. It was considered, and denied, and re-applied for, and denied again. By four, Rimuru had wandered past and stopped in the doorway and said, very quietly, "Oh no, you made a kotatsu," in a voice of such deep longing that Hakari took pity on him and let him put his feet in for exactly two minutes, timed by Nano, before he was evicted.

By five, when Momo came with the second pan of hot stones, there was a queue.

It went down the corridor. Milim at the front. Then Umaru, with Suika on her back. Then the cooper, who had heard about it from somebody and wanted to see the design. Then Mau, who had come to see what the queue was for, and had stayed, because the queue was warm and full of people complaining in a friendly way about the cold.

Nano stood in the doorway of the big room with her schedule in her hand and looked at the queue for a long time.

"This was not anticipated," she said.

"You should have anticipated it," said Karane, from under the quilt. "You anticipate everything."

"I anticipated five," said Nano. "The optimal number of persons for a kotatsu of these dimensions is five." She looked down the queue. "There are more than five."

"Then build another one," said Senku, from the back. "I've got the table legs."

Nano considered this.

Then she turned the schedule over, and on the back, in her small precise square handwriting, under the heading *PROJECT K (2)*, she began to make a list.

Mau, at the back of the queue, watched her write. The first name on the list was *THE FORT*. The second was *THE COMMON ROOM*. The third, after a pause, and then very carefully, was *THE ROOM WITH THE EAST WINDOW (FRIEREN HAS COLD FEET)*.

He did not ask how she knew. Everyone in the house knew.
