# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 21
## Chapter 15 — Speed Limit

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 21  
**Reading order:** 15  
**Continuity state:** Day 31 | the slope behind the washing court; trays and a queue | Okarun's sled; Momo: 'Absolutely not' | Fern's speed limit: the speed of the fastest child | the Fastest Child (the girl with the bean) wins; Okarun hits a root, Momo catches him | hands under the snow | Aira: 'Not interested', then goes again

The slope behind the washing court had been a slope all year, and nobody had thought anything of it. Then it snowed for three days, and on the morning of the thirty-first the refuge children discovered that if you sat on a tray from the Arrival House kitchen at the top of it, you went down very fast, screaming, and ended up in the hay bales at the bottom.

By noon there were nine trays on the slope and a queue.

By one o'clock Okarun had found out.

By ten past one, Fern had been called.



"Absolutely not," said Momo.

She was standing at the top of the slope with her hands on her hips, looking at Okarun, who was holding a sled. Not a tray. A real sled, with runners, which Wakana had built him that morning in the workshop in under an hour, because Okarun had asked very nicely and Wakana could not resist a technical problem.

"It's just a sled," said Okarun.

"It's not *just a sled* when *you're* on it."

"I'll go slow."

"You have never gone slow in your life."

"I've gone slow!"

"When?"

Okarun opened his mouth, and thought, and closed it again.

From the roof of the toll booth, which had been carried out to the top of the slope for the occasion, because Turbo Granny had refused to miss this and refused to be carried by anyone and had therefore been carried, booth and all, by Milim, a voice shrieked: "Let him go fast! Let him go *fast!* I've been dead a hundred years and I haven't had a decent run since!"

"You're not *on* the sled," said Momo.

"I'm always on the sled," said Turbo Granny. "I'm in his *legs*."

That was true, more or less. Momo knew it was true. It was the whole problem. Okarun on his own was a skinny boy with glasses who could not run up a flight of stairs without wheezing. Okarun with Turbo Granny in his legs was the fastest thing on two feet that anyone in the settlement had ever seen. On the night of Last Defense he had carried a wounded Searcher out from the north line and gone back for another, faster than anything had a right to be, without anyone counting his trips. Okarun with Turbo Granny in his legs and a sled under him, on a snow slope, was a thing that Momo had seen in her mind about forty times since one o'clock, and every time it ended in the washing court, through a wall.

"Not interested," said Aira.

Everyone looked at her.

She was standing a little apart from the queue, in a white coat with a fur collar, with her arms folded and her chin up, looking at the slope with total disdain.

"Sledding," said Aira, "is for children."

Behind her, the girl who had put a bean up her nose went past on a tray, screaming happily, and disappeared over the edge.

"Not interested," said Aira again, a little louder.



Fern arrived at twenty past one, with the ledger.

She looked at the slope. She looked at the hay bales at the bottom, which were slightly scattered. She looked at the nine trays and the queue of children and the toll booth with Turbo Granny on it and Okarun with his sled. Then she opened the ledger, and wrote something, and closed it.

"Speed limit," said Fern.

"What?" said Okarun.

"There is now a speed limit," said Fern, "on this slope. For persons with supernatural assistance." She looked at Okarun, and then, pointedly, at the booth roof. "The speed limit is the speed of the fastest child."

"That's not fair," said Turbo Granny.

"It's in the ledger," said Fern, and went back inside.

Okarun looked at Momo.

Momo looked at Okarun.

"The speed of the fastest child," said Momo slowly.

"The fastest child," said Okarun, equally slowly, "is the one with the bean."

They both turned to look at the girl with the bean, who had just climbed back up to the top of the slope with her tray, red-faced and triumphant.

"Hey," said Momo, crouching down beside her. "How fast can you go?"



It became, within twenty minutes, an official event.

Umaru declared it. She had come out to see what the shrieking was, and she had seen the speed limit in the ledger, and she had immediately understood that this was a matter of national importance. A race. The fort against the speed limit. Okarun's sled against the girl with the bean, who was now officially the Fastest Child, a title Umaru conferred on her in front of everyone with a spare sash, so that she stood at the top of the slope in a coat three sizes too big and a sash that went round her twice, glowing.

"Rules," said Umaru. "Okarun may not go faster than the Fastest Child. If he passes her, he is disqualified. If she passes him, she wins. If they arrive together—"

"They won't," said Turbo Granny.

"—it is a draw, and everyone gets a pear."

The children cheered. Somebody had produced a sack of dried pears from the stall in the plaza.

Okarun got on his sled. The girl with the bean got on her tray. They lined up at the top of the slope, side by side, with the whole crowd behind them and the hay bales far below.

Momo stood behind Okarun with her hands on his shoulders.

"Slow," she said in his ear. "You hear me? *Slow.*"

"I'll be slow."

"You have Granny in your legs."

"I'll tell her to be slow."

"I'll be *whatever I want*," shrieked the booth roof.

"Go!" said Umaru.



The girl with the bean went off the edge like a stone.

Okarun went off the edge like a much heavier stone that was trying, with every fibre of its being, not to be a stone. He was holding back. Mau, watching from the bottom of the slope by the hay bales with Frieren beside him, could see it: the sled wanted to go, and Turbo Granny wanted to go, and Okarun was dragging his heels in the snow on both sides, sending up two great white wings of spray, gritting his teeth, keeping exactly level with the girl's tray, not one inch in front.

Halfway down, the girl looked over and saw him there, beside her, grimacing, with snow in his glasses.

She laughed so hard she nearly fell off.

And then the slope dipped, and both of them hit the dip at the same time, and the tray and the sled left the ground together and came down together, and Okarun's sled hit a buried root.

It stopped.

Okarun did not.

He went over the front of it in a long, slow, beautiful arc, still in sitting position, with his arms out. Behind him, at the top of the slope, Momo had already raised one hand, the way she did when she reached for something with her power instead of her arm. Something invisible caught him in mid-air, a long way short of the hay, and turned him, gently, the right way up. Then it set him down in a deep drift of soft snow on his back, with a sound like a pillow being dropped.

The girl with the bean slid past him on her tray, still laughing, and went into the hay bales, and came out the other side wearing a hay bale, and raised both arms in the air.

"The Fastest Child," announced Umaru from the top of the slope, "*wins.*"

The crowd went mad.



Okarun lay on his back in the drift and looked up at the white sky.

After a while Momo came down the slope, not on a sled, on her feet, sliding a little, and stood over him with her hands on her hips.

"You were slow," she said.

"I was so slow."

"You were the slowest thing I've ever seen."

"Granny's furious."

"Granny's always furious." Momo looked down at him for a moment. Then she lay down in the snow beside him, on her back, so that they were both looking at the sky. "You caught a root."

"You caught *me*."

"Somebody had to."

They lay there. The snow was very cold, and soaked through their coats almost at once, and neither of them got up. Above them, at the top of the slope, the children were queuing again, and the girl with the bean was being carried round the top of the slope on Milim's shoulders, still wearing the hay bale, and Turbo Granny was shrieking that it had been fixed.

Under the snow, Momo's hand found Okarun's. Neither of them said anything about it.

A little way up the slope, Aira stood with her arms folded and her chin up and watched them, and watched the queue, and watched the girl on Milim's shoulders.

Then she walked to the nearest abandoned tray, picked it up, carried it to the top of the slope, sat down on it in her white coat with the fur collar, and pushed off.

She went down the slope at a speed that was, very precisely, the speed of the fastest child. Not one inch faster. She did it with perfect posture and an expression of total disdain all the way down, and went into the hay bales without a sound, and came out the other side with hay in her fur collar.

She stood up, and brushed herself off, and walked past Momo and Okarun lying in the snow.

"Not interested," she said.

Then she went straight back up to the top for another go.
