# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 21
## Chapter 19 — Diablo Does Nothing

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 21  
**Reading order:** 19  
**Continuity state:** Day 35 | Rimuru orders Diablo a day off; Umaru's card: NO EXCEPTIONS | Ciel counts: four minutes and eleven seconds | the dust, the grimoires, the cups, the cushion; the house spotless by dusk | 'the most difficult day of my existence' | Kufufufu; the card kept all winter

On the thirty-fifth day, Rimuru gave Diablo an order.

"Take the day off," said Rimuru.

Diablo, who was standing behind Rimuru's chair at breakfast as he did every morning, holding a teapot that nobody had asked him to hold, went very still.

"I beg your pardon, Rimuru-sama?"

"The day off. All of it. Today." Rimuru took the teapot out of his hands and put it on the table. "Umaru's Ministry has given every person in this house a free day this winter. Everyone's had one. Fern had one. *Senku* had one, and he spent it asleep in the workshop with his face on a blueprint, but it counts." He looked up at Diablo. "You haven't had one."

"I do not require—"

"I know you don't require it. I'm ordering it." Rimuru picked up his spoon. "Do nothing. All day. That's an order."

There was a long pause.

Diablo's face did several things. None of them were expressions Mau had ever seen it do before.

"Nothing," said Diablo.

"Nothing."

"For the whole day."

"For the whole day."

Diablo bowed. Very low. It was the bow of a man accepting a sentence.

"As you command, Rimuru-sama," he said.

Umaru, at the north end of the table, had already produced a card from her sash. She held it out. It was one of the Ministry's UNIVERSAL cards, with the cushion and the teacup and the sleeping cat on the seal, and across the bottom, in crayon: *DIABLO. FREE DAY. NO EXCEPTIONS.*

Diablo took it as if it might explode.



He did nothing for four minutes.

Mau knew, because Ciel told him. Ciel, it turned out, had decided to count.

*He has sat down,* said Ciel, to two heads, at one minute past eight. *In the chair by the cold hearth. He is doing nothing.*

*He is still doing nothing,* said Ciel, at two minutes past.

*He is looking at the mantelpiece.*

*There is dust on the mantelpiece.*

*He is not doing anything about the dust,* said Ciel, at three minutes past. *His hands are on his knees. His knuckles are white.*

At four minutes and eleven seconds past eight, the dust on the mantelpiece was gone.

Nobody saw it go. Mau was looking straight at the mantelpiece and did not see it go. One moment there was a fine grey film along the top of it, and the next moment there was not, and Diablo was sitting in the chair by the cold hearth with his hands on his knees, exactly as before, looking at the ceiling with an expression of complete innocence.

*Four minutes and eleven seconds,* said Ciel.

"Diablo," said Rimuru, without looking up from his soup.

"Yes, Rimuru-sama."

"Did you just dust the mantelpiece?"

"I did nothing, Rimuru-sama."

"The dust is gone."

"Dust is mysterious," said Diablo.



It went on like that all day.

Diablo did nothing, with tremendous discipline, and things kept happening around him.

At nine, Frieren's grimoires, all six of the old ones from the inn, which she had left in a precarious stack on the end of the long table, slid sideways and began to fall. Diablo was in the chair by the hearth on the other side of the room. The grimoires did not hit the floor. They were simply back on the table a moment later, squared, stacked, in order of age, with their spines aligned. Diablo was looking at the ceiling.

"Did you—" said Frieren.

"Nothing," said Diablo.

At ten, Momo came out of the kitchen with a tray of cups and tripped on the edge of the rug. The cups did not fall. They were on the long table, every one of them, upright and full, before Momo had finished tripping. Momo stood holding an empty tray and stared at Diablo, who was looking at the ceiling.

"I didn't see anything," said Diablo.

"I didn't say anything," said Momo.

"Then we are in agreement."

At eleven, Milim tried to get on top of the fort's Milim Beam, missed, and fell. She landed on a cushion that had not been there a moment before. She looked at the cushion, and then at Diablo, and said, "Did you put that there?"

"I am on my free day," said Diablo, to the ceiling. "I cannot be held responsible for the movements of cushions."



By the middle of the afternoon, the whole house had noticed.

The common room was spotless. Every surface. The windows shone. The lamp chimneys had been cleaned. The fort's curtain had been mended where Milim had torn it in the autumn, with stitches so small and even nobody could find them afterward. Somebody had re-chalked Senku's QUIET tally on the board in a neater hand. The firewood by the stove had been split, stacked, and sorted by length. The drift that had been blocking the back door since the snow had been cleared, and the path to the washing court swept, and the ice on the yard sanded, so that nobody would slip. And Diablo had not moved from the chair by the cold hearth all day.

"I want to know how he's doing it," said Senku, at the workshop door, with real scientific anguish.

"He's not doing anything," said Umaru. "He has a card."

"*Somebody's* doing it."

"Dust is mysterious," said Umaru, with great authority, and went away.



At dusk, Rimuru came and sat on the floor beside Diablo's chair, by the cold hearth, with his back against the wall.

Diablo was still sitting exactly as he had sat since one minute past eight: hands on knees, back straight, eyes on the ceiling. His face was very calm. A muscle in his jaw had been jumping since about noon.

"How's your day off?" said Rimuru.

"Restful, Rimuru-sama."

"Liar."

Diablo was quiet for a moment.

"It has been," he said, carefully, "the most difficult day of my existence."

Rimuru laughed. He tipped his head back against the wall and laughed, and Diablo, very slightly, at the corner of his mouth, did something that might have been a smile.

"I did nothing, Rimuru-sama," he said. "I want you to know that. I did nothing at all."

"For how long?"

*Four minutes and eleven seconds,* said Ciel, to two heads, with immense satisfaction. *I counted.*

"Four minutes," said Rimuru. "Ciel says four minutes and eleven seconds."

"I believe," said Diablo, with great dignity, "that they were the longest four minutes and eleven seconds of my life." He paused. "Kufufufu."

Rimuru looked round the common room. At the shining windows. The clean lamps. The mended curtain. The split wood. The neat stack of six grimoires, squared by age. Through the door, the swept path, and the sanded yard, and the cleared back step. At Momo's cups, still in a perfect row on the long table. At every person in the house, who had been walked past all day by a man who was doing nothing, and had found everything done.

"You know," said Rimuru, "it's actually kind of incredible."

"I did nothing."

"I know. That's what's incredible."

Diablo looked at the ceiling.

"Tomorrow," he said, "may I go back to doing things?"

"Tomorrow you can do whatever you want."

Diablo stood up. He bowed, very low, the bow of a man whose sentence has been served. Then he picked up the teapot from the long table, the one nobody had asked him to hold, and went to stand behind Rimuru's chair.

It was not tomorrow yet. Nobody mentioned it.

Umaru's card was still in his breast pocket. He kept it there for the rest of the winter. When anyone asked him about it, he said that it was a record of the greatest ordeal of his life, and he would not be parted from it, and then he said "Kufufufu," and nobody asked him again.
