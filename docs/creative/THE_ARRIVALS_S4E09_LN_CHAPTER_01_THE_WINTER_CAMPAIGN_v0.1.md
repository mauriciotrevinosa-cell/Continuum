# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 21
## Chapter 20 — The Winter Campaign

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 21  
**Reading order:** 20  
**Continuity state:** Day 36 | the fort declares war on the yard; 31 to 12 | the pack builds its walls in eleven minutes; the Milim Wall, 'Gently' | Senku's catapult, plus or minus a cow | they lose magnificently; Diablo returns every snowball; Mikasa down Eren's collar | Frieren and Fern, two hundred snowballs | Seiko: 'Dinner.' | 'Nobody's frightened'

The declaration of war was posted on the board on the morning of the thirty-sixth day, in crayon, under the four rings and Senku's QUIET tally, with the Ministry's seal at the bottom.

*BY ORDER OF THE PRESIDENT. THE FORT DECLARES A WINTER CAMPAIGN. ALL TERRITORY OUTSIDE THE MUSLIN BETWEEN THE KITCHEN DOOR AND THE INK TREE IS HEREBY CLAIMED. CITIZENS WILL ASSEMBLE AT THE SECOND BELL. NON-CITIZENS MAY RESIST IF THEY WISH. THEY WILL LOSE.*

Below it, in a different hand, very small: *Sister-in-law included.*

Below that, in a third hand, smaller still: *Under protest. —Agott.*



The non-citizens assembled in the workshop at the first bell to discuss resistance.

There were not many of them. That was the problem. The fort had thirty citizens, plus a sister-in-law, plus an honorary member with a bowl who had not yet decided which side he was on. The non-citizens were whoever was left: Mau, Yuta, Senku, Stark, Okarun, Kaneki, Eren, Himmel, Wakana, Qifrey, Jinshi and Diablo. Twelve.

"Thirty-one to twelve," said Senku, looking at a piece of paper on which he had written the numbers. "Thirty-two if Rimuru defects. Which he will."

"He won't," said Mau.

"He's already defected," said Yuta. "I saw him at breakfast. Milim gave him a sash."

"They have Milim," said Okarun, with real fear.

"They have *Mikasa*," said Eren.

"They have *Maki*," said Yuta, and nobody needed him to say anything else.

"They have Frieren," said Mau, quietly. "And Fern."

There was a long silence in the workshop.

"We have Diablo," said Himmel brightly.

Everyone turned to look at Diablo, who was standing in the corner of the workshop with an expression of intense, barely contained joy. It was the morning after his day off. He had been permitted to do things again for exactly one hour, and he had already done, by Senku's estimate, about forty of them.

"I would be honoured," said Diablo, "to resist."

"No killing," said Mau.

"Of course not."

"No *intimidation*."

Diablo's face fell, very slightly.

"No intimidation," he agreed, with the air of a man surrendering his best weapon.

"And Senku has a catapult," said Wakana.

Senku looked up from his paper.

"I don't have a catapult."

"You will by the second bell," said Wakana. "I've got the timber."

Senku looked at him for a long moment. Then he turned his paper over and started drawing.



The fort built its walls at the second bell.

It took eleven minutes. Mau watched it happen from the workshop window, and he would remember it for a long time afterward, because it was the first time he had ever seen the pack at full size doing something together that was not frightened.

Umaru stood on an upturned bucket in the middle of the yard in her sash and her hood, with a rolled-up map of the yard under her arm, and gave orders. Coco and Tetia laid out the lines of the walls in the snow with sticks, very precisely, the way they laid out glyphs. Mai, who had built the fort's north wall and could build anything straighter than anyone, packed snow into the lines with the side of her hand, and Maki beside her packed it twice as fast. Kita and Nijika rolled great balls of snow across the yard for the corners, with Bocchi pushing behind and saying *sorry* to the snowballs. The five from the big room built a second wall behind the first, because Nano had calculated that a defence in depth would increase their survival time by forty percent. Momo stood in the middle of it all with one hand raised and lifted the heavy blocks into place without touching them. Ori carried water to pour over the finished walls, so they would freeze hard. Rem set up the toll booth at the gap in the wall, as a customs post, with her cup on its hook and a sign reading *TOLL: ONE SNOWBALL*. Turbo Granny sat on the booth's roof and screamed encouragement.

And then Milim stepped up to the last open stretch of wall, the long one facing the workshop, and looked at it, and looked at Umaru.

"Gently," said Umaru.

"I know."

"*Gently*, Milim."

"I *know.*"

Milim put out one hand, flat, palm down, over the snow.

The snow rose. All of it, the whole stretch, in one smooth movement, as if a giant had lifted a blanket from underneath. It rose up into a wall as high as Milim's head, smooth and white and perfectly straight, and stopped, and stayed. Behind it, every child in the refuge families who had come to watch made a sound like a kettle.

Milim looked at the wall. She was breathing a little hard. Not from effort. From holding back.

"Gently," she said, very proudly.

Umaru, on her bucket, made a mark on her map.

"The Milim Wall," she said. "Passed."



In the workshop, Senku's catapult was finished.

It was small. It was built from Wakana's timber and two lengths of rope and a bucket, and it could throw a snowball, Senku said, roughly forty yards with an accuracy of about plus or minus a cow. Yuta had warmed his hands with cursed energy and was packing snowballs at a rate of one a second, very hard, very round. Okarun had Turbo Granny's speed in his legs and was going to run ammunition. Kaneki, Eren and Himmel were going to hold the gap at the toll booth. Jinshi had been made quartermaster, because Jinshi could organize anything. Qifrey had declined to fight, on the grounds that his students were on the other side and it would be unbecoming, and had instead been made a neutral observer, which meant he stood at the workshop door with a cup of tea, smiling.

Mau stood at the window and looked out at the Milim Wall, and the defence in depth, and the toll booth, and Maki and Mikasa side by side behind the first wall, making snowballs with expressions of total professional calm.

"We're going to lose," he said.

"Ten billion percent," said Senku, happily.

"Then why are we doing it?"

Senku looked up from the catapult.

"Because they declared war," he said. "And it's snowing. And nobody's frightened." He picked up a snowball and weighed it in his hand. "When was the last time this whole house did something stupid together just because it was snowing?"

Mau thought about it.

He could not remember.

"Fire," said Senku, and pulled the lever.



They lost.

They lost magnificently, completely, and in about twenty minutes. Senku's catapult hit the Milim Wall dead centre four times and made no impression on it at all, and then hit Umaru's bucket, which was a triumph, and then Momo picked the catapult up with her mind and turned it round so that it faced the workshop. Okarun ran ammunition so fast that he ran straight into the defence in depth and was captured by Hakari, who sat on him. Himmel stood on the toll booth's roof with a snowball raised and his cloak flowing, posing heroically, and was struck by eleven snowballs simultaneously, and fell off, and lay in the snow saying *worth it*. Eren held the gap at the toll booth for nearly three minutes with a grim, flat competence that surprised everyone, until Mikasa came over the wall behind him and put a snowball down the back of his collar, and he made a noise nobody had ever heard him make, and Mikasa very nearly smiled.

Diablo did not intimidate anyone. He simply moved through the yard like a dark wind and returned every snowball that was thrown at him, perfectly, to the exact person who had thrown it, within a second, without ever being hit himself. It was deeply unnerving. Agott, struck in the face by her own snowball for the third time, declared him a war crime.

And Mau stood in the middle of the yard with his arms full of snowballs that Yuta had made him, and looked up, and saw Frieren on top of the Milim Wall.

She was standing on it, in her winter coat, with her staff. Fern was beside her. They were both looking down at him with the same expression.

"Frieren," said Mau. "No."

Frieren raised her staff.

A hundred snowballs lifted off the ground all round her, perfectly round, perfectly white, and hung in the air.

"Frieren. *No magic.* It's a *snowball fight*—"

"There's no rule," said Frieren, "against magic."

"There's no rule *for*—"

"Fern," said Frieren.

"Yes, Frieren-sama," said Fern, and raised her own staff, and another hundred snowballs lifted off the ground beside the first.

Mau looked at the two hundred snowballs hanging in the air over the yard.

He looked at Yuta, beside him. Yuta looked back. Yuta had the expression of a man who has seen Rika manifest, and the end of the world, and this was worse.

"It's been an honour," said Yuta.

"It's been an honour," said Mau.

The snowballs came down.



At the third bell, Seiko had her armchair carried to the kitchen door, and opened her fan, and closed it with a snap that carried across the entire yard.

"Dinner," said Seiko.

The war ended instantly.

The fort climbed out from behind its walls, red-faced and soaked and triumphant. The non-citizens climbed out from under the snow. Himmel was still lying where he had fallen off the toll booth, saying *worth it* to the sky. Okarun was still under Hakari. Senku's catapult was upside down in a drift, facing the wrong way. Diablo was the only person in the yard who was completely dry, and he was smiling with such open happiness that several children backed away from him.

Mau lay on his back in the snow by the ink tree, where the two hundred snowballs had put him, and looked up at the white sky, and could not stop laughing.

Frieren came down off the Milim Wall and stood over him, with her staff, and looked down at him.

"You lost," she said.

"I lost."

"Badly."

"I lost so badly."

She looked at him a moment longer. Then she held out her hand, and he took it, and she pulled him up out of the snow. When he was standing she did not let go of his hand. She stood beside him in the trampled white yard, among the walls and the wreckage and the whole house going in to dinner, soaked and loud and laughing, and looked at all of it.

"Senku was right," she said.

"About what?"

"Nobody's frightened," said Frieren.

She went in to dinner, still holding his hand. Behind them, the Milim Wall stood in the yard, perfectly straight, as high as a dragon's head. It stayed there until the thaw, with the toll booth in its gap and Rem's sign still hanging: *TOLL: ONE SNOWBALL*. Every child in the settlement paid it, every day, until spring.
