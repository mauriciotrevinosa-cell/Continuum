The Searchers had a board of their own in the hall of Arrival House, and on the sixtieth day Rimuru took it down.

It had been there since before the Move. A long board of planks, painted white, ruled into columns. *ROUTE. TEAM. OUT. BACK. FOUND.* For a year and a half it had been the heart of the settlement's searching: every team that went out along the roads and the rivers and the edges of the hills, looking for the people who had arrived in this world alone and did not know where they were. Every name that came back. Ori's, once, in a column of its own. Half the house had come in through that board, one way or another. Under *FOUND*, the chalk had been rubbed out and rewritten so many times that the white paint had worn through to the wood.

The last entry under *FOUND* was two months old.

Rimuru took the board off its hooks and carried it to the long table in the common room and laid it down flat, and the Searchers gathered round it, and the house gathered round them.

"We're not looking for people anymore," said Rimuru.

Nobody said anything.

They had all known it. Fern had read *arrivals, zero* every night for fifty-nine nights. The long routes had been cut back since the voice. The short routes found nothing but snow. The Searchers had been going out every morning along roads where nobody was, and coming back every evening with nothing to write under *FOUND*, and nobody had said it out loud.

"Nobody's coming," said Rimuru. "Not over the bridge. Not along the roads. The window's closed, the world's scared, and something's walking the low woods by the ford that throws carts around." He looked at the Searchers, one after another. Okarun. Aira. The two from the refuge families who had joined after Last Defense. The three from the second community. Mikasa, who walked the long routes. "And when someone does come, they're going to come because one of us has a coin and says their name. They'll come to Arrival House. We won't have to go and find them."

Okarun looked at the board.

"So what do we do?" he said. "Just—stop?"

"No," said Rimuru.

He turned the board over.

The back of it was bare wood. He had painted it white the night before, in the workshop, with Senku, and ruled new columns across it in black. He had written new headings at the top, in his own handwriting, which was not as neat as Fern's but was much more cheerful.

*MATERIAL. WHERE. HOW MUCH. WHO. BACK.*

The Searchers stared at it.

"We're going to build a house," said Rimuru. "A real one. You've all seen the plan. And when Ciel's finished, we're going to have the ground for it." He tapped the board. "But a house isn't ground. A house is stone and timber and clay and lime and iron and glass. And nobody's going to bring us any of it, because nobody's coming." He looked up. "So we go and get it."



Senku had the list.

Of course he did. He had been writing the list in his head since the night of the plan, and on paper since story night, and now he laid it out on the long table beside the board, three pages of it, in small square capitals, and the Searchers leaned over it.

"Stone," said Senku. "Upriver, past the falls, there's a cliff of good grey stone. I saw it in the ten days, when we went looking for him." He did not look at Mau. "Hard. Takes a clean edge. Close enough to cart, if somebody fixes the track."

"Timber," said Senku. "The old forest. East. Where the witches live." He paused. "We'll need permission."

"Clay," said Senku. "The riverbank below the bridge. Good clay. Wakana's tested it. It fires red." He turned a page. "Lime. There's a chalk outcrop in the north hills. We burn it, we get lime, we get mortar." He looked up. "The north hills are where the lights are."

Nobody said anything.

"Iron," said Senku. "Bog iron in the marsh west of the old inn. Low grade. Enough for nails and hinges and tools." He turned the last page. "Sand for glass. The bend in the river. Charcoal from the deadfall in the forest, if the witches say yes." He put the pages down. "That's the house. That's every material in it that we can't make out of nothing."

He looked round at the Searchers.

"You know every road and river and ridge within two days of here," said Senku. "Better than anyone. You've walked all of them, looking for people. Now walk them looking for this."



There was a silence round the long table.

Then Okarun picked up a piece of chalk.

He looked at it for a moment. Then he leaned over the board, the new side, and under *MATERIAL*, in the first row, in big untidy letters, he wrote *STONE*. Under *WHERE*, he wrote *UPRIVER — CLIFF*. Under *WHO*, after a pause, he wrote his own name.

"Turbo can carry a lot," he said, not looking up. "If the track's fixed."

"I'll fix the track," said Wakana.

Aira took the chalk from him. Under *MATERIAL* she wrote *CLAY*, and under *WHO*, *AIRA*. Then, after a moment, she added *+ MOMO* in smaller letters, and did not look at anyone.

Mikasa took the chalk next. She looked at the board for a long time. Then she wrote *LIME*, and *NORTH HILLS*, and under *WHO*, *MIKASA*.

"Not alone," said Eren, from the window.

Mikasa looked at him.

Eren came over to the table, and took the chalk out of her hand, and wrote his own name under hers. *EREN.* He gave the chalk back without saying anything and went back to the window.

Mikasa looked at the two names together on the board for a long moment. Then she put the chalk down.

It went round the table. It always went round. One of the refuge Searchers wrote *IRON*. One from the second community wrote *SAND*. Kaneki, who was not a Searcher, came over from the corner and wrote *CHARCOAL* and his own name, and said that he did not sleep at night anyway, and the forest was quiet at night, and nobody argued.

Then there was one row left. *TIMBER. EAST. THE OLD FOREST.* Under *WHO*, nobody had written anything.

Everyone looked at Mau.

"The witches," said Rimuru. "It's their forest. Somebody has to ask."

"The tea," said Mau. "The next one. I'll ask."

He took the chalk and wrote his own name.



That night, at the long table, Fern read the ledger.

She read the day's entries. Arrival House opened at the eighth bell. Intake, none. Arrivals, zero. And then she paused, and turned the page, and read a new heading she had written that afternoon, in her small square hand, after the Searchers' board had been turned over.

"*Materials,*" said Fern.

She read the board. All of it. Stone, Okarun. Clay, Aira and Momo. Lime, Mikasa and Eren. Iron. Sand. Charcoal, Kaneki. Timber, Mau, pending permission.

When she had finished she looked up.

"Searchers," she said. "Out. Every day. Back by dark."

It was exactly what she had said every night for a year and a half, when the Searchers had gone out looking for people. The same words. The same voice.

Okarun put his face in his hands. Aira looked at the ceiling. One of the refuge Searchers, a woman who had come in through Arrival House herself the week after Last Defense, found by a team that had gone out looking, wiped her eyes with the back of her wrist and said nothing at all.

In the hall of Arrival House, the board hung on its hooks again, new side out. On the back of it, against the wall where nobody could see, the old columns were still there. *ROUTE. TEAM. OUT. BACK. FOUND.* With the white paint under *FOUND* worn through to the wood.

Nobody had painted over it. Nobody ever would.
