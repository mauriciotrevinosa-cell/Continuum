# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 23
## Chapter 5 — First Run

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 23  
**Reading order:** 5

*When: Day 109, two days after the rules. The watch at the stake has not missed a night.*

Mikasa led the first planned run, because someone had to, and because she was the one who would come back.

Mau had already crossed the threshold once. He had asked to stay outside this time. What the five were doing now was the reconnaissance he had proposed, with a route back and people who knew they were walking.

They stood at the red stake at the second bell of the hundred-and-ninth morning, the five of them, with the whole house behind them on the path and the blue light in front of them, humming. The window hung in the air beside the gate, where it always hung, with its boxes and its numbers. *GATE: WEEK 1. GRADE: NORMAL. CHANGES IN: 3 DAYS. SETTLEMENT BALANCE: 0 CRYSTALS.*

Rimuru knelt at the stake and put his hand flat on Anko's marker. A thread of darkness rose out of his shadow and sank into the shadow of the stake, and was gone.

"I can see the gate," he said. "Go when you're ready."

Mikasa looked back once. Not at the house. At Eren, beside her. Then she drew her blades, and walked past the stake, across forty paces of snow, into the blue.



It was like walking into cold water, and then it was not like anything.

Then it was stone.

She was standing in a corridor. That was her first thought, and she held onto it the way she held onto every first thought in a place she did not know: *corridor*. Stone walls on either side, rough, close, rising into a darkness she could not see the top of. Stone underfoot, worn smooth, as if a great many feet had walked here, though nothing said whose. The air was cold and still and smelled of wet rock and something else, faint and sharp, like the air before lightning.

And it was lit. Not by any torch. Set into the walls, at uneven intervals, there were crystals. Pale blue, some as small as a fingernail and some as large as a fist, growing out of the stone like frost, each giving off a faint steady glow. The corridor ran ahead of her into the blue dimness, straight, for about forty paces, and then turned.

Behind her, where she had come in, the gate stood in the corridor wall like a window onto snow. She could see the north field through it, white and bright, and the red stake, and Rimuru kneeling at it, very small and far away and slightly blurred, as if through old glass.

Eren came through behind her. Then Yuta, with his sword already out. Then Maki, with her spear. Then Milim, last, who stepped through the gate, stopped, looked round at the corridor with an expression of total, transcendent happiness, and then, visibly, remembered she was the Guardian, and put her hands behind her back.

"Okay," said Milim, very quietly. "Okay. At the back."

Mikasa looked down at her own shadow on the stone. It was faint, in the crystal light. But she thought she saw something move in it. A thread of darkness, thinner than a hair, curling once, like a finger raised in greeting.

*I see you,* said Rimuru's voice. It was not loud. It did not come from anywhere. It was simply there, small and clear, in the back of her head, the way Ciel's voice was there for Mau. *All five. The corridor. Ciel says the crystals in the walls are the same as the ones in the window. Go slow.*

Mikasa went slow.



The corridor turned, and turned again, and opened into a room.

It was round, about the size of the new common room, with a low domed ceiling crusted with crystals, so that the whole room was lit a pale cold blue. There were three openings in the far wall, dark, leading away. There were bones on the floor near the walls, small ones, old and dry, that nobody looked at too closely.

And in the far corner, against the wall, there was a chest.

It was wooden, bound in dark metal, about the size of a man's travelling trunk. Its lid was closed. It sat in the corner of the round room in the blue light with an air of extreme, deliberate innocence.

Nobody moved toward it.

"Rule six," said Maki.

"Rule three," said Yuta. "First room and back. That's what we agreed."

"It might have something in it."

"It might *eat* us."

Eren looked at the chest for a long time.

"We leave it," he said.

"Frieren's going to kill us," said Yuta.

"Frieren," said Maki, "is going to kill us *if* we leave it, and *also* if we open it, and *especially* if we open it and it's a grimoire and we didn't wait for her." She looked at the chest. "We leave it. For her."

*Agreed,* said Rimuru's voice. A pause. *Ciel says... Ciel says she can't tell from here whether it's a chest. Which she says is interesting, and also that you should not touch it.*

They left it.

That was when the wolves came.



There were four of them. They came out of the middle opening in the far wall all at once, fast and silent, the way wolves came in Mikasa's dreams. But they were not wolves. They were the shape of wolves, the size of large dogs, grey, with hides that caught the crystal light strangely, dully, like wet slate. Their eyes were the same pale blue as the crystals. And their teeth, when they opened their mouths, were not white. They were the colour of iron.

Mikasa was moving before she thought.

She had done this a thousand times. More. She went in low and fast at the first one with both blades, the way she had gone in at a hundred titans, at the nape, at the one place, with all her weight and all her speed behind the edge.

The blade hit the wolf's neck.

It skidded.

It skidded off the grey hide with a shriek of metal and a shower of sparks, as if she had struck stone, and the shock of it went up her arm to the shoulder. The wolf was not even cut. It turned its head and looked at her with its pale blue eyes, and opened its iron jaws.

She was already moving again, rolling away across the stone, and the jaws closed on the air where her arm had been. She came up on one knee with both blades up. Her left blade had a nick in it. A *nick*. In the edge. She had never in her life put a nick in an edge on anything living.

"*Steel doesn't work!*" she shouted.

She heard Eren behind her, hitting the second wolf, the same skid, the same shriek, the same sparks. She heard him swear.

Then Maki was past her.

Maki did not shout. She went in at the first wolf with her spear, low, at the throat, the cursed tool she had brought over the bridge, the one she never let anyone else touch. The point went in.

It went in like a knife into bread.

The wolf made no sound. It simply stopped, with the spear in its throat, and stood very still for an instant. Then it came apart. Not in blood. In light. It broke up from the inside, all at once, into a cloud of pale blue sparks that hung in the air for a moment and then fell, softly, like snow, and went out. Where it had stood, on the stone, lay a single small crystal, blue, the size of a fingertip, glowing faintly.

Maki looked at her spear. Then at the crystal. Then at the other three wolves.

"*Cursed tools work,*" she said.

Yuta was already moving. His sword was ordinary steel, but it did not look ordinary now. The blade was wrapped in a faint, dark shimmer, his cursed energy running along it from hilt to tip, and when he cut at the second wolf, the one Eren's blade had skidded off, the shimmering edge went through its shoulder as if there were nothing there.

The wolf came apart in blue sparks. A crystal fell.

"*Cursed energy works,*" said Yuta, breathing hard.

The third wolf went for Eren. He had no cursed energy and no cursed tool. He had two ordinary blades, one of them already bent. He did not run. He planted his feet and put both blades up crossed in front of him, the way he had once stood against things fifty times his size, and the wolf hit them, and the blades held, and its iron teeth closed on the steel a hand's breadth from his face and began to bend it.

Maki's spear took it from the side. Blue sparks. A crystal.

The fourth went for Mikasa, from behind.

She did not see it. She was watching Eren. She was watching the iron teeth on his crossed blades, a hand's breadth from his face, and she did not see the fourth wolf come out of the left-hand opening behind her, low and silent, until it was in the air.

She heard it. She turned. Too late. Its jaws were open and its pale eyes were on her throat.

And then it was not there.

It was on the far wall. It had hit the far wall of the round room, very hard, at a speed nobody had seen, and it was coming apart against the crystals there in a burst of blue sparks, and a small crystal was falling, glittering, down the wall to the floor.

Milim stood where the wolf had been, with one hand out, the flat of it, as if she had just brushed a fly off a table.

She looked at her hand. Then at the far wall. Then at Mikasa.

"*Gently,*" said Milim, in a small worried voice. "That was gently. Wasn't it? It was attacking someone. You said if it was attacking someone." She looked at the far wall again, where a crack now ran up through the crystals to the ceiling. "...That was *mostly* gently."

Mikasa looked at her for a long moment, on one knee, with her nicked blades.

"Thank you," said Mikasa.

Milim's whole face lit up. Then, visibly, she remembered she was the Guardian, and put her hands behind her back again, and went back to stand by the entrance, very straight.

*COME OUT,* said Rimuru's voice, in the back of all their heads at once. *Now. That's enough. Rule two.*



They came out.

They came out at once, without argument, the way the contract said. Mikasa went last, walking backward down the corridor with her nicked blades up, watching the round room until the turn of the corridor hid it. The chest sat in its corner the whole time, closed, innocent, and nobody touched it.

Then the cold water. Then snow, and white light, and the north field, and the red stake, and the whole house standing on the path.

They walked out of the blue light one after another, across forty paces of snow, to the stake. Rimuru was kneeling there with his hand still flat on the marker and his face grey with concentration, and when the last of them stepped past it, Mikasa, he let out a long breath and sat back in the snow.

Nobody in the house said anything. They looked at the five of them. At Eren's bent blade. At Mikasa's nicked ones. At Yuta's sword, with the dark shimmer still fading from the edge. At Milim, standing very straight at the back with her hands behind her.

Then Maki held out her hand, and opened it.

Four small crystals lay on her palm, blue, glowing faintly. Yuta held out one more. The one from the far wall, which Milim had gone back for, without being asked, very carefully.

"Five," said Maki. "And these." She held up her spear. "Steel doesn't work. On anything in there. Mikasa's blades, Eren's blades, nothing. Not a scratch." She looked at Mikasa's nicked edge. "It *damages* steel." She held up the spear. "Cursed tools work." She nodded at Yuta's sword. "Cursed energy works. It goes through them like they're not there." She looked at Milim. "And the Guardian works."

"*Mostly gently,*" said Milim, very quietly, from the back.

Anko had already opened *GATE. LOOT REGISTER. VOL. 1.* She wrote, in her small precise capitals:

*Day 109. Party: Maki, Yuta, Mikasa, Eren, Milim (Guardian). Depth: first room. Crystals: 5 (small). Injuries: none. Steel: useless.*

She underlined *useless*.

"And the chest," said Yuta.

Everyone looked at him.

"There was a chest," said Yuta. "In the first room. In the corner." He looked, very carefully, at nobody in particular. "We left it. Rule three. And rule six." A pause. "For later."

There was a silence on the path.

Mau did not turn round. He did not need to. He could feel Frieren, beside him, go completely, utterly still.

"You *left*," said Frieren, "a *chest*."

"Rule three," said Yuta weakly.

"In the *first room*."

"It might have been a mimic!"

"It might," said Frieren, "have been a *grimoire*."

"Frieren-sama," said Fern.

Frieren closed her eyes. She stood very still in the snow for a long moment, breathing slowly through her nose, with her hands folded inside the sleeves of her shawl, the way a person stands who is very old and very patient and has been tested beyond endurance.

"The window says the gate changes in three days," she said at last, without opening her eyes. "What happens to the chest when it changes?"

Nobody answered.

Umaru, at the back, by her sign, said in a very small voice: "Usually... it resets. Everything inside. New rooms. New monsters." A pause. "New chests."

"And the old chest?"

"...Gone," said Umaru.

Frieren opened her eyes. She looked at the blue light, forty paces past the stake. Then she looked at Mau.

"Three days," she said.

"Not before we've checked the route," said Mau. "That was the plan—"

"This isn't the first time," said Frieren. "This is the *second* time." She turned and began walking back down the path toward the settlement, very upright, in her shawl. "I'm going to talk to Fern about the second time."

Fern, who was standing right there, closed the ledger and followed her, with the expression of someone who has been talked to about the second time before, by this person, in a great many places, for a very long time.



That evening, Fern took the five crystals to the window.

She held them out on her palm, in front of it, not knowing what to do. And the window knew. The five small crystals lifted off her palm, all at once, and drifted forward into the blue light of the window, and went in, and were gone.

The number at the bottom changed.

*SETTLEMENT BALANCE: 50 CRYSTALS.*

Fern looked at it for a long time. Then she opened the ledger, and wrote: *Day 109. In: 5 small crystals. Balance: 50. Out: nothing.*

She looked at the list under *WEAPONS*, which she could see from where she stood. *Hunter's Dagger, 120. Hand Axe (Gate-forged), 340.*

She did a sum in her head.

"Six more trips," she said, to nobody, "for one axe."

Behind her, Stark, who had come out with her and was looking at the window over her shoulder, said nothing at all. But he was looking at the *Hand Axe (Gate-forged)* with the expression of a man reading a name he already knew he would carry.
