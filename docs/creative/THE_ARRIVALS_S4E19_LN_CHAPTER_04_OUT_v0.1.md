*When: Day 118, first light, before the merchant's cart came up the bridge road. The gate is on the last day of its second week, and nobody has been in since the queen. Frieren has not put the mimic grimoire down since the reign ended. The west wing's second bent stands finished; the third waits for Day 120.*

Ciel kept her word.

On the morning of the hundred-and-fourteenth day, in his office, with the door shut and Diablo outside it, Rimuru heard all of it. The Noise was pressing again. Softly. Not at the gate, not at the walls, not at anything Ciel could name. Leaning, the way a person leans toward a door when they think they have heard a step behind it.

Rimuru went to find Mau, and found him in the yard, splitting kindling for Momo, and did not have to say very much.

"I know," said Mau. He set the next piece on the block. "Since the last night of the reign. It's leaning."

"At what?"

"I don't know." The hatchet came down. "Not at the gate. I keep checking. It's not pulling, it's not walking me anywhere." He picked up the two halves and put them on the pile. "It's like it's waiting for something. And it doesn't know what either."

They agreed to watch it. Four days. No gate, no tests, nothing clever. Ciel watched it every hour. It did not grow. It did not stop.

On the evening of the hundred-and-seventeenth day, at the long table, Mau said, "I want to go in tomorrow. First room and back."

Frieren looked up from the grimoire.

"If it's the gate," said Mau, "I want to know in daylight. Awake. With someone. On purpose." He looked down the table. "Not find out in my sleep."

"Me," said Yuta.

Everyone looked at him. He was sitting at the cold-hearth end, beside Maki, with his bowl in both hands.

"Just me," said Yuta. "Small. First room and back. Nothing clever." He put the bowl down. "If something happens, one person pulling is faster than five deciding."

Maki looked at him sideways for a long moment. She did not say anything. Then she went back to her food.

Frieren looked at Mau. Then at Yuta. Then she looked back down at the grimoire.

"First room," she said.



They went at first light.

Rimuru put the threads in their shadows at the red stake, one each, cool and quick. Fern opened the contract against her ledger. Yuta's name was already on it, second of the five from the first run, under Maki's. Mau did not reach for the pen.

"All five," he said.

Fern wrote the day beside the old line in the ledger, *Signature did not hold. Agrees to the rules*, and closed the book.

Frieren was sitting on her folded shawl in the snow by the stake, with the grimoire open on her knees. She did not look up when they walked to the gate. Mau noticed that she did not turn a page, either.



The ruin was the same as it had been on the day of the mimics. Dressed stone. Old pillars carved with patterns nobody could read. The crystals in the walls, lighting everything a soft cold blue. The chest rooms empty now, the week nearly over, nothing left in them but dust.

The Noise was the same too.

Mau stood just inside the gate and listened to it, the way Ciel had taught him. It leaned. It had been leaning for four days. And it did not lean any harder in here than it had in the yard, splitting kindling.

*Not the gate,* said Ciel, very quietly. *Or not only the gate.*

"Then we go to the first room," said Yuta, "and we come back, and we know that much." He drew his sword. "Stay behind me."

Mau stayed behind him. Hands in his pockets. Eyes on Yuta's back. That was the practice.

The first skeleton came out of a side passage the way they always came: four-limbed, too many joints, the long skull and the blue eyes, scuttling, jaw open. Yuta did not hurry. He stepped into it and cut once, and the cursed energy along the blade went through it like light through water, and it came apart in blue sparks that fell softly on the stone. A crystal clinked.

"One," said Yuta.

The second one came from behind.

Mau did not hear it. There was a passage there that had been empty on the day of the mimics, and he had not looked at it, because he had been looking at Yuta's back, because that was the practice. Something hit him across the shoulders from behind, hard, a long raking blow, and he went forward a step and felt the cloth of his coat tear and the skin under it open and the hot clean sting of it.

And something under him turned.

*Click.*

He felt it in his shadow, on the stone under his boots, before he felt anything else. Once. A sound like a lock moving, very small, very far down. And for an instant, on the floor, in the blue light, there was a shape in his shadow that was not his. Pale. Round. Spoked. There and gone, like a fish turning under ice.

Rimuru's thread, curled in the same shadow, was shoved aside like a leaf.

The skeleton struck again. The same blow, the same claws, across the same shoulders.

It slid off him. Like rain off glass. He felt it land, and he felt it not hurt.

Yuta was already moving.

He had been moving since the click. Mau understood that afterward. Yuta had heard it, and he had not turned to look, and he had not cut the second skeleton, and he had not called for Rimuru, or Ciel, or anyone. He had one hand in the back of Mau's collar and the other round his arm before the second blow had finished sliding off him, and he said one word, the way the rule said it should be said.

"*Out.*"

They went.

Down the corridor of dressed stone at a run, Yuta half dragging him, the skeleton scuttling after them and then not, the crystals going past in a blue smear, the gate ahead of them like a held breath, and through it. Before Mau had found his feet. Before Ciel had finished saying his name. Before anything in him, The Noise or the wheel or whatever else was down there, had the time to decide what it thought.

Snow. Grey light. The red stake.

Yuta did not let go of his collar.



Frieren was on her feet. The grimoire was in her hands. She had not dropped it; she was holding it closed against her chest with both arms, very hard, the way she had held it in the round room the day she found it.

"I felt it," said Rimuru. He was at the stake, white. "On the thread. Something pushed it."

*The wheel turned,* said Ciel. Aloud, through Rimuru, so all of them could hear. *Once. Outside the seal.* A pause. *It should not have been able to.*

Nobody said anything.

*The blow was small and fast,* said Ciel, *and the wheel was small and fast, and it went through a mended place. One of the old ones. The place is holding again.* Another pause. *Rimuru and I have closed it. It did not turn twice.*

"Is he hurt?" said Frieren.

*A cut across the shoulders. Shallow. Maomao should see it.* A pause, and then, more carefully: *The second blow did not hurt him at all.*

Frieren understood that. Mau watched her understand it. Everyone at the stake who knew what the wheel was understood it: that one turn had been enough, against a thing that small, to make the next blow nothing.

"It didn't decide anything," said Mau. His shoulders were stinging. His voice came out steadier than he felt. "It wasn't the Noise. The thing hit me, and it turned. That's all."

*That is what it does,* said Ciel. *It is the only thing it does.* A pause. *That is why it matters that it could.*

Fern had the ledger open again. She was writing. Small and upright and fast.

Yuta still had his hand in Mau's collar.

Mau turned round, carefully, so as not to pull it loose. Yuta's face was very calm. His hand was shaking.

"Yuta."

"Sorry." Yuta did not let go. "Give me a second."

They stood in the snow. Frieren came and stood on Mau's other side, with the grimoire, and did not say anything. After a while Yuta's fingers opened, one at a time, and he looked down at his own hand as if it belonged to someone else.

"You've seen it before," said Mau. "In the plaza."

"I've seen it," said Yuta. "And I've seen what happens when nobody stops it turning." He flexed his hand. "The last time I watched that wheel go round and round and nobody could reach it, it was learning how to get through my teacher's Infinity." He looked at Mau. "So I don't let it go round. Not once more than it has to. I don't look at it. I don't think about it. I just take you out."

Mau did not say anything.

"One turn's a warning," said Yuta. "That's all it gets." Then, quieter: "That's all it gets from me."



Maomao cleaned the cut in the clinic and said it was nothing, in the voice she used for things she was going to keep an eye on. Mau sat on the edge of the cot with his shirt off and Frieren in the doorway with the grimoire, and let her.

Fern put it in the ledger, where anyone in the house could read it. *Day 118. Yuta, Mau. First room. Struck from behind. Rule two: Yuta. Both out.* Under it, after a moment, she added: *Out in under a minute.* Nobody made a speech about it. That night, at the long table, Maki read the page over Fern's shoulder, and put her hand on the back of Yuta's neck, briefly, roughly, on her way past, and sat down.

Later, in the east room, with the snow light on the floor and Rimuru very quiet in the loft, Frieren read the grimoire in the reading chair and turned the pages again.

Mau lay on the bed and listened.

The Noise was still leaning. The same as it had been in the yard. The same as it had been inside. Not toward the gate. Not toward the wheel, curled asleep again in his shadow behind the mended place.

Toward something he could not see yet.
