*When: Day 124, before dawn. The gate's third week. The west wing's second room had been finished two evenings before.*

She came before the door was open.

That was the difference, afterward, that everyone remembered. Seika had come at the eleventh bell, with Jinshi's key already turned and Kessoku on the bench inside. Ram came in the dark before dawn, with the door of Arrival House still locked and Jinshi still asleep in his temporary bed in Maomao's downstairs room in the real house, behind the word *temporary* in ink.

The fort was on the step. Rem was on the step, between Umaru and Ori, with the empty teapot in her lap. By five, half the house had come out too, the way it had on the night of Nijika's coin, one by one, in blankets, without being called. Mau and Frieren. Fern and Stark. Momo and Vamola, under one blanket. Nazuna, upside down from the bell tower, awake, which at that hour she would have been anyway. Seika, in the ugly coat, who stood at the end of the step with her arms folded and said nothing, and looked at Rem, every so often, with an expression nobody could read. Rimuru, with a pillow, which he gave to Rem, who put it in her lap on top of the teapot and held both.

Nobody talked much. It was very cold. The stars were going pale over the black hills.

At a quarter to six, in the last of the dark, the bell in the tower over Arrival House rang.

Once.

Nobody had pulled it. Everyone on the step looked up at once, at the tower, at the rope hanging by the door, which had not moved. The note went out over the plaza and the yard and the dark fields, round and deep, and hung there humming.

And from inside the locked building, behind them, came a sound.

Not a chair scraping. A thud. Something heavy, falling against a wooden floor.

Rem was on her feet. The teapot and the pillow went down the step and nobody caught them. She was at the door, both hands on it, pushing, and it did not move, because it was locked, because Jinshi had the only key and Jinshi was asleep.

"*Nee-sama!*"

Diablo was there. Nobody had seen him come. He was simply there, beside Rem at the locked door, in his butler's coat, and he put one hand flat on the wood beside the lock, and said, very politely, "Excuse me," and the lock turned over by itself with a click.

Rem was through the door before it had finished opening.



Mau came in behind her, with Frieren. Then Umaru and Ori. Then everyone else, crowding into the dark intake hall, where there was no lamp lit yet and the only light was the grey of the coming dawn through the windows.

The door of the second witness room, at the back, was half open.

Rem was already there.

She was on her knees on the floor of the second witness room, just inside the door, holding someone.

The someone was small. Smaller than Rem. Slighter. She was lying half across Rem's lap where she had fallen, in a maid's dress exactly like Rem's, black and white, with a white headband, and her hair was pink. Not the pink of Bocchi's. Paler. Like the inside of a shell. Cut short, to the jaw, on one side, so that it fell across her face.

Her eyes were open.

They were red. Not the colour. The whites. Red and raw, as if she had been staring into something bright for days without blinking. There were dark shadows under them, darker than Seika's had been. Her face was grey. Her lips were grey. She was breathing very fast and very shallow, the way a bird breathes when you hold it in your hand.

She was looking up at Rem.

"...Rem," she said.

Her voice was almost nothing. Dry. Cracked. But it was a voice that had clearly, all its life, been used to saying things in a tone that made people stand up straighter.

"Nee-sama." Rem was holding her so hard her arms were shaking. "Nee-sama. Nee-sama. I'm here. I'm here. I'm—"

"I know," said the girl with pink hair. "I can see you. Obviously." She blinked, slowly, with her red eyes. "Stop shouting. You're very loud."

Rem made a sound that was half a sob and half a laugh and pressed her face into her sister's hair.

The girl lay in her arms and did not push her away. Her hand came up, slowly, shaking, as if it weighed a great deal, and found the back of Rem's head, and rested there. Very briefly. Like a lid.

"You were in the middle of a sentence," said Ram, into Rem's shoulder.

Then her hand slid off Rem's head, and her eyes closed, and she went completely limp.



"*Maomao!*"

It was Umaru who shouted it. Maomao was already moving. She came through the crowd in the intake hall in her nightclothes with her satchel, which she had brought to the step of Arrival House at four in the morning because she brought it everywhere, and knelt on the floor of the second witness room beside Rem, and put two fingers on the side of Ram's throat, and her other hand flat on her chest.

She knelt very still for a long moment.

"Her heart's going," said Maomao. "Fast. Too fast. And weak." She lifted one of Ram's eyelids, looked at the red raw white of the eye, and let it close. "She's not hurt. No wound. No fever. No poison I can smell." She frowned. "She's *empty*."

"Empty?" said Rem.

"Like someone who hasn't eaten in a week," said Maomao. "But it's not food." She looked at Ram's grey face. "Her body's starving for something. I don't know what. I don't have a name for it."

"I do," said Frieren.

She had come in behind Mau. She was standing in the doorway of the second witness room in her shawl, looking down at the girl in Rem's lap, and her face had gone very still.

"Mana," said Frieren.

Everyone turned.

Frieren came into the room and knelt down on Ram's other side, across from Maomao. She did not touch her at once. She held one hand over Ram's forehead, palm down, the way she had held it over the chests in the dungeon, not quite touching. Her eyes went half closed.

"Her body makes almost none," said Frieren slowly. "And holds almost none. Like a cup with a crack in it." She moved her hand, very slightly, to the middle of Ram's forehead, under the short pink fringe. She stopped there. "There's something missing. Here." She looked at Rem. "Something that should be here. That used to take mana in from the air for her, all the time, without her trying."

Rem's face had gone white.

"Her horn," said Rem.

"Yes," said Frieren. "Without it, she can't fill herself. Someone has to fill her. From outside." She looked down at Ram's grey face. "And she's been spending what little she had. A great deal of it. For days. On something that costs a great deal." She touched, very lightly, the corner of one of Ram's closed eyes. "Looking. Very hard. For a very long time."

Rem did not say anything. She held her sister and did not say anything at all.

"She'll die," said Maomao quietly. "If it's empty and nobody fills it. Won't she."

Frieren did not answer that. She did not need to.

She put her hand down, flat, on Ram's forehead, under the fringe, where the horn should have been.

And something went out of her.

Mau saw it. He did not know how. He had never been able to see mana the way Frieren could, the way Fern could. But he saw it now, or felt it, the way you feel heat from a fire with your eyes shut. Something went out of Frieren's hand, into Ram, steady, slow, like water poured very carefully into a cracked cup. Not much. A little. Then a little more. Frieren's face did not change. But her breathing slowed, and went deep, the way it went deep when she was holding something very precisely.

Ram's breathing slowed too.

It slowed, and slowed, and deepened. Her chest stopped fluttering. Her face, under the short pink hair, went from grey to pale, which was better, and then, very faintly, a little pink at the cheeks.

Maomao, with two fingers on Ram's throat, let out a breath.

"Slower," she said. "Stronger." She looked at Frieren. "Whatever you're doing. Keep doing it."

"I am," said Frieren.

She kept doing it for a long time. The grey dawn came up through the window of the second witness room. The crowd in the doorway did not move or speak. Rem knelt on the floor with her sister in her lap and Frieren's hand on her sister's head and looked at Frieren's face the whole time, and did not blink.

At last Frieren took her hand away.

Ram stirred.

Her eyes opened. They were still red at the whites, still shadowed. But they were clearer. They moved. They looked up at the ceiling of the second witness room. Then at Rem, above her. Then, slowly, sideways, at Frieren, kneeling beside her with one hand on her knee.

Ram looked at Frieren for a long moment.

"Who," said Ram, in her dry cracked voice, "is this."

"Frieren," said Rem. "She's— she's family. She saved you. She—"

"Her mana," said Ram, ignoring her, still looking at Frieren, "smells *ancient*."

Frieren considered this.

"It is," she said.

Ram looked at her a moment longer, with her red eyes, with an expression of deep, exhausted, suspicious assessment.

"Acceptable," said Ram.

And closed her eyes again, and went to sleep, properly this time, breathing slow and deep, in her sister's lap, on the floor of the second witness room, with her hand fallen open on Rem's apron.



The register girl wrote it down, later, when there was light to write by.

*Day 124. Before the door opened. Ram.* She had asked Rem for the family name, very quietly, and Rem had said they did not have one, not here, not the way the register meant, and the register girl had nodded and left the space blank. Then, beside it, in the column she had ruled on her first day: *Arrived.*

And in the margin, very small: *by coin. The second.*

She looked at it for a while. Then she added, smaller still, under *second*:

*(asleep.)*



They carried Ram to the west wing at seven. Stark carried her, because Rem would not let anyone with less steady arms do it, and Rem walked beside him the whole way across the plaza and the yard with one hand on her sister's ankle, as if Ram might disappear again if she let go.

They put her in the room with the west window, beside Rem's turn. The one Wakana had finished two evenings before. Rem tucked her in herself. Then she sat down on the floor beside the bed, with her back against the wall, the way Seika had slept on the floor beside Nijika's bed.

She did not move from there all day.

Frieren came back at noon, and put her hand on Ram's forehead again for a while, and went away. And again at dusk. Ram did not wake either time. But her colour held.

Mau stood in the doorway at dusk, after Frieren had gone, and looked in.

Rem was sitting on the floor against the wall beside the bed, in the last red light from the west window. She was holding her sister's hand, which had fallen off the edge of the bed in her sleep. The way, Mau thought, a hand falls off the edge of a bed toward someone who is lying on the floor beside it.

Rem looked up and saw him.

She did not say anything. She did not need to. Her face said it.

It had carried.
