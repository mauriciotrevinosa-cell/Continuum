# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 24
## Chapter 3 — Not Yet

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 24  
**Reading order:** 3  
**Continuity state:** Days 132-133 | different witnesses recognize prior techniques and their cost; demonstration pauses without repeating trauma as spectacle | Gojo's measurement is not complete; Infinity and Blue work | Ciel warns that synchronisation is pending, not immunity | lunch: Sukuna, rematch, Fern stops an unplanned departure | Gojo to Mau: 'Rematch?' / 'We'll see' | the scar conversation follows

*When: Day 132, afternoon, into the lunch of Day 133. Seiko's coin is inside Gojo's collar; the fort has issued him a ONE DAY PASS he has already lost twice.*

The snowball stopped an inch from his coat.

It was Milim's idea. Of course it was. After the sweets and the plaza, Gojo had said he wanted to stretch his legs, and Milim had taken him to the south field, where the fort sledded and the snow was still deep. Half the house followed. When he said to throw something at him, Rimuru told Milim not to throw herself.

She considered this, and threw the snowball instead.

Now it hung in front of Gojo's chest, perfectly still.

"Infinity," he said, pleased. "Good."

"We've met it," said Rimuru.

Gojo turned toward him. Yuta nodded, but was watching Mau rather than the snowball. Frieren had taken Mau's hand. Her fingers were tight enough that he looked down at them.

"Not like this," Rimuru added. "Not choosing exactly what to stop."

Gojo explained the slowing to the people who had not seen it before, briefly, with his hands in his pockets. For the others, the snowball was not a new trick. It was a familiar distance, this time holding back one harmless thing and allowing the rest of the world through.

He let the snowball fall.

"Now the fun one."

Yuta's expression changed before Gojo raised his hand.

"Tell us first," said Rimuru. "What you're going to do."

Gojo looked round the small crowd. At Frieren's hand closed around Mau's. At Yuta. The smile eased.

"Blue. Small. Just the loose snow. Nothing aimed at anyone."

Rimuru waited a moment, then nodded. He moved the nearest onlookers back before Gojo raised his hand.

Mau felt the air lean toward it. Loose snow rose in thin streams, not blown upward but drawn inward, toward a small blue point above Gojo's palm. Gojo moved his hand, and the streams moved with it, neat as thread through a needle.

Mau watched the place where the blue stopped and Gojo's fingers began.

His own hand had disappeared inside it once. He remembered the force before he remembered its colour. His fingers closed against Frieren's, and she felt them do it.

"We've seen that too," said Yuta, quietly. "Mau used it against Sukuna."

Gojo looked at Mau without moving the little point.

"And later," said Rimuru. He did not explain containment in front of the newer arrivals. He was watching the clean edge above Gojo's palm. "Let it go."

Gojo closed his hand. The loose snow fell. Nothing followed it. No answering force in his other hand. No convergence.

For a moment, Frieren kept looking at both his empty hands.

"I didn't know," said Gojo.

"You weren't here," said Yuta.

The people at the back had gone quiet too. Some were remembering the white field. Some had waited at the inn and seen who came home. Others had only learned enough since arriving to understand why the silence had changed.

Gojo lowered his hands. This time he did not give the next part of the explanation.

*Master,* said Ciel.

Rimuru's expression changed.

"Gojo-san. A moment."

Gojo turned toward him.

Rimuru repeated Ciel's words aloud, sentence by sentence, so the others could hear her explanation. He kept relaying her answers as Gojo spoke. *The world measures those who arrive. I call it Continuum. Once it has measured what a person is, it restricts what they can use. The restriction can ease as the world and its threats grow.*

Gojo looked down at his hand.

"Hasn't stopped me yet."

*No,* said Ciel. *That is the problem. It has not finished measuring you.*

Yuta looked at Rimuru then.

*I felt the measurement begin at Arrival House,* Ciel continued. *I mistook the beginning for completion. It is still resolving the structure of your technique and the eyes that hold it. Each answer opens another part of the reading. I cannot tell you when it will finish.*

Gojo stood very still for a moment. Then the grin returned.

"Taking its time with me."

*Taking its time is not refusing to do it. You are not exempt.*

"I heard you."

*Then do not rely on having tomorrow what you have in your hand now.*

He flexed his fingers. Nothing impeded the movement. Nothing impeded the pressure that gathered behind it either. He let it go without forming anything.

"A world that decides how strong I'm allowed to be," he said. "I've had rooms full of people try that."

*This is not a room full of people.*

"Yeah. I'm getting that."

He looked across the snow at the house, at the fence where the fort was arguing over whether Infinity counted as cheating.

He did not make another Blue that afternoon. When Milim asked for the other one, he shook his head. "Not today." Then he went back to answering questions about the snowball, with both hands in his pockets.



It was Yuta who told him about Sukuna. At lunch, on the hundred-and-thirty-third day, because there was no way not to.

He did it badly. He had clearly rehearsed it, and the rehearsal fell apart at the first sentence. Gojo had asked, between rice cakes, what the house fought. What came out of the hills. What the gate was for. What the blue light in the north field was guarding against. And Yuta had put down his cup and looked at the table and said:

"Sukuna's here."

Gojo stopped chewing.

The long table went quiet. All of it. Even the fort, behind its curtain, went quiet.

"In this world," said Yuta. "Not in the settlement. Out there. He came over before most of us. He's—" He stopped. "He's been here a long time. He's fought us. He fought Mau. He put his arm through Mau's chest, once." He did not look up. "He's still out there."

Gojo did not say anything for a long moment.

He sat at the long table with the rice cake in his hand and his blindfolded face turned toward Yuta, and his whole body had gone very still, the way a cat goes still at a window.

"Sukuna," he said.

"Yes."

"Ryomen Sukuna. Four arms. Two faces. Very rude."

"Yes."

"The guy who cut me in half."

Yuta did not answer that. He did not need to.

Gojo put the rice cake down. Very carefully. On the plate. As if it might break.

"Okay," he said.

Then he stood up.

"I want my rematch," said Gojo.

He said it lightly. Almost cheerfully. The way you might say you wanted a second helping. But his hands, at his sides, had closed. And the air in the common room had changed, very slightly, the way it changes before a storm.

There was a silence.

Then, at the far end of the long table, Milim stood up so fast her bench went over backward.

"*Can I come?*" said Milim. Her face was lit up like a lamp. "Can I watch? Can I *help*? I'll hold your coat! I'll hold *him*! I've wanted to hit him for *so long*, he put his arm through Mau, nobody's let me—"

"*No,*" said half the house, at once.

"*Milim*," said Rimuru.

"*Gojo-sensei*," said Yuta.

"Absolutely not," said Fern, very quietly, from the head of the table, and the common room went quiet again, because Fern did not raise her voice and did not need to. "Nobody is going anywhere. You arrived yesterday. You don't know what the world is doing to you yet. You don't know the roads. You don't know where he is." She looked at Gojo. "You're in a house with a fort and a slate that says you like sweets. Sit down."

Gojo looked at her for a long moment, standing at the long table with his fists closed.

Then, slowly, he sat down.

"Okay," he said. "Not today."

"Not any day you haven't talked to the long table about first," said Fern.

"Okay, Mom," said Gojo.

Fern's face did something extraordinary.

Down the table, Stark choked on his tea.

And then Gojo turned his head. Not toward Fern. Not toward Yuta. Toward the place beside Frieren, halfway down the bench, where Mau had been sitting the whole time without saying anything.

It was the first time he had looked at Mau since the plaza.

"What about you?" said Gojo. "Mr. Stole-All-Of-Mine." His voice was light. His face, under the blindfold, was not. "You've got my Void in a box. You fought him. You're the one he put his arm through." A pause. "What do you think? Rematch?"

The long table looked at Mau.

Mau looked back at the tall man in the blindfold. He felt it again, the pressure, the reading. All of him. Every line.

He thought about Sukuna in the hills. About an arm through his chest. About a great pale wheel that Sukuna had once worn, that was now, somehow, in Mau's shadow. About a voice from under the wall that had said *let me out*.

"We'll see," said Mau.

Gojo did not move.

"*We'll see*," he repeated, slowly.

"We'll see," said Mau again.

For a long moment the two of them looked at each other across the long table, the tall man in the blindfold and the man with the shaking hands that he no longer put under the table, and nobody in the common room breathed.

Then Gojo grinned. It was not the bright grin. It was a different one. Sharper. Interested.

"Okay," he said softly. "We'll see."

He picked up his rice cake again, and ate it, and asked Rem for more tea, and the common room let its breath out, and the conversation came back, ragged, the way it does after a storm that has not quite broken.



Mau did not see what happened next. He heard about it later, from Frieren, who saw everything.

Gojo did not look at Mau again during the meal. But when Yuta reached across the table for the honey, and his hair fell back from his forehead for a moment, Gojo's blindfolded face turned toward him. Just for a moment. Toward the thin pale line of old stitches across Yuta's brow.

And this time the grin did not come back afterward.

He sat very still at the long table with his tea in his hand and did not drink it, and his blindfolded face stayed turned toward Yuta for a long time. Long enough for Maki to notice, and go still herself. Long enough for Frieren to notice, and put her hand on Mau's knee under the table.

Then Gojo put the tea down, untouched.

"Yuta," he said.

His voice was perfectly pleasant. That was the worst part, Frieren said afterward. It was perfectly pleasant.

"After lunch," said Gojo, "you and I are going to have a talk. About your forehead."
