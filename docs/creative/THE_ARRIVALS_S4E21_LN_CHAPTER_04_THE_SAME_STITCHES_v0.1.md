*When: Day 133, the afternoon after the lunch where Sukuna's name was said. The gate changed into its fifth week at dawn; Mikasa and Maki are due to go in tomorrow.*

They went to the south field, because Gojo walked there, and Yuta followed him.

Nobody else was invited. Everybody else went anyway. Not close. Not to listen. The house had learned, a long time ago, in the plaza and on the roof and at the stake, that when two people walked out into a field to have a talk with that particular quality of silence between them, you did not let them be alone in it. So the house drifted after them, in ones and twos, and stopped at the top of the slope, by the fence, and stood there in the cold, pretending to look at the view.

Mau stood at the fence between Frieren and Rimuru. Maki stood a little apart, with her spear, which she had not been carrying at lunch, and which nobody had seen her go and get.

Down in the field, in the snow, Gojo stopped and turned round.

Yuta stopped too. Ten paces from him.

"Take it off," said Gojo.

He did not mean anything Yuta was wearing. Everyone at the fence knew what he meant.

Yuta did not move. Then, slowly, he lifted one hand and pushed his hair back off his forehead, and held it there, so that the thin pale line of old stitches stood out in the grey afternoon light, from temple to temple, neat and careful, as if someone had once sewn him shut.

Gojo looked at it for a long time.

"I know those," he said.

His voice was very quiet. It carried anyway, the way voices carry over snow.

"The last time I saw those," said Gojo, "they were on Suguru's face." A pause. "My best friend. Dead. A year dead. Walking around. Smiling at me with someone else inside him." His hands were in his pockets. They did not move. "Same stitches. Same place. Same neat little line, temple to temple." He tilted his head. "So tell me why my student has them."



Yuta told him.

He did it standing in the snow, ten paces away, with his hand still holding his hair back, so that Gojo could see the whole line the whole time. He did not look away. Mau, at the fence, had never seen Yuta look at anyone the way he looked at his teacher then: steady, and terrified, and absolutely unwilling to make it smaller than it was.

He told him about the end. About after. About Shinjuku, and the fight, and the place where it had gone wrong. About Gojo, in two pieces, on the ground. About Sukuna, still standing.

About a technique. Kenjaku's. The one that moved a brain from one body to another. About Yuta, standing over his teacher's body, with Sukuna still standing, and nobody left who could stop him, and a body on the ground that had once been the strongest in the world.

"I needed your eyes," said Yuta. "I needed your technique. I needed everything you had, and you were gone, and there wasn't time." His voice did not break. It was very close. "So I put myself in you. And I got up. And I kept fighting."

Gojo did not say anything.

"I wore you," said Yuta. "To fight him. With your hands." He swallowed. "But I came back. After Shinjuku. To my own body." He let his hair fall, at last, over the scar. "And I lived, Gojo-sensei. With Maki. We had a family. I got old." He looked down at his own young hands. "These hands. Older."

The field was completely silent.

At the fence, Maki had not moved. Her knuckles on the spear were white.

"And then the Goddess," said Yuta. "And this world. The same body, young again. Mine." He touched his forehead. "Except for this. Continuum kept this. I don't know why." He looked up. "I think because it's true."

Gojo stood in the snow, ten paces away, with his hands in his pockets, and did not move for a very long time.

"So," he said at last. Very lightly. "You're telling me you had grandchildren."

"*Gojo-sensei*—"

"No, no, I'm just—processing. I'm processing." He took one hand out of his pocket and pressed it flat against his own chest, over his heart, as if checking it was still there. "You. Had grandchildren. With *Maki*." He turned his head toward the fence. "*Hi, Maki.*"

Maki said nothing at all.

"And they called you Grandpa," said Gojo. "The kid who used to apologize for taking up space."

"They called me Grandpa," said Yuta quietly.

Gojo laughed.

It was the wrong laugh. Everyone at the fence heard it. It went up into the grey sky over the south field and it was not bright and it was not careless and it did not stop when it should have.

"Okay," said Gojo, still laughing. "Okay. Okay." He took his hand off his chest. "Take it off."

"I can't take it off, it's a—"

"I know it's a scar." The laugh stopped. All at once, like a door shutting. "I know what it is. I know *exactly* what it is. I've seen it. On Suguru. Every day for months I saw it on Suguru, and every day I knew what it meant, and I didn't—" He stopped. His whole body had gone very still. "Every time I look at you now, I'm going to see it. Every time. On my student. On the kid I *saved*. That *thing's* mark. On *you*."

"It's not his," said Yuta. "It's mine. I chose it. I'm not him. I'm not—"

"Don't say his name like it belongs to you."

Yuta went very still.

"You took my student's life," Gojo said. "Wore him. Told everyone you were him. And then you had a family with Maki. Grandchildren. All of it, pretending to be Yuta."

"Gojo-sensei. I am Yuta."

"Suguru's mouth said his name too."

He was looking at the stitches now, not the boy beneath them. Mau could see the mistake happening and could not find a sentence fast enough to stop it.

It went across the field like a crack of ice.

And then Gojo was moving.



He was not fast. That was the strange thing, afterward. Everyone agreed. He could have crossed the distance with Blue. Instead, he walked. His eyes were on the scar, and he wanted Yuta to see him coming. Fast. Ten paces through the snow toward his student, with his hand out, as if he were going to take Yuta by the head and—what? Nobody knew. Yuta did not know. Gojo did not know. He was just walking, with his hand out, toward the scar.

Yuta did not move. He stood in the snow with his arms at his sides and let his teacher come.

Mau went over the fence.

He did not decide to. His body went. He was over the fence and down the slope and across the snow before he had finished thinking *no*, and he was between them, with his back to Yuta, facing Gojo, with his hand up.

Gojo stopped. A pace away. His outstretched hand an inch from Mau's raised palm.

It did not touch. It could not. Mau felt it at once: the Infinity, the endless slowing, his own palm and Gojo's fingers getting closer and closer and closer forever and never arriving.

"Move," said Gojo.

"No," said Mau.

"This isn't about you, Stole-All-Of-Mine."

"He told you," said Mau. "He stood there and he told you all of it. He held his hair up the whole time so you could see." He did not lower his hand. "Now you're walking at him like you want to tear it off his face. He's not going to fight you. He's never going to fight you. So I am."

"You're going to fight me," said Gojo. Not a question.

"I'm going to stand here."

"Standing here *is* fighting me," said Gojo, and the air changed.

Rimuru was there. On Mau's left. In human shape, with his hands out of his sleeves for once, small and very calm. "Gojo-san," he said. "Please. Sit down. Breathe. Nobody here wants—"

"*Move,*" said Gojo, to both of them.

And behind Mau, very quietly, Yuta said: "Mau. Don't. He's my teacher. It's fine. Let him—"

"It's not fine," said Mau. "You're shaking."

Yuta did not answer.

Mau sent the words through the connection, without taking his eyes off Gojo.

*Ciel. Tell Rimuru to take Yuta. Up the slope. Start the drills.*

Rimuru heard. His eyes met Mau's for an instant.

*You too,* he sent back.

*I need him looking this way while you move.*

Rimuru stepped behind Mau and put a hand on Yuta's arm. "Come with me."

Yuta looked at Mau. "He thinks—"

"I know," Mau said. "Go up."

Gojo moved his head to follow them. Mau moved with it, staying in his sight.

"It's Yuta," Mau said. "You don't have to believe me yet. But you're not going through me to hurt him."

Rimuru took Yuta up the slope. At the fence, Maki met them. Fern had already heard Ciel's warning; she turned the crowd toward the house, and Anko opened the path. The practiced calls travelled along the rail. Away from the field. Keep the route clear. Account for the fort.

The same drills they had hoped not to need again.

Gojo watched the movement behind Mau. Then he looked back at him.

"No one else?"

"No one else."

Gojo took a step away from the line of the retreating people. Mau followed it. There was nobody between them now, and nobody beside either of them.

"Shall we?" Gojo said.

Mau raised his hand.

Gojo's fingers moved. Mau reached for his wrist and found the inch of distance he could never finish crossing.

And Mau looked at him.

He did not mean to. That was what he told Ciel afterward. He did not mean to do anything. He only looked, the way you look at the face of someone who is about to hurt someone you love. Hard. All the way in.

And something behind his breastbone looked with him.

It had been listening for two days. Since the plaza. Since the blindfolded face turned toward him and read every line. Now it lifted its head, and opened its eyes, and *looked back*.

At the blindfold. Through it. At the eyes behind it.

Six of them. No. Two. Two eyes that were six. Two eyes that saw everything: every particle of cursed energy in the field, every flake of snow, the exact speed of every thought. Two eyes that had been born that way, and had nearly burned out the brain behind them before it learned to bear it, and had borne it ever since.

Mau saw them. The way they worked. The roadmap of them. All of it, in one look.

And far down inside him, The Noise turned. Not toward Gojo. Toward the *seeing*. Toward the eyes. The way it had once turned toward a Void full of infinite information in a plaza, and been stopped before it finished.

Analyze took the roadmap.

The wheel took Analyze.

And Gojo stopped dead.

He stood a pace from Mau with his hand still raised between them, and his whole body had gone rigid. His blindfolded face snapped toward Mau's eyes.

"*What,*" said Gojo, in a voice nobody at the fence had heard from him before, "*are you doing to my eyes?*"
