*When: Day 133, the same afternoon, the same field. The house at the fence at the top of the slope.*

Nobody had ever looked at his eyes like that.

Gojo told Yuta that, later, much later, on the roof, in a voice with nothing bright left in it. People had looked at him. Everyone looked at him. They looked at the blindfold and the white hair and the height and the grin. Enemies looked at him, and tried to see past the Infinity, and could not. Old men in rooms looked at him and counted what he was worth.

Nobody had ever looked *into* them. Into the eyes. Into the way they worked. Nobody had ever reached in through the blindfold and taken hold of the seeing itself and *read* it, line by line, like a manual.

He felt it the way you feel a hand in your pocket in a crowd. All at once. Total. Unbearable.

And the Infinity, which had stood between him and every blow for his whole life, did not stop it. Because it was not a blow. It was a *look*.



"*What are you doing to my eyes?*"

Mau did not know. He opened his mouth to say so.

He did not get the chance.

Gojo turned on him. Not a punch. Not anything a person would do. He turned, and the air between them turned with him, the endless slowing of the Infinity pointed outward now, pushing. Mau went backward across the snow as if a wall had walked into him. He did not fall. His feet slid. He came to a stop ten paces back, crouched, with his hands in the snow, and looked up.

Gojo was coming.

Mau stood before he reached him. He put himself on the line between Gojo and Yuta again, and Gojo saw him do it.

The first punch stopped short of Mau's face.

Mau's hands went up anyway. He had seen the shoulder move, had felt his own weight shift to meet it. Then Gojo's other hand caught his sleeve, pulled him off balance and drove a knee into his ribs. There was nothing infinite about the pain. Mau lost his breath and a whole step of ground.

He reached for the arm holding him. His fingers slowed before the cloth. Gojo let go, and Mau's hand finished closing on empty air.

There was no opening he could keep.

He tried the outside of the wrist, then the elbow, then the shoulder. Each time, the distance that looked like an inch became something his arm could not finish crossing. Gojo walked him backward through the snow without hurrying. A hand on his coat when Gojo wanted it there. Nothing to take hold of when Mau reached back.

"You can stand there," Gojo said. "You can't stop me."

Mau spat into the snow. There was blood in it.

Behind Gojo, Yuta took a step toward them. Mau saw him start and tried to shake his head. Gojo followed his glance, and Mau had to move again to stay between them.

That was all he had managed. To keep getting in the way.

Gojo's next blow met both his forearms. Mau's heels cut two tracks in the snow. He held for a moment, arms shaking, close enough to see a loose white hair caught against the blindfold.

Then Gojo changed the angle, and the hold was gone.

Mau staggered sideways. He caught himself with one hand, pushed up, and found Gojo already turning past him toward Yuta.

He went back across the line.

This time, something in Mau had stopped listening and started *answering*.

*Mau,* said Ciel, very sharply. *Mau. Stop. Stop looking. Analyze is—*

He could not stop looking. That was the thing. It was not him looking any more. Something behind his breastbone had its eyes wide open and was drinking in the man walking toward him: every particle, every line, the shape of the Infinity, the shape of the eyes that held it steady. And the more it drank, the more it wanted, and the wheel turned under it, and turned, and turned. Faster than it had ever turned. Making a road for Analyze to run down. Because it had understood, somewhere very deep, that this was the best way. The fastest way. To *have* this.

*It is taking the guardrails down,* said Ciel. Her voice had changed. *Not breaking them. Lifting them. On purpose. For a moment. Because it thinks it needs the room—Mau—*

The walls inside him opened.

Not all the way. A crack. A door left ajar. But through the crack came everything that had been sitting behind it, very patiently, since the plaza.



At the fence, Frieren made a sound Mau had never heard her make.

Down in the field, the snow around Mau lifted.

Not blown. Lifted. Every flake in a circle twenty paces wide rose off the ground at once and hung in the air, perfectly still, like the snowball an inch from Gojo's coat. Because it had stopped. Because the space around Mau had begun, very gently, to slow everything down forever.

"Oh," said Gojo, stopping. "*Oh*, you little—"

Gojo lifted one hand. Mau lifted his too, a fraction after him.

At the fence, Yuta's old teacher and the person who had stepped in front of him stood on opposite sides of the hanging snow. Neither was watching the snow. Both were watching the other's hand.

The resemblance was worse than the lifted flakes.

Frieren put her palm on the top rail. She could feel the timber beneath it. She held on to that while the space below the slope stopped looking like a place she could simply fly into and bring him back from.

And then the domains opened.

There was no warning. There never was, Yuta said afterward. A domain did not announce itself. One moment there was the south field and the snow and the grey sky and the house at the fence. The next, there was something else.

Mau's came first, because Mau's was not his. It came up out of the crack in him the way water comes up through a cracked floor. It was not one thing. It was two.

To the left, open, with no walls at all, a shrine. A great dark shape of bone and teeth and cut stone that stood in the snow as if it had always been there, and the air around it was full of a sound like a thousand knives being drawn at once. The snow nearest it was coming apart. Not melting. Cut. Into finer and finer pieces, until it was dust, until it was nothing.

To the right, closed, a sphere. A perfect black sphere, folding round Mau and part of the field, and inside it, if you looked, which nobody at the fence could bear to for long, there was everything. All at once. Every piece of information there was. Without end.

At the fence, Rimuru, who had scrambled back up the slope, said, very quietly: "The plaza." And then: "*Everyone back from the fence. Now. Seal protocol. Now.*"

Nobody moved. Nobody could.

Because Gojo had stopped dead in the middle of the field, between the shrine and the sphere, and his whole body had gone still. His blindfolded face was turned toward the black sphere.

"*That's mine,*" he said.

It came out of him like something torn.

"That's *mine*," said Gojo again. "That's my Void. That's my *domain*. That's—" He turned his head. Toward the shrine. Toward the bone and teeth and the sound of knives. And his voice changed again, and went very quiet. "And that's *his*."

The shrine went on cutting the snow into nothing.

"You've got both," said Gojo. "In one body. Mine and his. The man who killed me and—" He stopped. "And me."

And then, because he was Satoru Gojo, and had been the strongest in the world for his whole life, and had just been reached into, read, robbed, and shown his own killer's shrine standing next to his own Void in a stranger's chest, he did the only thing his body had ever known how to do when someone pushed him.

He pushed back.



Continuum had not finished measuring Gojo. Ciel said so afterward, very precisely, at the long table. *The reading began at the door. It did not close there. I warned him in the field. By the time it closed, the fight had already been stopped.* A pause. *Mau's walls opened during that interval. Those are different failures. Do not mistake them for the same one.*

Mau felt Gojo gather himself. No weight lifting. No restriction breaking. The man in front of him had been carrying this the whole time, and now he stopped keeping it small.

His domain came up like a wave meeting a wave.

Another black sphere, inside the first. Or around it. Or through it. Nobody at the fence could tell. Two Voids, the same Void, pressing against each other in the middle of the south field: the stolen one and the true one. And where they touched, the air screamed, and the light did something light does not do, and Mau, inside both, felt *everything* hit him at once.

Not information. More than information. Two infinities of it, folding into each other.

He should have gone down. Anyone would have gone down. The plaza had nearly killed him with one.

He did not go down. Because the wheel was turning, and turning, and Analyze had the roadmap, and the roadmap said: *this is what the eyes are for. This is how you bear it. This is how you see everything and do not die.*

And Mau, inside two Voids, for one instant, bore it.

There was no longer a field he could look across. There were more edges than space to put them in. Every direction arrived with its distance, its pressure, its next possible movement. Gojo was in front of him, and the shape of the Infinity was in front of him, and he could not make either of them smaller.

He brought his left hand back.

Gojo moved.

Mau saw the foot leave the snow. He did not see it land. The man in front of him was gone, and the hand coming toward his head belonged to the man beside him.

Mau vanished before it reached him.

He arrived badly. A pace too far, with his weight still going the way it had gone in the place he had left. His knee struck the ground. The snow under it had a different texture, and the eyes told him exactly how different, down to things he had no names for.

He could not shut any of it out.

Gojo was there before Mau stood. Mau rolled beneath the reaching arm, came up inside the arc of it and drove his palm toward Gojo's chest.

It slowed.

Still Infinity. Even here. Even with everything open.

Gojo caught him at the back of the neck and turned him. For a fraction of the movement Mau's feet left the ground. He disappeared out of the grip and came back on the other side, coughing, his left hand raised before he knew what he was trying to do with it.

Gojo had already turned to meet him.

Their hands stopped inches apart. Between them, the suspended flakes flattened into a narrow white seam. Mau felt the pressure through his teeth. Behind the shape of Gojo's head, the two black boundaries shuddered against each other; beyond those, the shrine's cutting went on.

The hand stopped. The rest of him did not.

Gojo opened the last inch to take hold of him. Mau's shoulder struck him inside the turn, and both of them moved. Not far. Enough to tear the frozen crust beneath their boots into strips. Enough for Mau to feel, at last, the weight of another body instead of a distance he could never cross.

Then Gojo was out of reach again.

Mau had gained nothing he could hold. His ribs hurt. His hand would not quite close. Every time he moved, the seeing gave him more than he had asked for, and something inside him tried to use all of it at once.

Gojo looked at him across the disturbed snow. His breath had become visible between them.

Mau could see where Yuta was. He moved back toward him before Gojo moved again.



And then a third thing happened, which nobody had planned, least of all Yuta.

Yuta had been standing behind Mau the whole time, with the dark film of his cursed energy up round him, because he had promised not to fight his teacher. But he was inside the edge of two domains now. Two infinities and a shrine. And his body, which had lived a whole life somewhere else, and had been a special grade, and had once, in that life, had a domain of its own, *remembered*.

For a few seconds, caught in the overlap, Yuta's own limit slipped too.

The ground under Yuta opened.

Not a hole. A field. A field of swords, hundreds of them, driven point-down into the snow in a great ring round him, hilts up, glittering. And above them, faint, enormous, half there and half not, the shape of something with long arms and a crown of hair, leaning over Yuta as if to shelter him, with her face turned toward the two Voids and her mouth open in a silent scream.

*Rika,* said Ciel, sounding, for the first time Mau had ever heard, frightened. *That is—Yuta's—that should not be possible at his—*

Three domains. A shrine. Two Voids. A ring of swords. The south field in the grey afternoon, and the house at the fence, and the snow hanging in the air in a circle twenty paces wide, not falling.

One of the swords stood beside Mau's boot. Another was behind Gojo. They had not been there when either of them chose where to put his feet.

Yuta's hand hovered above a hilt. It did not close.

He was looking from his teacher to Mau, trying to get a word through a space that had become crowded with the things all three of their bodies could do. Rika's pale shape bent lower over him.

On the slope, Maki was already moving. The others saw the empty place beside them before they saw her below it, spear low, finding the edge she could approach.

There was no safe turn to wait for. The field did not have turns anymore.



And then Gojo stopped.

Not because anything stopped him. He simply stopped. In the middle of the clash, in the middle of the screaming light, with his Void pressing against Mau's and Yuta's swords glittering around them. He stood there, and tilted his head, and said, in a completely different voice, curious and almost puzzled:

"Wait."

Everything held.

"Wait," said Gojo again. "That's *mine*." He was looking at Mau. Not at the Void. At the way Mau was standing. "That stance. The way you're holding the Infinity. With the left hand back, like that." He tilted his head the other way. "That's how *I* do it. Not how a copy does it. How *I* do it, when I'm—" He stopped. "You've fought me before."

Mau stood inside two Voids and looked at him.

And he remembered.

Not the way he remembered things from before the clearing, which was not at all. He remembered the way he remembered the Trial: all at once, from underneath, in the body before the mind. A scenario. One of them. Among the hundreds. A street of white light, white as this snow. A tall man with white hair and something over his eyes, and a smile like a door left open on purpose, coming at him with Infinity in one hand and Red in the other. And Mau losing. And losing. And losing. And on the last try, the one that counted, standing exactly like this, with the left hand back.

*In the Trial,* said Ciel, very quietly. *He was one of them. You fought him many times. You never told anyone.*

"You fought me," said Gojo. Softly. Almost wondering. "Somewhere. A lot. And you learned how I move." He grinned. It was not a nice grin. It was a delighted one, and it was terrifying. "And you *beat* me. Once. Didn't you."

Mau did not answer.

He did not need to. His body answered for him.

Because the Trial was not over in his body. It had never been over. And his body, standing in the snow with the left hand back, facing a tall man with white hair and Infinity, did not know it was not in the Trial any more. It only knew what it had done, the last time, the time that counted.

So it did it again.

And Gojo, seeing it come, grinned wider, and let go too.



From the fence, the next exchanges were impossible to put in order. Inside them, Mau's body remembered where to put its hands.

Gojo's hand came up and something red bloomed in it. Not fire. Not light. A point of pure outward force, compressed with a precision Mau's borrowed seeing could follow but his body could not reproduce. Red. He threw it the way you throw a ball to a friend.

It hit Mau in the chest.

Mau's chest was not there.

It had gone soft. Not torn. *Soft.* His whole body, in the instant before the Red arrived, had stopped being a body and become something that gave. Blue, translucent, like a great drop of water in the shape of a man. The Red went into it and was swallowed. The body rippled out from the impact like a pond when a stone goes in. Then it closed, and held, and was Mau again, standing, with his coat torn open and his chest whole underneath.

Up at the fence, Rimuru made a sound like he had been hit.

"That's *mine*," said Rimuru, faintly, to nobody. "That's my body. He just—that's *me*—"

Mau came back with his fingers already spread.

A white bolt left his hand. Gojo turned his face a little and let it spend itself against the distance that protected him. Mau sent the second toward the ground beside his feet. Gojo moved before it struck, and the snow burst where he had stood.

At the fence, Frieren knew the shape of the casting.

Then Mau's other hand closed around something dark.

It was small at first. A black centre with the light bending at its edge. The spell Frieren had once used to pull the ground apart beneath an enemy. In Mau's hand its outline shook. Snow, loosened earth and the splinters from a buried root began moving toward it.

"Mau," Frieren said.

He was not hearing her. His body was reaching into the places the Trial had taught it to reach. A spell for distance. A body that gave under a blow. A way out of a grip. Something that could make the place his enemy stood cease to be a place to stand.

He pushed the dark centre toward Gojo.

Gojo's foot slid. He set it again, looked at the drawing-in snow, and raised two fingers.

Red struck the spell from the side.

The inward pull and the outward force met at an angle. For a moment they held, and the hanging snow flattened into a crooked sheet between them. Then the spell's trembling edge broke. The centre folded shut, and a fan of snow and earth flew across the empty lower field.

Gojo had turned the collision away from the house.

Mau felt the failure up his arm. His fingers went numb. He could still see the next casting, its shape and every correction it needed, but his hand would not make the shape cleanly.

Gojo did not give him time to try.

Blue caught the space beside Mau's shoulder and drew him off his feet. Mau blinked out of the pull and arrived crouched, one hand dragging in the snow. Gojo was waiting there. Mau's forearm met a blow meant for his jaw, and he lost the ground he had just found.

The next blink was shorter. Worse. Gojo followed that one too.

"Too much," Gojo said through his teeth. "You're trying to do all of it."

Mau answered with a barrier. It rose in overlapping pale planes between them. Gojo changed the pull instead of striking it head-on; the nearest plane turned, and the rest lost their alignment. Mau let them fall before they could trap his arm between them.

Something white opened behind his shoulder. Not a completed spell. His hand shook, and it went out.

He was paying for every answer. Nothing he reached for stayed easy. The borrowed seeing found another way to meet Gojo, and his body followed it before it had finished surviving the last.

Gojo changed his grip on his own domain.

The black boundary bent. Mau felt the pressure shift from the place he had braced against to a place he had not known needed bracing. The shrine's cutting scored a fresh line through the snow between them. Gojo stepped across it at the instant it passed and drove Mau back toward the seam between the two Voids.

He was not simply using more force. He was changing the question faster than Mau could finish answering it: the distance, the angle, the part of the clash he was willing to hold and the part he was willing to risk.

Mau's imitation did not follow cleanly. His left eye burned. He kept his feet, but only because the body beneath them remembered losing on a street of white light, over and over, until it had learned one last answer.

The air above his shadow changed.

And Mau, re-forming, lifted his head, and looked at Gojo, and above Mau's head, in the grey air over the south field, the wheel appeared.

It rose out of his shadow and climbed into the air above him and hung there. Great and pale and slowly turning. Eight spokes. The adaptation wheel. The one that had risen in the plaza, with an unfinished body beneath it. There was no body now. Only the wheel, above his head, like a crown, turning.

Gojo stopped.

Everything about him stopped.

He stood in the snow with the Red still fading from his fingers and his blindfolded face turned upward, toward the great pale wheel turning slowly over Mau's head, and the delight went out of his face the way a candle goes out.

"That's how he did it," said Gojo.

His voice was very quiet. Very clear.

"That's how he killed me," said Gojo. "He wore that. Over his head. Just like that. It turned, and turned, and every time it turned it understood a little more of my Infinity. And then it turned one last time." He did not move. "And then I was in two pieces on the ground."

The wheel turned once more over Mau's head. *Click.* A sound like a great lock moving.

Mau did not know it had turned. Mau was not there. Mau's body was in the Trial, on the last try, the one that counted, and it was doing what it had done, and it was nearly done.

His left foot slid back. His right shoulder lowered.

Blue formed at one hand. Red at the other.

They did not sit neatly in his palms. The inward pull dragged at his fingers; the outward pressure cracked the skin across his knuckles. His wrists shook as he brought them toward the same line.

Gojo saw what he was making.

The fear stayed in his face. He raised his own hands anyway.

His Blue was steady. His Red narrowed until its edge stopped scattering light. Mau could see the difference: one man forcing two incompatible answers toward each other, the other arranging exactly where they would meet.

At the fence, Yuta saw both preparations and understood them before anyone else did.

"*Rika.*"

Rimuru caught the nearest two people at the rail and pulled them down the other side. Frieren tried to leave the fence. Rimuru caught her sleeve before she could enter the line between them.

A violet edge appeared between Mau's hands.

Across from him, the same colour gathered with a terrible, quieter precision.

Neither had released it. There was still that last movement to make, the space between preparation and an attack leaving the hand. Gojo's shoulder turned. Mau began to bring his fingers forward.

They were aimed at each other. The house was behind one of them. The road and the rest of the field were behind the other.

Neither body was remembering those places now.

Frieren called his name.

The wheel moved toward the next spoke.

Neither Purple left its maker's hands.



Rika caught him.

She came out of Yuta's ring all at once. Not the faint shape over the field of swords. All of her. Huge and pale and long-armed, with her crown of hair, out of the ring on Yuta's hand and across the snow, and she wrapped both her enormous arms round Mau from behind, broke the alignment of his hands, lifted him off the ground and *held* him, the way you hold a child having a nightmare, his arms pinned, his face pressed into her.

The violet edge tore apart into the separate forces that had not quite become it. Mau lost the casting. Rika held his arms away from each other until both hands were empty.

Mau screamed.

It was not pain. It was the Trial. It was the body being stopped one instant before the thing it had done four hundred times, and not understanding why.

And at the same moment, in front of Gojo, out of nowhere, there was a spear.

It was at his throat. The point of it, Maki's cursed tool, the one she never let anyone touch, an inch from his neck. Inside the Infinity. Somehow inside it. Nobody ever worked out how. Maki said afterward that she simply hadn't asked the Infinity's permission, and the house decided that was the best answer anyone was going to get.

Maki stood at the other end of the spear, in the snow, with her feet planted and her face perfectly calm.

Gojo looked from its point to Rika's closed arms. He opened his hands. His own preparation went out before he moved them any farther.

"Sit down, Satoru," said Maki.

Gojo did not move.

"Sit," said Maki, "*down*."

And then Continuum caught up.

Mau felt his walls come back. Yuta's brief opening closed with them. And on Gojo, for the first time since he had arrived, Continuum's measurement finished and the limit landed.

Not a weight returning. A weight he had never carried before.

He reached for the familiar inward pull to steady himself. There was nothing there. The space stayed space. Red was gone too. The domain would not answer. Only the distance at his skin remained, Infinity holding an inch of falling snow away from his sleeve.

*It has finished,* said Ciel through Rimuru. *Only now.*

Gojo's knees went. He went down in the snow, to one knee, then both. His hand, where the Red had been, was smoking faintly. Under the blindfold, a thin line of blood had started from his nose.

Behind Mau, the ring of swords was gone. Yuta was on his hands and knees in the snow, retching, shaking, grey to the lips.

Over Mau's head, the wheel stopped turning. It hung there a moment longer, pale and still. Then it sank. Slowly. Down through the air, and through Mau, and back into his shadow, like a stone into dark water.

The shrine was gone. The Voids were gone. The snow that had hung in the air in a circle twenty paces wide fell all at once, softly, everywhere, like the end of a held note.

And Mau, in Rika's arms, stopped screaming, and went limp, and his head fell forward against her pale enormous arm.



The south field was very quiet.

Up at the fence, nobody moved for a long moment. Then everybody moved at once.

Frieren was first. Of course she was. She was over the fence and down the slope before anyone else had finished starting. She did not run. She flew, low, a foot above the snow, faster than Mau had ever seen her move. And then she was on her knees beside Rika, and Rika, very carefully, as if handing over something that might break, laid Mau down in the snow in front of her.

He was breathing. Fast and shallow. His left eye was open, and it was black, and red at the centre, and it was shaking. His right eye was closed. There was blood at the corner of his mouth.

Frieren put her hands on either side of his face.

"Mau," she said.

He did not answer.

"*Mau.*"

Behind her, Maki lowered the spear. She looked down at Gojo, on his knees in the snow, with blood running from under the blindfold, breathing hard.

"You came here," said Maki, "and the first thing you did was fight."

Gojo did not answer.

"Yuta held his hair up," said Maki. "The whole time. So you could see it." She turned away from him. "Think about that."

She walked to Yuta, on his hands and knees in the snow, and knelt beside him, and put her hand flat on his back, and left it there while he shook.

Gojo knelt alone in the snow in the middle of the south field and looked at the place where the wheel had been. Above Mau's head. Turning.

Then, very slowly, he reached up and pulled the blindfold off.

His eyes were blue. Blue like the sky. And for the first time since he had walked out of the door of Arrival House at dawn the day before, there was nothing in them at all but fear.
