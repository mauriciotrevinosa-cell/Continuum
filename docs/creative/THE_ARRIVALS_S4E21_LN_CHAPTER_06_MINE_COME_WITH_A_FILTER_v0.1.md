## Chapter 6 — Mine Come With a Filter

*When: The night of Day 133 into the night of Day 134. Mau in Maomao's clinic; Gojo outside the door; Yuta on the roof.*

Gojo sat outside the clinic door all night.

Nobody asked him to. Nobody asked him not to. Maomao had shut the door on everyone except Frieren and Rimuru, and Gojo had walked up from the south field behind the stretcher with his blindfold in his hand and blood drying on his upper lip. When the door shut, he sat down on the floor of the corridor with his back against the wall opposite it, and stayed there.

Diablo found him there at midnight. Diablo had sat outside a door like that once, after Foundation, while Rimuru slept as a slime on a pillow. He stood in the corridor in his butler's coat and looked down at the tall white-haired man on the floor, with his blindfold in his lap and his blue eyes open, staring at the door.

"You are in my place," said Diablo.

Gojo did not look up.

"I know," he said.

Diablo looked at him for a long moment, with an expression of the most refined and total contempt.

Then he sat down beside him. On the floor. In his butler's coat. Against the wall. And the two of them sat there together all night, the oldest demon in the settlement and the man who had been the strongest in his world, not speaking, watching a door.



Inside, Maomao worked.

There was nothing to stitch. That was the strange part. She said it to Rimuru twice, low, as if repeating it might make it make sense. No wound. The chest where the Red had gone in was whole, the skin unbroken, warm. The blood at his mouth had come from biting his own tongue. His heart was fast and his breathing was shallow and he would not wake. And his left eye, when she lifted the lid, was black, with red at the centre, and it was not still. It moved. Not the way an eye moves in sleep. Fast. Precise. As if it were reading something very small, written on the inside of his own skull.

"Ciel," said Rimuru.

*Looking,* said Ciel.

She looked for a long time. Rimuru sat on the stool by the bed with his eyes closed and his hand on Mau's wrist, and Frieren sat on the floor on the other side with her back against the bed and Mau's hand in both of hers, and nobody said anything.

*The guardrails are back,* said Ciel at last. *All of them. Where they were. The wheel is in his shadow. The domains are closed. The walls are whole.* A pause. *Continuum has him again, the same as before. He is what he was this morning.*

"Then why won't he wake?" said Frieren.

There was a longer pause.

*Because something is still running,* said Ciel. *Underneath. Analyze took something, in the field. When he looked at Gojo's eyes. It took the whole shape of them. A roadmap. Every line.* Another pause. *And it has not stopped reading it.*

Rimuru opened his eyes.

"Can you stop it?"

*Not without stopping Analyze,* said Ciel. *And Analyze is part of what holds the walls. If I stop it, the walls are weaker.* A pause, very quiet. *It is not attacking anything. It is not breaking anything. It is building. Very slowly. From what it saw.* Another pause. *I do not know what it will be when it is finished. I do not know how long. I will watch it every minute. I will tell you the moment it changes.*

Frieren did not say anything. She held Mau's hand in both of hers and looked at his face. At the closed right eye. At the left, black and red, moving very fast under the lid, reading something nobody else could see.

"How long?" she said.

*I don't know,* said Ciel. *I'm sorry.*

Frieren put her forehead down against the back of his hand and stayed there.



She came out at dawn.

She opened the clinic door and stepped into the corridor, and the two men on the floor against the far wall looked up at her: Diablo with polite attention, Gojo with his blue eyes red-rimmed and his blindfold still in his lap.

Frieren looked at Gojo for a long moment.

He got up. Slowly. He was very tall, standing, and she was not, and she had to tilt her head back to keep looking at him, and she did.

"He's breathing," said Frieren. "Ciel says the walls are back. He'll wake. She doesn't know when."

Gojo did not say anything.

"Something's still running in him," said Frieren. "From your eyes. Analyze took them. The shape of them. It's building something." She did not look away. "Nobody knows what."

Gojo closed his eyes.

"I'm sorry," he said.

He said it plainly. Without a grin. Without anything bright in it. Just the words, in the grey corridor, at dawn, to a small elf with her hands in her sleeves who had sat on the floor all night holding the hand of someone he had put there.

"I came here," said Gojo, "and the first thing I did was fight."

Frieren looked at him for a long moment.

"He'd have stood in front of anyone," she said. "That's what he does. If it hadn't been you, it would have been something else. Something with teeth. Something from the hills." She paused. "He always goes in front. I can't stop it. I've tried. For two years. He goes in front."

"I know the type," said Gojo quietly.

"Yes," said Frieren. "I thought you might." She turned back toward the clinic door. Then she stopped, with her hand on it. "He beat you. Once. In the Trial."

"I know," said Gojo. "I felt it. In the stance."

"He never told anyone," said Frieren. "He never tells anyone what happened in there." She did not turn round. "Four hundred and thirty-seven times. He gave us the number, and almost nothing of what was in them." A pause. "I don't know how many of those were you."

She went back in and shut the door.

Gojo stood in the corridor looking at it.

Behind him, still on the floor, Diablo said, very quietly, with no contempt in it at all: "Sit down. It's going to be a while."

Gojo sat down.



Yuta was on the roof that night.

It was not a roof night. It was the fourth day, not the seventh. But Yuta had not slept since the field, and Maki had stopped trying to make him, and at midnight she had brought him a blanket and a cup of tea on the ridge, and kissed his hair, and gone back down, because she knew he was waiting for someone.

Gojo came up the ladder at one.

He did not say anything. He climbed up onto the ridge and sat down beside his student, a little apart, with his long legs drawn up and his arms round them, the blindfold back on. The two of them sat there in the cold looking out at the dark settlement and the blue light of the gate in the north field.

"I looked good, though," said Gojo, after a long time. "Right? In me. When you were—wearing me." He tilted his head. "I assume I looked incredible."

Yuta did not say anything for a moment.

"You looked like you," he said.

"So incredible."

"Mostly I didn't look in mirrors," said Yuta. "For a long time. It was—hard." He pulled the blanket tighter. "Maki made me. In the end. She stood me in front of one and made me look until I could. She said if I was going to live in it I had to be able to see it." He was quiet for a moment. "It took a year."

Gojo did not say anything.

"I'm not him," said Yuta quietly. "Kenjaku. I know it's the same scar. I know what you saw. On the step, and at lunch, and in the field." He did not look at his teacher. "But I didn't take you to use you. I took you because you were the strongest, and you were gone, and I couldn't let it be over. I couldn't let him win because you weren't there." His voice was very steady. "And afterward, I lived. I tried to live the kind of life you'd have wanted somebody to live in you. I tried every day." He swallowed. "I don't know if I did it right."

Gojo was quiet for a very long time.

Then he reached over, slowly, the way you reach for something that might startle, and pushed Yuta's hair back off his forehead with two long fingers, and looked at the scar. Properly. In the starlight. The whole thin pale line, temple to temple.

"It's not his," said Gojo.

Yuta went still.

"It's not his," said Gojo again, softly. "I was so angry, because it looked like his. Because the last time I saw it, it meant someone I loved was gone and something wearing him was smiling at me." His fingers did not move. "But this one means someone I love *got up*. Off the ground. When I couldn't. And kept going." He let the hair fall back. "And had grandchildren. With *Maki*. Which I'm still processing."

Yuta made a sound. It was not quite a laugh.

"I'm so angry," said Gojo. "Not at you. At everything that made you have to." He put his hands back round his knees. "You're you. And you had to wear me. Because I wasn't there." He was quiet. "I'm sorry I wasn't there."

"You were," said Yuta, very quietly. "You were the only reason I could do it. You were in every—" He stopped. "You taught me. That's how. That's the whole reason."

They sat on the ridge a long time after that, not saying anything.

"Keep it," said Gojo at last. "The scar. Don't let anyone take it off you. Not Continuum. Not some doctor. Not anyone."

"I wasn't going to."

"I know," said Gojo. "I'm just saying it anyway." A pause. "That's what teachers do."



Mau woke on the evening of the hundred-and-thirty-fourth day.

He woke the way he always woke after something bad: all at once, from nothing, with his body already braced for the ceiling to change. It did not change. It was the clinic ceiling, clay, washed white, with a beam across it. Frieren was asleep on the floor beside the bed with her head against the mattress and his hand in hers.

And there was someone else in the room.

Gojo was sitting on the stool in the corner, with his long legs stretched out across half the floor and his blindfold on, perfectly still, facing the bed.

Mau looked at him.

"How long?" said Mau. His voice came out like gravel.

"A day," said Gojo. "And a bit." He did not move. "Your elf's been on the floor the whole time. The demon's outside. The slime's been in and out. The doctor's been yelling at everyone. Your fort sent a basket." He paused. "It had a note in it. In crayon. It says *THE MINISTRY DOES NOT AUTHORIZE FIGHTS WITH TALL MEN*."

Mau almost laughed. It hurt.

"I'm sorry," he said.

"Don't." Gojo's voice was flat. "Don't do that. I came here and the first thing I did was fight, and you stood in front of my student, and I put you in a bed." He tilted his head. "You don't get to apologise to me. Not for this."

Mau did not say anything.

Gojo sat in the corner, very still, for a long moment. Then he leaned forward, with his elbows on his knees.

"That thing your eyes did," said Gojo. "In the field. Before the domains. When you looked at me."

"I didn't mean to."

"I know you didn't." Gojo's voice was quiet. "That's the problem. That's the whole problem." He tapped his own blindfold with one finger. "Do you know what these are? What it's like?"

"No."

"It's like being able to see everything," said Gojo. "All the time. Every particle. Every flicker of energy. Every thought someone's about to have, a half-second before they have it. All at once. Forever." He paused. "When I was a baby it nearly killed me. My brain couldn't hold it. It was too much. It was *everything*." He leaned back. "I survived because I was born with them. My brain grew up around them. Learned to filter. To decide what not to see." He tapped the blindfold again. "That's what this is for. It's not to stop me seeing. It's to help me *not look*."

Mau lay very still.

"What you did," said Gojo, "was look. All the way. At everything I am. With no filter." He was quiet for a moment. "Your eye's been doing it ever since. The doctor showed me. It's still reading."

*He is right,* said Ciel, very quietly, inside Mau. *It has not stopped.*

"That's what my eyes do," said Gojo. "Mine come with a filter." He looked at Mau through the blindfold, and Mau felt it again, the pressure, the being seen, but gentler now. Careful. "Yours don't."

The clinic was very quiet. On the floor, Frieren stirred and did not wake.

"So here's what's going to happen," said Gojo. He stood up. He was so tall the top of his white hair nearly brushed the beam. "I'm going to teach you."

Mau stared at him.

"Not because I want to," said Gojo. "I hate teaching. Everyone I ever taught got hurt." He put his hands in his pockets. "Because there is a wheel in your shadow that turned over somebody else's head once, in another world, until it understood my Infinity. And then I ended up in two pieces on the ground." He was quiet for a moment. "And this afternoon it turned over *yours*. With my Infinity in front of it." He looked down at Mau. "And if it ever finishes turning on someone, it's going to be *me* again. Or him." He nodded at nothing, toward the north. "Or someone you love. And I've done that once already."

He turned to the door.

"Gojo-san," said Mau.

Gojo stopped.

"In the Trial," said Mau. "When I beat you. Once." He swallowed. "You laughed. At the end. When it was over. You said—"

He stopped. He did not know how to finish. He did not know if it had been Gojo at all, in there, or something the Trial had built in Gojo's shape.

Gojo stood in the doorway with his back to him for a long moment.

"What did I say?" he said quietly.

"You said: *finally. Somebody who can keep up*."

Gojo did not turn round.

Then his shoulders moved. Once. It might have been a laugh.

"Yeah," he said. "That sounds like me."

He went out.



That night, very late, on the roof of the real house, Yuta was alone on the ridge again when the ladder creaked.

Gojo climbed up and sat down beside him.

They looked out at the settlement for a while. The lamps in the real house going out, one by one. The new west wing against the stars. The fort's window, still lit, where somebody was writing another basket note in crayon. The ink tree in the yard, silver-green in the starlight. The blue light of the gate in the north field, humming. The clinic window, with one lamp still burning in it, and an elf asleep on the floor inside.

"Yuta," said Gojo.

"Mm."

"This place," said Gojo. He was quiet for a moment. "There's a slime who's also a guy, and a dragon who's Head of Fun, and a demon who sat on a floor with me all night. There's a fort that sends notes in crayon. There's an old woman who gave me her coin so I could call the person I love." He paused. "There's a kid with my Void and his Shrine in one chest who stood in front of you without thinking." Another pause. "And you. Grown up. With a scar that means you got up."

Yuta did not say anything.

"I love this," said Gojo.

He said it simply. Quietly. Not brightly at all.

Yuta closed his eyes.

"I told them you would," he said. "On this roof. Before you came. I said you were going to love it."

"Yeah?"

"I said you were going to be insufferable," said Yuta. "And then I said you were going to love it."

Gojo laughed, very softly, in the dark.

"Both," he said. "Obviously."

Under his collar, against his chest, a small warm coin lay where he had tucked it, waiting. He put his hand over it, through the cloth, and did not take it out.

Not yet.
