# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 24
## Chapter 7 — Only Work

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 24  
**Reading order:** 7  
**Continuity state:** Day 135 | Marin at the bottom of a glue pot: when did we last go out? | never, not once since the bridge | Wakana: 'Oh.' | the pale blue cloth he's been saving | Milim, horrified: it's everyone | 'UMARU, I NEED THE STAMP'

*When: Day 135, two days after the south field. Mau is back on his feet with his left eye still black and red; Analyze is still building; Gojo has started calling the clinic "the Mau Hotel".*

Marin noticed it on a Tuesday, in the workshop, at the bottom of a pot of glue.

It was not a dramatic moment. She was sitting on the end of Wakana's workbench with her legs crossed, painting the edge of a shingle for the west wing's last eave. Red, because she had decided every eave on the west wing should have one red shingle at the corner, for luck, and nobody had stopped her. Wakana was at the other end of the bench, bent over a joint, with his tongue between his teeth and a pencil behind his ear and sawdust in his hair, and he had not looked up in an hour.

She dipped her brush. She reached the bottom of the glue pot. She looked into it.

And she thought: *when was the last time we did anything that wasn't this?*

She put the brush down.

She thought about it properly. She was good at thinking about things properly, when she decided to, which was not often, because most of the time it was more fun not to. She went back through the days. The ridge beam. The frame. The clay walls. The movie room, the slides, the bath. The crown, the stage, the pallets. The F1 cars, in the winter, with every name underneath. The fort frame, before that, in the Muslin. Fern's treaty. The bunk beds for Mikasa.

All of it together. Side by side. At this bench, or one like it. Making things.

She went back further. Past the move. Past the Muslin. Into the old inn, and the months before that, and the first weeks, after the bridge.

And she could not find one. Not one day that was only theirs. Not one evening where they had not been making something for someone else.

"Wakana," said Marin.

"Mm," said Wakana, not looking up.

"When did we last go out?"

Wakana's pencil stopped.

He looked up, slowly, with the joint still in his hands. He looked at her, sitting on the end of the bench with the red brush in her lap and the empty glue pot beside her, with an expression on her face he did not see very often, because Marin's face mostly did something much louder.

"Out?" said Wakana.

"Out," said Marin. "Together. Not to get clay. Not to fix a roof. Not to measure anything." She looked at the empty glue pot. "Just us. Somewhere. Doing something that isn't for the house."

Wakana opened his mouth.

He closed it again.

She watched him go back through the days the way she had. She watched him get to the end of them and find nothing there, and his face fall, very slightly, the way a dropped stitch falls.

"I—" said Wakana.

"I know," said Marin. "Me neither."

"I didn't notice," said Wakana. He put the joint down very carefully on the bench. "I'm sorry. I didn't notice."

"Neither did I. Until the glue." She picked up the empty pot and showed it to him, as if it were evidence. "I love this. I love the bench. I love making things with you. I love that everything in this house has your hands in it and my paint on it." She put the pot down. "But since we got here, all we do is *work*. We don't go *out*. We haven't been on a date in—" She stopped. "I don't think we've ever been on a date. Here. Not once."

Wakana looked at her for a long time.

"We went to the sea," he said.

"With forty people."

"We sat on the roof of the old inn."

"Fixing it."

"We—" He stopped. He looked at the bench. At the red shingle, half painted. At the joint he had been cutting, for the west wing, for someone else's room.

"Oh," said Wakana, very quietly.

"Yeah," said Marin.



She did not cry. That was the thing she was proudest of, afterward. She had wanted to, a little, in the workshop, looking at his face. But she did not. Instead she slid off the end of the bench, and wiped her hands on her apron, and put the lid on the red paint, and said:

"Okay. I'm fixing it."

"How?"

"I don't know yet." She was already at the door. "But I'm going to need help. And there's exactly one person in this house whose actual official job is fun."

Wakana watched her go.

Then he looked down at the bench. At the joint, unfinished. At the shingle. At his own hands, which had sawdust ground into every line of them, and had not held anything that was not a tool, or a piece of wood, or a paintbrush she had handed him to hold for a second, in a very long time.

He put the pencil down.

He sat on the stool in the empty workshop, among everything he had made for everyone, and thought about what Marin would want. Not what the house needed. Not what was next on the plan. What *she* would want, if somebody asked her, and nobody ever did, because Marin was always the one doing the asking.

He thought about it for a long time.

Then he got up, and went to the back of the workshop, where there was a chest he kept his own things in. Not tools. Things. He opened it, and looked inside, at a roll of cloth he had been saving since the autumn, from the last of the refuge stores. Pale blue. Very fine. The best cloth in the settlement. He had been saving it, he realised now, without ever deciding what for.

He took it out.



Milim was on the roof of the fort when Marin found her, which was not allowed, and she was lying on her back looking at the sky, which was.

"Milim," said Marin. "I need the Head of Fun."

Milim sat up so fast she nearly fell through the fort's roof.

"*Official business?*"

"Official business."

Milim slid off the roof and landed in front of her in a small explosion of cushions, with her eyes enormous.

"What kind?"

Marin told her.

She told her about the glue pot and the workshop and the days. About going back through them and finding nothing. About Wakana's face. About the sea with forty people and the roof they were fixing. About never once, here, not once.

Milim listened to all of it with her hands clasped under her chin and an expression of growing, enormous, righteous horror.

"*Never?*" said Milim.

"Never."

"Not *once*?"

"Not once."

"But that's—" Milim stopped. She looked round the common room, through the fort's arch. At the long table. At Fern with the ledger. At Yuta and Maki on the bench. At Bocchi and Kita in the corner, with their hands together, which they did now, in front of everyone, since the morning after the snow. At Okarun and Momo arguing over a pot. At Stark reading over Fern's shoulder and being told to stop. "That's *everyone*," said Milim slowly. "Isn't it. That's not just you. It's everyone. All of them. They all just *work*."

Marin looked at the common room too.

"Yeah," she said. "I think it is."

Milim stood there for a long moment, small and furious, in the middle of the fort.

Then she turned round, and climbed up onto the toll booth's platform, and cupped her hands round her mouth.

"*UMARU,*" she shouted. "*I NEED THE STAMP.*"
