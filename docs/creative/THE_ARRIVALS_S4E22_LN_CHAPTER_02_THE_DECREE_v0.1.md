# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 24
## Chapter 8 — The Decree

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 24  
**Reading order:** 8  
**Continuity state:** Day 135 evening | the joint decree: DATING DAY, three days; the fort covers chores 30-0; no spying (this means you, Anko) | the official couples list | Fern objects; Seiko: 'About time' | 'Not yet', Gojo's hand at his collar | first sign: Mau sees the crack in Senku's lens from across the table; Ciel: 'That was not you'

*When: The evening of Day 135. In the workshop, Wakana has a roll of pale blue cloth on the bench and has not told anyone why.*

The decree went up on the board at dinner, in crayon, under the four rings, over Senku's QUIET tally, and it had the Ministry's seal on it twice, because Umaru had pressed too hard the first time and decided the smudge was official.

*DECREE OF THE MINISTRY OF FORT AFFAIRS*
*AND THE OFFICE OF THE HEAD OF FUN*
*(JOINT)*

*WHEREAS everyone in this house only works;*
*WHEREAS nobody in this house has been on a date since the bridge (verified by the Head of Fun);*
*WHEREAS this is a disgrace;*

*IT IS DECREED:*
*1. DATING DAY. Every couple in this house must go on a date. Within three days.*
*2. Who plans it is up to the couple. (The Ministry does not plan dates. The Ministry is not a matchmaker.)*
*3. The fort will cover chores for any couple on a date. (Voted 30–0.)*
*4. Nobody may spy. (This means you, Anko.)*
*5. The Ministry reserves the right to ask how it went.*

And under that, in Umaru's large crooked capitals, a list.

*COUPLES (OFFICIAL):*
*MAU / FRIEREN*
*FERN / STARK*
*YUTA / MAKI*
*MARIN / WAKANA*
*BOCCHI / KITA*
*OKARUN / MOMO*
*MAOMAO / JINSHI (TEMPORARY)*

The common room read it in silence.

Then Fern stood up.



"No," said Fern.

"*Fern*—"

"No," said Fern again. She was holding the ledger against her chest the way she held it when she was about to be reasonable at someone. "The west wing's last eave isn't on. The gate changes in five days and Mikasa's party goes in the day after. The washing court's short a woman. Mau was in the clinic until this morning." She looked at Mau, at the end of the bench, with his black-and-red eye, and then away. "We don't have three days for everyone to go and—"

"Fern," said Seiko.

The common room went quiet.

Seiko was on her high seat at the north end, with her paper fan. She had not said anything since the decree went up. She had read it twice, slowly, with her eyebrows rising a little further each time.

"Sit down," said Seiko.

"Seiko-san, with respect, the ledger—"

"The ledger," said Seiko, "will still be there in three days." She snapped the fan shut. "I have watched every one of you work since the snow. Every single day. Building, carrying, fixing, measuring, writing things down. And every evening I have watched the people in this house who love each other sit at this table too tired to look at one another." She pointed the fan at the decree. "That child is right. It's a disgrace." She opened the fan again. "About time somebody said it."

Fern stood very still with the ledger against her chest.

"The fort will cover chores," said Umaru, from the arch, very helpfully. "Thirty to nothing."

"*Thirty*—"

"The witches voted too," said Umaru. "By pass. They said yes. Even the sleepy one."

At the witches' end of the table, Sekhmet, lying on the bench with her eyes closed, raised one hand slightly off the wood and lowered it again.

Fern looked at the decree. At the list. At her own name, second on it, beside Stark's, in large crooked crayon.

Stark, beside her, was looking very hard at his plate.

"Fine," said Fern. She sat down. "Three days."

The fort cheered. Behind its curtain, someone blew a whistle Senku had made for them in the winter and that Fern had confiscated twice.

"*But*," said Fern, over the noise, "I'm writing it in the ledger. *As a scheduled activity.*"

"That's fine," said Milim, generously. "Dates can be in the ledger."

"Thank you," said Fern, with enormous dignity, and opened the ledger, and wrote, in her smallest neatest hand: *Days 137–139. Dating Day (decree). Chores: the fort.*

Then she looked at the line for a long moment, and her ears went pink, and she closed the ledger very fast.



Gojo was delighted.

He had been eating, at the cold-hearth end, beside Yuta, and when the decree went up he put down his spoon and turned his whole body round on the bench to look at it. When Fern stood up he made a small sound of pure joy. When Seiko said *about time* he actually clapped, once, before Yuta stopped him.

"Oh, this is *great*," said Gojo. "This is the best thing that's happened since I got here."

"You got here three days ago," said Yuta.

"And this is the best thing." He leaned forward, blindfolded face turned toward the list. "Okay. Okay. Tell me everything. Who are these people? Which ones are good? Which ones are a disaster?"

"I'm not doing this."

"Yuta. You're on the list. With *Maki*. Who held a spear to my throat. Which, by the way, I'm still thinking about." He turned his head. "Is the elf one good? The elf one's good, right? The elf and Stole-All-Of-Mine? She sat on a floor for a whole day."

"Don't call him that," said Yuta.

"He doesn't mind."

"He minds."

Gojo turned his blindfolded face down the long table, toward Mau at the end of the bench, who was not minding, very visibly, and was trying not to laugh.

"See," said Gojo. "Doesn't mind." He turned back to the list. "And *temporary*. Who's temporary? I love temporary."

Halfway down the table, Maomao put her cup down very carefully and looked at Umaru's crayon with an expression that could have curdled milk.

"*Temporary*," said Maomao, "is accurate."

Beside her, Jinshi said nothing at all. But his ears, Mau noticed, had gone the colour of the vermilion columns at the gate.

Then Gojo stopped. He tilted his head at the list.

"Hey," he said. "Where am I?"

Umaru looked at him from the arch of the fort.

"You're not a couple," she said.

"Not *yet*," said Gojo.

There was a short pause at the long table.

Gojo did not say anything else. He did not explain it. He picked up his spoon again and went back to his soup. But his free hand, Mau saw, had drifted up to the high collar of his coat, where a small warm coin was tucked against his chest, and it rested there for a moment, through the cloth, before he took it away.



The first sign came at the end of dinner, and nobody understood it.

Senku was grinding a lens. He did it at the table sometimes, when he could not stop thinking about something, with a little stone wheel and a dish of grit and water, and nobody minded because the sound was soothing. He was making a new lens for the lantern, a better one. He had been grinding it for three days.

Mau, at the end of the bench, glanced at it. Just glanced. The way you glance at anything on a table.

"There's a crack," said Mau.

Senku looked up.

"In the lens," said Mau. He did not know why he was saying it. "On the left side. About two-thirds of the way in. Very small. It goes in at an angle." He frowned. "It's going to split when you heat it."

Senku looked at him for a long moment. Then he picked up the lens, and held it up to the lamp, and turned it, slowly, looking.

He looked for a long time.

"There's no crack," said Senku.

"Left side. Two-thirds in. At an angle."

Senku looked again. He turned the lens. He tilted it. He held it closer to the lamp.

His face changed.

"*How*," said Senku, very quietly, "did you see that from *there*?"

Down the table, someone laughed. It was a good laugh, a warm one. *Mau's got eagle eyes now.* And the long table laughed with it, and somebody said Gojo must have rubbed off on him, and Gojo said, loudly, that he would like it on record that he did not rub off on anybody, he was very clean. The common room laughed again, and moved on, and the noise of it came back up round them, the ordinary loud warm noise of a house at dinner.

Senku did not laugh.

He sat at the table with the lens in his hand, looking at the crack. It was real. Two-thirds in. At an angle. So fine that it had taken him half a minute at the lamp to find it, knowing exactly where to look.

Then he looked down the table at Mau.

At the left eye. Black and red. Moving, very slightly, very fast, even now, while Mau was laughing at something Kita had said.

Senku put the lens down.

Across the table, Gojo had stopped laughing too. He had not moved. His blindfolded face was turned toward Mau, and it was very still, and the hand at his collar had closed.

*That,* said Ciel, very quietly, inside Mau, where only he could hear, *was not you.*
