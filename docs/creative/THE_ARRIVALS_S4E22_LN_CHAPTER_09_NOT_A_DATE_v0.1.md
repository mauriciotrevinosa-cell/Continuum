## Chapter 15 — Not a Date

*When: Day 139, the third dating day, evening into the dawn of Day 140. The same day as the muddy south field and the birches.*

The five were not on the list.

Hakari noticed it on the evening of the decree, standing in front of the board with her hands on her hips, reading Umaru's crayon. She noticed it again on the morning of the first dating day, and the second. By the third, she had stopped noticing it and started being loud about it, which was how Hakari dealt with most things.

"We're not on it," said Hakari.

She said it in the big room, after lunch on the hundred-and-thirty-ninth day, standing in the middle of the floor between the five beds, with her hair still damp from the bath and her arms folded. The other four were where they always were. Karane on her bed with a book she was not reading. Nano at the little desk by the window, writing a schedule. Shizuka cross-legged on the rug under the sunk kotatsu, with her notebook in her lap. Kusuri in the corner with her glow jars, measuring something into a small glass dish.

"On what?" said Karane, not looking up.

"The list. The decree. *Couples (official).*" Hakari waved an arm at the wall, in the direction of the common room. "Everybody's on it. Mau and Frieren. Fern and Stark. *Maomao* and *Jinshi*. Even *temporary* is on it." She turned round in a slow circle. "We're not on it."

"We're not a couple," said Karane.

"I *know* we're not a couple," said Hakari. "There's *five* of us."

There was a short silence in the big room.

Shizuka wrote something in her notebook and turned it round. She did not speak, mostly. She wrote, and turned the notebook round, and the words she wrote were always very short and very exact.

*Does it apply to us?*

"That's my *point*," said Hakari. "Does it? Are we—" She stopped. She stood in the middle of the floor with her arms folded and her face, for once, uncertain. "What *are* we? Here?"

Nobody answered at once.

Because they all knew what she meant. They had known since the bridge. It was the thing none of them said.

In their world, there had been a boy. One boy. And there had been something called the click. A moment, the first time you saw him, when something in your chest went *click*, like a key in a lock, and you knew. Destiny, the god who did it called it. You were meant for him. You would love him. And all five of them had heard it, one after another, and all five of them had loved him. Because they were *meant* to.

He had not come over the bridge.

They had. All five of them. Without him.

And in this world, there was no god of love. There was a Goddess who did other things. And there was no click.

"Do you still hear it?" said Kusuri suddenly, from her corner.

Everyone looked at her.

"The click," said Kusuri. She did not look up from her glass dish. "When you think about him. Do you still hear it?" She tipped something into the dish, very carefully. "I've been wondering. For a long time. As a chemist." She paused. "I don't. I think of him and I miss him, and it's warm. But I don't hear the click." She looked up at last. "I think it stopped at the bridge. I think it was never ours. It was the god's."

The big room was very quiet.

Karane had put her book down.

"So what's left?" said Karane. Her voice was not quite a Karane voice. "If the click was the god's. What's left, that's ours?"

Nobody answered.



Nano solved it. Nano solved most things.

She did it at the little desk by the window, without turning round, after a long silence. She put down her pen, and picked up the schedule she had been writing, and turned it round so they could see.

It was a schedule for the evening. In her small exact hand.

*18:00. Dinner (common room).*
*19:30. Walk. North field. All five.*
*20:15. Stop at the red stake. Look at the gate. (Gojo says the light is "interesting".)*
*20:45. Return via the washing court.*
*21:00. Kotatsu. All five.*
*21:30. Unscheduled.*

"A five-person date," said Nano.

Everyone stared at her.

"The decree says every couple," said Nano, in her flat precise voice. "It does not say couples only. It says nothing about households." She turned back to the desk. "I have scheduled a household date. All five. I have checked the decree. There is no rule against it." She paused. "I have already filed it with the Ministry."

"You *filed* it?"

"At lunch." Nano wrote something else on the schedule. "Umaru stamped it. She said, and I am quoting, *the Ministry recognizes the Big Room as a household, and households may date*."

There was a long silence.

Then Hakari started to laugh. She sat down on the end of Karane's bed, because her legs had given out, and laughed until she had to wipe her eyes. And Karane, beside her, said *this is stupid*, and *this is the stupidest thing*, and then, very quietly, *nine o'clock kotatsu is fine*.

Shizuka wrote something in her notebook and turned it round.

*21:30. Unscheduled.*

She had underlined it.

Nano looked at the notebook for a long moment.

"Yes," said Nano. "That one is on purpose."



They went for the walk.

All five of them, in the evening, across the north field, along the line of Anko's stakes, in their coats. Hakari in front, loud, talking about nothing. Karane beside her, telling her to be quiet. Nano with the schedule. Shizuka beside Nano, close, with her notebook. Kusuri at the back with a lantern, looking at the sky.

At the red stake they stopped and looked at the gate, standing up out of the snow forty paces away, blue and humming. Gojo was right. The light was interesting. It moved, at the edges, like deep water.

Nobody said anything for a while.

Then Karane, without looking at anyone, put her hand out sideways, in the cold, and found Hakari's.

She did not say anything about it. She looked at the gate. Hakari looked at the gate. Neither of them looked at their hands.

Behind them, Nano was writing something on the schedule in the light of Kusuri's lantern. Shizuka was reading it over her shoulder. Shizuka took the pencil out of Nano's fingers, gently, and wrote something underneath. Nano read it, and went very still, and then, very carefully, folded the schedule in half and put it in her pocket. She did not look at Shizuka. But she stood closer to her for the rest of the walk.

Kusuri, at the back, with the lantern, watched all of it.

She did not say anything. She was a chemist. You did not interrupt an experiment while it was still running. You took notes.

She took notes.

*The click,* she wrote, in her own small notebook, by lantern light, at the red stake, *was the god's.*

*This,* she wrote underneath, after a long time, *might be ours.*

*Further observation required.*

At twenty-one hundred, they were in the kotatsu, all five, with their legs tangled under the quilt and the hot stones glowing. At twenty-one thirty, which was unscheduled, nobody moved.



The decree did not apply to Eren and Mikasa.

Mikasa noticed. She noticed on the evening it went up, reading the list. *Couples (official).* She noticed that her name was not on it, and that Eren's was not on it, and that this was correct, and that nobody had made a mistake.

She did not say anything.

She did not say anything for three days. She went into the gate with Maki and Yuta and came out. She walked the north field with Eren in the evenings, the way they always did. She noticed, on the second dating day, that he had noticed too. He had stood in front of the board for a long time with his arms folded and his face doing the thing it did when he was thinking about something he had decided not to think about.

On the night of the third dating day, in the room with two exits, in the dark, in the bunks, Eren spoke from the top bunk.

"The decree," he said.

Mikasa, on the bottom bunk, looked at the slats above her.

"It's for couples," said Eren.

"I know," said Mikasa.

"We're not."

"I know."

There was a long silence in the dark.

"Do you want to go for a walk?" said Eren. "Tomorrow. At dawn." A pause. "It's not a date."

Mikasa did not say anything for a moment.

"It's a walk," she said.

"It's a walk," Eren agreed.



They walked out to the red stake at dawn on the hundred-and-fortieth day, which was not a dating day, because the decree was over.

It was cold and clear. The gate stood up out of the last of the snow in the north field, blue and humming. Beyond it, the black hills went grey and then pink as the light came. Eren stood at the stake with his hands in his pockets and looked at the hills. Mikasa stood beside him and looked at the hills too.

Neither of them said anything for a long time.

"When this is over," said Eren.

Mikasa waited.

He did not finish. He stood at the stake and looked at the hills, and his jaw was set the way it set when he was holding something back.

"When what's over?" said Mikasa quietly.

"All of it." He did not look at her. "The road. The towns. The devil in the woods. Whatever's coming out of those hills." He paused. "When it's over, I—"

He stopped again.

Mikasa looked at him. At his profile against the pink sky. At the hands in his pockets, which were clenched. She thought about the thing he never said, and the reason he never said it. The reason he had carried with him from his own world, like a weight sewn into the lining of his coat. A number. A count. A clock nobody else in the house could hear, ticking down toward a day he had known about since before he came over the bridge.

"Say it," said Mikasa.

Eren did not look at her.

"Not yet," he said.

Mikasa did not push. She had never pushed. She stood beside him at the red stake and looked at the hills until the sun came up over them.

Then, on the way back, crossing the north field, she walked a little closer to him than she usually did. Her shoulder against his. Not holding. Just there.

He did not move away.

At the last cottage, Eren said, to the path, very quietly: "It wasn't a date."

"It was a walk," said Mikasa.

"It was a good walk," said Eren.

Mikasa did not say anything. But that afternoon, Anko, crossing the plaza, saw her standing alone at the north end, where she always stood for an hour, looking at the road. And Mikasa Ackerman, who did not smile where people could see, was smiling at the hills.
