*When: Day 141, the same morning, the same long table. Yuka's porridge has been sitting in front of her untouched for some minutes, because she keeps looking at her grandparents.*

Nobody saw him cross the room.

That was what they all agreed on afterward, at the long table, in the evening, with Fern writing it down. Nobody saw him get up. Nobody saw him move. One moment Mau was on the bench at the far end of the common room beside Frieren, with his eyes shut. The next moment he was not.

The next moment he was standing at the long table, beside Yuka's chair, very close, bent slightly forward, looking at the ring on her chest.

Yuka, who had a spoon of porridge halfway to her mouth, froze.

The common room froze.

He was very close. Closer than anyone had stood to her since she came through the door, except her grandparents. His face was perhaps a hand's breadth from her collarbone. He was not touching her. He was not touching anything. He was simply there, bent over her, with his eyes open, looking at the ring on its cord with an expression of total, absorbed, oblivious attention. The way Senku looked at a lens. The way Echidna looked at a book she had not read.

His eyes were blue. Dark blue. The blue of the sea a long way out. And they were moving, very slightly, very fast, reading the ring.

Yuka did not move.

She sat with the spoon in the air and looked at the stranger who had appeared out of nowhere a hand's breadth from her chest. He was staring at it as if it were the most fascinating thing in the world. She felt her face go hot. Completely. From the collar of her top to the roots of her hair, all at once, like a kettle.

"Uh," said Yuka.

He did not react.

"Uh, hi," said Yuka. Her voice came out higher than she meant. "I'm. Hi. Who—"

He did not react. He did not seem to hear her. He was looking at the ring. Only the ring. His hand had come up, slowly, as if of its own accord, and stopped in the air an inch from it, fingers half open, not quite touching.

Yuka looked past him.

And saw, at the far end of the common room, by the bench where he must have been sitting, an elf.

A small elf, with long white hair in two tails, in an orange hoodie that was much too big for her. She had stood up. She was not moving. She was looking down the length of the common room at Yuka, and at the stranger bent over Yuka, and at his hand an inch from the ring on Yuka's chest.

Her face was perfectly serene.

Yuka had grown up in a family of jujutsu sorcerers. She had faced curses since she was small. She had faced an alien from several hundred million light years away, alone, in Tokyo, and won. She knew, with the deep animal certainty of someone who had been in a great many fights, what she was looking at.

She was looking at the single most dangerous thing in the room.

And it was looking at her.

*Okay*, thought Yuka, very clearly. *Okay. If I say one wrong thing, I'm going to have to try and call Rika. And I have never once managed to call Rika. And I am going to die at breakfast on my first day in another world because of a ring.*

"Uh," said Yuka, to the stranger, very carefully, without taking her eyes off the elf. "Oppa?" Her voice cracked. "Oppa. *Stoppa.*"

Nobody at the long table had any idea what that meant.

The stranger did not react at all.



"Mau?" said Yuta.

He had half risen from his place across the table. His face was doing something very complicated.

Mau did not react.

"*Mau*," said Maki, more sharply.

Nothing. He stood bent over Yuka's chair with his hand an inch from the ring and his ocean eyes moving very fast, reading.

"Mau?" said Rimuru, from the hearth, very gently.

Nothing.

At the far end of the room, Frieren had not moved. She had not said anything. She stood by the bench with her hands at her sides and her face perfectly serene, looking down the length of the common room.

Then she said, quite quietly, in a voice that carried to every corner of the room: "*Mau.*"

He blinked.

He straightened up. He looked at his own hand, an inch from a stranger's chest, in the air. He looked at the ring. He looked at the stranger, a girl with dark hair and a face gone completely scarlet, holding a spoon of porridge in the air and staring past him at something behind him. He looked round. At the long table. At thirty faces looking back at him. At the bench at the far end of the room, where he had been sitting. Where he had been, he was quite sure, a moment ago.

"What am I doing here?" said Mau.

Nobody answered.

He took his hand back. Very fast. He stepped away from the girl's chair, two steps, three, and nearly fell over the end of the bench.

"I'm sorry," said Mau. "I'm so sorry. I don't—I was on the bench. I had my eyes shut. And then Ciel said open them, and I opened them, and—" He stopped. He looked at the girl. At the ring. "I'm sorry. I don't know you. I didn't mean to—I'm sorry."

Yuka stared at him.

She put the spoon down, very slowly, in the porridge.

"You," she said, "*teleported*."

"I don't know what I did."

"You teleported across a *room*," said Yuka, "to look at my *ring*."

"I don't—"

"And then you just *stood* there."

"I'm sorry—"

"And she," said Yuka, and pointed past him, down the length of the common room, very carefully, with one finger, "has been *looking* at me."

Mau turned round.

Frieren was standing by the bench at the far end, with her hands at her sides. When he turned, she tilted her head, very slightly, with her serene face, as if to say: *yes?*

Mau turned back to Yuka.

"That's Frieren," he said.

"I gathered," said Yuka faintly.

"She's—" Mau stopped. He did not know how to finish. He looked at the girl's scarlet face, and the ring on her chest, and the spoon in the porridge, and understood all at once exactly what it must have looked like, from here, with the elf watching. And he went scarlet too.

"*Oh*," said Mau. "No. No, no, I wasn't—it was the *ring*, I was only looking at the—"

"I *know* you were only looking at the ring," said Yuka. "*I* know that. Does *she* know that?"

There was a short pause.

At the far end of the room, Frieren sat back down on the bench. She picked up her tea. She drank some of it. She did not take her eyes off them.

Across the table, very slowly, Gojo put his face in his hands. His shoulders had started to shake.

"Oh, this is the *best*," said Gojo, muffled. "This is the best breakfast I've ever had."



Ciel told him afterward, in the yard, under the ink tree, with Rimuru beside him and Gojo standing a little way off with his arms folded. Mau heard her through the thread; Rimuru repeated the explanation aloud for Gojo.

*It was the ring,* said Ciel. *You are right. It was only the ring. But not the way you think.*

Mau sat on the bench round the trunk with his eyes shut. He had kept them shut since breakfast.

*You know the shells,* said Ciel. *The finished patterns that are missing something. The ones that cannot work until they get a piece they don't have.* A pause. *Copy is one of them. Yuta's technique. It is in you, finished, and it cannot work, because it needs Rika. It needs the ring.*

Mau did not say anything.

*There are two of the same ring in this settlement now,* said Ciel. *Yuta's. And the girl's. The same ring, from two different times.* Another pause. *The thing in you that is missing a piece saw the piece. Twice. In one room. And it reached.*

"Like the dog," said Rimuru quietly.

*Like the dog,* said Ciel. *In the Muslin. When his shadow became a hole. It was the same kind of thing. A shell, missing a piece, trying to supply it.* She was quiet for a moment. *That was the first dangerous leak. This is the same kind. Only this time, it did not leak. It moved him. Across the room. To the piece.* A pause. *I did not know it could do that.*

"The blink," said Mau. "The Noise. It did it before. In the battle. Before the Trial. I didn't know then either."

*Yes,* said Ciel. *But then you were reaching for a person, to save them. This time something in you reached for a requirement, and took you with it.* Her voice went quieter. *And it could see the requirement. With the new eyes. It could see exactly what the ring is. Every line. Every year of Yuta's cursed energy poured into it.* A pause. *Before, the shells only felt what they were missing. Now they can see it.*

Nobody said anything for a long moment.

"So it's going to keep reaching," said Rimuru.

*Yes.*

"For everything it's missing. Now that it can see everything."

*Yes,* said Ciel. *I think so. I think that is what the eyes have done. They have given every shell in him a way to look for its missing piece.* A pause, very quiet. *And some of the pieces are in this settlement.*

Gojo, a little way off, unfolded his arms.

"Like what?" he said.

There was a pause.

*The ring,* said Ciel. *A shadow medium. Ink. A yokai partner. Six Eyes.* A pause. *He has the eyes now. That one is supplied.* Another pause. *The others are still missing.*

Rimuru gave Gojo her list, including the last part.

"Ink," said Rimuru slowly. "There's an ink tree in the middle of the yard."

*Yes.*

"A shadow medium," said Gojo. "The girl."

Everyone turned to look at him.

Gojo was looking across the yard, toward the real house, where through the common-room window you could see the long table, and Yuka at it, between her grandparents, eating her porridge at last and talking with her mouth full, very loudly, to Milim.

"Zen'in blood," said Gojo. "On the grandmother's side. And the girl's shadow." He tilted his head. "Didn't you see it? At the table?" He looked back at Mau, on the bench, with his eyes shut. "Her shadow's like yours. Deep. Something lives in it." He was quiet for a moment. "The difference is, hers is a shadow. Yours is a door."

Mau sat very still.

*She is the Ten Shadows,* said Ciel, very quietly. *I felt it when she came in. I did not say. I wanted to be sure.* A pause. *She is the shadow medium. The thing your shadow has been reaching for since the Muslin.*

Nobody said anything.

Under the ink tree, with its great silver-green crown moving very slightly in a wind you could not feel, Mau sat with his eyes shut and his hands on his knees. He thought about a malformed black dog with one white ear, climbing out of a hole in his shadow in the dark, a long time ago, and Ciel saying *don't name it*.

He thought about a girl at the long table, with a shadow that was only a shadow, and a ring on a cord round her neck.

"I need to talk to her," he said.

"About the ring?" said Rimuru.

"About the dog," said Mau.
