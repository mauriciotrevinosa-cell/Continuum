# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 24
## Chapter 20 — A Door, Not a Shadow

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 24  
**Reading order:** 20

*When: Day 142, the morning after Yuka came. Gojo's lessons in not looking go on in the yard, two hours at a time, and are exactly as awful as he promised. Mau keeps his eyes shut between them.*

Yuka found him before he found her.

She came out into the yard on the hundred-and-forty-second morning, while he was sitting on the bench round the ink tree with his eyes shut between lessons. She came straight across the yard to him, the way her grandmother walked: as if the ground were in her way and should consider moving. She stopped in front of the bench, close, with her arms folded, and her ring on its cord outside her coat.

He knew she was there. He could not see her. He could feel her. Ever since the eyes, he could feel people when they came near, the way you feel heat from a stove. And she felt like a stove someone had turned all the way up.

"Teleport Oppa," said Yuka.

Mau opened his eyes. Carefully. Only to her face. Only the surface.

"That's not my name," he said.

"It is now." She did not unfold her arms. "You teleported across a room and stared at my ring in front of thirty people and your girlfriend, and then you said *what am I doing here*. You don't get a normal name after that." She tilted her head. "Grandpa says it wasn't you. He says it's something in you that wants the ring. Because of a shell." She frowned. "I don't know what a shell is."

"Neither do I," said Mau. "Not really."

Yuka looked at him for a long moment.

Then she sat down on the bench beside him. Not close. Not far. The way you sit beside someone you have decided not to hit yet.

"Gojo-san says your shadow's wrong," she said. "He said it at breakfast. After you left." She looked down at the ground in front of the bench, at his shadow on the bare earth of the root circle, thin and grey in the morning light. "He said mine's a shadow and yours is a door."

Mau did not say anything.

Yuka looked at his shadow for a long time.

"He's right," she said quietly.



She showed him hers first.

She did it there, under the ink tree, in the morning, with nobody watching except Rimuru, who had come out of the house with a cup of tea when he saw them on the bench and had stopped at a careful distance. And Gojo, who was standing in the door of the real house with his arms folded, not pretending he was not watching.

Yuka put her hands together in front of her. Fingers laced in a particular way. A shape Mau had never seen anyone make and knew, somehow, from underneath, from somewhere behind his breastbone, the way he knew the shape of a fast car.

Her shadow, on the bare earth, moved.

It did not open. That was the first thing he saw. It did not become a hole. It *deepened*. Like a pool when you look into it and realise it goes down further than you thought. And out of it, smoothly, the way something comes up out of still water, came a dog.

It was white. All white. Long-legged and lean, the size of a large wolf, with a ruff of white fur at its neck. It came up out of Yuka's shadow and stood beside her on the root circle, solid, real, with its ears up, looking at Mau with calm pale eyes.

It was the most beautiful thing he had seen since the sea inside Frieren.

"Divine Dog," said Yuka. Her voice had gone quiet and careful, the voice of someone handling something she loved. "White. There's supposed to be two. A white one and a black one. That's how the technique starts. You get the dogs first." She put her hand on the white dog's head, and it leaned into her palm. "I've only ever been able to call her. The black one won't come. I don't know why." She looked at Mau. "It's the Zen'in technique. My granny's family. It skips around. Generations. It landed on me." A pause. "Nobody at home knows I have it. Not even Tsurugi. Granny would've known. She'd have looked at my shadow when I was little and known." Her voice wobbled. "She was gone before it came out."

Mau looked at the white dog.

And far down inside him, something lifted its head.

Not the wheel. Something else. Something smaller and darker, curled at the bottom of the door in his shadow, that had been curled there since a night in the Muslin, a long time ago. It lifted its head at the sight of the white dog, the way a dog lifts its head when it hears another dog barking far away.

*Mau,* said Ciel, sharply.

His shadow, on the earth beside Yuka's, moved.

It did not deepen. It *tore*.



It was not much. That was the only mercy. A dark line, opening in the grey of his shadow on the bare earth, like a seam splitting. Black under it. Not darkness. Absence. The thing that lived at the bottom of a door.

And from the bottom of it, slowly, something began to climb.

Rimuru's teacup hit the ground. Gojo was off the doorstep. Mau did not move. He could not. He sat on the bench and watched it come up out of the seam in his shadow, the way he had watched it once before, in the dark, with Ciel saying *don't move, don't name it, don't shape your hands*.

A head. Black. Wrong. Too long in the jaw, the shape of it smeared, as if someone had tried to draw a dog from memory and kept rubbing it out. Eyes that were not quite eyes. One ear, black, laid flat. And the other ear, white. Up. Pricked. Listening.

It looked at the white dog standing beside Yuka.

The white dog looked back.

And Yuka, on the bench beside Mau, went completely still.

"Oh," she said, very quietly.

The black thing hung half out of the seam in his shadow, with its smeared jaw and its one white ear, looking at the white dog.

"That's both of them," said Yuka.

Mau did not understand.

"The dogs," said Yuka. She was not afraid. That was the strange thing. She was looking at the thing climbing out of his shadow with an expression he did not expect, which was something very close to pity. "There's supposed to be two. White and black. Two dogs. That's the start." She looked at the black thing's one white ear. "Your shadow didn't know how to make two. So it made one. Wrong. Both of them, smashed into one." She swallowed. "It's been trying to come out and be what it's supposed to be. And it can't. Because it's half of two things."

Gojo had stopped a few paces away. He was not moving any more. He was standing on the root circle with his hand half raised, looking at the black thing, and then at Yuka.

*Close the seam,* said Ciel. *Mau. Close it. Now. Don't let it look at the white one any longer, it is—*

"Don't," said Yuka.

Everyone looked at her.

"Don't close it," said Yuka. "Not like that. Not by pushing it back down." She stood up from the bench, slowly, with her hand still on the white dog's head. "That's what's been happening, isn't it? Since whenever. It tries to come out, and something pushes it back down, and it waits, and it tries again." She looked at the black thing. "That's not how you do it. That's never how you do it. You can't keep a shikigami in the dark and push it down every time it moves." Her voice was steady. "You have to let it come up. And then you have to beat it. Properly. On purpose. Before it's yours." She looked at Mau. "That's the rule. That's the whole technique. You tame them. You fight them, and you win, and then they're yours. And then they come when you call, and not when they want."

*It is too dangerous,* said Ciel. *His walls. The eyes. The wheel. If he fights it, Analyze will want to help, and if Analyze helps, the walls—*

"It's *more* dangerous if he doesn't," said Yuka.

She said it to the air, to nobody, because she could not hear Ciel. But she said it as if she knew exactly what Ciel had said. Maybe she did. Maybe it was on Mau's face.

"Look at it," said Yuka. "It's halfway out. On a bench. In the middle of breakfast. Because it saw my dog." She looked at the black thing with its one white ear. "And now he's got eyes that see everything. It's going to see more. It's going to keep coming up. In the night. At the table. When he's asleep." She looked at Mau. "You can tame it on purpose, with people round you, in a field, when you choose. Or you can wait until it comes up on its own, wherever it wants, whenever it wants." She paused. "That's the choice. That's always the choice."

Nobody said anything.

The black thing hung half out of the seam in Mau's shadow, looking at the white dog. Its white ear twitched.

Gojo, a few paces away, lowered his hand.

"She's right," said Gojo quietly.

Rimuru turned to look at him.

"The kid's right," said Gojo. "I've seen this. In my world. Zen'in kids who got the Ten Shadows and were too scared to tame anything, or weren't allowed to. Their shadows got worse every year." He did not take his eyes off the black thing. "One of them had a shikigami come up on its own in the middle of a family dinner. They lost half the house." He was quiet for a moment. "You don't keep them down. You can't. You choose when."

*The walls are tired,* said Ciel, very quietly. *From the fight. From the eyes. If he does this, they will be more tired.*

Rimuru repeated it aloud. His voice was not steady.

Gojo looked at the black thing for a long moment.

"Then we do it with everyone there," he said. "Tomorrow. In the field. With every guardrail we've got. With the slime holding the walls and me standing there and the spear girl and the dragon and the ring." He looked at Yuka. "And her. Because she's the only one who knows how."

Yuka looked at him. Then at Mau.

"Okay?" she said.

Mau looked at the black thing climbing out of his shadow. At its smeared jaw. At its one white ear, up, listening. At the way it was looking at the white dog. Not angry. Not hungry. Something else. The way you look through a window at something you were supposed to be and never got to.

"Okay," said Mau.

He looked at the black thing.

"Go back down," he said to it. Quietly. Not pushing. Asking. "Tomorrow. I'll come and get you tomorrow. On purpose."

The black thing looked at him with its eyes that were not quite eyes.

Then, very slowly, it sank. Back down into the seam in his shadow. Not pushed. Going. Its white ear the last thing to disappear, up, listening, until the seam closed over it and his shadow on the bare earth was only grey again, thin, ordinary.

Yuka let her breath out.

Beside her, the white dog lay down on the root circle and put its head on its paws.

"It listened," said Yuka. She sounded surprised. "It actually listened." She looked at Mau. "Nobody's ever asked it before. Have they?"

Mau did not answer.

At the door of the real house, Frieren was standing. Mau did not know how long she had been there. She had her arms folded inside the sleeves of the orange hoodie, and her face was perfectly serene. But her eyes had not left his shadow the whole time.

*Tomorrow,* said Ciel, very quietly. *I will hold everything I can.*
