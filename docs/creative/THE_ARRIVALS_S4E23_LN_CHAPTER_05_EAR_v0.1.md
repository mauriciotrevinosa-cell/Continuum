*When: Day 143, the south field, at noon. The fort has been told to stay inside; the fort is at every window. Ciel has been holding everything she can since dawn.*

They did it in the south field, because that was where things were done now.

The house had decided it at the long table the night before, with Fern writing. It had taken three hours. The witches had come, uninvited, and sat at their end and listened. At the end of three hours Pride had unfolded her hands and laid them flat on the table and said, *We will stand at the edge. All seven. If something comes out that is not a dog, there will be seven of us between it and your children.* And Fern had looked at her for a long moment, and written it down.

So at noon on the hundred-and-forty-third day there was a circle in the south field.

Not a drawn one. A circle of people. Rimuru at the north point, in human shape, with his eyes closed and his hands loose at his sides, holding the walls. Ciel was inside him and inside Mau at once, the way she was, holding everything she could. Gojo at the east, with his blindfold on and his hands in his pockets and his whole body very still. Maki at the south, with her spear. Yuta at the west, with the ring on his hand, and Rika's presence round him like weather. Milim behind Mau, as Guardian, with her arms folded and her face, for once, perfectly serious.

And outside them, at the edge of the field, in a loose wide ring: the witches. All seven. Standing in the thin grass in their strange robes, with Sekhmet held upright between Minerva and Daphne. None of them speaking.

Frieren stood with Yuka, inside the circle, near Mau. She had not asked. Nobody had told her she could not.

Mau stood in the middle of the patch of dead earth where nothing would grow for a while.

"Okay," said Yuka. She stood a few paces away from him, with the white dog at her side. "Here's how it goes."



"You open the door," said Yuka. "Not all the way. Just enough. Like yesterday. You let it come up." She was speaking very steadily, the way her grandmother spoke, and only her hand on the white dog's head was gripping a little too hard. "And then it's going to fight you. Because that's what they do. It doesn't know you're its person yet. It thinks you're the thing that's been keeping it in the dark."

"I have been," said Mau.

"Not on purpose," said Yuka. "But yes." She looked at him. "So you have to beat it. Not kill it. Beat it. Make it stop. Make it lie down. Make it understand you're stronger than it, and that being yours is better than being in the dark." She paused. "And here's the important part."

Mau waited.

"You don't fight it with your body," said Yuka. "Not really. Not with your fists. You fight it with your shadow. With the thing that holds it." She pointed at his shadow on the dead earth. "Your shadow's where it lives. Your shadow's what has to win. If you try to win with—" She looked at Gojo. At Rimuru. At the circle round them. "With anything else you've got. With anything big. With Infinity or the wheel or whatever else is in there." She swallowed. "Then it's not a taming. Then it's just a fight. And it'll never be yours."

*She is right,* said Ciel, very quietly. *And that is the danger. Everything else in you is going to want to help.*

"Small," said Mau.

"Small," said Yuka. "Just you and your shadow and the dog." She looked at the circle again. "And if it goes wrong—"

"Then we're here," said Gojo, from the east point, without moving.

Yuka looked at him. Then back at Mau.

"Ready?" she said.

Mau looked down at his shadow.

"No," he said. "But I said I'd come and get it."



He opened the door.

It was like yesterday. A seam, splitting, in the grey of his shadow on the dead earth. Black under it. Not darkness. Absence. And out of the bottom of it, faster this time, because it had been waiting, because he had said *tomorrow* and it had listened, the black thing came.

It came all the way out.

It was big. Bigger than Yuka's white dog. Bigger than a wolf. The size of a pony, black and wrong, the shape of it smeared at the edges as if the world could not quite decide where it ended. Its jaw was too long. Its eyes were not quite eyes. One ear was black and laid flat against its skull. The other was white, and up, and listening.

It stood on the dead earth in the middle of the circle and looked at Mau.

And it lunged.

He did not move out of the way. Yuka had said: *your shadow has to win*. So he did not use his feet. He stood on the dead earth, and when the black thing came at him, he did not reach for anything in himself, not the Infinity in its box, not the wheel, not the slime that had swallowed Gojo's Red. He reached for his shadow. The grey thin ordinary shape on the earth under his feet. He *held* it.

The black thing hit him.

It did not hit his body. It hit his shadow. Its smeared jaw closed on the grey shape on the earth, and Mau felt it, all through him, like teeth in a part of himself he had never known he could feel. He staggered. He did not fall. He held on.

The black thing shook its head, the way a dog shakes a rope, with his shadow in its jaw.

And everything in him woke up.

He felt it happen. All at once. The way he had felt it in the south field ten days ago, when the guardrails lifted. Everything behind his breastbone, everything behind the walls, every shell and every half-built thing and every blueprint, lifted its head at once. Because something was hurting him. Because something had its teeth in him. Because it wanted to *help*.

The Infinity stirred in its box. The wheel turned, once, in his shadow, under the black thing's jaw. *Click.*

The ground under the circle trembled.

*The walls,* said Ciel. Her voice was very tight. *Mau. The walls are shaking. Analyze is trying to help. It wants to give you something. It wants to give you everything. Don't let it. Don't take it. Don't—*

At the north point, Rimuru's face had gone grey. His hands, at his sides, were shaking. The seal was ringing through him like a bell struck too hard.

At the edge of the field, the seven witches had stepped forward. One step. All of them at once.

Mau stood on the dead earth with the black thing's teeth in his shadow and everything in him begging to be let out, to help, to win this for him. The Infinity. The Void. The wheel. All of it so close. All of it so easy. He could end this in one breath. He could take the black thing apart with something he had stolen from someone else and stand there afterward with nothing in his shadow at all.

*Small*, Yuka had said. *Just you and your shadow and the dog.*

He did not take it.

He let everything in him beg. He let the walls shake. He let Rimuru go grey and the witches step forward and the wheel turn once more under the black thing's jaw. *Click.* He did not take any of it.

He held his shadow.

Just his shadow. The grey thin ordinary shape on the dead earth. The thing that had been a door since the Muslin. He held it the way you hold a rope someone is pulling at the other end. Not to pull back. To not let go.

The black thing pulled.

He did not let go.

It pulled harder. It shook his shadow in its jaw until he was on his knees on the dead earth, until there was blood in his mouth again, until his hands were flat on the ground and shaking so hard that the earth under them was shaking too. Or the earth was shaking on its own, from the walls, from the seal ringing through Rimuru like a bell. Nobody could tell.

He did not let go.

And then, very slowly, the black thing stopped pulling.

It did not let go of his shadow. It stood there on the dead earth with its smeared jaw still closed on the grey shape, and looked at him. On his knees. Bleeding at the mouth. Shaking. Not letting go. Not taking anything. Not using anything. Just holding.

It looked at him for a long time, with its eyes that were not quite eyes.

Its white ear twitched.

Mau lifted one shaking hand off the ground. Slowly. He did not reach for anything sealed. He reached for the black thing. For its head. Between the ears. The black one, flat. The white one, up.

He put his hand there.

Just that. His palm flat on its head. The way Frieren had put her hand over his eyes on the riverbank. The way Mai had laid one finger against the edge of his hand on a long table under a lamp.

"I'm here," said Mau. "I came. I said I would."

The black thing stood very still under his hand.

Then it let go of his shadow.

It stood there a moment longer, black and too large and wrong at the edges. Then, slowly, it folded its legs, and lay down on the dead earth in front of him. It put its smeared too-long head on its paws. Its white ear stayed up, listening. Its black ear lay flat. Its eyes that were not quite eyes closed.

The ground stopped shaking.



Nobody in the circle moved.

At the north point, Rimuru sat down in the grass very suddenly, as if someone had cut his strings. His face was grey and wet. He did not say anything. He sat there with his hands flat on the ground, breathing.

At the east point, Gojo had not moved the whole time. He did not move now. But under the blindfold, Mau could feel it, his face had changed.

At the edge of the field, the seven witches stepped back. One step. All at once. Pride's hands, at her sides, unclenched.

Yuka was standing a few paces away, with the white dog pressed against her legs, and both her hands over her mouth.

"You didn't use anything," she said, through her fingers. "You had all of it right there. I could see it. Everything. Wanting out." She lowered her hands. "And you didn't use any of it. You just held on."

Mau did not answer. He was on his knees on the dead earth with his hand on the black thing's head and blood on his chin and his whole body shaking. He did not have anything left to answer with.

*The walls are holding,* said Ciel. Very quietly. Very tired. *They held. Just.* A pause. *But they are tired, Mau. They are very tired.*

And then Frieren walked into the middle of the circle.

Nobody stopped her. Nobody could have. She walked across the dead earth in the orange hoodie with her hands in the front pocket, past Yuka, past the white dog. She stopped beside Mau, kneeling, with his hand on the black thing's head.

She looked down at the black thing for a long time.

It opened its eyes. They were not quite eyes. It looked up at her from where it lay with its head on its paws, at the small white-haired elf standing over it, and its white ear turned toward her, listening.

Frieren looked at the ear.

"Ear," said Frieren.

There was a pause.

"...Ear?" said Mau.

"It has one," said Frieren. She tilted her head at the black thing, at its one white ear, up, listening, and its other black one, flat. "It's the ear it listens with." She looked down at Mau, kneeling beside it with his hand on its head. "And it listened to you. When you asked it to go back down. When you came to get it." She paused. "So. Ear."

Mau looked at her.

She was standing over him in the south field in the orange hoodie, in the thin noon light, with her serene face. She had just given a name to the thing Ciel had told him, a long time ago, in the dark, in the Muslin, never to name. She had given it a name the same way she had given him his, on the first morning of his life, in a clearing, reading letters off the back of a shirt. By looking at what was there and saying it out loud.

*Maybe your name is Mau.*

His throat closed.

"I like it," said Mau.

Frieren's mouth moved, very slightly, at one corner. She did not say anything else.

Under his hand, the black thing that was not quite a dog let out a long slow breath, the way a dog does when it finally lies down somewhere it is allowed to be. And its white ear, up, listening, turned toward the sound of its name.

Ear.



They carried him back to the house. Stark carried him, because Frieren would not let anyone with less steady arms do it.

Ear went back down into his shadow before they reached the fence. Not pushed. Going. Like a dog going to its bed. And when Mau looked down at his shadow on the path, once, between Stark's arms, it was not a seam and it was not a hole and it was not quite a door any more. It was a shadow. With something in it. Listening.

He slept for a day and a half.

*I am here,* said Ciel, very quietly, all through it. *I am holding them. They are holding.*

And then, more quietly still, so quietly that even Mau, asleep, almost did not hear:

*But they are so tired.*
