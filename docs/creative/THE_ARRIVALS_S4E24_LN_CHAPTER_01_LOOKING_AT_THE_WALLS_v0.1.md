# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 24
## Chapter 22 — Looking at the Walls

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1  
**Volume:** 24  
**Reading order:** 22

*When: Day 145, a day and a half after the taming. Ear is asleep in Mau's shadow; the walls are holding, and tired; the gate is in its sixth week. Gojo has not slept since the south field.*

He woke at the reset hour, the way he always woke.

The ceiling was the ceiling. Clay, washed white, with a beam across it. The east window was grey with the snow-light. Frieren's hair was across his arm. Her cold foot was against his leg under the blanket. She was awake, he could feel it, the way he felt everyone now, like heat from a stove. She was lying very still beside him, waiting to say *one*.

He opened his eyes.

And they looked.

Not at the ceiling. Not at the window, or at Frieren, or at the room. He had learned, over their last few lessons with Gojo, how to keep them off things. How to look at the grain of a table and only the grain. How to keep his eyes on the surface of a face and not go through. He had been getting better. Gojo had said so, grudgingly, at their last lesson. *You're terrible. You're less terrible than Thursday.*

But he had been asleep for a day and a half. And while he was asleep, nobody had been holding his eyes on anything.

So they had turned round.

They had gone on reading while he slept. The room. The beam. The weight of Frieren's mana beside him. The same small things, deeper each time, without anyone choosing where to stop.

They had not exhausted the world. They had found something nearer.

The joins between what he felt and what he was allowed to feel.

So when he woke, and opened them, they did not look out.

They looked in.



It was like turning round in a dark room and finding a wall you had always known was there, and seeing it, for the first time, lit.

He saw the walls.

The guardrails. The seal. The thing Rimuru and Ciel had built round everything he carried, after the containment, at the old inn, a long time ago, and rebuilt, and rebuilt, and held every day since. He had felt them. He had always felt them. A pressure. A shape. A weight he carried the way you carry a heavy coat.

He had never seen them.

He saw them now. All of them. Every line. Every join. Every place where Ciel had folded something shut and Rimuru had pinned it. Every place where they had been mended after the plaza, after the reversion night, after the south field. Every place where they had been mended after the taming, a day and a half ago, very fast, by someone very tired.

They were beautiful. That was the first thing he thought. Lying on his back in the grey light, with Frieren's foot against his leg. They were the most beautiful made thing he had ever seen. More than the frame. More than the ink tree. Someone had made them for him out of love and exhaustion, every day for months, and he had never once seen what they looked like.

The second thing he thought was: *they're so thin.*

Here. And here. And here, by the join Ciel had made after the south field, where Analyze had lifted them on purpose, for a moment, and set them down again not quite straight. And here, very fresh, from a day and a half ago, from the dog's teeth in his shadow, where everything in him had begged to help and pushed and pushed against the walls from inside. And the walls had held. Just.

They were holding. He could see them holding.

He could see exactly how.

And far down, behind his breastbone, under all of it, The Noise read what he saw.



*Mau,* said Ciel.

He had never heard her voice like that. Not in the plaza. Not in the south field. Not on the reversion night. It was very quiet and very precise, the way it always was. And underneath, for the first time, it was afraid.

*Mau. Close your eyes.*

He tried.

They did not close.

*Mau. They are reading the walls. The eyes are reading the walls. Close them. Close them now.*

He could not. He lay on his back with his eyes open, looking at nothing, looking in, and his eyelids would not come down. It was not that he did not want them to. It was that the thing in him that was looking did not want to stop. And it was much, much stronger than he was.

Beside him, Frieren had gone completely still.

"Mau," she said. "Your eyes."

At first he could not turn his head to her. He could feel her, the heat of her, propped up on one elbow now, looking down at his face. He could feel her hand come up, toward his eyes, to cover them, the way she had on the riverbank.

It stopped an inch from his face.

It did not touch. It could not. It went slower and slower and slower toward his eyes, an inch away, half an inch, and never arrived.

*Infinity,* said Ciel. *It's open. Something opened it. Mau—*

The wheel turned.

He felt it. In his shadow, on the sheet under him, under the bed, under the floor. Once. Not in answer to anything. Not at a threat. At the walls. Because the eyes had read the walls, every line, every join, every thin place, and what the eyes read reached the wheel. And the wheel took walls the way it had taken them on the reversion night. As an injury. As something done to him. Something to adapt to. To get past.

*Click.*

The east window cracked.

Not broke. Cracked. A single line, straight across Senku's glass, from one corner to the other, with a sound like ice on a pond.



Frieren was out of the bed.

He heard her move. He could not see her. His eyes were open and looking in, at the walls, at the wheel turning under them. He could hear her on the floor, on her feet, and her voice, very loud, louder than he had ever heard it.

"*Rimuru.*"

Something moved above the closet. Rimuru had woken in the loft, inside their room. Frieren released the latch at the foot of its ladder. Mau heard the ladder creak, and felt someone small and blue come down very fast, already reaching, taking human shape as he reached the floor.

*The eyes are reading the walls,* said Ciel, to Rimuru, to both of them, at once. *They have read all of it. Every join. Every thin place. The wheel has them. It is turning. At the walls. It thinks the walls are hurting him.* A pause, very fast. *It is going to adapt to them. All of them. At once.*

"Ring it," said Rimuru. His voice was perfectly calm. That was how Mau knew how frightened he was. "Frieren. The bell. Three long, one short. Now. Everyone out."

Frieren did not move.

"*Frieren.*"

"I'm not leaving him," said Frieren.

"You're not leaving him. You're ringing the bell. Then you're getting the children out. Then you come back to the line and you wait." Rimuru's voice did not change. "That's the protocol. You promised him."

*Click.*

The shelf in the middle of the floor went over.

It went over all at once, as if something had pushed it. Mau heard it hit the boards. The grimoire on it, with the four older pressed flowers between its pages, and his comb, and the cup. All of it scattering across the floor. The fifth flower, the one from their date, was still in Frieren's hoodie pocket. Five altogether. He knew where she had kept them.

And on the east windowsill, under the cracked glass, on Eren's stands, the two cars rocked. The red and white one and the red one, with Ori's grey stone between them. They rocked on their stands. And he heard, from very far away, the small sharp sound of something tipping over.

"*Frieren,*" said Rimuru.

Frieren went.

He heard her go. Fast. Out of the door, down the corridor. And then, a moment later, from the tower over Arrival House, across the yard and the plaza, the bell. Three long strokes and one short. Again. Again. The seal pattern. The one every person in the settlement had learned in the plaza, a long time ago, and hoped never to hear again.



He heard the bell. He tried to move toward it.

For a moment only his right knee moved. It dragged against the sheet. He tried again, not with his eyes, not with anything behind the walls. With the leg that had walked this house every day.

His foot went over the edge of the bed.

Rimuru turned toward him. "Mau."

"Out," said Mau. It was hardly a word. "Need. Out."

He could not make the looking stop. He could still make his body do that much.

The other foot found the boards. He sat up crookedly, with the sheet caught around his shoulders and one hand gripping the mattress. For a few seconds he could do nothing else. Somewhere inside, a seam was being taken apart while Ciel tried to close it.

Then he stood.

His knees nearly folded. He caught himself against the bed with his own hand. Rimuru moved beside him, keeping the walls held, not trying to spend the same strength carrying his body.

Gojo was in the doorway.

He reached toward Mau. His hand slowed before it could get to his shoulder. He stopped reaching.

"Okay," said Gojo. "One step. Toward my voice."

Mau took it.

He could not look at the doorway without looking through it. He knew where it was anyway. He knew where the shelf lay because he had heard it fall. He went around it, badly, with the sheet trailing from him and his feet barely leaving the boards.

At the top of the main stair he stopped.

"Here," said Gojo, one tread below him. "Stay with this step."

Mau bent his knees. One foot down. Then the other. He had to stop again at the turn. His breathing sounded too loud. Gojo stayed below him; Rimuru stayed beside him. Neither told him to hurry.

The bell did not stop.

By the last tread he was bent almost double. He crossed the common room without straightening, past the long table, toward the cold light in the open door.

"Not here," he said. He did not know whether anyone heard him.

Gojo did.

"Not the house," he said. "We're getting you out of the house."

Mau crossed the threshold under the vermilion columns. His bare foot came down outside, in the cold. Then the other.

He had made it out.

He tried to take another step. His legs gave way.

Gojo caught the loose sheet and drew it taut beneath him, keeping his hands outside the slowing space. It had stayed against Mau all the way down. He lifted him in it, the way Rika had held him in the south field. Not one Infinity cancelling another. A man using the bedding because he still could not reach the man inside it.

"I've got you," said Gojo, through his teeth. "Enough walking."

Behind them, the house was standing. The front door was open. No one had to climb through wreckage to follow.

The bell went on ringing.

Through the eyes, still open, still looking in, Mau saw nothing of the yard. He saw the walls. Thinner. He saw the wheel. Turning. *Click.* He felt, from very far away, the ground under Gojo's feet tremble as they crossed the yard. He felt the ink tree's roots, under the root circle, shudder. He felt the whole settlement wake up at once, to the bell. Every heat. Every person. All of them, all at once, every one of them, running.

He felt the fort, in a body, in hoods, going out through the north door of the real house toward the refuge cellars, exactly the way Umaru had drilled them. Thirty of them. He could count them. He did not want to count them. He counted them.

He felt Frieren, at the bottom of the main stair, with a child in her arms. Ori. Holding something in her own fists, two small cars and a grey stone, pressed against her chest. Being carried out of the house by an elf who was not looking back.

He felt the witches, all seven, coming out of the north wing into the dawn. Not running. Walking. Toward the south field. Toward where Gojo was carrying him.

He felt Rimuru, beside Gojo, running, with his hands out, holding the walls with everything he had.

*Click.*

"South field," said Gojo. "Open ground. Away from the cellars. Away from everyone." He was running now. "The slime holds the walls. The ring kid and the dragon at the line. Everybody else behind the witches." He looked down at Mau in his arms, at the ocean eyes, open, looking at nothing. "And you hold on. You hear me? You hold on."

Mau heard him.

He could not answer.

Because the wheel was turning, and turning, faster than it had ever turned. Not in the south field. Not in the plaza. Faster. And far down, under the walls, under everything, behind every shell and half-built thing and blueprint he had ever carried, something that did not have a name had started, very slowly, to wake.

And Ciel, inside him, very quietly, said the thing she had never once said in all the time he had known her.

*I don't know if I can hold this.*

Inside the walls, Mau tried to say *stop*.

Analyze was still working. Not waiting its turn behind the finished things. Working on what the eyes were showing it, comparing a repaired join to a remembered shape, trying another construction before the first had failed.

Ciel closed a seam. A different pressure found its edge. She closed that too, and felt the first seam change beneath her.

*It isn't only escaping,* she told Rimuru. *It's building while I hold it.*

Mau could feel the work. He could not make it his work. He could not tell it which part of him it was allowed to spend.

He tried to remember Frieren's foot against his leg. Only that. Cold. Real. Before the bell.

For a moment he could not remember which leg.
