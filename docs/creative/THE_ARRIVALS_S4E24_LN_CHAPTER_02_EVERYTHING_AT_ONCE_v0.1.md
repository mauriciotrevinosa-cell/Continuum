## Chapter 23 — Everything at Once

*When: Day 145, dawn, the south field. The bell has been ringing the seal pattern for four minutes. The fort is in the refuge cellars; the witches are walking toward the field.*

Frieren stood at the line.

There was no line, not really. There was the fence at the top of the slope, where the house had stood and watched the shrine and two Voids open in the snow, twelve days ago. There was the place Rimuru had pointed at, running past her with his hands out, and said *here, no closer, nobody closer than here*. And there was Milim's hand round her wrist.

"You promised him," said Milim.

Frieren did not answer.

"In the plaza," said Milim. She did not let go. Her small hand was round Frieren's wrist like an iron band, and her face, for once, was not bright at all. "You promised him you'd go out with everyone. And I promised you I'd hold you if you tried to go back." Her voice was very steady. "So I'm holding you."

Frieren stood at the fence with her wrist in Milim's hand and looked down the slope at the south field.

Ori was behind her, in the refuge cellars, with the fort. Frieren had carried her there herself. She had put her down at the bottom of the cellar stair with her two small cars and her grey stone clutched to her chest, and Umaru's arms round her, and turned round and run. She had not looked back. She had not been able to look back. And now she was at the fence, with Milim holding her wrist, and she was looking at the only thing in either world she could not stop looking at.

Gojo had laid him down in the middle of the field.

On the patch of dead earth, where nothing would grow for a while. Where Maki had laughed. Where, two days ago, a black dog with one white ear had lain down in front of him and been given a name.

He was lying on his back, on the sheet Gojo had carried him out in. He was not moving. His eyes were open. From the fence Frieren could see them, even at this distance, even in the grey dawn: blue. The blue of the sea a long way out. Looking straight up at the sky. Looking at nothing.

Gojo was kneeling beside him.

Rimuru was standing over both of them, with his hands out flat in the air above Mau's chest, the way you hold your hands over a fire. His whole small body was shaking.

And above Mau, in the air, turning, was the wheel.



It came up out of his shadow while she watched.

It climbed into the air over the field, pale and enormous, eight spokes. It was bigger than in the plaza. Bigger than twelve days ago, when it had hung over his head like a crown and Gojo had said *that's how he killed me*. It went up and up, until it hung over the whole south field like a second sun that gave no light, turning.

*Click.*

She felt it. Everyone at the fence felt it. A sound that was not a sound, that went through the ground and up through the soles of their feet and into their teeth.

"The walls," said Yuta, beside her. He had come up the slope a moment ago with Rika round him and Yuka at his side and the white dog pressed against Yuka's legs. His voice was barely there. "That's a wall. Going."

*Click.*

"Another one," said Yuta.

Frieren did not say anything. She watched.

And then the first thing came out.



It was Blue.

A small blue light, rising out of Mau's chest like a bubble out of water. It floated up into the grey air over the field, perfectly round, pulling. Frieren could see it pulling. The snow at the edges of the field began to slide toward it. Blades of last year's grass bent toward it. Then Red came, out of the same place, and rose beside it. And the two of them began, very slowly, to orbit.

Not touching. Never touching. Two lights going round each other over Mau's chest, blue and red, so close that from the fence they blurred into violet.

"He's not doing that," said Yuta. "In the plaza he did that on purpose. To keep them apart." He swallowed. "He's not doing it now. It's just *happening*."

Then the shrine.

It came up out of the earth to the left of Mau, all at once, the way it had in the south field twelve days ago. Bone and teeth and dark cut stone. And with it came the sound: a thousand knives drawn at once, everywhere, all over the field. The snow at the edges of the field did not slide any more. It came apart. Cut into finer and finer pieces, until it was dust, until it was nothing. The fence posts at the bottom of the slope, the ones Yuta and Maki had leaned against in the mud, split. One after another. Down the middle. Without a sound.

"*Back,*" said Yuta. "Everybody back. Further."

Nobody moved back. Nobody could.

Because then the Void opened.

Not round Mau. Round nothing. A black sphere, perfect, the size of a house, in the middle of the field. And then it was not there. And then it was, somewhere else, further toward the river. And then gone. And then back. Flickering. Opening and closing. Like an eye blinking. Like something that did not know where it was supposed to be.

And the snow over the field, all of it, every flake in the air, stopped.

Stopped falling. Hung. Infinity. The air over the whole south field had begun, very gently, to slow everything down forever.

*Click.*

At the fence, Frieren did not breathe.

Because that was the finished things. The things in him that were whole. She had seen those before, in the plaza. She knew those.

And now the walls were going. And behind the finished things were the other things.



They did not come out right.

That was what she would remember, afterward, at the reset hour, with her foot against his shin. Not the Void. Not the shrine. Not the wheel. The things that came after. The things that came out wrong.

A circle of light drew itself in the air over Mau's knees. Lines and curves, very fine, very precise, the shape of a glyph. Coco's kind of glyph. Witch Hat. Drawn in light instead of ink. It got halfway. Two-thirds. And then it stopped, because there was no ink, because it had never had ink, because that was the piece it was missing. It hung there unfinished in the air and *sparked*. Hissed. Spat little flashes of white like a wire too close to water. And then it broke, and drew itself again, and got two-thirds, and broke.

Over and over. Like someone trying to remember a word.

A hand came up out of the earth beside Mau's shoulder. Huge. Pale. Long-fingered. Half of a hand. The shape of Rika's. It reached up out of the ground toward the sky and closed on nothing and opened and reached again, and there was no arm behind it, no Rika, nothing but the hand. And at the fence, Rika, round Yuta, made a sound like a scream with no air in it. And Yuta put both hands over the ring on his finger and held on.

"He's reaching for her," said Yuta, very fast. "For Rika. For the ring. Copy. It needs her. It's—" He stopped. "It's reaching for *her*."

The air in a line across the bottom of the field tore. Not cut. *Tore.* A long rip, as if something had run through it faster than anything should run, and the air had not had time to get out of the way. Then another rip. Then another. Something running, and running, with nothing running.

On the far side of the fence, Okarun made a sound. He had his hand clamped over the pocket of his coat, where a small cat-shaped body lived. And from inside the pocket, very small, very frightened, Turbo Granny's voice said: *That's mine. That's my speed. That's—it's looking for me. It's looking for ME.*

*Click.*

And then the light came.



Frieren did not have a word for it. Nobody did. She tried, afterward, many times, and could not.

It came up out of Mau like water out of a spring. Pale light. Not white. Not any colour. Currents of it. Rivers. Rising out of his chest and his hands and his open eyes, branching, splitting, finer and finer, the way the veins had branched in the flowers on the riverbank. Rising into the air over the field and spreading out under the turning wheel like roots under a tree, like a tree growing upside down into the sky.

And in the currents, there were shapes.

Not things. Shapes. Things that had never been finished. Things that had never been started. Things that were only ideas, only blueprints, only the faintest outline of something that might one day be something. They formed in the light, and came apart, and formed again into something else. A shape like a wing that was not a wing. A shape like a door. A shape like a face that turned toward the fence and had no face. A shape like a sound. A shape like nothing she had ever seen in a thousand years, which turned slowly in the light over the south field and then folded up into itself and was gone.

The currents of light reached out across the field.

Not at random. Frieren saw it, and her whole body went cold. They were *reaching*. Some of them toward the fence. Toward Yuta's ring. Toward Okarun's pocket. Toward Yuka, whose shadow had gone deep and dark under her feet, and whose white dog was howling. And some of them, long and pale and branching, out past the fence, past the slope, toward the settlement.

Toward the yard.

Toward the ink tree.

"*No,*" said Yuka. She was staring at the light reaching toward her. Her white dog stood in front of her with its hackles up and its teeth bared, howling at the light. "No, no, no—what *is* that, what is it, what does it *want*—"

"Everything it doesn't have," said Gojo.

He was standing up. He had been kneeling beside Mau in the middle of the field and now he was standing, in the hanging snow, with the wheel turning over him and the currents of light reaching past him, and he was looking up.

His voice carried. It carried the way voices carry over snow. Very quietly.

"It can see everything it's missing," said Gojo. "Every piece. The eyes showed it. And now there's nothing stopping it from going to get them."

And down in the middle of the field, beside Mau, something black came up out of his shadow.

Ear.

It came up on its own. Nobody called it. It came up out of the shadow under Mau the way a dog comes out from under a bed when there is a noise in the night. Black and too large and wrong at the edges, with its smeared jaw and its one white ear. It stood over Mau on the dead earth, with its four legs planted on either side of him, and faced outward, toward the light, toward the shapes, toward the wheel.

And it growled.

At the fence, Yuka made a sound that was half a sob.

"It's guarding him," she said. "He tamed it two days ago and it's *guarding* him. It doesn't even know what's—it's just standing there—"

Ear stood over Mau in the middle of the south field and growled at everything coming out of him. Its white ear was up, listening; the black one lay flat back.



At the bottom of the slope, the seven witches had stopped walking.

They were standing in a line across the bottom of the field, between the light and the fence. Between the light and the slope that led up to the settlement. Seven of them, in their strange robes, in the hanging snow. Sekhmet held upright between Minerva and Daphne. Satella with her ALWAYS card held against her chest. Echidna in black, with her milk-white hair, looking at the shapes in the light with an expression of such pure, starving, terrified interest that it was almost grief. Carmilla, not hiding. Not hiding at all.

Pride at the end, with her hands open at her sides.

The currents of light reached them.

And broke round them. Like water round stones. Seven stones in a line across the bottom of the field. The light parted, and went round, and did not get past. And where it touched them, the witches did not move. Their robes moved. Their hair moved. Pride's open hands, at her sides, were shaking. They did not move.

"Seven," said Pride.

Frieren heard it. Everyone at the fence heard it. Very quiet. Very clear.

"Seven," said Echidna.

"Seven," said Daphne. And Satella, and Minerva, and Carmilla. And Sekhmet, between them, with her eyes wide open for once, both of them, looking at the light.

"*Seven,*" said Sekhmet.

And the light went round them, and did not get past.



And then, at the fence, Frieren heard it.

She did not know how. It was not loud. It was not anything. It came from the middle of the field, from the light, from somewhere far down under the currents and the shapes and the turning wheel. From under everything. From the place where the walls had been. A voice. Broken. Unused. As if it had not spoken for a very long time, and did not remember how, and was trying.

*Let me out.*

Frieren went cold all the way through.

She had not heard it in the plaza. Only Mau had heard it, and Ciel. She knew about it. Everyone knew about it. *Let me out.* A voice from under the wall, that was not the dog, not the wheel, not any counted thing. Nobody knew whose.

She heard it now.

So did Gojo. She saw him go still, in the middle of the field, in the hanging snow, with his head turned toward Mau. Toward the place under the light. His blindfolded face very still.

"There's someone in there," said Gojo. Very quietly. To nobody. "There's someone *in* there."

*Let me out*, said the voice again. Fainter. Further down. As if it were being carried away under the light. Or as if it were coming up.

Frieren could not tell which.



*Click.*

The wheel over the field turned once more.

And Frieren, at the fence, with her wrist in Milim's iron hand, saw something she had not seen in the plaza. Something she had not seen in the south field twelve days ago. Something she had never seen at all.

She saw Mau's hand move.

His right hand. On the dead earth beside him. Under Ear's planted legs. It moved. The fingers curled. Slowly. Into the earth. As if holding on to something.

He was still in there.

He was still holding on to something.

And the walls were going, one after another, *click*, *click*, and the wheel was turning over him, turning toward *everything*. And the light was reaching for every missing piece in the settlement. And there was a voice under all of it saying *let me out*. And he was lying on his back in the middle of it all with his ocean eyes open and his fingers in the earth, holding on.

Frieren made a sound she had never made in a thousand years.

Milim's hand on her wrist tightened.

"I know," said Milim, very quietly, beside her, not looking at her. Her small face was wet. "I know. I know. I've got you."

In the middle of the field, Gojo looked down at the man on the ground, at the hand in the earth, at the dog standing guard over him with its one white ear.

Then he looked up at the wheel.

Then, very slowly, he reached up to the back of his own head, and began to untie his blindfold.
