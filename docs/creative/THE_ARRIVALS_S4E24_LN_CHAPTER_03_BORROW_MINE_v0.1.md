## Chapter 24 — Borrow Mine

*When: Day 145, the same dawn, the same field, a minute later. The witches hold the bottom of the slope. Frieren is at the fence with Milim's hand round her wrist.*

Gojo knelt down beside him in the hanging snow and took the blindfold off.

He had not taken it off in front of anyone since the doorway of the real house, when a girl had looked at him and said *Grandpa*. He took it off now. In the middle of the south field. Under the wheel. With the currents of light pouring up out of the man on the ground and branching out over the field. With the shrine cutting the air into nothing and the Void flickering open and shut and Blue and Red orbiting each other overhead. With all of it hitting his eyes at once.

His eyes were blue like the sky. They took it in. All of it. Everything. Every current, every shape, every cut in the air, every flicker of the Void. And his face did not change at all.

That was what Frieren remembered. From the fence. He took the blindfold off and looked straight into the middle of everything, and he could bear it. Because he had been born with eyes like that, and his brain had grown up round them, and learned, a long time ago, when he was a baby, how to decide what not to see.

Ear growled at him.

It stood over Mau on the dead earth with its four legs planted and its smeared jaw drawn back, and its one white ear flattened, and growled. At Gojo. At the tall white-haired man kneeling beside its person, too close.

Gojo looked at it.

"I know," he said, very quietly. "I know. You're doing great." He held up one hand, slowly, palm out, the way you hold out your hand to a dog you do not know. "I'm the teacher. I'm here to help. I'm going to put something over his eyes. That's all."

Ear growled.

Then its white ear came up. Off its skull. Up. Turning toward Gojo's voice.

Listening.

It stopped growling. It did not move off Mau. But it let him come.



Gojo leaned over Mau, and lifted his head off the dead earth, very carefully, with one long hand under the back of his skull. Mau's eyes were open. Blue. The blue of the sea a long way out. Looking straight up past Gojo at the wheel, at the light, at everything. Moving. Reading. Very fast.

Gojo looked down into them.

"Hey," he said. "Stole-All-Of-Mine. Hey."

Mau did not answer. His eyes did not move off the sky.

"Yeah," said Gojo softly. "I know. I know what you're seeing. I can see it too." He shifted his grip. "It's a lot, huh. It's everything. It's the walls and the wheel and every little piece you're missing and you can't stop looking at any of it. Because it's all there. Because if it's there, you have to see it." His voice did not change. "That's what these do. That's what they always do. They'll show you everything, forever, until you're dead, if you let them."

He held up the blindfold.

"Mine come with a filter," said Gojo. "Yours don't." He looked down at Mau. "So borrow mine."

He put it over Mau's eyes.

He tied it at the back of Mau's head, gently, with his long fingers, the way you tie something on a child. The black band of cloth that he had worn every day of his life since he was small. Across the ocean eyes. Pulled snug. Knotted once.

The currents of light over the field flickered.

Not stopped. Flickered. As if something underneath them had lost its grip, for an instant, and caught it again.

"Okay," said Gojo, very quietly. "Okay. Now listen to me. Not the light. Not the wheel. Me. Just my voice. Only my voice." He still had one hand under the back of Mau's head. "You don't have to see it. That's the whole trick. That's all I've got. You don't have to see something to know it's there." He paused. "The walls are there. You don't have to read them. You know they're there. That's enough. Let them be walls."

Under the blindfold, Mau's face moved. Very slightly.

"Pick one thing," said Gojo. "One. Only one. Everything else, you let it be there and you don't look. You just pick one thing and look at that." His voice was very steady. "Pick my voice. It's not very interesting. I talk too much. Ask anyone. Ask Yuta." A pause. "Just look at my voice."

The wheel overhead turned.

*Click.*

But slower. Mau felt it. Frieren, at the fence, felt it. Everyone felt it. A half-beat slower than the last.

"Good," said Gojo. "Good. Keep doing that. Look at my voice."



*Rimuru,* said Ciel.

She said it through Rimuru, aloud, so that everyone in the middle of the field could hear. Rimuru was still standing over Mau with his hands out flat in the air, shaking. His face was the colour of ash. Blood was coming from his nose, very slowly, the way it had come from Gojo's after the south field.

*Rimuru. I watched his eyes.*

"Whose?" said Rimuru, through his teeth.

*Gojo's. In the south field. When Analyze took the roadmap. I watched too. I was inside it. I saw how they work.* A pause, very fast. *And I saw the part Analyze did not take. The part it did not know it needed. Because it was not in the eyes.* Another pause. *It was behind them. In him. In how his mind decides what not to see. The filter. It grew up round the eyes when he was a baby. It is not a technique. It is a habit. A shape.*

Rimuru did not say anything. His hands were shaking.

*I can build that,* said Ciel. *Into the walls. Not a wall. A filter. A place where the eyes stop and choose. Like his.* A pause. *I can build it into everything. New walls. Different walls. Walls that the eyes do not see as injuries, because the eyes are the ones choosing not to look.*

"How long?" said Gojo, without looking up from Mau.

*Minutes,* said Ciel. *Several. If he keeps looking at your voice. If you keep talking.*

Gojo laughed. It was short and cracked.

"Kid," he said, "talking is the one thing I'm actually good at."

*And Rimuru holds,* said Ciel. *Everything that's left. Everything still up. While I build behind it.*

Rimuru's face was grey and wet. His hands shook so hard that the air between them shimmered. But he nodded.

"Go," he said.



So Gojo talked.

He knelt in the middle of the south field with the blindfold off and his hand under the back of Mau's head and the wheel turning overhead, slower, slower, and he talked. About anything. About everything. About nothing at all.

He talked about the airport. How clean it was. How everyone he had ever lost had been there, standing around with their luggage, and how Suguru had been there, and Nanami, and they had been arguing about whether to go north or south. How he had been about to say *south*, because south was warmer. And then somebody said his name.

He talked about rice cakes. About a festival in the hills, a long time ago, and a stall he had bought out, and a curse on a train, and a boy fighting it while he lay across three seats saying *you're doing great*.

He talked about Yuta. About the first time he saw him. A thin terrified boy in a classroom with a curse in his ring that killed people, and everyone afraid of him. About deciding, that first day, that Yuta was going to be all right. And about being right.

He talked about Shoko.

His voice changed a little when he talked about Shoko. Only a little. He talked about a doctor who smoked too much, and never slept, and patched up everyone he ever brought her without asking where they had been. Who had looked at him once, across a table, in the middle of the worst week of his life, and said *you look terrible*, and handed him a coffee. And who had been the one, at the end, who had to—

He stopped.

"Yeah," said Gojo quietly. "Anyway. You'd like her."

Under the blindfold, Mau's lips moved.

Gojo bent closer.

"What?"

"*Keep talking,*" said Mau. It was barely a breath. "Please. Just keep. Talking."

Gojo looked down at him for a long moment.

"Okay," he said. "Okay. I'm talking."

And he did.



Behind Gojo's voice, while he talked, the walls came back.

Frieren watched it from the fence. Not the walls. Nobody could see the walls. She watched what they did.

The currents of light pulled back first. Slowly. Like a tide going out. The long pale branches that had reached across the field toward the fence, toward the ring, toward Okarun's pocket, toward the settlement and the yard and the ink tree, drew back. One by one. Shortening. Thinning. Folding back into themselves and down, toward the man on the ground, toward his chest, until they were only threads, and then only light, and then not there.

The shapes went. The wing that was not a wing. The door. The face with no face. They came apart in the light and did not form again.

The half-hand of Rika sank back into the earth, still reaching, until only its fingertips were showing, and then not those. At the fence, Rika, round Yuta, let out a long breath like a sob.

The unfinished glyph over Mau's knees drew itself one last time, got two-thirds of the way, and went out. It did not spark. It just went out, like a lamp.

The rips in the air at the bottom of the field stopped. In Okarun's pocket, very small, Turbo Granny said: *It stopped. It stopped looking for me. It stopped.*

The Void stopped flickering. It opened once more, in the middle of the field, perfect and black, and then closed, the way an eye closes in sleep. And did not open again.

The shrine stopped cutting. It stood in the snow a moment longer, bone and teeth and dark stone, silent. Then it was not there. And the snow that had been cut into dust lay on the ground in a fine grey powder, like ash.

Blue and Red, still orbiting over Mau's chest, slowed. Then Blue, very quietly, ate itself, the way it had in the plaza. And Red, alone, burst, the way it had in the plaza, into a small brief shower of sparks, like fireworks, that fell over the field and went out.

And the snow fell.

All of it. All at once. Every flake that had hung in the air over the south field since the bell began, falling, softly, everywhere, like the end of a held note.

The wheel turned once more.

*Click.*

It was very slow. The slowest yet. And then it did not turn again. It hung over the field, pale and enormous and still. And then, slowly, it began to come down. Smaller as it came. Down through the falling snow. Down past Gojo kneeling and Rimuru shaking and Ear standing guard. Down into the shadow under Mau on the dead earth. And into it. Like a stone into dark water.

Gone.

Under the blindfold, Mau's face went slack.

His hand, in the earth, uncurled.



*Done,* said Ciel.

Her voice was almost nothing.

*New walls. A filter in all of them. The eyes are behind it. They are choosing. The wheel is in his shadow. It is not turning.* A pause. *They are new. They are not tired. They will hold.* Another pause, so faint it was barely there. *I don't know how long. But they will hold.*

Rimuru sat down in the snow.

He did not decide to. His legs went. He sat down in the falling snow beside Mau and put his face in his hands. And his shoulders went, and kept going, and he did not make any sound at all.

Gojo did not move. He knelt with his hand still under the back of Mau's head, looking down at the black blindfold across the ocean eyes.

Mau was breathing. Very shallow. Very fast.

From under the blindfold, at the corners, two thin lines of red had started to run. Down his temples. Into his hair. Into the dead earth.

Gojo looked at them.

"No," he said quietly. "No, no, no. Hey. *Hey.*"

He put two fingers against Mau's throat.

The pulse was there. Thin. Too fast. Uneven. Like something running down a corridor that was getting longer.

Maomao was already coming. Frieren saw her come over the fence with her satchel, past the witches, who stepped aside for her. She ran across the field through the falling snow. She knelt on Mau's other side and put her hands on him, his throat, his chest, his wrists, very fast. And her face, which was always calm, was not calm.

"I can't," said Maomao. "His heart. It's—it's not a wound, there's nothing to stitch, it's *inside*, it's like his whole body's been running for a week and it's—" She stopped. "I don't have anything for this. I don't have anything. Herbs won't—I can't—"

"RCT," said Gojo.

"Yuta's out," said Maomao. "Look at him."

At the fence, Yuta was on his knees in the snow with Rika gone back into the ring and Maki holding him up, grey to the lips.

"Mau's own—"

"Closes scratches," said Maomao. "This isn't a *scratch*."

Gojo knelt in the snow with his fingers on Mau's throat.

Then he took his hand away. He reached up, to the high collar of his coat, inside. Where something small had been tucked against his chest for thirteen days. Since an old woman held it out to him on her palm at the long table and said *for the doctor. If you want her.*

He took out Seiko's coin.

He looked at it for one second, in his palm, in the falling snow. Small. Faceless. Warm.

Then he closed his fist round it so hard his knuckles went white.

He did not close his eyes. He did not need to think of her. He had been thinking of her for thirteen days. On the roof, and in the corridor outside the clinic, and in the doorway of the real house. And every time anyone at the long table had cut themselves or burned themselves or bruised a rib, and Maomao had patched them, and he had thought *she'd have done that faster*.

A woman with tired eyes and a cigarette, in a white coat, in a dark room, who never asked where you had been.

*You are sure? Shoko Ieiri.*

"*Yes,*" said Gojo. "God. Yes. *Now*. Please."

The coin went warm in his fist. Then warmer.

Then it was gone.

Gojo opened his hand and looked at the empty palm for a long moment in the falling snow. Then he put it back under Mau's head, very gently, with the other.

"Okay," he said, to the man in the blindfold. His voice was not steady at all. "Okay. Hold on. The doctor's coming. The real one. Hold on."

At the fence, Milim let go of Frieren's wrist.

She did not decide to. Her hand simply opened. And Frieren went over the fence, and down the slope, and across the field through the falling snow, past the witches, past Rimuru in the snow with his face in his hands. Faster than she had moved in the plaza. Faster than she had moved in a thousand years. She went down on her knees on the dead earth beside him, across from Gojo, and put her hands on his face. On either side of the black blindfold. On the two thin lines of red running into his hair.

She did not say anything.

She did not say his name. She did not say *one* or *still here*. She put her forehead down against his, against the blindfold, and stayed there. Ear stood over all three of them, with its legs planted and its white ear up, listening, in the falling snow. And from very far away, across the plaza, the bell in the tower over Arrival House finally stopped ringing.
