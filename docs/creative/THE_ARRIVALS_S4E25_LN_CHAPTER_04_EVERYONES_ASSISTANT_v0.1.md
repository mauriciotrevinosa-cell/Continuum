*When: Day 150, evening. Rimuru woke as a slime the day before and has been eating steadily ever since; Shoko has taken over half of Maomao's clinic and all of her worst patients; the fort's roster still says thirty, three times a day.*

Diablo called a meeting.

This had never happened. In all the time Diablo had been in the settlement, since before the Muslin, since before the move, he had never called anything. He opened doors. He bowed. He stood outside rooms. He said *Kufufufu*, and was gone, and did things nobody had asked him to and nobody ever thanked him for, because nobody ever saw him do them. He did not call meetings.

He called one on the hundred-and-fiftieth evening.

He did it at dinner. He came into the common room in his butler's coat while the long table was eating, and stood at the head of it, beside Fern, which was not his place. He waited until the noise had died down by itself, which took longer than he clearly thought it should. Then he said, in his smooth polite terrible voice:

"After dinner. Here. Everyone. If you would be so kind."

Then he went out.

The long table looked at the door he had gone through.

"Did Diablo," said Umaru slowly, from the arch of the fort, "just call a *meeting*?"

Nobody answered. Nobody knew.

At the cold-hearth end, in human shape for the first time since the bell, very pale, with a plate in front of him he had been eating from steadily for an hour, Rimuru had gone completely still.



They all came. Of course they did.

The whole house, and the fort in its hoods, and the witches at their end, and Seiko on her high seat. Gojo, with his blindfold back on, at the cold-hearth end beside Yuta. Shoko beside him, in her white coat, with her arms folded, looking at all of it with the flat assessing look of a woman who had been in this house five days and had already decided which of them were going to be trouble. Yuka, between her grandparents, with the white dog under the table. Seika and Ram, side by side at the far end, arms folded, faces identical. Mau, at the end of the bench beside Frieren, looking carefully at the table.

Diablo came in last.

He walked to the head of the long table, where Fern had moved aside for him, very slightly, without being asked. And he stood there in his butler's coat with his hands folded behind his back and looked at all of them.

He did not say anything for a long time.

"I have served Rimuru-sama," said Diablo at last, "since before any of you were here."

The common room was silent.

"I came to him in his own world," said Diablo. "When he called. On the worst night his city ever had, when he needed help to bring his people back. I was meant to answer and leave." He did not look at Rimuru. He looked at all of them. "I stayed. I chose him. I am a very old demon, and I had chosen nothing in my existence. I chose him. To serve. As his assistant. His instrument. His hand." He paused. "That has been the whole of what I am. For a very long time."

At the cold-hearth end, Rimuru had put his fork down.

"I came here with him," said Diablo. "Over the bridge. Into this world. I stood outside his door. I opened doors for him. I did what he asked, and what he did not ask, because I knew what he would want before he knew it himself." He paused again. "That is what an assistant does."

Nobody said anything.

"And I have watched," said Diablo. "For two years. I have watched him in this house. Among you." His voice did not change. But something in it shifted. "I have watched him laugh. On a roof. In the snow. For the first time since a knife in a street, in his other life. I have watched him do nothing at all for an entire day, very badly, because a child gave him a card. I have watched him sit by a cold hearth every morning and say *looked, quiet* for someone who cannot speak aloud. I have watched him sleep. Properly. As a slime on a pillow." He paused. "I have watched him be happier here than I have ever seen him in any world."

Rimuru was looking at him now. Not moving. Not saying anything.

"That is because of you," said Diablo.

The common room was very quiet.

"Not because of me," said Diablo. "I stood outside his door. That is all I did. You are the ones who made him laugh. You are the ones who gave him a card. You are the ones who sat on a roof with him." He looked down the long table. At Yuta. At Mau. At Milim, very still, with her hands in her lap. At the fort. At Seiko. "You are the ones who made him happy. All of you."

He turned to Rimuru.

And he bowed. Very low. Lower than he had ever bowed to anyone in the settlement, lower than he bowed to Frieren as queen. All the way down, with one hand on his chest.

"Rimuru-sama," said Diablo. "I would like to ask you to release me."

The common room went completely silent.

Then everyone spoke at once.

"*WHAT*—"

"*Release* you—"

"Diablo, what are you—"

"He's *leaving*?"

"*Is he leaving?*" said Milim, on her feet, with her face gone white. "Is Diablo *leaving*? He can't leave, he's the—he's *Diablo*—"

Rimuru had not moved. He sat at the cold-hearth end with his fork on the table and his face very still, looking at the demon bowed in front of him.

"Release you," he said quietly. "From what?"

Diablo straightened up.

"From being your assistant," he said. "Only yours."

He turned round. To the long table. To all of them.

"I would like," said Diablo, "to be everyone's."



Nobody said anything for a long time.

"I would like to help," said Diablo. "Not only Rimuru-sama. Everyone." He folded his hands behind his back. "I would like to help the Guardian." He inclined his head toward Milim, who was still standing, with her mouth open. "She guards the fort. She guarded the gate. She held the elf at the fence, which I could not have done." A pause. "A guardian should not have to guard alone. I would like to stand with her."

Milim's mouth closed. Then opened again. Nothing came out.

"I would like to help the doctor," said Diablo, with a glance at Shoko, who raised one eyebrow very slightly. "And the apothecary. And the kitchen. And the fort, if the fort will have me. And the long table." He paused. "I would like to stand outside every door in this house. Not only one."

He looked at Rimuru again.

"I would like to be part of the house," said Diablo. "Not his instrument in it. Part of it."

There was a long silence.

Rimuru looked at him.

Then, very slowly, Rimuru smiled. It was not a big smile. It was small and crooked and wet at the edges, and it was the smile Mau had seen on the roof in the snow, the night Rimuru laughed for the first time.

"Diablo," said Rimuru. "You don't need me to release you. You never needed my permission to be anything." He stood up. He came round the end of the long table, small and pale and very tired, and stood in front of the tall demon in the butler's coat, and looked up at him. "But if you want it—" He paused. "Then yes. Go. Be everyone's. Be part of the house." His voice cracked, a little. "You already are. You have been for a long time. You just never said."

Diablo looked down at him for a long moment.

Then he bowed again. Not as low. Just enough.

"Thank you, Rimuru-sama," he said.

He straightened up. He turned to the long table.

"I accept, then," said Diablo, "the position."

The fort cheered. Behind its curtain, someone blew Senku's whistle, and Fern did not confiscate it. Umaru was standing on the toll booth platform shouting something about a sash, and a new title, and *ASSISTANT TO EVERYONE (OFFICIAL)*, and the Ministry stamp. Milim had come round the long table and was standing in front of Diablo, very small, looking up at him with an expression nobody had ever seen on her face, which was something very close to shy.

"You're going to guard with me?" said Milim.

"If the Guardian will have me," said Diablo.

"I'll *have* you," said Milim. Her voice wobbled. "I'll have you so much."

"Kufufufu," said Diablo, which was, Mau decided, a yes.

And then Diablo turned back to the whole long table, to all of them, cheering, with the whistle going and Umaru shouting and Milim in front of him. He held up one gloved hand.

The common room went quiet. Not at once. But it went quiet.

Diablo smiled.

It was the most beautiful smile Mau had ever seen on any face, and the most frightening.

"I will help every one of you," said Diablo, pleasantly. "I will stand outside your doors. I will guard your fort. I will carry your patients and polish your cars and open every lock in this house at any hour you ask." He paused. "I will be everyone's assistant, gladly, for as long as this house stands."

He let the smile widen, very slightly.

"But if any of you," said Diablo, "ever betray Rimuru-sama—"

The common room went very still.

"—or make him cry," said Diablo, gently, "I will not hesitate to kill you."

There was a long, perfect silence.

Then, at the far end of the long table, Ram said: "Acceptable."

And Seika, beside her, said: "Hah."

And the whole common room, all at once, started to laugh. It was a helpless, shaky, enormous laugh, the first real laugh the house had had since the bell. It went on for a long time. Milim was laughing so hard she had to sit down on the floor. Rimuru was laughing with his face in his hands. Shoko, in her white coat, was looking at the demon at the head of the table with an expression of deep and growing professional interest. Gojo was laughing so loudly that Yuta had to hit him.

Diablo stood at the head of the long table through all of it, with his hands folded behind his back and his smile perfectly pleasant.

He had not been joking. Everyone knew he had not been joking.

That was what made it so funny.



Later, much later, when the common room had emptied and the lamps were going out, Mau passed the door of Rimuru's office on his way up and saw them through the gap.

Rimuru was at his desk, in human shape, asleep, with his head on his arms on a sheet of paper covered in diagrams of walls. The new walls. Drawn properly, this time. Not crossed out.

Diablo was standing beside him.

Not outside the door. Inside. Beside the desk. He had a blanket over his arm. As Mau watched, he unfolded it, and laid it over Rimuru's shoulders, very carefully, the way you would cover someone you did not want to wake.

Then he went out, and closed the door softly behind him. And he did not stand outside it.

He walked away down the corridor, toward the fort, where a small pink-haired dragon was waiting at the arch with her arms folded, pretending she had not been waiting at all.

"*Guardian,*" said Diablo, with a small bow.

"*Assistant,*" said Milim, with an enormous one.

And the two of them went into the fort together, for the night watch.
