## Chapter 4 — Not Like Him

*When: Day 172, dawn, the north field. Aira is on a cottage step with Mikasa's old blades across her knees. Mau is awake and informed in the east room, keeping the recovery limits he agreed to. Frieren is at the red stake with her staff, and has not said a word since she came out.*

The titan came down the ridge.

The ridge and the foot of the shepherd's track lay outside the marked protection. The families remained inside it. Anyone fighting in the grass below the ridge would be outside too.

It did not run, at first. It walked. Stiffly, the way something walks that has been standing still all night and has not quite remembered how legs work. It came down the shepherd's track off the crest of the ridge in the first gold light, enormous, pale, with its arms swinging loosely at its sides and its head tilted, and its wide flat smiling face turned toward the north field. Toward the people in it.

Each stride was the length of a cottage.

"It's slow," said Eren. His voice was very steady. He stood at the north end of the field beside Anko, with his hands at his sides, watching it come. "It's slow for the first few minutes. Until the sun's properly on it. Then it won't be." He did not take his eyes off it. "Himmel. Stark. When it reaches the bottom of the track. Not before."

In the long grass at the foot of the ridge, two hundred paces out, Himmel lifted one hand without looking back. *Understood.*

Stark did not lift anything. He was crouched beside Himmel with his axe in both hands, and he was shaking so hard that the blade trembled against the frost. He had always shaken before fights. In his world, in this one. Everyone who had ever fought beside him knew that. He shook, and he was terrified, and he stood up anyway, every time, because that was what Stark was.

"It's bigger than I thought," said Stark, through his teeth.

"They always are," said Himmel pleasantly.

"How do you *do* that? How are you so *calm*?"

"I'm not," said Himmel. "I'm terrified." He smiled. "I just brushed my hair."

The titan reached the bottom of the track.

It stopped there for a moment, in the open ground at the foot of the ridge, swaying very slightly, as if listening. Its head turned. Slowly. Not toward the settlement any more. Down. Toward the long grass. Toward the two men crouched in it.

It had seen them.

Its smile got wider. And it bent, from the waist, with a terrible loose ease, the way a child bends to pick up something it has dropped, and reached down for them with one enormous pale hand.

"*Now,*" said Eren.



Himmel went left. Stark went right.

They went at the same moment, out of the grass, under the reaching hand, which closed on the place where they had been and came up full of frost and torn grass. And they ran, the two of them, low and fast, between the titan's feet. And Stark, still shaking, still terrified, swung his axe in a great flat arc into the back of its left heel, where Eren had told him, at the cord that ran up from the heel to the calf.

The axe went in. All the way.

Steam burst out of the wound, white and hot and stinking, and the titan's left leg went out from under it.

Himmel was behind its right knee. He did not swing. He thrust. A single clean stroke, upward, into the soft place at the back of the joint, the way you would take down a knight in armour. And pulled out. And stepped back.

The right knee buckled.

The titan went down.

It fell forward, slowly, enormous, onto its knees. The ground shook. Frost leapt off the grass for fifty paces round. Its hands came down flat in front of it, and it caught itself on them, on all fours, with its great smiling face hanging a storey above the ground, and its head turned. Toward Himmel. Toward Stark. Its eyes wide and unblinking and delighted.

The wounds were already closing. Steam pouring out of them. Eren could see the cords knitting back together from where he stood.

"It's not low enough!" said Eren. "Its neck—Milim, *now*—"

Milim had been waiting for exactly that word.

She went up off the frost beside Mikasa with a sound like a door slamming, with one small arm round Mikasa's waist. She did not go gently. She went so fast that the air cracked behind her. She crossed the two hundred paces of field in the time it took to breathe in, and went up over the titan's back in a great pink arc, and dropped Mikasa onto it, between its shoulder blades, as lightly as setting down a cup.

"*Mostly gently!*" Milim shouted, from somewhere overhead.

Mikasa landed running.

She ran up its spine. It was like running up a hill made of warm skin, slick with frost and steam. Both grey blades were out. The nape was ahead of her, at the top of the hill, below the base of the enormous skull. A strip the width of a hand and the length of a forearm. Eren had told them exactly.

The titan felt her.

It reared. Its whole back heaved under her feet, and it twisted, and its hand came up off the ground and swung round behind it, back over its own shoulder, toward her, the way a man swats at a fly on his neck.

It did not reach her.

It reached Himmel.

He had been closest. He had stepped in again, to cut the right knee a second time before it could heal. And the swinging hand came down past its own shoulder and caught him, mid-stride, round the body, and closed, and lifted him off the ground.

Stark screamed his name.

The titan brought its hand up toward its face. Toward its smile. Its mouth was opening. Wide. Wider than a mouth should open. Its teeth were the size of tombstones, white and even and human. And Himmel was in its fist, with his arms pinned, being lifted toward them. His face was very calm. His sword was still in his hand, pinned against his side, useless.

At the north end of the field, Eren made a sound.

It was not a word. It came out of him from somewhere very deep and very old. From a street in a town behind a wall, a long time ago, when he was ten, watching something smile and lift his mother toward its mouth while he could not move.

His hand came up. Toward his face. Toward his mouth.

His teeth were bared. His hand was an inch from them. He knew exactly how it went. Bite down. Hard. Draw blood. And the lightning, and the heat, and the thing he was would come up out of him and he would be fifteen metres tall and he would tear that smiling face off its—

*You don't transform.*

*Promise me.*

His hand stopped an inch from his teeth.

It shook there. In front of his mouth. His whole arm shook. His whole body. He stood at the north end of the field with his hand in front of his face and his teeth bared and every part of him screaming to bite down. And he did not.

Mikasa saw it.

She was on the titan's back, running, and she should not have been able to see anything but the nape in front of her. But she saw him. She always saw him. Across the field, at the north end, with his hand in front of his mouth, holding still. Keeping his promise. Trusting them.

She reached the top of the hill.

She cut.

Two strokes. Crossing. Deep. The gate's grey blades went into the nape like needles into cloth. All the way. Through the strip the width of a hand and the length of a forearm. Steam exploded out of it in a white roar that blinded her.

The titan stopped.

All of it. At once. Its hand stopped, halfway to its mouth, with Himmel in it. Its mouth stopped, open. Its smile stopped. Its eyes stayed open, wide and delighted and unblinking, staring at nothing.

Then its fingers loosened.

Himmel fell. Thirty feet. Toward the frozen ground.

Something small and pink caught him halfway down.

"*Gently,*" said Milim, very seriously, holding the hero in both arms in the air with an expression of enormous concentration. "Gently, gently, gently." And set him down in the grass beside Stark as carefully as if he were made of glass.

Himmel sat in the frost. He looked up at her. His hair was ruined.

"Thank you," said Himmel.

"You're welcome," said Milim. Her lip was wobbling. "You nearly got *eaten*."

"I know," said Himmel. "I was there."



It took a long time to die. Or to stop. Whatever it was that it did.

It did not fall. It stayed where it was, on its knees in the frost at the foot of the ridge, with one hand flat on the ground and the other lifted, empty, halfway to its open mouth. Steam poured off it. Not from the wounds. From all of it. From every part of its enormous pale body, white and hot and thick, rolling away across the north field in the morning light. And as the steam came off it, it got less. The way the devil had got less. Not melting. Just going. The great pale shape blurred at the edges and thinned, and its face went soft, and its smile went soft, and the steam poured up into the gold sky.

Mikasa had jumped down off its back before it started. She stood in the grass beside Stark and Himmel and Milim, with her grey blades still out, and watched it go.

At the north end of the field, Rimuru had not moved.

He had been standing very still the whole time, with his shadow stretched out long and thin across the frost, all the way from his feet to the foot of the ridge. At first light, when the titan stood on the ridge and turned its head, the long dawn shadows had reached across the field toward each other. His and its. And touched. And in the moment they touched, something had gone out of his shadow and into the titan's. A thread. Very thin. Very dark.

He had been reading it ever since.

His eyes were closed. His face was perfectly still. Anko, beside him, was watching him and not the titan.

The steam thinned. The titan was half its size now. A quarter. A shape in the steam. A suggestion of a shape. Then there was nothing on its knees at the foot of the ridge. Only a great patch of frost melted to bare black earth, in the shape of two knees and a hand, steaming faintly, and a smell on the wind like hot iron and something else, something underneath it, almost sweet.

Nothing left. No bones. No body. Nothing.

Rimuru opened his eyes.

He did not say anything for a long moment. He looked across the field, at the bare black patch at the foot of the ridge. Then at Eren, standing beside him with his hand at his side now, lowered, shaking, and his face white as paper.

"It's empty," said Rimuru.



They stood round him in the north field while the sun came up, and he told them. Mikasa. Himmel, with his ruined hair. Stark, still shaking. Milim, still wobbling. Anko. Nazuna, who had come down out of the sky. Frieren, who had come up from the red stake, at last, with her staff, and stood beside Rimuru with her serene face. And Eren.

"*There was nothing inside it,*" said Rimuru, in Ciel's flat clear voice. "*I had a thread in it from the moment the shadows touched. I read all of it. Every part. While it was alive and while it was going.*" A pause. "*It was a shape. Only a shape. Your shape, Eren. Your world's. A body made in the shape of the things that came over your walls. And nothing in it.*"

"Nothing," said Eren.

"*No person,*" said Rimuru, for her. "*No mind. No thread going anywhere. It was not connected to anything. Not to a person. Not to a place. Not to anything new in this world that I can find.*" Another pause. "*It was hungry. That was all. It was a hunger in the shape of a man.*"

Eren did not say anything for a long time.

"Every titan I ever killed," he said at last, very quietly, "was a person once."

The north field was quiet.

"In my world," said Eren. "Every one. They were people. Someone did something to them. Injected them. Turned them. They were people, and they didn't know they were people any more, and they walked around smiling and eating, and every time I cut one down I killed a person." He looked at the bare black patch at the foot of the ridge. "I didn't know that for a long time. And then I did. And after that every one was—" He stopped.

"*This one was never a person,*" said Rimuru, for Ciel. And then, more gently, in his own voice, because some things should not be said in a flat clear voice: "Eren. This one was never anyone. There was nobody in it to kill."

Eren closed his eyes.

"Then where did it come from?" said Anko.

Nobody knew.

"*It is in the shape of his world,*" said Rimuru, for Ciel. "*And not connected to his world. It is not like him. It is not like what he carries. It did not come through the gate, or the hills, or anywhere I can trace.*"

"So someone brought it," said Anko slowly. "From his world. In his world's shape."

Fern had come out. Nobody had seen her come. She was standing at the edge of the group with the ledger under her arm and her face very still.

"The same way someone brought us," said Fern.

Nobody answered that. Nobody needed to. They had all, every one of them, come over a bridge from somewhere into this world without being asked. There was only one thing any of them knew of that brought things.

"The Goddess," said Stark, very quietly.

"Or something like her," said Fern. She opened the ledger. She wrote, in her small upright hand: *Day 172. A titan. Empty. Not like Eren. Nothing behind it.* And then, after a moment, under that: *Brought?* She looked at the question mark for a long time. Then she closed the ledger. "We don't know," she said. "We'll assume it until we do."

The sun was all the way up now. The north field was gold and white with frost. The steam was gone. The smell was going.



Eren turned to Rimuru.

He did it very suddenly. As if he had been holding it back for the whole conversation and could not any more.

"Ask her to look at me," he said.

Rimuru looked at him.

"Ciel," said Eren. His voice had gone strange. Tight. "Ask her to look at me. At what I am. At the—at my titan." He swallowed. "I'm asking. I know the rule. She only looks if you ask. I'm asking."

Mikasa had gone very still beside him.

Rimuru looked at Eren for a long moment. Then he nodded, and closed his eyes.

Nobody said anything. The north field was very quiet. Somewhere behind them, in the settlement, a door opened, and someone called something, and was hushed.

It took a long time. Longer than the titan had taken. Rimuru stood with his eyes closed and his face still, and Eren stood in front of him with his fists closed at his sides, and Mikasa stood half a step from Eren, and the sun climbed.

Then Rimuru opened his eyes.

"*It is there,*" he said, for Ciel. "*What you carry. Your titan. It is small. About a tenth of what it was. Measured, the way everyone is measured.*" A pause. "*But it is there. It is yours. It is not like the empty one. There is a person in it. You.*"

"And the—" Eren's voice cracked. He stopped. Started again. "In my world. The power. It came with a clock. Thirteen years. From the day you get it. Thirteen years, and then you die. Everyone who ever had it. Thirteen years, every time." He was not looking at anyone. "I got it when I was ten. I found out what it meant later. I've been counting ever since. Every day. I know exactly how many I've got left." His voice was perfectly flat. "Is it there? The clock? Can she see it?"

Rimuru was quiet for a moment.

"*In your world,*" he said, for Ciel, slowly, "*the power was connected to something. I can see the place where the connection was. Like a root that went down into the ground, into something underneath. A road. A path. Something every one of you who carried it was tied to. And the clock was in the road. Not in you. The road kept count.*" A pause. "*Here, there is no road. The root goes down into nothing. It is not tied to anything.*"

"So the clock—"

"*Nothing is counting,*" said Rimuru, for Ciel. And then he said it again, in his own voice, because he could see Eren's face. "Eren. Nothing is counting. She can't find thirteen. She can't find any number at all. There's nothing there to run out."

Eren did not move.

"*You will get as old as you get,*" said Rimuru, for Ciel, very quietly. "*The same as anyone.*"

The north field was completely silent.

Eren stood in the frost with the sun on his face. He did not say anything. He did not move at all. His fists were closed at his sides. His face did not do anything, not for a long time, the way Mikasa's face did not do anything when the most important thing in the world had just happened to her.

Then he turned round, and walked away.

Not fast. Not running. He walked away across the north field, south, toward the settlement, with his hands at his sides. Past the red stake. Past the gate, humming blue. Past all of them. He did not look at any of them. He did not look at Mikasa. He walked away down the road into the settlement in the morning light, and did not look back once, and was gone.

Mikasa did not follow him.

She stood in the north field with her grey blades still in her hands, and watched him go, all the way, until he turned the corner by the cottages and was out of sight. Her face did not do anything at all.

Frieren was watching her.

"You're not going after him," said Frieren quietly.

"No," said Mikasa.

"Why?"

Mikasa looked at the corner where he had gone.

"Because he's never had to think about it before," she said. "Not once. Not since he found out. He's always known exactly how long he had." Her voice was very low. "He needs to find out what it's like. Not knowing." She sheathed her blades. One. Then the other. "He'll come and find me. When he's ready."

Frieren did not say anything for a long moment. She looked at Mikasa. At the girl who had waited, beside a boy, half a step away, for years, without ever pushing.

"You've known him a long time," said Frieren.

"Since we were nine."

"And you've waited."

"Yes."

Frieren looked at the road, at the corner where Eren had gone. Then back at Mikasa.

"It gets easier," said Frieren. "The waiting." She paused. "And then it gets much harder. At the very end. When you can see it's nearly over." She put her hand, very briefly, on Mikasa's arm. "That's when you'll know it was worth it."

Mikasa looked down at the elf's hand on her sleeve.

She did not say anything. But she did not move her arm away.



Aira was still on the step of the cottage when Mikasa came down the road.

The lantern had burned out. The sun was up, gold on the frost. The cottage door behind Aira had opened at some point, and the bean-girl was sitting on the step beside her in a blanket, with her mother standing in the doorway behind them both with her hand over her mouth. They had all seen the steam. Everyone in the settlement had seen the steam, rising off the foot of the ridge into the dawn sky.

Aira had Mikasa's old blades across her knees. She had not let go of them all night.

She looked up when Mikasa stopped in front of her.

"It's done," said Mikasa.

Aira let out a breath. It went on for a long time.

Then she held the old blades up. Hilts first. Both of them.

"I said I'd give them back," said Aira.

Mikasa looked at the blades. At the nick in the left one.

"Keep one," she said.

Aira stared at her.

"Steel works out here," said Mikasa. "On things from the woods." She took the right blade, and left the left one, with the nick, across Aira's knees. "You held the door. You should have something to hold it with."

She went on up the road toward the house, with one blade at her hip and one grey pair, and did not look back.

Aira sat on the step for a long time, with the old blade across her knees and the bean-girl leaning against her side in her blanket. The bean-girl looked at the blade. At the nick.

"Is that yours now?" said the bean-girl.

Aira looked at it.

"I think so," she said. Her voice had gone strange. "I think it's mine now."
