## Chapter 14 — Something Thinks It Can Help

*When: Day 195, the same morning, a quarter of an hour later. Coco is in the fort, in Qifrey's arms, and has not let go of him. The plaza is full of people not going into Arrival House. Jinshi is standing in its doorway like a wall.*

Rimuru came at a run, in human shape, with his coat half on. Yuta came behind him, walking fast, with his hand on the ring at his chest. And Frieren came behind both of them, not running, with her staff, and her serene face, and the old silver compass in the pocket of the orange hoodie. Nobody had asked her to come. She came anyway, because it was Mau, and because it was a woman made of crystal, and because, she said afterward, she had felt in the night that something was going to have to go back to somewhere.

Jinshi let them past.

They stopped in the doorway of the intake hall, all three of them, beside Mau, and looked at it.

The crystal woman stood in the light from the high windows, reaching down for the empty air. The hundred small rainbows lay on the boards round her feet. Her mouth was open on a word that had not finished.

Nobody said anything for a long moment.

"Ciel said you said something thinks it can help," said Rimuru. Very quietly.

"Yes," said Mau.

"What thinks it can help?"

Mau did not take his eyes off the crystal.

"The wheel," he said.

Yuta's hand tightened on the ring.

"It's not turning," said Mau quickly. "It's not reaching. It's not doing anything it did in the field. It's just—" He stopped. He did not know how to say it. "It's looking at her. Through me. The way Senku looks at a broken lens. The way Maomao looks at a cut." He paused. "It woke up when I came in, and looked at her, and said *I think I can help with that*. And then it went quiet. It's waiting." He looked at Rimuru at last. "It's never said anything like that before. In two years. It's only ever wanted things."

Rimuru looked at him for a long time.

"Ciel," he said. Not to Mau. To the air.

In both their heads, at once, she answered. Carefully.

*I feel it,* said Ciel. *It is true. The wheel is awake and it is not pressing. It is not at the walls. It is—* A pause. *It is attending. I have no better word. It is attending to the crystal the way it attended in the plaza, and to the Infinity that leaked. The way it attends to anything it believes it can adapt to.*

"Adapt," said Yuta.

*That is what it does,* said Ciel. *It is the only thing it does. It turns, and with each turn it learns the shape of what is hurting it, until it is not hurt any more. Sukuna built a whole technique out of it. In the field it turned at the walls because it had decided the walls were an injury.* A pause. *Now it has looked at a binding it has never seen, and decided it could learn the shape of it.*



Gojo was in the doorway behind them.

Nobody had heard him come back. He had come back without Shoko, with his blindfold pushed up on his forehead and his bare sky-coloured eyes on the crystal woman. He was not smiling.

"It's not stone," said Gojo.

They turned round.

"I looked," said Gojo. "Before Jinshi threw us out. I couldn't help it." He was looking at the crystal with an expression none of them had seen on him before. Very still. Very careful. "It's not stone, and it's not glass, and it's not ice. It's a spell. A finished one. It went in, and it went all the way through her, every part, in a shape, and then it closed. And it's been closed ever since." He tilted his head. "It's not trying to do anything any more. It's not spreading. It's not reaching for anyone. It's just—" He searched for the word. "Asleep. Holding her. Like a hand closed round something."

"And her?" said Frieren quietly.

Gojo looked at her.

"She's in there," he said. "All of her. Not moving. Not thinking, I don't think. But there." He paused. "It's not dead, if that's what you're asking. It's waiting."

Frieren did not say anything. But her hand had closed round the compass in her pocket.

Mau looked at the crystal. At the reaching hand. At the open mouth.

"Ciel," he said. "Would anything happen to Rimuru if he absorbed her?"

Rimuru went very still beside him.

Ciel was quiet for a moment. Mau could feel her working. Turning it over. Reading what Gojo had said, and what the wheel was attending to, and what she could feel of the crystal through Mau's eyes.

*No,* said Ciel at last. *Nothing would happen to him. The crystal is no longer trying to crystallize anything. It is not spreading. If he took her into his stomach, she would be held, the way everything he takes in is held. Outside time. Still. Safe. And I could read every part of her. Every molecule.* A pause. *It would be safe.*

Then, more slowly:

*But I don't understand why. Why it stopped. A binding this complete should go on reaching. It doesn't. It closed, and stayed closed, and I do not know why.*

"Because it finished," said Gojo quietly, from the doorway. "It did what it was drawn to do. To the person who was standing in it." He looked at the reaching hand. "It wasn't drawn for anyone else."

Nobody said anything.

Mau stood in the doorway of the intake hall, with the three of them beside him and Gojo behind, looking at the woman made of crystal. And he felt the wheel, far back behind the walls, attending. Waiting. Very still.

And he remembered something.

The atelier floor, four months ago, on the day the ink came back. A small witch with her face still wet, turning her notebook round so he could see. Two lattices, one inside the other, and an empty middle, and his own name in the margin with a question mark after it. *Do you think you could be the one? Who finds the missing piece?*

*I don't know,* he had said. *But if I am, I'll do everything I can.*

He felt the shape of an idea come up into his head. All at once, the way the worst ideas always came.

"I have a plan," said Mau.

"No," said Rimuru.

"I haven't said it yet."

"I know what your face looks like when you have a plan," said Rimuru. "It's that face. No."

"Let him say it," said Yuta quietly.

Rimuru looked at Yuta. Yuta looked back. Calmly. With his hand on the ring.

Rimuru closed his mouth.



Mau said it.

He said it in the doorway of the intake hall, quietly, carefully, in order, the way Frieren told him things when she had decided he should know them exactly. He did not look at the crystal while he said it. He looked at Rimuru.

"You take her in," said Mau. "Into your stomach. Where it's safe, and still, and Ciel can read every part of her. That's the first thing." He paused. "And then you take me in too."

Rimuru's face did not change. But something behind it did.

"Echidna's book," said Mau. "The pale one. Coco showed me the page, on the atelier floor, the day the ink came back. The woman in the other world worked out the undoing. All of it. Except the centre. To undo it in a person, you have to know the shape it took inside them. From inside. The order it moved through them." He paused. "Nobody can know that from outside. No spell. No book. Coco looked for five weeks."

"So you—" said Yuta slowly.

"So I go inside," said Mau. "Into Rimuru. With her. And I touch her." He held out his right hand, palm down, as if resting it on something. "And I let it take me."

The intake hall was completely silent.

"The crystal," said Mau. "It's not reaching for anyone. Gojo said. It was drawn for her. But if I touch it, and I *let* it, if I open the door instead of keeping it shut—it'll start on me. My hand. My arm." He did not look away from Rimuru. "And the wheel will feel it. It'll feel me starting to crystallize. And it'll decide that's an injury. And it'll turn."

"And adapt," said Ciel, aloud, through Rimuru's mouth. Very quietly.

"And adapt," said Mau. "From inside. In a living body. Mine. It'll learn the shape of the binding the way it learns everything. One turn at a time. Until it can undo it. In me." He paused. "And I'll be touching her. Joined to her. When it happens. So it won't just learn to undo crystal in me. It'll learn that crystal is the thing that's dangerous. All of it. Hers too."

"*Two reference points,*" said Rimuru, for Ciel. Slowly. "*The binding as it is in her, complete. And the binding as it moves in him, live, from the beginning. And the wheel undoing it, step by step, from inside.*" A pause. "*I would have the whole shape. The order. The centre.*" Another pause, longer. "*I could make a skill. In Rimuru. Not only for Mau. The inverse. To undo it in someone else.*"

Mau nodded.

"That's the plan," he said.



Nobody said anything for a long time.

Yuta did not speak. He stood beside Rimuru with his hand on the ring and his face very still, and did not say anything. He was waiting. Mau knew what he was waiting for. He knew because of the roof. Because of the rule they had made, the three of them, on the ridge of the house in the snow, before Gojo, before the bell: *if any of us starts carrying something he shouldn't, the other two say so*. And Yuta had added, later, quietly, in his own voice: *and when it's somebody's body, they speak first*.

It was Rimuru's body.

So Yuta waited for Rimuru.

Rimuru was looking at Mau.

He was not saying anything. His face, in human shape, was doing something Mau had seen it do very rarely. It was going very still, and very white, and very quiet, the way it had gone in the snow at the bottom of the south field with his face in his hands. And under the stillness, something was rising. Mau could see it come up. Slowly. Into Rimuru's jaw. Into his hands, which had closed. Into his eyes.

"You want me," said Rimuru, very quietly, "to *eat you*."

"Not eat. Hold."

"To eat you," said Rimuru, "and a woman made of a spell that turned her to crystal, and put you in my stomach, together. And then you're going to *let it take you*. On *purpose*. Inside me. And wait for the thing that nearly killed you in the south field to wake up and start *turning*." His voice was rising. "In *my stomach*. Where I can feel *all of it*. Where if it goes wrong—where if the wheel decides my stomach is an injury too—"

"It won't," said Mau.

"*You don't know that*—"

"No," said Mau. "I don't."

"That's the most—" Rimuru's voice cracked. "That's the most *ridiculous*—that's the most *reckless*, that's the most *you* thing you've ever—you nearly *died*, Mau, fifty days ago, on your back, with everything coming out of you, and now you want to *open the door on purpose*, inside me, and—"

*Rimuru,* said Ciel.

He stopped.

*It is the best idea,* said Ciel.

Rimuru stared at the air.

*I have been through it,* said Ciel. Aloud, now, through his mouth, so all of them could hear. Very calm. Very clear. *Forty times. While you were shouting. It is the best idea anyone could have had.* A pause. *It controls every variable. Inside your stomach, I can see everything. Every molecule of her. Every molecule of him. Every turn of the wheel. I can stop it at any moment. I can pull him out at any moment. Nothing leaves. Nothing spreads. There is no risk to her at all. None. She is held, and safe, and still, and I am reading her.* Another pause. *And I can make the skill. I could not make it any other way. There is no other way to get the centre.*

"And the risk?" said Rimuru. Hoarsely.

*All of it,* said Ciel, *sits with—*

She stopped.

There was a pause. A small one. The kind of pause Ciel did not usually make.

*—with the two idiots who accepted,* said Ciel.

Rimuru blinked.

"Hey," he said.

*Not my words,* said Ciel, primly. *They had a ceremony. There were sashes. Ori tied one. Milim tied the other.* A pause. *I was there. I was taking minutes.*

Rimuru stared at the air for a moment longer.

Then, all at once, he laughed.

It came out of him the way it had come out of him on the roof, in the snow, the first night. Short and helpless and cracked. He put his hand over his face. His shoulders shook.

"This is the most *Mau* idea," said Rimuru, through his hand, "I have ever heard in my entire life."

*Yes,* said Ciel. *It is also the best option.*

Rimuru took his hand away from his face. His eyes were wet. He looked at Mau. Then at the crystal woman, reaching down for the empty air. Then at Mau again.

"Five minutes," said Rimuru. "You said five. In the field it was everything at once. This is five minutes, inside me, with Ciel watching every molecule and her hand on the lever." He did not look away. "If it's more than five, she pulls you. If the wheel turns wrong, she pulls you. If you so much as *flinch*, she pulls you."

"If I get trapped," said Mau, "she takes the guardrails off. All of them."

The intake hall went silent again.

"I've talked to her about it," said Mau. Quietly. "Just now. While you were shouting. If it goes wrong, if the wheel gets stuck halfway, if I can't come back—she takes the walls down. Completely. And everything in me can come out and get me out." He looked at Rimuru. "It's not a sacrifice. It's the emergency exit. It's the worst thing that could happen, and it's still not me dying in your stomach." He paused. "Ciel agreed."

*I agreed,* said Ciel. *Reluctantly. I put that on the record as well.*

Rimuru looked at him for a long time.

Then he looked at Yuta.

Yuta had not said anything the whole time. He had stood there with his hand on the ring and waited for Rimuru, because it was Rimuru's body. And now Rimuru had spoken, and shouted, and laughed. And now, at last, it was Yuta's turn.

"Before you two idiots do anything," said Yuta.

Both of them turned and looked at him.

"*Ciel's words,*" said Rimuru and Mau, at exactly the same time.

Yuta almost smiled. It did not quite reach his face. He took his hand off the ring.

"We have to ask one person's permission," he said quietly. "Before anyone moves a finger."

"Obviously," said Mau.

"Obviously," said Rimuru. He rubbed his eyes with the back of his hand. "We weren't going to *not* ask. We just didn't want to say anything to her until we knew it was possible. In case it wasn't." He looked at the crystal. "I wasn't going to walk into that fort and give a little girl hope and then find out Ciel said no."

"I know," said Yuta. "I just wanted to hear you say it."



Frieren had not said anything the whole time.

She was standing a little apart from them, by the wall of the intake hall, with her staff, looking at the crystal woman. At the reaching hand. At the open mouth. At the hundred rainbows on the floor.

Mau went over to her.

He did not say anything. He stood beside her and looked at the crystal too.

"You'll be inside him," said Frieren, after a while. Very quietly. "Where I can't see."

"Yes."

"Where I can't reach."

"Yes."

She was quiet for a long moment.

"Five minutes," she said.

"Five minutes."

"I've waited longer," said Frieren. "For less." She did not look at him. "You said you'd tell me first. Before. If something ever called you." She paused. "Something called you. Just now. In this room."

"I'm telling you," said Mau.

"I know," said Frieren. "You told me in front of all of them. Before you'd done anything." She was quiet. "That's what I needed."

Mau looked at her.

She turned her head, at last, and looked up at him. Her green eyes were very calm. Not serene. Calm, the way deep water is calm.

"Go and ask her," said Frieren. "The little witch. Ask her properly." She looked back at the crystal. "I'll stay here. With her mother. So she's not alone while you're gone."

She sat down on the bench by the wall of the intake hall, with her staff across her knees, facing the crystal woman. Facing the reaching hand. And settled, the way she settled at the fence, at the bottom of a field, outside a white wall. The way she had always settled, Himmel had said, facing the way someone went.

Mau looked at her for a moment longer.

Then he turned round, and went out of the intake hall, past Jinshi in the door, and across the plaza, toward the fort. Rimuru on one side of him. Yuta on the other.

The three of them.

Gojo watched them go from the doorway, with his blindfold pushed up and his hands in his pockets.

"Huh," he said, to nobody. Very quietly. "The three musketeers."
