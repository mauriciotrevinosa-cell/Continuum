*When: Day 195, late morning. Frieren is on the bench in the intake hall, facing the crystal, with her staff across her knees. Gojo is in the doorway behind her. The whole house is in the plaza or at a window, and nobody is saying anything.*

The fort had closed round her.

That was the only word for it. When Qifrey carried her in through the arch, still crying, the fort had simply closed. Umaru had let the curtain fall behind them. And twenty small people in hoods had come in from every corner and every cushion and sat down round the two of them in a ring, close, without a word. Not touching. Not asking. Just there. A wall made of the fort.

Agott was at the front of the ring, with her arms folded, glaring at anyone who came near the curtain. Tetia was beside her, crying too, quietly, because Tetia cried when anyone cried. Richeh was awake. Ori was there, and Wren beside Ori, and Yuka with the white dog lying across the front of the ring like a gate. And in the middle of all of them, on the cushions, Qifrey sat with his back against the fort's wall and Coco in his arms, and she had not let go of him since the arch.

She was not screaming any more. She was not crying any more, not really. She was just holding on. Her face in his coat. Her fists in it. Very still.

When the curtain moved, the whole ring turned round at once.

Mau stood in the arch. Rimuru on one side of him. Yuta on the other.

Agott stood up.

"No," she said.

"Agott," said Mau.

"No," said Agott. Her voice was shaking. "Not now. Not anybody. She doesn't want anyone. Go *away*."

In the middle of the ring, Coco lifted her head.

Her face was blotched and swollen and wet. Her hair had come all the way loose. She looked at the three of them in the arch, over Qifrey's arm. And Mau watched her look at their faces. At his, and Rimuru's, and Yuta's. Three people who did not come into the fort, ever, together, all at once, unless something was very wrong or very important.

He watched her start to understand.

"Go away," said Coco. Very small. "Please. Not yet. I can't—" She stopped. "Please."

Mau did not go away.

He came in. Through the arch. Past Agott, who did not stop him, though she looked as if she wanted to. Into the ring. And he knelt down on the cushions in front of Coco and Qifrey. Not close. Not touching. At her level, the way you kneel in front of someone small.

Rimuru knelt on one side of him. Yuta on the other.

"Coco," said Mau.

She looked at him.

"I think we can help," said Mau.

The fort went completely silent.

Coco did not move. She did not breathe. She looked at him over Qifrey's arm with her swollen eyes, and her face did not do anything at all.

"You asked me once," said Mau. "On the atelier floor. The day the ink came back. If I thought I could be the one who finds the missing piece." He did not look away from her. "I said I didn't know. I said if I was, I'd do everything I could." He paused. "I think I might be. So this is me doing everything I can."

Coco did not move.

"I think we can help," said Mau again, quietly. "Maybe. There's a way. It came to me in the hall, with her. And Rimuru and Yuta and Ciel have gone through it. And Ciel says it's the best way anybody could find." He did not look away from her. "But before we move a finger. Before anybody does anything at all. We want your opinion. And your permission."

Coco stared at him.

"If you say no," said Mau, "we won't do anything. Nothing. We'll go away, and we won't bring it up again unless you do." He paused. "But we want you to hear the whole plan first. All of it. Before you answer. Every part. Even the parts you won't like." He looked at Qifrey. "Both of you."

Qifrey's arms had tightened round Coco. His face was very white.

"Will you hear it?" said Mau.

Coco did not answer for a long time.

Then, slowly, she let go of Qifrey's coat. One fist. Then the other. She sat up, in his arms, and wiped her face on her sleeve, hard, the way children wipe their faces when they have decided to stop crying. She looked at the three of them kneeling in front of her.

"Tell me," said Coco.



They told her.

All of it. Mau told it, mostly, because it was his plan, and Rimuru filled in what happened in his stomach, and Ciel spoke through Rimuru for the parts only Ciel could say. Yuta did not say anything. He knelt beside them with his hand on the ring and watched Coco's face the whole time, the way Mikasa watched a fight.

Rimuru taking her mother in, where it was safe and still and outside time. Ciel reading every part of her. Every molecule. Then Mau going in after her. Touching the crystal. Letting it take him. The wheel waking, and deciding it was an injury, and turning. Learning the shape. From inside. In a living body. And Mau joined to her mother the whole time, so the wheel would learn that all of it was dangerous. Two reference points. The centre that the woman in the pale book had never found. And then Ciel making a skill, in Rimuru. Not just for Mau. The inverse. To undo it in someone else.

In her.

Coco did not say anything through any of it. She sat in Qifrey's arms and listened with her swollen eyes very wide and her face very still. Once, when Mau said *the centre*, her hand moved, very slightly, toward the pocket of her dress where her private notebook was. Then it stopped.

When they had finished, nobody in the fort said anything for a long time.

Then Coco spoke.

"Two questions," she said.

They waited.

"Is there any risk to her?" said Coco. "To my mother. While you do it. Any. At all."

"No," said Mau.

"No," said Rimuru.

"*No,*" said Ciel, through Rimuru's mouth, very clearly. "*None. She is held. She is still. Nothing can reach her in there that I do not let reach her. She is not part of the risk. She is there so that I can see what the binding is, whole, beside what it does when it begins. She reduces the variables. She makes it safer.*" A pause. "*I would not have agreed otherwise.*"

Coco looked at Yuta. As if checking.

Yuta, who had not said a word, shook his head. Slowly. *No.*

Coco let out a breath.

"Second question," she said. "What's the risk?" She looked at Mau. "Not to her. The risk. Whose is it?"

Mau looked back at her.

He thought about lying. Not lying. Making it smaller. He could say *a little tired*. He could say *Ciel's watching*. He could say any number of true things that were not the whole truth, and she would believe him, because she wanted to.

"For Rimuru, none," said Mau. "He holds us. That's all he does. For Yuta, none. He's there to watch the walls and keep Rimuru steady." He did not look away from her. "The risk is mine. I'm the one who lets it in."

The fort was very quiet.

"It'll start on my hand," said Mau. "And go up. The way it went through her. And the wheel has to turn fast enough to catch it before it gets too far." He paused. "If it does, it learns the shape, and undoes it, and I come out tired. Very tired. Probably for a few days."

"And if it doesn't?" said Coco.

Mau was quiet for a moment.

"Then Ciel takes the walls down," he said. "All of them. Everything in me comes out at once, the way it did in the field. And it gets me out." He held her eyes. "That's not a sacrifice. I want you to know that. I'm not going in there to give anything up. I've talked to Ciel. It's the emergency exit. The worst thing that can happen, and it's still not me staying in there." He paused. "It just means the field again. And Shoko shouting at Gojo again. And a lot of people very angry with me again."

"*I would be very angry,*" said Ciel, through Rimuru. "*I have put that on the record.*"

Coco did not laugh. But something moved in her face.

"How long?" she said.

"Five minutes," said Mau. "Ciel thinks five. Eight turns, maybe. Maybe fewer." He paused. "At the controlled trial, a long time ago now, I held a path open for nineteen minutes. With Frieren and Rimuru beside me, and Ciel counting. With the walls up." He almost smiled. "If I held a path for nineteen minutes, I can hold five."

Coco looked at him for a long time.

"Sure you can hold five?" she said.

Mau looked back at her. At the small girl in her teacher's arms, with ink on her fingers and her eyes swollen and her whole world, for years, turned to crystal behind her.

"I can try," said Mau.

He did not say *yes*. He did not say *of course*. He said *I can try*, because it was true, and because she had asked him for the truth, and because she was a witch, and witches knew the difference.

Coco knew the difference.

She closed her eyes.



She did not answer at once. She sat in Qifrey's arms with her eyes closed for a long time. Long enough that Agott, at the front of the ring, looked round at the others with her face very white. Long enough that Tetia stopped crying, to listen. Long enough that the white dog lifted its head.

When she opened her eyes, she did not look at Mau. She looked at the pocket of her dress.

She took out her private notebook.

She opened it. To the page with the two lattices. The outer one, and the small one nested inside it, with the empty centre. And beside the empty centre, the word she had written on a snowy night a long time ago and never crossed out. *Mau?*

And under it, in her careful letters, the three lines.

*Not until Fern says.*

*And not unless he wants to.*

*Find another way first.*

She looked at it for a long moment. Then she looked up at Mau.

"I wrote these," said Coco. "After the tea. When Echidna said about the wheel." She held the notebook out, so he could see. "I looked for another way. For five weeks. There isn't one."

"I know," said Mau.

"So that's that one." She put her finger on the third line. "And the first one. Fern." She looked at him. "We have to ask Fern. I promised."

"We'll ask Fern," said Mau.

"And this one," said Coco. Her finger moved up. To the middle line. *And not unless he wants to.* She looked at him over the notebook. Very straight. Very serious. A witch asking a witch's question.

"Do you want to?" said Coco. "Not because I'm crying. Not because it's sad. Not because you're the one who always goes in front." She did not blink. "Do *you* want to?"

Mau did not answer at once.

He thought about it. Properly. Kneeling on the cushions in the fort, with twenty small faces in hoods round him, and Rimuru on one side and Yuta on the other. He thought about The Noise, far back behind the walls, turned toward her since the doorway of the intake hall, reading, and about the thought that had come up under it, his own: *I think I can help with that.* He thought about the crystal woman reaching down for the empty air. About Frieren on the bench in the intake hall, facing her. About a little girl drawing something she thought would be beautiful.

"Something in me turned toward her," said Mau. "The Noise. It read her the minute I walked in, the way it read the gate. That's true." He paused. "And I want to. Me. Separately." He did not look away. "I'd want to even if it had never turned. I'd want to if it was only me and a spoon." He almost smiled. "I can't do it with a spoon. But I'd want to."

Coco looked at him for a long moment.

Then she took her pencil out of the spine of the notebook. And under *And not unless he wants to*, very small, she made a mark. A tick.

She looked at it.

Then she closed the notebook and put it back in her pocket, and turned round in Qifrey's arms, so that she could see his face.

"Qifrey," she said.

He looked down at her.

He had not said anything the whole time. He had sat with his back against the fort's wall and held her, and listened to all of it, every part, with his face very white and his arms very tight. He had said *no* to Echidna, at the tea, in front of everyone, about exactly this. About the wheel. About the shape of the thing she never found.

"It's not mine to say," said Qifrey.

His voice was very quiet.

"At the tea," he said, "Echidna held it out across a table. Like a thing you could take. She didn't care what it cost, or who paid." He looked down at Coco. "That's not what this is. He came here and knelt down and asked you. He told you every part. Even the parts you wouldn't like." He looked at Mau, over her head. "It's not mine to say. It's yours. And his." He paused. "I'm afraid. But it's not mine."

Coco looked at him for a long time.

Then she turned back to the three of them.

"Yes," said Coco.



Fern was at the long table with the ledger when they came in. She had not gone out into the plaza with everyone else. She had stayed, the way she always stayed, at the head of the table, with the book open, waiting for whoever would come and sit on the bench and ask her something.

They came. Coco first, with her hand in Qifrey's. Then the three of them.

Coco sat down on the bench across from Fern. Where she had sat the morning before, to ask about the coin.

"You said," said Coco, "if I ever wanted to fix her, I had to come back and sit on this bench and ask you again." She swallowed. "Separately. A different question."

"I did," said Fern.

"I'm asking," said Coco.

Fern looked at her. Then up, at Mau, standing behind her with Rimuru and Yuta.

"I said I'd want to know what it costs," said Fern. "And who pays."

Mau told her.

All of it, again. Shorter, this time, because Fern did not need it long. She needed it exact. The stomach. The touch. The wheel. Eight turns, five minutes. Two reference points. The skill. The walls, and the emergency exit, and the field again if it went wrong. Exhaustion. A few days.

Fern wrote it down. All of it. In her small upright hand, in the ledger, on a fresh page. When he had finished, she looked at it for a long time.

"Five minutes," said Fern.

"Five minutes."

"I'll count them," said Fern. She looked up. "From the moment you touch it. I'll stand outside, at the door of the intake hall, with the ledger. And I'll count. Out loud. And if it gets to six, Ciel pulls you, whatever the wheel's doing." She looked at Rimuru. "Tell her."

"*She heard,*" said Rimuru, for Ciel. "*Six. I will pull him at six.*"

Fern nodded.

She looked at the page for a moment longer. Then she turned the ledger round, so that it faced Coco across the table. And held out the pen.

"It's your mother," said Fern. "You sign it. Not me."

Coco looked at the pen.

She took it. Her hand was shaking. She signed at the bottom of the page, under all of it, under Fern's small upright account of what it cost and who paid. *Coco.* In her careful letters. With a small drop of ink at the end, where her hand had shaken.

Then she held the pen out to Mau.

He took it. He signed under her. Then Rimuru. Then Yuta.

Fern took the ledger back. She looked at the four names. Then, under them, she wrote one more line, in her own hand.

*Approved. F.*

And then, after a moment, smaller:

*Come back.*
