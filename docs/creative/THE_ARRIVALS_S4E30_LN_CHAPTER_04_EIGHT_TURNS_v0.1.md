## Chapter 16 — Eight Turns

*When: Day 195, noon, the intake hall of Arrival House. Four names and "Approved. F." in the ledger. Shoko is on the step outside with her bag. The plaza is full and silent. Echidna has walked across from the north wing, the way she walked out once to watch Nokotan go home, and is standing at the back of the crowd in black.*

They cleared the intake hall of everything but the crystal.

Jinshi did it. The benches along the walls, except the one Frieren sat on. The register desk. The rack of coats that nobody had worn in months. He carried them out himself, one at a time, without being asked, and stacked them in the plaza, and came back for the next. When the hall was empty, it was very large and very bright. The light came down from the high windows onto the bare boards and into the crystal woman, and broke into its hundred small rainbows round her feet, and there was nothing else in the room to catch it.

Fern stood in the doorway with the ledger open in her arms.

Coco stood beside her. Qifrey behind Coco, with both hands on her shoulders. Not holding her back. Just there.

Frieren had not moved from the bench by the wall. She sat facing the crystal with her staff across her knees, the way she had sat since the morning, and she did not look at anyone else.

Yuta stood at the edge of the bare floor with his hand on the ring, and his cursed energy up round him like a dark film, very thin, very steady. Watching the walls. Not the walls of the hall. The other ones.

Gojo was at the door, behind Fern. His blindfold was pushed up. He did not say anything.

And at the back of the crowd in the plaza, at the very edge, in her black dress, with her pale hands folded, Echidna stood and watched the doorway of Arrival House with an expression that nobody could read. Qifrey had seen her. He had looked at her once, across the plaza, for a long moment. And then he had turned his back on her, and put his hands on Coco's shoulders, and not looked again.

Rimuru went to the middle of the hall.

He stood in front of the crystal woman in human shape for a moment, looking up at her. At the reaching hand. At the open mouth.

"I'm sorry," he said to her, very quietly. "This is going to be strange. I promise it's safe." He paused. "I'm going to hold you for a little while. That's all."

Then he let go of his shape.

It went the way it always went. Not a melting. A loosening. The man in the coat became, all at once and without any fuss, a slime. Blue. Pale. Clear at the edges. Not the small slime who slept on a pillow in the loft or sat in a bowl at the long table. A large one. Larger than anyone in the house had seen him in a long time. He rose up off the boards like a wave rising, smooth and gentle and very quiet, until he was taller than the crystal woman. And then he leaned forward, over her, and came down round her, the way a wave comes down round a stone on a beach.

And she was gone.

Not gone. Inside. Coco made a sound. You could see her, for a moment, through the clear blue, the reaching hand and the open mouth and the hundred small rainbows, suspended in the slime like a fly in amber. And then the blue closed and deepened, and she was not there to see any more. She was somewhere else. Somewhere still.

*She's held,* said Ciel, in Mau's head. *She's still. She's safe. I'm reading her.* A pause. *Oh. Oh, there is so much of her.*

Mau looked at the doorway.

Coco was looking back at him. Her face was very white. Her eyes were very wide. Qifrey's hands were on her shoulders.

Mau did not say anything to her. There was nothing to say that he had not already said in the fort. He nodded to her, once.

Then he walked across the bare floor to the great blue slime that was his brother, and put his hand on it.

"Ready," he said.

*Ready,* said Rimuru. In his head, and in his own voice, from somewhere inside the blue. *Come in. Don't be weird about it.*

Mau stepped forward, and the slime took him.



It was dark inside, and still.

Not dark like night. Dark like the deep end of water, with no water in it. He was not wet. He was not cold. He was standing on nothing, in a soft blue dimness that went on in every direction, with no floor and no walls and no sound at all. Not even his own breathing. It was the quietest place he had ever been.

She was in front of him.

The crystal woman. Standing on nothing, in the blue dark, exactly as she had stood in the intake hall. Clear all the way through. Reaching down. Her mouth open on a word. There was no light in here to break into rainbows. She simply stood there, very clear, very still, a woman made of glass in the deep.

*I'm here,* said Ciel. Very close. Closer than she usually was. *Every part of you. Every part of her. I can see it all. I'm counting.* A pause. *Rimuru's here too. He's holding everything. Yuta's on the walls. Fern is at the door with the ledger.* Another pause. *Frieren hasn't moved.*

"I know," said Mau.

He could not hear his own voice. But Ciel could.

He looked at the crystal woman.

He had planned to touch her shoulder. Or her arm. Anywhere. It did not matter where. Ciel had said it did not matter where, as long as he was touching her when it began. He had planned it in the fort.

He did not touch her shoulder.

He looked at the hand. The reaching one. Reaching forward and down. Toward something small. Toward the height of a child's head. Toward where a little girl had been standing, years ago, in a room by a stream, the moment it closed.

He knelt down.

He knelt on nothing, in the blue dark, until his head was at the height of a child's. And he put his hand up. Into hers. Into the crystal hand that had been reaching for Coco for years and never arrived.

It was cold.

It was very cold. And then it was not.

*It's started,* said Ciel.

He felt it go into his fingers.

Not pain. That was the first thing, the thing he had not known and could not have known until now. It did not hurt. It went into the tips of his fingers like cold water going into cloth, and spread. Down his fingers. Into his palm. And where it went, his hand stopped being a hand he could feel, and became something very still, and very clear, and very quiet. And the feeling it left was not cold. It was the opposite of cold.

It was like someone putting a blanket on you.

He looked down at his hand in hers. It was clear. Glass. He could see the blue dark through it. He could see the crystal woman's fingers through his fingers.

*Wrist,* said Ciel. *It's at the wrist. Mau—*

"Ciel," he said. "Tell Rimuru. Tell him to tell Coco."

*Tell her what?*

"That it doesn't hurt," said Mau. "That it doesn't hurt to crystallize. It's like someone put a blanket on you." He watched the clearness creep past his wrist. "And five minutes. Tell her five minutes."



In the intake hall, the great blue slime said, in Rimuru's voice, very gently, toward the door:

"Coco."

Coco went rigid under Qifrey's hands.

"He says to tell you," said Rimuru. "That it doesn't hurt. To crystallize." A pause. "He says it's like someone put a blanket on you." Another pause. "And five minutes. He says five minutes."

Coco did not move.

She stood in the doorway of the intake hall with her teacher's hands on her shoulders and her eyes on the blue slime in the middle of the bare floor. And her face did something nobody in the doorway had ever seen it do.

It let go.

Not of anything you could see. Something underneath. Something she had been holding since a room by a stream, years ago. Since the moment it closed. Since she had stood there and watched the spell go into her mother like water closing over a stone, and her mother's face, reaching for her, frightened, and had thought, every day after, every single day, *it must have hurt*. *It must have hurt so much*. *I did that, and it hurt her, and she was frightened, and it hurt*.

It doesn't hurt.

It's like someone put a blanket on you.

Coco's knees went.

Qifrey caught her. He did not lift her this time. He went down with her, onto the boards of the doorway, and knelt with her, with his arms round her from behind. And she knelt there in his arms and cried. Not the way she had cried in the fort. Not screaming. Quietly. With her face in her hands. The way you cry when something has finally been put down.

"One," said Fern.

She said it out loud, very clearly, from the doorway, with the ledger open in her arms, looking at the sun's line on the floor. She did not look at Coco. She did not stop counting.

"One minute."



Inside, the wheel came.

It came before the clearness reached his elbow. Mau felt it come. Not the way it had come in the south field, roaring, pressing, everything at once. Quietly. From behind the walls. It came up and out, through the place where his shadow would have been if there had been any light in here to make a shadow, and it rose. Over his head. In the blue dark.

The wheel.

Pale. Eight-spoked. The size of a cartwheel. Hanging in the dark over his kneeling head, very still, the way it had hung over Sukuna, once, a long time ago, in a city.

*There it is,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *There it is.*

And as Mau watched, looking up, the wheel began to go clear.

From the rim inward. The same clearness that was in his hand, his wrist, his forearm. It crept over the pale wheel like frost creeping over a window. One spoke. Then two. Then all of it. Until the wheel hung over his head made of crystal. Clear as the woman. Clear as his hand.

It took the binding.

Then it turned.

One click. One eighth of a full turn. Slow. Heavy. The crystal spokes moving through the dark with a sound he could not hear but could feel, in his teeth, in his chest, in the place behind his breastbone where things lifted their heads.

*One,* said Ciel. *Turn one.*

The clearness in his arm stopped.

It had been at his elbow. Creeping. And now it stopped. It did not go back. It did not go forward. It simply stopped, where it was, at the elbow, like water that has reached the place where it is going to freeze.

*It's adapted,* said Ciel. Fast now. Very clear. *It's adapted to stop it advancing. The binding can't go further. It's stopped.* A pause. *Now it has to go back. It's waiting for the regression. It's learning how.*

"Is it working?" said Mau.

*It is reading,* said Ciel. *It is reading the binding. In you. From the beginning. The order it went in. Fingers. Palm. Wrist. And in her—* A pause. A long one. *Oh. In her. It's reading her too. Through your hand. You were right. Joined. It's reading both.*



"Two," said Fern, at the door. "Two minutes."

The great blue slime in the middle of the intake hall did not move. But from inside it, in Rimuru's voice, quietly, steadily, came the count. Not Fern's. Ciel's.

"Turn two," said Rimuru. For Coco. Toward the door. "It's the second turn. The crystal in his arm stopped at the elbow. It's not going any further. The wheel is reading it." A pause. "Ciel says it's reading her mother too. Through his hand. He's holding her hand."

Coco lifted her face out of her hands.

"Her hand?" she said. Her voice was broken. "Which—which hand?"

The slime was quiet for a moment.

"The one that was reaching," said Rimuru, very gently. "He knelt down. To the height of—" He stopped. "He knelt down, and put his hand in the one that was reaching. That's where it started."

Coco did not say anything.

She knelt in the doorway, in Qifrey's arms, and looked at the blue slime with her wet swollen face. And Qifrey, behind her, holding her, closed his eyes.



*Three,* said Ciel.

The wheel turned. Crystal spokes in the blue dark. One more eighth.

*Four.*

Mau knelt on nothing with his hand in the crystal hand and his arm clear to the elbow, and the wheel turning over his head. He could not feel his arm. He could feel the wheel. Every turn. Every click. Like a key turning in a lock, one notch at a time, in a door that had been shut for years.

And with every turn, he felt something else.

He felt the shape.

Not with his eyes. His eyes were not doing anything. With something underneath them. With the part of him that the wheel was using. He felt the shape of the thing that had gone into the woman in front of him, years ago, in a room by a stream. The order it had gone in. The way it had moved through her.

It had gone in through her hand.

The reaching one. First. Before anything else. Into her fingers, her palm, her wrist. And then up her arm. And then, not into her head, not into her chest, not anywhere else. Into her heart. Straight into her heart, along the line of the reaching arm, as if it were following the reach. As if the reach were a road, and it had walked down it.

And from her heart, everywhere. All at once. Every part of her.

*The centre,* said Ciel. Very softly. *Mau. That's the centre. That's what the woman in the book never found.* A pause. *It went in along the reach. The shape it took in her is the shape of her reaching. Toward—*

She did not finish.

She did not need to.

*Five,* said Ciel.

The wheel turned.

And the clearness in his arm began, very slowly, to go back.



"Three," said Fern. "Three minutes."

"Turn five," said Rimuru. "It's going back." His voice cracked, for the first time. "Coco. The crystal in his arm is going back. It's past his elbow, going back down. It's at his forearm." A pause. "It's working."

Coco made a sound.

In the doorway behind her, Gojo had not moved. He was standing with his blindfold pushed up, looking at the blue slime with his bare sky-coloured eyes. He could not see inside it. Nobody could see inside it. But he was looking at it as if he could hear something, very far away, that nobody else could hear.

"Huh," he said, very quietly, to nobody.

On the bench by the wall, Frieren had not moved at all. Her staff was across her knees. Her hands were on her staff. She was facing the slime. Facing the place where Mau had gone. Her face was perfectly calm.

Her knuckles, on the staff, were white.



*Six.*

The wheel turned. The clearness was at his wrist.

*Seven.*

It was in his palm. Going back. Out of his palm, into his fingers. He could feel his palm again. Warm. Alive. The blanket coming off.

*It has it,* said Ciel. Fast. Almost breathless, if Ciel could have been breathless. *It has the whole shape. Mau, I have it. I have the whole map. Both reference points. The binding in her, complete. The binding in you, from the beginning, and the wheel undoing it, step by step.* A pause. *I have the centre.*

"Then finish it," said Mau.

*One more.*

The wheel turned.

The eighth click.

It was different. He felt it before it happened. The other seven had been notches. Partial. A key going round one step at a time. The eighth was the last step. The key going all the way round. The lock opening.

The wheel completed.

All eight spokes, all the way round, back to where they had started. And as it came round to the beginning, the crystal went out of it. All at once. Not like frost melting. Like a breath let go. The wheel was pale again, over his head, in the blue dark, ordinary, the way it had always been.

And the clearness went out of his fingers.

The last of it. From the tips. Gone. His hand was his hand. Warm. Alive. Holding a crystal hand that was still crystal, still reaching, still clear, but that he could feel now, cold and smooth, under his own warm fingers.

*Done,* said Ciel.

The wheel went back down. Through the place where his shadow would have been. Behind the walls. Quiet. Asleep.

*Walls up,* said Ciel. *All of them. Holding. It went back down on its own. It didn't fight. It didn't want anything else.* A pause. *Mau. It worked.*

He knelt in the blue dark with his warm hand in the crystal hand.

He could not feel anything else. His whole body had gone very far away. Very heavy. Like the end of the longest day there had ever been.

"Ciel," he said. "Can you make it?"

*I'm making it,* said Ciel. *Now. While you go out. The skill. Not just the undoing in you. The inverse. For her.* A pause. *Go out, Mau. Rimuru's letting you out. Go out and lie down before you fall down.*

He did not want to let go of the hand.

He looked at it. The crystal fingers round his. Reaching. For years. For something at the height of a child's head.

"I'm sorry it took so long," he said to her. Very quietly. Though she could not hear. "She's outside. She's right outside. She's been waiting."

Then he let go.



"Five," said Fern, at the door.

She said it very clearly, looking at the sun's line on the floor. Five minutes.

And the great blue slime in the middle of the intake hall leaned forward, very gently, and opened. And Mau came out of it.

He came out on his knees. He did not stand up. He tried. He got one foot under him and his whole body said *no*, very clearly, and he stayed where he was, on his knees on the bare boards of the intake hall, in the light from the high windows, with his head down.

He looked at his right hand.

It was a hand. Warm. Pink. His.

He held it up, toward the doorway. Toward Coco.

"I told you," said Mau. His voice came out like gravel, from very far away. "I managed to adapt." He tried to smile. "Ciel has it. She's changing it so it can go the other way. For her."

He tried to stand up again.

The floor tilted.

Coco was across the hall before Qifrey could catch her. Before anyone could. Out of his arms, across the bare boards, in four steps, with her hair all the way loose and her face wet. And she caught him. A girl half his size. She caught him as he went sideways, with both arms round him, and went down with him onto the boards, and held on.

"*Thank you*," said Coco.

She said it into his shoulder. Over and over. Crying.

"Thank you. Thank you. Thank you. *Thank you*—"

Mau could not say anything. He could not lift his arms. He knelt on the boards with a little witch holding him up, crying *thank you* into his shirt, and the light coming down from the high windows onto both of them. And he let her.

Behind them, by the wall, Frieren stood up off the bench.

She did not come over. Not yet. She stood there with her staff in her hand, looking at the two of them on the floor. At Mau's hand, pink and warm, lying open on the boards beside him.

Her knuckles were not white any more.

"Five minutes," said Frieren. Very quietly. To nobody.

At the door, Fern closed the ledger. She did not write anything. She had counted, and he had come back, and she did not need to write that down. She would remember it.

And at the back of the crowd in the plaza, very far away, in her black dress, with her pale hands folded, Echidna turned round and walked back toward the north wing alone. She did not wait to see the rest. She had seen what she had come to see.

The shape of the thing the woman in the pale book had never found.
