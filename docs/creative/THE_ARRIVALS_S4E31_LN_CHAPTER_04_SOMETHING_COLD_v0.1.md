*When: The evening of Day 211, the same day. It is still hot after sunset. Jiji has worn a blanket in the sun for eleven hours and has drunk nine cups of hot tea. Nobody outside the Dandadan end of the table knows why.*

It was too hot for soup.

Everyone said so. Momo made soup anyway, because Momo always made soup, and because it was leek and potato, and because the summer had come on so suddenly that the kitchen had not had time to think of anything else. But the common room was close and warm with the day's heat still in the clay walls, and the windows were open, and there was not a breath of wind. And the long table ate its soup slowly, and sweated, and complained.

Jiji ate his with a blanket round his shoulders.

He sat at the Dandadan end of the long table, between Okarun and Momo, with Aira across from him and Vamola beside Aira and Seiko on her high seat above them, fanning herself with her paper fan. Turbo Granny sat on the edge of Okarun's bowl. And every few minutes, without being asked, Momo topped up Jiji's tea from a small pot she kept beside her, which was steaming.

"I'm going to die," said Jiji. Pleasantly. Sweating. "In a blanket. In summer. Of tea."

"Better than the other thing," said Okarun.

"You keep *saying* that."

At the cold-hearth end, Gojo was fanning himself with his blindfold, which he had taken off and was flapping like a handkerchief. "Can't somebody *do* something," he said. "Rimuru. Make it cold. You can do ice. I've seen you do ice."

"I'm eating," said Rimuru, who was a slime in a bowl.

"Frieren," said Gojo. "You're an elf. Elves have spells for everything. Do a spell."

Frieren, halfway down the bench beside Mau, did not look up from her soup.

"I have a spell," she said.

The long table looked at her.

"Frost," said Frieren. She put her spoon down. "From the grimoire. The one in the mimic. I've been reading it every night." She paused. "It's not very strong. It makes frost. On things. That's all." She held up one finger. "It's for cooling drinks."

"*Do it,*" said Gojo, Milim, Yuka and half the fort, all at once.

Frieren considered this for a moment. Then she picked up her own cup of water, and held it up, and her lips moved. A very small, very pale light came off the tip of her finger. And the water in the cup went, all at once, so cold that a skin of frost bloomed on the outside of it, white and glittering, and a thin breath of cold mist rose off it into the hot room.

The long table made a sound of pure longing.

"Again," said Gojo. "Mine. Do mine."

Frieren sighed. But she did it. She went down the long table with her finger out, the way Rem went down it with the teapot, touching each cup. Gojo's. Shoko's. Yuta's. Maki's. Milim's, twice, because Milim asked. And at each one the water went white with frost, and a little breath of cold came off it, and someone sighed with relief.

She came to the Dandadan end.

She touched Aira's cup. Vamola's. Seiko's, which Seiko held out without a word over her fan. Momo's. Okarun's.

She reached across, to touch Jiji's.

And as her finger passed over his shoulder, over the edge of the blanket, the small pale light at the tip of it sent off, the way a candle sends off a spark, one tiny flake of frost. It drifted down through the hot air. Very slowly. Glittering.

And landed on the back of Jiji's neck. On the bare skin. Just above the blanket.

Cold.



Okarun saw it land.

He saw it the way you see the one thing in a room that matters. A single glittering flake, the size of a grain of salt, settling on the back of Jiji's neck. On the bare skin. And Jiji's whole body going very still, all at once, the way a body goes still when something cold touches it unexpectedly.

"*Jiji*—" said Momo.

Okarun did not think.

His hand was already moving. His bowl was in it. His soup. Leek and potato, with the good bacon, still hot. He threw it.

All of it. Straight at Jiji. At his neck, his shoulders, his head. And at exactly the same moment, from the other side, Momo threw hers. And from across the table, half out of her seat, Aira threw hers. And Seiko, on her high seat, dropped her fan and shouted something in a voice nobody had ever heard her use. And Turbo Granny shrieked *HOT WATER, HOT WATER, SOMEBODY GET HOT WATER* from the edge of the empty bowl.

Three bowls of hot leek and potato soup hit Jiji at once.

He sat there.

Soaked. Dripping. Soup in his hair. Soup down the back of his neck. Soup running off his chin and his nose and the ends of his fingers. A piece of the good bacon slid slowly down his forehead and fell off the end of his nose into his lap.

The long table had gone completely silent.

Every face at it was turned toward the Dandadan end. Forty faces. Frozen. Frieren, standing behind Jiji with her finger still out and the light on it fading. Gojo, with his frosted cup halfway to his mouth. Mau, with his spoon in the air. Fern, at the head of the table, with her pen stopped over the ledger.

Nobody understood what had just happened.

Okarun and Momo and Aira were staring at Jiji. Not at the soup. At his face. At his eyes. Waiting. Okarun had his hands up, ready. Momo had her hands up. Aira was halfway across the table.

Waiting for the thing.

Jiji sat very still in his soup, with his eyes closed.

One second.

Two.

Five.

Ten.

Nothing happened.

Jiji opened his eyes.

They were his eyes. Brown. Ordinary. A little stung from the soup. He blinked them. He looked down at himself. At the blanket, soaked through. At the bacon in his lap. At his hands, dripping. He turned them over, slowly. He looked at them for a long time.

"Huh," said Jiji.

His voice was quite normal.

"Nothing happened," he said.

Okarun did not lower his hands.

"Jiji," he said. Very carefully. "Are you—is it—"

"Nothing," said Jiji. He was still looking at his hands. "I felt it. The cold. On my neck." He put his hand up to the back of his neck, where the flake had landed, and touched it. "And usually. When I get cold. I feel it. Coming up. From behind. Like something waking up in the back of my head." He took his hand away. "I always feel it. Every time. Since it got in me."

"And?" said Momo. Her voice was very small.

Jiji looked at her.

"It's not there," he said.

The long table did not breathe.

"It's not there," said Jiji again. Slowly. As if he were checking. He closed his eyes again, and was very still, and seemed to be looking for something. Inside. In the back of his own head. For a long time.

Then he opened them.

"It's gone," he said. His voice had gone strange. "It's not *there*. It's not asleep. It's not waiting. It's *not there*." He looked at Momo. At Okarun. At Aira. "There's nothing in the back of my head. For the first time since—" He stopped. "There's nothing there but me."



It was Rimuru who explained it. Or Ciel, through him.

"*The petition named him,*" said Rimuru, from his bowl, in Ciel's flat clear voice. Into the silence. "*Only him.*"

Every face turned toward the slime.

"*I have been thinking about it all day,*" said Ciel. "*Since he came. I read him at the eighth bell, the way I read everyone who comes. And I found him. All of him. Body. Mind. Memories. Skills. Scars.*" A pause. "*And nothing else. There was a place in him where something had been. A shape. Like a room someone has moved out of. But nothing in it.*" Another pause. "*I did not understand it until now.*"

"Understand what?" said Fern, from the head of the table. Her pen was moving.

"*The coin brings the person,*" said Ciel, through Rimuru. "*Not what lives in them.*"

The long table was very quiet.

"*What is theirs comes,*" said Ciel. "*Their body. Their techniques. Their scars. What they carry.*" A pause. "*Gojo's eyes came, because they are his. Yuka's shadows came, because they are hers. Shoko's hands. Byakuya's forty years of axles.*" Another pause. "*But what lives in a person without being that person stays behind. It is not named in the petition. The petition names only him.*" A pause. "*So the thing that lived in him did not come. It is still in his world. Without him.*"

"And Coco's mother?" said Fern sharply. "She came as crystal."

"*A condition is not a passenger,*" said Ciel. "*The binding was not living in her. It was something that had been done to her. It was the state she was in. So she came in that state.*" A pause. "*He was not in a state. He had a passenger. The passenger was not invited.*"

Fern wrote. Fast. In her small upright hand, on a fresh page of the ledger. Everyone at the long table could hear the pen.

When she had finished, she turned the ledger round, so the table could see.

*THE COIN BRINGS THE PERSON. NOT WHAT LIVES IN THEM.*

*What is theirs comes: body, techniques, conditions, scars, what they carry.*

*What lives in them without being them stays behind.*

*(Day 211. Learned at dinner. With soup.)*

She looked at it for a moment. Then she added one more line, smaller:

*Every petition from now on: ask what lives in them.*

She turned the ledger back round and closed it.



Jiji was crying.

He did not seem to know it. He sat at the Dandadan end of the long table in his soaked blanket, with soup in his hair and bacon in his lap, and tears running down his face and mixing with the soup. He was not making any sound. He was just sitting there, very still, with his hands open in his lap, crying.

"It's gone," he said again. To nobody. "It's really gone."

Momo put her arms round him.

Soup and all. She did not care. She put her arms round his soaked shoulders from the side and held on, and put her face against his wet hair, and did not say anything.

Okarun did not put his arms round him. Okarun sat on his other side, with his empty soup bowl still in his hand, and looked at the table. His face was red. His jaw was set.

"Good," he said. Very quietly. To the table.

Jiji turned his head and looked at him.

"Good," said Okarun again, without looking up. "That's—good." His voice did something. "That's good, Jiji."

Jiji looked at him for a long moment, with tears and soup running down his face.

Then, slowly, the grin came back. Not all at once. A little at a time. From one side.

"The coin worked," said Jiji.

Okarun closed his eyes.

"Shut up," he said.

"The coin *worked*, Okarun—"

"*Shut UP*—"

"You brought me without it. Without the thing. You didn't know that would happen, nobody knew, and you brought me anyway, and it *worked*, and it's *gone*." Jiji was laughing now, through the crying, through the soup. "Your coin. Your *love*. It left the Evil Eye behind—"

"That's *not* what Ciel said—"

"It's *basically* what Ciel said—"

"*Ciel*," said Okarun desperately, to the slime in the bowl. "Tell him that's not what you said."

"*It is not precisely what I said,*" said Ciel, through Rimuru. A pause. "*But I can see why he would hear it that way. I will not correct him.*"

Okarun put his face in his hands.

Jiji put his soaked arm round Okarun's shoulders and pulled him in, sideways, against his soup-covered side, and held on. And Okarun let him. He sat there with his face in his hands and his best friend's wet arm round his shoulders, getting soup all over his coat, and did not move away.



At the head of the long table, Fern had opened the ledger again.

She turned to the page of the List. To the line she had written on the morning of the two-hundred-and-tenth day. *Okarun: Jiji.* With *(For Momo)* crossed out beside it, very neatly, with one line.

She looked at it for a moment.

Then, beside it, in the margin, in very small upright letters, she wrote:

*HERE.*

And under that, smaller still, so small that only someone who leaned very close to the page would ever see it:

*(For him.)*



Frieren was still standing behind Jiji's chair, at the Dandadan end of the long table, with her finger out. The light on it had gone out. She was looking down at the back of Jiji's neck. At the small wet place where one flake of frost had landed, and melted, and been covered in soup.

She looked at it for a long time.

Then she looked at her own finger.

"I didn't know," said Frieren. Very quietly. To Mau, who had come down the bench to stand beside her. "About the cold. About the thing in him." She looked at the soaked boy, laughing and crying with his arm round Okarun. "I could have—"

"Nothing happened," said Mau.

"I know." Frieren lowered her hand. "But I didn't *know* nothing would happen." She was quiet for a moment. "Nobody did. Not even them. They threw their soup before they'd even thought." She looked at Okarun and Momo and Aira. At the empty bowls in their hands. "Three bowls. At once. Without thinking."

Mau looked at them too.

"That's what you'd do," he said. "If it was me."

Frieren turned her head and looked at him.

"Throw soup?"

"Throw whatever you had," said Mau. "Before you'd thought."

Frieren looked at him for a long moment, in the hot evening, in the common room, with forty people round them who had just watched three people throw soup at a boy for love.

"I'd throw a lot more than soup," said Frieren.

She went back to her place on the bench. She sat down. She picked up her cup of water, which was no longer cold, and looked at it. And then, very deliberately, she touched it with one finger, and it went white with frost again, and a small breath of cold mist rose off it.

She drank.

At the cold-hearth end, Gojo, who had watched the whole thing with his bare blue eyes and his frosted cup forgotten in his hand, said, to Shoko, very quietly:

"I want a coin."

Shoko looked at him.

"I want one," said Gojo. "So I can find out who I'd throw soup at." He looked down at his cup. "I think I already know." He paused. "I just want to see if it'd work."

Shoko did not say anything for a long moment. She looked at him. At his face, which was not doing anything bright for once.

Then she picked up his frosted cup, and drank from it, and put it back down in front of him.

"It'd work," said Shoko.
