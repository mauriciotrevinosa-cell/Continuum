# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 1 — After Sleeping

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 1
**Continuity state:** Day 221, afternoon into night | the wall remains a proposal | Mikasa brings Eren home from the north end; fear and disagreement do not separate them | the household rests and maintains the watch | Mau and Frieren wait for a rested discussion; no overnight construction or final decision

*When: Day 221, after the breakfast at which Eren proposed closing the city. Hollis is still in Fallow. The thing from the low woods has gone; its prints stop at the lime. Nobody has yet agreed to build a wall.*

Mikasa found Eren with the empty porridge bowl.

He was sitting on the last step at the north end. He had carried the bowl outside after breakfast, apparently, and then forgotten what he meant to do with it. His spoon lay in the grass between his boots. Beyond him, the road was empty. The strip of lime across it had been renewed where feet had scuffed it. Anko had written down the prints before anyone touched them.

Mikasa stood behind him for a moment.

"You took Momo's bowl."

Eren looked down.

"Oh."

"She wants it."

He held it up. Mikasa took it, but did not go.

At the far side of the road, Himmel was talking to Brenna. Neither was looking at the settlement. They had taken over the watch after breakfast, and had stayed through the small, ordinary noises of a morning in which everybody wanted to go to sleep and nobody quite trusted themselves to.

"There's someone here," said Mikasa.

"I know."

"Then come upstairs."

Eren pressed both palms against his knees. He was going to stand. For a second she thought he would. Then he looked past her, toward the low woods.

"If it comes back."

"They'll ring."

"And if—"

"Eren."

He stopped.

She came round the step and stood in front of him. The bowl was between them, held in both of her hands. There was a smear of porridge drying on its rim.

"You're going to fall asleep here," she said. "And then I'll have to carry you."

He looked at her at last.

"You could."

"Yes."

There was no threat in it. That made him laugh once, without much air, and made the laugh stop halfway out. Mikasa waited while he bent down for the spoon.

He stood. Slowly. She did not take his arm until he reached for hers.

They went back together.



The house had not gone quiet all at once.

First the kitchen grew quieter. Momo stopped setting things down quite so hard. Okarun washed the porridge bowls while she leaned against the counter with her eyes closed. Whenever he asked where something belonged, she pointed without opening them. He put three bowls on the wrong shelf. She let him.

Then the fort stopped arguing about whether sleeping counted as abandoning a post. Umaru had checked the slate, checked it again, and found thirty on it both times. Milim sat at the arch until the hoods were down. She was still there when Diablo arrived to relieve her, holding a cup she had not drunk.

"They're asleep," she whispered.

"I shall keep it that way."

"Even if somebody needs to come in?"

"Then I shall be very polite to them somewhere else."

Milim considered this. Then she gave him the cup and went to find Ori.

Fern put the ledger away before she went upstairs. Stark saw her look back at it from the stair.

"It'll still be empty," he said.

She looked at him.

"That page. When you come down."

"Someone could write on it."

"I'll tell them not to."

She put her hand into his. "You need to sleep too."

"I know."

"That wasn't an instruction to guard the book."

"I know."

He said it a little too quickly. She looked at him again, and he smiled, caught, and went up beside her.



Eren woke when Mikasa moved.

The room was dark enough that he thought it was morning. Then he saw the light along the edge of the curtain, low and yellow, and remembered that they had gone to bed in the middle of the day.

Mikasa had one foot on the floor. She stopped when he touched her wrist.

"Where?"

"Water."

His hand opened at once.

She sat back on the edge of the bed instead.

"I'm not going out," she said.

"I didn't mean—"

"I know."

She turned her hand over and took his. He looked at the space where he had caught her wrist. The movement had happened before he knew he was awake.

"Sorry."

"You can come get water with me."

He shook his head. Then, after a moment, nodded.

They drank from the same cup at the little stand by the door. Eren finished it and filled it again for her. There was nobody in the corridor. Somewhere downstairs a spoon struck a pot, and he felt his shoulders go tight.

Mikasa watched him listen.

"Just the kitchen," she said.

"I know."

He put the cup down too carefully.

She went back to bed. He followed, and sat beside her with his feet still on the boards.

"You think I'm wrong."

Mikasa drew her knees up under the blanket.

"I think you were frightened."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the one I have now."

He looked down at his hands.

"I keep seeing her run. The little girl. I wasn't close enough. I had her hand and I still wasn't close enough."

Mikasa leaned forward. Not to touch him yet. To hear him properly.

"She made it."

"Because Gojo was there. Because Aira was there. Because you came."

"Yes."

He turned his head. The agreement surprised him.

"I don't want to need all three of you every time," he said.

"Neither do I."

"Then why did it sound like—"

"I asked what you were asking for."

He was quiet.

Mikasa put her hand on his back, between the shoulder blades. He had come back from the north end with his body held as if he was still standing there. Under her palm, it slowly stopped.

"A place to stop something," she said. "And a place nobody goes out of. You said both."

He looked at the curtain.

"I did."

"I can want one and be afraid of the other."

He listened to the kitchen again. The spoon did not strike the pot a second time.

"If we keep going out, somebody's going to die."

Mikasa's hand stayed where it was.

"Somebody could," she said.

He looked at her sharply. She did not look away.

"If we stay, somebody could too," she said. "I don't know how to make it a choice where they can't."

He wanted to say something to that. He had a dozen things he could say. He could name the people beyond the road, and the ones inside it, and the distances between them. He could put the little girl's hand into every sentence.

He said none of them.

"I don't know either."

Mikasa moved her hand from his back to his waist and leaned against him. He put his arm round her. They sat like that while the light at the curtain climbed a little farther down the wall.

"I'm not against you," she said.

"I know."

This time it did not come out quickly.

She lay down and pulled his sleeve until he came with her. He had slept for part of an afternoon and could have slept for another day. He thought of getting up to check the road. Mikasa's head was on his shoulder, and the thought stayed a thought.



In the east room, Mau had slept through his first attempt to sit in the reading chair.

Frieren had woken him long enough to move him into the bed. She was now beside him with the grimoire open and her thumb resting in a place she had not turned from for some time.

"Has anybody decided?" he asked.

"No."

"You'd tell me."

She looked at him over the book.

"We'd wake you."

He nodded. He had not meant that question to sound like a request for permission. She had not answered it that way.

From above the closet came the small settling noise of Rimuru turning on his pillow. Through Ori's connecting door, a pink foot was sticking out from under her blanket. Someone had tried to cover it. It had come out again.

"Tomorrow," said Mau.

"Tomorrow we talk."

"I didn't say we decide tomorrow."

"I know what you said."

He looked at her. She was still watching him.

"I meant it," he said.

"Then keep meaning it when everyone's rested and cross with you."

He smiled a little. "Are you cross with me?"

"You fell asleep on my book."

"That's not about the wall."

"It's still a complaint."

He shifted closer, leaving the page clear. She lowered the book and let him put his head against her shoulder.

Downstairs, Momo had started warming the soup. Someone would have to wake them for it. For once, they were going to let that person do the work.
