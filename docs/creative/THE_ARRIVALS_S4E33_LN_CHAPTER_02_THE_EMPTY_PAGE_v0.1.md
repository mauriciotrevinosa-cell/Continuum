# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 2 — The Empty Page

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 2
**Continuity state:** Day 222, afternoon | the promised rested discussion begins | the difference between a defence and permanent confinement is argued through ordinary needs | fields, supplies, departure and the uncertain line remain practical problems | Rimuru and Senku may measure and inventory; no wall approved yet | Hollis's road evidence still missing

*When: Day 222, after a night's sleep and an uneventful watch. The page Fern left under Eren's proposal is still blank. Hollis has not returned from Fallow.*

Fern opened the ledger to the page nobody had written on.

There was a ring from a cup near the bottom. Someone had put the book under the teapot after she went to bed. The proposal at the top was dry and untouched.

She turned it round so Eren could see.

"Start with what you need it to do."

Eren had brought no speech. He had a strip of wood from the workshop, which he put down across the table. It was the length of his forearm.

"Make them stop here," he said, touching it. "Not at somebody's door."

Nobody spoke over him.

"Give whoever's on watch somewhere to stand. So they can see over what's coming. So one person doesn't have to wait in the road with a sword."

Himmel looked at the strip. Then at Aira, who was cleaning dirt from the nick in her old blade with the corner of a cloth.

"Yes," he said.

Fern wrote that down.

The room was fuller than it was at an ordinary afternoon meal, but it did not have the stillness of the morning before. Somebody was finding a place to sit. Momo was wiping water from the table. At the fort curtain a small argument continued until Umaru put her head through and said that if they wanted to hear, they had to stop explaining to each other what had already been said.

"And the gates?" said Fern.

"North. South. Shut at night."

"Before dusk?" asked Mikasa.

"Before dusk."

"And if someone's late?"

Eren looked at her.

"We open them."

"Then someone has to be there. With the key, or whatever it uses. Every night."

"I can do that," said Eren.

"You can't do every night," said Mikasa.

"I didn't mean—"

"You just did," said Anko. Not sharply. She had her notebook beside the ledger. "We already have watches. Write people before you write gates."

Fern moved her pen to a fresh line.

"And no more runs?" she asked.

This time Eren took longer.

"For now."

Momo stopped wiping the table.

"For how long?"

"Until we know."

"Know what?"

He did not answer immediately. Momo sat down with the wet cloth still in her hand.

"I can make the soup without the bacon," she said. "I'm already doing that twice a week. I can change the bread. I can use less oil. But if you mean forever, I need to know while there's still time to change things."

"Not forever," said Eren.

"Then put that on the page."

Fern put it on the page.



Daphne would not put the hives on the table.

Rimuru had drawn them as little squares on a loose sheet. There were also the fields, the river path and the buildings, and the line copied from the Searchers' marks. Daphne pushed his sheet away.

"You have put the bees beside the kitchen."

"I haven't moved them," said Rimuru. "It's a drawing."

"A bad one."

Senku leaned over. "The distance isn't to scale."

"They are not little boxes you carry in with the cushions," said Daphne. "You put them in the wrong place and next season everyone asks why there's no honey."

Rimuru drew an arrow back toward the hives' actual place.

"We'd have to measure where everything is before we set the wall," he said. "The worked ground. The drains. Where the carts turn."

"Before you set it," said Eren. "Not before you decide we need one."

"I can agree we need something in the way," said Rimuru. "I can't agree to a shape that shuts the fields out. Or shuts the water in."

He put his finger on the river path.

"If we build across this badly, the first hard rain gives you a pond at the north door."

Senku found a short clean piece of chalk and drew a ditch on the far side of Eren's strip of wood.

"Earth here first," he said. "Timber where we can afford it. You don't make every pace the same kind of obstacle. And you don't build any of it until somebody has counted what it'll take."

"Stone," said Eren.

"Some. Eventually. If you've got enough people left who aren't making food or standing watch."

Wakana had been turning the wooden strip in his fingers. He put it down.

"The gates need good wood. We can't use all of it on the wall and then make the part that moves out of scraps."

Rimuru nodded. Nobody asked him for Foundation. It was gone; its work stood underneath their feet and in the land around them. He looked at the pegs in the strip of wood and began a list of things they would have to find with their hands.



"Do we still walk?" asked Shizuka.

Her voice was small enough that Fern nearly missed it. Nano had stopped drawing a neat margin on the scrap beside her. She looked up, making room for the question.

"Where?" said Eren.

Shizuka looked at the book on her lap, then at him.

"To the river."

"That's inside," said Rimuru. "Most of the path. We need to check the rest."

"What if I want the bit that isn't?" said Karane.

Hakari, beside her, did not make a joke. Her foot was against Karane's under the bench.

"Then you'd tell whoever's on watch," said Mikasa. "And we'd work out whether someone should come with you."

"That's what we do now," said Karane.

"Yes."

Karane looked at Eren. "I don't want to start being wrong for wanting it."

Eren rubbed one thumb along the edge of the wooden strip.

"You wouldn't be."

"And if somebody wants to leave?" asked Brenna.

She had come from Arrival House. Her scar was dry now, a pale tight place above her eye. She put no hand to it when she spoke.

"They leave," said Mau.

He had waited until then. He was beside Frieren, with one sleeve caught under her elbow. He freed it before he went on.

"We can ask them where they're going. We can help them plan it. We don't lock the gate because we think they should stay."

Eren looked at him.

"I don't want to keep prisoners."

"I know. But it'll be somebody who's frightened asking to close it. Every time. We should say it while we're not standing at the latch."

Brenna nodded once. Fern wrote more slowly.

From the far end, Pride said, "And you must say who may close it."

Mau turned toward her.

"Not a new ruler," she said. "The person on the watch must not have to decide whether you will be angry with them for doing their work."

"The watch closes it when something's coming," said Anko. "Then calls the rest. We can argue after nobody's in the road."

Eren put his hand flat on the table. "Yes."

That went on the page too.



The question of the line made everyone quieter.

"It didn't cross," said Eren.

"No," said Anko. "And it didn't try."

"The titans—"

"The little one ran at the cottages. We stopped it before we found out anything else."

Rimuru let her finish.

"I can feel changes at the marks," he said. "That isn't the same as knowing every kind of thing the protection stops. We build something ordinary that we can inspect. We don't call it proof of what we can't inspect."

Gojo, at the cold-hearth end, had his chin in his hand.

"And you don't go out there with a monster just to see if the line works," he said.

"Nobody suggested that," said Shoko.

"I was contributing before you could accuse me of not contributing."

She pushed his cup farther from the map. He moved it back to drink, but kept it off the paper.

They stopped, finally, because the room was getting hot and half the table had gone dry-mouthed. The page was no longer empty. It was not an agreement either.

Fern read it back.

Watch places. Routes kept clear. Measurements. Materials. Gates opening for people, not only goods. The words *not forever* beneath *no more runs*, and a question mark beside them.

Then she touched the one space still unfilled.

"Hollis."

Eren nodded.

"We need to ask him," said Mau. "What stopping the road would actually stop."

Nobody took the question mark off the page.

Rimuru gathered the drawing. Senku took the chalk. They could count and measure without pretending the decision had already happened.

Momo got up to put the cloth in a basin. "Next time," she said, "do it after we eat."

"This was after we ate," said Fern.

Momo looked at the length of the writing on the page.

"Then feed us halfway through."
