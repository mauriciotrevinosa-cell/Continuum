# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 3 — Count Us In

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 3
**Continuity state:** Day 223 | Kessoku work on a four-person arrangement of the ninth harvest song for the weekly tea | Bocchi changes the arrangement rather than disappearing into it | Kita's care is that of her established girlfriend; Ryo makes a substantive musical choice; Nijika protects rehearsal without evading shared work | Seika listens without taking over | music continues while the wall remains undecided

*When: Day 223, afternoon. Rimuru and Senku are measuring, not building. Kessoku have the music room to themselves. The ninth song is familiar from the old book and earlier work; this arrangement is for the four of them, not a first discovery of its existence.*

Ryo had crossed out six notes.

Bocchi found the marks when she came down the private stair with her guitar and saw the book open on the rug. The old page itself was untouched. Ryo had made a copy on a loose sheet and put a line through the part Bocchi had spent the morning working out.

"I wrote those," said Bocchi.

It came out louder than she intended. She looked behind her, as if someone else might have said it.

Ryo was on the floor with her bass across her knees. She looked up.

"I know."

"All six."

"I counted them."

Bocchi stood beside the rug. There was a correct way to ask why somebody had removed six notes, and several thousand wrong ones. She could feel all the wrong ones arriving together.

"They fit," she said.

"Yes."

That was worse.

Kita came through the other door with four cups, only three of which matched. Nijika followed, carrying a practice pad and two sticks under one arm, and the strip of cloth she used to wipe the sticks under the other.

"What happened?" asked Nijika.

"I have been censored," said Bocchi.

Kita put a cup down too quickly. A little water went over the side.

"You said that very clearly."

"Please don't sound pleased."

"I'm sorry."

Kita was still smiling. Not at the notes. Bocchi knew the difference, which did not make it easier to look at her.

"Ryo?" said Nijika.

Ryo picked up her loose sheet.

"Listen to the bottom."



They sat in their usual places. It took longer than it should have because Kita's cup had to be rescued from under Nijika's elbow, and because Ryo said the rug had developed a hollow that suited her back and she did not want to move six inches to the left.

"The entire room cannot be arranged around your spine," said Nijika.

"It already is. You just haven't noticed."

Kita moved the cup. Bocchi checked her tuning against Ryo's open string, made a small adjustment, and checked again. The sound had been dull when she came down the stair. It settled into the room now, the little beat between the two notes slowing until she could no longer hear it.

"From the turn," said Ryo.

They played.

The ninth song was the harvest one. The old notation did not give them everything, and the words were worse. There were marks Seika understood and marks she could only compare with the other songs. Kita had been singing the line on an open vowel while they decided where the words belonged. Nijika kept a light pulse on the pad instead of trying to make it sound like a finished performance.

Bocchi reached the six notes.

She played them. They fitted. They crossed the change in the melody and arrived precisely where they ought to arrive. She had checked it until her wrist hurt.

Ryo stopped.

"Again. Without them."

Bocchi's hand stayed on the neck.

"Then there's nothing."

"There's me."

Ryo played the passage alone. Down low, almost hidden when all four of them were playing, the bass did not follow the melody back. It stayed on the same note for a little longer than Bocchi expected, then moved while the guitar would have been filling the space above it.

Nijika stopped tapping her knee.

"Oh."

Ryo played it again.

The first time through the turn sounded finished. The second did not. The bass held the same small piece in place while the rest of the tune went away from it, and when it finally moved, the returning melody sounded different.

"You weren't playing that yesterday," said Bocchi.

"I found it this morning."

"You could have told me."

Ryo considered this.

"Yes."

Nijika pointed a stick at her. "That's the useful part of the answer. Keep that part."

Bocchi put the loose sheet on the rug between them.

"The first three," she said.

Ryo looked at her.

"I want to keep the first three. And leave the rest."

"Try it."

This time they all heard the bass.



Kita ran out of breath before the end of the phrase.

She lifted a hand, apologising, and Nijika stopped at once. Ryo let her last note die against her palm.

"Sorry. Again."

"Don't start again yet," said Bocchi.

Kita was holding the cup with both hands. "I can get it."

"I know. It's just—"

Bocchi looked at the book. Then at the copied page. She could hear the problem clearly while nobody was playing, which was a frustrating way to be able to hear something.

"It keeps going where you need air."

"That's generally the problem with me and singing."

"No. The tune. There."

She pointed, not quite touching Kita's paper. Kita moved the paper closer until their knees touched.

"Show me."

Bocchi played the melody slowly. Kita hummed it with her. At the end of the second phrase she stopped, drew breath, and came back in. The guitar went on for a beat without her.

"That," said Bocchi. "What if we let you?"

Nijika tried the beat with one hand on the pad, leaving the other still. Ryo watched her, then answered the empty part on the bass.

They tried it together. Kita did not have to steal the breath this time. She took it, looked at Bocchi, and sang the next line as if she had meant the break.

"It's a song people worked to," said Nijika, lowering her sticks. "They'd have needed to breathe too."

"We don't know how they sang it," said Bocchi.

"We know how she does," said Ryo.

Kita looked down at the old page. She put a small mark on their copy, leaving the original where it was.

"Then I like this one."



They were halfway through the next attempt when something fell in the yard.

The sound was not particularly loud. A short wooden clatter, then someone saying sorry. Nijika's sticks stopped. Kita turned her head toward the door. Bocchi's hand closed across all six strings.

Ryo was the first one to move again. She put her bass carefully on the rug and went to look.

"Measuring rods," she said from the doorway. "Stark."

From outside came Fern's voice, saying, "You don't have to carry all of them at once."

Nijika let her breath out through her nose.

"Of course it's Stark."

They waited while Ryo came back. Bocchi looked down at her strings. Her palm had left a faint damp place on them.

"Do we need to help?" asked Kita.

Nijika looked at the open door. Then at the page with the new breath mark.

"After this."

"If they start building—"

"Then after whatever we've agreed to do."

She put the sticks across her knees. "I've been thinking about it. We keep saying we'll practise when everyone's finished. But nobody's going to finish a wall in an afternoon."

"We haven't agreed to one," said Ryo.

"If we do."

Bocchi's eyes went to the private stair. From here she could see the first two treads. Upstairs there was room for her guitar, and a cupboard where nobody asked anything until she came out. She was very good at waiting until there was a better time.

"I can carry things," she said.

"We know," said Nijika.

"Not big things. Some things."

"We're not trying to get out of carrying things."

Kita rested her hand on Bocchi's wrist, lightly enough that she could have moved it away without anyone seeing.

"I want to keep playing with you," she said.

The last two words made Bocchi look up.

Kita did not make them smaller.

"All of you," she added, turning to the others. "But also you."

Ryo leaned back against the bench. Nijika suddenly found something to adjust on the practice pad.

Bocchi turned her wrist under Kita's hand. Their fingers met. They had done this before, often enough that she knew where Kita would put her thumb. It still made it difficult to assemble an ordinary sentence.

"I want that too," she managed.

Ryo nodded at the page. "Good. We need you for the three notes they let you keep."

Bocchi made a small offended noise. Nijika laughed so hard she dropped a stick.



Seika came down while they were arguing about the opening.

She had stopped wearing the ugly coat in the summer, but she still arrived in doorways with the same expression. Nijika saw her and immediately straightened. Seika looked from the sticks on her knees to the old book, then at the four cups on the rug.

"Working?"

"Yes," said Nijika. Too quickly.

Seika sat on the edge of the little stage.

"Then why have you stopped?"

They played the passage for her. It went wrong at the opening, exactly where they had been arguing. Nijika began to explain.

Seika held up one hand.

"What are you counting?"

"Four."

"Before the first note?"

Nijika looked at Bocchi. Bocchi looked at Kita. Ryo closed her eyes briefly.

"I was counting the pickup," said Bocchi.

"I wasn't," said Kita.

Nijika put her face in one hand.

Seika waited. They worked it out between themselves, and played it again, and this time all four of them arrived together.

She did not tell them it was beautiful. She pointed at the break where Kita took her breath.

"Keep that."

Then she got up.

"We were going to ask," said Nijika. "About if we have to change the times. For work. If they build."

"Ask the people you're working with."

"And the rehearsals?"

Seika looked at her.

"Put them down too. Or people will keep assuming they don't exist."

She left before any of them could thank her.

Nijika found the spare piece of paper on which she kept forgetting to write the week. She put their names at the top. Then the afternoon, and the next one, and the bits of work they had already promised to do. She left spaces for the things they had not been asked about yet.

Ryo took the pencil from her and wrote a time beside one of the spaces.

"You can't put lunch down as rehearsal," said Nijika.

"I make better decisions afterwards."

Kita took the paper away from both of them and put it on the bench where nobody could sit on it.

"From the beginning?"

Bocchi looked at the three notes that were still hers. She could hear Ryo's answer waiting underneath them.

"I'll count," she said.

She tapped the guitar twice, very lightly, looked round at all three of them, and brought them in.
