# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 5 — Hollis's Road

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 5
**Continuity state:** Day 225 | Hollis returns from Fallow with guarded cart and limited supplies before a final wall decision | food and the cart are attended to before consultation | Fallow still trades unevenly; Ashby's rejection is not undone | essential goods, families and guard costs make total closure consequential | the existing four-three-three arrangement and guards-first commitment remain | Hollis agrees to join the next discussion; no new propaganda clues

*When: Day 225, midday into evening. Hollis has been away on the eastern route. The house has measured its worked ground and counted available materials; no defence has been authorised or begun.*

The mule came up the road before anyone could see the cart.

It had acquired a piece of string round one ear. Hollis had tied it there to keep a fly away, apparently, and the fly had accepted this as an invitation to investigate the other ear. The mule was angry about both arrangements.

Dunmore walked beside its head. Another guard stayed at the rear of the cart until they were well inside the familiar marked ground. Mikasa met them there and looked beyond them down the road before she asked anything.

"Everyone?"

"Everyone," said Hollis.

He sounded hoarse. He climbed down slowly, with one hand on the cart's side and the other pressed to his lower back. The mule immediately tried to turn away from him.

"You've been fed," he told it.

The mule flattened an ear. The string hung down the side of its face.

Stark took its lead while Dunmore went to the back of the cart. Mikasa helped them lift a split crate down without letting its bottom fall out. There were three intact lamp chimneys left in the straw from the last return, set apart from the new goods. Hollis had not tried to sell those in Fallow. He had brought them home.

"For the next run," he said, when Wakana touched the edge of the crate.

Wakana nodded and took them to the workshop.

Momo appeared in the kitchen door. She looked at the way Hollis was standing.

"Sit first."

"I've got your salt."

"It can wait while you sit."

She pointed him into the house. He took his hat off before he went in.



They let him finish eating before they asked about the wall.

That was harder than it sounded. Rimuru had come down from his office with the measurements, then seen the bowl and put them on the bench beside him instead. Fern closed the ledger when Hollis sat. Eren came in halfway through the meal, saw everyone waiting, and took a place without speaking.

Hollis ate slowly enough that nobody could mistake it for delay. He was hungry. There was a red pressure mark across one thumb where he had been gripping the reins. Rem brought tea and left the pot near his hand.

When the bowl was empty, he leaned back and looked round.

"Something's happened."

"Two titans," said Mikasa.

He put the spoon down.

She told him the part he needed. The small one running. The cottages. Aira's blade. The family that had left. The thing from the woods walking the line the night after.

Hollis looked toward the north windows.

"The little girl?"

"She's all right."

He nodded. Then nodded again, as if he had to make the answer settle somewhere.

Eren put the drawing on the table.

"I want a wall."

Hollis looked at the drawing, and listened while Eren explained it. He let him finish all the way to the end. Even the part about no more runs.

Then he took his hat off his knee and put it beside the empty bowl.

"That's my road too," he said.

"That's why we waited," said Mau.

Hollis looked at him. At Rimuru beside him. Fern opened the ledger again.



Fallow had bought the cream.

Not all of it. Not quickly. One seller had refused to let Hollis put the pots on his counter after someone asked where they came from. Another had taken twelve and hidden them under the table until the first seller went home.

"He sold eleven," said Hollis. "Kept one for his hands."

"Did he say that?" asked Maomao.

"He tried it while I was there."

She made a small sound and folded her arms.

Hollis had sold honey too. Glass more slowly. People had money, some of them, and things they needed, and nobody quite wanted to be the first one seen buying from him. He had stayed longer than he meant to because leaving with everything unsold would have cost the guards their time for nothing.

"They still get paid first," said Fern.

"I know. That's why I can still hire them."

He put a small wrapped packet on the table. Salt. The bolt of cloth was on the cart. There was oil, less than he wanted, and ordinary grain bought in two small lots because he could not afford one large one at the first price.

"I've written what each cost. Not what I wish it cost."

Fern took the packet, weighed it in her hand, and set it beside the ledger.

"Can they keep selling to us?" she asked.

"Some of them will. I can't promise for how long."

"And Ashby?"

Hollis rubbed the red place on his thumb.

"Not going back through that gate next week."

Brenna was by the door. She had come from Arrival House after putting the spare bedding out to air. She said nothing. Hollis looked at her scar and did not ask whether it hurt.

Eren leaned over the drawing.

"So we can't depend on the towns."

"No," said Hollis. "You can't depend on them. That isn't the same as getting nothing from them."

He pointed at the packet.

"That's here because somebody was willing to sell it. If I hadn't gone, it wouldn't be. You can decide you don't need it. That's for you to say. But don't work it out as if closing the gate creates it on this side."

Momo looked at the salt. Then at the amount Fern had written down.

"We could use less," she said.

"We already use less," said Seiko.

"I know."

Nobody laughed.

"The dungeon gives us things," said Rimuru. "Some food. Materials. Not everything, and not always the thing we need that week."

Hollis nodded as if that was another field with an uncertain harvest. He did not ask to see it. He had never tried to turn their stranger arrangements into something he could promise to a customer.

"Then count what comes out of it," he said. "And keep counting what's in the store. Before anybody tells the kitchen she can do without a road."



Cobb came in while they were looking at the costs.

He had a folded scrap in his hand. He stopped when he saw the drawing on the table, then looked at Hollis.

"The east turning?"

"We passed it on the way back."

"Could you take something next time?"

He opened the scrap. There was a short list on it, written with more pressure than the paper needed. Something for his sister. A message about when he might come. He had not put a date.

Hollis took it and read it.

"If we're going that way."

"All right."

Cobb looked at Eren. Not accusing him. Waiting to find out whether the request he had just made was about to become wrong.

"We haven't decided," said Eren.

Cobb nodded and left the note beside Hollis's hat.

The room stayed quiet after he went.

Hollis folded the scrap again, along the same worn crease.

"I can carry pots without visiting every market," he said. "I can carry a letter without a full cart. Those aren't the same journey. They don't have the same risk."

"And if we send you because we want something?" asked Mau.

"Then you ask me whether I'll go."

"I don't want to ask you to stand in front of another gate for us."

Hollis met his eyes.

"I was selling things before you got here. I'll be selling things if you never buy another packet of salt. You didn't invent my road."

Mau looked down. Hollis waited until he looked back.

"You can make it a bad bargain," the merchant said. "You can forget the people you pay. You haven't. Keep not forgetting. Don't decide what I'm allowed to be frightened of instead."

Fern drew a line beside the existing division. Four parts, three, three. She had not changed it.

"No run without the people walking it agreeing," she said. "Including the stretch we walk. No assumption that the last agreement covers the next one."

Brenna came forward from the door.

"And no turning a paid guard into the last thing between you and a rumour with teeth because you've built something to stand behind."

"No," said Mikasa.

It was quick and unqualified. Brenna looked at her, then pulled out the bench and sat down.



They took the costs out to the cart after that.

Senku wanted to see the axle. Byakuya was already under it, making the mule increasingly suspicious by speaking from somewhere the mule could not see. The repair was old and the road had worked it loose. It could be tightened. It needed time, not a miracle.

Hollis stood with his hat back on and watched Stark hold the cart steady while they worked. He had stopped rubbing his thumb. Rem had put a cloth round it without asking him to explain how he had hurt it.

Eren came to stand beside him.

"Would you still come?" he asked. "If we built it."

Hollis looked at him.

"If there's somewhere to open."

Eren looked down the road. It was clear as far as the turn.

"And if it's shut when you get here?"

"Then I'd like someone to come and find out why I'm late before they start congratulating themselves on being safe."

Eren nodded slowly.

"Tomorrow," he said. "Will you stay for the discussion?"

"I need that axle."

"It won't just be about the axle."

"I gathered."

Hollis took the string off the mule's ear. The fly had gone some time ago. He smoothed the flattened hair with two fingers, and the animal leaned into his hand despite itself.

"I'll stay," he said.

They unloaded the salt and the oil before dark. The drawing went back to Rimuru's office, still a drawing, and Hollis took the old bed offered to him in Arrival House, where the register girl asked him to keep his muddy boots off the clean blanket.

He promised that he would try.
