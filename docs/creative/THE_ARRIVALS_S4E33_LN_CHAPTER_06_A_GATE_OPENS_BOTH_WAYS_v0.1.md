# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 6 — A Gate Opens Both Ways

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 6
**Continuity state:** Day 226 | Hollis participates before the family reaches its decision | staged earth and timber defence agreed within the marked ground, north and south gates with watch places and unfinished sections protected by existing rota | no imprisonment or permanent abolition of travel | essential traffic is individually agreed with guards and household walkers; guard payment preserved | materials, food work and time limit construction; Foundation remains spent | the first work is prepared, not magically completed

*When: Day 226, after the midday meal. Hollis is present; the cart's repair is nearly done. Rimuru and Senku have measurements, Wakana has a timber count, and nobody has dug the first trench.*

Eren moved the north gate.

It was only a little block on Rimuru's drawing, but moving it meant erasing part of a line he had drawn himself. He rubbed the chalk away with his thumb, leaving a grey smear, and put the block back where a cart could actually turn.

"Here?"

Hollis leaned over the table.

"Loaded?"

"Loaded."

"And with someone coming the other way?"

Eren looked at the width between the marks. He moved the block again.

"Then here."

Hollis nodded. "My mule will still object. But that's his character."

They had eaten first. Momo had insisted. There was bread in the middle of the table and enough water that nobody could use getting a drink as an excuse to leave before they said what was troubling them. Fern had the ledger. Rimuru had spread the drawings over the empty places between bowls.

The plan was smaller than Eren's first picture of it.

Not in the amount of ground it had to take care of. In what they could honestly put on the paper and expect to find standing a few weeks later. Earth where earth would do. Timber where they had it. Good timber saved for moving parts and places where the watch needed a firm footing. The stone they already had would go where it was useful; the rest would wait for hands to bring it.

Wakana had put three samples on the table. One sound. One split. One with a dark place near its end.

Milim picked up the largest.

"I can carry more than that."

"You can," said Wakana. "I still can't join rotten wood into good wood."

She turned the sample over. Pressed the dark place with one finger, much more gently than she wanted to.

It gave.

"Oh."

She put it down. After a moment she moved it away from the piece he had said they could use.



Rimuru traced the proposed work with a finger.

"We start here. Not the whole distance at once. Clear the approach. Shape the earth. Put something between the road and the first doors before we worry about making every bit look the same."

"And the open parts?" asked Eren.

"Are open parts. Until we finish them."

"We don't stop the watch," said Mikasa.

Anko put her notebook beside the map. The names were already there. The new work had to fit round them, not replace them with a gate nobody had built yet.

"Two people who know how to relieve each other," she said. "No one does a day lifting timber and then takes the whole night because they're feeling responsible."

She looked at Eren when she said it. Then at Himmel. Then at Gojo.

"I was being very unresponsible yesterday," said Gojo.

"Keep practising," said Shoko.

Anko turned the notebook back toward herself before anybody could start laughing for long enough to lose the question.

"We need to see the approaches. The gates shut before dark. If something's coming, the people there close them and ring. They don't wait to find one of the people sitting at this table."

Fern wrote. Eren nodded.

"But closing them isn't the end of counting," said Mikasa. "If someone's outside, somebody knows. We keep a name, a route, when to start looking."

"And someone who can answer when they get back," said Hollis. "Not a very well-made door and nobody behind it."

"Yes," said Eren.



Karane had brought the river question back.

She had not meant to. She was sitting with Hakari, and Shizuka had been turning a page without reading it for most of the discussion. Nano had noticed. So had Karane. Eventually she put both hands on her knees and said it.

"Going out. What are we actually agreeing to?"

Fern laid her pen across the ledger.

"Tell the watch where you're going. The same as now."

"Asking them?"

"Telling them. Then listening if they've seen something on that road."

"And then?"

Mikasa looked at her. "Then we work out whether it's a good time and who can go. If you still want to."

"That's for walking. What about leaving?"

It was Brenna again. She had not let the word become another word for a cart.

"People can leave," said Eren.

He said it before Mau could answer for him.

"We tell them what we know. We help them with the road. We don't keep them because it's safer here."

Brenna looked at him for a long moment. Then she put her hand on the edge of the table and sat back.

"Good."

Shizuka's book closed quietly.

"I'd still like the river," she said.

Rimuru turned the drawing toward her. He showed her the path they had measured, where it ran within the marks, and the point beyond which they would still use the ordinary precautions.

"You don't lose this."

She leaned over it. Nano moved the cup out of her way. Hakari asked whether the bank would still have somewhere to sit, and Senku said it was a defence, not a proposal to remove all the grass.

Karane gave him a look.

"I was checking."

"Good. Check before he puts a ditch through it."

Rimuru looked mildly wounded. He checked the drawing anyway.



The road took longer.

Momo could not make a list short enough that it contained nothing they needed from outside. Maomao put a second list beside hers. Wakana a third. Some of those needs would change with work and time. None disappeared because Fern wrote them on a clean page.

Hollis put down the costs he had brought from Fallow.

"I'm not going back to Ashby on the next run. Not with this cart. Not after what happened. I can ask in Fallow about a smaller order. Oil. Salt. The things you've marked here."

"With guards," said Fern.

"With guards."

"And the low woods?" said Brenna.

Mikasa and Himmel looked at one another.

"We still walk our part," said Himmel. "If we're going. We don't send you through it and call the rest of the road your business."

"You need enough people left here," said Brenna.

"We do."

It was a condition, not an argument he could win by promising to be careful. Fern drew a short line beside the proposed trip.

Not today. Not every road. Not a permanent promise that the same people would always go. A particular journey, with what it carried written down, and the people taking it able to say no before the cart moved. A household escort only when the watch could cover their absence. The guards' payment came first, as it already did.

Hollis looked at Eren.

"I won't tell you it's safe."

"I don't want you to."

"Then don't tell me you're stopping the road for my sake. Decide what you can send. I'll decide what I can carry. We ask one another before we make the next promise."

Eren rubbed the last of the chalk from his thumb.

"All right."

Across the table, Cobb was holding the folded note for his sister. He did not put it into a pocket. Hollis held out his hand, and Cobb passed it across.



They came back to the line before Fern wrote the decision.

Senku had measured from the visible marks, not from an invisible surface nobody could point to. The first work would stand within them, with room for people to inspect the outside face and water to leave where it needed to leave. The inhabited paths and doors had to remain usable. Nothing on the page let them call an abandoned section finished just because the chalk joined up.

"This slows something down," said Rimuru. "Gives us a place to meet it. That's what we're asking it to do."

"It isn't the protection," said Anko.

"No."

"And we still don't know why it stood there all night and didn't cross."

Eren looked at the white line on the drawing. Then past it, at the windows with the afternoon in them.

"I want it to mean we can sleep," he said.

Nobody hurried to answer that.

"More of us," said Mikasa. "More often."

He nodded. He had wanted a bigger answer. She could see it in the way he kept his eyes on the paper until he was ready to lift them.

"Then that's what I want us to build."

Rimuru looked down the table, toward Mau. The two of them had been the faces on the road; they were still sitting among the people who would live with the choice.

"I want it too," said Rimuru. "With the gates. With the routes we just said. Not a wall first and everybody else's questions afterwards."

Mau had been quiet long enough that Frieren moved her elbow off his sleeve.

"What?" she asked softly.

He looked at her. Then at the drawing.

"I'm thinking about how much of it is because they're frightened of me."

"Some," said Anko.

He turned toward her.

"And some is because a titan ran at a child," she said. "We can't make those the same problem just because you feel worse about one of them."

He looked down at his hands. Frieren's hand found one of them under the table. She did not explain him to the room.

"I want the things we said to stay on the page," he said at last. "Especially the part where we don't stop somebody leaving because we're afraid."

Fern turned the ledger toward him. They were there.

He read them. Then nodded.



Fern did not put *safe* at the end.

She wrote the first work, the gates, the watch, the road conditions, and the things to check before they began. She wrote the people who had agreed to measure again where the route turned. She left room for what the first rain would teach them.

Then she read it aloud.

This time Eren did not wait until she reached the bottom to answer.

"The water," he said. "We marked that drain wrong yesterday. Use Senku's sheet."

She changed it. Wakana passed the sound piece of timber to Stark, who had offered to help sort what they already had. Milim asked whether she could do the heavy carrying once someone had told her which wood was good. Nijika moved a promised work period around a rehearsal and checked it with the people she was helping. Nobody asked Bocchi to put the guitar away for the duration of a wall.

They had chosen something which would take weeks.

Outside, while Fern was still finishing the names, Eren found the bean-girl on the step with Aira. She was watching a beetle make very slow progress over the toe of Aira's boot.

"Is there going to be a gate?" she asked him.

"Yes."

"Can I open it?"

He looked at the road, then at Aira. She waited for his answer without helping him.

"It'll be heavy," he said. "You can help somebody."

The girl thought about this, then pointed at his thumb.

"You've got white on you."

Eren looked at the chalk he had missed. He rubbed it on his trousers and only spread it farther.

Mikasa came down the step behind him carrying the drawing rolled in one hand.

"Come on," she said. "He wants to show you where the water goes."

He went with her. At the table, the split timber had been left in a separate pile. Somebody had put a cup on the piece they could use.
