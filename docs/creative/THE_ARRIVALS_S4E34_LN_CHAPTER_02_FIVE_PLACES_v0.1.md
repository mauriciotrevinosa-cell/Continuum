# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 8 — Five Places

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 8
**Continuity state:** Day 230 | the five keep their low table through summer, without hot stones | Nano proposes a household-work schedule; five different needs complicate it | Kusuri counts approved cream and return jars, not new healing potions | Karane and Hakari carry a crate together; Shizuka names an unclaimed afternoon | their room stays shared; coins and future relationships remain undecided

*When: Day 230, after lunch. The first defence works have made almost everyone late for something. The five's kotatsu has no hot stones in it in summer, but they have kept the table, which is where arguments fit best.*

There were five places at the table and six pieces of bread.

This was the first difficulty.

"Divide the last piece," said Nano.

"Into five?" said Karane.

"Yes."

"It's the size of your thumb."

"That does not make it indivisible."

Kusuri, who had been listening with half her attention while turning a clean jar against the light, ate it.

There was a silence.

"Resolved," said Nano.

"You can't call that resolved!"

"The disputed item no longer exists."

Shizuka put one hand over her mouth. The other went to her book. She wrote for a moment and turned it round.

*I was the one who brought six.*

"Then you should have eaten it," said Karane.

Shizuka looked at Kusuri. Kusuri looked at the empty place on the cloth where the bread had been, with the sudden guilty attention of someone noticing that an experiment had used the entire specimen.

"I'll get another."

Shizuka caught her sleeve and shook her head. She was laughing now. Very quietly, but enough that all of them could see.

Hakari pushed her own half-piece across the table toward her.

Karane watched that happen. Then she moved her cup away from Nano's sheet of paper, because it had begun to soak up tea.

"You've brought a schedule," she said.

"I have brought a proposed schedule."

"You've put my name on every morning."

"You are awake every morning."

"So are you!"



The quilt had been folded and laid across the foot of Hakari's bed. The iron tray stood empty beneath the low table, and Kusuri had washed it and was using it to keep the cleaned jars from rolling away. There were five beds in the room, arranged as they had always wanted them, and enough of their things between the beds that no one visiting could mistake the place for an efficient arrangement of furniture.

Nano's schedule was the efficient part.

It was an attempt to fit washing, the kitchen turn, work in the stores, errands, rest, and time in the room into a week that had acquired a trench and two gates. The trouble was that the gates had not acquired a fixed ending. Somebody always needed another cloth or another measure of something, and then dinner was late, and then Kusuri came in with clay on her stockings and said she had forgotten to eat, and everyone argued with her at once.

"We are not all available for every task," Nano said. "This prevents six people asking the same person."

"There's five of us."

"Six people asking from outside."

Karane read the top line again.

"Why is Hakari on meals?"

"I volunteered," said Hakari.

"For all of them?"

Hakari looked down at the paper. Her smile changed slightly.

"Oh. Not all of them."

Nano took the pencil.

"Specify."

Hakari specified. She wanted to take the food out once, not become the person who never ate with the others because she was carrying everybody's food. Nano crossed out four boxes. There was tea on the fourth one now, and the pencil tore the paper.

"Your schedule has a hole in it," said Karane.

"So does the defence," said Nano. "Both are in progress."

Kusuri laughed into the jar she was holding. Then she put it down before she dropped it.



The jars were from the last cream shipment. Some had come back in Hollis's baskets, empty and badly washed. Some had not come back at all. Kusuri was sorting the returned ones into three rows: sound, chipped, and the row Karane called *absolutely not*, because the rim had a long crack you could feel with a fingernail.

"That one leaks," said Karane.

"I know."

"Then why have you kept it?"

"For something dry."

"With a crack in it?"

Kusuri looked at the jar, reluctantly, and moved it to the third row.

She had wanted to make another batch that morning. The ordinary hand cream. The one they knew, the one Maomao would let leave the clinic without standing over it for an hour. She had been certain there was enough of everything, because she remembered the shelf looking full. Then she had opened the cupboard.

Remembering a full shelf was not the same as having one.

She told them this with great irritation, as if the cupboard had misrepresented itself.

"How many can you make?" asked Nano.

Kusuri gave her the number she had written down with Maomao. Nano wrote it beside *stores*. Then Kusuri took the pencil away and crossed out two of the planned uses beneath it.

"We don't have enough for that."

"That box is a request," said Nano.

"Then that box is a no."

Karane grinned.

"That's how you do it."

"The people carrying timber still need it," said Hakari.

"I know," said Kusuri. "That's why I counted before I promised."

She put the pencil down. Her face had become serious for a moment, in the way the others recognized from the times something in a pot was not doing what she thought it ought to do.

Shizuka wrote in her book. Turned it round.

*Can we carry the finished ones?*

Kusuri looked at the sentence, then at her.

"Yes. If you don't run."

Karane looked at Shizuka's bare feet under the table, then at the corridor outside.

"I'll carry the heavy end."

"You put yourself on every morning," Nano observed.

"That's different."



They packed the sound jars after tea.

Kusuri checked the lids. Nano counted. Shizuka wrote the labels in ordinary ink, slowly, with her book propped open beside the sheet so that she could copy the words exactly. Hakari folded the cloth between the jars. Karane held the crate steady, which would have been an uncomplicated task if Hakari had not kept putting her hand in the same place.

"Move."

"I have to tuck it under."

"Then tell me before you put your fingers there."

"All right. I'm putting my fingers there."

"Not now, I've got mine there!"

Hakari's shoulders shook. Karane glared at her. Then the crate shifted, and both of them caught it together, and neither said anything for a moment.

They got the cloth under.

Shizuka finished the last label. It was not quite straight. She looked at it for a long time before Kusuri took it and pasted it onto the jar.

"I can read it."

Shizuka looked up.

"That's what it has to do."

Nano marked the crate complete. Hakari lifted one end. Karane lifted the other, then adjusted her grip without letting Hakari take the extra weight.

"Both hands."

"I am using both hands."

"Use them better."

They carried it out into the corridor, still arguing. Kusuri went ahead to clear the way. Shizuka followed with the labels that had not been used, held between the pages of her book. Nano stayed to put the cleaned jars where they could not be knocked over in the dark.

When the four came back, the bread cloth was empty again. There were five clean cups on the table, and the afternoon light had moved onto Karane's bed.

Nano's schedule lay in the middle.

She had made one alteration while they were out. A broad line across two of the afternoon boxes, in pencil, not ink. No name in either one.

Karane sat down and looked at it.

"What's that?"

"Unallocated time."

"You scheduled nothing again."

"It worked before."

Hakari lowered her head onto her arms. Kusuri sat on the floor instead of her cushion, because her feet hurt. Shizuka opened her book and began to write, and Nano watched, expecting another proposed alteration.

Shizuka turned the book round.

*Mine too.*

Nano considered this.

Then she put her name above the line, and slid the pencil to Karane.

Five names went onto a period that had no task. Kusuri's was crooked. Hakari's touched Karane's. Shizuka fitted hers into the small space between Nano's and the edge.

From downstairs somebody called for an empty jar.

Kusuri lifted her head.

Karane reached out, caught the back of her sleeve, and pulled her gently down again.

"There's a whole row by the door. They can come and get one."

After a moment, Kusuri put her head on the cushion.

Hakari reached across the table for Karane's hand, missed it, and got her sleeve instead. Karane let her keep it.
