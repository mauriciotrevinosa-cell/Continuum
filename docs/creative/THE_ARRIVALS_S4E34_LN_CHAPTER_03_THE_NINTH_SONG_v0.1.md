# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 9 — The Ninth Song

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 9
**Continuity state:** Day 232 | the weekly tea makes room for Kessoku and Seika | the ninth harvest song, already requested and rehearsed, is played as their arrangement, not an exact recovered performance | Bocchi chooses to play with her band and keeps the written rests | Kita's quiet vocal part; Nijika and Ryo carry the pulse | Carmilla asks for a place in the listening; Pride's chair; Minerva rests her hands; Sekhmet stays awake | no large concert or new coin decision

*When: Day 232, at the weekly tea. There is timber in the yard and a row of holes that everyone keeps calling the wall. In the music room, the ninth song has acquired a bass part that Ryo refuses to explain until she has played it.*

Bocchi wanted the benches moved.

She had been thinking about asking all morning. At breakfast, and on the stairs, and while Kita was tying back her hair, and while Nijika wrapped the pot lid in a cloth so that it would not make the kind of noise the tea did not need.

Then she came into the common room and found the seven already there, and almost decided that it did not matter.

It mattered. The far bench was too close to the wall. If Ryo sat there her bass would be in somebody's lap. Nijika needed space to reach the edge of the drum, and Bocchi needed to be able to see her hand.

"Bocchi-chan?" Kita said quietly.

Bocchi looked at the bench.

"Could we—"

Every head turned.

Her voice went away.

Pride waited. She did not ask another question in the space where Bocchi was already trying to answer the first.

Bocchi swallowed.

"The bench. That one. A little farther out."

Mau, setting down the kettle at the hearth end, took one end. Nijika took the other. They moved it until Bocchi held up her hand. He put his end down and went to get his cup.

It was that easy. This irritated Bocchi slightly. She had spent a whole morning on it.

Kita leaned close enough that only she could hear.

"Good?"

Bocchi nodded.

"Good."

Kita's fingers touched hers behind the guitar, briefly. Then Kita took her place where Bocchi could see her too.



Echidna's book lay open on the table.

The ninth song had an old stain across its bottom corner, which Seika had examined from every angle. It concealed two marks, and the uncertainty had become a very large part of their lives for a while.

Ryo had wanted to play through the gap.

Bocchi had wanted to leave it empty.

Nijika had made them try both, repeatedly, with Seika sitting on the floor of the music room looking as if she were being made to judge a dispute between two very stubborn tradespeople.

Now Ryo sat on the moved bench and rested her fingers on the strings.

"She won," she told Echidna.

Echidna looked at Bocchi.

"The gap," Seika said. "We still don't know what's under the stain. We're not going to pretend."

"I know what the marks were," said Echidna.

There was a silence.

Seika looked at her for a long moment.

"You could have said."

"You did not ask me. You asked for the book."

"We spent three afternoons on that corner."

"Did you?"

Echidna looked genuinely interested. Ryo lowered her head onto the top of the bass for one moment, then lifted it again.

Bocchi looked at the stained corner. At the little place she had learned to leave alone. Then she looked at Echidna.

"Can we play it," she asked, "this way first?"

Echidna's attention sharpened.

"Your way."

"It's not—only mine."

Nijika tapped one finger against the cloth on the pot lid. Kita smiled at her. Ryo kept her face perfectly serious.

"Our way," said Bocchi.

"Please," said Echidna.



There were more people than usual at the tea. The four of Kessoku, Seika, the seven, Mau at his usual place with his cup. Momo had brought a second loaf, because the first had not survived everyone arriving.

Carmilla had made room beside her before Bocchi asked. Now she looked at the guitar, and at the place Bocchi had chosen to stand in front of the bench.

"You're standing," she said.

Bocchi looked down at her feet, as if she had forgotten.

"We practised it like that."

"Oh."

Carmilla's hand moved against the wood. Three small taps. Bocchi saw them, and touched three fingers to the side of the guitar where nobody else would notice.

Then Carmilla moved her cup and took her hands off the table.

"I'll listen," she said.

"You can—" Bocchi stopped. "You can sit closer. If you want. Not behind."

Carmilla looked toward Minerva, who sat next to her. Minerva had already moved her elbow to make room.

Carmilla came forward.

At the far end Pride settled into her own chair. It rocked once as she put down her cup. Nijika looked at it with a drummer's involuntary attention.

"That makes a noise," Nijika said.

"It has done so for a very long time."

"Could it not make it," said Ryo, "for about four minutes?"

Pride considered her.

Then she adjusted the way she sat, carefully, and the chair became still.

"About four," she agreed.

Sekhmet lay along the end of the bench with a cushion under her head, her face turned toward them.

"Wake me before you start," she said.

"We're starting now," Nijika told her.

Sekhmet opened her eyes.



Ryo went first.

Not loudly. Two low notes, separated by enough space that you could hear the room between them. She did not look at her hand. She looked at Nijika, who counted without speaking, with the small movement of her wrist.

The third note did not come where Bocchi had first expected it. That had been why she had fought with the bass part. Ryo had waited until the guitar's beginning, then put it beneath the phrase as if she had been standing there the whole time.

This time Bocchi knew where it would be.

She came in.

The song was not sad in the way the eighth had been sad. It went round. A short phrase, then the same phrase a little higher, then the first again, with one note changed. At first it seemed simple enough to play while looking at something else.

Then the turn arrived, and it was not simple.

The beat stayed where it was while the melody leaned away from it. Nijika kept her hand light. No great crash of the lid, no call for the room to pay attention. A muted touch and another, the little sounds that made room for Ryo's low line and the high note Bocchi had to reach without squeezing it too hard.

Kita began to sing.

No words. They had not tried to make words for an old song whose words they did not have. A narrow clear line, held through the turn, then let go just before Bocchi's fingers went down the strings again.

Seika stopped looking at the book.

Bocchi felt it happen, the room changing from faces into places the sound could go. The table. The air beneath it. The pale square of light on the floor. The corner where Carmilla sat with both hands on her knees, listening.

She did not forget the other three. That was the difference from the times she disappeared into a song alone. She could hear them. Ryo, holding the low part exactly where it had to be. Nijika, asking her with a glance whether she was ready for the turn. Kita breathing before the next line, because she had learned where Bocchi needed the space.

They went round again.

Daphne put down her bread. It had been halfway to her mouth for a while. She was looking at Nijika's hand.

Minerva's fingers began to close on her knees, then opened. The old bandages lay in clean white lines across them. She had carried two people to the clinic since the work began. This afternoon nobody at the table needed carrying.

At the stained corner, they stopped.

Not the performance. Only the phrase.

Ryo left the place empty. Nijika's hand stayed raised. Kita did not fill it with her voice.

The room waited with them.

Then Bocchi took them into the last part.

It did not get bigger. It became fuller. The first phrase came back with the bass beneath it and Kita above it, and the altered note was not a mistake any more, not a question. It was the place the song came home to.

Nijika put down the final beat with her fingers, not the stick.

Ryo let the last string go quiet.

Bocchi stayed standing.



"Again," said Sekhmet.

Everybody looked at her.

She had not closed her eyes. She was looking at the ceiling with both of them open.

"In a moment," Nijika said, laughing.

"Yes. In a moment."

Carmilla leaned across the bench.

"That place," she said to Bocchi. "When you all stopped. I thought you'd lost it."

"No."

"Then I realized you were waiting together."

Bocchi nodded. Her hands had begun to shake again now that they had nothing to do. She put one on the side of the guitar.

Carmilla looked at it.

"I liked it," she said. "The waiting too."

Kita came to sit beside Bocchi, and pulled her down gently by the cuff of her sleeve. Not because she was going to run. Because there was a place for her there, between her girlfriend and her friend.

Ryo reached for the bread. Daphne moved the last piece out of reach without looking. Ryo looked at her with professional respect.

"That was quick."

"It is mine."

Pride shifted in the chair at last. It gave its familiar small complaint. Nijika looked at it and grinned.

"Four minutes," Pride said.

"Nearly five."

"You had a space in it."

"That counts," said Ryo.

Echidna closed the book with one finger still inside it. She looked at Seika, then at the four of them, then at the book again.

"There was a repeat," she said. "Under the stain. And a mark for the end."

Ryo sat very straight.

"A repeat."

"But not there," Echidna added.

Ryo sat back.

Seika laughed once. A short, unwilling laugh, which made Nijika look at her and then away again before her sister could object.

"Will you show us?" Bocchi asked.

Echidna opened the book. Turned it toward her.

"Now?" she said.

"After we play it once more," said Bocchi.

At the end of the bench Sekhmet made a small approving sound, and closed her eyes just long enough to say:

"Wake me before you start."
