# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 10 — The Night Shift Is Still Ours

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 10
**Continuity state:** Night of Day 234 into dawn of Day 235 | Anko and Nazuna adjust their familiar rounds to unfinished works and a watched gate | a loose work cloth causes a report and is identified without pursuing beyond the line | Nazuna makes Anko stop after relief arrives | their friendship and shared room continue; Nazuna's loft remains above Anko's bed; the saved cigarette is not used

*When: The night of Day 234. There are more posts than there were yesterday, but the fence between them is still incomplete. Nazuna woke an hour before dinner and considers everyone else's sleep schedule a personal disadvantage.*

Nazuna had stolen Anko's pencil.

She had not taken the notebook. That would have been an act of war. She had taken the pencil, carried it up to the loft over Anko's bed, and put it behind her ear, where it did not belong.

Anko stood below the ladder with her coat on.

"Give it back."

"You have another one."

"I've got a short one."

"So do I."

"Mine was long when you took it."

Nazuna took it from behind her ear and examined the point. It had snapped while she was making her alterations to the watch sheet. She held the pencil out over the ladder.

Anko looked at it.

"You could have come down."

"I could have."

Anko took it. Nazuna climbed down, after all, with her own coat over one arm and the amended sheet in the other.

"What did you change?"

"Your bit."

"My bit was fine."

"Your bit ended after dawn. Then you went to the clinic, and then you went to the stores, and then you said you'd only close your eyes for a minute."

"I did close my eyes."

"Standing up."

Anko took the sheet. Nazuna had crossed out nothing official. She had drawn an arrow after the handover, toward a small uneven picture of a bed.

It was a terrible bed. Three legs. A pillow like a tooth.

Anko looked at it for a long time.

"That's not where my bed is."

"It's a diagram."

"It's a threat."

"Both," said Nazuna, and put her coat on.



They went out by the kitchen, where Momo was washing the last bowls.

She had left two small pieces of pear bread under an upturned bowl. Anko found them immediately. Nazuna found the bowl, turned it over, and looked inside in case there was a third piece.

"There isn't," said Momo.

"I was checking."

"Check the field."

Okarun, drying the bowls at her elbow, tried not to laugh. Turbo Granny, on the windowsill, did it for him.

"Put that coat on properly," she called after Anko. "You'll catch your death."

Anko looked back at her.

"You haven't got a coat."

"I'm a cat."

"And I'm going out."

They went out before the cat could win.



There was a lantern at the south approach and a man beside it. Not standing on the outside with a spear and hoping the lime would keep the dark away. Standing where he had been told to stand, inside the mark, with the unfinished work behind him and an open view of the path.

Anko spoke to him, checked the time for his relief, and looked at the timber on the ground where the gate would hang.

One of the braces had been moved since sunset.

She crouched beside it. Looked at the earth. At the boot marks.

"Wakana," said Nazuna.

"Could be."

"It is. He was down here before dinner."

"You saw him?"

"From the roof. Trying three different pegs. He got cross with the second one."

Anko stood up and asked the watcher. He had seen Wakana too. She wrote the changed brace down, because somebody on the next round would otherwise have to crouch beside it and wonder all over again.

Nazuna leaned over her shoulder.

"Put down the cross bit."

"I'm not putting down the cross bit."

"It could identify him."

"Go north."

Nazuna went north. Not on foot for very long. At the corner of the yard she lifted into the air, turned once to look back, and disappeared above the roofline.

Anko went on along the marked route. The ground was different now. Trenches inside the boundary, earth piled in low rough banks, and gaps left clear so that people could reach the watches without climbing over the work. She had to watch her feet as well as the woods.

At the west turn, she stopped and moved a coil of rope out of the path.

Then she wrote *rope* under the changed brace.

This was what her notebook had become on the quiet nights. A shape on the ridge. A smell from the woods. A loose board. Someone's shovel where a person would walk in the dark. All of it could hurt somebody. All of it needed seeing before the next person came.



Nazuna landed beside her at half past midnight.

Too quickly.

Anko had her hand in her coat before she recognized the boots.

Nazuna noticed. Her cheerful expression went away for a moment.

"Sorry."

Anko took the hand out.

"What?"

"North side. Near the posts. Something's moving. Low. I couldn't make it out from above."

"Outside?"

"Couldn't tell. That's why I'm here."

They did not go running toward it. Anko passed the report to the watcher at the west turn and told him where they were going. He sent word along the inside path. Then they went together, Nazuna staying on the ground because Anko had asked her to.

They could see it when they reached the north approach. A pale movement near the new timbers, low against the earth, appearing and disappearing between two posts.

The watcher had seen it too. His lantern was shuttered. He was holding the shutter with fingers that were too tight.

"Since when?" Anko asked.

He told her. She looked at Nazuna.

"Stay here. Look from this side."

Nazuna rose a little. Not high. Not toward the woods. Enough to see over the piled earth from the inside.

For a long moment she did not speak.

Then she came down.

"Cloth."

Nobody moved.

"A work cover. Caught round the bottom of the post. It's lifting in the wind."

Anko waited while the watcher brought the lantern low and opened the shutter. From that angle, once the light reached the ground, they could all see it. A strip from the cloth laid over the pegs, snagged and blowing out through the gap.

Nazuna's shoulders dropped.

Anko called back along the path that they had identified it. Then she and the watcher went to untangle the cloth, on their own side of the mark, with Nazuna looking past them into the field.

When it was folded and tied down, the watcher started to apologize.

"Don't," said Anko.

"But it was—"

"You saw something. You called it. We know what it is now."

Nazuna leaned on the post.

"I've mistaken a deer for worse."

Anko looked at her.

"You didn't put that in the notebook."

"You weren't there."

"I am now."

Nazuna folded her arms. Anko took out the pencil. The point was still annoyingly short.

She wrote *cloth secured*.



They sat for a few minutes on the empty cart behind the north watch. It had a loose wheel that the cooper meant to look at in the morning. Neither sat over that wheel. There were quite enough things to fall off in the settlement without choosing one deliberately.

Nazuna looked at the sky.

"I miss lights," she said.

"We've got a lantern."

"Not that. Streets. Signs. Somewhere selling something terrible at three in the morning."

"You complained about them."

"Yeah. They were terrible."

Anko took the second piece of bread from her pocket. It had been squashed against the tin she kept there. She pulled it into two pieces and gave Nazuna the larger one because Nazuna would complain if she saw the difference later.

Nazuna saw it immediately.

"You didn't eat yours."

"I was saving it."

"For a strip of cloth?"

"For this."

They ate without talking. The new posts stood dark against the pale part of the sky. Beyond them there was still a great deal of field, and beyond that the ridge.

Nazuna kept looking at it. Not at one place for too long. The titan had stood on it once, and every shape there had become a question for a while. She had not stopped looking because of that. She had become more careful about how close she went.

Anko did not ask what she was thinking. She knew enough.



Relief came before dawn.

Anko showed the next watcher the altered brace on the sheet, and the rope moved off the path, and the cloth that had caused the call. Nazuna described what she had seen from above, so that nobody later would turn *cloth* into *a thing that vanished*.

Then Anko put the notebook away.

She began to say something about checking the stores on the way in.

Nazuna held up the picture of the bed.

"Three legs," Anko said.

"It's still a bed."

They went back by the kitchen. Momo had not come down yet. The bowls were dry on the shelf. At the sill Turbo Granny lay in a patch of first light with her eyes closed, and said, without opening them:

"Took you long enough."

Neither gave her the satisfaction of answering.

Upstairs, Anko hung the coat beside her bed. The scratched tin remained in its pocket. She did not open it.

Nazuna climbed to the loft, found the place where she had left Anko's good sharpener, and dropped it carefully onto the blanket below.

Anko looked at it.

"Anything else of mine up there?"

There was a pause.

"No."

"Nazuna."

A sock came down.

Anko stared at it. Then she put it beside the sharpener, took off her boots, and lay down before she found a reason not to.
