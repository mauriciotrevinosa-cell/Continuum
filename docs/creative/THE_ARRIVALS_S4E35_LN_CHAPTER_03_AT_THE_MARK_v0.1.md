# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 15 — At the Mark

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 15
**Continuity state:** Day 242, morning | workers and cottage family withdrawn before the encounter | two source-world demons attack from outside the marked north boundary | Fern intercepts the first spell beyond the lime; Frieren kills the nearer attacker, while Stark and Himmel stop the flank with Mikasa covering the road | neither demon crosses; protection is not tested or proved universal | a short daylight check finds a false patient bundle, not an injured third person | local encounter contained; no pursuit, new ability or claim that the wider threat is ended

*When: The morning of Day 242. Breakfast is being served in the real house, including to the woman and child from the north cottage. The building crews have been kept inside. Anko has the watch, Brenna the road, and the north gate is still an unfinished frame with one leaf resting on a block.*

The first thing Fern did was move a wheelbarrow.

Someone had left it beside the inside post, turned sideways. Fine for a person with a hammer. Very bad for a person coming back quickly with someone behind them.

She put it against the water barrel, handles flat on the ground.

Stark watched her.

“What?” she said.

“Nothing.”

“You were about to say something.”

“I like that you're here.”

She looked away before answering.

“Watch the road.”

He did.

Himmel was on his left, sword still sheathed. Beyond the gate posts the lime showed white across the brown road. Anko had marked a standing place a little back from it with a heel in the dust. No one treated the heel-mark as magic. It was only somewhere they had agreed to stand.

Frieren stood beside Fern. Mikasa was farther right, where the ground rose toward the bank. Brenna had placed Wren behind an earth pile with a view of the grass above it, then stayed at the side of the frame where she could see both her and the road.

No workers on the walk. No one carrying water between them. No children at the cottage window.

The two figures came round the bend while the light was still low.

The pale coat first. The dark cloak behind it.

“You came,” said the nearer demon.

“We were already here,” said Frieren.

It looked at the empty working area, then past it at the cottages. Its gaze paused at the closed shutter.

“Where is she?”

Himmel's hand went to the hilt of his sword.

“You said your mother was at the bend.”

For the first time, the demon looked mildly inconvenienced.

“Yes.”

“Show us.”

It raised a hand and pointed behind it. A low shape lay at the edge of the road beyond the bank, half covered in cloth. Visible now, from the standing place. The two demons had brought it round the bend before approaching.

No face showed. No hand.

“Ask her to speak,” said Fern.

“She can't.”

“You didn't say that yesterday.”

“You didn't ask.”

The shorter demon made a small sound. It could have been amusement. It had moved toward the bank while they were looking at the bundle.

“Stop there,” said Mikasa.

It stopped.

Himmel saw the angle change. The pale coat on the road. The dark cloak partway up the slope. A little more space between them than before, and now the nearer one was no longer in the other's way.

“Come back down beside him,” he said.

The shorter demon looked at Mikasa's blades.

“Are you the healer?” it asked her.

“No.”

“Then why are you here?”

“Come down,” said Himmel.

The pale-coated demon sighed.

It was a very convincing sound. Tired. Patient with unreasonable people. Brenna had made that sound at a gate herself, often enough to recognise what it was meant to do to the people hearing it.

“We've asked politely,” it said.

“I heard,” said Frieren.

“We've waited.”

“You can still leave.”

It looked at her for a long time.

Then, quite thoughtfully, it said, “Mother.”

No shutter opened.

The demon looked again at the cottages.

“That made them move yesterday.”

Stark felt his stomach turn.

“It doesn't always work,” said Frieren.

“What does it mean?”

She did not answer.

The shorter demon laughed. This time there was no other word for it.

The pale-coated one raised its hand.

Not high. Not with a shouted name or a warning large enough to wait for. Its fingers opened beside the seam of its coat, and a point of light appeared between them.

“Down,” said Fern.

Her staff was already up.

The first spell struck her shield outside the lime with a noise like a plank splitting. Light spread across the transparent planes, sharp and white. Dust lifted from the road beneath it. The demon's hand turned, trying to drag the attack sideways round the edge.

Frieren fired through the space it left open.

One clean flash.

The pale coat jerked backward. The spell against Fern's shield stopped.

At the bank, the second demon moved.

It had been waiting for them to look at the first. It sprang diagonally down the slope, toward the gap between Stark and Mikasa, with one arm stretched out and light gathering along it.

“Right,” said Brenna.

Wren's arrow struck the road just ahead of its leading foot.

The demon shortened its stride to avoid the shaft. Only a little. Enough.

Himmel was already across the lime.

He went to the space between the two attackers, not to the falling coat. His sword came free as he moved. The shorter demon changed the direction of its raised hand toward him.

Stark stepped out beside him.

The axe took the demon across the forearm before the light could settle into an attack. Stark felt the impact up his wrists. The demon spun away from him, quick and furious, and went low toward the bank again.

Mikasa moved along the inside of the boundary to keep the cottages behind her.

She did not run past Stark. There was no opening for a second pair of blades there. She held her place and watched the demon's shoulders, ready for it to get through.

Fern sent a second shot into the dirt above its head.

The bank broke. Earth fell between the demon and the turn it had been trying to make. It came back toward the road instead.

Toward Himmel.

His sword went in before it could bring its uninjured hand round.

The demon looked at him.

There was surprise in its face. Not a plea. Not understanding. Surprise that the distance had disappeared while it was using its words on someone else.

Himmel took his sword out and stepped back.

Stark stayed beside him until the body fell.



It had taken less time than unloading a cart.

That was what Brenna thought afterwards, and hated thinking. They had prepared the standing places and moved the barrow and eaten early. They had had time to be afraid all through the night. Then one lifted hand, a shield, the sound of an axe, and the road was emptying itself of the two bodies.

Fern kept her staff raised.

Frieren did too.

The pale coat was breaking away in dark fragments, losing its shape from the wound outward. At the bank, the shorter one's cloak collapsed into the dirt as there stopped being anything beneath it.

Stark stared at the place where the arm had been.

“Back,” said Himmel.

Stark looked at him.

“With me.”

They came back over the lime together.

Mikasa counted them with her eyes. Two out. Two back. She kept her blades in her hands and her face toward the bend.

No one went forward for a fallen weapon. No one ran to the bundle.

“Anyone hit?” said Brenna.

Himmel shook his head.

“Stark.”

“No.”

“Show me.”

He looked down. His sleeve was torn at the elbow. The wood rail had done that yesterday; for a second he could not remember and went very cold.

“The gate,” he said. “That was the gate.”

Brenna checked anyway. A scrape, dry already. Nothing fresh.

Fern's hand trembled once on the staff. She tightened it. Frieren saw, and lowered her own staff enough to touch Fern's sleeve with two fingers.

Then she looked back at the road.



They checked the bundle only after the light had moved clear of the bank.

Himmel and Mikasa went, within sight of Frieren and Fern the whole way. Stark stayed at the line. Wren watched the high grass; Brenna watched the approach behind them. Anko had the return road kept empty.

There was no woman beneath the cloth.

Two lengths of fallen branch, a sack filled with grass, and a rolled cloak propped to resemble a head. One branch had snapped under the weight of the bundle being moved. The fresh break was pale.

Himmel stood looking at it.

Then he used the point of his sword to turn back the other edge of the cloth. Empty ground. No person concealed on the other side of the bank.

They went no farther than they had agreed. They could not check every hollow in the woods. They could check this place, in the daylight, with the others watching.

When they came back, Brenna asked him quietly, “Nothing?”

“No patient,” he said. “A bundle of wood.”

She looked past him at the road.

“I'll tell her,” said Aira.

She had come down from the house after Anko sent word that the fight was over. She was alone. The mother had stayed with her daughter, and the daughter had stayed with Momo, and nobody had asked the child to come and see what was left.

Himmel nodded.

“Tell her exactly,” he said.

Aira went back up the street.



Stark sat on the hinge block again.

The axe lay across his knees. He wiped the blade with a rag, stopped, and wiped it again in the same place.

Fern sat beside him, on the end that was too narrow for another person. He shifted to make room without looking up.

“I'm all right,” he said.

“I know.”

“You can go and eat.”

“I ate.”

He nodded. He had watched her do it. He had forgotten for a moment.

Across the road, Frieren was talking to Brenna. Neither looked pleased. The leaf on its block cast a thin stripe of shade over the empty wheel ruts. Beyond it, the lime was still visible where Himmel and Stark had stepped across and back.

“Did it try?” asked Brenna.

“To cross?” said Frieren.

Brenna nodded.

“It attacked us. We stopped it before it got here.”

“So we still don't know.”

“No.”

Brenna took that in. Then she picked up her rope and began moving the working barrier farther back, to where the water barrel stood.

Stark looked down at the rag.

“I thought the small one was going to say it again.”

“I know,” said Fern.

“While Himmel—”

He stopped.

Fern reached over and took the rag out of his hand. She folded it so the clean side was out, then put it back.

“It didn't get another chance,” she said.

He looked at her, and nodded once.

They sat until Wakana came down with the missing hinge pin in his hand. He looked at the road, then at the two of them, and stopped a little way away.

“Not yet,” said Fern.

Wakana put the pin in his pocket.

“All right,” he said. “I'll start at the other end.”
