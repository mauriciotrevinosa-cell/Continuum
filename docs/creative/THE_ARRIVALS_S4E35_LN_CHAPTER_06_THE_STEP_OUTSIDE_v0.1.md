# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 18 — The Step Outside

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 18
**Continuity state:** Day 244 | Aira and Mikasa are home from the ford | the household tests the unfinished gate as a working entrance, not a perfect shield | road guards and noncombatant workers practise distance, clear retreat and calling someone rather than following a stranger | an ordinary departure remains possible; Eren changes a snagging threshold instead of tightening control | north defence still unfinished; paid partners and outside trade remain | Aira visits the empty cottage without reclaiming its occupants or sealing it forever; no new threat or season-ending door event

*When: Day 244. Aira slept in her own place last night. Brenna and Dunmore are away on the agreed ford stop with the woman and child; Hollis is still at the settlement. The gate builders are back at work. There is one hung north leaf, gaps in the earthwork, and enough mistakes left in the hinge to keep Wakana occupied all morning.*

The gate failed its first test because of a bucket.

Not a monster. Not a spell. Cobb had been told to bring a bucket through the opening, and had brought it with his arm straight, and the handle had caught on the end of the temporary bar.

The bucket stopped. Cobb did not.

Half the water went down his trousers.

For a moment everyone watched him in silence.

“Again,” said Anko.

“With the bucket?”

“With a bucket that fits through the gate, yes.”

“It's a normal bucket.”

Wakana crouched beside the bar. Eren crouched beside him. The end stuck a hand's breadth farther into the passage than it needed to. They both looked at it with the grave attention men sometimes gave a piece of wood when they would rather not look at a wet pair of trousers.

“We can shorten that,” said Wakana.

“We will,” said Eren.

Cobb wrung out the side of his coat.

“Before I try again?”

Anko glanced at him.

“You'd prefer that?”

“A bit.”

She made a mark in her notebook. Not a long policy. Just *bar end*, with a short angry line beneath it.

They were practising because Brenna had asked before she left. *Show me a tired person carrying something. Then show me two. Don't show me Stark opening it with one hand and tell me it's finished.*

Stark was offended by having been used as a bad example while absent. He said so from the hinge block, where he was holding a length of rope that was not attached to anything dangerous and was still somehow his fault.

“You're very good at opening things,” said Fern.

“That's what I thought.”

“That isn't the same test.”

He sighed.

She smiled into her cup.



The second test was Tam pretending to be a traveller.

He was not very good at it.

“Hello,” he said, from outside the marker. “I'm lost.”

“You live east of here,” said Cobb.

“I'm pretending.”

“I know where.”

“Cobb,” said Anko.

Cobb looked at her.

“Let your brother get lost.”

Tam tried again. This time he stood at the place Brenna had chosen for someone arriving while work was going on, well clear of the lime and the timber being moved. Wren waited off to one side with her bow unstrung. Himmel stood inside the frame with Mikasa, watching the places a second person could approach.

“Hello,” said Tam. “Somebody's hurt down the road.”

Cobb's face changed.

The joke had gone out of it without being asked to.

“Wait there,” he said.

He turned his head toward Himmel, then stopped and looked back at his brother, as if embarrassed to be practising something so simple in front of someone who knew how many mistakes he usually made.

“Himmel,” he called. “Someone needs help.”

Himmel came forward. He did not tell Cobb to stop caring. He asked Tam where, and how many people, and whether he could show the place without taking anyone out of sight.

Tam answered with the details Anko had given him. A person at the bend. No visible wound. Can't walk.

“Do I come in now?” he asked, at the end.

“We haven't got there yet,” said Anko.

“It's hot out here.”

“Then we've learned that too.”

Wakana looked up from the shortened bar.

“We could put a little shade there.”

“Inside the work boundary,” said Anko. “Not a reason for the builders to move the line.”

“I meant a cloth on two poles.”

“Show me on the drawing.”

Tam stayed where he was, looking aggrieved. Cobb brought him the remaining half-bucket of water and set it down at the agreed waiting place without going down the road with him.

“There,” he said. “Now you're lost with water.”

Tam drank.



Aira came down after the test.

She stood beside Mikasa while Eren planed the end of the bar smooth. Small curls of wood lay across his boot. He picked one off, looked at it, and put it down with the others.

“How's the hand?” said Mikasa.

Aira opened and closed it.

“Fine.”

Mikasa looked at her.

“Mostly,” said Aira.

“Don't grip so hard on a long walk.”

“I know.”

“You can carry it and still let your fingers move.”

Aira looked down at the old blade. She had cleaned it again that morning. It did not need cleaning. There was a little dark place beside the nick that she had mistaken for dirt until she remembered it had always been there.

“She asked if I was going back with someone,” she said.

Mikasa waited.

“At the room. She wanted to know I wasn't walking alone.”

Eren stopped planing.

“She's like that,” said Aira. “You stand outside her door and she asks whether you've had soup.”

“Her mother does too,” said Mikasa.

Aira nodded.

The three of them watched Cobb bring the empty bucket back through the opening. This time it did not catch.

“Did you see them in?” said Eren.

“Yes,” said Aira. “The room. Both of us saw it.”

“Good.”

He picked the plane up again, and Aira was glad he had something small to do with his hands.



At midday, Momo brought food to the work area and refused to let anyone eat it in the gate passage.

“We just cleared it,” said Fern.

“Then keep it clear.”

The bench went by the barrel. The food went on the bench. The cups went wherever people had clean enough hands to take them, which excluded half the builders until Momo pointed at the water.

Diablo had carried the pot down with her. He set it on the cloth and held the lid while she served.

Tam looked at him, then looked quickly away.

Diablo noticed.

“Something on my face?”

“No.”

Tam reddened. Cobb stared hard into his bowl.

“They said,” Tam began, then stopped.

“I expect they did,” said Diablo.

Himmel was washing his hands a few paces away. He looked over but did not come and answer for either of them.

Tam tried again.

“You're not the same as those two.”

“No.”

“I know. I just—”

“You may ask,” said Diablo. “Preferably before your soup gets cold.”

Tam looked at the bowl.

“Would you have come for us?”

Diablo's expression changed. Less smile, more attention.

“If you needed bringing back?”

Tam nodded.

“We have an agreement about the road. You walk it for this household. This household does not stop counting you when you step outside.”

It was not a promise that he could reach every road in time. Tam did not ask him to make it one. He nodded again, much more awkwardly, and took the bowl Momo was holding out to him.

“It's the same soup,” said Cobb under his breath.

“I can see that.”

“Third bowl?”

“Shut up.”

Momo filled both of them without asking.



In the afternoon, Aira went to the cottage.

She did not take the sword inside. She leaned it against the step, under the shutter, and used the key Jinshi had given her for checking the latch.

The room smelled of dry cloth and the cooking fire that had not been lit. There was a lighter patch on the shelf where the cup had stood. She had not noticed the patch yesterday. Yesterday there had still been too much to carry.

She checked the window peg. It held.

Then she sat on the bed for a moment, where she had sat with the mother two evenings before, and looked at the empty table.

No packed shirts. No beans. No cup sliding toward her when the mattress dipped.

She did not stay very long.

Outside, the step needed sweeping. A little bark from the gate work had blown down the street and lodged against the door. She got the broom from the side wall and began to push it away.

Someone caught the far end before it could go under the hedge.

Mau. He had been coming back from carrying a bag of wooden pegs, with Frieren beside him carrying nothing because the bag, she said, contained a knot she had already advised against.

“You can leave it,” said Aira.

“I'm holding it already.”

She swept the bark into his hand. He looked at the amount, then at the small bag he had been very carefully trusted with, and put the bark in his pocket instead.

Frieren watched him do it.

“That's going to end up on the bed.”

“It isn't.”

“You'll forget.”

“I'm remembering right now.”

Aira almost smiled.

Mau did not ask whether she was all right. He looked at the closed shutter, then at the broom, then held out his free hand.

“The other side?”

“I can do it.”

“Okay.”

He waited while she finished. Frieren leaned against the low wall, looking down the street toward the gate where somebody was laughing at Tam. The three of them heard it, and did not have to stop hearing it because of the empty room behind them.

When Aira was done, she put the broom back where the girl would know to find it. Then she picked up the blade and locked the door.

At the water barrel, Wakana had hung the gate leaf properly at last.

He called them over to watch it move. Eren took one end, Tam the other, and they swung it inward slowly, clear of the bucket and the shortened bar. It travelled most of the way before stopping with a deep, disagreeable scrape.

Everyone looked at the bottom edge.

“The ground,” said Wakana.

“I thought we'd finished the ground,” said Tam.

“That was the hinge.”

Mau took the bark out of his pocket and dropped it on the scrap pile before Frieren could look at him again. Aira leaned her sword against the bench, picked up a shovel, and asked where they wanted the soil.
