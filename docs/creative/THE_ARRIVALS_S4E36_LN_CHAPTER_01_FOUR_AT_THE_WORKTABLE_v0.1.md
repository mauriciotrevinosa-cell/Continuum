# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 19 — Four at the Worktable

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 19
**Continuity state:** Day 245 | Senku, Maomao and Kusuri make room for Shoko at the worktable | clinic cloths are scarce, dust gets in and covered wet cloth stays wet | four different approaches to a drying/storage case; ordinary materials and test pieces | the first closed prototype fails; no sterilisation or healing breakthrough | the four agree to keep working, without making Shoko give up her own afternoon

*When: Day 245. The departure cart has gone. There is work at both approaches, and dust from that work has reached a shelf in the clinic that Maomao cleaned yesterday.*

Shoko brought the shelf cloth to the laboratory in a bowl.

It was white. Or it had been. There was a faint grey line along one fold, where the cupboard door did not sit quite square.

Senku looked up.

"If that's a complaint about the door, Wakana's—"

"It isn't."

"Then what is it?"

Shoko put the bowl on the part of the worktable that contained the least fire.

"Something I can't use."

Maomao, at the other end, looked at the cloth. Then she looked at Kusuri.

"I told you."

"You told me the cover was wrong. You didn't tell me what cover would be right."

"I was hoping you might notice."

"I noticed three things! You disliked all of them!"

Senku reached for the cloth.

Shoko lifted the bowl out of reach.

He stopped with his hand still in the air.

"Where has that been?" she asked.

He looked at his fingers. There was a black line across two of them from something he had been grinding.

He put the hand down.

Kusuri laughed once, then inspected her own hands before anybody could ask.



The difficulty was not that nobody washed the cloths.

Everybody washed the cloths. That was part of the difficulty. One person washed them, and another found them damp and moved them, and somebody else took a basket through the yard while the earth was being shifted. A clean piece could arrive at the clinic folded beautifully, smelling faintly of the workshop and with a small wood shaving tucked inside it.

It had been worse since the works began. More hands hurt. More wraps needed washing. Less room to hang things away from dust. The road could not produce an endless supply of new linen, and Maomao had begun counting the sound pieces the way Fern counted lamp oil.

Kusuri had tried a cover over the drying frame.

"It kept the dirt off," she said.

"It kept everything in," Maomao answered.

"That was the idea."

Shoko sat down. Not on the stool beside the door, where a visitor might sit while being told what the other three had already decided. At the fourth edge of the table. She moved a sealed jar with great care and set the bowl in its place.

"Show me."

Kusuri went for the covered frame.

Senku moved the thing that could catch fire farther away.



They opened the cover together.

The cloth inside looked clean. That was the first thing Kusuri pointed out.

Shoko did not disagree. She waited while Maomao lifted the corner, then touched the underside of the fold. Maomao's mouth became a thin straight line.

"Still damp."

"The top one wasn't," Kusuri said.

"The bottom one is."

Senku crouched to look at the small gap beneath the frame.

"You've stopped the air."

"I stopped the dust."

"Same route."

Kusuri sat back on her heels and looked at him with the expression she used when a perfectly correct fact had arrived at an irritating time.

Shoko lifted one of the plain cloth samples that had not been near a patient. She looked at it against the light. Put it down again.

"I don't need a pretty box," she said. "I need to take one out without wondering whether I ought to send the whole basket back."

"A drying case," said Senku.

"A case that lets it dry," said Maomao. "Those aren't the same thing."

"That's what I said."

"You said the first one."

Shoko put her chin in her hand.

"You do this a lot."

"Yes," said Kusuri.

"I wasn't asking you."

"I know."



They drew it on the back of a failed glass tally.

Senku drew a frame. Maomao drew a line through the place where used wraps had been put down that morning. Kusuri added removable trays. Shoko put a finger on the front.

"That needs to open with one hand."

Senku looked up.

"A latch?"

"A latch I can open when the other hand is busy. Without touching everything inside."

He changed the front.

"Not a big tray either," Shoko said. "If I take the whole thing to a bed, people will put things back into it."

"They do that now," said Maomao.

"I know. I was there."

Kusuri drew three smaller trays instead of one. Senku took the pencil and changed the runners, because the new trays would fall straight through his first design. Maomao watched him finish and then crossed out the shelf above them.

"Not under bottles."

Senku looked at the drawing.

"That's not a bottle shelf."

"It will be. Within a day."

Shoko nodded.

"Ten billion percent," Senku said reluctantly, and removed it.

After that they argued about the sides. Too open and the work dust would reach the folds. Too closed and Kusuri's clean-looking damp basket would happen again. A cloth cover was washable, which mattered to Maomao, and not every scrap of cloth was equally useful, which mattered to Kusuri. Senku wanted to compare them under the same conditions. Shoko wanted somebody to remember which pieces had only been tests.

Kusuri wrote *TEST* on the paper beside them in large letters.

"Not on the cloth," Shoko said.

Kusuri put down the ink brush she had just picked up.



The afternoon got into the room without any of them noticing.

At some point Jinshi put down four cups near the door. Maomao looked over, saw him, and moved one of the cups farther from Senku's elbow.

"Thank you."

Jinshi paused. She had said it while looking at the drawing, as if it were a normal thing to say to him.

"There's bread too," he said.

"Then put it somewhere he hasn't used."

Jinshi looked at the worktable.

"Where?"

"A reasonable question," Shoko said, and made a small clean place at her end.

They ate in pieces while they worked. Kusuri put one of the test covers beside the open window. Senku moved it. She moved it back.

"The other one isn't by the window."

"I know."

"Then you're comparing two things at once."

"The window is where it might actually go."

"First find out what the cover does. Then find out what the window does."

Shoko, chewing, looked from him to Kusuri.

"He's right about that one."

Senku grinned.

"And you're putting it beside a sill people lean on with dirty sleeves," she added.

The grin went away.

Maomao drank her tea.



They had a small version by late afternoon. Not a clinic full of them. One case made from sound offcuts, with temporary runners and two covers they did not trust yet.

The first tray caught at the corner. The second did not catch, but tipped. Shoko put two fingers on its edge and showed them how little it took to empty it onto a person's knees.

Senku stared at it.

"Again," he said.

Shoko did it again.

Kusuri made the mark on the tally before he could explain why it had happened.

"I know why," he told her.

"Then the next one won't do it."

Maomao looked at Kusuri. Kusuri looked back, delighted. Shoko laughed quietly into her cup.

Outside, someone went past the window slowly, stopped, and then went past it again in the other direction.

White hair. Black blindfold. Hands very carefully not tapping the wall.

Shoko glanced up.

"He said he'd meet me."

The other three looked toward the window at the same time.

"He seems to be doing a thorough job," Maomao said.

Shoko stood. Looked at the case, the drawing, the trays that still tipped if used by a person rather than by their inventor.

"Don't use it tonight. I'll look again."

"I wasn't going to—" Senku began.

"I know. I'm saying it anyway."

She took her cup to the door. Gojo had stopped pretending to walk anywhere and was waiting beside it.

Maomao slid the drawing back into the middle of the table after Shoko left. There were marks on all four sides now. Senku's square frame. Kusuri's trays. Her own crossed-out shelf. The front Shoko had made them change.

Kusuri leaned over it, reached for the pencil, then stopped.

"We need her for the catch."

"Tomorrow," said Maomao.

Senku turned the little case round and pulled out the tray with one hand. It caught at the corner.

He looked at it for a moment.

Then he put it back on the table, in front of Shoko's empty stool.
