# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 20 — The Door Between the Rooms

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 20
**Continuity state:** Day 246, evening | Ori wants a small supper and to show Mau and Frieren her ordinary guessing game | the connecting door is shared space, not a clinic post | no magic or eye-reading used for the game | Frieren tends Ori's tangled hair; Mau washes the bowls | Rimuru uses the loft ladder over their closet; Milim returns to the adjacent bed | no new parenthood declaration, origin revelation or promised family-day event

*When: The evening of Day 246. Momo has made enough supper for everybody, and Ori has asked for three bowls on a tray. She has been carrying an empty cup around all afternoon because she does not want to explain the rest until they are there.*

"Can I borrow you?" Ori asked.

Mau looked up from the peg he was holding. He had finished sorting the basket a while ago and had kept the last good one because it was smooth under his thumb.

"For what?"

"Supper."

He looked toward the long table, where supper was already happening.

"I was going to do that."

"Upstairs."

"With you?"

Ori nodded.

"All right."

She did not go. She looked over his shoulder.

Frieren was at the table with her grimoire beside her plate, reading while she waited for somebody to tell her she should not. She had heard the whole conversation. Everyone involved knew that she had heard it.

Mau turned his head.

"You too."

"She hasn't asked me."

Ori went pink.

"Frieren."

Frieren looked up.

"Can I borrow you too?"

"Yes."

She closed the book at once. Mau looked at her. Frieren looked back with a serene expression that suggested there had never been any possibility of another answer.



They took the tray upstairs together.

Ori had intended to carry it by herself. She had got it almost as far as the first step when the bowls slid toward one edge, and Mau put a hand beneath the other side. Between them they got it upstairs without putting supper on the floor.

There was no table waiting in the east room. There was the shelf, exactly in the middle of the way, and the bed, and Frieren's reading chair, and Ori's connecting door open beyond them.

"The floor?" Mau suggested.

Ori looked at Frieren.

"For supper," Frieren agreed. "Not inside my book."

They put a cloth down between the bed and the reading chair, where the shelf did not quite reach. Ori fetched her stool, looked at the other two settling on the floor, and took the stool back.

Then she came through the connecting doorway with three cups and a small wooden bead.

Mau looked at the cups.

"More tea?"

"A game."

Frieren leaned forward a little.

"After supper."

"I know," said Ori. "I only brought it."

Mau hid his smile in the first mouthful of soup.



Ori had learned the game at the fort. Or something like it. At the fort it involved a bowl, a piece of bread, and a great deal of disagreement about whether the white dog counted as a player. Ori's version used three cups, because she thought that would be enough.

She explained it while they ate. A bead under a cup. The cups moved. They had to say where it had gone. No looking underneath until the guessing was finished.

Frieren asked two questions about the order of the turns.

Mau asked whether she had eaten anything since lunch.

Ori looked at him.

"It's not that sort of game."

"I know. That's a separate question."

"Yes. Bread."

"How much bread?"

"Some."

Frieren lifted one eyebrow.

Ori sighed, picked up another piece, and ate it with great dignity. Mau put a little more soup in her bowl while she was occupied.

From above the closet, something moved. The loft latch opened, and Rimuru appeared at the top of his own ladder, looked down at the three of them on the floor, and stopped.

"Am I interrupting?"

"Yes," said Frieren.

"Oh."

"She's explaining a game," Mau told him.

"Oh," Rimuru said again, in an entirely different voice.

He came down, stepped carefully round the edge of their cloth, and went to the corridor door.

"Office," he said, before Frieren could ask. "For a little while. I have paper downstairs that doesn't have soup on it."

"Nothing has soup on it," said Ori.

Rimuru looked at the small drop beside Mau's knee.

Mau put his hand over it.

Rimuru smiled and let himself out.



They cleared the bowls before they began.

Ori did not want the bead getting lost under somebody's plate. She put it on the bare floor, set a cup over it, then looked at Mau's eyes for a moment.

He understood what she was asking.

"Just cups," he said.

"Just cups."

He did not ask the eyes for anything. He did not reach farther. They were small, ordinary cups, with a bead under one of them and his filter doing the quiet work it had learned to do.

Ori moved them slowly the first time.

Frieren guessed correctly. Mau did too.

Ori made it faster.

Mau lost the bead at once. He pointed anyway, with great confidence, and Ori lifted the cup to show nothing under it.

"How?" he said.

"I moved it."

"I was there for that part."

Frieren guessed correctly again.

Ori narrowed her eyes.

"No spells."

"I haven't used one."

"You moved your hand."

Frieren held up the hand. There was a crumb between two fingers.

Ori examined it. Then she took it and put it on the empty plate, and prepared another round.

This time she moved the cups so quickly that one caught against another and the bead rolled out between them.

All three looked at it.

"The fourth cup," Mau said.

Ori covered her face.

Frieren picked up the bead, set it under the nearest cup, and waited until Ori looked through her fingers.

"Again. A little slower."



Mau was worse at moving the cups than he was at following them.

He could remember which cup he was supposed to be hiding it under. He could also remember how Frieren and Ori were looking at his hands, and the second thing made the first much harder than it should have been.

He moved the wrong cup. Stopped. Tried to undo it.

"You can't take it back," said Ori.

"I'm the person moving it."

"That's why."

Frieren nodded.

"Quite right."

He looked at her.

"You're enjoying this."

"Yes."

Ori guessed. She was wrong. She looked at the empty cup with such disappointment that he almost moved the bead for her.

Almost.

Then she lifted the other cup, found it, and laughed at herself. He was glad he had left it where it was.

By the time it was Frieren's turn, they had forgotten to keep score. Frieren looked at both of them, put the bead beneath the middle cup, and moved it so little that Mau began to laugh before she stopped.

"Which one?" she asked.

Ori pointed.

Frieren lifted the cup. The bead was there.

"An excellent guess."

"You didn't move it."

"I moved the cup."

"You know what I mean."

"I don't know," Frieren said, "what you mean at all."

Ori leaned against her while she laughed. Not on purpose, at first. Then on purpose, and Frieren's arm came round her shoulders without either of them saying anything about it.



There was a knot in Ori's hair at the back.

Frieren found it when she lifted the hair clear of the collar, and touched it with two fingers, and then looked at Mau.

"The comb."

He fetched it from the shelf. The shelf was still in the way. He stepped round it, as he did every day, and brought the comb back.

Ori turned a little so that Frieren could reach. The cups remained on the floor in front of her. She moved one with her bare toe while Frieren worked at the knot.

"Hold still."

"I am."

"Your head too."

Mau gathered the bowls on the tray. He was about to ask whether they wanted anything else when Ori said:

"Can we do it again?"

He looked at the cups.

"Tomorrow?"

"Not tomorrow if you're busy. Just—again."

"Yes," he said. "Ask us."

She nodded. Frieren eased the last of the knot loose, and Ori shut her eyes with the relief of it.

Milim appeared at the connecting doorway while Mau was standing up with the tray.

She looked at the cloth, the three cups, Ori sitting under Frieren's careful hand.

"What are you doing?"

"A game," said Ori.

Milim came in immediately.

"I'm good at games."

"We're finished."

"I'll be good at the next one."

Mau put the tray down again. He went through the connecting door, fetched another cup from the place Ori showed him, and came back.

Milim settled on the floor with all the concentration she brought to a contest. Frieren handed Ori the comb, tucked her own feet beneath her, and took the bead.

"Watch carefully," she told the dragon.

Mau sat down before she could show Milim her excellent technique.
