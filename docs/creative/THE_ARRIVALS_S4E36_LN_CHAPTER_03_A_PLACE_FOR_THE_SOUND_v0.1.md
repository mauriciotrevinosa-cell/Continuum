# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 21 — A Place for the Sound

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 21
**Continuity state:** Day 248 | Kessoku investigate a rattle in their existing music room rather than abandoning rehearsal | Bocchi identifies how it changes what she hears; Kita, Nijika and Ryo test with her | Wakana repairs an ordinary fastening; Marin uses a saved scrap for a removable pad | no new room, electric amplification or perfect soundproofing | the girlfriends keep an unfinished musical idea of their own

*When: Day 248, afternoon. The music room is the same one, with the same private stair. The ninth song is back in the book. What Bocchi is trying to play now has no name and, so far, no reliable middle.*

The room made a noise every time Ryo played the low note.

Not a musical noise. A small unpleasant buzz, like an insect trapped between a cup and a wall.

Ryo played the note again.

The room answered.

"Stop," said Nijika.

Ryo stopped. So did the room.

"Thank you."

Ryo played it again.

"Ryo."

"We have to establish whether it happens every time."

"We've established it seventeen times."

Kita put down her cup before laughing. Bocchi remained on the rug with her guitar across her knees, looking toward the window.

"It wasn't there before."

"The window?" asked Ryo.

"The noise."

"It may have been quieter."

"It wasn't there," Bocchi said again.

Nijika looked at her. Then at the window.

"All right. One more time."

Ryo's expression did not change. She was pleased.



They had already tried playing more quietly.

That had made the noise worse, in the sense that all of them could hear it more clearly. Bocchi began to hold back before the note, waiting for the buzz, and then came into the next part too late. Ryo watched her do it twice and took her hand off the strings.

"It's not your part."

"I know."

"You're looking at it like it's your part."

Bocchi looked at the little page on the floor. The middle was full of crossings-out. It would have been very convenient if everything wrong with the afternoon had been on the page. She could cross that out too.

Kita sat beside her.

"Show me where you hear it."

Bocchi played the first bit. Kita hummed the line she had been trying above it. At the lower change Ryo came in, and the buzz returned, and Bocchi's thumb caught against the string.

"There."

Kita nodded. She did not say it was barely noticeable. She had heard the way Bocchi played the moment before it happened, and the moment after.

"Let's find it."

They put the instruments down one at a time, carefully, except Ryo, who had to keep producing the offending note. Nijika went to the door. Kita went to the window. Bocchi stood between the two, trying to hear the room without adding all the things she thought the others might be thinking about her hearing it.

"Here," Kita said.

The fastening at the lower corner of the window had worked loose. Not enough to open the window. Enough for a little piece of metal to tremble against the wood when the bass found it.

Kita held it still with one finger.

Ryo played.

Nothing buzzed.

All four of them stared at Kita's finger.

"Stay there," said Ryo.

"I have a vocal part."

"You can sing there."

Nijika picked up a folded cloth and threw it at her. It fell short. Ryo looked at it on the floor, then back at Nijika.

"You should practise that."



Wakana came when they asked.

Marin came because she had been with him, and because the words *the bass makes the window angry* were not something she was willing to miss.

They found Kita still holding the fastening with one finger. Ryo had stopped playing and was eating something she had apparently had the whole time.

"You're making me do this for nothing," Kita told her.

"The silence is valuable."

Wakana tested the fastening. Pulled at the wood beside it. Looked at the little marks in the frame, and at where the screw sat.

"It moves," he said.

"We've got that far," said Nijika.

He looked apologetic. Marin put her hand on his shoulder before he could apologize for correctly identifying the problem they had asked him to identify.

"Can it stop moving?"

"Yes. But don't hold it there all afternoon."

Kita took away her finger with great relief.

While Wakana worked, Marin looked round the room. At the rug, the practice pad, the steps where Seika sometimes sat. At the copied music sheets laid flat under a small wooden weight, so that the breeze from the door did not mix yesterday's tune with today's.

"You need somewhere for all that."

"We have somewhere," Nijika said. "The floor."

"I can see that."

"It's very accessible," said Ryo.

Marin lifted one of the loose sheets by the empty edge. Bocchi made a small noise. Marin stopped at once.

"This one?"

"The order. It's—"

"Oh. Sorry. Show me."

Bocchi showed her. The beginning. The bit that had been the middle. The bit that might become the middle if she could get it to meet the ending. Marin laid them down exactly as they had been, with much more care than she had picked them up.

"You've got three middles."

"I know."

"That's quite a lot of middle."

Kita laughed. Bocchi looked at her, offended for a moment, then saw the way she was looking at the pages. Not as if they were something to be tidied out of sight.

"We could hang the finished ones," Kita suggested. "Clips. Beside the practice times."

"When there are finished ones," said Bocchi.

"There are some."

Ryo nodded at the page beneath her own knee.

"This one's finished. I am preventing it from escaping."



Wakana tightened the fastening, then tested it with his hand. The wood was sound. The noise went away.

Then Ryo played the note, and a different little noise came from the edge of the board.

Wakana closed his eyes briefly.

Marin touched his shoulder again.

"You fixed the first one."

"Yes."

"Now there's only this one."

He opened his eyes and looked at the edge. Asked Ryo to play. Touched one place, then another.

Marin went to the workshop and came back with a saved scrap of cloth. Not a new quilt, not one of the good things on somebody's bed. A small piece left from an old mending, rolled up with the bits that were still useful.

She folded it while Wakana showed her where the two surfaces met. A pad, removable, small enough that they could still use the fastening. He set it in place. She trimmed a loose thread rather than leaving it to tickle the glass.

Ryo played.

They waited.

Nothing.

Ryo played it once more because Nijika was watching her.

Still nothing.

"Don't promise the whole room won't make another sound," Marin told Wakana.

He looked at her.

"I wasn't going to."

"Good. You're coming upstairs with me when we're done. You've mended enough windows for one afternoon."

He went pink. Nijika found something to straighten on the practice pad, and Kita looked down at Bocchi with the sudden helpless smile people got when somebody else's affection made their own easier.



They played after the two had gone.

The bass no longer had the buzzing note beneath it. The room still sounded like their room. Not perfect. The door let some of the high notes out. The wall gave something back when Nijika forgot herself with the stick. A voice in the corridor could still become part of a quiet passage if the person used it loudly enough.

None of that meant they had to stop.

Bocchi played the beginning. Ryo let her have it alone this time. Kita waited until the second phrase, then tried the line she had hummed earlier, with a little less force at the top.

Bocchi heard somewhere for the middle to go.

She did not reach it. Not yet. But it was there, at the edge of what the other three were doing. She stopped, made a mark, and asked them to go back.

They went back.

At the third attempt Nijika changed the turn. At the fourth Kita stopped singing and listened. At the fifth Ryo put the low note in a different place, and Bocchi lifted her head so sharply that Kita laughed.

"That?"

"Again."

They did it again.

Much later, when Nijika had gone to put their next kitchen turn on the sheet and Ryo had gone to investigate what the kitchen turn might produce, Kita stayed on the rug beside Bocchi.

"Do you want me to stay?" she asked.

"Yes."

It came out at once. Bocchi blinked at herself.

Kita put her cup down and moved closer until their knees touched.

"Good."

Bocchi played the new middle slowly. Kita tried the line above it. It was too high. She stopped halfway, made a face, and came in lower.

"That one," Bocchi said.

Kita sang it again. When she finished, she rested her head against Bocchi's shoulder for a moment, and Bocchi kept her hand on the strings so that they could both hear how the last little sound went away.

Then she reached for the pencil with the hand Kita was not holding.
