# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 22 — Square Corners

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** Day 250 | five days of ordinary cloth-case trials produce a limited improvement, not sterile supplies | Kusuri's comparison overturns an attractive cover; Maomao protects washing and handling requirements | Senku adjusts the warped frame and removable runners | Shoko tests the case through an actual bedside reach, then joins the redesign | the fourth worktable place becomes habitual; no miracle medicine, limitless RCT or new industry

*When: Day 250. The little drying case has been rebuilt twice. Its trays have been taken out more often than its makers have admitted. All test cloths are still kept apart from the clinic's supplies.*

"It is dry," said Senku.

Shoko touched the cloth.

"Yes."

"It stayed dry after we closed it."

"Yes."

"And look at the other one."

"I'm looking."

He waited. Shoko looked at the other one, where Kusuri had put a small paper mark beside the edge. Then at the sample on the open shelf, with its familiar dust line.

"Better," she said.

Senku grinned.

"Now let me take one out," she added.

The grin remained for another second. Then he remembered the last time she had said that.



The first cover had looked very promising.

It lay in the rejected pile now. Smooth, neat, closely fitted, an excellent thing to point at if the person pointing did not plan to keep anything underneath it.

Kusuri had disliked it almost immediately. She had not been able to say why. That had irritated her enough that she set aside matching scraps and made them all wait while she compared it with the less impressive cover Maomao had suggested.

"You changed your mind," Senku had said when she showed him.

"I got an answer."

"That's what I said."

"No. You said it like I'd lost."

The attractive cover kept the folds damp. The other did not do that as badly. Neither kept all the dust out if somebody left the front hanging open, which somebody had, because he was measuring a runner.

Kusuri had written that down too.

Senku had objected to the wording, but not the result.

Now the four stood around the small case on the clinic table. Maomao had cleared a place for it, then cleared it again after Senku set down the tools he had just brought through the yard.

"Over there."

He moved them.

"Not on the clean wraps."

He moved them farther.

Shoko tried the front catch with one hand.

It opened.

Senku held his breath without meaning to.

She slid the first tray toward her. It came smoothly, stopped where it ought to stop, and did not tip onto her knees. She took a test piece off it, set it aside, and pushed the tray back.

"Again," said Kusuri.

Shoko looked at her.

Kusuri looked at Senku.

Senku folded his arms.

"Yes. Again."



It went wrong when Shoko did not stand where the drawing wanted her to stand.

She put the case to one side, as it might be beside the examination table. Asked Maomao to keep a hand where a patient would be. Turned toward the case without abandoning the other task.

The tray caught.

Not much. Only enough to make her pull harder, which would have been enough to bring several other things with it if they had been loose.

She stopped before that happened.

"There."

Senku moved to see.

"It's the angle."

"Yes."

"It came out straight before."

"Because I stood in front of it."

He looked at the case. At the table. At the place where Maomao was keeping her hand because Shoko had asked her to.

Kusuri set down the tally sheet without making a mark yet.

"We didn't try that."

"We did now," said Senku.

He crouched to look at the runner.

Maomao kept her hand where it was. Shoko did not tell her to move it. There was something useful about leaving the room arranged around what they were trying to do, instead of tidying the difficulty out of sight.

"Frame's not square," Senku said.

"The drawing was," said Kusuri.

"The wood wasn't at the meeting."

Shoko laughed. It surprised him enough that he looked up from the runner and hit his shoulder on the edge of the table.

Maomao finally moved her hand.

"Now," she said, "we have a patient."



They took it back to the worktable.

Not to start again. There were parts worth keeping now. The cover that did not look as good. The three removable trays. The low catch Shoko could reach without finding it with her whole hand. Kusuri's test sheet, which had become so full of corrections that she had made another copy and kept the old one beneath it.

Senku took the frame apart at one corner. Maomao held it steady. Kusuri kept the good runner clear of the scattered shavings. Shoko watched how the pieces fitted, then picked up the drawing and put a thumb on the edge.

"Could this be wider?"

Senku glanced over.

"The whole front?"

"Here. So there's room to come at it from that side."

He took the drawing. Looked at the place. Then at the case.

"A little."

"A little would help."

Maomao shifted her grip while he worked.

"You could have told us the first day."

"I hadn't used it the first day."

"You use drawers."

"And you use hands. We've still had four arguments about where mine go."

Kusuri put her face very close to the tally sheet. Her shoulders shook.

Maomao looked at her.

"Write that down too, if it's useful."

Kusuri did.



They took a break when the glue needed leaving alone.

It was not quite the same as deciding they had finished. Senku stayed looking at the corner for another minute after Shoko put down her cup. Kusuri almost touched it, remembered, and put both hands in her lap. Maomao looked through the clinic list and crossed out nothing, because a useful case would not make the rest of the list disappear.

"You don't like the waiting," Shoko said.

"I'm fine with waiting," Senku answered. "When I know what I'm waiting for."

"He likes it better if something glows," Kusuri said.

"It gives useful information."

"Sometimes it gives smoke," said Maomao.

Shoko leaned back on the fourth stool.

She had sat here enough now that nobody had put a box in its place this morning. There was still not enough room for four elbows. That seemed to be true at every worktable in every world. She moved hers, and Kusuri's notebook fitted between them.

"This is useful information too," Shoko said, looking at the case.

The other three glanced at it.

"How many times I have to put something down before I can use what you've made. How far I have to turn. Whether somebody can wash it without taking the whole room apart." She paused. "Those are the bits people forget when they're very pleased with something clever."

Senku looked at her over his cup.

"You think I'm pleased with this?"

"I think you were pleased with the one that fell on my knees."

"It didn't fall."

"Because I caught it."

Maomao nodded. Kusuri wrote something.

Senku looked at the three of them.

"You're all enjoying this."

"Yes," said Shoko.



The case worked better in the evening.

Not perfectly. Shoko found a rough edge. Maomao refused the first place they tried to put it. Kusuri insisted on checking the bottom cloth as well as the top one, and Senku let her finish before he began to explain the difference.

It kept the washed, dried cloths covered between uses. It did not make dirty cloth clean. It did not give them more linen. If the cover got dusty, somebody still had to take it away and wash it, and if the room was damp they still had to notice.

Shoko took out one folded piece while Maomao stood beside the examination table.

The tray did not catch.

She put it back and tried from the other side.

Still nothing.

For a while nobody said anything.

Then Kusuri reached for the tally, looked at the space on it, and made a small neat mark.

"This one?" Senku asked.

Shoko ran a finger along the rough edge once more.

"After you smooth that."

He took it away without arguing.

Maomao brought a small clean stack. Kusuri counted it. Shoko put the first tray into its runners, then turned toward the worktable, where the drawing was still spread out under three cups and a fourth cup's damp ring.

"Don't move my stool," she said.

"We haven't," Maomao told her.

"I know."

Kusuri came back from the shelf with her notebook tucked beneath her arm and an empty place made in the crook of the other.

"We're going to miss supper."

"We?" said Senku.

"Four of us. Again."

Shoko looked at the case one last time. Senku had come back with the smoothed piece, and was fitting it where she had pointed.

"Then wash your hands," she said. "All four."
