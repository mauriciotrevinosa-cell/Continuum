# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 23 — The Guardian's Afternoon

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 23
**Continuity state:** Day 251, afternoon | Ori chooses to draw an ordinary fort portrait; Milim attempts to sit for it | Umaru's official arrangements and Yuka's white dog complicate the sitting | family humour without a formal field day, new summoning or Ori-origin discussion | Mau and Frieren bring a shared snack and respect Ori's drawing rather than turning it into a medical check | the original portrait stays hers

*When: Day 251, after the kitchen turn. The fort is back inside its bay for the afternoon, out of the strongest sun. Milim has announced that she has nothing to do, which Umaru considers a useful opportunity.*

"Sit there," said Umaru.

Milim sat.

"Not on that."

Milim stood.

"The other cushion."

Milim looked at both cushions. Then at Umaru.

"They're the same."

"That one's for the picture."

"Then why's this one there?"

"Because there are cushions in the fort. Sit."

Milim sat on the correct cushion with the expression of someone who had complied with a very unreasonable demand and expected it to be remembered.

Ori sharpened her pencil.

She had asked Milim if she could draw her, which had been a simple question when it was between the two of them. Milim said yes. Then Umaru heard, and there was a debate about whether the Guardian should be wearing her sash, and whether Ori needed a better piece of paper, and whether the picture would go beside the fort register.

Ori had not said that it was for the register.

She said so now.

Umaru paused with the slate halfway to the curtain.

"It could be."

"I want to see what it looks like first."

"It'll look like me," Milim said helpfully.

"I hope so."

Yuka, sitting on the edge of the bay with the white dog's head on her knee, made a very small sound. Milim looked at her. She became serious at once.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Ori put the paper on the board she had borrowed and looked at Milim.

"Don't move for a little while."



Milim could stand in a circle with something dangerous for a very long time.

She could sit still for approximately as long as it took to remember that someone had asked her to.

Then she wanted to know what the picture looked like.

Ori moved the board away as Milim leaned forward.

"Not yet."

"I've been here ages."

"I've only done your head."

Milim touched her own head with both hands. The pose changed completely.

Ori stared at her.

Umaru put down the slate.

"Guardian. Still."

Milim lowered her hands. Folded them with tremendous determination. Put her chin up, then down, then halfway between the two.

"Like that?"

"How you were before," said Ori.

"When?"

Yuka put her face into the white dog's fur for a moment.



The white dog was much better at sitting still.

This was Ori's first mistake. She glanced at her, then at the empty space beside the shape that would eventually be Milim, and began to draw her ear.

Yuka noticed immediately.

"She's not in it."

"There's space."

"Then I should move her closer."

She tried. The dog stood, turned, stepped over one of the cushions, and lay down behind Milim, where all Ori could see was one white paw and a great deal of tail.

Milim turned to look at her.

"She can be in mine."

"You're moving again."

Milim turned back. The sash had caught beneath her elbow. Umaru came forward to straighten it and remained directly between Ori and the person she was drawing.

Ori set down the pencil.

Nobody noticed for a moment.

Then Yuka did. She stopped trying to persuade the dog to look in the correct direction.

"All right. Everybody go back."

"I'm adjusting the sash," said Umaru.

"She's drawing the Guardian. Not the sash."

Milim looked pleased.

"The sash identifies the office," Umaru said.

"So does Milim."

Umaru considered that. Then she moved aside.

Ori picked up the pencil again.

"Thank you."



For a little while it worked.

Milim looked at the curtain. The white dog stayed asleep behind her. Yuka left her there and took the unimportant cushion, the one nobody had been allowed to sit on, and leaned against the edge of the bay.

Umaru sat beside Ori with the slate face down on her knees. She did not say what the picture ought to look like. She watched the small marks become something, which was difficult not to talk about.

Ori had made Milim's eyes too far apart.

She knew this. She did not know how far apart they should be instead. She looked at Milim, then at the paper, then rubbed out one line with more force than the paper appreciated.

"You can use the other side," Umaru whispered.

"I know."

"Or another piece."

"This one's all right."

She kept working on it. Milim's eyes came a little closer together. Her hair went somewhere Ori had not planned but liked better.

Milim's foot began to move.

Ori did not stop her this time. She had already drawn the feet.



Mau brought the plate because Momo said somebody had to eat the pieces that had broken while she was cutting them.

Frieren followed because she had seen the plate. This was the explanation she gave when he asked, and he accepted it without further investigation.

They stopped at the curtain.

Milim sat on a cushion, unnaturally upright, with an enormous white dog behind her and the sort of expression people wore while trying not to sneeze. Yuka was lying on her stomach at the edge of the rug. Umaru had both hands clamped over her own mouth.

Ori was drawing.

Mau looked at Frieren.

Frieren held up one finger.

They put the plate down where Yuka could reach it and waited.

At last Milim said, "I have an itch."

Umaru made a strangled sound.

"Then scratch it," Ori said, without looking up.

Milim scratched it with such relief that all the stillness went out of her at once. She shook her hair loose, stretched both arms over her head, and nearly pulled her sash off.

Ori looked up.

Then at the page.

"Oh."

"What?" said Milim.

Ori turned the board slightly, studying it. She had got the head wrong. The sash was wrong. One shoulder belonged to somebody standing much farther away.

But the mouth was very nearly the one Milim was wearing now.

Ori went back to it.



It was finished when she said it was finished.

Not when Umaru asked, and not when Milim leaned so far forward that Ori could not see anything else, and not when Yuka offered a small piece of bread in the manner of someone trying to coax a reluctant animal from under a cupboard.

Ori looked at the paper, and made one more line, and put down the pencil.

"Now."

Milim came off the cushion immediately. She crouched beside the board, with the white dog looking over her shoulder because everybody was looking at something and she wanted to know what.

The drawing was recognizably Milim. Not the way a portrait by somebody very good at portraits would have been. The head was a little too large. The sash took up an improbable amount of the body. But there was a grin, and a small determined crease between the eyes, and two feet that had not been still for very long.

Milim stared at it.

Then she grinned exactly that grin.

"That's me."

"I know," said Ori, going pink.

"That's my face."

"I know."

Umaru reached for the slate.

"For the register?"

Ori looked at Milim.

"I want to keep it a bit."

Milim nodded at once.

"Then keep it."

Umaru put the slate down again. Mau, still at the curtain, caught Ori's eye.

"Can I see?"

She held it out to him. He took the board by the edges, looked for a long time, and showed Frieren without turning it where the wet edge of his cup could touch it.

"The mouth," Frieren said.

Ori nodded.

"I got that part."

"You did."

Milim had already taken two pieces from the plate. She offered one to Ori, who accepted it without putting down the drawing.

Yuka sat up.

"Now her," she said, pointing at the white dog.

The dog yawned.

Ori looked at the other side of the paper. It was still empty. She looked at Yuka, then at the dog, and shifted the board onto her knees again.

"Hold her head like that."

Yuka put her hand beneath her chin.

Umaru picked up the slate, turned it over, and wrote *QUIET* on it.

Milim took the last broken piece of bread with very careful, silent fingers.
