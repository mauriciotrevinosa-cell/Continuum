# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 24 — What We Leave on the Table

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** Day 252, evening | Momo, Okarun and Vamola prepare ordinary food for the next work morning | Fern and Stark sort returning kitchen things and share the remaining task | knots, a missing spoon and a board left unfinished, not a roll-call meeting | established affection continues without first-date resets | the cloth case and music-room repair stay modest practical changes; no S5 event or new coin choice

*When: The evening of Day 252. Work is expected at the approaches again in the morning. There is enough ordinary bread for it because Momo has counted, not because anything new has appeared in the dungeon.*

Okarun's knots were too good.

He discovered this when Momo gave him back the first bundle and asked him to open it with one hand.

He tried. The knot held. He put the bundle down, used the other hand, and looked up at her.

"One hand," Momo repeated.

"You said not to let it come open."

"While it's being carried. Not forever."

Vamola took the bundle. Pulled at the wrong end of the cloth. Pulled at the right one. Then she put it down too, and looked at Okarun with respect.

"Very strong."

"Thank you."

"Not a compliment," said Momo.

He went pink. She smiled, and stood closer to show him the knot she meant. Her hand covered his for a moment on the cloth, turning the end back the other way. Okarun stopped looking at the cloth.

"There."

"There," he agreed.

"Did you see it?"

"Yes."

She looked at his face.

"Liar. Again."



The long table had acquired several tasks after supper, none of which had asked permission of the others.

At the kitchen end were the cut loaves, the wrapping cloths and the basket. At Fern's end were returning bowls, a covered pot and three spoons where there ought to have been four. Between them somebody had left a short wooden board with a line halfway across it and a pencil laid very carefully at the stopping point.

Momo had put a plate on the board. Fern had moved the plate. Now nobody knew who the board belonged to, and everybody was treating the pencil as if it might belong to someone dangerous.

Stark picked it up.

Nothing happened.

"Put it with Wakana's things," Fern said.

"Is it his?"

"I don't know. He'll know whose it is."

Stark took the board to the end of the bench, out of the way of the food, and set the pencil back exactly where it had been. Then he came back for the pot.

"That's yours," Momo told him.

He looked down at it.

"Mine?"

"The one you took with the last party. For water."

"Oh. Yes."

"It has a spoon in it."

Fern looked at him.

Stark lifted the lid. The fourth spoon lay across the bottom.

He held it out before anybody asked.

"Thank you," Fern said.

She sounded as if he had done something helpful rather than found something he had put in the wrong place. He was grateful enough for that that he stayed to help with the rest.



Momo cut the last loaf while the first bundles were made.

Vamola got the broken end. She always seemed to be where the broken end happened. Momo had noticed this a long time ago and had stopped pretending it was an accident.

"Don't eat the other one."

Vamola looked at the other end.

"That's tomorrow."

"This is today."

"Yes."

Vamola ate today's piece. Slowly, to make it last through the part when there was still food in sight that belonged to another day.

Okarun tied his next bundle. Momo tried the end with two fingers. It came loose at once.

"Better."

He nodded, then tied it again. The cloth was old and soft. There was a neat mend near the corner in a colour that had nothing to do with the rest of it. He held the corner up while he worked so that it did not drag through the crumbs.

"You're getting flour on your sleeve," Momo said.

He looked down.

She dusted it off before he could put the bread down. Then she looked at her own white fingers, and at the larger white mark she had just made.

They both stared.

Vamola laughed first.

Momo put her floury hand over her own face, remembered too late, and made it worse.

Okarun began to laugh too. Very quietly at first. Then not quietly enough.

"Don't," Momo told him.

"I'm not—"

"You are."

She took the clean cloth Vamola offered, and Okarun caught the other edge and dampened it for her before she tried to rub flour into her eye.

Momo stood still while he wiped the mark near her temple. His hand was careful. It had been careful around her for so long that she knew when it was going to hesitate.

She caught his wrist before he took it away.

"You missed a bit."

He looked at her.

She was smiling.

At the other end of the table Stark looked down into the empty pot with sudden, determined concentration. Fern went on counting spoons, very slowly.



They did not finish everything at once.

Vamola took the first basket to the pantry. Okarun went for another cloth. Momo sat down because she had been standing since before supper and nobody was about to benefit from her continuing to stand while they looked for things.

Stark carried the pot to the kitchen shelf. He had to move it twice before Momo said it was in the right place. Then he came back and found Fern rubbing at a dried mark on one of the bowls with her thumb.

"That one's still dirty."

"I know."

"I'll do it."

"You don't have to."

He took the bowl she held out despite saying that. Their fingers touched round the rim. A small familiar thing, made less small by the fact that she did not immediately go back to her list.

He looked at the silver wire at her wrist, just visible under the sleeve.

"Still there," she said.

He went red.

"I wasn't checking."

"You were looking."

"I can look."

Fern looked up at him. Then, slowly, she drew the sleeve back over the bracelet, leaving the smallest part of it showing.

"Yes," she said.

He stood holding the bowl for another moment. Long enough that she had to remind him what he had volunteered to do with it.

"Water."

"Yes."

He went.



There were footsteps in the corridor above. A door opened, then a voice asking where a pencil had gone. Not loudly. The sort of ordinary complaint the house had learned to recognize without going to see whether anything was wrong.

Downstairs, Kusuri came to the kitchen for an empty jar and found the sound ones stacked beside the door, where somebody had put them after she had finally stayed in her own room for an afternoon. She checked one against the light, saw the smooth rim, and took it away.

"Clean one?" Momo asked.

"Yes. Just measuring. Not putting anything in it for anyone."

"Bring it back."

"I will."

She went past the worktable, where the case drawing still lay beside a cup Shoko had forgotten. Maomao would find the cup in the morning and say something about it. Shoko would answer. Senku would object to the part of the drawing Maomao put her finger on. Kusuri, already looking at the next blank space on the sheet, had something she intended to try before the others arrived.

Momo watched her go, then turned back to the bread.

Okarun had found the cloth. He had also found two others that might have been clean, and had brought all three so that she could decide instead of making the decision by hope.

"This one," she said.

Vamola put down the second basket and began to fold the rejected cloths away for washing. She hummed something she had heard through the music-room door, got the end wrong, and sang it again exactly the same way.

Nobody corrected it.



Stark brought the bowl back clean.

Fern stacked it with the others. He held the stack steady while she put the smaller ones inside, then lifted it when she showed him where to take it. No clatter. No apology from either of them.

When he came back she had closed the ledger.

"Done?"

"That part."

She looked at the board still waiting on the end of the bench. At the baskets Momo was covering. At the cups nobody had yet carried to the washing bowl.

Stark followed her look.

"The cups?"

"I'll take them."

"I'll take those two."

She passed them over. Kept the other two. They went to the kitchen together, and Okarun stepped back to let them through without dropping the bundle he was tying.

Momo looked at the knot.

"One hand."

He pulled the end. It opened.

She smiled at him, properly, with the last pale trace of flour still in her hair.

"There."

He tied it again. This time he did not need to look at her hands.

At the end of the bench the board remained exactly as it had been, with its line half drawn and its pencil waiting beside it. Momo put the covered basket safely away from it, then took the last broken bit of bread and split it between the two people still at her end of the table.
