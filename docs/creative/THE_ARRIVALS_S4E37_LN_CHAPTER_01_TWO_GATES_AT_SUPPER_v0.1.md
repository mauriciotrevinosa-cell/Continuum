# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 25 — Two Gates at Supper

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 25
**Continuity state:** Day 253 | the first earth-and-timber perimeter works and both controlled gates are usable; stonework, wet-ground repairs and some walk sections remain unfinished | a hand cart catches on the south threshold | Eren/Mikasa adjust a real gate without pretending it ends fear | Rimuru/Fern retain road and leaving rules | supper in the same family house; the vermilion columns remain its entrance | old cottage empty, ordinary work continues

*When: Day 253, from late afternoon into supper. Nearly four weeks have passed since the household made its first decision about the wall. The southern road is still a road. A closed gate has not made it safe.*

The cart got stuck on the first afternoon both gates could be used.

It was a very small cart. It had one wheel and two handles, and Momo had put three sacks on it because she had been told the surface was finished. The wheel reached the new south threshold, climbed halfway up, and stayed there.

Momo pushed.

The cart stayed.

She looked at Eren.

He put down the wooden mallet in his hand.

"I can lift it."

"I can lift it too," said Momo. "I was told I could roll it."

Mikasa, crouched beside the gatepost with a strip of wood and a piece of charcoal, covered her mouth with the back of her wrist. Eren saw her do it.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"It's a heavy cart."

"I know."

"You can both laugh after my sacks are inside," said Momo.

They lifted it together. Not with a transformation or a spell. Eren took one side of the frame, Mikasa the other, and Momo kept the handles level while the wheel came over the raised timber. On the inside, they set it down carefully.

Then Eren went back and looked at the threshold.

He had imagined a great many things when he first said *wall*. The thing on the road. The titan's smile. Hands finding the latch while somebody stood outside and had to be told to wait.

He had not imagined Momo and a sack of flour.

"Lower it?" he said.

Mikasa shook her head. "Water."

He looked at the narrow channel outside, the fall of the earth away from the entrance. Senku had made them pour water over this patch of ground twice before letting them set the timber. Nobody had enjoyed that afternoon, least of all the people who had just tamped it flat.

"A board on each side," he said. "A slope."

"That can be taken up."

"So we can see underneath."

Mikasa nodded and made a small mark on the strip of wood.

Beyond her, the new line of posts ran away along the inside of the marked boundary. Earth banked against its lower part. The timber was different colours where different trees had yielded it. Here and there a gap in the walking platform showed through; those sections had been roped off until the supports were finished. On the wetter turn, a patch of soil had already slumped and been dug out again.

It looked built. It also looked like people would be working on it tomorrow.

The lime remained outside it. Nobody had moved that line to make a drawing easier.

"We'll need to check it after rain," said Mikasa.

"I know."

She looked up at him.

"I wasn't reminding you. I was asking who."

Eren took the strip of wood from her. Under the little drawing of the removable boards, he wrote his name. Then, after looking at the amount of ground on the plan, he made room for another.

Mikasa put hers beside it.

Momo, halfway up the path with the cart rolling very easily now, called back: "Before breakfast."

Eren stared after her.

Mikasa began to laugh properly.



The north gate had a different problem.

People kept stopping to look at it.

Not the people on duty. They knew where they were meant to stand. The people passing through on an ordinary errand, with an empty basket or a tool over one shoulder, would get as far as the opening and slow down. The posts were taller than a person. The leaves of the gate were thick and rough. When they swung shut, the sound went through the ground under your feet.

It took a moment to keep walking after that.

Rimuru was standing just inside, in human shape, with Fern beside him and the day's movements written on the board. He had spent much of the afternoon explaining things they had already agreed, because agreeing in a common room and standing beside a gate were different experiences.

"You can leave," he said, again.

The woman with the basket looked at him.

She was there on an expected delivery, her name and her brother's already checked against the day's arrangements, and would go back to the fields before evening. There had never been a question about her moving into the house. Now she was holding the basket with both hands and looking at the new bar.

"I know."

"Do you want someone to walk the first stretch?"

"My brother's waiting by the bend."

Rimuru looked at the road. There was a man there, a little way beyond the marked ground, waving a hat with the impatience of somebody who had been told this would take five minutes.

"Right," he said. "Don't make him wait for us."

She smiled then, a little embarrassed, and went through. The people at the gate stepped aside without asking her for an explanation they already had.

Fern watched until the two baskets were moving together down the road.

"We should keep saying it," she said.

"Yeah."

"And doing it."

He nodded.

The gates could be shut. That had been the point of building them. But there were times on the board now, and names beside them, and people whose job it was to open them again. The road decisions still needed asking about. A gate did not make an escort appear or medicine arrive from somewhere else.

At the edge of the yard, a child from the fort had been sent to collect Rimuru. He could tell it was an official errand because she was carrying a spoon instead of wearing her hood.

It was Suika.

"Momo said the first supper is ready," she told him.

"The first?"

"She said there will be a second supper for the people who say one more thing and don't come. But it will be cold."

Fern closed the book.

"I'll hand over," she said. "You go."

Rimuru hesitated, then went with Suika. Somebody else was there to stand his place. They had written that down too.



At the family house, Himmel's two vermilion columns stood exactly where they had stood before the perimeter was built.

Marin had been touching up one of them. There was a narrow patch of red on the side of her wrist and another on Wakana's sleeve, which he had not noticed yet. He stood beside her holding the little pot while she inspected the work from different angles.

"More at the bottom?" she asked.

"It'll run if you put more on now."

"It's darker there."

"It's wet."

She considered this. Then she stood back, took his free hand, and pulled him toward the smell of supper.

"Come on. We can stare at it after."

He went, taking the paint with him because there was nowhere safe to put it down.

Mau moved along the bench when they came in. Frieren moved with him, keeping her shoulder against his. On his other side, Ori was folding a napkin she had already folded three different ways. She was trying to make it stand up.

"That was where I was going to put my cup," he said.

"You can put it somewhere else."

"It has a hat."

"It's a roof."

Frieren looked at it. "It needs a chimney."

Ori frowned at the top fold, and Mau quietly put his cup somewhere else.

At the next place, Yuta and Maki were arguing over whose turn it was to wash the training cloths. They had brought the argument all the way from the yard. Neither sounded as if they wanted it finished very quickly. Further down, Jiji had found a way to sit beside Okarun without having been invited, and Momo was putting a second bowl in front of him with the expression she used for people who were already going to eat it.

Eren arrived with sawdust on his sleeves.

Mikasa took a piece of it out of his hair before they sat down. He caught her wrist, looked at what she was holding, and laughed under his breath.

"Momo's cart," he said to Rimuru.

Rimuru looked up from his bowl.

"I heard."

"Of course you heard."

"I want the boards checked before another cart has to be carried."

"Tomorrow."

"Before breakfast," said Mikasa.

Momo, from the kitchen, pointed her spoon at her in agreement.

Eren looked down the table. At the open windows, the people reaching across one another for bread, the places that were occupied because somebody had moved over and made room. There were fewer people living beyond this house than there had been at the beginning of summer. He could feel the empty cottages when he went past them. The gates had not filled those places.

Outside, on the north side, somebody called the change of watch.

Mikasa's hand found his under the table. He held it, then let go long enough to take the bread she could not reach.

"Do you want the end?"

"Yes."

He gave it to her.

Across from them, Marin noticed the red mark on Wakana's sleeve.

"Oh."

He looked down.

"It'll wash," she said, much too quickly.

Wakana considered the colour, then her wrist, then the small pot he had brought all the way to the table.

"That one won't."

Marin put her face in his shoulder.

Nobody at their end of the table was any help at all.
