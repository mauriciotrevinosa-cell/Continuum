# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 27 — Rain on the Roof

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 27
**Continuity state:** Day 257 | a rainy ordinary day, not a disaster | the quartet's modest cloth case needs its cover and trays checked; no waterproof/sterile miracle | Momo/Vamola, Shizuka/Nano and Ryo share an unplanned common-room afternoon | Okarun/Jiji ordinary friendship; existing couples remain | Anko/Nazuna sleep and watch rota respected | wall drains inspected, household roof only a small repair; no new threat or season plot

*When: Day 257. It has been raining since before breakfast. People on the watch still have to go out. Almost everyone else has found a reason to stay under a roof.*

At first, everybody said it was only a shower.

By midday they had stopped saying it.

Rain ran off the long eaves in steady lines. The yard was dark under the ink tree, and the new walking boards outside had the pale shine of wet timber. Every time somebody came in, a different part of the common-room floor got wet.

Momo put a cloth by the door.

People stepped over it.

She moved it into the middle of the opening.

People stepped on it and then stood there talking until somebody else had to go round them.

She stood at the kitchen door with a spoon in her hand and watched this happen three times.

Then she said something to Vamola.

Vamola went to the doorway, put both feet on the cloth, and folded her arms.

The next person in was Jiji.

"Oh," he said. "Is this a game?"

Vamola pointed at his boots.

He looked down at them. Then at the cloth. He wiped his feet with exaggerated care, holding her gaze the entire time.

She nodded and moved aside.

"Excellent system," he said to Momo. "Very frightening."

"Take the boots off too."

"There are two stages?"

Okarun, coming in behind him with rain dripping off his hair, laughed. Jiji turned toward the sound at once.

"You can go first. Show me how."

"I know how to take off boots."

"I'm learning."

"You're being annoying."

"It's how I learn."

Vamola pointed at Okarun's feet too. Momo went back to the kitchen before either of them could ask for an appeal.



At the clinic worktable, the rain was causing an argument about a piece of cloth.

It was not the useful clean cloth inside the case. That was on the removable trays, under the cover they had made after discovering that a well-fitted box could keep dampness in as effectively as it kept dust out. This was the outer cover, with a new crease where it had sagged toward one corner overnight.

Kusuri stood on the dry side of the table with her notebook against her chest.

"I marked that corner."

"You marked every corner," said Maomao.

"So we would know."

"Now we know it's wet."

"That's a result."

"It's certainly something to write down," said Shoko.

Senku looked up at the thin dark line in the wall above the table. A little water had found its way in round the edge of a shutter fitting. It was not much. It had been enough to reach the cover.

He moved the case.

All three of them looked at him.

"First repair," he said.

Shoko began to laugh.

"That one I like."

"I was going to do that," said Kusuri.

"After writing it down?"

"It changes the conditions."

"Yes," said Maomao. "That's why we're doing it."

They took out the trays and checked what was actually dry. The damp cover stayed off. Nobody called the contents ruined merely because the outside had got wet, and nobody called them clean merely because they were inside a device the four of them had worked on. Shoko and Maomao sorted what could be used, Kusuri kept the piles separate, and Senku went to ask Wakana about the shutter.

On his way out, Shoko caught the edge of his sleeve.

"Tell him a small leak. Not 'the clinic is flooding'."

"I wasn't going to say flooding."

"Last time, somebody ran."

He considered this, then nodded and went.

Kusuri watched him leave.

"Four people," she said, "and nobody wrote *move the box*."

Maomao took the notebook from her, found a clean place at the bottom of the page, and wrote it.

Shoko had to put the tray down before she dropped it.



By the middle of the afternoon, the common room had acquired a bucket.

The drip above it was small. Wakana had looked from inside, marked the place, and said he would inspect it outside when the roof was no longer trying to throw people off it. Until then, the bucket was enough.

Ryo sat beside it with her bass across her knees.

She listened.

The bucket went *plink*.

She plucked a string, changed the note, and waited.

*Plink.*

Nano looked up from the other side of the table.

"It changes as it fills."

"I know."

"You'll have to keep changing the note."

"I'm practising."

Shizuka sat beside Nano with her book open and her feet clear of the damp part of the floor. She watched Ryo's fingers for a while, then turned a page and put her finger under a line.

Nano leaned closer.

"Yes," she said. "It is a very patient audience."

Ryo looked at the bucket.

"It pays better than some."

"It hasn't paid anything."

"Exactly."

Kita arrived carrying the cloth that had been drying near the kitchen, and stopped beside the bench. Behind her, Bocchi was trying to find a way past a pair of very wet boots without touching them or appearing to make an issue of them.

"Whose?" Kita asked.

"Jiji's," said Okarun from the floor by the door.

"You've put yours beside them," said Jiji.

"Because that's where the boots go."

"They're friends."

"They're boots."

Kita picked both pairs up and put them on the mat properly. Bocchi came through the new gap and sat beside her with the immense, silent gratitude of somebody whose obstacle had vanished without needing to be explained.

The bucket went *plink*.

Ryo answered it.

Bocchi looked at her, listened for two drops, and quietly reached for her guitar case.

"Don't encourage her," said Nijika, who had come in just in time to see it happen.

"It's raining," said Ryo.

"That's not an answer."

"We're indoors."

Nijika put her hands on her hips.

The bucket went *plink*.

Without thinking, she tapped two fingers against her own arm in the space before the next drop.

Ryo looked at her.

Nijika closed her eyes.

Kita started to laugh, and Bocchi bent over the strings so nobody would see she was laughing too.



Anko woke earlier than she had meant to.

There was a sound downstairs that kept almost becoming music and then turning back into a bucket. She lay still and listened to it, trying to decide whether it was something she had to deal with.

From the loft above her bed, Nazuna said, very sleepily, "Don't."

"I haven't done anything."

"You're thinking about getting up."

"How would you know?"

"You breathe like work."

Anko stared at the underside of the loft.

Then she pulled the blanket back over herself. Her coat hung where she had left it. The tin in its pocket was closed.

Downstairs, somebody missed a beat and swore. Somebody else laughed so hard that the dripping was briefly the most competent part of the arrangement.

Nazuna made a pleased sound and went quiet again.



The rain eased toward evening.

Eren and Mikasa came in from the drains with mud up to their knees and no claim that the outside jobs were finished. Two of the channels needed more work. The threshold boards had done what they were supposed to do, which pleased Momo until Eren put one muddy hand on the back of a chair.

Vamola pointed at him.

He looked down at his hand, then at Mikasa, who was already wiping hers.

"It's a good system," she said.

He went to wash.

Wakana brought back a strip of wood for the shutter repair. Marin followed him with a dry cloth over his head, rubbing his hair while he tried to walk. He said he could do that himself. She said yes, and kept doing it.

The bucket was nearly full. Rem moved it to empty it, and Ryo looked up in protest.

"It was in tune."

Rem looked at her. Then she looked at the bucket.

"I'll bring the same one back."

"It won't be the same."

"Ryo," said Seika from the doorway.

Ryo took the bass off her knees.

Rem went out with the bucket. When she returned, she put it down very carefully under the drip and looked at the whole band.

"Again from the beginning?" she asked.

Nijika put her face in her hands.

The first drop hit the empty bottom with a loud, completely different note.

Ryo reached for her bass.
