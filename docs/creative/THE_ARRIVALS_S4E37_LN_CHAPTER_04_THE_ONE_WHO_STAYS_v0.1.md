# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 26
## Chapter 28 — The One Who Stays

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S4 v0.1
**Volume:** 26
**Reading order:** 28

*When: Day 259, late morning. The boards from the south gate have dried on their rack. The common-room washing is still not finished. Rem has made a plan for the afternoon and intends to keep it.*

Rem told him to take the other end.

It was a sheet.

Mau looked at the long wet weight of it, then at the place she had pointed to, and took it. Between them it stopped trailing in the mud. Rem gave him a small nod and went on toward the line.

He followed.

The washing court was quiet in a way he still had not learned to like. The women from the valley had made noise even when they were not speaking to him. A bucket set down too hard. A sheet slapped against a board. Someone singing badly enough that other people had to sing over her.

Now the troughs were being used by the house. Water still ran. Washing still had to be done. But he caught himself listening for the start of a song that belonged to somebody who had chosen another place to live.

Rem looked back.

"A little higher."

He lifted his end.

"Not that high."

He lowered it.

"You're very good at carrying people through emergencies," she said. "Sheets are more difficult?"

"People usually tell me when I'm doing it wrong."

"I'm telling you."

"I noticed."

They got the sheet over the line. Rem took the pegs out of the little pocket she had made in the cloth at her waist, and Mau held the edge flat while she fastened it. It took longer than it would have if she had done it alone. He could tell. She did not look as if she wanted him to apologise for that.

At the next line, Ram was laying out something much smaller with extreme care.

"You don't have to supervise me," Rem called.

"I'm supervising him."

Mau looked round the edge of the sheet. Ram looked at him over it with her face completely straight.

"Has he dropped it yet?"

"Not yet," said Rem.

"A record."

He put his head back behind the sheet. Rem's shoulders were shaking a little.



There were only three things left in the basket when Ram went in.

She had gone to get ready. Rem said this without being asked, while Mau untangled the last pair of sleeves. He looked up.

"For what?"

"We said we'd go to the orchard edge. Inside the marked ground. Just us."

"Do you need anything?"

"An afternoon."

"You can have one."

Rem looked at him then. Not angrily. With the raised brows she used when somebody had accidentally claimed authority over the wrong thing.

He caught it.

"You already have one," he corrected.

"Yes."

She took the sleeves from him, separated them at once, and handed one back.

"I'm trying to leave the rest alone," she said. "There are people who can do it. They keep telling me."

"I know some people like that."

"You should listen to them."

"You're very comfortable giving advice you don't take."

"So are you."

They looked at each other for a moment. Then he laughed, and she smiled, and the work went on.

He had used to see her moving through a room and think the room was being cared for. It had taken him too long to learn to ask where she was going when she finished. With Ram here, there were afternoons with destinations now. There were conversations he did not hear and things the sisters laughed about before he came near them.

He liked seeing them turn back to each other.

"You look pleased," said Rem.

"I am."

"About the washing?"

"I was trying not to make it a speech."

She considered this with enormous gravity.

"Keep trying."

He pegged the sleeve to the line.



After they had emptied the basket, she sat on the low bench by the water.

Mau picked up the basket. Rem reached for it, then stopped herself. She folded her hands in her lap instead.

"I'll bring it up," he said.

"I know. Sit for a minute."

He sat.

On the other side of the hanging sheets, a little wind stirred the hems. Their shadows moved over the ground, pale and uneven. He could see a strip of the house between them, and part of an open window. Somewhere in there somebody was arguing about whether an empty jar counted as putting something away.

"Do you miss them?" Rem asked.

He knew which people she meant.

"Yes."

"Will you go and see them?"

"When the road's right. When we can ask without making it something they have to say yes to."

She nodded.

"The cooper said he might come back."

"He might."

"He didn't promise."

"I didn't ask him to."

Rem looked at the water for a while.

"That's hard for you."

"A little."

"More than a little."

He rubbed the damp edge of his sleeve between his fingers.

"Sometimes I think if I find the right thing to say, nobody has to leave. Then I remember what I'd be asking them to give up, just so I don't have to watch them go."

She sat quietly beside him.

"I can be sad about it," he said.

"Yes."

"And still help carry the bags."

"Yes."

He leaned back against the warm edge of the trough. The wood was damp where somebody had washed it down. It had not been here when he first came to this world. He remembered the work of making this court, and the day it became a place people used without needing to admire it first.

"I keep thinking about you staying," he said. "Before Ram came. When you could have gone."

"We talked about that."

"I know. I'm not asking you to tell me again."

Rem looked at him.

"I just like that you're here," he said.

Her face softened. It happened before she could decide whether to allow it, and then she allowed it anyway.

"I like being here."

The words had weight because he knew she had wanted something else before. Because she had spent time making the choice. He did not try to make them larger than she had said them.

She turned a little on the bench, facing him.

"And you?"

"I like being here too."

"I mean when it's difficult."

He waited.

"When your body does something before you can ask it not to," she said. "When something in you decides it has an answer. You can still come and say it's difficult. You don't have to wait until it's dangerous enough that nobody can be annoyed with you."

He looked down at his hands.

"You'd be annoyed anyway."

"Of course."

"That's comforting."

"You seem to need reminding."

He nodded, slowly.

"I'm trying to say it earlier."

"I know. I can tell."

He looked at her again. She was not handing him a pass for all the times he would get it wrong. She was telling him she had noticed the times he had tried and somebody had heard him. It was a different sort of kindness, and one he could bear to sit beside.

"Will you tell me?" he asked.

"What?"

"If it gets difficult for you. Before you've finished everybody else's work."

Rem took a breath. She looked toward the house, where her sister was getting ready for an afternoon that belonged to the two of them.

"I'll try."

"That's allowed?"

She turned back to him.

"From both of us," she said.

He smiled.



Ram found them still sitting there.

She had changed nothing about her appearance that Mau could identify, but Rem looked at her at once and stood, pleased. There were apparently details in a sister's readiness that nobody else got to read.

"I thought you were getting the basket," said Ram.

"He has it."

Ram looked at the basket by Mau's feet.

"A dangerous task. We should call someone."

"I'll manage."

"That's what people always say at the start."

Rem took her sister's arm before he could answer. At the turn of the path, she looked back.

"The dry things are on the second line."

Mau nodded.

"The first one still drips."

He nodded again.

Ram pulled very gently on her arm.

"Afternoon."

"Yes," said Rem, laughing a little now. "All right."

They went toward the trees together. He stayed until they were out of sight, then picked up the basket and went to collect the dry things.

It took him two attempts to fold the sheet.

On the third, Frieren appeared beside him, took the far corner without a word, and brought it neatly to his.

"She told you?" he asked.

"No. I saw the sheet moving over the line. I thought you were in it."

He looked down at the folds round his knees.

"I nearly was."

She took another corner.

"Hold that."
