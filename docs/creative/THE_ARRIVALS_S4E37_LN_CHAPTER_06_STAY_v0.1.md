*When: Day 262, early evening. There are still bowls on the common-room table. The day has not been arranged around an emergency.*

He had promised to come back for the rest of the washing-up.

It was a small promise. Momo had put a cloth in his hand and told him that carrying four bowls outside did not count as disappearing until everyone else had finished. He had said he knew. Frieren had taken two bowls from the stack before it could tip.

They were in the yard together when the cold came back.

Not all at once. His fingers felt it first, through the warm water left inside the bowl. Then his cheek. A thin morning cold, out of place in the last light of the day.

He stopped walking.

Frieren took another step before she noticed. She turned with the bowls against her hip.

"Mau?"

He looked toward the far side of the yard.

The work shed was there. The bench where Wakana had left the shorter board. A coil of rope was hanging from a peg. He could see every one of them, and beyond them he could see something else, though he could not have said where *beyond* was.

"Something's wrong," he said.

Frieren set her bowls down on the bench.

He was still holding his. She took them, carefully, one at a time.

*Ciel.*

*I am here.*

"It's cold," he said aloud. "Like yesterday. More than yesterday."

Frieren put her empty hand in his.

The part of him that wanted to move had already chosen a direction. He could feel it in the turn of his ankle, in the way his weight had shifted before he had thought about taking a step.

He put his other foot flat.

"Don't let me go without telling you."

Frieren's fingers tightened.

"You're telling me."

"I know."

Inside the house a chair scraped back. Rimuru came out without his coat, looked at Mau, then past him. He did not ask him to explain from the beginning.

"Ciel says stay where you are," he said. "She's looking."

"I'm trying."

There was no noise when the first line appeared.

That was worse than a sound would have been. The yard kept its ordinary evening sounds. Water ran from the pump. A voice somewhere upstairs called for somebody who did not answer. From the kitchen came the knock of a lid being put down too hard.

And a line of light stood upright beside the shed.

It was very narrow. Too exact to belong to the rough boards behind it. Mau watched its upper end turn, watched it draw a corner across the air, and knew it before the second side had reached the ground.

"The lake," he said.

Frieren looked at the frame.

"Yes."

He heard the word catch. She did not say it again.

Rimuru moved a little to one side, keeping clear of the space between them and the light.

"Nobody goes near it," he called toward the house. His voice was level and loud enough to carry. "Keep the path clear."

The door had not opened yet.

Mau tried looking at Frieren instead. The turn of her face was there, the small loose strand beside her ear, the place where her collar had folded under itself. He could see her. But the unfinished frame kept its place in his attention, as if closing his eyes would only move it inside them.

*Is it pulling you?* Ciel asked.

*Yes.*

*Can you keep your feet where they are?*

*Right now.*

*Tell me if that changes.*

"It's pulling," he told Frieren.

She stepped closer. Not in front of him like somebody preparing to be struck. Beside him, her shoulder touching his arm, both of her feet on the same ground as his.

"I have you."

He nodded. He did not trust himself to make the nod bigger.

The light drew back.

On the other side there was grass.

The morning light was wrong for this evening. It lay pale over the clearing, over the low plants and the frost lifting in little wet points. Flowers grew near a shallow place in the ground.

He knew their shapes.

He had known them before he had known most of the names people called him by. Before the rooms. Before the fort. Before he understood how many people could fit around one table without anybody agreeing on how to sit.

There was no one there.

It was not a blue gate in the yard on a dungeon morning. There was no waiting team, no count, no route chosen together. Just the clearing where he had once opened his eyes, shown to him with a gentleness that made his knees want to give.

He took half a step.

Frieren moved with him.

Her hand did not slip.

"Mau."

"I know."

He stopped with his weight on the front of his foot. It hurt to put it back. Not like a torn muscle. Like making himself finish a word he had been trying not to say.

He pushed his heel down.

Rimuru was saying something to someone near the door of the house. Mau heard Fern's answer, short and clear. He did not turn to count who had come outside. He could not spend the effort on that.

The cold was in his chest now.

There was nothing on the other side that looked like a threat. That was the part he could not explain while he was standing there. If it had shown him teeth, if it had shown him a person waiting to hurt somebody, there would have been something ordinary to be afraid of.

It showed him a place before he had failed anybody.

He knew what was wrong with that sentence. Knowing did not stop the wanting.

"I can see it," Frieren said.

He looked at her.

Her eyes were on the flowers. She had made them. She knew exactly what the door was offering him.

"You weren't happy there," she said quietly. "You were frightened."

He remembered.

Not a peaceful version of himself, waiting to be returned to. A man in the grass who could not answer a simple question. A voice trying not to frighten him further. Her hand moving, and the flowers coming up because she did not know what else to give him.

He looked down at the hand holding his now.

It was shaking.

"Frieren."

"I'm here."

"I know."

He turned his fingers inside hers. It was a small movement. It took a long time.

Behind Rimuru, Ori stood at the edge of the path with Fern beside her. Mau saw her when he raised his head. Her face was pale. She had stopped when Rimuru asked, but her hands were empty and lifted a little, as if she had left something unfinished to come here.

"Dad?"

"Stay there," he said. "I can hear you."

She swallowed and stayed. Fern's hand rested against her back. Neither of them reached toward the light.

The door waited.

Mau felt the pull gather again. He could not tell whether it had grown stronger or whether he was tired. The distinction did not help his legs.

*I can't do this quietly,* he told Ciel.

*You do not have to.*

"Rimuru," he said.

"Right here."

"Talk to me."

Rimuru looked at his face, then at the hand between him and Frieren.

"You owe Momo the washing-up."

Mau made a sound that did not quite become a laugh.

"I know."

"She specifically said you would do this."

"She did not mean this."

"I'm not arguing with her."

The yard did not become safe. Nothing in his chest stopped pulling. But he could hear Rimuru, and the little angry break in his voice beneath the joke, and he could hear the kitchen behind him. The things on this side were not an idea of home. They were people waiting for him to answer.

Frieren brought their joined hands against her chest.

"Look at me."

He did.

Her face was wet. He had not seen the first tear fall. She was not trying to hide it or to make it into something he would know how to comfort.

"I don't know how to make it stop," he said.

"I know."

"I don't want to leave you. I made you a promise."

She closed her eyes once. When she opened them she was still looking straight at him.

"Then stay."

He could not promise her an eternity. He could not tell her what he was, or how long the wanting would last, or what his body would ask him to do in the next moment.

He could put his foot back beside hers.

He did.

The flowers moved in a wind that did not reach the yard.

Mau held Frieren's hand with both of his.

"I'm staying."
