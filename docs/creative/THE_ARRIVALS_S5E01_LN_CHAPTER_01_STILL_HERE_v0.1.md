# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 1 — Still Here

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 1
**Continuity state:** Continuation of Stay; the pull eases without being cured; the household keeps the path clear

*When: Day 262, early evening.*

Frieren did not let go when he said it.

For a while Mau was grateful that she did not ask him to say it again. His hands were doing enough talking. One held hers; the other was closed around both of them, too tightly, and every time he tried to loosen it he felt his weight begin to move.

Rimuru brought the bench closer without putting it between him and the door.

"Behind your knees," he said.

Mau sat badly. Frieren sat with him, a little sideways, because he still had her hand.

The clearing remained where the shed should have ended. Morning on the other side. A length of rope in the evening on this one. He kept looking at the rope. Wakana had wound it in uneven loops. The last loop was longer than the others.

*The pressure has changed,* Ciel said.

*Stopped?*

*No. Less force is required to resist it while you are sitting. That is what I can tell you.*

He repeated that aloud.

Frieren's thumb moved once over the bone in his hand.

"Then sit."

"Excellent plan," Rimuru said. "I was about to recommend a complicated alternative involving more sitting."

Mau looked at him.

Rimuru's mouth was doing something that was nearly his usual smile. His shoulders were not.

"You can stop trying to make me laugh."

"I can't. It's a condition."

Behind them, Fern spoke to somebody at the house entrance. Mau heard Stark's answer and a quieter question from Ori. Nobody crowded the light. A chair scraped across the stones, stopped, and scraped back again.

"Do you want them here?" Frieren asked.

He looked at Ori. She was watching him from beside Fern, very carefully not moving closer.

"Soon. I want to be able to look at her properly."

Frieren nodded. She did not decide that meant no.

The cold receded from his teeth first. He had not noticed it was there until his jaw unclenched. His legs still felt as if he had walked a long way carrying something heavy, but his feet stayed where he put them.

Rimuru asked him to move one heel.

He did.

Then the other.

"Don't make it an examination," Mau said.

"Then stop performing so badly."

That one worked. Only a little.

Shoko came out carrying an ordinary cup. She stopped on his side of the bench rather than going to inspect the opening. Her eyes went to his hands, then his mouth.

"Can you swallow?"

"I think so."

"We have an inexpensive way of checking."

The water had no taste. He drank too quickly and had to stop.

Shoko waited without taking the cup away.

"Is it doing anything to my temperature?" he asked.

She touched his wrist and then the side of his neck. "Your skin's cold. That doesn't tell me why."

"Useful."

"Usually."

Frieren's shoulder was against his. On the bench behind her, the bowls had started to dry with pale marks around their insides.

Momo would hate that.

It was such an ordinary thought that he wanted to keep it. He looked at the bowls again and saw Momo coming down the path with a cloth tucked into her belt. Okarun followed her, carrying the washing bucket.

She stopped short of the bench.

"I'm taking these," she said. "You can owe me a different job."

"I didn't finish."

"I can see that."

She collected the bowls. One had a chip that caught the edge of her cloth. She turned it in her fingers and handed it to Okarun separately.

"Not on the bottom."

"I know."

"You keep putting it on the bottom."

They went back arguing softly about a bowl.

Mau watched until they were inside.

"I can stand now," he said.

Shoko considered his face. "To go somewhere, or to prove that you can?"

He had an answer ready. He disliked it when he heard it in his own head.

"I'd rather sit for another minute."

"There you go. A medical advance."

She left him the cup and went to speak with Rimuru.

Frieren leaned closer.

"You're still here."

"I know."

"I was checking."

He turned toward her properly. There were drying tracks on her face, and she looked annoyed that he had noticed them. He touched one with his free thumb.

"You too."

The light beyond the rope did not flicker. It had all the patience in the world.

Frieren took the cup before he could spill it and put it on the ground between their feet.
