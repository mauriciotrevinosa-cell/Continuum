# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 3 — First

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 3
**Continuity state:** Ori hears the private questions first; chosen family stays intact; the fort gives them space

*When: Day 262, before the household meeting.*

Umaru moved the toll cup without asking for payment.

That frightened Ori more than the chair waiting beside the open door of the fort.

"I'll want it back," Umaru told Mau, pointing at the cushion she had taken from the good pile. "That one's structurally important."

"I'll try not to damage the foundations."

"You always say that."

She lifted a hanging corner for Frieren. Fern stopped outside with Rimuru, and Umaru went with them after looking once at Ori.

The fort was warm. Somebody had left a game halfway through, and a piece was sitting in the toll cup. Ori took it out and set it beside the board. Its owner would accuse somebody of stealing it otherwise.

Mau sat where she could see his face. Frieren lowered herself beside him, folding her legs out of the path. There was not enough space for three people who all wanted to be nearest the same person.

Ori solved it by sitting against Mau's chest.

"Tell me."

He did. The first question, the second, and his answer. He did not call it a bargain. He had not been offered one yet.

Ori heard herself breathing too quickly.

"She said my life?"

"Yes."

"And you said yes."

"Yes."

"You could have thought first."

Mau's arms settled around her without holding her still. "I don't think I would have changed it."

She wanted to push away. It was inconvenient that she wanted to stay exactly where she was.

"Did you tell Rimuru?"

"He heard me ask for you. Not this."

"Ciel?"

"I'm not asking her to keep you out of it."

Frieren had gone very quiet beside them. Her hand lay on Ori's shoulder, and Ori could feel a slight movement through her sleeve. She was stroking the same place without noticing.

"You promised," Ori said.

"I'm telling you first. As promised."

"I wanted there not to be anything to tell."

His chin lowered against her hair.

"Me too."

Outside, somebody asked whether they should move the bench. Fern told them where to put it. People were doing things with their hands because there was not yet anything useful to do about the door.

"Do you think she'd do it?" Ori asked.

He took longer with that answer.

"I think she wants to see what I'll do."

"That's not the same."

"No."

"Maybe she wouldn't."

"Maybe."

Ori turned until she could look up at him. "Then don't make it sound decided."

He looked at her for a long time.

"I haven't decided what to ask for. I haven't even heard what she's offering. But I can't promise you I'd stay if she made it that choice."

There it was. A whole answer, and she hated part of it.

"I don't like that."

"You don't have to."

Frieren's hand stopped.

"Neither do I," she said.

It helped, a little, that she said it in the same voice she used when somebody tried to take a grimoire out of her bag. Something that belonged to her was being handled, and she intended to object.

Ori leaned sideways until Frieren had to put an arm around both of them to stop her sliding off Mau's lap.

"You can't disappear with him," she said.

Frieren looked at her.

"I know."

"I mean it."

"I know."

That had already been promised. Ori remembered the river and did not make her promise it again.

The cloth lifted at the entrance. Milim's face appeared under it, oddly close to the floor because she had bent down instead of floating through.

"Umaru said I should ask."

Ori moved her knee. Milim took that as permission and folded herself into the space, bumping Mau's elbow and then apologizing to it.

Her eyes were wet.

"You can't stop being family," she told Ori.

"I'm not trying to."

"Good."

Milim looked at Mau as if checking whether he intended to disagree.

"If you're his daughter, you're my family too. I want you to have a long life."

"You could start by not kicking me in your sleep."

"That's different."

Ori laughed once. It came out painfully, but Milim smiled at her as though it had been a perfectly good laugh.

Nobody made it into a sign that everything was better.

Mau shifted beneath her. He had been cold outside; inside the fort, his shirt was warm where her cheek had rested against it.

"We have to tell them," he said after a while.

Ori looked at the game piece beside the board. She reached for it and put it back in the cup.

"They'll think it went missing," she said.

"It didn't."

"I know."

She made him wait until she was ready to get up.
