# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 4 — Outside the Curtain

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 4
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. While Mau tells Ori first inside the fort, the household gathers in the common room without knowing what the voice asked; Fern opens a clean page and does not write on it; no new decision

*When: Day 262, the same few minutes that Mau, Frieren and Ori spend inside the fort. The door in the shed is open. Most of the house does not yet know why the evening stopped.*

Fern stood outside the fort's curtain with her back to it, the way you stand outside a door you are guarding and do not want to listen through.

She could hear voices behind the cloth. Not words. She did not try for words. Ori's, high and quick. Mau's, low. Long silences between them that were worse than the voices.

Rimuru stood beside her, in human shape, with his hands in his coat. He was not looking at the curtain either. He was looking at the floor, about a pace in front of his own feet, at a knot in the boards that nobody had ever sanded out.

"Ciel?" said Fern quietly.

"Says the pull hasn't changed," said Rimuru. "Says she can't hear the other voice at all. She can only hear what he says back to it." He paused. "She doesn't like that. She's very polite about not liking it."

"Neither do I," said Fern.

"I know. You've got your ledger face."

Fern did not answer that. She did have the ledger. It was under her arm, closed, where it had been since the bowls came in from the bench. She had picked it up without thinking when Mau sat down outside and had not put it down since.

Umaru came back from wherever she had taken the toll cup, without it. She stood on Fern's other side and looked at the curtain, and then at Fern, and then at Rimuru, and folded her arms in exactly the way Fern was folding hers.

"I didn't charge him," she said.

"I saw," said Fern.

"The fort is going to have questions."

"The fort can ask me."

Umaru considered this. "That's very brave."

Behind them, the common room was filling.

Not quickly. Nobody had called anyone. Nobody had rung the kitchen bell or sent the fort running down corridors. But people had seen Mau sit down on the bench with his hand in Frieren's and Shoko kneeling in front of him, and people had seen Rimuru walk across the yard faster than Rimuru walked, and people had seen Ori go down the path with both hands hidden in her sleeves. And a house this full did not need a bell.

They came in from the yard and down the stairs and through the kitchen door, one and two at a time, and found somewhere to stand, and did not ask.

Momo came first, because she was already in the kitchen. She had a cloth in her hands and a chipped bowl in the cloth, the one Okarun kept putting on the bottom of the pile, and she stood at the kitchen door turning it over and over and did not seem to know she was holding it. Okarun came behind her with the washing bucket and put it down by the door and stood next to her. Turbo Granny was on his shoulder, and for once in her life she was not talking.

Seiko came down from her high seat, which took a while, and refused help, which took longer.

Kessoku came in from the music room with their instruments still in their hands, because they had been halfway through something when Kita looked out of the window. Ryo had her bass. Nijika had her sticks. Bocchi had her guitar held against her chest the way she held it when she wanted something between herself and the room. Seika came behind them with her arms folded, looked round the common room once, and pointed at the bench by the wall. They sat on it in a row without arguing, which was how Fern knew Seika was frightened too.

Coco came with her mother. Her mother did not know yet what any of it meant. She looked at the faces in the room and understood enough, and held Coco's hand, and said nothing.

Senku and Byakuya came in from the lab with soot on their hands. Senku went straight to the window that looked out on the yard and stood at it with his hands on the sill, looking at the shed. Byakuya stood behind him and did not say anything, which for Byakuya was a great effort and very noticeable.

Maomao came from the clinic with her bag. She set it down by the cold hearth, open, and sat beside it, and did not take anything out of it.

Gojo came last. He came in from the yard with his glasses pushed up and his bare eyes very bright, and stood just inside the outer door with his back to the frame, where he could see the curtain and the stair and the yard all at once. Shoko came in a moment later and stood beside him, close enough that their sleeves touched.

"Can you see it?" said Shoko quietly.

"I can see everything about it," said Gojo. "None of it tells me anything." He was looking at the shed through the open door. "It's not a technique. It's not a curse. It's not a gate like the dungeon. It's just a door." He paused. "I hate it."

"You hate most things you can't explain."

"I hate this one more."

The room filled until there was nowhere left to stand that was not somebody's place. And then it was quiet.

Not silent. A house this full could not be silent. Somebody shifted. Somebody coughed. The fire in the kitchen stove popped once. Milim, floating near the ceiling because she had forgotten not to, bumped her head on a beam and said *ow* very softly, and nobody laughed.

Fern turned round at last and looked at them all.

Forty faces. More. People who had stood in this room for Ram's coin and Coco's mother and the night of the bell, and knew how to stand in it. Nobody was asking her anything. They were waiting. Not for her to explain. For the curtain to move.

She opened the ledger.

She turned to a clean page. The pages before it were full: the list, the coins, the rota, Hollis's column, *Proposed: close the city* and the space under it that the house had spent the weeks since filling. She turned past all of that to a page with nothing on it.

She wrote, at the top, in her small upright hand:

*Day 262.*

Then she stopped.

She held the pen over the page for a long time. Rimuru watched her do it. So did Umaru. So, Fern realized, did most of the room.

She did not know what to write. She did not know what had been asked. She did not know what had been answered. She did not know what the door was, or what it wanted, or why the voice had come back after so long. Every other night that had needed a page, she had known at least the shape of the thing. Tonight she only knew the date.

She put the pen down in the fold of the ledger and left the page open with nothing on it but the day.

"Not yet?" said Rimuru quietly.

"Not yet," said Fern. "I don't write what I don't know."

Rimuru looked at the page. Then at her.

"That's very Mau of you," he said.

Fern looked at him sharply. Then, very slightly, something at the corner of her mouth gave.

"Don't tell him," she said.

* * *

Echidna came in from the north wing while they were waiting.

She did not hurry. She never hurried. She came down the corridor in her black dress with her pale hands folded and stopped at the edge of the common room, just inside the arch, where she could see everything and stand in nobody's place. Behind her, the other witches did not come. Satella's face appeared at the end of the corridor once, and went away again.

Qifrey saw her and did not look away this time. He looked at her for a long moment, across the whole common room. She looked back.

Neither of them said anything.

Then Echidna turned her head toward the fort's curtain, and listened to the voices behind it that nobody in the room could make out, and her face did something that Fern had never seen it do. It was very small and very brief. It might have been sympathy. It might have been interest. With Echidna, Fern had never been able to tell the difference, and she was not sure there was one.

"The Goddess," said Echidna, to nobody. Very quietly. "After so long." She paused. "How interesting."

Across the room, Coco's mother, who did not know who the woman in black was, put her arm round her daughter's shoulders without knowing why.

Umaru leaned toward Fern.

"I don't like her saying *interesting*," she whispered.

"Neither do I," said Fern.

"Should the fort have a position on it?"

"Later."

Umaru nodded, satisfied, and wrote something on the Ministry slate, very small, and turned it face down against her chest before anyone could see.

Behind them, the curtain moved.

It was only Milim's face, at the bottom of it, bent close to the floor because she had remembered for once not to float through. She looked up at Fern.

"Umaru said I should ask," she whispered. "Can I go in?"

Fern looked at Umaru. Umaru looked at the curtain, and at the voices behind it, which had gone quiet, and then back at Milim.

"Ask them," said Umaru. "Not us."

Milim nodded seriously and went under the cloth.

Fern turned back to the room. To the forty faces, waiting. To the open ledger in her arms with only the date on its page.

She stood there and waited with them.
