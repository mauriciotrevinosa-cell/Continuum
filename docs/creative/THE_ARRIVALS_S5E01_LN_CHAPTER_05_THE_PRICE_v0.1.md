# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 5 — The Price

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 5
**Continuity state:** Mau reports the threat and states his choice; Ori objects; nobody's species or guilt is established

*When: Day 262, household meeting.*

The room did not look like a meeting when they came out.

There were still dishes at one end of the table. Momo had told people to move the clean ones, and Ryo had interpreted that as an instruction to finish the bread. Nijika removed the plate from her reach without looking at it.

Mau stayed near the fort until his legs felt ordinary. Then he asked them to listen.

He did not have to ask twice.

Rimuru stood beside the table. Yuta came from the east corridor with Maki; Shoko moved a chair out with her foot. Frieren did not sit in it. She stayed close enough that Mau could feel the back of her hand against his.

He told them what had been asked.

Not everything he feared it might mean. The words, as well as he could remember them.

Anko asked him to repeat the last question.

He did.

"That's not a request," she said.

"I know."

"Has she said she'll do anything?" Yuta asked.

"Not yet."

"Then we have time to think."

People began doing that aloud. Qifrey asked about the frame rather than the landscape beyond it. Echidna, seated near the end, wanted to know whether the pull had changed with the voice. Ciel had no new answer; Rimuru relayed that instead of offering one for her.

Fern put a hand on the back of an empty chair.

"Before we start deciding what she meant, did you answer anything else?"

Mau repeated the first question too. What Ori was to him. How the voice had moved past his question without answering it. He caught himself putting a reason into the telling and went back.

"No. She didn't say that. That's what I thought she was doing."

Fern nodded. She wanted the distinction, not an apology for taking so long.

"And she hasn't actually asked you to go yet," Rimuru said.

"Not in those words."

"Then we aren't there yet."

Mau looked at his friend. He wanted to live in that small difference. It was a place where they could still examine a frame, ask another question, find something the person asking had overlooked.

Qifrey asked whether anyone had approached it since Mau sat down. The answer was no. Anko asked for the path to remain clear while they talked. Stark went to tell the people nearest the yard, returning with the same half-folded cloth he had been carrying when someone called him.

No one had become ready merely by coming into the room.

Gojo stayed by the outer door with his arms folded. He looked toward the yard at intervals. Mau could see him checking the same absence of an obvious place to strike.

Ori was watching Mau, not the doorway.

"Don't go," she said.

The conversations stopped unevenly.

"If she makes it that choice," Ori went on, "don't go. Even if—"

"No."

She drew back as if he had raised his voice.

He had not. That made it worse.

Mau lowered himself into the chair Shoko had moved for him. Now Ori was looking down at him.

"Finish," he said.

She did not want to now. He waited.

"Even if it costs my life."

He looked at her hands. They were outside her sleeves at last, clenched against her legs.

"Please don't ask me to leave you to die."

"Then don't ask me to watch you do it."

There was nothing quick he could say to that.

He rested his palms on his knees so he would not reach for her before she wanted him to.

"I don't want to die."

Ori's mouth moved. She pressed it shut again.

"I know what it looks like," he said to the table. "That something happens, and somehow I'm the part we spend. I know none of you want that. I'm not saying you do."

Rimuru's fingers tightened around the edge of a chair.

"I don't enjoy being the one standing in front. I'm frightened. I'm frightened now. And I don't know what I am, or what I can do, or why things keep finding me."

Frieren's hand found his shoulder.

"But some of them do want me. And if something comes for one of you because it wants me, I don't know how to stand here and let that happen. I'd keep thinking I could have stopped it."

"Thinking it doesn't make it your fault," Maki said.

"If I know what she wants—"

"You know what she asked. Those aren't always the same thing."

He stopped. Across the table Yuta had not taken his eyes off him. Rimuru was still holding the chair, as if he had meant to bring it over and forgotten which of them needed it.

Mau had started explaining how he could bear the choice before hearing what it would do to them. He could feel himself doing it. Knowing did not supply a different answer.

He nodded, once. He had heard her. It did not untie the thing under his ribs.

"I know. But that's what I'm afraid of."

Ori sat on the next chair without being asked. He turned toward her.

"If I can stop something happening to you, I'll try. You're my family. You're my daughter. Please don't ask me to make a place at the table where you should have been."

Her face crumpled. She wiped it angrily with the heel of her hand.

He waited until she let him take that hand.

Frieren's fingers had stopped moving on his shoulder. When he looked up, her face was so still that he wanted to ask her a question in private, with no one watching either of them.

"And you," he said.

"Mau."

"I'd live an eternity with you just to keep seeing that smile."

She did not smile.

He touched her hand.

"I want a life with you. Longer, remember? Longer than you, if there's a way. So you don't have to be alone."

He could not make it a fact. He could only say what he wanted, in the room where everybody could hear how much.

Frieren looked down at their hands.

"You don't know how."

"No."

"Then don't say it as if you do."

He opened his mouth. Before he found the words, a voice spoke from nowhere anybody could turn toward.

"Are you sure?"

The bread plate slipped against Nijika's cup.

"Sure of what?" Anko asked.

"Here is the offer," the voice said. "Walk through that door, and I will not touch Ori directly."

Mau pushed his chair back.

"Before I accept," he said, "we need to talk."
