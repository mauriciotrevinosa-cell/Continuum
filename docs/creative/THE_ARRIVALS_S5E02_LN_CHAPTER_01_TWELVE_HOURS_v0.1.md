# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 6 — Twelve Hours

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 6
**Continuity state:** Spoken deal: crossing for no direct divine harm to Ori and Frieren; twelve-hour deadline begins; no promise of safe return

*When: Day 262, approximately half past seven in the evening.*

Mau wanted everyone covered.

He knew it was too much to ask before he said it. That was no reason not to ask.

"The settlement," he said. "Not just Ori."

The voice gave a small sound. It might have been amusement.

"Don't get greedy."

"You're the one who wants me to do something."

Mau waited. It was the only part of the bargain he could see how to hold on to: she wanted him to take the step, and he was still on this side of it.

"Ori and Frieren," the Goddess said.

Frieren looked at him sharply. He had not asked her permission to use her name; he had not needed to. It had already been chosen for him.

"What does that mean?" he asked.

"I will not harm either of them directly."

"And everything else?"

"Will remain a world."

Anko said, "Say that properly."

"Other people may hurt them. They may fall ill. They may make choices neither of you enjoy. I am not offering to prevent their lives. I am offering my own restraint."

Mau hated that it was something he wanted.

Ori's shoulders had drawn in. Frieren was watching him with the expression she used when a dangerous object had been put into somebody's hands and they were already testing the weight.

"You aren't promising I'll come back," he said.

"No."

"Do you know if I can?"

"No."

Somebody behind the table made an angry sound. Mau did not turn. If he looked at every face he would spend the night doing it, and there were questions left.

"You don't know where it goes."

"I know where it begins."

"That's not enough."

"For which of us?"

Rimuru leaned toward him.

"We don't have to answer her in the same breath."

Mau looked at his friend. "I know."

"Do you? Because you're already getting to the part where you tell us what you can bear."

The words were quiet enough to remain between the people nearest them. Mau sat back. He had not noticed how far he had leaned over the table.

"I want something that changes what she can ask next time," he said. "If I just go, she still gets to ask it again."

"And this doesn't change everything else."

"No."

He looked toward Ori. She was holding Milim's hand so tightly that their fingers had changed colour. He wanted an agreement broad enough that he could look at everybody in the room when he made it. The offer was not going to become that because he wanted it.

"Can I take someone with me?" he asked the Goddess.

"The door is calling you."

"That isn't what I asked."

She gave him no better answer. He could not call that permission, or a route someone else could safely test for him.

Yuta met his eyes. "Then we don't put another person through it on a guess."

Mau nodded. He hated how much of their thinking had ended in a thing they could not try.

He put both palms on the table. The wood was warmer than his fingers. There was a scratch under his right thumb that he had made with a bowl some time ago, a small accident nobody had bothered to name.

"Give me a day."

"Twelve hours."

Frieren said, "No."

The voice went on as if she had not spoken.

"Say your goodbyes tonight. Then go."

"And if I don't?"

Silence opened around the question. Even the people who had wanted him to ask it seemed to stop breathing.

"Then there is no agreement," the voice said. "And Ori dies."

Mau looked at Ori.

She looked furious. It was better than the empty look she had worn when the voice spoke about seeing through her eyes. She was here, and furious with him, with the room, with whoever could say that sentence so easily.

He thought the voice wanted his answer more than it wanted her dead. He thought that might be the whole point.

He could not put Ori into the space between *thought* and *knew*.

"I don't consent to this," Ori said.

Mau turned to her. She was looking past him, toward the voice.

"Not to being the reason. Not to any of it."

Nobody told her that her refusal made the threat impossible. Frieren put an arm around her shoulders, and this time Ori leaned into it without taking her eyes off the empty space.

Mau wanted to wait until he could do this without hurting her. He could feel how easily waiting for that could turn into pretending he had not heard the condition.

"You didn't choose it," he said. "I know."

It was not enough. She did not pretend otherwise for him.

He turned back to the Goddess.

"Twelve hours from now," he said.

"Yes."

"I walk through that door. You will not directly harm Ori or Frieren. That's what we're agreeing to. Not that they're safe from everything. Not that I come back."

"Yes."

He looked around the table once. Yuta's face was very still. Maki's hand was on his forearm. Gojo was watching the place where the voice was not standing.

Rimuru did not nod him into it.

Mau was grateful for that, too.

"Deal."

Nothing lit up. No mark appeared on his hand. There was only a chair leg catching on a floorboard as Frieren stepped away from the table.

"I am counting from now," Rimuru said, after a moment. Ciel's count, spoken in his voice. "We won't lose the time."

Mau tried to answer. The voice had gone, and without it the room was full of everything he had not said to the people in it.

Frieren was already at the foot of the stairs.

"Frieren."

She stopped with one hand on the rail.

"If you're going," she said, "then go."

She went upstairs before he could pretend that was what she meant.
