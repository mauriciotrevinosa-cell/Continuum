# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 8 — Space

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 8
**Continuity state:** Frieren retreats to the east room; Maki asks for room to talk; Mau respects the boundary

*When: Day 262, shortly after the agreement.*

Mau reached the first landing before Maki caught up with him.

"Wait."

"I need to—"

"I know. Wait."

He stopped. They could see Frieren at the other end of the corridor. She had one sleeve caught under her fingers and was walking with the concentration of somebody carrying a cup filled to the brim.

The east-room door closed.

He took a step.

Maki did not put a hand on him. She stood where he would have to choose to walk around her.

"Let me talk to her."

"I don't have all night."

"You have a night. Give her some of it."

The words stopped him more effectively than a hand would have.

He looked past her at the door. He wanted to knock, to make Frieren answer him, to get the part of the night where she would not look at him over with. It was an ugly thing to find under how much he wanted to see her.

"All right," he said.

Maki's shoulders lowered a fraction.

"I'm not asking you to leave her alone until morning."

"Tell her that, too."

"I can tell her you're downstairs. You can tell her the rest."

He went back down slowly, one hand on the rail. At the turn he heard Maki knock.

* * *

Frieren opened the door because Maki knocked differently from Mau.

Then she wished she had not known that.

The room was untidy in the small, inhabited ways that she had stopped noticing. Mau's book was face down where he knew she disliked it. Her book was lying on his side of the bed because she had been using his side of the bed. The shelf made her turn sideways to let Maki in.

"He isn't coming?" Frieren asked.

"He's downstairs."

"I didn't ask you to stop him."

"I asked him to give us a little time."

Maki waited for her to choose somewhere to sit. Frieren chose the reading chair, then changed her mind and sat on the edge of the bed.

"There's no time," she said.

"There is a little."

It was unbearable that both could be true.

Maki took the chair. She did not touch the grimoire lying open on its arm.

Frieren could see the loft door from here. It was open and empty. Rimuru had been downstairs when the cold came. His blanket was still up there, one corner hanging untidily over the edge.

Everybody's things were waiting for a normal night.

"I asked him," Frieren said.

Maki looked at her.

"To stay longer. I asked him before any of this."

"You told me."

Of course she had. The river, and the things they had trusted each other with. Frieren put her hands flat on the blanket. She did not want to tell that conversation again. It had not stopped being true because another door had opened.

"Now he says eternity in front of everyone. As if wanting it is enough to make it possible."

"Do you think he doesn't want it?"

"That's not what I said."

"Then what are you afraid he heard?"

Frieren could answer that. She had been answering it ever since his chair scraped back from the table.

"That if he cannot do it, he has failed me."

Maki was silent.

Frieren watched the light change on the blanket. She had already begun arranging a defence of him in her head. He had been frightened. Ori had been in front of him. He had been trying to give everyone something to hold on to.

All of it could be true. She had heard the words anyway.

"I wanted him to look," she said. "For a way to stay longer. I still do."

"I know."

"Not until it kills him."

Maki looked at her hands.

"Then that's the difference he needs to hear."

"He knows that."

"Maybe. Does he know you're frightened he doesn't?"

Frieren started to answer and stopped. Downstairs a chair moved over the floor. It might have been his. She was trying so hard not to go and find out that even sitting still felt like doing something to him.

The east window showed the roofline and the last of the sky. From this side of the house the shed was out of sight. Frieren was relieved not to see it and ashamed of the relief.

"I want to be there when he leaves," she said.

"Then be there."

"I don't want to let him."

Maki leaned forward, elbows on her knees.

"You don't have to like what he's doing."

Frieren looked toward the door. "If I let him in now, I'll stop being angry."

"You might not."

"I'll hold him. Then I'll try to make it easier for him. And when he's gone, all of it will still be here."

Maki's gaze moved to her hands on the blanket.

"Then don't make it easier yet."

Frieren sat with that for a while.

When she finally said she wanted to sleep, Maki got up. She asked if Frieren wanted water. Frieren said no, then yes, then watched her set a cup on the shelf beside the closed book that held the flowers.

Maki picked up the book Frieren had moved twice and held it out rather than choosing a place for it.

Frieren put it on her own side of the bed. Then she saw the other one on his pillow and left it there.

"Do you want me to come back?" Maki asked.

She almost said no. It was the answer that would let her keep the next part private, whether privacy helped or not.

"Not yet."

"All right."

"I don't know how long."

"You can tell us."

Maki did not ask her to be reasonable.

Frieren was grateful until the door closed. Then she was alone with the book on his pillow, and she was not grateful for anything.
