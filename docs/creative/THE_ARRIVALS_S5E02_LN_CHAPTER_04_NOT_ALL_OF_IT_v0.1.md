# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 9 — Not All of It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 9
**Continuity state:** Ordinary visits and family choices continue; no sixty-person farewell procession or repayment of the fort day

*When: Day 262, evening.*

Bocchi brought her cup over and forgot to bring somewhere to put it.

Mau moved his own from the small table between their chairs. She set hers down with exaggerated care, then looked appalled at the amount of care she had used.

"Kita says I don't have to say anything important."

"What if you accidentally do?"

"I haven't planned that far."

He smiled. She took the chair Yuta had vacated to help Momo carry something from the kitchen.

For a while she told him about a passage Ryo insisted was simple. It was simple when Ryo played it. This, Bocchi said, was a problem with several of Ryo's opinions.

Mau asked her to show him the rhythm on the chair arm.

She did. Then he tried it, and she laughed before she could stop herself.

"That wasn't it."

"You said simple."

"Ryo said simple."

He tried again. She moved one of his fingers to a different beat.

Neither of them mentioned the door until Bocchi's eyes went there of their own accord.

"You can still tell me important things," he said.

"I know."

"Or that Ryo's wrong."

"That's also important."

She stayed until Kita came to fetch her, and left the cup behind. He called her back for it. She looked absurdly grateful to have something ordinary to come back for.

Later Umaru arrived with Yuka and a folded piece of paper.

"Not a contract," she announced.

"Promising start."

"It's a reminder."

He unfolded it. The fort's handwriting occupied almost the whole page. The day he owed them had acquired a border, a drawing of a very angry hamster, and a second underlining beneath *FOR US*.

"You can't use tonight," Umaru said. "This doesn't count."

"I wasn't going to."

"Or say the time in the fort before the meeting counted."

"I wasn't going to do that either."

Yuka leaned over to inspect his face. "He really wasn't."

"He'll try something."

"I'm sitting here," Mau said.

"Exactly. Planning."

Umaru took the paper back before he could fold it badly. Yuka stayed when she went to find Milim.

"Oppa."

He looked at her.

"Don't make a joke about being late. Tomorrow. If you come back late."

His reply changed before he said it.

"All right."



She nodded once. "After that you can."

He almost laughed, but she put her arms around him first. He held her until she let go. Her dog waited a few steps away, white head lowered between her paws.

"You too," he told the dog.

Her ears moved. Yuka wiped her face with a sleeve and said she thought that counted as a response.

Rem came after they left. She did not bring a tray.

Mau noticed. She noticed him noticing.

"I thought I'd sit," she said.

"Good."

She took Bocchi's chair. For some time they watched the yard. The opening did not get easier to look at with practice.

"I told someone," he said at last.

"I heard."

"Early."

"Yes."

He looked at her. "I wish that had fixed it."

Rem rested her hands in her lap. "It means we're here with you while it isn't fixed."

He nodded.

She did not ask him to be grateful for that. After a while she asked for his water, because her throat was dry, and he passed it over.

When Maki came downstairs, the room had thinned rather than emptied. Some people had gone to their rooms. Others were arguing over where to put blankets for the people who would not sleep.

"She's gone to bed," Maki said.

Mau stood.

Rem kept the cup. "Walk," she told him.

"I am."

"I know. I like hearing it."

He stopped for a second at that, then went upstairs with his hand brushing the rail.
