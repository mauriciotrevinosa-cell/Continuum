# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 11 — Crackers

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 11
**Continuity state:** Mai offers company without taking over; Frieren invites the crackers into the east room

*When: Day 262, late evening.*

Mai was sitting on the third step from the bottom.

Mau nearly walked past her. She moved one foot and he stopped.

"You look awful."

"Thank you."

"Did she let you in?"

"No."

Mai looked toward the turn in the stairs. He could not tell what she had heard, and she did not pretend to know the rest.

"We talked," he said. "She asked for a little more time."

"And you gave it to her."

"I'm standing down here."

"That could mean several things."

He sat beside her. For a minute they listened to the lower room. Ryo was objecting to somebody putting away her plate while there was still a crumb on it. She sounded almost normal until she had to repeat herself.

"Do you want me to ask her?" Mai said.

He looked at her.

"Company," she clarified. "Not whether she's ready to forgive you for whatever she hasn't told me."

"Yes. If she'd like that."

Mai stood. "That sounds like crackers."

"It usually does."

She went to find her sister.

* * *

Frieren had moved off the floor by the time they knocked.

Not far. She was sitting on the edge of the bed again, holding the cup Maki had left, and the water had gone warm against her hands.

"I didn't ask for a meeting," she said through the door.

"Then don't conduct one," Seiko said. "We're bringing food."

Frieren opened it.

Maki was carrying a plate. Mai had the tin. Seiko had brought her own cup, an act of confidence that might have annoyed Frieren on a different evening.

Turbo Granny looked out of Seiko's pocket.

"I was comfortable," she announced.

"You were complaining."

"I was comfortable complaining."

They waited while Frieren moved the book from the chair. Seiko would have tripped over the shelf if Frieren had not caught her elbow.

"Still there, then."

"It belongs there."

Maki looked at the narrow space beside it and made no comment. Mai did.

"That is the centre of the floor."

"The room has several centres."

"Wonderful. We've found a new branch of geometry."

Frieren sat on the bed. The tin opened. For a little while nobody asked her anything, which was why she could bear them there.

Seiko inspected a cracker as if it had disappointed her personally. Turbo Granny demanded a piece, received one, and complained about its size. Maki had left enough space between them that Frieren could put her hand down without touching anybody.

She found herself moving it closer.

"I know he means it," she said.

Maki did not ask what.

"I know he wants to stay."

Mai put the lid under the tin. "You don't sound very convinced."

"I am convinced. That's the problem."

She looked at the cup. The water moved when her fingers tightened.

"He can want something until it hurts him. That doesn't tell him how to stop."

"He came back downstairs when you asked him to," Maki said.

Frieren looked at her.

"He didn't like it. He did it."

That was an inconveniently small thing to put beside the door in the yard. Frieren could not dismiss it for being small.

Seiko pulled her chair a little nearer the bed.

"What did he tell you?"

Frieren did not repeat all of it. Some of it belonged to the wood between them. She told them about the mornings, and the shelf, and how he had said he wanted to look for a way rather than already knowing one.

When she got to the books, she had to stop.

Maki passed her the cloth from beside the cup.

Mai looked out of the east window. "That's an awful lot of nonsense to remember just to make someone feel better."

Frieren laughed once into the cloth, unwillingly.

"They're not nonsense."

"He seems to agree."

Outside the window, the last pale strip of sky had gone.
