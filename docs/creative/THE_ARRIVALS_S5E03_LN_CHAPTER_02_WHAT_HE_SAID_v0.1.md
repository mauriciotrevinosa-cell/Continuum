# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 12 — What He Said

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 12
**Continuity state:** The night conversation continues; belief and fear coexist; nobody resolves Frieren's feelings for her

*When: Day 262 into the morning of Day 263.*

The second plate was mostly broken pieces.

Maki apologized for it when she came back. Momo had said that if they wanted anything less broken they could begin baking it themselves, preferably without occupying her doorway.

"Fair," Mai said, and took the largest piece.

Frieren had stopped holding the cup. That seemed to have happened without her agreeing to put it down.

"You don't have to be cheerful when you see him," Seiko said.

"I know."

"Do you?"

Frieren pulled a thread of the blanket straight between her fingers. "I don't want the last thing I give him to be more fear."

The room was silent long enough for her to dislike what she had said.

"It doesn't have to be the last thing," Maki answered.

"You don't know that."

"No. Neither do you."

Seiko reached for another broken cracker. "You can be frightened and still tell him what you came to tell him."

"Which is?"

"I'd hate to invent it for you. You've been very rude about our geometry."

Frieren looked up. Mai's mouth moved at one corner.

It was irritating to be allowed to decide while so many people were waiting for her to decide.

She said that aloud.

"Yes," Maki said.

Nothing after it. No way to improve the feeling. Frieren rested her head against the wall and looked toward the shelf.

There were five flowers in the place they had made for them. She could distinguish the fifth without going over. Keeping it had not been a question. When something was about to be lost, she always knew she wanted it.

The difficult thing was wanting it while it was still there and not making a cage out of her hands.

"If he changes his mind," she said, "about longer. If he finds out what it would cost him."

"Then he'll have to tell you," Mai said.

"He might not."

Maki looked at her. "He might."

Frieren closed her eyes. Downstairs something thumped, then somebody laughed at the attempt to hush it. There were people spending a night awake with him. Yuta. Rimuru. Others coming and going. She could not hear Mau's voice distinctly enough to make out a word.

"I want him to know he can," she said.

"That sounds like something to tell him."

She nodded.

It did not make her want him for less time. She did not think anything could.

Later they talked about other things because they could not keep looking at the same fear without blinking. Seiko complained about the chair. Mai said it had been expensive enough in arguments to be comfortable. Frieren said she would tell Mau they had insulted it, and Maki asked which of the two of them would take that worse.

They laughed. Frieren laughed with them, then cried again without warning and was furious with her face.

Nobody told her that meant they had made no progress.

At some point Fern knocked softly with fresh water. She set it inside and looked at Frieren rather than counting the empty cups.

"I will come for you before he goes," she said.

"I'll come down."

Fern did not argue about which of them had said it first.

"Then I will tell you when he's waiting outside."

After she left, Frieren lay back against her own pillow and put an arm over her eyes. They stayed. She heard Maki and Mai speak quietly about the plate, Seiko move a foot away from the shelf, the tin being closed.

She slept in small pieces without meaning to. Waking was always the same: the weight of tomorrow arriving before she remembered whether tomorrow had arrived yet.

Once she heard the connecting door move. Ori looked in, saw the company and the open tin, and came to the bed without asking why everybody was still awake.

Frieren lifted her arm. Ori lay against her for a while, careful of the others' legs. She had a piece of red ribbon wound around her finger.

"Milim's asleep," she said.

"Good."

"She wanted to stay awake. She said she'd wake up instantly if I told her to."

"Will she?"

"Not if I ask nicely."

Frieren touched the hair at Ori's temple. Ori closed her eyes, then opened them again as if she had done something wrong.

"You can sleep," Frieren said.

"You too."

They stayed like that until Ori grew heavy and Mai offered to help her back through her own door.

The window was no longer black when Frieren looked at it next.

She sat up too quickly. Maki put a hand out, then let her find her own balance.

"How much?"

"Less than an hour," she said. "Fern will tell us."

Frieren looked toward the closed door. He had spoken to her through it for all that time and had left it closed because she asked.

She put her feet on the floor.

"I know what I want to tell him," she said.
