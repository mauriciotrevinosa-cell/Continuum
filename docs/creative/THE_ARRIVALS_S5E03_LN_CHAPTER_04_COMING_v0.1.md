# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 14 — Coming

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 14
**Continuity state:** Frieren prepares to come down; Mau crosses at the limit; their farewell is missed without condemning her need for space

*When: Day 263, at the twelve-hour deadline.*

The button would not go through.

Frieren pulled it back, looked at it, and found she had been trying to put it through the wrong hole.

Mai waited beside the door without reaching to help.

"I can do it," Frieren said.

"I know."

She did it. The little success made her eyes sting again, which was absurd, and then she had to stand still because if she began walking like this she would have to stop in the corridor.

Maki was putting the cups together. She did not seem to know why she was doing it until she looked down at them.

"Leave them," Seiko said.

Maki left them.

Frieren picked up the book from Mau's pillow and put it on his side table. He would complain that she had lost his place. He would be right. She made herself stop rearranging things for someone who had not gone yet.

Fern had come. He was waiting outside. She knew that. She had said she was coming.

She put her hand on the latch.

"I don't want him to go," she said.

Nobody argued her past it.

She opened the door.

* * *

Mau stood when Ciel's count reached the last minute.

Rimuru did not speak the count aloud. Mau could see it in the way his friend's mouth had stopped moving.

Ori got up with him. She took his hand, then let him have it back before he had to ask.

"Tell me first next time, too," she said.

"I will."

"Not just this once."

"Not just this once."

He looked at the house again.

The voice returned as plainly as if it had never left.

"Now, or there is no agreement."

Mau's stomach dropped. He had spent twelve hours knowing that sentence would arrive and still wanted another minute to prepare for it.

He turned toward the frame.

"Mau," Rimuru said.

He looked back.

Rimuru had several things on his face and chose none of them to say. He put a hand against Mau's arm, squeezed once, and stepped away.

Mau took a step.

The pull changed. It did not carry his feet. It made the space in front of them feel already crossed.

He put down his foot anyway.

Behind the open common-room door somebody was running. He heard the turn on the stones before he saw her.

Frieren.

Her hair was loose around her face. She had stopped trying to wipe it dry.

For a moment he forgot the clearing. There was only the familiar wrongness of seeing her hurry, the way she had one hand lifted, reaching before there was anything she could reach.

He smiled.

Not because he knew what she had decided. Because she was coming to tell him.

His second foot went through.

* * *

Frieren saw his face change before the light closed.

She had enough time to see the smile. Not enough to be sure he had heard her.

Then she was in the yard with her hand lifted toward the shed.

The rope hung from its peg. The longest loop moved in the morning air.

She went another step before her body accepted that there was nowhere else to go.

Ori was standing beside the bench. Her hands were empty again.

Frieren lowered her own hand.

For one awful moment all she could remember saying to him was *don't come in*.
