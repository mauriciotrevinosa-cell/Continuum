# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 14 — Coming

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (expanded 2026-10-17 for pacing)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 14
**Continuity state:** Frieren prepares to come down; Mau crosses at the limit; their farewell is missed without condemning her need for space

*When: Day 263, at the twelve-hour deadline. The sun is not yet over the hills. The fort is at the common-room window. Fern is at the foot of the stair, where she said she would be.*

The button would not go through.

Frieren pulled it back, looked at it, and found she had been trying to put it through the wrong hole. Her fingers were cold. They were always cold. This morning they were cold in a way that did not belong to her, that belonged to the night and the floor and the door, and they would not do what she told them.

Mai waited beside the door without reaching to help.

"I can do it," said Frieren.

"I know."

She did it. The little success made her eyes sting again, which was absurd, and then she had to stand still, because if she began walking like this she would have to stop in the corridor, and she did not want to stop in the corridor. She wanted to arrive.

She caught her reflection in the dark half of the window glass, where the yard had not yet grown light enough to see through. Her eyes were red. Her hair was a disaster. She had slept in it, in pieces, against the wall, against Ori, against the pillow.

She thought about doing something about it.

She did not. He had seen her worse. He had seen her at the old inn the first time she let anything show, crying about Fern, after seven days of searching a world that did not have her in it anymore. He had stayed. Hair did not matter. It had never mattered to him once.

Maki was putting the cups together on the floor. She did not seem to know why she was doing it until she looked down at them in her hands.

"Leave them," said Seiko.

Maki left them.

Seiko had not got up from the reading chair. She sat with her own cup in her lap and Turbo Granny asleep in her pocket, snoring, and looked at Frieren over the top of her glasses.

"Go on," she said. "We'll still be old when you come back."

Frieren picked up the book from Mau's pillow, the one he had left face down and open, and put it on his side of the table. Then she picked it up again and put it back on the pillow, face down, open at the same place, exactly as he had left it. He would complain that she had lost his place. He would be right. She made herself stop rearranging things for someone who had not gone yet.

The flowers on the shelf. Five. She did not go over to them. She knew where every one of them was.

Fern had come. Fern had knocked, and said through the door, very quietly, *He's in the yard. He's waiting.* And gone down again to stand at the foot of the stair, because she had said she would. Frieren knew that. She had said she was coming. She had said it to Fern, and to Ori, and to the room. She had said it to the door.

She put her hand on the latch.

"I don't want him to go," she said.

Nobody argued her past it. Maki did not say *he'll come back*. Mai did not say *you'll be all right*. Seiko did not say anything at all. They let it be true in the room, all of it, without trying to make it smaller.

She opened the door.

* * *

Mau stood when Ciel's count reached the last minute.

Rimuru did not speak the count aloud. Mau could see it in the way his friend's mouth had stopped moving. Ciel was counting in both their heads at once, very quietly, the way she had counted at the beginning of the Trial: just a number, one at a time, so that he would know where he was.

He did not look at the stair any more. He had looked at it for an hour. If he looked at it now, he would not be able to stop.

Ori got up with him. She took his hand, then let him have it back before he had to ask for it.

"Tell me first next time, too," she said.

"I will."

"Not just this once."

"Not just this once."

He looked at the house again. The window with the fort's hoods in it. Momo at the kitchen door with flour on her hands. Maomao on the step beside her open bag. Coco with her mother. Bocchi had come out onto the step with her guitar held against her chest like a shield, and Kita was behind her with both hands on her shoulders.

Fern was in the common-room doorway, beside Kaneki, looking up the stair.

The voice returned as plainly as if it had never left.

*Now, or there is no agreement.*

Everyone in the yard heard it. Milim, on the arm of the bench, went rigid. Gojo's head turned toward the sky, toward nothing, the way it turned toward the things his eyes could find and could not name. Somewhere behind the common-room window, one of the fort said *no* in a very small voice, and somebody else hushed it.

Mau's stomach dropped. He had spent twelve hours knowing that sentence would arrive and still wanted another minute to prepare for it.

He turned toward the frame.

"Mau," said Rimuru.

He looked back.

Rimuru had several things on his face and chose none of them to say. He was in human shape, and very pale, and his eyes were doing the thing they had done at the bottom of the south field in the snow. He put a hand against Mau's arm, squeezed once, hard, and stepped away.

Yuta did not say anything either. He met Mau's eyes and nodded, once, the way he had nodded on the roof when the three of them made their rule. *If any of us starts carrying something he shouldn't.* This was not that. This was the other thing. Mau had told them. He had told everyone. He had told them first.

He stepped over the rope.

The red cloth brushed his knee.

He took a step toward the frame.

The pull changed. It did not carry his feet. It made the space in front of them feel already crossed, as if the ground between him and the clearing had been folded, and he was only walking over the crease.

He put his foot down anyway.

Behind the open common-room door somebody was running. He heard it on the stair first, too fast for the stair, a foot missing a step and catching the next one. Then on the boards of the common room. Then on the stones of the yard. He heard the turn on the stones before he saw her.

Frieren.

Her hair was loose round her face. She had stopped trying to wipe her eyes. She was running, which she never did, because elves did not need to and she had always said that running was for people who had planned badly. She was running across the yard in the grey morning with one hand lifted, reaching, before there was anything she could reach.

For a moment he forgot the clearing.

There was only the familiar wrongness of seeing her hurry. Her mouth was moving. She was saying something. He could not hear it over the pull. He could see her face, and he knew it. He had known it for years. It was not the face she made when something was wrong and she had decided he was not going to carry it. It was the other one. The one from the lake. The one from the night under the tree when she had said *longer*.

She had decided.

He did not know what. He did not need to. She was coming to tell him.

He smiled.

His second foot went through.

* * *

Frieren saw his face change before the light closed.

She had enough time to see the smile. Not enough to be sure he had heard her.

Then the frame was only the back of a shed, rough boards and a cobweb in the corner, and the clearing was gone, and the wrong morning was gone, and she was in the yard with her hand lifted toward a wall.

The rope hung between its stakes. The red cloth moved in the morning air. On the near side of it, in the dew, there were his footprints, two of them, and then nothing.

She went another step before her body accepted that there was nowhere else to go.

Nobody in the yard moved.

Ori was standing beside the bench. Her hands were empty again. The red ribbon had fallen in the grass at her feet and she had not noticed.

Frieren lowered her own hand.

It took a long time. It felt like lowering something heavy.

She stood in the middle of the yard in the grey light with her loose hair and her red eyes and her cold hands, in front of the whole house, and looked at the back wall of a shed.

For one awful moment all she could remember saying to him was *don't come in*.
