# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 15 — A Very Boring Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 15
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. Gojo watches the door all night and cannot read it; Shoko sits with him; their slow closeness continues without a confession or a new power; the unlit cigarette stays unlit

*When: Day 263, from about two in the morning until the sky begins to grey. The yard. Behind them, the kitchen window is lit; Momo is baking.*

At two in the morning, Gojo was still looking at the door.

He had moved a chair out into the yard at some point after midnight, a plain one from the long table, and turned it backward and sat astride it with his arms folded on the back and his chin on his arms. He had pushed his glasses up into his hair hours ago and not put them back down. His eyes were bare. In the dark they were the only bright thing in the yard except the wrong morning coming through the back of the shed.

He was not blinking much.

Shoko came out with two cups and sat down on the step of the kitchen door, a few paces behind him and to one side, where she could see his face and the shed both.

She did not say anything. She held one cup out toward him.

He did not look round. He reached back with one hand, without looking, and took it. His fingers found the handle on the first try, which meant he had known exactly where she was the whole time she was crossing the yard.

"It's tea," she said. "Momo's. It's been stewing since midnight. It's terrible."

He drank some.

"It's terrible," he agreed.

He drank the rest.

* * *

For a long time they did not talk.

The yard was cool. Summer nights in the valley were short and cool, and the dew was already coming down on the grass and the rope and the red cloth tied in the middle of it. The ink tree was a black shape against a sky that had too many stars in it, strange stars, the ones Byakuya and Senku had been arguing about naming. Somewhere in the long grass beyond the yard, insects were making a noise like a very small saw.

The kitchen window behind Shoko was yellow. Inside, she could hear Momo kneading, and Okarun's voice, low, and once, much later, Kaneki's.

"Anything?" she said, at last.

"Everything," said Gojo.

She waited.

"I can see everything about it," he said. "Every edge. Where it starts. Where the wall should be and isn't. How the light on the other side doesn't match the light on this side, and by how much." He tilted his head a fraction. "I can see the pull. It's like a slope. Very gentle. It goes one way. Toward the clearing. It doesn't go anywhere else. It's not pulling at you, or me, or anybody in the house. Just him." He paused. "I can see all of that, and none of it tells me what it is."

"You've said."

"I'm saying it again. I like saying it. It's the only thing about it I can say for certain."

Shoko turned her cup round on her knee.

"It's not a technique," said Gojo. "There's no energy in it. Nothing cursed. Nothing like the dungeon gate, nothing like Rimuru's shadow relay, nothing like anything I've ever seen. It's not built. It's not cast. It's just there. Like it was always there and somebody let us notice it." He was quiet. "I've spent my whole life being able to look at things and know what they are. That was the whole point of me."

"And now you can't."

"And now I can't." He breathed out. "It's very boring."

Shoko looked at him.

"Boring," she said.

"Yes."

"You've been staring at it for four hours."

"Very boring for four hours." He did not look away from the shed. "If I stop looking at it, I'll have to think about why I'm looking at it."

Shoko did not say anything to that for a while.

She took the cigarette out from behind her ear, where she had tucked it at some point in the evening without noticing, and held it between two fingers, unlit, the way she always held it. She looked at it.

"I used to sit up like this," she said. "In my world. At the school. Waiting for people to come back." She turned the cigarette over. "Some of them came back. Some of them came back and I fixed them. Some of them came back and there was nothing to fix." She paused. "And some of them didn't come back, and I sat up anyway, until somebody came and told me to stop."

Gojo had gone very still.

"I didn't know how to stop," said Shoko. "So I just sat. With one of these." She lifted the cigarette slightly. "I smoked them then. One every hour. So I'd know how long it had been."

"You don't smoke this one."

"No."

"Why not?"

She looked at the cigarette for a long time.

"Because it's the last one," she said. "And as long as I don't smoke it, I'm not counting."

* * *

At some point after three, Gojo got up off the chair and came and sat down on the step beside her.

It was not a big step. Their shoulders were touching. He did not seem to notice. Or he noticed, and decided not to say so, which was a thing Shoko had only recently learned he was capable of.

He still did not look away from the shed.

"In my world," he said, "they put me in a box."

Shoko did not say anything. She had heard him say it once before, at the long table, the morning Eren asked to close the city. *The best box anyone ever made.*

"I was in it for a while," said Gojo. "It doesn't matter how long. It was very quiet in there. I could see everything outside it. I could see everything that was happening while I was in it, and I couldn't do anything about any of it." He paused. "That's what this feels like. I'm looking at that door, and I can see every edge of it, and there's nothing I can do."

"You're not in a box."

"No. He's going into one." Gojo's jaw moved. "In the morning. And I'll be standing here, seeing everything, doing nothing."

Shoko looked at the side of his face, lit by the wrong morning. At the jaw, set. At the bare blue eyes that had not blinked in a very long time.

"You won't be doing nothing," she said.

"What will I be doing?"

"Standing here," said Shoko. "So he knows someone's looking. So that when he comes out, the first thing he sees is someone who saw him go."

Gojo did not answer for a while.

"That's a very small job," he said at last.

"Most of the important ones are."

He laughed. It was short and quiet and did not sound like his usual laugh, the bright loud one he used when he wanted a room to look at him. It sounded like the one Shoko had heard on the step of Arrival House, once, the day Senku's father came. *Dead guy came back, saw his kid.*

"You're very wise at three in the morning," said Gojo.

"I'm a doctor. We're all wise at three in the morning. It's the only time anyone listens to us."

* * *

The sky began to grey at about half past four.

It happened slowly, the way it does in summer, so that you do not notice it happening until you look up and the stars are fewer than they were. The wrong morning in the shed and the real morning over the hills began, very slightly, to look like each other. Gojo saw it before Shoko did. She felt him go tense against her shoulder.

"They're matching," he said.

"Is that bad?"

"I don't know." He narrowed his eyes. "I don't know. I hate not knowing."

"You've said."

"I'll keep saying it."

Behind them, the kitchen door opened. Momo came out with a plate in her floury hands and a cloth over it, and stopped on the step above them, and looked down at the two of them sitting there shoulder to shoulder in the dew.

She did not say anything about it. She put the plate down on the step between Shoko's feet and lifted the corner of the cloth. Pear bread, still warm.

"For the people who stayed up," she said. "Not for him. His is inside."

Then she went back in.

Gojo looked at the bread. Then at Shoko.

She tore a piece off and held it out to him.

He took it without looking, the way he had taken the cup. His fingers brushed hers. He did not seem to notice. Or he noticed, and did not say so.

They sat on the step and ate Momo's pear bread and watched the door, and the sky went on getting lighter.

When the dew began to soak through, Gojo carried his chair back inside and set it backward by the outer door, where he could still see the shed through the frame. Shoko took the window bench with her cup. Neither of them said anything else until Mau woke up on the bench with Kaneki's coat over his knees.
