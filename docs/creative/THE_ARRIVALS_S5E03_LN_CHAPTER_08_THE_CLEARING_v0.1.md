# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 15 — The Clearing

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (expanded 2026-10-17 for pacing)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 15
**Continuity state:** Arrival clearing imagery; Mau encounters the fragmented representation of Noise; no outside link is assumed

*When: Day 263, inside the door; subjective duration unmeasured.*

He was lying in the grass.

It was such an exact beginning that he did not move at first.

The grass was cool against the back of his neck. Damp, the way grass is damp in the first hour after dawn, before the sun has decided anything. There was a smell of crushed stems and earth and something faintly sweet that he had never been able to name, the first time, and still could not. A small insect walked across the back of his hand, considered his knuckle, and went on.

There should have been a face above him. Light hair, a controlled voice, somebody trying to decide whether he was dangerous or hurt or simply very inconvenient.

There was sky.

Pale. Enormous. Without a single cloud in it. A sky that did not seem to belong to any particular hour, only to the idea of morning.

He lay there and looked at it for a long time, because the last thing he had seen was Frieren running across the yard with one hand lifted, and he wanted to keep that a moment longer before he let anything else in.

She had been coming. She had decided something. Her mouth had been moving.

He did not know what she had said.

He found that he could bear that. He had not expected to be able to. He held the not-knowing carefully, the way you hold a cup that is too full, and it did not spill.

Mau sat up.

The flowers were there. Close enough that he could see the uneven place where one stem had bent. He knew that bend. Or he thought he did. He had spent so long returning to the beginning in his head that he no longer trusted recognition to tell him which part had been there the first time and which part he had put there since, a little at a time, every time he remembered it.

"Frieren?"

He said it before he could help it.

The word went out into the clearing and did not come back. No echo. No birds startled out of the trees. The clearing simply took it, the way still water takes a stone.

No answer.

*Ciel.*

Nothing he could recognize as her reply. Not silence, exactly. Ciel's silences had a shape: the shape of someone listening, thinking, choosing. This was not that. This was the absence of a room where someone might be.

He waited and tried once more.

*Ciel. If you can hear me, I'm all right. I'm in the clearing. I don't know what it is.*

Nothing.

He stopped before he could turn the silence into certainty that something had happened to her. Ciel was with Rimuru. Rimuru was in the yard. The yard was on the other side of a door that he had walked through on purpose, knowing he would not know what was here. That was the bargain. He had made it with his eyes open. He did not get to be frightened of the part he had agreed to.

He was frightened anyway. He let that be true, and put it beside the cup that had not spilled, and went on.

His feet were bare in the grass.

He looked down at them. He had crossed in his shoes. He remembered tying them badly on the bench in the grey light, and Yuta crouching in front of him and saying, very calmly, *you've got another few minutes to do that properly*, and redoing the left one himself without asking, the way you would for a child, and neither of them saying anything about it.

The shoes were gone. His feet were clean. There was no dew on them, though the grass was wet.

This was not reliable ground for an inventory.

He stood up anyway, slowly, testing it. The ground held. It felt like ground. He took a step toward the edge of the clearing, where the trees began, dark and close and very still.

He walked for what felt like a minute.

The trees did not get closer. They did not get further away. When he looked down, the flowers with the bent stem were at his feet again, exactly where they had been, and the flattened place in the grass where he had been lying was beside them, the shape of his shoulders still pressed into it.

He tried the other direction. The same. He tried walking with his eyes shut, counting steps, the way he had once counted in the white place without knowing he was counting. Thirty steps. He opened his eyes. The flowers. The flattened grass.

So the clearing did not have edges. Or it had them and they all led back here.

He stood and breathed and did not panic, because panicking would have been something to do, and there was nothing here that wanted him to do anything.

Except.

There was a table a little way off.

He had not seen it arrive. It sat where it could not have been hidden by anything, in the open grass between him and the trees, as if it had always been there and he had only just been allowed to notice it. A plain wooden table. Two chairs. And a shape in one of them.

Mau got up slowly. He had not realized he had sat back down.

The shape had shoulders because he was looking for shoulders. Its edges did not stay still when he stopped walking. A head was bent over the table, or something occupying the place a head ought to have been. It was dark, but not the dark of shadow or night. The dark of a gap. The dark of the place in a drawing where someone has rubbed out a line too many times and gone through the paper.

He thought of the dark in his own shadow, where Ear slept, where the wheel slept, and did not decide they were the same thing merely because both were dark.

He walked toward the table. This time the distance closed.

The empty chair was on the near side.

He touched its back. Wood under his palm. He could feel a small splinter that should not have mattered here, catching very slightly at his skin, and he was absurdly grateful to it. It was the first thing in the clearing that had pushed back.

He sat.

The shape did not look up. It sat with whatever it had for hands resting on the table, very still, the way something sits when it has been waiting so long that waiting has become its whole posture.

Then:

`let—me—out`

The words did not come from a mouth.

They came from everywhere at once, and from inside his own chest, and from the grain of the table under his hands. Broken. Each word arriving a little after it should have, as if it had to be assembled first from parts that did not fit.

Mau's hands went cold on the table.

He had heard that before.

Under the wall, in the plaza, the day the seal broke and went back up. And again since, at the worst moments, when something behind his breastbone pressed and pressed and said it in a voice that was not quite a voice. *Let me out.* He had never known who was saying it. He had been afraid, for a long time, that it was him.

"Who are you?" he said.

The shape moved. Its edges dragged after it, refusing to arrive at the same time. A shoulder came first. Then the place where a head should be, turning toward him. Then, a long moment later, the rest.

`what—you—call`

It stopped so long that he thought that was all he would get. The flowers moved in a wind he could not feel.

`noise`

He looked at the table between them. Then past it, at the flowers, and the flattened grass, and the place where he had been lying.

He waited for fear to tell him what to do. There was plenty of it. None of it was new enough to be useful. He had been afraid of this thing for so long that the fear had worn smooth, like a stone carried in a pocket.

"The Noise," he said slowly.

The shape did not nod. It did not do anything.

"Then we're inside me," he said.

The shape did not answer.

"This is—" He looked round at the clearing that had no edges. At the sky that belonged to no hour. "This is where the things are. What the walls are holding. Everything Ciel locked away. Everything Gojo taught me to keep in." He looked back at the shape. "You're on the other side of all of it."

Its head lifted.

For the first time he had the uncomfortable impression that it was trying to see him, and did not know which of the things in front of it counted as his face.

`protect—you`

It said the two words with something like anger.

Not at him. He understood that slowly, the way you understand the meaning of a word in a language you are just beginning to learn. The anger was not pointed at him. It was pointed at everything else. At whatever it thought he needed protecting from.

Mau kept his hands on the table. The splinter was still there under his palm. He pressed against it, gently, to be sure.

"We should talk about that," he said.
