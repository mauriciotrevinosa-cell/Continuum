# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 19 — Protect

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 19
**Continuity state:** Noise is revealed as a constructed defence mechanism; agreement concerns control, not ending construction, adaptation or leaks

*When: Day 263, inside the door; subjective duration unmeasured.*

`protect—you`

It said it again when he did not answer quickly enough.

Mau looked at the dark hands on the table. They did not have fingers until he looked at the places where fingers ought to have been. He remembered his own hand closing around Frieren's, outside, without waiting for him to decide how tightly.

"From what?"

The space behind the shape changed.

Not a room he could enter. Edges, impressions, the wrong pressure against a boundary. Something trying to find the next thing it needed. He could not count it. He had the sudden, sick understanding that counting had never been the important part.

`hurts`

"Some of it does."

`stop—hurts`

"By doing what?"

The shape leaned forward. He saw the movement begin in its whole outline, as if no part of it had learned that a body could move one thing at a time.

`move—you`

"Away."

`safe`

He remembered the half-step in the yard. The first crossing at the weekly gate, not knowing he had begun to walk. Coming back into his own awareness because Frieren had him.

"You can't make that choice without me."

The edges of the table tightened under his palms. He was not sure whether the pressure was happening or being shown to him. He kept his hands there.

`protect`

"Look at what happens. My hands shake. My body tries to do things it can't hold. They have to get out of the way. People I love are frightened to come near me, and I'm frightened to come near them."

The shape became very still.

"You aren't protecting me from that. You're part of how it happens."

There was no satisfaction in saying it. He felt as though he had accused his own lungs of needing air in the wrong place.

`can't—stop`

It broke apart at the shoulder and came together badly.

`building`

Mau looked at it for a long time.

There was no stranger's face inside the dark. No second life waiting to tell him which of his memories had been stolen. What sat across from him was a thing he had made without knowing how to ask for it: find what was needed, learn it, make the body able to survive. Again. Again, even when the attempt to survive became the danger.

Construction had made a way to protect the thing doing the constructing.

One of its first answers. Before he had learned how to give an answer of his own.

"I made you," he said.

The shape did not look pleased or offended. It leaned toward him, waiting for the next condition.

"You're not somebody trapped in there. You're what my body kept trying to do."

`protect—you`

This time the words were almost quiet.

He put one hand nearer the centre of the table.

"You did. When I didn't know what was happening. When I couldn't tell what I needed. You did your job."

He had expected that to be harder to say. It was harder to go on.

"But I have to be allowed to need things you can't build. I have to be allowed to go to people, even if being near them is frightening. And if I'm going to get hurt, I have to know what I'm choosing."

The dark shook once.

`not—safe`

"No."

He looked past it at the flowers.

"I'm going to fall over. Get things wrong. Hurt. Get up again. I don't want you to stop trying to help me do that. I want you to stop deciding that the answer is to take me away from everybody before I can say anything."

There was no answer for so long that his hands began to ache on the table.

"Trust me," he said. "We're the same thing. Let me think. Let me tell you what matters."

`you—went`

The pressure behind it shifted. For a moment Mau knew exactly which movement it meant: his own feet passing the frame while the people he loved remained on the other side.

He had asked to choose and immediately supplied the defence with a choice that frightened it.

"Yes," he said. "I did."

`not—safe`

"No. I was trying to keep Ori safe. That's something you can't work out by moving my body farther from a thing that can hurt it."

The shape leaned toward him and stopped. Its hand came apart above the table. He waited while it found the edge of itself again.

"I don't know whether coming here was right," he said. "If you need me to prove that before you let me choose, we'll never get anywhere."

The flowers stirred without a wind he could feel.

He wanted the shape to agree quickly. He wanted to get up, find the way out, turn this place into something he had finished successfully. He recognized the desire well enough not to mistake it for an answer.

"I'm asking you to leave room for me to be wrong," he said more quietly. "And still be the one who has to live with it. With them."

It made no reply.

He stayed in the chair.

The shape turned its head toward the shifting pressure behind it.

`can't—stop—building`

"I heard you. I'm not asking you to."

`body—changes`

"I know."

`still—comes—through`

He took a breath. There was grass under his bare feet. He could feel every bent blade against his skin.

"Then we keep dealing with what comes through. We don't pretend it's safe because we've talked. I tell them. We find a way to hold it."

He waited until the shape turned back.

"But you don't take my choices away to protect me."

The dark at its shoulder settled. Not into skin. Into an edge that no longer had to drag itself into place.

`trust`

Mau nodded.

`you—choose`

He let out the breath he had been holding.

"Thank you."

The empty chair was in front of him before he noticed the shape had gone.

He kept his hand on the table a moment longer.

Then he looked around for a way home.
