# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 20 — Minute Three

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 20
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. Outside, in the first minutes after the crossing: Ciel's thread to Mau goes quiet inside Rimuru; Fern counts aloud because counting is what she can do; Yuta keeps Rimuru standing; Ori keeps the ribbon; no outside link to Mau and no new power

*When: Day 263, the first minutes after Mau crosses. The yard. The frame is only the back wall of a shed again.*

The thread went quiet at the exact moment the light closed.

Rimuru felt it the way you feel a step that is not there in the dark. One moment there was a line running out of him, thin and sure, the way it had run for a very long time now: from Ciel, through him, to the place behind Mau's breastbone where she lived her other half. He had stopped noticing it years ago, the way you stop noticing your own heartbeat. And the next moment there was nothing at the end of it.

Not cut. Not broken. He would have known broken. Just nothing. As if the line went on out into the dark and the dark had no bottom.

He stood very still in the middle of the yard.

*Ciel*, he said.

*I am here*, said Ciel. Very close. Closer than she usually came. *I am still here, with you.*

*And him?*

There was a pause. Ciel did not pause.

*I cannot reach him*, she said. *I am still reaching. There is nothing to hold.*

Rimuru's hands had closed in his coat pockets. He took them out. They were shaking. He put them back.

"Rimuru," said Yuta, beside him. Quietly.

"The thread's gone quiet," said Rimuru. His voice came out flat and strange. "Not cut. Quiet. She can't feel him."

Yuta did not say anything. He moved half a step closer, so that his shoulder was against Rimuru's. That was all. He did not put his arm round him or tell him it would be all right or ask what it meant. He just stood there, close, the way he had stood on the roof in the snow the night the three of them made their rule, and let Rimuru lean on him if he wanted to.

Rimuru did not lean. But he did not move away either.

* * *

"One," said Fern.

She was standing at the edge of the yard, by the kitchen door, with the ledger open in her arms. She was not looking at the shed. She was looking at the line the sun made on the stones as it came over the hills, very slowly, the way she had looked at it in the intake hall on the day of Coco's mother.

"One minute," said Fern.

Nobody asked her why. Nobody needed to. It was what she could do. It was the only thing in the yard that anyone could do.

The house did not move.

Most of it was still where it had been when he crossed. Momo at the kitchen door, flour on her hands. Maomao on the step beside her open bag. Coco with her mother. The fort at the common-room window, a row of hoods with their noses against the glass. Bocchi on the step with her guitar held against her chest, Kita behind her. Senku in the middle of the yard where he had stood up too fast, with Byakuya's hand on his shoulder. Gojo by the shed, bare-eyed, looking at the back wall as if he could stare a hole through it.

And Frieren.

Frieren had not moved at all. She was standing where she had stopped, a few paces from the rope, with her hand lowered at her side now and her hair loose round her face. She was looking at the wall. She was not crying. Her face was very still, the way it went when she was holding something so carefully that she could not afford to feel its weight yet.

Nobody went to her. Not yet. Not even Maki, who was watching her from the bench with the expression of someone measuring a distance.

"Two," said Fern. "Two minutes."

* * *

Ori had picked up the ribbon.

She did not remember doing it. She was standing by the bench where she had been sitting with him, and the red ribbon had been in the grass at her feet, and now it was in her hands. She was winding it round her fingers. Round and round. The way she wound it round Milim's ankle at night so that Milim would not float into the ceiling.

Milim was beside her. Floating, a hand's breadth above the dew, forgetting not to. Milim's face had gone very small and very fierce, the way it went before she broke something.

"I could break it," Milim said, very low. "The wall. I could."

"He said no," said Ori.

"I know he said no."

"Then don't."

Milim's fists closed. Then opened again. She drifted down until her feet were on the grass. She took Ori's free hand, the one that was not winding the ribbon, and held it so tightly that it hurt, and Ori let her.

"He told me first," said Ori. To nobody. To the wall. "Before. He told me in the fort. Before anything. Like he promised."

"I know," said Milim.

"He kept it."

"I know."

Ori wound the ribbon round her fingers again. Round. Round.

"I still don't like it," she said.

* * *

"Three," said Fern. "Three minutes."

Rimuru closed his eyes.

*Ciel.*

*Still nothing*, said Ciel, before he could ask. *I am still reaching.* A pause. *Rimuru. I want to tell you something, and I want you to hear it properly.*

*Tell me.*

*In the Trial,* said Ciel, *I could not reach him either. Not for five hours and forty-one minutes. I could only get one number through, at the beginning of each death. That was all.* Her voice was very level. *And he came back.*

Rimuru did not say anything.

*I am not saying that he will come back,* said Ciel. *I do not know that. I do not tell you things I do not know.* A pause. *I am saying that I have been here before. Reaching for him, and finding nothing to hold. And I did not stop reaching. And I am not going to stop now.*

Rimuru opened his eyes.

The wall of the shed. The cobweb with the drop of dew in it. Frieren, not moving. Gojo, not blinking. Fern, counting. Ori, winding a ribbon round her fingers. Yuta's shoulder against his.

"Okay," said Rimuru, aloud. To Ciel. To the yard. "Okay. We keep reaching."

Yuta looked at him.

"Ciel says she's been here before," said Rimuru. "In the Trial. She couldn't reach him then either. And she didn't stop." He swallowed. "So we don't stop."

Yuta was quiet for a moment.

"We weren't going to stop," he said.

"I know," said Rimuru. "I just wanted to hear somebody say it."

Yuta almost smiled. It did not quite reach his face.

"Then I'll say it," he said. "We don't stop."

"Four," said Fern. "Four minutes."

Across the yard, Maki got up off the bench and walked over to stand beside Frieren. She did not say anything. She did not touch her. She just stood there, a pace to her left, looking at the same wall.

And Frieren, without turning her head, said something to her, very quietly, that nobody else in the yard could hear.

Fern kept counting.
