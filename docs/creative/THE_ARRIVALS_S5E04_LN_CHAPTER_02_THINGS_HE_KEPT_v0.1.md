# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 17 — Things He Kept

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 17
**Continuity state:** Mau relives witnessed S1–S2 relationship memories; these are not access to Frieren's private past

*When: Day 263, inside the door.*

The table was gone when he stood.

He was looking at the common room of the old inn.

The first thing he recognized was a patch in the window. He had forgotten the exact shape of it. A bit too much light came through one edge and made a pale mark on the floor where it should not have been.

Frieren was sitting beside the dying fire with a book she was not reading.

He could see himself watching her.

That was wrong. He had only ever been inside that body, waiting to be told which question was too much. Now he was standing near the hearth and feeling the same uncertainty at once.

He knew what she would say. Still, when she closed the book, he waited.

*I was thinking about Fern.*

The old room held its old silences. He knew how little of himself he had to offer then. No story from home, no useful memory of a time when he had made somebody feel better. He had sat closer because she was alone, and stopped before touching her because he did not know if that would help.

She had talked about the windows. What Fern would have complained about. What she should have seen sooner.

He watched her hand go to the ornament.

It hurt to see it when he already knew Fern was alive. He wanted to tell this Frieren that she would hear Fern correcting her again, that there would be a house too full for silence and that she would complain about being made to wake up in it.

He could not put anything into the scene that he had not brought there the first time.

The young man by the fire said he did not know how to fix it.

Frieren looked at him.

Then came the promise with no method inside it. Things would be okay. He was there to help her.

Mau had spent so long being ashamed of promises he could not guarantee. He watched this one arrive in a room where it had not fixed anything either.

She leaned against him anyway.

He remembered how carefully he had put his arm around her. The surprise of her fingers gripping his shirt. Her shoulders taking so long to stop holding themselves up.

Then the tears.

He stood beside the hearth until the wood settled, though the part of him in the chair could not know he was standing there.

The next room was brighter.

Chocolate on a plate. Fern's face. Frieren with the extraordinary dignity she reserved for things she would later insist she had not done.

He laughed before the memory reached the part where everyone else laughed.

For a moment he missed being that confused. Then he remembered how lonely confusion could be and let himself be glad he was no longer in it.

A cold edge of water touched his foot.

The lake. Her beside it, with all the distance they had each made by trying to give the other something neither had asked for. He wanted to reach into that evening and move the two of them nearer by the shoulders.

He did not. He watched them find their own way.

The scene changed before he could decide whether he had been waiting for it to change.

Banners. Uneven light. The inn so crowded with people doing unnecessary things beautifully that he had to turn sideways to carry what somebody had handed him.

Their festival.

Momo was calling for something. It was not the last time she would call for him that evening. He could see himself go, then stop because Frieren had spoken.

*Festival,* the other Mau said, as if that explained why he was agreeing to everything.

She nearly accepted it.

He remembered wanting another year before he had learned how to ask for that without thinking it was too much.

Music began. Bocchi in the corner, Kita near her. Somebody laughed out of time with the song. Frieren looked toward the sound and then back at him, and the scene gave him the plainest thing it had kept: she had wanted him there, too.

The memories did not keep to a scale he could measure. A few seconds at the fire lasted longer than an evening. A kiss and the words after Sukuna were so immediate that he had to put a hand to his chest, then found no wound under it.

He heard her say she loved him.

He heard the answer he had given and remembered why a room full of people later looked as though they might throw something at him.

He had loved her before he understood what he had failed to ask.

The scene moved on to the choice they eventually said aloud, and he stayed until both of them had answered it.

None of this showed him a road out.

It kept showing him people staying in rooms together after the important words had been said.
