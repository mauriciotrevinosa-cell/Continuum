# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 22 — A Door

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** S3–S4 lived memories lead to last night's corridor; exit is not a learned universal portal technique

*When: Day 263, inside the door, followed by return to the exterior morning.*

There was mud on the boardwalk.

Mau stepped over the same warped edge he had stepped over while carrying water, and the room ahead of him was the cheap first fitting of the life they had built afterward.

The Muslin.

He could hear Frieren coughing before he reached it.

He had forgotten how much she complained. Not about the illness. About the soup, the blanket, the tea, the fact that he was standing in the wrong place to hold the book.

He watched himself accept each new instruction with the delighted concentration of somebody who had been given an important job.

"You were enjoying it," he told the other Mau.

The other Mau did not answer. He was carrying a jug and trying very hard to remember walking to fill it.

Frieren said she liked hearing him on the stairs.

The sound stayed while the walls changed.

A larger room. The reading chair he had said no to. The shelf nobody could defend except by insisting it had always belonged where it was. Ori's door, no longer a distance somebody had measured for her. A red ribbon, a window, the small cars whose histories were somebody else's memory but whose place on the sill had become his own.

He knew these rooms with his eyes closed.

That did not keep the next memory from hurting.

Frieren's face in his neck. His own voice admitting what he had still believed after Sukuna, even after he had apologized for deciding alone. Her being alive would not have been enough if he had made her survive him.

Both of us.

He stayed with that memory longer than he wanted. He did not ask it to turn into a different one.

Then there were other mornings. Her cold foot against his shin. One. Still here. Sometimes Ori's voice from her own side of the door. Two.

There was laughter he could not place until he saw the roof. There were the five flowers on the shelf. There was Frieren holding a book open at a page he had asked her to explain, and her annoyance when he knew where her explanation was going before she finished.

Not every memory was a rescue. He was relieved by how many of them had no reason to be important.

Her stealing something from his plate. Him knowing she was going to and moving the plate nearer. An argument that ended without being solved because one of them had fallen asleep.

The hand on the back of his neck while she read.

He had been waiting for a big moment to tell him what to do. The rooms kept offering smaller ones.

Then he was in the corridor.

The lamp by the stair was low. He could see himself sitting against the east-room door with his knees drawn up a little, talking toward somebody he was not allowed to see.

Last night.

He knew where the knots in the wood were. He knew which side of the door she had sat on. He could not see her, because he had not seen her then.

He heard her ask him not to come in.

He heard himself agree.

The Mau on the floor got up and put his hand against the door before going away. Mau stood where he had been sitting and tried to remember what he had expected a way out to look like.

A landscape. A voice explaining the rule. Somebody with a task he could finish correctly enough to be released.

He put his hand on the latch.

There was no reason to believe this image was a door he could use. He wanted to open it anyway. Not to take last night away from her. To get back to the morning she had said she would come to.

"I'm coming home," he said.

The door opened.

There was no bedroom beyond it.

For one moment there was air under both his feet.

Then there was only air.

* * *

The cold hit his face hard enough to take the breath out of him.

Mau tried to find the ground and found sky instead. His stomach seemed to have stayed wherever the room was. He could not make his arms do anything useful.

A voice reached him through the rush of air.

"So it was a door to your heart. How interesting."

He did not have enough breath to answer.

He shut his eyes.
