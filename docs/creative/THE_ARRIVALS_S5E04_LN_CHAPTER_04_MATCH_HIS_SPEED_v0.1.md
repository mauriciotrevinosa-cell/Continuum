# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 19 — Match His Speed (Part 1)

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (expanded 2026-10-17 for pacing)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 19 (part 1)
**Continuity state:** The household sees Mau falling; Senku directs a matching descent; Milim catches without a violent interception

*When: Day 263, the same morning, a few minutes after the crossing. Nobody in the yard has gone back inside.*

Frieren had not moved far from the shed.

She knew she should. People needed the path. Momo had to get back to the kitchen; the fort had to be told something, anything, by someone who was not frightened; Ori was standing in the sun with her ribbon in the grass at her feet, and somebody ought to pick it up and ask whether she wanted water. And Mau had been gone for several minutes that had not been kind enough to contain anything she could do.

She kept looking at the place where the frame had been.

It was only a wall now. The back of a shed. Rough boards that Wakana had planed badly in the winter and meant to replace. A cobweb in the corner with a single drop of dew in it. She had looked at this wall a hundred times without seeing it. Now she could not look at anything else.

Behind her, the yard was very quiet. Not silent. A house this full could not be silent. Someone was crying, softly, by the kitchen door; Frieren thought it was Momo and did not turn round to be sure. Someone else was talking in a low voice to the fort through the common-room window. Byakuya was saying something to Senku that she could not hear, and Senku was not answering, and Byakuya kept saying it anyway, gently, the way you keep talking to someone in the dark so they know where you are.

Nobody was telling her to move. She was grateful for that, and ashamed of being grateful, and too tired to decide which.

Maki came and stood beside her.

Did not say anything. Did not touch her. Just stood there, a pace to her left, looking at the same wall.

Frieren was beginning to hate how grateful she was when people did that.

"I told him not to come in," she said.

Maki looked at her.

"Last night," said Frieren. "Through the door. I told him not to come in. And he didn't. And then I was going to tell him—" She stopped. "And now the last thing he heard me say on purpose was *don't come in*."

"He heard the rest of what you said, too," said Maki. "All night. I was there for some of it. He sat outside that door for a long time."

"That isn't the same."

"No," Maki agreed. "But it isn't nothing."

Frieren tried to hold on to that and could not quite find a place to put her hands.

Ori had come up on her other side without anyone seeing her move. She had picked up the ribbon. She was holding it in both hands, very tight.

"He saw you," said Ori.

Frieren looked down at her.

"When you came out of the house. He saw you." Ori did not look away from the wall. "I was right there. He smiled. Not the face he makes when he's pretending. The real one." Her voice was very steady and very small. "He knew you'd come."

Frieren did not say anything. She could not.

She put her hand on Ori's head. Ori let her.
