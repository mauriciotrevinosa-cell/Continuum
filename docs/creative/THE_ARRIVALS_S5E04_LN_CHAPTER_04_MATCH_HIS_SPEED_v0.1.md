# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 19 — Match His Speed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 19
**Continuity state:** The household sees Mau falling; Senku directs a matching descent; Milim catches without a violent interception

*When: Day 263, the same morning, a few minutes after the crossing.*

Frieren had not moved far from the shed.

She knew she should. People needed the path. Ori was standing in the sun, and somebody ought to ask whether she wanted water, and Mau had been gone for several minutes that had not been kind enough to contain anything she could do.

She kept looking at the place where the frame had been.

Maki came beside her. Did not say anything. Frieren was beginning to hate how grateful she was when people did that.

"I told him not to come in," she said.

Maki looked at her.

"Last night."

"He heard the rest of what you said, too."

Frieren tried to hold on to that and could not quite find a place to put her hands.

Gojo turned his head.

The movement was small. Everybody close to him noticed it because he had been looking at the same part of the yard for so long.

He looked up.

"That's him."

Milim was in the air before Frieren found the shape against the sky.

"Milim!" Senku shouted.

"I'm helping!"

"I know. Above him. Match his fall before you take his weight. Don't hit him on the way up."

She changed direction in the air, already climbing.

"Same speed," he called. "Then slow both of you. Go!"

Frieren had begun to rise without noticing. She stopped because she could see Milim now, a small bright shape going past the darker one instead of toward it.

Mau was turning. Not deliberately. His body had no useful shape for the fall, one arm away from his side, his hair pulled back from his face.

She could not reach him before Milim.

She watched instead.

Milim came down above him, fast enough that for a terrible moment there seemed to be no distance left in which she could change anything. Then the distance between them stopped shrinking so quickly. She turned with him, put her arms under him, and kept going down.

Together.

Only then did she begin to slow.

The leaves over Frieren's head moved in the air displaced by the descent. She realized she had been holding her staff so hard her hand hurt.

Beside her, Senku let out a breath between his teeth.

Nobody cheered. Milim was still carrying him. Frieren would not let the sound of relief arrive ahead of what she could see.

They came down inside the yard, clear of the tree and the people waiting beneath it. Milim's feet touched first. She bent her knees a little as she landed, not from the weight so much as from the carefulness of it.

Mau's head rested against her shoulder.

His eyes were closed.

"He's breathing," Milim said before anybody could ask.

Frieren reached them with Shoko beside her. Shoko put two fingers against Mau's neck. Milim stood absolutely still.

"Mau," Shoko said. "Can you hear me?"

His mouth moved.

Frieren leaned nearer without meaning to.

"I liked the stairs better," he said.

Her knees stopped holding her quite properly. She caught Milim's sleeve and then found Mau looking at her.

He had enough of himself back to smile.
