# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 23 — Match His Speed (Part 2)

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (expanded 2026-10-17 for pacing)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 23 (part 2)
**Continuity state:** The household sees Mau falling; Senku directs a matching descent; Milim catches without a violent interception

*When: Day 263, the same minutes. The sky over the north field.*

Gojo turned his head.

The movement was small. Everybody close to him noticed it only because he had been looking at the same part of the yard for so long, standing well clear of where the frame had been, hands in his pockets, glasses down, as still as Frieren had ever seen him. And now his head turned. Not toward the shed. Upward.

He pushed his glasses up into his hair.

His bare eyes, sky-coloured, went to a point in the sky above the north field, high, higher than the ink tree, higher than the ridge. A point where there was nothing.

Shoko saw his face and put her cup down on the bench.

"Satoru?"

He did not answer her. He was looking. The way he looked at things that nobody else could see yet. His whole body had gone very still in a different way, a sharper way, the way a bowstring goes still.

"That's him," said Gojo.

Frieren's head snapped up.

She could not see anything. Grey sky, paling to gold in the east. A single bird very far off. Nothing else.

"Where—"

Milim was already in the air.

She did not run. She did not crouch. She was standing on the arm of the bench, and then she was not, and there was a sound like a sheet being snapped out to dry, and a gust of wind that knocked Coco's hat off and flattened the grass in a ring, and Milim was a small bright shape going up, fast, faster than anything had any right to go up, toward the point Gojo was looking at.

Toward the speck that Frieren could see now. Just. A dark fleck against the gold, very high, getting larger.

Falling.

"*Milim!*" Senku shouted.

He had come off the bench so fast that he had knocked his father's arm, and the paper with the columns about the door had gone flying across the dew. He was standing in the middle of the yard with his head back and his hands cupped round his mouth.

"*I'm helping!*" Milim's voice came down thin from very high up.

"*I know!*" Senku's voice cracked. He did not seem to care. "*Listen to me! If you go straight up at him, you'll hit him! You're going up and he's coming down, you'll meet in the middle at both your speeds added together, and that'll kill him! Do you understand? You'll kill him!*"

The bright shape faltered. Swung wide.

"*Go above him!*" Senku shouted. "*Get above him! Then turn round and fall with him! Match his speed! Same speed, same direction, so there's no difference between you! Then take his weight! Then slow down! Both of you, together, slowly! Not all at once!*" He was out of breath. He took one more. "*Match his speed, then slow down! Go!*"

For a moment nobody in the yard breathed.

Then, very high up, the bright shape changed course. It went wide of the falling speck, out to one side, and up, past it, above it. And turned.

Frieren had begun to rise without noticing.

Her feet had left the grass. She was a hand's breadth up, then two, the staff already in her hand, her body already doing what it had done for a thousand years when something she loved was falling. She stopped. Not because she decided to. Because she could see Milim now, clearly, a small bright shape going past the darker one instead of toward it, and she understood, in the part of her that did arithmetic without asking, that she was too slow. Milim was faster than anything. Frieren was not. If she went up now, she would only be one more thing in the air for Milim to avoid.

She could not reach him before Milim.

She came back down onto the grass. It was the hardest thing she had done all morning. Harder than opening the door.

She watched instead.

Mau was turning as he fell. Not deliberately. His body had no useful shape for the fall: one arm away from his side, his legs loose, his hair pulled straight back from his face by the wind. He was not trying to do anything. He was not using anything. No light round him. No wheel. Nothing at all coming out of him to slow him down. Just a person, falling, the way a person falls.

That was the worst part. Frieren had seen him do impossible things. She had never seen him do nothing.

Milim came down above him.

Fast. Fast enough that for a terrible moment there seemed to be no distance left in which she could change anything, and Frieren heard someone behind her make a sound, and thought it might have been herself. Then the distance between them stopped shrinking so quickly. Milim was not diving at him any more. She was falling with him. The same speed. The same direction. Two shapes, one bright, one dark, side by side in the gold air, and the gap between them closing slowly, slowly, a hand's breadth at a time.

She turned with him as he turned.

She reached out.

She put her arms under him, one under his knees and one under his shoulders, the way Gojo had carried him across the plaza once. And held on. And kept going down.

Together.

Only then did she begin to slow.

Not all at once. Senku had said not all at once, and Milim, who had never once in her life done anything not all at once, did not do it all at once. She slowed the way you let out a rope, a little and then a little more. The two of them came down out of the gold sky over the north field, over the ridge of the roof, over the ink tree, slower and slower, until the leaves over Frieren's head moved in the air pushed down by their descent, and she realized she had been holding her staff so hard that her hand hurt.

Beside her, Senku let out a breath between his teeth. His hands were shaking. Byakuya had come up behind him and put one big hand on his son's shoulder, and Senku did not shake it off.

Nobody cheered.

Milim was still carrying him. Frieren would not let the sound of relief arrive ahead of what she could see.

They came down inside the yard, clear of the tree and the people waiting beneath it, on the open stones by the well. Milim's feet touched first. She bent her knees a little as she landed, not from the weight so much as from the carefulness of it, the way you set down something that might spill.

Mau's head rested against her shoulder.

His eyes were closed.

"He's breathing," said Milim, before anybody could ask. Her voice was very high and very fast. "He's breathing, I can feel it, he's breathing, I didn't hit him, I matched, I did what Senku said, he's *breathing*."

Frieren was already there. She did not remember crossing the yard. Shoko was beside her with her bag. Shoko put two fingers against the side of Mau's neck, under his jaw, and looked at nothing, counting.

Milim stood absolutely still. She did not put him down. She did not seem to know she was still holding him. Her arms were locked.

"Mau," said Shoko. "Can you hear me?"

Nothing.

"Mau."

His mouth moved.

Frieren leaned nearer without meaning to. Close enough to feel his breath. It was warm. Too warm.

"I liked the stairs better," he said.

Her knees stopped holding her quite properly. She caught Milim's sleeve, and then found Mau looking at her, his eyes open now, grey and tired and very far away and coming closer.

He had enough of himself back to smile.

Not the face he made when he was pretending. Ori had been right. The real one.
