# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 24 — Hot

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** Frieren offers an exit from her promise; Mau affirms choice; Ori's fear persists; fever requires ordinary care

*When: Day 263, morning after the return.*

Milim looked down at him with increasing suspicion.

"You're hot."

"Tell me something I don't know."

Shoko's fingers were still at his neck. "Unfortunately, she means your temperature."

"Oh."

The attempt to smile made his teeth knock together.

Frieren put her hand against his cheek. He was looking at her as though she had been the difficult one to find.

"You came," he said.

She wanted to tell him she had tried to. The sentence would not fit past everything else.

"Yes."

Shoko let her touch his face while she checked his eyes. Then she asked Milim to lower him to the bench. Frieren went with them, staying close enough that he could reach her without lifting his arm.

He did reach. His hand caught in her sleeve.

She bent and kissed him. It was not a careful answer to anything she had planned to say. When she pulled away, she could hear herself breathing.

"I believe you," she said.

His fingers tightened.

"About choosing. I believe you. But you have to tell me if that changes. If it begins to feel like something you're doing because I asked, and you want to stop. You have to tell me. I'll listen."

He watched her face.

"I promise."

She kept looking until he stopped trying to make the promise easier with his expression.

"I don't expect to get bored of you," he said.

"I'm very interesting."

"You're also in the way," Shoko said. "Briefly."

Frieren moved enough to let her work. She did not move her hand out of his.

Ori was a little way behind Maki, with her mouth pressed into a straight line. Mau saw her and lifted his free arm.

"See? I told you I'd be—"

He stopped at the look she gave him.

"You're back," she said.

"I'm back."

"That's the part I wanted."

He nodded. She came into the space beside the bench and put her arms around him carefully enough that he tried to tell her she did not need to be so careful.

Shoko said that actually she did.

Ori held him carefully.

He shivered again. His shirt was damp at the back. Frieren had been looking at his face so hard she had not noticed how unsteady the rest of him was.

"You can tell us later," Rimuru said.

Mau looked at him. "I talked to—"

"Later."

There was no suggestion that later meant never. He let it go.

They took him to the clinic first. Not to repair a technique or decide he was safe because something extraordinary had returned him in one piece. Shoko wanted pulse, breath, temperature, answers she could ask him twice and compare.

He complained that he seemed to have caught something in a place that had not contained anybody else.

"You're assuming that explains the fever," Maomao said.

"It was a good complaint."

"Keep it. We may find a use for it."

Frieren sat beside the bed while they worked. She could hear Rimuru outside answering what he could and saying he did not know the rest. Yuta brought a blanket and waited until Shoko decided it would help rather than adding it on top of everything.

Nothing burst. Nothing began shining under Mau's skin.

His forehead stayed hot.

After they had watched him long enough to agree that carrying him upstairs would not improve the mystery by adding a fall, Yuta and Rimuru helped. Milim offered to carry him the whole way and accepted the job of taking the blanket instead.

Frieren went up ahead and opened their door.

The cups were still where Maki had left them. His book was on the side table, with his place almost certainly lost. The room looked like a place people had left in a hurry and meant to come back to.

Mau saw it and closed his eyes.

"There," he said.

She helped him onto the bed. Shoko checked him again, gave instructions, and promised to come back without making Frieren ask how soon.

When they were alone for a moment, he lifted the blanket on the other side.

"You too."

She climbed in without asking if he was certain. She had spent the whole night giving him that question in different words.

His hand found the back of her head.

"I heard you," he said.

"When?"

"Last night. All of it."

She put her face against his neck, very carefully, because he was hot and shaking and here.
