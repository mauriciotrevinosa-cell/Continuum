# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 21 — The First Soup

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 21
**Continuity state:** Frieren takes a turn caring for Mau; fever and guardrails remain; care is shared rather than another household exclusion

*When: Day 263, afternoon and evening.*

The first soup was too thin.

Frieren tasted it in the kitchen, frowned, and looked into the pot as if somebody had removed an essential part while she was not watching.

Momo looked in with her.

"More water?" Frieren asked.

"You already did that."

"He's supposed to have fluids."

"He can have them in a cup."

Frieren put down the ladle. She had followed most of the instructions and modified the rest for medical reasons she was beginning to suspect would not survive Maomao hearing them.

"It was good before," she said.

"Before you made it twice as large. Yes."

Momo handed her the knife and a second board. She did not take over the whole task. Frieren appreciated that enough to obey when she was told which pieces were small enough and which still needed cutting.

Ram came through carrying a stack of cups. "Has the patient complained?"

"Not yet," Momo said.

"How considerate of him."

"He hasn't seen it."

Ram considered the pot and took her cups away.

Upstairs, Mau was sleeping. Frieren knew because she had checked before coming down and Shoko had promised to stay until she returned. She still wanted to carry the pot up whole so she would not have to come back for more.

Momo gave her a bowl instead.

"You can leave some food downstairs. He isn't going to eat it all at once."

"He might wake up hungry."

"Then we have stairs."

Frieren looked at her.

Momo looked back until Frieren picked up the bowl.

Mau was awake when she opened the east-room door. Shoko was sitting in the reading chair, apparently quite comfortable despite the position of the shelf. He looked at the bowl, then at Frieren's face, with a caution he did not usually apply to food.

"What?" she asked.

"Nothing."

"Momo helped."

"That was not my question."

"You didn't ask a question."

"I was being polite."

Shoko stood. "I'm going to eat something that hasn't been prescribed. Call if anything changes."

She made Mau answer the last part, not Frieren. He nodded, and said it aloud when she waited.

Then they were alone with the soup.

Frieren set it on the side table, where it was meant to be instead of on a stack of books. Mau noticed the choice and smiled.

"Sit up a little," she said.

"I can hold it."

"I know."

She waited while he got his shoulder against the pillow. He was still clumsy with tiredness, and the hand he reached out with shook enough that he stopped and took a breath before trying again.

She handed him the bowl when he was ready.

He tasted it.

She watched him taste it.

"It's soup," he said.

"Yes."

"Unmistakably."

"Mau."

"It's good. I was considering my response in light of your five-soup standards."

She sat on the bed beside him. "I only complained about the first four."

"That's almost all of them."

"You improved."

He laughed and had to put the bowl down. She took it without comment until the shivering stopped.

It was harder when he was not making jokes. She could bring soup, change the water, ask whether he wanted the window shut. None of those things explained the heat under his skin.

"Ciel?" he asked quietly.

Frieren waited for him to choose what to repeat.

"She's still watching. Nothing new that she can identify. That doesn't mean it's stopped."

"I know."

He took another spoonful. She watched him swallow it and only then discovered how hungry she was herself.

He pushed the bowl a little toward her.

"You need some."

"That's yours."

"There are stairs."

She narrowed her eyes at him.

He tried not to look pleased with himself.

After they had both eaten, Yuta knocked to ask whether they needed anything before he went up to the roof.

"It's tonight?" Mau asked.

"It still is."

For a moment Mau tried to count the night before as part of the proper week. Then he gave up. He did not have to climb anything to keep the night from being lost.

"Tell them I'm being forced to listen to a book."

"Willingly," Frieren said.

Yuta said he would report both versions. Maki came later with the cracker tin; the ordinary evening at Seiko's room could go on without Frieren leaving the bed. Frieren kept two pieces and sent the rest back. Rimuru would come to his loft when he was ready to sleep.

She read to Mau from the book he had lost his place in. He did not point that out. She began at a passage she liked, which turned out to require a great deal of explanation before the passage made sense.

His eyes closed halfway through.

"You're not listening."

"I'm listening horizontally."

She put the book against his shoulder and let him fall asleep before she started the next explanation.
