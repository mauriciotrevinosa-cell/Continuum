# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 22 — Five

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** Fever care continues; Rimuru's loft remains his sleeping place; shared help and ordinary humour replace a second crisis

*When: Day 264, the next morning and afternoon.*

When Mau woke, there were three cups beside the bed and no suitable way to ask which one was for drinking.

Frieren was asleep against his uninjured and otherwise unremarkable shoulder. He had begun thinking of it that way because she kept checking that he was comfortable on it.

Above the closet, a small sound of movement told him Rimuru was awake.

"Left," Rimuru said quietly.

Mau looked at the cups.

"Thank you."

"I watched the entire system develop. There were originally two cups. The third was a compromise."

Mau picked up the left one and drank. His mouth felt less like cloth than it had the evening before.

Frieren opened one eye.

"Did you ask for help?"

"I drank water."

"That wasn't what I asked."

"He consulted a specialist," Rimuru said from the loft.

She looked up toward the open little door. "You could come down and be useful."

"I could. I'm considering the full range of possibilities."

He came down a few minutes later, in no hurry, and took two cups downstairs. The blanket in his loft stayed where it was. Mau watched him go and was absurdly relieved by a household nuisance resuming its usual place.

Frieren sat up and felt his forehead with the back of her hand.

"Better," she decided.

"You should tell Shoko. She may want to hire you."

"I would need a better chair."

"There isn't room."

"The shelf could move."

They looked at each other.

"You heard yourself," he said.

"I'm ill from concern. It doesn't count."

He laughed into the pillow. She got up to fetch the cloth and made him turn his face so she could replace it without wetting his collar.

Shoko arrived with Maomao after breakfast. They asked different questions and checked the same things twice. Mau could answer more comfortably today. He was not allowed to convert that into a plan to get up and help with anything.

"A little better is not a job description," Shoko told him.

"I was only thinking."

"You have a very busy face."

Maomao checked the water and told Frieren that leaving him uncovered would not make him feel abandoned.

"He said he was cold."

"He also said he wanted breakfast. You didn't bring the kitchen."

"She considered it," Mau said.

Frieren looked at him. He concentrated on appearing unoccupied.

Later Fern brought fresh cloths. Frieren let her in immediately, then asked whether she had eaten. Fern looked sufficiently surprised that Mau turned his face into the pillow again.

"I have," Fern said. "Have you?"

"We shared."

Fern inspected the bowl.

"I'll bring enough for both of you."

Stark came with her the next time and moved the water jug within reach without moving the shelf. It took considerable ingenuity. Mau admired the final arrangement and was told not to try improving it.

Umaru arrived with Suika and an item of food she declared medically necessary.

"For whom?" Frieren asked.

"The room."

"Rooms don't eat."

"This one does. Constantly."

Mau let Suika have the chair while Umaru argued her case from the foot of the bed. Suika told him about something she had found outside. She did not make it an assignment he had to come and inspect.

He listened until Umaru accused him of looking too sleepy to recognize the injustice being done to her snack.

"You're safe," he said. "I'll recognize your injustice anywhere."

She left him a small piece anyway, after checking that Shoko had not forbidden that particular piece. It was smaller when Frieren handed it to him.

"Medical tax," she said.

In the afternoon he asked how many soups had actually been made.

"Two," Frieren said.

"And how many times have you brought them upstairs?"

She thought about it.

"Five."

He closed his eyes.

"There it is."

She put the bowl in his hands. "Mine only needed two recipes."
