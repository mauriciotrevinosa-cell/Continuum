# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 24 — Sitting

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** Mau chooses the household briefing; Frieren stays against him; account of Noise is shared without memory access or a cure

*When: Day 265, afternoon.*

Mau had been downstairs for less than a minute when Frieren decided where he would sit.

He objected to the decision only because the chair was already occupied by a folded blanket.

She moved the blanket, seated him, and put herself across his lap as though the arrangement had been tested for generations.

"Are you going to let me stand up?" he asked.

"No."

"Frieren."

She turned, wrapped her arms around him, and settled closer.

"They can hear you sitting. I'm not in the way."

Across the table, Rimuru looked delighted. Shoko inspected Mau's face and apparently decided he was in no medical danger from his girlfriend.

"You said an eternity together," Frieren added.

Mau gave up trying not to smile.

"Yes. An eternity together."

"Then start here."

He rested one arm behind her shoulders. "All right."

The people who had come to hear him found places without arranging themselves in ranks. Ori sat where she could see his face past Frieren's hair. Fern had her ledger, but it was closed. Yuta and Maki took the near end of the table. Gojo stood by Shoko until she moved her cup and made room.

There were others in the open doorways. Mau waited until Rimuru had repeated that this was Mau telling them what he wanted to tell, not an examination anyone had to finish for him.

"It wasn't somebody else inside me," Mau began.

He watched that arrive on their faces before continuing.

"There was a shape. At a table. It spoke badly. In pieces. It said it was what I called the Noise."

Ori's hands went still around her cup.

"It was a defence," he said. "Something Construction made very early. To protect me. Not another person waiting to come out. The way my body learned to keep finding things it thought I needed."

"Did it tell you that?" Qifrey asked.

"Part of it. Part was what I could understand from being there. I can't show you what it felt like."

"You don't have to," Frieren said.

He moved his hand over her sleeve.

"It thinks moving me out of danger is protecting me. It doesn't think about what that does to any of you. Or what it does to me to be moved without knowing I've begun."

Rimuru leaned forward a little.

"What did you ask it for?"

"Let me choose. Not stop building. It said it can't. My body will keep changing. Things can still come through. But I asked it not to try to take control to protect me. To trust that I can think, even when I decide to do something frightening."

"And?" Anko asked.

"It agreed."

Nobody cheered. Mau was glad. He had wanted to tell them something important without having it become an announcement that they would never be frightened again.

"So the walls stay," Yuta said.

"Yes."

"The current limits stay," Shoko added.

"Yes. I haven't tested anything."

Rimuru listened to something only he and Mau could hear, then spoke it aloud.

"Ciel says she has your account, not a recording of that conversation. We'll compare it with what actually changes. Slowly."

"Good," Mau said. "If something happens, I still want to know."

Fern's hand rested on the closed ledger. "We remember."

There were questions about the place. He described the clearing, the table, the way the scene had changed when he was looking for a route home.

"What scenes?" Gojo asked.

Mau looked down at Frieren. She looked back without moving away from him.

"Things I remember," he said. "Mostly us. Not anything from before I got here."

Gojo nodded and did not ask him to perform one of them for the room.

"There was our door at the end. From the night before. I opened it. Then I was falling."

"That's not a method I'd recommend repeating," Senku said.

"I have concerns about the landing arrangements myself."

Milim raised her hand.

"You were excellent," Mau told her.

She lowered it, satisfied.

He told them the last words he had heard. A door to his heart. He did not know whether that was an explanation, a discovery, or the voice finding something pleasing to call what had happened.

Echidna turned her cup once on its saucer. "A comparison is not a mechanism."

"I'm learning to dislike those," Rimuru said.

"Keep the useful ones," Senku answered.

They did not get a new theory of everything out of the afternoon. Mau answered until he began taking too long to find the start of a sentence. Shoko noticed before he did and reached for the water.

Frieren lifted her head.

"Later."

He looked around the table. Some questions would keep. Some might never have answers. Ori was looking at him in a way that told him she had one that did not belong to everybody.

"Later," he agreed.

Frieren settled against him again. He could feel her breathing. From where she sat she could not see the smile Rimuru was still failing to hide.

"Don't," Mau told him.

"I haven't said anything."

"You're thinking loudly."

"That is a very serious accusation from this side of the room."
