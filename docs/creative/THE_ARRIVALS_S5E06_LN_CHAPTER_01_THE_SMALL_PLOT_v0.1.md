# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 31 — The Small Plot

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 31
**Continuity state:** Mau starts the tobacco thank-you project after recovery; no plants, instant growth or destroyed cigarettes; Anko objects comically

*When: Day 266, late morning.*

The box was empty when Mau showed it to Shoko.

She looked into it, then at him.

"A remarkably honest gift."

"It isn't the gift yet."

"That improves its chances."

Wakana had made the shallow frame from short offcuts, after Mau asked whether they were needed for anything else. It had holes in the bottom and a tray underneath. Nobody had pretended that meant it contained a plant.

Mau set it on the bench outside the clinic. He had carried it from the workshop with a pause in the middle, mostly because Frieren had stopped beside a book somebody was carrying and needed to ask where it had come from.

He was not going to tell Shoko that part. She had authorized the pause, not its literary cause.

"The cigarette," he said.

Her expression changed.

"I haven't smoked it."

"I know. I wanted to ask whether Ciel could look at it. Without breaking it."

"For this?"

He nodded toward the box.

"I don't have seeds. I don't know if there's a plant like it here. I thought we could ask Hollis to look for something, and if we find a real starting point—"

"You could have begun with that. Instead of presenting me with a coffin for a very small vegetable."

He laughed. "I wanted to have something to show you."

Shoko sat on the bench and took the cigarette out carefully. It had been protected well enough that it still looked like a thing intended for an ordinary pocket in another world.

"She can look," she said. "Tell her I want it back in the same condition."

Mau repeated that inwardly. Ciel answered him without the instant triumph he would once have wanted from her. Observation could give them a reference. It could not certify that a seed they had not found would grow into the right plant. There was work left between looking and having something living.

He told Shoko that.

"Good," she said.

"Good?"

"I've had several people this week tell me that something difficult is simple. I prefer you knowing which part you haven't done."

He waited while she put the cigarette away.

"It might not work," he said.

"It's a garden. That happens."

He smiled at the box. "I wanted to say thank you. For more than the clinic."

Shoko looked down at her own hands. She rubbed one thumb over the place where the paper had rested.

"You can say it while I'm sitting here," she said. "You don't have to grow it first."

"Thank you."

She nudged the empty box with one finger.

"You're welcome. Keep trying this, though. I want to find out how long you can be patient with a plant."

Anko had heard enough of the conversation to be outraged before she reached them.

"A garden."

Mau looked around.

"An entire garden," she said. "For Shoko."

"It's a box."

"The intention is a garden. I've been here longer."

Shoko leaned back against the clinic wall. "You could ask to share."

"I could. I'm considering whether that would weaken my position."

Mau looked from one of them to the other. "There's nothing in it."

"There is gratitude in it," Anko said. "Apparently a selective crop."

Frieren had returned with the information that the book was not new and she had already read it. She considered the empty box.

"You could grow something useful."

"Nobody asked the elf," Anko said.

"I was standing here."

"Yes. A well-known hazard."

Mau sat because they had all been standing long enough and because he wanted to keep hearing this argument without Shoko deciding it was time he went upstairs.

Anko looked at him.

"I'm thinking about what to ask for."

"I can tell."

"Something appropriate to my years of service."

"Do you want me to be nervous now or when you've decided?"

"Both."

Shoko was laughing. Gojo, coming around the corner, slowed when he heard it and then tried to look as though he had not slowed.

He inspected the box.

"Impressive plant."

Anko pointed at him. "You. Explain the scale of this insult."

Gojo looked at Shoko, at Mau, and finally at the empty soil tray.

"I think I need to hear both sides."

"Coward," Shoko said.

He took the remaining place on the bench anyway.
