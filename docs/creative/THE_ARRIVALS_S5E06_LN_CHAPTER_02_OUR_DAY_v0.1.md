# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 27 — Our Day

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 27
**Continuity state:** Mau, Ori and Frieren begin their promised family day; Milim joins breakfast but the three-person day and fort debt remain distinct

*When: Day 267, morning.*

Ori came through their door with a ribbon in her hand.

Milim was at the other end of it, asleep and floating high enough that Ori had to duck the slack under the frame.

Mau opened one eye.

"Good morning."

"It is," Ori said. "For some of us."

Frieren put the blanket over her head.

"No."

"You agreed."

"Yesterday."

"That's usually how tomorrow works."

Mau sat up carefully. Nothing tilted. His body still felt like it had spent too long being somebody else's problem, but it had allowed him an ordinary night. He wanted to use the morning before Shoko found something elaborate for him not to do.

He tugged the edge of the blanket.

Frieren tugged it back.

"Our day," he reminded her.

She emerged far enough to inspect Ori.

"What time is it?"

"Breakfast time."

"That isn't a time."

"Fern says it is."

There were arguments Frieren would fight for hours. This one had a name attached that made a second approach advisable. She sat up and leaned against Mau while claiming that did not mean she was awake.

Above the closet, Rimuru made the mistake of moving.

Milim opened her eyes.

"Rimuru!"

"Don't—"

She untied her own ankle with a speed that made Ori's whole journey seem a personal insult, floated up to the loft and acquired Rimuru before he finished objecting.

Mau watched his friend attempt to retain a dignified human shape while being delivered from his sleeping place like an inconvenient pillow.

"Excellent service," he said.

"You've recovered enough to be unkind."

"That's what Shoko said."

Ori wound the ribbon around her hand. "She said not to use that as your only measure."

Frieren made a small sound against his shoulder. He looked down and found her smiling with her eyes still closed.

They went downstairs together after he had persuaded her that wearing the blanket was not an alternative to getting dressed. Milim did bring the blanket. She explained that she had not been included in the ruling.

Yuka was already at the table. She greeted Mau, then asked Ori where they were going.

"Out," Ori said.

"Helpful."

"Not far. And not with anybody else today."

Yuka looked at Frieren and Mau, who had both begun accepting food without realizing they were expected to confirm something.

"Family day," Mau said.

"We've had those," Ori said. "This one is the one we promised to choose. Nobody gets a job on the way."

Fern, passing behind her chair, said, "Agreed."

Milim stopped eating.

Ori turned before she could ask.

"We still have the fort day. All of us."

Umaru raised her head from a heated discussion with Suika about the division of something on their plate.

"In writing."

"In enormous writing," Mau said.

"You could still pretend you didn't see it."

Ori put a hand on Milim's arm. "Tomorrow you can hear everything we did. Today I'm borrowing them."

Milim considered this. "Both?"

"Both."

She looked at Frieren, apparently evaluating the fairness of a system that allowed one person to acquire two companions for a day.

"Bring back a story," she decided.

"That we can do," Mau said.

Shoko checked him before they left the house. Walking and sitting, water, no proving anything by getting farther away than he wanted to walk back. Mau accepted the conditions because none interfered with a plan he had actually made.

They took food and one book. Frieren had negotiated the book at length. Ori had finally said that if she could carry it herself and still look at things, she could bring it.

They did not go out through either of the perimeter gates. Ori led them along the inside path, past the places where work stopped and resumed with weather and available hands.

At the first turn she held up a small piece of paper.

The blank side of a used tally. One corner had been cut crookedly.

"I'm making a map," she said.

Mau looked at the path they all knew.

"Of what?"

"I'll know when it's finished."
