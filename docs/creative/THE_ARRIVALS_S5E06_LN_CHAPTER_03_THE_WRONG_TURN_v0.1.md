# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 33 — The Wrong Turn

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 33
**Continuity state:** A quiet outing stays inside the protected perimeter; Ori's map, a picnic and ordinary mistakes fill the family day

*When: Day 267, late morning and afternoon.*

The first thing on the map was a bench that was not there.

Ori had drawn it before they reached the place because she remembered one being there. What stood there now was a pair of supports and a plank leaning against them.

Mau looked at it.

Ori looked at Mau.

"No jobs."

He took his hand away from the plank.

"I was looking."

"You were measuring it with your eyes."

Frieren looked at the two supports. "The plank would fit."

Ori turned to her.

"You too."

"I hadn't offered."

"You were about to make him offer."

Mau laughed. He had to admit it was possible.

They found a place farther along where the ground was dry and the shade stayed broad enough for three people. Frieren put the book down first. Mau put the food where the book had been. Ori moved both so she could sit between them without sitting on lunch.

"Your map needs to show the dispute," Mau said.

She drew a very small angry face beside the missing bench.

"Who's that?"

"The bench."

"Fair. It has had a difficult morning."

There was very little on the paper yet. A crooked path, the bench, something like the back of a house. Ori had begun a tree and stopped at the number of branches she felt like drawing.

Mau asked whether he could add something.

She gave him the pencil after some thought.

He drew a person beside the tree. Or meant to. The legs were too short, and when he corrected them the person seemed to have acquired more knees than the others could accept.

"Is that me?" Frieren asked.

"It could be anybody."

"It has my ears."

"That narrows it down."

Ori took the pencil before he could improve the situation further. She added a book to the creature's hands.

"There. Now we know."

Frieren inspected the result.

"I don't stand like that."

"It's a map," Ori said. "Not a portrait."

"I have objections to both."

They ate while Frieren explained a better method of representing an elf on paper, mostly involving giving the elf the pencil. Ori pointed out that the last time Frieren had possession of something she liked, recovering it required negotiations with Fern.

Mau let them argue and watched a small insect attempt to lift a crumb larger than its head.

Frieren noticed him watching.

"That isn't a job either," she said.

"I'm learning."

He moved a smaller crumb near the insect without touching it. Ori watched until it found that one and changed direction.

"I think that's cheating."

"It can object."

They stayed long enough that the patch of shade moved off Mau's foot. He moved the foot rather than proposing that the afternoon had gone on too long. Frieren opened her book and read half a page aloud because she had found something irritating.

Ori asked a question.

Frieren closed the book over one finger and answered it properly.

For a while Mau listened to them disagree about whether a spell that made a stain less visible counted as cleaning. Ori thought it invited dishonesty. Frieren thought it demonstrated sophistication.

"Would it work on your soup bowl?" Mau asked.

She looked at him over the book.

"You're nearly well enough to make your own soup."

"An excellent argument for continued rest."

The wrong turn happened when they got up.

Ori meant to return by the path that would put a different side of the house on her paper. Mau thought she meant the shorter path. Frieren went wherever the remaining strip of shade went, reading while they negotiated.

They ended up at the same empty supports.

For a moment none of them said anything.

Then Ori put a second angry face on the map.

"It's the same bench," Mau said.

"It's angrier now."

They did not repair it. They went the other way.
