# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 34 — Tomorrow Also

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 34
**Continuity state:** The family day reaches its ordinary end; future fort day and V28 outings remain open; no anniversary date or Exchange occurs

*When: Day 267, late afternoon and evening.*

They came home by the workshop because it was the way Ori had wanted the first time.

Wakana looked up from his work when Mau stopped in the doorway. Marin was sitting nearby with a bit of painted material across her knees, looking at it from several angles as though one might eventually apologize.

"The bench near the inside path," Mau said. "Is it waiting for something?"

"Dry ground under the west support," Wakana answered. "Then I can fasten the seat."

Ori looked at Mau.

He spread his hands.

"I asked a question."

"A dangerous beginning," Marin said.

Frieren turned the map around so Wakana could see the angry faces.

"It's becoming impatient."

He looked at the drawing. Then at Ori.

"I'll tell it I'm sorry."

She seemed pleased with that and did not offer him the pencil.

They went inside before the conversation could become help. Mau was tired now, an ordinary heaviness in his legs that did not require a new name. He said so before anybody had to catch him denying it.

"Then sit," Frieren said.

"Both of you keep saying that."

"You keep standing."

Ori chose the bench in the courtyard. The real one. She made a point of touching it before committing it to paper.

Frieren sat beside Mau, and he leaned against her for a while instead of giving her the place against him. She put her book away and rested a hand in his hair.

Ori finished the map on her knees.

It was not useful for finding any place they had been. The path bent in ways it did not bend outside the paper. Lunch took up more space than the house. There were three people under a tree, and one was unmistakably an elf even after Frieren had lost her attempt to make herself taller.

At the edge, Ori added the crooked line of the bench they were sitting on.

"Done," she said.

Mau held out a hand. She gave it to him without the pencil.

He looked for a long time.

"Can we keep it?"

"We?"

"All three."

Ori considered where shared ownership ought to live. Then she said the connecting door could have it on her side, so they would have to come through to see it.

"An ingenious toll," Frieren said.

"You don't have to pay."

"I should hope not."

The paper went carefully into the book while they carried everything inside.

Milim wanted the story before supper. Ori told her about the bench with increasing attention to its emotional life. By the end Mau had nearly repaired it six times, which was a gross exaggeration. He said so. Frieren said five, which was worse because it sounded measured.

Yuka asked to see the map. Ori showed her. Umaru looked over her shoulder and immediately wanted to know where the fort was.

"We didn't go there."

"A significant flaw."

"It's today's map."

"Then we need another day."

"You already have one," Mau said.

Umaru nodded with the gravity of somebody preserving a major constitutional victory.

At supper they sat in their usual places until Ori asked to move one over so she could finish telling Milim something. Frieren moved her bowl. Stark moved his cup. The rearrangement passed around the end of the table and eventually inconvenienced Rimuru, who objected on principle and passed the bread anyway.

Mau ate until he was no longer hungry. When Momo asked whether he was feeling well enough to help tomorrow, he said yes, and Shoko specified that tomorrow would begin with a check rather than a bucket.

He accepted that too.

Afterward, he and Frieren went upstairs with Ori. Milim had gone to find Rimuru. The corridor had the familiar scatter of things people meant to put away before bed, and Mau stopped long enough to move a shoe out of the walking space.

"A job," Ori said.

"Self-defence."

She accepted the distinction.

They fixed the map to the inside of her connecting door. Nothing grand. It took two attempts to make it sit straight enough for Ori to accept that the door itself might not be straight.

Then Frieren went through to get ready for bed, leaving the door open behind her.

Mau stayed a moment.

"Was it the day you wanted?"

Ori looked at her map.

"I didn't know what it would be."

"Neither did I."

"That's probably why we got lost."

"We weren't lost."

"We met the same bench twice."

He laughed. She put an arm around him and held him without checking how warm he was.

"I liked it," she said into his shirt.

"Me too."

From the other room, Frieren called his name.

"She's going to ask you to find her place," Ori said.

"Probably."

"Don't let her blame me."

"I make no promises about your mother."

She released him, pleased with the answer, and went to straighten the map one more time.

He crossed the little threshold back into his own room. Frieren was in bed with the book open and an expression of patient accusation.

"You lost it," she said.

"You used it to carry a map."

"You distracted me."

He closed their corridor door and went around the shelf.

She lifted the blanket.

He got in.

"Tomorrow," she said, "I'll explain it from the beginning."

"Of course you will."

She moved closer and put the book where they could both see it.
