# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 35 — Before Work

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 35
**Continuity state:** Shoko, Maomao and Ciel amend the post-bell restriction only for a bounded ordinary outing; no combat-kit restoration

*When: Day 268, morning.*

Mau arrived in the clinic carrying a bucket.

Shoko looked at it. Then she looked at him.

"I didn't fill it."

"A remarkable medical achievement. Put it down."

He put it beside the door and sat where she pointed. Through the open window came the scrape of something being dragged across the yard, followed by Momo telling somebody that dragging it was the problem. The house was getting on with the morning. He liked hearing it from a room where, for once, nobody was hurrying.

Shoko took his wrist. Maomao had yesterday's notes under one hand and a cup he did not trust under the other.

"What is that?"

"Water," Maomao said.

"You understand why I asked."

"Yes. Drink it."

He drank it. It was water.

They asked about sleep, food, the ache behind his eyes. He told them about being tired after the walk yesterday, and about sitting down before it became something else. Shoko made him stand, turn, and walk the length of the room. She watched his feet rather than his face.

*The overnight readings have remained within the current resting range,* Ciel said.

Mau repeated the reading aloud, identifying it as Ciel's rather than his own impression of a good morning.

"Does Rimuru have the same account?" Shoko asked.

"Ciel is telling him too. He can give you his part."

"Then let her. I'd like to compare what he has with what you're telling me."

Mau asked Ciel to call Rimuru over. He came in from the courtyard a little later, heard Shoko's question, and separated what Ciel had told him from what he had actually noticed during the night in his loft. Shoko asked two more questions before letting him go back to his own morning.

Mau had sat again without being asked. Maomao noticed, which was an unfortunate thing to have noticed.

"I'd like to go through the gate," he said.

Shoko's fingers stopped on the page.

He let her finish looking at him.

"Not alone. Not to try anything. An ordinary run, on a route somebody has already checked. Yuta and Milim, if they're willing. Yuka asked about going with me before all this."

"And what's your job?"

"Carry a bag. Come back when I'm told."

"You can do that in the yard."

"I know. I'd still like to go."

"Why today?"

He looked at the bucket by the door. He could give her an answer about useful work, finding supplies, bringing back something the house might need. It would sound very sensible, and leave out most of what had brought him here.

"Because I'm starting to wait for something else to happen. I don't like finding myself relieved when a day ends before it can."

Shoko put the pen down.

"A gate won't make the world stop surprising you."

"I know. I'm asking to go somewhere with them. Do the small part I can do. Come back for lunch."

"And if the answer is not today?"

He had hoped she wouldn't ask. He considered it until he could give her something other than the answer designed to get him through the door.

"Then I'd be disappointed. I'd ask what we're waiting to see change. I wouldn't go round you to ask someone else."

Maomao set down the cup.

"How far?"

He had expected a longer conversation before that question. It made him answer more carefully.

"As far as we agree before leaving. No new openings in the walls. No taming. If something starts wanting something, we turn around."

The last sentence left a small silence in the room. Outside, whatever Momo was trying to move finally lifted off the ground. Somebody applauded. Momo told them to get out of the way.

"Do you feel it wanting something now?" Maomao asked.

Mau listened inwardly before he answered.

"No."

Ciel had concerns. She delivered them without pretending she could see the whole interior of a place he had not entered. The normal relay could observe the party through Rimuru's threads. It could not make the gate safe by observing it. His body had improved; the reasons for the old restrictions had not disappeared with the fever.

He waited through all of it.

Shoko tapped the page once with her pen.

"One short run. No fighting planned around you. Yuta knows the stop conditions, and you tell him before you're too tired to enjoy being sensible. If any of the three of us says stop, you stop."

"Yes."

"This changes the gate restriction for that outing. It doesn't change everything else."

"I know."

She held his gaze until it was clear he did.

"And eat breakfast first."

He glanced at Maomao.

"Is breakfast going to be water too?"

She took the cup back.

"That depends on your behaviour."

By the time he got outside, Momo and Okarun had moved the washing bench into the sun. Okarun was holding one end with an expression of considerable betrayal.

"It was lighter when she was helping," he said.

Mau looked at the empty bucket in his hand.

"I've been cleared for this."

Momo pointed toward the well.

"Congratulations."

He went to fill it.
