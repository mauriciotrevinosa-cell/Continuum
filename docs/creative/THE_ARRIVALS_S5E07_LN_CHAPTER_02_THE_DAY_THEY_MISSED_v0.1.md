# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 31 — The Day They Missed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 31
**Continuity state:** The overdue anniversary is acknowledged without inventing an exact date or erasing previous dates; Frieren has not yet given away her compass

*When: Day 268, after breakfast.*

Shoko found Frieren on the courtyard bench, holding Mau's abandoned cup and reading a book too large to belong comfortably to either hand.

"He's at the well," Frieren said.

"I already found him."

"Then you may sit down."

It was not Shoko's bench, but she appreciated the permission. She sat. For a while there was only the book, the washing in the sun, and the sound of Milim arguing that breakfast had been too small to count as breakfast.

"How long have you two been together?" Shoko asked.

Frieren held her place with one finger.

"Which part?"

"The part where he's your boyfriend."

"Over a year."

She thought about it a little longer.

"Quite a lot over a year."

"Did you do anything?"

"We do things often."

"For the anniversary."

Frieren looked up from the page.

She remembered the answer she had given him when he asked. The place, his face, the unreasonable amount of time it had taken him to get to the question. She could remember those things without effort. Finding their day among all the days since was harder. There had been a festival. Bread. Recoveries. An afternoon she still thought of when the light fell a certain way. None of those had been an anniversary.

"We missed it," she said.

Shoko did not ask which of the things that had happened to them had occupied that particular day. Frieren was grateful. She did not want to turn it into an inquiry.

"You could have one late."

"Mm."

Mau came through the courtyard with the bucket. He had filled it less than he would once have filled it. Frieren watched him discover that she and Shoko were looking at him, and become suspicious.

"What did I do?"

"You've forgotten our anniversary," she said.

He stopped so abruptly that some of the water escaped.

Shoko took the cup from her before the book could follow it.

"We had an anniversary?" Mau said, and immediately closed his eyes. "That was a terrible way to say that."

"It was."

"I meant—"

"I know what you meant."

He put the bucket down and sat on the other side of her. The bench had not been designed for three people and a grimoire. Shoko got up.

"I'll leave you to recover from this new condition."

Mau waited until she was inside.

"I was going to ask you to dinner."

"When?"

"When I could do it properly."

"You could have asked while doing it badly."

"I thought you'd like the other version."

She did like it. She also liked the way he was looking at her now, caught between an apology and a plan. She let him stay there a moment longer than was kind.

"I forgot too," she said.

His shoulders dropped.

"You could have opened with that."

"You spilled the water very quickly."

He laughed, and she moved the book so she could lean against his arm.

"I don't want us to keep missing things just because there are other things," he said after a while.

"Neither do I."

"Then dinner. A real one. I'll ask you again when I have a day."

"You can ask me now."

He turned toward her as far as the bench allowed.

"Frieren, would you go on an extremely late anniversary date with me?"

"Yes."

"That was fast."

"You took long enough."

She returned to her page. He stayed beside her until Momo called for the bucket, and then he got up, kissed the top of her head, and carried the surviving water inside.

Frieren read the same line three times.

Then she closed the book and went upstairs to find something she had kept for a long time.
