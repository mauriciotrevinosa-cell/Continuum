# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 37 — Four Will Go

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 37
**Continuity state:** Mau informs Ori and Frieren before a short agreed run; Yuka, Yuta and Milim choose to accompany him; Rimuru remains home

*When: Day 268, late morning.*

Yuka had packed enough rope to rescue the whole house.

Yuta lifted the bag, looked inside, and took half of it out.

"What if we need it?"

"Then we'll still have rope."

"What if we need more?"

"Then we probably shouldn't have gone there."

She looked at Mau for support. He was fastening a buckle on his own much smaller bag.

"I'm not getting between you and your grandfather about rope."

"Coward."

"Experienced."

Yuta smiled without looking up. Maki leaned in through the doorway, checked what he was doing, and told him not to replace the rope with his own rope when Yuka wasn't looking.

"I wasn't going to."

"You were thinking about it."

He put down the second coil.

Mau left them to it and went upstairs. Frieren was sitting by the shelf with an old cloth in her lap. She folded it around whatever she had been holding before he could see.

He stopped at the connecting door. Ori's map was on the other side, visible through the open space. A new pencil line had appeared near the bench. He was reasonably sure it had not been that crooked yesterday.

Ori came to the threshold when he called.

"I'm going on a short gate run," he told them. "Yuta, Yuka, Milim and me. The doctors agreed this morning. We're using the checked part, and Rimuru is keeping the relay."

Ori's eyes moved from his face to the bag.

"Today?"

"Before lunch. If the route stays the route. I wanted to tell you before we finished getting ready."

Ori's fingers went around the strap. She didn't pull it.

"I thought we'd be doing this for a little longer."

"What?"

She looked back through her door, toward the map. The answer was already there: finding out what a day together looked like when nobody had to leave in the middle of it.

"Being here."

He put his bag down before answering.

"I still want that. This isn't how every morning has to be now."

"You don't know what every morning will be."

"No. I know I'm asking for one of them."

Frieren unfolded the edge of the cloth in her lap, then folded it back. She had been listening to him without looking at the bag.

"What if there's a room beyond the checked route?" she asked. "An interesting one."

"Yuta tells me to turn round."

"And what do you do?"

"Complain on the way back."

She studied him a moment longer.

"You'll have to stop doing that if you want anyone to take you again."

"I'm willing to learn."

He told them the actual exit conditions. No new door, no new operation in the walls, no following a pull to see where it went. Frieren asked him to repeat the last part. He did, without making the second answer into a joke.

"An unusual expedition for you," she said.

"I'm branching out."

Ori touched the strap where it crossed his shoulder.

"Will you tell me when you're back?"

"I'll come find you."

She nodded. Then she asked him to bring back a description of the place, because the fort's maps were becoming repetitive.

"No promises about my drawing."

"I'll draw it. You can explain where things go."

"A much better arrangement."

Frieren watched him kiss Ori's forehead and turn toward her. When he bent to kiss her too, she caught his sleeve.

"You don't need to bring me a grimoire."

He tried to arrange his face.

"I didn't say I was—"

"You asked yesterday whether the normal gate ever has books."

"It was a general question."

"Of course."

Her fingers loosened. She kissed him, once, without making him lean any farther.

"Come back with your bag. If there happens to be a book in it, I'll inspect it."

"Very generous."

Downstairs, Milim had solved the problem of supplies by putting the largest food parcel in her own bag. Yuka was contesting its ownership. Rimuru stood beside them with his coat open, looking at the two of them as though a relay across a dungeon would be the restful part of the day.

"One of us home," Mau said to him.

"I got the good end of the arrangement."

"You have the paperwork."

"I'd forgotten the paperwork."

They went over the route with Yuta. Rimuru would watch from the stake and through their shadows; a lost thread meant stop and investigate the loss, not walk farther until it returned. They would leave before the agreed time even if there was something interesting one room ahead.

Yuka tightened the last strap.

"I'm ready."

Mau pointed at the extra coil now tied beneath her bag.

"Your grandfather lost?"

"Compromise," Yuta said.

Maki made a sound from the doorway.

He did not look at her.
