# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 4 — Week Twenty-Four

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 4
**Continuity state:** The ordinary blue gate remains outside the divine boundary; the party confirms a physical return route and uses the existing shadow relay

*When: Day 268, midday.*

The blue opening stood forty paces beyond the red stake, just where it had stood before.

Mau was glad of that in a way he could not explain without making the morning difficult. He stopped at the marker while Anko checked the time and the four names on her page. The interface still called this week twenty-four. Normal. Five days until the next change.

Nothing on it suggested that an anniversary had anything to do with the place.

Yuka stood beside him with her hands in her pockets. Her white dog had stayed home. She had chosen a short run with the others over asking permission for a new summon, and now she looked at the entrance with the expression she usually wore just before deciding that asking one more question would be worth it.

"What?" Mau said.

"If we find a chest, I get to look before Milim hits it."

"If it bites you, I get to hit it," Milim said.

"That's reasonable," Yuta said.

Yuka sighed. Nobody was taking the right side of this.

Rimuru set his threads. Mau felt a small coldness at the edge of his shadow and then no sensation at all.

"Can you hear me?"

The voice came from near their feet.

"Unfortunately," Milim said.

"Wonderful. I'll treasure that."

Yuta went through first. Yuka followed, then Mau, then Milim. The air changed against Mau's face. He stepped from grass onto pale stone, and the gate stood behind him in an alcove of the same stone, blue light washing the soles of Yuta's boots.

They checked it before going farther. Yuka reached back through far enough to feel outside air. Rimuru confirmed he could see the movement at both ends. Yuta marked the inner arch in chalk and pointed out the turn that would hide it from view.

Mau drew it badly on a folded scrap for Ori.

"You're making a map?" Yuka asked.

"Providing material for a better map."

"That wall doesn't do that."

"She's going to fix it."

The checked route ran through three low rooms. Roots, or things made to look like roots, had broken through the ceiling in the second. A dry fountain occupied the third. On the scouting sheet there were two marks beside it, one for a passage ahead and one for the chest that had tried to eat Stark's boot.

The passage was still there. The chest was not.

Yuta stopped at the line marked on the sheet. Milim went no farther than his shoulder, which Mau privately counted as a victory large enough to reward with the food she was already eating.

Something moved behind the fountain.

Yuka saw it first. She lifted a hand. All four stopped.

The creature that came out was low and plated, with too many joints in its forelegs and a seam of blue light where eyes ought to have been. Milim put herself between it and Mau. Yuta waited until it committed to the lunge before cutting across the thin join behind its front plate.

It struck stone, folded, and broke apart into sparks.

A crystal clicked against the fountain's dry basin.

"One," Yuka said, adding it to the pouch.

"I helped," Milim said.

"You were eating."

"I ate defensively."

Mau sat on the fountain edge while Yuta checked the passage mouth. He was not tired enough to need it yet. Sitting anyway gave him a better view of the stonework, and something at the bottom of the basin that he had taken for a crack.

It moved in the opposite direction from the light.

Not toward him. Not yet.

He stopped drawing.

"Yuta. There's something else here."
