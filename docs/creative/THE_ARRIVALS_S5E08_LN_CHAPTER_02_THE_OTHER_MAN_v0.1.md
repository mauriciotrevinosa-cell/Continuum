# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 41 — The Other Man

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 41
**Continuity state:** Jin-Woo appears in Mau's place; Yuta's party holds the location and hears the Goddess's public announcement

*When: Day 268, the same five exterior minutes.*

Yuta's hand closed on a black sleeve.

Wrong cloth. Wrong arm.

He let go and stepped back, putting his shoulder in front of Yuka.

The stranger stood with one hand half raised. He was young, dark-haired, and so still that Yuta's first thought was that he had been frozen halfway through killing something.

Then his eyes moved.

They took in Yuta, the blade, the fountain, Milim. They returned to the shadow beside his own feet.

"Where am I?"

"Where's Mau?" Milim demanded.

The man looked at her.

"Who?"

Yuka was already looking in the basin. The ant was gone. She checked the floor, then the place Mau's hand had been.

"He was right here. Oppa was right here."

Yuta lowered his sword a little. The stranger did not look relaxed by the gesture.

"A dungeon," Yuta said. "In a place called Continuum. Somebody was sitting there a moment ago. Then you were."

The stranger looked up as if trying to see through the ceiling.

He took a step. His expression changed.

Yuta recognized the calculation before he recognized its reason: somebody reaching for a tool that had always been there and finding resistance in its place.

"No," the man said quietly.

He opened his hand. Darkness gathered, then failed to go where he wanted it to go.

Milim shifted forward.

"You give him back."

"I didn't bring him."

"Then who did?"

"Milim," Yuta said.

She stopped. He kept his eyes on the stranger.

"We can help each other faster if we're not fighting."

For a moment the man's face made Yuta think he would answer with something terrible. Then he looked at Yuka, whose hands were still on the fountain rim, and the answer changed.

"Sung Jin-Woo," he said. "My name."

Yuta gave his own. He gave the others' names too. It was a small, absurd formality, and for a moment he was grateful to have one.

Rimuru's voice came up from the floor.

"I've lost Mau's thread. Yuta, don't follow anything. Stay where you are."

"We are."

"Who is that?" Jin-Woo asked.

"Home," Yuka said before Yuta could answer.

The stranger shut his eyes. When he opened them there was something rawer behind the control.

"There's a war. Where I came from. I can't stay here."

Yuta had no answer for what that meant about Mau.

He found himself checking the same empty place again, as though Mau might come back if somebody looked carefully enough. His ring was cold against his chest. He did not touch it. There was nothing here for Rika to strike, and no wound yet that he could put his hands over.

The voice came from everywhere.

Not through Rimuru. Not from the stranger.

The stone under Yuta's boots seemed to carry it, as plainly as the air.

"Mau has left this universe. The error is mine. I am retrieving him."

Yuka made a small sound.

Jin-Woo looked straight up. Something in his face sharpened, and he addressed the voice as though he was used to things larger than a room having somebody behind them.

"Then send me back. Now."

There was no answer.

Yuta heard Rimuru breathing through the thread. He waited for the sensible next instruction. None came.

Milim had gone very quiet. She moved to the passage mouth, turned her back to them, and stood guard with both fists closed.

Yuka started counting. Yuta did not know what she was counting until she reached sixty, paused, and began again.

He crouched beside her.

"He's coming back to this spot," he said. "We keep it clear."

"You don't know that."

"No. But if he does, we're going to be here."

She moved the bags away from the fountain. Her hands shook once on a strap. Then she made them do the buckle.

Jin-Woo stood on Mau's side of the basin with his eyes closed, waiting on a connection none of them could see.

Yuta stayed beside his granddaughter.

Nobody left.
