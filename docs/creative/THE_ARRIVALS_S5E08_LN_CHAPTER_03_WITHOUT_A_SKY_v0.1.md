# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 42 — Without A Sky

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 42
**Continuity state:** Unrestricted patterns demand a cost his body cannot safely pay; Noise assists chosen survival without taking control

*When: Day 268, later in Mau's roughly forty subjective minutes.*

Mau lost the sandwich when he fell the third time.

The bag tore against a broken edge. He felt the strap go loose, tried to catch it, and saw the food parcel tumble into a crack between two slabs.

For a moment he was angry about the sandwich.

Then something came through the soldiers above him and he had to stop being angry.

He had found a strip of ground where the dark army was thickest. That was not safety. It was only the part of the fight where somebody else was also trying to keep the next blade away from him.

His first shield failed. The second held long enough to burn a circle through his sleeve. He couldn't tell which failures came from his hands and which came from trying to force too many incompatible answers through the same breath.

The world wasn't stopping him.

His body was.

He dragged himself upright against a stone column that was slowly falling over. The movement around him made no sense. The soldiers could pass through one another's dark; their enemies seemed able to step across distances without crossing the space between. Twice he had run toward what looked like an opening and arrived in front of the thing he had been running from.

The second time, he reached the split column before seeing the same bent weapon above him.

He knew it by the missing piece near its tip. He had watched that piece break off when the creature used it to lever a soldier out of the ground.

Mau stopped with one foot halfway over the crack.

He had run away from the weapon. It was in front of him now, without the bearer having turned far enough to account for it.

The soldier at his shoulder struck upward. Metal met the creature's wrist. The bent blade came down sideways instead, and Mau threw himself behind the lower slab before the edge reached his chest.

It struck the slab. His cheek filled with grit.

For a moment he stayed there, looking at a gap too narrow for his shoulder. The weapon lifted. He had time to move, but nowhere he trusted enough to run.

The dark soldier came apart above him.

Mau reached for the pull he could picture most clearly, then caught himself reaching for far too much. The stone under his fingers began to loosen. He wanted the weapon moved, not the whole ridge brought down on top of him.

"There," he said, pointing with his other hand although nobody needed the gesture. "Only there."

The Noise held the competing shapes back while he tried to reduce the one he had chosen.

The pull caught the broken end of the blade.

It did not wrench it from the creature. It changed the next strike by the width of his body. The edge hit the side of the slab instead of coming through the gap where he lay.

Mau crawled out under the raised arm. His knee refused the first attempt to stand. He dragged it after him and used the column to push himself up.

Blood ran across his upper lip. When he wiped it, his sleeve came away wet.

Behind him the soldier rose again. It put itself back into the same fight as if the last few seconds had not cost anything that would run out.

Mau watched it pass, then looked at the opening beyond the column.

He could run for that and meet the same weapon a third time. He could not keep spending pieces of himself to prove the landscape would not let him leave.

He stopped trying to find an exit in the landscape.

"You can see the connection," he said inwardly. "The one we came through."

A pressure answered behind his thoughts. Attention, working. No promise.

"Then keep it. Please."

He turned back toward the fight.

There were spells he remembered too well. He could feel where a room full of killing would begin. The shape of a domain offered itself with awful familiarity, and for one instant he imagined standing inside it where nobody could reach him.

Then he looked at the soldiers.

He did not know what the sure hit would decide they were.

He pushed the shape away.

"Not here."

An enemy struck the soldier beside him. Mau pulled the collapsing body behind the ridge before he remembered it could get up again. The soldier did so against his arm, gathering its own pieces with a sound like cloth dragged through water.

It looked at him.

Mau laughed once, badly.

"Right. You have that."

He did not.

The thought steadied him. He had to make the next minute work, and then the one after it. Nothing grander. His hand could still close. His legs could still move. He chose a push to turn a weapon, a pull just strong enough to spoil a landing, the short distance in front of his skin when there was no other choice.

Every choice left something behind. Blood from his nose. A strip of skin on his fingers. The ability to focus his right eye without seeing two edges to everything.

Inside him, something kept building.

He could feel it following the army's dark threads, tracing a pattern even while helping him keep the others apart. He wanted to tell it to stop looking. He also knew, with the frightened part of himself that was keeping count, that the line it followed might be the only way anybody could find him.

"Keep the way back," he said again.

He did not ask for more.

A rank of soldiers went down all at once.

Beyond them, the light bent.

Mau braced his feet and opened a shape he had once seen Frieren make. Space gathered at a point in front of the breach. Too small, too uneven. The pull dragged loose stone, a broken blade, the edge of his own shirt toward it.

He closed it before it took his arm.

The approaching thing had lost its footing. That was enough to let him run.

He ran through the gap the soldiers made without knowing whether they meant to make it. His chest hurt. His shadow no longer fell in a direction he understood.

At the top of the next rise, his knee folded.

He caught himself on both hands and couldn't get up again.

For the first time since arriving, he could see a long way across the battlefield.

There was no end of it.

"I was supposed to be back for lunch," he said.

Something enormous noticed him.

The dark soldiers turned toward it.

He tried to stand.
