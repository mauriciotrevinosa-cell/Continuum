# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 38 — Hers

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 38
**Continuity state:** The Goddess retrieves Mau without revealing a human form; Jin-Woo returns to his war; a private notification is not household knowledge

*When: Day 268, the retrieval.*

The light arrived before the heat.

Mau raised an arm he could barely feel. White filled the spaces between his fingers. For a moment he thought the thing beyond the soldiers had opened its eye.

Then the battlefield cast shadows in a new direction.

A sun stood where nothing had stood before.

He could not look at its middle. He could not have said whether there was a middle to look at. The beings beyond the broken ranks moved toward it, and the air changed as if a hand had been laid across the whole fight.

"He is mine."

He knew the voice.

He was too tired to decide how he felt about knowing it.

The ground below his hands moved away. He tried to hold on, not because he wanted to stay but because falling had become the thing his body knew to be afraid of.

There was nothing to hold.

"The others," he managed.

"Where you left them."

It was the first useful thing anybody had said to him since the fountain.

He let his hand open.

The dark threads around the soldiers pulled taut. He felt something in himself answer them, not a command, not a voice; an unfinished structure reaching after the shape it had learned. He tried to ask what it was taking.

The question never got to his mouth.

Light went through the place where his ribs hurt. The army folded into a thin black line. For one brief instant he saw the fountain through it, Yuta crouching, Yuka beside him, a stranger in a black coat turning toward the same opening.

Then the stranger passed him.

No collision. Only the impossible impression of somebody looking back from the other side of a pane of glass.

Mau tried to say sorry.

The room came up beneath him.

He expected the stone to break his knees. Something caught the speed out of the fall instead. His breath left him in a long, harmless rush. Hands reached him before the floor did.

Yuta's hands.

"I've got you."

Mau caught his sleeve.

"Lunch," he said, because it was the last thing he remembered being supposed to do.

Yuta made a sound that might have been a laugh if he had had enough breath for it.

"We'll sort that out."

The pressure in Mau's head eased. The world had edges again. He could feel the walls, the familiar resistance, all the things he had once hated because they stood between him and what he thought he ought to be able to do.

He wanted to sleep inside them.

Somebody brushed his hair away from his eyes.

He slept.

***

Jin-Woo returned with a hand already open.

The nearest soldier turned. The gap in the line closed around him as if the army itself had finally found its next breath. The weight that had pressed on him in the stone room was gone.

He reached through the shadows.

They answered.

The boy was no longer among them. Neither was the light. The war remained exactly as urgent as it had been before somebody had taken him out of it.

He could be angry later.

He gave an order, and the ranks moved.

Only then did the message appear at the edge of his sight.

He had time to read it once before another attack crossed the field.

*A Shadow Monarch is in the making.*

His gaze stopped on the final two words.

He dismissed the notice without answering it and went back to his war.
