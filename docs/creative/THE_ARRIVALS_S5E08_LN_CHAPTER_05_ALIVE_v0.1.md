# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 10 — Alive

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 10
**Continuity state:** Rimuru relays alive/asleep/fine first; Frieren receives the conditional elf-route possibility privately only after Mau is home and settled

*When: Day 268, outside the gate and the return home.*

Frieren was halfway across the field when Rimuru called her name.

She did not slow down.

Anko stood at the red stake with a watch in one hand. Farther out, the blue gate went on shining as though nothing important had happened. Frieren reached Rimuru and saw the effort in his face before she heard his voice.

"He's alive. He's asleep. Ciel says he's fine."

She stopped.

The words had come in the right order. She would remember that later.

"Where?"

"Back with them. Yuta has him. They're coming out."

She looked past him.

"Can I—"

"Keep this side clear. Please. They're almost here."

The gate bent around Milim first. She came through backward, holding the bags and looking at the light as though she intended to remember an offence. Yuka followed. Then Yuta, carrying Mau against his chest.

His head lay against Yuta's shoulder. Frieren could see his face. Blood had dried beneath his nose. His shirt had a tear near the collar, but the skin beneath it was whole.

She reached them before they reached the stake.

Yuta stopped long enough for her to touch Mau's cheek.

Warm. Not the old fever. Breathing.

She put her fingers against the side of his neck and waited for another beat even after she had found the first.

"Home," Yuta said quietly.

She nodded and walked beside him.

Ori met them at the courtyard entrance with Shoko. She had come out without finishing fastening one shoe. Frieren took her hand before the girl could reach for Mau and make Yuta stop again.

"He's sleeping," she said.

"Why?"

"We don't know all of it yet. He came back."

Shoko went ahead. Maomao cleared the clinic bed. There were too many people in the doorway for a moment; Maki moved them back without raising her voice.

Frieren stayed until the examination had reached the part where watching could not help. Yuta stood on the other side of the bed. He was looking at his own hands, flexing them once and then leaving them still.

Rimuru relayed what Ciel could actually establish. The external restrictions had caught again. Mau's current readings were tired rather than failing. The fresh injuries had not returned with him in the state Yuta had seen for that single instant at the fountain.

"How?" Shoko asked.

Rimuru listened.

"Ciel says something caught him on the way back," Rimuru relayed. "She didn't do it. Ciel, I mean. That part wasn't her work."

"Do you know what did?" Shoko asked.

"Not yet. We're separating what she observed from what she can explain."

Nobody mistook that for an explanation of the whole event.

They moved him upstairs only after Shoko was satisfied that sleep was what he needed. Frieren helped turn the blanket back. Ori carried the folded map paper that had come out of the torn bag. She set it beside the five flowers on the shelf, away from a damp patch near the fold.

Yuta left when Maki came to get him. Frieren thanked him. He looked as though he did not know what to do with it, and she thanked him again before he could decide not to need it.

Later, when Ori had gone to tell Milim that they were both allowed to come back after supper, Rimuru waited beside the shelf.

"Ciel has something else. Just for you and Mau, for now."

Frieren looked at the bed.

"Tell me."

"It's preliminary. The changes she can measure after his return, and what he's carrying back—not a record of the fight."

She nodded.

"Her first estimate is twice the longest he has ever held an open state. It's a comparison from the condition he returned in, not a recording and not a new safe time. He needs the rest. But there's a new structure being built around physical capacity. Something read an existing system. It's still being studied."

"Something?"

"The Noise. Most likely. We'll need his account. There's a seed she doesn't have a complete name for."

Frieren let that settle before he continued.

"It may matter to the compatibility problem. The route we tried. A body that can gradually hold more is a different starting point. Not an answer yet. Not something he can use tomorrow to solve it."

She looked at Mau's closed eyes.

Once, she would have reached for the possibility first. She could feel it there, bright and terrible, asking her to make hope out of a thing that had frightened all of them.

"Let him sleep," she said.

Rimuru's shoulders eased.

"Yes."

She pulled the chair close to the bed. Her old silver compass was in her pocket. She took it out and laid it face down on her knee, not wanting its useless little certainty just then.

Mau moved one hand in his sleep.

She put hers where he would find it.
