# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 44 — Everyone Heard

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 44
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. The settlement hears the Goddess's public announcement ("Mau has left this universe. The error is mine. I am retrieving him.") during the five exterior minutes; small reactions across the house; Frieren runs for the field; no new knowledge beyond the words

*When: Day 268, midday, the same five exterior minutes. The gate party has been inside for less than half an hour.*

Momo was carrying the big soup pot from the stove to the long table when the voice came, and she did not drop it.

She would be proud of that afterward. She would tell Okarun about it more than once, and he would let her. She did not drop it. She stopped in the middle of the kitchen with the pot in both arms, and the soup slopped once against the lid and settled, and she stood there holding it.

*Mau has left this universe.*

It did not come from anywhere. It came from the walls, and the floor, and the pot in her arms, and the inside of her own head, all at once, the way it had come on the day of the Trial and on the morning the world was evolving. Every person in the settlement heard it at the same moment, at the same volume, as if the Goddess were standing beside each of them separately.

*The error is mine. I am retrieving him.*

Then nothing.

Momo put the pot down on the long table. Very carefully. She took her hands away from it. She looked at them.

Behind her, Okarun had come in from the yard with an armful of firewood. He was standing in the doorway with his mouth open. The firewood was on the floor. He did not seem to have noticed letting go of it.

"Left," he said. "Left the *universe*?"

On his shoulder, Turbo Granny said nothing at all.

* * *

In the music room, Bocchi's hand slipped on the strings and made a sound like something tearing.

Nobody laughed. Kita was already beside her, before the sound had finished, one hand flat between Bocchi's shoulder blades. Ryo had stopped with her fingers still on the bass, looking at the ceiling as if the voice might be up there. Nijika had dropped a stick. Seika, at the back, had stood up, and was standing very straight with her arms at her sides and her face doing nothing whatsoever, which was what Seika's face did when she was more frightened than she would ever say.

"Retrieving," said Seika, after a moment. Her voice was very level. "She said retrieving. Not lost. Retrieving."

Nobody answered her.

Bocchi had both arms round her guitar. She was holding it against her chest the way she held it when the room was too big. Her eyes were very wide.

"He said lunch," she said. Very small. "This morning. He said they'd be back for lunch."

Kita did not take her hand away.

* * *

In the lab, Senku put down the lens he had been grinding.

He did it slowly. He set it on its cloth, exactly where it went, and laid the grinding tool beside it, and wiped his hands on his coat. Then he stood at the bench with his hands flat on it and looked at nothing.

Byakuya, across the lab, had stopped too. He looked at his son's back.

"Senku," he said.

"Five minutes," said Senku. "Or less. The gate party went in at eleven. If she's retrieving, it's happening now. She said *now*. Present tense." He did not turn round. "So we wait. We don't know how long. We wait."

Byakuya came across the lab and stood beside him at the bench. He did not put a hand on his shoulder. He just stood there, close, the way he had stood in the doorway of Arrival House on the morning he came back.

"I know how to wait," said Byakuya quietly. "I did it for a very long time."

Senku did not say anything. But after a moment he leaned, very slightly, so that their shoulders touched.

* * *

In the fort, the whole fort went silent at once.

Twenty small people in hoods, in the middle of a game nobody would explain, stopped moving. Umaru was on the toll booth platform with the Ministry slate. Coco was at the low table with the game board. Tetia was halfway through a sentence. The white dog, which had stayed home because Yuka had gone with the party, lifted its head off its paws and looked at the curtain.

Then Tetia burst into tears, all at once, the way Tetia did everything.

And Agott, from her corner, said, "Oh, for—" in a voice that cracked in the middle, and got up, and went and sat beside her, stiffly, and put one hand on her back.

Umaru did not cry. She stood on the toll booth platform with the slate in both hands and looked at it for a long time. Then she wiped it clean with her sleeve, all of it, the whole morning's decrees. And wrote, in very large letters:

*THE MINISTRY IS WAITING.*

She held it up so the whole fort could see. Nobody said anything. But the fort, one hood at a time, sat down where it was, facing the curtain, and waited with her.

* * *

In the room at the end of the west wing, Coco's mother had been sewing.

She had never heard the voice before. She had come to this world inside a crystal, and woken in the intake hall reaching for a word she had not finished, and in all the weeks since she had heard people talk about the Goddess the way you talk about weather in a country you have just moved to. Something everyone else understood. Something she would learn.

Now the voice was in her room, and in her needle, and in her own head.

She sat very still on the edge of the bed with the sewing in her lap.

Then she got up and went to find her daughter. Not because she thought Coco needed her. Because she did not know what she was supposed to do, in this world, when a voice from nowhere said that a young man who had once put his hand in hers inside a slime had left the universe. And the only thing she knew how to do, in any world, was to go and stand next to her child.

She found Coco in the fort, at the low table, with the game board in front of her and her inky hands flat on it.

She did not say anything. She sat down on the cushion beside her, which took a while, because the cushions were low and the fort was not built for mothers. And Coco, without looking round, moved over to make room.

* * *

In the yard, Frieren was already running.

She had been on the bench under the ink tree with a book she was not reading. She had been not reading it for half an hour, since the four of them went through the blue light, and she had told herself several times that this was only a normal run, on a checked route, with Yuta and Milim, and that he would be back for lunch, and that he had promised Ori to describe the rooms.

The book was on the grass now. She did not remember dropping it.

She was across the yard and out past the end of the street before anyone else had moved. Not flying. Running. On her own feet, over the stones and then the grass, toward the north field and the red stake and the blue gate forty paces beyond it.

Behind her, somebody called her name. She did not stop.

Ahead, at the red stake, Anko was standing with her watch in her hand and her face white. And beside her stood Rimuru, who had been at the stake keeping the relay since the party went in. His eyes were half closed and one hand was out toward the gate, holding a line that was holding nothing.

Frieren ran. The field was longer than it had ever been. The stake did not get closer the way it should have. She counted her own steps without meaning to, the way she had counted at the white wall, and lost the count, and started again.

Somewhere around the middle of the field, she saw Rimuru's face change.

She did not know yet what it meant. She only saw his head come up, and his hand close round something she could not see, and his mouth open to call her name before she reached him.
