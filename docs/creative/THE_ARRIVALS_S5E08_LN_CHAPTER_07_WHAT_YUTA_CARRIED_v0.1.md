# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 46 — What Yuta Carried

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 46
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. The night of Day 268: Yuta cannot stop seeing the instant he caught Mau with wounds that were gone a breath later; Maki keeps him company plainly; Yuka comes in with the rope; no new explanation of the healing

*When: Day 268, late at night. Mau is asleep upstairs in the east room with Frieren in the chair beside him. The house has gone to bed early, and has not slept.*

Yuta was washing his hands for the fourth time when Maki came in.

He did not hear her. That was how he knew how tired he was. He always heard Maki. He had heard her coming down corridors in two different worlds, at every hour, in every kind of weather. Tonight he was standing at the basin in their room with his sleeves pushed up and the water gone cold, scrubbing at his palms with the rough soap Momo made, and he did not know she was there until she put the lamp down on the shelf beside him.

He stopped.

His hands were red. The soap had made them sting. There was nothing on them. There had been nothing on them the first time he washed them either.

Maki looked at his hands. Then at his face.

She did not ask. She picked up the towel from its hook and held it out.

He took it. He dried his hands, slowly, one finger at a time. Then he stood holding the towel and did not know what to do next.

"Sit down," said Maki.

He sat on the edge of the bed. She sat beside him. Not touching. Close enough.

* * *

For a long time neither of them said anything.

The room was small and plain. Two beds pushed together. A window that looked out over the south side of the house, toward the timber yard and the dark line of the low woods. His sword case leaning against the wall. Her spear beside it. A pair of Yuka's socks on the floor by the door, because Yuka had come in that morning to borrow rope and had taken her boots off to sit on their bed and argue about it, and had forgotten the socks.

Yuta looked at the socks for a while.

"He was hurt," he said at last.

Maki did not say anything.

"When he came back. At the fountain." His voice was quiet and very even. "For an instant. Before he landed. I saw him. He was—" He stopped. "His hand was open. Here." He touched his own palm. "The skin. Open. His nose was bleeding. There was something in his shoulder. His shirt was burned through at the sleeve. And his face." He stopped again. "I've seen that face before. On people who weren't going to—" He did not finish that either.

"And then," said Maki.

"And then I caught him," said Yuta. "And he was fine."

The lamp ticked on the shelf.

"Not fine," said Yuta. "Tired. Bleeding a little from the nose. But the hand was closed. The shoulder was whole. The burn was gone. In the time it took him to fall from there to my arms." He looked at his own hands again. "I've spent my whole life learning to put people back together. Reverse cursed technique. I know what it costs. I know how long it takes. I know what it feels like when it works." He turned his palms up. "That wasn't me. I didn't do anything. I didn't have time to do anything. I just held on."

"Ciel said something caught him," said Maki. "On the way back. Not her."

"I know what Ciel said."

"But you keep washing your hands."

Yuta was quiet for a long moment.

"I keep feeling it," he said. "His weight. Coming down. The second before it landed. Not knowing what I'd be holding when it did." He closed his hands. "I had a second where I thought I was going to catch him the way I caught—" He stopped. "The way I've caught people before. And have it be too late. And have to stand there with him in my arms in front of Yuka and Milim and that stranger and know there was nothing my hands could do."

Maki did not say *but there was nothing wrong with him*. She did not say *but he's fine*. She did not say *you shouldn't think like that*. She sat beside him on the edge of the bed and let it be true in the room, all of it, without trying to make it smaller.

After a while she said:

"You held on."

Yuta looked at her.

"That's what you did," said Maki. "You said it yourself. You didn't heal him. You didn't fix him. You held on. You stood at that fountain for five minutes with a stranger who'd just come out of a war, and you kept the place clear, and you kept Yuka from going after him, and you kept Milim from punching anybody, and when he came back you were there." She paused. "That's a job. It's not a small one."

"It doesn't feel like enough."

"It never does," said Maki. "That's how you know it was the right one."

* * *

The door opened without a knock.

Yuka came in. She was in her nightclothes, with her hair down and her feet bare, and she had the rope over one shoulder. The coil she had packed that morning, the one Yuta had tried and failed to take half of. She stood in the doorway and looked at the two of them sitting on the edge of the bed.

"I couldn't sleep," she said.

"Neither could we," said Maki.

Yuka came in and shut the door behind her. She saw her socks on the floor and picked them up and looked at them as if she could not remember owning socks. Then she sat down on the floor at the end of the bed, cross-legged, with the rope in her lap.

"I didn't use any of it," she said. "The rope."

"I know," said Yuta.

"I packed it in case." She turned the coil round in her hands. "In case somebody fell. In case we needed to go down something, or across something, or pull somebody out of something." She stopped. "And then somebody did fall. And there wasn't anywhere to throw it."

Yuta got down off the bed.

He sat on the floor beside her. Close. His shoulder against hers. He was her grandfather, and he was not much older than she was, and neither of them had ever known quite what to do with that. They had worked it out the way the house worked everything out, a little at a time, by sitting near each other.

"Next time," he said, "there might be somewhere to throw it."

"You don't know that."

"No," said Yuta. "But I'll carry half of it. So you're not carrying all of it by yourself."

Yuka looked at him for a long moment.

Then she unwound one loop of the rope from the coil and put it in his hands.

He held it.

Maki got down off the bed too, which was very undignified for Maki, and sat on Yuka's other side, so that Yuka was between them on the floor at the end of the bed with the rope across all three of their laps.

"This is stupid," said Yuka. Her voice wobbled.

"Very," Maki agreed.

"We're sitting on the floor holding a rope."

"Yes."

"In the middle of the night."

"You started it," said Maki. "You brought the rope."

Yuka laughed. It came out wet. She put her face in her hands, and her shoulders went, and Yuta put his arm round her, and Maki put her hand on the back of Yuka's neck and left it there.

They sat there for a long time.

At some point Yuka fell asleep against Yuta's shoulder, with the rope still in her lap. Neither of them moved her. Maki took the lamp down off the shelf and turned it very low and set it on the floor beside them.

Yuta looked at his hands, in the low light. One of them was round his granddaughter's shoulders. The other was holding a loop of rope.

He did not get up to wash them again.
