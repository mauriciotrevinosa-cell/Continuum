# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 45 — The Missing Minutes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 45
**Continuity state:** Mau wakes after the Exchange rest; chosen recollection supplies the household's first account of the war rather than a fabricated live recording

*When: Day 269, morning.*

The ceiling was wood.

Mau looked at it for a long time before the relief found the rest of him.

There was a little knot in the beam above the bed that looked like a face if the morning light came from the wrong direction. It looked like a face now. He had never been so pleased to see it.

His hand was caught beneath Frieren's.

She was asleep in the chair, folded at an angle that would give somebody else a terrible neck. The book in her lap had closed around one finger. He moved his thumb against her hand.

She woke at once.

"Hi," he said.

Her eyes stayed on his face. Then she moved the book to the floor and came to sit on the bed.

"How long?" he asked.

"It's morning."

He tried to count backward and stopped at the fountain.

"Ori?"

"Knows you're here. Sleeping. She'll be in after breakfast, if you don't wake Milim first."

He smiled a little. His mouth felt dry enough to split.

Frieren helped him sit against the pillows and gave him water. He looked at the skin across his knuckles. Whole. Fine new lines, almost too pale to see, marked the places he remembered coming apart.

He flexed his fingers.

"I wasn't like this."

"Yuta saw."

His hand stopped moving.

"For an instant," she said. "He caught you."

Mau set the cup on the bedside table with both hands.

Shoko arrived before he could think of a way to say any of the rest. She inspected his eyes, listened to his breathing, and asked him to describe the pain without using the word fine.

"Heavy," he said. "Everywhere. Like I've been awake too long."

"Anything sharp?"

"Not now."

"Then now is what we're checking."

She let him eat sitting up. The soup had been kept for him; Rem came to tell him so, and to ask him to stop looking guilty at it. He drank some while Frieren held the bowl. When she offered to let him take it, he shook his head.

"This is working."

"I agree," Frieren said.

Only after he had eaten did Yuta come in. He stayed near the door until Mau held out a hand.

"You got somebody else," Mau said.

Yuta sat beside the bed.

"You saw him?"

"Coming back. I think. Dark coat?"

"Sung Jin-Woo. He gave us his name. He wanted to go back."

Mau closed his eyes.

"There were people—soldiers, I think. Made of shadows. They were waiting for him. Holding. I landed in the place he was supposed to be."

He described the ground first, because that was easier than describing the rest. The stone that had fallen apart. The soldiers getting up. The space where the sky ought to have been. Frieren's fingers rested against his wrist, and when his breath shortened she waited instead of asking the next question.

"How long?" Yuta asked finally.

"I don't know. A long time. Not hours. It felt like most of an hour."

Yuta looked at Frieren.

"Anko had a watch," he said. "A little over five minutes here."

Mau stared at him.

He had eaten half a sandwich before leaving. There should have been a useful calculation somewhere in that fact. He couldn't find one.

"Was she loud?" he asked.

Neither of them needed to ask who.

"Everyone heard," Frieren said.

He looked down at their hands.

"I didn't mean to go."

"We know," Yuta said.

The answer came so quickly that Mau had to look at him again.

Yuta's face was tired. He had evidently slept, but sleep had not finished with whatever he had brought home from the fountain.

"You didn't get up," he said. "You told us what you could feel. Then you were gone. We need to understand it. That's different."

Mau nodded. A small movement. Enough.

From the connecting door came Ori's voice, asking whether breakfast was over for him yet.

Frieren called her in.

She came to the bed, looked at him carefully, and put his terrible map on the blanket.

"You have to explain this," she said.

He touched the line she meant.

"That was meant to be a fountain."

"I know. Milim told me."

"Then why—"

"I wanted you to tell me."

He made room beside him. She climbed up, and he began with the blue door.
