# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 47 — What We Can Say

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 47
**Continuity state:** The small debrief distinguishes observed contact and accidental activation from intent; any later outing requires a fresh morning check and unchanged basic rules

*When: Day 269, afternoon.*

Yuka brought the rope to the debrief.

She did not mean to. She had been carrying it while deciding where the unused supplies belonged, and when Yuta called her she came with it still looped over one shoulder.

Mau was in the reading chair. Frieren had taken the bed. Rimuru sat on the floor where he could hear without getting between anyone and the door. Shoko stood by the window, and Maomao had found the only place on the shelf where a page could lie flat.

Yuka put down the rope.

"I didn't use any."

"Good," Yuta said.

It was such an ordinary answer that she almost got angry with him. Then she saw him looking at Mau and remembered he had spent those minutes there too.

They began with the ant.

Not the war. Not the voice everyone had heard. The dry basin, the injured-looking leg, the way it had crossed to Mau's hand after they stopped approaching it. Yuka described it from her side. Milim, summoned from the connecting room, corrected the position of one bag and demanded to know whether anybody had actually eaten the food they brought home.

"Later," Shoko said.

"That's what everyone keeps saying."

Mau told them the word. He told them where it had come from.

Yuka looked at his face rather than his hand.

"So it didn't make you walk again."

"No."

"It made a mistake."

"I think so. I asked. It apologized."

The room went quiet for a moment. Rimuru's eyes moved toward the window. Frieren looked at Mau, then down at the hem of the blanket under her hand.

Yuka had imagined something inside him making a decision over his head. She had been angry at that thing while being unable to find a place to put the anger. An accident did not make the fountain less empty. It changed what she wanted to ask it.

"Can it happen with a different word?"

"We don't know yet," Rimuru said.

They wrote that down.

It was the best part of the meeting, unexpectedly. Not knowing something could go onto a page instead of becoming an argument.

They agreed to keep contact with unknown remains out of Mau's job, even apparently harmless ones. Read functions without attempting activation; when that distinction was not clear, stop the read. No new experiment because the old one had ended without a funeral.

Mau agreed before anyone had to persuade him.

Then he looked at Shoko.

"I'd still like to finish the ordinary run. Later. Not today."

Yuka heard the breath Frieren took beside him.

"Why?" Maomao asked.

"Because I wanted to go with them. Because we haven't reached the end of the checked route. And I'd like the first thing after coming home not to be that I can never go anywhere again."

He said it without looking around for somebody to defend him.

Yuta rested his hands on his knees.

"I'd go with you," he said. "Same route. We check what changed before making plans about anything beyond it."

Milim nodded. Yuka heard herself say yes a moment after he did.

Shoko did not answer immediately. She went through the readings with Maomao and Ciel, relayed by Rimuru. Yesterday's harm had not stayed in his tissues as it should have. None of them could reproduce the intervention that had done that. He was still tired. They would not use the absence of injuries to pretend he had had an ordinary afternoon.

"Tomorrow's examination," Shoko said finally. "If the recovery continues, a return can be considered under the same restrictions. That's the answer today."

Mau nodded.

Frieren pushed her foot against his ankle.

"And if there's another ant," she said, "you may admire it from the correct side."

He laughed softly.

"I deserved that."

Yuka picked up the rope when they stood. Yuta reached for it automatically. She let him take one loop and kept the other.

They carried it downstairs between them, badly, until Maki made them stop blocking the corridor.
