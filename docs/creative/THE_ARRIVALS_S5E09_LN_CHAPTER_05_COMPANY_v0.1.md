# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 15 — Company

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 15
**Continuity state:** Frieren chooses ordinary closeness; the previous choice conversation remains repaired; Mau's companions receive their own account rather than automatic mind access

*When: Day 269, evening.*

After supper, Frieren brought the wrong book to bed.

Mau recognized the cover before she opened it. One of the flower books from the market. Not the one they had been reading, and not at a place she usually pretended to have forgotten.

"A change?"

"You haven't heard this part."

"You haven't learned this part."

"Then we're both fortunate."

She arranged herself against him. He was sitting up, the blanket over his legs, tired in a way that let him know where all his bones were. There was comfort in being able to name the tiredness so simply.

She read a passage, stopped, and argued with a note in the margin.

Mau watched her ear move when she disapproved. He had missed it for less than an hour by his own reckoning. He did not know why the missing could still feel so enormous.

"It was very far," he said.

She put a finger against the page.

"The other place."

"Yes."

"Did you think about coming back?"

"Mostly I thought about the next thing that was going to hit me."

She did not move away from the answer.

"Then about coming back," he said. "And that I'd lost the food. Ridiculous things."

"Food is important."

"Milim agrees."

She was quiet long enough for him to hear somebody crossing the corridor. The steps stopped at their door. Gojo asked whether this was an inappropriate time, in a voice that strongly suggested he hoped it was.

"Yes," Frieren said.

"Good. Yuta wanted to know."

"I did not," Yuta said from farther away.

Mau laughed. "Come in for a minute."

Frieren did not get off him. Gojo opened the door, assessed the situation, and wisely chose the side of the shelf without the five flowers. Yuta stayed by the door. Rimuru peered down from the loft above the closet and said he had been here the entire time, which was a discouraging thing to learn after any conversation.

"You were asleep," Frieren said.

"I was trying to be polite."

Mau told them a little more. Not every wound. The dark soldiers rising, the way distance had become unreliable, the relief of getting the world's pressure back. Gojo lost his smile at that part.

"No cap isn't the same as control," he said.

"I worked that out."

"Expensive lesson."

"I didn't sign up."

Gojo nodded. He left the rest of the observation unsaid, and Mau was grateful for it.

Yuta asked whether the stranger had seen him. Mau described the brief passing look on the way back. Yuta gave him the name again, and this time he remembered it.

Sung Jin-Woo.

A name belonged to someone. It helped to have one instead of only the war.

They did not stay long. Gojo left first, taking Yuta with him to find out whether there was tea left that Shoko had not claimed. Rimuru went back to his loft with a remark about some people respecting bedtime.

Frieren reopened the book.

"You're still taking me to dinner," she said.

Mau looked down at her.

"I know."

"Good. I don't want this to become a reason you stop asking."

He rested his cheek against her hair.

"It won't."

She began the passage again. This time she read the troublesome note in a different voice, making the long-dead author sound so thoroughly foolish that Mau laughed until she had to wait for him.

They did not get very far.

Before putting the book away, he asked her what she wanted to eat.

She considered the matter with proper seriousness.

"Something you don't have to apologize for burning."

"High expectations."

"It's our anniversary."
