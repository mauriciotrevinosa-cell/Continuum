# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 45 — The Way They Left

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 45
**Continuity state:** A fresh medical check permits the second limited outing; the normal instance is reentered before reset with the same four and Rimuru at home

*When: Day 270, morning.*

This time Yuta carried the food.

Milim objected as soon as she saw him fasten the bag.

"It's a supply arrangement," he said.

"It's an accusation."

Mau leaned against the courtyard wall and tried not to laugh before they had even left. His examination had taken longer than the first one. Shoko had made him walk, rest, and walk again. Maomao had asked the same question in three different ways until he stopped trying to make the answer convenient.

They had said yes. To this, today, with the same conditions.

He had gone upstairs to tell Ori first. She had given him the corrected map, which he now carried inside his shirt rather than in the bag. Frieren had kissed him goodbye and then put a finger on the place where the map lay.

"This comes back too."

"It has better odds than the lunch."

She had not laughed immediately. When she did, it was small and real, and he had waited for it without trying to help.

At the stake, Rimuru checked the new plan with them. The three known rooms first. The corridor beyond, scouted only as far as they could keep the return turns clear. One larger room had been seen from its threshold by another party; if its occupant was beyond what Yuta and Milim could handle without Mau, they came out. There was no agreement to pursue a different floor or a passage behind the last room.

Yuka repeated the turn-back time. Anko wrote it down.

"If we return early, do I get credit?" Mau asked.

"You get lunch," she said.

"A powerful incentive."

The blue surface took them one after another.

The entrance was still an alcove of pale stone. The chalk mark survived. Yuta checked it, and the way back, before he went through the turn.

Mau felt his shoulders tighten when they reached the fountain.

He had expected it. Expecting it did not make the basin less ordinary. There was no black shape under the roots, no torn edge, no little leg caught against the stone. The crystal pouch hung from Yuka's belt. She touched it once and took her hand away.

Yuta came to stand beside him.

"We can leave."

Mau looked at the rim where he had put his palm.

"Give me a second."

Yuta gave him several.

The silence inside him stayed a working silence, not a pull. He told Ciel what he could feel. She checked what she could, and did not say the place was safe simply because she had no warning to give.

Mau breathed until the room became a room again.

"All right."

Yuka looked at him from the passage mouth.

"Ready, Oppa?"

He adjusted the strap over his shoulder.

"You still have all the rope?"

"Grandpa tried. He failed."

Yuta sighed. Milim grinned at him with her whole face.

They went on.

The next corridor bent twice. Mau marked the turns on Ori's map without improving its quality. In the little room beyond, a chest stood under a stone window that looked out on nothing at all.

Milim stopped well short of it.

"Your turn," she told Yuka.

Yuka considered the chest, then the hinge, then the space underneath.

"That one," she said, pointing at its shadow.

It moved when the chest did not.

Yuta shifted his grip. Milim's grin became considerably worse for the chest.

"Can I?"

"Now," Yuka said.

Mau stood by the correct side of the doorway and let them.
