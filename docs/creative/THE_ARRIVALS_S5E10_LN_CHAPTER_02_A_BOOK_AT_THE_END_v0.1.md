# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 51 — A Book At The End

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 51
**Continuity state:** Yuta and Milim defeat the normal instance's guardian with Yuka's route work; three physical grimoires are found, without guaranteed future drops or new shop stock

*When: Day 270, late morning.*

The larger room had once belonged to books, or had been made to look as if it had.

Shelves climbed three walls. Most held nothing but blocks of pale stone cut to the proportions of books. The fourth wall had a doorway with no door, and above it a carved symbol Mau could not read.

In the middle stood a figure made from the same stone as the shelves.

It was taller than Yuta and narrow through the waist, with long arms ending in plates instead of hands. When they reached the threshold, one plate turned toward them. A seam opened across it.

Yuta stepped back before the first shard struck the doorway.

"Behind the turn," he said to Mau.

Mau went. Yuka came with him long enough to uncoil the rope.

"The left shelf leans," she said.

He had noticed. Not the reason it mattered.

She showed him the base: a gap where the guardian's first movement had shifted it against the floor. Above, the carved books were all one piece with the shelf. Heavy. Mau passed her the rope without trying to make her plan his plan.

Milim took another shard against her gauntlet. She came back to the doorway, scowling at the mark it had left.

"I can break it."

"Stay out of the seam," Yuta said. "It opens before it fires."

"Then I'll break it before that."

"That's the plan."

Yuka waited for the next volley. Stone cracked against the threshold. Milim went low, fast enough that Mau lost the start of the movement, and struck the nearest arm before its plate could turn. The seam burst. The guardian pivoted toward her, leaving its back to Yuta.

His blade cut a white line down one leg.

The stone did not give. Something inside it did. The knee bent the wrong way, caught itself, and dragged the rest of the figure around with a grinding sound.

Yuka threw the loop over the leaning shelf's broken corner.

She did not try to pull the whole thing down. She pulled the rope across the path the guardian had to take to reach the doorway. It stepped into the line, still turning, and for an instant its weight went exactly where she wanted it.

The shelf shifted.

"Milim!"

Milim leaped away from the falling stone. Yuta went toward the figure instead, inside the angle where the plate could not reach him. The shelf struck its shoulder and held it down long enough for him to cut into the exposed join beneath the other arm.

Blue light ran through the seam.

The figure fell apart.

Mau kept waiting until Yuta said his name. Then he came out from behind the turn with the bag, feeling rather proud of having done the least exciting part correctly.

Milim was examining her gauntlet.

"I could have broken it."

"You did break it," Yuka said.

"More of it."

There were crystals among the fallen pieces. Yuka counted them into the pouch while Yuta checked the far doorway. A shallow recess lay behind it. No corridor beyond. No stairs down. Just a stone table with a wooden box on it, very much out of place among all the false books.

They checked the box from the doorway. They checked the table. They spent long enough checking that Milim sat on the floor and asked for her lunch.

The box was a box.

Inside were three books wrapped in waxed cloth.

Mau did not touch them until Yuta had lifted one and found ordinary pages beneath the cover. The script was unfamiliar. In the margin there was a drawing of a little candle with a carefully barred line of wind beside it.

He smiled before he could stop himself.

"She'll know," Yuta said.

"She'll tell me how badly I've misunderstood all of them."

"That sounds about right."

They packed the books between cloth and the spare food. Mau took the bag and found that its weight was ordinary too. Paper, covers, something they had actually carried out of a room.

He looked back once at the great shelves full of things that had never been books.

Three real ones seemed plenty.

They ate sitting near the entrance before leaving. Milim got the largest portion. Yuka made a point of keeping her own. Yuta leaned against the wall beside Mau with his boots stretched out.

"A successful expedition," Mau said.

Yuta looked at him.

"You're allowed to say that before something goes wrong."

"I know."

He ate the rest of the sandwich.

Nothing did.
