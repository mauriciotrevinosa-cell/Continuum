# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 52 — Level One

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 52
**Continuity state:** Three grimoires enter the loot record before private gifts; Mau's personal unfinished stats branch appears at level one; nobody else receives a System or a public bracelet display

*When: Day 270, afternoon.*

Frieren saw the bag before Mau saw her.

She was trying, badly, to look as if she had come to the stake for another reason. Fern stood beside Anko's page, checking the crystals against Yuka's count. Stark had come to carry anything heavy and found himself holding an empty food wrapper.

"We ate it," Milim explained.

"I can see that."

Mau handed over the bag for the loot record. Frieren stepped nearer.

"Three," Anko said.

"Three what?" Frieren asked.

Anko looked at her.

Frieren did not attempt the pretence again.

They unwrapped the books on a clean cloth. Fern checked for damp. Frieren's eyes moved over the first page, paused, and went back to the beginning.

"Well?" Mau asked.

"Keeping a candle lit in a draught."

Milim leaned over her shoulder.

"That's it?"

"That's a useful spell."

"I can do that with my hands."

"You can also burn down the table."

Milim considered the criticism fair enough not to answer.

The second book concerned finding a dropped needle. The third described a mark to keep rain off a small bundle. Frieren read the qualification on that one twice, then told Mau it was not a spell for staying dry simply because a person could theoretically curl up inside a bundle.

"I hadn't asked."

"You were going to."

Fern entered the books in the record before asking what the expedition wanted done with them. There were practical uses for all three. Nobody proposed hiding them from the house; nobody proposed that common use required keeping them away from the person who could actually study them.

"I'd like to give them to Frieren," Mau said. "If we can agree on that. She can teach anything that works."

Yuka and Yuta agreed. Milim wanted a demonstration of the candle when it was learned. Fern considered the proposed arrangement, wrote a note about study and access, and closed the page.

Frieren gathered the cloth.

"Are these dinner presents?"

"You're not supposed to know about that yet."

"I agreed to the dinner."

"The presents."

She looked at the books in her arms.

"Then you should have concealed them better."

***

The little window appeared while Mau was washing his hands.

He turned his head. It turned with him. He stepped back from the basin, and it stayed in the same place before his eyes.

Unlike the shop at the gate, nobody else stopped.

*Status.*

Under it, a name. His name. Then a line he could understand without knowing how it had been written.

*Level: 1.*

Several fields beneath it were still indistinct, as though the writing had been rubbed away before drying. The line for species had no answer.

He did not touch anything.

"Ciel."

*I can see that you are attending to something. Will you describe it?*

He did. Then he called Rimuru, who came up from the office and stood beside him, looking at the empty space where Mau pointed.

"Not the same as the shop, then."

"You can't see it?"

"No. Don't keep pushing it toward me."

Mau lowered his hand.

Umaru arrived because Rimuru had said the word status while crossing the common room. She demanded a description and received one, followed by a much longer silence than he expected.

"Don't spend anything," she said.

"I don't think there's anything to spend."

"Good. Keep it that way until we know what the fields do."

The window flickered. One of the lower sections shifted, filling in a little before becoming indistinct again. Mau felt the familiar work behind it, the Noise comparing an acquired shape to things that did not yet fit.

"It's still making it," he said.

Umaru's expression changed from alarm to an interest so concentrated that Rimuru took a small step back.

"Then we need paper."

"You always need paper."

"This is different paper."

She went to find it. Mau let the window close instead of trying to open the parts it would not show.

Level one.

It was the first number he had seen that seemed to admit it did not know everything about him.
