# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 55 — A Small Distance

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 55
**Continuity state:** Mau's first controlled natural levitation uses the existing tiny mana supply with efficiency and instruction; it is not an elf-route unlock or a level reward

*When: Day 272, afternoon.*

The floor was closer than Mau expected.

That was partly because he had managed a height of about two fingers, and partly because Frieren had put a blanket under him and told him to stop looking so pleased with himself.

"I haven't started looking pleased yet."

"You're preparing to."

He was sitting cross-legged above the blanket. His heels trembled. The effort felt different from lifting himself with a stolen push of space, different again from waiting for one of the dangerous patterns to supply a result his body could not pay for.

This was the little supply he had used for the flowers, made to do a new and very unreasonable thing.

Frieren had spent two mornings teaching him how to stop wasting it before she let him try. Gojo had watched the filtered part of the lesson and, with some restraint, restricted himself to two comments and one objection about posture. Ciel had called the first attempt inconclusive. The second had been a slow slide off a cushion.

Now the blanket wasn't touching him.

"Keep it level," Frieren said.

"Which part?"

"You."

He began to laugh and promptly sat down.

She looked at him.

"Very useful."

"I was up."

"For a little while."

"That's how up starts."

Her mouth moved at one corner. She helped him change the position of his hands and asked what had happened to the flow when he laughed.

He described it. She corrected the part he had mistaken for a successful adjustment. Ciel checked the effort against the readings they had agreed to watch. The boundaries had stayed shut; there was no reason to go looking behind them for a larger result.

*A short, controlled operation,* she said. *Not evidence of a new reserve.*

"Tell Gojo I know," Mau said.

"He isn't here," Frieren said.

"He'll want it said to him anyway."

He tried once more. Lower this time. He stayed there until she told him to come down, and did, with more dignity than the first landing.

Then she put a cup in his hands and made him rest.

Ori, who had been watching from the courtyard step, came to look beneath his legs.

"Could you go higher?"

"Eventually, I hope."

"Could you carry something?"

"That's a different lesson," Frieren said.

Ori looked at her. Then at Mau. Then, with a very suspicious amount of understanding, at the book beside the chair.

"Oh."

"Nothing has been agreed," Mau said.

Frieren picked up the book.

"Not yet."

They put the blanket away before trying to turn the result into an afternoon's entertainment. Mau's legs were tired in a new place. He went to tell Shoko that before she could hear a much more exciting version from Milim.

By supper she had heard it anyway.

"Two fingers," he said as he sat down.

Shoko held her hand above the table at exactly the right height.

"I'll update the aviation report."

Rimuru laughed into his cup.

Frieren passed Mau the bread with the expression of someone who had made an excellent investment and could afford to wait.
