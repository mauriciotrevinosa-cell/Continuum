# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 49 — The Price Of Dinner

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 49
**Continuity state:** Hollis supplies dinner goods through existing trade; the gate has reset on Day 273; Bocchi and Mau have reciprocal ordinary time while preparations continue

*When: Day 274, afternoon.*

Hollis did not laugh at the list.

This was generous of him, because Mau had crossed out the wine twice and put it back in the same place.

"For two?"

"Yes."

"These quantities are for rather more than two."

"I don't know how much any of it is."

"That," Hollis said, "is why you ask someone who does."

He went through it item by item. There were things he could get, things he could not promise, and one item Mau had described so badly that they agreed to abandon it before it acquired a price.

The wine came from a small remaining stock. Good, Hollis said, without pretending that expensive was a flavour. A little cheese. Preserved fruit. Something fresh he expected to bring on the next visit, if it survived the journey as well as he hoped.

Mau asked the price before he asked for anything else.

They worked out what could come from the things Mau was entitled to exchange, and what needed agreement before it came out of the house's supplies. Hollis would not turn a promise of future crystals into present food without telling him so. Mau appreciated it. He wanted a dinner, not a mysterious debt.

"Three evenings from now," he said.

"I can bring what I have by then. I won't promise what I don't."

"That's enough."

He hesitated before asking about the other request.

Hollis shook his head.

"Still looking. Starting material for a crop isn't the same thing as a little tobacco somebody kept dry."

"I know. Keep looking?"

"I said I would."

Mau thanked him and let the gift remain unfinished. It was easier now that he had actually shown Shoko what he meant to try. He could walk past the empty starter frame without treating it as a failure.

On the way back from the stores he heard a guitar stop, start, and stop in exactly the same place.

Bocchi sat under the shade with the instrument across her knees. Kita had gone to find a missing page; a small pile of possible pages lay beside Bocchi, none of them apparently the one.

"Can I?" Mau asked, pointing at the bench.

She nodded.

He sat without asking whether she was practising for anything. She played the passage again. This time he heard where it changed.

"That one," he said.

She stopped.

"Which?"

He tried to sing the bit he meant. Her expression became so distressed that he stopped halfway.

"That bad?"

"I know which one you mean."

"A merciful answer."

She played it for him. Then the other version. He listened properly this time and gave an answer she could disagree with.

She did, quietly, for nearly a minute.

It made him so happy that he had to look at the tree instead of her.

"What?" she asked.

"Nothing. I liked the argument."

Her shoulders rose. Then she looked at the list sticking out of his pocket.

"Is that for dinner?"

He took it out.

"Am I concealing any part of this successfully?"

She considered telling him something kind. He could see the whole effort on her face.

"Not from her," she said.

He laughed. "Then help me with one thing. Does this sound romantic or like I'm preparing for winter?"

She read the list. She pointed at the quantity Hollis had crossed out.

"Winter."

Kita came back with the page at that moment, found them laughing over expensive cheese, and demanded the whole explanation before she would hand it over.

Mau stayed until Momo called him to peel something.

On the way inside he passed the new gate report on the wall. Week twenty-five had begun yesterday. The next change was Day two hundred and eighty.

He checked his dinner day against it one more time.

Three days after dinner.

He folded the list and went to find Fern.
