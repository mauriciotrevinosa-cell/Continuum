# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 56 — Room For Two

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 56
**Continuity state:** Repeated low-load trials allow brief domestic levitation with Frieren at low height; the one-metre unloaded exercise is not cargo capacity or sustained combat flight

*When: Day 276, late morning.*

The first passenger was a book.

Frieren selected it with care. Mau had expected a thin one. He should have known better.

"That's a house brick."

"It's an appropriate text."

"For building houses?"

She put it on his stomach. He lay flat on the blanket in the courtyard, already hovering low enough that the grass could touch the cloth beneath him. The extra weight settled him a little. He corrected too quickly, felt the flow wobble, and came down before Ciel could tell him to.

Frieren took the book off.

"Again after the rest."

They had spent several days making very little progress in a very useful direction. He could rise close to a metre now when he carried nothing and concentrated on doing only that. It was higher than the blanket had any right to be. It did not mean he could carry the courtyard or take somebody over the roof.

With weight, they stayed low.

With movement, they stayed slower than a walk.

Frieren taught him the adjustment. Ciel watched the load and the familiar warnings. The Noise kept its own work behind the boundaries, and no sudden flood of borrowed strength arrived to make the exercise impressive.

It was, Mau thought, the best thing about it.

After the book came a folded blanket. After that, a carefully measured sack. By the time Frieren said she could test it herself, he had spent so long with ordinary objects balanced over his ribs that an elf seemed a perfectly reasonable next choice.

She sat beside his hip first, keeping one foot on the ground.

"You can stop," she said.

"You can stop looking as if you've waited three hundred years to do this."

"I haven't."

"Only two hundred?"

She lifted the grounded foot.

They sank. Mau corrected slowly this time, enough to find the balance without chasing the height. Frieren shifted closer and settled across him with the book open against his chest.

For a few breaths nothing happened.

Nothing was exactly what they had been working toward.

Then she started reading.

Mau looked down the line of her hair at the grass beneath them.

"Comfortable?"

"Quite."

"Any medical remarks from the audience?"

Shoko, sitting at the table with Maomao, lifted her cup.

"Don't turn the corner into the kitchen until you can stop before it."

Frieren looked up.

"A good idea."

"That wasn't the idea," Shoko said.

Rimuru had come outside with a plate. He stopped chewing.

Mau saw his face and began to laugh. Frieren put a hand on his chest.

"Level."

He managed it this time.

They moved the length of the blanket, slowly, toward the table. Not all the way to the kitchen. Not even close. Frieren made the journey seem much farther by behaving as though she had been transported to another principality and wished to inspect its bread.

Rimuru held the plate out of reach.

"I refuse to encourage this."

"You don't have to encourage it," she said. "You may pass it."

He looked at Mau.

Mau was grinning too much to help him.

"You've lost completely," Rimuru said.

"Yes."

The bread changed hands.

They came down when the agreed short interval ended. Frieren got off before he sat up. His arms were not shaking. The tiredness was there, but it was the tiredness they had expected, and he said so.

Shoko made him sit and drink anyway.

Frieren stayed beside him with the bread and the book. She looked extremely pleased with an operation that had covered less ground than a person could cross in three steps.

"Tomorrow," she said.

"Tomorrow I have plans."

"After tomorrow, then."

He leaned against her shoulder.

"After tomorrow."
