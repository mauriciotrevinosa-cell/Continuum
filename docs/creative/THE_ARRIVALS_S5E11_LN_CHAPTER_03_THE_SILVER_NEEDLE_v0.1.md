# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 61 — The Silver Needle

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 61
**Continuity state:** Frieren prepares the existing compass as her own anniversary gift; dinner supplies arrive through Hollis and household preparations preserve privacy

*When: Day 277, afternoon.*

The crack had not spread.

Frieren held the compass toward the window and checked it again. Old silver, tarnished in the places no cloth could easily reach. A fine break across the face. The needle shifted when she moved it, then pointed back toward the hand holding it, faithfully useless to its owner.

Gojo had stood inside a mimic to get it.

She remembered his pleased expression when the thing failed to bite him, and the much quieter one when he gave her what he had found. She had kept the compass near the books afterward. Sometimes in a pocket. Sometimes on the arm of the reading chair. An object she liked without needing it to justify being kept.

She had taken it to the field after the other voice spoke. It had gone on pointing at her while she waited for news of Mau.

It could not help with that.

But it could help him find the direction of home, if home happened to be reading something and refusing to move until she had finished the paragraph.

She turned it over.

Mai, sitting on the bed, watched her test the catch.

"You're sure you want to give it away?"

"I'm giving it to him."

"I noticed."

Frieren closed it and set it on the clean cloth.

"I want him to have it."

Mai accepted that. Frieren had already told her they would miss the usual roof gathering tonight. Mai had said she would save any interesting gossip, which was a threat Frieren intended to repay later. She helped fold the cloth without telling Frieren how the conversation ought to go. Outside the window, somebody was trying to move a small table without letting it scrape. By the third attempt, both women had stopped pretending not to hear.

"He knows you have ears, right?" Mai asked.

"He forgets when he's concentrating."

Downstairs, Mau received the last parcel from Hollis and nearly lost the cheese before it reached the kitchen. Milim had asked only to look. He had made the mistake of believing the distinction would survive contact with it.

Rem rescued the parcel. Ram told Milim that inspecting it from farther away would develop her character.

Momo took the fresh ingredients, checked them, and set Mau to washing his hands before he touched anything. He had arranged the meal with her beforehand, choosing work he could actually do rather than planning to surprise Frieren with several untested skills at once.

He peeled, cut, and followed instructions. Rem kept the timing. Ram tasted a little sauce and told him it had survived him remarkably well.

He thanked her with exaggerated politeness. She accepted it with none.

Ori arrived with a flower that she had been careful not to break at the stem.

"For the table."

He took it as though it were glass.

"Will you put it there?"

She nodded. Then she asked whether they would eat before everybody else or after.

"A little after. Why?"

"Milim wants to know how long you need."

"Tell Milim I'll know if she takes the cheese."

"That isn't what she meant."

He looked at her. She looked back with the patient face she had acquired from Frieren in ways neither of them would admit.

"I won't be late coming back," he said.

"You can be late. It's dinner. Just tell us."

She took the little cup for the flower and went out to the courtyard.

Mau stood with wet hands over the basin for a moment longer than the washing needed.

Momo nudged the towel toward him.

"You'll burn something if you keep thinking that hard."

"I haven't got anything on the fire."

"Give it time."

He dried his hands and went back to the meal.

By the time Frieren came downstairs, the small table stood in the sheltered part of the courtyard, clear of the path around the tree. Two chairs. A clean cloth. Ori's flower. No crowd waiting to see how he had done.

Mau stood beside it with a bottle whose label he had stopped trying to pronounce.

Frieren touched the wrapped compass in her pocket.

For once, she left her book upstairs.
