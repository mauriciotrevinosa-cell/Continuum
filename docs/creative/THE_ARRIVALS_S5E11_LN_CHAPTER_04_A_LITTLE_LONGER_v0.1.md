# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 24 — A Little Longer

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** The overdue anniversary dinner occurs; Frieren gives Mau the established S4 compass that points at her, transferring custody without inventing new tracking powers or an engagement

*When: Day 277, evening.*

Mau pulled out her chair.

Frieren sat down before he could decide whether he was doing it incorrectly. He looked relieved enough that she almost asked him to try again.

She decided to be kind.

"It smells good."

"That part had supervision."

"A wise beginning."

He sat opposite her. The table was small enough that she could reach his hand without leaning. She liked that. The last daylight came through the courtyard leaves and made the glass on the table look more expensive than it was.

He poured a little wine. Not very much. She watched him measure his own portion by eye and stop before she had to say anything.

"Happy anniversary," he said.

She lifted her glass.

"Eventually."

He laughed.

They drank. Then he asked whether she liked it, which made her drink again to decide. He watched with such attention that she took her time.

"I do."

His shoulders loosened. She wondered how many things he had been waiting for her to like before allowing himself to eat.

She gave him bread.

"Now you."

The food was simple enough that they could talk over it. He told her which part he had made, then revised the description when she asked a precise question. She thanked him for the work he had actually done. He looked happier with that than he would have looked with a more extravagant compliment.

They discussed the books. The rain mark had a limitation in the third diagram that she wanted to test before trusting it with anything important. The needle spell appeared useful even when one already knew where the needle had fallen, which Mau said sounded suspiciously like losing a thing with extra steps.

She explained why he was wrong.

He listened with his chin on his hand.

"What?" she asked when she noticed his expression.

"I like this part."

"The correction?"

"The whole thing."

She looked down at her plate and found that the next explanation had become harder to begin.

He let her have the silence without making her blush more on purpose. After a while he talked about the first place he remembered them eating together, and she corrected his recollection of how much he had eaten. They disagreed about a walk for long enough to establish that they meant two different walks.

There had been more than enough of them to do that.

She was glad.

When the plates were mostly empty, Mau rested his hand between them.

"I'm sorry we missed the day."

"We both did."

"I know. I still wanted to say it."

She turned his hand palm up and put hers against it.

"Then thank you for asking me now."

For a while they stayed like that. The sounds from the common room carried through the open door: dishes, somebody laughing, a protest that might have belonged to Umaru. Nobody came out to require an answer from them.

Frieren took the cloth-wrapped object from her pocket.

"I brought something too."

Mau looked at it, then at her.

"You didn't have to."

"Neither did you. Open it."

He unfolded the cloth carefully. The silver caught the light. He saw the crack first, then the needle.

"Your compass."

"Yours."

His thumb stopped on the rim.

She had thought through the words upstairs. They seemed foolish now that he was looking at her, so she used fewer of them.

"It points at me."

"I know."

"Then you'll know which way."

He turned it. The needle swung, settled, and came back to her. He turned it again, farther this time, and watched it do the same thing. His face changed slowly, losing the answer he might have made to keep the moment easy.

"Frieren."

"Gojo found it. In a mimic. He gave it to me."

"I remember."

"I like it very much."

He looked up.

"That's why?"

She nodded.

He held it between both hands. The tarnish and the old crack remained. She had not tried to make it look new for him. She wanted him to have the thing she had kept, not a better-looking replacement.

"For our anniversary," she said, because that part mattered too.

He stood, came around the small table, and crouched beside her chair. She turned toward him before he could ask.

The kiss was warm and a little awkward with his hand still protecting the compass. She put a hand behind his neck and kept him there until the awkwardness had become something she liked.

When he drew back, his forehead stayed against hers.

"Thank you."

She touched the closed silver case.

"You should put it somewhere you'll remember."

"I think I can manage that."

"You lost a whole anniversary."

He laughed against her cheek. She did too, quietly, unable to keep her face arranged now that he was close enough to see.

He put the compass in an inner pocket, away from the edge where it might fall out if he bent. Then he sat in the other chair, closer this time, so she did not have to reach across the table to find his hand.

The light went slowly from the leaves.

They had dessert after that, and no one interrupted it.
