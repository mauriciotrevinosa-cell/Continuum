# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 27
## Chapter 59 — Your Turn

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 27
**Reading order:** 59
**Continuity state:** Mau reveals the agreed Frieren-led outing after the Day280 reset; three preparation days remain; the compass goes home with Mau and the couple's ordinary closeness continues

*When: Day 277, late evening.*

There was one more thing.

Mau waited until Frieren had finished the last of the preserved fruit before saying so. It was a precaution. She had been paying the fruit a great deal of attention, and he wanted a fair chance.

"Another present?"

"Sort of."

"You already gave me three books."

"I know."

"And dinner."

"You're making this sound like a complaint."

"It isn't. Continue."

He turned his chair a little toward hers.

"I like going places with you," he said. "When you're showing me something."

Frieren looked at him with the expression she gave a statement whose obviousness might be concealing a useful second part.

"You haven't seen most of what I could show you."

"I know. I'd like to be there when you find some of it too."

She stopped turning her empty glass.

"You've spent a lot of time waiting at doors," he said. "For me. For somebody to tell you what's happening. When you talk about finding a spell, you look like you've already started walking toward it. I wanted more of that part. With you."

Her eyes went to his face instead of the garden beyond him.

"What have you done?"

"Asked a few people some questions."

"Mau."

"The gate changes in three days."

She became still in a very particular way.

"I spoke with Fern about supplies and coverage. And with the people who need to know before I promise anything about myself. The next outing, if you want it—I'd like you to plan it. Lead it. We take enough to look properly. Every room the plan and the place will let us reach. Not rush through looking for the shortest way to the reward."

Frieren blinked once.

"From the beginning?"

"From the entrance. New week. New map. Your route. We agree the actual team and limits before we go."

"And the chests?"

"You may investigate the chests. With the usual objection to putting your head in them."

She ignored the qualification so completely that he knew she had heard it.

"The books were at the end," she said.

"Past the guardian. In a box. They won't all have books."

"But one did."

"One did."

She looked past him toward the doorway, and he could almost see the list beginning.

"We'll need a party."

"I thought you might say that."

"Rations that don't crumble into the books. Two kinds of cloth. Chalk. Separate bags for wet things. Rope, but not the kind Yuka insists is sufficient when it hasn't been checked for fraying."

"She's going to be thrilled to hear that."

"A way to mark a chest after inspecting it. And—"

She stopped. Looked back at him.

"Thank you."

The change was so sudden that he forgot the joke he had been preparing.

"I wanted you to have something to be excited about. For yourself."

"I am."

"I noticed."

She leaned over and kissed him again, less carefully this time. He caught the chair before one of them could blame the other for what happened to it.

Then she sat back with colour in her face and informed him that three days might be barely enough.

He loved her so much in that moment that it made him stupid.

"We can start tonight."

She looked delighted.

He realized what he had done.

***

They carried their own plates inside. Momo inspected the empty ones, accepted the compliment without turning it into a question about every dish, and told Mau where to leave the bottle.

Gojo was at the far end of the room with Shoko. He looked at the two of them, opened his mouth, and met Frieren's eyes.

He drank his tea.

Shoko smiled into hers.

Ori was still awake when they went upstairs. She came through the connecting door to ask whether her flower had looked good on the table. Mau told her it had been the best thing there. Frieren disagreed just enough to make Ori demand an explanation.

"He had wine," she said.

"That isn't a flower."

"A serious flaw."

Ori inspected both of them, satisfied herself that the evening had worked, and went back to get Milim before she fell asleep in the wrong room.

Frieren shut their corridor door. Mau took the compass out and put it beside the bed while he changed. The needle found her where she stood by the shelf.

She watched him check it.

"It still works."

"I'm being thorough."

He placed it where he would find it in the morning. Tomorrow it would go back into his inner pocket. Tonight she was three steps away, taking a book down with one hand and trying to remember where she had left her planning paper.

He found the paper for her.

She took it to bed, made him sit beside her, and began writing a list across his knees.

"You said tonight," she reminded him when he looked at the length of it.

"I remember."

He helped her with the names she wanted to ask, without deciding their answers for them. When she got to spare socks, he insisted on a second line for his own and she granted it with an expression of enormous generosity.

Eventually he leaned back against the pillows. She folded the list, put it inside the book, and came to lie against him.

"I loved it," she said into the quiet.

"The dinner?"

"All of it."

He held her closer.

The compass lay closed within reach. The list waited in the book. Outside their connecting door, Ori's map stirred once in the draft and settled against the wood.

In three days there would be a new place to see.

For tonight, they stayed where they were.
