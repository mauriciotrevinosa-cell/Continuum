# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 2 — With Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 2
**Continuity state:** Frieren asks Fern personally to explore together; Fern and Stark choose participation while preserving their own relationship and agreed household coverage

*When: Day 278, afternoon.*

Fern knew why Frieren had come before she saw the paper.

It was the expression. Frieren had worn it while asking for another grimoire shelf, while explaining why a chest was probably safe, and once while trying to persuade Fern that a spell for cleaning a fish was relevant to an expedition nowhere near water.

She wore it now in the doorway.

"You're busy."

"I'm finishing."

"I can wait."

Fern put down the pen. "You can come in."

Frieren did. She did not immediately put the list on the table, which made Fern look at her a little more carefully.

"I'd like you to come with me."

Fern waited.

"To the gate," Frieren added.

"I understood."

"Not to manage it. I have a plan."

"That is what worries me."

Frieren sat opposite her and finally unfolded the paper.

It was a plan. An actual one, with provisions, spare clothing, the known rules, and questions that could not be answered before the next entrance appeared. Fern recognized two additions in Mau's handwriting. Socks, twice. She wondered what negotiation had produced that.

"We used to do this," Frieren said.

Fern looked up.

"Travel. Look at something neither of us had seen. You'd tell me we were taking too long. I'd find something worth taking longer for."

"You'd find something."

"Often worth it."

Fern tried to maintain the expression the answer deserved.

Frieren's voice became quieter.

"I'd like to do that with you here too."

The ledger lay closed between them. For a moment Fern wished she had put it away before this conversation, and then she was glad she had not: Frieren had found her where she actually was and asked her to come along anyway.

"Stark?" she asked.

"If he wants to. I haven't asked him yet."

"He'll say yes before you've finished."

"Then I should begin."

Fern touched the edge of the list.

"I'll come. We settle the remaining coverage first. And if you put your head in a chest—"

"You may object."

"I may do rather more than object."

Frieren smiled at her with a warmth that made the threat difficult to keep properly severe.

"Good."

They found Stark behind the workshop, checking the edge of the gate axe. He looked up when both of them appeared and immediately checked whether he had forgotten something.

Frieren asked him.

"Yes," he said.

Fern folded her arms.

He looked between them.

"What?"

"You might listen to the plan," she said.

"I was going to."

They sat on the low wall while Frieren explained. Stark asked about the length, the loads, the team, and what they would do if the new route required climbing. His questions changed the packing list. Fern watched Frieren make room for the changes instead of treating them as objections to be worn down.

By the time they finished, the sun had moved off the workshop door.

Frieren went to find more cloth. Fern stayed on the wall beside Stark.

"I said you'd agree too quickly," she told him.

"Would you rather I hadn't?"

"No."

He smiled and went back to the axe. She watched him work until the next familiar sound from the courtyard made her turn.

Frieren was asking who had moved the butter-stained list.

Fern closed her eyes.

"We should make a copy."

Stark said that sounded like a very good idea.
