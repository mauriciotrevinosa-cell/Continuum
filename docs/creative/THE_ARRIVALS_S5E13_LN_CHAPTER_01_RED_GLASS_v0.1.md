# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 6 — Red Glass

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 6
**Continuity state:** Rimuru loses the ordinary gate relay and cannot pull or enter; outside responders act within minutes, not days

*When: Day 280, the first exterior minutes.*

Rimuru heard Milim say something about the cold.

Then he heard half of Mau's name, or half of somebody saying it, and the thread went empty.

He stood up so quickly that the world tilted.

"Yuta."

The red had already reached the rim.

Yuta handed his cup to Maki and was moving before Rimuru had finished saying the rest. Gojo reached the surface first. He stopped short of touching it, lifted his blindfold, and looked.

Rimuru tried the pull he had prepared.

Nothing came.

Not resistance from somebody heavy at the far end. No far end he could reach.

He stopped before he could tear himself apart trying harder at the wrong thing.

"The relay's cut. I can't bring them out."

Gojo's hand hovered beside the red surface.

"I can see the boundary. Not through it."

Maki took the branch Stark had left near the stake, tested it once against the surface, and let Yuta examine the place where it bent. Physical entry failed too. They did not need to put a person into it to learn that.

Anko had the time. She said it aloud. Rimuru repeated it so somebody would remember if he stopped being able to.

"No one else through," he said. "No new method alone. We check before touching it again."

Gojo looked at him.

For a moment Rimuru expected an argument, and knew he might not have the spare strength to win it.

Gojo put the blindfold back down.

"All right."

Maki went to alert the house. Yuta stayed with the gate. Rimuru wanted both of them everywhere at once, which meant he needed to stop deciding with the part of him that wanted things.

He asked Ciel what she had.

The answer was not Mau.

Their normal line through the entrance had ended when it sealed. What remained with Rimuru could observe this side; it could not supply the experience of the part with Mau simply because both hosts had heard the same voice before.

He reported that accurately.

Anko wrote it down.

The interface still said normal. That almost made him angrier than the red.

"Bring the last report," he said. "I want to know exactly what it said before this."

Yuta crouched by the place their six shadows had crossed. There was no trace to follow. He stayed there anyway, looking, as though he could at least refuse to miss the first change.

Rimuru sat down before his legs made the choice for him.

They had been gone less than four minutes.

He had never hated a number so much.
