# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 8 — Not Just Her

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 8
**Continuity state:** Mau's bounded Infinity stops the elf's arrow as a deliberate signal; Frieren had seen it and remains the leader

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day one, afternoon.*

Mau saw the movement above the stream before he saw the bow.

A pale sleeve. The narrow angle of an elbow. A face almost the colour of the bark, with pointed ears half hidden by the branches.

He looked at Frieren.

Her fingers had already changed on her staff.

She knew.

The arrow came anyway.

Mau had a moment in which to let her answer it. She was going to. He could see the shape of the defence beginning beside her shoulder.

He asked for the narrow opening instead.

The Noise gave him the one pattern. Ciel kept the rest behind the boundary and told him where to stop. He stepped half a pace, lifted his hand, and let the distance in front of it become enough.

The arrow hung.

Its tip stopped a finger's breadth from the place his palm would have been. The feathers shivered. Beyond it, on the branch, the archer went perfectly still.

"Close after the drop," Ciel told him privately.

He tilted his hand away, let the arrow fall into the soft bank, and closed.

No second pattern followed.

His head gave a single unpleasant pulse. He told Frieren that too, before the satisfaction could turn into a lie about how easy it had been.

She was looking at him.

"I saw it."

"I know."

"Then why?"

He kept his eyes on the branch.

"So they know you aren't the only one who sees where they are. Maybe they'll think twice before trying the same surprise."

Frieren considered that. Then she looked up at the archer.

"Reasonable. Don't expect it to work every time."

"I won't."

She raised her voice.

"We see you. We aren't looking for a fight."

The answer came from farther left.

A second arrow struck the defence she had already placed around the group.

"Apparently," she said, "they are."

Fern moved Yuka behind the large root. Stark came forward, axe up. Milim stayed beside Mau for once without being told, staring into the trees with the eager, unpleasant attention she had given the guardian's damaged plate.

Three figures shifted among the trunks. Long white hair, winter-pale clothes, bows cut in a shape Mau had never seen. One moved toward a higher branch. Another began drawing the next arrow.

Frieren struck the branch, not the face above it.

Wood cracked. The archer fell, caught a lower limb, and vanished behind the rise. Fern's second shot broke the bow of the one who had stayed to fire. The third went with them.

Stark started forward.

"Stay," Frieren said.

He stopped.

They listened until the movement was gone. Yuka counted the arrows from cover; Mau kept his hand down and did not reopen the thing that had worked simply because it had worked.

"They'll come back," Milim said.

"Perhaps," Frieren said. "We won't follow them into the place they chose."

She asked Fern to watch the high side while they moved. Asked Stark to take the narrow bank first. Asked Mau whether the pulse had stopped.

He answered each question she actually asked him.

They went on, closer together now.

The arrow stayed in the mud behind them.
