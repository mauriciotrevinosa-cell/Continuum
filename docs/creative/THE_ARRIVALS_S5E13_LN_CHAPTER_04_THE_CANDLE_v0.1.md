# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 9 — The Candle

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 9
**Continuity state:** The six establish their first camp; Frieren and Fern's established bond develops through an ordinary shared task and earned praise

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day one, evening.*

The first fire smoked.

Stark turned the wood, changed the gap beneath it, and made it smoke in a different direction. Milim, who had been pleased with the first direction, objected.

Fern moved the pot.

"Dry pieces inside," she said. "The outside of the branch was wet."

"I know."

"Then why did you put that one there?"

"I was hoping it didn't know."

Yuka laughed into her scarf.

The shelter was a hollow under a stone lip, open enough to leave a clear retreat and sheltered enough to keep the wind from stripping the heat away. They had checked it before putting the bags down. Frieren had wanted another hour of walking; the light and Fern's look had persuaded her to spend that hour making a better camp instead.

Now she sat beside Fern with a stub of candle between them.

"The diagram is clear here," she said.

"You brought the new book?"

"A copy of the part I wanted to study. The book stayed dry at home."

Fern took the folded page. Mau had drawn the candle in the corner, badly. Frieren's notes occupied the rest.

It was a small spell. A careful enclosure around the flame, with room left for the air it needed. Frieren had understood why the first line worked and been less satisfied with the third. Fern pointed to the place where the copied diagram had become too narrow.

Frieren leaned closer.

"Mm."

They tried it.

The flame leaned in the draught, righted itself, and went out.

"Improvement," Stark said.

Fern looked at him.

"It was very straight before it died."

She tried to stay severe and could not quite do it.

On the next attempt the little flame stayed lit. Fern adjusted the enclosure with a precision that let them see the difference in the moving air rather than merely hope it had changed.

Frieren watched.

"That's good," she said. "You found the part I was treating too broadly."

Fern's hand became very still.

"It isn't complete yet."

"No. But that was good."

Mau, across the fire with Yuka, heard the difference in the silence afterward. He kept his eyes on the knot she was teaching him to tie.

Milim leaned over to inspect the candle. Fern moved it before her hair could become an experiment.

"Not a food spell," Milim said.

"A shocking disappointment," Mau agreed.

Supper was warm. The fire improved after Stark stopped arguing with wet wood and split the dry centre properly. They took off damp outer layers and kept the inner ones on. Yuka marked the day's route beside the entrance she had drawn from memory.

Frieren put the small copied page away, satisfied in a way she had not been when she began.

When Mau made room beside him, she sat there with her cup. Fern watched them for a moment, then took her own place next to Stark.

Nobody needed to explain the arrangement.

The candle lasted until they chose to put it out.
