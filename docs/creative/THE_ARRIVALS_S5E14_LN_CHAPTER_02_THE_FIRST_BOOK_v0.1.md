# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 12 — The First Book

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 12
**Continuity state:** The first mini-boss is defeated through Frieren's plan; a physical grimoire is earned and carried in Mau's established bag, beginning the two-book wager

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day two, afternoon.*

The antlers were the wrong colour for the trees.

Fern noticed first. A clear branching shape beyond the next ridge, almost invisible until it passed in front of the dark stone behind it.

Then the animal beneath it moved, and Frieren saw that its legs ended in narrow points which cut into the frozen ground without leaving ordinary hoofprints.

She stopped the party below the rise.

It had seen them too.

Its head lowered. The light caught in the antlers and separated into thin, sharp lines.

"Down," Frieren said.

They were already moving when the first line passed overhead. Stone split at the back of the ridge. Stark crouched beside her, waiting for the next word instead of rushing the thing on the open slope.

It fired through its antlers. The branching points shaped the attack. Frieren could see the pattern now, a narrow fan, strong ahead and weak at the base where the branches joined.

"Fern. Left side, make it turn. Stark, wait for Milim."

Milim looked at her.

"You can stop the charge. Not by catching the points."

"I wasn't going to catch the points."

Frieren gave her a look.

"I wasn't," Milim repeated, less convincingly.

Fern's shot struck the slope to the creature's left. It turned its head toward her. Frieren broke the outer branch on the other side before the next fan could spread. The creature screamed, a glassy sound that made Mau press his hands against his ears, and came down the slope at them.

Milim met the shoulder, low.

Snow and dirt flew. She slid, heels cutting long trenches, but the charge lost its straight line. Stark came in at the angle Frieren had left for him and struck the base of the antlers with the gate axe.

They broke with a sound like a window falling into pieces.

Fern and Frieren fired together into the exposed shape beneath.

The creature collapsed. Blue sparks ran along the narrow legs and took it apart before the head reached the ground.

For a moment there was only the falling snow.

"Points," Milim said to Stark, showing him her unpierced hands.

"Very good," he said.

She grinned at him as though he had issued a medal.

They waited for the last sparks before approaching. The crystals were larger than the ones near the entrance, and the cold surrounding them faded when Yuka wrapped them in cloth. Beyond the place where the creature had stood was a stone recess, sheltered from the snow by the ledge above it.

Inside: a chest.

Frieren became very interested.

Fern became very watchful.

It took them longer than the fight to agree the chest was not going to eat anybody. Mau did not contribute an opinion to the debate; experience had taught him that would leave him responsible whichever way it went.

The chest opened.

Frieren reached in with care and lifted a book wrapped in dark cloth. She read the first lines, turned a page, and smiled.

Mau knew the smile before she looked at him.

"One."

"I can count."

"I thought I'd help."

He took out the dry inner wrapping from his bag. She put the book into it herself, so she could check every fold before he closed it.

"What's it do?" Yuka asked.

"It helps dry the inside of a wet boot."

Yuka looked at the snow clinging to all six pairs.

"Can you learn it now?"

Frieren hugged the wrapped book once before surrendering it to Mau's bag.

"I'll make time."
