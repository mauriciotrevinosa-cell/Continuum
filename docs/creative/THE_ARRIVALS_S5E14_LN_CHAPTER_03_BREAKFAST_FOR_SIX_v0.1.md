# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 13 — Breakfast For Six

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 13
**Continuity state:** A complete ordinary camp morning lets all six share work and humour without an emergency or a new power lesson

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day three, morning.*

Yuka had put too much water in the pot.

This was not immediately obvious, because she had also put in something that absorbed water with surprising enthusiasm. By the time Stark asked whether the pot ought to be doing that, breakfast had become a single slow movement toward the rim.

Fern took it off the fire.

"I followed the amount," Yuka said.

"For how many?" Mau asked.

"Six."

"Six what?"

She looked at the little marks on the wrapper.

Milim leaned over her shoulder. "People who eat like me."

That, unfortunately, explained a great deal.

They divided it into smaller portions to finish cooking. It took longer. Nobody went hungry. The first portion was very thick, the second much better, and the third something Milim described as an improvement on the concept of breakfast.

Yuka sat on a dry root with her bowl and accepted that praise without investigating it.

Frieren emerged from the shelter with a crease across one cheek and somebody else's scarf around her neck.

Mau looked at it.

"You have your own."

"This one was closer."

"It was on me."

"Exactly."

He gave her a bowl. She sat against his side and began eating before asking why breakfast looked like that. Yuka pointed to Milim. Milim accepted responsibility for something she had not done because the result was in her favour.

The weather had cleared. Sun reached the low branches in a thin, pale sheet, bringing drops down from places that had seemed dry when they chose the camp. Stark moved a roll before the dripping reached it. Fern moved another without looking up from her food.

They had become better at those little decisions already.

After eating, Mau washed the pot while Yuka dried the bowls. Milim went with Stark to collect the dry pieces from a fallen trunk. Fern checked the water. Frieren sat in the opening with the new grimoire and announced that she was making herself useful.

"A bold claim," Mau said.

She held out her hand for his boot.

He looked at it, then at her face.

"One boot," she said. "I understand the limit."

He gave it to her and sat with his sock above the blanket to keep it clean.

The first attempt warmed the leather and did very little inside it. The second produced a faint thread of steam from the toe. Frieren stopped before trying to improve it through persistence alone.

She put two fingers inside.

"Better. Not dry."

Mau pulled it on.

"Still better."

She looked pleased in a way that made the half-success seem quite enough for breakfast.

Yuka offered her own boot. Frieren asked for the other one first so she could keep the comparison fair. For several minutes, half the camp was sitting around a fire with one socked foot held carefully out of the snow.

Milim returned carrying wood and stopped at the sight.

"Did I miss a game?"

Mau looked at the solemn arrangement of bare socks and damp boots.

"Yes. We were losing."

She sat down immediately.
