# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 13 — The Wrong Piece of Wood

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 13
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. Red Gate night two: Stark and Milim share the middle watch; Stark admits he is always afraid; Milim admits she was afraid during the Exchange; no new power or plan

*When: Red Gate night two (Day 280 outside, minutes into the hour). The glass-antlered creature is dead and the first book is wrapped in Mau's bag. It is very cold.*

Stark put another piece of wood on the fire and Milim told him it was the wrong one.

"It's wood," said Stark.

"It's the wet kind."

"It's not wet."

"It's going to be wet. On the inside. You can tell by the bark." She pointed. "That bark is the kind that has wet on the inside."

Stark looked at the piece of wood. It hissed, softly, as the fire found it. A thin thread of steam came out of one end.

He looked at Milim.

Milim looked extremely pleased.

"How do you know that?" said Stark.

"I've been watching you put the wrong pieces on for two nights," said Milim. "I learned which ones are wrong. It's mostly the ones you pick."

Stark considered arguing. He considered it for long enough that the wet piece began to burn properly, despite everything, and he decided that this counted as a win for him and did not need to be said aloud.

They had the middle watch. Frieren had divided the night again, the same way as the first: pairs, short stretches, nobody keeping the whole dark. Fern and Yuka had the first. Mau and Frieren would have the last. Stark had been half asleep when Fern touched his shoulder, and had sat up so fast he nearly hit his head on the stone lip of the shelter, and she had looked at him with an expression he had been trying to name ever since.

Not worry. Fern did not show worry where anyone could see it.

Something like: *I know you're afraid, and I'm going to pretend I don't, because that's what you'd want.*

He sat with Milim at the mouth of the shelter and watched the dark.

* * *

The forest at night was very quiet and very large.

Not like the forests in his world. Those had always been loud: owls, foxes, wind, the shriek of something being eaten. This one was quiet the way a held breath is quiet. Snow on every branch. Stars overhead that did not belong to any sky Stark knew, too many of them, too bright, too still. And far off, from the direction of the dark ridge, every so often, a sound like stone being dragged over stone.

Milim heard it too. She sat up straighter every time.

"What is that?" she said, the third time.

"Something big."

"How big?"

"Bigger than the antler thing."

Milim's eyes brightened.

"Don't," said Stark.

"I didn't say anything."

"You were going to say you wanted to go and look."

"I was going to say I wanted to go and *punch* it," said Milim. "That's different." She settled back down. "But Frieren said stay. So I'm staying." She paused. "I'm very good at staying now. Ask anyone."

Stark did not say anything.

She looked at him sideways.

"You're scared," she said.

It was not a question. Milim did not ask questions about things she could see.

Stark looked at the fire.

"Yes," he said.

"Of the big thing?"

"Of everything." He picked up a stick and poked the embers, though they did not need it. "The antler thing. The archers. The ice. The dark. That sound." He put the stick down. "I'm always scared. Since I was a kid. Before every fight. During. After." He shrugged. "I just do it anyway."

Milim was quiet for a long time. For Milim, a long time was about four breaths.

"I'm not scared of things," she said. "Fights. Monsters. Big things. I've never been scared of a monster in my whole life." She looked at the dark. "I don't think I know how."

"That sounds nice," said Stark.

"It isn't," said Milim.

Stark looked at her.

She was not smiling. She had her knees drawn up and her arms round them, and her face had gone small and fierce, the way it went before she broke something. But there was nothing to break here. Only the fire and the snow and the dark.

"When Mau went," she said. "In the dungeon. At the fountain. He was sitting there drawing his terrible map, and the ant touched his hand, and then he wasn't there." She was looking at the fire. "And there was a man instead. A man in a black coat. And I didn't know where Mau was. And I couldn't punch anything. There wasn't anything to punch. There was just a fountain and a man and a voice saying he'd left the *universe*."

Stark did not say anything.

"I didn't know what that was," said Milim. "The thing I felt. I thought I was angry. I'm always angry when something takes something. But I wasn't angry." She swallowed. "Yuta said that's what scared feels like. After. When we were walking home. He said, *that's what it feels like, Milim. That's what the rest of us feel all the time.*"

She was quiet.

"I don't like it," she said. "I don't know how you do it all the time."

Stark looked at her for a long moment. At the most powerful person he had ever met, sitting at the mouth of a cave in a frozen forest with her arms round her knees, telling him she had been frightened once and did not know how other people survived it.

"You get used to it," he said. "A bit. Not all the way."

"How?"

He thought about it. Properly. He had never had to explain it to anyone. Eisen had never explained it to him. Eisen had just shown him, by being afraid, and going anyway, and not pretending.

"You find somebody who's scared too," said Stark slowly. "And you stand next to them. And you both know the other one's scared. And you go anyway." He shrugged. "It's easier when you're not the only one."

Milim looked at him.

"Fern's scared," she said.

Stark went slightly red.

"Sometimes," he said.

"She looked at you funny when she woke you up."

"I know."

"Is that what that was? Her knowing you're scared and pretending she doesn't?"

Stark stared at her.

"How did you—"

"I've been watching you put the wrong pieces of wood on the fire for two nights," said Milim, with great dignity. "I watch things."

* * *

They did not talk much after that.

The stone-dragging sound came again, and then not again. The stars wheeled very slowly. Milim got up twice to walk to the edge of the light and look into the dark, and came back each time and sat down a little closer to Stark than before, until by the end of the watch she was sitting right next to him with her shoulder against his arm.

He did not say anything about it.

At one point she said, very quietly, "If the big thing comes. Tomorrow. Or whenever."

"Yes."

"I'll be scared. Probably. I think. Now I know what it is."

"Probably," said Stark.

"So you have to stand next to me."

Stark looked down at her.

"I'll stand next to you," he said.

"And I'll stand next to you," said Milim. "Then we'll both know."

"Then we'll both know," Stark agreed.

She nodded, satisfied, as if they had signed something.

When their watch was over, Stark woke Mau, and Mau woke Frieren, and Frieren woke slowly and complained about it, the way she always did. Milim crawled back to her sleeping roll and lay down beside Yuka and was asleep almost at once.

Stark lay down in his own place, next to Fern.

He thought she was asleep. But after a moment, in the dark, her hand found his, and held it.

He did not say anything. Neither did she.

In the morning, Milim watched him put the first piece of wood on the breakfast fire.

"Wrong one," she said.

"It's not."

It hissed.

Milim said nothing at all. She just looked at him. And Stark, for the first time on that whole frozen journey, laughed out loud.
