# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 14 — The Long Way

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 14
**Continuity state:** A broken ravine crossing changes Frieren's route; Yuka's rope and Stark's practical work matter; Mau's low levitation cannot replace a safe crossing

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day three, afternoon.*

The map promised a short line that the ground refused to provide.

Frieren stood at the ravine edge and looked down. A fallen stone arch had once crossed it. Or looked as though it had. Now the middle was a long way below them, broken into pieces too large to move and too far apart to walk.

The far bank was not far enough away to be comforting.

Milim could cross. Frieren and Fern could fly across with an expenditure they could estimate. That did not make carrying all the people and loads over an unknown drop a sensible next step.

Mau looked at the gap and kept his suggestion to himself.

Frieren turned toward him.

"I wasn't going to say it."

"Good."

"I can go about a metre above the ground. There's a great deal of ground missing."

Her mouth softened. "Exactly."

They investigated the banks before deciding. Yuka used the rope to measure the safer sloping part with Stark checking the anchor. Fern watched the opposite side. No one went over the edge attached to a knot nobody else had inspected.

The direct crossing could be made possible, at a cost. Frieren looked at the route they had already travelled and the ridge she could see beyond the ravine.

"We go round."

Milim stared at her.

"That's farther."

"Yes."

"We can get across."

"Yes."

Milim waited for the rest of the explanation and received it: the unknown landing, the loads, the flight effort they might need later. The direct route saved walking, not necessarily time they could afford to spend carelessly.

It was an argument about travelling rather than a contest of strength. Milim did not much like it, but she accepted the route.

Yuka recovered the rope with an expression of complete vindication.

"Useful," she told Mau.

"I never doubted you."

"You doubted the rope."

"Your grandfather doubted the rope. I merely failed to defend it."

"Coward," she said again, pleased to have the familiar answer back.

The longer route led them through trees bent low by old snow. Stark cut a fallen branch that blocked the path, taking a moment to study where its weight lay before making the cut. Yuka held the rope clear. Mau carried the loose pieces aside after they fell, and Milim, who could have done all of that much faster and much less neatly, gathered the smaller wood for camp.

They found a sheltered ledge on the far side near evening.

From it, Frieren could see a bare shoulder of rock below the dark ridge. Something had passed over the snow there, leaving a wide trail neither the archers nor the glass-antlered creature could have made.

She marked it rather than following in failing light.

At camp, Stark asked whether the long way had been worth it.

Frieren looked at the dry ground beneath their rolls, the recovered rope, the wood ready beside the fire.

"I think so."

Fern handed him the pot.

"You may complain while filling that."

He went to the water, still complaining. She went with him, which substantially improved his opinion of the route.
