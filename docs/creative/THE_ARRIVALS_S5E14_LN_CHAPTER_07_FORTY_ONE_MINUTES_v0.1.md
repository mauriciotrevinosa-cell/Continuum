# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 17 — Forty-One Minutes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 17
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. Back at the house during the exterior hour of the Red Gate: Rem carries tea out to the stake; Momo cooks because she cannot help it; Byakuya and Coco's mother keep each other company; Bocchi plays; nobody has news, and nobody pretends to; no contact with the party

*When: Day 280, the exterior hour, around minute forty-one. Inside the gate, the six have already lived several days. Outside, the tea Rem made at the start of the hour is still warm.*

At minute forty-one, Rem carried the second pot of tea out to the stake.

She did not ask anyone whether they wanted it. She had stopped asking people whether they wanted tea a long time ago, round about the time she stopped pouring it every night and started sitting down. Now she poured when she thought someone needed something to hold. And the people at the stake needed something to hold.

She went out across the field with the brown pot in both hands and a stack of cups on a tray balanced against her hip. The grass was dry. The sun was well up. It was a perfectly ordinary summer morning, which was the worst thing about it.

At the stake, nobody had moved much.

Anko was standing exactly where she had been standing at minute seventeen, with the watch in her hand. Shoko and Maomao sat on the grass beside the emergency kit, which was open and untouched. Senku crouched a careful distance from the red surface with a notebook on his knee, writing very small numbers and crossing them out. Gojo stood near Yuta, blindfold up, looking at the gate the way he had looked at the door in the shed: as if staring hard enough might make it explain itself.

Rimuru sat on the grass with his eyes half closed and one hand flat on the ground, holding a thread that held nothing.

Ori and Umaru sat a little way off, side by side, not talking.

Rem went round with the tray.

Anko took a cup without looking at it. Shoko said thank you and meant it. Maomao sniffed the tea suspiciously, out of habit, and drank it anyway. Senku did not notice the cup until Rem put it on the grass beside his notebook, and then picked it up and drank half of it in one go and went back to his numbers.

Gojo took one. He looked at Rem for a moment over the rim of it.

"You made this an hour ago," he said.

"Forty-one minutes ago," said Rem. "The first pot. This is the second."

"It's still hot."

"I wrapped it."

Gojo looked at the pot, which was wrapped in a folded towel and a knitted cover that Rem had made in the winter for exactly this purpose and never told anyone about. He looked at her.

"You knew we'd be out here a long time," he said.

"I didn't know," said Rem. "I hoped we wouldn't be." She poured him a little more. "But I wrapped it anyway."

She went on to Yuta and Maki. Yuta took his cup and held it without drinking. Maki drank hers and then took Yuta's, and drank some of that too, and gave it back to him with a look that said *you'll drink the rest*. He did.

Last, Rem came to Rimuru.

He did not open his eyes. She knelt down on the grass beside him and put the cup by his hand, close enough that his fingers would find it.

"Nothing?" she said quietly.

"Nothing," said Rimuru. "Not cut. Not quiet, like the Exchange. Just a wall. I can feel the wall. I can't feel anything past it."

Rem did not say anything.

"Ciel says the inside of a gate like this might keep its own time," said Rimuru. "Like the dungeon floors do, sometimes. She doesn't know. She's guessing." He opened his eyes. "She says that might mean it's been longer for them. Much longer. Days, maybe."

"Days," said Rem.

"Maybe. She's guessing."

Rem looked at the red surface for a long time.

"Then they've slept," she said at last. "And eaten. And Frieren's found something she wants to look at for too long. And Fern's told her off. And Milim's put her thumb in something." She picked up the teapot. "And they're wondering what we're doing."

Rimuru looked at her.

"We're drinking tea," said Rem. "So they'll know, when they come out, that someone was."

* * *

Back at the house, Momo was cooking.

Nobody had asked her to. Nobody needed anything cooked. It was mid-morning, and breakfast was over, and lunch was hours off. But Momo had stood at the kitchen window for twenty minutes watching the field, and then she had turned round and taken down the big pot and started cutting onions, and now the kitchen smelled the way it smelled before a feast.

Okarun sat on the bench at the long table and did not tell her to stop.

"What are you making?" he said, after a while.

"I don't know yet."

"You've cut nine onions."

"I know how many onions I've cut."

"That's a lot of onions for not knowing."

Momo stopped. She looked at the pile.

"Milim will be hungry," she said. "Whenever they come back. And Stark eats like a horse. And Mau never eats enough unless someone puts it in front of him." She went back to cutting. "And if it's been longer for them, inside—if it's been days—they'll have been eating trail food. Dry things. They'll want something hot."

Okarun did not say anything. He got up, and went to the cupboard, and got down the second pot.

* * *

In the common room, Byakuya and Coco's mother were sitting on the bench by the window, side by side.

They had not arranged to. Coco's mother had been there first, with her sewing, watching the field. Byakuya had come in from the lab a few minutes later, without Senku, who was at the stake, and had stood in the middle of the common room for a while looking lost. Then he had seen her by the window and come and sat down beside her without asking. And she had moved her sewing basket to make room without being asked.

They had not said much.

"Your daughter's in there," said Byakuya at last. Not a question.

"No," said Coco's mother. "She's in the fort. She's not in the party." She paused. "But the young man is. Mau. The one who—" She stopped. "The one who put his hand in mine."

"Ah," said Byakuya.

"And the elf is. Who sat with me while I was in the slime. Coco told me. She sat on a bench and faced me the whole time, so I wouldn't be alone." Coco's mother looked down at her sewing. "I never thanked her. I didn't know how. I've been waiting for the right time."

Byakuya was quiet for a moment.

"My son's at the stake," he said. "Writing numbers. He does that when he's frightened. He did it when he was five." He looked out of the window at the field. "I used to think he didn't feel things. Because he wrote numbers instead. It took me years to work out the numbers *were* the feeling."

Coco's mother looked at him.

"What do you do?" she said. "When you're frightened. If he writes numbers."

Byakuya thought about it.

"I wait," he said. "I'm very good at waiting. I spent my whole life once waiting for a boy to wake up." He smiled a little. "And I sit next to people. That helps."

Coco's mother looked at him for a long time.

Then she went back to her sewing. But she moved, very slightly, along the bench, so that their shoulders were almost touching.

* * *

At the top of the house, in the music room with the door open, Bocchi was playing.

Not the ninth song. Not anything the band had worked on. Something slow and quiet that went round and round and did not seem to be going anywhere in particular. Kita was sitting on the floor with her back against Bocchi's chair, listening. Ryo was lying on the floor on her back with her eyes closed. Nijika was not drumming. She had her sticks in her lap and was turning them over.

Seika stood in the doorway.

She had come up to tell them that this was not a rehearsal and they should either rehearse or stop. She had come up the stairs with the sentence ready. She stood in the doorway now and did not say it.

She listened to the slow thing going round and round.

"What is it?" she said at last. Quietly.

Bocchi did not stop. "I don't know," she said. "It doesn't have a name. I just—" She stopped playing for a moment. Then started again. "It's for while we wait. So the waiting has something in it."

Seika looked at her for a long time.

Then she came into the room and sat down on the floor beside Nijika, which Seika never did, and put her hand over her sister's on the sticks.

"Then keep going," she said.

Bocchi kept going.

Down in the field, at the stake, Anko looked at her watch and said, very quietly, "Forty-two."

And Rem poured the last of the second pot.
