# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 18 — Five Points

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 18
**Continuity state:** Mau's new branch awards unspent points after gate combat; the others do not acquire its XP and his existing reservoirs remain separate

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day four, evening.*

The window offered him five points.

It did not explain why five, or apologize for choosing the moment when he was trying to get grit out of a sleeve.

Mau dismissed it until they had made camp.

Then he asked the others to listen while he described it. They could not see what he saw. Frieren looked at the place he indicated with the polite concentration of somebody being shown an invisible insect.

"Level two," he said. "Five available points. There's more underneath now. Strength. Agility. Endurance. Intelligence. Perception. Some values. A separate resource line that still isn't fully named."

"And what does intelligence mean?" Fern asked.

"I don't know."

"Then don't put anything in it."

"Umaru said that about all of them."

"An excellent beginning."

He kept the points where they were. The urge to improve something was there, bright and simple in a way few things inside him had ever been. Press here. Become better. It was precisely simple enough to deserve suspicion.

Ciel could compare the response to what she observed of him now. She could not turn the field names into guarantees. The new branch had reacted to the creatures in this gate. Walking, carrying bags, and practising his own mana had not produced the same response.

His fist still hurt. The number had not repaired it.

He said that aloud too.

Stark examined his own hands as though making certain no invisible window had arrived with the last crystal.

"Nothing here."

"I don't think this is everyone's," Mau said. "It's what came back with me."

Yuka copied the labels onto a little piece of paper for him. He checked each one before she folded it. Not a build plan. A record of what they actually saw, or what he could report seeing, instead of what somebody thought a system with that name ought to do.

"I'll take it to Umaru," he said.

"She'll want a larger page," Yuka said.

"She'll want a wall."

Milim looked interested.

"Can you put points in food?"

"Not yet."

"Then it isn't finished."

The verdict pleased her. She went to help Stark with the fire, which involved several new opinions about how he had arranged the wood.

Later Mau held the bruised hand near the warmth and tried not to make it into a larger story about himself. He had hit one thing, let somebody else finish the fight, and come away with a sore hand and a number.

Frieren sat beside him with the boot book.

"You stopped," she said.

He looked at her.

"After the one you meant to do."

"I had help."

"I know. You still stopped."

She put his better hand on the open page to keep it from closing while she adjusted a fold in the blanket.

He stayed there with her, holding the book down, and found that he liked that part at least as much as the number.
