# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 20 — At The Snowline

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 20
**Continuity state:** The hostile archers return; Frieren offers passage rather than pursuing them, then defends the party; one dissolves as a gate creature without defining every elf as one

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day five, afternoon.*

The afternoon walk was meant to take them only to a better camp, still short of the pass.

The first arrow struck the tree ahead of Stark.

A warning, perhaps. Or a poor shot. Frieren did not let the uncertainty decide their next step.

She stopped the party below the low rise and raised her empty hand where it could be seen.

"We are passing through. Let us pass, and we won't follow you."

A pale figure stepped between two trees.

Close now, the resemblance to an elf was stronger and less comforting. Long ears. A narrow, beautiful face. Eyes that moved across the six of them and rested on the bags.

The figure said something Frieren did not understand.

She answered that plainly.

It lifted the bow.

Fern's defence caught the arrow aimed at Yuka. Stark moved in front of her. Milim brought a second shaft down with her gauntlet, hard enough to snap it.

Frieren fired.

The figure broke apart where the shot struck. Not blood. Blue fragments, the same dissolving light they had seen in the creatures by the pond. A crystal fell into the snow beneath the tree.

Yuka stopped breathing for a moment.

The other archers moved.

Frieren did not chase the vanishing shapes. She held the route with Fern, forcing the next two shots away from the group until the movement retreated up the slope. Stark waited for her signal before advancing to the shelter of the rocks.

Only when they had solid cover did she lower the staff.

Yuka looked back.

"It looked like you."

Frieren followed her gaze.

"A little."

"Could it understand?"

"I don't know."

She did not turn the crystal into a complete answer. They had seen what one had become. They had not learned the whole nature of the place by killing it.

Mau put the water where Yuka could reach it. She drank, then gave it back with an unsteady hand that steadied before she took her bag again.

Frieren waited until everyone was ready.

"We won't use their route," she said. "We go through the pass we found. Close together. No one follows a retreating shape."

Milim looked disappointed for half a moment. Then she looked at Yuka and took the heavier loose bundle without saying why.

They walked until the trees thinned.

The dark ridge stood ahead. At its foot the snow had been scraped away in a wide curve, exposing black stone. Something heavy had landed there more than once.

Stark crouched to examine one of the marks.

"Claws," he said.

The ground moved faintly beneath his hand.
