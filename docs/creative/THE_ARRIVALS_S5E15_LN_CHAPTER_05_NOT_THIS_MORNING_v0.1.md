# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 22 — Not This Morning

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** Mau chooses rest after the short combat opening; the party uses that interval for nearby reconnaissance, repair and ordinary companionship rather than coercion

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day five, morning.*

Mau woke before Frieren and did not get up.

This was unusual enough that she noticed when she opened her eyes.

"What hurts?"

"Mostly yesterday."

She moved carefully to look at his hand. The bruise had settled into a dull colour. He could close the fingers without sharp pain. The heaviness behind his eyes was another matter.

"I'm not trying anything this morning," he said.

"Good."

"I thought you'd have more to say."

"I can."

"No, that was plenty."

She rested against him for a moment before getting up. They had enough food for the planned week. They still needed a way out. Those facts did not mean every person had to spend every waking moment forcing progress out of the ground.

Frieren planned a short look at the nearby ridge with Fern, Stark and Yuka. Near enough to return quickly, along the marked ground they could see from camp. Milim stayed with Mau, partly as guard and partly because she had discovered a small slope excellent for sliding a flat piece of bark.

She insisted this was research.

Mau, sitting with the torn edge of his bag and a needle, agreed.

Yuka checked his water before leaving. She made no attempt to hide it this time. He thanked her and asked her to bring back an improved description of the ridge so he could ruin it on the map.

She promised to make it very simple.

After the others left, he repaired the bag. Slowly. He was not particularly good at it. Wakana would have chosen a cleaner line and someone else might have invented a reason that only a perfect repair counted. Mau pulled the seam tight, checked that it held, and decided a very ugly working bag would do.

Milim slid past the shelter for the fourth time.

"You should try."

"I am resting."

"It's sitting down very quickly."

He considered the argument, then looked at the steep part she had not mentioned.

"I'll watch your research."

She accepted that, which surprised him. The next slide ended badly. She came back covered in snow and announced that the bark had betrayed her.

He helped brush the snow out of her hair.

"You can tell Rimuru that part."

"He'll say I told you so."

"Only if you tell him you already knew."

She thought about this and decided it was valuable advice.

By the time the others returned, Mau had repaired the bag, dried the last two damp cloths, and won an argument about whether bark could have malicious intent. His head felt clearer. He said so when Frieren asked, without volunteering for anything larger.

She showed him the new line on the map.

"A pass," she said. "And something beyond it that made the ground shake."

He looked at the direction she meant.

"Tomorrow?"

"We approach carefully. We don't arrive tired because we were impatient today."

He liked the answer enough to finish mending one of her straps without telling her he had noticed it fraying.
