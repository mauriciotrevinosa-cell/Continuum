# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 21 — What Was Left

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 21
**Continuity state:** Food, crystals and limits are counted separately; no secret inventory or healing supply appears; the party chooses the final approach together

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate night five.*

Fern counted the remaining food after supper.

Yuka helped with the parcels. Mau helped by not calling half a parcel a whole one because the wrapping still had corners. Milim watched all three with the grave attention the task deserved.

They had enough for the next two days. A little flexibility if meals became smaller. No room for pretending they had packed a second week somewhere nobody had looked.

"Tomorrow we find out what's in the pass," Frieren said.

"And if it isn't the way out?" Stark asked.

"We reassess while we can still choose. We don't spend the last food walking toward a thing merely because it's large."

He nodded. She had wanted the question asked. Mau could see it in the way she waited for the others instead of closing the map at once.

Yuka asked about the archers. Fern asked whether they could approach the high ground from below the wind. Milim asked whether the great claw marks meant something large enough to carry them out.

"We don't know that there's an out above us," Frieren said.

Milim looked disappointed. Then thoughtful.

"Still worth looking."

"From here," Frieren said.

The question got a place on the map. So did the alternatives. They had less information than they wanted and more than they had started with. That was what the days had given them.

Afterward Yuka counted the crystals into separate cloth parcels. Everything belonged in the return record. Mau's invisible points did not make the crystals his, any more than Fern's list made the food hers.

"A very profitable week," Milim said, peering at the little pile.

"If we get it home," Yuka said.

Mau thought she might regret saying it. She did not take it back. Milim sat beside her and helped tie the cloth instead.

They made a carrying frame from two sound pieces of fallen wood and the spare straps. Stark checked the joins. Nobody intended to make Mau carry it over a mountain; the awkward case-sized space might be useful for whatever else they found, and it kept separate bundles together.

Mau tested its balance near the ground with part of the load while the others were close enough to catch it. A short hover, slow, stopped before it became a performance. The new level did not fly it for him. He still needed the careful little flow he and Frieren had practised.

"That much," he said when he landed. "Not more without testing."

Frieren removed one of the parcels before he could decide to test the maximum tonight.

"Not tonight."

He accepted the rest and sat down.

They ate the last of the hot water as a very disappointing drink. Milim described the meal she wanted when they got home. Stark improved it. Yuka added fruit. Mau said Momo would be delighted to learn they had planned her entire day while she was not present.

Fern looked at them over her cup.

"You may all help make it."

The meal became less elaborate very quickly.

Frieren laughed, an unguarded little sound, and leaned against Mau as though five nights in a cold place had not used up her ability to enjoy them.

He stayed awake for his own watch and slept when it ended.
