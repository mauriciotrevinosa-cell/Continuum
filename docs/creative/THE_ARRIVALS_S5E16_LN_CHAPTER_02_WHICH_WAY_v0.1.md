# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 22 — Which Way

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** The compass is used only as a direction toward Frieren; the couple shares a reciprocal quiet interval and the lived week does not become only combat

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day six, midday.*

The needle turned while Mau was washing the pot.

He had set the compass on a dry rock well away from the water. Now it moved, following a line through the trees toward the place Frieren had gone with Fern to examine the pass.

He watched it settle.

It did not tell him whether she was cold, cross, or delighted by a peculiar stone. It did not draw him a safe path. It simply knew the direction she was in.

He found that enough to like it more every time he opened it.

Stark came down with the water containers and stopped beside him.

"Still points at her?"

"Very consistently."

"Useful."

"She says that about most of the things she keeps."

Stark smiled. He filled the first container and took it back to camp. The second stayed within Mau's reach until he had finished the pot; there was no reason both jobs had to be done by the person who happened to get there first.

When Frieren returned, the compass had turned toward the path before Mau heard her steps.

She noticed him closing it.

"Were you lost?"

"Terribly. The pot was no help."

She sat beside him on the dry bank.

They had a clearer route now. A narrow passage through the rock, with open ground beyond it and a shape moving at the far end. Not a good place to approach in the last light. Frieren intended to look properly before committing the party.

For the next few minutes, she intended to sit down.

Mau gave her his shoulder.

She rested against it, watching the water rather than opening a book immediately.

"Was it what you wanted?" he asked.

She looked up.

"Not the red part. The adventure. Before the dinner, when I was trying to arrange it—I wanted you to have the front. I didn't mean this."

"I know."

"I know you know."

She took his hand, turning it so she could see the fading bruise without making that the whole reason to hold it.

"I wanted to go with them," she said. "With you. We have."

He waited while she found the rest.

"I want to take us home too."

"We will help."

She nodded. Then, after a moment, she asked what he wanted to do first at home.

He nearly said check on everyone. The answer came so easily that he stopped to consider whether it was the one she had asked for.

"A bath."

"Sensible."

"And something with fresh fruit. Sit somewhere without boots. Listen to you tell me what the next useless book does."

"Boot drying is useful."

"So you've said several times to my very grateful toes."

She laughed and put her head back on his shoulder.

They went back after a while. He carried one container, she the other. The compass lay closed in his pocket.

That evening she read beside him, and he tried the small supported hover once with her keeping her weight partly under her own control. They covered no important distance. When he stopped, she stayed leaning over him long enough to kiss his nose.

Yuka looked away too late and demanded that someone find her a different camp.

No one did.
