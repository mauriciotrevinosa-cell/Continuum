# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 23 — The High Pass

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 23
**Continuity state:** Frieren scouts and plans the final encounter without attacking tired; all six have distinct jobs and a route back to shelter

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day six, late afternoon.*

Stark knew the shape before he saw the whole body.

The fold of a wing against the rock. The long curve of a neck. A claw scraping loose stone from a ledge as though it had never considered anything below it worth keeping intact.

He lay beside Frieren behind the last low rise and breathed through his nose.

"Dragon," he said.

"Yes."

Smaller than the one he remembered from his old world. The fact did not make his hands stop remembering the weight of the axe before that fight.

This one had room to fly.

The hollow beyond the pass opened between high stone shoulders, with a broken line of pillars down the centre. Scrapes marked their upper edges. The dragon used them when it turned. Several had fallen, leaving low shelter that would not survive a direct hit but might keep a person out of the first blast.

Frieren watched it make a short circuit of the hollow.

"Agile," she said.

Stark nodded. The fear was still there. It had never stopped being there simply because he had done the work once.

He could work with it.

They went back before the light fell. Fern listened to the description and asked for the distance between the pillars. Yuka drew the hollow as Stark remembered it, then gave him the pencil to fix the part he could see more clearly than he could explain.

Milim's eyes brightened at the word dragon.

Frieren pointed at her before she could propose a solution involving herself and the entire sky.

"Stay with our timing."

"I said I would."

"I'm reminding you."

Milim folded her arms. Then she listened.

Mau would take the sheltered ground with Stark, avoiding the first approach. He could watch and signal; any narrow opening he requested would be checked with Ciel, not stacked into a chain because the fight made him excited. Milim could interrupt a landing and protect the ground pair. Fern and Frieren would move above, forcing turns rather than wasting shot after shot on where the dragon had been.

Yuka would mark the ground route and hold the retreat line clear. She knew the knots, had watched where the loose rock slid, and could tell when a path they had chosen stopped being usable. Her dog would stay unsummoned for the opening; they did not know what a dragon's first answer would cost a small scout.

They discussed what would make them retreat.

It took longer than discussing how they might win. Stark liked that more than he expected.

Before sleeping, he checked the axe one final time. Fern sat beside him, sewing a short tear in her outer layer.

"Afraid?" she asked without looking up.

"Yes."

"Me too."

He turned toward her.

She finished the stitch before meeting his eyes.

"We have a plan," she said.

He nodded.

For a while they sat together without trying to improve the answer.
