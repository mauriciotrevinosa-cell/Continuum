# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 27 — Before the Dragon

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 27
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. Red Gate night six, the last watch before the dragon: Frieren and Mau keep watch together; she admits she likes leading and is afraid for Stark; he does not promise the small Red; the compass points at her from his pocket; nothing new is planned or unlocked

*When: Red Gate night six, the last watch, a few hours before dawn and the dragon. The plan is drawn on Yuka's map. Everyone else is asleep, or pretending to be.*

Frieren did not wake up complaining for once.

Mau touched her shoulder for the last watch and she opened her eyes at once, as if she had been lying there waiting. Maybe she had. She got up without a word and pulled her coat round her and came to sit with him at the mouth of the shelter, and the fire between them was low and red, and beyond it the snow was blue under the strange stars.

Fern and Stark had the watch before. Fern had woken him with her hand on his arm and a look that said *she's not asleep, she hasn't been asleep*, and then gone back to her roll beside Stark and turned her back to the fire very deliberately.

So Mau sat with Frieren and did not ask why she had not been sleeping.

He knew why. The same reason he had not.

Over the pass, in the hollow with the broken pillars, something very large was sleeping too. Or not sleeping. They had heard it twice since sunset, the sound of stone dragged over stone, and once, much later, a long low noise like wind through a cave, that was not wind.

* * *

"I like it," said Frieren, after a long time.

Mau looked at her.

"Leading." She was looking at the fire. "I didn't know I would. On the journey, with Himmel, I never led. Himmel led. He always led. I walked behind and found things and he decided where we went." She paused. "I thought that was what I was. Someone who walks behind and finds things."

"And now?"

"And now I've been deciding where six people go for six days," said Frieren. "And I like it. I like choosing the long way round. I like it when Fern says *we should stop here* and I listen and she's right. I like it when Stark asks a question and it changes the plan." She turned a stick in the embers. "I like being the one who says *we go round*."

Mau smiled a little.

"You're good at it," he said.

"I'm slow at it."

"You're careful at it. That's different."

Frieren did not answer that. But she moved a little closer to him on the cold stone.

"I'm afraid for Stark," she said.

Mau did not say anything.

"He's fought a dragon before," said Frieren. "In his world. Before he met us. He thinks I don't know how afraid he was. Eisen told me." She was quiet. "He's going to stand behind that pillar tomorrow, with you, and wait for my signal. And he's going to be afraid the whole time. And he's going to do it anyway, because I asked."

"Because he wants to," said Mau.

"Because I asked," said Frieren again. "That's what leading is. I've been learning it all week. You ask people to stand somewhere frightening. And they do it. And if something goes wrong, it's because you asked."

Mau looked at her face in the low red light.

"That's what Rimuru carries," he said slowly. "Every day. On the council. When the house decides something and it goes wrong." He paused. "And Fern. With the ledger. Every time she writes a name on the gate list."

Frieren looked at him.

"I never thought about it like that," she said. "Fern."

"I think about it a lot," said Mau. "Because I always used to be the one who went in front. I never had to ask anyone to stand somewhere. I just stood there myself." He looked at his bandaged hand. "It's much harder to ask."

Frieren was quiet for a long time.

"Then I'll ask carefully," she said at last.

"You already do."

* * *

Later, she said, "The small Red."

Mau had known she would.

"You drew the line on the map," she said. "Past the broken pillar. If you can make it. If Ciel says yes."

"Yes."

"Can you make it?"

Mau looked at the fire.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "I made one in the plaza, once, when the walls broke. It went very wrong. This week the defence has worked twice and the strike once. Small. Ciel helping. Each time it worked." He opened his good hand and closed it. "I think I can make a small one. One. I think. I won't know until I try."

"Then don't promise it."

"I'm not promising it."

"Good." Frieren did not look away from him. "Because I won't build the fight around it. I already told you. If you call it off, Stark stays in cover, and Fern and I force another turn, and that's the plan too." She paused. "I don't want you making something you can't hold because you think the fight needs it."

"I know."

"Do you?"

He looked at her. At the green eyes that had watched him come back from the white place, and from the sky, and from the edge of the universe.

"I do," he said. "I'll try once. If Ciel says the edges are wrong, I'll stop. If it starts pulling at anything else, I'll stop. If you call it off, I'll stop." He held up his bandaged hand. "And I'll tell you what it cost. After."

Frieren looked at him for a long moment.

Then she nodded, once.

"That's the plan," she said.

* * *

The sky began to grey at the edge of the ridge.

Neither of them had noticed the time going. Mau reached into his pocket without thinking and took out the compass, and opened it, and watched the needle swing and settle.

It pointed at her. Sitting right beside him. A hand's breadth away.

"It still works," said Frieren.

"I'm being thorough."

"I'm right here."

"I know." He looked at the needle. "That's the bit I like. Tomorrow, when you're up there in the air above the dragon and I'm behind a pillar with Stark, I'll be able to open this and know exactly where you are." He closed it. "That's all it does. But it's enough."

Frieren looked at the closed silver case in his hand.

"I didn't give it to you for dragons," she said.

"I know. You gave it to me for home."

"Yes."

"Tomorrow," said Mau, "you're home."

Frieren did not say anything for a long time. Then she leaned against him, her head on his shoulder, and they watched the grey come up over the ridge and turn slowly to pale gold, and listened to the very large thing in the hollow beyond the pass begin, quite slowly, to wake.

Behind them, Fern sat up. Then Stark. Then Yuka, rubbing her eyes. Then Milim, all at once, wide awake, already looking at the pass.

Frieren lifted her head off Mau's shoulder.

"Breakfast first," she said. "Then the dragon."

Nobody argued.
