# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 24 — The Dragon

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** The planned six-person encounter begins; Milim roars back and follows Frieren's timing while the others establish their positions

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day seven, morning.*

The dragon screamed at Milim.

She screamed back.

For one splendid, appalling instant, both sides seemed equally offended that the other had come into the hollow.

"Milim," Frieren said.

"I'm in position!"

She was. Down on the lower shelf, gauntlets up, feet planted where Stark had shown her the stone would hold. Frieren decided to accept the report and moved left through the air.

Fern took the opposite side.

The first Zoltraak was not meant to kill. It crossed the dragon's path where going straight would carry it into the broken pillars. The beast banked sharply, just as it had when Frieren watched it yesterday, and exposed the thin underside of one wing to Fern's shot.

The hit scored a pale line. Not enough to ground it.

Enough to make it change its mind about which mage to chase.

"Down," Fern called.

Stark and Mau moved behind the fallen pillar. Heat swept over it. Stone cracked above their heads, scattering chips into Yuka's marked path.

She pulled the loose line clear before it could snag under their feet.

"Right is blocked! Left still open!"

Stark changed his next movement without stopping to ask her twice.

Milim met the descending claw with both gauntlets. The impact drove her backward across the shelf. She did not try to win the whole fight in that one contest. She shoved sideways, spoiling the landing, and rolled clear when Frieren called her name.

The axe struck the exposed foreleg.

Stark felt the jolt through both arms. The gate-forged edge bit, caught, and came free when the dragon recoiled. He ducked before the tail took the place his ribs had occupied.

Mau caught the back of his strap and helped him into cover.

"Still afraid?" Mau asked, breathless.

"Very."

"Good. I was worried I was overdoing it."

The next blast ended the conversation.

Fern's defence held long enough to take the worst from the shelter. Frieren crossed above the dragon's head, drew its gaze, and fired from a new angle. It folded its wing to avoid the direct line and twisted farther than she had expected.

Fast. Faster after the wound.

She went up, not back, and felt the edge of its jaw pass beneath her boot.

"Fern, high right. Stark, wait."

The dragon climbed after her.

For a moment the whole hollow lay open below it: the broken pillars, the six small figures, the path Yuka had kept clear. Frieren saw the next turn forming in the body before the wings completed it.

Not toward her.

Toward the ground pair.

She changed the shot before firing, driving the beast away from the direct plunge. Fern closed the other angle. Milim jumped to the lower pillar and struck the wing joint as it passed, taking a glancing blow that threw her into the snow.

She got up, furious.

"I'm fine!"

"Then move left," Frieren said.

Milim moved.

The dragon came down toward the gap they had left.

Mau looked up from behind the pillar.

His left hand opened.
