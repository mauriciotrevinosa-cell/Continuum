# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 27 — Two

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 27
**Continuity state:** The second grimoire, fire crystal, roughly100 removable bracelets, owner-bound party key and unused one-time linked-entrance relocation reward are obtained; no immediate upgrades are applied

*When: Day 280 outside / Red Gate day seven, the rewards.*

The way out appeared at the far end of the hollow.

Blue light gathered between the two sound pillars beyond the dragon's place.

Fern stopped moving with the empty wrapping still in her hands.

"Frieren."

Not an alarm. The difference made all of them look.

For a moment Mau could not let himself understand the colour. He had spent a week trying not to look for a door in every place the snow made a clean edge. He had stopped asking Ciel whether anything had changed because hearing no again wasn't helping anyone.

Now the light was simply there.

Milim took a step toward it. Then stopped and looked at Frieren.

"We go together," Frieren said.

They approached far enough to see the opening clearly without passing through. Beyond the light there was grass. Ordinary grass, without frost along every blade.

Stark put a hand on the stone beside him. Yuka made a small sound and laughed at it, then covered her mouth because she hadn't meant to laugh yet.

"Blue," she said.

"I see it," Mau answered.

She looked at him as if that hadn't been the point, and he realized she needed somebody else to say it too.

"Blue again."

They did not send one person through and divide the party before knowing more. They traced the clear approach together. It stayed open. When they turned back for the scattered things, Fern kept glancing over her shoulder.

So did Mau.

Gathering the rewards no longer felt like finding something worth staying for. It was the last work between them and the grass beyond that light.

Crystals. Several sizes, wrapped separately.

One larger piece with a warm red seam inside it.

A long wooden case, banded shut with plain metal.

And a book.

Frieren reached the book before Mau finished standing up.

She opened it, read the first page, and turned toward him.

He knew the look.

"Your Majesty?"

"Not yet. Help me collect everything."

He laughed, tired enough that it came out softer than he meant. She put the book beside the first one in the dry wrapping. Two. Two days, freely offered before they knew this place would keep them a week.

This book softened bread that had gone hard. Milim looked deeply offended that it had arrived after six nights of travel rations.

"We could have used it."

"We can use it later," Frieren said.

"Later isn't now."

"A profound difficulty."

They opened the case after checking it. Inside, in shallow rows, lay bracelets: plain bands with small dark faces, adjustable enough to fit different wrists. Yuka counted a hundred.

Fern tried one after they had tested the first band without finding an active attack. The face lit beneath her fingers. A small display appeared angled toward her.

Not a sudden gift of someone else's skills. A list, incomplete until compared with its wearer: available operations, locked ones, a recovery section still gathering its reference.

"It measures," she said. "It doesn't grant the things it names."

She removed it. The display went out. When she put it back on, it waited for her to call it again.

They checked sharing with a single harmless field she chose to show. Frieren could see that much. Not the rest before Fern selected it. Nobody treated an unfamiliar measuring tool as permission to open another person's whole interior.

Mau's branch supplied an appraisal when he looked at the red crystal. Gate material. Fire imbuement. A compatible mounting required; a charge that would be consumed in use.

"Not something we push into the axe and swing indoors," he said.

Stark looked at the crystal with considerable interest anyway.

The axe itself appeared as gate equipment, rank E. The tool's rank, not a verdict about the person holding it. The unfamiliar scale raised more questions than it answered, and Fern wrote it down for later rather than assigning everyone a letter on the spot.

A key formed in Mau's open hand.

He stopped so suddenly that Yuka nearly walked into him.

All six could see it. Milim took it when he offered, turned it over, and gave it back. Real weight. Real edges.

The small notice beside it named an owner.

*Mau. Entry requires the owner. Party entry permitted. Opening in seven days.*

No name for the place beyond it yet.

Another reward waited below: permission to relocate the linked normal entrance and its shop once. Not every gate, not a duplicate. A site would have to be chosen and confirmed before anything moved.

And a shop expansion available to apply there, not a new catalogue they had already inspected.

Mau left both claims unused.

"Home first," Frieren said.

He closed his hand around the key.

"Home first."
