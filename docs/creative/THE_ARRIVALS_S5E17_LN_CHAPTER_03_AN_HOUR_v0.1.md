# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 32 — An Hour

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 32
**Continuity state:** The six return after one lived interior week; Mau carries the reward frame in a short low hover while Frieren supports her own weight; exterior restrictions return

*When: Day 280, approximately one exterior hour after entry.*

Rimuru had just asked Anko to repeat the time when the red moved.

He stood. Yuta was already nearer the surface, Maki beside him. Gojo lifted the blindfold again.

The colour thinned. Blue showed at the middle.

"Clear the path," Shoko said.

The first thing through was a wooden frame full of bundles.

The second was Mau beneath it, horizontal and very close to the ground, looking deeply committed to an arrangement he might no longer be enjoying.

Frieren sat above his hips with two books held safely against her. Her own flight kept most of her weight from settling on him. A packed week's worth of empty food space had become room for rewards; the other four still carried their own remaining equipment.

For one astonished moment nobody spoke.

"A little to the left," Frieren said.

"I can see the left."

"Then use it."

He cleared the opening.

The familiar resistance returned across his body like a heavy blanket. He lowered at once, as they had planned, the frame settling on its padded supports just outside the gate rather than becoming a demonstration all the way home.

Frieren got off before he sat up.

Milim came through next, then Yuka, then Stark and Fern. Six. All six. Rimuru counted them himself before anyone could tell him the number.

Yuka saw Yuta and dropped the bag.

He caught her against him. Maki reached them a moment later and put both arms around whatever was left available.

Milim was describing the dragon at considerable volume before she noticed Ori beside Umaru. Then she changed direction and nearly lifted both girls from the ground.

Mau looked from one face to another.

"How long?"

Anko checked the watch as though she distrusted it personally.

"An hour."

Stark laughed.

Nobody else joined him until he tried to explain the reason and failed.

"A week," Fern said. Her voice was steady, but her hand had found his sleeve. "For us. Seven days."

Rimuru looked at their clothes, the worn bags, the smoke caught in every fold. Frieren's hair was not arranged the way it had been when she entered. Mau's fingers were wrapped. Stark had a new cut on one sleeve. All of that history had happened while Anko turned a watch over in her palm.

"Of course," Rimuru said faintly. "Of course it was."

Then he sat down on the grass, not because he had intended to but because his knees had ended the discussion.

Mau crouched beside him.

"Hey."

"Don't hey me yet. Give me a minute."

"All right."

He stayed there. Frieren held the books and let him.

Shoko checked each of them before allowing the reunion to turn into six competing explanations. No one needed carrying to the clinic immediately. Mau's hand needed cleaning; Milim had a bruise she was pretending belonged to the dragon; everyone needed water, food, and a rest measured by the week they had lived rather than by the watch outside.

When Mau stood, Ori was waiting.

He knelt again before she could decide how carefully to hug him.

"I have a better map," he said into her hair.

She held him tighter.

Behind them, the gate returned to blue. The reward frame stayed exactly where they had set it, inconveniently blocking half the path.

Anko looked at it, looked at the six, and allowed it one more minute before complaining.
