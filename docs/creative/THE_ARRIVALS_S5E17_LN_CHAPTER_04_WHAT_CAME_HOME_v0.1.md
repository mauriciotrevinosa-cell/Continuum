# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 29 — What Came Home

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 29
**Continuity state:** Medical care and chosen reports follow the return; loot and shadow resources remain distinct and shared-clock relief does not erase the week

*When: Day 280, late morning.*

Shoko let Mau finish saying one dragon before holding up a hand.

"The hand first."

He gave her the hand.

The cloth came away cleanly. The damage was small and real, where the edge of the Red had touched his fingers. Not another ruined forearm. Not something he could call nothing because they had once seen much worse.

He described the operation. The earlier strike by the pond. The headache after the arrow. The recovery between them.

Ciel, with the normal host connection restored, could help compare the local account with what Rimuru had missed. She did not pretend she had been relaying all week to someone who plainly had not received it.

"No demonstrations today," Shoko said.

"I wasn't going to."

"Tell the dragon."

He smiled despite himself. "I will."

They discussed that part carefully. One registered shape. Ten storage places did not mean ten affordable summons. The separate reserve had drained quickly while the first stood; storing it stopped the drain but did not refund the expenditure. Mau had not acquired an endless heart or a new supply for every other operation inside him.

Rimuru listened. Maomao listened. Frieren stayed beside the bed rather than trying to supply his answers for him.

The heartbeat under Shoko's fingers was still one heartbeat.

Downstairs, Fern and Anko made the return record. Books. Crystals. The case of bracelets. A fire component needing examination before use. An unspent relocation claim and a pending shop expansion. The key remained with Mau, its owner condition recorded; seven days before the opening did not require seven days of waiting at the stake.

Nobody moved an entrance through the house because they were excited to have the option.

Yuka showed Yuta the corrected route, talking faster at the parts where she had been afraid. He let her finish. Maki asked about the dog and received an exact answer about how long she had been called, what she had found, and when Yuka had brought her back.

"You did well," Maki said.

Yuka looked down at the map.

Then she began explaining the rope.

At the other end of the room, Milim told Ori about the breakfast that had almost escaped the pot. Ori laughed hard enough to interrupt the dragon story, which Milim considered an excellent reason to repeat the breakfast part.

Momo had not yet made the grand return meal they had designed without her. She offered what was actually available and watched them eat it as though it might be the best food anybody had invented.

Stark ate slowly. Fern leaned against his shoulder between mouthfuls. Neither hurried to prove a week was nothing just because the sun outside had barely moved.

Mau came down after the cleaning and check, ate, and put the paper with his points where Umaru could see it.

"I didn't spend them."

She looked at the number. Then at him.

"Good. Now we need a much larger page."

He had missed her so much that the complaint made him laugh.

She pushed a cup toward him and told him not to get sentimental over responsible planning.
