# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 30 — Two Books, Two Days

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 30
**Continuity state:** The party rests after its lived week; Queen2 is still to begin by agreement, the fort day remains owed and future key/bracelet study waits

*When: Day 280, afternoon and evening.*

Frieren put the two new books on the bed and looked at Mau.

He sat down with considerable care.

"Your Majesty."

"Not today."

"A merciful sovereign."

"A tired one."

He laughed and lay back. She came beside him, leaving the books safely beyond the place either of them would roll into. The older five flowers stayed on the shelf. The map from the family day remained on Ori's side of the connecting door. They had been away a week without the room having time to change.

It was a strange kindness.

Rimuru went up to the loft above the closet after checking that Mau really intended to sleep rather than rise and organize every reward before evening. He said he was going to the office later. Not now.

Frieren found Mau looking at the key on the bedside table.

"Seven days," she said.

"It can have them."

She put the compass beside it, then moved the key farther back so he would not confuse the familiar silver with a thing he did not yet understand.

He watched the care of the movement.

"You enjoyed the books?"

"I haven't read the second one."

"The getting them."

She settled against him.

"Yes. I enjoyed being there with you. With all of them."

He had a great deal he could have said about the red barrier, the fear, the way her voice had kept the group together. He said the part she had answered instead.

"Me too."

They slept through part of the afternoon.

When Mau woke, Ori sat on the floor with the new map and the old one spread beside each other. Frieren was still asleep against him. Her hand had caught the edge of his shirt again.

"This one's better," Ori whispered.

"Yuka did most of it."

"I could tell."

He tried to look offended without waking the elf. Ori grinned and moved the two maps together, comparing the stream with the terribly drawn fountain on the scrap from the first outing.

"We still have the fort day," she said.

"I haven't forgotten."

"I know. I'm putting where we want to go on the back. Not the dungeon. Here."

He looked at the lines she was adding.

"Good. We need a day that doesn't change colour."

She laughed into one hand.

Frieren stirred.

"If it has chests, I'm coming."

"It has the fort," Ori said.

"A dangerous place."

"You're already invited."

Frieren opened one eye. "Then I'll attend."

That evening, the six found one another at supper without having arranged to. Yuka sat near Fern to ask about the frost rings. Milim made Stark repeat the part where the dragon had been rude, and he improved the impression until she protested that she had been much louder than that.

Gojo asked about the fight. Shoko put a hand over his cup before he could turn the answer into a proposed experiment. He accepted the warning and asked a different question about the weather.

Afterward Mau went upstairs with Frieren. She chose the new bread book, gave him the less sore hand to hold it with, and let him find a comfortable place against her.

One shadow rested beneath the ordinary shadow at his feet. He did not call it to prove it was still there.

Outside the window the house went on with a day it had not known to miss.

Frieren opened to the first page.

"Two days," she reminded him.

"I remember."

"Soon."

He kissed her shoulder.

"Soon."
