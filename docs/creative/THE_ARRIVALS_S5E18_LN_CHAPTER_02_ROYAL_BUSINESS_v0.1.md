# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 2 — Royal Business

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 2
**Continuity state:** Frieren studies the two new books with Mau while ordinary shared work continues; the old five kept flowers and separate grimoire acquisitions remain intact

*When: Day 281, late morning.*

The first royal duty was holding a page down.

Mau performed it with distinction until a gust turned the page on top of his hand.

Frieren looked at him.

"Sabotage," he said.

"By whom?"

"The climate."

She put the dried twig back across the corner and resumed explaining the bread spell.

It was not a spell for creating bread. That distinction disappointed Milim, who had stopped on the way to find Ori and stayed long enough to hear only the useful half of the description. It did not turn mouldy bread edible. It did not put missing food back. Frieren read the conditions with proper attention before allowing anybody to offer the worst loaf in the house as a test.

Mau appreciated that. His experience of magic had given him a great respect for a thing willing to say what it did not do.

They took a hard end of yesterday's bread to the kitchen and asked Momo before using it. Rem found a clean plate. Ram inspected the sample and suggested it might be useful as a doorstop if the experiment failed.

Frieren chose the smallest piece.

The first attempt softened the edge. The middle stayed stubborn. She stopped, changed the angle of the flow, and made Mau explain what he could see through his ordinary eyes before deciding whether his impression matched hers.

"It looks like bread being offended."

"A valuable contribution."

Rem pressed the edge with a fingertip.

"Better here."

That was valuable. Frieren changed the next attempt accordingly.

They ended with a small piece that was edible without winning a fight against it. Not fresh from the oven. Softer. A useful, limited result worth testing again another day.

Milim reappeared just in time to volunteer to eat the evidence.

They let her.

"I approve," she said.

"An exacting review," Ram observed.

Mau washed the plate afterward. Frieren dried it, still wearing the crown. Momo looked at the two of them, decided the arrangement was producing clean dishes, and allowed the monarchy to continue.

They returned to the bench with the books.

These two were not the three from the earlier outing. Nor the two flower books Mau had bought at the market. Frieren had begun keeping the copied notes separately, and now he could tell which part of a page she had learned by the way she stopped explaining and asked him to look.

He liked being able to tell.

After a while he shifted the book onto his knees so she could lean against him without bending her wrist awkwardly.

"A good subject," she said.

"An excellent page weight."

She kissed his cheek without closing the book.

The wind tried again and lost.
