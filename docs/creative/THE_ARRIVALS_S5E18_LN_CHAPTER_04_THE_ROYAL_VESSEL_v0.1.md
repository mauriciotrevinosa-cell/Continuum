# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 34 — The Royal Vessel

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 34
**Continuity state:** A short controlled kitchen approach develops the existing low hover comedy; Frieren supports part of her own weight and medical/space limits remain

*When: Day 282, late morning.*

Rimuru saw the book first.

It came around the courtyard edge at the height of a seated person's face, followed by silver hair, old gold paint, and Mau looking down the path with the concentration of a man transporting something breakable and highly opinionated.

"No," Rimuru said.

Frieren looked up.

"You haven't heard the request."

"I can see it arriving."

They were barely above the paving. Frieren kept much of her own weight lifted, as they had done on the way out of the gate; Mau provided the slow movement and balance. The stops had been tested, the path kept clear, and Ciel had already vetoed the narrow turn through the kitchen doorway.

That last fact had not prevented Frieren from selecting the doorway as a destination.

Mau lowered them at the open approach before the threshold.

"Here," he said.

She looked from him to the door.

"Very well."

"An honour to cross the entire known courtyard, Your Majesty."

"The known courtyard is small."

He sat up while she got off. No shaking hands. Ordinary breathlessness he had expected and did not attempt to conceal. They rested on the bench before making any request of the kitchen.

Rimuru stood in front of them with his cup.

"I thought a dangerous new capacity might lead to something dignified."

"You became a slime," Mau said.

"Unnecessary."

Frieren held out her cup. "Since you're standing."

"The pass says Mau only."

"That was a request."

Rimuru looked at her, then at Mau, then remembered someone else had once lost an argument using those exact distinctions and decided not to help history repeat itself.

He took the cup.

Momo, seeing the delivery arrive from the wrong person, asked no questions until she had made him admit what happened. Then she laughed so loudly that Okarun came to find the cause and stayed to enjoy it.

Mau rested his head against the wall.

"Happy?" he asked Frieren.

She looked at him over the open book.

"Extremely."

It was such an immediate, unguarded answer that he stopped preparing a complaint.

She noticed. Put the book down. Leaned over and kissed him, crown slipping slightly forward as she did.

From the doorway Okarun made an approving sound and was pulled back by Momo before he could turn it into a comment.

Rimuru returned with the tea.

"I want this entered as a public service," he said.

Frieren took the cup with both hands.

"Your service is appreciated."

He walked away before she could improve the title.

They returned to the ordinary chairs after the rest. The hover did not become the afternoon's only way of moving. Mau found that pleased him too: he could do a small impossible thing, use it for something foolish and happy, then put it away.

Frieren opened the next page.

He sat where she could lean on him.
