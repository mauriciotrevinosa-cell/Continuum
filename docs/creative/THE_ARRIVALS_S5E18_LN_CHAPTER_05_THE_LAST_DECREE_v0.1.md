# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 39 — The Last Decree

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 39
**Continuity state:** Queen2's two lived days conclude; the couple chooses a small reciprocal outing for tomorrow without spending the fort day or making another lifelong binding promise

*When: Day 282, evening.*

The crown came off before supper.

Mau noticed as soon as Frieren sat beside him. Her hair had kept a small flattened line where the rim rested, and she was trying to smooth it with one hand while holding the bread book with the other.

He took the book.

"A crisis of succession?"

"It was uncomfortable."

"Two days is a long time to be oak."

She gave him a look, then leaned nearer so he could help with the flattened place.

At the other end of the table, Yuka was explaining the pond to Maki again, this time with Stark helping place the turns. Milim objected to being represented by the smallest cup. Yuta quietly replaced it with the largest one before she could improve the demonstration by breaking the pond.

Fern ate while she listened. Somebody asked her a question about the bracelets and she said they could wait until after the meal. Nobody burst into flame because they did.

Mau passed the bread to her when she reached.

"Thank you," she said.

He nodded and took his own piece.

Later, upstairs, Frieren put the crown away with more care than its two days of absurdity might have justified. Mau watched her turn it so the old flowers did not catch against the edge.

The five pressed flowers on the shelf stayed where they were. Different flowers. A different history, one he had learned not to gather into a single sentimental pile just because he loved the person who kept them.

"Finished?" he asked.

"One more decree."

He leaned against the pillows.

"I've been expecting it."

She came to sit beside him, without the crown now.

"Tomorrow you choose something. Just for a little while. Something you want to do with me."

"That's a decree?"

"A request."

He looked at her.

"You can say no," she added.

"I wasn't going to. I was thinking."

She waited. It was a harder question than what needed doing, which usually arrived with a useful list of people who needed it done.

"Walk with me," he said at last. "Inside the boundary. No plan to find anything. We take something to eat, stop somewhere, come back when we want."

"No books?"

"One small one. For an emergency."

Her face warmed into a smile.

"Agreed."

They settled against the pillows. The monarchy had not moved the loft, annexed the fort or solved the food budget. It had produced dry boots, a little softer bread, two days of ridiculous names and a great deal of time he would otherwise have found reasons to give away.

Frieren rested her hand on his chest.

"Good subject," she said.

"Retired."

"Temporarily."

"That wager was for one dungeon."

"I remember."

He could hear the smile before he saw it.

He kissed her and did not offer another treaty.
