# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 36 — Nothing To Find

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 36
**Continuity state:** Frieren follows through on a small outing centred on Mau's choice; the separate fort day remains tomorrow and no gate opening or new power is attempted

*When: Day 283, late morning and afternoon.*

Frieren brought one small book.

Mau inspected it at the door.

"An emergency?"

"It might be."

"You haven't seen the emergency yet."

"Preparation is important."

He accepted that and took the food she handed him. She had arranged it herself after asking what he liked, rather than gathering whatever was nearest and declaring him fed. He noticed. She noticed him noticing and told him to stop standing in the doorway.

They walked the inner path, where the house stayed behind the trees and the boundary was a known line beyond them. Nobody needed them to inspect something. The key had several days left on it. The shop questions would wait for the people and pages they required.

Near the shallow water, Mau stopped to look at a handful of flat stones.

Frieren watched him choose one.

"A specimen?"

"I want to try something extremely ordinary."

He sent it low across the surface. It struck once, twice, then sank.

She looked at the little rings.

"That's it?"

"That's it. Your turn."

Her first throw went straight down with a small, decisive splash. Mau admired it with enough restraint to remain alive.

She selected another stone. This one skipped six times in a line that was much too even.

He turned toward her.

"Magic?"

"A little."

"The entire point was the throwing."

"It travelled much farther."

"Yes, Your—no. You're retired."

She laughed and gave him the next stone to demonstrate.

They spent a little while being bad at an activity that did not matter. He used his unhurt hand and stopped when the angle began tiring his arm. She tried one throw without helping it and looked so pleased with the second skip that he almost applauded.

Then they sat and ate what she had brought.

The little book stayed closed beside her. Mau did not point it out. He was learning that a thing did not have to be remarked upon every time to count as having happened.

They talked about the meal, about Milim's verdict on the bread, about which turn would bring them back through the quieter part of the yard. At some point Frieren leaned against him and closed her eyes without being asleep.

"Good?" she asked.

"Very."

"Even with the magic?"

"I'll overlook it."

She kissed his jaw, pleased with his generosity.

They came home by the workshop. Wakana was at the door speaking with Marin, and this time the bench they passed had all its proper supports. Mau admired it without offering to adjust anything.

Frieren put her hand in his.

The book made it home unread.
