# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 43 — The Other Ending

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 43
**Continuity state:** Bocchi shares the passage she chose to change, with Kita and the band retaining their own agency; the fort day is not a concert milestone or a cure for anxiety

*When: Day 284, afternoon.*

Bocchi had meant to play only the passage.

Then she heard the noise in the room and nearly decided it was not the right time. Somebody was still arguing about the points. A cup had gone missing. Milim was explaining a victory that appeared to grow every time she repeated it.

Kita looked at her hands.

"Now?"

Bocchi nodded before the answer could become too large to give.

Kita moved the loose page out from under the guitar's edge. Nothing ceremonial. No announcement requiring the whole room to become an audience.

Bocchi began.

The first notes came out too carefully. She let them go and tried the second phrase the way she had been playing it when nobody was waiting for it to mean anything.

Mau turned toward her.

He did not tell the room to be quiet. She liked that. The people near enough to hear gradually lowered their voices; the rest went on with what they were doing, and she could be part of the afternoon without becoming responsible for all of it.

She played the old ending once.

Then the new one.

Kita had liked the little space before the change. Ryo had objected to the way the last part hurried itself. Nijika had tapped the rhythm against the table until Bocchi heard what the objection actually meant. None of that made the choice stop being hers.

Now she could hear the place where it settled.

She stopped before she could explain it badly.

Mau looked at the page, then at her.

"You kept the part I thought was the end."

"It isn't the end anymore."

"I can hear that."

He tried to mark the rhythm with two fingers against his knee. Got it wrong. Kita corrected him. He tried again and got close enough for Bocchi to show him the difference without having to defend the whole piece.

That was the conversation she had wanted.

Not whether it proved she was improving. Not whether she was brave enough to play in front of people. Just this part, this little interval, which had been bothering her and now did something she liked.

She played it again.

Frieren, sitting on the other side of Mau with her book closed, asked why the earlier version felt as though it stopped walking too soon.

Bocchi looked at her in surprise.

Then she knew how to answer.

It took a little while. Kita stayed beside her, offering a word when she needed one and leaving her the rest. Mau listened. The argument about the points resumed at the far end of the room, now with a different proposed winner.

Afterward Bocchi put the guitar down.

Kita touched her fingers briefly, a private little approval that did not need a room full of people looking at it.

Mau asked whether she would play him the next version when she had one.

She considered pretending there would not be another version.

"Probably several," she said.

"Then I'll need several afternoons."

She smiled at him before she could think too much about it.
