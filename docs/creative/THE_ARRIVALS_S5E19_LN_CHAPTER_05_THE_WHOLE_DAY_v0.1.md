# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 40 — The Whole Day

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 40
**Continuity state:** The owed fort day reaches supper and its ordinary end; no gate tests or work consume it; ongoing friendships persist without claiming the separate full Mau-day is delivered

*When: Day 284, evening into bedtime.*

The fort came down for supper in parts.

Not because the day was over. Because people needed food, and no one had arranged to turn the kitchen into a second fort simply to preserve a border.

Mau helped carry the light things after being informed that carrying dishes to eat with was allowed, while disappearing into a washing task for an hour was not. Frieren took the next stack before anybody could make the permission another discussion.

They ate among the rest of the house. People who had spent the afternoon elsewhere asked about the game, received three contradictory accounts, and chose the one most flattering to their preferred competitor.

Milim's lead grew again.

Nazuna joined them after waking for the evening and listened to Anko reconstruct the rules from the fragments that reached her. By the end she wanted to know why the sock had not been declared the winner.

Suika looked as though she might amend the result on the spot.

Mau laughed, and Umaru warned him that laughing at the ministry could cost a point.

"I have very few left."

"Then be careful with them."

After supper they returned for the part of the day Bocchi had left on her small page: a while together without needing a performance or a game to make it worth staying.

Somebody brought a book. Somebody argued quietly over the last drawing. Yuka sat beside Umaru and discussed a game they wanted to try next time, not a strategy for turning Mau's unspent points into their sole shared interest.

Coco and Suika compared the marked maps. Ori added a little detail to one corner and asked before deciding it could stay there.

Frieren leaned against Mau. He rested where he had been put, without looking around for an unclaimed task.

It was remarkably difficult for the first few minutes.

Then it wasn't.

Later he went with Ori to the window to look at the first clear patch of sky. Suika joined them, pointing out a faint light she had noticed before the cloud moved. They did not name a constellation from another world and pretend it had followed them here. They watched until a second light showed beside it.

"There," Ori said.

"I see it."

"You say that when you don't sometimes."

"Then this time I'll point."

He did. She checked his direction and accepted it.

The day ended gradually. People took cups down, put a roll where it belonged, went to wash or sleep. Nobody stood to announce that the visit had fixed every old distance between them. Mau had been there. They had done things they wanted. There would be details to remember when they met at breakfast.

Umaru folded the day's page before he left.

"Debt settled," she said.

"This one."

"This one," she agreed. "We may invite you without a debt next time."

"A radical development."

She pushed his arm and let him go.

He and Frieren walked back with Ori. At her connecting door, the old family map still hung where she had chosen it. The fort's page stayed with the fort. Neither needed to replace the other to be kept.

Frieren went through to their room while Mau waited for Ori to put her last things away.

"Good day?" he asked.

She looked at him with a face he knew well enough now not to hurry.

"You stayed."

"I said I would."

"I know."

She hugged him. Then she went to find Milim before the ribbon became tomorrow morning's problem.

Mau crossed the little threshold into his own room. Frieren had left a place beside her and no crown on it.

He lay down.

For once, the only thing he had promised to do before sleeping was already done.
