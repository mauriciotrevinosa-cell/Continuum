# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 12 — A Larger Page

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 12
**Continuity state:** Umaru begins a real provisional build sheet while Mau retains ten unspent points; Intelligence, shadow reserve, CE and existing mana are not interchangeable

*When: Day 285, late morning.*

Umaru had found the larger page.

It was not large enough.

Mau watched her put a second sheet beside it, then a third, and decided not to ask where she intended the wall to go when the table became insufficient.

Yuka sat on the opposite bench with a pencil she had already sharpened beyond necessity. She had asked to help. Umaru had given her the work of copying exactly what Mau could show, which was more useful than being allowed to invent a solution.

"First," Umaru said, "we don't spend anything."

"I remember."

"Second, we don't call two things the same because a window uses a familiar word."

She underlined Intelligence.

"That one especially. It isn't a test of whether you're clever."

"A relief."

"Unconfirmed."

Yuka laughed before looking down to copy the line.

Mau let them see the small set of fields, not the private places beyond them. Ciel could help trace which part of the new structure answered to a label. That was not the same as buying a point to find out whether something broke.

The resource associated with that branch led toward the shallow reserve he had used for the dragon. It did not currently lead toward his ordinary mana. It did not lead toward cursed energy either.

He explained that slowly enough for Umaru to separate three boxes.

"Then this isn't a universal mana button," she said.

"No."

"Not yet?"

He considered the work he could feel behind the interface.

"Something keeps trying to join things. I don't know what it will manage."

She drew the possible connection in a broken line instead of a solid one.

He liked that. It let hope exist on the page without pretending it had already happened.

They recorded the ten points as unspent, the dragon as stored rather than standing, the limit of ten registrations separately from what he could afford to summon. Yuka put a firm border around the last distinction after he described how fast the reserve had fallen during the first manifestation.

"One large thing can cost more than several little things," she said.

"Probably," Umaru said. "But we don't have several little things to test. So that's a question."

Yuka changed the mark.

They stopped before the pages became a plan for a person none of them had met yet. There would be more information after the key opened. There would also be days when Mau didn't go through a gate at all.

He looked at Umaru's careful boxes.

"You don't have to make this your whole afternoon."

She looked offended.

"I enjoy it."

"I know. I meant—"

"I also enjoy beating Yuka at the game she brought yesterday. Both activities can exist."

"You haven't beaten me," Yuka said.

"Not yet."

Mau put the pencil down between them before the argument became a reason to enlarge the table again.

Yuka brought the pieces out while Umaru folded the new page away from the playing space. Mau reached for the nearest one and was immediately told he was not playing that side.

"You could have mentioned that before I made my opening move."

"You could have waited until your turn," Yuka said.

He withdrew the piece with dignity. Umaru considered the positions and made a move so quickly that he became suspicious.

"Is that good?"

"For me."

"An informative teacher."

Yuka pointed at the space he had stopped watching. He saw it, reached toward it, and caught her looking at Umaru instead of him.

"You're helping me so I attack her."

"I'm helping the game develop."

He chose a different move. It was worse. Umaru's face became so carefully polite that Yuka put her head down on her arms.

Mau looked at the board again, then back at them.

"I think the correct response is that I was testing a theory."

"Which one?" Umaru asked.

"That I can lose this quickly without either of you helping."

Yuka laughed against her sleeve. They made him finish the round, and then Umaru set the first position up again to show him the move he had missed. He understood it this time, which was not quite the same as trusting either instructor.

She informed him his existing intelligence was also in need of careful allocation.

The ten points stayed untouched.
