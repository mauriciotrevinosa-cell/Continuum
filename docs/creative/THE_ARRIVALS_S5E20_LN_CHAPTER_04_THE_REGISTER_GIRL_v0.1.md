# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 48 — The Register Girl

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 48
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. After Jinshi closes the arrivals book, the register girl walks the empty rooms of Arrival House one last time and reads back the names she wrote; the building stays usable; no new arrivals and no decision about its future use

*When: Day 285, early evening, the same day Jinshi closed the book. The keys are with Fern. The building is not locked.*

The register girl went back after supper, because nobody had told her not to.

She had been the register girl for a long time. Long enough that most of the house had forgotten she had another name, and she had stopped minding. She had sat at the desk in the intake hall through the refuge wave and the bridge and the bad season and the coins. She had written down every name that came through the door, in the big book with the cloth cover, in her best handwriting, which was not very good. She had screamed once, the morning a woman made of crystal appeared in the middle of the floor, and she was still a little embarrassed about it.

Now the book was closed, and on Fern's shelf, and the job was finished.

She walked across the plaza in the long gold light and up the step and through the door, which was not locked, and stood in the intake hall.

* * *

It was very quiet.

The benches were along the walls, where Jinshi had put them back after Coco's mother. The register desk was in its corner, with the chair tucked under it, very neatly, the way Jinshi had left it that afternoon. The high windows let the evening in, and the light lay across the bare boards in long slanting bars, and the dust turned slowly in it.

She had never been in the hall when it was this quiet. There had always been someone. A family with their bundles. A frightened boy with a cart. Hollis's six, sleeping on borrowed mattresses with their weapons against the wall. Jinshi at the door with the key.

She went and sat in her chair at the desk.

The desk was bare. The book was gone. She put her hands flat on the wood where it had always lain, and closed her eyes, and remembered.

* * *

The first name she had ever written was a family's.

She did not remember the name now. She remembered the handwriting. Shaking, because she had been nervous, and the pen had blotted, and she had been so afraid that the family would think she had written their name badly on purpose. The father had looked at the blot and said, *that's all right*, and she had nearly cried.

After that there had been so many.

The refuge families, coming in waves, with their children and their animals and their one good pot. The bee family, with three hives in a basket. Dessa, who came alone and washed at the troughs and sang badly. The bean-girl and her mother. The people who went south in the end, and the people who stayed. The family who left on the cart after the titans, with the father saying *ten paces, I can't*.

She had written all of them. And when they left, she had not crossed them out. She had written *gone on* beside their names, small, the way Fern did in the ledger, so the book would remember that they had been there.

Then the coins.

She opened her eyes and looked at the middle of the floor.

The coins had come here. Not to the house. Here, to the intake hall, one at a time, at the eighth bell or at dawn or in the middle of a hot afternoon. A woman who looked round at everything and frowned, as if the hall had been badly managed. A girl with pink hair who stood very straight and looked for her sister. A tall man in a blindfold. A doctor with one bag. A girl who took one look at the room and shouted. A big man in a white suit with patches on it, who said *huh!* very loudly and asked who did the joinery.

And the crystal woman. Reaching.

The register girl looked at the place on the boards where the crystal woman had stood. There was nothing there now. Not a mark. But she could see it anyway, the hundred small rainbows round its feet, and Coco's mother's hand reaching down for the height of a child's head.

She had written her down too. Not her name; nobody knew her name then. She had written *a woman, crystal, reaching*. And later, when Coco told her, she had gone back and written the name beside it, small, in her best handwriting.

* * *

She got up from the desk and walked through the building.

She had not meant to. Her feet did it. Down the short corridor to the rooms at the back, where the guests had slept. Six small rooms, three on each side. The doors were open. The beds were made, with the blankets folded at the foot, the way she had always folded them.

The first room had been a refuge family's, for a week, before the cottages were finished.

The second had been Hollis's guards. Brenna had slept in it with her crossbow against the wall. Dunmore had slept on the floor because he said the bed was too soft. Cobb and Tam had shared the third, and argued all night, and in the morning Cobb had asked her, very shyly, if he could have a second breakfast.

The fourth had been the friend of Ori's. She had not said her name for two days. The register girl had written *a friend of Ori's* in the book and underlined it, and then, when the girl finally said *Wren* at the long table, the register girl had gone back across the plaza in the dark to the intake hall and found the page and written it in the space.

She stood in the doorway of the fourth room for a while.

The fifth had been empty for a long time.

The sixth had never been slept in. For some reason it made her think of the deer.

She smiled at that, standing in the corridor. In the winter, the first guest since the window had come through the door with a paper bag and antlers, ducking under the lintel, and four deer had decided to come in too. They had stood on the clean floor between the empty benches, chewing. Jinshi had bowed to her as if she were a visiting dignitary, and written, in his most beautiful hand, *Guests: one. Deer.*

When Hollis's six came, months later, and Jinshi saw the register with real names in it, he had said, *guests, six, not a deer*, and sounded as if he might cry.

She understood that now. Standing in the empty corridor.

* * *

When she came back into the intake hall, Jinshi was standing in the doorway.

He did not have the key. Fern had the key. He stood there with his hands at his sides and looked at her across the bare floor.

"I thought you might be here," he said.

"I didn't have anything to write," said the register girl. "I just wanted to look."

Jinshi came in. He stood beside her in the middle of the floor, where the crystal had stood, and looked round at the empty hall with her.

"I sat at that desk this morning," he said, "and opened the book, and there was nothing to write. And I didn't want to close it." He paused. "Maomao said, *you did the job. That doesn't mean the job has to continue forever*."

"She's right," said the register girl.

"She usually is. It's very annoying."

The register girl almost laughed.

"Do you think anyone else will come?" she said. "Ever? Through the door?"

Jinshi was quiet for a long time.

"I don't know," he said. "Maybe. The coins aren't all spent. There are people waiting for people." He looked at the door. "If somebody comes, the house will know. And somebody will open this door, and light the stove, and make up a bed." He looked at her. "And I expect it'll be you."

The register girl looked at him.

"I don't have the book any more," she said.

"Fern has the book," said Jinshi. "It's on her shelf. It's not gone. It's resting." He paused. "Like the building. Like us, a bit."

They stood there together for a while in the gold light, in the empty intake hall, not saying anything.

Then Jinshi went to the high windows and closed the shutters, one by one, very carefully, and the light went out of the hall in long slow pieces. And the register girl went to her desk, and pushed the chair a little further under it, so it would be ready.

They went out together and pulled the door closed behind them.

Not locked. Just closed.

In case.
