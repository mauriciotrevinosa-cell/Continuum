# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 47 — One Move

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 47
**Continuity state:** The original normal entrance and its linked shop move once to the agreed interior site; no duplicate gate, reset or house alteration occurs

*When: Day 286, late morning.*

The count came back empty.

No party inside. No one waiting to return through an entrance about to leave its old place. Anko had checked the register against the people, rather than assuming an empty line meant an empty dungeon.

Mau went out to the old site with her and Stark. Rimuru stayed at the prepared ground inside the boundary, keeping the agreed coverage instead of turning a short walk into both of them leaving home unplanned.

The blue gate shone forty paces beyond the stake.

It looked perfectly content there.

"Last chance to object," Anko said.

"I thought you liked the plan."

"I like asking before the thing that only happens once."

Mau accepted that and opened the reward notice.

The description still named the linked entrance and shop. No other gate. No extra copy left on the road. Current instance and change time retained.

He chose the claim.

The blue opening narrowed. It folded toward its middle, not violently, just steadily, until the air where it had been held a flat mark of light. The shop window went with it. Anko stood at the old distance and checked that nothing remained to read.

A small anchor seal settled into Mau's palm.

He held it flat, as instructed, and did not discover a new game involving tossing it to Stark.

They walked back past the stake. Thirty paces in, Rimuru stood beside the plain peg. The panels left a clear approach on the house side and room around the place the opening would occupy.

Mau set the seal at the prepared mark.

The final question appeared.

He looked at the others. Then confirmed.

Blue rose between the pegs.

The air filled the familiar shape, and the shop window returned at its usual height. Behind them the field stayed empty. No second entrance waited where the first had been.

Anko went back to check anyway. From the red stake no shop window appeared; it became readable only as she approached the new clearing from inside the boundary. The window, as well as the opening, had its new place there.

"One move used," Mau read.

Rimuru let out the breath he had been holding.

"Good. Now no one decides it would look better beside the kitchen."

"I wasn't going to," Frieren said.

They all looked at her.

"I wasn't."

The weekly change still pointed to tomorrow. Moving the doorway had not refreshed the rewards, restored a dead boss or promised the next expedition a kinder room. It had put the entrance in a place they could manage without crossing the protection line every time they wanted the shop.

They checked the normal approach and the relay arrangements without sending somebody in merely to celebrate the move. A later party would still agree its route and return conditions.

Anko came back from the old site.

"Gone. Both."

She wrote the new location on the watch sheet and the old location beside it, so nobody following yesterday's instructions would decide the gate had vanished again.

Mau looked past the screen at the red stake.

It seemed farther away now, though it had not moved at all.
