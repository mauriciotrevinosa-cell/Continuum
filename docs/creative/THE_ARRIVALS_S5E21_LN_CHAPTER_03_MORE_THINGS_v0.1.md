# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 53 — More Things

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 53
**Continuity state:** The modest shop expansion is inspected and applied; E–S catalogue grades, limited collectible stock and eligible discovery registration do not create healing abundance or a new economy

*When: Day 286, midday.*

The upgrade did not arrive with a trumpet.

It added a line beneath the familiar categories and made Fern ask everyone to stop touching things until she had read it.

Mau kept his hands at his sides. Milim hid hers behind her back as though that proved they had always been there.

The old shared balance remained. The old basic goods remained. Expansion offered more stock, not a second purse under Mau's name.

Weapons and armour now carried grades where the catalogue had enough information to assign them. E through S. The prices climbed much faster than Milim's interest in reading them.

Stark looked at the axe illustration beside the lowest equipment listings, then at his own axe.

"The letter still doesn't tell me whether I can use it well," he said.

"Correct," Fern said. "Nor does buying a better letter restore anyone's missing abilities."

They inspected the armour. Weight, fit, protection, movement: ordinary consequences accompanied the better materials. Nothing offered them the rare invisible equipment from Umaru's hopeful list.

Healing draughts had not become a cheap shelf of solutions. The missing entries still directed them toward rare finds and bosses. Useful medicine had limits. A new category did not put back what a body had lost.

Senku looked relieved and disappointed in equal measure.

Under travel supplies were simple lights, bindings, coverings and tools. One description offered future registration of eligible discoveries for purchase. Not every discovered object; not unique rewards; not an automatic right to reproduce anything a person could imagine.

They had not found a lantern to register. They left that line alone.

Another notice mentioned rest facilities for eligible long instances. It made no promise of resurrection, a saved battle, or a chest that could be opened forever. They recorded the wording for when they had an instance to ask about.

Then Frieren found the collectibles.

She went very still.

Mau recognized the danger before the rest of the table did.

"Fern," she said.

"No."

"I haven't asked."

"I can see the page you're looking at."

There were small magical objects, a few pieces whose uses were not obvious, and among them the outline of a book. Stock was limited. Availability could change. Nothing on the page promised that a buyer would never have seen the volume before.

Frieren read the price.

Then she looked at the shared balance.

Then at Mau.

"We have many crystals."

"We also have food, tools, medicine and repairs," Fern said.

"Some of the crystals."

"How many?"

Frieren considered an answer that would not make the conversation end immediately.

Mau took the chance to move one step out of the line between them.

It did not help.

"Tell her," Frieren said.

"Tell her what?"

"That books are useful."

Fern folded her arms.

Mau looked at the two women and discovered that retirement from royal service had lasted only a few days.
