# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 49 — Already Read

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 49
**Continuity state:** Frieren buys one budgeted grimoire and discovers it duplicates the candle book from V28; no universal blind lottery or guaranteed novelty is invented

*When: Day 286, early afternoon.*

They agreed on one.

Not all the crystals. Not a promise to buy every book that appeared. One volume from a small agreed allowance, after the essentials already reserved remained reserved.

Frieren accepted the terms with the dignity of somebody enduring an injustice for the sake of peace.

Mau did not comment on the dignity. He wanted to survive to see the purchase.

The catalogue called it a ward for a small flame. There was no open set of pages to inspect. A short description mentioned wind, a common practical school, and a narrow protected space.

"Could be related to the candle one," Mau said.

"There are several ways to solve a problem," Frieren answered.

That was true. She was quite sure it was true while she bought it.

The book appeared in the collection recess beside the window. Real paper, a plain dark cover, a title in the script she could read more comfortably than the catalogue's translated description.

She opened it.

Mau watched her eyes move across the first diagram.

Then back.

Then, very slowly, to him.

"The candle?" he asked.

She turned another page, as if the book might redeem itself by being a different book farther in.

It did not.

Same diagram. Same little mistake they had found while copying the enclosure. Same passage Fern had helped her understand at the first camp.

Frieren sat down on the low bench beside the screen.

The book rested open on her knees. Her shoulders lowered by a fraction that somehow made her look much more defeated than the dragon had managed.

Mau sat beside her.

"At least we know it works."

She looked at him.

"Too soon?"

"A little."

He waited with her. Fern checked the entry and found no promise the purchase had broken. The shop had supplied the volume it described. It had not compared the buyer's library with its catalogue.

"We can keep the second copy for practice," she said. "So the first doesn't have to travel."

Frieren acknowledged this reasonable use with the expression of someone who would have preferred an unreasonable new spell.

After Fern went to finish the record, Mau leaned nearer.

"Look at it this way. The books are in there too. We got five different ones by exploring."

"Not every room has one."

"No. But we can look. Without spending all the crystals for the privilege of discovering you've already read it."

She turned the duplicated page once more.

"You'd come?"

"I was already carrying the bags."

"You could carry more."

"A dangerous answer to a kind offer."

Her mouth finally moved.

"We'll choose a normal outing," she said. "When there's time. Just looking, with a proper party and a way back."

"And if the first chest has teeth?"

"Then we look at the next one."

He laughed. She closed the book and rested it against his arm, accepting the consolation without having to pretend the duplicate was what she wanted.

On the way home she stopped being depressed long enough to explain why the little mistake in the diagram was interesting.

Mau listened from the beginning.
