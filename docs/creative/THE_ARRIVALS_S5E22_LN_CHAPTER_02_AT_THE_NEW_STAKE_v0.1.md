# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 57 — At The New Stake

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 57
**Continuity state:** Mau checks the key at the known relocated site after giving his ordinary whereabouts; Noise alerts him to Sukuna before he can raise an alarm

*When: Day 286, night.*

The key had less than a night left to count.

Mau checked it after supper, then checked it again while pretending to look for a cloth to wrap it in.

Frieren noticed.

"It won't hurry because you're interested."

"I know."

"You may tell it that too."

He smiled and put it away beside the compass, in a different pocket so he could tell them apart by touch.

"I'm going to look at the new site. Five minutes. Then back."

"Rimuru knows?"

"He's downstairs. I'll tell him on the way."

She nodded and returned to the duplicated candle book. She and Fern had found a use for a practice copy that did not require Frieren to stop feeling a little cheated by owning it.

Mau went down, told Rimuru, and took the familiar path.

The new screen made the blue light less obvious from the house. It still shone onto the dry ground between the pegs, steady and ordinary after the much less ordinary work of moving it. The boundary stake remained farther out in the dark. Nothing about the relocation had changed what it marked.

He stood by the inside peg and opened the key's little notice.

Tomorrow. Not a place name. Not a promise about what waited there.

He had begun to imagine a first short visit with people who knew how to stop him from mistaking curiosity for a route when something inside him changed.

The Noise did not whisper.

It roared.

No word. A violent, inward recognition that sent his stomach cold and his hand toward a pocket containing nothing useful against the thing he had suddenly realized was there.

He turned toward the field.

A figure stood beyond the protection line.

Mau knew the face before the light reached it.

His body remembered a hand through his chest. The tightness of another presence held against him. White swallowing everything at the end.

He tried to breathe.

"I heard you wanted to talk," Sukuna said.

Mau did not move.

The distance between the gate and the red stake had seemed comfortingly clear that morning. Now he could count every step in it and none of them seemed enough.

*Mau,* Ciel said.

He found his voice.

"Don't come closer."

Sukuna looked at the marker, then back at him.

"I haven't."

The world remained still around the words. No cut. No force against the shield in the field. No sudden gathering of the sky.

Mau could hear his own heartbeat.

"Why?"

"You asked. I became curious."

He made it sound like a social call.

Mau had never wanted to hit someone quite so badly while being so certain it would be a terrible first decision.

"If you came to fight—"

"I didn't. Not tonight."

"I'm meant to believe that?"

Sukuna smiled.

"No. You're meant to ask what it would take to make it useful."

Mau kept his hand away from the key.

Inside him the Noise stayed at the edge, waiting, terrible with attention. It did not take his feet and walk him toward the figure in the dark.

He was glad of that enough to find the next question.

"Then give me a guarantee."
