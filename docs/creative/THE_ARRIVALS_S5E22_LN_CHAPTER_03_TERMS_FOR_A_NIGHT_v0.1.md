# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 58 — Terms For A Night

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 58
**Continuity state:** Mau and Sukuna make a bilateral outside-gate truce binding only their own conduct; the protection permits one limited non-hostile crossing without being overridden by the vow

*When: Day 286, the same night.*

"A binding vow," Sukuna said.

Mau's mouth went dry.

He knew enough to hear the difference between a promise and an offered trap. He also knew who had made the offer.

"Say the terms. Slowly."

For a moment Sukuna looked genuinely entertained.

Then he did.

Conversation without attack. Safe departure when either chose to end it. No pursuit until the next sunrise. Neither of them using somebody else to do what he had agreed not to do himself.

"My people haven't agreed to anything," Mau said.

"Then manage them."

"No. I don't bind them because I'm the one standing here. You can have my conduct. Not theirs."

The smile thinned.

"Would you let them strike me while I kept my part?"

Mau looked at the darkness behind Sukuna. No visible companion. No way to prove there was nobody else.

"I would tell them not to. If I saw an attack coming, I'd try to stop it. Without hurting them. That's what I can promise. I won't swear that every person I know loses the right to choose because you found me before I called them."

Sukuna considered him for a little while.

"Your effort, then. A real effort. Not a word spoken after the blow."

"The same goes for anyone with you. Anyone acting at your request."

"Agreed."

They went through the rest.

No direct injury. No delayed injury left in a body or a possession. No technique, mark or hidden agent placed during the meeting and left behind for afterward. No using the conversation to enter the house. Only the open ground beside the marked site, if the protection allowed it; the vow did not buy authority over that boundary.

Either could end the meeting. Sukuna would withdraw beyond the line, away from the settlement, before sunrise. Neither would follow, direct an attack, or assist an attack against the other during the withdrawal or the remainder of the night.

Self-defence against an unrelated danger was not an excuse to strike the other party. Protecting someone from an incoming attack was permitted if it did not become a way to injure the people they had just named as protected.

At sunrise the personal restraint ended. Nothing lasting had been purchased beyond what they had actually said.

Mau repeated the limits that mattered most to him.

The people who had not agreed were not signers. Their independent action was not automatically his breach. His promise was what he would choose to do when he knew about it, including a genuine attempt to prevent an attack he could reach.

"You're very concerned about the wording," Sukuna said.

"You've given me reasons."

He felt Ciel listening. No certainty arrived telling him a vow with Sukuna was safe simply because he had found several holes before accepting it.

"Do you understand what you are offering?" she asked privately.

He thought of putting himself between the figure beyond the stake and somebody coming from the house. He understood enough to be afraid of it.

"Yes," he said, to both of them.

He asked Ciel to tell Rimuru where he was and that the meeting must not be attacked. Then he looked back at Sukuna.

"Until sunrise. Those terms."

"Those terms."

Something settled between them.

Not a paper. Not a name written and vanishing from a gate's rules. Mau could still feel the old refusal in that other place, separate from the choice he had made here.

Sukuna took one step.

Mau's entire body tightened.

The red stake stayed beside him. Nothing struck the approaching figure. No great force rolled through the trees to prove a rule somebody had misunderstood.

Sukuna came across the line.

He stopped on the open ground they had named, with the house still beyond the screening panels and the route behind him clear.

Mau could not hear himself breathe.

"Interesting," Sukuna said.

"That doesn't make this yours."

"I didn't say it did."

The quiet crossing was worse than an impact would have been. An impact would have given the fear an ordinary shape. This left a thing Mau knew was dangerous standing on the side of the line he had learned to call home.

The vow had not switched the protection off. He had no evidence that it could. What he had was one enemy who, for this interval, had bound his own intent to the conduct they had agreed.

That was a smaller and much more frightening fact.

Mau kept himself between Sukuna and the house.

"Talk," he said.
