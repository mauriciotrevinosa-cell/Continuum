# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 59 — A Higher Sky

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 59
**Continuity state:** Sukuna admits the lost WCS route and the larger horizon he glimpsed during the interrupted S2 link; claims about propaganda and allies remain his claims; Mau intercepts the departure attack

*When: Day 286, the same night.*

Sukuna looked at him as though the answer might be written somewhere on the skin the shirt concealed.

Mau did not give him another question to enjoy.

"I underestimated them," Sukuna said at last.

"Us."

"That too."

He spoke without remorse. If anything, the admission seemed to interest him. An error found, a calculation worth doing again.

Mau's hand stayed curled beside his thigh, careful of the tender fingers. He hated being aware of the bandage. Hated that Sukuna could be aware of it too.

"I paid to leave," Sukuna said.

"I know you left."

"You don't know the price."

Mau waited.

"A route I was rebuilding. A way to extend the target beyond the thing standing in front of me. To cut what contains it. Your teacher's little distance would not have been the obstacle he imagines."

Gojo's name did not need saying.

Mau felt cold in a new place.

"You gave that up."

"That pathway. For survival at that moment."

"And you have it back?"

Sukuna's smile returned.

"If I did, why would I give you the answer?"

It was not a yes. Mau made himself keep it separate from one.

"A cost doesn't become nothing because I can still think," Sukuna added. "But the sky here is higher than I thought."

Mau looked at him.

"When I nearly joined with you—"

"Tried."

"When you tried to keep me from leaving your body," Sukuna said, enjoying the correction too much. "I saw enough. Not the end of it. Enough to know I had been treating the beginning as the limit."

The memory pressed against Mau's ribs. A hand behind him. Contact neither of them could break. The Noise reaching for an integration it did not know how to stop wanting.

Seconds. Not a completed joining. Not a second life together inside his head.

"You saw something you wanted," Mau said.

"I saw something I had not yet understood. There is a difference."

"For you?"

"Occasionally."

He looked past Mau toward the screened blue light.

"I came to thank you for that."

Mau had no answer that would not give him what he wanted from the sentence. He chose another question.

"The stories in the towns. The pages. Are they yours?"

Sukuna laughed.

"No."

"Pell is telling people you were a hero who came to talk and we almost killed you."

"A fine story."

"Somebody wants them afraid of us."

"They saw your sun. They hardly needed instruction."

"It wasn't a sun."

"How reassuring. You should tell them again."

Mau watched his face. The denial might be true. It might be a useful arrangement with a distinction hidden in the word yours. Nothing in the vow required an enemy to become an honest narrator of everything outside it.

He did not pretend otherwise.

"Keep climbing," Sukuna said. "I would be disappointed if that was all I got from you."

"You didn't get it from just me."

"I remember. Some of my friends have their own accounts to settle with yours."

Friends. From that mouth, the word sounded like a knife somebody had tried to wrap as a gift.

"Who?"

"You ask a great deal for an evening without blood."

Mau kept his eyes on him. No names came. No proof, beyond the choice to say it.

Behind the screen, movement touched the dark. Mau knew before he saw them that he was no longer the only person from the house in the field.

Gojo stopped where he could see both faces. Frieren stood farther to the side, staff low, eyes fixed on the person who had once stood so close behind Mau that the memory still had no distance in it.

They had waited through the warning relayed from Rimuru. They had come anyway. Mau was glad they had, and afraid of what the next moment would make him do.

"Anything else?" he asked Sukuna.

"Only that I look forward to the next time."

Sukuna turned.

He passed close enough for the two silhouettes to overlap from Frieren's angle. His hand rose to adjust the cloth across his own chest. An ordinary movement from a body that had never made an ordinary movement beside him in Frieren's memory.

The Noise flared a warning.

Not at Sukuna.

At the line of light forming to Mau's left.

He moved into its path.

"Frieren—"

Her spell checked as soon as she saw him. The narrow defence he asked for caught the rest of its dying edge before it could reach the retreating figure. A fraction of a breath; then closed. His head rang.

Gojo stepped forward.

Mau put himself in that route too, hand lifted without striking.

"Until sunrise," he said. "Please. Let him leave."

Gojo looked down at the bandaged fingers between them. At Mau's face. Then beyond him.

Sukuna was smiling.

"Until next time," he said.

He went back across the line and into the dark.

Mau stayed where he was until the last sound of the departure had gone.
