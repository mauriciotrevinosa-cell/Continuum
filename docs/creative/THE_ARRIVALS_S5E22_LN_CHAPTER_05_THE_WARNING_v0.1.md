# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 60 — The Warning

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 60
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. Inside the house while Mau talks with Sukuna under the truce: Ciel's relay reaches Rimuru with the name and "do not attack"; Rimuru, Gojo, Frieren and the household decide how to wait; Gojo and Frieren go to watch from the screen; nobody breaks the truce; no new information about Sukuna

*When: Day 286, night, the same minutes as "Terms for a Night". Supper is cleared. Most of the house is in the common room.*

Rimuru was halfway through a sentence about the bowls when Ciel spoke, and he stopped in the middle of it.

Nobody noticed at first. He was standing by the cold hearth with a dish towel over his shoulder, telling Okarun that the blue bowls went on the left shelf, not the right, because Momo had said so and Momo was not to be argued with about shelves. And then he stopped. Not because he had finished. Because his whole body had gone still, the way a slime goes still when something touches it.

Okarun looked at him.

"Rimuru?"

Rimuru put the dish towel down on the long table. Very carefully.

*Mau is at the new gate site,* Ciel said. Her voice was very level. Too level. *Sukuna is there. Beyond the line. They are speaking under a binding vow. Mau's own conduct only. He asks that no one attack the meeting.*

Rimuru did not move.

*Repeat that,* he said.

She repeated it, word for word, exactly the same.

Across the common room, Gojo had looked up from the card game he was losing to Yuka. He was looking at Rimuru's face. Not at anything Rimuru had said, because Rimuru had not said anything aloud. Just at his face.

Gojo put his cards down.

"What," he said.

* * *

Rimuru told them.

He did it standing by the hearth with his hand flat on the long table, in the voice he used at the council, and it came out in the order Ciel had given it to him. Mau. The new site. Sukuna. Beyond the line. A vow. Talking. Mau's conduct only. Do not attack.

The common room went very quiet.

Not silent. A house this full could not be silent. But the card game stopped, and the conversation by the window stopped, and Momo came to the kitchen door with a cloth in her hands and did not come any further.

Frieren was on her feet.

Rimuru had not seen her stand. She had been sitting in the corner with Fern, with the duplicated candle book between them, and now she was standing, and the book was on the floor, and her face had gone the colour of the lamp-glass.

"Where," she said.

"The new site. By the screen."

She was already moving toward the door.

"Frieren," said Rimuru. "He asked—"

"I heard what he asked."

"He's in a vow. If anyone attacks—"

"I'm not going to attack," said Frieren. She did not stop walking. "I'm going to watch."

Gojo was beside her before she reached the door. He did not stop her. He fell into step with her, half a pace to one side, the way he had stood beside Aira at the cottages.

"Same," he said. "Watching. Not touching." He glanced back at Rimuru. "We'll stay behind the screen. He won't even know we're there unless he turns round."

"He'll know," said Rimuru. "Sukuna will know."

"Then Sukuna will know two people are watching him," said Gojo. "That's not an attack. That's manners."

Rimuru looked at the two of them in the doorway. At Gojo with his blindfold already up, his bare eyes very bright. At Frieren with her staff in her hand, though nobody had seen her pick it up.

He thought about saying no.

He thought about what Mau would say. *Don't come. I've got it. Stay inside.* He thought about the last time Mau had gone somewhere alone with that voice, and come back with Sukuna's hand through him.

"Behind the screen," said Rimuru. "Not past it. Not a step. If he raises his hand to signal, you come back. If Sukuna does anything at all—anything—you tell me before you move. Through Ciel, through me, through anything."

"Yes," said Gojo.

Frieren did not answer.

"Frieren."

She turned her head and looked at him. Her eyes were very green and very still and there was something underneath them that Rimuru had seen only once before, in the field, on the day of the bell, when she stood at the fence and Milim held her wrist.

"I won't break his promise," she said. "I won't make him a liar. Not to that thing." She paused. "But I won't sit in here either."

Rimuru looked at her for a long moment.

"Go," he said.

They went.

* * *

The common room stayed where it was.

Nobody went after them. Rimuru had not said they could not. But nobody did. They stood or sat where they had been, with their cards and their cups and their dish towels, and looked at the door that Frieren and Gojo had gone through, and did not move.

Fern had picked up the candle book from the floor. She was holding it in both hands against her chest, the way she held the ledger when something was wrong.

Stark came and stood beside her. He did not touch her. Close enough.

"She'll be all right," he said. Very quietly.

"I know she'll be all right," said Fern. "Sukuna can't hurt her. Not tonight. Not under that vow." She did not look away from the door. "It's not her body I'm worried about."

Stark did not say anything.

Ori had come down the stairs.

She stood at the bottom with Milim behind her. Milim was floating a hand's breadth above the last step, her face very small and very fierce. Ori was not floating. She stood with both hands at her sides and looked at Rimuru across the common room.

"Is it him?" she said. "The one who—"

"Yes," said Rimuru.

Ori's face did not change. But her hands closed.

"Mau's talking to him," she said. "On purpose."

"Under a vow. Nobody gets hurt tonight. Not him, not us." Rimuru swallowed. "He asked that nobody attack."

"Did he tell you first?"

Rimuru did not answer at once.

"He told me when he had terms," he said at last. "Not when he saw him." He heard his own voice, and did not like it. "I'd rather have had the name first. I'm going to tell him that."

Ori looked at him for a long time.

"Good," she said.

Then she came across the common room and sat down on the bench by the cold hearth, right next to where Rimuru was standing, and folded her hands in her lap, and waited. Milim drifted down and sat beside her.

* * *

They waited a long time.

Rimuru stood by the hearth with his eyes half closed and his thread reaching out into the dark, through the yard, past the tree, toward the dry ground and the screen and the two blue frames. He could feel Mau at the end of it. Not words. Just Mau. His heartbeat, too fast. His breathing, held. The Noise, awake behind the walls, terrible with attention, not moving.

And another presence. On the far side. Very still. Very large. Very interested.

Rimuru made himself breathe.

Once, Ciel said, *He has crossed the line. Into the open ground. The protection let him. The vow holds.*

Rimuru said it aloud. The common room went stiller.

"He crossed," said Seika, from the bench by the window. Flatly. "The line. The one that's supposed to—"

"The protection let him," said Rimuru. "Ciel says it's a limited crossing. Non-hostile. The vow doesn't override it. It just—it allowed it."

Nobody said anything.

Coco's mother, who had come down from the west wing with her daughter when the room went quiet, put her hand on Coco's shoulder. She did not understand all of it. She understood enough.

Byakuya, beside Senku, said very quietly, "So the line isn't a wall."

"It was never tested," said Senku. "Now we know one thing about it." He was writing in his notebook. His hand was shaking. "One thing."

Later, Ciel said, *Frieren and Gojo are behind the screen. Sukuna knows. He hasn't turned.*

And later still: *He is leaving.*

Rimuru opened his eyes.

*And—*

Ciel stopped. For one heartbeat. For Ciel, it was an eternity.

*A spell. Frieren's. Mau stepped into it. He's closed his defence. He's all right. He's asked Gojo to let Sukuna go. Gojo has stopped.*

Rimuru let out a breath he did not know he had been holding.

"He's leaving," he said aloud. "Sukuna's leaving. Frieren fired, and Mau stepped into it to keep the vow, and he's all right, and Gojo's let him go." He put his hand flat on the long table. "They're coming back."

Ori stood up.

She did not run to the door. She stood in the middle of the common room with her hands clenched at her sides and looked at the door, and waited for it to open.

When it did, it was Gojo first, with his blindfold down and no smile at all. Then Frieren, with her staff, and her face very still, and her hand trembling on the staff.

And then Mau. Walking. On his own feet. With a headache he would admit to Shoko in about four minutes.

He stopped just inside the door and looked round at the common room. At all of them, standing exactly where they had been, waiting.

At Ori.

"I'm back," he said.

"I know," said Ori. "Rimuru told me." She did not move. "He said you told him when you had terms. Not when you saw him."

Mau looked at Rimuru.

Rimuru looked back.

"Later," said Rimuru. "We're going to talk about it later. All of us."

Mau nodded slowly.

"Later," he agreed.

Then Ori crossed the common room and put her arms round him, very hard, and did not say anything else at all.
