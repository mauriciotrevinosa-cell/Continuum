# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 61 — What You Promised

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 61
**Continuity state:** The family challenges Mau's risk without becoming involuntary signers or reopening the repaired romance; he reports exact terms, costs and Sukuna's claims

*When: Day 286, later that night.*

"What did you promise him?" Gojo asked.

No joke in the voice.

Mau had expected anger. It was the quiet that made him sit down before somebody told him to.

They were back inside, away from the field. Rimuru stood by the door while Shoko checked the small headache he had admitted to after intercepting the spell. Frieren sat near him, staff out of reach now because nobody needed it to continue the conversation.

Mau repeated the terms.

Not a summary designed to sound better. The actual limits, including the part where he had promised to make a real effort to stop an attack he saw coming. His effort. No one else's signature supplied by his being the person Sukuna had chosen to approach.

"You stepped in front of her," Gojo said.

"I promised I would try to stop it."

"I saw."

Mau looked at his hands.

"He didn't get the right to hurt anyone. Not tonight. Not a mark left for later. Not a place inside the house. He had to leave. Sunrise ends it."

"And if you misunderstood one part?" Shoko asked.

He had no clever answer.

"Then I took a risk I can't make sound small."

Frieren spoke for the first time.

"I saw his hand move."

Mau looked at her.

Her face had held its stillness all the way from the field. He could see what it cost now, the effort going into keeping the old image separate from the boy sitting alive in front of her.

"I know," he said.

"You said talking. Rimuru said no one was hurt. I waited. Then he was beside you and he moved his hand."

"He wasn't attacking."

"I saw that when you moved."

She looked down at her own fingers.

Neither of them asked the other to call the moment harmless. He had chosen a risk. She had answered the shape of an old terror before every part of the new information reached her. Both were real. Neither had to become the only person at fault for the room to keep making sense.

"I should have made the limits clearer before you got there," he said.

"You should not have had to negotiate with him alone," Gojo said.

"I wasn't waiting for him."

"I know. That isn't an accusation. It's the part we improve."

Rimuru moved from the door.

"You called after you had terms," Rimuru said.

"I wanted you to know what was happening."

"I wanted to know while you were finding out."

Mau looked up. Rimuru had kept his voice level, but his fingers were pressed flat against the back of the empty chair.

"You don't need a useful report before telling me something's wrong," he went on. "His name would have done. Your location would have done. I can cope with not knowing the rest yet."

Mau had asked Ciel to send the clear thing he could explain. Until Rimuru said it, he had not quite heard how much time could disappear into deciding what that thing was.

"All right," he said. "When I know somebody's there. Not when I've worked out why."

"And don't make talking mean stay away," Gojo said.

"I didn't want you thinking he could attack."

"You could have needed help without him being free to attack. Those aren't the same answer."

Mau rubbed his uninjured thumb against the edge of the chair. He had been careful with so many words in the field and still found a place to be careless in the first ones he sent home.

He asked Ciel to carry the same distinction next time: who, where, immediate danger if they knew it, any restraint already accepted. No waiting to finish a negotiation before giving the first warning.

Frieren looked at Rimuru.

"Tell me when you know," she said. "Even if you only know the name."

"I will."

She nodded, once. It did not make her hand stop trembling when she reached for the cup. Mau left his hand beside hers until she chose to take it.

Then Mau told them what Sukuna had said.

The lost route. The different ceiling glimpsed during their interrupted contact. The denial about the propaganda. The unnamed friends.

Gojo stopped looking angry at the words target beyond the thing in front of me.

He looked attentive instead.

"Say that part again."

Mau did.

The room changed a little around the second telling. Not safer. Less simple.

"Tomorrow," Shoko said when Gojo began the next question. "His head doesn't need to become your laboratory because Sukuna left you one."

Gojo looked at her, then at Mau.

"Tomorrow."

Later, upstairs, Frieren put the compass on the bedside table while Mau changed. It pointed at her as steadily as it had at the dinner.

She was not angry with the object for being unable to tell her more.

When he got into bed, she touched the place on his chest the shirt covered.

"He doesn't get this one again," she said quietly.

Mau put his hand over hers.

"No."

It was not a vow to the universe. It was the answer he had, here, with her.

She came closer. He held her, and they let the rest wait until morning.
