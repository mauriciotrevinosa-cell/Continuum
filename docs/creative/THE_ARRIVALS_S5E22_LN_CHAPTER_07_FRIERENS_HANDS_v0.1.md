# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 62 — Frieren's Hands

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (breathing chapter, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 62
**Continuity state:** Breathing chapter. Later the night of the Sukuna truce: Frieren cannot sleep and goes to Fern, not for a task but for herself (answering Fern's "ask for me sometimes"); she admits her spell left her hand before she decided; Fern does not absolve or blame; no new decision about Sukuna

*When: Day 286, after midnight. Mau is asleep in the east room. The compass is on the bedside table, pointing at the empty side of the bed.*

Frieren waited until his breathing was slow and even, and then a little longer to be sure, and then she got up.

She did it very carefully. She was good at getting out of a bed without waking the person in it. She had done it on the journey a hundred times, rising before Himmel to look at some flower or some ruin, and he had never once woken. Mau woke more easily. He had learned to, in the bad season. But tonight he was very tired, and his head hurt, and Shoko had given him something for it. He did not stir.

She stood beside the bed and looked at him in the dark.

Then she looked at the compass on the bedside table. The cracked silver face caught a little of the starlight from the window. The needle had swung when she stood up. It pointed at her now, standing a pace from the bed, faithful and useless.

She turned it face down so it would not watch her go.

* * *

The corridor was dark. The lamp at the turn of the stair had burned out at some point and nobody had refilled it. Frieren did not light it. She walked down the north corridor in the dark, past Ori's connecting door, past the narrow room at the turn with REM on the card in ink, to the door at the top of the main stair.

Fern's door. Beside it, behind the dotted wall that was still a wall, Stark's.

She stood in front of it for a long time.

She did not know what she was going to say. She did not have a reason. There was nothing to record, nothing to check, no supply to count or route to plan. She had not come to ask Fern to do anything. She had come because she could not lie in the dark beside Mau any longer with her hands doing what they were doing, and the only person in this house she had ever known how to go to at night, in another life, was behind this door.

She knocked. Very softly. Three times.

The door opened almost at once.

Fern was in her nightdress, with her hair down and a shawl round her shoulders, and she was not surprised. She looked at Frieren standing in the dark corridor with her hands held together in front of her, and she did not ask what was wrong.

"Come in," said Fern. "Quietly. Stark's asleep next door. Not that he ever wakes."

* * *

Fern poured water.

There was a jug on the shelf and two cups beside it, because Fern always kept two cups, though she rarely needed the second. She filled both and gave one to Frieren and did not say anything. Frieren sat on the chair by the window. Through the dotted wall, Stark was snoring, slow and deep and entirely untroubled.

Fern sat down on the edge of the bed with the other cup.

Frieren held the cup in both hands.

Her hands were trembling. She could see it in the surface of the water, the small fast ripples. She had been watching them tremble since the field.

"I saw his hand move," she said.

Fern did not say anything.

"Sukuna's," said Frieren. "When he turned to go. He passed close to Mau. And his hand came up. To his chest. To fix his clothes." She was looking at the water. "It was nothing. An ordinary movement. Gojo saw it was nothing. Mau saw it was nothing." She stopped. "I saw a hand come up beside Mau's chest. And my spell was already gone before I knew I'd made it."

The water trembled.

"I didn't decide," said Frieren. "That's what I keep coming back to. I've been alive for more than a thousand years. I've fought demons and dragons and things with no names. Every spell I have ever cast, I decided. Even the fast ones. Even the ones I'd cast ten thousand times. There was always a moment, very small, where I chose." She looked up at last. "Tonight there wasn't. My hand did it. Before me."

Fern was quiet for a long moment.

"And Mau stepped into it," she said.

"And Mau stepped into it," said Frieren. "To keep his promise. To that thing. He put himself between my spell and Sukuna's back." Her voice did something. "Because he'd given his word, and I would have made him a liar, and he would rather take my spell than be a liar to Sukuna."

"He closed his defence in time."

"He closed his defence in time," Frieren agreed. "And he has a headache. From my spell. Because I didn't decide."

Fern put her cup down on the floor.

* * *

"The last time," said Fern slowly, "Sukuna put his hand through him."

Frieren did not move.

"I saw you afterward," said Fern. I saw your face when Yuta was working on him." She was not looking away. "You were standing very still and your hands were doing nothing at all. Nothing. You couldn't do anything. Yuta was doing it and you couldn't help."

Frieren's hands had gone white round the cup.

"And tonight," said Fern, "a hand came up beside his chest again. And your body remembered standing there doing nothing." She paused. "So it did something. Before you could stop it."

Frieren did not say anything. She could not.

"I'm not saying it was right," said Fern. "It wasn't. It could have broken his vow. It hurt him, a little." She picked up her cup again. "But I'm not going to tell you it was wrong either. Not like that. Not like you chose it." She drank. "You didn't choose it. Your hands remembered something terrible. That's not the same as wanting to do something terrible."

"It's still my hands."

"Yes." Fern looked at her steadily. "So we work on it. You and me. The way you taught me to work on everything. One small thing at a time, every day, until it holds."

Frieren stared at her.

"I remember everything you taught me," said Fern. "You think I don't. You think I only remember the times you overslept and wanted to look at a flower for three hours." Her mouth moved at one corner. "I remember those too. But I remember the other things. You were very patient with me. You never once made me feel that being slow was the same as failing."

Frieren looked down at her own hands.

"I didn't know you'd kept that," she said.

"I kept all of it," said Fern.

* * *

They sat for a long time.

The water sat untouched in the cups. Through the wall, Stark turned over in his sleep and said something that might have been *wrong piece of wood*, and Fern looked toward the wall with an expression that Frieren had never seen on her face before, and then looked back quickly before Frieren could see it properly.

At last Frieren said, very quietly:

"I didn't come here because I needed something checked."

Fern looked at her.

"You said," said Frieren. "In the courtyard. Yesterday. *I still want you to ask for me sometimes. When you don't need anything recorded.*" She turned the cold cup in her hands. "I didn't come for a task. I came because I couldn't stay in the dark with my hands, and I wanted you." She paused. "Just you. The way I used to, a long time ago, in other inns."

Fern did not say anything for a long moment.

Then she got up off the edge of the bed, and took the cold cup out of Frieren's hands, and set it on the windowsill. And sat down on the floor beside Frieren's chair, with her back against Frieren's knees, the way she used to sit when she was very small and could not sleep and Frieren was reading.

"Then stay," said Fern. "Until your hands are tired."

Frieren put one hand on Fern's hair.

It was still trembling a little. Fern did not say anything about it. She leaned her head back against Frieren's knee and closed her eyes.

After a while, the trembling stopped.

* * *

When Frieren went back to the east room, the sky was beginning to grey.

Mau was still asleep. She stood beside the bed for a moment. Then she turned the compass face up again, and watched the needle swing round and find her, standing there.

She got back into bed very carefully. He did not wake. But in his sleep he turned toward her, and his hand found hers on the blanket, and held it.

Her hand did not tremble.
