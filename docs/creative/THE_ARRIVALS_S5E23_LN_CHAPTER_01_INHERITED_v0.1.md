# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 26 — Inherited

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 26
**Continuity state:** The truce expires at sunrise; Gojo explains source-grounded inherited technique and physical cost without unverified pi or systematic infant-killing lore

*When: Day 287, early morning.*

Sunrise found Sukuna absent and Mau awake.

He had not gone out to check where the enemy had gone. There had been enough promises about the night. He was not going to turn their expiry into an excuse to begin a pursuit nobody had planned.

Gojo was already at the table when he came down.

Two cups waited. One remained well away from Senku's growing collection of objects that might become measuring equipment if everybody stopped watching them.

"Your head?" Gojo asked.

"Better. Shoko knows I'm here."

"An excellent opening."

Mau sat down.

For a while they drank without bringing Sukuna back into the room. Then Mau put his cup down and asked the question he had been turning over since the field.

"What is your technique?"

Gojo looked at him.

"Have my lessons been that bad?"

"Not the moves. The thing that makes those moves possible."

That took the joke out of the answer without making it unfriendly.

Gojo explained the difference between energy and the technique using it. Innate structure, inheritance in his own case, a bodily mechanism he had paid dearly to understand. Learned applications were not separate wishes granted because their names sounded related.

"So Sukuna didn't need a new technique to change what he was trying to cut," Mau said.

"Changing the target isn't the same as getting a whole new instrument. But finding an application doesn't mean you can pay for it, or execute it, or make it work under every condition."

He looked at Mau's bandage as he said the last part.

Mau accepted the look.

"And you inherited the instrument."

"And information. Manuals. Useful history. Not a recording of every person who ever held it."

"How much did they do differently?"

"I can tell you what I know. I can't turn the things I didn't see into facts because they'd make a satisfying family story."

Senku made a small approving sound without looking up from his page.

Gojo pointed at him.

"That was not permission to become smug."

"Too late."

He described his own work when he was younger: learning to make the defence discriminate, reducing what required deliberate attention, testing what it could and could not recognize. It had taken practice. Understanding, attempts, failures. The familiar shape of progress under the extraordinary technique.

Shoko arrived during the explanation and took the remaining cup.

"He worked at it," she said. "Annoyingly, he was very good. That doesn't mean every idea he had was automatically possible."

"A moving tribute."

"I've had time to prepare it."

Mau looked between them, hearing an ease he liked without deciding it had solved every old thing they carried.

Then he asked Gojo what part of space he actually controlled.

Senku finally put down the pencil.

"Yes," he said. "That question."
