# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 65 — The Door Beside It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 65
**Continuity state:** The key creates its companion entrance beside the relocated normal gate after seven exterior days; owner-party permission is clear but the interior is not yet classified

*When: Day 287, the key's opening time.*

The normal gate had changed at dawn.

Its new week carried an E classification beside the familiar count. A different catalogue field from the axe's rank, even where the same letter appeared. Anko wrote that distinction at the top of the sheet after three people tried to use one as an explanation of the other.

The new map was unknown.

That fact mattered more than the letter after the week that had called itself normal and turned red.

Later in the morning, the key reached its appointed time.

Mau felt it warm in his pocket before Ciel called his attention to the change. He put it on his palm in the open ground beside the screen. Rimuru stood with him. Frieren and Anko waited where they could see without crowding the site.

No one was in the normal entrance.

The key pointed toward the space left beside it.

Light drew a second frame there: taller, darker in the middle, edged with a pattern none of them had seen on the blue opening. It did not replace the first. Both stood on the same dry ground, with room to approach either without stepping into the other.

The key's little notice changed.

Owner present. Party permitted.

Mau passed it to Rimuru and stepped back far enough to test the distinction without touching the surface. The frame remained, but its offered entry did not recognize the hand holding the key as a substitute for him.

He returned. The invitation came back.

"So carrying it isn't ownership," Anko said.

She wrote it down.

They did not assume the rest. Whether the same route would remain available, how time behaved, what waited beyond the dark: all questions for a first visit designed to answer only a few at once.

"We don't have to go in now," Rimuru said.

"I know."

Mau meant it. He could be curious without spending every bit of the morning proving curiosity had authority over him.

They set the next watch's instructions, marked which entrance was which, and kept the approach clear. No secret third door led into somebody's room. No shop multiplied itself because another frame had appeared.

Frieren came to stand beside Mau.

"When?"

"After we choose the first party and agree what a first look means. Tomorrow, if the check is good."

She looked at the dark middle for a while.

"Tell me before you finish choosing."

He touched her hand.

"I will."

They went back toward the house together. Behind them, two entrances waited. Neither required the whole family to spend its day standing there.
