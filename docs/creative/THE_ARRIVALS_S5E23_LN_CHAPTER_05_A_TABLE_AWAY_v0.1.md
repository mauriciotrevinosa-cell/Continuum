# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 30 — A Table Away

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 30
**Continuity state:** Coco's mother, the workshop circle and Nazuna have ordinary chosen lives alongside shared duties; the house is not reorganized solely around Mau's next gate

*When: Day 287, late afternoon.*

Marin had chosen the wrong trim on purpose.

At least, that was what she claimed after Coco's mother examined it and made a polite suggestion that improved the whole piece.

"I wanted another opinion."

Wakana looked at her over the material.

"Of course."

"A considered opinion."

"I heard it."

Coco's mother laughed and stopped trying to be so polite about the next suggestion.

They sat outside the workshop where the light was good. Not making uniforms for an emergency. Not filling an order someone had decided their hands owed the house. Marin wanted the thing for herself. Wakana liked solving a particular seam. Their visitor had an eye for the colour beside it and was enjoying being asked.

Coco came by with her own folded page and waited until the talk reached a gap.

"Can I show you something after?"

Her mother moved a little on the bench.

"You can sit. Show me now."

It was a drawing, ordinary pencil, nothing to activate. Coco explained the part that had changed while the adults listened without turning it into a commission for another useful object.

Wakana asked one practical question. Marin asked a very different one. Coco answered both and then disagreed with her mother about which corner she liked best.

They were still pleasantly disagreeing when Nazuna arrived, awake for the evening. She had done the small shared task she had offered with Anko, then come to see what the noise near the workshop was about.

Anko followed more slowly with two cups.

"That's not a straight line," Nazuna said, looking at the drawing.

"It isn't meant to be," Coco answered.

"Good. Neither am I."

Marin laughed so hard she had to put the material down.

For a while the conversation went somewhere no plan for the household would have predicted: bad lines, stubborn cloth, how people managed to put things away in a place nobody could later find. Anko blamed everyone. Wakana quietly suggested a common place for the missing item. Marin explained that this would interfere with the discovery process.

Mau crossed the yard on the way to the stores and lifted a hand. Nobody stopped talking to give him a report. He smiled at that, answered Coco's brief wave, and kept going.

The work on the table was not finished when the light began to go.

They made a place to leave it safely and agreed when they wanted to come back.

Not because a job was waiting to own tomorrow.

Because the afternoon had been good enough to want another one.
