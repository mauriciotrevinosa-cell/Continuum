# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 28
## Chapter 62 — What Fits In A Bag

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 28
**Reading order:** 62
**Continuity state:** The first visit has finite supplies and an hour limit; the Red Gate dragon remains one stored shadow, ten points remain unspent, and exit mechanics still require testing

*When: Day 287, after supper.*

Yuta took something out of Mau's bag.

Mau put it back.

Yuta waited.

"It's useful," Mau said.

"So is walking without that digging into your back."

The small pan went into Yuta's pack instead. It was not a confiscation. This was somehow more irritating, because Mau had been ready to defend a confiscation.

They packed water, light, dressings, cord, food that belonged to people who could use it. Kaneki checked his own supplies without being handed a protein bar as an answer to a body nobody had finished understanding. Gojo objected to the number of practical things and added something sweet to his own bag.

Nobody removed it.

The agreed first limit was an hour outside. The sandglass going in with them was smaller than the one staying with Anko. They would compare them immediately, not discover a week later that the sunset had been lying.

Anko wrote the four names beneath the new entrance's mark.

"If you can come out sooner, come out sooner."

"You make it sound as though we're trying to get our money's worth," Mau said.

"I've met you."

Shoko checked his hand while the others argued over whose bag held the cord.

The fingers closed more comfortably. They were still his fingers, with a week's worth of reasons not to make them the handle of a dangerous technique just to see whether the pain had gone.

"Use your people," she said.

"That's a terrible way to phrase it."

"Then try listening to them."

He could do that.

When he opened the private page afterward, ten points waited where he had left them. The new branch still offered places to put them. Umaru's plan was on paper, and the unfinished connections beneath the page were still unfinished.

He closed it.

Below his feet, the single dragon rested in storage. Calling it would draw from the shallow reserve whether the fight was impressive or not. Having room to register more did not make the current one free to keep outside all morning.

He asked Ciel whether the first visit needed it.

*Not before we know what is there.*

That was an answer he liked for its simplicity.

The packed bags stayed by the door. Mau went back upstairs with his own hands empty and found Frieren reading the copy of the book she already knew.

"Any good?"

"Very familiar."

He sat beside her. She moved over without pretending she had not left him the space.
