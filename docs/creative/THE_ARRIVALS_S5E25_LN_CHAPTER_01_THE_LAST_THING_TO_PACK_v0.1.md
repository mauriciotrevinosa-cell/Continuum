# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 1 — The Last Thing To Pack

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 1
**Continuity state:** Mau leaves after the agreed check and breakfast with his chosen first party; the compass remains his anniversary gift, and ordinary home plans continue

*When: Day 288, morning.*

The bag was where he had left it.

Mau looked inside anyway.

Water, cord, the small wrapped packet Yuta had allowed him to keep. The pan was in somebody else's pack. He remembered the argument clearly enough to know there was no point starting it again before breakfast.

Behind him, Frieren was trying to put her sleeve on without taking her eyes off a page.

"Other hand," he said.

She tried the same hand again.

He put the bag down and went to help. She gave him the book instead, which was not the kind of assistance he had meant to offer.

"Hold that."

"You've marked the place."

"I know."

She dressed while reading over his arm. He stayed where she could see, turning the book slightly when her hair fell into the way. On the shelf behind her, the closed book with the flowers leaned against the others. Five kept things. Nothing about the new entrance outside had required them to move.

When she was finished, she took her book back and inspected him.

"You slept?"

"Yes."

"Before morning?"

"An ambitious additional condition."

She waited.

"Mostly," he admitted. "I told Shoko about the part that wasn't."

The check had been ordinary, which still pleased him enough to want to say it aloud. No new pain. The fingers closing more easily. Shoko had made him explain what the visit was for, then pointed out that finding the first frightening thing did not oblige him to find the next one as well.

He had agreed before she finished looking at him.

Frieren closed the book over her finger.

"Where's the compass?"

He patted the inner pocket. "Here. Key on the other side."

"Good."

"You sound like you packed me."

"Someone should check the useful things."

He kissed her before she could decide which category that placed him in. She kept a hand on his collar afterward, smoothing a fold that did not need smoothing.

"I'll be here," she said.

He did not ask her to spend the morning waiting at the screen. She had a book to read, Fern had promised to show her something after breakfast, and Ori wanted an opinion about whether the fort's map needed another border.

"I'd like to hear about the other things too," he said. "When I come back. Not just tell you about mine."

She looked at him for a moment, then nodded.

At breakfast Yuka pushed a cup away from the place she had kept beside her.

"You remembered the game?"

"I was going to ask whether you remembered losing it."

"I haven't played you yet."

"I'm preparing."

She made a face and passed him the bread. Across the table Yuta caught the look, smiled into his own cup, and let them finish arguing before asking where the spare cord had gone.

Kaneki came in while they ate. He had his own bag and accepted coffee when Rem offered, without taking the plate she had not offered him. Mau shifted his feet to make room on the bench.

"Ready?" he asked.

"For the first hour."

"An unusually exact answer."

"I've heard the rest is still being decided."

Gojo arrived last, already wearing the expression of somebody whose lateness was clearly part of a lesson. Shoko came through the other door with a cup in her hand.

"If you tell him you were testing whether he'd leave without you," she said, "I'll tell him where you were."

"Terrible clinical discretion."

"He was looking for the other glove," she told Mau.

Gojo held up both hands. The evidence was regrettably complete.

They left the table after everyone had finished eating. Mau took his bowl through to the kitchen rather than making the packed bag a reason not to. Yuka put her own beside it and told him she would have the game ready after lunch.

At the yard door Ori came up with a little folded page.

"For the sand."

It was not another map. She had drawn two long empty shapes and put a small mark at the top of each.

He understood when she pointed.

"So you can show me how much. When you're back."

He took it carefully.

"I can do that."

She hugged him, then went to find Milim, who was asking Frieren whether two breakfasts would count as a long enough morning for lunch to begin.

Mau watched them go until Yuta lifted the pan from his pack and said it could still be returned to its owner if he wanted.

"No," Mau said promptly.

Yuta looked pleased with himself.

They walked to the screen together.
