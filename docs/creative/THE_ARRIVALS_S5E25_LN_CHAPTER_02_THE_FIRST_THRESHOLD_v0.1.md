# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 2 — The First Threshold

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 2
**Continuity state:** The owner registers the four-person expedition; the entrance notice distinguishes persistent progress from a weekly reset and provides a physical withdrawal route

*When: Day 288, late morning.*

Anko had put a second mark on the board.

The blue opening had its usual entry. The darker frame had four names beneath a simple empty square. There were no hopeful lines running down the page toward a hundred places nobody had seen yet.

Rimuru stood beside her with the larger sandglass. He turned it over, checked that the sand ran freely, and set it upright again without beginning the count.

"I would like a very dull report," he told Mau.

"I brought three interesting people."

"A terrible first decision."

Kaneki looked at Gojo. Gojo inclined his head as though he had been thanked.

Mau took out the key.

The offered entry sharpened when he approached. New lines appeared beneath the owner field. He read them once without moving, then asked the others to look while he called the visible part outward.

Four places in the current expedition. Names to be confirmed before entry. Progress retained between visits; withdrawal did not restore the party's condition or renew collected rewards.

"That sounds promising," Gojo said.

"The last place said normal," Yuta answered.

"I said promising."

Mau kept reading.

A return threshold waited inside the entrance. The owner was required to begin an expedition. Companions admitted with him could withdraw through a secured threshold, and return to that active expedition while he remained inside. If the owner withdrew, the entry would wait for him again.

Not a promise that every room was safe. Not a way out of anywhere he happened to be standing.

He looked at the dark middle of the frame.

"We test that before anything else."

"I go out and back," Yuta said. "You stay where the threshold can be seen. No one follows a corridor while I'm gone."

Rimuru checked the outside of the notice. He could see the same four names Mau had selected, no place for him supplied merely by holding the key.

"If you need another person?"

The invitation offered changes at the starting threshold, with the owner there to admit them. Mau showed him rather than guessing how a rescue might fit into an unfamiliar rule.

Rimuru considered it.

"Then Yuta coming out still gives us someone who knows where you are. We don't all vanish into the same question."

"Unless the first thing we learn is that the notice lied," Anko said.

No one laughed. She had earned the objection.

Mau closed his hand around the key.

"If the first return doesn't work the way it says, we don't leave the entrance looking for a better answer."

Gojo walked around the frame far enough to inspect it from the other side. He came back without touching the light.

"I can see a boundary," he said. "I can't tell you what's beyond it by admiring the edge."

Mau liked him better for not trying.

They confirmed the names. No mark appeared on anybody's skin. The page folded away from the public field until only the four small indicators remained.

Anko held up the outside glass. Yuta held the smaller one in answer.

"An hour," she said. "Coming out to tell us the first test worked doesn't buy another hour."

"We've met," Mau said.

"Repeatedly. I'm trying to improve the experience."

Rimuru stepped beside him before they crossed.

For a moment Mau expected a quiet version of the things they had already said at the table. Rimuru only tugged the loose edge of his collar out from beneath the strap.

"There. One less thing to spend the whole trip pretending isn't uncomfortable."

"Thank you."

"You can repay me with dullness."

Mau nodded.

He went through with Yuta on his right. Behind them Gojo and Kaneki entered before the last indicator changed. The key stayed solid in his palm, unexpectedly ordinary against the cold of the frame.

For a breath the sound of the courtyard stretched thin.

Then his foot found a dry tiled floor.

The first thing Mau did was turn around.
