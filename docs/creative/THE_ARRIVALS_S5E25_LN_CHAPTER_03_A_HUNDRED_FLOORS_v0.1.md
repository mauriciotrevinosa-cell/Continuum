# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 3 — A Hundred Floors

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 3
**Continuity state:** The four test an independent return at the vestibule and discover the hundred-floor campaign; the clock comparison applies to the observed interval, not every future room

*When: Day 288, inside the key gate; the first measured interval.*

The doorway was still behind him.

Mau let his next breath out carefully. He had not meant to hold it. The tiled floor continued beneath all four pairs of feet, and the light in the frame had not changed to red.

Beyond it Anko was visible as a blurred shape beside the screen.

Yuta put the small sandglass on the floor. Turned it. Then stepped back through before anyone could decide there was something interesting farther in.

Mau waited.

It was much more difficult than watching a person go out through the kitchen door. The same size of absence, with none of the confidence. He kept his eyes on the frame until Yuta returned carrying a small piece of Anko's chalk.

"She wants it back."

Mau laughed once, mostly from relief.

"That's proof enough for me."

"It shouldn't be," Gojo said. "I could have supplied chalk."

Yuta turned toward him. "She also said if you make that joke, the charge doubles."

"Now I'm convinced."

The first interval inside had been little more than the time it took to exchange a question. Outside, Anko had watched the same short run of sand. They kept the glass moving and waited through a longer interval before Yuta crossed again.

This time he took the glass with him and came back without it.

"Still close," he said. "Not a week while she blinks."

They had a useful comparison, not a law for every place the door might someday show them. Mau asked Ciel to keep track of the measured intervals and told the others what she could compare.

Her reply did not bring Rimuru with it.

The familiar private contact to the other host had thinned at entry. At the frame's edge she could distinguish a response, enough for Mau to relay that they were standing inside and the exit worked. It was not a view down every passage or a promise the thread would follow them through the whole place.

Yuta would carry the outside report if they lost it.

Only after the second return did they look properly at the room.

It was a vestibule in the remains of a building much larger than the inn. The ceiling still held, supported by dark square pillars. Dust lay along the edges of the tiles, but the broad path to the next opening had been cleared by something other than feet.

Kaneki stood beside that opening without crossing it. He raised a hand when Mau approached.

"Listen."

At first Mau heard only a low continuous rush. Wind, perhaps. Then he found the small crackle inside it.

Fire.

Beyond the broken lintel, a street ran between buildings whose upper floors were burning.

They were too straight to belong to a town Mau had seen here. Rows of windows. Broad blackened walls. A sign hanging from a bent support, its marks recognizable as writing without becoming words he could remember knowing.

For a moment he waited for the sight to give him a past.

It gave him smoke, distance, and the smell of hot stone.

"Do you know it?" Yuta asked.

Mau shook his head.

Gojo looked along the street. Something changed in his face, too quickly for Mau to name. He said only that they should not assume a building was empty because it had lost its windows.

The notice came up when Mau stepped to the lintel.

*Floor 1 of 100.*

Below it, the first objective named a sealed route through the street. Clear the sentry post to open the next floor. The map showed only the space close enough to have been seen; most of it remained dark.

"A hundred," Mau said.

Kaneki looked back toward the return frame.

"We were going to be pleased with one room."

"We still can be," Yuta said.

The reply brought Mau back to the glass that was no longer with them. Anko was holding it outside. The hour had not stopped because the place had offered them a hundred reasons to stay.

He read the progress section aloud. The route would retain cleared stages. Secured transfer points would have to be earned farther in. Until then, their known way out was the doorway behind them.

He looked down the street again.

Something moved under a dead traffic signal.

Not a person coming out of a house. A plated shape unfolding from where it had been standing so still he had mistaken it for another piece of wreckage.

Kaneki had heard it first. He stepped back from the opening, leaving all four of them room to move.

"There's your first room," Gojo said quietly.

Mau did not go forward until Yuta had asked whether everybody could still see the way back.
