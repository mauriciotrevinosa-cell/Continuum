# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 6 — The Smaller Glass

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 6
**Continuity state:** All four return within the first hour; the observed clock and costs are reported, the first floor stays cleared, and Mau returns to the people whose plans continued at home

*When: Day 288, before lunch.*

Anko was still holding the chalk.

For an instant that was the only thing Mau could think about. The little piece Yuta had brought in had been handed back at the second test. Anko had kept it ready beside the names, and there it was, pale against her fingers.

He was on the right side of the frame.

Rimuru counted them without making the movement obvious. One, two, three, four. Kaneki came out last and set down the small wrapped bundle.

"Dull?" Rimuru asked.

Mau looked at him.

"I've done badly."

"I can see."

The larger glass had not finished its hour. Anko showed the smaller one beside it and listened while Ciel's count was relayed. Their inside visit had used just under fifty outside minutes. Close enough to begin planning ordinary mornings, not enough to promise that every future room would keep the same clock.

They recorded it that way.

"A hundred floors," Yuta told her.

Anko's chalk stopped.

"You saw them?"

"The notice. We saw one street."

She wrote the difference before continuing.

Mau went through the four names and ended the expedition. The frame stayed, but its offer waited for the owner to begin another visit. The ordinary blue entrance beside it went on belonging to its own week.

One floor cleared. One extra stored sentry. Level four, fifteen unspent points. No one tried to turn that into a report on how much of Mau had been restored.

Shoko looked at the scraped knuckle and asked what else hurt.

"Nothing new. Tired. Less than the last long trip."

"Which was a week."

"A poor comparison. I heard it when I said it."

She took him to the clinic while the others finished the map. Yuta came to answer her questions about the encounter, then left when she told him she had enough people answering for one patient.

Mau told her about the extraction attempt before showing the small change in the new page. She made him distinguish the drain from the feeling that had followed opening a technique with his own body. He could do that today. It did not explain all of either.

"No more demonstrations before lunch," she said.

"I have an appointment."

"Medical?"

"Potentially humiliating."

She looked over his shoulder toward the two girls waiting beyond the open door.

"I'll leave the treatment to them."

Ori had brought the folded page. He took it and drew a line low on the first shape, then another almost at the same height on the second. She studied them before looking up.

"Nearly together."

"Today. Where we went."

"You can put the next one on the back."

He gave the paper back to her rather than keeping the beginning of another record she had chosen to make.

Yuka waited until Ori had folded it.

"Lunch," she said. "Then the game. No telling everything three times and forgetting."

"I've only told it twice."

"Excellent. There's time to eat."

Frieren was at the far end of the table when he came in. Her book lay open beside Fern's, with a disagreement marked by a pencil neither of them was using for anything magical. She looked at his hand before his face, then relaxed enough to make room beside her.

"How was yours?" he asked.

She put a finger on the place they had been discussing.

"Fern was right about this."

Fern took her cup and looked almost unbearably pleased.

Mau sat down to hear why.
