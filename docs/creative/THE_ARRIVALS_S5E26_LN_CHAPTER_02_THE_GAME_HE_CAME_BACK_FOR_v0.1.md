# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 7 — The Game He Came Back For

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 7
**Continuity state:** Mau and Yuta keep Yuka's game appointment; Maki joins by choice, and the family interaction is not reduced to a dungeon debrief

*When: Day 288, afternoon.*

Yuka had put the cards where nobody could pretend not to see them.

Yuta made the mistake of asking whether she wanted another explanation of the morning first.

"I heard the explanation."

"You came in halfway through."

"There are a hundred floors. You saw one street. Oppa came back with another thing in his shadow. You all need lunch."

Mau looked at Yuta.

"She could do the reports."

"She could," Maki said, taking the fourth chair. "But then you'd want her to do all of them. Deal."

Yuka shuffled with the exaggerated care of someone who intended to notice if anyone implied she couldn't. Mau had a cup of water under the table, out of reach of the cards, and nothing else waiting in his hands.

No page. No key.

She explained the game while giving each of them a hand. Make pairs. Put them down. Draw from the person beside you when the turn reached you. One of the little hand-drawn crowns had no partner.

"You don't want that one at the end," she said.

Mau looked at his hand, then at Maki.

"I see a possible problem."

"For you?"

"A broad range of people."

Yuta took a card from his granddaughter and smiled before he could help it. She narrowed her eyes at him. He tried to put the smile away, which made her much more certain it meant something.

Maki drew from Mau without looking at his face.

He felt this ought to be illegal.

"You're supposed to wonder where it is."

"I do."

"You didn't look worried."

"You were worried enough for both of us."

Yuka laughed, then looked down and discovered that her next draw had produced the crown.

All the laughter left her face so carefully that even Yuta noticed.

"Very subtle," he said.

"You don't know anything."

"I have raised—"

He stopped as Maki looked up.

"I have played cards before," he finished.

Mau put his cup down very quietly.

Yuka studied her grandfather, judged that his face would not improve the history lesson, and rearranged her hand. When Mau reached toward the card at the end, she began talking about the map.

He stopped.

She stopped.

"That's the one," he said.

"You don't know that."

He took the card beside it.

It was the crown.

Maki's laugh arrived first. Yuta followed a moment later, head lowered over the table. Yuka did not even attempt to be kind.

"Very subtle," she told Mau.

He looked at the little crown, then at her rather pleased face.

"I've walked into a much older institution than I understood."

They played until the room's other conversations began changing around them. Rimuru came by, took one look at Mau's hand, and declined to be drawn into the dispute over whether spectators were allowed to become accomplices.

Mau lost.

Yuta lost the next round, which was much more satisfying to everyone, including him once Maki stopped laughing at the exact way it had happened.

At last Yuka gathered the cards and pushed a few loose ones into the right order.

"That was the appointment," she said.

Mau looked at her. For a moment he thought she meant it was over in some larger way.

Then she tapped the little stack.

"Umaru wants a different game next time. I'll teach you before she does. She explains the parts that help her."

"A family failing?" he asked Yuta.

"Apparently," Yuta said.

Maki got up to put the cups away. Yuka went with her, still explaining how the crown had been obvious before Mau began thinking about it.

Mau stayed beside Yuta for a little while. The game had taken most of the afternoon he had meant to leave free.

He liked what had happened to it.
