# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 8 — The Same Small Pull

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 8
**Continuity state:** Gojo's inside result is compared with an outside baseline; one controlled variation suggests better selection but no restored Red, Purple, Domain or global recovery percentage

*When: Day 289, morning.*

Senku had brought something Gojo disliked more than an opinion.

A mark.

"Same distance," he said, tapping the ground with the end of the measuring stick.

"It's a very small distance."

"Then this should be easy to repeat."

Gojo looked at Mau. Mau discovered an absorbing interest in the three wooden pieces on the board.

Byakuya had cut them to match closely enough that the differences could be checked rather than argued about. One sat on the marked line. Two waited beside it. Shoko had taken the chair in the shade and made it clear that her presence did not turn this into permission to try everything at once.

Rimuru came out after speaking with Jinshi about an entry that did not need Fern fetched from breakfast. He sat on the low wall, leaving Mau the end of the bench.

"Are you performing today?" he asked.

"I'm trying to remain an informed audience."

"An advanced role."

Gojo raised a hand.

The little Blue gathered over the board. The first piece slid toward it. One of the waiting pieces trembled, turned half a face, and moved too.

Senku caught it before it reached the first.

"That one wasn't invited."

"It felt left out," Gojo said.

He closed the pull. The size had not changed since before the visit. He rolled his wrist, listening to a cost only he could describe properly, then gave Senku the answer without turning it into a joke.

"More resistance here than inside. About what I expected from before. The second piece moved because I let the edge spread. Not because I asked it to."

Byakuya moved both pieces back.

"Then that's the thing we try changing."

They did not begin by asking for a larger sphere. Gojo watched the places on the board where the effect had caught, placed his hand at the same height, and tried to end the operation before the second piece's movement became useful to anyone.

The first attempt brought the wrong piece a little farther.

He closed it and waited through the agreed rest.

"I thought I had that," he said.

"Good," Senku answered.

Gojo looked at him.

"Now we know which thought didn't work."

"You're enjoying this."

"Immensely."

Mau felt a private flicker of amusement from Ciel. Gojo glanced toward him, but Mau only smiled. There had been no spoken comment for the room to hear.

On the third attempt, the chosen piece moved alone. Not very far. It stopped short of the middle when Gojo closed the operation.

Everyone looked at the waiting piece.

It had turned, barely.

"Better," Byakuya said. "Not separate yet."

Gojo leaned forward to inspect the mark, thinking now about something more interesting than whether he had looked impressive.

"Again?" he asked Shoko.

She looked at the time they had agreed on.

"One. Then tell me how the last one feels after you've stopped."

The fourth attempt did not improve on the third. It also did not collapse into something larger, which Senku wrote down after Gojo stopped trying to make the answer sound entertaining.

They left the pieces where they were and sat together in the shade.

"No great revelation?" Rimuru asked.

Gojo accepted the water Mau passed him.

"I've discovered the board is a tyrant."

"Does it have a title?"

"Not one I can use in front of Shoko."

She laughed without looking up from the note she was making.

Mau watched his teacher drink. He had seen a man make space refuse a weapon yesterday. This morning he had watched him care about whether a piece of wood turned before it should.

He liked being allowed to see both.
