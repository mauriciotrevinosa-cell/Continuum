# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 9 — Her Kind Of Morning

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 9
**Continuity state:** Frieren, Fern and Stark take their own ordinary-gate outing; the weekly E entrance stays separate from Mau's tower, and discovery does not require a new grimoire every visit

*When: Day 289, late morning; the normal gate.*

Stark heard the chest before Frieren announced she had found it.

That was encouraging. Usually the interval was shorter.

"I don't think you should—"

"It has been a disappointing corridor," she said.

Fern raised her light and examined the hinges. Something inside the lid changed its breathing to match the movement of the glow.

"Frieren."

"I heard it."

"Then why are you still getting closer?"

Frieren picked up a broken length of wood from the floor and pushed the lid with it.

Teeth closed on the wood.

For a moment all three of them stared at the half that remained in her hand.

"Better," Fern admitted.

"Considerably," Stark said.

Frieren looked offended by how little enthusiasm they had found for her restraint. She backed away before the mimic could decide the experiment had improved its appetite and let Fern put the chest out of the way with one clean shot.

They had come in through the normal entrance, with Anko's current map and a return time agreed before leaving. The E at the top had told them less than they would have liked. It had not named which corridor would be wet or which broken step would roll under a boot.

Fern had marked the step. Stark had found the draught behind the false wall. Frieren, following it, had found the chest and enough disappointment to make both of them immediately suspicious.

Now she turned toward the draught again.

"Not that way," Fern said. "Look at the dust."

The disturbed line ran past a narrow side opening, not toward the false wall itself. Frieren held the light lower and saw it.

"You're right."

Stark waited for the explanation without noticing, at first, that Fern had not begun giving it. She was looking down at the marked line with the small concentrated smile she sometimes tried to hide by becoming practical.

"Shall we?" Frieren asked her.

They took the side route. There was room enough for the axe without carrying it sideways, then a low stretch where Stark had to duck. He went first there, testing the stones instead of asking the two mages to float past a problem they would still need a sound way back through.

At the end, another small chamber waited.

No breathing this time.

They checked anyway.

The case beneath the shelf held a lantern, plain enough that Frieren looked twice before accepting the absence of a book. A little chamber in its base contained a fitted charge. Its light rose when Fern turned the control, then dimmed when she turned it back.

Stark could see the charge beginning to shorten along one fine line.

"Uses it up," he said.

"Yes."

"Useful, though."

Frieren lifted it, checking the weight.

"It would have been more useful with a grimoire beside it."

Fern took the empty cloth from her bag.

"We can carry this without explaining to Shoko why you have teeth marks on your head. An unusually successful morning."

Stark laughed and tried to hide it in a cough.

Frieren gave them the look she reserved for people failing to appreciate the possibilities of the world. Then she wrapped the lantern herself and asked Fern whether they had time to see the dry end of the passage before returning.

They did.

It held a small cache of ordinary crystals, no book, and a narrow high window looking into another part of the constructed ruin. They stood there for a while, sharing a drink and considering where the unseen corridor might turn.

Stark set the axe against his knee. Fern stood near enough that their shoulders touched when she leaned to look.

Frieren did not rush either of them.

There was time to mark the route properly.
