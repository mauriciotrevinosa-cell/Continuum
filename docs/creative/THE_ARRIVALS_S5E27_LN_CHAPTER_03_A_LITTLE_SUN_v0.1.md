# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 13 — A Little Sun

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 13
**Continuity state:** Kaneki directs light chosen work and enjoys coffee/rest; Mai and Maki have their own practical task and ordinary humour

*When: Day 290, late morning.*

Kaneki was moving the last of a stack of dry wood under cover.

There wasn't much left. He had already done the difficult part, making a space where the newer pieces could sit apart from the older ones. Mau stopped, looked at the two piles, and asked which was going where.

Kaneki pointed.

"Those first. Keep the gap underneath."

Mau picked up an armful.

"I had a system," Kaneki said after the second trip.

"I'm following it."

"You put that one backward."

Mau looked at the piece in his hand. Kaneki looked at him. Then the corner of Kaneki's mouth moved.

"Very funny."

"You checked."

They finished without needing much conversation. Mau held the cover while Kaneki secured it, and Kaneki waited while Mau worked a splinter out of his palm.

"Let me see."

"It's out."

Kaneki turned his hand over anyway, checked, and let go.

They sat for a little while with coffee. Mau's tasted stronger than he'd expected. He tried not to show it.

"You can put it down," Kaneki said.

"It's good."

"That's not what I said."

Mau put it down. Kaneki smiled properly this time.

"What have you got after this?" Mau asked.

"A few things. Nothing that needs doing this minute."

"That's new."

"A little."

Kaneki moved his chair a few inches so his knees were in the sun. Mau did the same.

The covered wood stayed exactly where they had put it.

* * *

Mai was trying to make a strip of cloth stay flat while Maki marked it.

"Hold that," Mai told Mau when he passed.

He held it.

"Not there."

He moved his hand.

"There."

Maki drew the line and sat back. Mai lifted the cloth, inspected it, and decided it would do.

"What is it?" Mau asked.

"A strap," said Mai.

"I got that far."

"Then you're doing well."

There was a second strip on the bench. Mai placed it under his hand without asking whether he had anywhere else to be. Maki's expression suggested she had been waiting for this to happen to somebody else.

"How was practice?" she asked.

"Three stones. Lost twice."

"Against the stones?"

"He was helping them."

Maki nodded as though this was a perfectly plausible tactical disadvantage.

Mai smoothed a frayed edge with her thumb. "You're leaning on it."

Mau straightened. "Do I get instructions before the mistake?"

"Then how would you learn?"

He stayed for the next strip too. Mai did not have to tell him where to hold it. When they were finished, Maki swept the scraps into her palm and Mai caught one that had stuck to his sleeve.

"You missed one," she said.

She put it with the others.

At the far end of the passage, Bocchi was standing with her guitar, looking in their direction and then away again.

Mau lifted a hand.

She lifted hers very quickly.

Mai glanced over. "Go. We've used you enough."

"That sounds generous."

"Don't ruin it."
