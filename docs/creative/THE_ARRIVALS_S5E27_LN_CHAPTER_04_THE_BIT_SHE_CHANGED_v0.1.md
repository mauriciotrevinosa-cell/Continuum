# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 14 — The Bit She Changed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 14
**Continuity state:** Bocchi asks for specific listening and chooses her own musical change; Kita and Carmilla retain independent bonds, followed by shared lunch and cleanup

*When: Day 290, before and during lunch.*

Bocchi wanted to know whether he had a minute.

He asked if she needed help carrying something.

"No," she said. Then, with an effort: "Listening."

They found somewhere to sit where they would not be in the way. Bocchi settled the guitar against herself. Mau waited while she adjusted a string that, to his ears, had sounded right already.

"It's not finished."

"Okay."

"And I changed it this morning. So if it—"

"Bocchi."

She looked up.

"Do you want to tell me about it first, or play it first?"

She considered that. Her fingers stopped twisting at the tuning peg.

"Play it."

"Okay."

It began with a few notes spaced far apart. Then the gap shortened, and the tune found something beneath it that Mau hadn't known he was waiting for. He looked at her hands, then away so she wouldn't have to feel watched.

Partway through, she stopped.

"That part's wrong."

"Play it again?"

She did. This time she went farther.

Kita came looking for her before they were done. She paused when she heard the guitar, then leaned against the wall rather than interrupting.

When Bocchi stopped, Mau let the last note finish.

"The bit you played twice," he said. "I like the second way. It feels like it's going somewhere and then decides to stay."

Bocchi looked down at the strings.

"That's the bit I changed."

"Then I like your change."

Kita smiled. "I said something like that. With less staying."

"You said it sounded like a window," Bocchi told her.

"An open one."

Mau nodded. "I was going to say that next."

Bocchi laughed. Quietly, but he heard it.

Kita reached for her hand, waited until Bocchi had put down the pick, and took it.

"Nijika's got Ryo. We should go before she loses her."

Bocchi stood, then hesitated.

"When it's finished—"

"I'd like to hear it."

"Even if it's different?"

"Especially."

She nodded. This time she did not have to check his face to see whether he meant it.

He watched them go a little way, their hands joined and Bocchi carrying her guitar in the other one. Then he realized he had been tapping the changed part against his knee.

He was still trying to get it right when the call for lunch came.

* * *

At lunch he got the tune wrong, and Carmilla, passing behind him, supplied three notes under her breath.

Mau turned.

"You know it?"

"A little."

"She's changed it."

"I know."

There was something pleased in her voice. She went to find her place before he could say anything else.

Mau sat down between Yuka and Rimuru. Frieren was farther along with Fern, her book closed over one finger. Stark was listening to something Fern said with the careful attention of someone who knew a question was coming.

"How were your stones?" Rimuru asked.

"How do you know about the stones?"

"Satoru. He was very proud of them."

"They're talented."

Rimuru passed him a bowl. "I told him so."

Yuka wanted to know whether the practice was difficult. Mau described it, and she immediately arranged three crumbs beside her plate to demonstrate why he should have stepped differently.

"He kept moving," Mau said.

"So move the other way."

"I'll tell him you said that."

"Tell him to invite me. I'll show you."

Rimuru grinned at his bowl.

"Not a word," Mau told him.

"Wasn't going to say anything."

The meal took longer than Mau had expected. Somebody brought more water. Yuka got up to fetch it and came back caught in a conversation with Umaru, who had brought the cards to prove her point from breakfast. They spread four of them beside the crumbs and argued in lowered voices until Fern looked over.

"After lunch," she said.

They gathered the cards.

When the dishes began to move, Mau stood and took a stack to the kitchen. Rem was already there. She handed him a cloth rather than taking the plates from him.

"Those can go on the shelf when they're dry."

He wiped. She washed. A little later someone came in with the cups, and the work rearranged itself around another pair of hands.

He was reaching for the last bowl when Minerva appeared in the doorway.

"Tea," she said.

"Now?"

"When you've finished that."

She took a cloth and dried the bowl herself.
