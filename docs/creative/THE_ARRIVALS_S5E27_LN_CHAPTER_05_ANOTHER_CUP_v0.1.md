# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 15 — Another Cup

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 15
**Continuity state:** The seven witches have their own plans and humour; Echidna and Coco agree binding work tomorrow, Satella chooses a river walk with Minerva

*When: Day 290, early afternoon.*

Sekhmet had occupied the bench before anyone else arrived.

"We could sit outside," Daphne said.

"You could," Sekhmet agreed.

Minerva set down the pot. "There's shade."

"There's shade here."

Mau looked at the stretch of bench between her feet and the end.

"Can I have a little of it?"

She moved one foot. He sat.

The others gathered at their own pace. Echidna brought a book, which she put beside her cup without opening. Carmilla sat near the end, where she could see the passage. Satella took the chair beside Mau and turned it a little toward the open window. Pride arrived last, on time.

"You have something in your hair," she told him.

Mau reached up.

"Other side."

He found a flake of dry wood.

"It's been a productive morning," he said.

"So I see."

Minerva poured. Mau reached for the pot when her own cup was still empty, and she surrendered it without comment.

Daphne was talking about peas.

Not growing them. Eating them before they were supposed to be eaten. She had discovered that the difference between these two stages was of great importance to other people.

"There are more on the plant," she said.

"There are fewer on the plant," Echidna replied. "That is the objection."

"They were good."

"An excellent defence. Unlikely to succeed with the person counting them."

Mau passed the plate of bread to Daphne before she had to reach across Satella.

"You could ask which ones are ready."

"I did ask."

"And?"

"I disagreed."

Sekhmet made a faint sound that might have been laughter.

The discussion lasted until Daphne had finished the bread nearest her. By then Echidna had opened the book, not to read but to show Carmilla a loose leaf she was worried about. The thread at the binding had worn through.

Mau leaned over to see.

"Can it be fixed?"

"Yes."

"Do you need—"

"Coco and I are doing it tomorrow."

"Oh."

"You may watch if you can resist improving the arrangement."

"I can watch."

Echidna looked at him over the book.

"I can try to watch."

"Better."

Carmilla touched the edge of the loose page and asked whether the sewing would show. Echidna explained. Satella listened until the conversation moved to a kind of stitch, then looked out of the window again.

"Is it cooler at the river?" she asked.

"A little," Mau said. "In the morning."

"I went yesterday," Carmilla told her. "Before everyone else."

"Was it quiet?"

"Very."

Satella turned her cup in both hands. "I might go."

"You should," said Minerva. "I'll come if you want."

Satella nodded. It took a moment, but the nod was definite.

Mau had been about to offer to go with her. He drank his tea instead.

Pride watched him over the rim of her cup.

"What?" he asked.

"Nothing. How was your morning?"

He told them about the knot. Sekhmet wanted to know whether sleeping in the air was more restful, and Minerva said that was not the useful part of the story. Then he told them about the stones, which proved even less dignified on the third telling.

Carmilla laughed with one hand over her mouth.

"I heard a song," he added.

Her hand came down.

"The new part?"

"I think so. It was good."

"It is," she said.

They sat for another cup. Daphne wanted to know what the game after lunch involved, and Mau admitted that he was not certain he understood the rules anymore.

"You agreed anyway?" Pride asked.

"They're teaching me."

"Are they?"

"That's what they said."

She smiled.

When he got up, Sekhmet's foot moved back into the warm place he had left. Minerva caught the empty pot before he could pick it up.

"Your game," she reminded him.

He thanked her and went to find out whether he had been taught the rules or merely told them.
