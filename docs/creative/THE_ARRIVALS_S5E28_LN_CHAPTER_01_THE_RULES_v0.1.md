# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 16 — The Rules

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 16
**Continuity state:** The card game has actual turns and choices among Umaru, Yuka, Milim, Ori and Mau; Frieren reads beside them, no training or System gain

*When: Day 290, afternoon.*

Umaru had chosen a table where there was room to put down the cards properly.

She had also chosen Mau's seat.

"There."

He sat.

Yuka was opposite him, Milim beside her. Ori had pulled up a chair to watch, bringing the ribbon with her. It lay coiled on the table, safe from Milim's absent-minded fingers.

Frieren sat a little apart with her book open. One foot rested against Mau's ankle under the table.

Umaru dealt and turned a five of clubs face up in the middle.

"Same suit or same number," she repeated. "Empty your hand first. If you can't play, draw. Colour isn't a suit."

"Are we using your rules or Yuka's?" he asked.

"The rules."

"That's encouraging."

The first round went quickly. Mau was still arranging his cards when Milim put a seven of spades on the five of clubs with a cry of triumph.

"You can't do that," Yuka said.

"I just did."

"It's the wrong one."

Milim examined it. "It's nearly the same."

Umaru took the card and returned it. "Both black doesn't count. Clubs on clubs, or a five on a five."

Milim listened with the expression she wore when somebody was telling her a deeply unreasonable law of nature.

Mau waited. On his next turn he put a black spade on a black club, making precisely the mistake Umaru had just explained.

Yuka put her face in her hands.

"Oppa."

"I know."

"You were sitting here."

"I know."

"I think he's doing the face," Milim said suspiciously.

Frieren turned a page. "No."

Mau looked at her.

"That was real," she said.

Ori laughed so hard she had to move the ribbon away from her elbow.

They played again.

It became easier after Mau stopped trying to remember every instruction at once. He began to notice when Yuka's silence meant a good hand and when it meant she was waiting for somebody else to make a mistake. Milim could not conceal either condition. Umaru could conceal anything she wanted until she began to win, when her shoulders rose a fraction.

"You do that," he told her.

"Do what?"

He imitated it.

She stared at him, betrayed.

Yuka leaned across to study Umaru's shoulders. Milim did the same. Ori was now watching them instead of the cards.

"You're all distracting me," Umaru said.

"We're learning," said Mau.

She won that round anyway.

On the next, the top card was a seven of spades. Mau could keep spades in play, or use his seven of hearts to change the suit. He remembered Yuka had been holding hearts while Umaru tried to make everyone stay on spades.

He put down the seven. Umaru had to draw. A little later Yuka used the hearts to shed two cards on successive turns, ruining whatever Umaru had been preparing.

Mau looked at Yuka.

She looked back, very innocent.

He lost by one turn.

"You helped her," Umaru accused him.

"I helped me. Badly."

"He's improving," Yuka said.

"I'm proud."

She kicked him lightly under the table.

Frieren's foot moved away from the confusion, then returned to his ankle when it was safe.

They let Ori take the next hand. Milim moved her chair over to advise her, which chiefly consisted of whispering the value of each card loudly enough for everyone to hear. Ori listened, thanked her, and made her own choice.

Mau leaned back while they argued about the result.

Umaru took his empty cup and pushed hers toward him. There was still some water in it.

"Here."

He drank.

"Another after this?" she asked.

"Yuta's waiting for me later."

"After that."

He looked at the half-finished game, at the room beyond it, where the light had moved across the floor without asking anyone to notice.

"After that," he said.

Umaru nodded and took her cup back.

Frieren lifted her eyes from the book.

"Listen to this."

All five of them paused. She read two lines, frowned at a word, and read them again. Mau asked her what the word meant. She began to explain, and Yuka went back to sorting the cards, listening. Milim lowered her head onto her arms. Ori rested her chin on her hand.

Umaru dealt the next round slowly enough that Frieren could finish.
