# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 17 — Without Stones

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 17
**Continuity state:** A light chosen Yuta timing practice stops before exhaustion; grandfather and brotherly bonds continue into dinner, Gojo and Shoko have separate ordinary outings

*When: Day 290, late afternoon and supper.*

Yuta had two practice weapons and no stones.

"Already better," Mau told him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

They started slowly. Yuta had him step out of reach, then back into it, without hurrying the return. Mau got the distance right and the timing wrong. The next time he reversed the problem.

"You're trying to finish before you start," Yuta said.

"That would save time."

Yuta smiled. "It would. Try it the other way."

There was no need to hit hard. Sometimes Yuta stopped halfway and let Mau see exactly where his weight had gone. Sometimes he let the mistake finish, a light touch to the shoulder or the wrist, and waited for Mau to work out why.

Once Mau found the gap. He stopped short, surprised by it.

Yuta nodded.

"Again."

They put the weapons away before it became something either of them would need to recover from.

Mau sat on the low step and rubbed the heel of his hand. Yuta sat beside him. For a while they watched the shadows under the trees grow long enough to join.

"Maki says you lost to stones."

Mau closed his eyes.

Yuta's laugh was quiet, but it lasted.

"Did you have a good day?" he asked when he had finished.

"Yeah. You?"

"Yeah."

"Yuka's getting very good at cards," Mau said.

"I'm still recovering from the other day."

"She looks at me like Maki when I do something stupid."

"I know that look."

Mau turned toward him. Yuta was trying to keep a straight face.

"Does it work on you?"

"Usually."

"Good. I thought it was just me."

Yuta picked a loose thread from his sleeve. "I like hearing her from the other room. When she's playing."

Mau understood that. The noise travelled farther than anybody needed it to, and sometimes that was the nice part.

"Come play with us," he said.

"I'm not very good."

"Neither am I. It's how they get you to stay."

"Does that work?"

"Usually."

Yuta laughed again. They sat until the light on the step had gone.

Then Yuta got up and held out his hand.

Mau took it.

"Food?"

"Please."

* * *

The evening meal was noisier than lunch.

Gojo came in late, still objecting to something Byakuya had measured that afternoon. Shoko came back from a walk with Seiko a little later, her hair disturbed by the wind. Mau caught Gojo looking up, caught himself about to smile, and concentrated on his plate.

Across from him, Maki noticed all three things.

She said nothing, which was worse.

Ori had a story to tell him. It involved a game she and Milim had been playing, a rule Milim had introduced after losing, and the argument that followed. Milim objected to the word *losing*.

Mau asked Ori what she had done.

"I said if it counted for her it counted for me."

"And?"

"She doesn't like the rule anymore."

"It was a bad rule," Milim said.

"You invented it."

"And I fixed it."

Rimuru nodded solemnly. "Good administration."

Frieren, beside Mau now, put down her spoon. "Don't encourage her."

"I was supporting a colleague."

"You were enjoying it," Mau said.

Rimuru smiled. "A little."
