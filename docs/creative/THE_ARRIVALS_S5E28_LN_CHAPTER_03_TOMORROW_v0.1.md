# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 18 — Tomorrow

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 18
**Continuity state:** The full day returns to games, Bocchi music and family bedtime; current couples, loft and connecting door persist, with no night training or cliffhanger alarm

*When: Day 290, evening through bedtime.*

Yuka wanted to finish the card game. Umaru wanted to start a better one. They argued about whether these were the same thing while the plates were cleared.

Mau took his to the kitchen, dried what was handed to him, and went back before they had to come looking.

This time he won a round.

It was not a particularly impressive win. Milim had misunderstood something, Yuka had been laughing at her, and Umaru had taken a risk that did not work. Still, when he put the last card down, the silence was satisfying.

He lifted both hands.

"I would like to thank my teachers."

"No," said Umaru.

"For their patience."

"Absolutely not."

Yuka was already gathering the cards. "Again."

He lost the next one.

Later, when there were fewer people at the table, Bocchi came back with Kita to retrieve something from the room. Mau tapped the little rhythm against the edge of his cup.

He got it wrong.

Bocchi stopped, listened, and corrected him with two fingers against her own palm.

He tried again.

She nodded.

"That one."

Kita waited for her at the door. Bocchi caught up, glanced back once, and then they were gone.

Mau kept the rhythm for a while after that, quietly enough that Frieren did not have to raise her voice when she resumed telling him about the word in her book.

* * *

Milim was asleep before they reached the room.

This time she had begun drifting while Ori was still talking to her. Rimuru helped steer her around the corner, muttering that he was being promoted to furniture. Ori had kept the ribbon.

Mau followed them to the adjoining doorway and waited while Ori made sure the knot at the bedpost would hold. Frieren checked that there was enough length for Milim to turn without tangling herself.

"She'll say it counts as being up again," Ori said.

"You have the ruling," Mau reminded her.

Ori looked at Frieren.

"It stands," Frieren said.

Ori smiled, then came back for a hug before either of them could go. Mau held her until she let go. She reached for Frieren next.

"Tomorrow?" Ori asked.

"Tomorrow," Mau said.

She left the door open a little.

In their room, Frieren had put her book beside the bed with the place carefully marked. She sat down and began taking the ties from her hair. Mau undid the stubborn one she could not quite reach, then stayed behind her to separate the strands that had caught around it.

"You smell like wood," she said.

"It's better than the coffee."

She tilted her head, considering him.

"The coffee was this morning."

"Long day."

"Mm."

He finished with her hair and sat beside her. Through the floor came the softened sound of somebody moving a bench. A door closed farther along the passage. Someone laughed, then tried to laugh more quietly.

Frieren took his hand.

"Did you finish the game?"

"I won once."

"I saw."

"You were reading."

"You made it difficult."

He leaned against her, laughing, and she let his shoulder stay where it landed.

After a while she got into bed. He put out the light and found his place beside her in the dark. Her hair was loose against the pillow. He moved a strand away from her mouth before it could catch there again.

"Good day?" he asked.

"Yes."

She turned toward him.

"Yours?"

He thought of the tune. Of Kaneki putting his knees in the sun, of Ori winning an argument before breakfast, of the pause before Yuka realized he had finally won. He was going to tell her one of those things.

But he yawned instead.

Frieren smiled where he could barely see it.

"Tomorrow," she said.

He put his hand under her cheek.

"Tomorrow."
