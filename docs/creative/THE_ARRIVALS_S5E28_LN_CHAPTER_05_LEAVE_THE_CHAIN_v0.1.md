# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 20 — Leave The Chain

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 20
**Continuity state:** The four coordinate a limited fight with a kept retreat; Mau briefly manifests the common sentry at real reserve cost, without extracting a new shadow or restoring other powers

*When: Day 291, the second floor.*

Milim stepped into the crossing and waved.

"Over here!"

The chain stopped scraping.

For a moment the carrier stood with its back to her. Then the two figures in the stalls turned, and the heavy shoulders followed.

Milim looked pleased that her part was working.

She waited until the chain lifted.

"Now?"

"Now," Yuta said.

She went sideways instead of forward. The chain struck the place she had left, throwing chips from the paving. The carrier dragged it back with enough force to bend the nearest stall.

The smaller figures came through the gaps.

Yuka sent the dog toward the one on the ground. She did not ask her to catch the hooked blade. The dog cut across its path, drawing it into a turn as Yuka moved behind the end of the stall. When it committed to the wrong side, Yuta met it from the other.

His first cut took the weapon arm. The second separated the narrow neck joint. Both pieces struck the floor before the dog was allowed near them.

Mau kept watching the roof.

The other figure had stopped climbing. It was crouched above the crossing, where Milim would have to land if she kept moving around the chain.

"Above you. Don't come back yet."

Milim changed direction in the air. A blade passed below her boots.

The figure on the roof frame recoiled for another strike.

Mau reached for the smaller of the two shapes he had stored.

The sentry rose beside him, its long weapon clearing the ground. The drain began with it: slight, definite, belonging to the separate reserve Ciel had helped him distinguish.

"The frame," he told it. "Hook that leg. Pull down."

It obeyed.

The hooked end caught behind the climbing figure's knee. Mau felt the moment of resistance through an attention that was not quite a second pair of hands. He almost tried to haul with his own shoulders.

*Keep the instruction steady,* Ciel said privately.

He planted his feet instead.

The sentry pulled. Metal screamed against metal. The figure came off the frame sideways, too far from Milim to reach her, and struck the lane at Yuta's feet.

Yuta ended its movement before it could rise.

"Back," Mau told the sentry.

It folded away. The drain stopped; what he had used stayed used.

The carrier still held the middle of the lane.

Milim landed on the far side, outside the chain's broad arc. It turned after her. The hooks overhead began to move.

"It's pulling the top one," Yuka called.

Mau saw the line tighten. If Milim came straight toward them, the hanging chain would meet her halfway.

"Stay there."

She stayed.

Yuta was already moving along the stalls, keeping the supporting posts between himself and the carrier. Yuka called the dog to her, then let her sink back into shadow. Mau stepped back into the entrance so the two of them had an open route behind him.

The overhead chain snapped down between Milim and the others.

It caught nobody.

For a heartbeat the carrier had both arms extended, one hauling the chain from the ground and the other committed to the line above.

"Can I hit it now?" Milim asked.

Yuta reached the last post.

"Yes."

She crossed the empty space fast enough that Mau heard the impact before he understood her change of position. Both gauntlets struck the carrier's chest. It stumbled backward into the taut upper chain.

Yuta cut the exposed joint behind its knee.

The chain went slack. Milim caught the next swing against one gauntlet, shifted inside its reach, and drove the other hand under the broken breastplate.

The armour broke around the blow.

Mau waited until its last movement had stopped before he came out of the entrance.

Milim was standing among a great deal of bent metal, looking back at them.

"I left the chain."

Yuka began to laugh.

"You did."

"Everyone kept saying it."

Yuta checked her hands. She let him, still explaining why the second blow had needed to go upward. Mau checked Yuka next, and she turned his scraped hand over in retaliation.

The old mark had not opened. A new red line ran along the outside of his wrist, where the rough wall had caught it when he stepped back. He had not noticed until she touched it.

Yuka sat on the low stone beside him afterward, both feet planted while she drank. Calling the dog back had stopped the demand, not removed the effort already spent. Yuta waited until she had finished and her breathing was easy before they considered moving on.

"That one wasn't a monster," she said.

"I'm expanding my enemies."

She let go with a small, disapproving squeeze.

The objective for the second floor completed. Crystals settled where the carrier had fallen; no extraction outline remained beneath it. Mau put them in the bag after Yuta had checked the lane.

No new level appeared.

He looked at the unchanged four, then closed the page before he could start resenting a number for failing to applaud.

"Next room?" Milim asked.

Yuta looked at Mau. There was still time inside the agreed visit, if the next passage proved short enough to leave properly.

"We look," Mau said. "Then decide."
