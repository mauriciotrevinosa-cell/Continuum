# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 21 — The Way Back Through

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 21
**Continuity state:** The party uses observation and a physical route to clear the next passage, returns within two outside hours, and records Level5 with twenty unspent points; no transfer checkpoint is earned

*When: Day 291, the third floor and return before lunch.*

Floor three was quiet enough that Milim lowered her voice.

The passage opened onto a bridge over darkness. Nothing burned here. A row of standing lamps lit the crossing in strips, leaving long gaps between them.

Halfway across, a body lay on the paving.

It had the same empty joints as the guardians behind them. A sword lay beyond its hand.

"Dead?" Yuka asked.

Yuta did not answer immediately. He studied the body, then the distance from its hand to the sword.

"Not taking its word for it."

Yuka stayed beside the entrance this time, letting her own eyes follow the lines of the bridge. The dog remained stored. There was no reason to keep asking for effort merely because the next room was interesting.

Mau brought the map out again. Yuta had drawn their two turns with an economy that made his own square vestibule look insulting.

The new objective offered the far arch. Secure the passage.

There was no instruction to kill the thing in the middle.

"We don't have to go today," Yuta said. "We can go back with this much."

Milim examined the ceiling. There was none above the bridge, only the darkness continuing upward.

"It wants us to go to it."

"Probably," Mau said.

"Then why don't we make it come here?"

He looked at her.

She spread both hands. "You make me wait all the time."

Yuka's mouth twitched. Yuta looked down at the small pieces of stone by the entrance.

They discussed it before anyone threw one.

The first struck the sword and slid off the bridge. No movement.

The second landed beside the body.

Its fingers closed.

Yuka saw them first. "There."

The figure rose without reaching for the sword.

That was wrong enough for Mau to keep his feet behind the threshold.

It walked toward them with one hand empty at its side. The blade stayed where it had fallen, halfway across. Then, as the figure passed the nearest lamp, something thin caught the light between its wrist and the weapon.

"There's a line," Mau said. "Don't go between it and the sword."

Yuta moved to one side of the opening. Milim took the other. Yuka stepped back with the dog, leaving the middle clear.

The figure came faster.

At the last lamp its hand snapped forward. The sword lifted from the paving and followed the line, skimming the bridge at knee height.

Mau had room to step back. He took it.

The blade crossed the empty threshold and struck stone. Milim caught the figure from the side before it could reach the weapon. She drove it against the arch, keeping its right arm away from Yuta.

He severed the line.

The blade stopped dragging itself across the floor.

Yuka brought the dog up for one short lunge. She caught the figure's ankle long enough to unbalance it, then withdrew at Yuka's call as Yuta stepped in. His cut passed through the joint under the head.

Yuka let the white shape return to shadow at once and leaned against the stone while the last armour fell. Milim checked her before going to look at the bridge. She nodded, but accepted the bottle Yuta passed back.

Milim let the broken armour fall away from her gauntlet.

They waited. The sword did not move again.

"Now can we cross?" she asked.

"Let's look at the lamps first," Yuka said.

Milim sighed, but helped her look.

There were no more lines. They crossed in pairs without touching the rail. At the far arch the objective changed to complete, and the door beyond it opened onto a broad stair.

Mau's branch rose to level five. Twenty points waiting now.

He showed Yuka before closing it.

"Umaru will want to see," she said.

"Umaru will want to put me in a chair and tell me why every choice is wrong."

"Don't make one before she gets there."

He looked up the stair, toward the pale four above its first landing.

Yuta asked Ciel's time through him. They would still need the entire route back, not a convenient jump from the place where they had decided to stop.

Mau checked the bag, the pencil, the folded map. Two registered shadows still. He had not brought the sentry out again.

"Let's go home."

Milim put her head into the stairwell once, came back, and took the crystals from him so he could fold the map without dropping it.

The bridge felt longer when they crossed it in the other direction. At the market Yuka paused to show him the mark the chain had left in the paving. He drew it beside Yuta's neat line, then added a small arrow when she told him it looked like a potato.

They passed the empty first post and reached the vestibule with dust in their shoes.

Anko was waiting outside the return frame.

"Hour and forty-three," she said. "All four?"

"All four," Yuta answered.

Only when Mau had stepped out after Milim did the expedition list close. His page kept the cleared stages.

He showed Anko the crystals, then the map.

She turned it around, looked at his arrow, and asked what the potato was.

Yuka bent over laughing. Mau handed the bag to Yuta and went to find someone who would appreciate his contribution to cartography.

Frieren was at the table with Fern, studying the lantern's charge from their own outing.

She listened to the explanation. Asked him where the sword had been. Then put her finger on the drawing.

"You should make this wider. You said four people could stand there."

He sat down beside her.

"You're all very demanding."

"You brought paper," she said. "That was a good idea."

He found the pencil again.
