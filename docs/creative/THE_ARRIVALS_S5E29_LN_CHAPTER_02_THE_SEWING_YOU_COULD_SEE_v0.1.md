# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 22 — The Sewing You Could See

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** Coco and Echidna keep their binding appointment; Coco's mother chooses to join after shared cleaning, and a finished ordinary repair is enjoyed without new magic

*When: Day 291, early afternoon.*

The cloth in her hand was already dry.

Coco's mother rubbed once more at the edge of the table, noticed what she was doing, and folded it instead. Ram had taken the bucket to the next room. There was nothing left here that would not survive until tomorrow.

From the library came Coco's voice.

"It's shorter now."

"It was too long before," Echidna answered.

"I thought the extra would help."

"It helped it become a knot."

Her daughter made a small, frustrated sound.

She left the cloth with the bucket and went to the library.

Echidna's book lay open on a clean board, its loose leaf set safely apart. Coco had a needle and an expression of fierce concentration. Beside her sat a piece of folded scrap paper pierced with practice holes.

Her mother stopped at the door.

"May I?"

Coco looked up. "Yes. Come here."

It was pleasant, being invited with such certainty. She took the chair Coco pulled out, careful not to catch the trailing thread with her sleeve.

"We're practising first," Coco explained. "The paper in the book is softer. If you pull too hard, it tears instead of becoming tight."

"A warning addressed to both of us," Echidna said.

"I only did it once."

Echidna raised the practice fold. Two holes had become one long hole.

"Once was quite sufficient."

Coco's mother held out her hand for the damaged paper. Coco passed it over, then began another fold while Echidna showed her how to leave room for the thread to settle.

Her mother watched the needle go in and out.

For a while the wish to help was nearly indistinguishable from reaching. The thread had caught against the corner. She could see exactly where to lift it.

Coco stopped. Turned the paper. Found the snag herself.

Her mother put her hands in her lap.

"You can hold this side," Coco said a little later.

She did.

The second line of stitching sat flat. Coco ran a fingertip beside it without pressing on the holes.

"Is that enough?"

Echidna examined both sides.

"For the practice, yes. Now we need to find where the old thread failed."

They took longer over the book than the practice. The worn strand had not broken in the place Coco expected. Echidna separated the folded sections with a thin strip of card, showing them how the loose leaf had begun pulling on its neighbour.

"If I just fastened that one—"

"You would have a very secure page attached to another page about to come out."

Coco frowned, then moved the strip of card back to look again.

Her mother found herself leaning closer too.

They removed only what they had to. Coco put the old thread in a small dish rather than dropping it on the floor. Her mother held the board steady while Echidna guided the new line through the sound holes. Then Coco took over, slower than she had been with the practice fold.

Once the needle missed.

Nobody spoke while she backed it out and tried again.

When Mau appeared in the doorway, there was a thread between Coco's fingers and an answer beginning on his face.

Echidna looked at him.

He shut his mouth.

Coco's mother had to turn toward the board to hide a smile.

"Can I look?" he asked.

"From there," Coco said, entirely occupied.

He leaned against the doorframe. Watched her finish the stitch, thank her mother for holding it, and ask Echidna whether the tension was right.

The answer was yes.

Mau smiled at Coco when she finally looked up. He went away without improving anything.

The repaired section had to rest under the board before they handled it much. Echidna set a modest weight on top and put the dish of old thread out of harm's way.

Coco turned to her mother.

"Do you want to try one? The scrap, I mean."

She looked at the waiting needle.

"You'll have to show me where to start."

Coco moved her chair closer.

The first attempt was untidy. The second caught in precisely the place Coco's had caught. Her daughter waited while she found it, then laughed when she looked up with the freed thread between her fingers.

Later, Echidna lifted the weight and opened the book carefully. The leaf turned with its neighbours instead of falling into Coco's hand.

"You can see the sewing," Coco said.

"Yes."

"Carmilla asked. I thought it might disappear inside."

Echidna turned another page. "It needn't disappear to be a good repair."

Coco considered that, then asked her mother to hold their practice fold beside the book.

The stitches were not as even. They looked at the two lines together until Coco began pointing out which part they would do differently next time.
