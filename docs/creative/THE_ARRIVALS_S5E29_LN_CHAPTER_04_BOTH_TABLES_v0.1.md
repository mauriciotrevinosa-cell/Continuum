# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 24 — Both Tables

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** The seven-day gatherings both occur after dinner in their established separate spaces; ordinary humour, chosen confidences and Gojo's plans do not become shared private knowledge

*When: Day 291, evening; the roof and crackers cycle.*

Rimuru reached the roof first and objected to that fact until Yuta came through the hatch with the cups.

"I was promised assistance."

"You chose to carry the least," Mau said, following him.

"I chose to inspect the location."

"You live here."

"A diligent inspector revisits."

Mau sat beside him, far enough from the hatch to leave room for the next person. The roof ladder at the end of the east corridor had become warm under everyone's hands. A little of the day's heat remained in the boards beneath them.

Yuta passed the cups around.

Below, the evening sounds of the house came through the open windows. Someone was moving a bench. Someone else objected to the way it was being moved. Farther off, a bird kept beginning the same call and abandoning it.

Mau looked down into his cup.

"I think the bird's lost the words."

Rimuru listened.

"Happens to great performers."

Yuta made the mistake of laughing while he drank. Mau reached for the cup before it could fall, and Rimuru patted Yuta's back with considerably more ceremony than help.

"We're very good at this," he said.

"At what?" Yuta managed.

"Still deciding."

They stayed with the undecided thing for a while. Mau told them about the map. Yuta defended its first two turns and refused responsibility for the potato. Rimuru wanted to know whether the chain had looked like a potato before or after meeting Milim.

Mau had brought the page in his pocket.

He regretted this almost immediately.

Gojo came through the hatch while all three of them were looking at it.

"A private briefing?"

"Cartographic persecution," Mau said.

Gojo sat down to help.

The sky had begun losing colour by the time the page went away. Yuta stretched his legs and leaned back on his palms. Rimuru had found a comfortable angle against a folded cloth and was displaying no interest in inspecting anywhere else.

Gojo turned his cup once.

"If someone wanted an afternoon that didn't involve this house—"

"Sensible," Rimuru said.

"—without travelling for half of it."

Mau looked at him. "The river bend's quiet farther along the inside path."

"I know."

"Then you have a place."

"I'm considering improvements."

Yuta looked up. "To the river?"

Rimuru put a hand over his mouth. Mau stared into his cup until it was safe to speak.

"Ask her if she'd like to go," he said. "Before you improve the geography."

Gojo looked from one face to the next.

"An exceptional investment, raising pupils."

"I'm helping," Yuta said.

He sounded so sincere that Gojo laughed.

* * *

Frieren found Mai sitting on the floor beside Seiko's bed, already opening the crackers.

"You started."

"You're late."

"Mau had the map."

"A compelling excuse," Seiko said. "Sit down."

Maki moved her foot out of the way. Turbo Granny had taken a place she would later deny having wanted. Shoko sat near the window, invited by Seiko on their walk the day before.

Frieren accepted the plate Mai offered. There was a broken cracker on top, which she took before someone could pretend the pieces did not count.

"Did he get lost?" Maki asked.

"No. Yuta drew the turns."

Maki nodded, satisfied.

They talked about the boats first. Momo had brought the remains back past Seiko's door, and Jiji's explanation had continued long after anyone was willing to inspect the evidence. Mai wanted to know whether a drowned paper boat could qualify as a design choice.

"Depends how loudly you defend it," Shoko said.

"Then he has a fleet," Seiko replied.

Frieren took another cracker while they laughed.

The window was open. She could hear a low burst of voices from above, too indistinct to make into words. Mau's laugh was easier to find than the others.

Mai glanced toward the ceiling, then at her.

"What?" Frieren asked.

"Nothing."

"You have a face."

"So do you. It's been doing that since you came in."

Frieren looked down at the cracker in her hand. Shoko's cup hid very little of her expression.

"He came back before lunch," Frieren said.

"We noticed," Maki answered gently.

It was enough. Nobody asked her to turn it into a speech. Seiko asked Shoko whether Satoru had finally thought of somewhere to take her, and Shoko raised an eyebrow.

"He hasn't offered a where yet."

"Then don't do his work for him."

"I haven't."

Turbo Granny made a noise of contempt at the entire arrangement. Mai gave her a small broken cracker without looking, which did not improve the dignity of the protest.

Frieren stayed until the plate was empty.

When she left, the boys were coming down from the hatch with the cups. Mau stopped beside her, the folded map protruding from his pocket. Gojo went past them toward the stairs, and Yuta went to find Maki.

Rimuru looked at the couple blocking the passage.

"Some of us would like access to our own sleeping arrangements."

Frieren opened the east-room door for him with an expression of great generosity.

Mau waited until he had gone inside before taking her hand.

"How was it?"

"Good. Yours?"

"I don't think I'm drawing the next map."

"You should. I want to see if the potato improves."

He laughed again. Up close this time, where she did not have to listen for it through the window.
