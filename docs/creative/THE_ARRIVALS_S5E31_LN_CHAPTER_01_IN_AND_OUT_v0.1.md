# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 29 — In and Out

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 29
**Continuity state:** R25 + R26. Rimuru and Mau play a game and agree on a simple shared sign so either can go out without hiding it from the other (following the Sukuna-night lesson: the name first); then Gojo and Yuta join them to finish a real task (re-hanging the roof hatch); humour rotates; no trauma confession, no Ciel interface

*When: Day 297, afternoon. A wind came through in the night and loosened half the screen panels and the roof hatch. Wakana has fixed the panels. The hatch is on the list.*

Rimuru was losing at cards, and he was losing on purpose, and Mau knew it.

"You're doing the face," said Mau.

"I don't have a face. I'm a slime."

"You're in human shape."

"I'm in human shape *temporarily*," said Rimuru. "The face is borrowed." He put down a card. It was the wrong card. It was so obviously the wrong card that Mau looked at it for a long moment before picking it up.

They were on the bench under the ink tree, in the shade, with Yuka's cards between them on an upturned crate. Yuka had taught them both the game. Yuka was not here. Yuka was at the river with Umaru and Milim, and had left the cards with instructions, which Rimuru had immediately ignored.

"Why are you losing?" said Mau.

"I'm not losing. I'm investing in your confidence."

"Rimuru."

Rimuru put his cards face down on the crate.

He looked at the ink tree for a while. At the dark leaves, moving a little in the leftover wind. At the yard, where Wakana was carrying the last of the screen panels back to the workshop. At the roof, where the hatch hung crooked on one hinge.

"I was going to ask you something," he said. "And I wanted you to be winning when I asked."

Mau put his cards down too.

"Ask."

* * *

"The night with Sukuna," said Rimuru.

Mau did not say anything.

"I'm not going to go through all of it again. We did that. You said the name first, next time. Not when you've worked out why." Rimuru turned a card over on the crate, idly, and turned it back. "But I've been thinking about the other way round. When I go somewhere. When I'm in the office with the door shut, or out at the stake, or in the loft with the hatch closed. You don't always know where I am either."

"Ciel knows."

"Ciel knows where I am," said Rimuru. "That's not the same as you knowing." He looked at Mau. "And I don't want you to have to ask Ciel where I am. I want you to just know. The way I want to just know about you."

Mau looked at him for a long moment.

"Like a sign," he said slowly. "Something we both look at."

"Something stupid," said Rimuru. "Something so stupid nobody else will want to use it. So it's ours."

Mau thought about it.

"The closet door," he said. "In the east room. Under the loft. We both walk past it every day. You climb up through it every night."

"A peg," said Rimuru. "Two pegs. One for each of us. In, it hangs here. Out, it hangs there. And if you're out, you write where on a scrap and stick it on the peg. Just a word. *Tower. River. Stake.*" He paused. "And the name, if there's a name. Before the why."

"And if I forget?"

"You'll forget," said Rimuru. "I'll forget. That's why it's two pegs and not two promises. Pegs are easier to remember than promises." He picked up his cards again. "And whoever's in has the house. That's the other half. If I'm out, you've got it. If you're out, I've got it. Nobody has to ask."

Mau looked at the crooked hatch on the roof.

"That's very sensible," he said.

"I'm a very sensible slime."

"You've been losing at cards for twenty minutes so I'd be in a good mood."

"Sensible," said Rimuru, "is not the same as dignified."

Mau laughed.

He picked up his cards. Then he looked at them, and looked at Rimuru, and put down a card that was very obviously the wrong card.

Rimuru stared at it.

"What are you doing?"

"Investing in your confidence," said Mau.

* * *

Gojo found them twenty minutes later, still playing badly on purpose, both of them losing, neither of them able to finish a round.

He stood over the crate with his hands in his pockets and watched for a while.

"This is the worst game I've ever seen," he said.

"We're being kind to each other," said Rimuru.

"It's horrible. Stop it." Gojo looked up at the roof. "Fern says the hatch has to be back on before the roof night, and Wakana's busy with the panels, and Yuta's coming with the tools. So you two are going to stop being kind and come and hold things."

"Is that an order?" said Mau.

"It's a request," said Gojo, "from your teacher. With an implied lesson about doing something useful with your hands that isn't destroying things."

Mau and Rimuru looked at each other.

"He's very proud of that," said Rimuru.

"He's been practising it," said Mau.

"I can hear you," said Gojo.

* * *

The roof hatch was at the end of the east corridor, at the top of the short ladder, where the four of them went up on roof nights. The wind had wrenched it half off. One hinge had pulled clean out of the frame, screws and all. The other was bent. The hatch hung sideways, letting in a long bar of sun and a great deal of dust.

Yuta was already on the ladder with Wakana's tool roll over his shoulder. He looked down at them.

"I need someone to hold the hatch," he said. "And someone to hold the ladder. And someone to pass me screws."

"And someone to supervise," said Gojo.

"Nobody needs supervising," said Yuta.

"Then someone to watch," said Gojo, and sat down on the floor of the corridor with his back against the wall.

Rimuru held the ladder. Mau went up behind Yuta and held the hatch, which was heavier than it looked and kept trying to swing. Yuta worked on the frame, digging out the splintered wood where the hinge had torn free, very carefully, with a small chisel.

"You're good at this," said Mau.

"Maki made me fix the cupboard door in our room," said Yuta. "Three times. Until it stopped squeaking." He blew dust out of the hole. "She said if I could heal a person, I could heal a cupboard."

"Could you?"

"It took longer than a person," said Yuta. "Cupboards don't help."

From the floor of the corridor, Gojo said, "Screw."

Rimuru looked at him. "What?"

"He needs a screw. He's about to ask. Pass him a screw."

Rimuru found a screw in the tin and passed it up the ladder. Yuta took it without looking, and then looked at Gojo.

"How did you know?"

"I can see everything," said Gojo. "It's very boring. But occasionally useful for screws."

* * *

It took most of the afternoon.

The first hinge went back in badly and had to come out again. The second hinge was too bent to use and Yuta sent Rimuru down to the workshop for another, and Rimuru came back with two, because Wakana had said that whoever was doing it would probably break the first one. Yuta broke the first one.

Gojo did not help with anything. He sat on the floor and watched, and said *screw* at intervals, always just before Yuta needed one, and once said *left* when Mau's grip on the hatch started to slip, and once said *no* very firmly when Rimuru suggested he could just turn into a slime and hold the hatch from the inside.

"Why not?" said Rimuru.

"Because then Shoko would want to know why there's slime in the hinges," said Gojo, "and I would have to explain, and she would look at me."

"She looks at you anyway."

"She looks at me differently when there's slime involved."

Yuta laughed so suddenly that he nearly dropped the chisel. Mau caught it. Rimuru caught the ladder. Gojo did not move at all, but said, "Good catch," as if he had planned it.

When the hatch was finally back on, it swung open and closed without catching. Yuta opened and closed it four times, listening to it, the way Wakana listened to things he built.

"It squeaks," he said.

"It's fine," said Mau.

"It squeaks a little."

"Maki would make you fix it again," said Rimuru.

Yuta looked at the hinge for a long moment.

"Tomorrow," he said.

* * *

That evening, Mau went into the east room and looked at the closet door.

There were two pegs on it now. Small wooden pegs, side by side, at the height of his chest. Rimuru must have put them in while Mau was holding the hatch. One had a scrap of cloth tied round it, blue. The other had a scrap of cloth tied round it, grey.

Under each peg, in Rimuru's handwriting, small: *IN*. And a little further along: *OUT*.

The blue one hung on *IN*.

Mau looked at it for a long time. Then he took the grey one off the hook, and hung it on *IN* too, next to the blue.

From the loft above the closet, Rimuru's voice said, sleepily, "There you are."

"Here I am," said Mau.

"Both in."

"Both in."

"Good," said Rimuru. "Then nobody has the house. We both do."

Mau laughed and went to bed. Frieren, already in it, looked at the closet door, and the two pegs, and the two little scraps of cloth.

"What is that?" she said.

"A very stupid system," said Mau. "So nobody has to ask."

Frieren looked at the pegs for a while longer.

"Can I have one?" she said.

Mau stopped.

From the loft, there was a long pause.

"I'll make a third peg," said Rimuru. "Tomorrow. Green."
