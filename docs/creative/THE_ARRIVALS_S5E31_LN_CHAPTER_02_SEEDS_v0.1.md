# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 30 — Seeds

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 30
**Continuity state:** R32. Hollis brings a twist of seed from a farm near Fallow, possibly a relative of the plant Mau is looking for; Ciel compares it to the reference she observed without certifying it; Mau and Shoko plant the first seeds in the box from Day 266; Anko keeps her comic grievance and has narrowed her request to three options; not a finished gift, no instant growth

*When: Day 298, morning. Hollis's cart came up the road at dawn with Brenna and Cobb. It is the first run since the gate moved inside the boundary.*

Hollis did not unload the cart first. He came straight to Mau with his hat in one hand and something small in the other.

"I said I'd keep looking," he said.

He held it out. A twist of brown paper, folded over at the top and tied with string. Small enough to sit in his palm with room to spare.

Mau looked at it.

"Is that—"

"I don't know what it is," said Hollis. "That's the honest answer, and you'd know if I gave you another one." He turned the twist over in his fingers. "There's a farm past Fallow. Up a valley. An old man there grows a leaf on the edge of his field for himself. Dries it in his barn. Smokes it in a pipe in the evenings, on his step." He shrugged. "He says his father grew it, and his father's father. He doesn't know where it came from before that. He didn't want to sell. I traded him a lamp chimney."

"One of Wakana's?"

"One of the good ones," said Hollis. "He hadn't had a whole chimney in two winters." He held out the twist again. "Seed. From last year's plants. He said they'd sprout if I kept them dry, and I kept them dry."

Mau took it.

It weighed almost nothing. He could feel the seeds inside, through the paper, very small, like grit.

"I don't know if it's the right plant," said Hollis. "I don't know what the right plant is. I only know what you described, and what the doctor's got behind her ear. And the old man's leaf smelled like something somebody might smoke on a step in the evening." He put his hat back on. "That's as close as I could get."

Mau looked up at him.

"That's very close," he said. "Thank you."

Hollis looked embarrassed.

"Don't thank me yet," he said. "Thank me if it grows."

* * *

He took it to the clinic.

Shoko was finishing her morning list. She looked up when he came in with the twist of paper held carefully in both hands, and her face did something very small and very quick.

"Hollis found something," said Mau. "Maybe."

He put it on the desk between them. Shoko looked at it for a long time without touching it.

"Maybe," she said.

"He doesn't know if it's the right plant. An old man near Fallow grows it for his pipe." Mau paused. "I'd like to ask Ciel to compare. With the one you've got. If you'll let her look again."

Shoko took the cigarette from behind her ear.

She did it slowly. She held it for a moment between two fingers, the way she always held it, and then she laid it on the desk beside the twist of paper, very carefully, the way you lay down something you have carried a very long way.

"Go on," she said.

Mau untied the string. He opened the twist, just enough to see. Small brown seeds, dozens of them, round and dull, nothing remarkable at all.

*I can compare what I can observe,* said Ciel. *I cannot compare a seed to a leaf. They are different stages of a thing. I can tell you whether the dried leaf in her cigarette and the dried leaf this seed would become share structure. I cannot tell you that until the seed becomes a leaf.*

Mau said it aloud.

Shoko almost smiled.

"So we have to grow it to find out if it's worth growing."

"Yes."

"That's gardening," said Shoko. "I've heard about it." She picked up the cigarette again and put it back behind her ear. "Ciel can look again when there's a leaf. Not before. I'm not having you take it apart to compare it with a seed."

"I wasn't going to."

"I know," said Shoko. "I'm saying it anyway."

* * *

The box was where he had left it, on the bench outside the clinic, where Wakana had built it out of offcuts on Day 266. A shallow frame with holes in the bottom and a tray underneath. It had been empty for more than a month. Mau had filled it with soil a fortnight ago, from the edge of the south field, the part that had gone green again, because it seemed right somehow, and then he had not had anything to put in it.

He looked at it now. Soil. Damp. Waiting.

"How many?" said Shoko.

"I don't know." He looked at the twist. "Not all of them. In case it's wrong. In case we do it wrong."

"Half," said Shoko. "Keep half dry. If these die, we try again with the others, and we'll know what we did."

Mau looked at her.

"That's very sensible," he said.

"I'm a doctor. Sensible is most of the job." She held out her hand. "Give me some. You did the box. I should do some of the planting."

He tipped a few seeds into her palm.

They planted them together, on the bench outside the clinic, in the morning sun. Not deep. Daphne had told Mau, when he asked her last week, that small seeds went near the top, *barely covered, or they get lost looking for the light*. So they pressed each one into the soil with a fingertip, a little apart, and drew the soil over them, very lightly, and Mau poured water from a cup, slowly, so it would not wash them away.

Then they sat on the bench and looked at the box.

Nothing happened. Of course nothing happened.

"It'll take weeks," said Shoko. "If it takes at all."

"I know."

"You'll come and look at it every morning."

"Probably."

"You'll want it to grow faster."

"Definitely."

Shoko looked at him sideways.

"Don't you dare do anything to it," she said. "No spells. No shadows. No asking Ciel to *help*. It grows like a plant or it doesn't grow."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were thinking about it."

Mau did not deny it.

* * *

Anko found them there.

She came round the corner of the clinic with her notebook, saw the two of them sitting side by side on the bench looking at a box of dirt, and stopped.

She looked at the box. Then at Mau. Then at Shoko. Then at the box again.

"You planted it," she said.

"Some of it," said Mau.

"The garden."

"It's a box."

"The garden," said Anko, "for Shoko." She opened her notebook and wrote something with great force. "I'd like it on the record that I've been here longer."

"It's on the record," said Shoko. "You've told everyone."

"I've narrowed it down," said Anko.

Mau looked at her.

"What I want," said Anko. "For my years of service. You said I could think about it. I've thought about it." She turned a page. "Three options."

"What are they?"

Anko closed the notebook.

"I'm not telling you yet," she said. "I'm letting you be nervous."

"I've been nervous since Day 266."

"Good," said Anko. "It's working." She looked at the box one more time. "When it sprouts, I'll decide."

"That could be weeks."

"Then you'll be nervous for weeks," said Anko, and walked away, writing.

Shoko watched her go. Then she put her hand over her mouth, and her shoulders shook, and she laughed. Not loudly. But it went on for a long time.

Mau watched her laugh.

He thought about the morning she came, Day 145, with one bag and a cigarette and a face that did not seem to know how to do anything except count. He thought about her standing on the step of Arrival House while a dead man saw his kid. He thought about her saying *it'd work*, at the long table, with Gojo's frosted cup in her hand.

"What?" said Shoko, when she had stopped.

"Nothing," said Mau. "You laugh more now."

Shoko looked at him for a long moment.

Then she looked at the box of dirt on the bench in front of them, with its seeds pressed in very lightly, waiting to find the light.

"Don't tell anyone," she said.

* * *

Hollis came by before he left, with the cart loaded and Brenna and Cobb waiting at the end of the road.

He looked at the box on the bench.

"You planted it."

"Half," said Mau. "Shoko said keep half dry. In case."

"She's a sensible woman," said Hollis. He looked at the soil. "The old man said it comes up in about ten days. If it likes you." He put his hat on. "I'll be back in two weeks. I'll look."

"Will you tell the old man? If it grows?"

Hollis thought about it.

"I'll tell him," he said. "He'll pretend he doesn't care." He smiled a little. "He'll care."

He went down the road to his cart. Cobb, at the mule's head, lifted one hand to Mau. Mau lifted his back.

Behind him, on the bench outside the clinic, the box of soil sat in the sun, doing nothing at all, the way seeds do before anything happens.
