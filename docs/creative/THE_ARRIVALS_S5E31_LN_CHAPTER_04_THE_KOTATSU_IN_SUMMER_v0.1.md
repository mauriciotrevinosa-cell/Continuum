# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 32 — The Kotatsu in Summer

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 32
**Continuity state:** The five (Hakari, Karane, Shizuka, Nano, Kusuri) have an afternoon of their own choosing in their room; each contributes differently; their inter-girl threads stay as observation, with no new ship conceded (DH-055); they remember the S2 festival at the inn (S2E22) and wonder whether there will be one this summer, as a seed only; Rentarou is not raised

*When: Day 300, afternoon. Too hot to do anything outside. The five's room has the shutters half closed and the kotatsu with its blanket folded away for summer.*

The kotatsu had no blanket on it in summer, which Karane said made it just a table, and Hakari said made it a summer kotatsu, which was different, and Nano said was a table with a heater in it that nobody was using, which was the correct answer.

They were all sitting round it anyway.

The room was the one with five beds and no walls, at the end of the south side, past Marin and Wakana. The shutters were half closed against the heat, so the light came in in long gold bars and lay across the floor and the low table and the five of them. It was the hottest afternoon of the summer so far. Nobody had said so, because saying so would make it hotter.

Kusuri was lying flat on her back on the floor with a damp cloth over her face.

"I'm conducting an experiment," she said, from under the cloth.

"You're lying down," said Karane.

"I'm lying down *scientifically*."

Shizuka, at the end of the table, opened her book. It was the small book she carried everywhere, the one she spoke through, and she turned to a page and held it up so the others could see. It said, in her neat handwriting, *It is very hot.*

"We know," said Karane. "We're all here. We're all hot."

Shizuka turned the page. *I am saying it so it is written down.*

Nano, beside her, nodded once. "That is a reasonable archival practice."

Shizuka turned another page. *Thank you.*

Nano nodded again. She did not say anything else, but she moved her cup a little to the left so that Shizuka had more room for the book.

Hakari saw that. Hakari saw most things. She smiled at her own cup and did not say anything about it either.

* * *

It was Hakari who brought it up.

She had been quiet for a long time, which was unusual for Hakari, who was not quiet. She had been looking out through the gap in the shutters at the yard, where the fort had set up the Summer Annexe under the ink tree and twenty small people were lying in the shade doing nothing with great dedication.

"Do you remember the festival?" she said.

Karane looked up.

"At the inn," said Hakari. "Back at the beginning. Before—" She stopped. They all knew before what. "Before everything. When the outside wasn't ready to celebrate with us, so we celebrated each other."

"I remember," said Karane. She said it gruffly, the way Karane said everything that mattered. "Wakana made banners out of the repaired fabric."

"They were beautiful."

"They were *patched*."

"They were beautiful *and* patched," said Hakari.

Kusuri took the cloth off her face.

"Coco and Qifrey made the little lights," she said. "The ones that floated. Only where Rimuru approved." She stared at the ceiling. "Momo ignored several approvals."

"Umaru got removed from the snack table," said Nano.

"For quality control," said Hakari.

"For eating the quality," said Nano.

Shizuka held up her book. *I remember the music. Bocchi played in the corner. Kita decorated it. Bocchi did not look at the crowd once.*

"Because Kita was standing close enough that she forgot there was a crowd," said Hakari softly. "I saw."

Kusuri sat up.

"There weren't any stalls," she said. "I wanted to do a stall. I had an idea. Little folded papers, a different herb in each, and people have to guess what it does." Her eyes were very bright. "There wasn't time to ask. And then afterward there wasn't—" She waved a hand. "Afterward."

"That sounds like a very dangerous stall," said Karane.

"It sounds like *data*."

Hakari laughed. Then Karane. Then even Nano made a small sound that might have been a laugh, and looked surprised at herself.

When it was quiet again, Hakari looked back out through the gap in the shutters.

"Do you think there'll be one this year?" she said.

Nobody answered at once.

"There were a lot of things after," said Karane slowly. "After the festival. Sukuna. The bad season. The Trial." She did not look at anyone. "And this year there's the tower, and the gate, and the red week, and the thing in the woods, and—" She stopped. "Nobody's talked about a festival."

"Nobody talked about it back then either," said Hakari. "Until someone did."

Kusuri sat up again.

"I want to run the stall," she said. "This time."

They all looked at her.

"The fortune one," said Kusuri. "With the herbs. I've got much better herbs now. Maomao's garden. Daphne's field." Her eyes were very bright. "I could do *twelve* different papers. Nobody would know which one they'd get."

"Nobody would be safe," said Karane.

"That's the *fun*."

Shizuka was writing in her book. She held it up. *If there is a festival, I would like to help with the lights. I liked the lights.*

Nano looked at the page for a long moment.

"I would also help with the lights," she said. "If help is required. With the lights."

Shizuka looked at her.

Then she turned to a fresh page, very slowly, and wrote one word, and held it up. Just to Nano. Not to the others.

Hakari could not see what it said. She did not try to. She looked out of the window again, smiling, and let it be theirs.

* * *

"We should ask," said Karane, after a while.

"Ask who?" said Hakari.

"Fern. Rimuru. Whoever decides things." Karane folded her arms. "Not now. It's too hot to ask anything now. But at some point. If nobody else does." She scowled at the table. "I'm not saying I want a festival. I'm saying if there's going to be one, someone should make sure the banners are a good colour this time."

Hakari stared at her.

"You liked the banners," she said.

"I didn't say that."

"You *liked* the banners."

"I said someone should make sure they're a good colour," said Karane. "That's all I said." She was going red. "It's a quality control issue."

Hakari reached across the summer kotatsu and took Karane's hand.

Karane went redder. She did not take her hand away.

Kusuri, flat on her back again with the cloth over her face, said, "I can see that through the cloth, you know. The cloth is very thin."

"Then stop looking," said Karane.

"I'm lying down scientifically. I see everything."

Shizuka held up her book. *Further observation required.*

Nano read it. Then, very seriously, she took out the little notebook she kept for numbers, and opened it to a page near the back, and wrote something down. Shizuka leaned over to look. Nano tilted the notebook so she could see.

It said, in Nano's precise small hand, under a date: *Further observation. (N.)*

Shizuka looked at it for a long time.

Then she took the pencil out of Nano's fingers, and under it, in her own neat writing, added: *(S.)*

* * *

They did not go anywhere that afternoon. It was too hot. They sat round the summer kotatsu with the shutters half closed and talked about the festival that might or might not happen, and what they would wear, and whether Kusuri should be allowed anywhere near the herbs, and whether Bocchi would let anyone stand close enough this time.

At one point Momo knocked and put her head round the door with a jug of water from the cold jar, and found all five of them lying on the floor in a star shape with their feet under the kotatsu, arguing about lights.

"What are you doing?" said Momo.

"Planning," said Hakari.

"Planning what?"

The five looked at each other.

"Nothing," said Karane. "Yet."

Momo looked at them for a long moment. Then she put the jug down on the kotatsu, and went away, and they heard her laughing all the way down the corridor.

When the light went gold and then orange through the shutters, Kusuri sat up and took the cloth off her face.

"I've concluded the experiment," she said.

"What was the result?" said Nano.

Kusuri looked round at the four of them, lying on the floor round the summer kotatsu, with the empty jug and the half-closed shutters and the orange light.

"It was a good afternoon," she said. "Statistically."

Nobody argued.
