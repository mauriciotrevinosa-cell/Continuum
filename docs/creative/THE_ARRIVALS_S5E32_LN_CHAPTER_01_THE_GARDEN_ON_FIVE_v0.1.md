# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 33 — The Garden on Five

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 33
**Continuity state:** Fourth tower visit: Mau (owner) with Kaneki, Milim and Yuka; floor 5 is an overgrown terrace; clearing it earns the first secured transfer point (later visits can begin at floor 5 instead of walking floors 1–4); the transfer point holds only the owner's progress; level and points rise in Mau's branch only; no extraction

*When: Day 302, morning. Two and a half hours agreed outside, because of the walk. Yuka has been promised her turn for a week and has packed more rope than anyone has ever needed.*

Yuka had packed the rope again.

Not all of it. She had learned. But a long coil over one shoulder, and a shorter one at her belt, and a look on her face that dared anyone to say anything.

Nobody said anything. Yuta, standing by the screen with Maki to see them off, looked at the rope and then very carefully at the sky.

"She's going to need it," said Maki, to nobody.

"She's going to need *some* of it," said Yuta.

"She heard that," said Yuka.

Milim floated beside her, a hand's breadth above the dry ground, holding a parcel of food that was not hers. Kaneki stood a little apart with his own small pack, listening to something, the way he always seemed to be listening to something.

Mau took out the key.

"Four floors to walk," he said. "Then the stair to five. Two and a half hours. If we can't get back from five in time, we don't go up."

"We'll go up," said Milim.

"We'll see," said Mau.

"That means yes," Milim told Yuka. "When he says *we'll see* like that, it means yes."

Yuka looked at Mau.

Mau looked at the frame.

"It means we'll see," he said.

* * *

They walked the four floors.

The burning street. The market with its chain marks. The bridge with its strips of light and the place where the sword had lain. And down the stair into the flooded platform, where the row of lamps Frieren had lit were still lit, all of them, soft and blue on the black water.

Yuka stopped at the bottom of the stair and stared.

"Frieren did this?"

"From the far end first," said Mau. "So the thing in the water had to come into the light."

Yuka looked at the long row of blue lamps for a long time.

"She said she'd teach me," she said.

"She will."

"She said next time I come." Yuka scowled. "This is a time I came. She isn't here."

"Then next time you both come," said Mau.

Yuka thought about it, and seemed to decide that was acceptable, and walked on through the shallow water with her rope over her shoulder and her eyes on the lamps.

Kaneki stopped at the channel. He stood at the edge of the black water for a moment, very still.

"Nothing," he said. "It's empty. Whatever was in there is gone."

"Frieren killed it."

"I know," said Kaneki. "I can hear that it's gone. It's very quiet where it used to be." He looked at the water a moment longer, then turned away.

At the far end of the platform, past the place where the first blue lamp had always burned, the stair went up. The pale five on the wall.

* * *

The fifth floor had plants.

That was the first strange thing. Every floor so far had been stone and fire and metal and water, a ruined city with nothing alive in it but the things that tried to kill them. Five was green.

They came up the stair onto a wide flat terrace, open on all sides, with low broken walls round its edge and nothing beyond them but grey mist. And the whole terrace was overgrown. Long grass, waist-high on Yuka. Tangles of something like briar, dark green, with long thorns. A tree, a real tree, growing up out of a crack in the paving in the middle of the terrace, bent and twisted, with pale flowers on it. And in the long grass, broken things: benches, a fountain with no water, a row of tall pots with their plants long since escaped.

"A garden," said Yuka. "Somebody's roof garden."

"Nobody's been here a long time," said Kaneki.

The notice came up.

*Floor 5 of 100.*

*Objective: reach the seal.*

Below it, a new line Mau had not seen before, in a different colour.

*Transfer point. Owner progress may be secured here.*

He read it twice. Then he showed them.

"What does it mean?" said Milim.

"I think," said Mau slowly, "it means if we reach the seal, next time we don't have to walk. We start here. From five."

Yuka's eyes went very wide.

"No more burning street?"

"No more burning street."

"No more chain market?"

"Not unless we want it."

"No more *bridge*," said Milim, who had not liked the bridge.

Mau looked at the map. The seal was marked at the far side of the terrace, past the tree, past a great deal of grass and briar. A small round mark.

"Kaneki," he said. "Can you hear anything?"

Kaneki was already listening. He stood at the top of the stair with his eyes half closed and his head a little on one side.

"Yes," he said quietly. "In the grass. Low. A lot of them. Small." He opened his eyes. "Moving when we move."

* * *

They were dogs. Or they were the idea of dogs, made out of thorn.

They came out of the long grass when Mau took his first step onto the terrace, low and fast and dark green, with briar for fur and thorns for teeth and eyes like the pale flowers on the tree. Six. Eight. More, in the grass behind, waiting.

Milim went for the first one so fast that Mau did not see her move.

"Milim—"

"I've got them!"

She had. She hit the first one and it came apart in a spray of green and thorns. She hit the second. The third went past her, low, through the long grass, straight at Yuka.

Yuka did not run.

She stepped back, one step, and her hand went to the rope at her belt, and the short coil came off it in one movement and went out low across the grass, like a whip, and caught the thorn-dog round the front legs.

It went down in a tangle.

Kaneki was there before it could get up. He did not use anything. He put his boot on it, hard, where the neck would be, and it came apart under his heel.

Yuka stared at the rope in her hand.

"It worked," she said. "I didn't know if it would work."

"It worked," said Kaneki.

"I've been practising. On the fence posts. Every evening." She looked at Mau, fierce and astonished. "Grandpa said I was wasting rope."

"Grandpa was wrong," said Mau.

"Tell him."

"I'll tell him."

More were coming out of the grass.

* * *

It was not a hard fight, after that. It was a long one.

The thorn-dogs kept coming out of the grass in twos and threes, never all at once, and Milim took most of them, laughing, and Yuka took the ones that got past with the rope, and Kaneki finished the ones Yuka caught. Mau stayed in the middle with his back to the tree and watched the grass, and called out when he saw movement, and did not use anything at all.

"You're not doing anything," said Milim, between dogs.

"I'm watching."

"That's not *doing*."

"It's my job," said Mau. "Today."

Milim considered this while hitting a dog.

"That's a boring job," she said.

"It's a very important boring job."

"Gojo says that."

"Gojo's right," said Mau, and pointed. "Left. Two."

Milim went left.

After the eleventh dog, the grass went quiet.

They waited. Kaneki listened for a long time, with his eyes closed and his head on one side. Then he opened his eyes.

"That's all," he said. "There's nothing else moving. Just wind."

"There isn't any wind," said Yuka.

"Then just grass," said Kaneki.

* * *

The seal was set into the paving at the far side of the terrace, past the twisted tree, in a little clearing where the grass did not grow. A round plate of dark stone, as wide as Mau's arm was long, with a pattern cut into it that was almost the same as the edge of the key's frame.

Mau stood in front of it.

He looked at the others. Yuka, with grass seeds in her hair and her rope coiled again over her shoulder. Milim, covered in small green scratches she had not noticed. Kaneki, standing a little apart, listening to the quiet.

"Ready?" said Mau.

"Do it," said Yuka.

He stepped onto the seal.

The pattern lit under his feet, pale, the same colour as the frame. The notice came up.

*Transfer point secured. Owner progress: Floor 5.*

*Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1 or Floor 5.*

Below it, his level. Seven. Thirty points, still unspent. He showed them.

"Thirty," said Yuka. "Umaru's going to need a bigger page."

"Umaru's going to need a wall," said Mau.

Milim was looking at the seal under his feet.

"So next time," she said slowly, "we come straight here."

"If we want."

"No bridge."

"No bridge."

Milim looked so happy that Mau had to look at the tree instead.

* * *

They did not go further. The time was good, but not good enough to try six and still walk home from five. Mau checked twice.

And they did not have to walk home.

Beside the seal, where the grass had been, a small frame had appeared. The same dark frame as the one in the screened clearing at home, only smaller. Through it, faint, Mau could see the dry ground and the screen panels and, very blurred, the shape of Anko standing by her board.

"It goes straight back," said Kaneki.

"From five," said Mau. "To home."

Yuka looked at the small frame. Then back across the terrace, at the long grass and the briar and the twisted tree with its pale flowers.

"Can I take a flower?" she said. "For Grandma. To show her the garden."

Mau looked at the tree.

"Ask the tree," he said.

Yuka went to the tree. She stood under it for a moment. Then she reached up and, very carefully, took one pale flower from the lowest branch, and held it in both hands.

Nothing happened. The tree did not mind.

"Thank you," she said to it, quietly, and came back.

* * *

They came out at an hour and twelve minutes. Anko looked at the watch three times.

"That's fast," she said.

"There's a door on five," said Mau. "Straight back."

Anko's pen stopped.

"A door," she said.

"A transfer point. We can start from five now."

Anko looked at him for a long moment. Then she wrote it down, very carefully, and underlined it twice, and then, after a moment, wrote something else in the margin that Mau could not read.

Yuta and Maki were still by the screen. They had not left.

Yuka walked straight past Mau and Milim and Kaneki to her grandmother, and held out the pale flower in both hands.

"From the garden," she said. "On five. There's a tree."

Maki looked at the flower.

She took it. She held it for a long moment, without saying anything.

"And Grandpa," said Yuka, turning to Yuta. "I used the rope. On the dogs. It worked. Mau said to tell you that you were wrong."

Yuta looked at Mau.

"I did say that," said Mau.

Yuta looked at his granddaughter, with grass seeds in her hair and her rope over her shoulder and her face fierce and bright.

"I was wrong," he said.

Yuka's face did something very complicated, and then she put her head down and walked very fast toward the house so that nobody would see what it was doing.

Maki watched her go. Then she looked at the flower in her hand.

"She practised every evening," said Maki quietly. "On the fence posts. I watched her from the window."

"You didn't tell her," said Yuta.

"She didn't want anyone to know." Maki put the flower behind her ear, very carefully, the way Shoko wore her cigarette. "Now everyone knows."
