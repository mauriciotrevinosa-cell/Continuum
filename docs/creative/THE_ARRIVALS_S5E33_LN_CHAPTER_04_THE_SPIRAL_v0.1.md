# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 40 — The Spiral

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 40
**Continuity state:** Fifth tower visit, the first to start from the floor 5 transfer point: Mau (owner) with Gojo, Yuta and Milim; floor 6 is a descending spiral ramp with a counterweight puzzle; Gojo's small Blue comes more easily inside the tower than outside, still small and with no new technique; level and points rise in Mau's branch only; no extraction

*When: Day 309, morning. Two hours agreed outside. Anko has added a new column to her board: START FROM.*

Anko's board had a new column. It said START FROM, and under it, in her firm hand, a number.

*5.*

"You're sure?" said Anko.

"We're sure," said Mau.

"The return frame's on five. If you go down to six and something goes wrong, you walk back up to five. Not to one." She tapped the board. "That's shorter. That's still walking."

"We know."

Anko looked at the four of them. At Mau with the key. At Yuta with his sword case over his shoulder. At Milim, floating, delighted, because there would be no bridge. At Gojo, who had pushed his blindfold up and was looking at the dark frame with an expression of great interest.

"Your teacher's coming," said Anko, to Mau.

"He asked."

"He asked very loudly," said Yuta.

Gojo did not look round. "I want to see what happens to my pull in there," he said. "Last time it came more easily. Once. I want to know if that was a fluke." He paused. "And Shoko's busy with the box."

"The box doesn't need her," said Mau.

"The box doesn't," Gojo agreed. "She just likes standing next to it."

Anko wrote *Gojo (research)* in the margin, and turned the big glass.

* * *

They came out on the terrace.

Mau had not been sure it would work. He had read the notice, *Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1 or Floor 5*, and chosen five, and stepped through the frame with the key in his hand. And there was no tiled vestibule, no burning street. There was the overgrown terrace, waist-high grass, the twisted tree with its pale flowers, the dark stone seal in its little clearing. Grey mist beyond the broken walls.

Milim did a full turn in the air.

"No bridge!"

"No bridge," said Mau.

Yuta was looking at the tree. One branch, the lowest, had a small bare place where a flower had been taken.

"Yuka," he said quietly.

"She asked it first," said Mau.

Yuta looked at the bare place a moment longer. Then he turned away, and Mau saw that he was smiling.

The stair down to six was on the far side of the seal. The pale six was painted on the wall at the top of it. It did not go down very far. It opened, almost at once, onto a ramp.

* * *

The ramp went round and round and down.

A spiral. Wide enough for a cart, or something like a cart. Concrete, cracked, with painted lines on it that had faded to ghosts. On the outer side, a low wall, and beyond it a long drop into darkness, round and round, down the middle of the spiral, further than any of them could see. On the inner side, a wall with numbers painted on it at intervals, and arrows, and signs with writing on them that was almost words.

Gojo stopped at the first sign.

"Parking," he said.

Mau looked at him.

"It's a car park," said Gojo. "For cars. Like carts, without horses." He was looking at the faded paint. "That part's numbers and letters I know. B2. Exit. An arrow." He pointed at the larger writing beside it. "That part isn't. It's not a script I can read." He looked down the spiral. "Frieren's going to want to draw all of this."

"But you know what a car park is," said Yuta.

"I've walked through plenty," said Gojo. He was quiet for a moment, looking at the sign. "It's a world with cars. Like mine. The writing isn't mine."

Mau thought of Senku and the traffic signal. *One lamp is one lamp.* He did not say anything. But he took out the folded paper Frieren had given him, the page headed *Things in the tower Senku knows*, and under it, in his bad handwriting, he wrote: *Car park (Gojo). Numbers and some letters he knows; the main writing he can't read.*

The notice came up.

*Floor 6 of 100.*

*Objective: bring up the gate.*

Below it, a small map. The spiral, going down. And at the bottom, a square. And a line from the square, going back up the middle of the spiral, to a point near the top.

"Gate," said Milim. "Where's the gate?"

They found out at the bottom.

* * *

It took them a long time to walk down. The spiral went round eleven times. Mau counted. Things came at them twice on the way: low, quick shapes with too many wheels instead of legs, rolling up the ramp in pairs, and Milim met them both times with enormous satisfaction and very little need for anyone else. Yuta stood behind her with his sword and did not have to use it. Gojo walked at the back with his hands in his pockets and did not have to do anything at all.

At the bottom, in a wide concrete room, there was the gate.

It was not a gate in a wall. It was a great metal shutter, the width of the room, standing up out of a slot in the floor, at the top of the long shaft that went up through the middle of the spiral. It had been lowered. It sat in its slot, closed, and on the other side of it, Mau could see another ramp, going up and out, to somewhere that had light.

Beside the shutter, on the wall, a big iron wheel. And above it, going up into the dark of the shaft, a heavy chain. And at the top of the chain, very far up, so far that it was only a shape, something hanging.

"A counterweight," said Yuta. "Somewhere up there. You turn the wheel, the weight comes down, the shutter comes up."

Milim took hold of the wheel and pulled.

Nothing happened. The wheel did not move at all.

Milim pulled harder. The wall creaked. The wheel did not move.

"It's stuck," said Milim. She looked offended.

Gojo had come up beside her. He was not looking at the wheel. He was looking up the shaft, at the shape hanging at the top of the chain.

"It's not stuck," he said. "The weight's caught. Up there. On something. It's hanging crooked." He tilted his head. "If Milim pulls any harder, she'll pull the wheel out of the wall and the weight will come down the shaft all at once, and the shutter will come up and then slam back down, and probably on someone." He paused. "Probably on Milim."

Milim let go of the wheel.

"Then what?" she said.

Gojo looked up the shaft for a long moment.

Then he took one hand out of his pocket.

* * *

The Blue came up over his palm, small. The size of a marble. The same size it had been in the yard with Senku and the wooden pieces. But Mau, watching, saw Gojo's face change as it formed. Very slightly. The face of a man who had braced for a weight and found it lighter than he expected.

"Huh," said Gojo, very quietly.

He lifted his hand, slowly, toward the shaft. The marble of blue went up into the dark. Not fast. Steadily. Up and up, past the chain, toward the shape hanging crooked at the top.

"I'm not going to pull the weight," said Gojo. His voice was very calm. Very focused. Not his usual voice. "I can't. It's too big. I'm going to pull the thing it's caught on. Just a little. Just to the side." He was looking up at the dark with his bare eyes. "Just enough."

Mau did not breathe.

Far up in the dark, something scraped.

The chain shivered. Then it went taut. Then, very slowly, the iron wheel on the wall began to turn on its own, as the weight at the top of the shaft came free and began to sink, steadily, the way it was meant to.

The shutter rose.

It came up out of its slot in the floor with a long deep groan, foot by foot, until it was high enough to walk under. And beyond it, the ramp going up and out, toward the light.

Gojo closed his hand. The marble went out.

He stood very still, looking at his own palm.

"That came easier," he said. "Again. Easier than outside." He flexed his fingers. "Not bigger. Easier. Like the air in here doesn't argue as much."

Yuta was looking at him.

"Is that good?"

"I don't know yet," said Gojo. He looked up the shaft. "Senku will want numbers. Shoko will want to know what it costs." He put his hand back in his pocket. "I want to know why."

The notice came up.

*Gate raised. Route to Floor 7 opened.*

Level eight. Thirty-five points, unspent. Mau showed them.

"Thirty-five," said Milim. "That's more than Yuka can count on her fingers."

"Yuka can count very high," said Yuta.

"Not on her *fingers*."

* * *

They did not go up the ramp to seven. They had walked down eleven turns of spiral and would have to walk back up eleven turns to the seal on five, and the time was good but not that good. Mau checked twice.

At the top of the spiral, on the terrace, by the seal, Gojo stopped.

He looked back down the stair toward six. Then at the small return frame beside the seal, with home faintly visible through it.

"Next time," he said, "I want to try two marbles."

"Ask Shoko first," said Mau.

"I always ask Shoko first now," said Gojo. "It's a new policy." He stepped through the frame. Then his voice came back through it, faintly. "It's very annoying."

Yuta laughed, and followed him.

Milim looked at Mau.

"He's happy," she said.

"He's very happy," said Mau.

"Because the marble was easier?"

Mau thought about it. About a man who had once been the strongest in his world, standing at the bottom of a car park in another world, being careful with a marble.

"Because he had to be careful," he said. "And it worked."

Milim considered this for a long moment. Then she nodded, as if it made sense, and went through the frame.

Mau went last. On the other side, Anko was already writing.
