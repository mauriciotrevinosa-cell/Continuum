# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 43 — A Day Off

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17; shortened 2026-10-19)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 43
**Continuity state:** Eren and Mikasa step back and spend an ordinary day. Mikasa does not stand her afternoon hour at the north end, and they sit in the south field where the dead earth has grown green. Eren's "tomorrows" (S4 V25) become an ordinary wish: to spend one on nothing. No talk of seeing the sea: Eren arrived beside it and Mikasa has been, with the house. No external threat, no new decision about walls or roads

*When: Day 313, from midday into evening. Hot, with a high thin haze. The gate list has no names on it today.*

Mikasa did not go to the north end.

Eren noticed at two o'clock. He was in the workshop holding the end of a plank for Taihei, and he looked up through the open door the way he looked up every afternoon at two, toward the place where the houses stopped and the road began.

Where Mikasa stood. Every afternoon. For an hour.

She was not there.

"Sorry," he said to Taihei, and put the plank down.

"Go," said Taihei, who had been in the house for six days and had already learned which things were not his to ask about.

* * *

He found her on the back step with a cup of Rem's tea in both hands, watching the fort argue under the ink tree. No blades on her. She looked up when he came round the corner. She did not look surprised.

"You noticed," she said.

"It's two o'clock."

He sat down beside her. Not half a step behind. Beside.

"I woke up and thought about the north end," said Mikasa. "The way I do every morning. And I thought, there's nothing on the road today. The gate list is empty. Nazuna saw nothing all night." She turned the cup. "And I didn't want to go." She was quiet. "I always want to go. I stood at every wall I could find, in our world. Watching for you. And here I kept doing it. Even when you were beside me." She looked at him. "This morning I didn't want to. And I didn't know what to do instead."

"Is it bad?" said Eren.

Mikasa thought about it seriously.

"No," she said. "I don't think so."

Eren stood up.

"Come on," he said. "Somewhere you've never stood guard."

* * *

They walked out past the barn to the south field.

Where, in the spring, on a bad morning, a light had come up out of the ground and the grass had died where it fell. It was not dead now. Something softer and paler had come back, all the way to the lime line, with small yellow flowers in it that had not been there before.

Eren walked into the middle of it and sat down. Mikasa stood at the edge a moment.

"Mau comes here every morning," she said. "To see if it's coming back."

"It came back," said Eren.

She sat down beside him.

* * *

For a long time they did not say anything.

The grass was warm. The yellow flowers smelled faintly sweet. Insects went about their business, paying no attention to the two people in the middle of their field. Nothing came down any road anywhere.

Eren lay back with his hands behind his head.

"I've never done this," he said. "Nothing. Not once in my whole life. There was always the wall, or training, or the next thing, or the end coming." He looked up at the haze. "I knew exactly how many tomorrows I had. I could count them. I can't now. You said. Thousands."

"Thousands," said Mikasa.

"I keep trying to spend them on something important. Every day. Like they'll run out if I don't." He closed his eyes. "Maybe I'm supposed to spend some of them on nothing."

Mikasa lay back in the grass beside him. Her shoulder against his.

"Then spend one on nothing," she said. "Today. With me."

He turned his head and looked at her, lying in the grass with her hair spread out on the pale flowers and no blades anywhere.

"Okay," he said.

* * *

They came back at dusk, with grass seeds in their hair and yellow pollen on their clothes. Nobody said anything about it, though several people looked.

Mau was on the back step with Frieren. He moved over to make room. Mikasa sat beside him, and Eren beside her.

"All afternoon?" said Mau.

"All afternoon," said Eren. "Doing nothing."

"How was it?"

Eren thought about it.

"Difficult," he said honestly. "For about an hour. Then it wasn't."

Frieren did not look up from her book. "That's how it goes. The first hour is the hard one."

Mikasa looked at her. "You know how to do nothing?"

"I'm very good at it," said Frieren. "A thousand years of practice." She turned a page. "Lesson one: bring a book, but don't open it."

"Tomorrow?" said Eren.

"Tomorrow I'll go to the north end," said Mikasa. "But only if I want to."

She leaned her head on his shoulder, there on the step, in front of everyone, in the dusk.

Nobody said anything about that either.
