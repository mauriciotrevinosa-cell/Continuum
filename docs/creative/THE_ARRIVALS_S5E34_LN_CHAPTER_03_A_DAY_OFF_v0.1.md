# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 43 — A Day Off

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 43
**Continuity state:** Eren and Mikasa step back and spend an ordinary day; Mikasa does not stand her afternoon hour at the north end; they sit in the south field where the dead earth has grown green; Eren's "tomorrows" (S4 V25) become an ordinary wish (the sea, someday); no external threat, no new decision about walls or roads

*When: Day 313, from midday into evening. Hot, with a high thin haze. The gate list has no names on it today.*

Mikasa did not go to the north end.

Eren noticed at two o'clock. He had been in the workshop with Wakana and Taihei, holding the end of a plank for the far wall of the next room while Taihei measured it, and he looked up through the open workshop door the way he looked up every afternoon at two, without thinking about it, toward the place at the north end of the street where the houses stopped and the road began.

Where Mikasa stood. Every afternoon. For an hour. Since the winter.

She was not there.

Eren put the plank down.

"Sorry," he said to Taihei. "I have to—"

"Go," said Taihei, who had been in the house for six days and had already learned which things were not his to ask about.

* * *

He found her on the back step of the main house.

She was sitting there with her knees up and her red scarf loose round her neck, not wound up the way she wore it when she was going anywhere. She was not holding her blades. They were not on her at all. She had a cup of something in both hands, and she was watching the fort's Summer Annexe under the ink tree, where twenty small people were lying in the shade arguing about something that involved a great deal of pointing.

She looked up when he came round the corner. She did not look surprised.

"You noticed," she said.

"It's two o'clock," said Eren.

"I know what time it is."

He sat down on the step beside her. Not half a step behind. Beside. He had stopped standing half a step behind her a long time ago, in the spring, and he still noticed every time that he didn't.

"Are you all right?" he said.

Mikasa looked at the cup in her hands.

"Yes," she said. "That's the strange part." She turned the cup. "I woke up this morning and I thought about the north end. The way I do every morning. When I'll go. How long I'll stand. What I'll look at." She paused. "And I thought, there's nothing on the road today. Anko said. The gate list is empty. Nazuna saw nothing all night. Hollis is in Fallow. There's nothing coming." She looked at the fort, at the small people under the tree. "And I didn't want to go."

Eren did not say anything.

"I always want to go," said Mikasa. "I've always wanted to go. In our world, I stood at every wall I could find. Waiting. Watching for you." She was quiet. "And here I kept doing it. Even when you were beside me. Even when there was nothing to watch for." She looked at him. "This morning I didn't want to. And I didn't know what to do instead."

Eren looked at her for a long moment.

"So you're sitting on the step," he said.

"So I'm sitting on the step," said Mikasa. "Drinking Rem's tea. Watching the fort argue about a beetle." She almost smiled. "It's very strange."

"Is it bad?"

Mikasa thought about it seriously.

"No," she said at last. "I don't think so."

* * *

Eren stood up.

"Come on," he said.

"Where?"

"Somewhere you've never stood guard," said Eren. "Somewhere there's nothing to watch."

Mikasa looked at him. Then she put down the cup on the step, and stood up, and did not reach for blades that were not there.

They walked out past the end of the yard, past the barn, along the edge of the field to the south. The south field. Where, a long time ago in the spring, on a bad morning, a light had come up out of the ground and the grass had died where it fell. Where the earth had been black, and then dark, and then green at the edges, and the boy who carried honey had said *it's still dead*.

It was not dead now.

The grass had come back. All of it. Not the same grass; something softer and paler, with small yellow flowers in it that had not been there before. It went all the way across the field to the lime line, waist-high in places, moving a little in the hot haze.

Eren walked into the middle of it and sat down.

Mikasa stood at the edge for a moment, looking at it.

"Mau comes here," she said. "Every morning. He said. To see if it's coming back."

"It came back," said Eren.

Mikasa walked into the grass and sat down beside him.

* * *

For a long time they did not say anything.

The grass was warm. The small yellow flowers smelled faintly of something sweet. Insects moved in it, very busy, paying no attention to the two people sitting in the middle of their field. Overhead, the high thin haze made the sun soft and white, and the strange sky went on being strange, and nothing came down any road anywhere.

Eren lay back in the grass with his hands behind his head.

"I've never done this," he said.

"Done what?"

"Nothing," said Eren. "In my whole life. I never once did nothing. There was always the wall, or the training, or the next thing, or the end coming." He looked up at the haze. "I knew exactly how many tomorrows I had. Every day. I could count them." He was quiet for a moment. "I can't count them now. You said. Thousands."

"Thousands," said Mikasa.

"I don't know what to do with thousands," said Eren. "I keep trying to spend them on something important. Every day. Like they'll run out if I don't." He closed his eyes. "Maybe I'm supposed to spend some of them on nothing."

Mikasa did not say anything for a long moment.

Then she lay back in the grass beside him. Close. Her shoulder against his.

"Then spend one on nothing," she said. "Today. With me."

Eren opened his eyes and turned his head and looked at her, lying in the grass with her scarf loose and her hair spread out on the pale flowers and no blades anywhere.

"Okay," he said.

* * *

At some point, much later, Eren said, "Is there a sea here?"

Mikasa had been half asleep. She opened her eyes.

"A sea?"

"In this world. Anywhere." He was looking at the sky. "Hollis talks about the coast. The coast road. The towns past the ridge go to the coast, he said. So there's a sea." He paused. "I'd like to see it. One day. Not now. Not with the roads like they are. One day."

Mikasa was quiet.

She knew what the sea had meant to him, in their world. She knew how his story ended there, in one of them. She had counted it on her fingers, alone, a long time ago. She did not say any of that.

"One day," she said.

"You'd come?"

Mikasa turned her head and looked at him.

"Eren," she said. "I've followed you to every wall in two worlds. I'm not going to miss the sea."

Eren laughed. Short and surprised. He put his hand over his eyes.

"It might be very boring," he said. "The sea. Just water."

"Then we'll be bored," said Mikasa. "Together. At the sea." She closed her eyes again. "One of the thousands."

* * *

They came back at dusk, with grass seeds in their hair and yellow pollen on their clothes, and nobody said anything about it, though several people looked.

Mau was on the back step with Frieren when they came round the corner of the barn. He looked at the two of them. At the grass seeds. At Mikasa's scarf, loose. At the absence of blades.

He did not say anything either. But he moved over on the step to make room.

Mikasa sat down beside him. Eren sat beside her.

"The south field," said Mau. "It's all grass now."

"With yellow flowers," said Mikasa. "We sat in it."

"All afternoon?"

"All afternoon," said Eren. "Doing nothing."

Mau looked at him for a long moment.

"How was it?" he said.

Eren thought about it.

"Difficult," he said honestly. "For about an hour." He looked at Mikasa. "Then it wasn't."

Frieren, on Mau's other side, said, without looking up from her book, "That's how it always goes. The first hour is the hard one."

Mikasa looked at her.

"You know how to do nothing?" she said.

"I'm very good at it," said Frieren. "I've had a thousand years of practice." She turned a page. "I'll teach you. If you like. Lesson one is: bring a book, but don't open it."

Mikasa looked at Eren. Eren looked at Mikasa.

"Tomorrow?" said Eren.

"Not tomorrow," said Mikasa. "Tomorrow I'll go to the north end." She paused. "But only if I want to."

She leaned her head on his shoulder, there on the step, in front of everyone, in the dusk.

Nobody said anything about that either.
