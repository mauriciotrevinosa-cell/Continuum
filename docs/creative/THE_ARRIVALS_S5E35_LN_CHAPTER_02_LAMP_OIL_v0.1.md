# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 46 — Lamp Oil

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 46
**Continuity state:** First festival preparations: Fern's list for Hollis; Senku, Byakuya and Wakana work on lanterns; Shizuka and Nano keep their wish to help with the lights; Coco and Qifrey agree to make floating lights again, privately and in ink, as at the S2E22 festival (only where Rimuru approves); the date is still undecided

*When: Day 318, all day. Three days since the petition. Fern has started a second page in the ledger for the festival and it is already half full.*

Fern's list for Hollis was three columns long, and the third column was mostly lamp oil.

She had written it the morning after the petition and rewritten it four times since. Mau saw the fourth version on the long table at breakfast, beside her porridge, which she had not touched. *Lamp oil. Wicks. Cloth (coloured, if he has it; any, if he doesn't). Flour. Sugar. Salt (more). Whatever fruit survives the road.* And at the bottom, underlined twice: *Lamp oil.*

"You've written lamp oil twice," said Mau.

"I've written it three times," said Fern. "Once in the second column. You didn't see."

"Is that a lot?"

Fern looked at him over the list.

"Forty-three people," she said. "One night. Lights everywhere. In the yard, on the roof, along the inside path, in the trees." She tapped the list. "Do you know how much oil a lamp burns in a night?"

"No."

"Neither do I," said Fern. "Exactly. Senku's working it out." She went back to the list. "He says the answer is *a great deal*. He says that's a technical term."

* * *

Senku was working it out in the lab, with Byakuya and Wakana and a great deal of glass.

Mau went down after breakfast because he had been told he could watch, by Senku, as long as he did not touch anything, breathe on anything, or offer to carry anything. The lab was full. Chimneys from the old chimney run, the lopsided ones that had not been good enough for Hollis, lined up along the bench like a row of slightly drunk soldiers. A box of wicks. A small barrel of oil, half empty. And a sheet of paper covered in Senku's small fast handwriting, in columns, with arrows.

"Forty lamps," said Senku, without looking up. "Minimum. If we want the yard and the path and the roof lit. Each burns about this much in an hour." He tapped a number. "The festival's from dusk to whenever people stop. Call it six hours." He tapped another number. "That's a barrel and a half. We've got half a barrel."

"So Hollis," said Byakuya.

"So Hollis," said Senku. "And it's still not enough for the trees."

"What about the trees?"

"Fern wants lights in the trees," said Senku. "Hakari wants lights in the trees. The petition says *lights in the trees*." He put down his pencil. "You can't hang an oil lamp in a tree. It'll set the tree on fire. Then we'll have a festival and no ink tree, and Coco will never speak to any of us again."

Wakana, at the end of the bench, was holding one of the lopsided chimneys up to the window and turning it.

"Paper," he said.

Senku looked at him.

"Paper lanterns," said Wakana. He put the chimney down. "Not oil. A candle in a paper shade. Small. Light. You hang them on a string between the branches." He paused. "My grandfather made them. For the summer festival at home. Every year." He went slightly red. "He let me help, when I was small. I did the folding."

Byakuya's face lit up.

"Paper lanterns!" he said. "We had those in the village. For the harvest. The kids made them out of—" He stopped. "Out of paper. We didn't have paper for the first ten years. We made them out of leaves." He laughed. "They burned very fast."

"Candles burn less than oil," said Senku slowly. He picked up his pencil again. "A small candle in a paper shade. Maybe a third." He was writing. "And paper's light. And if one catches, it burns out before it catches the branch, if the string's long enough." He looked up at Wakana. "How many can you make?"

"How much paper have we got?"

Senku looked at Byakuya. Byakuya looked at Senku.

"Hollis," they said together.

* * *

Shizuka and Nano came to the lab at noon.

They stood in the doorway, side by side. Shizuka had her book. Nano had nothing, but she was standing very straight, the way she stood when she had decided something.

Shizuka held up the book. It said, in her neat writing: *We would like to help with the lights.*

Senku looked at the book. Then at Nano.

"The petition," said Nano. "Clause seven. *Shizuka and Nano would like to help with the lights.*" She paused. "It was approved."

"I know it was approved," said Senku. "I voted for it. Under protest."

Shizuka turned the page. *We can fold.*

Wakana looked up from the bench.

"Paper lanterns," he said. "We need folding. A lot of folding." He held up one he had started: a small square of rough paper, folded into a shape like a box with no top, a little crooked. "Like this. But less crooked."

Shizuka came into the lab and took the paper out of his hands and looked at it for a long moment. Then she unfolded it, very carefully, and refolded it. Quickly. Precisely. In about ten seconds.

It was not crooked at all.

Wakana stared at it.

Shizuka held up her book. *I am good at folding.*

Nano, beside her, took a square of paper from the pile on the bench. She looked at Shizuka's finished lantern. Then at the paper in her own hands. Then she folded it, slowly, carefully, checking each crease against Shizuka's.

It took her much longer. It was very slightly crooked.

She looked at it. Then at Shizuka.

"Further practice required," said Nano.

Shizuka took the pencil out of the spine of her book and wrote, under the last page, very small, so only Nano could see it. Nano read it. And Mau, who was standing near enough to see Nano's face, saw her go faintly, faintly pink, the colour of a lantern with a candle just lit behind it.

* * *

In the afternoon, Mau went to find Coco.

He had been sent. Fern had sent him, with a question, because Fern said Coco would answer him faster than her, and Mau did not ask why.

Coco was at the table under the window in the Witch Hat room, drawing straight lines with a pencil. She had been drawing straight lines every afternoon since the exercise. Her mother sat on the end of the bed beside her, drawing them too. They were both getting very good.

"Fern wants to know," said Mau, from the doorway, "about the lights. At the festival. At the inn."

Coco put down her pencil.

"The floating ones," she said.

"The floating ones."

At the inn, at the festival at the beginning, Coco and Qifrey had made small lights that floated. Only where Rimuru approved. Momo had ignored several approvals. Mau remembered them: small soft globes of light drifting over the long table and the music corner and the banners Wakana had made out of repaired fabric. He remembered Frieren looking up at them.

"Fern wants to know if you'd make them again," he said. "If you want to. Not if you have to." He paused. "She said to say that last part exactly."

Coco looked at him for a long moment.

Then she looked at Qifrey, who was standing by the door, the way he always stood by doors.

"Can we?" she said.

Qifrey was quiet for a moment.

"In here," he said. "With the door shut. In ink. The way we did at the inn." He looked at Mau. "And only where Rimuru approves. That rule was a good rule."

"Momo ignored it," said Coco.

"Momo ignored it," Qifrey agreed. "Which is why it was a good rule. It gave her something to ignore." He almost smiled. "Tell Fern yes. Tell her the Witch Hat room will make the lights. And tell her not to ask how."

"I wasn't going to ask how," said Mau.

"I know," said Qifrey. "Tell her anyway."

Coco's mother was looking at her daughter with an expression Mau had seen before, in the room at the end of the west wing, on the day she arrived. The face of someone learning there was a whole life of small things she had never known.

"You made lights?" she said. "That float?"

"At a festival," said Coco. "A long time ago. At the beginning." She picked up her pencil again. "I'll show you. Not how. Just the lights. When they're done."

Her mother put her hand on her daughter's inky one.

"I'd like that," she said.

* * *

At dinner, Fern turned the ledger to the festival page and added three lines.

*Paper lanterns (Wakana, Shizuka, Nano). Candles, not oil, in the trees.*

*Floating lights (Witch Hat room). Do not ask how.*

*Lamp oil: still a great deal. Less than before.*

She looked at it for a long time. Then she looked down the long table.

"We still need a date," she said.

The long table went quiet.

"The house said *before the summer ends*," said Fern. "That's not a date. That's a direction." She looked at her list for Hollis. "He comes in eight days. If we ask him for paper and oil and cloth, he'll need two runs. Maybe three." She closed the ledger. "So not before then. After that, the house decides."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then, from the far end, Frieren said, without looking up from her book, "When the fireflies are gone."

Everyone looked at her.

"Ori said they'll last a few more weeks," said Frieren. She turned a page. "Nazuna's watched them every summer night. She'll know when the last one goes." She looked up. "Then the house can have its own lights instead."

Fern looked at her for a long moment.

Then she opened the ledger again and wrote, under *We still need a date*, very small:

*When the fireflies are gone. (Ask Nazuna.)*
