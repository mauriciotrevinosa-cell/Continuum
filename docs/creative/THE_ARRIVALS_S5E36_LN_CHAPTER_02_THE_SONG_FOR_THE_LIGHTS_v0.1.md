# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 50 — The Song for the Lights

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 50
**Continuity state:** R21 bridge toward R37. Kessoku begin rehearsing for the festival; the piece Bocchi has been changing all summer becomes hers to finish; Seika manages without deciding for her; Kita stays close; Bocchi invites Mau to hear it finished at the festival (the invitation he keeps later); Mau listens and does not decide anything

*When: Day 322, afternoon into evening. The music room door is shut, which Kessoku never do. Seika has put a sign on it.*

The sign on the music room door said, in Seika's sharp handwriting: *REHEARSAL. KNOCK AND WAIT. DO NOT KNOCK AND COME IN, MILIM.*

Mau knocked and waited.

After a while, the door opened a crack. Nijika's face, with a stick behind her ear.

"Bocchi asked for you," she said. "Come in. Quickly. Before Milim reads the sign."

He came in quickly.

The music room was hot, with the shutters closed against the afternoon, and full. Ryo on the floor with her back against the wall and her bass across her knees, looking bored, which meant she was concentrating. Kita on the stool by the window, with her guitar, tuning. Nijika behind the drums. Seika on the only chair, by the door, with her arms folded and a page of notes on her knee.

And Bocchi, in the corner, on the floor, with her guitar against her chest and her knees up, as small as she could make herself.

She looked up when Mau came in. She went pink. She did not look away.

"You came," she said.

"You asked."

"I asked Nijika to ask you," said Bocchi. "That's not the same."

"It's the same," said Mau, and sat down on the floor by the door, out of the way, where Seika could see him and decide if he was allowed to stay.

Seika looked at him for a long moment.

"You don't talk," she said. "You don't suggest. You don't say *that part was nice*. You listen." She looked at her notes. "If you want to say something after, you say it to Bocchi. Not to the band. To Bocchi." She paused. "It's hers."

"It's hers," Mau agreed.

* * *

It was the piece.

He knew it in the first few notes. The one Bocchi had been changing all summer. The one with the passage Ryo said was simple and was not, with the little space before the change that Kita liked, with the ending she had moved twice and moved back once. He had heard it on the shade bench, in the fort, on the night before the door. He had heard her play two versions and ask him which one stayed.

It had changed again.

It was longer. There was a new part at the beginning, slow, just Bocchi's guitar alone, a few notes far apart, like someone walking into a dark room and waiting for their eyes to adjust. Then Kita came in under it, very softly. Then Nijika, barely touching the drums, a heartbeat. Then Ryo, low, holding everything up from underneath.

And then the passage. The one that went somewhere and decided to stay.

Mau did not move. He did not breathe much. He sat on the floor by the door with his back against the wall and listened, and watched Bocchi's hands, and then made himself look away from her hands so she would not feel watched.

She stopped halfway through.

"That's wrong," she said.

"It's not wrong," said Ryo, from the floor. "It's slow."

"It's supposed to be slow."

"Then it's not wrong."

Bocchi looked at her guitar.

"It's slow wrong," she said. "Not slow right." She looked at Seika. "Can we go again? From the dark part?"

Seika looked at her notes. Then at Bocchi.

"You don't have to ask me," she said. "It's yours. Go again when you want."

Bocchi looked at her in surprise.

Then she went again.

* * *

They played it six times.

Each time it changed a little. Not much. A note here, a space there. Once, Kita came in a breath later than before, and Bocchi stopped, and looked at her, and Kita said "Was that wrong?" and Bocchi said "No. Do that again. Every time." And Kita did.

Seika did not say anything for five of the six. She sat on her chair with her arms folded and her notes on her knee and listened, the way she listened to bands at the live house in her own world, Nijika said, with a face like a stone and ears like a hawk.

After the sixth, she unfolded her arms.

"What's it called?" she said.

Bocchi looked at her guitar for a long time.

"It doesn't have a name," she said. "It's—it was just the thing I kept playing. When I couldn't sleep. When things happened." She plucked one string, softly. "I played it on the stairs while Mau slept with Kaneki's coat on him. I played it while we waited for the red gate. I didn't know it was a song. I thought it was just—" She stopped. "Waiting."

"It's a song," said Seika.

"It's a song," said Ryo, from the floor.

"It's *our* song," said Nijika, behind the drums, and touched the rim of the drum once, softly, like a full stop.

Bocchi looked at the four of them. Then down at the guitar.

"For the festival," she said. "When the fireflies are gone. Can we—" She stopped. She went red. "Can it be the one we play? At the end? When the lights are all lit?"

The music room was very quiet.

"It's yours," said Seika. "You decide."

Bocchi looked at Kita.

Kita, on her stool by the window, smiled at her. Not the bright smile she gave everyone. The other one. The small one that was only for Bocchi.

"Then it's the one," said Bocchi.

* * *

Afterward, when the others were putting things away, Bocchi came and sat down on the floor beside Mau by the door.

Not close. Close enough.

"You didn't say anything," she said.

"Seika said not to."

"You can say something now," said Bocchi. "To me. Not to the band."

Mau thought about it. Properly. The way she had asked him once, on the shade bench, which version stayed.

"The beginning," he said. "The new part. The slow part, where it's just you." He looked at the guitar in her lap. "It sounds like walking into a dark room. And waiting for your eyes to work." He paused. "And then everyone comes in, one at a time. And by the time the passage comes, the room's full."

Bocchi was very still.

"That's what it is," she said. Very quietly. "That's what I meant. I didn't have words for it."

"I don't think it needs words," said Mau. "It's very clear."

Bocchi looked down at her guitar for a long time.

"At the festival," she said. "When we play it. At the end." She did not look at him. "Will you come and listen? Not in the crowd. Close. Where I can see you." She swallowed. "I'll look at Kita. Mostly. But if I can't—if there are too many people—I'd like to be able to look at someone who already heard it."

Mau looked at her.

At the small girl on the floor with her guitar against her chest and her knees up, who had played for everyone at the inn without looking at the crowd once, because Kita stood close enough. Who had sat down beside him on the shade bench a dozen times this summer to ask him which version stayed.

"I'll come," he said. "Close. Where you can see me."

"You promise?"

"I promise."

Bocchi nodded. She did not look at him. But she held the guitar a little less tightly.

* * *

When Mau went out, Milim was standing in the corridor in front of the sign, reading it with great concentration, her lips moving.

"*Do not knock and come in, Milim,*" she read aloud. She looked at Mau, deeply offended. "That's me."

"It is you," said Mau.

"I wasn't going to come in. I was going to knock and *wait*." She folded her arms. "I'm very good at waiting now. Ask anyone."

"I know you are."

Milim looked at the shut door. From behind it, faintly, came the sound of a guitar. The slow part. Just Bocchi, alone, a few notes far apart.

Milim went very quiet.

"What is it?" she said.

"A song," said Mau. "For the festival. For when the lights are lit."

Milim listened for a long time, standing in the corridor in front of the sign, not knocking, not coming in.

"It sounds like waiting," she said at last.

"It is," said Mau. "That's what it's for."
