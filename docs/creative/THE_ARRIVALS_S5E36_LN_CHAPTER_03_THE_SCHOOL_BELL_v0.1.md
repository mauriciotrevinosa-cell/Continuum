# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 51 — The School Bell

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-17)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 51
**Continuity state:** Eighth tower visit, from the floor 5 transfer point (walking 5–8): Mau (owner) with Gojo, Yuta and Frieren; floor 9 is an empty school; teacher and student walk its corridors; the objective is ringing the bell; Gojo's small Blue and Yuta's healing stay at their current scale; Frieren draws the classrooms for Senku's page; level and points rise in Mau's branch only; no extraction

*When: Day 325, morning. Two and a half hours outside, with the long walk. Frieren asked for this one because Gojo told her the floor beyond the train "smelled like chalk".*

The ninth floor was a school, and Gojo stopped at the top of the stair and did not move for a long time.

It was not like Mau had imagined a school. He did not know what he had imagined. Something like the Witch Hat room, maybe, or the old inn's common room with the long table. This was a corridor. Long and straight, with a floor of worn wooden boards and windows down one side, tall, with grimy glass, letting in a grey light that came from nowhere. Down the other side, doors. Dozens of them. Each one with a small window in it at head height, and a number painted beside it, and a narrow shelf outside with nothing on it.

At the far end of the corridor, high on the wall, a bell. A real bell, brass, small, with a rope hanging from it.

And the smell. Chalk, and old wood, and something like floor polish, very faint.

Gojo stood at the top of the stair with his blindfold pushed up and his bare eyes on the long corridor.

Yuta had stopped too.

Neither of them said anything.

Frieren looked from one to the other.

"What is it?" she said.

Gojo did not answer at once.

"A school," he said at last. "Not ours. Ours was older. Wooden, in the mountains, with a shrine." He looked at the doors. "But a school. Classrooms. That's a corridor where you get told off for running."

"You got told off for running," said Yuta quietly.

"Every day," said Gojo. "Yaga had a whole speech." He almost smiled. "You got told off for walking too slowly."

"I was nervous."

"You were *terrified*," said Gojo. "You walked down the corridor on your first day like the floor was going to eat you." He looked at Yuta sideways. "And then you didn't run away."

Yuta did not say anything.

The notice came up.

*Floor 9 of 100.*

*Objective: ring the bell.*

* * *

They walked down the corridor.

Slowly. Gojo first, with his hands in his pockets, looking through the little windows in the doors as they passed. Yuta beside him, half a pace behind, the way a student walks beside a teacher. Mau behind them. Frieren at the back, with her notebook out, drawing.

Behind the little windows, the classrooms were empty. Rows of small desks. A board on the far wall, dark green, with faded marks on it that might once have been writing. A desk at the front, bigger than the others. Chairs pushed in. Everything very neat and very still, as if everyone had just stepped out for a moment and would be back.

"Where is everyone?" said Frieren, drawing.

"It's the tower," said Mau. "Nobody's anywhere."

"There were things on every other floor," said Frieren. "Dogs. Paper hands. The grey ones on the train." She looked up from her notebook. "Where are they here?"

Gojo stopped walking.

He was looking through the little window of a door on the right. Room 3-B, the number beside it said, in letters Mau could read because they were the letters Gojo could read.

"In there," said Gojo quietly.

Mau looked.

The classroom was not empty. In the desks, in neat rows, sitting very straight, were shapes. Small. The size of children. Grey, like the train shapes, smooth-faced, with their hands folded on their desks. Facing the front. Facing the big desk, and the dark board, where nothing was written.

They were very still.

"They're waiting," said Yuta. Very low.

"For the teacher," said Gojo.

* * *

Every classroom had them.

They looked, one after another, through the little windows. Rows of small grey shapes in every room, sitting very straight, hands folded, facing empty boards. Room after room. None of them moved. None of them turned their smooth faces toward the windows.

"If we ring the bell," said Frieren slowly, "what happens?"

Nobody answered.

"In a school," said Yuta at last, "the bell means the lesson's over. Everyone gets up. Everyone goes out into the corridor at once." He looked down the long corridor, at all the doors. "All of them. At once."

"Into the corridor," said Mau. "Where we are."

"Where we are," said Yuta.

They stood in the middle of the corridor and looked at the bell at the far end, high on the wall, with its rope.

"So we ring it and run," said Mau.

"No," said Gojo.

Everyone looked at him.

Gojo was looking at the classroom beside him. At the small grey shapes in their rows, with their hands folded, facing the empty board.

"They're not going to rush the corridor," he said. "They're children. Or the idea of children." He was very quiet. "When the bell goes, children don't attack anybody. They go home." He looked down the corridor. "They're going to come out of every door and walk to the stairs. Toward us. Because we're standing between them and the way out."

He looked at Yuta.

"So we don't stand between them and the way out," he said. "We stand where a teacher stands."

Yuta looked at him for a long moment.

"At the door," said Yuta slowly. "Of a classroom. Out of the way. While they go past."

"While they go past," said Gojo.

* * *

It was Yuta who rang the bell.

Gojo said a student should do it. "Teachers don't ring the bell," he said. "Somebody always runs down the corridor and rings it at the end of the day, and it's never the teacher." He looked at Yuta. "It was always you, at the end. You were always last to leave."

Yuta went to the end of the corridor. He took the rope in his hand. He looked back at the three of them, standing in the doorways of three classrooms along the corridor, out of the way, the way Gojo had said: Mau in 3-B, Frieren in 3-C with her notebook, Gojo in the doorway of 3-A, leaning on the frame with his arms folded, exactly the way he leaned on doorframes at home.

Yuta rang the bell.

It was very loud in the empty corridor. Bright and brass and sudden.

And every door opened at once.

They came out. The small grey shapes, out of every classroom, into the corridor, in a long quiet stream. Not running. Walking, the way children walk at the end of a long day: a little slow, a little bumping into each other, heads down. They filled the corridor and moved along it, toward the stair, toward the way out, past the three doorways where Mau and Frieren and Gojo stood out of the way.

None of them looked at the doorways. None of them lifted a smooth grey face. They just walked past, in their stream, the way children walk past a teacher at a classroom door at the end of the day.

Mau stood very still in the doorway of 3-B and watched them go by.

One of them bumped him. Very lightly. A small grey shoulder against his knee. It did not stop. It did not turn. It just went on, with the others, toward the stair.

Then they were gone.

Down the stair. All of them. The corridor was empty again, and quiet, and smelled of chalk.

* * *

Nobody said anything for a long moment.

Then Frieren, in the doorway of 3-C, said very quietly, "They went home."

"They went home," said Gojo.

The notice came up.

*Bell rung. Route to Floor 10 opened.*

Level eleven. Fifty points. Mau showed them. Nobody looked at it very long.

Yuta came back down the corridor from the bell. He stopped in front of the doorway where Gojo was leaning.

"You knew," he said. "That they wouldn't fight."

"I guessed," said Gojo.

"How?"

Gojo was quiet for a moment. He looked down the empty corridor, at all the open doors, at the empty desks in their rows.

"Because I was a teacher," he said. "For a while. In my world." He looked at Yuta. "And when the bell goes, nobody wants to fight. Everybody just wants to go home." He pushed himself off the doorframe. "Even the ones who are terrified on the first day."

Yuta looked at him.

"I didn't run away," said Yuta. "On the first day."

"You didn't," said Gojo. "You were the only one who walked down this corridor like the floor was going to eat you, and didn't run away." He put his hands in his pockets. "I noticed. I always noticed."

Yuta did not say anything for a long time.

Then he looked away, down the corridor, at the bell hanging still at the far end.

"Thank you," he said. "For noticing."

* * *

Frieren had drawn all of it.

On the walk back, down the five floors to the transfer point on five, she showed Mau her notebook. The corridor. The doors with their little windows. The rows of desks. The board. The bell. And under it, on the page headed *Things in the tower Senku knows*, she had added a new line in her careful hand:

*A school (Gojo, Yuta). The bell means go home.*

And under that, smaller:

*Gojo was a teacher. Yuta was his student. The corridor knew.*

Mau looked at the line for a long time.

"The corridor didn't know," he said. "It's just a corridor."

"I know," said Frieren. "But they walked down it like it knew." She closed the notebook. "That's the same thing, for a drawing."
