*When: Day 327, an hour before noon. Forty minutes used. Anko has written the four lines from the frame at the top of the board and drawn a box round them. Seiko has not moved from the bench.*

They went back in.

Nobody suggested not going back in. That was the thing Mau would remember. Maki stood at the dark frame for a long moment, reading Anko's box on the board, and then she turned round and held her spear out to him. Crosswise. In both hands.

"Take it," she said.

"It's yours."

"I know it's mine." She did not lower it. "You're going to need something longer than your arm. You don't have anything longer than your arm." She looked at him. "I'll get it back."

He took it.

It was heavier than it looked. The shaft was ash, old, smooth from her hands. It did not feel like his. It felt like holding someone else's arm.

"Three hours," said Anko. "You've got two hours and twenty minutes of it left." She looked at the board. At the box. "Come back in two hours and twenty minutes."

* * *

The walk from five was faster the second time. Nobody explained anything. Yuka did not stop in the arcade to talk about lamps. Yuta did not stand by the bell. They went down the spiral and through the arcade and through nine carriages of sleeping grey shapes and along the corridor of open doors without a word, and up the long concrete stair, round and round, and stopped at the heavy metal door with the pale ten on it.

The notice was there.

*Floor 10 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the first ten.*

And under it, one line that had not been there before.

*Operator: engage.*

Mau read it. He did not show anyone. He did not need to.

Maki put her hand on his shoulder. Briefly. Then took it away.

"Inside the reach," she said. "It's slow when it's close. It's fast when it's far. Its blade wants to be far from it. So you go close." She looked at him. "That's the whole lesson. Everything else is just doing it with something trying to kill you."

"Okay."

"I'll be with you," said Maki. "On your left. I don't carry. I open." She looked at the door. "You finish."

* * *

The guardian was standing in the painted circle.

Whole. As if nothing had happened. The heavy dark plates overlapping like scales. The long blade upright beside it. The red glow behind the visor, faint, like a coal.

It turned its head toward the door.

Toward Mau.

Not toward the four of them. Mau felt the difference at once. Before, its faceless helm had moved across all of them the way a lamp moves across a room. Now it was on him. Only him. And when Maki stepped up on his left, and Yuta on his right, and Yuka behind with her rope, it did not look at any of them.

It lifted the long blade off the roof.

"Go," said Maki.

* * *

He was not good at it.

That was the truth, and he knew it in the first breath. He had watched Maki do this. He had watched Yuta do it in the yard, and Mikasa, and Gojo once. Watching it was nothing. The first sweep of the long blade came round at chest height, and he did what Maki had done, he tried to catch it on the shaft and turn it like water, and it was not water. It was a wall coming at him. The shaft took it and the shock went up both his arms into his shoulders and he was off his feet, skidding backward across the roof on his back, the spear still somehow in his hands.

The guardian came after him.

Long heavy strides. The blade going up.

He put out the space.

Not much. Not the full width of his Infinity. A narrow edge of it, a hand's breadth, slanted, the way Gojo had told him a hundred times, *you don't need a wall, you need an angle*. The blade came down and met the angle and slid off it, sideways, and bit into the roof beside his head and stuck.

He was up.

"Close," said Maki, on his left. "Go *close*."

He went close.

* * *

It was better close.

She was right. It was always slower close. Its long blade wanted to be far away and could not be. Its elbows and knees were in the wrong places for something this near. Mau went in under the pole, inside the arc, close enough to see the scratches on its plates, the overlapping scales, the dull red behind the slit of the visor, and the guardian tried to step back to make room for its blade, and Maki was behind it.

She did not strike. She stood where it wanted to step.

It could not step back.

"Now look," said Maki. "Where's the gap?"

He looked. He had watched her drive her spear into it an hour ago. Under the arm. Where the plates of the shoulder did not quite meet the plates of the side.

The guardian's arm was down. The gap was shut.

"It's shut."

"Then open it."

* * *

He did not know how.

He tried with the spear. Jabbed at the arm, to make it lift. The guardian took the point on its plates and did not lift. It turned instead, fast, and the butt of its pole came round low and took him across the ribs.

He felt something give.

He went down on one knee. The air went out of him. His side was on fire. For a moment the grey roof went greyer.

*Mau.* Ciel. *Two ribs. Not broken. Badly bruised. You can move.* A pause. *It's going to hit you again.*

It was. The pole was already coming back.

The sentry rose out of his shadow.

He had not called it. He had thought it. And it came, the long hooked weapon clearing the roof, and caught the butt of the pole on its hook, and held it, for a heartbeat. He felt the cost of calling it go out of the cup, once, small, the way it always did when one of them stood up.

A heartbeat was enough to roll.

He came up on the guardian's far side with the spear in both hands and his ribs screaming, and Yuka's rope was already out across the roof, low, and caught its ankle.

* * *

It stumbled.

The same stumble as before. The whole heavy weight of it going the wrong way, its arm coming up, the way an arm always comes up when a body loses its footing.

The gap opened.

"*Now,*" said Maki.

"The other ankle," said Mau. To the sentry. "Hook. Pull."

The sentry's hook caught the other ankle. Pulled.

The guardian went over. Not all the way. Half. Down onto one knee, with its arm still up, its long blade swinging wild over all of them, and its side open under the arm, the plates apart, and the red glow behind the visor turning toward him.

He drove Maki's spear into the gap.

He did it with everything. Both hands. His whole weight behind it. His ribs. He did not use the space. He did not use the pull. He used a spear and his arms, the way anyone does it, the way she had, and the point went in under the arm between the plates and kept going.

The shaft cracked.

He heard it. A dry splitting sound, near the head, under his hands. He felt it go. Maki's spear, old ash, smooth from her hands. Cracking.

He kept pushing.

The long blade came down one last time, wild, at nothing. It caught the back of his left forearm on its way down. A long shallow line, opening, hot.

Then the red glow behind the visor went out.

* * *

The guardian fell.

Full length across the painted circle, heavy as a falling wall, and the whole roof shook. Mau went down with it, because he had not let go of the spear, and landed on his knees on its back with the cracked shaft still in his hands and his forearm bleeding onto its plates and his ribs on fire.

He knelt there.

Breathing.

Nobody touched him. Nobody came to help him up. Maki stood where she had stood, on his left, with her hands empty. Yuta stood on the far side with his sword drawn and not used. Yuka knelt with the slack rope in her hands. They had not finished it.

He had.

* * *

The notice came up.

*Operator engaged.*

*Guardian of the first ten defeated.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5 or 10.*

And below it, a new line.

*Shadow extraction available.*

Mau looked at the fallen guardian under him. As the blue light began to run along its heavy plates, a dark outline remained beneath it. Deeper than the sentry's. Much deeper.

*Raising it will cost,* said Ciel, quietly. *Once. When it stands up. More than the dragon did.* A pause. *Your cup is shallow. It will take nearly all of it. You'll walk home with almost nothing.* Another pause. *And it has to accept you. It knelt to nothing. It stood its roof alone. It won't rise for someone it doesn't think is worth rising for.*

He looked at the outline for a long time.

Then he put his hand flat on the guardian's back. On the heavy dark plates he was still kneeling on, with Maki's cracked spear beside him and his own blood on the armour.

"Arise," he said.

Nothing happened.

The dark under the guardian did not move. It lay there, deep, heavy, and did not rise. Mau felt it. Not refusing. Weighing. The way the guardian had weighed the four of them in the doorway the first time, before the notice came.

*It's looking at you,* said Ciel. *It's deciding.*

Behind him, Yuka had gone very still. Maki did not move. Yuta's hand had gone to his sword without his deciding.

Mau did not look at any of them.

He looked at the dark.

*You stood on your roof,* he thought at it. *Under a sky like a lid. On your own. For as long as there's been a roof.* He breathed. It hurt. *And the first time they came, they carried me in and you stopped. Because I wasn't the one fighting.* His hand pressed flat on the plates. *I was the one fighting the second time. I finished it. Badly. With somebody else's spear.* He did not look away. *That's what I've got. I'm not going to pretend it's more.*

*I'd like you to come anyway.*

The dark moved.

It came up out of the plates under his hand like water out of a spring, slow and heavy, and the guardian under him went dark from the inside, all of it, and rose. Mau slid off its back onto the roof. It stood up over him. Taller than Stark. The heavy dark plates. The long blade on its pole, upright. And behind the slit of the helm, where the dull red coal had been, two small cold lights. Blue.

It looked down at him.

Then it went down on one knee on the painted circle, and laid the long pole across its knee, and bowed its helm.

The cup went.

Almost all of it. He felt it pour out of him in one long breath, the way Ciel had said, and the edges of everything went thin, and he sat down on the roof very suddenly next to his own shadow.

"Oh," said Yuka. Very small.

"It's yours," said Maki.

"It's mine," said Mau. His voice did not sound like his. "And there's nothing left."

*A thin layer,* said Ciel. *On the floor of the cup. Almost nothing.* A pause. *If something else came up those stairs now, you'd have nothing to call it with.*

He looked at the kneeling guardian. At the cold blue lights. At the empty roof under the not-sky.

"Next time," he said, to nobody, "I want a little left."

Maki was looking at him.

"That's a good thing to learn on ten," she said.

"Back," said Mau, to the guardian, and it went down into the roof, and he did not have anything left to feel it go with.

* * *

He got off the guardian's back. Slowly. He held out the spear to her, crosswise, in both hands. The shaft was split near the head, a long pale crack running down the grain.

"I broke it," he said.

Maki took it. She looked at the crack for a long time. She ran her thumb along it.

"You didn't break it," she said. "You cracked it." She put it over her shoulder. "That's different. It'll hold. For a while." She looked at him. "I'll cut a new one when I find the right ash."

She did not say anything else about it.

* * *

They went home through the small frame. All four. Mau stepped onto the seal first, and the pattern lit under his feet.

*Owner progress: Floor 10.*

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Two hours and four minutes," she said. "Both times together." She looked at Mau's arm. At the way he was holding his side. At the cracked spear on Maki's shoulder.

She did not write *Mau stood on the seal.*

She wrote: *Mau finished it. (Maki opened.) (Yuka's rope.) (The sentry.) Kept it. (Cup empty.)*

And under it: *Arm, ribs. Shoko.*

Shoko was already coming across the dry ground from the clinic with her coffee.

* * *

That night, on the back step, Maki sat with the cracked spear across her knees and Yuta beside her, and Yuka asleep against her shoulder.

"We were carrying him," said Yuta. "The whole way. Nine floors."

"Yes," said Maki.

"We thought we were helping."

"We were helping." Maki ran her thumb down the crack. "Just not the way that tower wants." She was quiet. "*May not carry.* Somebody built that thing so he'd have to do it himself. Every guardian. Every time."

"Who?"

"I don't know." Maki looked at the dark screen across the yard. "Somebody who wants to see what he does when nobody can do it for him."

Yuta did not say anything for a long time.

"Then we can't let him stand at the back," he said at last. "Not ever again. Not on any floor."

"No," said Maki.

"He's not ready."

"No," said Maki. She looked down at her granddaughter asleep against her shoulder, and then at the crack in the spear. "So we make him good enough."
