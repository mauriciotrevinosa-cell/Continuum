## Chapter 55 — Through the Shield

*When: Day 328, dusk. The same breath. The house behind the lime has six windows lit. In the workshop, Senku has just dropped a lens and is looking at it on the floor.*

The cut did not stop.

It reached the shield and went through it.

Not broke it. Mau would have understood broken. He had seen Frieren's shield crack before, under a spell, and hold, and crack again, and hold. This was not that. The bright planes stood exactly where they were, whole and sharp and shining, and the line went through them as if they had been painted on the air. As if the space they stood in had simply been told to come apart and had agreed.

It reached her.

He was already running.

He did not remember letting go of the two demons. He did not remember the twenty paces. He was on the road, and then he was beside her, and she was turning, slowly, too slowly, with her staff still up and her shield still standing in front of her untouched.

And there was blood.

It came out of her all at once. Across the front of her. From the left shoulder down across her chest to the right side, a long straight line opening in her coat and in her. Dark in the dusk. Too much. Much too much, from someone so small.

Her staff fell first. It hit the road with a hollow wooden sound.

Then she did.

* * *

He caught her.

He was on his knees on the north road with her in his arms before she reached the ground, and he did not know how he had got there. Her head fell back against his arm. Her eyes were open. Looking at him. Surprised. Only surprised, as if somebody had told her something she had not expected to hear.

"Frieren," he said.

The blood was on his hands. On his arms. Warm. Coming through her coat into his.

"No," said Mau. "No, no. No. Frieren. *Frieren.*"

Her lips moved. Nothing came out.

"No no no no no no—"

* * *

Out of the woods to the left of the road, something stepped.

Tall. Thin. A devil. Mau saw it with the edge of his eye and did not look at it. It came out of the dark between the trees as easily as a man steps out of a doorway, and behind it, where it had been standing, the woods were not woods any more.

They were rows.

A long stretch of forest, from the edge of the road back into the dark as far as he could see, lying down. Every tree cut. Cleanly. At the same height. Laid flat in long neat lines, one after another, the trunks pale and wet where they had been opened, the way corn lies down behind a scythe. The way rumour had once said, in every town on the road, that a whole city had been cut.

It had done that to reach her.

It had done that to reach *her*.

* * *

The demons were laughing.

All four of them. Even the two he had held. Light, pleasant, delighted laughter, the laughter of someone watching a trick go exactly right.

"There," said the one in the middle. Its warm young voice again. Gentle. "You see?"

Mau did not look up.

"That's what happens," said the demon. "To all of them. Every one in your settlement. The children. The old woman. The girl who draws." It sounded sorry. It sounded exactly like something that was sorry. "And you won't be able to do anything. You couldn't do anything for her."

The devil in the woods made a sound that might have been a laugh.

The big one behind the demons made the same sound.

"Give yourself up," said the demon kindly, "and maybe we'll stop."

* * *

There was a silence.

It was a strange silence. It came down over the north road all at once, the way the dark comes down at the end of a long day. The crickets in the north field stopped. The wind stopped.

The demons stopped laughing.

They did not know why. Mau could see them not knowing why. They looked at each other. They looked at the boy kneeling in the road with the elf in his arms, not moving, his head down, his hands red to the elbow.

Then the ground began to shake.

Not much. Not at first. A tremor, coming up through the road. Through the soles of their feet. Small stones on the road shivering against each other. The hung leaf of the north gate, behind them, creaking on its hinge.

Then more.

* * *

*Mau.*

Ciel. Inside. Fast.

*Mau, the walls—you have to calm down. You have to—Mau, they're opening, all of them, at once, it's like the south field, it's like—Mau, please, listen to me, slow down—*

He was not listening.

He was not thinking.

He was not getting stronger. That was the thing nobody watching would understand. Nothing new was coming. Nothing he had not had yesterday. It was all the same. Everything that had been in his walls since the Muslin, since the clearing. Everything the Noise had built and kept and folded away, everything he had held shut every single day by choosing to, the way you hold a door shut against a wind.

He had stopped holding.

That was all.

He let go.

* * *

His shadow moved.

It had been lying across the road from his knees, long in the last light. Now it was not lying. It was rising at the edges. Shivering. Spreading. Going out across the brown road in every direction at once, wider than any shadow should go, darker than the dusk, and things moved in it that nobody standing on that road had any word for.

His left eye opened.

Under the hair he kept over it. The sclera went black. The iris went red. The valve, the one he kept closed, the one Marin had made a dark blue patch for with a small star on it. Open.

The tremor became a shudder. The north gate's hung leaf slammed against its frame. Somewhere far behind the lime, glass cracked in a window.

The demons had stopped smiling a long time ago.

One of them took a step back.

* * *

"Nobody move," said Mau.

He did not shout it. He said it quietly. Into Frieren's hair. Without lifting his head.

Nobody moved.

The four demons stood on the road like four statues. The big devil behind them stood with its wrong shoulders hunched and did not breathe. The tall devil at the edge of the cut woods stood among the rows it had made and did not take another step.

They were cold. All of them. Mau could feel it coming off them. The particular cold of things that had walked a long way to wake something up, and had woken it, and had only now understood what it was.

* * *

The demon on the left end of the line had come furthest. It was the proudest of them. Mau had felt that when he had his arm round its neck. A little too sure. A little too pleased.

It was the first to find its voice.

"It doesn't matter," it said. Too loudly. Its warm voice gone thin and high. "It's done. The elf was weak, she was always—"

It did not finish the word.

One moment Mau was kneeling on the road with Frieren in his arms.

The next moment, nothing about that had changed. He was still kneeling. She was still in his arms. His head was still down.

And the demon on the left end of the line was standing in exactly the same place, with its mouth still open on the last word, and half of it was not there.

Not cut. Not struck. Not there. From the left shoulder to the right hip, the way the line had gone across Frieren, half of the demon was simply missing, as if it had never been. The other half stood a moment longer, surprised.

Then it went to ash.

The way demons go. All at once. Grey dust, falling straight down onto the road in the still air, in a small neat pile, with nothing left on top of it.

Nobody had seen him move.

He had not moved. Not in any way that any of them could see. The way Diablo, at the foot of Rimuru's chair, sometimes was in one place and then in another without ever having been between them.

* * *

"Frieren," said Mau.

He was still saying it. He had not stopped. Under everything. Under the shaking ground and the silence and the ash.

"Frieren. Frieren. Frieren."

He put his hand flat on her chest. Over the long open line. His hand was shaking. Everything was shaking.

He had seen Shoko do it. Fifty times. A hundred. In the clinic, with her coffee going cold. He had seen Yuta do it, on the bay night, for two days. The light that is not light. The thing that goes the other way. *Reversal.* He had felt it go into himself.

He pushed.

It came. Something came. Rough. Wrong-handed. Not like Shoko's, smooth and patient and exactly the right amount. It came out of his hand in a hot crooked rush, the way water comes out of a pipe that has burst, and went into her.

The bleeding slowed.

It did not stop.

"No," said Mau. "No. Come on. *Come on.*"

He pushed harder. It went wrong. He felt it go wrong, spill sideways, miss. The line across her chest would not close. It was as if the cut was still happening. As if it had not finished.

Her eyes were closing.

"*Frieren.*"

Shoko.

The thought came from somewhere under all of it. The one clear thing in a shaking world. *Shoko.* The clinic. The room with the white cloth and the coffee and the hands that knew how.

He did not decide.

He was gone.

* * *

On the north road, the dust settled.

The ground stopped shaking. The north gate's hung leaf swung once more on its hinge and was still. The crickets did not start again.

Three demons and two devils stood in the near dark, on a road with a small grey pile of ash on it and a long dark stain where an elf had fallen, and a staff lying across the ruts.

The boy was gone.

None of them moved.

He had told them not to.
