# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 29
## Chapter 55 — Take Care of It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 29
**Reading order:** 55
**Continuity state:** Day 328, dusk, inside the house, mostly from Shoko's point of view. The house shakes and everyone feels a pressure, as if something long asleep had woken and were about to destroy everything. Those who were there remember Day 145 in the south field. By the old leak protocol, Gojo and Yuta go to the lime and Maki takes the children to the far rooms. Ciel tells Rimuru that Mau is not answering, and Rimuru goes. Mau appears in the clinic with Frieren cut and bleeding, lays her down, says only "Take care of it", and is gone. The tremor and the pressure go with him. Shoko and Maomao work: Shoko can keep Frieren alive, but the cut will not close under her hands ("like the cut is still happening"). Fern arrives and does not leave

*When: Day 328, dusk, the same minutes. In the clinic, Shoko has just poured the last cup from the evening pot. Maomao is labelling jars. Across the yard, Taihei is lifting the lid off a pan of rice.*

The cup moved first.

Shoko watched it. On the clinic table, beside the jar Maomao had just labelled, the coffee in her cup shivered. A ring went out across the surface. Then another. Then the cup began, very slightly, to walk across the wood.

Maomao looked up from the jars.

"Earthquake," she said.

"No," said Shoko.

She knew it was not. She had been in this house on the day the south field went white and the east window cracked. She had stood in the clinic door and felt the air press down on her like a hand. She had treated what was left afterward, and held a cold cloth on a forehead, and watched an elf climb into the clinic bed beside him without asking anybody's permission.

She knew this.

The jars began to rattle on the shelf.

* * *

Then the pressure came.

It came down over the whole house at once. Shoko felt it in her teeth. In the back of her neck. In the place behind her eyes where her own technique lived. A weight. Not heavy, exactly. Old. As if something that had been sleeping for a very long time underneath everything had opened its eyes, and was looking, and had not yet decided what to do with what it saw.

Every hair on her arms stood up.

Maomao had gone white. She put both hands flat on the table. She had been in the house on the day of the south field too.

"The south field," she said. Very quietly.

"Him," said Shoko.

* * *

Through the clinic door, across the yard, the house was moving.

Not running. The house had a protocol for this, written down after the plaza and written down again after the south field, and everyone who had been here long enough knew it in their legs. Maki came out of the back door with Ori by one hand and Yuka by the other, and Milim floating behind on the ribbon. Maki took them toward the far rooms, quickly, without a word. Ori was looking back over her shoulder at the north end.

Gojo came out of the house in his shirtsleeves with his blindfold already up. He did not run either. He walked very fast toward the north end of the lime, and Yuta was beside him, katana in hand, his face exactly like it had been on the bay night.

Rimuru came out after them.

He did not walk. He went. One moment he was on the back step with Diablo behind him, and his face was turned inward, the way it turned when Ciel was speaking to him, and it was a face Shoko had not seen him make before. The next moment he was simply gone in the direction of the north road.

*He's not answering,* Rimuru had said, to nobody, as he went. *Ciel says he's not answering.*

The ground shook harder. Somewhere above, a pane of glass cracked with a sound like ice.

* * *

Then he was there.

In the clinic. Between one breath and the next.

Shoko had not heard the door. There was no door. He was simply standing in the middle of the clinic floor, in the space between the table and the bed, where there had been nothing.

He had Frieren in his arms.

Shoko had seen a great many bad things in her life. In her own world, more than anyone should. She had learned a long time ago to look at the wound first and the person second, because the wound was the part you could do something about.

She looked at the wound.

From the left shoulder to the right hip. A single long line. Straight. Clean. Too clean. Through the coat and through the skin and through everything under the skin. The blood had soaked his shirt, his arms to the elbow, the front of his trousers. It was still coming.

Then she looked at him.

His face was white and perfectly still. His left eye was open under the hair. Black where it should be white. Red where it should be brown. Something was coming off him that made the air bend. Something like heat, but cold.

He was not looking at Shoko. He was not looking at anything.

He laid Frieren on the clinic bed. Gently. So gently. As if she were made of something that would break if he breathed on it. He put her head on the pillow. He moved one strand of her hair off her face.

Then he looked at Shoko.

Shoko had looked at Gojo Satoru's bare eyes across a classroom when she was sixteen. She had looked at things in her own world that had no names. She had never been looked at like this. There was nobody behind it. Nobody at all.

"Take care of it," said Mau.

And he was gone.

* * *

The shaking stopped.

All at once. The jars stopped rattling. The cup stopped walking. The pressure lifted off the whole house like a hand being taken away, and the air came back, and somewhere a bird started singing in the dark as if nothing had happened.

Shoko did not waste a second of it.

"Cloth," she said. "All of it. Water. Hot. Now."

Maomao was already moving.

* * *

Shoko put her hands on the line.

She did what she always did. The thing she had done ten thousand times. She went in. Not with her eyes. With the other thing, the thing behind her eyes, the thing that turned cursed energy over and made it into something that put bodies back together. She found the edges of the cut. She found the vessels. She found the place under the breastbone where the line went deepest.

She pushed.

It went in. The light that is not light. Smooth, patient, exactly the right amount.

The bleeding slowed.

Shoko kept pushing. The edges of the wound should have started to draw together under her hands. They always did. That was what it was for.

They did not.

She pushed harder. She felt the tissue take her energy, take it and hold it, and then—let it go. As if something underneath kept opening what she closed. As if the cut were not a thing that had happened, but a thing that was still happening, very slowly, in the same place, over and over.

"It won't close," said Shoko.

Maomao was beside her with the cloths. She looked at the wound. Her face did something Shoko had never seen it do.

"Why?"

"I don't know." Shoko did not take her hands away. "I can hold her. I can keep the blood in her. I can keep her heart going." She pushed again. The edges took it and let it go. "I can't close it. It's like the cut's still happening."

* * *

Fern came through the clinic door.

She had run. Fern never ran. Her hair had come half out of its tie and she was breathing hard and she stopped in the doorway and saw the bed.

She did not scream. She did not say anything at all.

She came across the clinic and knelt on the floor at the head of the bed and took Frieren's hand in both of hers. Frieren's fingers were cold. Fern held them against her own cheek.

"Master," she said. Very quietly. "Frieren-sama."

Frieren did not answer. Her eyes were closed. Her breathing was very small.

"She's alive," said Shoko. Without looking up. "I've got her. I'm not letting go."

"Where's Mau?"

Shoko did not answer.

Maomao did. Her voice was perfectly steady. Her hands were not.

"He said *take care of it*," she said. "And then he went back."

Fern looked at the clinic door. At the dark yard beyond it, and the north end of the lime, and the road.

She did not let go of Frieren's hand.

"Then we take care of it," said Fern.

* * *

Outside, at the north end of the lime, Gojo had stopped walking.

He stood at the line with his blindfold up and his bare eyes on the north road, a long way out, where the dark had gone very still. Yuta stood beside him with his katana drawn.

"Can you see him?" said Yuta.

Gojo did not answer for a long moment.

"Yes," he said.

"What's he doing?"

Gojo's face did not move.

"Don't go out there," he said. "Whatever you do. Don't go past the line."

"Satoru—"

"Everything out there is standing very still," said Gojo. Very quietly. "Everything. Like it's been told to." He did not look away. "I don't think he'd know us right now."
