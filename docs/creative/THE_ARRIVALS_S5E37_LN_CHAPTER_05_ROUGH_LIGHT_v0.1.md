*When: Day 328, night. Twenty minutes since the first tremor. In the clinic, Shoko has been holding the same line closed with her hands the entire time, and her arms have begun to shake. At the north end of the lime, Gojo has said "He's coming back" and nothing else.*

Rimuru met him halfway.

On the dark ground between the north road and the lime, where the grass gave way to dust. Rimuru had gone out as fast as he could go, which was very fast, with Ciel saying the same thing over and over inside him in a voice that was nothing like level. *He isn't answering. Rimuru, he isn't answering me. He's never not answered me. The walls are open, all of them, I can't—*

And then, halfway: *They're closing.*

And then: *He's coming back.*

And there he was.

* * *

He was walking.

That was the first thing Rimuru understood, and then immediately the first thing he understood was wrong. Mau was walking toward the house across the dark ground with Frieren's staff in his right hand and his arms dark to the elbow, and his feet were moving, step after step, the ordinary way.

But he was not where his feet were.

He would take a step, and be there. And take another step, and be twenty paces further on, between one footfall and the next, with nothing in between. Then three ordinary steps. Then forty paces gone in a blink. Then two steps. Like a lantern carried past a row of windows, seen and not seen and seen again.

And the light.

It was coming off him. Not much. A thin pale haze round the edges of him, the way heat comes off a road at noon, and under it, in it, every so often, a flicker of blue. Of red. Of something white. Coming out, and being caught, and pulled back in. Held. Over and over. As if he were carrying a bucket too full to carry and spilling a little with every step, and each time it spilled, he caught it.

Leaks. Rimuru knew leaks. Ciel had a list of them pinned in his head. The spoon. The bucket at the well. The door of light on the lake.

This was all of them at once, being held.

* * *

"Mau," said Rimuru.

He came up beside him. Matched his pace, when there was a pace to match. Mau flickered twenty paces ahead. Rimuru went after him.

"Mau. It's me."

Mau did not look at him.

He was looking at the house. Not at the house. Through it. His eyes were fixed on a point past the lime, past the yard, past the clinic wall, and they were not his eyes. Not quite. The left one was closed now, under his hair. The right one was open and fixed and very wide, and something in it was doing what Gojo's eyes did when the blindfold came up. Looking through things. Walls. Stone. Distance.

Rimuru did not have to ask what he was looking at.

"She's alive," said Rimuru. "Ciel can feel Shoko working. She's holding her. Mau. She's alive."

Nothing.

"Mau, talk to me. Please. Just—say something. Anything."

Nothing. Two steps. Forty paces gone.

Rimuru stopped trying to keep up.

He stood on the dark ground between the road and the house and watched Mau go, flickering, toward the lime, with the pale haze round him and the light spilling and being caught, and his eyes fixed through the walls on one thing.

*Ciel,* said Rimuru. *Is he in there?*

Ciel was quiet for a long moment.

*He's in there,* she said. *He's just not here.*

* * *

In the clinic, Shoko's arms were shaking.

She had been holding the line for twenty minutes. Holding it closed. Feeding it. Feeling it take what she gave and let it go, take it and let it go, like a tide that never went out. Frieren's breathing had stayed small and shallow the whole time, but it had stayed. That was all Shoko had. That it had stayed.

Maomao was beside her with the cloths, changing them. Fern was on the floor at the head of the bed with Frieren's hand against her cheek. None of them had said anything for a long time.

Then Mau was there.

Beside Shoko. Close enough to touch. Without a sound.

Maomao dropped a cloth. Shoko flinched so hard she nearly lost the line.

"*Mau*—"

He did not look at her.

He was looking at Frieren.

* * *

"Mau," said Shoko. "Mau, listen to me. She's alive. I've got her. But it won't close, I can't make it close, it's like—"

He did not hear her.

Maomao tried. "Mau. Look at me. *Look at me.*"

He did not look at her.

He stood beside the bed with the pale haze round him and Frieren's staff still in his hand, and his eyes on the long line across her chest, and Shoko, this close, saw something she would not be able to describe afterward, not to Gojo, not to anyone.

Something was turning inside him.

Not his shadow. Not anything she could see with her eyes. With the other sense. The one behind her eyes. Inside his chest, under the bloody shirt, something was turning. Slowly. Round and round. Several somethings. Like wheels inside a clock, or leaves on the surface of a slow pool, turning round each other, and never quite meeting.

She had never felt anything like it.

He put the staff down. Leaned it against the bed, carefully, where Frieren could reach it.

Then he put his hand on her chest.

* * *

The light that came out of his hand was not Shoko's.

Shoko knew her own. Smooth. Patient. The exact amount, given at the exact rate, the way you pour water into a cup without spilling. She had spent her whole life learning to make it that way.

His was nothing like that.

It came out of him rough and hot and crooked, too much and too fast, the way it had come on the road. It spilled. It went sideways. It missed. It was wrong-handed, the way something is wrong-handed when you have only ever watched it done and never been taught. Shoko could see him trying to do it the way she did. She could see him remembering her hands. It did not come out that way.

But it came.

And it did not stop.

The light came off his hand and did not go into the wound the way hers had. It went *round* her. It spread out over Frieren's chest and her shoulders and her arms, a pale wavering glow, wrapping round her the way you wrap someone in a blanket when they are cold. Holding her. All of her.

Shoko took her hands away from the line.

She had to. There was no room. She stepped back, and Maomao stepped back with her, and they stood a pace from the bed and watched the light hold Frieren.

* * *

It was not working.

Shoko could see that it was not working. It was holding her, but it was not closing anything. It was too wild. It was going everywhere at once. The line across Frieren's chest glowed under it and did not close.

Mau made a sound. Small. Not a word.

Shoko stepped forward again.

She did not ask. She put her own hands over his, on Frieren's chest. Over the back of his hand, her palms on his knuckles. And she went in. Not into Frieren. Into the light. Into his.

It was like putting her hands into a river.

She did not try to stop it. She did not try to make it hers. She did what she would have done for a student, a long time ago, in another world, if she had ever been allowed to have one. She found the edges of it. She found where it was spilling. And she leaned on it, a little, there, and there, the way you put your hands on someone's shoulders to steady them, not to push.

*Slower,* she thought at it. *Narrower. Here. Not everywhere. Here.*

It listened.

Not because he heard her. He did not hear anything. But the light under her hands steadied. Narrowed. Found the line.

And Shoko felt why hers had not worked.

The cut was still happening. She had been right. It was not a wound that had been made. It was a wound that was still being made, very slowly, in the same place, over and over, because whatever had made it had told the space there to come apart and the space had not finished coming apart yet. She had been trying to close something that had not stopped opening.

His light did not try to close it.

His light had the same shape.

She could feel it. Under the roughness and the heat and the wrongness, there was a shape in his light that matched the shape of the cut. The same line. As if whatever was in him had seen that line before, a long time ago, and kept it, and knew it. And because it knew it, it did not fight it. It went along it, the whole length of it, from shoulder to hip.

And let it finish.

The cut stopped happening.

And then Shoko's part could work.

* * *

The line closed.

From the shoulder down. Slowly. Under both their hands. The edges drawing together the way they should have twenty minutes ago, the way they always did, the way a wound is supposed to when it has finally been allowed to be only a wound. Down across her chest. Down to the right side. Down to the end.

The light went out.

What was left was a scar. Shoko looked at it. She had expected a long one. A line from the shoulder to the hip, white and raised and terrible.

It was not that.

It was small. Hardly anything. A short pale mark in the middle of Frieren's chest, just over her heart, no longer than a finger joint. As if all the rest of the line had healed clean, and only that one place had needed to keep something to remember it by.

Frieren breathed.

Not small. Not shallow. A long, slow, full breath, all the way in, and all the way out. Her chest rose under the torn coat. Fell. Rose again.

Fern made a sound and put her face down against the edge of the bed.

* * *

Mau's hand was still on Frieren's chest.

It stayed there through one breath. Then another. Shoko kept her hands over his. She felt the turning inside him slow. Slow. Settle.

The pale haze round him thinned. Went out.

He blinked.

It was like watching someone come up out of deep water. His shoulders came down. His mouth opened. He took a breath of his own, a huge ragged gasping one, as if he had not taken one since the road.

And he heard.

Shoko saw him hear. She saw the clinic come back into his eyes. The table, the jars, the cloths on the floor. Maomao, white-faced, a pace away. Fern with her face against the bed. Shoko's hands over his.

"Mau," said Shoko. Gently. "Mau. Can you hear me?"

He looked at her. Really looked. For the first time.

"She's healed," said Shoko. "It's closed. She's breathing. She's going to sleep for a while, and then she's going to wake up." She did not let go of his hand. "Are you all right? Are you hurt? Do you need anything?"

He looked at Frieren. At the small pale mark over her heart. At her chest, rising and falling.

"No," said Mau.

His voice was a scrape. Hardly anything.

"I just want to be next to her."
