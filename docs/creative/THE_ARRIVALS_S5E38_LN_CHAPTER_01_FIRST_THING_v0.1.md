# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 1 — First Thing

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 1
**Continuity state:** V30 breather opening, the morning of the festival. Ori tells the house at breakfast as she claimed on the night of the last firefly; Fern turns the festival page into a day's list; Bocchi learns it is tonight; the house divides the work without anyone being assigned to stop living; no threat

*When: Day 337, breakfast. The fireflies went out last night. Ori has been awake since before the bread, sitting on the end of the long bench with her hands folded, waiting for the room to fill.*

Ori was first.

Not first to wake. Momo was always first to wake, and this morning Taihei had been second, standing at the left end of the long counter with his sleeves rolled, and the bread was already in when Ori came down the stairs in her nightdress with her shoes in her hand. She sat on the end of the long bench and folded her hands and did not say anything at all.

Momo looked at her.

"You're early."

"I'm waiting," said Ori.

"For what?"

"For everyone," said Ori. "I have to tell everyone. First thing." She looked at the door to the yard, and at the stair, and at the corridor to the west wing. "Before anyone else does."

Momo looked at Taihei. Taihei looked at the bread.

"Nobody else knows what it is," said Momo.

"Nazuna knows," said Ori. "But she's asleep. And she said I could."

Momo put a cup of milk down in front of her and went back to the oven, and did not ask anything else, because she had lived with this house long enough to know that when a child sat very straight on the end of a bench at dawn with her hands folded, the news was coming whether you asked or not.

* * *

They came down one at a time.

Fern first, with the ledger under her arm, because Fern always came down with the ledger. She saw Ori and stopped.

"Good morning."

"Good morning," said Ori. "Sit down, please. I'm waiting until there are more."

Fern sat down. She opened the ledger. She did not write anything. She had seen Ori's face.

Then Stark, yawning, and Anko from the night round with lime on her boots, and Senku with a pencil already behind his ear, and Byakuya behind him with a book. Yuta and Maki, and Yuka between them, still half asleep. Umaru in her hood and sash. Momo's back to the room, very busy with something that did not need to be so busy.

Milim came down the stairs without touching any of them, a hand's breadth off the boards, and landed beside Ori on the bench and put her chin on Ori's shoulder.

"Did you tell?"

"Not yet."

"Can I tell?"

"No," said Ori. "Yuka had to put her hand over your mouth last night. I get to tell."

Milim considered this, and found it fair, and sat on her own hands.

Kessoku came in together, because they had been up late in the music room, and Ryo went straight to the bread. Bocchi sat at the far end, small, with her hood half up.

Coco and the Witch Hat girls. Qifrey. Coco's mother, who now came to breakfast every morning and sat by the window and liked to watch the light come into the yard. Marin, talking. Wakana, not talking, with sawdust still on his sleeve from yesterday. The five, all at once, in a cluster, arguing about something. Seiko, slowly, to the high seat, with Turbo Granny in her lucky cat under one arm making remarks. Gojo, late, with his blindfold on and Shoko a step behind him with her coffee.

Rimuru came down the stair from the east corridor in human shape, with his hair still flat on one side.

And last, Mau and Frieren. She had his hand. She let it go at the bottom of the stair, the way she did in the mornings, and then took it back again halfway to the table, which was new this summer and which nobody mentioned anymore.

They saw Ori and stopped.

Ori looked at the room.

It was nearly full. Not quite. Nazuna was asleep. Diablo was on the step outside, where he liked to stand in the mornings. Eren and Mikasa were not down yet. Kaneki had gone to bed after his shift. Rem and Ram were in the kitchen doorway, Rem with a tray and Ram with nothing, supervising.

It was enough.

Ori stood up on the bench.

Nobody told her to get down. Seiko opened her mouth, and then closed it, and folded her hands on the head of her stick.

"The fireflies are gone," said Ori.

The room went quiet.

"Last night," said Ori. "The last one. Nazuna counted. It went up over the north field and it didn't come down." She swallowed. "Frieren said, when the fireflies are gone, the house lights its own. And Fern wrote it in the ledger. *Ask Nazuna.* And we asked Nazuna, and Nazuna said."

She looked at Fern.

Fern turned the ledger round, very slowly, so the room could see the festival page, half full in her small careful hand, with the line near the top that said *When the fireflies are gone. (Ask Nazuna.)*

"So it's tonight," said Ori. "The festival. Tonight."

* * *

For a moment nobody said anything.

Then Umaru stood up on her own bench, because she was not going to be the only Ministry official seated during a proclamation, and said, "The Ministry confirms," very loudly, and the room broke.

Everyone talked at once.

Senku had his pencil out. "Tonight. *Tonight.* I said forty lamps, I have twenty-two finished, I need the oil out of the store by noon—" Wakana was already standing up. Shizuka put down her spoon, very carefully, and looked at Nano, and Nano looked back at her, and both of them were thinking about the paper lanterns in the ink tree, eleven still to fold. Hakari said, "Lights in the trees," with enormous satisfaction, as if she had arranged the death of every firefly personally. Kusuri said, "My stall," and then, "Maomao," and then, "Where's Maomao," and Maomao said from the clinic door, "Here. Supervising. Sit down."

Marin had both hands over her mouth.

"The dress," she said through them. "It's finished. It's *finished*, I finished it three days ago, I didn't think it'd be—"

Wakana looked at her. Then away, very quickly. He went red to the ears.

At the far end of the table, Bocchi had gone perfectly white.

"Tonight," she whispered.

Kita, beside her, put a hand flat on her back.

"Tonight," said Kita.

"I thought there'd be—I thought we'd have—" Bocchi's voice went up and up. "Warning. A day. A *warning* day."

"Last night was the warning," said Ryo, through bread.

"I was *asleep*."

"You were awake until two," said Nijika. "Playing the slow part. Over and over. You were warning yourself."

Bocchi put her face down on the table. Kita kept her hand where it was. Seika, across from them, did not say anything at all, which was how Seika said: *you'll be fine*.

Mau looked across the room at Bocchi with her face on the table. She lifted her head, just enough, and found him. He nodded once. *Close. Where you can see me.* She put her face down again, but her shoulders came down a little.

* * *

Fern did not stand up. She did not need to.

She picked up her pen, and tapped it once on the edge of the ledger, and the long table went quiet the way a long table did when it had learned, over two years and a bit, that the tap meant Fern had made a list.

"Lamps," said Fern. "Senku, Wakana. Oil from the store, measured; Anko signs it out." She did not look up. "Paper lanterns in the ink tree: Shizuka, Nano, and anyone Shizuka chooses. Ask Shizuka. Not me."

Shizuka looked at the table, and then at Nano, and then, after a long moment, at Hakari and Karane. Hakari punched the air.

"Food," said Fern. "Momo decides. Momo has decided?"

"Momo decided a week ago," said Momo, without turning round from the oven.

"The fort's stand," said Fern.

Umaru stood up again.

"The President formally requests," said Umaru, "that the fort's stand be permitted to serve curry. Prepared by the President's brother. At the left end of the counter." She paused. "Subject to Momo."

Everyone looked at Momo.

Momo turned round. She looked at Taihei, who had stopped moving entirely, with the bread knife in his hand.

"One pot," said Momo. "The big one, not the biggest. Not before three. You clean the pot. And you don't make it so good that nobody eats anything else."

Taihei opened his mouth. Shut it.

"I'll try," he said, "to make it slightly worse."

Momo considered him.

"See that you do," she said, and turned back to the oven, and Umaru sat down very fast with her hood over her face so that nobody could see her expression.

"Kusuri's stall," said Fern. "Supervised."

"Supervised," said Maomao, from the door.

"Music," said Fern, and looked down the table at Kessoku.

Bocchi lifted her face off the table.

"At the end," she said. Very small. "When the lights are all lit. The—the last one is ours."

"The last one is theirs," said Seika, before anybody could negotiate.

Fern wrote it down.

"Lights," said Fern, and looked at Coco, and at Qifrey, and did not say anything else at all. The Witch Hat girls looked at each other. Qifrey put down his cup.

"Where Rimuru approves," he said. "Only there. And nobody asks how."

"Nobody asks how," said Rimuru, from the stair. "I've already approved. Over the yard. Not past the lime. Nothing that burns." He looked at Coco. "I'd like to see them again."

Coco went pink, and looked at her mother by the window, and her mother smiled.

* * *

Fern closed the ledger.

"Everyone else," she said, "goes about their day."

The table looked at her.

"It's a festival," said Fern. "Not a siege. Nobody is assigned to it who didn't ask. Stark is going to sleep after lunch because he was on the gate rota last night, and if anyone wakes him for a lamp, they answer to me." Stark, beside her, looked at her with an expression of enormous and silent gratitude. "Nazuna wakes at dusk. Nobody wakes her early either. The fireflies were hers."

She looked at Ori, still standing on the bench.

"Is that right?"

Ori looked at the room. At all of them. At the festival page, which Fern had written in for weeks, now turned into a day.

"That's right," said Ori.

She got down off the bench. She sat. She drank her milk, all of it, without stopping, and put the cup down, and wiped her mouth with the back of her hand.

Then she looked at Frieren, across the table, very serious.

"I told them first," she said.

"You did," said Frieren.

"Before anyone else."

"Before anyone else."

Ori nodded, satisfied. Then she got up and went straight out into the yard, still in her nightdress, toward the ink tree and its pale unlit lanterns, with Milim floating behind her and Yuka calling after both of them about shoes.

Frieren watched her go.

Mau, beside her, watched Frieren.

"Tonight," he said quietly.

"Tonight," said Frieren.

She did not let go of his hand under the table for the rest of breakfast. Seiko, from the high seat, saw that, and said nothing, and drank her tea, and looked extremely pleased with herself for no reason she was prepared to explain.
