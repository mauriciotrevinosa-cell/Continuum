# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 5 — Fortunes

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 5
**Continuity state:** Kusuri's supervised herb-fortune stall at the festival. Maomao checks every bundle and allows nothing to be eaten; the fortunes are gentle and funny, not prophecy; Kaneki stops by before his shift; Gojo and Shoko, Eren and Mikasa, and Mau and Frieren draw; Seiko declines ("I make them"); no plot decision

*When: Day 337, early evening, under the lit ink tree. Maomao has a list of every herb on the table and has initialled each one. Kusuri has a list of what each herb means and has initialled nothing.*

Kusuri's stall was a table from the workshop with a cloth over it and a sign that said, in her round careful hand, FORTUNES BY HERB. And underneath, in Maomao's sharp one: SUPERVISED. NOTHING IS FOR EATING.

On the table, in rows, were little bundles of dried herbs tied with thread. Each one different. Each one about as long as a finger. You did not choose. You closed your eyes and put your hand in the basket, and whatever you drew, Kusuri told you what it meant.

"It's very old," said Kusuri, to everyone who stopped. "Where I'm from. Every plant has a meaning. Not a magic one. A meaning people agreed on, a long time ago, so they could give each other flowers and say things without having to say them." She held up a sprig. "Like this. This is rosemary. It means remembrance."

"And what does it mean if you eat it?" said Hakari.

"Nothing," said Maomao, from her stool. "You don't eat it. That's the rule."

"But what if—"

"Then it means you're going to the clinic," said Maomao, "and I'm going to be very disappointed in you."

Hakari put her hand in the basket with great care, as if the basket might be listening.

* * *

She drew thyme.

"Courage," said Kusuri.

Hakari turned round to Karane with enormous satisfaction. Karane, very red, put her own hand in the basket, without being asked, and drew the same thing.

Kusuri looked at the two sprigs of thyme.

"That's a coincidence," said Kusuri, very carefully.

"There are eleven thyme in there," said Maomao, without looking up. "I counted."

"It's still a coincidence."

Karane put her sprig in her pocket very fast. Hakari waved hers in the air and went off to show Shizuka, who wrote *Further observation required* on her page without looking up from her folding, and turned the page round, and went back to work.

* * *

People came all evening.

Momo drew basil, which meant *good wishes*, and said that was a very kitchen sort of fortune and she was satisfied. Okarun drew mint, which meant *protection* and also *suspicion*, depending on the book, and Jiji said, "Suspicion. Definitely suspicion," and Okarun said, "Shut up," and Momo laughed so hard she had to put her plate down.

Marin drew lavender, which meant *devotion*, and turned round and showed it to Wakana in his blue yukata, and Wakana went so red that Kusuri offered to draw him one too, out of mercy. He drew sage. *Wisdom.* Marin said that was the least surprising fortune she had ever seen in her life.

Fern drew chamomile. *Patience in adversity.* She looked at it for a long time. Then at Stark, who was asleep in a chair at the end of the long table with a plate of curry balanced on his knee and his mouth open.

"Yes," said Fern. "That seems right."

Stark did not wake.

Gojo came with Shoko.

He made a great show of it. He pushed his blindfold up so he could see the basket, which made Kusuri go pink and Maomao narrow her eyes. He rolled up his sleeve. He wiggled his fingers.

"No looking," said Maomao.

"I'm not looking."

"You're always looking. That's your whole thing. Close them."

Gojo closed his eyes, with a long-suffering sigh. Shoko, beside him, put her hand over them, to be sure. He put his hand in the basket and rummaged far too long and drew out a sprig of something pale and feathery.

Kusuri looked at it.

"Yarrow," she said.

"And?"

"It's a healing herb," said Kusuri. "For wounds. Old soldiers used to carry it." She paused. "In the flower meanings, it means *a cure for the heart.*"

Gojo did not say anything for a second. Shoko's hand was still over his eyes. She did not take it away.

"Well," said Gojo. "That's a bit much for a festival."

"I don't make them up," said Kusuri. "They're very old."

Shoko took her hand away. She did not look at Gojo. She put her own hand in the basket, without closing her eyes, because nobody was going to tell her to, and drew a sprig and looked at it and put it in her coat pocket without showing anyone.

"What was it?" said Gojo.

"Mine," said Shoko.

Kusuri, who had seen, did not say anything either. She looked at the table, very busily, and rearranged three bundles that did not need rearranging.

* * *

Eren and Mikasa came together, which they did now, at most things.

Eren drew something small and green and looked at it suspiciously, as if it might be a trap.

"Parsley," said Kusuri. "That one means *festivity*." She paused. "And also *a feast*. It's mostly for cooking."

Eren looked at the parsley.

"That's it?" he said. "Festivity?"

"That's it."

He looked at it a long time. Then at the lit ink tree, and the drifting silver lights, and the curry, and Mikasa beside him.

"Okay," said Eren, and put it in his pocket, and did not take it out again all night.

Mikasa drew rosemary. *Remembrance.* She held it in her open palm and did not say what she remembered, and Eren, beside her, did not ask. But after a moment he put his hand under hers, so that the sprig was lying in two palms instead of one.

* * *

Kaneki came by just before his shift.

He did not usually come to things. He worked the night, at the kilns, and on a night like this he would be starting just as everyone else was finishing. But he had come out at dusk to see the lanterns go on, and stayed a little, at the edge, with a cup of something, and on his way to the timber yard he passed the stall.

Kusuri looked up at him.

"One?" she said. "Before you go?"

Kaneki hesitated.

Then he put his hand in the basket, without closing his eyes, the way Shoko had, and drew.

He drew two.

Two little bundles, tied with the same thread, stuck together where the dried stems had caught. Kusuri stared at them. Maomao, on her stool, leaned forward and looked too.

"That's two," said Kusuri.

"They stuck," said Maomao. "The thread caught. It happens."

"It's never happened."

"It's happened now."

Kaneki was looking at the two bundles in his hand.

"What are they?" he said.

Kusuri looked. She looked again, more carefully.

"Honeysuckle," she said. "Both of them." She paused. "It means *the bond of love*. Or *devoted affection*. People used to plant it by the door, so it would grow over the house." She looked up at him. "I'm sorry. I don't know why there are two. I tie them one at a time."

Kaneki did not say anything for a long moment.

Then he put both of them in his breast pocket, carefully, still stuck together.

"Thank you," he said, and went off toward the timber yard and the kilns. He did not look back.

Maomao watched him go. Then she looked at the basket, and at her list, and made a small mark next to *honeysuckle*, and did not say what it meant.

* * *

Mau and Frieren came last.

They had been walking. Round the yard, under the lamps, past the curry, slowly, the way they walked now when nobody needed them anywhere. Frieren had a small bowl of the curry, which she was eating very slowly, because Taihei had not made it slightly worse at all and everyone knew it, including Momo.

"Fortune?" said Kusuri.

Frieren put her hand in the basket without looking and drew a sprig of something with small grey leaves.

"Sage," said Kusuri. "Wisdom." She paused. "And also *a long life*."

Frieren looked at it.

"That seems unnecessary," she said.

Kusuri laughed before she could stop herself. Even Maomao's mouth moved.

Then Mau put his hand in.

He drew something that was not quite a herb. A sprig with small bright leaves and, at the end of it, one tiny dried flower, white, the size of a fingernail.

Kusuri looked at it for a long time.

"I don't know that one," she said. "That's not on my list." She looked at Maomao. "That's not on my list."

Maomao took it. She held it close to the lamp. She turned it over.

"It's a weed," she said. "From the north field. It must have got in with the yarrow." She handed it back to Mau. "It's not anything. It doesn't mean anything. Nobody agreed on it."

Mau looked at the small white flower in his hand.

"Then I'll keep it," he said.

Frieren, beside him, looked at the flower. She did not say anything. But he saw her eyes go to it, and stay, and he knew what she was thinking, because he was thinking it too. Of the five pressed flowers on the shelf in the east room. Of the fifth, from the date, which had come out of her pocket.

"It doesn't mean anything," said Maomao again, a little more firmly.

"Not yet," said Frieren.

She took his hand, the one without the flower, and they walked on under the ink tree.

* * *

Seiko came by much later, slowly, on her stick, with Turbo Granny under one arm complaining about the noise.

"Fortune, grandmother?" said Kusuri.

Seiko looked at the basket. Then at the girl behind it. Then across the yard, at Mau and Frieren under the lit tree, at their joined hands, at the small white flower Mau was still holding.

"No, dear," said Seiko. "I don't need them." She smiled. "At my age, I make them."

And she went back to the high seat, very pleased with herself, and drank her tea, and watched, and said nothing at all.

Yet.
