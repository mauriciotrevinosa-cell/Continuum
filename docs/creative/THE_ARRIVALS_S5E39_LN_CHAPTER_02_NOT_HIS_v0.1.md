# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 6 — Not His

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 6
**Continuity state:** Himmel's point of view at the festival (R49/R50 groundwork). He has long known and respected Mau and Frieren as a couple (S4 "Some Things Start", "Something Wet"); tonight he sees how easy they have become and feels a grief he thought he had put in order; he does not interrupt or claim anything; he dances with the fort; Mikasa mentions tomorrow's north-end hour, which plants his later offer; no confession, no departure

*When: Day 337, the middle of the festival. The curry pot is two-thirds empty. Himmel has re-brushed his hair twice since dusk, which is his usual number for a festival.*

Himmel loved festivals.

He had always loved them. In his own world, on the road, with the others, it had been the thing he looked forward to most. A village with lanterns strung across the square. A harvest dance. A market night with music. Eisen would grumble and Heiter would drink and Frieren would wander off to look for a grimoire nobody was selling, and Himmel would dance with every grandmother in the village. Then he would come back to the inn with his hair ruined and his feet aching, and he would be happy in a way that had nothing to do with demons or kings or the end of anything.

That was what a hero was for, he had always thought. Not the fighting. The fighting was just so there could be festivals.

He told the fort that, early in the evening, when Milim asked him why he was dancing with Seiko.

"Because she asked," said Himmel. "And because a festival is what we fight for."

Milim considered this, floating at the end of Ori's ribbon.

"I fight for snacks," she said.

"That's also valid."

"Will you dance with me?"

"I would be honoured."

He danced with Milim. Then with Ori, who was very serious about it and counted her steps aloud. Then with Yuka, who said she didn't know how and then turned out to know how perfectly well. Then with Umaru, who stepped on his feet on purpose and called it *Ministry protocol*. Then with all of the fort at once, in a circle, round the bottom of the lit ink tree, while Nijika kept time on an upturned bucket.

When the circle broke up, he was out of breath and laughing and his hair was ruined.

He went to the long table and sat down and brushed it.

* * *

He saw them from there.

He had not been looking. That was the truth. He had been looking at the lights, the silver ones Coco and her teacher had drawn, drifting over the yard in long slow curves. He was thinking that they looked a little like the stars over a place he had once camped with the others, the night before they reached the last of the mountains.

Then Frieren laughed.

He heard it across the yard. Not loud. Frieren did not laugh loud. But he would have known it anywhere, in any world, because there had been a whole decade when he had tried to make her do it. Ten years of terrible jokes, and showing off, and stopping at every festival. Not that laugh, exactly. He had heard her laugh, back then. Small, surprised laughs, at his poses and at Heiter, the kind you could pull out of her if you were patient and very silly.

This was a different one.

He looked before he could decide not to.

She was standing under the lit ink tree with Mau. He had said something. Himmel did not know what. She had her bowl of curry in one hand and his hand in the other, and she was laughing up at him with her whole face, easily. It came out of her without anyone having to work for it, the way water comes out of the ground when the ground is ready.

And Mau was looking down at her as if he had never seen anything better in his life.

Himmel looked away.

He looked at the lights. He looked at them very carefully. He picked out one, a small one, drifting over the clinic roof, and followed it with his eyes all the way to the edge of the lime, where it turned back, as if it knew.

He was fine.

He had known for a long time. Longer than anyone. He had known since the night he told her, and she said she didn't think she loved him like that, only that she had wanted more time with him. He had said that was all right. He had meant it. He still meant it. He had stood between his red columns at dawn and let her lean on him for the first time, and asked for nothing back. He had drunk the tea Mau brought him on the step and laughed with him about the cape.

None of that had been a lie.

It was only that he had thought it was finished. Put away. Folded up neatly and put on a shelf, the way you put away something you will never wear again but cannot quite give to anyone else.

And then she laughed like that, under a tree full of lanterns, and the shelf turned out to be lower than he thought.

* * *

"You've got curry on your cape."

Himmel looked up.

Mikasa was standing beside the long table with a cup in each hand. She held one out to him. Water.

"Thank you," said Himmel, and took it, and looked down at his cape, and found that he did have curry on it, a small yellow mark near the clasp. He had no idea when that had happened.

"Danced too hard," said Mikasa. It was not a question.

"A hero never dances too hard."

"Eren says that about fighting."

"Eren is wrong about fighting," said Himmel. "I'm right about dancing."

Mikasa almost smiled. She sat down on the bench beside him, which she did not usually do. Mikasa did not sit beside people very often, unless the people were Eren. She looked out at the yard. At the lit tree, and the drifting lights, and Eren on the other side of the yard trying to explain something to Senku with a great deal of hand movement.

"It's a good night," she said.

"It's a very good night."

She was quiet for a while.

"I have the north end tomorrow," she said. "My hour. In the afternoon." She turned the cup in her hands. "I always have it. Every day." She paused. "I was thinking, on a night like this, you want to sleep late. And then not get up. And then sit somewhere with someone and not stand anywhere at all."

Himmel looked at her.

Mikasa was not looking at him. She was looking across the yard at Eren.

"I won't skip it," she said. "I've skipped it once this summer. The day in the south field. It was—" She stopped. "It was a very good day. But I don't skip it. Someone has to stand there."

She drank her water.

Then she got up and went back across the yard to Eren, and put her hand on his arm in the middle of his explanation, and he stopped explaining and looked at her, and his whole face changed.

Himmel watched them.

*Someone has to stand there.*

He brushed his hair. Not because it needed it. Because his hands wanted something to do.

* * *

Later, Stark woke up.

He woke all at once, the way he always did, and nearly dropped the plate on his knee. He looked round wildly at the lanterns, and the lights, and the crowd, and Himmel at the other end of the bench.

"Did I miss it?" said Stark.

"Miss what?"

"The—" Stark looked round. "Whatever it was. I fell asleep. Fern said I could sleep after lunch. I didn't think it would be *this* long after lunch."

"You missed the lanterns going up," said Himmel. "And the lights. And the curry, mostly. And a great deal of dancing." He considered. "And the fortunes. Fern drew patience in adversity and looked straight at you."

Stark put his face in his hands.

Himmel laughed. He could not help it. It came out of him easily, the way laughter always had, and Stark looked up through his fingers, offended, and then started laughing too.

And for a while it was just that. Two people on a bench at the end of the long table under the lights, laughing, with curry on their clothes. Nothing heavier.

Himmel thought: *I'm all right.*

He thought it very firmly, the way you put your foot down on a stair in the dark.

Then Kessoku went to the corner by the workshop wall, and Bocchi sat down on an upturned crate with her guitar against her chest. Coco's lights drifted down low over the yard, the way Rimuru had told them to, to make a ceiling. Everyone turned.

Under the ink tree, Frieren and Mau turned too. Mau let go of Frieren's hand, gently, and said something to her. She nodded. He went across the yard toward the corner where Kessoku were, and stopped, close, at the front, where a girl on a crate could see him.

Frieren watched him go. Then she looked round, for somewhere to stand.

Her eyes passed over the long table. Over Himmel.

They stopped.

She did not come over. She did not need to. She looked at him across the yard, for a moment, with her face still bright from laughing, and nodded, very slightly. The way she nodded at people she had known a very long time. *There you are.*

Himmel nodded back.

Then Bocchi played the first note, alone, in the dark, and everybody stopped looking at anyone at all.
