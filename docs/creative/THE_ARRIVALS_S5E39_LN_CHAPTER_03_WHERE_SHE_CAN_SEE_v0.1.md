# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 7 — Where She Can See

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 7
**Continuity state:** R37 fulfilled. Kessoku close the festival with Bocchi's all-summer piece (V29 "The Song for the Lights": slow solo opening, then Kita, Nijika, Ryo, then the passage); Mau keeps his promise and stands close where she can see him; she looks at Kita mostly and at him once; Seika lets it be Bocchi's; the song is named afterwards by Bocchi; no power, no threat

*When: Day 337, late, the last thing of the festival. The curry pot is empty and Taihei is washing it, as agreed. Coco's lights have come down low over the yard to make a ceiling.*

The corner by the workshop wall had been the music corner all evening.

Nijika's bucket. Ryo's bass, leaning on the wall. Kita's guitar case open on the ground with nothing in it, because Ryo had said a case should be open at a performance and then been told that nobody here used money. Two lamps on posts. A crate for Bocchi to sit on, because she had said she could not play this one standing up, and Seika had said then she would sit, and that was the end of it.

They had played earlier. Three songs, in the middle of the evening, while people ate. Loud ones, fast ones, the kind that made the fort dance and Milim fly in circles until Yuka caught her ribbon. Bocchi had played those standing up, at the back, behind Kita, looking at the ground, and nobody had minded, because the songs had been very good and the ground had been very interesting.

This one was different.

Mau had stopped at the front.

Not in the crowd. Close. At the edge of the lamplight, three paces from the crate, where Bocchi could see him if she lifted her head. He had walked across the yard and stood there and not said anything, and Seika, by the wall, had looked at him and nodded once.

The yard went quiet without anyone telling it to.

Coco's lights came down. All of them, the dozens of small silver-gold lights that had drifted over the yard all night, came drifting down, slowly, over the music corner, and stopped at the height of the workshop roof, and hung there. A low ceiling of light. Rimuru, at the long table, had his chin on his hand and was watching Coco instead of the lights.

Then Shizuka stood up from the blanket under the ink tree. She took a candle snuffer from her lap. She looked at the lanterns in the low branches, the ones she had lit, and then at Bocchi on her crate.

She snuffed the lowest one.

Then Nano did the next. Then Hakari and Karane, one each. They went round the low branches, the whole lantern team, putting out the gold squares one by one, until the bottom of the ink tree was dark again. Only the high lanterns were still lit, the ones Frieren had climbed for. They hung up there in the dark, very small and very far away.

The two lamps on posts by the workshop wall. The silver ceiling. The high lanterns.

That was all the light there was.

Bocchi looked at it all.

Then she looked down at her guitar and played the first note.

* * *

It was the slow part.

Mau knew it in the first note. He had heard it in the music room with the shutters closed, and in the fort, and on the shade bench, and through a door in a corridor with Milim standing in front of a sign. Just Bocchi, alone. A few notes, far apart. Like someone walking into a dark room and standing still, waiting for their eyes to work.

The yard did not move.

Bocchi did not look up. She played with her head down, her hair over her face, her knees up, the guitar against her chest like something she was protecting. The notes came out of the dark one at a time and hung there under the silver ceiling.

Mau did not move either.

He stood three paces from the crate. He did not watch her hands. He had learned, in the music room, that watching her hands made her feel watched. He watched the guitar. The wood. The place where her arm rested on it.

The slow part went on. Longer than in the music room. She was letting it be longer. Letting the room be dark for as long as it needed.

Then Kita came in.

Very softly. Under it. Kita was standing beside the crate, close enough that her knee nearly touched Bocchi's shoulder. She played so quietly you could barely hear her, the way she had come in a breath late, once, in the music room, and Bocchi had said *Do that again. Every time.*

She came in a breath late.

Bocchi's head came up.

Not far. Just enough. She looked at Kita, beside her, in the low silver light, and Kita smiled at her. The small one. The one that was only for Bocchi.

Bocchi looked down again. But her shoulders had changed.

Then Nijika, on the bucket, barely touching it with the stick. A heartbeat. Then Ryo, by the wall, low, under everything, holding it up from underneath.

And then the passage.

* * *

Mau had heard it in pieces. He had heard it stopped and started six times in one afternoon. He had never heard it whole.

It went somewhere. That was the only way he could think of it. The slow dark part had been someone standing in a room waiting for their eyes to work, and then one by one people had come in, quietly, and stood with them. Now the passage went up out of that, all of them together, Bocchi's guitar climbing over Kita's and Ryo underneath. It was not loud. It was not fast. It went somewhere, and then it stopped there, and decided to stay.

The yard was perfectly still.

He could see faces at the edge of the lamplight. Seiko in the high seat with her hand on Turbo Granny's cat. Momo by the kitchen door with a cloth in her hands, not moving. Marin with her head on Wakana's blue shoulder. The fort in a row on the long bench, Milim held down by Ori and Yuka on either side and not even trying to float. Shoko, with her hand in her coat pocket, holding whatever she had drawn at the stall. Gojo beside her with his blindfold pushed up and his bare eyes on the music corner, very still. Himmel at the end of the long table with his chin on his hand.

Frieren, under the ink tree, alone in the dark where the low lanterns had been put out. Looking at the music corner. Looking at Mau.

He did not look back. Not now. Now was Bocchi's.

The passage came round again.

And this time, on the second turn, at the place where the song went up and decided to stay, Bocchi lifted her head.

She looked at Kita first. She had promised she would look at Kita, mostly. She did. A long look, with the song going up between them. Kita played with her eyes on Bocchi's face, and her mouth moved, very slightly, saying something nobody else could hear.

Then Bocchi's eyes went past Kita.

Past the lamps. Past the edge of the light. Into the yard, full of people, all of them looking at her. Mau saw her see them. He saw her see all of them at once, a whole yard of faces turned toward her under a ceiling of silver lights, and he saw her start to go.

Her hands faltered. Just slightly. One note came a hair too late.

Her eyes came round, fast, looking for something.

And found him.

Three paces away. At the edge of the light. Where he had said he would be. Someone who had already heard it. Who had sat on the floor of the music room with his back to the wall and listened six times and said *it sounds like walking into a dark room, and by the time the passage comes, the room's full.*

He did not do anything. He did not smile or nod or lift a hand. He stood there. That was the promise. Be where she could see him, and be someone who already knew.

Bocchi looked at him for the length of one note.

Then she looked back at Kita.

Her hands found the next note. On time. Exactly on time. And the song went up again, and stayed, and this time she did not look away from Kita at all until it was over.

* * *

It ended the way it began.

The others dropped away, one at a time, in reverse. Ryo first, then Nijika, then Kita, very softly, a breath late, until it was only Bocchi again. A few notes, far apart, in the dark.

But it was not the same dark. It was the same notes, and it sounded completely different, because everyone had heard what came in between. Now the room was full. You could hear it.

The last note hung under the silver ceiling for a long time.

Then Bocchi took her hand off the strings.

Nobody moved.

Then, from the long bench, very quietly, Ori started to clap. Not loudly. Slowly. And Milim, beside her, joined in, and Yuka, and then the whole yard at once, everyone, standing up, and Nijika hit the bucket once with both sticks in pure joy. Kita put her guitar down on the ground and put both arms round Bocchi on her crate and held on.

Bocchi had her face in Kita's shoulder.

Over Kita's shoulder, one eye showed. It found Mau, still standing three paces away.

He nodded, once.

The eye closed.

* * *

Afterwards, when the yard had broken up and people were drifting toward the long table and the last of the pear bread, Seika came and stood beside him.

She did not say anything for a while. She watched Bocchi, on the crate, with Kita still holding her and the other two packing up around them, Ryo very slowly, Nijika talking very fast.

"She looked at you," said Seika.

"Once."

"Once," Seika agreed. "And then she looked at Kita for the whole rest of it." She folded her arms. "That's the correct number of times."

Mau looked at her.

"I didn't do anything," he said.

"I know," said Seika. "That's why it was correct." She paused. "At the live house, I used to tell the new bands: find one face. Not the crowd. One face that already knows the song. You play to that face when the crowd's too big." She looked at Bocchi. "She found hers. And then she didn't need it."

Bocchi lifted her head off Kita's shoulder.

She said something to Kita, very quietly. Kita laughed and said something back, and then Bocchi looked round, at Seika and at Mau and at the yard, and said, louder than Mau had ever heard her say anything that was not a scream:

"It's called *The Room's Full.*"

Then she went bright red and put her face back in Kita's shoulder, and Ryo said, "That's a good name," and Nijika said, "That's a *great* name," and Seika said nothing at all. She wrote it down on the page of notes she kept in her pocket, in her sharp hand, very carefully, as if it were the most important thing she had written all summer.

Maybe it was.
