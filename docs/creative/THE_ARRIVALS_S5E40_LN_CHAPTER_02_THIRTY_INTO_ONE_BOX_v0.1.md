# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 10 — Thirty Into One Box

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 10
**Continuity state:** Mau spends points for the first time (level 12, 55 points): thirty into Intelligence, with Umaru's build sheet and Yuka copying, Frieren present, and Ciel measuring through Rimuru. The branch's shadow reserve deepens (roughly twice its former depth); it is not mana or CE, not a Black Heart, not unlimited. Twenty-five points stay unspent; registration places stay at ten. This follows his choice on floor 10 to keep "a little left"

*When: Day 338, after lunch. The long table has been cleared of the festival. Umaru has brought down the build sheets, all of them, and they cover half the table.*

Umaru had needed the wall after all.

Not the whole wall. A section of it, at the end of the long table nearest the stairs. She had pinned up three sheets of paper side by side, and a fourth below them, and drawn lines between them in two colours of chalk. Solid lines for things that were known. Broken lines for things that were only possible. There were a great many broken lines.

In the middle, in a box, in large letters: *55.*

"Fifty-five," said Umaru, tapping the box. "Unspent. Since the floor with the guardian." She looked at Mau. "You said you'd rather have a little left. For the next thing."

"I did."

"The Ministry has been thinking about that sentence," said Umaru, "for eleven days."

Yuka, at the table with her sharpened pencil and the copy book, nodded seriously.

"She has," said Yuka. "Out loud. At breakfast."

Frieren was at the far end of the bench with a book open in front of her, not reading it. Rimuru sat beside Mau with his chin on his hand. When Ciel spoke, he would repeat it out loud, so that everybody could hear it and nobody had to guess.

Mau looked at the box with *55* in it.

Ten days ago he would have said *later*. He would have said *a little left* and meant *not yet, there's time*. He had stood on the north road since then, with her blood to his elbows, and found out what *there's time* was worth.

He did not say that. The table knew. Umaru had started the sheets the morning after the porridge, without being asked.

"I'm listening," he said.

* * *

"Here's the problem," said Umaru.

She went to the first sheet. On it, in her careful hand, were the five words the window had shown him in the red gate. *Strength. Agility. Endurance. Intelligence. Perception.* Each in its own box. Under each box, a short list of what they had seen it do, which was almost nothing, because he had never spent a point in any of them.

"We don't know what these do," said Umaru. "Not for sure. We know what they're *called*. The words are familiar. But we said on the first day: we don't call two things the same just because a window uses a familiar word."

"I remember."

"So that's the first problem." She went to the second sheet. "The second problem is the guardian on ten. You kept it. And it emptied you. Ciel said—" She looked at Rimuru.

"*Calling them costs nothing, once they're his. Raising him took nearly all of it. The reserve is shallow,*" said Rimuru, in his own voice but Ciel's words. "*If anything had broken one of them, he'd have had nothing to bring it back with.*"

"Shallow," said Umaru. She underlined the word on the sheet. "The shadow reserve. That's the thing that paid for raising the dragon and the sentry. Once each. Not your mana. Not your cursed energy. A third box." She tapped the broken line running from it. "And when you said, on ten, *next time I want a little left*—you meant that box. Because that box is small."

"Yes."

"So the question," said Umaru, "is whether any of these five words puts more into that box." She turned round. "And we don't know. And the only way to find out is to spend one."

* * *

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Yuka had stopped copying.

"You said we don't spend anything," she said. "That was the rule. The first rule. You said it on the first day."

"We didn't spend anything," said Umaru. "While we didn't know enough to guess." She looked at the sheets. "Now we know more. We know the reserve is the thing that runs out. We know what it cost to raise two shadows, and we know what it would have cost to raise a third. We know the guardian was the first one, and there are going to be more, and they're going to get bigger." She paused. "If we wait until he needs it, he'll be choosing in a hurry. On a roof. With something coming at him."

She looked at Mau.

"The Ministry recommends choosing on an ordinary afternoon. At a table. After a festival. With pencils."

Frieren, at the end of the bench, closed her book.

"That's sensible," she said.

Umaru went slightly pink under her hood.

"Which one?" said Mau.

Umaru went back to the first sheet. She did not hesitate.

"Intelligence," she said, and tapped it. "When we traced the branch the first time, the reserve led toward that word. Not cleanly. But it was the one it leaned at." She looked at Rimuru. "Ciel said so."

"*The line from the reserve runs nearest to that field,*" said Rimuru. "*Nearest is not the same as connected.*"

"Nearest," said Umaru. "Not the same as connected. I wrote that down." She tapped the broken line. "So we test it. One point. We watch. Ciel measures."

"One?" said Mau.

"One," said Umaru firmly. "If one does nothing, we've lost one. If one does something, we know which way to go."

* * *

He opened the window.

He did not show them all of it. He never did. He let them see the small set of fields, the five words and the number beside each, all of them at their starting values. The *55* at the top. Below that, the separate line for the reserve. It still was not fully named. It was a bar, mostly, with a shallow fill in it, like the bottom of a cup.

Yuka leaned over to look, then remembered she should not, and leaned back and looked at the copy book instead.

*Ready,* said Ciel. *I'm watching the reserve, the branch, and the boundaries. If anything other than the reserve changes, I'll tell you before you spend another.*

"She's ready," said Rimuru.

Mau put one point into Intelligence.

It was very simple. It should not have been, he thought, after everything. But it was. He chose the word, and he chose to add, and the number beside it went up by one, and *55* became *54*.

Nothing happened in his arms. Nothing happened in his chest. The wheel in his shadow did not stir. His eyes behind their filter did not change.

But the bar moved.

Not the fill. The bar. The cup got a little deeper. The fill stayed where it was, and the bottom of the cup went down beneath it, slightly, so that the same amount of shadow sat in a slightly larger space.

*There,* said Ciel. *The reserve's capacity increased. Its present level did not. No other boundary moved. Mana, cursed energy, the Six Eyes, the Noise's other work, all unchanged.*

Mau let out a breath he had not known he was holding.

"It works," said Rimuru. "Capacity up. Not the level, just the size. Nothing else moved."

Umaru did not say anything. She turned to the wall, picked up the chalk, and drew over the broken line between the reserve and the word *Intelligence*. Slowly. Carefully. Until it was solid.

Then she stood back and looked at it for a long time.

Yuka was looking at her.

"You're happy," said Yuka.

"The Ministry is satisfied with the result of a controlled test," said Umaru, very stiffly, with her back to the room, and did not turn round for a while.

* * *

"How much?" said Frieren.

Everyone looked at her.

"How much for the guardian on ten?" said Frieren. "How deep would it need to be, to keep something like that. And still have some left."

Mau asked.

*Hard to say exactly,* said Ciel. *The guardian was the first of its kind. The next ones will be bigger. But if you want room to hold one like it in storage, with the dragon and the sentry, and still keep a working margin, I'd estimate the reserve needs to be roughly twice as deep as it was this morning.* A pause. *One point added about three parts in a hundred.*

Rimuru repeated it.

Umaru turned round from the wall. She had already done the arithmetic.

"Thirty," she said. "About thirty points. To double it." She looked at the *54* in the box. "And that leaves twenty-four. For the other four words. Or for later."

"Or for later," said Mau.

"You'd keep them?"

He looked at the window. At the bar, a little deeper now. At the other four words, still at their starting values, still not understood.

"I said I'd rather have a little left," he said. "I meant the reserve. But I think I meant this too." He looked at Umaru. "We know what one of these does. We don't know the others. I'd rather keep some points for when we do."

Umaru looked at him for a long moment.

Then she nodded.

"Twenty-nine more," she said. "Into Intelligence. One at a time. Ciel watches each one." She turned to Yuka. "You count."

* * *

It took a long time.

One at a time, the way she said. He put a point in, and the cup got a little deeper, and Ciel said *unchanged* about everything else, and Rimuru repeated it, and Yuka made a mark in the copy book. Then the next. Then the next. Around the fifteenth, Milim came down the stairs and asked what they were doing, and Yuka said *counting*, and Milim said that sounded boring, and stayed anyway, floating over the end of the table with her chin in her hands, watching Yuka's marks.

At the thirtieth, Yuka said, "Thirty," and put down her pencil.

Mau looked at the window.

The bar was a good deal deeper now. The shallow fill sat in the bottom of it, the same amount as before. It was nowhere near full, because spending points did not fill it. Only rest did that, slowly. But there was room in it now. Room for the dragon in storage, and the sentry, and something else beside them. And a margin left over, after.

*Twenty-five unspent,* said Ciel. *Capacity roughly doubled. Present level unchanged. Registration still ten places. No boundary moved except the one we were testing.* She paused. *That was a very clean afternoon's work.*

Rimuru repeated it. All of it. Including the last part.

Umaru wrote *25* in the box on the wall, very large, and drew a small solid circle round it.

Then she sat down at the table, rather suddenly, as if her legs had decided for her. She pulled her hood all the way down over her face.

"The Ministry," she said, from inside it, "would like a glass of milk."

Frieren got up from the end of the bench and went to the kitchen and came back with a glass of milk, and set it down in front of Umaru without saying anything, and went back to her book.

Mau looked at the wall. At the four sheets with their lines, most of them still broken, and one of them solid now, from the reserve to a single word. At the *25* in its circle.

"Thank you," he said.

Umaru, inside her hood, drank her milk.

"Choosing on an ordinary afternoon," she said. "At a table. With pencils." She put the glass down. "It's not a heroic way to get stronger."

"No," said Mau. "It's a better one."
