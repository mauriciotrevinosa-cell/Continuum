# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 11 — Lantern

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 11
**Continuity state:** Ori names the Red Gate dragon shadow "Lantern". It is a brief controlled manifestation in the north field inside the lime at dawn, with Shoko's limit (under two minutes) and Rimuru and Frieren present; the deeper reserve makes it easier to hold but not free; the dragon obeys and does not speak; the floor guardians will carry names from their notices, but this one did not; no combat

*When: Day 339, just after dawn. Dew on the long grass in the north field. Nazuna has gone in to sleep. Shoko has come out in her coat with her coffee and a watch, and has said "two minutes" four times.*

It had been Ori's idea.

She had asked at breakfast the day before, after the slate, after the milk, very quietly, on the way past Mau's chair. She did not ask the table. She asked him.

"Does the dragon have a name?"

Mau had stopped with his spoon halfway to his mouth.

"No," he said. "The window didn't give it one. It just says *registered*."

Ori considered this.

"That's not a name," she said. "That's a list."

"It is a list."

"Everyone in this house has a name," said Ori. "Even the deer had a name in the guest book. *Deer.*" She paused. "I think the dragon should have one. If it's going to live in your shadow. With Ear." She looked at him very seriously. "Ear has a name. Frieren gave it. That was fair, because she found it."

"Who found the dragon?"

"Everyone," said Ori. "Frieren and Fern and Stark and Milim and Yuka and you. In the red gate. I wasn't there." She paused. "So I thought, maybe, I could be the one who names it. Because I wasn't there. So I'd have a part in it too."

Mau had put down his spoon.

* * *

Shoko had said yes with conditions.

"Two minutes," she said, in the yard, before they went out. "Standing. Not two minutes and then a little bit more because she's having a moment. Two minutes. I'll be watching the watch." She looked at Mau. "You doubled the box yesterday. That doesn't mean you fill it today. The level's the same as it was."

"I know."

"Then two minutes."

"Two minutes."

She looked at Rimuru.

"Two minutes," said Rimuru. "I'll call it."

So they went out to the north field at dawn. Mau, and Ori in her coat, and Milim floating on her ribbon, which Ori held. Frieren, and Rimuru, and Shoko with her coffee. Yuka came too, because Yuka had seen the dragon in the red gate, at the high pass, at the snowline, when it had been alive and trying to kill them. She wanted to see it again from the other side.

They stopped well inside the lime, in the long grass where the fireflies had been. The ditch was nearly dry. The grass was pale and wet with dew. The sky over the hills was going from grey to gold.

Mau stepped away from the others, ten paces, into the open.

"Ready?" said Rimuru.

"Ready."

*Watching,* said Ciel.

Mau looked down at his shadow, long and thin in the low gold light, stretching away from him across the wet grass toward the house.

"Come out," he said. Quietly. Not a command he needed to shout.

* * *

It came out.

It did not burst out. It rose, the way it had risen at the snowline. A darkness gathered in the long shadow at his feet and deepened, and then the deepening stood up out of the grass. Wings first, drawing themselves out of the black and folding. A long neck. A head, lowering. A body behind it, enormous, dark, with no shine on it at all, as if the dawn light could not quite find where its edges were.

It was very big. Much bigger in the open field than it had seemed in the red gate, where everything had been big.

Mau felt the reserve go down.

It went down the way it had gone down before, steady and definite. But the cup was deeper now. The same amount was leaving, and there was more room around it, and he did not feel the floor coming up to meet him the way he had at the snowline.

*Easier,* said Ciel. *Not cheaper. The same rate. More margin.*

He kept his hand loose at his side.

The dragon lowered its head until its jaw was level with his shoulder. It looked at him. Where its eyes should have been, there were two small lights, very faint, a pale cold blue, deep inside the dark of its skull, like candles behind dark glass.

Behind him, Milim made a very small sound.

He turned his head.

"Ori," he said. "You can come. Slowly."

* * *

Ori came.

She let go of Milim's ribbon. She gave it to Yuka, who took it without a word. Then she walked across the wet grass, slowly, in her coat, ten paces, toward the boy and the enormous dark shape bending over him. She did not run. She did not hesitate either. Frieren, behind her, took one step forward and then stopped herself. She stood very still with her staff in her hand.

Ori stopped beside Mau.

The dragon turned its head and looked at her.

The two small blue lights moved. Not much. Just enough that you could see the head had turned and the lights were on her now.

Ori looked up at it.

She was very small next to it. She had to tip her head all the way back to see its face. The dragon's jaw was longer than she was tall. It could have swallowed her without opening its mouth very wide.

It did not move.

"Hello," said Ori.

The dragon did not answer. Mau had not thought it would. It had not made a sound since the snowline. Shadows did not, he thought. Not this one.

Ori looked at it for a long moment. At the dark wings folded along its sides. At the long neck. At the two small cold lights deep in its skull, looking down at her.

"Your eyes look like the lanterns," she said. "In the tree. After the candles go in. When you look up at them from underneath, and they're very far up, and they're the only light there is."

The dragon looked at her.

"That's what I'm going to call you," said Ori. "Lantern. Because of your eyes." She paused. "And because you're the first one. The first light that's his. Before you, he only had Ear, and Ear's a door, not a light." She looked at Mau. "Is that all right?"

Mau looked at the dragon.

It had not moved. The two small lights were still on Ori.

"It's not mine to say," said Mau. "You're the one who named it."

Ori turned back to the dragon.

"Lantern," she said again, more quietly. As if to see how it sounded. As if trying it on.

And the dragon lowered its head.

Not far. A little. Until its jaw was level with Ori's face instead of Mau's shoulder. The two small lights were very close now, and very faint, cold blue, steady.

Ori did not step back.

She reached up, very slowly, and put her hand flat on the dark of its jaw. Her hand did not go through. It stopped where the shape was, the way a hand stops on cool stone in shade.

"Lantern," said Ori.

* * *

"One minute forty," said Rimuru, behind them.

"Twenty seconds," said Shoko. "I'm counting. Don't make me count out loud."

Mau did not hurry. He let Ori take her hand away in her own time. She did, after a moment. She stepped back, one step, two, and looked up at the dragon once more.

"Goodbye, Lantern," she said. "See you next time."

"Back," said Mau.

The dragon folded.

It went down into his shadow the way it had come out, all at once and very smoothly. Wings, neck, head, the two small lights last, going out in the dark like candles. Then there was only the long thin shadow on the wet grass, and the dawn, and the drain stopping.

What he had used stayed used.

But there was more left than there had been at the snowline. A good deal more. He stood in the long grass and felt the margin there, quiet, like money in a pocket you do not intend to spend.

"Two minutes," said Shoko. She closed the watch. "Exactly. Well done." She looked at him over her coffee. "How do you feel?"

"Fine," said Mau. "A bit lighter. Like after carrying something."

"Then go and eat," said Shoko. "And don't do it again today."

* * *

On the way back across the field, Ori walked beside Frieren.

She did not say anything for a long time. Then, at the edge of the yard, she said, "Its jaw was cold."

"Was it?"

"Like a stone in the shade." Ori looked at her hand. "It didn't move. When I touched it. It just let me."

Frieren looked down at her.

"You were very brave," she said.

"I wasn't brave. It was only big." Ori thought about it. "Brave is when it's scary. It wasn't scary. It was just very big, and looking at me." She paused. "And then it had a name. And then it was Lantern."

Frieren did not say anything.

But that evening, Mau found a new page pinned to the end of Umaru's wall, under the build sheets, in Ori's careful printing.

*SHADOWS (NAMED)*

*1. Ear. (Frieren.) A door.*

*2. Lantern. (Ori.) A light.*

And underneath, smaller, in Umaru's hand, as an official amendment:

*3. The sentry. (Unnamed. Pending.)*
