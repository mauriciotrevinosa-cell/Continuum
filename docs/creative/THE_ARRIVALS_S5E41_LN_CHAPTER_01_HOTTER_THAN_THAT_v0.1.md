# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 14 — Hotter Than That

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 14
**Continuity state:** The black iron from floor 11 resists the house's forge; Senku needs a hotter fire; Kaneki's charcoal kilns become part of the plan (denser charcoal, a longer burn); Wakana designs a small crucible furnace; Marin claims the padding; materials do not become armour instantly, and the first working piece is weeks away

*When: Day 341, all day. The rock that is going to be armour sits on Senku's workbench on a folded cloth. Stark has visited it three times.*

The black iron did not care about their forge.

Senku put a sliver of it in the hottest part of the fire, in the workshop forge, the one they used for nails and hinges and the brass wheels for the lamps. He pumped the bellows until his arms ached. Then Wakana pumped them. Then Stark, who could pump a bellows like a man trying to start a storm.

The fire went white at the centre.

The sliver of black iron sat in it, in the white heat, for twenty minutes.

Then Senku took it out with the tongs and laid it on the anvil, and they all looked at it.

It was exactly the same. Black. Dense. Faintly shining, like wet stone. Not red. Not even warm at the edges. When Senku touched it with the back of his finger, very carefully, it was only a little warmer than the room.

"Huh," said Senku.

Nobody said anything else for a while.

"That's not possible," said Wakana eventually. "That fire would soften ordinary iron in five minutes."

"Ten billion percent not possible," Senku agreed. He was staring at the sliver with an expression Mau had seen on his face at the telescope, on the night of first light. "Which means it's not ordinary iron. Which means it's not iron. Which means—" He stopped. He picked up a pencil. "Which means I need a hotter fire."

"How hot?" said Stark.

Senku did a sum on the edge of the workbench, in pencil, very small.

"Hotter than that," he said, and pointed at the forge. "Much hotter. I don't know how much yet." He looked at the sliver. "I'll find out by making it hotter until it gives up."

* * *

Kaneki came in at noon.

He did not usually come into the workshop at noon. He slept in the day, after his shift, in his room past Rem's turn. But Stark had gone to find him at eleven. He had knocked on his door and said, very apologetically, *I'm sorry, I know you're asleep, but Senku says it's about charcoal,* and Kaneki had got up.

He stood in the workshop doorway in his shirtsleeves with his hair flat on one side, looking at the sliver on the anvil.

"Charcoal," he said.

"Your charcoal," said Senku. "From the kilns." He pointed at the forge. "That's coal from the river bank and some of yours mixed in. It's not hot enough. I need to know if you can make it hotter."

Kaneki came in and looked at the forge. At the coal in it, and the charcoal. He picked up a piece of his own charcoal from the bucket and turned it over in his fingers, and held it to his ear, and tapped it with his nail.

It made a small clear sound. Almost a ring.

"That's from the middle kiln," he said. "That's my best."

"It's not hot enough."

Kaneki looked at the charcoal in his hand.

"It could be denser," he said slowly. "If I burned it longer. Slower. Less air. If I picked the wood." He turned the piece over. "Hardwood. The oak from the far side of the timber yard. The ones Wakana won't use because they're too twisted for beams." He looked at Wakana. "You've got a whole stack of them."

"They're useless," said Wakana. "For building."

"They're not useless for this," said Kaneki.

Senku was writing.

"How long?" he said, without looking up.

Kaneki thought about it.

"A week to burn. Maybe ten days, if I want it really dense. Then it has to cool slowly, or it cracks." He paused. "Two weeks. For the first batch. If I start tonight."

"Two weeks," said Senku. He wrote it down. Then he looked up at Kaneki, and for once he did not say *ten billion percent* or *doable* or anything at all for a moment.

"You'd do that?" he said. "On top of the night shift?"

Kaneki put the piece of charcoal back in the bucket.

"I'm at the kilns anyway," he said. "All night. Every night." He shrugged. "I like it there. It's a place to stand. It might as well be making something someone needs."

He went back to bed.

Senku looked at the door he had gone out of for a long time.

"I like him very much," he said eventually.

"Everyone does," said Wakana. "He'd hate to know."

* * *

Wakana spent the afternoon drawing the furnace.

Not a forge. A furnace. Small, tall, round, like a chimney with a fat belly. Clay and stone. A pot inside it, a crucible, to hold the metal. A hole at the bottom for the bellows. A hole at the top for the heat to go up through. And a second bellows, because Senku had said *hotter than that* and Wakana believed him.

"It's how they made the best steel at home," said Wakana, drawing. "A long time ago. My grandfather showed me pictures. Before there were factories." He shaded the belly of the furnace. "They'd build it, use it once, and break it open to get the metal out."

"Once?" said Stark.

"Once. Every time."

Stark looked at the drawing in dismay.

"That's a lot of building."

"It's a lot of building," Wakana agreed. "But you learn something every time you break one open." He turned the drawing round so Senku could see it. "Will that be hot enough?"

Senku looked at it for a long time.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "It's hotter than the forge. A lot hotter. With Kaneki's charcoal—" He stopped. "I don't know. The black iron didn't even notice the forge. It might not notice this either." He tapped the drawing. "But we'll find out. And if it's not, we'll know how much hotter we need. That's the second step."

"How many steps are there?" said Stark.

Senku looked at the sliver on the anvil.

"More than two," he said.

* * *

Marin came in at four with a tape measure round her neck.

She did not come in for the furnace. She came in for Stark.

"Stand up," she said. "Arms out."

Stark stood up. He put his arms out, because when Marin had a tape measure round her neck you did what she said. She went round him with the tape, very fast, writing numbers on the back of her hand. Shoulders. Chest. Arm. The other arm. Waist.

"What's this for?" said Stark.

"Padding," said Marin, writing. "Armour's no good without padding underneath. It rubs. It bruises. It slips." She measured his neck. "Everyone always thinks about the metal. Nobody thinks about the cloth that goes under it, and then they wonder why they've got sores." She wrote. "When the iron's ready—*if* the iron's ever ready—it's going to need a quilted jacket underneath. Fitted. So I'm starting now." She looked up at him. "You're first because you carried the rock."

Stark looked down at her.

"I didn't think anyone would think about the cloth," he said.

"Wakana did," said Marin. "He told me about the jackets from his grandfather's pictures. Under the old armour. Padded. Stitched in rows." She lowered the tape. "And then I thought, well, nobody's going to sew those except me."

Wakana, at the bench, did not look up from his drawing. But Mau saw his ears go red.

"How long?" said Stark.

"For the jacket?" Marin considered. "A week. If I don't do anything else." She smiled. "I'm going to do other things. So two weeks."

"That's the same as the charcoal," said Stark.

"Then they'll be ready at the same time," said Marin. "That's called planning."

* * *

Mau watched all of it from the stool by the door.

He had come in the morning to see the sliver go into the fire, because the black iron had come from his floor and he wanted to know. He had stayed all day. He had not said very much. Nobody had needed him to. They had needed Kaneki's charcoal and Wakana's drawing and Marin's tape measure and Stark's arms, and Senku saying *I don't know* and then *we'll find out*.

At the end of the afternoon, Senku put the sliver back on its folded cloth on the workbench, beside the rest of the rock.

"Two weeks," he said, to nobody in particular. "Charcoal. Furnace. Padding. Then we try." He looked at the black iron. "And it probably won't work the first time."

"And then?" said Mau.

"And then we break the furnace open and see what we learned," said Senku. "And build another one."

Mau looked at the rock.

He thought about the tower. About floor 11 taking almost three hours. The floors after it would be bigger and slower, with harder things in them, and stranger materials at the end of them. None of it would become anything useful in an afternoon.

He thought it should probably bother him. It didn't.

"I'll bring more," he said. "From the next floors. If there's anything."

"Bring everything," said Senku. "Rocks. Leaves. Dirt. Anything the notice calls *rare*." He was already writing a list. "Don't touch the trees."

"Don't touch the trees," said Mau.

He went out into the yard. Behind him, through the workshop door, he could hear Wakana and Senku arguing about the height of the furnace chimney, Stark asking whether *two weeks* meant exactly two weeks or Marin's kind of two weeks, and Marin saying *Marin's kind*. Across the timber yard, very faintly, he could hear Kaneki, who was supposed to be asleep, sorting through the stack of twisted oak.
