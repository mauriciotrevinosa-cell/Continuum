# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 15 — The Drowned Library

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 15
**Continuity state:** Floors 12–14 summarised from Anko's board (two visits, Days 341–342). Floor 15 (Day 343) with Mau (owner), Fern, Stark and Gojo: a flooded library with ink-eels in knee-deep water; the objective is the reading room; Fern's precise light spells; Gojo's small Blue clears a shelf-jam; Fern takes an ordinary book in an unknown script for Frieren. Mau reaches level 17 (fifty unspent). No extraction

*When: Day 343, morning. Anko's board now has three columns for the tower: DATE, PARTY, FLOOR. Under FLOOR, in her hand: 12, 13, 14. Under today, nothing yet.*

Anko's board said it plainly. She did not believe in describing things that had gone well.

*Day 341, afternoon. Mau, Yuta, Maki, Yuka. From 10. Floor 12: rope bridges over a gorge. Yuka's rope. Cleared. Two hours ten.*

*Day 342, morning. Mau, Frieren, Kaneki, Milim. From 10. Floor 13: a maze of hedges that moved at night. Was night. Milim flew over it. Cleared. Floor 14: a hot spring cave, very full of something that did not like being bathed near. Milim bathed near it. Cleared. Three hours exactly. Milim reprimanded (by Yuka, on return).*

And underneath, in a different pencil, smaller:

*Milim says it started it.*

Mau stood in front of the board in the morning with Fern beside him, reading it.

"She did say it started it," said Mau.

"Did it?"

Mau thought about the thing in the hot spring cave. Large and pale and slow, with too many arms, rising out of the steaming water. Milim had said *hello* to it very loudly, from about a hand's breadth away.

"Partly," he said.

Fern looked at him. Then wrote on the board, under START FROM: *10*. And under PARTY: *Mau, Fern, Stark, Gojo.*

"Three hours," she said. "I want to be back for the ledger before Jinshi leaves his questions."

* * *

The fifteenth floor was a library, and it was underwater.

Not all the way. Knee-deep. They came up the stair and stood at the top and looked at it. A great hall, so tall the top of it was lost in shadow, full of shelves. Shelves taller than houses, in long rows, packed tight with books. Ladders on rails. Galleries going up and up, with iron railings. Everything a library should be.

And all of it standing in dark still water up to the knee.

The water was black. Not muddy. Black, like ink. It lay perfectly flat between the shelves. On the surface, here and there, books floated face down, open, their pages spread like the wings of drowned birds.

There was no light, except a faint grey glow from somewhere very high up.

Gojo stood at the top of the stair with his blindfold pushed up and his bare eyes on the water.

"Oh, I don't like that," he said.

"The water?" said Stark.

"What's in the water," said Gojo. "Lots of little things. Long. Moving." He tilted his head. "Not lots. A great many."

The notice came up.

*Floor 15 of 100.*

*Objective: find the reading room.*

Fern raised her hand. A small light came out of her palm, white and steady, and rose above her head and hung there, lighting the water and the nearest shelves. Then another. Then another, each one exactly the same size, exactly the same height, set down in the air in a row going out between the shelves. Like Senku's lamps along a path.

Gojo looked at the row of lights.

"That's very neat," he said.

"It's a light spell," said Fern. "An ordinary one."

"It's very *neat*," said Gojo. "Every one the same. Frieren's would be wonky."

"Mistress Frieren's are not wonky," said Fern. "Mistress Frieren's are *casual*." She stepped down into the water. "Come on."

* * *

They found the eels in the first row.

Fern's lights went out over the water, and the water moved. Something long and black and thin, the same black as the water, as if the water itself had grown a body. It slid between the shelves just under the surface. Then another. Then a dozen. As long as Stark's arm, with no eyes, and mouths full of tiny teeth like a comb.

They did not attack. Not at first. They circled. Round and round the four of them, just under the surface, closer and closer, testing.

"Don't let them touch you," said Gojo. "I don't know what they do. But my eyes say they're mostly mouth."

Mau put up the small flat space. Not over them, this time. Around their legs. A little sleeve of Infinity just below the waterline, where the eels were. The first eel struck. It stopped a finger's width from Stark's shin and slowed, and slowed, and hung there in the black water, mouth open, going nowhere.

It drew back. The others drew back with it.

They did not come again.

"That's a useful trick," said Stark, looking down at the eel that was not quite touching him.

"It costs," said Mau. "A little. Every minute."

"Then let's be quick," said Fern, and set another light in the air.

* * *

They went through the library for an hour.

It was enormous. The rows went on and on, and turned, and branched, and came to dead ends where a shelf had fallen across the path and lay half sunk in the black water with its books spilling out. Twice they had to go back. Once they had to climb a ladder to a gallery and walk along it, high above the water, and come down another ladder further on. Mau let the sleeve go while they were up there, to save it. When they came down, he put it back.

Fern led.

She did not say much. She walked through the black water with her row of lights going out ahead of her, and every few turns she stopped, and looked at the shelves, and chose a way. Mau did not ask how. He had seen her do it in the red gate, at the snowline, reading a slope.

"The books are in order," she said at last, when Stark asked. "Look." She pointed at the spines. "The marks on them. They're not words I can read. But they repeat. The same mark at the start of every row, and then the next mark, and then the next." She looked down the row. "It's a library. Libraries are in order. The reading room will be at the end of the order." She paused. "Or the beginning. We'll find out which."

Gojo looked at her for a long moment.

"You'd have been a terrible person to have as a student," he said.

"Why?"

"You'd have already read the textbook," said Gojo, "and found the mistake on page forty."

Fern considered this.

"Page thirty-eight," she said, and walked on.

* * *

The jam was near the end.

Three great shelves had fallen against each other across the last row. They lay in a heap, wedged, half in the water and half out, so tall and so tangled you could not climb over them. Beyond them, through the gaps, Mau could see light. Warm light. Lamplight. A doorway.

"The reading room," said Fern.

Stark looked at the heap of shelves.

"I can cut through," he said. "With the axe. It'll take a while."

"It'll take a long while," said Gojo. "And they're very old shelves. And they're very wet. And if you cut the wrong one—" He made a gesture with his hand: everything coming down. "Let me."

He stepped forward. He held up one hand, loosely, with two fingers out, and looked at the heap with his bare eyes for a long moment.

Then a small blue light appeared at the end of his fingers. The size of a marble.

He flicked it, very gently, into a gap between two of the fallen shelves.

It went in. And then there was a sound like a breath taken in very fast. The heap moved. Not outward. Inward, toward the marble, very slightly. Everything shifted a hand's breadth toward the small blue point, and in shifting, it came unwedged. The shelves slid. One fell flat into the water with a great slow splash. Another tipped back against the wall. The third rolled aside.

The way was open.

The marble went out.

Gojo lowered his hand. He looked at it.

"Easier," he said. "In here. Every time. Same size, though." He flexed his fingers. "Still a marble."

"It was a very useful marble," said Fern.

"Thank you," said Gojo. "I'll tell it."

* * *

The reading room was small and dry.

Three steps up out of the black water into a round room with a domed ceiling and a lamp burning on a table in the middle. Chairs round the table. Shelves round the walls, dry, with books on them, upright and in order. No eels. No water. Just warm light and the smell of old paper.

*Floor 15 cleared.*

*Level 17. Fifty points unspent.*

Mau let the sleeve go. He felt it leave him. A little tired. Not much.

Fern was standing in front of the shelves.

She was not looking at the notice. She was looking at the books. Her hand was half raised, as if she wanted to touch one and was not sure she should.

The notice came up again, small.

*Items: ordinary books. Eligible to take: one.*

"One," said Mau.

Fern looked at him.

"You choose," said Mau. "You found the reading room."

Fern looked at the shelves for a long time. She did not choose quickly. She walked along the wall, slowly, reading the spines she could not read, touching none of them. Then she stopped. She took down a small book, bound in faded green cloth, about the size of her hand.

She opened it. The pages were covered in small close writing in a script none of them knew. Here and there, in the margins, there were tiny drawings. A leaf. A key. A bird.

"I can't read it," said Fern.

"Nobody can," said Gojo, looking over her shoulder. "It's not numbers. It's not letters I know." He tilted his head. "It's very old, though. Older than the shelves."

Fern closed the book. She held it against her chest.

"Mistress Frieren can't read it either," she said. "She'll hate that." She paused. "She'll love it."

"Which one?" said Stark.

"Both," said Fern. "That's what she likes most. A book she can't read yet."

* * *

They went home through the return frame. On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Two hours forty," she said. "From ten." She wrote it on the board. Then she looked at Fern, holding a small green book against her chest. "What's that?"

"A present," said Fern.

Anko wrote it down. *Floor 15. Drowned library. Cleared. A present (Fern).*

That evening, in the east room, Frieren sat in the chair Mau had said no to with the small green book open on her knees. She did not read it, because she couldn't. She turned the pages very slowly, one by one, and looked at the little drawings in the margins, the leaf and the key and the bird. She did not look up for three hours.

Fern, passing the open door on her way to bed, looked in and saw her.

She did not say anything. She went on to her own room. But she was smiling, Stark said afterwards, all the way down the corridor.
