# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 16 — Something in the Box

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 16
**Continuity state:** Anko's request (V29 "The True Leaf", Day 316): an empty box outside her door that Mau waters every morning with a cup. After twenty-eight mornings, a single seedling has come up that nobody planted; Anko rules that whatever grows is the request; Nazuna, going to bed, says the wind brought it; what it is stays unknown; Shoko's tobacco seedlings are growing separately and are not ready

*When: Day 344, just after dawn. Twenty-eight mornings since the true leaf. Mau has a cup of water in his hand, as he does every morning. Anko is coming in from the night round with lime on her boots, as she does every morning.*

The box was outside Anko's door, on the left, where she had asked for it.

It was a plain wooden box, as long as Mau's forearm and half as wide, that Wakana had made out of offcuts the day after the true leaf. Inside it was dirt. Good dirt, from the same place as Shoko's box, sifted by Mau himself. Nothing else. Anko had been very clear about that.

*Nothing in it,* she had said, on the bench outside the clinic, with Shoko's box between them and the first true leaf open in the sun. *A box of my own. Empty. And you water it. Every morning. With a cup.*

*What for?*

*That's my business.*

So he had. Every morning for twenty-eight mornings. He came down before breakfast with a cup from the kitchen barrel. He went along the corridor to the door of the room Anko shared with Nazuna, where the loft was. He poured the cup of water slowly into the empty box, so it soaked in evenly, and went to breakfast.

Nobody had asked him about it. Momo had noticed the cup going missing every morning and started leaving one out for him on the end of the counter. That was all.

This morning he came round the corner with the cup and stopped.

There was something in the box.

* * *

It was very small.

A single stem, no taller than the first joint of his little finger, pale green, coming straight up out of the middle of the dirt. Two leaves at the top of it, round, like a pair of hands held out to catch something. That was all. It had not been there yesterday. He was sure. He had looked at the box every morning for twenty-eight mornings and it had been dirt every time.

He crouched down beside it.

He did not water it. He was suddenly not sure he was allowed to.

He was still crouched there with the full cup in his hand when Anko came along the corridor from the yard. Lime on her boots. Her coat over her arm. A long night's round in her face, the way it always was in the mornings.

She saw him crouched by her box and stopped.

"What," she said.

Mau looked up at her.

"There's something in it."

Anko did not move for a moment. Then she came the rest of the way along the corridor and crouched down on the other side of the box, very slowly. Her knees cracked. She looked at the small green stem with its two round leaves.

She did not say anything for a long time.

"I didn't put anything in it," said Mau. "I swear. I only watered it."

"I know you didn't."

"It's the same dirt as Shoko's box. Maybe there was a seed in it. From the tobacco."

"It's not tobacco," said Anko. "Tobacco leaves are pointed. Those are round." She looked at it. "And Shoko sifted her dirt three times. Ciel checked it. There was nothing in it but dirt. Same with mine. I asked." She paused. "I asked Ciel to check mine. The first day. Before you watered it."

Mau looked at her.

"You asked Ciel to check it was empty?"

"I wanted it empty," said Anko. "I was very clear."

* * *

The door behind them opened.

Nazuna came out, in her coat, yawning, on her way down to breakfast before she went to sleep. She ate a little breakfast every morning now, at the end of her night, and then went up to the loft and slept until dusk. She saw them both crouched on the floor by the box and stopped in the doorway.

"Is someone dead?" she said.

"Something's alive," said Anko.

Nazuna came out and looked over Anko's shoulder at the box.

"Oh," she said. "That."

They both looked up at her.

"You knew?" said Anko.

"I didn't *know*," said Nazuna. "I saw. Last night. Coming in at three for my coat." She yawned again. "There was a wind. A warm one, off the north field, about midnight. It blew all down the corridor because someone left the yard door open." She looked pointedly at Anko. "And it blew a lot of stuff in. Dust. Bits of grass. Seeds. The fluffy kind." She shrugged. "I watched one land in your box. It stuck. I thought, *huh*. And then I went back out."

Anko stared at her.

"Last night?" said Mau. "It came up in one night?"

"No," said Nazuna. "It was a *different* night. Not last night. A long time ago. The first week, maybe." She frowned. "I don't remember. Nights all look the same from the stake." She looked at the seedling. "It's been in there a while. It just took this long to come up. Seeds do that. They wait." She turned to go. "Anyway. It's the wind's. Not yours. Not his. Nobody put it there. It just came."

She went down the corridor toward the kitchen.

At the corner she stopped and looked back.

"It's from the north field," she said. "Probably. The wind was from there. Same place as the fireflies." She paused. "Same place as the weed in your pocket, at the festival." And she went round the corner and was gone.

* * *

Anko and Mau crouched on either side of the box and looked at the small green stem.

"So it's not empty," said Mau eventually.

"No."

"You wanted it empty."

Anko was quiet for a long time. She looked at the seedling with an expression he could not read. Anko's face was always hard to read in the mornings, after the round, with lime on her boots and the whole night behind her eyes.

"Do you know why I asked for an empty box?" she said.

"You said it was your business."

"It is my business." She looked at the seedling. "I'll tell you anyway."

He waited.

"Everyone in this house has something growing," said Anko. "Shoko has her tobacco. Senku has his lamps and his telescope. Wakana has his rooms. Coco has her lights. Even Nazuna had her fireflies." She paused. "I have the board. The sightings. The round. The register. All of it's watching things. Writing down what came. Nothing I've got is *growing*." She turned the coat over on her arm. "When you said, *what would you like*, the day of the leaf, I thought: I don't know. I don't know what I'd want to grow. So I asked for an empty box. And somebody to water it every morning. In case I worked it out."

"Did you?"

"No," said Anko.

She looked at the seedling.

"And now something's growing in it anyway," she said. "That I didn't choose. That nobody chose. That just came on the wind, from the same field as the fireflies, and waited, and came up when it was ready." Her voice was very flat, the way it went when she was saying something that mattered. "That's the most *me* thing that could possibly have happened."

Mau did not say anything.

Anko stood up. Her knees cracked again.

"Water it," she said.

"Are you sure?"

"I'm sure." She looked down at him. "Whatever comes up is the request. That's my ruling. I don't need to know what it is. I'll find out when it gets there." She went to her door. "Every morning. Same as before. A cup."

"A cup," said Mau.

He poured it. Slowly, round the edge of the box, not on the seedling itself, so the water soaked in evenly and the small stem didn't bend. The dirt went dark. The two round leaves at the top of the stem stayed exactly where they were, held out, like hands.

Anko watched from her doorway.

"What is it?" said Mau. "Do you want me to ask Ciel? Or Kusuri?"

"No," said Anko.

"Not even to know?"

"I want to find out," said Anko, "the slow way." She almost smiled. "I've been writing down what comes for years. I'm good at waiting to see what it is."

She went in and shut the door.

* * *

That afternoon, Shoko came by on her way to the clinic and stopped beside the box.

She looked at the seedling for a long time. Then she looked at her own box, further down the corridor by the clinic door. The tobacco plants, a hand high now, with broad pointed leaves, nowhere near ready. Weeks from ready. Ciel said so, and Shoko said so, and Gojo had stopped asking.

"Hers came up on its own," said Shoko, to nobody.

Gojo, beside her, looked at the box too.

"Mm."

"Nobody planted it," said Shoko. "The wind did."

"Better gardener than any of us," said Gojo.

Shoko almost laughed. She looked at the small green stem with its two round leaves. Then she took something out of her coat pocket and looked at it. Mau, coming along the corridor with the empty morning cup to return to the kitchen, saw what it was. A little bundle of dried herb, tied with Kusuri's thread, from the festival. *Mine*, she had said, and put it away without showing anyone.

She looked at it, and at the seedling, and put it back in her pocket.

"Come on," she said to Gojo. "I've got a clinic."

They went on down the corridor together. Mau put the cup back on the end of Momo's counter, where it would be waiting tomorrow.
