# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 18 — Find North

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 18
**Continuity state:** Floors 16–18 summarised from Anko's board. Floor 19 (Day 345) with Mau (owner), Frieren, Yuta and Rem: an open plain under a turning night sky where only one star holds still; the objective is "find north"; Frieren and Mau find the fixed star by watching the sky turn (the roof star-naming, V29 "First Light"); Rem's morning star against the night-hounds; Yuta's sword. Mau reaches level 21 (seventy unspent). No extraction offered

*When: Day 345, morning outside, night inside. Anko's board now reads 16, 17, 18 under FLOOR. Under today: 19. Beside it, in Umaru's hand, an unauthorised note: ONE MORE AND IT'S TWENTY.*

Anko's board, again, said it plainly.

*Day 344, evening. Mau, Maki, Stark, Kaneki. From 15. Floor 16: a salt flat that cracked underfoot. Stark fell in once. Retrieved. Cleared.*

*Day 345, before dawn. Mau, Yuta, Yuka, Milim. From 15. Floor 17: stairs that went the wrong way. Yuka worked it out. Floor 18: a market with no people in it and too many doors. Milim opened all of them. Cleared, both. Three hours.*

Underneath, smaller: *From 15???*

"There's no transfer point at fifteen," said Anko, at the board, with her pencil. "You said every ten. Ten, twenty."

"There isn't," said Mau. "We walked from ten. Through eleven to fifteen. They're cleared, so it's quick. Just walking." He paused. "Long walking."

Anko looked at the board.

She crossed out *From 15???* and wrote *From 10 (walked to 15)*, and under that, *Long walking*, and underlined it.

Then she wrote today.

*Day 345. Mau, Frieren, Yuta, Rem.*

Rem, beside her, looked at her own name on the board with an expression of great seriousness. She had her morning star over her shoulder. She had asked at breakfast, very politely, in her turn-of-the-corridor voice. Ram had said *go*, and Rem had let herself be decided for.

"Three hours," said Anko.

"Three hours," said Rem. "Rem will be back for dinner. Ram said."

* * *

They walked from ten.

Through the iron wood, without touching the trees. Across the rope bridges on twelve. Through the hedge maze on thirteen, which was day now, so the hedges stood still. Past the hot spring on fourteen, where the pale thing with too many arms rose a little out of the steam to watch them go by and then sank back. Through the drowned library on fifteen, with Mau's sleeve of space round their legs in the black water. Over the salt flat on sixteen, which cracked under Rem's feet and did not break. Up the wrong stairs on seventeen, the way Yuka had worked out. Through the market on eighteen, where all the doors Milim had opened were still open.

It took an hour.

"Long walking," said Yuta, at the top of the stair to nineteen.

"Long walking," Mau agreed.

Frieren went up the last steps first.

She stopped at the top. She did not say anything. She stood very still, looking up.

* * *

It was night.

Not the grey light of the other floors. Real night, or something that looked exactly like it. A wide flat plain of dark grass going away in every direction to a horizon that was perfectly level, all the way round. Above it, the sky, enormous, full of stars.

And the stars were moving.

Not falling. Turning. The whole sky was turning, slowly, very slowly, around a point somewhere overhead, the way the sky turned at home if you lay on the roof for hours and watched. But faster. Much faster. You could see it. The stars wheeling in long arcs across the dark, steadily, like a great slow wheel.

The notice came up.

*Floor 19 of 100.*

*Objective: find north.*

Mau read it out.

Yuta looked round at the plain. Level grass in every direction. No hills. No trees. No path. Nothing to tell one way from another.

"North of what?" he said.

Rem was looking at the grass.

"There are no footprints," she said. "No path. Nothing that went anywhere." She looked up at the turning sky. "Rem thinks the only thing that goes anywhere here is up there."

* * *

The hounds came while they were still standing at the top of the stair.

They came out of the dark grass on every side at once, low and fast, making no sound. Black, with eyes like small pale stars. Eight of them. Ten.

Rem did not wait to be told.

She swung the morning star off her shoulder in one movement and the chain sang out. The iron ball went round in a great flat circle at the height of a hound's head. The first two that reached the top of the stair met it and went tumbling back into the grass and did not get up. Rem planted her feet and swung again. Her face had gone very calm, the way it went in the kitchen when the pear bread was rising and nobody was allowed to open the oven.

Yuta took the other side. His sword out, quiet and quick, the way he moved in the yard at dawn with Maki watching.

Frieren did not fight.

She was looking up.

"Keep them off me," she said. "I need to watch the sky."

* * *

Mau stood beside her.

He kept the small space up round them both, not a sleeve this time but a flat round wall at knee height. A hound that got past Rem's chain hit it, slowed and slowed, and hung there with its pale star eyes, going nowhere. Yuta came and took it from behind.

"What are you looking for?" said Mau.

"The one that doesn't move," said Frieren.

He looked up.

The sky turned. All of it, every star, wheeling in its long arc across the dark. He looked for one that was still and could not find it. They all moved. The near ones fast, the far ones slow. All of them going round.

"Look at the middle," said Frieren. "Not the edges. The middle of the turning. Where the arcs get small." She did not take her eyes off the sky. "I've slept outside for a very long time. Every sky I ever slept under turned around something. On the road, when there was no road, that was how we knew which way we were going." She paused. "The thing in the middle is the only one that stays."

Mau thought of the east roof. The telescope's first light. Suika with her glasses off, saying *there are ones behind the ones*. The small steady star he had named, low over the hills.

He looked at the middle of the turning.

The arcs got smaller toward the centre. Tighter. Little circles, then littler. And at the very centre, where the littlest circle should have been—

A star.

Small. Not bright. Very steady. Not moving at all, while the whole sky wheeled round it.

"There," said Mau.

Frieren looked where he was looking.

She did not say anything for a moment. Then she nodded, slowly.

"There," she said. "That's north." She looked down from the sky, at the plain, at the dark grass under the still star. "And it's straight ahead. Under it. If you walk toward the one that doesn't move—" She stopped. Then, very quietly, as if surprised: "It looks like *Still Here*."

Mau looked at the small steady star.

It did. A little apart from the turning. Small. Steady. Like the one he had named on the roof, low over the hills.

"It's not the same star," he said.

"I know," said Frieren. "It's a tower." She looked at it a moment longer. "It looks like it anyway."

* * *

They walked toward it.

Straight across the dark plain, with the sky wheeling overhead and the one still star in front of them. Rem walked on one side with the morning star swinging loose from her hand. Yuta walked on the other with his sword out. The hounds came twice more out of the grass. The first time Rem met them. The second time Yuta did. The third time they did not come at all.

They walked for a long time. Forty minutes, by Ciel's count. The horizon did not get any nearer. The grass did not change. The only thing that told them they were going anywhere was the still star, getting very slowly higher in front of them.

Then the grass ended.

At a low stone wall, waist-high, perfectly straight, going off to the left and right into the dark. In the wall, directly under the still star, a gate. Small and iron. Open.

Beyond the gate, a stair, going up.

*Floor 19 cleared.*

*Level 21. Seventy points unspent.*

"Twenty-one," said Mau.

"How many points?" said Yuta.

"Seventy."

Yuta whistled. Rem looked at Mau with interest.

"That's a great many," said Rem. "Umaru will need the whole wall."

"Umaru will need a second wall," said Yuta.

Frieren was still looking up at the still star.

"Twenty is next," she said. "Every tenth floor is a guardian. Ten was a guardian." She looked at the stair beyond the gate. "We should rest before twenty. Properly. Not walk straight in."

"We're not walking straight in," said Mau. "We're going home."

"Good," said Frieren.

* * *

They went home through the small return frame by the gate. On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Two hours fifty-five," she said. "From ten. Walking to eighteen." She wrote it down. "Nineteen?"

"A night sky," said Mau. "That turned. We had to find north."

"How?"

"The one that doesn't move," said Frieren.

Anko looked at her for a long moment. Then wrote, under *Floor 19. Cleared*: *Found north (Frieren, Mau). The one that doesn't move.*

That evening at dinner, Senku asked Frieren to tell him about the sky on nineteen. She did, carefully, in order: the turning, the arcs, the still star at the centre. Senku listened with his fork forgotten halfway to his mouth. When she finished, he put the fork down.

"A sky that turns around one star," he said. "Faster than ours. Inside a building." He looked at the ceiling. "That means something. I don't know what. Somebody built that on purpose, ten billion percent."

"Built it how?" said Byakuya.

"I don't know," said Senku. "Yet." He picked up his fork again. Then put it down. "Did you write it down? The arcs. How fast."

Frieren took a folded slip out of her sleeve and passed it down the table.

She had drawn it. The turning sky, the arcs getting smaller toward the middle, the still star at the centre. And under it, in her careful hand: *Things in the tower Senku knows. A sky that turns around north. (He will want to know how fast.)*

Senku looked at the slip for a long time.

Then he folded it very carefully and put it in his breast pocket. He did not say *ten billion percent* or anything else for the rest of dinner.
