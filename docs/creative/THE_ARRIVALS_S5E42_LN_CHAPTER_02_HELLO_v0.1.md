# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 19 — Hello

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 19
**Continuity state:** The first connection fragment. At the reset hour, after Frieren's cold foot ("One. Still here."), a voice reaches Mau through the shadow branch: "Hello." Then nothing; Mau answers and gets no reply. Ciel registers something reaching the branch, source unknown; Mau does not recognise the voice (he never met Jin-Woo; Yuta's party did). He tells Frieren at once and Rimuru in the morning, nobody else. No location, no army, no conversation; it is not household knowledge

*When: Day 346, the reset hour, a little before dawn. Nineteen floors behind him. Seventy points unspent. Lantern and the sentry are in storage, in the deeper part of his shadow, where the cup is twice the size it was.*

He woke the way he always woke at that hour.

All at once. Braced. Every muscle ready, his heart already going, his eyes open in the dark. The Trial's hour, that his body still kept even though the Trial was over. Even though there were no more loops. Even though it had been nearly a year.

For a second, he did not know where he was.

Then a foot touched his shin under the blanket. Cold. Deliberately cold, the way only an elf's foot could be cold in the middle of summer.

"One," said Frieren, into the pillow. "Still here."

He breathed out.

"Still here," he said.

The foot stayed where it was. She did not wake properly. She never did, at this hour. She woke just enough to put her foot on his shin and say the word, and then she went back down. In a minute her breathing would slow again. He would lie in the dark beside her and wait for his heart to come down, the way it always did, and then he would sleep again until the light came.

He lay in the dark.

The east room was very quiet. The connecting door was open a crack. Through it, faintly, Ori breathing, and Milim's ribbon creaking a little where it was tied to the bed frame. Above him, in the loft, Rimuru was a slime on a pillow, silent.

His heart came down.

He closed his eyes.

* * *

*Hello.*

He opened them.

It had not come through his ears. He knew that at once, the way you know whether a sound was in the room or in a dream. The room was silent. Ori breathing. The ribbon. Nothing else.

It had come from underneath.

From his shadow. From the part of it that was not the door where Ear slept, and not the old dark that the wheel sank into, but the new part. The deep part. The branch. The cup where Lantern and the sentry were.

A voice. One word. Male. Low. Very tired, the way a person is tired when they have been doing something hard for a long time without stopping.

*Hello.*

Mau lay perfectly still.

Under the blanket, Frieren's foot was still on his shin. He felt it go tense. Not because she had heard anything. Because he had. She could feel it in him, the way she could always feel when his heart changed.

"Mau," she said. Not into the pillow now.

"Wait," he said.

He listened. Not with his ears. With the other thing. The way he listened for Ear in the dark, or for the shadows in the cup.

Nothing.

The branch was quiet. Lantern was quiet. The sentry was quiet. The cup sat in his shadow with its two shapes in it and its new depth, and there was nothing else there at all.

*Ciel,* he said inwardly. *Did you—*

*I registered something,* said Ciel. Very quiet. Very careful. *A contact. Something reaching the branch from outside it. Brief. I could not tell from where.* A pause. *I did not hear words. I felt it arrive. You heard words?*

*One word.*

*What was it?*

*Hello.*

Ciel was silent for a moment.

*Answer it,* she said. *If you want to. I'll watch the boundaries.*

* * *

He did not know how.

He had never spoken into his shadow before. He had called things out of it. *Come out. Back. Arise.* That was not the same. That was a command, going one way.

He thought about the voice. How tired it had sounded.

He did not say it aloud. He said it the way he said things to Ciel, inwardly, but down instead of in. Toward the branch. Toward wherever the voice had come from.

*Hello?*

Nothing.

He waited.

The branch was quiet. The cup was quiet. Nothing reached it. Nothing answered.

He waited a long time. Long enough for Frieren to take her foot off his shin and turn over onto her side and put her hand flat on his chest, over his heart, and leave it there. Long enough for the window to go from black to grey.

Nothing came.

*It's gone,* said Ciel eventually. *Whatever reached the branch isn't reaching it now. No change to anything. The shadows are as they were. The reserve is as it was.* A pause. *I don't know what that was.*

*Neither do I.*

* * *

"Tell me," said Frieren.

She had not moved. Her hand was still on his chest. Her eyes were open now, in the grey light, looking at him across the pillow.

He told her.

All of it. The waking, the foot, his heart coming down. The word. Where it had come from. That it had not been in the room. That it had been a man, and tired. That he had answered, and nothing had come back. That Ciel had felt something arrive and could not tell from where.

Frieren listened without interrupting. She always did, when he told her things like this. She listened the way she listened to a spell being explained, to every part, in order, so that she could hold the whole shape of it.

When he finished, she was quiet for a long time.

"Did you know the voice?" she said.

"No."

"Not at all?"

He thought about it. Carefully.

"No," he said. "I've never heard it before. I'd remember."

Frieren looked at him.

"The man who stood in your place," she said slowly. "In the exchange. When you went through and came back. Yuta's party met him. He gave them his name." She paused. "You didn't meet him. You were somewhere else."

"I know."

"Could it be him?"

Mau looked at the grey window.

"I don't know," he said. "I've never heard his voice. I only know what Yuta said. That he wanted to go back. To his war." He was quiet for a moment. "This one sounded tired. Like he'd been in a war a long time."

Frieren did not say anything.

Then she moved closer, so her forehead was against his shoulder, and her hand was still flat on his chest.

"Who do we tell?" she said.

He had already thought about it. He had thought about it in the long grey wait while nothing came.

"Rimuru," he said. "In the morning. Because Ciel is his too, and she felt it." He paused. "Nobody else. Not yet. It was one word. I don't know what it was. I don't want the whole house waiting for my shadow to talk."

"Yuta?"

"Yuta heard the man's voice," said Mau. "In the exchange. If it happens again, and there's more, I'll ask him if it sounds the same." He looked at the ceiling. "Not for one word."

Frieren considered this.

"That's sensible," she said.

"You keep saying that."

"You keep being sensible," said Frieren. "It's very new. I'm still getting used to it."

He almost laughed.

* * *

He told Rimuru at breakfast.

Not at the long table. On the back step, with two cups of tea, before anyone else was out. He told him exactly what he had told Frieren. Rimuru listened with his chin on his knees. When Mau finished, he did not say anything for a while.

*She told me,* he said at last, inwardly, on the thread. *Ciel. At dawn. She said something reached the branch.* A pause. *She didn't know it was a word. Only that it came.*

"One word," said Mau aloud.

"*Hello*," said Rimuru.

"Hello."

Rimuru turned his cup round in his hands.

"That's a very polite thing," he said, "for something to say when it reaches into somebody's shadow in the middle of the night."

"It sounded tired."

"Everyone sounds tired at that hour."

"Not like that."

Rimuru looked at him sideways.

"Okay," he said. "So we wait. If it comes again, you tell me and Frieren and Ciel. If there's more than one word, we think about Yuta. If it starts saying anything that sounds like a threat, or a demand, or anything except *hello*, we think about everyone." He drank his tea. "Until then, it's one polite word from somewhere we don't know. That's not nothing. It's not much."

"That's what I thought."

"Good." Rimuru put his cup down. "Did you answer it?"

"I said hello back."

"Of course you did." Rimuru almost smiled. "Rude not to."

They sat on the back step and drank their tea while the yard filled with morning. Momo started the bread. Taihei came out to fetch water. Ori came down the stairs with Milim on the ribbon behind her. Nobody looked at Mau's shadow, long and thin across the step in the low sun, with something new in it that nobody could see, and that had said one word, once, and then nothing at all.
