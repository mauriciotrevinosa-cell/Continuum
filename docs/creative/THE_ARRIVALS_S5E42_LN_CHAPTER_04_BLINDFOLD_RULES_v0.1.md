# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 21 — Blindfold Rules

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 21
**Continuity state:** The five (Hakari, Karane, Shizuka, Nano, Kusuri) spend an afternoon on a game they chose themselves: Kusuri's leftover festival herbs, guessed by smell while blindfolded, with Nano as scorekeeper and then as player. Each contributes differently (Nano is not only the sums; Kusuri does not speak for all five). The inter-girl threads are only touched lightly (DH-055: no "click", no new ship granted). No Mau; no threat

*When: Day 346, late afternoon, in the kotatsu room with the kotatsu blanket off because it is summer. Kusuri has a basket. Nano has a slate. Hakari has already lost twice and the game has not started.*

The game was Kusuri's idea, which surprised everyone, including Kusuri.

She had the basket from the festival. The herb bundles that nobody had drawn were still in it, tied with her thread: rosemary, thyme, sage, basil, mint, lavender, chamomile, parsley, a few others. She had carried the basket back to the kotatsu room after the festival and put it on the shelf and looked at it for nine days. She did not know what to do with it.

Then, this morning, she had said at breakfast, very fast, before she could stop herself: "Does anyone want to play a game? With the herbs? This afternoon? I made it up. It might be bad."

Hakari had said *yes* before Kusuri finished the sentence. Karane had said *fine* in a voice that meant *yes*. Nano had said, "Define the rules first, please." Shizuka had written *Yes* on her page and underlined it twice.

So here they were.

* * *

"It's very simple," said Kusuri.

She held up a strip of cloth.

"Blindfold. You put it on. I hold a bundle under your nose. You smell it. You guess what it is." She paused. "And then—this is the part I made up—you have to guess what it *means*. From the festival. The flower meanings."

"Two points," said Nano, writing on the slate. "One for the herb. One for the meaning."

"Yes," said Kusuri. "Exactly."

"What if I get the meaning but not the herb?" said Hakari.

"How would you get the meaning without the herb?"

"Vibes," said Hakari.

Nano wrote *Vibes: zero points* on the slate.

Hakari looked offended.

"That's not fair."

"It is perfectly fair," said Nano. "Vibes are not data."

* * *

Hakari went first, because she would not stop asking.

Kusuri tied the cloth round her eyes. Hakari sat very straight on the floor with her hands on her knees, vibrating. Kusuri chose a bundle from the basket and held it under her nose.

Hakari sniffed. Hard. Like a dog.

"Mint," she said immediately.

"Yes!" said Kusuri.

"And it means—" Hakari thought about it. "Suspicion! Okarun got mint. Jiji said suspicion."

"It means protection *or* suspicion," said Kusuri. "Depending on the book. So—yes. Half."

"Half a point," said Nano, writing.

"There's no such thing as half a point!"

"There is now," said Nano. "I have invented it. I am the scorekeeper."

Hakari took off the blindfold and looked at the slate, where it said *Hakari: 1.5*, and decided that was a very good score, and said so at length.

* * *

Karane went next.

She made a great show of not caring. She let Kusuri tie the blindfold with her arms folded and her chin up and an expression of tremendous indifference. Kusuri held a bundle under her nose.

Karane sniffed. Once. Delicately.

Then went very red under the blindfold.

"Thyme," she said.

"Yes."

"It means *courage*," said Karane, very fast, very quietly. "Everyone knows that."

"Two points," said Nano.

Hakari, beside her, said, "That's mine! That's my fortune! We both got thyme! At the festival!"

"I know," said Karane, still blindfolded, still red. "I was there."

"You kept yours," said Hakari. "In your pocket. I saw you."

Karane did not say anything at all.

She took off the blindfold. She did not look at Hakari. She looked at the floor, with her arms still folded, and her ears going redder and redder.

Shizuka, in the corner, took the pencil out of the spine of her book and wrote on her page. She did not show it to anyone. She closed the book on it.

Nano looked at the closed book. Then at the slate. Then at Shizuka.

She did not write anything down.

* * *

Shizuka went third.

She let Kusuri tie the blindfold without moving at all. She sat with her book on her knees and her pencil in her hand, very still. When Kusuri held the bundle under her nose, she did not sniff. She breathed in slowly, the way you breathe in at the start of a long sentence.

Then she opened her book, blindfolded, and wrote on the page without looking, and turned it round.

*Lavender. Devotion.*

The writing was perfectly straight, as if she had been able to see the lines.

"Two points," said Kusuri, impressed.

"How did you write that without looking?" said Hakari.

Shizuka turned the page and wrote again.

*I know where the lines are.*

She took off the blindfold. Then, very carefully, she held it out across the low table to Nano.

"I am the scorekeeper," said Nano.

Shizuka wrote on her page and turned it round.

*Kusuri can keep score. You should play.*

Nano looked at the page for a long time.

"I do not see the purpose," she said. "I know the flower meanings. I read Kusuri's list on the night of the festival. All of them. I can recite it." She paused. "There is no uncertainty for me in this game. It would not be a game. It would be a test I have already passed."

Shizuka wrote.

*Then it will be very short. Play anyway.*

Nano looked at her.

Then she put down the slate and gave it to Kusuri. She took the blindfold and tied it round her own eyes, precisely, with the knot exactly at the back.

* * *

Kusuri looked at the basket.

She looked at it for a long time. Then she reached past all the bundles from the festival. Right to the bottom, where there was a small cloth bag she had not shown anyone. She took out a sprig of something from her own stores, from her own room, that had never been in the basket at the festival at all.

She held it under Nano's nose.

Nano breathed in.

Then she was very still.

"That is not on the list," she said.

"No," said Kusuri.

"I do not know it." Nano breathed in again. "It smells—green. And something sweet underneath. Like the yard after rain." She paused. "I do not know what it is. I do not know what it means." Her voice was very level, as it always was. But her hands, on her knees, had closed. "I cannot score this."

"Guess," said Kusuri.

"I do not guess. Guessing is not—"

"Vibes," said Hakari, from the floor.

Nano turned her blindfolded face toward Hakari. Then back toward Kusuri.

She breathed in once more.

"It smells like something you would give someone," said Nano slowly, "when you did not want them to know you were giving it to them." She paused. "That is not data. That is—" She stopped. "That is a guess."

Kusuri looked at the sprig in her hand.

"It's sweet woodruff," she said. "It doesn't smell of much when it's fresh. It only smells like that when it dries." She paused. "In the old books, people used to put it in pillows. Or in a drawer with someone's clothes. Where they'd find it later and not know who put it there." She looked at Nano. "It means *humility*. And *patience*. And—in one book—*hidden worth*."

Nano did not say anything.

She took off the blindfold. She did not look at Kusuri. She did not look at anyone. She looked at the sprig of sweet woodruff in Kusuri's hand.

"Zero points," said Nano. "I got neither."

"One point," said Kusuri, writing on the slate. "For the guess. I'm the scorekeeper now. I can invent things too."

Nano looked at the slate. At *Nano: 1*.

Then, very quietly, she said, "May I keep it?"

Kusuri gave her the sprig.

Nano put it in her pocket, the way Karane had put away her thyme, and did not say anything else. But Shizuka, in the corner, wrote something on her page. This time she turned it round and showed it only to Nano. Nano read it. Her face did not change at all, because Nano's face did not change.

She sat a little closer to Shizuka for the rest of the game, though, so that their shoulders nearly touched.

* * *

Kusuri played last. Hakari held the bundle, because she insisted. She held it far too close and nearly put it up Kusuri's nose. Kusuri said "Chamomile, patience in adversity," before Hakari had even finished lifting it, and everyone laughed. Even Nano made a small sound that was not quite a laugh, and Karane laughed with her arms still folded.

Final score, on Kusuri's slate, in Kusuri's hand:

*Hakari: 1.5. Karane: 2. Shizuka: 2. Nano: 1. Kusuri: 2.*

And underneath, smaller:

*Vibes: 0. (Disputed.)*

They put the herbs back in the basket. Kusuri put the basket back on the shelf. It did not look like something she didn't know what to do with any more. It looked like a game they could play again.

On the way out, Hakari stopped in the doorway.

"Kusuri," she said.

"Mm?"

"It wasn't bad," said Hakari. "The game. You said at breakfast it might be bad." She grinned. "It wasn't bad."

Kusuri went pink and did not answer, because she did not know how. But she carried the slate to dinner and propped it up at the end of the long table next to Umaru's, where people could see it.
