# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 22 — The Empty Throne

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** Floor 20 (Day 347), the second guardian floor. Party: Mau (owner), Gojo, Yuta and Maki, walking from the floor 10 transfer through nineteen (about two hours); a ruined throne hall with a red-armoured knight kneeling before an empty throne. The notice names him: Igris, the Blood-Red Commander. He asks for single combat with the owner, and the party agrees to terms (they step in only if Mau calls or falls). The notice is how Mau knows the name; this is a copy, not the original

*When: Day 347, dawn outside. Five hours agreed, because of the long walking. Shoko has examined Mau, said "fine," and then said it again more quietly to Frieren at the door.*

They left before breakfast, because of the walking.

Anko's board said it, in her plain hand:

*Day 347. Mau, Gojo, Yuta, Maki. From 10. Floor 20 (guardian). Five hours.*

And under it, smaller, in Frieren's hand, which nobody had seen her write:

*Come back for the roof.*

It was the seventh night. Day 347. Roof and crackers. Mau saw the line and did not say anything about it, and neither did anyone else. But Gojo, reading it over his shoulder, put his hand on Mau's head and messed up his hair, which he had not done before.

"We'll be back for the roof," said Gojo. "I'm not missing Rimuru complaining about the draught. It's the best part of my week."

"Rimuru doesn't have the draught any more," said Yuta. "Taihei does."

"Then I'm not missing Taihei complaining about the draught," said Gojo. "Even better. New material."

Maki had her spear over her shoulder. The old shaft, with the crack in it. She had not cut the new one yet. Below the blade, tied tight with grip-thread, there was a small silver flower.

Mau saw it. He looked at Maki.

Maki looked back at him and did not explain.

* * *

They walked from ten.

Through the iron wood. Over the rope bridges. Through the hedge maze and past the hot spring and through the drowned library, with Mau's sleeve round their legs in the black water. Over the salt flat. Up the wrong stairs. Through the empty market with all its open doors. Across the night plain on nineteen, with the sky wheeling overhead and the one still star in front of them, to the low wall and the small iron gate.

Two hours. Mau kept count with Ciel. Long walking.

At the top of the stair beyond the gate, they stopped.

There was a door. Tall, dark, wooden, with iron bands across it, and a great iron ring for a handle. Above it, painted on the stone in faded red, a pale *20*.

And the notice, before Mau touched anything.

*Floor 20 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the second ten.*

*Transfer point beyond.*

Mau read it out.

"Second ten," said Yuta.

"Bigger than the first," said Maki. She had been inside the reach of the first. She knew what *bigger* meant.

Gojo pushed his blindfold up. He looked at the door with his bare eyes for a long moment.

"There's one thing behind there," he said. "One. Not a crowd. Not dogs." His voice had gone quiet, which it did not often do. "And it's very, very good at something."

"At what?" said Mau.

"Standing still," said Gojo. "Waiting. The way a good swordsman waits." He lowered the blindfold. "I knew people like that. In my world. You don't see them move until they already have."

* * *

Mau opened the door.

It was a hall. A great long hall, in ruins. Tall stone pillars down both sides, some of them cracked, some broken off halfway up. A roof so high it was lost in grey shadow, with holes in it where grey light came down in long slanting bars. The floor was flagstones, cracked, with dust on them and broken stone. Banners hung from the pillars, faded red, torn, moving very slightly though there was no wind.

At the far end of the hall, up three wide steps, a throne.

Stone. Tall-backed. Empty.

And kneeling in front of it, on the lowest step, with its back to them, a knight.

Red armour. Dark red, the colour of old blood, the plates overlapping, every edge sharp. A long red cloak spread out on the steps behind it. A helm with a crest. A sword, very long, very straight, its point resting on the stone in front of it, both gauntleted hands folded on the hilt.

It did not move.

The four of them stood in the doorway.

"Oh," said Gojo softly. "Oh, I see."

The notice came up. Not the usual small one at the edge. A larger one, in the middle of Mau's sight.

*Guardian: Igris, the Blood-Red Commander.*

Mau read it twice.

He had not known the guardians would have names. The one on ten had not. It had just been *the guardian*. This one had a name and a title. Somebody, somewhere, had decided the thing kneeling in front of the empty throne should be called something.

"Igris," he said aloud.

The knight lifted its head.

* * *

It stood up.

It did it slowly, the way an old soldier stands, all at once and without effort, in one long movement. The cloak fell into place behind it. It turned round on the steps and looked at them down the length of the hall.

Its helm had no face behind it. Like the sentries, like the guardian on ten. But behind the visor, where eyes should have been, there were two small lights. Not dull red, like the guardian. Bright. A clear sharp red, steady, like coals that had been blown on.

It looked at the four of them.

Then it lifted the long sword, point up, and held it in front of its helm. Very straight. Very still.

A salute.

Yuta made a small sound.

"That's—" he said. "That's a salute. That's how you start a duel. A proper one."

The knight lowered the sword. It pointed it, slowly, down the length of the hall.

At Mau.

Only at Mau.

The notice changed.

*The guardian requests single combat with the Operator.*

* * *

Nobody said anything for a long moment.

"No," said Gojo.

Mau looked at him.

"No," said Gojo again. He had pushed his blindfold up. His bare eyes were on the knight, and his voice was very level, and very quiet. "I can see it. All of it. It's not like the one on ten. The one on ten was big and strong and stupid. This one isn't stupid. This one has fought a thousand times and won every one." He did not look away from the knight. "You're not walking down there alone."

"It asked for the owner," said Mau.

"I don't care what it asked for."

"If I don't—" Mau looked at the notice. "I don't know what happens if I refuse. Maybe it fights all four of us. Maybe the floor doesn't clear. Maybe nothing." He looked at the knight, still pointing its sword at him down the hall, waiting. "It asked properly. With a salute."

Gojo turned his head and looked at him.

Yuta was looking at the knight. Maki was looking at Mau.

"Terms," said Maki.

Everyone looked at her.

"Terms," said Maki again. She leaned on her spear. "A duel has terms. You don't just walk in." She looked at Gojo. "We stand at the door. All three of us. We don't move unless he calls us. Or unless he goes down and doesn't get up." She looked at Mau. "If you call, you call. No pride about it. If you go down, we come. No discussion. That's the terms."

Gojo looked at her for a long time.

"And if it doesn't like those terms?"

"Then it can say so," said Maki. "It saluted. It can read."

Mau looked at the notice.

He thought the terms, carefully, the way he would have thought them to Ciel. *Single combat. They stay at the door. If I call, they come. If I fall, they come.*

The notice did not change for a moment.

Then, beneath *The guardian requests single combat with the owner*, a new line.

*Terms acknowledged.*

At the far end of the hall, the knight lowered its sword. And then, slowly, it lifted it again, point up, in front of its helm.

The salute again. For the terms.

* * *

Gojo let out a long breath.

"I hate this," he said.

"I know," said Mau.

"I'm going to stand at the door with my eyes open the whole time."

"I know."

"If I see something I don't like, I'm calling it for you."

"That's not the terms."

"I'm amending the terms," said Gojo. "Unilaterally. Umaru does it all the time."

Yuta put a hand on Gojo's arm. Gojo looked at it. Then at Yuta. He did not say anything else.

Maki came and stood in front of Mau.

She did not say *be careful*. She did not say *you can do it*. She looked at him for a long moment. Then she unslung her spear from her shoulder and held it out to him, crosswise, in both hands. The old shaft, with the crack. The small silver flower tied below the blade.

"You haven't got a weapon," she said.

"I've got—"

"You've got your hands and your eyes and that space thing," said Maki. "That's not a weapon. That's a way to not get hit." She held the spear out. "It's got a sword. You need something longer than a sword."

Mau looked at the spear.

"It's cracked," he said.

"It'll hold," said Maki. "For one fight." She looked at the silver flower. "Mai made that. It's not for anything. Bring it back."

He took the spear.

It was heavier than it looked. Balanced near the blade. He had watched Maki use it a hundred times in the yard, against Yuta, against Mikasa, once against Gojo. He had watched her inside the reach of the guardian on ten. He had never held it.

His eyes, behind their filter, read it. The weight. The balance. The crack in the shaft, and how far it went, and how much it would take.

"Thank you," he said.

"Bring it back," said Maki again, and went and stood at the door with the others.

* * *

Mau walked down the hall.

Between the broken pillars, under the torn red banners, through the long bars of grey light. The knight stood on the lowest step in front of the empty throne and watched him come.

He stopped ten paces away.

The knight lowered its sword from the salute and held it ready. Two-handed. Point forward. Very still.

Its red eyes were on him.

Mau held the spear the way Maki held it. He had seen it a hundred times. He held it, and felt his eyes read the knight in front of him. The armour. The sword. The weight on the back foot. The way it waited.

Gojo was right. It had fought a thousand times.

He took a breath.

At the door, behind him, very faint, he heard Gojo say something under his breath. He thought it might have been his name.

The knight moved.
