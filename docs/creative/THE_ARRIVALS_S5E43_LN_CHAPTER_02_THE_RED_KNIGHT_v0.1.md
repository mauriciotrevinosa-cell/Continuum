# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 23 — The Red Knight

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30

*When: Day 347, morning outside, the grey light of floor 20 inside. Ten paces. The knight has just moved.*

The first cut came faster than anything Mau had ever seen.

He did not see it start. Gojo had been right. One moment the knight was standing on the lowest step with its sword held ready, and the next it was not there. It was inside the ten paces with the long blade already coming across, flat and level, at the height of his neck.

The space caught it.

He had put it up before he walked down the hall, a thin flat skin of Infinity, close round him, the way he had held it in the drowned library. The blade hit it a hand's breadth from his shoulder and slowed. And slowed. And hung there, in the air beside his neck, going nowhere.

The knight did not push.

That was the first thing he learned. The guardian on ten had pushed. It had leaned its whole weight on its weapon when the space stopped it, trying to force through, the way something strong and stupid would. The knight did not. The moment the blade slowed, it drew back. Smoothly, without hurry. It stepped back two paces and held the sword ready again. The red lights behind its visor were on him.

Studying.

*It's learning you,* said Ciel, very quietly.

*I know.*

* * *

It came again.

Not at his neck this time. Low, at his knee, from the left. The space caught it. Then high, from the right, at his head, faster. The space caught that too. Then from straight in front, a thrust, the point coming at his chest, and the space caught it and held it, a finger's width from his shirt.

Every time, the knight drew back. Every time, it came again from somewhere different.

And every time, Mau felt the space cost a little more.

Not the reserve. The other thing. The thing he used for the space and the pull and his eyes. It was not deep either. It had never been deep. He was spending it every time the knight came at him, and the knight was not spending anything at all.

At the door, he heard Gojo say something under his breath. He did not catch it.

He did not need to. He knew what Gojo would say, because Gojo had already shown him, weeks ago, in the yard, with three small stones on the ground and no techniques at all. Where you put your attention, and how much, and how little. Mau had lost to the stones. Maki had told the whole house. But he had understood the thing underneath: the space was only as wide as his attention. Something that could see where he was looking could find where he wasn't.

The knight came again.

From behind.

* * *

He did not see it go round him. He felt the space thin at his back, where his attention was not, and turned. Too late. Not by much. The blade came through the thin place, slowed, nearly stopped.

Nearly.

It touched his upper arm. The left one. Lightly, the very edge of it. It opened his sleeve and the skin under it in a long shallow line, and he felt the cold of it first and the heat after.

He stepped away. The knight stepped back. Two paces. Sword ready.

Blood ran down inside his sleeve.

"*Left!*"

Gojo. From the door. Loud, sharp, the way he had never heard Gojo shout.

Mau did not look round. He knew Gojo had shouted too late, and that he knew it. He knew Gojo had broken the terms by shouting at all, and that he did not care. He heard Maki say something low and furious. He heard Yuta say *Satoru*. He heard Gojo say nothing else.

He held the spear and looked at the knight.

*It found the edge,* said Ciel. *Where your attention wasn't. It will do it again. It's faster at finding it every time.*

*I know.*

*The cut is shallow. Shoko will want it cleaned. It won't slow you yet.*

*Yet.*

* * *

He stopped looking at the sword.

He did not decide to. It came from somewhere else. The low step under the trees, the same day as the stones, in the evening light. Yuta with two practice weapons, stopping halfway through a move to let him see exactly where his weight had gone. *You're trying to finish before you start.* Not the weapon. The weight. The weapon was only the last thing to arrive.

He watched the knight's feet.

They were very still, in their red armoured boots, on the cracked flagstones. Weight on the back foot. Waiting.

He watched its shoulders.

And his eyes, behind their filter, did what they did. They read. The way the armour sat. The way the plates moved over each other when it breathed. If it breathed. The tiny shift in the weight, the left shoulder dropping a hair. Before the feet moved. Before the sword moved.

*There,* he thought.

And then he thought further. That was the new part. He had never thought further, in a fight. He had reacted, and held, and hoped.

*It finds where I'm not looking. Every time. Faster every time.*

*Then I'll choose where I'm not looking.*

He let the space go thin on his right side. Not by accident. On purpose. He held it everywhere else, thick, and left the right thin, at the hip, like a door left open a crack. And he watched the knight's left shoulder.

It dropped.

The knight came. From the right, low, at his hip. Exactly where he had left the door open. Of course it did. It had found the place he was not looking. He had made the place.

He was already moving.

Not back. In. Toward the blade, along it, inside the reach, the way Maki had stepped inside the reach of the guardian on ten. The long sword was committed to the cut. It had to go somewhere. It went where the door had been. It opened his thigh as he stepped past it, a long hot line across the outside of his right leg. He had known it would. He had chosen it. A cut on the leg for one step inside.

The blade went past behind him.

He was inside.

* * *

He had the spear. He had watched Maki use it a hundred times.

He did not try to do what she did. He could not. He did not have her hands, or her years, or the thing in her body that made her what she was. He had a cracked spear and his eyes and a heartbeat inside the knight's reach before it turned.

He did the one thing he had seen her do on ten.

He drove the spear up under the knight's arm. Into the gap, where the plates of the shoulder met the plates of the chest, and there was only something darker underneath.

It went in.

The knight made no sound.

It turned, very fast, faster than anything that big should turn, and the long sword came round. The pommel, not the blade. Too close for the blade. The heavy iron end of the hilt came round at his head.

He put the space there.

All of it. Everything he had left, in one small hard place the size of his hand, at the side of his head, where the pommel was coming. Too small. Too late. The pommel hit the edge of the space and slid along it and came down past his head into his ribs instead.

He heard them go. Two of them. On the left. A sound like green sticks.

The breath went out of him. All of it. He did not fall, because he was holding on to the spear, and the spear was in the knight. But the shock of the space had gone back up the knight's arm too, and the knight staggered, one step, sideways, on the cracked stone.

He used the pull.

Small. The smallest thing he could make. A tug, at the knight's sword arm, at the wrist, toward the floor. Not enough to move it far. A hair. Just enough that when the knight tried to bring the sword back up, it came up a hair late, and a hair off line.

He pulled the spear out.

And drove it in again. Same place. Deeper.

The crack in the shaft groaned. He felt it in his hands. A long splintering shiver down the wood. It held.

* * *

The knight stopped.

It stood very still, with the spear in its side, and the sword in its hand, and the red lights behind its visor on him.

Then, slowly, it went down on one knee.

It did not fall. It knelt. The way it had been kneeling when they came in, in front of the empty throne. It put the point of its long sword on the stone in front of it and folded both gauntleted hands on the hilt. It bowed its head.

The red lights behind its visor dimmed. Not out. Dimmed, like coals going grey at the edges.

Mau let go of the spear.

He stepped back. Two paces. One of them did not hold, and he went down on one knee and got up again. His left sleeve was wet to the wrist. His right trouser leg was wet to the boot. Every breath went into his left side like a knife and stopped halfway. The thing he used for the space and the pull and his eyes was nearly empty, so empty the edges of things looked thin. He stood in the grey light in the ruined hall and looked at the red knight, kneeling, with Maki's spear in its side and its head bowed.

The notice came up.

*Guardian defeated.*

He stood there.

He heard feet behind him. Running. Gojo first, because Gojo had been moving before the notice even finished. Then Yuta. Then Maki, last, not running, walking fast. She stopped beside the kneeling knight and looked at her spear in its side, and at the long crack running down the shaft, and at the small silver flower below the blade. Not a scratch on it.

Gojo had Mau by both shoulders. Mau made a sound when he did, and Gojo let go of the left one at once.

"Your ribs," he said. His bare eyes were on Mau's side. "Two. I can see them. And your leg. And your arm."

"The arm's shallow."

"The *leg* isn't," said Gojo. His voice was very strange. "I saw it go in. You let it. You opened your right side on purpose and you let it cut you so you could step past it." He was reading all of it, the way his eyes read everything. "It found the place you weren't looking. Because you *gave* it the place."

"It was going to find one anyway," said Mau. Every word went into his side. "I wanted to know which one."

"I know. I'm sorry you had to watch."

"Don't apologise to me," said Gojo. His voice was very strange. "Don't you dare apologise to me."

He took off his own scarf and tied it round Mau's thigh, over the cut, tight, the way you tie something when your hands need something to do. Yuta, without being asked, tore the end off his own sleeve and did the arm.

"I shouted," he said, tying. "I broke the terms."

"I heard."

"It was too late anyway."

"I know."

Gojo finished the knot. He looked at it. Then he looked at Mau.

"You stopped watching the sword," he said.

"Yuta showed me. Where the weight goes."

Gojo turned his head and looked at Yuta. Yuta was standing a little way off, very pale, with his hand on his own sword hilt, as if he had been about to draw it for the whole fight and had only just remembered to let go.

"And where you put the space," said Gojo. "That was mine."

"That was your stones."

Gojo looked at him for a long time.

"Well," he said. "At least you were listening." And then, much more quietly, so only Mau could hear: "Don't do that again. Don't make me stand at a door."

* * *

Maki pulled her spear out of the knight's side.

It came out clean. She held it up and looked along the shaft at the crack. It ran from below the blade nearly to the grip now, a long split, white in the dark wood.

"It held," she said.

"You said it would."

"I said for one fight." She looked at the crack. "That was one fight." She turned the spear so the small silver flower caught the grey light. "Mai's flower's fine."

"I brought it back."

"You brought it back," said Maki.

She did not say anything else. But she put her hand on the back of his neck for a moment, the way she had put it on Yuka's on the roof on ten. Just for a moment. Then she took it away and went to stand beside Yuta. She put her hand on his arm until he took his hand off his sword.

The knight knelt before the empty throne with its head bowed and its red lights dimmed. It was waiting.

Under it, between the flagstones, a darkness was beginning to gather.
