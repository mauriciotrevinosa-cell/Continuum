# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 24 — My Liege

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** The extraction of Igris. The deeper reserve makes it possible; the cost is real and larger than the dragon's. The copy speaks seldom and formally ("My liege"), knightly and disciplined, with his own judgement; his memories of any original are not established. He returns Maki's spear with a bow to its owner and notices the silver flower. The floor 20 transfer point is secured. Mau reaches level 22 (seventy-five unspent); three registered shadows (Lantern, the sentry, Igris) out of ten places

*When: Day 347, the grey light of floor 20. The knight is kneeling before the empty throne. Under it, between the flagstones, the dark is gathering.*

The notice came up while Gojo was still looking at the scarf on his arm.

*Shadow extraction available.*

Mau looked at the kneeling knight.

Under it, between the cracked flagstones, the darkness was deep. Deeper than the sentry's had been. Deeper than the dragon's, at the snowline. He could feel the depth of it from where he stood, the way you feel cold coming off a well.

He thought about floor 10. The guardian under the blue light, and the outline beneath it, and Ciel saying *you could raise it, you could not hold it*. He had closed the notice and said *not this one*, because the cup was too shallow, and he would rather have a little left for the next thing.

This was the next thing.

*Ciel,* he said. *Can I?*

She was quiet for a long moment. He felt her looking. Not at the knight. At him. At the cup, twice as deep as it had been on floor 10, and at the shallow fill sitting in the bottom of it, and at the other thing, the one he used for the space and the pull, nearly empty after the fight.

*The reserve can hold it,* she said at last. *In storage, with Lantern and the sentry. With a margin left after. That's what the thirty points were for.* A pause. *Raising it will cost more than the dragon did. A good deal more. You'll feel it. And the other reserve, the one for the space, is nearly gone, and this won't touch it, but you're tired, and tired makes the focus harder.* Another pause. *I'd do it now, while it's waiting. I don't know how long it waits.*

Mau looked at the others.

Gojo was looking at the knight with his bare eyes. Yuta was looking at Mau. Maki was leaning on her cracked spear, looking at nothing in particular, the way she looked when she had already decided something and was waiting for everyone else to catch up.

"Ciel says I can," said Mau. "It'll cost. More than the dragon."

"Then do it sitting down," said Maki.

* * *

He did not sit down.

He walked the two paces back to the kneeling knight and stood in front of it, close, closer than he had been when he drove the spear in. Its head was still bowed. The red lights behind its visor were dim, grey at the edges. Its hands were still folded on the hilt of the long sword, point on the stone.

It had saluted him twice. Once to start. Once for the terms.

He thought about that.

He did not need to be angrier than the thing had been. He had learned that with Ear, a long time ago, and again with the dragon at the snowline. He needed to know what he was asking it to follow.

He knew.

*I'm not asking you to serve a throne,* he thought, at the bowed red helm. *There isn't one. There's a house. With people in it who stand at doors. I'm asking you to stand at one too.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the stone.

It came up through the knight. That was the strangest thing. The red armour did not dissolve into blue light first, the way the guardian on ten had. The dark came up out of the flagstones underneath it and rose through it, through the plates, through the cloak, through the bowed helm. The red went black where it passed, not burned, but deepened, as if the colour were sinking into a well. Until the knight was not red at all. It was dark, all of it, armour and cloak and sword, with no shine on it, the way Lantern had no shine.

Except the eyes.

Behind the visor, the two small lights came back. Not dim. Not grey. Bright. And not red any more. A cold clear blue, like Lantern's, but sharper. Two points of it, steady, burning.

Mau felt the cost.

Ciel had been right. It was a great deal more than the dragon. The reserve went down, steady and definite and fast, much faster than it had gone for Lantern, the shallow fill draining out of the bottom of the cup like water out of a cracked jar. He felt his knees go. He locked them. He kept the intention steady, the way he had kept it at the snowline. *A house. A door. Stand at it.*

The draining slowed.

Stopped.

The knight lifted its head.

* * *

It looked at him.

The two cold blue lights behind its visor were on his face. It did not move for a long moment. Then, very slowly, it took its hands off the hilt of the long sword. It lifted the sword off the stone. And it turned the blade, and laid it flat across both its open palms, and held it up toward him, kneeling, with its head bowed again.

An offering.

Mau did not know what to do. He had never been offered a sword.

*It's a knight's gesture,* said Ciel, very quietly. *The sword is yours to command. You don't have to take it. You acknowledge it.*

He put his hand on the flat of the blade. Just for a moment. It was cold, like Lantern's jaw. Like stone in shade.

"Thank you," he said.

The knight drew the sword back. It stood up, all at once, in the long smooth movement of an old soldier, and was very tall in front of him. Taller than Stark. The dark cloak fell into place behind it.

And it spoke.

"My liege."

* * *

Nobody moved.

The voice was low. Not loud. It did not echo in the ruined hall the way it should have. It was dry and level and very calm, like a man who had given a great many orders on a great many fields and had never once needed to raise his voice to be obeyed. Two words. That was all.

Behind him, Mau heard Yuta take a breath.

"You can talk," said Mau.

The knight inclined its helm. Very slightly.

"When there is need, my liege."

"I'm not—" Mau stopped. He did not know how to say it. "I'm not a liege. I'm not a king. I don't have a throne." He looked past the knight at the empty stone chair at the top of the steps. "That's not mine. I don't want it."

The knight looked at him for a long moment with its cold blue eyes.

"Yes, my liege," it said.

Gojo made a sound behind him. It might have been a laugh. It might have been something else.

"My name's Mau," said Mau.

The knight considered this.

"My liege," it said, "is called Mau." As if filing it. As if it were a fact about the liege, and not a reason to call him anything different.

Mau opened his mouth. Then closed it.

"He's not going to stop," said Maki, from behind him. "Is he."

"No," said Mau. "I don't think he is."

* * *

The knight turned its head and looked at Maki.

It looked at her for a long time. At her, and at the cracked spear she was leaning on, with the long white split down the shaft. And at the small silver flower tied below the blade.

Then it came down the steps. It walked past Mau. It walked across the cracked flagstones to where Maki stood. It stopped in front of her and looked down at her from its great height. She looked back up at it without moving at all.

It bowed.

Not to Mau. To her. A short, precise, formal bow from the waist, the way a knight bows to someone who has fought well.

"The spear," it said. "Was yours."

"It was," said Maki.

"It held." The cold blue eyes went to the long crack in the shaft. Then to the silver flower. "It should not have. It held." It straightened. "That is a good weapon. With a good thing on it."

Maki looked at the knight.

"My sister made the flower," she said.

The knight inclined its helm.

"Then your sister," it said, "also makes good things."

It turned and walked back to Mau and stood beside him, a pace behind and to the left, exactly where a knight stands beside the person he serves. It did not say anything else.

Maki stood very still with her cracked spear for a long moment. Then she looked at the silver flower on it. Mau saw something cross her face that he had never seen on Maki's face before. He could not have said what it was.

* * *

*Floor 20 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10 or 20.*

*Level 22. Seventy-five points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 3. Places: 10.*

Mau read it out. All of it. Then he sat down on the bottom step of the throne, very suddenly, because his legs had decided for him. Maki had been right about doing it sitting down.

The knight stood beside him.

"Back," said Mau. "For now."

"Yes, my liege."

It went down into his shadow. All at once, smoothly, the dark cloak and the dark armour and the long sword, the two cold blue lights last. And the drain stopped. What he had spent stayed spent. The cup was much emptier than it had been at the start of the day. But there was something left in the bottom of it. A margin. A little, for the next thing.

Gojo crouched down in front of him.

"How do you feel?"

"Like I carried a house."

"You sort of did." Gojo looked at the scarf on his arm. Then at Mau's face. "You told it something. When you raised it. I couldn't hear what. I can't hear what anybody thinks." He tapped his own temple. "But I saw what it did to the way you were holding it. You didn't pull it up by force. You told it where it was going."

"Ciel said—"

"I know what Ciel said. I'm saying what I saw." Gojo was quiet for a moment. "That's a very good way to raise something."

Yuta sat down on the step beside Mau. He did not say anything. He took Mau's left wrist, very gently, below the scarf, and held it for a moment. Mau felt the warmth of Yuta's cursed energy go into the cut, not healing it, Yuta did not do that without being asked, just warming it, the way he warmed the air on the roof on cold nights.

"Shoko's going to be furious," said Yuta.

"I know."

"And then she's going to clean it."

"I know."

"And then she's going to tell Frieren."

Mau closed his eyes.

"I'll tell Frieren," he said. "First. Before Shoko does." He opened them. "That's the rule. I tell her first."

Yuta nodded.

Beyond the empty throne, at the back of the hall, where the grey light came down in long bars through the broken roof, a seal had appeared in the flagstones. Round, dark, patterned. And beside it, a small frame, with home faintly visible through it.

Maki held out her hand.

Mau took it, and she pulled him to his feet.

"Roof," she said. "Tonight. You promised Frieren."

"I promised."

"Then we'd better go home," said Maki, "so you can get yelled at in time."
