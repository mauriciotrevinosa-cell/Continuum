# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 25 — Corner

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 25
**Continuity state:** Return from floor 20. Mau tells Frieren first, before Shoko, as is his rule. Shoko stitches the arm, closes the deep thigh cut partly with her technique and stitches the rest, and binds two cracked ribs; no shadow use for the rest of the day. Ori adds Igris to her list and names the sentry "Corner" (it guarded a corner post on floor 1) without seeing it. Then the seventh-night roof with five (Rimuru, Yuta, Gojo, Taihei, Mau); Gojo tells the duel with exaggeration and then without it. No new power; no threat

*When: Day 347, afternoon into night. Four hours and twenty minutes, by Anko's watch. The seventh night. Frieren is at the screen in front of Anko's board, and has been for some time.*

Frieren was at the screen when they came through.

She was standing in front of Anko's board with her arms folded and her staff leaning against her shoulder. She had been there long enough that Anko had brought her a stool, and she had not sat on it. When the frame lit and the four of them came through, Mau first, she looked at him.

At his face. Then, at once, at the scarf round his right thigh. Gojo's scarf, dark with blood. At the strip of Yuta's sleeve round his arm. At the way he was walking, held up between Maki and Yuta, with one hand pressed to his left side.

She did not move.

Mau walked straight to her. He did not stop at the board. He did not wait for Anko to write anything. He walked across the dry ground to where Frieren stood with her arms folded, and stopped in front of her, close.

"I'm telling you first," he said. "Before Shoko. Before anyone."

Frieren looked at the scarf.

"Tell me," she said.

* * *

He told her.

All of it, standing there on the dry ground by the screen, with Anko pretending to write on the board and Gojo and Yuta and Maki standing a little way off, very carefully not listening. The long walk. The door. The empty throne. The knight kneeling. The name. The salute. The single combat, and the terms, and Maki's spear. The space catching the blade, and the blade finding where the space wasn't. The cut. Gojo shouting. Watching the weight instead of the sword. The door he left open on purpose at his right hip, and the cut he took through it to step inside. The pommel, and his ribs. The spear under the arm, twice. The knight kneeling. *Arise.* The cold blue eyes. *My liege.*

Frieren listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she did not say anything for a long moment.

Then she unfolded her arms. She reached out and took his left wrist, very carefully. She did not touch anything else. She held his wrist and looked at the scarf round his thigh, and at the hand pressed to his side, and at his face.

"You let it come in close," she said.

"I had to. The sword was too long. I had to be inside it."

"You let it come in close," said Frieren again, "with a sword, on purpose, while Gojo stood at a door." Her voice was very level. Very quiet. "And it cut you. Because you let it. On purpose."

"Once on purpose. The ribs weren't on purpose."

"*Once on purpose*," said Frieren.

She did not let go of his wrist.

"I'm angry," she said.

"I know."

"Not at you. Not *only* at you." She looked at the scarf. "At the tower. For making a thing that asks for you alone. At the notice. At the knight, for being good enough to find the place you weren't looking." She paused. "At myself, for not being at the door."

"You'd have hated the door."

"I'd have hated the door," Frieren agreed. "Gojo hated the door. I can see it on him. He'll be insufferable about it for a week." She was quiet for a moment. "I'd still rather have been at it."

Mau looked at her.

"Next time," he said. "If there's a next one like that. You can stand at the door."

"I'll stand at the door," said Frieren. "And I won't shout. Gojo shouted. I can tell. He's got the face of someone who shouted and it didn't help." She let go of his wrist. "Now go to Shoko. Before she comes out here and finds out I knew first."

"That's the rule."

"I know it's the rule," said Frieren. "I'm the one who made you keep it."

* * *

Shoko was not furious.

That was worse. She took Yuta's sleeve off his arm and looked at the cut there, a long shallow line, and cleaned it, and closed it with three small stitches without saying anything. Shallow cuts got thread, in her clinic. Then she took Gojo's scarf off his thigh and looked at that cut for a long time. That one was not shallow. She put her hands on it the other way, the way that closed things, and held them there until the deepest part had knitted. Then she stitched the rest, very neat, without saying anything. Then she felt his ribs, one at a time, very gently, while he tried not to make a sound and failed. She bound them, tight, white, from under his arm to his hip.

"Two," she said. "Cracked. Not broken through. You'll breathe like an old man for a week." She did not put her hands on those. "Ribs knit on their own. I'm keeping my hands for the next time you let something cut you on purpose."

Then she sat back on her stool and looked at him.

"How much of the cup did you use?"

"Most of it. For the extraction." He thought about it. "And most of the other one. For the space. In the fight."

"Both."

"Both."

Shoko wrote it down.

"Nothing," she said. "For the rest of today. No shadows out. No space. No pull. Nothing. If Ori wants to meet the knight, Ori meets the knight tomorrow." She looked at him. "Is that clear?"

"Clear."

"And you're going up on the roof tonight."

"It's the seventh night."

"I know what night it is." Shoko put down her pen. "Go up. Sit. Drink whatever Yuta calls tea. Don't lift anything heavier than the cup." She paused. "And don't let Satoru tell the story without you there to correct it."

From the clinic door, where Gojo had been leaning with his arms folded the whole time, came a wounded sound.

* * *

Ori was waiting at the end of the long table.

She had the list. The page from Umaru's wall, *SHADOWS (NAMED)*, unpinned and brought down and laid flat on the table in front of her with a pencil beside it. She had heard. Everyone had heard. The house always heard.

"There's a new one," she said, when Mau sat down.

"There's a new one."

"Does it have a name?"

"It had one already. On the notice." He looked at the page. "Igris."

Ori picked up the pencil. She wrote, very carefully, under *2. Lantern. (Ori.) A light.*:

*4. Igris. (The notice.)*

Then she stopped, with the pencil over the page.

"What is he?" she said. "Ear's a door. Lantern's a light. What's Igris?"

Mau thought about it. About the knight kneeling before the empty throne. The salute. The sword laid across both palms. Standing a pace behind and to the left. *My liege.* Bowing to Maki for the spear.

"A knight," he said.

Ori wrote it.

*4. Igris. (The notice.) A knight.*

Then she looked at the line below, in Umaru's hand. *The sentry. (Unnamed. Pending.)*

"Shoko says you can't take them out today," said Ori.

"She does."

"So I can't see the sentry."

"Not today."

Ori looked at the line for a long time.

"Where did you find it?" she said. "The sentry. On the first floor."

"At a post," said Mau. "On a street corner. It was guarding the corner, with a long hooked weapon. When we cleared the post, it stayed."

Ori thought about this. She thought about it very seriously. Mau could see her turning it over, the way she turned everything over.

"Corner," she said.

Mau looked at her.

"It guarded a corner," said Ori. "So it's Corner." She paused. "And Igris is a knight, and knights stand at doors, and Corner stands at a corner. So they go together." She looked up at him. "Is that all right? Even though I haven't seen it?"

"You named Lantern after seeing it."

"I know." Ori looked at the page. "But Corner already did the thing. Guarding the corner. I don't need to see it to know what it did." She paused. "And it's been waiting. In your shadow. With no name. Since the first floor." She looked at him. "That's a long time to wait with no name."

Mau did not say anything for a moment.

"It's all right," he said. "Corner."

Ori crossed out Umaru's line, very neatly, one straight stroke. She wrote underneath:

*5. Corner. (Ori.) Guards a corner.*

Then she looked at the list. Four names. Four lines.

"It's getting long," she said, satisfied.

* * *

The roof hatch squeaked five times.

Rimuru first, complaining about the hatch. Then Yuta with the cups, five of them, on a tray. Then Taihei, carefully, getting better at the ladder. Then Mau, slowly, one rung at a time, with his ribs bound and his leg stitched, Taihei reaching down from above and Rimuru below, because Shoko had said nothing heavier than the cup and he had decided that included himself.

Then, twenty minutes later, Gojo, pretending he had always been going to.

He sat down in his usual spot. He picked up his cup. He looked round at the four of them.

"So," said Gojo. "Has anyone heard the story yet?"

"Everyone's heard the story," said Rimuru. "The whole house heard the story by dinner. Anko wrote it on the board."

"Anko's version," said Gojo, "is eleven words long."

"Anko's version is very good," said Rimuru. "*Floor 20. A knight. Single combat. Mau won. Cut (arm). Igris.*"

"That's ten words."

"She counts *Floor 20* as one."

Gojo put down his cup.

"Then you need the real version," he said. "With the parts Anko left out. Like the part where I was magnificent at the door."

Yuta, beside him, put his face in his hands.

* * *

Gojo told it.

Badly, at first. On purpose. The knight was nine feet tall. The hall was the size of a city. The sword was longer than the house. Mau had been thrown across the room twice and got up both times, bleeding from every limb, saying something heroic. Gojo himself had held back an entire army of invisible enemies at the door with one hand while shouting essential tactical advice with the other.

Taihei, by the draught, listened to all of it with his mouth slightly open.

"Is that true?" he said to Mau.

"None of it," said Mau.

"The sword was very long," said Yuta, through his hands.

"The sword was very long," Mau agreed. "The rest of it isn't true."

Gojo picked up his cup again.

He looked into it for a long time. The wind came off the hills, warm. Somewhere below, Ori's voice said something, and Milim's said *no*, and then a door closed.

"The real version," said Gojo eventually, in a different voice, "is that it found the place he wasn't looking. And I saw it find it. Before it went in. Half a second before." He turned the cup in his hands. "And I shouted. And it didn't matter. Because half a second is nothing, and I knew it was nothing, and I shouted anyway." He was quiet. "I've never stood at a door before. In my whole life. Somebody else always stood at the door. And I went in."

Nobody said anything.

"And then he stopped watching the sword," said Gojo. "Because Yuta showed him how. And he went in close, because I showed him the stones. And he used Maki's spear, because Maki gave it to him. And he raised the thing by telling it where it was going." He looked at Mau. "None of that was me at the door. All of it was before. All of it was things somebody gave him, weeks ago, that he kept."

He drank his tea.

"That's the real version," he said. "Anko's is better."

* * *

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Then Taihei, by the draught, said quietly, "I didn't give him anything."

Everyone looked at him.

"I mean—" Taihei went red. "Everyone in that story gave him something. The stones. The weight. The spear. And I was in the kitchen making bread." He looked at his hands. "I'm just saying. I don't think I'd be any use. In a story like that."

Mau looked at him.

"You gave me bread," he said. "This morning. Before I left. At five o'clock, because Momo wasn't up yet and you were. You said *eat this, it's a long walk.*" He paused. "I ate it on the stairs on twelve. It was the only thing I ate all day."

Taihei stared at him.

"That's not the same."

"It's exactly the same," said Gojo. "You're in the story now. Anko will want to add it." He raised his cup. "*Floor 20. A knight. Single combat. Bread (Taihei). Mau won.*"

"That's eleven," said Rimuru.

"*Bread (Taihei)* counts as one," said Gojo. "Ministry rules."

Taihei put his face in his hands, exactly the way Yuta had. And for a while the five of them sat on the roof in the warm dark without saying anything else. The hatch squeaked when the wind caught it, and the stars came out over the hills. Low over the hills, a little apart, was the small steady one Mau had named. Still here.
