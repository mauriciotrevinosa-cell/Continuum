# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 26 — The Front Row

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 26

*When: Day 348, midday. The day after floor 20. Mau's left arm is in its white bandage. Momo has made the cold noodle soup she makes on the hottest days. Everyone is at the long table, because it is too hot to be anywhere else.*

Seiko waited until they were both eating.

She had said she would. *At the long table. In front of everybody. When you're both eating something. That's the only way to say it properly. Ask any grandmother.* Frieren had told Mau that, the night of the crackers table, lying in the dark. *She said it was dangerous.* And Mau had said *Seiko says everything is dangerous*, and Frieren had said *she meant it this time*, and they had both forgotten about it by morning.

Seiko had not forgotten.

She sat in the high seat with Turbo Granny in her lucky cat on the table beside her, and a bowl of Momo's cold noodles in front of her that she had barely touched. She watched. She watched the whole first half of lunch. She watched the long table, all of it, the noise and the elbows and Milim trying to drink the soup straight from the bowl and Ori stopping her. She watched Mau at his place with his bandaged arm, eating one-handed. She watched Frieren beside him, close, her shoulder against his good arm. She watched Frieren lean over without a word and pick up the noodles he could not manage with one hand and put them in his bowl for him, and Mau not even notice she had done it, because she did it every day now, and it had stopped being something to notice.

Seiko put down her spoon.

"You know," she said, to the whole table, very comfortably, "when the two of you marry, I want to be in the front row."

* * *

Mau choked.

He had a mouthful of cold soup and it went the wrong way, all at once. He coughed so hard he nearly went off the bench. Yuta, across from him, half stood up. Rimuru, beside Yuta, put his cup down very fast. Frieren hit him on the back, twice, hard, without looking at him, because she was not looking at anything.

Frieren had gone red.

Not pink. Not the faint colour at the tips of her ears that the house had learned to watch for. Red. All of her face, from her collar to the roots of her hair. She sat very straight on the bench with her hand still on Mau's back, staring at the middle of the long table. She had the expression of someone who had been hit very gently with something very large.

The long table went silent for exactly one second.

Then it exploded.

Hakari shrieked. Umaru stood up on her bench, because there was clearly going to be a proclamation and she was not going to be seated for it. Milim said "*Marry?*" at a volume that carried to the river, and then, "What's marry?" and Ori said, "It's like the dress Marin made but for two people," which was not right and was somehow not wrong. Momo, at the kitchen door, put her hand over her mouth. Marin grabbed Wakana's arm with both hands. Gojo laughed out loud, a real laugh, startled out of him, and Shoko beside him said "*Seiko*," in the voice of a doctor reproaching a patient, and was smiling.

Seiko sat in the high seat and drank her tea and looked enormously pleased with herself.

"I said it was dangerous," she said, to Frieren.

Frieren did not answer. Frieren could not answer. Frieren was still red, still staring at the middle of the table, with her hand still on Mau's back.

Mau stopped coughing.

He sat up. His eyes were watering. He was laughing too, a little, the way you laugh after you choke, helplessly, because it was funny. Seiko in the high seat, and Frieren red to the roots of her hair, and Milim asking what *marry* was. He looked at Frieren. She looked back at him, finally, sideways, out of her red face, with an expression of pure accusation, as if this were somehow his fault.

He laughed again.

And then the word reached him.

* * *

*Marry.*

It did not come back as a word. It came back as frost.

Frost on the grass, white, in the first clearing. The one where he woke. The one where everything started. And flowers, everywhere. White and blue. Thousands of them, in the frost. And he had made them. Every single one. With his own mana, on the elf route, after years. After all the years of failing.

He had made every one.

The long table was still loud around him. He could hear it. Hakari, and Umaru beginning a proclamation, and Gojo still laughing. But it had gone thin. Far away. As if it were happening in another room, through a wall.

Everyone coming up through the trees. In twos and threes and crowds. Fern complaining about a dress. Milim on Rimuru's shoulders. Bocchi with her guitar.

Everyone came. Because he asked.

*Look at me,* he thought. *Look at the table. Look at the soup. Look at Frieren.*

He looked at Frieren.

She was still red. Still sideways. Still accusing.

And behind her, through her, over her, there was frost. And white flowers, and blue. And a seam in the sky.

* * *

His chest closed.

That was what it felt like. Not pain. A door shutting, very fast, inside his ribs. His breath went in and did not come out. He tried again. It went in and stopped halfway, as if there were a wall in his chest, a finger's width from his heart.

He knew what this was.

He had had it before. Not often. Not for a long time now. He had thought—

He had thought it was better. It *was* better. That was the thing. It had been better for months. He had walked through a door into the Goddess's place and come back. He had walked into a red gate and come back. He had fought a red knight yesterday with a cracked spear and won. He had not thought about the wedding in weeks.

*The seam in the sky.*

His hands had gone cold. Both of them. The good one and the bandaged one. Pins in his fingers. He looked down at them on the table and they did not look like his.

*The slashes. Cutting the air and the trees and the flowers.*

His heart was going much too fast. He could hear it. He could hear it over the long table, over everything, the way he had heard it inside Infinity at the end, *so quiet I could hear my own heart*.

*Every step I took, the space stretched to fit it.*

*Her hand was here.*

He looked at Frieren's hand.

It was on his back. He could see her arm going behind him. He could not feel it.

He could not feel it.

* * *

"Mau."

Frieren's voice. Not red any more. Not accusing. Very level. Very close.

She had seen. Of course she had seen. She had felt his breath stop under her hand. She had felt his back go rigid. She had looked at his face, sideways, and every bit of colour had gone out of her own.

"Mau," she said again. "Look at me."

He tried.

He could see her. Her face. Her eyes, very close. But there was frost behind her, and flowers, and he could not tell which one was in front.

"I can't—" he said. Or tried to. It came out as breath.

"I know," said Frieren. "You don't have to talk." She took her hand off his back.

He felt it go. That, he felt. The going.

She took his cold hand. The good one. In both of hers. She held it hard, against the table, her fingers round his, so tight it almost hurt.

"Here," said Frieren. "I'm here. Not a finger's width. *Here.*"

He felt that.

Her hands. Cold, because Frieren's hands were always cold, even in summer, even in the hottest part of the day. Cold and hard and there. Not stopped. Not hanging in the air in front of his chest. Round his fingers. On his skin.

He breathed in.

It went in. Halfway. Stopped.

"Again," said Frieren.

He breathed in again.

* * *

The long table had gone quiet.

Not all at once. In a wave, from their end of it outward, as people saw. Yuta first, across from him, already standing. Rimuru beside Yuta, very still. Gojo had stopped laughing in the middle of a breath. Shoko's hand was on Gojo's arm, and her face had gone from smiling to working in less than a second, the way a doctor's does. Umaru was still standing on her bench, with her proclamation stopped halfway out of her mouth. Seiko in the high seat had put down her tea.

And at the far end of the long table, Himmel.

He had not been laughing. Mau saw that, even through the frost. Even with his breath stopping halfway. Himmel had not been laughing with the rest of them when Seiko said it. He had been sitting very still at the end of the long table with his spoon in his hand and his face doing nothing at all. And now he was looking at Mau. At Frieren holding Mau's hand in both of hers. And his face was doing something, and it was not nothing any more.

Mau saw it.

He saw it the way he saw everything, with the eyes behind their filter. Too clearly. All of it at once. The spoon in Himmel's hand, not moving. The tiny pull at the corner of his mouth. Something going out of his face, the way the colour went out of the high lanterns when the candles burned down.

*He heard*, thought Mau. *He heard Seiko. He heard* marry. *And it went into him too.*

His breath stopped again.

"*Mau*," said Frieren. "Look at me. Not at the table. At *me*."

He looked at her.

"Outside," she said. "Can you stand? We're going outside. To the step. Away from all of this. Can you stand?"

He did not know.

He stood.

* * *

She took him out through the kitchen.

Past Momo at the door, who stepped aside without a word and put her hand flat on his good shoulder as he went by. Past Taihei at the left end of the counter, who stopped stirring. Out through the back door onto the step, into the hot white midday light, where the yard was empty and quiet and nobody was looking at him.

He sat down on the step. His legs decided.

Frieren sat down beside him. She did not let go of his hand.

"Breathe," she said. "In. Count with me. One."

He breathed in.

"Two."

Out.

"Three."

The frost was still there. The flowers. The seam. But they were thinner now. Further away. On the other side of the yard, where the sun was too bright to look at. And her hands round his were cold and hard and real, and the step under him was warm from the sun, and somewhere behind him in the kitchen Momo was saying, very quietly, *give them a minute*.

"Four," said Frieren.

He breathed.

Behind him, the kitchen door opened.

Not all the way. A crack. He did not turn round. He did not need to. He could hear who it was, the way he could always hear who it was. Gojo's long stride, stopping. Rimuru, smaller, quieter. Yuta last, very careful, the way he walked when he did not want anyone to hear him come.

They did not come out onto the step.

They stood in the kitchen doorway, the three of them, and waited.
