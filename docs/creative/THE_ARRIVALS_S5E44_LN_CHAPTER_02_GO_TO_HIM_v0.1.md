# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 27 — Go to Him

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 27
**Continuity state:** R55. On the back step, still shaking, Mau decides two things: Frieren should go to Himmel, who heard and was hurt, and he wants to be somewhere that is not the house for a while. Frieren sees Gojo, Rimuru and Yuta in the doorway; they offer and Mau accepts, so nobody follows him in secret. Rimuru arranges cover before both he and Mau leave (Diablo and Fern hold the house, and Anko writes the board). Shoko checks him: not an injury, two hours, the cleared garden on five only, no floor. Frieren knows exactly how he is before she goes

*When: Day 348, a few minutes later. The back step. The yard is white with midday sun. Frieren is still holding his hand.*

His breath came back slowly.

Not all at once. In pieces. A little further in each time, and a little further out, while Frieren counted. Her hands stayed hard round his fingers. The frost on the other side of the yard thinned, and thinned, and became sun on dry ground, and the white and blue flowers became the dust where nothing grew in the heat, and the seam in the sky became the sky.

Just the sky. Hot and empty and blue.

"Twelve," said Frieren.

He breathed out.

"I'm all right," he said.

"You're not."

"I'm—" He stopped. He tried again, honestly. "I'm better than a minute ago."

Frieren looked at him.

"That's true," she said. "Say that one."

"I'm better than a minute ago."

She nodded. She did not let go of his hand.

* * *

He sat on the step and looked at the yard.

His hands were coming back. The pins were going out of his fingers. His heart was still too fast, but it was slowing, the way it slowed at the reset hour after her foot touched his shin. The step was warm. The sun was very bright.

He thought about Himmel's face.

The spoon not moving. The colour going out of him, the way it went out of the high lanterns when the candles burned down. Himmel had not laughed when Seiko said it. He had been sitting at the end of the long table, very still. And when he looked at Mau, falling apart, and Frieren holding his hand in both of hers, something in his face had gone.

*He heard,* Mau thought again. *It went into him too.*

"Frieren," he said.

"Mm."

"Himmel heard."

She did not say anything.

"I saw his face," said Mau. "When Seiko said it. He didn't laugh. And then when I—" He stopped. "He looked at us. At you holding my hand. And it went into him." He looked at her. "You saw it too."

Frieren was very still beside him.

"I saw it," she said at last.

"Go to him."

She turned her head and looked at him.

"I'm not leaving you on a step," said Frieren.

"You're not."

"You just told me you were better than a minute ago. That's not *better*. That's a minute." Her hands tightened. "I'm not—"

"You're not leaving me on a step," said Mau. "Look."

He turned his head. Not much. Just enough. Toward the kitchen door behind them, open a crack.

Frieren turned too.

* * *

They were in the doorway.

All three of them. They had been there the whole time, since the door first opened, while Frieren counted. Gojo in front, filling most of the gap, with his blindfold pushed up and his bare eyes on the back of Mau's head. Rimuru beside him, half a head shorter, very still, in the way Rimuru was still when he was listening to Ciel and to everything else at once. Yuta behind them both, where he could see over Rimuru's shoulder, with his hands at his sides and his face pale.

They had not come out.

They had stood in the doorway and waited, the way you wait at the edge of something that is not yours to walk into until someone says you can.

Frieren looked at them for a long moment.

Gojo looked back at her. He did not say anything clever. He did not say anything at all. He looked at her, and then at Mau, and then back at her, and lifted one hand off the door frame, very slightly. The way you lift a hand to ask a question in a room where you do not want to interrupt.

*Can we?*

Frieren looked at Mau.

"I want to go somewhere," said Mau. His voice was still not quite his. "For a while. Somewhere that isn't here. Not because of anyone. Not because of Seiko. Because everywhere I look in the yard there's—" He stopped. "There's still frost. A bit. On the edges."

Frieren's hands tightened again.

"Not to fight," said Mau. "Not a floor. Somewhere quiet. Where it's different." He thought about it. "The garden. On five. The terrace with the tree. It's cleared. Nothing's there any more. It's just a garden in the mist." He looked at the doorway. "I'd like to go there. For an hour. With them. If they want."

"We want," said Gojo.

He said it before anyone else could. Two words. No joke in them at all.

* * *

Frieren looked at the three of them in the doorway.

Then at Mau.

She did not let go of his hand yet. She looked at him for a long time. Mau understood that she was not deciding whether to let him go. She was looking at him the way she looked at a spell before she cast it. All of it. Every part. So she would know exactly what she was leaving and exactly what she was going to.

"Tell me how you are," she said. "Honestly. Before I go."

"I'm not fine," said Mau. "My hands are still cold. My heart's still too fast. There's frost on the edges of the yard and I know it isn't there. I'm better than a minute ago." He paused. "And I'm going with three people who love me, to a garden where nothing is. And you're going to Himmel, who heard, and it hurt him. And you'll come back and I'll come back and we'll tell each other." He looked at her. "That's how I am."

Frieren looked at him.

"That's a very good answer," she said.

"I've been practising."

"I know." Her hands loosened. Slowly. Not all at once. She let go one finger at a time, so that he could feel each one going, and not lose all of it at once. "Tell me when you're back. First."

"First."

"Before Shoko."

"Before anyone."

She let go.

* * *

It took ten minutes to leave, because it had to.

Rimuru did it. He came out onto the step and crouched down in front of Mau and looked at him, close, for a moment. Then he stood up and turned round and was suddenly someone else. The one who had built a nation once, and knew exactly who needed to be told what, and in what order.

"Diablo," he said, not loudly.

Diablo was already there. He had been on the front step, where he liked to stand in the mornings. He was at the corner of the house now, as if he had been there all along.

"Rimuru-sama."

"Mau and I are both going out. To the tower. The garden on five. Not a floor. Two hours." Rimuru looked at him. "That means both of us out at once. You know what that means."

"I hold the house," said Diablo.

"You and Fern. Tell her. She'll want to know before she has to work it out." Rimuru paused. "And Anko. Board first. Then her round."

"At once."

Diablo went. Not fast. He never went fast. He was gone, though.

Then Shoko came out.

She did not crouch. She sat down on the step on Mau's other side, where Frieren had been, and took his wrist without asking. She held it for a long moment with her cool fingers on his pulse, looking at nothing.

"Too fast," she said. "Coming down." She let go. "Panic isn't a wound. I can't stitch it. I can't put my hands on it the other way and close it." She looked at him. "What you need for this one isn't in my clinic."

"I know."

"Two hours," said Shoko. "The garden only. No floor. No shadows out. No space. You sit under a tree and you breathe and you let three idiots keep you company." She looked at the doorway. "And you come back."

"I come back."

"Satoru."

"Shoko."

"No heroics. Not today."

"Not today," said Gojo. He came out onto the step at last. He held out his hand.

Mau looked at it.

Then he took it, and Gojo pulled him to his feet, gently, the way Maki had pulled him up off the throne steps the day before.

* * *

Anko's board, when they got to the screen, said it plainly:

*Day 348. Mau, Gojo, Rimuru, Yuta. From 5. The garden. Not a floor. Two hours.*

And under it, smaller, in Fern's hand, added while they were walking out:

*House: Diablo, Fern. All well.*

Mau looked at the board for a long moment.

Then he looked back across the dry ground toward the house. Toward the front gate, between the vermilion columns, where Himmel stood at dawn every day facing the road.

Frieren was walking toward it.

Not fast. Not slow. With her staff in her hand and her shoulders very straight. She did not look back. She did not need to. She had looked at him for a long time on the step, every part, and she knew exactly what she had left. She knew it was in good hands.

"Ready?" said Yuta, beside him.

Mau watched her reach the gate.

"Ready," he said.

They went through the frame.
