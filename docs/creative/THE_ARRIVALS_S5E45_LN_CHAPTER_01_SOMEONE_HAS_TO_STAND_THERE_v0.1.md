# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 31 — Someone Has to Stand There

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 31
**Continuity state:** R52. Himmel goes to the north end at Mikasa's afternoon hour and offers to stand it. Mikasa refuses, then accepts a shared arrangement (Himmel takes alternate afternoons) so that she and Eren can have time; Eren's "tomorrows" become ordinary afternoons. Himmel faces the field and the lime, not the road, and finds that he likes it. It is a chosen distance and his own thing, not a new post imposed by anyone and not a departure

*When: Day 349, mid-afternoon. Hot. The north end of the settlement, past the timber yard, where the lime line runs white across the field toward the red stake. Mikasa has been standing there for a quarter of an hour. Himmel is walking toward her.*

Mikasa heard him coming from a long way off.

Everyone did. Himmel did not know how to walk quietly. He walked the way he did everything, as if there might be an audience, with his cape moving and his boots finding every loose stone. She heard him from the timber yard. She did not turn round.

She stood where she always stood. At the north end, a little back from the lime, where you could see the field and the line and the red stake and the low hills beyond. She had her blades at her hip, the new ones and the one old one. Her hands were at her sides. Her eyes were on the hills.

He stopped beside her.

Not in front. Beside. A pace away.

She did not look at him.

"You're in my light," she said.

Himmel looked down. The sun was behind them. His shadow fell forward, across the dry grass, toward the lime. It was nowhere near her.

"I'm not," he said.

"You're in it anyway."

He almost laughed. He did not. He stood beside her and looked where she was looking. The field, the lime, the red stake, the hills. He stood there for a long time without saying anything. That surprised her, a little. Himmel was not good at standing without saying anything.

* * *

"At the festival," said Himmel eventually. "You said something."

Mikasa did not answer.

"About this," said Himmel. "Your hour. Every day." He looked at the hills. "You said on a night like that you'd want to sleep late, and not get up, and sit somewhere with someone and not stand anywhere at all." He paused. "And then you said you wouldn't skip it. Because someone has to stand there."

"I remember what I said."

"I've been thinking about it," said Himmel. "For twelve days." He looked at his boots. "I'm not very good at thinking about things for twelve days. Usually I think about them for about a minute and then do something heroic and regret it." He paused. "This one I thought about."

Mikasa turned her head and looked at him for the first time.

"What," she said.

"I'd like to stand here," said Himmel.

* * *

She did not say anything for a long moment.

Then she said, "No."

"I thought you'd say that."

"It's mine," said Mikasa. Her voice was very flat. "It's been mine for a long time. Before the titan. Before the wall." She looked back at the hills. "Every day. One hour. I stand here and I look at the hills and if something comes, I see it first." Her hand moved, very slightly, toward the blades at her hip, and stopped. "I don't give it to people."

"I'm not asking you to give it to me."

"Then what are you asking?"

Himmel thought about it.

"Half," he said.

Mikasa looked at him.

"Not all of it," said Himmel. "Half. Every other day. You stand here on your days and I stand here on mine." He looked at the hills. "And on my days, you sleep late. Or you don't sleep late. You sit in the south field where the green came back. With Eren. And you don't stand anywhere at all." He paused. "That's all. That's the whole offer."

Mikasa did not say anything.

"I'm good at standing," said Himmel. "I've been standing at the gate every morning for longer than I can remember. Facing the road." He looked at his boots again. "I've decided I'd like to stop facing the road. And I thought—if I'm going to stand somewhere, it might as well be somewhere that means something to someone. Instead of somewhere that only means something to me."

* * *

Mikasa looked at him for a long time.

Then she looked at the hills again.

"Why," she said.

"I told you why."

"You told me what," said Mikasa. "Not why." She did not look away from the hills. "You've stood at that gate every morning since you came. Facing the road. Everyone knows. Nobody asks. And now you want to stop." She paused. "Why now."

Himmel was quiet for a long moment.

He thought about saying something heroic. He had a great many heroic things he could say. He had spent ten years on a road saying them. They were all very good and he meant most of them.

He did not say any of them.

"Because I was waiting for something on that road," he said. "And it came. A long time ago. And it went past me, to someone else." He looked at the hills. "And I didn't notice I was still waiting for it until a few days ago, at lunch, when someone said a word." He paused. "And I thought, I've been standing at a gate facing an empty road every morning, waiting for a thing that's already arrived somewhere else. That's not a hero. That's a man who forgot to go inside."

Mikasa did not say anything.

"So I'd like to stand somewhere else," said Himmel. "Facing something. Not waiting for someone to come. Watching so they can be somewhere else." He looked at her. "You know about that. You've done it every day for years."

* * *

Mikasa was quiet for a very long time.

The wind came across the field from the hills, warm, and moved the long dry grass by the lime, and moved Himmel's cape, and moved Mikasa's dark hair.

"Eren," she said at last.

Himmel waited.

"He walks the line," said Mikasa. "Still. Some days. The whole thing. Not twice any more. Once." She looked at the hills. "And the days he doesn't, he comes here. At the end of my hour. And waits. At the timber yard. Where I can't see him. He thinks I can't see him." She paused. "I can always see him."

Himmel did not say anything.

"The day in the south field," said Mikasa. "When I didn't stand here. We sat in the grass. The pale grass with the yellow flowers, where the dead earth came back." Her voice was very flat. "He talked about the sea. That he wanted to see it again. With me. Someday." She stopped. "He's never said *someday* before. In his whole life. He used to say *when it's over*."

"Kaneki told him to stop saying that."

Mikasa turned her head and looked at him sharply.

"How do you know that?"

"I don't," said Himmel. "I guessed. Kaneki's the only person Eren talks to at night. And Eren's started saying *someday*. Someone must have told him." He shrugged. "I'm very perceptive. It's part of being a hero."

Mikasa looked at him for a long time.

Then something happened to her face that Himmel had never seen it do. It was not a smile. Mikasa did not smile very often. It was something at the edges, near her eyes. It came and went very fast.

"Every other day," she said.

Himmel looked at her.

"Every other day," said Mikasa again. "Not half. Every other day. You stand on yours. I stand on mine. If you miss one, I'll know." She looked back at the hills. "And if something comes on your day, you don't wait to see what it is. You don't pose. You don't make a speech. You run to the timber yard and you shout. Loud. And then you come back and stand in front of it." She paused. "Can you do that?"

"I'm very good at shouting," said Himmel.

"That's not what I asked."

Himmel was quiet for a moment.

"Yes," he said. "I can do that. I can stand in front of something without making a speech." He paused. "Probably. I'll practise."

* * *

Mikasa did not say anything else for the rest of her hour.

Neither did Himmel. He stood beside her, a pace away, and looked at the field and the lime and the red stake and the hills. The sun moved. The wind came and went. Nothing came over the hills. Nothing ever did, most days. That was not the point.

At the end of the hour, Mikasa turned round.

She looked toward the timber yard. Mau would have said she did not need to look. She always knew where Eren was. But she looked anyway.

He was there. At the edge of the timber yard, half hidden behind a stack of Wakana's useless twisted oak, pretending to inspect it. He saw her look and straightened up very fast. He looked at the oak with tremendous concentration, as if he had been examining it closely for the last ten minutes.

"He thinks I can't see him," said Mikasa.

"I know," said Himmel. "I could see him from the gate."

Mikasa almost smiled again. The thing at the edges. Gone very fast.

"Tomorrow is yours," she said.

"Tomorrow is mine."

"Don't be late."

"A hero," said Himmel, "is never late. He arrives exactly when he means to."

Mikasa looked at him with an expression of tremendous patience. Then she walked away across the dry ground toward the timber yard. She did not walk fast. She did not walk slow. She walked toward the young man behind the stack of twisted oak, who was still pretending.

Himmel watched her go.

He watched her reach the timber yard. He watched Eren stop pretending to look at the oak and look at her instead. He watched her say something, and Eren's face change. He watched them stand there for a moment, close. Then they walked away together, not toward the house but toward the south field, where the green had come back.

Then Himmel turned round.

He stood at the north end, a little back from the lime, where Mikasa had stood. He looked at the field. At the long dry grass. At the white line running across it toward the red stake. At the low hills, with nothing coming over them.

It was not the road.

The road went away from the house, toward somewhere else, toward something he was waiting for. This did not go anywhere. It was a field and a line and some hills. He was not standing here waiting for anyone to come. He was standing here so that two people could walk away toward the south field and not look back.

He found, after a while, that he liked it.

He did not brush his hair. He did not need to. Nobody was watching.

He stood there until the sun went gold on the hills. Then he went in to dinner. At the long table, when Stark asked where he had been all afternoon, he said, "Practising," and would not say what.
