*When: Day 350, morning. Anko's board: 21, 22, 23, 24 under FLOOR, in four different parties' days. Under today: 25. START FROM: 20, underlined twice, because it is the first time.*

Anko's board had grown a second column.

She had run out of room on the first one somewhere around floor 18 and nailed up another plank beside it. On the new plank, in her plain hand:

*Day 349, evening. Mau. From 20. Floor 21: a frozen waterfall, climbed. Floor 22: wind. A great deal of wind. Cleared, both.*

*(Alone.) (The day after the front row.) (Fingers frostbitten, two. Shoko, at midnight, because Frieren took him.)*

*Day 350, before dawn. Mau. From 20. Floor 23: a field of standing stones that hummed. Floor 24: a kitchen. A very large kitchen. Cleared, both.*

*(NOT alone.) (Kaneki, Milim, Rem were at the screen before him. They had been there since three. Kaneki heard which stones. Rem did not want to leave the kitchen. Rem removed by Milim.)*

The night before, Anko had seen the shape of it. She had stood in her doorway at midnight while Frieren walked Mau across the yard to the clinic with his frostbitten hand held in both of hers, and she had understood that there was not going to be a rule. There could not be a rule. You could not forbid him his own door.

So she had done the other thing. She had gone to the kilns, where Kaneki was raking, and told him what time Mau had been leaving. And Kaneki had told Milim. And Milim had told Rem, because Rem was awake making the bread. And at three in the morning, when Mau came out of the back door with the bag over his shoulder and his bandaged fingers, three people had been sitting on the bench by the dark screen in the cold, waiting for him.

"Good morning," Rem had said. "We're coming."

He had not argued. He had looked at the three of them for a long moment, and then sat down on the bench beside Kaneki, and put his face in his bandaged hands for a while, and then stood up, and they had gone in.

Underneath, smaller, in Rem's careful hand, added without permission:

*The kitchen had copper pans. Rem would like to go back.*

Mau stood in front of the board with Frieren, Fern and Stark, reading it.

"She really didn't want to leave," said Stark.

"There was a stove," said Mau, "with eight rings. And a bread oven you could stand up in." He thought about it. "Milim had to carry her."

"Milim carried *Rem*?"

"Under one arm. Rem kept saying *just one more minute*."

Fern looked at the board. Then wrote, under today:

*Day 350. Mau, Frieren, Fern, Stark. From 20.*

"We're not going to the kitchen," she said.

* * *

The twenty-fifth floor was a bridge.

They came up the stair from twenty-four, past the kitchen, which Mau did not look at because Fern was watching him. They stood at the top and looked at it.

A chasm. Wide and black and bottomless, going down into a darkness so deep it had colour, a dull red glow very far below, like the inside of a furnace seen through smoke. Across it, a bridge. Stone. Narrow, barely wide enough for two people side by side, with no railing. Long, very long. It went out over the red-black chasm in a single span and disappeared into heat-shimmer before it reached the other side.

Heat came up out of the chasm in waves. Mau felt it on his face.

And on the bridge, here and there, things were moving.

Small. Red. Quick. The size of a cat, or a child, with too many legs and a glow inside them like coals. They scuttled along the stone, and over the edge, and up again, and the bridge was dotted with them as far as the shimmer let you see.

The notice came up.

*Floor 25 of 100.*

*Objective: cross.*

"Cross," said Mau.

Stark looked at the narrow bridge. At the bottomless red chasm on either side of it. At the small glowing things scuttling over the stone.

"I don't like heights," he said.

"I know," said Fern.

"I *really* don't like heights with fire under them."

"I know," said Fern again, and put her hand flat on his back for a moment, and took it away.

* * *

They went out onto the bridge.

Frieren first, floating, a hand above the stone, because she did not need the stone and could see further from above it. Fern behind her, on foot, her light spells going out ahead in a neat row. Stark behind Fern, close, with the axe held across his body and his eyes fixed on the stone directly in front of his boots, because he had decided that was the only thing he was going to look at. Mau last.

The fire-things came at them in the first hundred paces.

They did not come one at a time. They came in a rush, a dozen of them, scuttling up out of the chasm onto the bridge in front of Frieren with their legs clicking on the stone and their coal-glow brightening. Frieren dealt with the first ones. Thin, precise lines from her staff, one each, and they went over the edge glowing and fell away into the red dark.

But there were more. Behind them. Coming up over the edges of the bridge on both sides, from below, faster than she could pick them off.

"They're coming from underneath," said Fern. Calmly. Setting another light. "From the chasm. All along. We can't clear the whole bridge."

"We don't need to clear it," said Mau. "We need to cross it."

Frieren looked back at him.

"The far end," she said. "Something needs to hold the far end. So we have somewhere to cross *to*. Otherwise they just keep coming up in front of us all the way."

Mau looked at the far end of the bridge.

He could not see it. It was lost in the heat-shimmer. But he knew it was there.

He looked down at his shadow on the stone.

* * *

"Igris," he said.

The knight came up.

The dark gathered in his shadow on the stone and stood up beside him, tall, armoured, cloaked, with the long sword already drawn, two cold blue lights behind its visor. It looked at him once.

"The far end," said Mau. "Hold it. So we have somewhere to cross to."

"My liege."

It went. Past Stark, past Fern, past Frieren floating above the stone. It went along the narrow bridge with long unhurried strides, and the fire-things that came up over the edges in its path were simply not there any more when it had passed. It went on into the heat-shimmer, and a little way into it, and stopped. Mau could just see it, tall and dark at the edge of the shimmer, where the far end must be.

Calling him had cost nothing. He had been paid for once, on the night he was raised, and he was Mau's now. The knight could stand at the far end of the bridge all day and it would not cost a drop.

*Calling him is free,* said Ciel. *Standing is free. But if they break him out there, bringing him back costs. A lot.* A pause. *Don't let them break him.*

*I won't.*

Far ahead, at the edge of the shimmer, the knight turned to face the fire-things coming up over the edge of the bridge.

"My liege," said Igris. The voice carried, dry and level, the whole length of the bridge. "The far end is held."

And it was.

* * *

Mau had seen Maki fight. He had seen Stark and Yuta and Mikasa and Frieren. He had seen Gojo, once, at the start of everything, when the whole field had been white.

He had never seen anything fight like Igris.

It did not look fast. That was the strange part. It looked unhurried. It stood at the far end of the bridge with its long sword, and the fire-things came up over the edges at it from both sides. It moved, and they were gone. One movement. A step, a turn, the long blade going round in a flat arc. Four of them went over the edge at once, glowing, falling. Another step. Another arc. It never moved more than two paces from the place where it had stood up. It never hurried. It looked like a man sweeping a floor.

The fire-things stopped coming up at the far end.

They still came up near the party. Frieren and Fern dealt with those, thin lines and neat lights, while Stark walked with his eyes on the stone and the axe across his chest. But the far end was quiet. There was a place to cross *to*.

They crossed.

It took a long time. The bridge was very long and very narrow and the heat came up on both sides. When they reached the middle, Stark said, quite calmly, without looking up from his boots, "I'm going to be sick." Fern said, "No, you're not." Stark said, "No, I'm not," and wasn't.

Mau watched the far end the whole way, the knight tall and dark in the shimmer, holding it. Four minutes. Five. Six. The cup did not move. It did not need to.

At seven, they reached the far end.

* * *

The knight stood aside to let them pass.

It stood on the last span of the bridge with its sword lowered, at the edge of solid ground. Beyond it was a wide stone platform, and a stair going up. The fire-things had stopped. Even the ones near the party. They had gone back down into the chasm, as if they had understood that the far end was not going to be theirs.

Fern stepped off the bridge onto the platform first.

She stopped beside the knight. She looked up at it. She had to look a long way up.

The knight inclined its helm toward her.

"My lady," said Igris.

Fern looked at it for a long moment.

"You held the far end," she said. "Very cleanly. You didn't waste a single step." She paused. "That was very well done."

The knight was silent. Then it inclined its helm again, a little lower.

"My lady is kind," it said.

Fern went pink. She walked on to the stair very fast without looking back. Stark, stepping off the bridge onto solid ground at last, looked at the knight, and then at Fern, and then at the knight again, with an expression of deep and silent suspicion.

Then Frieren came down off the bridge.

She came floating, the way she came down off anything, and landed beside the knight. She looked up at it. The knight looked down at her.

It did not say *my lady*.

It did not say anything at all.

It bowed. Not the short formal bow it had given Maki on twenty. Not the inclination of the helm it had given Fern. A slow, deep bow, from the waist, with one gauntleted hand on its breastplate and the long sword held point-down at its side. The bow of a knight to someone who matters more than a lady.

It held it for a long moment.

Then it straightened and said nothing, and stood aside.

Frieren looked at it for a long time.

Then she turned and looked at Mau, still on the last span of the bridge.

"Why did it do that?" she said.

"I don't know," said Mau honestly.

"It bowed to Fern. It said *my lady*. It bowed to me and didn't say anything."

"I know."

"Why?"

Mau looked at the knight. It stood with its sword lowered at the edge of the bridge. Its cold blue eyes were on nothing in particular, the way a knight's eyes are when it is standing guard and does not intend to be part of the conversation.

"I don't know," he said again. "I didn't tell it anything about you."

Frieren looked at the knight a moment longer.

"Hm," she said. And went on toward the stair. She did not say anything else about it. But Mau saw her look back at the knight once, from the bottom step, with an expression he had seen on her face before, when she found a grimoire she could not read yet.

* * *

*Floor 25 cleared.*

*Level 27. One hundred points unspent.*

"Back," said Mau.

"Yes, my liege."

The knight went down into the stone. The cold blue lights went last. Seven and a half minutes, and not a drop. Mau checked the cup with Ciel. Where it had been this morning. Nothing broken, nothing to remake. The margin was all still there.

"One hundred," said Fern, from the stair. She had heard him read the notice. "Points."

"One hundred."

Fern looked at him.

"Umaru is going to need the other wall," she said. "And you're going to need to sit at a table. With pencils." She paused. "Soon. Not today. But soon."

"I know."

"If the guardian on thirty is bigger than the one on twenty," said Fern, "and the one on forty is bigger than that—"

"I know," said Mau. "I'll talk to Umaru."

Fern nodded, satisfied. She went up the stair with her row of lights, and Stark went after her, carefully not looking down at the chasm. Frieren waited for Mau at the bottom step.

When he reached her, she said, without looking at him: "It bowed to me."

"It did."

"Like I mattered more than a lady."

Mau did not say anything.

Frieren looked at the stair.

"I'm going to find out why," she said, and went up.

Behind them, in the chasm, very far down, the red glow pulsed once and was still.
