# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 33 — A Knight in the Yard

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 33
**Continuity state:** Igris meets the house in the yard at dusk, within Shoko's two-minute limit. Ori adds a line to the list; Milim challenges him and he declines without his liege's leave; he calls Ori "little lady"; he gives Frieren the same deep wordless bow, in front of everyone; Seiko laughs; Frieren asks him directly and he answers only "It is not mine to say." The house starts to wonder. Comedy, not a power change

*When: Day 350, dusk. The yard. Shoko is on the back step with her watch. Two minutes. She has said it three times, the last time to Igris directly, before he existed in the yard.*

Ori had asked at lunch.

Not *can I see him*. Ori did not ask things that way. She had come and stood beside Mau's place at the long table with the list in her hand, *SHADOWS (NAMED)*, and pointed at line three.

*4. Igris. (The notice.) A knight.*

"I wrote him down," she said, "and I've never seen him." She looked at the list. "That's not right. I saw Lantern before I named it. I didn't see Corner, but Corner was waiting with no name and that's different." She looked at Mau. "Igris already has a name. So I should meet him. Before I write anything else about him."

Mau had looked at Shoko, across the table.

Shoko had looked at the ceiling for a long moment. Then she had said, "Dusk. Two minutes. In the yard. Not one second more," and gone back to her soup.

So: dusk. The yard.

* * *

More people came than Mau expected.

Ori, of course, at the front, with the list and a pencil. Milim beside her, floating on the red ribbon, which Yuka held, because Yuka had decided at some point this summer that the ribbon was her job when Ori's hands were busy. Umaru with her slate. Maki, leaning on the new spear shaft she had finally cut, straight ash, with the silver flower tied below the blade exactly where it had been on the old one. Frieren on the back step beside Shoko, with her arms folded.

And then, somehow, everyone else. People came out of the kitchen and the workshop and the clinic, the way they came out for anything at dusk. Seiko was helped onto the bench under the ink tree by Mai. Gojo lay full length on the long table with his hands behind his head, which Momo had told him not to do four times. Stark stood behind Fern. Himmel stood a little apart, with his hands behind his back, having come in from the north end only an hour before.

"This is a lot of people," said Mau.

"It's a knight," said Umaru. "Everyone wants to see a knight."

"Ready," said Shoko, from the step. "Starting now."

Mau looked down at his shadow on the dry ground.

"Igris."

* * *

The dark gathered at his feet and stood up.

It came up the way it had come up on the bridge. All at once and very smoothly. Tall. Taller than Stark. Dark armour with no shine on it, the long dark cloak falling into place, the long sword in its sheath at its hip now, not drawn. Two cold blue lights behind its visor.

The yard went silent.

Igris looked at the yard. At all of them. At the ink tree with its dark burned-out lanterns, and the long table with Gojo lying on it, and the fort, and the kitchen door with Momo in it holding a ladle. It looked at everything for a long moment, without moving, the way a soldier looks at a new camp.

Then it turned to Mau.

"My liege," it said. "This is your house."

"This is my house."

Igris inclined its helm.

"It is a good house," it said.

Behind him, Mau heard Seiko make a small pleased sound.

* * *

Ori came forward.

She did not run. She did not hesitate either. She walked across the dry ground with the list held against her chest, the way she had walked across the wet grass toward Lantern, and stopped in front of the knight. She tipped her head all the way back to look at its helm.

Igris looked down at her.

"Hello," said Ori. "I'm Ori. I wrote you on the list."

The knight was silent for a moment.

Then, very slowly, it went down on one knee in front of her.

It did it the way it had knelt in front of the empty throne. All at once and without effort, in one long movement. The dark cloak spread out on the dry ground behind it. It knelt so that its helm was level with Ori's face. It looked at her with its cold blue eyes.

"Little lady," it said.

Ori looked at it.

"Is that what you call me?"

"If the little lady permits."

Ori thought about this. Seriously. Mau could see her turning it over, looking for the place where it was not true.

"Fern's a lady," she said. "You called her *my lady*. On the bridge. Mau told me." She looked at the knight. "So I'm a little one."

"Yes," said Igris.

"Because I'm small?"

"Because the little lady is young," said Igris, "and will not always be little."

Ori considered this for a long moment.

Then she nodded, satisfied, and opened the list against her chest and wrote, very carefully, after *A knight*:

*Calls me little lady. (Because I won't always be little.)*

* * *

Milim could not wait any longer.

She had been floating on the end of the ribbon, vibrating, for the whole of Ori's conversation. Yuka had been holding on with both hands. Now Milim pulled. Yuka was not expecting it, and the ribbon went taut, and Milim was suddenly a body-length closer to the knight, hovering in the air in front of it with her fists up.

"Fight me," said Milim.

The yard went very quiet again.

Igris, still kneeling, turned its helm and looked at her. At the small pink-haired girl floating in the air in front of it with her fists up and a red ribbon round her ankle. At her eyes, which were very bright.

It looked at her for a long time.

Mau, watching, had the strong impression it was measuring something. Something very large, in something very small.

"No," said Igris.

Milim's fists wobbled.

"*Why not?*"

"Not without my liege's leave," said Igris. It looked at Mau. Then back at Milim. "And my liege's leave would be unwise to give." A pause. "The little dragon would win."

Milim stopped vibrating.

She hung in the air in front of the knight with her mouth open. Then her fists came down. Then her whole face changed, very slowly, into an expression of pure, enormous, radiant delight.

"He knows," she whispered to Ori. "He *knows*."

"Everyone knows," said Ori.

"But *he* knows," said Milim. "He's only been here one minute and he *knows*." She drifted back down on the ribbon, glowing, and Yuka reeled her in with an expression of deep relief.

"One minute," said Shoko, from the step.

* * *

Then Igris stood up.

It stood in one long smooth movement and turned, and looked across the yard. Past Ori, past Milim on her ribbon, past the long table and Gojo lying on it.

At the back step.

At Frieren, standing beside Shoko with her arms folded.

It walked toward her.

The yard watched it go. Nobody moved. It walked across the dry ground with long unhurried strides until it stood at the foot of the back step, below her, and looked up.

Frieren looked down at it.

She did not unfold her arms.

And the knight bowed.

The same bow it had given her at the far end of the bridge. Slow. Deep. From the waist. One gauntleted hand flat on its breastplate. The other holding the sheathed sword at its side. Its helm went down until it was lower than Frieren's knees.

It held it.

The yard was perfectly silent.

Then, from the bench under the ink tree, Seiko laughed.

Not loudly. A small, delighted, cackling sound, the sound of an old woman who had just seen something she had been waiting to see. Turbo Granny, in her cat beside her, said, "*What? What is it?*" and Seiko said, "Hush," and kept laughing.

Frieren did not look at Seiko.

She looked at the knight's bowed helm.

"Why," she said.

The knight did not straighten.

"Why do you do that," said Frieren. "You bowed to Fern. You said *my lady*. You knelt to Ori and called her *little lady*. You bow to me and say nothing." She looked at the bowed helm. "Why."

The knight was silent for a long moment.

Then, without straightening, it said: "It is not mine to say."

"Then whose is it?"

The knight said nothing.

"Two minutes," said Shoko.

* * *

"Back," said Mau.

The knight straightened. It turned and looked at him across the yard.

"My liege," it said.

Then it went down into his shadow, all at once, smoothly. The cold blue lights went last, still turned toward the back step.

The yard let out its breath.

Everyone started talking at once. Umaru was writing on her slate faster than Mau had ever seen her write. Milim was telling the five that the knight *knew*. Gojo had sat up on the long table and was looking at Frieren with his bare eyes and an expression of enormous interest. Momo was telling Gojo to get off the table. Himmel was looking at the place where the knight had stood, with a very strange expression. He brushed his hair.

Seiko was still laughing quietly under the ink tree.

Frieren came down the back step.

She walked across the yard to where Mau stood. She stopped in front of him.

"You didn't tell it anything about me," she said.

"I didn't."

"Then why does it bow like that?"

"I don't know," said Mau. "I really don't."

Frieren looked at him for a long time.

Then she turned and looked at Seiko, still laughing under the ink tree.

"*Seiko*," she said.

"Nothing, dear," said Seiko. "Nothing at all. I'm just an old woman enjoying a knight." She wiped her eyes. "That knight has very good manners. Very old-fashioned. Very correct." She smiled. "He knows exactly who you are."

"Who am I?"

Seiko looked at her, and then at Mau, and then back at her.

"Not tonight, dear," she said comfortably. "I've learned my lesson about saying things at the wrong time." And she got up, with Mai's help, and went in to dinner, still smiling.

Frieren stood in the middle of the yard with her arms folded.

"I'm going to find out," she said to nobody.

Ori, beside her, had the list open. She was writing. When she finished, she showed it to Mau.

*4. Igris. (The notice.) A knight. Calls me little lady. (Because I won't always be little.) Bows to Frieren. (Won't say why.)*
