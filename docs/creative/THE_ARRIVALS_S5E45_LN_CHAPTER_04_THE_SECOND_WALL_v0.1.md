# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 34 — The Second Wall

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30

*When: Day 351, after lunch. Umaru has nailed up a second plank beside the first wall section, the way Anko did for the tower board. Fern is at the table with the ledger, because Umaru asked her to witness.*

Umaru had needed the second wall.

She had nailed it up herself, that morning, with Wakana holding the plank and saying nothing about how she held the hammer. Now it stood beside the first. On it were two new sheets, pinned side by side, with fresh chalk lines in two colours.

In the middle, in a box: *100.*

And underneath, smaller, in Yuka's neat hand: *(One hundred. Fern says soon. Not today. But soon. It is now soon.)*

"Fern said soon," said Umaru, tapping the box. "On the bridge. In front of witnesses."

"I was there," said Fern, from the table. "I'm the witness."

"She's the witness," said Umaru, satisfied. She turned to Mau. "So. One hundred."

Mau looked at the box.

"I'm listening," he said.

* * *

"Here's what we know," said Umaru.

She went to the first wall. The old sheets. The solid line from the reserve to *Intelligence*, which she had drawn thirteen days ago and gone over twice since, very carefully, so it would not fade.

"Thirty points into Intelligence doubled the cup," she said. "That's known. Ciel measured it. One at a time." She tapped the solid line. "And it worked. You raised Igris. You stored him. You stood him on the bridge for seven and a half minutes and you still had a margin."

"Yes."

"Here's what else we know." She went to the second wall. The new sheets. On the first one, in her square Ministry hand, a list:

*Floor 10: guardian. (Not kept. Cup too shallow.)*

*Floor 20: Igris. (Kept. Cup doubled.)*

*Floor 30: ?*

*Floor 40: ?*

*Floor 50: ?*

"Every ten," said Umaru, "is a guardian. Every guardian is bigger than the last." She tapped *Floor 30*. "If you want to keep the next one—"

"I don't have to keep every one."

"I know you don't," said Umaru. "That's not what I'm saying." She turned round. "I'm saying, *if you want to*. If the next one is something you'd want. You should be able to choose. Not have the cup choose for you." She looked at him. "On floor 10, you didn't get to choose. The cup was too small. You chose *not this one*, but it was really the cup choosing and you agreeing."

Mau did not say anything.

That was true.

"The Ministry," said Umaru, "would like you to be the one choosing. Every time."

* * *

"How many?" said Mau.

Umaru had done the arithmetic. Of course she had. It was on the second sheet, in neat columns, with Yuka's checks beside each line.

"Fifty," she said. "Into Intelligence. That makes eighty. Ciel says each point adds about the same as the first thirty did. If that holds, fifty more makes the cup about three and a half times what it was before the festival." She tapped the column. "That's enough to keep something the size of Igris and something bigger beside it. And still have a margin. *If* that holds."

"And the other fifty?"

"Forty-nine," said Umaru.

Everyone looked at her.

"One," said Umaru, "goes somewhere else. As a test." She went to the first wall again. To the other four words. *Strength. Agility. Endurance. Perception.* Each in its own box, each with a short list under it that said almost nothing, because nothing had ever gone into them. "We know what one of these does. We don't know the others. We should find out. One point. Somewhere new." She paused. "And then we keep forty-nine. For when we know more."

Fern, at the table, made a small mark in the ledger.

"That's very sensible," she said.

Umaru went pink under her hood.

"Which one?" said Mau.

Umaru looked at the four boxes. She looked at them for a long time.

"Endurance," she said. "Because of your arm." She did not look at the white bandage. "On twenty. It found the place you weren't looking and it went through. If Endurance does what the word says, then next time it goes through, maybe it goes through less." She paused. "Or maybe it does nothing. Then we know that too."

* * *

He opened the window.

The small set of fields. The five words. *Intelligence* at thirty now, the others still at their starting values. The *100* at the top. The bar for the reserve below, the cup, twice as deep as it had been on the morning of the festival, with the fill sitting in it at about half. He had rested for three days since the bridge.

*Ready,* said Ciel. *Same as before. One at a time. I'll watch everything.*

"She's ready," said Rimuru, from the end of the bench, where he had been sitting with his chin on his hand the whole time.

Mau put one point into Intelligence.

The cup got a little deeper. *Unchanged,* said Ciel, about everything else. Rimuru repeated it. Yuka made a mark.

Then the next.

It took a long time. It always did. One at a time, the way Umaru said. Around the twentieth, Ori came down the stairs and sat at the end of the long table with her list, watching. Around the thirtieth, Milim came and floated over the table with her chin in her hands. Yuka told her she could stay if she counted too, and Milim counted, out loud, very seriously, and got it wrong twice, and Yuka corrected her twice without looking up.

At fifty, Yuka put down her pencil.

"Fifty," she said. "Eighty in Intelligence."

Mau looked at the window.

The cup was very deep now. Much deeper than it had been on the morning of the festival, when it had been a shallow thing with a little in the bottom. The fill sat in it at the same level it had been at before he started. Spending points did not fill it. Only rest did that. But the space around the fill was enormous now, by comparison. Room for Lantern and Corner and Igris in storage, and something larger beside them. And a margin after that. A real one.

*Capacity roughly three and a half times what it was before the festival,* said Ciel. *Present level unchanged. It will take a while to fill. Rest fills it. Not points.* A pause. *Still no change to anything else. Mana, cursed energy, the eyes, the Noise's other work. Only the reserve.*

Rimuru repeated it.

Umaru, at the wall, wrote *80* in the Intelligence box and drew a solid circle round it. Then she wrote, beside the cup on her diagram, *3.5x*, and drew a box round that too.

* * *

"Now Endurance," she said. "One."

Mau put one point into Endurance.

The number beside the word went from its starting value to one more. That was all he saw.

Nothing happened in his arm. Nothing happened anywhere. The cut under the white bandage felt exactly the way it had felt a moment before, healing, a little tight. His body felt like his body.

*Ciel?*

She was quiet for a long time. Longer than she had been for any of the Intelligence points.

*Something changed,* she said at last. *I think. It's very small. Smaller than I can measure clearly. Somewhere in how your body holds together. I can't tell you what it does. Not from one point.* A pause. *I'm sorry. I'd need more points to see the shape of it. Or time. Or the next time something tries to go through you.*

Rimuru repeated it. All of it.

Umaru stood at the wall with the chalk in her hand for a long moment.

Then she wrote, under *Endurance*: *1 point. Something changed. Too small to see. (Ciel.)*

And under that, smaller: *Question. Not answered. Keep watching.*

She drew it as a broken line. Not solid.

"That's fine," she said. "That's a result. We know one point isn't enough to see. We know it does *something*." She stepped back from the wall. "Next time, we'll know more."

"How?"

"You'll get hit again," said Umaru, very matter-of-factly. "Eventually. In the tower. And Ciel will watch. And we'll find out if it went through less." She looked at him. "Not on purpose. But you will."

"That's a very Ministry way to run an experiment."

"It's the only way to run that one," said Umaru. "The Ministry doesn't stab people for science."

* * *

Forty-nine.

Umaru wrote it in the box on the second wall, very large, with a solid circle round it, below *100*, which she crossed out with one straight line.

Then she sat down at the table, very suddenly, the way she had sat down the first time, as if her legs had decided.

"The Ministry would like a glass of milk," she said, from inside her hood.

Frieren was not there this time. She was at the river with Fern's ledger slip and the green book. Mau got up himself and went to the kitchen. Taihei, at the left end of the counter, was already pouring it, because he had heard. He handed it to Mau without a word. Mau carried it back and set it down in front of Umaru.

She drank it.

"Taihei poured that," she said.

"How do you know?"

"He pours it to the line," said Umaru. "Exactly to the line. Every time." She put the glass down. "Frieren pours it to wherever she stops thinking about it."

Mau looked at the glass. It was filled exactly to the little ridge near the top. Not a drop over.

"The Ministry," said Umaru, "is very satisfied." She did not say with what.

Fern, at the end of the table, closed the ledger. She had written something on the day's page, under the date. Mau saw it as she closed it.

*Mau: 50 Intelligence, 1 Endurance. Cup 3.5x. 49 kept. Witnessed. A very sensible afternoon.*

And under that, smaller, in her hand, which nobody else was supposed to see:

*He chose. Not the cup. (Umaru.)*
