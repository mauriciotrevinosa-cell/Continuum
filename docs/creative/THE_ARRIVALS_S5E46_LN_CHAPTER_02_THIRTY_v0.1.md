# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 36 — Thirty

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 36

Mau reaches level 32 (seventy-four unspent)

*When: Day 353, morning. Anko's board, second plank: 26, 27, 28, 29. Under today: 30 (guardian). START FROM: 20. Five hours, because the walk from 20 is long.*

The board said it, as usual, plainly.

*Day 351, noon. Mau. From 25. Floor 26: a staircase made of bells. Floor 27: a garden of glass. Cleared, both.*

*(Alone.) (Noon. Nobody was watching the screen at noon.)*

*Day 352, after dinner. Mau. From 25. Floor 28: an orchard where the fruit fell upward. Floor 29: a long cold corridor with snow blowing down it from the far end. Cleared, both.*

*(Alone.) (He went while we were all at the table. He said he was going to wash.)*

There was nothing else under it. Not Gojo's hand, joking. Not Rem's, wanting the kitchen. Just the brackets, and under the second one, pressed so hard it had gone through the paper to the wood, a single line Anko had not meant anyone to read:

*He's faster every day.*

* * *

Stark found the board first, on the morning of thirty.

He did not read things, as a rule. He let Fern read things and tell him. But he was up early, because Fern was angry with him about something he did not yet understand, and he came past the screen with his coat over his arm and stopped and read every line on the second plank.

Then he went and woke Maki. And Maki went and found Kaneki at the kilns, at the end of his shift. And the three of them came back to the screen together in the grey light and sat down on the bench in front of the dark frame, side by side, Stark in the middle, and waited.

Mau came out of the back door at dawn with the bag over his shoulder.

He saw them. He stopped.

"Thirty," said Stark.

"I know," said Mau. "It's a guardian. I was going to ask—"

"No you weren't," said Stark.

He said it very simply. Not angry. Stark was almost never angry. He said it the way he said things about the weather.

"You were going to say *just me, I'll be quick*," said Stark. "Like you said to Anko. Like you said yesterday when you said you were going to wash." He looked at his hands. "I read the board. All of it. Nineteen floors since the north road. Most of them on your own."

Mau did not say anything.

"My brother stood in front of the demons by himself," said Stark. "In my village. So I could run." He looked at his hands. "Everyone says that's what a hero does. Stands in front, alone, so nobody else has to."

He was quiet.

"I've thought about it every day since," said Stark. "It's the loneliest thing I ever saw."

* * *

Maki had her new spear across her knees. Straight ash, freshly cut, with the silver flower below the blade. The old cracked one was leaning against the bench beside her, where she could reach it.

"Igris was yours," she said. "Single combat. He asked for it. That was right." She looked at him. "This one isn't asking. The notice says assist. It says we can't carry." She tapped the board with the butt of the spear. "It doesn't say we have to sit at home while you get killed on twenty-six at noon."

"I didn't get killed."

"Not yet," said Maki.

Kaneki had not said anything. He sat on the end of the bench with his hands in his pockets and looked at Mau the way he looked at people at the kilns at night.

"You're not getting stronger to keep up with us," he said at last. Very quietly. "You're getting stronger so you won't need us."

Mau looked at him.

"I know what that looks like," said Kaneki. "I did it. For a long time." He did not take his eyes off Mau. "It doesn't end where you think."

* * *

Nobody said anything for a long time.

The sun came up over the hills behind the house. The light reached the dark frame of the screen and did not go into it.

"I stopped," said Mau. "On the road. For one second."

"We know," said Stark.

"I don't want there to be a second."

"We know," said Stark. "So we're coming." He stood up. He picked up his axe. He held out his hand for the bag on Mau's shoulder. "You fight it. It's yours. We heard the notice. We're not going to take it from you." He waited. "But you don't go up that stair on your own today. Not to thirty."

Mau looked at Stark's hand.

Then he took the bag off his shoulder and gave it to him.

Anko, in her doorway with her coffee, had been watching the whole time. She came over and wrote under today, in her plain hand:

*Mau, Maki, Stark, Kaneki. From 20. Floor 30 (guardian).*

And in brackets, for the first time in days, a different word:

*(Together.)*

"Five hours," she said.

* * *

Thirty was a blizzard.

They came up the stair from twenty-nine through snow blowing down it in their faces. They came out at the top into white. Wind, very loud, very cold, full of snow, coming sideways so hard you could barely stand. Snow underfoot, deep, to the knee. No sky. No ground beyond ten paces. No walls, no edges, no anything. Just white, moving, everywhere.

Mau put up the space round the four of them. Thin. Not against attack. Against the wind. The snow hit it and slowed and slid off sideways, and inside it, suddenly, they could hear each other.

"Thank you," said Stark, through his teeth.

The notice came up. It had to fight the snow to be read.

*Floor 30 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the third ten.*

*Transfer point beyond.*

No second notice. No request.

"No single combat," said Maki. She sounded almost disappointed. Then she looked at Mau's bandaged arm, which was not bandaged any more, only healing, a long pink line. "Good."

"Where is it?" said Stark.

Nobody could see anything. White. Wind. Snow.

Kaneki had his head on one side.

He stood very still inside the thin space with his eyes half closed, the way he stood at the kilns, the way he had stood at the top of the stair on eleven listening to the leaves.

"Breathing," he said. "Big. Very big. Slow." He turned his head, a little, one way, then the other. "That way. Not far. Thirty paces. It's lying down. In the snow." He paused. "It knows we're here. It's waiting for us to get closer."

"Like Igris," said Mau.

"No," said Kaneki. "Igris waited like a soldier." He listened. "This waits like an animal. It's going to come all at once."

* * *

It came all at once.

Mau saw it through his eyes before he saw it through the snow. A shape rising out of the white, thirty paces ahead, huge. Not tall. Wide. A bear. White, so white it was nearly invisible against the snow, but with something over its shoulders and back and head that was not fur. Ice. Thick plates of it, blue-white, grown over it like armour. Its eyes were dull red.

It was bigger than Lantern.

The notice came up, in the middle of his sight, the way it had for Igris.

*Guardian: Tank, the Great White Bear.*

"Tank," said Mau aloud.

The bear charged.

* * *

Stark met it.

Mau had heard about this. Fern talked about it sometimes, when Stark was not there. In the red gate, at the snowline, in front of the dragon. A thing Stark did that nobody else in the house could do. Not Maki. Not Yuta. Not even Mikasa.

He stood in front of it.

He did not dodge. He did not step aside. He planted his feet in the deep snow with his axe held crosswise in both hands, and the great white armoured bear came out of the blizzard at him like a falling wall. He met it. The axe took the bear's head on the flat of the blade. The shock of it went through Stark and into the snow beneath him, and the snow blew out round his feet in a ring. He slid back. One pace. Two. He did not go down.

The bear stopped.

Stark was still standing. His arms were shaking. His face was white. He was still standing.

"*Now*," he said, through his teeth.

* * *

Maki was already moving.

She went in from the side, low, through the deep snow. Faster than anyone should be able to move through snow that deep. Inside the bear's reach while its head was still on Stark's axe. The new spear, straight ash, with the silver flower below the blade. She drove it at the place where the ice plates on the bear's shoulder met. The gap.

The ice cracked.

Not through. The plates were thick. But they cracked, a long white line, and the bear roared, a sound that filled the whole blizzard. It swung its head away from Stark toward Maki.

Kaneki hit it from the other side.

Mau did not see how. He saw Kaneki move, faster than a man that size should move. Then the bear's head snapped sideways, away from Maki, as if something very heavy had struck it. And Kaneki was standing in the snow beside it, breathing hard, with his fists closed.

The bear reared up.

Up and up, on its hind legs, enormous, white, ice-plated, with its forelegs raised and its claws out, higher than the wind. And came down.

At all of them.

* * *

"*Igris*," said Mau. "*Lantern*."

He had not decided to call both. He did. The words came out together.

Igris came up out of the snow beside Stark, tall and dark, sword already drawn. He met the bear's right foreleg as it came down. The long blade turned it. Not stopped it. Turned it. The claws went into the snow a hand's breadth from Stark's shoulder.

And Lantern came up behind the bear.

Out of Mau's shadow, which stretched long and pale-blue across the white snow. Up, wings first, unfolding into the blizzard. Huge. Dark. No shine on it at all. The two small cold lights in its skull. It rose behind the rearing bear, bigger than the bear, and it did not breathe fire. Mau did not know if it could. It did not need to. It brought its whole dark weight down on the bear's back, the way it had once come down on the pillars at the high pass, alive, trying to kill them.

The bear went down.

Face first into the snow, under Lantern's weight, with its forelegs pinned. Igris standing over its right shoulder with his sword at the cracked ice. Maki at its left with her spear in the gap.

Mau felt the reserve go.

Fast. Much faster than Igris alone on the bridge. Faster than raising Igris on twenty. Two of them standing at once, and one of them as big as a house, and the cup was emptying like a jar with the bottom knocked out. Even three and a half times as deep, he could feel the floor of it coming up.

*Thirty seconds,* said Ciel. *Maybe forty. Not more.*

"*Maki*," he said.

Maki did not drive the spear in.

She had it in the gap, in the crack in the ice, the point resting where it would go. She could have. Mau saw that she could have. One push.

She looked at him through the blowing snow.

"Yours," she said. And let go of the shaft, and stepped back.

*Twenty seconds,* said Ciel.

He climbed. Up the bear's pinned shoulder, over the cracked blue ice, slipping, the wind tearing at him, to where Maki's spear stood up out of the gap. He put both hands on the shaft. He did not have Maki's strength. He did not have Stark's. He had a marble.

He put the small pull at the tip of the spear. Inside the ice. Inside the crack. And let it pull.

The ice plates round the gap folded inward on themselves, all at once, with a sound like a frozen lake breaking. The spear went down through them. He went down with it, his whole weight on the shaft.

The bear threw its head back one last time. Under Lantern's weight, under Igris's blade, it threw its great armoured head back, and the side of its skull caught Mau across the shoulder and threw him off its back into the snow.

He felt his collarbone go.

*Ten seconds,* said Ciel.

The bear went still.

"*Back*," said Mau. "Both. *Back*."

Lantern folded down into his shadow. All at once. The cold lights last. Igris stayed one heartbeat longer, sword at the cracked ice, to be sure. Then he went too.

The drain stopped.

Mau lay in the snow where he had been thrown and breathed, with his left arm held against his chest and the white sky going round, and Kaneki already kneeling beside him, not touching the shoulder, just there.

* * *

*Guardian defeated.*

Nobody said anything for a while.

Stark sat down in the snow. He did not decide to. He was standing, and then he was sitting, with his axe across his knees and his arms still shaking. He looked at the bear lying in front of him, white and huge and still, with Maki's spear standing up out of its shoulder where Mau had driven it.

"I stood in front of it," he said. To nobody.

"You stood in front of it," said Maki. She pulled her spear out. The silver flower was still there, below the blade. Not a scratch.

"Fern's going to—" Stark stopped. "Fern's going to be very angry. And then she's going to tell everyone."

"Fern's going to be very angry," Maki agreed. "And then she's going to tell everyone." She held out her hand. "Get up. You'll freeze."

Stark took her hand. She pulled him up.

Kaneki was looking at his own fists. He opened them. He closed them again.

"That hurt," he said, very mildly. "Hitting it. The ice." He looked at his knuckles. They were split. Already, very slowly, Mau saw the skin beginning to close. Kaneki saw him see it. He put his hands in his pockets.

Mau looked at the bear.

The thin space was still up round them, against the wind. The cup was low. Lower than it had been at any point since the festival. Not empty. There was a little in the bottom. A margin.

*Can I?* he asked Ciel. *The extraction. If it offers.*

*If it offers,* said Ciel, *yes. The cup can hold it in storage. Raising it will take most of what you have left.* A pause. *You won't be able to call anything else out for the rest of the day. Possibly tomorrow. You'll be very tired.*

*That's fine.*

*I know it's fine,* said Ciel. *I'm telling you so you know.*

The notice came up.

*Shadow extraction available.*

Under the great white bear, in the snow, the dark was gathering.
