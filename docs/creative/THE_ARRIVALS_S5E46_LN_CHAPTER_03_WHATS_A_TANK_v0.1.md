# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 37 — What's a Tank?

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 37
**Continuity state:** Tank is extracted (it does not speak), with Mau's collarbone broken. The floor 30 transfer point is secured; four registered shadows (Lantern, Corner, Igris, Tank) out of ten places; the reserve is nearly empty, so no shadows for a day. At home: Fern is angry with Stark and then tells everyone; Ori asks what a tank is, and Yuta, Taihei and Senku explain from their worlds; Ori writes "A big thing that doesn't stop." No new mechanic

*When: Day 353, the snowfield on thirty, then home. The blizzard is still blowing outside the thin space. Under the great white bear, the dark is gathering.*

He did it sitting down.

He had learned. Maki did not have to tell him this time. He sat down in the snow beside the great white bear, inside the thin space, with the blizzard roaring outside it. He put his hand flat on the snow, near the dark that was gathering under the bear's huge still side.

He thought about what he was asking.

It was not like Igris. Igris had saluted. Igris had asked for single combat with terms. Igris had knelt with his sword. You could ask Igris to stand at a door, and he would understand what a door was.

This was a bear.

It had lain in the snow and waited like an animal. It had come all at once. It had met Stark's axe with its whole weight and roared when Maki cracked its ice. It had not wanted anything except for them to not be there.

*I'm not asking you to understand the house,* he thought, at the white still bulk of it. *You don't need to. I'm asking you to stand where I put you. And not stop.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up through the snow.

Through the bear, the way it had come up through Igris. The white fur went dark where it passed. The blue-white ice plates on its shoulders and back went black, not melted, deepened, until the whole great shape was dark. Dark fur, dark ice, dark claws, lying in the white snow like a hole cut in the world.

Then it stood up.

Slowly. Ponderously. The way a mountain might stand up if a mountain could. Four legs. Then, for a moment, on two, rearing up into the blizzard, enormous. Then down again, onto all fours. The snow shook.

It turned its great dark head and looked at Mau. Where its dull red eyes had been, there were two small cold lights. Blue. Like the others.

It did not speak.

Mau had not thought it would. Lantern did not speak. Corner did not speak. Igris spoke, but Igris had been a knight. This had been a bear. It looked at him with its cold lights for a long moment.

Then it lowered its head. All the way. Until its dark jaw was resting in the snow at his feet.

Mau felt the cost.

It took nearly everything. Ciel had said it would. The cup had been low after Lantern and Igris stood together, and raising the bear drained what was left almost to the floor. He felt his whole body go heavy, sitting in the snow. Like he had been carrying something up a very long stair and had just put it down and could not remember how to stand without it.

"Back," he said.

The bear folded down into his shadow. All at once. The cold lights went last.

The drain stopped.

There was almost nothing left in the cup. A thin layer at the bottom. A margin so small he could barely feel it.

But it was there.

* * *

*Floor 30 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20 or 30.*

*Level 32. Seventy-four points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 4. Places: 10.*

Mau read it out, sitting in the snow. Maki listened. Kaneki listened, with his hands in his pockets. Stark listened, still holding his axe as if he had forgotten how to put it down.

"Four," said Maki.

"Four."

"Can you stand?"

Mau thought about it.

"No," he said honestly.

Maki took his good arm. Kaneki put an arm round his waist, under the broken shoulder, without touching it. He stood.

* * *

Fern was at the screen.

She was standing in front of Anko's board with her arms folded, exactly the way Frieren had stood for the bridge, and the throne, and the garden. When the frame lit and the four of them came through, she looked at them. At Mau, held up between Maki and Kaneki. At Kaneki's split knuckles, nearly healed. At Maki, untouched. At Stark.

At Stark, white-faced, with snow still melting on his coat, holding his axe across his body as if it might be taken away.

Fern looked at him for a long time.

"What did you do," she said.

Stark opened his mouth.

"He stood in front of it," said Maki.

Fern did not look at Maki. She kept looking at Stark.

"What was it?"

"A bear," said Stark. His voice came out very small. "A very big bear. With ice on it. Bigger than the dragon." He swallowed. "It came out of the snow. All at once. And I stood in front of it." He looked at his axe. "It stopped. I didn't."

Fern's face did not change.

She walked across the dry ground to where Stark stood. She stopped in front of him. She looked up at him. She took the axe out of his hands, very carefully, the way you take something from someone who has forgotten they are holding it, and handed it to Anko, who took it without a word. Then she put both hands flat on his chest, over his coat. She did not push. She left them there.

"You idiot," said Fern.

"I know."

"You complete idiot."

"I know."

"A bear bigger than the dragon," said Fern. "With ice on it. All at once. And you stood in front of it." Her voice was perfectly level. Her hands were shaking. "You stood in front of it."

"Someone had to," said Stark.

Fern did not say anything.

Then she put her forehead against his chest, between her hands, and stood like that for a long time, at the screen, in front of everyone.

* * *

By dinner, the whole house knew.

Fern had told it. Not in a list. Not in the ledger. At the long table, before anyone had sat down, standing at the head of it with her hands flat on the wood. Plainly, in order: the blizzard, the bear, the charge, Stark. Every word of it. Then she sat down and helped herself to the stew and did not look at Stark once for the whole meal.

Stark sat beside her, very red, eating very slowly.

Halfway through dinner, Fern put a second piece of bread on his plate without a word. Then a third.

He did not say anything. He ate all three.

* * *

Ori had the list.

She brought it to Mau after dinner, at the end of the long table, with the pencil. She did not ask. She sat down beside him and laid the list flat between them.

*SHADOWS (NAMED)*

*1. Ear. (Frieren.) A door.*

*2. Lantern. (Ori.) A light.*

*3. Igris. (The notice.) A knight. Calls me little lady. (Because I won't always be little.) Bows to Frieren. (Won't say why.)*

*4. Corner. (Ori.) Guards a corner.*

She had already written the next line:

*5. Tank. (The notice.)*

And then she had stopped.

"What's a tank?" she said.

Mau looked at the list.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "The notice called it that. It's a bear. A very big one. With ice on it. Stark stood in front of it."

"But what's a *tank*?" said Ori. "Ear's a door. Lantern's a light. Igris is a knight. Corner guards a corner. What does *tank* mean?"

Mau did not know.

He looked down the long table.

* * *

Yuta knew.

He was sitting with Maki and Yuka, three places down. He had heard. He looked up.

"It's a—" Yuta stopped. He thought about it. "In my world. It's a machine. For fighting. Very big. Very heavy. Made of metal, all over, so nothing can get through. It goes forward and it doesn't stop. Things hit it and bounce off." He paused. "And it has a—a sort of long—" He made a shape with his hands. "Never mind that part."

Ori looked at him.

"A machine that doesn't stop?"

"A machine that doesn't stop."

Taihei, at the end of the long counter, had come out of the kitchen with a cloth over his shoulder.

"They had them in my world too," he said. "On the news. Big. Slow. Very loud." He thought about it. "They go over anything. Walls. Fences. Cars. They just keep going." He paused. "My sister used to play a game with them in it. For hours." He went slightly red. "She was very good."

Umaru, beside Ori, pulled her hood down over her face.

Senku leaned over from across the table.

"Also," he said, "in some games, and in the way people talk about fighting, a *tank* is whoever stands at the front and takes all the hits so nobody else has to." He pointed his fork down the table at Stark. "Like him. Today. Ten billion percent. He tanked a bear."

Everyone looked at Stark.

Stark, with his third piece of bread halfway to his mouth, went even redder.

"I didn't *tank* anything," he said. "I stood in front of it."

"That's what tanking is," said Senku.

Fern, beside Stark, did not look up from her stew. But Mau saw her mouth move, very slightly. Almost a smile.

* * *

Ori looked at the list for a long time.

A machine that doesn't stop. That goes over walls. That things bounce off. That stands at the front and takes the hits so nobody else has to.

She picked up the pencil.

*5. Tank. (The notice.) A big thing that doesn't stop.*

She looked at it.

Then, smaller, underneath:

*(Like Stark.)*

She turned the list round and showed it to Mau.

He read it.

"That's right," he said.

"I know," said Ori. And then, because she had seen Fern almost smile, she got up and took the list down the long table and showed it to Fern too.

Fern read it.

She did not say anything. She picked up her pencil, the ledger pencil she always carried, and very carefully, under Ori's line, in her own small neat hand, added one word:

*(Idiot.)*

Then she gave the list back to Ori and went back to her stew.

Stark, beside her, read it upside down. He put his face in his hands. Across the table, Maki laughed out loud. Nobody in the house had heard Maki laugh out loud at dinner in a long time.
