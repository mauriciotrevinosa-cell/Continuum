# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 38 — The Winner's Rule

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 38
**Continuity state:** The R39 bridge. Yuka and Umaru have changed a rule of their card game (V29 "The Rules": same suit or same number, empty your hand first): now the winner of each round adds one rule for the next. They come looking for Mau to play while he is too tired for shadows. Ori and Milim join; Mau wins once by accident and adds a kind rule; the game becomes theirs, not a debrief. No training, no XP

*When: Day 353, after dinner. Mau cannot call a shadow until tomorrow at the earliest. Yuka and Umaru have come to find him at the end of the long table with the cards and a sheet of paper that says RULES (AMENDED) at the top.*

Yuka and Umaru found him at the end of the long table.

They did not ask whether he was tired. They could see he was tired. Everyone could see he was tired. He had been sitting at the end of the long table since Ori took the list away, with his chin on his hand, watching the lamps come on. He had not moved in some time.

Umaru put the cards down in front of him.

Yuka put down the sheet of paper.

*RULES (AMENDED)*

*1. Same suit or same number.*

*2. Empty your hand first.*

*3. If you can't play, draw.*

*4. Colour isn't a suit.*

*5. NEW: The winner of each round adds ONE rule. The rule lasts until the end of the night.*

And under that, in Umaru's square hand: *(Ministry-approved.)*

And under that, in Yuka's neat one: *(Yuka's idea. Umaru approved it after.)*

Mau looked at the sheet.

"You changed it," he said.

"We changed it," said Yuka. "Yesterday. When you were on twenty-nine." She sat down opposite him. "We played it eleven times. It's much better."

"It's much worse," said Umaru, sitting beside him. "It's chaos. It's a constitutional crisis every round." She dealt. "It's much better."

* * *

He looked at the cards she had dealt him.

"I'm very tired," he said. "I don't think I'll win."

"Good," said Umaru. "Then you won't make a rule."

"Is that the point?"

"The point," said Yuka, very seriously, "is that you sit at a table and play cards and don't think about bears." She turned the first card face up in the middle. A three of hearts. "Ori said you looked like you were thinking about bears."

"I was thinking about bears."

"Then stop," said Yuka. "Play a heart. Or a three."

He had a three. A three of spades. He put it down.

"See," said Yuka. "You've stopped thinking about bears already."

He had not. But he had started thinking about threes as well, which was something.

* * *

Ori came, with Milim on the ribbon.

She sat down at the end of the table without asking, the way she sat down at things now, and Milim floated over the table with her chin in her hands. Umaru dealt them both in without a word. Umaru dealt everyone in, always. It was in the constitution, somewhere, Mau suspected. Article something. *Nobody watches the President's game. Everybody plays.*

Milim won the first round.

She did not win it fairly. Nobody was sure she won it fairly. She put down her last card, a king of diamonds on a king of clubs, with a shriek of triumph, and Yuka looked at it for a long time and said, "That's—actually that's allowed," and Milim floated straight up to the ceiling and came down again.

"I get a rule!" said Milim. "I get to make a rule!"

"One rule," said Umaru. "Lasts until the end of the night."

Milim thought about it. Very hard. Mau could see her thinking, floating over the table with her eyes screwed shut and her fists clenched.

"Everyone," said Milim, "has to say *roar* when they play a seven."

Umaru wrote it down on the sheet.

*6. Milim: Say ROAR when you play a seven.*

* * *

Yuka won the second round.

She won it very properly, very carefully. Then she sat and looked at the sheet for a long time, with the pencil in her hand.

"If you play a card of the same suit as the person before you," she said slowly, "you have to tell them something true. About anything. Something small." She looked up. "Everyone here knows the big true things about everyone. The worlds. The coins. The bad days." She went slightly pink. "I'd like to know the small ones."

Umaru wrote it down.

*7. Yuka: Same suit as the person before you = tell them one small true thing.*

"That's going to take forever," said Umaru.

"I know," said Yuka. "That's the point."

* * *

It did take forever.

Ori played a heart on Yuka's heart, and had to tell Yuka something true. She thought about it for a very long time, and then said, "I like your rope," and Yuka went pink. Milim played a seven, and said "*ROAR*" so loudly that Momo came out of the kitchen to see what had happened. Umaru played a club on Mau's club and had to tell him something true. She sat with her hood half over her face for so long that Yuka started counting.

"Onii-chan," said Umaru eventually, "pours milk to the line."

Mau looked at her.

"That's not about me," he said.

"The rule says *something true*," said Umaru. "It doesn't say *about you*." She put down her card. "That's the true thing I wanted to say."

Mau did not say anything.

Then he played a club on Umaru's club, and had to tell her something true.

He thought about it.

"You choose on ordinary afternoons," he said. "At tables. With pencils." He looked at her. "And it's the best way anyone's ever helped me get stronger."

Umaru did not say anything. She pulled her hood all the way down. From inside it, after a moment, a very small voice said, "That's two true things. The rule says one."

"Then I owe you one back," said Mau.

* * *

He won the fourth round.

He did not mean to. He was too tired to mean to. He looked down at his hand and found he only had one card left, a seven of hearts. The card in the middle was a seven of spades. He put it down, and said "*roar*" very quietly, because it was the rule. And then there were no cards in his hand.

Everyone looked at him.

"You won," said Yuka.

"I didn't mean to."

"That's not a rule," said Umaru. "You won. You make a rule."

Mau looked at the sheet.

*RULES (AMENDED)*

All of them. Milim's roar. Yuka's small true things. A rule Ori had made in the third round, *If Milim floats off the bench she has to draw two*, which had already cost Milim nine cards. A rule Umaru had made in the second round that nobody fully understood but everyone was afraid of.

He picked up the pencil.

He thought about bears. About the snow. About Stark standing in front of something bigger than the dragon, and Fern's forehead against his chest at the screen. About Ori writing *(Like Stark.)* and Fern writing *(Idiot.)* underneath. About sitting at the end of the long table too tired to move. And Yuka and Umaru putting down the cards and the sheet without asking whether he was tired, because they could see he was.

He wrote:

*8. Mau: When it's your turn, before you play, you can pass to anyone at the table who looks like they need a minute. They get to skip. You play for them.*

He turned it round.

Yuka read it. Umaru read it, from inside her hood. Ori read it, very slowly, out loud.

"Anyone who looks like they need a minute," said Ori.

"Yes."

"How do you know if they need a minute?"

Mau thought about it.

"You look at them," he said. "Properly. You'll know."

Ori considered this.

Then she looked round the table. At Yuka. At Umaru with her hood down. At Milim floating with nine extra cards. At Mau.

"You need a minute," she said.

"I'm fine."

"You need a minute," said Ori again. Very seriously. "You look like you were thinking about bears." She picked up her cards. "I'm passing to you. You skip. I'll play for you."

Mau opened his mouth.

"That's the rule," said Yuka. "You made it."

He closed his mouth.

Ori played for him. She played very well. Better than he would have.

* * *

They played until Momo came out and said it was very late and some people had been up a stair made of snow that morning.

Umaru gathered up the cards. Yuka folded the sheet, *RULES (AMENDED)*, eight rules now, very carefully along its creases.

"Same time tomorrow?" said Yuka.

"I might be on thirty-one tomorrow."

"Then the day after," said Yuka. "We'll wait." She tucked the sheet into her pocket. "The rules last until the end of the night. But we're keeping this sheet. So we can remember what everyone made."

"We're keeping it in the Ministry archive," said Umaru.

"Where's the Ministry archive?"

"In my room," said Umaru. "Under the bed. With the constitution."

Milim, floating at the end of her ribbon, said sleepily, "I'm keeping the roar. Forever. Not just the night."

"That's not how rules work," said Umaru.

"*ROAR*," said Milim, and fell asleep in the air.

Yuka reeled her in. Ori took the other end of the ribbon. The three of them went up the stairs together, with Milim floating asleep behind them like a kite, and Umaru following with the cards.

At the bottom of the stair, Umaru stopped.

She turned round and looked back at Mau, still at the end of the long table.

"You owe me one," she said. "A true thing. Small."

"I know."

"Tomorrow," said Umaru. "Or the day after." She paused. "I'll wait."

And she went up.

Mau sat at the end of the long table in the lamplight with nothing in front of him. He was very tired. He had won a round of cards by accident and made a rule. A girl who looked sixteen and was much younger had used it on him in the first turn.

He was not thinking about bears any more.
