# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 44 — Allowed to Be a Different Colour

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 44
**Continuity state:** Ichika meets the fort, with Touka watching. Ori leads; Milim startles her and Ichika's eyes change (half-ghoul, as in her world, where that meant danger); the fort does not flinch; Ori compares her to the shadows' blue eyes ("a different colour; it's allowed"); Umaru registers her as the fort's youngest citizen under the constitution, with a small sash; Touka's guard comes down a little. No combat, no ghoul hunger scene, no power

*When: Day 357, mid-morning. The Summer Annexe under the ink tree, where the fort spends hot days. Ichika has been in this world for a little over a day. Touka is standing at the edge of the shade with her arms folded, the way she stood behind a counter when she did not trust a customer.*

Ori had planned it.

Of course she had. She had come to the room with two windows at breakfast with a page written out in her careful printing, and stood in the doorway, and waited until Touka looked up.

"I'm Ori," she said. "I live through the connecting door from Mau and Frieren. I'm in charge of the fort, sort of, except Umaru is the President, so I'm not really in charge, but I organise things." She held up the page. "I'd like to invite Ichika to the fort. This morning. Under the ink tree. In the shade." She paused. "You can come too. You should come too. Because she doesn't know us yet."

Touka had looked at the page.

*FORT. MORNING. INK TREE. ICHIKA. (TOUKA TOO.)*

And under it, smaller: *Nobody touches her unless she wants.*

Touka had looked at that line for a long time.

Then she had said, "All right," in a voice that meant *we'll see*.

* * *

The Summer Annexe was a blanket and a canvas awning under the ink tree, with the dark burned-out lanterns still hanging in the branches above it. The fort had moved its summer operations there when the heat came. There were cushions. A crate of things that might be toys or might be supplies, depending on who you asked. A slate propped against the trunk that said, in Umaru's square Ministry hand, *SUMMER ANNEXE. CITIZENS AND GUESTS. NO GROWN-UPS UNLESS INVITED.*

Under it, smaller: *(Grown-ups invited today: Touka.)*

Touka looked at that line too.

The fort was there. All of it. Ori on the blanket, cross-legged, with her page. Umaru in her hood and sash, sitting very straight on the best cushion, because she was the President. Yuka beside her, holding the end of a red ribbon that went up into the air. On the other end of it was Milim, floating a body-length above the blanket with her chin in her hands, trying very hard to stay still.

Kaneki brought Ichika.

He carried her on his hip from the house, across the yard, in the clothes Marin had found for her that morning, which were too big. He set her down at the edge of the blanket. She did not let go of his finger.

She looked at the fort.

The fort looked at her.

Nobody moved.

* * *

"Hello," said Ori. "I'm Ori."

Ichika held on to her father's finger and did not say anything.

"That's Umaru," said Ori, pointing. "She's the President. That's Yuka. She has a rope. And that's Milim, up there. She floats."

Ichika looked up at Milim.

Milim, who had been trying very hard to stay still for the whole of the introduction, waved. A small careful wave. With one finger.

Ichika looked at the floating girl for a long time.

She let go of her father's finger.

She took one step onto the blanket. Then another. She stood in the middle of it, very small, in clothes that were too big, and looked up at Milim floating above her on the end of the ribbon.

Milim could not stand it any longer.

She came down. All at once. Not falling. Swooping. The way she swooped when she was happy, straight down out of the air on the end of the ribbon. Right down in front of Ichika's face, upside down, with her hair hanging and her eyes bright, and she said "*Hello!*" at a volume that carried to the river.

Ichika's eyes changed.

* * *

It happened very fast.

One moment they were a child's eyes, dark, wide, startled. The next, one of them was not. The white of it went black. The dark centre went red. Bright, sharp red, in a ring of black, like a coal in ash. Just the one eye. The other stayed as it was.

Ichika made a small sound and put both hands over her face.

The Summer Annexe went silent.

At the edge of the shade, Touka had moved. Mau, watching from the back step with Frieren, saw her move. It was very fast. Faster than a woman in an apron should move. One moment she was standing with her arms folded at the edge of the shade, and the next she was at the edge of the blanket, half a step from her daughter, with her whole body changed. Ready. The way an animal is ready when it thinks its child is about to be hurt.

Because in her world, Mau thought, that eye had meant something. That eye meant people would be afraid. That eye meant people would come with weapons.

Kaneki had not moved. He stood at the edge of the blanket with his hand out, very still, toward Touka. Not touching her. Just there.

Milim, upside down in the air, had stopped.

She hung there in front of Ichika, with her eyes very wide. She looked at the small girl with her hands over her face. At the red eye showing between her fingers.

Nobody said anything.

* * *

Then Ori got up off the blanket.

She did not hurry. She walked across the blanket, round Milim, and crouched down in front of Ichika so their faces were level. She did not touch her. It said so on the page. *Nobody touches her unless she wants.*

"Your eye changed," said Ori.

Ichika did not take her hands away.

"That's all right," said Ori. "It's allowed." She thought about it. "We have lots of eyes here. Lantern's eyes are blue. Lantern is a dragon. Mau keeps him in his shadow. His eyes are two little blue lights, like lanterns." She paused. "Igris's eyes are blue too. Igris is a knight. He calls me little lady." She paused again. "Gojo's eyes are blue. Very blue. He has to wear a cloth over them sometimes because they see too much. And Mau's are blue too, but a different blue. Darker. Like the sea."

Ichika's fingers moved. A little. One eye looked out between them. The red one.

"Yours is red," said Ori. "That's a different colour." She considered it. "That's allowed. Everyone here's allowed to be a different colour. That's the rule."

"Whose rule?" said Umaru, from her cushion.

"Mine," said Ori. "I just made it."

Umaru considered this. Then she picked up her slate and her chalk.

"The President," she said, "ratifies."

* * *

Ichika took her hands away from her face.

Slowly. One, then the other. The red eye was still red. She looked at Ori with it. Ori looked back, crouched on the blanket, very seriously, and did not look away.

Then Milim, still upside down, still hanging in the air in front of them, said in a voice so small that nobody had ever heard Milim use it before:

"I'm sorry I scared you."

Ichika looked up at her.

"I do that," said Milim. "I come down too fast. Yuka says I'm too much at once." She hung there, upside down, with her hair dangling. "Your eye's *really* good, though. It's like a fire. Like a little fire." She paused. "Can you do it on purpose?"

Ichika looked at her for a long time.

Then, very slowly, her other eye changed too.

Both of them. Black and red. Like two coals in ash. She looked up at Milim with both of them, very seriously, the way Touka looked at a customer she was deciding about.

Milim's whole face lit up.

"*BOTH*," she said, at a volume that carried to the river again, and then remembered and put both hands over her mouth, and said through them, much more quietly, "*both*. That's so good. That's the best eyes."

And Ichika laughed.

A small sound. A child's sound. Startled out of her, at the girl hanging upside down in the air with both hands over her mouth.

At the edge of the blanket, Mau saw Touka's shoulders come down. Not all the way. A little.

Kaneki's hand, held out toward her, touched her arm.

She let it.

* * *

Umaru wrote on her slate for a long time.

Then she turned it round.

*CONSTITUTION OF THE FORT. AMENDMENT (EYES).*

*1. Everyone is allowed to be a different colour. (Ori. Ratified.)*

*2. Ichika is a citizen. The youngest.*

*3. Milim has to come down slower. (Yuka.)*

"Number three wasn't my idea," said Milim.

"Number three is the most important one," said Yuka, and reeled in the ribbon a little, so that Milim was the right way up again.

Umaru reached into the crate of things that might be toys or might be supplies. She took out a strip of cloth. It was red, like the ribbon, and much too long. She folded it over and over until it was short enough. Then she held it out to Ichika.

"Citizens wear a sash," said Umaru. "This is a small one. For a small citizen." She paused. "You'll get a bigger one when you're bigger."

Ichika looked at the folded red cloth.

She looked at her mother, at the edge of the blanket.

Touka did not say anything. But she nodded. Once. Very slightly.

Ichika took the sash.

Ori helped her put it on, because Ichika asked her to, by holding it out to her without saying anything. It went over one shoulder and across. It was still much too long. It dragged on the blanket behind her.

Ichika looked down at it.

Then she sat down on the blanket, in the middle of the fort, with her too-big clothes and her too-long sash and her eyes still black and red, and looked at all of them.

"Again," she said, to Milim. Her first word to anyone in the fort.

Milim floated up, very slowly, a body-length. And came down, very slowly, the right way up this time, and landed on the blanket beside Ichika with enormous care.

Ichika laughed again.

* * *

At the back step, Frieren leaned against Mau's shoulder.

"Ori made a rule," she said.

"I heard."

"*Everyone's allowed to be a different colour.*" Frieren watched the Summer Annexe. "She made it up on the spot. Because a little girl's eye changed and her mother was frightened."

Mau watched Touka.

She was still standing at the edge of the blanket. Her arms were not folded any more. Kaneki was beside her, close, with his hand on her arm. She was watching her daughter sit in the middle of the fort with both eyes red and a sash too long for her, laughing at a girl who floated.

"In her world," said Mau quietly, "that eye meant someone would come with a weapon."

Frieren did not say anything for a while.

"Not here," she said.

"Not here."

Under the ink tree, Umaru was writing a fourth amendment on her slate. Mau could not read it from the step. Later, when he passed the Summer Annexe on the way to lunch, he saw it.

*4. Touka is invited. Every day. (Grown-up, but allowed.)*
