# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 46 — The Tunnels

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 46
**Continuity state:** Floors 31–35 summarised from Anko's board. Floors 36–39 (Day 359) with Mau (owner), Gojo, Yuta and Frieren: tunnels of packed earth that become a hive, with soldier-ants the size of dogs, then of horses, all coming from above. Gojo's eyes count them; the party avoids the queen-sized chambers; the guardian of forty is plainly ahead. Mau reaches level 41 (one hundred and nineteen unspent). No extraction; ant soldiers dissolve

*When: Day 359, morning. Anko's board, second plank, nearly full: 31, 32, 33, 34, 35. Under today: 36 to 39, in Anko's hand, and a question mark after 39. START FROM: 30.*

The board said it, plainly, as it always did.

*Day 357, evening. Mau, Maki, Kaneki, Stark. From 30. Floor 31: a lake you walk across on stepping stones that sink. Floor 32: a library again. Not drowned. Locked. Stark broke the door. Cleared, both.*

*Day 358. Mau, Rem, Fern, Yuka. From 30. Floor 33: a market of masks. Floor 34: a long stair down instead of up. Floor 35: a mill with a water wheel that ran backwards. Fern stopped it. Cleared, all three. Four hours. Rem found no kitchen. Rem disappointed.*

Under that, in Rem's careful hand, without permission:

*Rem is not disappointed. Rem is patient.*

Mau stood in front of the board on the morning of Day 359 with Gojo, Yuta and Frieren.

"Three floors in a day," said Gojo. "Four hours. Fern's very efficient."

"Fern stopped a water wheel," said Yuta. "With one hand. I saw her face afterwards. She was very pleased with herself."

"She's allowed to be pleased with herself," said Frieren. "She stopped a water wheel."

Anko wrote under today: *Mau, Gojo, Yuta, Frieren. From 30.*

Then she looked at Mau.

"Forty is next," she said. "After thirty-nine."

"I know."

"Every ten is a guardian."

"I know."

Anko looked at him a moment longer. Then she wrote, after the date, *36–39. Not 40.*, and underlined *Not 40* twice.

"Not today," she said. "Today you look. You come back. You rest. Forty is another day."

* * *

Thirty-six was tunnels.

They came up the stair from thirty-five, past the backwards mill, into the dark. Not the grey light of most floors. Real dark, close and warm, smelling of earth. Fern was not here to set her neat row of lights, so Frieren set them. They were not neat. They were casual. They hung at slightly different heights and slightly different sizes, and Gojo looked at them and said "*wonky*" under his breath, and Frieren pretended not to hear.

The light showed walls of packed earth. A tunnel, round, wide enough for two to walk side by side, going away into the dark. The floor was smooth. Worn smooth. As if a great many things had walked over it for a very long time.

Gojo pushed his blindfold up.

He looked down the tunnel with his bare eyes for a long moment.

"Oh," he said.

"What?" said Yuta.

"It goes on," said Gojo. "A very long way. And it branches. Up, mostly. Lots of branches going up." He tilted his head. "And there are things in the branches. Moving." He was quiet for a moment. "A lot of things."

The notice came up.

*Floor 36 of 100.*

*Objective: reach the stair.*

* * *

The ants came in the first branch.

Not small ants. Mau had thought, when Gojo said *things in the branches*, of small ants. Insects. These were the size of dogs. Black, shining, segmented, with long jointed legs and heads that were mostly jaw. They came out of a side tunnel above them, out of the roof, dropping down onto the tunnel floor one after another with a sound like dry sticks breaking, and turned their heads toward the light.

Six of them. Then ten.

Yuta drew his sword.

Frieren raised her staff.

Gojo held up his hand with two fingers out, and a small blue marble came into being at the end of them, and he flicked it, almost lazily, into the middle of the ants.

They went toward it. All of them. Pulled, a hand's breadth, toward the small blue point, piling into each other. Their legs tangled. In the moment they were tangled, Yuta went through them with his sword and Frieren went through them with thin precise lines of light. It was over.

The ants dissolved into blue light on the tunnel floor.

Gojo looked at his hand.

"Easier," he said. "Still. Every time. Still a marble." He flexed his fingers. "I'm starting to take it personally."

* * *

They went through thirty-six and thirty-seven.

It was all tunnels. Packed earth, worn smooth, going on and on and branching. Always upward. The ants came out of the branches above them, again and again, in sixes and tens and twenties. They got bigger. On thirty-six they were the size of dogs. On thirty-seven, some of them were the size of ponies, with jaws that could take a man's arm off at the shoulder.

Mau put the thin space round the four of them. Not for the wind. For the jaws. An ant the size of a pony came out of a side tunnel at Yuta's back, faster than anything that size should move. Its jaws closed a hand's breadth from Yuta's neck, on nothing, on the space. Hung there. Yuta turned and took its head off.

"Thank you," said Yuta.

"Gojo would say it was sloppy."

"Gojo is right here," said Gojo. "And Gojo says it was fine." He looked at the dissolving ant. "Mostly fine."

Thirty-eight was worse.

The tunnels got wider. Then they stopped being tunnels and became chambers. Great round rooms of packed earth with openings in the walls and the roof leading off in every direction. In some of the chambers there were things that were not ants. Pale. Soft. Rounded. Lying in rows along the walls. Not moving.

Frieren looked at them for a long time.

"Eggs," she said.

Nobody said anything.

"It's a hive," said Frieren. "We're inside a hive. And we're going up." She looked at the openings in the roof. "Toward the middle of it."

* * *

Gojo stopped at the top of the stair to thirty-nine.

He did not go up. He stood on the last step with his bare eyes looking up into the dark, and he did not move for a long time.

"What?" said Mau.

"I can see forty," said Gojo.

Everyone looked at him.

"Not all of it," said Gojo. "The edge of it. Through thirty-nine. Up." His voice had gone very quiet. "There's something at the top. In the middle. In the biggest chamber." He paused. "It's not like the others. It's not like Igris or the bear. Igris was very good. The bear was very big." He was quiet again. "This one is very *hungry*."

"Hungry," said Yuta.

"Everything else on these floors wants us gone," said Gojo. "That one wants to *eat* us. All of us. And everything else. And then get bigger." He lowered the blindfold. "That's forty. That's the guardian."

Anko had underlined *Not 40* twice.

"We look at thirty-nine," said Mau. "We don't go up to forty. Not today."

"Not today," Gojo agreed. He sounded relieved. That frightened Mau more than anything else he had said.

* * *

Thirty-nine was the edge of the hive.

Chambers. Huge ones. Openings everywhere. And in the middle of the floor, in the largest chamber they had seen yet, a pit. Round. Deep. Its sides worn smooth. At the bottom of it, in the dark, the stair going up to forty.

The ants on thirty-nine were the size of horses.

There were not many. Four. They came at the party from four openings at once. That was worse than twenty smaller ones, because each of them could take a man in half. Frieren dealt with one. Yuta dealt with one. Gojo's marble tangled the third long enough for Mau to call Igris.

He had not decided to. It came out.

The knight came up out of the earth beside him with his long sword already drawn, and the fourth ant came at him, and Igris did not hurry. One step. One turn. The long blade went round in a flat arc. The ant came apart in two halves and dissolved before it hit the ground.

"My liege," said Igris.

"Thank you. Back."

"My liege." He went down into the earth.

The thread stopped. Fifty seconds. The cup barely noticed.

Frieren was looking at the place where Igris had been.

"He's getting faster," she said.

"He's always been fast."

"No," said Frieren. "Faster. Than on the bridge. Than in the yard." She looked at Mau. "Is that the cup? Or is that him?"

Mau did not know.

*I don't know either,* said Ciel. *It might be both. The deeper the reserve, the less he has to economise when he moves. Or he might be learning. Or both.* A pause. *I'm noting it.*

* * *

*Floor 39 cleared.*

*Level 41. One hundred and nineteen points unspent.*

Mau read it out.

"One hundred and nineteen," said Yuta.

"Umaru's going to need a third wall," said Gojo.

They stood at the edge of the pit in the largest chamber of the hive on thirty-nine and looked down at the stair at the bottom of it. The stair that went up to forty. To the middle of the hive. To something very hungry.

Nobody said anything.

Then Frieren put her hand on Mau's arm.

"Home," she said. "Rest. Talk to Umaru. Sit at a table." She looked at the pit. "Then come back."

"Not today," said Mau.

"Not today," said Frieren.

They went home through the small frame by the edge of the pit. On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Three hours forty," she said. "From thirty. To thirty-nine." She wrote it down. Then she looked at their faces. All four. One at a time. Gojo's last.

"What's on forty?" she said.

Gojo looked at her.

"Something hungry," he said.

Anko looked at him for a long time. Then she wrote, under *Floor 39. Cleared*:

*Forty: something hungry. (Gojo.) Rest first.*

And underlined *Rest first* three times.
