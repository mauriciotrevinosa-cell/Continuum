# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 47 — Who Are You?

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30

*When: Day 360, the reset hour. Thirteen nights since the first word. Thirty-nine floors behind him. Five shadows in the cup: Lantern, Corner, Igris, Tank, and the room for one more.*

He woke the way he always woke.

All at once. Braced. His heart already going. The Trial's hour.

Frieren's cold foot touched his shin under the blanket.

"One," she said, into the pillow. "Still here."

"Still here."

He lay in the dark. The connecting door was open a crack. Ori breathing. The ribbon creaking. Above, in the loft, Rimuru, silent. His heart came down.

He did not close his eyes.

He had not closed them at the reset hour for thirteen nights. Not since the first word. He lay in the dark every night after Frieren's foot and waited, listening down, toward the branch, the cup, the deep part of his shadow where the shapes were.

Nothing had come. Thirteen nights.

He had started to think it would not come again. That it had been one word, once, from somewhere he did not know. Something that had reached the branch by accident, the way a voice reaches you through a wall. That it was over.

He was wrong.

* * *

*Who are you?*

The same voice.

He knew it at once. Low. Male. Tired. The way a person is tired when they have been doing something hard for a long time. It came from underneath. From the branch, the deep part of his shadow, where Lantern was, and Corner, and Igris, and Tank.

Three words this time.

Not *hello*. A question.

Under the blanket, Frieren's foot went tense against his shin.

"Mau," she said. Not into the pillow.

"It's back," he said, very quietly.

She did not say anything. She put her hand flat on his chest, over his heart, and left it there.

*Ciel.*

*I felt it,* said Ciel. *The same. Something reaching the branch from outside. Brief. I still can't tell from where.* A pause. *Longer than last time. You heard words?*

*Three. Who are you.*

Ciel was silent for a moment.

*Answer it,* she said. *If you want to. I'm watching everything.*

* * *

He did not know what to say.

He had thought about it for thirteen nights. What he would say if it came back. He had thought of a great many things. Questions, mostly. *Who are you. Where are you. How are you in my shadow. What do you want.*

But it had asked first.

*Who are you?*

He thought about that. Lying in the dark, with Frieren's hand on his chest. A tired voice, from somewhere very far away, reaching into the shadow of someone it did not know. Asking.

He did not say it aloud. He said it the way he said things to Ciel, inwardly, but down instead of in. Toward the branch. Toward wherever the voice had come from.

*Mau,* he said. *My name's Mau.*

He waited. Nothing.

He thought about what else. What else was true, that a stranger might need to know.

*I live in a house,* he said. *With a lot of people.* He paused. *I don't know where you are. I don't know how you're reaching me.* He paused again. *I think you might be the one who stood in my place once. In the exchange. When I went somewhere else and came back.* He waited. *If you are, they said you wanted to go back to your war.* He was quiet for a moment. *I hope you got there.*

Nothing.

The branch was quiet. The cup was quiet. Five shapes in it, sleeping. Nothing else.

He waited a long time. Long enough for the window to go from black to grey. Long enough for Frieren to move closer, so her forehead was against his shoulder and her hand was still flat on his chest.

Nothing came.

*It's gone,* said Ciel eventually. *Same as before. No change to anything. The shadows are as they were.* A pause. *But it lasted longer. And it asked a question. Last time it only said hello.*

*It's getting clearer?*

*I don't know,* said Ciel. *It might be. Or it might be the same and you're listening better. Or it might be something else.* Another pause. *I'm writing it down. The date. The time. The words.*

* * *

"Tell me," said Frieren.

He told her. The words. His answer. That nothing came back.

She listened, the way she always listened. Every part.

"You told it your name," she said.

"It asked."

"And that you live in a house."

"That's true."

"And that you hope it got back to its war." Frieren lifted her head and looked at him in the grey light. "You told a voice in your shadow that you hope it's all right."

"It sounded tired," said Mau.

Frieren looked at him for a long time.

"That's very like you," she said. And put her head back on his shoulder.

* * *

He told Rimuru on the back step at dawn.

Touka had come out with three cups, because it was dawn and she was open early, and handed one to each of them and gone back in without asking what they were talking about. She had heard *voice* and *shadow* and decided, visibly, that it was none of her business. Mau liked her very much for that.

Rimuru listened with his chin on his knees.

*Ciel told me,* he said inwardly, when Mau finished. *At the hour. The same as last time. Something reached the branch.* A pause. *She said it lasted longer.*

"Three words," said Mau aloud. "A question."

Rimuru turned his cup in his hands.

"More than one word," he said.

"More than one word."

"That's the rule we made," said Rimuru. "More than one word, we think about Yuta." He looked at Mau sideways. "So we think about Yuta."

* * *

Yuta was in the yard at seven, with Maki, doing the slow morning practice they did every day. Steps and turns and stillness, with the practice swords, in the cool before the heat. Yuka was on the back step, watching. Mau waited until they stopped.

He asked Yuta to walk with him. To the edge of the timber yard. Away from the house.

Yuta came. He did not ask why. He could see it on Mau's face.

They stopped by the stack of Wakana's twisted oak, half of it gone now into the kilns.

"In the exchange," said Mau. "When I went somewhere else and you stayed. The man who stood in my place."

Yuta went very still.

"Sung Jin-Woo," he said.

"What did he sound like?"

Yuta looked at him.

"Why?"

Mau told him.

Both nights. *Hello*, thirteen nights ago. *Who are you*, this morning. The branch. Ciel. His answer. That nothing came back. That he had told Frieren and Rimuru and nobody else. Now Yuta, because it was more than one word, and Yuta was the only one who had heard the man's voice.

Yuta listened without interrupting.

When Mau finished, he did not say anything for a long time.

"Low," he said at last. "His voice. Not loud. Very calm. Like someone used to things much bigger than a room." He was quiet. "And tired. Under the calm. Like he'd been fighting for a long time and had a lot more fighting to do." He looked at Mau. "He wanted to go back. To his war. He said so. He stood on your side of the basin with his eyes closed, waiting for something none of us could see." He paused. "He was very polite. To the Goddess. Even when she was being—herself."

Mau did not say anything.

"Is it him?" he said.

Yuta thought about it. Carefully. The way Yuta thought about everything.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "It sounds like him. What you're telling me. Low. Tired. Polite." He paused. "But I can't hear it. I can only hear you describing it. And lots of people are low and tired and polite." He looked at the timber yard. "If I heard it myself, I'd know. I think. But it's in your shadow. Not mine."

"I know."

"So I don't know," said Yuta. "It might be him. It might be someone else." He looked back at Mau. "If it's him—"

"If it's him, what?"

Yuta was quiet for a long moment.

"If it's him," he said slowly, "then something's connecting you. Him and you. Through whatever it is you both have. The shadows." He looked at Mau's shadow, long in the early light on the dry ground. "He stood in your place, once. Maybe that left something. A door. A thread." He paused. "Maybe he's finding it from the other side."

* * *

They walked back toward the house.

"Who else knows?" said Yuta.

"Frieren. Rimuru. Ciel. You."

"Not Gojo?"

"Not yet." Mau looked at the house. "It's three words. I don't want the whole house listening to my shadow. I don't want Gojo standing at the door of my sleep." He paused. "If there's more. If it says something that matters. Then I'll tell the house."

Yuta nodded.

"That's sensible," he said.

"Frieren says that."

"Frieren's right," said Yuta. Then, after a moment: "Mau."

"Mm."

"You told him you hoped he got back to his war." Yuta did not look at him. "If it's him. You told a man who stood in your place, who you've never met, who might be fighting gods for all we know, that you hope he's all right."

"He sounded tired."

Yuta was quiet for a long time.

"I'd want someone to say that to me," he said at last. "If I was tired. Somewhere very far away. Fighting." He put his hands in his pockets. "I'd want someone to reach into the dark and say *I hope you got there*."

They walked the rest of the way to the house without saying anything else.

On the back step, Touka was standing with a fourth cup. She had seen them coming across the yard. She held it out to Yuta.

"You look like you need one," she said. "Both of you."

Yuta took it.

"I'm not grateful," said Touka. "I'm open early."

"I know," said Yuta, and drank.
