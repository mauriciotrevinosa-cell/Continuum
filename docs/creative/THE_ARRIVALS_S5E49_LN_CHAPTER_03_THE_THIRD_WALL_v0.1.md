# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 48 — The Third Wall

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 48
**Continuity state:** The third spending session before floor 40 (level 41, one hundred and nineteen unspent). Umaru plans from Gojo's word "hungry"; sixty more go into Intelligence (one hundred and forty in total). The reserve is roughly five and a half times its pre-festival depth, with the present level unchanged and refilled by rest. Endurance stays at one point; its effect is still unmeasured. Fifty-nine stay unspent; registration places are still ten. Yuka asks why he keeps any. The reserve is still not mana, CE or a Black Heart

*When: Day 360, afternoon. Umaru has nailed up a third plank. Wakana held it again and said nothing again. Anko's board says Rest first, three times.*

The third wall had one sheet on it.

Umaru had not needed more. She had pinned up one sheet, in the middle of the new plank, and written on it in her square Ministry hand, very large:

*FORTY: SOMETHING HUNGRY. (GOJO.)*

And under it, smaller:

*Wants to eat. Then get bigger.*

And under that, smaller still, in Yuka's neat hand:

*(Gojo looked relieved not to go up. Gojo is never relieved.)*

Mau looked at the sheet for a long time.

"That last line," he said.

"Yuka insisted," said Umaru.

"It's the most important line," said Yuka, from the table, with her sharpened pencil. "Gojo is never relieved. If Gojo's relieved, it means something."

Mau could not argue with that.

* * *

"One hundred and nineteen," said Umaru, at the first wall, tapping the box. "Here's what the Ministry thinks."

She went to the third wall. The one sheet.

"*Hungry*," she said. "That's different. Igris wanted to fight you properly. The bear wanted you gone. This one wants to *eat*." She turned round. "If it wants to eat, it'll want to eat the shadows too. Igris. Tank. Lantern. Anything you put in front of it." She looked at him. "So you'll need to put things in front of it for longer. And maybe more than one at once. Like the bear."

"The bear nearly emptied the cup."

"The bear nearly emptied the cup at three and a half times," said Umaru. "So we make it more than three and a half." She went to the first wall. "Sixty. Into Intelligence. That makes a hundred and forty. Ciel says if it still holds, that's about five and a half times what it was before the festival." She paused. "Enough to stand Igris and Tank together for a while. Or Lantern and one other. And keep a margin."

"And the rest?"

"Fifty-nine," said Umaru. "Kept."

Yuka looked up from the table.

"Why?" she said.

Everyone looked at her.

"Why keep any?" said Yuka. She was looking at Mau, not at Umaru. "Every time, you keep some. Twenty-five. Forty-nine. Now fifty-nine. Why not put them all in? If the cup's what matters." She paused. "I'm not arguing. I want to know."

Mau thought about it.

"Because Intelligence is the one we understand," he said slowly. "We know what it does. It makes the cup bigger. That's all." He looked at the first wall. At the other four words. *Strength. Agility. Endurance. Perception.* "We don't know the others. Endurance did something. Too small to see." He paused. "One day, maybe, we'll find out what one of the others does. And it'll matter. And I'll want to have something to put there." He looked at Yuka. "If I put everything in the one thing I understand, I'll never find out about the others."

Yuka thought about this.

"That's like the rope," she said. "Grandma makes me practise with the short coil too. Even though the long one's better. In case one day the short one's the only one I've got."

"That's exactly like the rope."

Yuka nodded, satisfied, and wrote something in the copy book. Mau saw it upside down.

*Keeps points like Grandma keeps the short coil.*

* * *

It took a long time.

It always did. One at a time. Ciel watching. Rimuru repeating. Yuka counting. Around the thirtieth, Ichika came to the end of the long table in her too-long sash. She stood there holding on to the edge of it with both hands, watching Yuka's marks. Nobody told her to go away. Around the fortieth, Touka came looking for her. She found her and stood in the kitchen doorway with her arms folded and watched too. She did not ask what they were doing.

At sixty, Yuka put down her pencil.

"Sixty," she said. "A hundred and forty."

Mau looked at the window.

The cup was very deep now. Deep enough that the fill at the bottom of it looked very small. It was the same fill. It had been refilling since the bear, slowly, with rest. It was nowhere near full. It would take days to fill a cup this deep. But the space around it was enormous.

*Roughly five and a half times,* said Ciel. *Present level unchanged. Still no change to anything else. The eyes, mana, cursed energy, the Noise's other work. Only this.*

A pause.

*There's one thing,* she said. *I don't know if it's the points or the time. But the cup refills a little faster now than it did on the morning of the festival. Not much. A little. As if the bigger it is, the more it wants to be full.*

Rimuru repeated it.

Umaru, at the wall, stopped with the chalk in her hand.

"Faster," she said.

"A little."

Umaru wrote it down on the first wall, under the cup on her diagram. *Refills a little faster? (Ciel. Not sure why.)* She drew it as a broken line.

"Question," she said. "Not answered. Keep watching."

* * *

Fifty-nine.

She wrote it in the box very large, with a solid circle round it, and crossed out *119*.

Then she sat down.

She did not ask for milk. She looked at the kitchen door, where Touka was standing with her arms folded.

Touka looked back at her.

Then she went into the kitchen and came out with a glass of milk, filled exactly to the line, and set it down in front of Umaru without a word.

Umaru looked at it.

"To the line," she said.

"Taihei told me," said Touka. "This morning. He said if the President sits down suddenly after counting, she wants milk. To the line." She looked at the glass. "He said it's very important that it's to the line."

Umaru pulled her hood down over her face and drank it.

Ichika, at the end of the table, looked at the glass. Then at her mother.

"Me too," she said. Her second word to anyone at the long table.

Touka looked at her for a moment. Then went back into the kitchen and came out with the small wooden cup from the kilns, full of water, to the line. She set it down in front of Ichika.

Ichika drank it, very seriously, with both hands. She was looking at Umaru the whole time, the way you look at something you intend to learn.

* * *

Before he left the table, Mau looked at the third wall once more.

*FORTY: SOMETHING HUNGRY. (GOJO.)*

*Wants to eat. Then get bigger.*

*(Gojo looked relieved not to go up. Gojo is never relieved.)*

Under it, someone had added a line while they were counting. In a hand he did not recognise at first. Then he did. It was square and careful and a little crooked, the hand of a man who had written a great many lists in a great many kitchens.

*Eat something before you go. (Taihei.)*

Mau looked at it for a long time.

Then he went to find Frieren. She was at the river with Fern and the green book, counting. He told her: tomorrow. Forty. After a breakfast that Taihei would insist on.

She closed the green book.

"I'll be at the door," she said.

"It didn't ask for single combat. Not like twenty. Gojo looked from the stair, and it didn't ask for anything."

"I'll be at the door anyway," said Frieren. "Or beside you. Whichever is closer to the hungry thing." She looked at him. "I'm coming, Mau."

"I know," said Mau. "I was going to ask."
