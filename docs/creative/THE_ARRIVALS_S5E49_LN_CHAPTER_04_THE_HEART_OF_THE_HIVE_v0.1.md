*When: Day 361, morning outside. The seventh night falls tonight. Taihei made breakfast at four and stood over Mau until he ate all of it. Five hours agreed, from thirty, because of the walk through the hive.*

They walked from thirty.

Through the lake with the sinking stones, and the locked library with Stark's broken door, and the market of masks. Down the long stair that went the wrong way, and through the backwards mill, stopped now. Then the tunnels, thirty-six, thirty-seven, thirty-eight, with the soldier-ants coming out of the branches above them. Smaller now, because the floors were cleared. Fewer. Frieren's wonky lights going out ahead.

To the edge of the pit on thirty-nine.

They stood at the rim of it and looked down at the stair at the bottom.

Nobody said anything.

Gojo had his blindfold pushed up. He was looking down the pit, up the stair, into forty, with his bare eyes. He had not said anything since thirty-eight.

"Still hungry?" said Yuta.

"Hungrier," said Gojo. "It knows we're coming. It's been eating." He lowered the blindfold. "It's bigger than yesterday."

* * *

Forty was the heart of the hive.

They came up the stair out of the pit into a chamber so large Frieren's lights could not find the walls. A great round dark room of packed earth. The roof was lost somewhere above. The floor was smooth and worn and littered with things Mau did not look at closely. Openings in every wall, dozens of them, leading off into the dark.

And in the middle, in the dark, something moving.

It was not large. That was the first thing. Mau had expected something like the bear. Something huge. It was not. It was tall, taller than Igris, but thin. Long-limbed. Upright. It stood on two legs like a man. Black, shining, segmented, armoured like the soldier-ants but finer, sharper, every edge of it honed. Long arms. Hands with long fingers that ended in claws. A head that was partly a man's and partly an ant's. Jaws that opened sideways.

Behind it, along the walls, in the dark, the remains of soldier-ants. Dozens of them. Eaten.

It turned its head toward the light.

Its eyes were not dull red, like the others. They were bright. Hungry. A sharp purple-red, like a bruise lit from inside.

The notice came up.

*Guardian: Beru, the Ant King.*

*The King does not share his meal.*

*Single combat with the Operator. Companions may witness. Companions who interfere will be removed.*

Nobody moved.

On twenty, the knight had asked. *The guardian requests.* Mau had thought his terms at the notice, carefully, and the notice had said *Terms acknowledged*. This one had not asked anything. There was nothing to answer. There were no terms.

"Mau," said Gojo. Very quietly.

"I know."

Behind him he heard Frieren's staff come up. Then, slowly, come down. He heard Yuta's sword clear its sheath and stay where it was.

"Stay at the edge," said Mau, without turning round. "Please."

"Beru," he said aloud.

The thing opened its jaws.

And it was in front of him.

* * *

He did not see it move.

None of them did. Not even Gojo. One moment it was standing in the middle of the chamber, and the next it was in front of Mau with its claws already coming down at his face.

The space caught them.

He had it up. He had had it up since the top of the stair, thick, the way he had held it for Igris at the end. The claws hit it a hand's breadth from his eyes and slowed and slowed and stopped. And the Ant King did not draw back the way Igris had. It did not study. It pressed. It leaned its whole weight on its claws against the space and pressed, and the space held. Mau felt it cost. More than Igris. Much more.

It drew back, at last.

It looked at him. At his face, through the space. Its purple-red eyes narrowed.

Then it looked past him.

At the edge of the chamber, where the three of them stood at the top of the stair. At Yuta with his sword out and not moving. At Gojo with his blindfold up and his hands in his pockets, which was how Gojo stood when he was making himself not do something. At Frieren.

It looked at them the way it had looked at the soldier-ants along the wall. Mau saw it. He saw it understand the notice. Food, standing at the edge of its table, that was not allowed to fight back.

It came again.

He did not try to stop it this time. He put the space out flat and slanted, the way Gojo had taught him in the iron wood. *You don't need a wall, you need an angle.* The claws hit the angle at full stretch and slid, and the edge of the space opened a line down the Ant King's side as it went past, black plate parting like bark.

It landed. It looked at the line in its side.

Then it bent, very fast, and picked up the remains of one of the soldier-ants lying along the wall, and ate it.

Mau watched it eat.

He watched the line in its side close. The armour grew back over it, black and shining, while it chewed.

"It heals," said Frieren. Very calmly. From the edge. "When it eats. It heals."

"I know."

"There's a lot to eat in here."

"I know."

* * *

"*Igris. Tank.*"

They came up out of the floor on either side of him. The knight with his long sword drawn. The bear, huge and dark, rearing up and coming down again on all fours.

The Ant King looked at them.

Its purple-red eyes went from Igris to Tank and back again. Mau had the strong impression it was looking at them the way it had looked at the soldier-ants along the wall.

As food.

It went for Tank.

It was on the bear before Mau could say anything. A blur of black on the great dark back. Claws going in. Jaws going in. Tank reared up and roared, a sound like a mountain falling, and the Ant King tore. It did not fight the bear. It *ate* the bear. It tore into the dark shape the way it had torn into the soldier-ants.

Tank came apart.

Not dying. Coming apart. The dark shape of him broke into pieces under the claws and the jaws. The pieces went back down into the floor, into Mau's shadow, all at once, like water running down a drain.

Mau felt it.

Not in the cup. In something else. A hole, where Tank had been. Not gone. Broken. The shape of him was still there in the cup, but in pieces, waiting.

*He's not lost,* said Ciel, very fast. *He'll need remaking. It'll cost. Not now. Later. You can't afford it today.*

The Ant King stood where Tank had been. It looked a little bigger. Its armour looked a little sharper.

It turned toward Mau.

* * *

It came at him faster than the space.

That was the only way Mau could think of it afterward. The space was where his attention was, and the Ant King was faster than his attention. It came from the front and the space caught it. It was not there. It was at his left, and the space went left, late. It was not there. It was behind him.

The claws went across his back.

Three of them. From the right shoulder blade down to the left hip. He felt the coat go, and the shirt, and then the skin, in three long lines, and then the heat, and then his knees hitting the packed earth.

"*MAU*—"

Frieren. From the edge. He heard her staff come up, and the notice flicker at the corner of his sight, *Companions who interfere*—, and the staff come down again.

He knelt on the floor of the hive with his back opened and his blood on the earth and thought.

*Think. Don't react. Think.*

It was faster than his attention. He could not out-look it. Igris on twenty had been one sword, and patient. This was not patient. This was hungry, and fast, and every time something cut it, it ate and grew back.

*It heals when it eats.*

Along the walls of the chamber, the remains of soldier-ants. Dozens. Food. Every one of them a wound healed, a scale grown back, a step faster.

*Take the food away.*

* * *

He did not have a way to take the food away.

He had a bag. Ciel called it that sometimes, when she was being careful. Everything the Noise had ever watched and kept. Everything it had seen people do, every day, for years, and quietly built in the dark, the way it built the walls. Most of it he had never touched. Some of it he had touched once, in the bell, and nearly not come back.

He reached into it now. Carefully. Not for anything big.

For the kitchen.

Momo, every morning, at the long counter, with the bread in and the porridge on and her hands busy. And the other things moving without her hands. The kettle lifting itself off the hook. The lid sliding onto the pot. The spoon turning in the bowl on the far side of the room, because her hands were full and the spoon was not. He had watched it every morning for years. The Noise had watched it with him.

*Ciel. Momo's.*

*It's there,* said Ciel, very fast. *A pattern. Rough. You've never used it. It will cost the other reserve, not the cup. It will feel wrong. It isn't yours.*

*How long?*

*Seconds. Not much weight. A kettle, not a house.*

*The bodies are lighter than a kettle. Some of them.*

He did not wait for her to answer.

He reached. Not with his hands. With the thing he had watched in the kitchen for years. It felt wrong. Like writing with the wrong hand. Like a voice that was not his coming out of his mouth. The remains of the nearest soldier-ants, along the wall, *lifted*. Not far. Not neatly. They lurched off the packed earth like something kicked. And he threw them. Away from the Ant King. Across the chamber. Down the open mouths of the side tunnels, into the dark, where it would have to go and look.

Six. Ten. His head was splitting.

The Ant King turned. It looked at the wall where its food had been. At the empty earth.

It looked at him.

* * *

It came.

He could not stop it with the space. He knew that now. He could not out-look it. He could not out-think it fast enough to catch it.

He could get out of the way.

*Ciel. Okarun.*

*Mau—*

*Okarun. The sled. The slope. The speed in his legs.*

He had watched that too. Every winter morning on the slope above the north field, with the fort screaming on the sled and Turbo Granny shouting from her booth that she was in his *legs*. A skinny boy with glasses who could not climb a flight of stairs without wheezing. Then something else came into his legs and he was the fastest thing on the slope. Faster than anything Mau had ever seen run.

The Noise had watched it every time.

*It's there,* said Ciel. *It's rougher than Momo's. It isn't built for your body. Mau, it will hurt. Your legs will—*

He reached.

It went into his legs like fire.

Not speed at first. Pain. Every muscle from his hips to his ankles lit at once, as if someone had poured hot oil into them. Then the Ant King was in front of him with its claws coming down. And he was not there.

He was across the chamber.

He did not know how. He had not felt himself move. He was standing ten paces from where he had been, on legs that were screaming, with his back opened and his head splitting. At the edge of his sight, the hair falling over his eyes had gone, for a heartbeat, white at the tips.

The Ant King turned. Found him. Came.

He went again.

Across the chamber. Away from the walls. Away from the bodies he had not thrown yet. Into the open middle of the hive, where there was nothing but packed earth and Frieren's wonky lights, and nothing to eat at all.

It followed him. Fast. Hungry. Every time it reached for him, he was somewhere else. Every time it turned toward the wall, toward the food, he was in front of it again, close, closer than he should be, so that it turned back to him instead.

He was dragging it. Away from the food. Into the middle. Where it could not heal.

His legs were tearing. He could feel it. Something in the right calf, a long rip, like cloth. Something behind the left knee. Every step he took on Okarun's speed cost him something he would not get back for days.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *Mau. Twenty more seconds and you won't walk out of here.*

*Then twenty seconds.*

It was not twenty seconds.

It was eleven.

On the eleventh, the Ant King stopped following him.

It stopped in the middle of the chamber, in Frieren's wonky lights, with its purple-red eyes on him, and it did not move at all. Mau stood ten paces away on legs that were coming apart and waited for it to come.

It did not come.

It watched.

He moved. Okarun's speed. Across the chamber, to the left, away from the walls, the way he had been doing it.

It was there when he arrived.

Not chasing. Waiting. Standing exactly where he had gone, as if it had known before he did. Its claws were already moving. He went again, right, fire in his calves, and it was there too, a breath ahead of him, and the claws took him across the ribs this time, and he went down on the packed earth and rolled and came up and went again—

It was learning.

*It's learning you,* said Ciel. *Your speed has a shape. It has watched the shape six times. It knows where the shape ends.*

It was the most frightening thing she had ever said to him.

* * *

"*Igris*," he said.

Igris was already there. He crossed the open middle of the hive in four long strides and came between Mau and the Ant King with his long sword already moving. Blade and claw, blade and claw, so fast Mau's eyes behind their filter could barely follow.

The Ant King did not try to get round him this time.

It went through him.

Not cleanly. Not easily. It took the long sword on its armoured forearm, let the blade bite, let the blade stay there, stuck in the black plate, and while Igris's sword was caught it put its other claw into the knight's chest. Into the dark armour. Through it.

Igris did not fall. Igris was a shadow; he did not fall the way a man falls. He held. He held the Ant King's arm with his sword stuck in it, and the Ant King's claw in his chest, and he did not let go.

*He's holding,* said Ciel. *Mau, he's breaking. A minute. Less. If he comes apart, bringing him back will cost you more than you have left—*

At the edge, Gojo had a marble on his palm. Mau saw it there, small and blue and turning. He saw Gojo look at it, and at the notice, and close his hand on it.

The Ant King bent and picked up a soldier-ant from the floor at its feet, one he had not thrown, one he had missed, and ate it with its jaws while its claw was in Igris's chest. The place where the long sword was stuck in its forearm began to close round the blade while it chewed.

Mau lay on the packed earth where he had last fallen and looked at it.

At the thing that was faster than his attention, and stronger than his knight, and learned the shape of everything he did, and ate every wound away.

He had used the space. He had used the pull. He had used Momo's hands and Okarun's legs. He had used Igris and Tank and every trick Gojo had ever taught him in the yard.

It was not enough.

It was not going to be enough.

* * *

The Ant King pulled its claw out of Igris's chest.

It looked at Mau.

Then it was on him.

He did not see it cross the chamber. He felt the weight. It came down on him on the packed earth, a blur of black, all claws and jaws and hunger, and the space came up too late and too thin and broke like a plate, and the jaws closed on his left shoulder.

He screamed.

He had not screamed on twenty. He had not screamed on thirty with his collarbone gone. He screamed now. The jaws went into his shoulder and through it and he felt them meet, and the Ant King did not let go, it shook him, the way a dog shakes something it means to tear apart, and the claws came in at his sides, his arms, his ribs, opening him, and he understood with perfect cold clarity that it was not trying to kill him.

It was trying to eat him.

Alive. In pieces. The way it had eaten the bear.

"*MAU*—"

Yuta.

He had been at the edge with the others, with his sword drawn and Rika's presence round him like weather, not moving, because the notice said not to. He moved now. Mau heard him come. Fast, across the packed earth, the sword already going round, Rika's hand forming in the air above him, the size of a door—

The Ant King let go of Mau's shoulder.

It turned to meet him. It did not go for the sword. It went under it, the way it had gone under everything, and its claws opened Yuta's arm from the shoulder to the elbow, and Yuta's blood went across the packed earth—

The notice flashed.

*Companion interference.*

*Removed.*

Yuta was gone.

Not fallen. Not thrown. Gone. The place where he had been was empty, with his blood on the earth in front of it and nothing standing over it, and Rika's hand gone out of the air with him like a candle pinched between two fingers.

"*Yuta*—" That was Gojo. From the edge. Mau had never heard Gojo's voice crack before.

*He's out,* said Ciel, very fast. *Out of the tower. I can feel Rimuru. He's in the yard, on the ground by the screen. His arm—Rimuru's got him. He's alive, Mau. He's alive.*

At the edge, Frieren had taken a step.

Gojo's hand closed on her wrist.

"No," he said. His voice was not level at all. "You go, you're out. And then he's in here with nobody." He did not let go. "Stay. Watch. It's all it'll let us do."

Frieren stood where she was. Her staff was up. There was a spell in her hand, white, the strongest thing she had, ready. She did not loose it.

Mau lay on the floor of the hive in his own blood with his shoulder open to the bone and his arms opened and his legs torn and his back opened in three lines, and saw it all.

And knew what was left in the bag.

The thing he was never supposed to touch.

* * *

*Ciel.*

*No.*

She knew. Before he said it. She always knew.

*Ciel. The filter.*

*No. Mau. No. You don't know what happens. Nobody knows. The guardrails—the last time they were unfiltered, you slept and they went down into the room and kept going, and you nearly—*

*I know.*

*You'll burn. Your eyes. Your head. Everything in it. You can't hold everything they'll show you. Nobody can. Gojo can't, without the—Mau. Please.*

*Ciel.*

He was looking at the Ant King. At Yuta's blood on the packed earth, and nobody standing over it. At Igris, broken, still on his feet. At Frieren at the edge, with a spell in her hand she was not allowed to loose, and Gojo's hand on her wrist.

*It's going to go for her,* said Mau. *The notice says they can't touch it. It doesn't say it can't touch them. It already looked at her.*

Ciel did not answer.

*I stopped for one second on the road,* said Mau. *I'm not stopping now.*

A long silence.

The longest he had ever heard from her.

*All of it?* said Ciel at last. Very quietly.

*All of it.*

*I'll be here,* said Ciel. *I'll hold what I can. I'll tell you when you're dying.* A pause. *I'm sorry.*

*Don't be.*

She took the filter off.
