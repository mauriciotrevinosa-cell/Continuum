# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 50 — My King

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 50
**Continuity state:** Mau is badly hurt (back clawed, both calves torn by the borrowed speed) and cannot walk; Shoko orders three days in bed, and the roof night comes to the east room. The extraction of Beru. It nearly empties the deeper reserve. The copy speaks lavishly and devotedly ("My King!"), with the personality the creator asked to keep; his knowledge of any original is not established. He notices Frieren standing in "the place of the King's guard" and also remembers her Zoltraak; he promises to "speak with her later" (setting up the challenge). The floor 40 transfer point is secured. Mau reaches level 42 (sixty-four unspent). Five registered shadows: Lantern, Corner, Igris, Tank (broken, awaiting remaking) and Beru. That evening's roof night is short; Yuta has a new scar

*When: Day 361, the heart of the hive on forty. The Ant King lies broken on the packed earth. Under it, the dark is gathering, deeper than anything yet. The cup is low. Not empty.*

*Can I?* he said.

Ciel did not answer at once.

He felt her look. At the cup, five and a half times deep, with not very much left in the bottom of it after two and a half minutes of Igris at full speed. At the thing he used for the space and the pull, empty. At the broken shape of Tank, in pieces, waiting. At him.

*Yes,* she said at last. *Barely. It'll take almost everything that's left. You won't be able to call anything else out for two days. Maybe three. You'll be very, very tired.* A pause. *And it's hungry, Mau. Even now. I can feel it in the depth. It was the hungriest thing on any floor. If you raise it, it'll be yours. It'll still be hungry.*

*Hungry for what?*

*I don't know,* said Ciel. *That's the honest answer. I don't know what a shadow is hungry for. We'll find out.*

He looked at the others.

Gojo was still crouched beside Yuta. Yuta was sitting on the floor of the hive with his arm across his knees, the long cut closing pink. Frieren was beside Mau, so close her shoulder was against his.

"Ciel says I can," he said. "Barely. And it'll still be hungry."

Gojo looked at the Ant King. Then at Frieren. Then at Mau.

"It went for her," he said. "At the end. Not to eat. Not to heal. It went straight for her." He paused. "After she took its arm."

"I know."

"You're going to raise a thing that went straight for her."

Mau looked at Frieren.

Frieren looked back at him. She did not say anything for a long moment.

"It's not the same thing," she said. "After." She looked at the Ant King. "Lantern tried to kill us at the high pass. Igris cut you on twenty. The bear nearly took Stark's head off." She paused. "They're not what they were. They're what you ask them to be."

"You're already sitting down," said Gojo. "Stay there. You couldn't get up if you tried."

That was true. His legs had not answered him since he let Okarun's speed go. His back was three long burning lines under the torn coat. He stayed where he was, on the packed earth, with Frieren's hand under his arm.

* * *

He thought about what he was asking.

It was harder than the others. The dragon had been fierce. The knight had been honourable. The bear had been an animal. This had been hungry. It had eaten its own soldiers to heal and eaten Tank to grow. And at the very end, with its arm gone, it had decided something, and gone for the person he loved most in any world, and stopped a finger's width from her face.

He did not need to be angrier than it had been. He knew that.

He did not know what to ask it.

He sat on the packed earth beside the broken black shape, in Frieren's wonky lights, and thought.

*You were hungry,* he thought, at the dark eyes. *You ate to get bigger. To be more. Because that's all there was in here.* He looked at the chamber. At the walls. At the remains of the soldier-ants. *There's more than eating out there. There's a house. People in it. People I'd stand in front of anything for.*

He looked at Frieren, beside him.

*If you're going to be hungry,* he thought, *be hungry to keep them. Not to eat them. To keep them.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the earth like a well overflowing.

It came up through the Ant King. Through the black armour, the long limbs, the broken shoulder, the head pulled back. It did not deepen the black, because the black was already black. It changed it. The shine went out of it. The bright purple-red went out of the eyes. And the whole long broken shape drew itself together, the way water draws itself into a drop.

The left arm came back.

Mau felt the cost.

It took everything. Almost everything. The little that was left in the bottom of the cup went out of it like the last water out of a tipped jar. He felt his whole body go heavy, sitting on the earth. Heavier than after the bear. He put a hand flat on the ground to stay upright. Frieren's hand came under his arm and held it.

The thing stood up.

Tall. Thin. Long-limbed. Upright on two legs. Dark now, all of it, armour and claws and jaws, with no shine on it at all. Where the purple-red eyes had been there were two small cold lights. Blue. Like the others.

It looked at him.

Then it went down on one knee.

Not slowly, like Igris. All at once. As if its legs had been cut out from under it by something it could not resist. It knelt on the packed earth in front of him with its head bowed so low its jaws nearly touched the ground. Its claws were spread flat on the earth on either side of it.

And it spoke.

"*My King!*"

* * *

The voice filled the chamber.

It was not dry and level like Igris's. It was high and fierce and trembling with something that might have been joy or might have been tears or might have been both. It went up and down. It did not echo. It did not need to.

"*My King!*" it said again. "I, Beru, who was the King of this hive, who ate and ate and was never full, who would have devoured the world if the world had come up the stair—I kneel! I kneel before the one who broke me! I am yours! My claws are yours! My jaws are yours! My hunger is yours! *Command me!*"

Nobody said anything.

Gojo, crouched beside Yuta, had his mouth slightly open.

Yuta had stopped healing his arm.

Frieren, holding Mau up, went very still.

"I'm not a king," said Mau.

The thing lifted its head. Its cold blue eyes found him.

"My King is modest," it said. "That is fitting. The greatest kings are modest." It bowed its head again. "But my King broke me. My King held the space with nothing and pulled my head back with nothing and let the knight's blade through my throat." It trembled. "Only a king could do that. *My* king."

"My name is Mau."

"My King is called Mau," said Beru, as if filing it, exactly the way Igris had. "It is a beautiful name. A king's name. Short. Strong. The name of a king who does not need a long name because his deeds are long enough—"

"Please stop calling me king."

Beru considered this.

"My Liege-King," it said.

"That's worse."

"My Lord-King-Liege—"

"*Mau*."

There was a silence.

"My King," said Beru firmly, "is called Mau."

* * *

Behind him, Gojo laughed.

He could not help it. It came out of him all at once, crouched on the floor of the hive beside Yuta. Yuta, after a moment, started laughing too, very quietly, into his good hand. Mau sat on the earth too tired to stand, with Frieren holding his arm and a shadow kneeling in front of him calling him king, and found that he was laughing as well. Not much. But laughing.

Beru looked at all of them with its cold blue eyes.

"My King's companions are joyful," it said. "That is fitting." Then it saw Frieren.

It stopped.

It looked at her for a long time. At her standing beside Mau, close, with her shoulder against his, holding his arm. At her staff. At her face.

Its head tilted. The way an insect's head tilts.

"Who," said Beru slowly, "is this one?"

Frieren looked back at it.

"Who stands," said Beru, "at my King's side? Where the King's guard should stand?" It looked at the place where her shoulder touched Mau's. "Close. Very close. Closer than a guard." It was quiet for a moment. "And I know you." Its voice changed. "You are the one who put the white light through my shoulder. You are the one who took my arm."

"Yes," said Frieren.

Beru looked at her for a very long time.

"That was a very great spell," it said at last. Slowly. Grudgingly. As if it did not want to say it. "For an elf."

Frieren's eyebrows went up a fraction.

"For an elf," she said.

"For anyone," Beru corrected itself, very fast. "For anyone at all. I have never felt anything like it. It went through me as if I were paper." It bowed its head, slightly, toward her. Only slightly. "I acknowledge it."

Then it lifted its head again, and its cold blue eyes went to where her shoulder touched Mau's.

"But the place at my King's side," said Beru, "is the place of his guard. His champion. His strongest." It paused. "I will speak with you about it. Later. When my King is rested."

Frieren looked at it.

"Will you," she said.

"I will," said Beru.

Frieren did not say anything else. But Mau, sitting on the earth with her hand under his arm, felt her fingers tighten. Very slightly. The way they tightened when she had found a grimoire she intended to read, whatever it cost.

* * *

"*Back*," said Mau.

"My King!" said Beru, and went down into the earth all at once, the cold blue lights last.

The drain stopped.

There was nothing in the cup. Almost nothing. A thin layer on the floor of it. So thin he could barely feel it. But it was there.

*Floor 40 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30 or 40.*

*Level 42. Sixty-four points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 5. Places: 10.*

He read it out. All of it. Then he did not say anything else for a while.

"Five," said Gojo.

"Five."

"One of them's in pieces."

"Tank," said Mau. "Ciel says I can remake him. Later. When there's something in the cup."

"And one of them," said Gojo, "is going to *speak* with Frieren. *Later*." He looked at Frieren with his bare eyes and an expression of enormous, delighted interest. "About the place at your side."

Frieren did not look at him.

"I heard," she said.

"Are you going to—"

"I'm going to take Mau home," said Frieren, "and put him to bed, and tell Shoko, and sit with Yuta while Maki looks at his arm. And then it's the seventh night." She looked at Gojo. "Later is later."

Gojo looked at her for a long moment.

"I'm going to be there," he said. "For *later*. I'm going to bring a chair."

* * *

The roof that night was short.

Mau could not go up. His legs would not take a ladder. Shoko had bound his back and both calves and said *bed, three days*, and meant it.

So the roof came down.

At the hour, the hatch did not squeak. Instead there was a knock on the east-room door, and Yuta came in with the tray and five cups, and Rimuru behind him, and Taihei, and Gojo last, pretending he had always been going to. They sat on the floor round the bed. Frieren moved the reading chair to make room. Through the connecting door, Ori pretended to be asleep and listened to every word.

"It's not the roof," said Rimuru. "There's no draught."

"I'll open the window," said Taihei.

He opened the window. There was a draught. Rimuru sat in it, with dignity. Mau drank whatever Yuta called tea, propped up on the pillows, and did not say very much. Nobody asked him to.

Yuta had a new scar. A long pink line from his shoulder to his elbow, healed, still tender. Maki had looked at it in the clinic for a long time without saying anything. Then she had put her hand flat on it and said, "Two," and Yuta had said, "Two," and that was all.

Gojo told the story. Not the real version. Not even the exaggerated version. A third version, in which the Ant King had been very rude about elves and was going to regret it.

"It said *for an elf*," said Gojo, with enormous satisfaction. "To *Frieren*. After she put a Zoltraak through it."

Rimuru put his face in his hands.

"It's going to challenge her," said Gojo. "It said so. *Later.* About standing next to Mau." He leaned back against the end of the bed and looked at the ceiling. "I've been alive a very long time, in a manner of speaking, and I have never been so excited about anything as I am about *later*."

Taihei, by the draught, looked at Mau.

"Is that true?" he said. "The shadow's going to fight Frieren?"

Mau looked past Taihei, out of the open window. Low over the hills, a little apart, the small steady one. Still here.

"I think," he said slowly, "it's going to try."

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Taihei said, very quietly, "I'll make breakfast. Early. For whoever's watching."

And Gojo laughed so hard he nearly fell over sideways into the reading chair, and Yuta caught him by the back of the coat with his good arm. The five of them sat in the east room in the warm dark, with the window open for Rimuru's draught, until Shoko knocked on the door and sent them all to bed.
