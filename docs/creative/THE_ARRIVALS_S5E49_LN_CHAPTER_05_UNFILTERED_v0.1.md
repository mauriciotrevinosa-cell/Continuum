*When: Day 361, floor 40, the heart of the hive. The same breath. Yuta's blood is on the packed earth where the tower took him. Igris has a hole through his chest and is still standing. At the edge, Gojo's hand is on Frieren's wrist.*

The world came in.

All of it.

It did not come in through his eyes. That was the first thing he understood, and it was the last clear thing for a long time. It came in through everything. Through the skin of his face, through his teeth, through the back of his skull. The filter had been a door kept almost shut for a year, and Ciel had opened it all the way, and what was on the other side was not a room.

It was everything.

The packed earth of the hive. Every grain of it. He could see every grain. Not see. *Know.* The weight of it, the damp of it, the way it had been pressed down by a thousand thousand ant feet over a thousand years. The air. Every mote in the air, turning in Frieren's lights. The lights. Not light any more. Threads of mana, pulled out of her staff in a shape she had learned a thousand years ago from her teacher, and he could see the shape, every turn of it, every knot.

Igris. Not a knight. A shape of cold dark standing on the earth with a hole through the middle of it, and a thread running from the hole down into the ground, into Mau's own shadow, into the cup. His. He could see that it was his. And the place where Yuta had been: a smear of warmth on the air, fading, the last of a furnace, the ghost of a hand the size of a door. His nose started to bleed.

Gojo.

Gojo was looking at him.

Gojo's eyes were open, bare, and Gojo's eyes were what his eyes were trying to be, and for one moment the two of them looked at each other across the dark chamber, the real thing and the copy, and Gojo's face went white.

"*Mau*," said Gojo. "Mau, *don't*—"

The Ant King.

* * *

He saw it before it moved.

That was what the eyes were for. That was what Gojo's eyes had always been for, and why nobody could ever touch him. Not faster. *Earlier.* Before a body moves, something in it moves first. The energy. The intention. A tightening in the threads, a hand's breadth ahead of the muscle. A fraction of a second. Less.

Enough.

The Ant King turned from him toward the edge. Toward Frieren. Mau saw the threads in its legs tighten toward her a fraction before the legs did.

Mau was on his feet.

He did not remember standing. His legs were torn. He was standing on them. Blood was running out of his eyes now, not tears, blood, warm down both cheeks, and his head was burning from the inside, a white-hot wire drawn through the middle of it from one temple to the other, and it did not matter, because he could see.

*Mau,* said Ciel. Very far away. *Your head. I'm holding what I can. Three minutes. Maybe less. Then I'm putting it back whether you say yes or not.*

*Three minutes.*

* * *

Okarun's speed.

Not the way he had used it before. Not ten paces of fire and the shape of it learned by a thing that watched shapes. One heartbeat. Exactly one. Into his legs and out again, before the pain could finish arriving.

He was between the Ant King and Frieren.

It was already reaching. He had seen it reach before it reached. Its claws came at him, and he did not put up the space, and he did not move.

He put out his left arm.

On twenty, against Igris, he had left a gap at his hip, on purpose, so that he knew where the sword would go. A lesson, Gojo had called it afterward, furious. *You let him cut you so you'd know where.*

He let the Ant King cut him so he would know where.

The claws went into his forearm. Through it. He felt them go between the bones. And he closed his hand, and turned his arm, and the claws were caught, held in the meat of him the way Igris's sword had been held in its armour a minute ago.

The Ant King screamed and pulled.

It could not pull. Not at once. For one heartbeat it was held.

He did not call anyone. There was nobody he was allowed to call.

The pull. His own. Not Gojo's marble: the small hard knot of drawing-in that Gojo had been teaching him in the yard for a year. He put it exactly where he saw the threads bunch, behind the left shoulder, a hand left of the spine, where the armour was thinnest and the weight was. It pulled.

The Ant King's left side came back toward it. Its left arm came up away from its body, the joint opening.

"*Igris. The joint.*"

The knight was already moving.

He had not needed to tell him. He would think about that afterward. Igris had a hole through his chest and should not have been standing, and he had seen the joint open in the same instant Mau had, and the long sword was already coming round, and Mau's voice and the blade arrived together.

Dark. Heavy. Through the left shoulder, through the armour, through the joint.

The Ant King's left arm fell to the packed earth.

* * *

It tore its claws out of his forearm.

He let them go. The arm was ruined anyway. He felt it go, the claws coming out the way they had gone in, and the blood after them.

And it went for her.

He had known it would. She was standing at the edge where the notice had put her, not allowed to lift a hand against it, and it knew that. He had seen it on the stair, at the very start, when it looked at her for the first time. He had seen it on the road, in another way, in another month. *The elf was weak.* He had seen it in a thousand loops in a Trial that had taught him, over and over, that this was the moment, this one, the one where it went for her and he was one second too slow.

He saw the threads in its legs tighten toward her a hand's breadth before the legs.

He put the space in front of her.

All of it. Thick and hard and flat, between Frieren and the thing coming at her. He had seen exactly where it would arrive. He put the space exactly there. Not a wall across the room. A plate the size of a face, a finger's width from hers.

The Ant King hit it.

It stopped. Dead. A finger's width from her face.

She did not flinch.

She did not loose the spell in her hand, either. He saw it there, with the eyes, white and tight and older than anything else in the hive, held. He saw what it cost her to hold it.

* * *

Behind it, there was food.

Not much. A few soldier-ant remains along the far wall, the ones he had missed. Its head was already turning toward them. To eat. To heal the arm. To grow it back. He saw the threads in its neck turn toward the wall.

*Momo's.*

*Mau—your head—*

*All of them.*

He reached into the kitchen. Not for six. Not for ten. For every single one. Every scrap of soldier-ant left on the floor of the hive, along every wall, in every corner his eyes could see, which was all of them. He could see every one. He took them all at once, the wrong-handed way, the way it felt like a voice not his own coming out of his mouth, and *threw* them. Up. Out. Into the mouths of the side tunnels, all round the chamber, into the dark, every one, gone.

Something in his head tore.

He felt it. Like a thread snapping. Behind his left eye.

*Mau.* Ciel. *Mau. One minute. You're bleeding inside. I'm putting it back in one minute.*

The floor of the hive was clean. Nothing on it. Nothing to eat. Nothing anywhere.

The Ant King turned its head toward the walls and saw nothing.

It turned back to him.

And for the first time since the stair, Mau saw something in its threads he had not seen there before. Not hunger.

Fear.

It was the first time anything in the tower had been afraid of him.

It did not last.

He watched it go. He watched the fear in its threads turn over, the way a coal turns over in a fire and shows its red underside, and become something else. Something worse. The hunger had been for food. This was not for food.

This was for him.

*It's stopped wanting to win,* said Ciel. Very quiet. *It wants to kill you. Mau. That's different.* A pause. *Two minutes.*

The Ant King's one arm came up.

Its jaws opened, wider than jaws should open.

And it came.
