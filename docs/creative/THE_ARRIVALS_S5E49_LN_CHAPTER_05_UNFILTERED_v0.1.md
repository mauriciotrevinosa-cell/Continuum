*When: Day 361, floor 40, the heart of the hive. The same breath. Yuta's sword is going round and round. Igris has a hole through his chest and is still standing.*

The world came in.

All of it.

It did not come in through his eyes. That was the first thing he understood, and it was the last clear thing for a long time. It came in through everything. Through the skin of his face, through his teeth, through the back of his skull. The filter had been a door kept almost shut for a year, and Ciel had opened it all the way, and what was on the other side was not a room.

It was everything.

The packed earth of the hive. Every grain of it. He could see every grain. Not see. *Know.* The weight of it, the damp of it, the way it had been pressed down by a thousand thousand ant feet over a thousand years. The air. Every mote in the air, turning in Frieren's lights. The lights. Not light any more. Threads of mana, pulled out of her staff in a shape she had learned a thousand years ago from her teacher, and he could see the shape, every turn of it, every knot.

Yuta. Not Yuta. A furnace. Cursed energy pouring off him like heat off a road, dark and warm and enormous, and behind him, wrapped round him, *in* him, something vaster, something that loved him so much it had become a curse to do it. Rika. He had never seen her. He had felt her. He saw her now. She looked at him through the furnace and he looked back and his nose started to bleed.

Gojo.

Gojo was looking at him.

Gojo's eyes were open, bare, and Gojo's eyes were what his eyes were trying to be, and for one moment the two of them looked at each other across the dark chamber, the real thing and the copy, and Gojo's face went white.

"*Mau*," said Gojo. "Mau, *don't*—"

The Ant King.

* * *

He saw it before it moved.

That was what the eyes were for. That was what Gojo's eyes had always been for, and why nobody could ever touch him. Not faster. *Earlier.* Before a body moves, something in it moves first. The energy. The intention. A tightening in the threads, a hand's breadth ahead of the muscle. A fraction of a second. Less.

Enough.

The Ant King turned from Yuta toward Frieren. Mau saw the threads in its legs tighten toward her a fraction before the legs did.

"*Yuta. Back. Two steps. Left.*"

Yuta went back. Two steps. Left. He did not ask why. The claws went through the place he had been.

Mau was on his feet.

He did not remember standing. His legs were torn. He was standing on them. Blood was running out of his eyes now, not tears, blood, warm down both cheeks, and his head was burning from the inside, a white-hot wire drawn through the middle of it from one temple to the other, and it did not matter, because he could see.

*Mau,* said Ciel. Very far away. *Your head. I'm holding what I can. Three minutes. Maybe less. Then I'm putting it back whether you say yes or not.*

*Three minutes.*

* * *

Okarun's speed.

Not the way he had used it before. Not ten paces of fire and the shape of it learned by a thing that watched shapes. One heartbeat. Exactly one. Into his legs and out again, before the pain could finish arriving.

He was between the Ant King and Frieren.

It was already reaching. He had seen it reach before it reached. Its claws came at him, and he did not put up the space, and he did not move.

He put out his left arm.

On twenty, against Igris, he had left a gap at his hip, on purpose, so that he knew where the sword would go. A lesson, Gojo had called it afterward, furious. *You let him cut you so you'd know where.*

He let the Ant King cut him so he would know where.

The claws went into his forearm. Through it. He felt them go between the bones. And he closed his hand, and turned his arm, and the claws were caught, held in the meat of him the way Igris's sword had been held in its armour a minute ago.

The Ant King screamed and pulled.

It could not pull. Not at once. For one heartbeat it was held.

"*Gojo. Behind it. A hand left of the spine. Now.*"

The marble went. Exactly where he said. Not past it, this time. Into the place he had seen the threads bunch, behind the left shoulder, where the armour was thinnest and the weight was. It pulled.

The Ant King's left side came back toward the marble. Its left arm came up off the floor, away from its body, the joint opening.

"*Frieren. The shoulder. The joint. Now.*"

She was already firing.

He had not needed to tell her. He would think about that afterward. She had seen it too. Not with eyes like his. With a thousand years of watching things try to kill her. She had seen the joint open and her staff was already turning, and his voice and her Zoltraak arrived at the same moment.

White. Narrow. Clean. Through the left shoulder. Through the armour, through the joint, out the other side.

The Ant King's left arm hung.

* * *

It tore its claws out of his forearm.

He let them go. The arm was ruined anyway. He felt it go, the claws coming out the way they had gone in, and the blood after them.

And it went for her.

He had known it would. He had seen it on the stair, at the very start, when it looked at her for the first time. He had seen it on the road, in another way, in another month. *The elf was weak.* He had seen it in a thousand loops in a Trial that had taught him, over and over, that this was the moment, this one, the one where it went for her and he was one second too slow.

He saw the threads in its legs tighten toward her a hand's breadth before the legs.

He put the space in front of her.

All of it. Thick and hard and flat, between Frieren and the thing coming at her. He had seen exactly where it would arrive. He put the space exactly there. Not a wall across the room. A plate the size of a face, a finger's width from hers.

The Ant King hit it.

It stopped. Dead. A finger's width from her face.

She did not flinch.

* * *

Behind it, there was food.

Not much. A few soldier-ant remains along the far wall, the ones he had missed. Its head was already turning toward them. To eat. To heal the arm. To grow it back. He saw the threads in its neck turn toward the wall.

*Momo's.*

*Mau—your head—*

*All of them.*

He reached into the kitchen. Not for six. Not for ten. For every single one. Every scrap of soldier-ant left on the floor of the hive, along every wall, in every corner his eyes could see, which was all of them. He could see every one. He took them all at once, the wrong-handed way, the way it felt like a voice not his own coming out of his mouth, and *threw* them. Up. Out. Into the mouths of the side tunnels, all round the chamber, into the dark, every one, gone.

Something in his head tore.

He felt it. Like a thread snapping. Behind his left eye.

*Mau.* Ciel. *Mau. One minute. You're bleeding inside. I'm putting it back in one minute.*

The floor of the hive was clean. Nothing on it. Nothing to eat. Nothing anywhere.

The Ant King turned its head toward the walls and saw nothing.

It turned back to him.

And for the first time since the stair, Mau saw something in its threads he had not seen there before. Not hunger.

Fear.

* * *

It came at him.

Everything it had. One arm, the claws of it, the jaws. It came at him faster than anything he had ever seen move, and he saw every movement of it before it made it, and he did not get out of the way of all of them.

He could not. His legs were gone. His left arm was gone. He was standing in the middle of the dark hive with blood running out of his eyes and his nose and his ears now, and he saw everything, and he could not move fast enough to avoid everything he saw.

So he chose.

The claws across his ribs, he let through. They opened his side. He had chosen that one; it put the claw where he wanted it. The jaws at his throat he turned with the space, a slant, a hand's breadth, and they closed on his shoulder instead, the same shoulder, the bite going in where the last one had been, to the bone. He had chosen that too. It held the jaws where he wanted them. Its head was against his chest. Its back was to everything.

Its left shoulder, where Frieren's Zoltraak had gone through, was a hand's breadth from his right hand.

"*Igris,*" said Mau. "*The throat. When I say.*"

Igris was behind it. Broken. A hole through his chest. On his feet.

*"My liege."*

Okarun's speed. One heartbeat. Into his right arm, not his legs. Fire from the shoulder to the fingers.

He put his right hand into the wound.

Into the place where the Zoltraak had gone through the armour, through the joint, into the open inside of the Ant King. His hand went in to the wrist. Into the heat of it.

And he made the point. The smallest point he had ever made. The way Gojo made the marble. The way the space and the pull were the same thing turned inside out. Not outside it. Inside it. In the middle of the Ant King's chest, where every thread in its body met, where he could see them all meet.

"*Now.*"

And pulled.

* * *

The Ant King folded.

From the inside. Its chest came in round his hand, toward the point, all of it at once, armour and threads and the hunger at the centre of it, with a sound Mau would hear for the rest of his life. Its head came back off his shoulder, the jaws tearing out of him, its throat open, arched—

Igris's sword went through it. At the word. In the same instant. Exactly where Mau had seen the throat would be.

The threads went out.

All of them. Every one. He saw them go. Like lamps going out down a long corridor, one after another, all the way to the end.

The Ant King fell.

* * *

*Mau. Now. I'm putting it back. Now.*

*Yes.*

The filter came down.

The world went away. Not dark. Red. Everything red, and blurred, and very far off, and the burning wire through the middle of his head going from white to red to something that was only very, very heavy. The grains of the floor went. The motes went. Frieren's threads, Yuta's furnace, Rika's terrible loving face. Gojo's eyes. All of it gone, all at once, like a door slammed shut on a storm.

He could not see.

He was on the floor. He did not remember falling. He was lying on the packed earth of the hive on his back, and the red over everything was his own blood in his eyes, and he could not see through it. Shapes. Light, somewhere. Dark. A shape moving fast toward him.

*Ciel.*

*I'm here.* Her voice was shaking. *I'm here. I've got you. The bleeding inside your head stopped when the filter went back. It stopped. You're not dying.* A pause. *You were. For about forty seconds. You're not now.*

*Frieren.*

*She's alive. She's not hurt. She's running to you.*

A shape. Cold hands on his face. Both of them. One on each side.

"Mau." Frieren's voice. Very close. Not level at all. "*Mau.* Can you see me?"

"No," said Mau.

He felt her hands go still on his face.

"Red," he said. "Everything's red. I can't—" He breathed. It hurt. Everything hurt. Every single part of him that he could feel was open or torn or bitten through. "I'm sorry. I can't see you."

"You don't have to see me," said Frieren. "I'm here." Her thumbs moved, very carefully, under his eyes. Wiping. "I'm here. You stopped it. A finger's width. You stopped it."

"I saw it," said Mau. "Before it moved. I saw it."

"I know."

"I saw everything."

"I know," said Frieren. "Gojo saw you see it." Her voice did something. "He's sitting down. He sat down when it fell. He can't get up."

Somewhere off in the red, Mau heard Gojo say something. He could not hear what. It did not sound like a joke.

* * *

The notice came up.

He could not read it. It hung somewhere in the red in front of him, pale shapes, and he could not make them into letters.

"What does it say?" he said.

Frieren looked. He felt her look.

"*Guardian defeated,*" she read. Very quietly. "*Operator: trial passed.*"

A pause.

"*Shadow extraction available.*"
