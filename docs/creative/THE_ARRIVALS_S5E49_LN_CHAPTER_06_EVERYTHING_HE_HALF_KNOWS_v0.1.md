*When: Day 361, floor 40. The heart of the hive. Two minutes on Ciel's count. The food is gone down the side tunnels. Beru has one arm, and has stopped being hungry for anything but him.*

He saw it coming before it came.

That was all the eyes gave him. Not speed. *Earlier.* The threads in its legs tightening, its weight shifting forward onto the toes, the jaw unhinging a fraction before the leap. He saw all of it in the time it takes to blink.

And he saw what was in his own walls.

That was the thing nobody had warned him about. Not Ciel, not Gojo, not the guardrails pinned inside his door. With the filter gone, the eyes did not only look outward. They looked *in*. Down through the walls, into the place where the Noise kept everything it had ever watched, and he saw it all laid out like tools on a bench. Not folded. Not dark. Every pattern he had half-learned in a year, and every line of how it was put together.

The space. The pull. Momo's kettle. Okarun's legs.

Gojo's red, the one that pushed instead of pulled.

Frieren's white line, the spell that a demon had made and people had stolen back.

A blue and a red, side by side, and the shape of what happened when you made them touch, from a memory of Yuta's of a city at night.

And deeper, at the bottom, something he did not look at. Something shaped like a building. With mouths.

The Ant King leapt.

* * *

*Red.*

He had made it once before. Small. In a red gate, at a dragon's eye. It had cost him a day of headache. He had never made it again.

He made it now.

Not small. Not careful. He saw how it was built, every thread of it, Gojo's technique turned inside out, the pull reversed into a push. He built it in his right palm in the half-second between the Ant King's toes leaving the floor and its jaws arriving, and he put his palm flat against its chest as it hit him.

And let go.

The Ant King went backward across the hive the way a stone goes out of a sling. Across the open middle, past Yuta, past the place where Tank had come apart, into the far wall of packed earth so hard the wall caved round it and closed over it and it disappeared into the dark.

Mau went backward too. The other way. Ten paces, on his back, across the floor, with his right hand on fire from the wrist down and something in his shoulder that went *pop*.

He got up.

*That cost the other reserve,* said Ciel. *A quarter of it. In one push.* A pause. *Mau. It's getting up.*

It was. The wall bulged and split and the Ant King came out of it, earth pouring off its black plates, one arm, purple-red eyes. It did not shake itself. It did not pause. It looked across the hive at him and came again.

* * *

*Zoltraak.*

He had watched her cast it a thousand times. The white line. The demon's spell made human. He had never once let it out on purpose.

He let it out.

It did not come out of a staff. He did not have a staff. It came out of the end of his right forefinger, which was already burned from the Red, and it was not white and clean like hers. It was narrow and crooked, the colour of something that had been white once and had been left in the sun too long. Wrong-handed. Wrong-everything. It hurt coming out the way a splinter hurts coming out.

It went through the Ant King's left thigh.

Not the way hers went through things. It did not go clean. It burned in and stuck and burned out the other side, and the leg buckled, and the Ant King went down on one knee halfway across the hive.

From somewhere behind him, he heard Frieren draw breath.

"*That's mine*," she said. Very quietly. Not angry. Something else. "*He's casting mine.*"

He did not look round.

The Ant King was looking at its leg.

At the hole in it. Smoking.

Then it looked at him.

And Mau, with the eyes that saw everything, saw what it was going to do a full heartbeat before it did it, and could not stop it, because it was faster than his body even when it was not faster than his sight.

It came across the hive on three limbs. Not at his face. Not at his throat. At his left shoulder. At the place it had bitten before.

The jaws closed.

* * *

He felt it eat.

Not bite. Eat. The jaws closed on the old wound and worked, the way a jaw works when it is chewing, and something went out of his shoulder into its mouth, and he felt it swallow.

He saw its leg.

The hole his Zoltraak had burned through it. Closing. The black plates growing back over it while it chewed, the way they had grown back over Frieren's light when it ate its soldiers.

There was no food left in the hive.

So it was eating him.

* * *

"*Rika*," said Yuta.

A hand the size of a door came out of the air beside him, pale and too-jointed, and closed round the Ant King's head, and pulled.

The jaws tore out of his shoulder. Something came with them. He did not look at what.

Rika held the Ant King up in the air by its head, at the full length of her terrible arm, and it thrashed, and its one claw raked her wrist, and she did not let go. She looked at Mau. The look Yuta had told him about. *To see where he was hurt.*

*Companions may assist.*

She could not finish it. He knew that. She knew it. She could hold it.

She held it.

Gojo's marble went past it, close, and pulled, and its thrashing went the wrong way.

Frieren's light went through its other thigh. Clean. White. Hers.

And Mau, on his knees on the packed earth with his shoulder open to the bone again and his right hand burned and his head a white roar, looked at the bottom of the bench of tools in his walls.

At a blue, and a red.

* * *

*Ciel. Purple.*

*You can't.* Her voice came out of him like a cry. *Mau, you can't, you built that once, in the second season, with Yuta's memory and everything you had, and it nearly took you with it. Your left hand doesn't work. Your left hand has holes in it.*

*I can see how it's built.*

*Seeing isn't—*

*I can see how it's built,* said Mau. *It's two things. I know both. I just have to make them touch.*

A long silence. Not long. Less than a breath.

*One minute,* said Ciel. Very small. *After this, one minute. Then the filter goes back whatever you say.*

He held up his left hand.

There were holes in it. Three, through the forearm, where the claws had gone between the bones. The fingers would not close properly. He did not need them to close. He needed them to hold.

Blue. In the left palm. The pull. The marble, Gojo's marble, but his own, the size of a thumbnail, and he saw every thread of it and held it in a hand that was bleeding through its own palm.

Red. In the right palm. The push. Burned hand, burned fingers. A smaller one than before. He could not afford the size of the last one.

He brought his hands together in front of his chest.

* * *

Across the hive, Rika's hand opened.

Not because she meant to. Because the Ant King had stopped thrashing and *twisted*, all at once, the way a cat twists, and its one claw went into her thumb and she let go for a quarter of a heartbeat, and a quarter of a heartbeat was enough. It dropped. It hit the floor already moving. Not at Rika. Not at Yuta. Not at Frieren.

At him.

It had seen his hands.

It had learned the shape of the Red already. It knew what a thing in his palm meant. It came low and fast and to the left, his left, the side with the ruined arm, where it knew his aim would be worst.

And Mau saw it. Before it moved. Where it would be. Not where it was.

He did not aim at the Ant King.

He aimed at the place it was going to be in a third of a second.

The blue and the red touched.

* * *

It was not loud.

That was the thing he would remember. He had expected thunder. It made no sound at all. A ball of something that was not light and not dark, purple, the colour of a bruise on the inside of the world, went out from between his palms across the hive, and where it went, there was nothing.

Not cut. Not burned. Gone. A line of nothing as wide as a man, from his hands across the packed earth floor to the far wall, and through the far wall, and through whatever was behind the far wall, and on, into the dark of the tunnels, further than he could see even with the eyes.

The Ant King had been in the place it was going to be.

A third of its right side went with the purple. The plates. The flesh under the plates. Most of the hip. Gone, clean, a bite out of the shape of it as if the world had taken one.

It screamed.

And the backlash took Mau off his feet.

He went backward across the floor of the hive and hit the packed earth and rolled and lay on his back, and the eyes showed him every mote of dust falling on him out of the hole Purple had torn in the roof, and he could not feel his left arm at all, and his right hand was not a hand any more, it was a burn, and blood was coming out of his nose and his mouth and his ears.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *Mau. Mau. Forty seconds. Your head is bleeding inside. Forty seconds, then I'm putting it back, I don't care, I'm putting it—*

*It's not dead.*

* * *

It was not dead.

It was crawling.

It came across the floor of the hive on one arm and one leg, with a third of its side gone and the purple-red eyes fixed on him and the jaws open, dragging what was left. Not fast now. Not fast at all. But coming. Because it was Beru. Because it had been the King of this hive, and had eaten and eaten and never been full, and had decided, at the end, that the thing it wanted most in all its hunger was the boy lying on its floor.

Frieren fired. It took the light in its back and kept crawling.

Gojo's marble pulled at it. It leaned against the pull and kept crawling.

Rika's hand came down. It went under the hand, flattening itself against the floor, and kept crawling.

It reached him.

It came up over his legs, over his chest, its weight on him, cold and black and broken, and its jaws came down at his throat, and he turned his head, and they closed on his shoulder again. The same shoulder. The same wound. And it began to eat.

And the hole in its side began, very slowly, to close.

* * *

*Twenty seconds,* said Ciel. She was not saying it any more. She was screaming it. *Twenty. Mau. Let go. Let go of everything, I'll put it back, Gojo can—*

*Companions may assist.*

*Companions may not carry.*

He did not let go.

He brought his arms up. Both of them. The left one he could not feel and the right one that was a burn. He put them round the Ant King. Round its back, its broken side, its one shoulder. He held it against his chest, the way you hold someone you will not let fall, and its jaws were in him and it was eating him and he held it closer.

"*Not finished*," he said. Into its armour. Through blood. "*Neither of us.*"

And under him, under the floor of the hive, under the walls, at the very bottom of the bench of tools, the thing he had not looked at opened its eyes.

He felt the walls give.

Not break. *Open.* The way a door opens, from the inside, onto a room he had never once let himself go into.

Something came up out of the dark under the two of them.

It was shaped like a building.

It had mouths.
