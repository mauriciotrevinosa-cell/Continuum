## Chapter 51 — My King

*When: Day 361, the heart of the hive on forty. The Ant King lies broken on the packed earth. Under it, the dark is gathering, deeper than anything yet. The cup is low. Not empty. Mau cannot see any of it.*

*Can I?* he said.

Ciel did not answer at once.

He felt her look. At the cup, five and a half times deep, with not very much left in the bottom of it after Igris, broken and still fighting. At the thing he used for the space and the pull, empty. At the broken shape of Tank, in pieces, waiting. At him.

*Yes,* she said at last. *Barely. It'll take almost everything that's left. You won't be able to call anything else out for two days. Maybe three. You'll be very, very tired.* A pause. *And it's hungry, Mau. Even now. I can feel it in the depth. It was the hungriest thing on any floor. If you raise it, it'll be yours. It'll still be hungry.*

*Hungry for what?*

*I don't know,* said Ciel. *That's the honest answer. I don't know what a shadow is hungry for. We'll find out.*

He turned his head toward the others. Shapes in the red. He knew them by where they were.

Gojo was still crouched beside Yuta. Yuta was sitting on the floor of the hive with his arm across his knees, the long cut closing pink. Frieren was beside Mau, so close her shoulder was against his.

"Ciel says I can," he said. "Barely. And it'll still be hungry."

Gojo looked at the Ant King. Then at Frieren. Then at Mau.

"It went for her," he said. "At the end. Not to eat. Not to heal. It went straight for her." He paused. "After she took its arm."

"I know."

"You're going to raise a thing that went straight for her."

Mau turned his face toward Frieren. He could not see her. He could feel her shoulder.

Frieren looked at him. She did not say anything for a long moment.

"It's not the same thing," she said. "After." She looked at the Ant King. "Lantern tried to kill us at the high pass. Igris cut you on twenty. The bear nearly took Stark's head off." She paused. "They're not what they were. They're what you ask them to be."

"You're already sitting down," said Gojo. "Stay there. You couldn't get up if you tried."

That was true. His legs had not answered him since the last heartbeat of Okarun's speed. His back was three long burning lines under the torn coat. His left shoulder was a hole. Both his forearms were wrapped in strips of Frieren's coat, and Yuta's, and the blood was coming through all of them. And over everything, red, the blood in his eyes, and behind it nothing. He stayed where he was, on the packed earth, with Frieren's hand under his arm.

* * *

He thought about what he was asking.

It was harder than the others. The dragon had been fierce. The knight had been honourable. The bear had been an animal. This had been hungry. It had eaten its own soldiers to heal and eaten Tank to grow. And at the very end, with its arm gone, it had decided something, and gone for the person he loved most in any world, and stopped a finger's width from her face.

He did not need to be angrier than it had been. He knew that.

He did not know what to ask it.

He sat on the packed earth beside the broken black shape that he could not see, with Frieren's hand guiding his to the edge of it, and thought.

*You were hungry,* he thought, at where Frieren said its eyes were. *You ate to get bigger. To be more. Because that's all there was in here.* He did not need to see the chamber. He had seen every grain of it, for three minutes. The walls. The places the food had been. *There's more than eating out there. There's a house. People in it. People I'd stand in front of anything for.*

He turned toward Frieren, beside him.

*If you're going to be hungry,* he thought, *be hungry to keep them. Not to eat them. To keep them.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the earth like a well overflowing.

It came up through the Ant King. Through the black armour, the long limbs, the broken shoulder, the head pulled back. It did not deepen the black, because the black was already black. It changed it. The shine went out of it. The bright purple-red went out of the eyes. And the whole long broken shape drew itself together, the way water draws itself into a drop.

The left arm came back.

Mau felt the cost.

It took everything. Almost everything. The little that was left in the bottom of the cup went out of it like the last water out of a tipped jar. He felt his whole body go heavy, sitting on the earth. Heavier than after the bear. He put a hand flat on the ground to stay upright. Frieren's hand came under his arm and held it.

The thing stood up.

Tall. Thin. Long-limbed. Upright on two legs. Dark now, all of it, armour and claws and jaws, with no shine on it at all. Where the purple-red eyes had been there were two small cold lights. Blue. Like the others.

"It's standing," said Frieren, very quietly, at his ear. "It's looking at you."

Then, she told him later, it went down on one knee.

Not slowly, like Igris. All at once. As if its legs had been cut out from under it by something it could not resist. It knelt on the packed earth in front of him with its head bowed so low its jaws nearly touched the ground. Its claws were spread flat on the earth on either side of it.

And it spoke.

"*My King!*"

* * *

The voice filled the chamber.

It was not dry and level like Igris's. It was high and fierce and trembling with something that might have been joy or might have been tears or might have been both. It went up and down. It did not echo. It did not need to.

"*My King!*" it said again. "I, Beru, who was the King of this hive, who ate and ate and was never full, who would have devoured the world if the world had come up the stair—I kneel! I kneel before the one who broke me! I am yours! My claws are yours! My jaws are yours! My hunger is yours! *Command me!*"

Nobody said anything.

Gojo, crouched beside Yuta, had his mouth slightly open.

Yuta had stopped healing his arm.

Frieren, holding Mau up, went very still.

"I'm not a king," said Mau.

The thing lifted its head. Its cold blue eyes found him.

"My King is modest," it said. "That is fitting. The greatest kings are modest." It bowed its head again. "But my King broke me. My King *saw* me. Before I moved. Every time." It trembled. "He let me into his own arm to hold me. He put his hand inside me and pulled me in from the middle and let the knight's blade through my throat." It trembled harder. "Only a king could do that. *My* king."

"My name is Mau."

"My King is called Mau," said Beru, as if filing it, exactly the way Igris had. "It is a beautiful name. A king's name. Short. Strong. The name of a king who does not need a long name because his deeds are long enough—"

"Please stop calling me king."

Beru considered this.

"My Liege-King," it said.

"That's worse."

"My Lord-King-Liege—"

"*Mau*."

There was a silence.

"My King," said Beru firmly, "is called Mau."

* * *

Behind him, Gojo laughed.

He could not help it. It came out of him all at once, crouched on the floor of the hive beside Yuta. Yuta, after a moment, started laughing too, very quietly, into his good hand. Mau sat on the earth too tired to stand, with Frieren holding his arm and a shadow kneeling in front of him calling him king, and found that he was laughing as well. Not much. But laughing.

Beru looked at all of them with its cold blue eyes.

"My King's companions are joyful," it said. "That is fitting." Then it saw Frieren.

It stopped.

It looked at her for a long time. At her standing beside Mau, close, with her shoulder against his, holding his arm. At her staff. At her face.

Its head tilted. The way an insect's head tilts.

"Who," said Beru slowly, "is this one?"

Frieren looked back at it.

"Who stands," said Beru, "at my King's side? Where the King's guard should stand?" It looked at the place where her shoulder touched Mau's. "Close. Very close. Closer than a guard." It was quiet for a moment. "And I know you." Its voice changed. "You are the one who put the white light through my shoulder. You are the one who took my arm."

"Yes," said Frieren.

Beru looked at her for a very long time.

"That was a very great spell," it said at last. Slowly. Grudgingly. As if it did not want to say it. "For an elf."

Frieren's eyebrows went up a fraction.

"For an elf," she said.

"For anyone," Beru corrected itself, very fast. "For anyone at all. I have never felt anything like it. It went through me as if I were paper." It bowed its head, slightly, toward her. Only slightly. "I acknowledge it."

Then it lifted its head again, and its cold blue eyes went to where her shoulder touched Mau's.

"But the place at my King's side," said Beru, "is the place of his guard. His champion. His strongest." It paused. "I will speak with you about it. Later. When my King is rested."

Frieren looked at it.

"Will you," she said.

"I will," said Beru.

Frieren did not say anything else. But Mau, sitting on the earth with her hand under his arm, felt her fingers tighten. Very slightly. The way they tightened when she had found a grimoire she intended to read, whatever it cost.

* * *

"*Back*," said Mau.

"My King!" said Beru, and went down into the earth all at once, the cold blue lights last.

Raising him had cost almost everything. There was nothing in the cup. Almost nothing. A thin layer on the floor of it. So thin he could barely feel it. But it was there.

*Floor 40 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30 or 40.*

*Level 42. Sixty-four points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 6. Places: 10.*

He read it out. All of it. Then he did not say anything else for a while.

"Six," said Gojo.

"Six."

"One of them's in pieces."

"Tank," said Mau. "Ciel says I can remake him. Later. When there's something in the cup."

"And one of them," said Gojo, "is going to *speak* with Frieren. *Later*." He looked at Frieren with his bare eyes and an expression of enormous, delighted interest. "About the place at your side."

Frieren did not look at him.

"I heard," she said.

"Are you going to—"

"I'm going to take Mau home," said Frieren, "and put him to bed, and tell Shoko, and sit with Yuta while Maki looks at his arm. And then it's the seventh night." She looked at Gojo. "Later is later."

Gojo looked at her for a long moment.

"I'm going to be there," he said. "For *later*. I'm going to bring a chair."

* * *

They came through the frame in the early afternoon.

Anko saw them first. She always saw them first. She was at the board with the pencil behind her ear, and the frame lit, and she turned round with the watch already in her hand.

She did not look at the watch.

Gojo came through first, sideways, because he was holding something up. Yuta came through on the other side of it, sideways too, his opened arm bound in a strip of his own coat, holding up the other half. Between them, with an arm over each of their shoulders and his feet not quite taking his weight, Mau.

He did not look like Mau.

That was what the yard would say afterward, the ones who said anything. He did not look like anyone. His head hung. His coat was gone; what was left of his shirt was not a colour any more. His left shoulder was bound in something dark and wet. Both forearms. His back, through the rags, in three long lines. His calves. And over his eyes, tied round his head, a strip of Frieren's coat, because he could not see and the light hurt, and under the strip, down both cheeks, dried and fresh, red.

Frieren came through last, with her staff in one hand and the other on his back, not holding him up, just there, touching him, as if he might stop being there if she let go.

He was saying something. Very quietly. Over and over. Anko could not hear it. Later Yuta told her. He was saying *I'm all right*. To nobody. With his head hanging, held up between two people, blind and bleeding through every bandage he had. *I'm all right. I'm all right.*

* * *

The yard stopped.

It was the middle of the afternoon. People were everywhere. Taihei at the kitchen door with a pot. Momo at the window. Marin and Wakana at the workshop with a door between them. Kessoku on the back step, Bocchi with her guitar across her knees. Seiko in her chair in the shade. Himmel at the gate. Rem hanging washing. The five at the long table by the window with a card game.

All of it stopped.

Momo's bowl hit the floor of the kitchen. Nobody heard it break. Taihei did not move from the doorway. At the long table, Karane stood up so fast her chair went over, and then nobody else stood up at all.

Ori was on the back step.

She had been waiting there since the morning. She always waited there. She saw him come through the frame, held up between Gojo and Yuta, with his head hanging and the cloth over his eyes, and she stood up, and Mau would never know what her face did, because he could not see it.

Maki was behind her. Maki put both hands on Ori's shoulders. Not to hold her back. To hold her up.

Ori did not run to him. She did not cry. She stood on the back step with Maki's hands on her shoulders, and watched them carry him across the dry ground to the clinic, and her mouth was moving. Maki bent down to hear.

She was counting his breaths.

* * *

Shoko was in the clinic door before they reached it.

She did not say anything. She looked at him once, all of him, and stepped aside, and they brought him in and laid him on the bed, and Shoko said "*out*," to everyone, not loudly, and everyone went out except Yuta, because Yuta's hands were already on Mau's calves, and Shoko looked at Yuta's own bound arm and said nothing and let him stay.

The door closed.

Frieren was standing outside it.

She stood on the dry ground in front of the clinic door with her staff in her hand. Then she sat down. Not on the step. On the ground. Straight down, all at once, the way a puppet goes down when the strings are cut, with the staff across her knees.

Fern was beside her before anyone saw her cross the yard.

Fern did not say anything. She sat down on the ground beside her teacher and put her arm round her, which she had not done since she was very small and Frieren had been the one doing it.

And Frieren cried.

Not quietly. Not the way she did things, slowly, a thousand years late. All at once. With her face against Fern's shoulder and her hands in the front of Fern's coat, the way a child cries, with her whole body, and the sound of it went across the yard, and everyone in the yard heard it, and nobody looked away.

"He didn't look alive," said Frieren. Into Fern's coat. In pieces. "Fern. When it let go of him. When it—it had its jaws in him, it was *eating* him, and then it stopped and I looked and he didn't look *alive*. He was lying there and his eyes were bleeding and he couldn't see me and he kept saying *I'm all right* and he didn't look—" She could not finish. "He didn't look like anyone. He looked like something that used to be him."

"He's alive," said Fern. Her own voice was not steady. "He's in there. Shoko has him."

"He took the filter off," said Frieren. "Ciel told Rimuru. He asked her to. Because it was going to come for me." She shook. "Because of the road. Because he waited one second on the road." She lifted her face. It was wet and it was wrecked and it was a face no one in the house had ever seen her wear. "He's going to keep doing it, Fern. Every floor. Every time. He's going to keep taking things off until there's nothing left to take off."

Fern did not have an answer.

She held her.

* * *

Inside the clinic, through the shut door, Shoko worked for three hours.

At the end of the three hours she came out. She looked at the yard. Half the house was still in it. Nobody had gone in for dinner. Taihei had brought the pot out and put it on the bench by the kitchen door, and nobody had eaten from it.

"He'll live," said Shoko.

She said it to Frieren. Frieren was still on the ground. She had stopped crying. She had not moved.

"His eyes will come back," said Shoko. "Two days. Maybe three. His back will scar. His shoulder will scar. I closed what I could and Yuta closed the rest and Yuta is now asleep on my floor and I'm not waking him." She looked at the yard. At all of them. "He'll live," she said again. Louder. For everyone.

Then, quietly, to Frieren only, as she turned back to the door:

"He's killing himself to get somewhere. And he feels better every time he gets closer."

* * *

The roof that night came down to the east room. It was short.

Mau could not go up. His legs would not take a ladder. His eyes would not take light. Shoko had bound his back and his shoulder and both forearms and both calves, and laid a folded wet cloth over his eyes, and said *bed, three days, and you don't take that cloth off until I say*, and meant it.

At the hour, there was a knock on the east-room door, and Yuta came in with the tray and five cups, grey, swaying, with his own arm bound. He had woken on Shoko's floor at dark and come anyway. Rimuru behind him. Taihei. Gojo last. Nobody pretended anything. They sat on the floor round the bed. Frieren did not move from the reading chair. Through the connecting door, Ori was not pretending to be asleep. She was sitting on the floor just inside her own room, against the door frame, where she could hear him breathe.

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Gojo tried. Once. He started to tell it the way he told things, the third version, the funny one, the Ant King being rude about elves. He got as far as *for an elf* and stopped. He sat with his back against the end of the bed and his blindfold down and did not say anything else.

"He looked at me," said Gojo, after a while. Very quietly. "On forty. When the filter came off. He looked right at me with my own eyes." He was quiet. "I've never seen anyone else see like that. I always thought I'd like it. Not being the only one." He put his hand over the blindfold. "I didn't like it. I didn't like it at all."

Taihei, by the open window, looked at the bed. At Mau under the wet cloth.

"Will it really fight her?" he said. "The ant. Later. Like it said?"

Mau turned his bandaged eyes toward the window. He could not see it. He knew where it was.

"I think it's going to try," he said.

In the reading chair, very quietly, Frieren said:

"Good."

Nobody asked her what she meant.
