*When: Day 361, floor 40, the heart of the hive. Twenty seconds on Ciel's count. Beru's jaws are in his shoulder. His arms are round Beru. Under the floor, something has opened its eyes.*

It came up round them like a mouth closing.

That was how Frieren would describe it, later, when she could. Not a building rising. A mouth closing. Dark and pale at once, beams that were bones and roofs that were tongues, an eave hung with things that were almost hands, and everywhere, in the walls of it, in the floor of it, in the posts, mouths. Open. Lined with teeth.

It did not rise all the way.

It flickered. Half there. A shape the Noise had drawn in the dark from something it had read, a year and more ago, through a tether to a man who wore another man's face. Never finished. Never built. Folded so small at the bottom of everything that even the eyes had not wanted to look at it.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *No. Not that one. Not that one. You don't know what it—it cuts everything inside it. Everything. Not just him. You. It doesn't know who you are. It cuts everything inside—*

*Then make it small.*

He did not know he could. He did it anyway. The eyes showed him the edges of it, every line of the half-built shape, and where its walls wanted to go, far out across the hive, over Yuta, over Frieren, over Gojo, over everything. He took the edges in his hands that were not hands any more, and pulled them in.

In.

In.

Until the shrine was the size of his arms. Round him. Round the thing in his arms. Nothing else.

* * *

At the edge of the hive, Gojo had gone white.

Not the white of fear. Something older than fear. He had his blindfold up and his bare eyes on the flickering shape of bone and mouths in the middle of the hive floor, closing round the boy and the broken ant king, and his face had gone the colour of the packed earth.

"*That's his*," said Gojo.

Nobody asked who.

Frieren knew it too. Not from the Sukuna night. From after. From the south field on Day 145, at the fence, with Milim's hand on her wrist, when everything had come out of Mau at once and a shape like this one had flickered over the field and cut the fence posts in rows. She had not let herself remember it in daylight since.

She lifted her staff.

Gojo's hand came down on her wrist.

"*No*," he said. "*It'll cut anything that goes in. Anything. Even yours.*" His Infinity came up, round her and round Yuta beside her, the shimmer like air above a summer road. "*He made it small. Look. He made it small so it wouldn't reach us.*" His voice did something. "*He's inside it with the thing he's killing. On purpose.*"

* * *

Inside the shrine, it rained.

That was the only word Mau had for it afterward. Rain. Not water. Cuts. Falling out of the eaves and the bones and the mouths, thin and invisible and everywhere, the way rain is everywhere. Some of them fell straight, a single clean line. Some of them fell in a hundred pieces at once, small, many, like hail.

They fell on the Ant King.

Its black plates opened. Line after line after line. Across its back, its one arm, its legs, the half-closed hole in its side. It screamed into his shoulder with its jaws still in him.

They fell on him.

Across his back. His arms that were holding it. His legs. His neck. His face. He felt each one and did not let go. He could not tell which of the lines on him were Beru's claws and which were the rain and which were the shrine and which were the fight before. He held on.

*Mau.* Ciel had stopped screaming. Her voice had gone very quiet, the way voices go quiet at a bedside. *Ten seconds. You're bleeding from everywhere. Ten.*

The Ant King was still eating.

Cut to ribbons, its plates hanging off it in strips, its purple-red eyes still on fire. Still eating. Still alive. Because it was Beru, and it had been the King of this hive, and it did not know how to stop.

Neither did he.

* * *

At the bottom of the pattern, under the rain, there was one more line.

He saw it with the eyes. The last thread of the shape. Not a cut. Something else. A single word written at the very end of what the Noise had read through the tether, like a signature at the bottom of a letter.

*Open.*

He did not know what it would do.

He opened his hands.

They were round the Ant King's back, and they were burned, and they opened, and between them, against its chest and his, something caught.

Fire.

Not Tusk's blue, not a dragon's breath. Something narrow. Something with a shape. It drew itself out of nothing between his ruined palms, long and thin and pointed, like an arrow drawn back on a bow that was not there, and it was the hottest thing he had ever felt, hotter than the Red, hotter than the light that had come out of the ground on a bad morning in the spring.

The Ant King felt it.

For the first time since the stair, it stopped eating.

Its jaws came out of his shoulder. Its head came back. Its purple-red eyes looked at him, at no distance at all, and in its threads, in the very last of them, he saw it understand.

"*Not finished*," said Mau. "*Now.*"

He let the arrow go.

* * *

It went into the Ant King's chest at no distance at all.

It did not go through. It went *in*, and stayed in, and burned.

The light came out of the Ant King's eyes first. Then its jaws. Then every line the rain had cut in it. White and gold and terrible, pouring out of the cuts the way light pours out of a lantern with holes in it. It burned from the inside, all of it at once, and Mau held on, because his hands were on its back and he could not have let go now if he had wanted to.

His hands burned.

He felt them go. Not hurt. *Go.* The skin. The fingers. Black, from the fingertips to the elbows, the way paper goes black at the edge of a fire before it catches. He held on.

The shrine came down.

All at once. The bones and the eaves and the mouths, flickering, folding, collapsing back down into the floor of the hive and into the dark under him, into the bottom of his walls, the way a tent comes down when the pole breaks. The rain stopped.

Fire and smoke filled the place where it had been.

*Now,* said Ciel. Not asking. *Now. Now. I'm putting it back.*

She put it back.

* * *

The world went out.

Not dark. Red. Then less than red. A heavy, hot nothing with fire somewhere in it and smoke in his mouth and something on top of him that was not moving any more.

He could not see.

He lay on his back on the floor of the hive with the Ant King's weight on his chest and smoke all round him and fire somewhere near, and he could not see anything at all. Not the hive. Not the smoke. Not his own hands, which he could not feel. Red, and heavy, and nothing.

*Ciel.*

*I'm here.* She was crying. *I've got you. The bleeding in your head stopped when the filter went back. You're not—you were dying, Mau. For forty seconds. You were dying. You're not now.*

*Is it dead?*

*It's dead.* A pause. *Mau. The notice.*

He could not see it. It hung somewhere in the red. Pale shapes.

*Read it.*

*Operator: trial passed,* said Ciel. *Guardian defeated.* A pause. *Shadow extraction available.*

* * *

He could not see it. He could feel it.

On top of him. Under him. The Ant King's weight on his chest, burned out, still. And under it, under both of them, in the packed earth of the hive, the dark. Gathering. Deeper than Igris's. Deeper than the bear's. Deeper than anything yet.

It was not asking.

It was weighing.

He felt it weigh him. The way Roof's dark had weighed him on a rooftop under a lid of a sky, a month ago. Except that Roof had weighed whether he was worth rising for. This was weighing whether he was worth *kneeling* to.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *The cup. There's almost nothing. And you can't see it. You can't even see it.*

*I don't need to see it.*

He could not lift his hands. He did not need to. They were on its back. He was holding it. He had been holding it the whole time.

*You were hungry,* he thought, at the dark, through his burned palms. *You ate everything. Your soldiers. My bear. Me.* He breathed smoke. *You'd have eaten the world, if the world came up your stair.*

*I've got a house. People I'd stand in front of anything for. People I just stood inside a thing with mouths for.*

*If you're going to be hungry, be hungry to keep them.*

"Arise," he said.

Into the smoke. Blind. With his voice cracked and his mouth full of ash.

The dark rose.

* * *

From the edge of the hive, through Gojo's Infinity, Frieren watched the smoke.

It had been standing there since the shape of bone and mouths came down. A column of it, grey and black, with fire inside, in the middle of the hive floor where the boy and the ant king had been. She could not see into it. Gojo could not see into it. Gojo had said so, in a voice she had never heard him use, and then stopped saying anything.

Yuta had Rika's hand on his shoulder. He did not seem to know it was there.

Then something moved in the smoke.

A shape. Low. Walking.

Mau came out of the smoke.

He was walking. She did not know how. One step. His face was turned up, his eyes open and seeing nothing, red running from them down his cheeks and into the soot. His shirt was gone. There was nothing on him that was not cut, a thousand thin lines across his chest and arms and face, as if he had walked through rain made of knives. His shoulder was open. His arms, from the fingertips to the elbows, were black.

Two steps.

He was saying something. Very quietly. She could not hear it. She knew what it was. *I'm all right.*

And behind him, out of the smoke, something rose.

Tall. Taller than Igris. Thin. Long-limbed. Black, all of it, armour and claws and jaws, with no shine on it at all. Both arms. Whole. Unhurt. Two small cold lights where the purple-red eyes had been. Blue.

It came up out of the smoke at his back like a shadow standing up off a wall.

Three steps.

Mau's knees went.

He did not fall. Before he could, two long dark arms came round him from behind. Carefully. So carefully. The way you catch something precious that has been thrown. The tall dark shape caught him, and held him up, and lowered him slowly, until he was kneeling on the packed earth of the hive with its claws spread flat and gentle on his ruined shoulders, and its head bowed over his.

Nobody in the hive breathed.

Then it spoke.

Very quietly. Not the scream from before. Not a voice anyone had heard it use.

"*My King*," said Beru.
