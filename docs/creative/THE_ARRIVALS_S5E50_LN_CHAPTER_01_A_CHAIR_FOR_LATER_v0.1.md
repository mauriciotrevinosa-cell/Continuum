# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 51 — A Chair for Later

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 51
**Continuity state:** Two rest days after floor 40 (Days 362–363), a comedy breather. Shoko forbids shadows until Day 364. The house hears about Beru's "later": Gojo has Wakana build him a chair; Umaru drafts duel rules; Fern worries; Frieren keeps counting the green book and does not discuss it; Ori asks Frieren if she is frightened, and she says no. Mau sleeps a great deal. No fight yet

*When: Day 362 and Day 363. Mau has slept for most of both. The cup is refilling, a little faster than it used to, Ciel thinks. Nothing can come out of his shadow until Shoko says so. Shoko has said so: Day 364. Dusk. Not before.*

Mau slept for most of Day 362.

He could not have done anything else. His legs would not hold him, and his back was bound from shoulder to hip. He did not mean to sleep, though. He woke at the reset hour, the way he always did, and Frieren's cold foot touched his shin, and she said *one, still here*. Then he closed his eyes for a moment, and when he opened them again it was the middle of the afternoon. The light was gold through the east window, and Frieren was in the reading chair he had said no to, with the green book open on her knees.

"You slept," she said, without looking up.

"How long?"

"Since the foot."

He lay there.

His whole body felt like it had been emptied out and filled again with something heavier. Not pain. Weight. The cup was almost dry. The thing he used for the space and the pull was dry. Ciel said both were refilling. Ciel said the cup was refilling a little faster than it used to. Ciel said *a little*, very firmly, the way she said things she did not want him to build on.

He lay in bed and looked at the ceiling.

"Beru," he said.

Frieren turned a page.

"Later," she said.

"He said he'd speak with you."

"I heard him."

"About the place at my side."

"I heard him," said Frieren again, and turned another page. "Later is later. Go back to sleep."

He went back to sleep.

* * *

The house did not go back to sleep.

The house had heard. Of course it had. Gojo had told it in the east room on the seventh night, and then at breakfast on Day 362 he told it again, at length, with gestures. By lunch everyone knew. By dinner, the five had a bet going that Kusuri was keeping on her slate, and Umaru had confiscated it for unauthorised gambling, and then quietly added her own name to one column.

Gojo went to Wakana in the workshop.

"I need a chair," he said.

Wakana looked at him over the bench.

"You have a chair. Several chairs. You sit in Rimuru's chair at the long table when he's not looking."

"I need a *special* chair," said Gojo. "For *later*." He spread his hands. "For watching. A good view. Comfortable. With somewhere to put a cup."

Wakana looked at him for a long time.

"You want me to build you a chair," he said slowly, "so you can sit comfortably and watch Frieren fight a shadow."

"Yes."

"The shadow that went straight for her face on forty."

"That one."

Wakana put down his plane.

"No," he said.

Gojo looked wounded.

"It's not going to *hurt* her," he said. "Frieren's—Frieren. She put a Zoltraak through it while it was trying to eat Yuta. It's not going to hurt her." He paused. "And it's Mau's now. It's not going to do anything Mau doesn't let it." He paused again. "I just want a good seat."

Wakana looked at him for a long time.

Then he picked up his plane again.

"A stool," he said. "Not a chair. A stool. With a back." He started planing. "Three legs. For uneven ground. You're not getting armrests."

"Can I have somewhere to put a cup?"

"You can hold your cup," said Wakana.

Gojo considered this.

"Deal," he said.

* * *

Umaru drafted rules.

She sat at the end of the long table all afternoon on Day 362 with a fresh sheet of paper and her chalk and her slate. Yuka sat beside her with the copy book, and Ori sat on her other side with the list. By evening there was a sheet pinned to the third wall, beside *FORTY: SOMETHING HUNGRY*, in Umaru's square Ministry hand.

*MINISTRY-SUPERVISED CHALLENGE. (LATER.) RULES. (DRAFT.)*

*1. Not inside the lime. (Too many windows.)*

*2. North field. Well inside the line. Dusk. (Shoko.)*

*3. Nothing gets hit that isn't the challenger or the challenged. Not trees. Not the stake. Not Nazuna. (Nazuna asked.)*

*4. If anyone says STOP, it stops. Anyone. (Ori.)*

*5. The Ministry is not responsible for the outcome. (The Ministry is very interested in the outcome.)*

And underneath, smaller, in Yuka's neat hand:

*6. Gojo gets a stool. Not a chair. (Wakana.)*

Fern stood in front of the sheet for a long time on the evening of Day 362.

Then she went to find Frieren.

* * *

Frieren was in the reading chair with the green book.

Fern stood in the doorway of the east room. Mau was asleep in the bed. Through the connecting door, Ori was asleep too, with Milim floating over her on the ribbon.

"Mistress Frieren."

Frieren turned a page.

"There are *rules*," said Fern. "On the wall. For *later*. Umaru wrote them. Gojo is getting a stool."

"I saw."

"You're going to fight a shadow."

Frieren looked up from the book.

"I don't know what I'm going to do," she said. "It hasn't said what it wants yet. It said it would speak with me. About the place at his side." She looked at Mau, asleep. "It might want to fight. It might want to argue. It might want to make a speech. It made a very long speech when it woke up." She turned a page. "I'll find out later."

"You're not worried."

"No."

Fern looked at her for a long moment.

"It went straight for your face," she said. "On forty. Gojo told me. It stopped a finger's width away."

Frieren closed the green book on her finger.

"It went straight for my face," she agreed. "And Mau stopped it. And then Mau raised it." She looked at Fern. "It's not what it was, Fern. Lantern isn't. Igris isn't. They're what he asks them to be." She paused. "He asked it to be hungry to keep us. Not to eat us. I heard him. Under his breath. When he raised it."

Fern did not say anything.

"So I'm not worried," said Frieren. "It wants the place at his side because it wants to keep him. That's the same thing I want." She opened the book again. "We'll work it out."

Fern stood in the doorway for a long time.

Then she said, "I'm going to be there."

"Everyone's going to be there," said Frieren. "Gojo's getting a stool."

* * *

Ori asked on Day 363.

She came through the connecting door in the morning while Mau was still asleep. She stood beside the reading chair, where Frieren was counting, and waited until Frieren looked up.

"Are you frightened?" said Ori.

Frieren looked at her.

"Of Beru?"

"Of later."

Frieren thought about it. Properly. The way she thought about everything Ori asked.

"No," she said.

"Why not?"

"Because I've been alive a very long time," said Frieren. "And I've met a great many things that wanted the place I was standing in." She closed the book on her finger. "Demons. Kings. A dragon once, on a mountain, when I was younger than you'd think." She paused. "Beru is the first one that wanted it because it loves the same person I do."

Ori considered this.

"That's different," she said.

"That's very different."

"Is it going to be all right?"

Frieren looked at the bed. At Mau, asleep, with one arm over his face against the light.

"I think," she said slowly, "it's going to be very funny."

Ori looked at her for a long moment.

Then she nodded, satisfied, and went back through the connecting door. A moment later, faintly, Mau heard her tell Milim, "Frieren says it's going to be funny," and Milim say, "*YES*," and Yuka's voice saying, "Shh, Oppa's sleeping."

* * *

Mau woke properly on the evening of Day 363.

He sat up in bed. The light was gold. His back pulled under Shoko's bandages, three long tight lines. His legs answered him. They had not, the day before. Yuta had come twice, once each day, and sat on the end of the bed with his hands on Mau's calves for a long time, saying nothing, until the torn places had knitted enough to stand on. Shoko had watched from the door both times and said nothing either. The cup was not full. Not close. But there was something in it now. More than a thin layer. A real amount. Enough to raise one thing. Enough to remake one thing, maybe.

*Tomorrow,* said Ciel. *Dusk. Shoko said. And you'll have enough for Beru to stand for a while. And for one remaking, if it comes to that.*

*If what comes to that?*

Ciel was quiet for a moment.

*If someone puts a Zoltraak through him,* she said. *Again.*

Mau sat on the edge of the bed for a long time.

Then he got up and went down to dinner. At the long table, Gojo had a stool beside his place. Three legs. A back. No armrests. Very well made. Wakana was pretending not to look at it.

On the third wall, under Umaru's rules, someone had added a seventh, in a hand Mau did not recognise at first. Then he did. It was dry and level and very calm. He did not know how a shadow could write. He was quite sure it could not. He was quite sure, when he looked at Rimuru across the table, that Rimuru had written it, very carefully, in a hand that was not his own, and was now looking extremely innocent.

*7. My liege's knight requests a place to stand. (Near the queen.)*

Frieren, beside Mau, read it.

She looked at it for a long time.

"Queen," she said.

"I don't know who wrote that."

"Rimuru wrote that."

"Rimuru wrote that," Mau agreed.

Frieren looked across the table at Rimuru. Rimuru looked back at her with an expression of perfect, impenetrable innocence.

"*Queen*," said Frieren again.

And for the second time that summer, very slowly, from the collar up, she went red.
