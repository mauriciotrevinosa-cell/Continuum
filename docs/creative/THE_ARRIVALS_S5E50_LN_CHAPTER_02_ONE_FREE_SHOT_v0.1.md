# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 56 — One Free Shot

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 56
**Continuity state:** The creator's Beru/Frieren sequence, part 1. North field at dusk, well inside the lime, under Umaru's Ministry rules, with the house watching and Gojo on his stool. Beru challenges Frieren for the place at his King's side (closeness to Mau as guard and champion, not romance). Frieren accepts; Beru, confident, grants her one shot and promises not to move; she fires Zoltraak angled upward, and one would have been enough. The slope does not laugh. Ori's face says do something; Mau kneels and puts his hand on her back ("It's gone"). Frieren: "He had his jaws in you… I still needed to do that." *When: Day 364, dusk. The north field. The long grass is pale and dry. The ditch is dry. The fireflies have been gone for four weeks. Half the house is sitting on the slope below the red stake. Nazuna is on top of the stake, because Rule 3 says nothing gets hit that isn't the challenger or the challenged, and she wants to see it from up there.*

Everyone came.

Mau had known everyone would come. He had not known *everyone*. He came up the slope from the house at dusk slowly, three days after forty, with Frieren on one side and Yuta on the other, not holding him up, close enough to. Both his hands were still wrapped to the elbow in Shoko's white cloth, black and raw underneath, and he could not close either of them. The thin lines from the shrine were all over his face and neck, scabbed, a thousand of them. The left edge of everything he looked at was smeared. He stopped at the top of the slope, and looked at the north field, and did not say anything for a moment.

They were on the slope below the red stake. All of them. And half of them, he saw, were not looking at the field, where Beru would be. They were looking at him. At his hands. At his face. The way you look at someone who came home three days ago in a way nobody in the house would forget.

Shoko had said yes to this only because Beru would not wait and because she would be sitting at the end of the slope, with her bag. She was. She did not take her eyes off his hands the whole time.

They were all there. The fort in a row at the front, with Ichika in the middle in her too-long sash, holding on to Ori's sleeve. Kessoku, with Bocchi's guitar, which nobody had asked her to bring. The five, with Kusuri's slate, which Umaru had un-confiscated for the occasion. The witches. Touka, with Kaneki, with a cup. Seiko on a bench somebody had carried up, with Turbo Granny in her cat beside her. Eren and Mikasa, a little apart. Himmel, a little further apart, with his arms folded and an expression Mau could not read. Fern, at the front, with the ledger. Stark behind her.

And Gojo.

In the middle of the slope, on a three-legged stool with a back and no armrests, holding a cup of Touka's coffee in both hands. Wakana was beside him, looking at the stool with the faint, critical air of a man who has noticed a leg is very slightly shorter than the other two and is never going to say so.

"This is a lot of people," said Mau.

"It's a challenge," said Umaru, at the front, with her slate. "Everyone wants to see a challenge."

"Rule 1," said Yuka, beside her, reading off the copy book. "Not inside the lime. Too many windows. Rule 2. North field. Well inside the line. Dusk."

"It's dusk," said Shoko, from the bench beside Seiko. She had her watch. "Rule 2 is satisfied. And I'm adding one. Nothing comes out of you after it's dark. That's an hour. Use it."

"An hour," said Mau.

"An hour," said Shoko.

* * *

He walked out into the long grass.

Thirty paces from the slope. Well inside the lime. He stopped where the grass was flattest and the ground was level, with the slope behind him and the open north field in front of him going away toward the hills, darkening.

Frieren walked out with him.

She stopped ten paces to his left. She stood in the long pale grass with her staff in her hand, loosely, the way she held it when she was reading. She had not brought the green book. She did not look at the crowd on the slope. She looked at Mau.

"Ready?" he said.

"I'm always ready," said Frieren. "I've been ready for a thousand years. Call him."

Mau looked down at his shadow, long and thin in the low gold light, stretching away across the grass toward the hills.

"Igris," he said first. "Witness."

The knight came up out of the grass beside him, tall and dark, with the long sword sheathed at his hip. He looked at the crowd on the slope. At Frieren, ten paces away. He looked at her for a long moment.

Then he crossed the ten paces and stood a pace behind her and to her left. Exactly where a knight stands beside someone he serves.

Rule 7. *My liege's knight requests a place to stand. (Near the queen.)*

On the slope, Rimuru put his face in his hands.

Frieren did not look round at the knight behind her. But the tips of her ears went faintly pink in the dusk.

"Beru," said Mau.

* * *

Beru came up all at once.

Not the way Igris came, smooth and unhurried. All at once, like something released, out of the grass in front of Mau. Tall, thin, long-limbed, dark, with the two small cold blue lights burning in his ant-man's head.

He went straight down on one knee.

"*My King!*" said Beru. The voice went out across the north field, high and fierce and trembling, and the crowd on the slope went perfectly silent. "My King has rested! My King has called! I, Beru, rise at my King's word—"

"Beru."

"—to serve, to guard, to *devour* whatever my King commands—"

"*Beru*."

Beru stopped.

He looked up at Mau with his cold blue eyes.

"You said you'd speak with Frieren," said Mau. "Later. About the place at my side." He looked across at Frieren, ten paces away, with Igris behind her. "It's later."

Beru turned his head.

He looked at Frieren for a long time. At her standing in the long grass with her staff held loosely. At the knight a pace behind her and to her left, where a knight stands.

His head tilted. The insect tilt.

"Igris," he said. "You stand behind the elf."

"I stand where I choose to stand," said Igris. Dry. Level. "Near the one I choose to stand near."

Beru looked at him.

Then he stood up.

* * *

He walked out into the long grass.

Ten paces toward Frieren. Then five more. He stopped in front of her, close. Tall. Thin. Dark. Looking down at her from his height with his cold blue eyes, the way he had looked at her across the heart of the hive.

Frieren looked up at him.

She did not move.

"Elf," said Beru.

"Frieren," said Frieren.

"Frieren," said Beru, as if filing it. "You stand at my King's side. Close. Closer than a guard. In the place that should belong to his champion. His strongest. His most devoted." He drew himself up. "I am his strongest. I am his most devoted. I was the King of a hive and I kneel to him alone." He paused. "I challenge you. For the place."

On the slope, someone made a very small sound. It might have been Kusuri. It might have been Gojo.

Frieren looked up at the Ant King.

"For the place at his side," she said.

"For the place at his side."

"All right," said Frieren.

Beru stopped. As if he had expected an argument.

"You accept?"

"I accept."

* * *

Beru looked at her for a long moment.

Then something happened to his posture. Mau saw it. A kind of loosening. A settling. The way a very large animal settles when it has decided the thing in front of it is not a threat.

"I am generous," said Beru. "My King is generous, and so I am generous." He spread his long arms wide. "I will grant you one strike."

The crowd on the slope went silent again.

"One strike," said Beru. "With everything you have. Your strongest spell. The white light that took my arm in the hive." He planted his feet in the long grass. "I will not move. I will not dodge. I will not defend. I will stand here and receive it." He lifted his ant-man's head proudly. "And when I am still standing afterward—as I will be, for I am Beru, who was King, and I have grown since the hive—you will yield the place at my King's side."

Frieren looked at him.

"One strike," she said.

"One."

"You won't move."

"I will not move."

"Everything I have."

"Everything you have," said Beru magnificently. "I have felt your white light once. It surprised me. It will not surprise me twice."

On the slope, Gojo had put his cup down very carefully on the grass beside his stool. He was leaning forward with his elbows on his knees and his bare eyes on Frieren. He had the expression of a man watching the best thing he has ever seen and trying very hard not to laugh before it happens.

Mau looked at Frieren.

Frieren did not look at him. She looked at Beru.

"Mau," she said. "Is that allowed?"

"It's his offer," said Mau. "He made it. In front of witnesses."

"Rule 3," said Umaru, from the slope, very fast. "Nothing gets hit that isn't the challenger or the challenged. Not trees. Not the stake. Not Nazuna."

"I heard," said Frieren.

She looked at Beru. At him standing in the long grass with his arms spread wide and his head held high. Behind him, the open north field went away toward the hills, and above the hills, the sky. Darkening. Empty. Very large.

She took three steps to her left.

Beru turned his head to follow her.

"I said I would not move," he said. "I will not move. You may stand wherever you like."

"I know," said Frieren. "I'm moving the angle."

She took one more step. And then she knelt down in the long grass, on one knee, very low. From down there, Beru stood above her against the sky. Nothing behind him but the dark empty sky above the hills.

Up. Not across.

Rule 3.

She raised her staff.

* * *

"Wait," said Beru.

Frieren waited.

"You're kneeling," said Beru. "Why are you kneeling?"

"So that it goes up," said Frieren. "Into the sky. Instead of across the field. So it doesn't hit the stake. Or Nazuna. Or the hills." She did not lower her staff. "Rule 3."

Beru looked down at her. At the angle. At the sky behind him.

"You are very careful," he said. "For a spell that will not move me."

"I'm always careful," said Frieren. "I've been careful for a thousand years. That's why I'm still here." She looked up at him along the length of her staff. "Ready?"

Beru spread his arms wider.

"I am Beru," he said. "I am always ready."

Frieren did not say *good*.

She did not say anything.

Mau, on the slope, saw her face, and his stomach went cold. It was not her careful face. It was not the face she wore for a thousand years of being careful. It was the face from outside the clinic, on the ground, three days ago, with her hands in the front of Fern's coat.

He opened his mouth.

She fired.

* * *

It was Zoltraak.

The killing magic. A demon's spell once, until humans studied it and made it theirs. It came out of the end of her staff white and narrow and straight up along the angle she had knelt for, and went through Beru.

Not through his shoulder. Through all of him. The whole tall thin dark shape. He was standing in the long grass with his arms spread wide and his head held high, and then he was standing in the middle of the light, and then he was not standing at all.

One strike. That was all it needed. Everybody on the slope saw that it was all it needed.

She fired again.

Into the place where he had been. Into the dark pieces still hanging in the air, falling. White. Up. Then again. And again. Zoltraak after Zoltraak, not one beam but a storm of them, straight up out of the north field into the darkening sky, so many and so fast that the whole slope went white and stayed white, every face on it, the red stake, Nazuna on top of it with her hands over her eyes.

There was nothing left of Beru to hit.

She kept hitting it.

Every piece. Every scrap of dark falling through the air. She found them. She found every one with a line of white light and broke it smaller, and smaller, and the pieces that were left went up into the sky with the light and did not come down.

The beams were going higher than anything Mau had ever seen her cast. Higher than the clouds. Somewhere very far up, where the first stars were, the light kept going.

Rule 3. Up. Not across. She did not hit the stake. She did not hit Nazuna. She did not hit the hills. She hit nothing but sky and the place where something that had once put its jaws in him had been.

She did not stop.

* * *

On the slope, the laughter had not started.

Gojo had leaned forward on his stool with his cup down, ready, the way he had been ready for days. He had been going to laugh. Everyone had known he was going to laugh. He did not laugh. He sat on his stool with his bare eyes on Frieren, and on the light going up, and his face went very still.

Seiko was not cackling.

Shoko had stood up.

At the front of the slope, the fort had stopped staring at the empty place in the grass. They were staring at Frieren. Milim had her mouth open. Yuka had her hand on Milim's arm. Ichika had hidden her face in Touka's coat. Ori was not hiding. Ori was looking at Frieren with an expression Mau had never seen on her face, and then she looked up the slope at him.

*Do something*, said Ori's face.

* * *

"Frieren," said Mau.

He was on his feet. He did not remember standing. He was walking down the slope, through the long grass, toward her, on legs that Yuta had knitted back together the day before, with the white light going up in front of him.

"*Frieren*."

The light kept going.

He reached her. She was still kneeling. Still firing. Her face was dry. Completely dry. Her eyes were on the sky and her staff was steady and her hands were white on it.

He knelt down beside her in the long grass. He did not touch the staff. He put his hand on her back. Flat. Between her shoulders. The way she put her hand on his chest at the reset hour.

"It's gone," he said. "He's gone. There's nothing left."

The light stopped.

All at once. The last beam going up and up into the dark until it was a star, and then not even that. The north field went dark. The slope went dark. Every face on it, dark, and very quiet.

Frieren lowered her staff.

She did not look at him. She looked at the place where Beru had been. At nothing. At grass.

"He had his jaws in you," she said.

Very quietly. Level. Too level.

"I know."

"In your shoulder. Twice. He was eating you." She did not look away from the grass. "I was standing four paces away. I had a spell in my hand. The notice said I couldn't—" She stopped. "You were saying *I'm all right* with your eyes bleeding and I couldn't do anything."

"I know."

"So I did it now," said Frieren.

She turned her head and looked at him at last.

"I know he isn't the same," she said. "I know he'll come back when you remake him. I know he'll be yours and he'll say *my King* and he'll be very loud and Ori will add a line." Her voice did not change. "I know all of it. I said so myself, in the hive. *They're what you ask them to be.*" She looked at the grass. "I still needed to do that."

Mau did not say anything.

He put his arm round her. She let him.

* * *

On the slope, very quietly, Milim said:

"That wasn't one."

Nobody answered her.

Gojo got up off his stool. He did not fall off it. He stood up slowly, and picked up his cup, and looked at the two of them kneeling in the dark grass, and then at the sky where the light had gone.

"No," he said. "It wasn't."

He sat back down.

Then, after a long moment, he did laugh. Once. Not his usual laugh. Something smaller, with something wet in it.

"*One free shot*," he said. To nobody. "He gave her *one*."

And somewhere at the front of the slope, Seiko started cackling after all, and then Kusuri, and then Milim, and the laughter came, late, a little shaky, a little too loud, the way laughter comes after a thing that frightened everybody and is over.

Frieren, in the grass, put her face against Mau's shoulder. The good one.

He felt her laugh too. Once. Into his shirt.

Or it might have been the other thing.
