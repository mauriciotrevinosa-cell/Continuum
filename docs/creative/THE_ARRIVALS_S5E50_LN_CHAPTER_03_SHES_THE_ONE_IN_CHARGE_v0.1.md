# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 57 — She's the One in Charge

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 57
**Continuity state:** The creator's Beru/Frieren sequence, part 2, kept intact. Mau remakes Beru (real cost from the reserve; no new life is created). Beru apologises to "the queen of my King" (the creator's "queen of his monarch") and swears never to be disrespectful again. Igris and the rest turn to look at Mau. Mau: "She's the one in charge." *When: Day 364, dusk, a minute later. The north field. The white light has gone out of the sky. The laughter on the slope has gone uneven and then quiet. Frieren is kneeling in the long grass with her face against Mau's good shoulder. Where Beru stood, there is nothing at all.*

Mau looked at his shadow.

It stretched long and thin across the long grass from where they knelt, toward the hills, darkening. Somewhere in it, in the cup, there was a shape. Broken. In pieces. Not gone.

*He's there,* said Ciel. Very careful. Very gentle. *All of him. In pieces.* A pause. *Very small pieces. She broke every one of them. Smaller than Tank, smaller than anything.* Another pause. *You can remake him. It'll cost more than it should. That's all right.*

*I know.*

*She needed to,* said Ciel. *I think. I've been watching her for three days. I think she needed to.*

*I know.*

On the slope, Gojo was sitting on his stool with his cup in both hands, watching the two of them in the grass, not laughing any more.

Frieren lifted her face off his shoulder.

"Do it," she said. Quietly. "Bring him back." She sat back on her heels. "I'm finished."

* * *

Mau stayed kneeling in the grass.

He could not put his hand flat. Not the way he always did. Both hands were wrapped and would not open. He laid the back of his right wrist on his own shadow instead, the cloth against the grass, and felt it hurt all the way up to the elbow, and did not say so. Frieren saw. She put her own hand flat on the shadow beside his wrist, as if to steady it. He felt for the shape in the cup. Broken. All the pieces of it, scattered through the dark, where Frieren's light had sent them.

He did not need to think about what he was asking. He had asked it already, in the hive. *Be hungry to keep them. Not to eat them. To keep them.*

He thought it again anyway.

*Come back,* he thought. *You lost. That's all right. Come back.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the grass.

Not all at once, this time. In pieces. The way the pieces had fallen, like ash, like leaves. They came up out of the ground and drew together in the air in front of Mau, slowly. A long thin leg. Another. Arms. A long-limbed body. A head that was part man and part ant. Jaws.

Two small cold blue lights.

Mau felt the cost. Ciel had been right. Not as much as the hive. Still a good deal. Bringing him back cost what it cost, all at once, as the pieces began to find each other. He kept his hand on the grass and held the intention steady.

The pieces found each other.

Beru stood in the long grass where he had been standing a minute before.

He did not kneel. He did not say *my King*. He stood there very still, tall and thin and dark, and did not move at all.

He was looking at his own hands.

He turned them over. Long fingers. Claws. He looked at them for a long time. Then he looked down at himself. At his chest. His shoulders. His legs. As if checking that they were all there.

Then, very slowly, he turned his head.

And looked at Frieren.

* * *

Frieren was standing exactly where she had been, ten paces away, in the long pale grass. Her staff was lowered. Igris was a pace behind her and to her left, where he had not moved from for the whole of it.

She looked back at Beru.

She did not say anything.

Beru looked at her for a very long time. The whole slope held its breath. Even Gojo, on his stool, had gone quiet.

Then Beru walked toward her.

Not the way he had walked before, tall and proud, to stand over her. Slowly. Carefully. The way you walk toward something you have just discovered is much larger than you thought. He stopped in front of her. Not close. A respectful distance. Two paces.

And he went down.

Not on one knee, the way he knelt to Mau. Both knees. And then further. His long body folded down in the long grass until his forehead was nearly on the ground in front of her feet. His long arms spread flat on either side of him. His claws open. The deepest bow Mau had ever seen anything make.

"*Queen*," said Beru.

The word went out across the north field. Not high and fierce, this time. Low. Trembling.

"Queen of my Monarch," said Beru. "Queen of my King. I was wrong. I was arrogant. I was a fool who had been a king of a hive and thought that made me a king of anything. I looked at you and saw an elf. I looked at the place at my King's side and thought it was a place for the strongest. I did not understand." He did not lift his head. "It *is* the place for the strongest. You are the strongest. You put me into the sky in one breath and I did not even feel myself go."

Frieren looked down at him.

"I gave you one strike," said Beru, into the grass. "I was generous. I was a fool. I will never be so generous again." His voice shook. "I apologise. To the queen of my Monarch. With my whole self, remade." He pressed his forehead to the ground. "I will never again be disrespectful to you. Not in word. Not in thought. Not in the place I choose to stand." A pause. "I swear it. On the hunger I was raised with. I swear it."

* * *

The north field was silent.

Then Igris, a pace behind Frieren, turned his head.

He looked past Frieren. Past the Ant King lying with his forehead on the ground in front of her feet. He looked across the long grass at Mau, kneeling by his shadow.

And on the slope, everyone else turned too.

All of them. The fort. Kessoku. The five. The witches. Touka and Kaneki. Seiko on her bench. Fern with her ledger. Eren and Mikasa. Himmel with his arms folded. Gojo, on his stool, with his blindfold half off. All of them turned their heads, slowly, from the elf and the shadow on the ground before her, to the boy kneeling by his own shadow in the long grass.

Waiting.

Mau looked at them.

At Igris, whose cold blue eyes were on him. At Beru, on the ground. At Frieren, who had not turned to look at him at all. She was still looking down at the Ant King at her feet, with her staff lowered and her face doing nothing whatsoever.

He stood up.

"She's the one in charge," said Mau.

Frieren did not look up from Beru.

"Correct," she said.

* * *

The slope exploded.

It was not one sound. It was every sound at once. Gojo fell off his stool at last, sideways into the long grass, and laughed until he wheezed, the real laugh this time, the one that had been waiting for three days. Seiko cackled. Turbo Granny shrieked *What did she say? What did she say?* and Seiko said *Correct, she said correct*, and cackled harder. Kusuri wrote *CORRECT* on her slate in letters so large they filled it. Umaru stood up on the bench Seiko was not using and began a proclamation that nobody could hear. Milim flew straight up to the end of her ribbon and came down again, and Yuka did not even try to stop her. Ichika, in the middle of the fort, in her too-long sash, laughed without knowing why, because everyone else was.

And at the edge of the slope, a little apart, with his arms folded, Himmel laughed.

Mau heard it over everything. He would have known it anywhere. Himmel's laugh. The one that had come out of him easily his whole life, before the shelf, before the road. Loud and helpless and completely free. He stood at the edge of the slope with his arms coming unfolded and his head back and laughed. He laughed at the elf who had put a challenger into the sky in one breath, and at the challenger lying on his face apologising, and at the boy who had stood up and said *she's the one in charge*.

Frieren heard it too.

She did not look round. But Mau saw her ears go pink at the tips. All the way pink.

* * *

Beru lifted his head.

Very slowly. He looked up at Frieren from the ground, his forehead an inch from the grass, with his cold blue eyes.

"Queen," he said.

"Don't call me that," said Frieren.

"Yes, Queen."

"Beru."

"Yes, Queen of my King."

Frieren looked at him for a long moment.

Then she looked across the grass at Mau.

"Does he ever stop?"

"No," said Mau. "He calls me king."

"Make him stop."

"I tried," said Mau. "He made it worse."

Frieren looked down at Beru again. At the long dark shape folded in the grass at her feet, with his claws spread flat and his head tilted up at her.

"Get up," she said.

Beru got up. All at once. He stood in front of her, tall and thin and dark, two paces away, a respectful distance. He did not look down at her the way he had before. He stood very straight, the way Igris stood, and looked at a point somewhere over her left shoulder.

"The place at his side," said Frieren.

"Is yours, Queen," said Beru. "It was always yours. I did not understand."

"I don't need all of it," said Frieren. "Just the part where I stand." She looked at him. "You can have the other side. If he lets you. If you want to keep him."

Beru went very still.

"The other side," he said.

"The other side," said Frieren. "Not *next* to him. That's mine. Behind him. Where a guard stands." She looked at Igris, a pace behind her and to her left. "Where that one stands. Behind me."

Beru looked at Igris.

Igris looked back at him. Dry. Level. Very calm.

"There is room," said Igris, "behind my liege."

Beru looked at the knight for a long time.

Then he crossed the grass to where Mau stood. He did not kneel. He stood a pace behind Mau and to his right. Exactly opposite where Igris stood behind Frieren.

"My King," he said. Very quietly. Not a proclamation. Just the words.

"Beru," said Mau.

"I lost," said Beru.

"You lost."

"In one strike," said Beru. "With everything she had. I told her to use everything she had." He was quiet. "She did."

"She always does," said Mau.

* * *

Shoko, on the bench, looked at her watch.

"Forty-one minutes," she said. "Nineteen left. And then nothing comes out of you after dark." She looked at Mau. "You've got enough left for them to walk home with you. Then they go back. Both of them."

"Both of them."

So they walked home.

Down the slope, through the crowd, toward the house in the last of the light. Frieren first, with Igris a pace behind her and to her left. Mau beside her, close, where she stood. And Beru a pace behind Mau and to his right, walking very carefully and not looking at anyone. The whole house parted for them on the slope and closed behind, laughing, talking, all the way down.

Gojo came last, carrying his stool.

At the edge of the yard, Ori caught up with them.

She had the list. *SHADOWS (NAMED)*. She had been writing on it the whole time. She held it up so Mau could see it, walking backwards in front of them.

*7. Beru. (The notice.) The Ant King. Talks a lot. Calls Mau king. Calls Frieren queen.*

And underneath, in her careful printing:

*Lost to Frieren. In one.*

And underneath that, smaller, in Fern's small neat hand, added at some point on the slope when nobody was looking:

*Correct.*
