# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 58 — Five Minutes a Day

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 58
**Continuity state:** Igris and Beru with voices at home. Shoko allows five minutes a day of shadows in the yard (her rule for the house getting used to them, not a cup cost: once raised, calling, standing and storing are free; only remaking a broken shadow costs). Beru apologises to Frieren every morning, and she stops him by the third day. Igris is dry and formal, with his own judgement, and corrects Beru. Ichika is not afraid of Beru, and he is gentle with her ("hungry to keep"). Touka is wary, then less so. Tank is remade quietly (a smaller cost than Beru). Neither shadow becomes a drone, a night guard or a weapon in the house; the night shifts come later

*When: Day 365 to Day 367, mornings, a little after dawn. The back step. Touka's coffee. Five minutes, by Shoko's watch, which Shoko has lent to Ori for the purpose, because Ori asked to be the one who counts.*

The five minutes started on Day 365.

His hands were out of the wrappings by then. Shoko had spent three nights on them, and Yuta two, and they had closed. Not healed. Closed. New skin, pink and shiny and tight, over the places that had been black, and the fingers would bend if he made them, slowly, the way old hinges bend. He could not hold a cup properly yet. Touka, on the first morning, had looked at him trying, and taken the cup out of his hands without a word, and held it while he drank, and then handed it to Frieren to hold for the rest, and gone back to the stove as if nothing had happened. Nobody said anything about it. Everybody saw.

Shoko had set the rule at breakfast the morning after the north field. Five minutes a day. Standing. In the yard. Not more. "It's not the cup," she said, before Mau could say it. "I know. Ciel told me. Once they're yours, it costs you nothing to call them. Only if they break." She looked down the table. "It's us. The house. We need to get used to them a little at a time. They're yours," she said to Mau. "They're part of the house now, whether anyone likes it or not. People should get to know them. Five minutes." She looked at the long table. "And if anyone gets eaten, the rule ends."

"Nobody's going to get eaten," said Mau.

"I know," said Shoko. "I'm saying it so it's on record."

Ori asked to keep the watch.

"I'll count," she said. "I'm good at counting. I counted the fireflies with Nazuna. Not really. But I listened."

Shoko looked at her for a long moment. Then she took the watch off its chain and gave it to Ori.

"Five minutes," she said. "Not five and a bit."

"Not five and a bit," said Ori. And wrote it on the list.

* * *

Day 365. Dawn.

Mau was on the back step with Frieren. Touka came out with three cups, the way she did every morning now, because she was open early. She handed them out and sat down on the step on Mau's other side and looked at the yard.

Ori came out of the kitchen door with the watch held in both hands.

"Ready," she said.

Mau looked at his shadow on the dry ground of the yard.

"Igris. Beru."

They came up together.

Igris on Frieren's side. Smooth and unhurried, the long cloak falling into place, a pace behind her and to her left. Beru on Mau's side. All at once, the way he did everything, a pace behind Mau and to his right.

Touka, on the step, went very still.

She had been on the slope in the north field. She had seen Beru go up into the sky. She had seen him come back in pieces and kneel in the grass. But that had been at a distance, at dusk, with the whole house laughing. This was the back step at dawn, with her coffee in her hand, and a tall thin dark thing with an insect's head and long claws standing a pace from her shoulder.

Beru saw her go still.

He looked at her with his cold blue eyes. Then at the cup in her hand. Then at Frieren.

Then he went down on both knees on the dry ground of the yard, in front of the back step, and bowed until his forehead nearly touched the dirt.

"Queen of my King," he said. "Good morning. I apologise."

Frieren looked down at him.

"For what?"

"For the north field," said Beru, into the dirt. "For my arrogance. For *one strike*. For *everything you have*." He trembled. "I will apologise every morning. For the rest of my existence. So that I never forget."

Frieren drank her coffee.

"Hm," she said.

* * *

Igris, a pace behind her, spoke.

"That is excessive," he said.

Beru lifted his head and looked at him.

"Excessive?"

"An apology made every morning," said Igris, dry and level, "is not an apology. It is a habit. Habits stop meaning anything." He looked at the Ant King on his knees in the dirt. "You were forgiven on the field. The queen said *correct*. That was the end of it."

"The queen did not say I was *forgiven*," said Beru. "The queen said my King was correct."

"It is the same thing," said Igris.

"It is not the same thing at all."

"It is the same thing," said Igris, "to anyone with sense."

Beru drew himself up on his knees.

"Are you saying I lack sense, knight?"

"I am saying," said Igris, "that you challenged a woman who has been killing things for a thousand years to strike you with everything she had, while you stood still." A pause. "I am not saying anything. I am describing."

Touka, on the step, made a sound into her coffee.

Mau looked at her. She had her hand over her mouth. Her shoulders were shaking.

"Is he always like that?" she said, very low. "The knight?"

"I don't know," said Mau honestly. "He's only been talking for two weeks."

"I like him," said Touka.

* * *

"Two minutes," said Ori, from the kitchen door, looking at the watch.

Beru was still on his knees in the dirt, glaring at Igris. Igris was looking at a point over Frieren's head, the way a knight looks when he is standing guard and does not intend to be part of an argument he has just won.

Then Ichika came out of the kitchen.

She came out behind Ori, barefoot, in her too-long sash, holding the small wooden cup of water in both hands. She had woken early, because Touka had woken early, and followed her mother down. She stopped in the kitchen doorway and looked at the yard.

At the tall thin dark thing on its knees in the dirt in front of the back step.

Touka moved. Mau saw it. The way she had moved in the Summer Annexe. Very fast. Half rising off the step, her cup tilting, toward her daughter.

Beru turned his head.

He looked at the small girl in the kitchen doorway with the wooden cup. At her face. At her eyes, which had gone, in the moment of seeing him, black and red. Both of them. Like coals.

He did not move.

He knelt in the dirt very still. Then, slowly, he bowed his head toward her. Not the deep bow he gave Frieren. Smaller. Gentler. The way you lower your head to look at something very small without frightening it.

"Little one," said Beru. Very quietly. Not high. Not fierce. Low and soft, the way nobody had heard Beru speak before. "Your eyes are red."

Ichika looked at him.

"Mine are blue," said Beru. "Now. They used to be another colour. Before." He did not move. "The fort says everyone is allowed to be a different colour. The little lady with the list told me. Yesterday. She told me all the rules."

Ichika looked at him for a long time with her black-and-red eyes.

Then she came down the kitchen step.

Touka was on her feet. Kaneki was in the kitchen doorway behind Ichika. He had come down too, and he had his hand out toward Touka, very still, the way he had held it out in the Summer Annexe. Not touching her. Just there.

Ichika walked across the yard. Barefoot. Holding the wooden cup in both hands. She stopped in front of the Ant King kneeling in the dirt.

She was not much taller than his bowed head.

She held out the cup.

Beru looked at it.

"I do not drink, little one," he said.

Ichika kept holding it out.

Beru looked at the small wooden cup of water, held out to him in both hands, filled exactly to the line. He looked at it for a long time.

Then, very carefully, with one long claw, he touched the rim of it. Just touched it. The way you touch something you have been given and cannot use but do not want to refuse.

"Thank you," said Beru. "Little one."

Ichika took the cup back. She drank it herself, very seriously, looking at him over the rim the whole time.

Then she turned round and walked back to her mother.

* * *

Touka sat down on the step.

She did not say anything. She put her arm round Ichika and pulled her in against her side, and looked at the Ant King kneeling in the dirt of the yard, who had not moved at all.

"He said *thank you*," said Ichika. Her third word to anyone outside the fort. More than one word. A sentence.

"He did," said Touka.

"His eyes are blue."

"They are."

"He was a different colour before," said Ichika. "He said."

Touka looked at Beru for a long time.

"So was I," she said quietly. Not to Ichika. Not to anyone. "Before."

Beru, kneeling in the dirt, lifted his head. He looked at Touka on the step. He did not say anything. But he inclined his head toward her. Just slightly. The way you nod at someone who has just told you something true.

"Four minutes," said Ori.

* * *

At five, Mau sent them back.

"Igris. Beru. Back."

"My liege."

"My King." Beru looked at Frieren. "Queen. I apologise—"

"Beru," said Frieren.

He stopped.

"Tomorrow," said Frieren, "don't."

Beru looked at her.

"Don't apologise," said Frieren. "Tomorrow. Or the day after. Igris is right. It stops meaning anything." She drank the last of her coffee. "Say good morning. That's enough."

Beru was silent for a long moment.

"Good morning," he said slowly. As if trying it on. "Queen of my King."

"Good morning, Beru."

He went down into the dirt all at once, the cold blue lights last. Igris went smoothly, the long cloak last.

Ori closed the watch.

"Five minutes," she said. "Exactly. Not five and a bit."

* * *

He did not apologise on Day 366.

He came up at dawn on Mau's side, a pace behind and to the right. He looked at Frieren on the step. He was silent for a long, visible, painful moment, as if something inside him were struggling very hard.

"Good morning," he said at last. "Queen of my King."

"Good morning, Beru," said Frieren.

Beru shook very slightly with the effort of not saying anything else.

Igris, behind Frieren, said nothing. But Mau saw the cold blue lights behind his visor turn, very briefly, toward the Ant King. He thought, if a knight with no face could look approving, that was what it looked like.

On Day 367, Mau remade Tank.

He did it at dawn, before the five minutes, sitting on the back step with his hand flat on the dry ground of the yard. It cost less than Beru had. Tank was simpler. A bear wanted to be a bear. The pieces came up out of the dirt and found each other: the great dark shape, the four legs, the ice plates, black now, the head lowering until its jaw rested on the ground at Mau's feet.

The two small cold lights.

Ichika, on the step beside Touka, looked at the bear for a long time.

Then she looked at Ori, at the kitchen door, with the watch.

"Different colour?" said Ichika.

"Different colour," said Ori. "It's allowed."

Ichika considered this. Then she got up off the step and walked across the yard, barefoot, to the enormous dark bear lying in the dirt. She put one small hand flat on its nose. The way Ori had put her hand on Lantern's jaw in the north field at dawn.

Tank did not move.

He breathed out, very slowly, through his dark nose. It stirred the dust of the yard round Ichika's bare feet.

"Cold," said Ichika, satisfied, and went back to her mother.

Ori wrote it on the list. She did not show anyone that line. But Mau saw it later, pinned to Umaru's wall.

*6. Tank. (The notice.) A big thing that doesn't stop. (Like Stark.) (Idiot.) Remade. Ichika says cold.*
