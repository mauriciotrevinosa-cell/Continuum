*When: Day 368, morning. The seventh night falls tonight. Anko's board has a third plank now. Under FLOOR on it: 41 to 48, over four days, because cleared floors are quick to walk. Two of the lines have *(No check.)* beside them, and one line has no floor at all. Under today: 46, 47, 48 already done before dawn. Then 49, then 50.*

The third plank had gone up on Day 365, the morning after the north field.

Anko had nailed it up herself. Then she had stood back from the board and looked at all three planks for a long time. Forty-eight floors. Thirty-odd parties. Nearly every name in the house somewhere on it.

*Day 365. Mau, Yuta, Maki, Kaneki. From 40. Floor 41: a field of mirrors. Kaneki listened. Floor 42: rain upward. Cleared, both.* *(No check.)*

*Day 366. Mau, Rem, Ram, Fern. From 40. Floor 43: a clock tower. Ram fixed it with one breath of wind. Floor 44: a forest of paper. Fern's lights did not burn it. Floor 45: a flooded street. Again. Cleared, all three.* *(No check.)*

*Day 367. Not today. (Shoko.) (He heard it.) (Frieren took him to the river bend. They came back at dark. He had not climbed anything.)*

*Day 368, before dawn. Checked: fine. Mau, Gojo, Stark, Eren. From 40. Floor 46: sand. Floor 47: a bridge again. Igris held the far end again. Floor 48: a garden with a gate that would only open for someone who had never been angry. Eren could not open it. Stark could not open it. Gojo could not open it. Mau opened it. Gojo is still talking about it. Cleared, all three.*

Under that, in Gojo's hand, added without permission:

*He has DEFINITELY been angry. I've SEEN it. —S.G.*

And under that, in Mau's hand, added the same evening:

*Not at the gate.*

* * *

"Eren went in," said Yuka, at the board, on the morning of Day 368.

"He asked," said Mau.

"He's never asked before."

"Himmel has the north end today," said Mau. "Eren had a morning with nothing in it." He paused. "He came to the clinic door while Shoko was looking at my eyes and said, *if there's room.* That was all."

Yuka looked at the board. At Eren's name, between Gojo and Stark.

"Was he all right?"

"He was very good," said Mau. "He fought like he'd been fighting all his life. And then at the gate on forty-eight, when he couldn't open it, he laughed." He looked at the board. "I've never heard Eren laugh in a fight."

Anko, writing under today, did not look up.

"He laughed at breakfast too," she said. "This morning. At something Mikasa said." She wrote. "I've lived here as long as he has. I'd never heard it before this summer."

She finished writing.

*Day 368. Mau, Frieren, Milim, Yuka. From 40. Floor 49. Then 50 (guardian).*

She looked at it. Then she looked at Mau.

"Fifty," she said.

"Ciel says the cup's full enough," said Mau. "Not full. Enough."

"And Gojo?"

"Gojo looked from forty-eight," said Mau. "He said it's not hungry. Not like Beru. He said it's—" He stopped. "He said it's *fast*. And it's in the sky."

Anko looked at the four names. Mau. Frieren, who could float. Milim, who could fly. Yuka, who had a rope.

"Sky," she said.

"Sky."

She wrote it under *Then 50*, and underlined it once.

* * *

Forty-nine was a stair.

They came up from forty-eight, past the garden with the gate that had opened for Mau and nobody else, and stood at the top of a stair that went up into the sky.

Not a stair of stone. A stair of cloud. White and soft-edged and faintly moving, like breath on a cold morning. It went up in a long slow curve into open blue, with nothing on either side of it. No walls. No railings. Below it, far below, more cloud, white and endless, like the floor of the world.

The wind came across it from the side.

It was not like the wind on twenty-two. That had been a great deal of wind, Yuka said, in one direction, all the time. This was gusts. Sudden. Strong. From nowhere. Then gone. Then again, from somewhere else.

The notice came up.

*Floor 49 of 100.*

*Objective: reach the top.*

Milim looked at the cloud stair. At the open sky on either side of it. At the white endless floor of cloud far below.

Her face lit up.

"*Sky*," she said.

"Not yet," said Yuka. She had the ribbon round her wrist, the red one, tied to Milim's ankle. She had the rope on her shoulder as well. The long coil and the short one. "Fifty is sky. This is a stair. You walk on stairs."

"I don't walk on anything."

"You walk on this one," said Yuka. "Because if the wind takes you off, I'm on the other end of the ribbon, and I'm not floating."

Milim looked at the ribbon. At Yuka's wrist. At Yuka.

"Oh," she said.

"Yes," said Yuka. "*Oh*."

* * *

They climbed.

Frieren first, floating a hand above the cloud, because cloud did not need to hold her. Mau behind her, on foot. The cloud held him, soft and springy under his boots, like walking on moss. Then Yuka, with the rope ready. Milim last, walking. Visibly walking. Every step a tremendous act of will.

The wind came halfway up.

It came all at once from the left, so hard it was like being shoved by a wall. Mau went down on one knee on the cloud. Frieren drifted sideways a body-length before she steadied. Yuka staggered.

And Milim went off the stair.

She did not mean to. The gust took her the way it took a leaf, off the edge of the cloud stair, out into the open sky. The red ribbon went taut between her ankle and Yuka's wrist.

Yuka went with her.

Off the edge. Into the open blue. With the white floor of cloud very far below.

"*YUKA*—"

The long coil was already off Yuka's shoulder. Mau saw it go. He saw her throw it, falling, without looking. The way she threw it at the fence posts every evening with Maki watching from the step. The way she had thrown it at the guardian's ankle on ten. It went out across the gap, the weighted end first, and wrapped round Mau's arm, round the arm he had put out without thinking, and pulled tight.

Mau held on.

He was on one knee on the cloud stair with the rope round his forearm and Yuka hanging off the end of it over the open sky. And Milim hanging off the end of the red ribbon below Yuka, upside down, with her eyes very wide.

"I've got you," said Mau.

"I know," said Yuka. Her voice was very calm. "Grandma says, if you're falling, throw at the thing that won't let go." She looked up at him along the rope. "You never let go."

Then Milim, below her, upside down on the ribbon, said, "*Can I fly now?*"

"*Yes*," said Yuka and Mau together.

Milim flew.

She did not swoop. She did not shriek. She went up, slowly, the right way up. She came up past Yuka on the rope, and got an arm round her, and lifted. Gently. Carefully. Like carrying something precious. She brought Yuka back up over the edge of the cloud stair and set her down on her feet beside Mau. Then she landed beside her, very carefully, the right way up.

"Slower," said Milim. "I did it slower. Like the rule."

"You did," said Yuka. Her hands were shaking now. They had not been shaking on the rope. "You did it very slowly."

"Number three of the amendment," said Milim, with enormous pride. "*Milim has to come down slower.*" She looked at the rope round Mau's forearm. "That was really good throwing."

"Grandma," said Yuka.

"Grandma," Milim agreed solemnly.

* * *

They reached the top.

It took a long time. The wind came twice more. The second time, Mau put the thin space round the four of them, against the gusts, the way he had on thirty against the blizzard. Inside it the wind stopped, and they could walk. It cost a little. Not the cup. The other thing. He did not mind.

At the top of the cloud stair there was nothing.

No door. No arch. No gate. The cloud just stopped, at a flat white edge, and beyond it was sky. Open. Blue. Enormous. With other clouds in it, far off. And shapes. Islands. Floating. Rock and grass and trees, hanging in the open sky with nothing under them. Dozens of them, scattered across the blue, at every height.

And between them, very far off, something moving. Fast. Too far to see clearly. A dark shape with wings, going from one floating island to another faster than anything Mau had ever seen fly.

*Floor 49 cleared.*

*Level 51. One hundred and nine points unspent.*

Mau read it out. Nobody said anything about the points.

They were all looking at the shape with wings.

"That's fifty," said Frieren. "Isn't it."

"I think so," said Mau.

Milim was looking at the dark shape going between the islands. Her face was doing something Mau had not seen it do before. Not excitement. Not delight. Something quieter. Something that looked almost like recognition.

"It flies like me," she said.

Yuka looked at her.

"How do you know?"

"I just know," said Milim. "It flies because it likes it. Not to get somewhere." She watched the shape bank round a floating island and climb. "It's fast because it *wants* to be fast."

Mau looked at the cup with Ciel.

Full enough. Not full. Enough.

"Rest," said Frieren beside him. Not a question. "First. At home. Then fifty."

"Tonight's the roof," said Mau.

"Then tomorrow."

Mau looked at the shape with wings, very far off, climbing into the blue.

"No," he said. "Today. This afternoon. After lunch." He looked at Frieren. "Ciel says enough. Gojo says not hungry. Fast, and in the sky." He looked at Milim, and at Yuka with the rope, and at Frieren, who could float. "And I've got the three people in the house who know the sky best."

Frieren looked at him for a long moment.

"After lunch," she said. "Taihei will insist."

"Taihei always insists."

They went home through the small frame at the top of the cloud stair. On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Two hours ten," she said. "From forty. To forty-nine." She looked at the four of them. At Yuka's hands, which had stopped shaking. At the red mark round Mau's forearm where the rope had pulled tight.

"What happened?" she said.

"Milim fell off," said Yuka. "I went with her. I threw at the thing that wouldn't let go." She paused. "Milim came up slower."

Anko looked at her for a long time.

Then she wrote it down. All of it. In the margin.

*Floor 49. Cloud stair. Wind. Milim fell. Yuka fell. Yuka threw at the thing that wouldn't let go (Mau). Milim came up slower (amendment 3). Cleared.*

And under it:

*Fifty: after lunch. (Sky.)*
