# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 30
## Chapter 62 — So It Can Keep Flying

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 30
**Reading order:** 62
**Continuity state:** The extraction of Kaisel, which does not speak. Mau is already on the island (he jumped). Milim carries Yuka across and Frieren floats over; Frieren saw him count "the far end first" and that is why she did not stop him. He raises Kaisel with the intention Milim asked for. The wing is whole in the shadow form. Floor 50 interface change: registration places rise from ten to twenty. The floor 50 transfer point is secured. Mau reaches level 52 (one hundred and fourteen unspent), with seven registered shadows. The first ride: Kaisel carries Mau (and then Milim flies beside them) in a short loop between the islands, at a real reserve cost. It is a mount with load and time limits, not free flight. Milim names nothing; the notice already did

*When: Day 368, afternoon. Floor 50. A low floating island with a single twisted tree, a stone's throw from the white edge of the cloud stair. Lantern has gone back into the shadow. Kaisel lies in the grass. Mau is sitting beside it with his left thigh bound in Milim's sash. Milim is standing by the tree with her back to it, looking at the sky.*

The notice came up while Milim still had her back turned.

*Operator: trial passed.*

*Guardian defeated.*

And then, below it:

*Shadow extraction available.*

Across the gap, on the white edge of the cloud stair, Frieren was standing with her staff lowered and Yuka beside her with the slack rope. Frieren had not called out when he jumped. She had not called out when the barb went through. Mau could see her face from here, small across the gap, and it was perfectly still, the way it went when something had frightened her more than she would say.

He would hear about the jump. Later. In the east room.

"Milim," he said. "Will you go and get them? Slowly."

Milim turned round. Her face was still wet.

"Slowly," she said.

She went back across the gap, floating, the right way up, at a walking pace. She came back with Yuka in her arms, and then went again for Frieren, who did not let herself be carried and floated across on her own beside her, and landed in the grass beside Mau, and knelt, and looked at the sash round his thigh, and at the blood coming through it.

"You jumped," said Frieren.

"Yes."

"Off the stair."

"Yes."

She looked at him for a long moment.

"The far end first," she said. "You counted it. Twice. Round the tree."

"Yes."

"I saw you count." She put her hand flat over the sash, over the wound, and pressed, hard, to slow the blood. "That's the only reason I didn't stop you."

* * *

He sat down.

He had learned. He sat in the grass beside the long dark shape of Kaisel with its broken wing and its open dull red eye, looking at the sky. He put his hand flat on the grass near the dark that was gathering.

He did not need to think very hard about what he was asking.

Milim had asked it for him.

*You fly because you like it,* he thought, at the wyvern's open eye. *Not to get somewhere. Because flying's the best thing there is.* He looked up at the sky. At the floating islands. At the open blue. *I'm not asking you to stop. I'm asking you to keep flying. With us. For us. Over a house. Where people will look up and see you and be glad.*

He looked at Milim, standing by the tree.

*She asked me,* he thought. *So you could keep flying. That's all I'm asking. Keep flying.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the grass.

Through the wyvern. Through the black scales with their red sheen, and the long lean body, and the narrow head. The red went out of the scales. The black deepened until there was no shine on it at all. The dull red went out of the eye.

The broken wing straightened.

Mau felt it happen. He felt the cost. Not as much as Beru. Not as much as Igris. More than Corner. The cup went down, steady and definite, and he held the intention.

The crumpled wing unfolded in the grass. Long. Ribbed. Whole. The other wing beside it. Both of them, dark, enormous, spreading out over the grass of the island under the twisted tree like a great dark cloak.

Kaisel stood up.

On two legs. The long neck rising. The narrow head. The long whip of a tail. Wings half spread, the way a bird holds its wings when it has just landed and has not decided yet whether to stay.

Two small cold blue lights where the dull red eyes had been.

It looked at Mau.

It did not speak. Mau had not thought it would. Lantern did not speak. Tank did not speak. It looked at him for a long moment with its cold blue lights.

Then it turned its head.

And looked at Milim.

* * *

Milim had not moved from the tree.

She looked back at the wyvern. At its wings, both of them, whole. At its cold blue eyes on her.

Kaisel lowered its head.

Not to the ground. Not the way Lantern lowered its head to Ori, or Tank to Mau's feet. It lowered its long neck in a slow curve until its narrow head was level with Milim's face, a hand's breadth away. And it breathed out. Very slowly. The air from its nostrils stirred her pink hair.

Milim made a small sound.

She put her hand on its head. Between the eyes. Where she had put it before, when it was broken.

"Both wings," she said.

Kaisel breathed out again.

"You can fly," said Milim. Her face was wet again. "You can keep flying."

* * *

*Floor 50 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 or 50.*

*Level 52. One hundred and fourteen points unspent.*

And then, below that, a line Mau had not seen before.

*Registration places increased: 10 → 20.*

He read it twice.

*Registered shadows: 7. Places: 20.*

*The interface changed,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *At fifty. Halfway. The places doubled.* A pause. *One place each, however strong. That doesn't mean you can raise twenty. Every one still has to think you're worth rising for.*

*I know.*

*I'm saying it so you know.*

He read it out. All of it. Milim listened by the tree with her hand still on Kaisel's head. Across the gap, on the white edge of the cloud stair, Frieren and Yuka listened too. Frieren could hear anything at that distance if she chose to. Yuka could not, so Frieren told her.

"Twenty," called Yuka across the gap.

"Twenty places," said Mau. "Six shadows."

"Umaru's going to need a fourth wall," called Yuka.

* * *

Mau looked at Kaisel.

At the wings, whole. At the long lean body, dark. At the cold blue eyes, still on Milim.

He had a thought. He did not decide to have it. It came.

*Ciel. Can it carry me?*

Ciel was quiet for a moment.

*Probably,* she said. *It's built to carry. You can feel that. In the shape of it.* A pause. *Calling it costs nothing now. It's yours. Flying is its own strength, not yours. But it's new, and it's never carried anyone. A few minutes. Not more. And not high. And not far from the island.* Another pause. *And Mau. Your own floating is still small. If it lets go of you, you're not going to float. You're going to fall.*

*Then I'll hold on.*

*Then hold on,* said Ciel.

He stood up.

He walked to Kaisel. The wyvern turned its head from Milim and looked at him. He put his hand on its neck, at the base, where the wings met the body. Cold. Like stone in shade. Like all of them.

"Can I?" he said.

Kaisel looked at him.

Then it lowered itself. Folded its legs. Brought its body down to the grass, wings half spread, the way a horse kneels.

Milim's mouth fell open.

Mau climbed on.

* * *

It was not like floating.

He had floated. A little. On his own, a hand's breadth, for a few breaths, in the yard, with Shoko watching and Frieren holding his hand. His own small natural hover, which existed and was real and was nowhere near enough to cross a gap like this.

This was not that.

Kaisel stood up under him. He held on to the base of its neck with both hands and his knees. It took two steps across the grass of the island, three, toward the edge.

And spread its wings.

And went.

Off the edge of the island. Into the open sky. The grass dropped away beneath them. The twisted tree. Milim's upturned face. The white floor of cloud, very, very far below. And the wings beat, once, twice, great slow strokes, and they were not falling. They were rising.

Mau did not breathe.

The wind was in his face. Cold and clean and enormous. The floating islands went past on either side. Rock and grass and trees, hanging in the blue. Kaisel banked, gently, round one of them. Mau felt the whole world tilt and come back. He held on, and did not fall, and laughed.

He did not mean to. It came out of him all at once, the way it had in the garden on five, and under the lanterns. Startled out of him by the sky.

Beside him, suddenly, there was Milim.

She had come up off the island. She was flying beside Kaisel's wing, a hand's breadth away, the right way up, at exactly its speed. Her face was turned toward him, and she was laughing too. The red ribbon was streaming from her ankle and wound round her wrist.

"*You're flying!*" she shouted, over the wind.

"*I'm being flown!*" he shouted back.

"*That counts!*"

They went round the island with the twisted tree. Once. Twice. Not high. Not far. Kaisel's wings beat slow and steady. Milim flew beside them, matching every turn. On the white edge of the cloud stair, two small figures watched. Yuka had both hands over her mouth. Frieren was standing very still, with her staff held loosely at her side, and her face turned up.

*Two minutes,* said Ciel. *It's tiring. Its own strength, not yours. Bring it down.*

Mau leaned. He did not know if it would understand. It did. Kaisel turned in a long slow curve, down, toward the island, and landed in the grass under the twisted tree. It folded its wings, and knelt.

Mau climbed off.

His legs did not work. He sat down in the grass. He was still laughing. He could not stop.

Milim landed beside him.

She did not say anything. She sat down in the grass beside him and looked at Kaisel, kneeling with its wings folded, its cold blue eyes on both of them.

"So it can keep flying," she said.

"So it can keep flying," said Mau.

* * *

They went home through the small frame by the seal. The seal had appeared at the far end of the island, past the twisted tree. Milim went back across the gap first, slowly, carrying Yuka. Then Frieren, floating. Mau went last. He had sent Kaisel back into the shadow, and the thread had stopped, and the cup was lower than he liked.

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Three hours five," she said. "From forty. To fifty." She looked at the four of them. At Milim's face, which had been wet and was dry now. At Mau, who could not seem to stop smiling.

"What happened?" she said.

Mau thought about it.

"A wyvern," he said. "Called Kaisel. It flies because it likes it." He paused. "Milim asked me to make it mine. So it could keep flying." He paused again. "And then it flew me. Round an island. Twice."

Anko looked at him for a long time.

Then she wrote it down.

*Floor 50. Sky. Kaisel (the notice). A wyvern. Flies because it likes it. (Milim.) Places: 20. Mau was flown. Twice. Cleared.*

And under it, smaller:

*Halfway.*
