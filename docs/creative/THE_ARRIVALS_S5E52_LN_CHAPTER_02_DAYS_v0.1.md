# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 2 — Days

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18, DH-126)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 2
**Continuity state:** Day 369, morning, from Fern's point of view. The ledger page *Days*, which she opened on Day 366 after "The Check" and the long room, now has more names than the six of its first week. Nobody was assigned. People came to her one at a time and asked for a day with Mau, somewhere he can't climb, and she wrote down what they chose. Himmel: tea at the columns. Frieren: the river bend. Ori: a plan of her own. Umaru: the card game and the small true thing she is owed. Mai: the garden on five. Anko: something she won't say yet. Stark: nothing at all, just a nap in the same field. Fern's rules for the page are no more than two in a week, never on a tower day, and never told to Mau as a schedule. Mau finds the page by accident, understands it, and does not say anything; he goes to the first name. The page is the house's answer to the long room, not a cage. No powers

*When: Day 369, early morning. The house is waking slowly after the flight. Fern has the ledger open at the long table before anyone else is down, the way she always does. It is not open at the accounts.*

The page said *Days* at the top.

Fern had written it on Day 366, after Shoko came to the long table with her coffee and said one sentence and went away again. She had turned to a fresh page that she had never used, the way she kept a fresh page at the back of every ledger for things she did not yet know she would need, and written the heading in her small exact hand, and then looked at the empty page under it for a long time.

She had not known what to put there.

The house had known.

* * *

Himmel came first.

The morning after the long room, before breakfast, with his hair brushed again, which Fern took as a good sign. He did not sit down. He stood at the end of the table with his hands behind his back, the way he stood at the gate.

"I hear you keep a page," he said.

Fern looked at him. She had told nobody.

"Seiko," said Himmel, by way of explanation. "Seiko hears everything." He paused. "I'd like a day. An evening. Not a long one." He looked toward the front of the house, toward the gate and the two red columns. "Tea, at the columns. At dusk. He brought me tea there once, a long time ago. I'd like him to do it again." He smiled. "I'll be facing the right way this time."

Fern wrote it down.

*Himmel. Tea at the columns. Dusk. (He'll be facing the right way.)*

* * *

Frieren came second.

She did not ask. She took the pencil out of Fern's hand and wrote it herself, under Himmel's line, in her own untidy hand.

*Frieren. The river bend. A spell I haven't cast for anyone.*

Then she gave the pencil back.

"Not yet," she said. "When he can walk that far. Or I'll float him." She looked at the page. "Don't tell him."

"I wasn't going to."

"I know," said Frieren. "I'm telling myself."

* * *

Ori came third, with her own piece of paper, already folded, which she unfolded on the table in front of Fern and smoothed out flat.

It was a plan. Numbered. Seven lines.

"It's for when Lantern's in pieces," said Ori. "If he ever is. It's in case." She looked at Fern very seriously. "You can copy it. You don't have to. It's on my wall too."

Fern copied it. All seven lines.

*Ori. (Her plan. Seven lines. In case.)*

* * *

Umaru came with a card.

"He owes me a small true thing," she said. "From the winner's rule. He's been owing it for weeks." She put the card down on the page, face up, like a stamp. "The Ministry wishes to collect. At the card table. With the fort." She paused. "On a day he's not hurt. If there is one."

Fern wrote it down.

*Umaru. The card table. The small true thing he owes.*

* * *

Then, over the next days, without Fern asking anyone:

Mai, who did not speak at the long table, came on the fifth day and stood beside Fern's chair until Fern looked up. "The garden," said Mai. "On five. Where the flower came from." That was all. Fern wrote it.

Anko, who wrote everything down, came on the sixth and said she had a day but did not know yet what it was for, and asked Fern to leave a line with her name and nothing after it. Fern left the line.

*Anko. ———*

Stark came on the seventh, scratching the back of his neck.

"I don't have anything," he said. "To do. With him. I'm not good at things." He looked at the page. "Can I just nap? In the same field? At the same time?"

Fern looked at him for a long moment.

"Yes," she said.

*Stark. A nap. Same field. Same time.*

She did not tell him it was the best line on the page. She thought it.

* * *

By the end of the first week there had been six names. By this morning, the morning after the flight, there were nine, and the page had rules, which Fern had written at the bottom in smaller letters, because every page in her ledgers had rules.

*No more than two in a week. He has to heal between.*

*Never on a tower day.*

*He is not told. It is not a schedule. It is not a duty. If he says no, it is no.*

And under that, smallest of all, because she had thought about the long room for three nights before she wrote it:

*This is not to stop him. It is so there is somewhere to come back to.*

* * *

Mau found it by accident.

He came down early, the morning after the flight, because his shoulder woke him and Frieren was finally asleep and he did not want to wake her by lying there thinking. The long table was empty except for Fern and the ledger. She had gone to the kitchen to see whether Rem had the bread in. The ledger was open.

He did not mean to read it.

He read it anyway. *Days.* Himmel's line. Frieren's, in her own untidy hand. Ori's seven lines, copied. Umaru's card. Mai. Anko, with nothing after her name. Stark's nap.

The rules at the bottom.

The last line, smallest of all.

He stood at the end of the long table for a long time.

* * *

Fern came back from the kitchen with two cups. She saw him standing at the ledger. She saw what it was open at.

She did not say anything. She put one cup down in front of his place and sat down in front of the other.

Mau did not say anything either.

He looked at the page a moment longer. Then he closed the ledger, carefully, the way she closed it, and pushed it back across the table to her, and sat down, and drank the tea.

"Fern," he said, after a while.

"Yes."

"Is Himmel facing the right way at dusk today?"

Fern looked at him over her cup.

"I don't know," she said. "You'd have to bring him tea and find out."

He almost smiled.

* * *

He did not go that day. The barb from fifty had gone through his thigh the afternoon before, and Shoko said *not today* to the stairs, let alone the gate.

But fifteen days later, six days after seventy, when Shoko let him walk as far as the gate for the first time with Yuta behind him, the first thing he did with it was ask Touka for two cups of tea.

Fern did not see him do it. She heard about it at dinner.

She opened the ledger at the page called *Days*, found Himmel's line, and wrote beside it, very small:

*Done.*
