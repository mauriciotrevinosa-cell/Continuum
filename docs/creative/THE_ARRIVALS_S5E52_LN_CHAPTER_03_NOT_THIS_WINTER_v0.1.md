*When: Day 370, early morning. The long table. Fern alone with the ledger, before Jinshi's questions. Mikasa has the north end today. She has come to the long table first.*

Fern heard her come down the stair.

She knew the step. Mikasa walked like someone who had been trained to walk quietly and had never stopped. You heard her only if you were listening for her, and Fern was always listening for everyone. She came down the stair and across the long room and stopped at the end of the long table, across from Fern, and did not sit down.

Fern looked up from the ledger.

She looked at Mikasa's face. Then at her right hand, at her side. Closed.

She put her pen down.

"Sit down," said Fern.

* * *

Mikasa sat down.

She put her closed hand on the table. She opened it.

One coin. Small, warm, no named metal. On her palm. She had carried it since the tenth night, on the bridge, when every stayer had felt one appear at sunset. Fern had seen it, once or twice, in all that time. Mikasa did not take it out.

She had taken it out now.

"Armin," said Mikasa.

Fern did not say anything.

She turned the ledger back. A long way back. To the winter, the snow, the long table. To Day 40, the day of the List. To a line in her own hand, from a long time ago, under the heading *THE LIST*:

*Mikasa: Armin. Not this winter.*

And further on, in another winter, under *COINS (HELD)*:

*Mikasa: Armin (proposed). Eren: Armin (proposed). The house waits.*

She turned the ledger round so Mikasa could see.

Mikasa looked at the two lines for a long time.

"Not this winter," she read.

"You said it on the day of the List," said Fern. "At the long table. When I made the agenda. Everyone said a name, or didn't. You said Armin. And then you said *not this winter*." She paused. "And Eren had said it before that. On the day of the first snow. *Armin. Maybe. Someday.* From the top bunk."

"I remember."

"And then the house waited."

"The house waited," said Mikasa.

She looked at the coin on her palm.

"It's summer," she said.

* * *

Fern picked up her pen again.

She did not write anything yet.

"Why now?" she said.

She always asked. Not to stop anyone. To understand. Nijika had told her first, and Rem, and Yuta, and Maki, and Umaru, and Kaneki three weeks ago with two coins on his palm. Every one of them had sat across from her at this table, and she had asked, and every one of them had a reason that was theirs.

Mikasa looked at the coin.

"Himmel," she said.

Fern's eyebrows went up a fraction.

"He stands my hour," said Mikasa. "Every other day. Since the festival. At the north end." She turned the coin over. "On his days, I don't stand anywhere. I sleep late. Or I don't. I sit in the south field with Eren. Where the grass came back." She was quiet for a moment. "I've stood somewhere every day since I can remember. In my world. Here. Someone always had to stand somewhere, and it was always me." She looked at the coin. "And now every other day, it isn't."

Fern did not say anything.

"Eren says *someday*," said Mikasa. "Now. He never said *someday*. He used to say *when it's over*. He said it for years. And now he says *someday*. About the sea. About a lot of things." She paused. "He laughed in the tower. Mau told me. On forty-eight, at a gate that wouldn't open for him. He laughed."

"I heard," said Fern.

"And there's room," said Mikasa. "Kaneki's family came. There's a room with two windows. And Rimuru chalked another one, at the end of the west wing. For whoever comes." She closed her hand round the coin. "There's room."

She looked at Fern.

"When I said *not this winter*," she said, "it was because we didn't know what was coming. The house was new. The walls weren't built. Eren had his number. I didn't know if we'd still be here in the spring." She was quiet. "I didn't want to bring Armin to a place where he'd have to stand somewhere every day too."

"And now?"

Mikasa opened her hand again. She looked at the coin.

"Now there are people who'll stand his hour for him," she said. "Every other day."

* * *

Fern wrote it down.

*Day 370. Mikasa. One coin (hers). Armin.*

Then she asked the questions.

"Eren?"

"Eren knows," said Mikasa. "I told him last night. In the south field." She paused. "He's keeping his. His coin. He said, *you say his name first. You always said it first.*" She looked at the table. "He said he'd keep his for something else. Someday."

Fern wrote: *Eren: knows. Keeps his own coin. ("Someday.")*

"When will Armin come from?"

Mikasa was quiet for a long time.

"I don't know," she said. "That's the part I don't know." She turned the coin over. "Eren and I left. Both of us. Not at the same time, I don't think. But we were both gone from there." She looked at the coin. "Every world keeps its own clock. Seika came from the same days Kessoku vanished. Ram came from wherever Ram was. Kaneki's daughter was older than he left her." She paused. "Armin will come from after. After we were both gone. That's all I know. I don't know how long after. I don't know what happened to him in between."

"Does that frighten you?"

Mikasa looked up at her.

"Yes," she said.

Fern waited.

"He'll know things," said Mikasa. "About our world. That happened after. That I don't know. That Eren doesn't know." She looked at the coin. "He'll have been looking for us. He would have. Armin always looked for things. He'll have looked for us for however long it was. And he won't have found us." She was quiet. "I don't know what that does to someone. Looking for that long."

"Kaneki's wife kept the shop open," said Fern quietly. "Every night. Until closing. In case he walked in."

Mikasa looked at her.

"She told me," said Fern. "Over the good cups." She paused. "She was angry. And then she got on with it." She looked at the coin. "Armin will be whatever he is. And then he'll get on with it. And you'll be here."

Mikasa did not say anything for a long time.

* * *

"The room," said Fern.

"Rimuru's chalk," said Mikasa. "At the end of the west wing. Past the room with two windows." She paused. "Wakana says five days. If Taihei does the far wall again."

Fern wrote: *Room: the chalked corner. Wakana, five days. Taihei, far wall.*

"Who waits?"

"Eren," said Mikasa. "At the hall." She was quiet. "And me. Inside. Where he'll appear." She looked at the coin. "And whoever wants. On the step. Not a crowd."

"Not a crowd," said Fern, writing.

"Armin's not like Touka," said Mikasa. "He won't hit anyone. He'll want to know everything. Everyone's name. Where everything is. How it works." She almost smiled. The thing at the edges. "He'll want a map."

Fern looked up.

"A map," she said.

"He always wanted maps," said Mikasa. "Of everything. The world outside. The sea. The places nobody had been." She looked at the coin. "He used to read about them. In a book his grandfather had. About the outside. Fire water and ice land and sand." She stopped. "He'll want a map of here."

Fern was quiet for a moment.

Then she wrote: *He'll want a map. (Anko. Hollis. Senku.)*

* * *

"When?" said Fern.

Mikasa looked at the coin for a long time.

"Tonight," she said. "At the north end. At the end of my hour." She closed her hand. "Where I stand."

Fern wrote it.

*Tonight. The north end. End of her hour.*

She looked at the page. At all of it. At the line from Day 40, a long way back: *Not this winter.* And this one.

"Thank you for telling me first," she said.

"That's the rule."

"That's the rule," said Fern. "Thank you anyway."

Mikasa stood up.

At the bottom of the stair, she stopped. She did not turn round.

"Fern."

"Mm."

"Himmel," said Mikasa. "Tomorrow is his day. At the north end." She paused. "Will you tell him? Before. That tomorrow, when he stands there, someone new will be in the house." She was quiet. "I'd like him to know. Before he stands my hour. That he's standing it for someone else too now."

Fern looked at her.

"I'll tell him," she said.

Mikasa went up.

Fern sat at the long table with the ledger open for a long time. Then she turned to the day's page and wrote one more line, very small:

*She stands her own hour tonight. Not because someone has to. Because it's where she wants him to find her.*
