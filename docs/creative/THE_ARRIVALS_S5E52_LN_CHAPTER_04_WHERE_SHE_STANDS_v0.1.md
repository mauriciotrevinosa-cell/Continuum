# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 4 — Where She Stands

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 4

*When: Day 370, dusk. The north end. The last ten minutes of Mikasa's hour. The lime line runs white across the field toward the red stake. The hills are going purple. Behind her, at the edge of the timber yard, a stack of Wakana's twisted oak, mostly gone into the kilns now, and someone behind it, pretending.*

Mikasa stood where she always stood.

A little back from the lime, where you could see the field and the line and the red stake and the low hills beyond. Her blades at her hip, the new ones and the one old one. Her hands at her sides. Her eyes on the hills.

She had stood here every day for longer than she could remember. Every afternoon. One hour. Before the titan. Before the wall. And now, since the festival, every other day, because Himmel stood the days between. On his days she sat in the south field with Eren, where the grass had come back. She had not known what to do with her hands at first. She was learning.

Today was hers.

She had stood the whole hour. She had watched the hills. Nothing had come over them. Nothing ever did, most days. That was not the point.

The coin was in her right hand.

It had been in her right hand for the whole hour. Closed. Warm. She had not taken it out of her pocket at the start of the hour. She had taken it out at the long table at dawn, in front of Fern, and she had not put it back since. She had carried it all day like that, in her closed hand, through breakfast and lunch and the hour. Now there were ten minutes left.

Behind her, at the edge of the timber yard, someone shifted behind the stack of twisted oak.

She did not turn round.

* * *

He always came.

At the end of her hour. Every day, for as long as she could remember here. He would come across the timber yard and stop behind something, a stack of wood or the corner of the bark shelter or the end of the barn, and wait. Where he thought she could not see him. Pretending to look at something.

She could always see him.

She had never told him. It had seemed like the kind of thing you did not tell someone. That you could see them waiting for you. That they had never once, in all that time, been hidden.

She thought about that now. With the coin warm in her closed hand and the hills going purple.

*Something with a tomorrow in it,* Kaneki had said to him. Eren had told her, in the south field, on the day she did not stand here. *Kaneki said to stop saying* when it's over. *He said to say something with a tomorrow in it.*

She turned round.

* * *

"Eren," she said.

Behind the stack of twisted oak, there was a silence.

Then a very small sound. The sound of someone who has been caught and is deciding whether to pretend they have not been.

"I know you're there," said Mikasa. "I've always known you're there. Every day. Behind the wood. Or the barn. Or the corner of the shelter." She paused. "You've never been hidden. Not once."

Another silence.

Then Eren came out from behind the stack of twisted oak.

He came slowly, with his hands in his pockets, the way he walked when he did not know what his face was doing. He stopped at the edge of the timber yard. He looked at her across the dry ground. His face was doing a great many things at once.

"Every day?" he said.

"Every day."

"You never said."

"You never asked," said Mikasa.

Eren looked at her for a long moment.

Then he laughed. Not much. The new one. The one she had heard for the first time this summer, at breakfast, at something she said. Startled out of him.

"I thought I was very good at hiding," he said.

"You're very bad at hiding," said Mikasa. "You're the worst at hiding of anyone I've ever known." She held out her free hand. "Come here. Don't wait over there. Stand here. With me."

* * *

He came.

Across the dry ground. He stopped beside her. Not in front. Beside. Close. Where she could feel him at her shoulder. He looked where she was looking. The field. The lime. The red stake. The hills, going purple, with nothing coming over them.

"I've never stood here," he said. "With you. In your hour."

"I know."

"Is it always this quiet?"

"Always," said Mikasa. "That's not the point."

"What's the point?"

Mikasa looked at the hills.

"Someone has to stand here," she said. "So that everyone behind them doesn't have to." She was quiet. "That's what I always thought. That's what Himmel says now, on his days. Someone has to." She opened her right hand. The coin lay on her palm, warm, in the last of the light. "But tonight I'm standing here for a different reason."

Eren looked at the coin.

"Because it's where you want him to find you," he said.

Mikasa looked at him.

"Fern told me," said Eren. "This afternoon. She found me in the timber yard. She said, *Mikasa's standing her own hour tonight. Not because someone has to.*" He looked at the hills. "She said, *because it's where she wants him to find her.*"

Mikasa did not say anything for a long time.

"Fern writes everything down," she said at last.

"Fern writes everything down," Eren agreed.

* * *

Five minutes left.

The hills had gone from purple to grey. The first stars were coming out above them. The small steady one, low and a little apart. Further up, the cluster Frieren had named. Somewhere behind them, at the far edge of the timber yard, the kilns had started breathing red, because Kaneki had started his shift.

Mikasa closed her hand round the coin.

You held it. You thought of the person. That was all. Ciel had said it was the simplest thing the Goddess had ever made.

She thought of Armin.

Not the way he had been at the end. She did not know how he had been at the end. She had not been there for the end. That was the hard part. She thought of him the way he had been on the wall, a long time ago, when they were small. Sitting on the top of it with his knees up and his grandfather's book open on them. Reading out loud about the outside. Fire water. Ice land. Sand. And the sea. A body of salt water so big you could not see the other side.

She thought of his face when he said *sea*.

The coin went warm.

Warmer.

And a voice, from nowhere, from everywhere, from the inside of her own closed hand:

*You are sure? Armin Arlert.*

Mikasa did not breathe.

Beside her, Eren had gone completely still. She felt it at her shoulder. She did not know whether everyone near a coin heard the voice. He had. She could tell.

"Yes," said Mikasa.

Her voice came out very level. The way it came out when something mattered too much to let it shake.

"Yes. I'm sure."

* * *

The coin went warmer still.

She held on. The way everyone before her had held on. Because you held on until it was done.

She held on, and thought of the boy on the wall with his grandfather's book. Of the outside, and the sea. Of the times he had stood between her and Eren when they fought, small and frightened and stubborn. Of the times he had been right when they were both wrong. Of how he had always, always, been the one who looked for things.

He would have looked for them. She knew it. Wherever he had been, after. He would have looked.

*We're here,* she thought. *Stop looking. We're here.*

And then it was gone.

Not cold. Gone. Her hand was empty. Her palm, when she opened it, was pink. As if she had been holding something warm for a very long time and had just put it down.

She looked at her empty palm in the starlight.

"It carried," said Eren. Very quietly. At her shoulder.

"It carried," said Mikasa.

* * *

They did not move for a long time.

They stood at the north end, a little back from the lime, side by side, close, and looked at the hills. Nothing came over them. Nothing ever did. The stars came out, more and more of them, strange ones.

"He'll come in the morning," said Eren eventually. "At the hall. At a bell. Fern said."

"I know."

"He'll want to know everything," said Eren. "Everyone's name. Where everything is. How it works." He paused. "He'll want a map."

Mikasa almost smiled. The thing at the edges.

"I told Fern that," she said. "This morning. She wrote it down. *He'll want a map.* Anko and Hollis and Senku."

"He'll drive Senku insane," said Eren.

"He'll drive Senku insane," Mikasa agreed. "Senku will love it."

Eren was quiet for a moment.

"Mikasa."

"Mm."

"When he comes," said Eren. "If he knows things. About after. About—" He stopped. He looked at the hills. "About me. Things I didn't do yet. That he saw."

Mikasa did not say anything.

"I don't want to ask him," said Eren. "Not at first. Maybe not ever." His voice was very flat. "I've got no number now. Ciel looked. There's no number. I don't want to find out about a number I don't have any more from someone who saw it run out."

Mikasa turned her head and looked at him.

"Then don't ask," she said.

"What if he tells me anyway?"

"He won't," said Mikasa. "Not unless you ask. He's Armin." She looked back at the hills. "He'll want to know about us. Not tell us about him. Not at first." She paused. "And when he does want to tell, he'll ask first. He always asked first."

Eren did not say anything for a long time.

Then he said, "Something with a tomorrow in it."

"What?"

"That's what I'll say to him," said Eren. "When he comes. Kaneki said. Say something with a tomorrow in it." He took his hands out of his pockets. "I'll say, *we'll take you to the sea.*"

Mikasa looked at him.

At the young man standing beside her at the north end, in her hour. He was not hiding behind anything. He had a tomorrow in his mouth.

"Yes," she said. "Say that."

* * *

They went back across the timber yard together.

Past the kilns, where Kaneki was raking the embers and Touka was on the bench with a cup. Touka looked up as they passed. She looked at Mikasa's right hand, open at her side, the palm still faintly pink in the red light. Then at Mikasa's face.

She did not say anything. She lifted her cup an inch. The way you lift a cup to someone across a counter.

Mikasa nodded.

At the house, at the end of the west wing, past the room with two windows, Wakana was still working by lamplight. Rimuru's chalk corner had walls now. Low ones. A frame. Taihei was at the far end of it, holding a beam while Wakana pegged it. Five days, Wakana had said. It would be five days.

Armin would come in the morning.

He would sleep somewhere else for five nights. Then he would have a room at the end of the west wing, with a window toward the hills.

Mikasa stopped and looked at the frame for a long moment.

"He'll want a window facing the sea," she said.

Wakana looked up from his pegging.

"Which way's the sea?" he said.

Mikasa thought about it. Eren had been found by the sea, a long time ago, by the Searchers. The house had gone there once, all of it, on a family trip, a long time ago now. She pointed. South and west, past the hills, a long way.

"That way," she said.

Wakana looked at where she was pointing. Then at the frame. Then at his chalk lines.

"Five days," he said. "And a window that way. Six."

"Six," said Mikasa.
