# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 6 — Found

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 6
**Continuity state:** Armin arrives in the intake hall at the eighth bell (Day 371). He was searching, with a lamp and a folded map, when something asked him and he said yes. He sees Mikasa first, then Eren. He does not speak about "after". Eren says the tomorrow he prepared ("We'll take you to the sea"); Armin answers that they saw the sea once, and Eren says "not this one". The register: Armin asks to sign and writes *Found them.* His world's history stays unspecified

*When: Day 371, the eighth bell. The intake hall of Arrival House. The register girl lit the stove at five. Mikasa has been standing in the middle of the stone floor since six. Eren is at the door, inside it this time, not behind anything. A few people are on the step outside. Not a crowd. Fern said.*

The register girl had the stove lit by five, and the hall was still cold.

It was always cold. It was the stone floor. She had stopped minding. She knelt in front of the stove and fed it and watched the light come up on the clay walls. Then she went to the desk, where the book was open to the page after Touka's and Ichika's. A fresh page. A sharpened pen.

Mikasa stood in the middle of the floor.

She had been standing there since six. Not moving. Her blades were not at her hip. She had left them in her room. She had decided, Mau thought, when he looked in through the door at seven, that she did not want Armin's first sight of her to be of someone armed. She stood on the stone floor in her plain clothes with her hands at her sides, and her right palm was still faintly pink.

Eren stood at the door.

Inside it. Not behind it. Not round the corner. Inside the door of the intake hall, where Mikasa could see him if she turned her head. Where Armin would see him. He had his hands in his pockets. He did not know what his face was doing. Mau could see that from the step.

The register girl did not say anything to either of them. She sat behind her desk with the pen in her hand and waited too.

* * *

The bell rang eight.

It came from the bell tower on the far side of the settlement, faint through the clay walls. One stroke at a time. Mikasa did not move. Eren took his hands out of his pockets.

Eight.

The air in the middle of the hall moved.

The shimmer. The way air moves over a road in summer. An arm's length in front of Mikasa, about the height of a young man.

And then there was a young man standing on the stone floor.

Slight. Not tall. Fair hair, cut short, falling across his forehead. He had a lamp in one hand. Not lit. An oil lamp, the kind you carry. In his other hand, a piece of paper, folded many times, worn soft at the creases.

He was looking at the paper.

Then he looked up.

* * *

He saw the hall first. Mau saw him see it. The high windows. The clay walls. The stove. The register girl behind her desk. His eyes went round all of it very fast, the way eyes go round a room when their owner is used to taking in a whole room at once, and remembering it.

Then he saw Mikasa.

He stopped.

Everything about him stopped. His eyes. His hands. His breath. He stood on the stone floor with the unlit lamp in one hand and the folded paper in the other and looked at the woman standing an arm's length in front of him with her hands at her sides.

"Mikasa," he said.

Not a question.

Mikasa did not move.

"Armin," she said.

The lamp fell out of his hand.

It hit the stone floor and the glass chimney cracked and the sound was very loud in the cold hall. He did not look at it. He did not seem to know he had dropped it. He was looking at Mikasa. His face was doing the thing faces do when they have been braced against something for so long that they have forgotten how to stop.

"I looked," he said.

His voice cracked.

"I looked," said Armin. "Everywhere. For both of you. I had a map. I marked everywhere I looked." He held up the folded paper in his shaking hand. "Every place. Every road. I crossed them off. One at a time. And you weren't—you were never—"

He stopped.

Mikasa crossed the arm's length between them.

She did not hit him. Mikasa did not hit people she loved. She put her arms round him, and held on, and Armin dropped the map too, and held on back. The two of them stood in the middle of the cold stone floor with a cracked lamp and a fallen map at their feet, holding on.

Neither of them said anything.

The register girl looked down at the book very carefully and did not look up.

* * *

After a long time, Armin lifted his head from Mikasa's shoulder.

He looked past her. At the door.

At Eren.

Eren had not moved from the door. He was standing inside it with his hands at his sides, where they had been since the bell. Not in his pockets. Not hiding. Not behind anything. His face was very white.

Armin looked at him for a long time.

Mau, on the step, could see Armin's face. He saw it do something complicated. Very fast. Many things, one after another, the way a page turns in a wind. Mau did not know what any of them were. He did not know what Armin had seen, after, in his world, that Eren had not seen yet, or would never see now. He saw Armin see Eren, and know things, and decide.

He saw him decide.

All of it went away. Folded up. Put somewhere. And what was left on Armin's face was only a young man looking at his oldest friend in a doorway.

"Eren," he said.

"Armin."

Eren came in from the door.

He walked across the stone floor. He stopped in front of Armin, with Mikasa still beside him. He looked at him. His face was doing a great many things.

He did not say *I'm sorry*. He did not say *what happened* or *how long* or *what did you see*. Mau saw him not say any of them. He saw him swallow them, one at a time.

"We'll take you to the sea," said Eren.

* * *

Armin stared at him.

Then he laughed.

It came out of him all at once, wet, broken, in the middle of his face still wet from Mikasa's shoulder. He laughed and put his hand over his eyes, and his shoulders shook.

"Eren," he said, through his hand. "We've *seen* the sea."

"Not this one," said Eren.

Armin took his hand away from his eyes.

"There's a sea here," said Eren. "In this world. South and west. Past the hills. A long way." He was talking very fast now, the way he talked when he had decided to say something and did not want to stop before it was out. "They found me there. By it. When I came. And the whole house went once. On a trip. And I want—" He stopped. "I want to take you. And Mikasa. Someday. When it's safe to go that far. To a sea that isn't—" He stopped again. "To a sea that's just a sea."

Armin looked at him for a long time.

"A sea that's just a sea," he said.

"Yes."

Armin's face did something again. Mau did not know what it was. Something that was very tired and very old, much older than his face. And then something that was not tired at all.

"Okay," he said. "Yes. Someday."

Eren looked at him.

Then Eren put his arms round both of them. Round Armin, and round Mikasa beside him, both at once, clumsily, the way you put your arms round people when you have never done it before and do not know where your hands go.

The three of them stood in the middle of the cold stone floor and held on.

* * *

The register girl wrote it down.

She did it while they were still holding on. She picked up the pen and found the fresh page and wrote the date at the top in her best handwriting, which was still not very good.

*Day 371. The eighth bell. By coin.*

*Armin Arlert.*

Then she stopped. She did not know what to write after it. *Friend of*. *Brother of*. Neither of them was right. She looked at the three of them in the middle of the floor and could not decide.

Armin saw her.

He let go of Eren and Mikasa. Slowly. He bent and picked up the folded map from the stone floor, worn soft at the creases. He did not pick up the lamp. He came to the desk.

"Is that a register?" he said.

"Yes," said the register girl.

"Can I sign it?"

She looked at him in astonishment. Taihei had asked. Nobody else ever had. People arrived and she wrote them down and that was all.

"Yes," she said. "If you want."

Armin took the pen.

He looked at his name in her handwriting. At the empty space after it, where she had not been able to decide. He looked at it for a long time.

Then he wrote, underneath, in a small neat careful hand, the hand of someone who had made a great many maps:

*Armin Arlert. Found them.*

He put the pen down.

* * *

Outside, on the step, Fern wrote something in the ledger.

Mau did not see what. But he saw Momo, on the step beside her, take the cloth off the basket on her arm. Pear bread. FOR TOMORROW (OR WHENEVER). The smell came out into the cold morning air, warm, golden.

The door of the hall opened.

Mikasa came out first. Then Armin, between her and Eren. He stopped on the threshold and looked at the plaza. At the few people on the step. At Fern with the ledger, and Momo with the basket, and Rem, and Maomao, and Anko with a stub of pencil behind her ear. At Mau and Frieren at the back, by the corner of the hall.

He looked at all of them very fast. The way he had looked at the hall. Taking it in. Remembering it.

Then he looked at the basket. At the smell.

"Is that bread?" he said.

"For tomorrow," said Momo. "Or whenever." She held it out. "Whenever is now."

Armin looked at the bread. At Momo. At Mikasa beside him, and Eren on his other side.

He took a piece.

He did not eat it straight away. He held it in both hands and looked at it. The way Touka had looked at the bread she could not eat. The way you look at something somebody made because you were coming.

"Thank you," he said.

Then he ate it. All of it. Standing on the step. With his eyes closed.

Anko, at the edge of the step, took the stub of pencil from behind her ear. She looked at the young man eating pear bread with his eyes closed between Eren and Mikasa. Then at the folded map in his other hand, worn soft at the creases.

"You make maps," she said.

Armin opened his eyes.

"I—yes," he said. "I used to. I made this one." He held up the folded paper. "Of everywhere I looked."

Anko looked at it.

"I've got a board," she said. "Sightings. Every road. Every route. Everywhere anyone's been from here, for years." She put the pencil back behind her ear. "When you've eaten, I'll show you."

Armin stared at her.

Mikasa, beside him, looked at Fern. Fern did not look up from the ledger. But Mau saw what she had written, later, on the day's page, under Armin's name.

*He'll want a map. (Anko. First thing.)*
