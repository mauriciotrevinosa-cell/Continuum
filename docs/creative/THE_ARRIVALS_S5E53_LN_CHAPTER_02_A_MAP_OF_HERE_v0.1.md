# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 7 — A Map of Here

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 7
**Continuity state:** Armin's first day. Anko shows him the SIGHTINGS board and the route records (the lime, the red stake, Ashby, Fallow, Hollis's runs); Senku shows him the star names and the floor-19 drawing; he starts a map of the house on a blank ledger page Fern gives him; Umaru appoints him Ministry Cartographer (provisional); the fort and Ichika trail him. His own thing is maps, not a military role. He asks questions and does not talk about "after"

*When: Day 371, the rest of the day. Armin has eaten two pieces of pear bread and a bowl of porridge and has been given a clean shirt by Marin, which fits. Anko is waiting at the Searchers' board with her pencil.*

Anko's board was in the long room, on the wall by the door to the yard.

It had been there since the old house. It had moved with them twice. It was three planks wide now, and covered. Pinned papers, some of them years old and gone soft and brown at the edges. Lines in chalk. Lines in ink. Lines in pencil, rubbed out and redrawn. A great rough map in the middle, drawn and redrawn so many times the paper had worn through in places and been patched from behind.

At the top, in Anko's plain hand, very old:

*SIGHTINGS.*

Armin stood in front of it and did not say anything for a very long time.

Anko stood beside him with her pencil behind her ear and let him look.

* * *

"This is the house," said Anko eventually. She pointed. A small square near the middle of the map, drawn and redrawn. "This is the lime. The line. Where the protection ends." A rough white circle round the square, much bigger. "Inside it, nothing that means us harm gets in. The Goddess's doing. We don't know how it works. We know it works." She paused. "It's held. Things have come right up to it. Nothing that meant us harm has crossed it."

Armin looked at the white circle.

"Nothing?"

"Nothing," said Anko. "Titans came. They stopped outside it. Something worse came, once, and walked along it in the night. It didn't come in." She paused. "The line held. I wrote that down. I also wrote down that we don't know where the edge of *held* is." She tapped a red mark at the top of the circle. "This is the red stake. North field. Where I start the round. Nazuna sits on it at night."

"Who's Nazuna?"

"You'll meet her at dusk. She sleeps in the day." Anko moved her finger. "This is the river. The low woods. The timber yard. The kilns, where Kaneki works nights." She moved it again, outside the circle now, along a dotted line going south. "This is the road. To Ashby. A town. A long walk. We trade there, sometimes. Through Hollis. He's a merchant. He brings things." Another dotted line, branching. "This is Fallow. A village. Hollis goes there too. That's where the tobacco seed came from. And the dice."

Armin's eyes were going over the map very fast. Left to right. Top to bottom. The way they had gone round the hall that morning.

"What's that?" he said. He pointed at a patch of the map, west, outside the circle, scribbled over in dark pencil so hard the paper had nearly torn.

Anko looked at it.

"Pell," she said. "A town. We don't go there." She did not say anything else.

Armin looked at her for a long moment.

He did not ask.

Anko saw him not ask. Mau, at the long table behind them with a cup of Touka's coffee, saw her see it. Her face changed very slightly.

"You can ask," she said. "Later. When you know more. It's a long story."

"I'll ask later," said Armin.

* * *

They stood at the board for two hours.

Armin asked everything. Mau had never heard anyone ask so many questions so fast. Not even Ori. Where does this road go. How far is that. How long does it take Hollis. What's past the hills. Has anyone been past the hills. Why not. What did they see. What's this mark. What's that one. Why is this one crossed out.

Anko answered every one.

Mau had never seen Anko answer so many questions. She answered them the way she wrote on the board. Plainly. Exactly. No more than she knew. When she did not know, she said so. *Nobody's been past the hills. Not on foot. Hollis says there's another town but he's never been. Nobody knows.*

At the end of the two hours, Armin stepped back from the board.

He looked at the whole thing. All three planks. Years of sightings and roads and marks.

"Nobody's drawn the house," he said.

Anko looked at him.

"It's on the map," she said. She tapped the small square in the middle.

"That's a square," said Armin. "That's where the house *is*. Nobody's drawn what it is." He looked at the square. "The rooms. The wings. Where everyone sleeps. The yard. The ink tree. The kilns. The workshop." He looked at Anko. "If something came. If someone was hurt. If someone new came and didn't know where anything was." He paused. "There's no map of the inside."

Anko was quiet for a long moment.

"No," she said. "There isn't."

"Can I make one?"

Anko took the pencil out from behind her ear.

She looked at it for a moment. Then she held it out to him.

"Ask Fern for paper," she said.

* * *

Fern gave him a page.

Not a scrap. A page from the back of the ledger, clean and blank, the good paper, the paper she did not give anyone. She tore it out very carefully along the binding and handed it to him across the long table without saying anything.

Armin looked at it.

"This is from your ledger," he said.

"It's from the back," said Fern. "Where the pages are still empty." She looked at him. "Mikasa said you'd want a map. I've been keeping that page since this morning."

Armin held the page in both hands for a long time.

"Thank you," he said.

"Draw the house," said Fern. "I'd like to see it."

* * *

He drew the house.

All afternoon. He started at the long table. Then he got up and walked, because he could not draw what he had not seen, and he had not seen most of it. He walked the east wing and the west wing and the yard and the timber yard. He walked with the page held flat on a board Wakana gave him, and Anko's pencil, and his eyes going everywhere.

He did not walk alone.

The fort followed him. At first at a distance. Then closer. Ori first, with her list, because she wanted to know what he was writing. Then Milim, floating, on the ribbon, which Yuka held. Then Umaru, in her hood and sash, because if anyone was making an official document of the house, the Ministry needed to be consulted.

And Ichika. In her too-long sash, barefoot, holding Ori's sleeve, watching the new person draw with the same expression she used to watch her father rake the embers.

"What's that?" said Ori, looking at the page.

"The east wing," said Armin. "That's the long corridor. That's the east room, at the end." He pointed. "Who sleeps there?"

"Mau and Frieren," said Ori. "And Rimuru in the loft. Over the closet. Not down the hall. Inside." She pointed. "And that's mine. Through the connecting door."

Armin drew the connecting door.

"What's that?" said Umaru, pointing at a small square he had drawn in the yard.

"The ink tree."

"That's not where the ink tree is," said Umaru. "It's further over. By the Summer Annexe." She took the pencil out of his hand without asking and moved it. "There. Officially."

Armin looked at the correction. Then at Umaru.

"Thank you," he said, quite seriously.

Umaru looked at him for a long moment from inside her hood.

"The Ministry," she said slowly, "has a position open."

"A position?"

"Cartographer," said Umaru. "Ministry Cartographer. Provisional." She paused. "It comes with a sash. Eventually. When the Ministry has more cloth."

Armin looked at her. At the hood. At the sash. At the very serious face inside the hood.

"I accept," he said.

Umaru nodded, satisfied, and wrote it on her slate.

*Ministry Cartographer (provisional): Armin. (Accepted.)*

* * *

Senku found him in the evening.

Armin was sitting on the back step with the board on his knees and the page nearly full. East wing, west wing, yard, timber yard, kilns, workshop, clinic, the room with two windows, the frame of the new room at the end of the west wing, with a window facing south and west. Every room labelled in his small neat hand with who slept there. The ink tree, moved by the Ministry. The Summer Annexe. The long table.

Senku stood over him and looked at it for a long time.

"Ten billion percent," said Senku at last, "that's the best map anyone's made since we got here."

Armin looked up.

"It's not finished," he said. "I haven't done the roof. Or the cellars. Or the bath."

"It's still the best map anyone's made since we got here," said Senku. He sat down on the step beside Armin, uninvited. "You like maps."

"I like knowing where things are."

"You like knowing where things *are*," said Senku, "or you like knowing where things are *in relation to each other*?"

Armin looked at him.

"The second one," he said.

Senku grinned. The very wide one.

He took a folded paper out of his breast pocket. He unfolded it on the step between them. Frieren's drawing, from floor 19. A turning sky. Arcs getting smaller toward the middle. A still star at the centre. And Frieren's careful hand underneath: *A sky that turns around north.*

"There's a tower," said Senku. "A hundred floors. Inside it, on the nineteenth floor, there's a sky. That turns. Around one star. Faster than ours." He tapped the still star. "Somebody built that on purpose. I want to know how." He looked at Armin. "You know where things are in relation to each other."

Armin looked at the drawing for a long time.

"A sky inside a building," he said.

"Inside a building."

"Turning around one point." Armin's finger moved over the arcs. "If it turns around one point, everything in it is moving in relation to that point. If you knew how fast, you could—" He stopped. He looked up at the real sky, going dark over the yard, with the first stars coming out. Too many of them. Strange ones. "Which one's yours?"

"*The Hundred and First*," said Senku. He pointed. "That one. Bright. Low in the east."

Armin found it.

"Why that name?"

"Long story," said Senku. "Ten billion percent worth it." He folded up Frieren's drawing and put it back in his pocket. "Tomorrow night. On the roof. With the telescope. I'll tell you."

Armin looked at him.

"There's a telescope?"

"There's a telescope," said Senku. "I made it. With my father. The lens has a flaw near the edge. You'll love it."

* * *

Mikasa found Armin on the back step after dark.

He was still there. The board on his knees. The page, full now, every corner of it. The house, all of it, every room, from above. He had added the roof, and a small square on it labelled *the hatch (squeaks)*.

She sat down beside him.

She did not say anything. She looked at the map for a long time.

Then she pointed at the frame at the end of the west wing. The new room. The one with a window facing south and west.

"That's yours," she said. "In six days."

Armin looked at it.

He had drawn the window. Facing south and west. He had labelled the room in his small neat hand, the way he had labelled every room. Then, when he did not know who it was for, he had written a question mark.

He took Anko's pencil. He rubbed out the question mark, very carefully, with the end of it.

He did not write his own name.

He wrote, instead, very small:

*Toward the sea.*
