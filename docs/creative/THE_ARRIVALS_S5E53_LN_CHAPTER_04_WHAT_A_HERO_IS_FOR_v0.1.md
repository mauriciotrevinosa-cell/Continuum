# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 8 — What a Hero Is For

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 8
**Continuity state:** Himmel's alternate-day north-end hour (Day 373), with Armin, who came to add the north field to his map. Himmel shows him the view (not the road); Armin asks what Himmel is watching for; Himmel says the fighting is so there can be festivals, and that he stands here so two people can sit in a field. Armin, from a world of soldiers, does not talk about it, but writes a line on the map. Himmel's chosen distance is visibly working. No threat

*When: Day 373, mid-afternoon. Himmel's day at the north end. Mikasa and Eren are in the south field, where the grass came back. Armin has come out with the map of the house on a board, because the north field is not on it yet.*

Himmel heard him coming from the timber yard.

Not loud. Lighter than Eren. More careful than Stark. A step that stopped every few paces, the way Anko's stopped on the round, and then went on. Someone looking at things as they walked.

Himmel did not turn round.

He stood where Mikasa stood, a little back from the lime, and looked at the field. The long dry grass. The white line running across it. The red stake. The low hills, hazy in the heat, with nothing coming over them.

The step stopped beside him. A pace away.

"Sorry," said a voice. Young. Polite. "I'm not—I didn't mean to interrupt. I'm making a map. Of the house. The north field isn't on it yet." A pause. "I can come back."

"You're not interrupting," said Himmel. "There's nothing to interrupt. That's the point of it."

He turned his head.

The new one. The one who had come at the eighth bell two days ago and signed the register *Found them*. Slight. Fair hair falling across his forehead. A board in his arms with a page pinned to it, and a pencil behind his ear that Himmel was fairly sure was Anko's.

"Armin," said Himmel. "Isn't it."

"Yes." Armin looked at him. His eyes went over Himmel very fast, the way they went over everything. The cape. The hair. The sword. The way he stood. "You're Himmel. Mikasa said. You stand her hour. Every other day."

"I do."

"Why?"

* * *

Himmel looked back at the field.

People did not usually ask him *why*. People asked him to pose, or to tell the story about the dragon, or to stop brushing his hair at the long table. Nobody had asked him why he did anything in a long time.

"Because someone has to stand here," he said. "And she's stood here every day for longer than anyone remembers. And she has someone now. Who'd like her to sit in a field with him and not stand anywhere at all." He paused. "So on my days, she sits in a field."

Armin was quiet for a moment.

"Eren," he said.

"Eren," said Himmel.

Armin looked across the settlement. South. Past the house and the workshop and the clinic, to where the land dipped. The dead earth had grown green there this summer, pale grass with small yellow flowers. You could not see it from the north end. But he looked toward it anyway.

"He's in the south field," said Armin. "Now. With her."

"He is."

"Sitting."

"Sitting," said Himmel. "In the grass. Not standing anywhere." He paused. "He laughs now. Did you know? He didn't, before. Not that anyone here heard. He laughs at breakfast. He laughed in the tower, at a gate that wouldn't open for him." He looked at the hills. "Mau told me. I've never heard Eren laugh in a fight. I've been here since before he came."

Armin did not say anything for a long time.

Himmel did not look at him. He had learned, this summer, when not to look at people. He kept his eyes on the hills.

When Armin spoke again, his voice was not quite steady.

"He laughs," he said.

"He laughs."

"Okay," said Armin. Very quietly. "Okay."

* * *

Armin put the board down on the dry grass.

He sat down beside it, cross-legged, a little back from the lime, and took Anko's pencil from behind his ear. He looked at the north field. At the long grass. At the white line. At the red stake. At the low hills.

He began to draw.

Himmel watched him do it. Out of the corner of his eye. Not looking. The pencil moved fast and certain. The field went onto the page. The line. The stake, with a small mark beside it. Himmel leaned, very slightly, and read it: *N. sits here at night.* The hills along the top edge, rough, with a note: *Nobody's been past. (Anko.)*

"You're very good at that," said Himmel.

"I've had a lot of practice," said Armin.

"Drawing fields?"

"Drawing where things are," said Armin. "So other people know. So they don't get lost. So they know where it's safe and where it isn't." He drew the ditch, dry now. "In my world, a map could keep people alive. If you knew where the wall was, and where the gates were, and where the—" He stopped. His pencil stopped. "Where the dangerous things were. If you knew, you could plan. If you didn't, people died."

Himmel did not say anything.

Armin started drawing again.

"What are you watching for?" he said. Without looking up. "Here. In the hour. What comes over the hills?"

Himmel looked at the hills.

"Nothing," he said. "Most days. Nothing comes."

"Then what are you watching for?"

"Nothing," said Himmel again. "That's what I'm watching for. I'm watching for nothing to come. And when nothing comes, the hour's done, and two people in a field got an afternoon." He paused. "If something came, I'd run to the timber yard and shout. Loud. And then I'd come back and stand in front of it. Mikasa made me promise. No speeches."

"No speeches?"

"I'm very good at speeches," said Himmel. "She says that's the problem."

Armin almost laughed. Himmel heard it start and stop.

* * *

"Can I ask you something?" said Armin.

"Always."

"Mikasa said you're a hero." Armin did not look up from the map. "In your world. She said you killed a demon king. With your friends. And saved everyone." He drew a line. "She said you pose."

"I do pose."

"Why?"

Himmel considered this. Nobody had ever asked him why he posed either.

"Because people should see a hero," he said. "Not just hear about one. They should see one standing there, ridiculous, with his cape out. And they should laugh. And think, *if that idiot can save the world, maybe it isn't so bad.*" He paused. "That's what a pose is for. It's not for me. It's so people laugh."

Armin's pencil had stopped.

"What's a hero for?" he said. Very quietly. "In your world. What was it for?"

Himmel looked at the hills.

He thought about the road. Ten years of it. Eisen, and Heiter, and Frieren wandering off to look at grimoires nobody was selling. Villages with lanterns strung across the square. Harvest dances. Every grandmother he had ever danced with.

"Festivals," he said.

Armin looked up.

"The fighting," said Himmel, "was so there could be festivals. That's all. That's the whole of it. You fight the demon king so that somewhere, in a village, someone can hang lanterns across the square and dance and not think about demons at all." He looked at the field. "If you do it right, nobody at the festival even remembers why they're safe. They just dance." He was quiet. "That's what a hero's for. So people can forget they needed one."

Armin did not say anything for a very long time.

He looked at the north field. At the long grass, and the line, and the stake, and the hills with nothing coming over them. At the man standing beside him with his cape and his ridiculous hair, watching for nothing.

"In my world," said Armin, and stopped.

Himmel waited.

Armin looked at his pencil.

"In my world," he said again, slowly, "a lot of people fought. And a lot of them died. And most of them never got to a festival." He was quiet. "I don't think most of them knew that's what it was for. I think they thought it was for surviving. Just surviving. Getting through one more day." He looked at the hills. "I wish someone had told them it was for festivals."

Himmel did not say anything.

He did not say *I'm sorry*. He did not ask *what happened*. He saw that Armin had said exactly as much as he wanted to say and not a word more, and that whatever was underneath it was folded up and put somewhere. Himmel knew about folding things up. He had been doing it for years.

He looked at the hills.

"There was a festival here," he said. "A month before you came. When the fireflies were gone." He paused. "I danced with every grandmother. And all the children. And Seiko. I got curry on my cape." He looked at Armin. "There'll be another one. Next year. Ori says. Frieren says. When the fireflies are gone again."

Armin looked at him.

"You should come," said Himmel. "To the next one. You and Eren and Mikasa. And dance." He paused. "Badly, if you like. Everyone dances badly here. That's part of it."

Armin looked at him for a long time.

Then he laughed. The real one. It came out of him startled, the way Eren's did now. The way Himmel's had come out of him on the slope in the north field, at the elf and the shadow on the ground.

"I can't dance," he said.

"Nobody can," said Himmel. "That's not the point."

* * *

Armin finished the north field.

He drew it all. The grass, the line, the stake, the hills, the dry ditch where the fireflies had been. Then he looked at it for a long time, at the place near the bottom edge, a little back from the line, where he and Himmel were.

He drew a small mark there. And wrote beside it, in his small neat careful hand:

*Someone stands here. Every day. So two people can sit in a field.*

And under it, smaller:

*(Himmel, alternate days. Mikasa, the others.)*

He turned the board round and showed Himmel.

Himmel read it.

He did not say anything for a while. Then he took the brush out of his coat and brushed his hair. Once. Not because it needed it.

"That's a very good map," he said.

"It's not finished."

"Maps never are," said Himmel. "That's the good thing about them."

* * *

At the end of the hour, Himmel turned round.

He looked south, across the settlement, toward the place where the land dipped and the green had come back. You could not see the south field from the north end. But he looked anyway.

And coming toward them from that direction, across the yard, slowly, two people. Side by side. Close. Not hurrying. A young man with his hands out of his pockets and a woman with no blades at her hip.

They had come to find Armin. At the end of the hour. Where they knew he would be.

Armin saw them.

He stood up with the board in his arms. He did not run to them. He stood beside Himmel, a little back from the lime, and watched them come.

"He's laughing," said Armin. Very quietly. "Look. He's laughing at something she said."

Himmel looked.

He was. Eren. Walking across the yard beside Mikasa with his head back, laughing, at something she had said. Mikasa's face was doing the thing at the edges that was not quite a smile.

"He does that now," said Himmel.

Armin watched them for a long time.

"Thank you," he said. "For standing here."

"I'm not standing here for you."

"I know," said Armin. "You're standing here for them." He looked at the two people coming across the yard. "That's why I'm saying thank you."

Then he went to meet them, with the board in his arms and Anko's pencil behind his ear.

Himmel stood at the north end and watched the three of them meet in the middle of the yard. He watched Eren put an arm round Armin's shoulders, clumsily, the way he did everything with his arms now. He watched Mikasa look at the map.

Then he turned back to the field.

The hour was done. Nothing had come over the hills. He stood there a little longer anyway, because he liked it. Then he went in to dinner.
