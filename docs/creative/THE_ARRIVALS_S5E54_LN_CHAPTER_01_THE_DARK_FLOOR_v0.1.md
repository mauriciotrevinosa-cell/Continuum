# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 10 — The Dark Floor

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 10
**Continuity state:** Floors 51–56 summarised from Anko's board. Floor 57 (Day 374) with Mau (owner), Maki, Yuka and Kaneki: a floor with no light at all, where Frieren-style light spells do not carry. Beru stands as a guide, using his senses in the dark, and is gentle and protective ("hungry to keep") of Yuka at a drop. The cost is per minute. Floors 58–59 are cleared the same day. Mau reaches level 61 (one hundred and fifty-nine unspent). The guardian of sixty is next

*When: Day 374, morning. Anko's board, fourth plank now, nailed up on Day 371 beside the other three. Under FLOOR on it: 51 to 56, six lines in three days. Under today: 57, 58, 59. START FROM: 50.*

Anko had nailed up a fourth plank.

She had not said anything about it. She had come down on the morning of Day 371, while everyone else was at the hall for Armin, and nailed it up beside the other three. When people came back from the hall it was there. Clean. Waiting.

By Day 374 it had six lines on it.

*Day 371, afternoon. Mau, Gojo, Stark, Rem. From 50. Floor 51: an orchard of iron fruit. Rem picked one. It was very heavy. Floor 52: a lake that was a mirror. Cleared, both.*

*Day 372, morning. Mau, Frieren, Fern, Milim. From 50. Floor 53: a city of bells. Milim rang none of them. (Yuka asked her not to.) Floor 54: a staircase down into the sea. Not the sea. A sea. Cleared, both.*

*Day 373. Mau, Yuta, Eren, Ram. From 50. Floor 55: a room full of clocks, all wrong. Ram corrected them. Floor 56: a garden of statues that turned their heads. Nobody liked it. Cleared, both.*

Under *Floor 56*, in Yuta's hand, added without permission:

*They turned their heads. Every time. Even when you weren't looking. —Y.*

And under that, in Eren's, added the same evening:

*Especially when you weren't looking.*

* * *

"Statues," said Maki, at the board, on the morning of Day 374. "That turned their heads."

"Eren didn't like it," said Mau.

"Eren doesn't like anything that watches him," said Maki. She looked at the board. "Neither do I."

Yuka, beside her, had her rope on her shoulder. Both coils. She was looking at today's line, where Anko was writing.

*Day 374. Mau, Maki, Yuka, Kaneki. From 50. Floors 57, 58, 59.*

"Three," said Yuka.

"Three," said Mau. "If they're kind."

"They're never kind," said Maki.

"Sometimes they're kind," said Yuka. "The kitchen was kind. Rem said."

Maki looked at her granddaughter for a long moment.

"Rem," she said, "would think a floor full of knives was kind if it had a good oven."

* * *

They walked from fifty.

Through the orchard of iron fruit, the mirror lake, the city of bells, which Yuka walked through very carefully so as not to brush any of them. Down the staircase into a sea that was not the sea. Through the room of wrong clocks, which Ram had corrected, so they were all right now and ticked in a way that made Kaneki's shoulders go tight. Through the garden of statues.

The statues turned their heads as they passed.

Every one. Stone faces, smooth, turning on stone necks with a sound like grinding teeth. Following them. Even after they had passed. Mau felt them on the back of his neck all the way to the stair.

"Especially when you're not looking," said Yuka, very quietly, on the stair.

"Eren was right," said Kaneki.

* * *

Fifty-seven was dark.

Not dim. Not grey. Dark. They came up the stair and the light from fifty-six went out behind them, the way a lamp goes out when you close a door. And then there was nothing. No light anywhere. Not in front, not behind, not above.

Mau could not see his own hand.

His eyes behind their filter could not see it either. That frightened him more than the dark. His eyes saw everything. Too much. That was the whole problem with them. Gojo had taught him to look away, to put a filter in, to not see. And now there was nothing to see. The eyes reached out into the dark the way they always reached, and found nothing to hold.

"Yuka," said Maki. Very calm. In the dark.

"I'm here, Grandma."

"Hold my sleeve."

"I'm holding it."

"Kaneki?"

"Here." Kaneki's voice, close. "I can hear. A bit. Not much. It's very quiet." A pause. "There's a floor. Stone. Going forward. And then—something. Further on. I can't tell what. Something open."

The notice came up.

It was the only thing Mau could see. It hung in the dark in front of him, faintly lit, the only light on the whole floor.

*Floor 57 of 100.*

*Objective: cross.*

He read it out.

"Frieren's light," said Yuka. "Can anyone—"

"None of us can make light," said Maki. "Not that kind." She was quiet. "We should have brought Fern."

"We didn't know," said Mau.

He stood in the dark and thought.

Then he looked down at where his shadow would be, if there were any light to make one.

* * *

"Beru," he said.

He did not know if it would work. A shadow in a place with no light. But the dark came up out of the stone at his feet anyway. He felt it, rather than saw it. A presence. Tall. Thin. Long-limbed. Standing a pace behind him and to his right, where Beru always stood now.

Then, in the dark, two small cold blue lights.

They were the only lights on the floor, besides the notice. Very small. Very faint. But Mau could see them. And by them, very faintly, the outline of the ant-man's head.

"My King," said Beru.

The voice came out of the dark. Not high and fierce. Low. Careful. The way he had spoken to Ichika in the yard.

"It's very dark, my King."

"I know."

"My King cannot see."

"No."

Beru was quiet for a moment.

"I can," he said.

Mau felt the cup start to go. The thread. Steady. Per minute. Not as much as Beru fighting. More than Beru standing in the yard.

"Can you take us across?" said Mau.

The two small lights tilted. The insect tilt.

"I was the King of a hive," said Beru. "My hive was underground. In the dark. Always." His voice was very quiet. "I have never needed light, my King. I know the dark the way you know the yard." He paused. "Yes. I can take you across."

* * *

He took them across.

Not by the hand. By sound. He went ahead, a pace in front of Mau, and spoke as he went. Very low. Very steady. A running voice in the dark, telling them where to put their feet.

"Forward. Four paces. The floor is flat. Now a step down. A small one. Now flat again. Left, my King. There is a wall on the right. Do not touch it. It is cold, and wet, and I do not like it."

Mau followed the voice and the two small cold blue lights. Behind him, Kaneki. Behind Kaneki, Maki. Behind Maki, holding her grandmother's sleeve, Yuka.

They walked for a long time.

Beru's voice did not stop. *Forward. Right. Small step. Wait.* Sometimes he stopped altogether, and the blue lights went very still, and Mau understood he was listening. Then: *Something passed. Far off. It did not notice us. Forward.*

Kaneki, behind Mau, said very quietly: "He hears better than me."

"He was an ant," said Mau.

"He was an ant," Kaneki agreed. There was something in his voice that might have been respect.

* * *

The drop came at the end.

Beru stopped. The two small blue lights went very still.

"Wait," he said. "All of you. Do not move."

They did not move.

"The floor ends," said Beru. "Here. In front of my King. There is a gap. Not wide. Two of my King's paces. And then the floor again." He was quiet. "Below the gap, nothing. A long way of nothing."

Nobody said anything.

"I can carry you across," said Beru. "One at a time. I will not drop you."

"Kaneki can jump two paces," said Maki. "So can I. So can Mau, if he can see where he's jumping." She paused. "Yuka can't see."

"I can jump two paces," said Yuka.

"In the dark?"

Yuka did not say anything.

The two small blue lights turned. Away from Mau. Back down the line. Past Kaneki. Past Maki. To the end, where Yuka was.

"Little rope," said Beru.

Silence.

"That is what I will call you," said Beru. "The little rope. Because you threw your rope at the ankle of the guardian of ten. And at the leg of the wyvern on fifty. And at my King, on the cloud stair, when you fell." The blue lights were very steady. "I was not there. But I heard. The little lady with the list told me all the stories. In the yard. In the five minutes."

Yuka did not say anything for a long moment.

"Ori told you," she said.

"The little lady tells me everything," said Beru. "She says I am a good listener." A pause. "I am. I was a king. Kings listen. Or they are eaten."

Yuka made a small sound. It might have been a laugh.

"Little rope," said Beru. "Let me carry you. Across the gap. I will hold you the way my King asked me to hold things. Hungry to keep. Not to eat." He paused. "I will not drop you. I have never dropped anything I wanted to keep."

Maki was very still in the dark.

Then Yuka let go of her grandmother's sleeve.

* * *

Beru carried her.

Mau did not see it. He heard it. The long-limbed shape moving back past him in the dark. Something lifting, very gently. Then a moment of nothing, while the blue lights went across the gap, two paces, over the long way of nothing. Then the sound of something being set down, very carefully, on stone.

"Little rope," said Beru, from the other side. "You are across."

"I'm across," said Yuka. Her voice was shaking. Not very much.

Then Kaneki jumped. Then Maki. Then Mau, toward the two small blue lights, two paces, and landed on stone. Beru's long cold hand was on his arm at once, steadying him, before he had even finished landing.

"My King," said Beru.

"Thank you."

"I did not drop her," said Beru. Very quietly. As if it mattered more than anything he had ever said. "My King asked me to be hungry to keep. Not to eat. I did not drop her."

"I know," said Mau.

* * *

The stair was just beyond. A faint grey light at the top of it, from fifty-eight.

*Floor 57 cleared.*

Mau sent Beru back. The thread stopped. Eleven minutes. The cup had noticed. Not badly.

At the top of the stair, in the grey light from fifty-eight, they could see each other again.

Yuka was standing very straight. Her hands were shaking. She was looking at the place in the dark where Beru had been.

Maki looked at her granddaughter for a long time.

Then she put her hand on the back of Yuka's neck and left it there.

"He called you little rope," she said.

"He did."

"Ori told him the stories."

"She did."

Maki was quiet.

"That's a good name," she said at last. "For you."

* * *

Fifty-eight was a forest of glass trees that chimed in the wind. Fifty-nine was a long hall of doors, every one locked except one, and Kaneki heard which. They cleared both before the three hours were up.

*Level 61. One hundred and fifty-nine points unspent.*

They went home through the small frame at the top of fifty-nine. On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Two hours fifty," she said. "From fifty. Three floors." She looked at the four of them. At Yuka, whose hands had stopped shaking. "What happened on fifty-seven?"

"It was dark," said Yuka. "No light at all. Beru took us across." She paused. "He carried me over a gap. He calls me little rope."

Anko looked at her for a long time.

Then she wrote it down.

*Floor 57. Dark. No light. Beru guided. Carried Yuka (little rope) over a gap. Didn't drop her.*

And under it, smaller:

*Bring Fern next time.*
