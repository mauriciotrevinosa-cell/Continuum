# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 11 — The Shaman

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 11
**Continuity state:** Floor 60 (Day 375 morning), the sixth guardian (DH-124: Tusk replaces the earlier Warden draft). Party: Mau (owner), Frieren, Fern and Stark. A war camp in red rock under an ember sky; Tusk, the High Orc Shaman, behind a ring of orc warriors, casting through the skull on his staff. Mau plans it aloud: Stark holds the ring (Marin's jacket takes a blade), Fern cuts the fire-wall's knot, Frieren reads the casts. Then a weight-spell flattens everyone, warriors included. Mau wraps himself in full Infinity so the weight never arrives, stands, and walks twelve paces under it. A fire lance gets through on his left side (burns, side and face) and his Beru-bitten shoulder reopens. Beru and Igris, raised inside his space, break the skull. Tusk kneels ("Strong") and Mau finishes him. Extracted: a speaking caster, formal and careful ("Great one"; "I will be your fire. Behind your people. To keep them."). Eight registered shadows, twenty places. Level 62 (one hundred and sixty-four unspent). Shoko: "I'm going to stop saying fine."

*When: Day 375, morning. The seventh night falls tonight. Shoko said fine, after a long look. Anko's board: under today, in her hand, 60 (guardian), and under that, in Fern's: BRING FERN, crossed out, and FERN IS COMING written beside it.*

Fern had crossed it out herself.

*Bring Fern next time*, Anko had written, under fifty-seven. Fern had read it at breakfast on Day 375 and gone to the board with her pencil. She had drawn one straight line through *Bring Fern*, very neatly, and written beside it *Fern is coming*.

Then she had gone to find Stark.

"Sixty," she said. "Today. Mau asked. Frieren's coming. You're coming." She looked at him. "Wear the jacket."

Stark looked at her.

"There's no armour yet. It's just padding."

"Wear it anyway," said Fern. "Padding is better than nothing. Marin said."

So Stark wore the jacket. Marin's quilted one, in rows. He walked to the screen with it on under his coat and looked, Mau thought, like a man who had been given something precious and was terrified of getting it dirty.

* * *

They walked from fifty.

Through the orchard and the mirror lake and the bells and the sea that was not the sea, the wrong clocks, the statues that turned their heads. Frieren stopped in the statue garden and looked at the stone faces turning toward her for a long time.

"I don't like them," she said.

"Nobody does," said Mau.

"No," said Frieren. "I mean I don't like *why*." She looked at the nearest statue. Its smooth stone face was turned toward her, following. "Things that watch you without being alive are usually made to watch you. By someone." She looked at the others. All of them, turned toward her. "Someone wanted these to watch people walk through this garden."

Nobody said anything.

They went on.

Through fifty-seven. In the dark, this time, Fern set her lights. They would not carry far, a few paces at most, as if the dark swallowed them. But they were enough to see the floor, and the cold wet wall on the right, and the gap at the end. They jumped it, all four, in the light.

Through the glass forest and the hall of doors.

To the stair to sixty.

* * *

Sixty was a war camp.

They came up the stair into heat. A great round hollow in red rock, open to a sky the colour of embers, ringed with tents of hide on poles and fires burning in iron bowls. Drums. Somewhere in the ring of tents, a great many drums, slow, all together, like one enormous heart.

In the middle of the hollow, on a mound of black stone, something was sitting.

Large. Larger than Stark. Green-grey skin under a cloak of hides and bones. Two tusks curving up out of its lower jaw, each as long as a forearm, ringed with bands of gold. A staff across its knees, a long knotted thing of pale bone with a skull bound at the top and something burning inside the skull. Its eyes were open. Not dull red, like the others. Yellow. Clever. Old.

It was not looking at them like an animal. It was looking at them like a man reading a letter.

The notice came up.

*Floor 60 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the sixth ten.*

*Guardian: Tusk, the High Orc Shaman.*

"Tusk," said Mau.

The thing on the mound smiled. Its tusks made the smile very wide.

It stood. It lifted the staff. The drums stopped.

And out of every tent round the hollow, at once, came warriors.

* * *

Orcs. Twenty. Thirty. Smaller than the shaman, but not small, in hide and iron, with axes and spears and long curved blades. They came out of the tents and stood in a ring round the hollow, between the four of them and the stair, and did not attack.

They waited.

The shaman lifted its staff higher. The thing burning in the skull flared.

"It's a caster," said Frieren. Very calmly. Her staff was already up. "The warriors are its wall. It doesn't fight. It stands behind them and burns things." She watched the skull. "That's the focus. Everything goes through the skull."

"*Operator,*" said Fern, very quietly, reading the notice that had come up in front of all of them now. "*Engage.*"

"I know," said Mau. "Stark. The ring. Hold them off me. Don't finish them, just hold." He looked at Fern. "Whatever it casts, cut it. You can see the knots." He looked at Frieren. "Tell me what it's going to do. Before it does it."

"And you?" said Stark.

Mau looked at the shaman on its mound, past thirty orcs and three fires.

"I walk to it," he said.

* * *

The warriors came all at once.

Stark met the first three. The axe going round low, not to kill, to put them on the ground. Under his coat, Marin's quilted jacket took a curved blade across the ribs, and held, and Stark grunted and kept going. Mau went through the gap Stark made. Not fast. He did not use the borrowed speed. He walked, with the space round him slanted, and the warriors' blades hit the slant and slid off it sideways, and he kept walking.

Twenty paces to the mound.

The shaman spoke.

Mau did not know the words. They came out of its tusked mouth harsh and old and rolling, and the skull on its staff burned white, and Frieren's voice behind him said, very fast:

"Fire. A wall. Left to right. *Fern*—"

The fire came. A wall of it, out of nothing, across the hollow from left to right, as high as a house, straight at him.

A thin line of light went into it. Fern's. Not at the fire. At a place in the air in front of the fire that Mau could not see and she could, where the spell was knotted together. The line cut the knot.

The wall of fire came apart. Into tatters. Into heat and smoke and nothing. It washed over the slant of his space and went past him on both sides, and he walked through the smoke.

Fifteen paces.

* * *

The shaman stopped smiling.

It looked at Fern. At the small girl in the purple coat with her staff level, who had cut its fire in half without moving her feet. Its yellow eyes narrowed. It said something else. Longer. Lower.

"*Not fire,*" said Frieren. Her voice changed. "Mau, it's—weight. It's making everything heavy. Everything under the sky. All of us. *Mau*—"

The air came down.

Not wind. Weight. As if the sky the colour of embers had been made of stone and had decided, all at once, to rest on them. Mau heard Stark go down. Heard the axe hit the rock. Heard Fern gasp and the line of her light go out. Heard Frieren, behind him, hit her knees. The orc warriors went down too, all thirty of them, flat on the rock round the hollow, crushed under the same weight as everyone else, and the shaman did not care.

Mau went down on one knee.

He could not breathe. The weight was on his chest, his skull, his spine, pressing him into the rock. His ribs bent. Something in his left shoulder, the one Beru had bitten, the one that had only just closed, opened again. He felt it go.

Twelve paces.

He could not walk twelve paces under this. Nobody could. That was the point of it. The shaman stood on its mound, the only thing standing in the whole hollow, with the skull on its staff burning white, and looked down at all of them, flattened, and waited for them to stop moving.

*The weight,* said Ciel. *It's pressing down. On everything. All of it, from above.*

*Pressing,* thought Mau. *Coming down. Arriving.*

He put up the space.

* * *

Not a slant. Not a wall. Not a plate in front of a face.

All round him. Above him. Close. A skin of it, a hand's breadth from his body on every side, the full width of his Infinity, the thing Gojo had spent half a year teaching him to hold for more than a breath.

The weight came down on it.

And did not arrive.

It pressed, and pressed, and slowed, and slowed, a hand's breadth above his skull, and could not get there. Could never get there. The way Beru's claws had not reached Frieren. The way the leaves had not reached her hat in the iron wood. The weight of the whole sky resting on everything in that hollow except the space round one boy.

He stood up.

It cost. It cost more than anything he had held since forty. The thing he used for the space was emptying like a jar tipped over, and he could feel the floor of it coming. But he stood. In the middle of a hollow full of people pressed flat to the rock, he stood up.

The shaman saw him stand.

Its yellow eyes went wide.

Mau walked.

* * *

Twelve paces.

Every one of them cost him. He could feel the space thinning with each step, the weight above it leaning harder, finding the places where his attention slipped. Ten paces. His shoulder was bleeding down his arm under the coat. Eight. The shaman was speaking again, fast, the words tumbling out of its tusked mouth, and the skull flared, and he did not know what it was casting and Frieren could not tell him because Frieren was flat on the rock behind him with the sky on her back.

Fire.

Not a wall this time. A lance. Straight down at him from the mound, white, the hottest thing he had ever seen, the kind of fire that does not burn things but unmakes them.

It hit the space.

The space held. Mostly. The fire went round it, the way water goes round a stone, and where it went round, the space was thinner, and on his left side, along his ribs and his arm and the side of his face, it got through.

He felt his skin go.

He kept walking.

Five paces.

* * *

He was at the foot of the mound.

The shaman was above him. Looking down. Its staff raised for another lance. The skull at the top of it blazing white. Everything it had, going through the skull. Frieren had said so.

*The focus.*

He did not have anything left for the space. It was thin as paper now. He did not have anything left for the speed. His legs would not take it. He had one thing.

He had two things.

"*Beru,*" he said. "*Igris.*"

They came up out of his shadow. Inside the space. Where the weight could not reach. The ant king and the knight, rising out of the dark at his feet, standing up in the only place in the hollow where anything could stand.

"*The skull,*" said Mau.

Beru did not hesitate. Beru never hesitated. He went up the mound like a black flame and his claws took the bone staff a hand's breadth below the skull, and closed, and the staff cracked. The skull came off. It hit the black rock and rolled, still burning white, and Igris's long sword came down on it, once, and split it in half.

The light inside it went out.

The weight lifted off the hollow all at once.

* * *

Behind him, he heard Stark breathe. A huge ragged gasp. Fern coughing. Frieren, getting to her feet.

The shaman stood on its mound with half a staff in its hands and no skull, and looked down at Mau. Its yellow eyes went from him to the knight, to the ant king, and back to him.

It did not raise the staff again.

It did something Mau had not expected. It lowered it. Slowly. All the way down, until the cracked end rested on the black rock.

"*Strong,*" it said.

The word was in the tower's tongue. Rough. Old. But a word.

Mau stood at the foot of the mound with his left side burned and his shoulder bleeding and his space gone, and looked up at it.

"Not strong," he said. "Just in the right place."

The shaman looked at him for a long moment.

Then it knelt. On its mound. And bent its tusked head, and held out its neck.

The notice came up.

*Operator: engage.*

He knew what it meant. He climbed the mound. He put his hand flat against the green-grey skin of its neck, the way he had put it on Kaisel's. He made the smallest point he could, and pulled.

It was quick. It had asked for that.

* * *

*Operator: trial passed.*

*Guardian defeated.*

*Shadow extraction available.*

He sat down on the mound. He had not decided to. His legs decided.

Frieren reached him first. She knelt on the black rock and looked at his face. At the left side of it, where the fire had got through. She did not say anything. She took off her coat, the one with the strip already torn from the hem, and folded it, and put it against the burn on his arm and side, and held it there.

Fern came next, with water. Then Stark, limping, with the jacket torn across the ribs and the padding coming out, and a long coil of iron chain over one shoulder that he had taken off one of the warriors without noticing.

"You walked," said Stark. "Under that."

"Yes."

"Nobody could walk under that."

"I didn't walk under it," said Mau. "I walked where it couldn't get to."

Stark looked at him for a long time. Then he sat down on the black rock beside him, heavily, the way he sat down after the bear.

"Gojo's going to cry," he said.

* * *

*Can I?* said Mau.

*Yes,* said Ciel. *The cup's deep enough. It's—* She paused. *It's a caster. It isn't like the others. It's thinking even now. In the dark under it. I can feel it think.* Another pause. *I don't know what it'll be like.*

He looked at the shaman lying on the mound, at the cracked bone staff beside it, at the two halves of the burned-out skull.

*It knelt,* he thought. *Before I finished it. It knelt because I walked to it under its own weight.*

He put his hand on the black rock beside it.

*You burned things,* he thought, at the dark. *From behind a wall of your own people. And then you flattened your own people to get me.* He looked at the orc warriors round the hollow, getting slowly up off the rock, dazed, looking at the shaman on its mound. *I don't want that. I want someone who stands behind my people and burns what comes for them. Not behind them to use them. Behind them to keep them.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the mound.

Through the shaman. Through the green-grey skin and the cloak of hides and the gold on its tusks. The gold went dark. The yellow went out of its eyes. Two small cold blue lights came on instead.

It stood up.

It was smaller than it had been. Not much. Shadow-sized. Its cloak hung from its shoulders like smoke. In its hand, a staff of dark bone, whole again, with a skull at the top, and inside the skull, not white fire. Blue.

It looked at Mau.

Then it bowed. Not like Igris, all at once. Not like Beru, collapsing. Slowly. Formally. One hand on its chest, the staff held out to the side. The bow of a scholar to a teacher.

"*Great one,*" it said.

Its voice was deep and rough and very, very careful with the words, as if each one were a stone it had to set exactly in place.

"*I am Tusk. I was the fire of a people who burned. I burned for them and then I burned them.*" It looked at the warriors round the hollow, getting up off the rock. "*You walked through my sky. Nobody has walked through my sky.*" It straightened. "*I will be your fire. Behind your people. To keep them.*"

Nobody said anything for a long time.

"*Great one*," said Stark at last, very quietly. "That's a new one."

"Please don't," said Mau.

"*Great one*," said Tusk, with great dignity, "*as you wish.*"

Frieren, holding her coat against Mau's burned side, made a small sound that might have been a laugh.

* * *

*Floor 60 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 or 60.*

*Level 62. One hundred and sixty-four points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 8. Places: 20.*

At the far end of the hollow, past the mound, between two of the hide tents, a seal had appeared. Round. Dark. Patterned. And beside it, a small frame, with home faintly visible.

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Three hours ten," she said. "From fifty. Sixty." She looked at the four of them. At Mau's face, the left side of it. At Frieren's coat held against him. At Stark, who still had a long coil of iron chain over his shoulder, because he had forgotten to take it off. "What's that?"

"Chain," said Stark. "From one of the warriors. It tried to hold me." He looked down at Marin's quilted jacket, torn across the ribs, the padding coming out. "And the jacket held instead. Mostly." He looked at it. "I want to show Marin."

Anko wrote it down.

*Floor 60. War camp. Tusk (High Orc Shaman). Mau walked to it under its weight. Kept. (Tusk: "Great one.")*

*Chain (Stark, for Marin). The jacket held.*

And under it, smaller, already reaching for the clinic list:

*Burns, left side and face. Shoulder reopened. Shoko.*

Shoko was already coming across the dry ground. She stopped in front of Mau and looked at the left side of his face for a long time.

"I said *fine* this morning," she said.

"You did."

"I'm going to stop saying *fine*," said Shoko. "It doesn't mean anything any more." She took his chin in her hand and turned his face to the light. "Clinic. Now. All of you."
