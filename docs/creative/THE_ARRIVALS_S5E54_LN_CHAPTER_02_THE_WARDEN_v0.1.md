# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 10 — The Warden

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 10
**Continuity state:** Floor 60 (Day 375 morning), the sixth guardian. Party: Mau (owner), Frieren, Fern and Stark (Fern brings light, after fifty-seven). It is a round hall of locked cells with a huge armoured jailer the notice names the Warden of Sixty. It fights to lock people in. Igris and Beru stand. Fern's precise spells unpick the chains; Stark takes the blows. Mau declines the extraction: he will not keep a thing whose only wish was to lock people in. He can choose not to, which is the point Umaru made. The floor 60 transfer point is secured. Mau reaches level 62 (one hundred and sixty-four unspent)

*When: Day 375, morning. The seventh night falls tonight. Anko's board: under today, in her hand, 60 (guardian), and under that, in Fern's: BRING FERN, crossed out, and FERN IS COMING written beside it.*

Fern had crossed it out herself.

*Bring Fern next time*, Anko had written, under fifty-seven. Fern had read it at breakfast on Day 375 and gone to the board with her pencil. She had drawn one straight line through *Bring Fern*, very neatly, and written beside it *Fern is coming*.

Then she had gone to find Stark.

"Sixty," she said. "Today. Mau asked. Frieren's coming. You're coming." She looked at him. "Wear the jacket."

Stark looked at her.

"There's no armour yet. It's just padding."

"Wear it anyway," said Fern. "Padding is better than nothing. Marin said."

So Stark wore the jacket. Marin's quilted one, in rows. He walked to the screen with it on under his coat and looked, Mau thought, like a man who had been given something precious and was terrified of getting it dirty.

* * *

They walked from fifty.

Through the orchard and the mirror lake and the bells and the sea that was not the sea, the wrong clocks, the statues that turned their heads. Frieren stopped in the statue garden and looked at the stone faces turning toward her for a long time.

"I don't like them," she said.

"Nobody does," said Mau.

"No," said Frieren. "I mean I don't like *why*." She looked at the nearest statue. Its smooth stone face was turned toward her, following. "Things that watch you without being alive are usually made to watch you. By someone." She looked at the others. All of them, turned toward her. "Someone wanted these to watch people walk through this garden."

Nobody said anything.

They went on.

Through fifty-seven. In the dark, this time, Fern set her lights. They would not carry far, a few paces at most, as if the dark swallowed them. But they were enough to see the floor, and the cold wet wall on the right, and the gap at the end. They jumped it, all four, in the light.

Through the glass forest and the hall of doors.

To the stair to sixty.

* * *

Sixty was a prison.

They came up the stair into a round hall, very large, with a high domed roof and grey light falling from somewhere above. The walls were cells. Tier on tier of them, going up and up round the curve of the hall, like a hive. Each one a small stone room with iron bars across the front and a heavy lock on the bars.

All of them locked.

All of them empty.

In the middle of the hall, on a raised stone floor, something was standing.

Tall. Broad. Armoured from head to foot in grey iron, heavy and plain. Not like Igris, all edges. Like a wall. In one hand it held a great iron ring with keys on it, dozens of them, each as long as a man's arm. In the other, a chain. Long. Heavy. Its links as thick as Stark's wrist. The chain went from its fist down to the floor and lay coiled round its feet like a sleeping snake.

Its helm had a slit in it. Behind the slit, dull red.

The notice came up.

*Floor 60 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the sixth ten.*

*Guardian: the Warden of Sixty.*

"The Warden," said Mau.

The thing in the middle of the hall turned its head toward them.

It lifted the ring of keys. It shook it, once. The keys rang, all of them together, a great clanging sound that went round and round the hall and up the tiers of empty cells.

And every lock on every cell clicked open.

* * *

Nothing came out of the cells.

They were empty. They stayed empty. The doors swung open on their hinges, all of them at once, tier on tier, with a sound like a hundred mouths opening. Then they stood open.

The Warden looked at the four of them.

At Mau.

Then it lifted the chain.

"It wants to put us in," said Fern. Very quietly. She was looking at the open cells. "All of them. It opened them for us."

"Then it's going to be disappointed," said Stark.

The chain came.

* * *

It came across the hall like a whip. Fast. Heavy. Swinging. The links singing in the air. Straight at Mau.

Stark met it.

He did not think about it. Mau saw him not think. He stepped in front of Mau with the axe up crosswise in both hands, the way he had stepped in front of the bear on thirty. The chain wrapped round the axe handle and round Stark's arms, and pulled.

Stark did not go.

He planted his feet on the stone and leaned back against the pull. The Warden leaned back against him, and for a long moment the chain was a straight iron bar between them, humming.

Under Stark's coat, the quilted jacket took the pressure of the links across his chest. Mau saw him register it. The pull, spread out across a hundred small stitches, instead of biting into one place. He saw Stark's face change.

"*Fern*," said Stark, through his teeth.

Fern was already moving.

She did not fire at the Warden. She fired at the chain. A thin, precise line of light. Not Zoltraak. Something smaller. Sharper. Something she had made herself, Mau thought, for exactly this. It hit one link, the one where the chain left the Warden's fist, at exactly the place where the iron was thinnest. The link parted.

The chain went slack.

Stark staggered back two paces with a long coil of iron chain round his arms and his axe.

"Thank you," he said.

"You're welcome," said Fern. "Don't do that again."

"I'm going to do that again."

"I know," said Fern. "Don't."

* * *

"*Igris. Beru.*"

They came up out of the floor on either side of him. The knight with his long sword. The ant-man with his claws.

The Warden lifted its ring of keys and shook it again. The keys rang.

Behind them, all round the hall, tier on tier, every cell door swung *shut*. Clang. Clang. Clang. And then, in the iron doorframes of the nearest cells, the bars began to move. Long. Reaching. Growing out of the frames like the arms of something, toward the middle of the hall. Toward the four of them.

Toward Mau.

"It's trying to lock us in where we stand," said Frieren. Calmly. Her staff was up. "Not in the cells. Here."

The bars reached.

Igris met them. The long sword went round in a flat arc and cut the nearest bars off where they grew from the frames. They fell clanging to the stone. More came. Igris cut those too. Unhurried. A man sweeping a floor.

Beru went for the Warden.

Not the way he had gone for Tank, in the hive, to eat. Fast. Straight. A blur of black across the hall, up onto the raised floor, at the Warden's chest. Claws at the grey iron plates. The Warden swung the ring of keys at him like a mace. Beru went under it, round it, and his claws found the gap at the Warden's neck.

The Warden roared. A sound like a door slamming.

Frieren fired.

Zoltraak. The real thing. Straight across the hall, white, past Beru, a hand's breadth from his shoulder, into the slit in the Warden's helm.

The dull red behind the slit went out.

The Warden stood very still on its raised floor with Beru's claws in its neck and a hole through its helm.

Then it fell.

All at once. Like a wall coming down. The ring of keys hit the stone and every key rang at once, and then was silent.

All round the hall, every cell door stood open. Every one. And stayed open.

* * *

*Guardian defeated.*

"Back," said Mau. "Both."

"My liege."

"My King." Beru looked at Frieren as he went down. "Queen. That was a very good spell. A hand's breadth from my shoulder. I did not flinch."

"You flinched a little," said Frieren.

"I did not flinch," said Beru, with great dignity, and went into the floor.

The drain stopped.

The notice came up.

*Shadow extraction available.*

Under the fallen Warden, on the raised stone floor, the dark was gathering. Deep. As deep as Igris had been, on twenty.

Mau looked at it.

*Can I?* he asked.

*Yes,* said Ciel. *Easily. The cup's deep enough. You'd barely notice.* A pause. *Do you want to?*

Mau looked at the Warden.

At the grey iron. At the ring of keys, dozens of them, each as long as an arm. At the open cells going up and up round the hall, tier on tier, every one empty. Every one had been opened for them, to put them in.

He thought about what he would ask it.

He thought about it for a long time.

"No," he said aloud.

Fern looked at him. Stark looked at him, with a coil of iron chain still round his arms. Frieren did not look at him. She was looking at the open cells.

"Not this one," said Mau.

He closed the notice.

* * *

"Why?" said Stark.

Mau looked at the Warden.

"Everything else wanted something," he said slowly. "Lantern wanted to win. Igris wanted a proper fight. The bear wanted us gone. Beru wanted to eat and get bigger. Kaisel wanted to fly." He looked at the ring of keys. "This one only wanted to lock people in. That's all it wanted. It opened every cell in the hall and tried to put us in them. And when that didn't work, it tried to grow bars round us where we stood."

Nobody said anything.

"I could raise it," said Mau. "Ciel says easily. And I could ask it to want something else." He looked at the open cells. "But I don't think I want something in my shadow whose first wish was to lock people in. Even if I asked it to stop." He was quiet. "I can't sign things that bind me. In the gate. My name won't hold." He looked at the Warden. "I don't think I want to keep a thing that was made to hold people."

Frieren turned her head and looked at him.

"Umaru said you should be the one choosing," she said. "Every time. Not the cup."

"This is me choosing."

"I know," said Frieren. "That's why it's right."

* * *

*Floor 60 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 or 60.*

*Level 62. One hundred and sixty-four points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 6. Places: 20.*

Six. Not seven.

At the far end of the hall, past the raised floor, through one of the open cells, a seal had appeared. Round. Dark. Patterned. And beside it, a small frame, with home faintly visible.

They went home through the open cell.

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Three hours ten," she said. "From fifty. Sixty." She looked at the four of them. At Stark, who still had a long coil of iron chain round his arms, because he had forgotten to take it off. "What's that?"

"Chain," said Stark. "From the Warden. Fern cut it." He looked at it. "I think I'll keep it."

"Why?"

Stark considered this.

"It tried to hold me," he said. "And the jacket held instead." He looked down at Marin's quilted jacket under his coat. "I want to show Marin."

Anko looked at him for a long moment. Then she wrote it down.

*Floor 60. Prison. The Warden. Cleared. Not kept (Mau chose). Chain (Stark, for Marin).*

And under it, smaller:

*The jacket held.*
