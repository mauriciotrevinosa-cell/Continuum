# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 11 — One Small True Thing

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 11
**Continuity state:** R39 payoff. Mau owes Umaru "one small true thing" (V30 "The Winner's Rule"). At the next card game, with Yuka, Ori, Milim and Ichika, he pays it. Yuka's rule 7 comes back; Ichika plays her first round, guided by Ori. The rules sheet lives in the Ministry archive under Umaru's bed. Ordinary game time: no training, no XP

*When: Day 375, afternoon, between the shaman and the roof. The end of the long table. Umaru has the cards. Yuka has the sheet, RULES (AMENDED), unfolded along its creases. Ichika is sitting on the bench beside Ori with her too-long sash, watching the cards the way she watches everything.*

Umaru dealt Mau in without asking.

She always did. It was in the constitution somewhere, he was nearly sure. Article something. *Nobody watches the President's game. Everybody plays.* She dealt him seven cards and pushed them across the table and did not look at him.

"You owe me one," she said, to the cards.

"I know."

"A true thing. Small. From the night of the winner's rule." She turned the first card face up in the middle. A four of spades. "Twenty-two days ago."

"I know," said Mau. "I've been thinking about it."

"For twenty-two days?"

"For twenty-two days."

Umaru looked at him from inside her hood for a long moment.

"The Ministry," she said, "is prepared to wait for the end of the round."

* * *

Yuka smoothed out the sheet.

*RULES (AMENDED)*. Eight rules, in five hands. Folded and unfolded so many times the creases had gone soft. It had lived, Umaru had told them, in the Ministry archive under her bed, with the constitution, since the night of the winner's rule.

"New game," said Yuka. "Same rules. All eight." She looked round the table. "Unless someone wins and adds one."

"Milim's roar," said Ori.

"Milim's roar," Yuka agreed. "Still."

"*Forever*," said Milim, floating over the end of the table on the ribbon.

"Until the end of the night," said Umaru.

"*Forever*," said Milim.

Ichika was looking at the sheet.

She could not read it. Mau did not know if she could read at all yet, in any language. She was looking at it anyway. At the eight lines in five hands. At Ori's small careful printing, and Yuka's neat one, and Umaru's square Ministry one, and Milim's large uneven scrawl, and his own.

Ori leaned over to her.

"These are the rules," said Ori. "Every time someone wins, they make one. Milim made that one." She pointed. "You have to say *roar* when you play a seven." She pointed again. "Yuka made that one. If you play the same suit as the person before you, you tell them something true. Small."

Ichika looked at the line.

"True?" she said.

"Something real," said Ori. "Not made up. Like *I like your rope.* Or *your eyes are a good colour.*" She thought about it. "Small."

Ichika considered this for a long time, with the serious face she had learned from her mother.

Then she nodded.

Umaru dealt her in.

* * *

Ichika did not know how to play.

Ori showed her. Very patiently. Same suit or same number. Ori held Ichika's cards for her, because Ichika's hands were too small to hold seven, and fanned them out on the table where Ichika could see. When it was Ichika's turn, Ori pointed. *That one. Or that one. You choose.*

Ichika chose.

She chose a three of spades. On the four of spades.

Same suit as the person before her. Which was Umaru.

The table went quiet.

Rule 7. *Same suit as the person before you = tell them one small true thing.*

Ichika looked at the three of spades on the table. Then at the sheet. Then at Ori.

"Something true," said Ori. "To Umaru. Small."

Ichika looked at Umaru.

At the hood. At the sash. At the very serious face inside the hood, looking back at her.

She thought about it. For a long time. Long enough that Milim started to float upward with impatience and Yuka reeled her in.

"Your sash," said Ichika at last. "Is like mine."

She touched the too-long red sash over her own shoulder. Then pointed at Umaru's.

Umaru did not say anything.

Then she pulled her hood all the way down over her face.

"That's a true thing," said Ori, satisfied. "It counts."

From inside the hood, a very small voice said, "The Ministry acknowledges."

* * *

The round went on.

Milim played a seven and said "*ROAR*" so loudly that Momo came out of the kitchen with a ladle. Yuka played a heart on Ori's heart and told her, "You count better than Nazuna," and Ori looked enormously pleased. Ichika played two more cards, guided by Ori, and neither was the same suit as the person before her, which seemed to disappoint her.

Then Umaru played a club.

And Mau, after her, had a club.

He looked at it in his hand.

Same suit as the person before you.

He could have played something else. He had a seven. He could have said *roar* and played that instead. Yuka was looking at him across the table. He could see her see that he had a choice.

He played the club.

On Umaru's club.

* * *

Umaru did not look up from her cards.

"Rule 7," she said, to the cards. "Same suit. One small true thing." A pause. "And you owe me one already. From twenty-two days ago." Another pause. "So that's two."

"That's two," said Mau.

The table was very quiet. Even Milim. Even Ichika, who did not know what was happening and could tell that something was.

Mau thought about it.

He had been thinking about it for twenty-two days. What was true, and small, and his to give. He had thought of a great many things. Most of them were true. Some of them were not small.

"The first one," he said. "From the winner's rule."

Umaru did not look up.

"The first time you put my points on the wall," said Mau. "The first day. After the red gate. When you said *we don't spend anything*." He looked at the cards in his hand. "I'd been carrying them around for days. In my head. A number. And I didn't know what to do with it. And it felt like—" He stopped. "It felt like being lost. In a place where everyone else knew the way." He looked at her. "And then you put it on the wall. With boxes. And lines. Solid for what we knew. Broken for what we didn't." He paused. "And it stopped feeling like being lost. It felt like someone was holding the map."

Umaru did not move.

"That's the first one," said Mau.

* * *

"And the second?" said Umaru. Very quietly. From inside the hood.

Mau looked at the table.

At the cards. At the sheet. At Ichika beside Ori in her too-long sash, watching him with her serious face. At Milim, floating, not making a sound for once. At Yuka, who had made the rule.

"The second one's smaller," he said.

"Small is the rule."

"When you sit down," said Mau, "after counting. Suddenly. Like your legs decided." He looked at her. "And you ask for milk. To the line." He paused. "Every time you do that, I know we did it right. The points. The walls. Whatever it was. I don't need Ciel to tell me." He looked at the cards. "I just need to see you sit down and ask for milk."

The end of the long table was completely silent.

Umaru did not say anything for a long time.

Then, very slowly, from inside the hood, a hand came out. It reached across the table. It took the four of clubs from the top of the pile, the card Mau had just played, and held it.

The hand went back inside the hood with the card.

"The Ministry," said Umaru's voice, very small, "is keeping this card."

"That's not a rule," said Yuka.

"It's a new rule," said Umaru. "Rule nine."

"You didn't win."

"The Ministry," said Umaru, "is invoking emergency powers."

Ori, beside Ichika, wrote it on the sheet. In her careful printing, under rule eight.

*9. Umaru: Emergency powers. (Keeps the card.)*

* * *

They finished the round. Yuka won it, properly, carefully. She sat with the pencil for a long time, the way she always did, and then wrote:

*10. Yuka: If someone tells you a true thing, you don't have to say anything back. You can just keep it.*

She turned it round.

Umaru read it from inside her hood. She did not say anything. Which was, Mau understood, the point.

At the end of the night, Yuka folded the sheet along its soft creases. Ten rules now, in six hands. Umaru took it and put it inside her hood with the four of clubs, presumably to carry them both to the Ministry archive under her bed.

Before she went up the stair, she stopped.

She did not turn round.

"That's both," she said. "Both true things." A pause. "You don't owe me any more."

"I know."

"I'm going to keep them anyway," said Umaru. "Rule ten."

She went up.

Ichika, at the end of the table, was looking at the place where the sheet had been. Then she looked at Ori.

"Again?" she said.

"Tomorrow," said Ori. "Or the day after. Umaru will deal you in." She paused. "She always does. It's the rule."
