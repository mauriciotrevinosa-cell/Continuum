# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 13 — Leaves Down

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 13
**Continuity state:** R32 return with real pacing. Sixty-eight days after the first seedlings (Day 308), Shoko primes the lowest leaves of her tobacco and hangs them to dry following the Fallow farmer's instructions Gojo had Hollis bring ("Hang in bunches, leaves down. Dark barn. Not too warm. One moon. Ready when it bends and does not break."). Gojo helps; Anko comes to see; Mau waters both boxes. The first smoke "from here" stays about a month off (S6). Shoko's one bent cigarette stays unlit

*When: Day 376, early morning. The clinic corridor, where Shoko's box stands by the door. Anko's box stands outside her own door further along. Mau has a cup of water from the end of Momo's counter, as he does every morning, for both.*

The leaves had changed colour at the bottom.

Mau saw it when he came with the cup. Not all of them. The lowest ones. The broad pointed leaves nearest the soil of Shoko's box, on all of the plants. They had been deep green all summer. Now they were going pale at the edges. Yellowing. Not dying. Ripening, the way an apple ripens from one side.

He crouched beside the box with the cup and looked at them for a long time.

He did not water them.

He went to find Shoko.

* * *

Shoko was in the clinic, at her desk, writing.

She looked up when he came to the door. She looked at his face. Then at the cup in his hand, still full.

"What," she said.

"The bottom leaves," said Mau. "On the tobacco. They've gone pale. At the edges."

Shoko put down her pen.

She did not get up at once. She sat at her desk with her hands flat on the paper and looked at him for a long moment. Then she looked out of the clinic window, at nothing in particular. Then back at him.

"All of them?"

"The bottom ones. On every plant."

Shoko got up.

She came out of the clinic and crouched beside her box in the corridor, next to him, and looked at the plants for a long time without saying anything. She touched one of the pale lower leaves with the tip of her finger. Then another. She lifted one, very gently, by its edge, and bent it, a little, between her finger and thumb.

It bent. It did not break.

"Sixty-eight days," said Shoko. Very quietly. "Since the first seedlings. Five threads." She let the leaf go. "I counted."

"Is it ready?"

"Those are," said Shoko. "The bottom ones. Not the plant. Just the bottom leaves." She was still looking at them. "You take them from the bottom up. A few at a time. As they go pale. That's what the farmer said. In the instructions." She paused. "Satoru had Hollis bring them."

"I remember."

"*Hang in bunches, leaves down*," said Shoko. As if reciting. "*Dark barn. Not too warm. One moon. Ready when it bends and does not break.*" She looked at the leaf she had bent. "That's for after. When they're dry. Not now. Now they just have to be the right colour to pick."

She looked at the pale lower leaves for a long time.

"Go and get Satoru," she said.

* * *

Gojo came at once.

He came along the corridor in his shirtsleeves with his blindfold on and his hair flat on one side, because Mau had knocked on his door and said *Shoko says the leaves*. He had not waited to fix his hair. He stopped beside the box and pushed the blindfold up and looked at the plants with his bare eyes.

He did not say anything clever.

He crouched down on the other side of the box from Shoko. He looked at the pale lower leaves the way he looked at everything with his bare eyes. Too much. All of it at once. Every vein in every leaf.

"They're ready," he said. "The bottom ones. I can see it. The colour's changing all the way in. Not just the edges." He looked at Shoko. "You were right. Sixty-eight days."

"I counted," said Shoko.

"I know you did," said Gojo.

They crouched on either side of the box in the clinic corridor at dawn, the two of them, and looked at the plants. They did not say anything for a long time. Mau stood a little way off with the cup still in his hand and did not say anything either.

"Will you help?" said Shoko at last.

"Pick them?"

"Pick them. Tie them. Hang them." She looked at the leaves. "It's fiddly work. Your hands are steadier than mine. Mine are steady for stitches. Not for this."

Gojo looked at her.

"Yes," he said.

* * *

They picked them together.

Mau watched. He had been asked to stay, by Shoko, with a look, so he stayed. He stood by the clinic door with the cup and watched the two of them crouch over the box and pick the lowest leaves from every plant, one at a time.

Shoko showed Gojo how. You held the stem of the plant steady with one hand. With the other, you took the pale leaf at its base, where it joined the stem, and snapped it downward. Clean. One movement. The leaf came away whole.

Gojo did the first one wrong. He pulled sideways and tore the leaf.

"Down," said Shoko. "Not sideways. *Down*."

"Down," said Gojo. He did the next one down. It came away clean.

"Like that."

"Like that," said Gojo, and did the next one. And the next. His hands were very steady. Mau watched them. The same hands that made the marble of Blue, that had once held a Purple ready and then let it go. Picking tobacco leaves, very carefully, one at a time, in a clinic corridor at dawn. They snapped each one down, and laid it flat in Shoko's open palm.

There were twenty-two leaves, in the end. The lowest two or three from every plant.

Shoko held them in both hands. A small stack of broad pale-green leaves, yellowing at the edges.

"That's not very many," said Gojo.

"It's the first ones," said Shoko. "From the bottom. There'll be more. Every few days. As they go pale." She looked at the stack. "This is the start."

* * *

They tied them in the barn.

Not the big barn. The small one at the back of the timber yard, where Wakana kept the good wood to season. It was dark in there, with no windows, only gaps between the boards where thin bars of morning light came through. It was cool, even in summer, because the timber yard shaded it. It smelled of sawdust and resin.

*Dark barn. Not too warm.*

Shoko had asked Wakana. Wakana had cleared a corner and put up a pole between two posts, at head height, and said nothing about why, because he did not need to.

Gojo tied the bunches.

Three or four leaves at a time, by their stems, with thin cord from the workshop. He tied each knot carefully, tight enough to hold and loose enough not to cut the stems. Shoko handed him the leaves. He tied them and hung them on the pole.

Leaves down.

When they were done, there were six small bunches hanging from the pole in the dark corner of the barn. Pale green, going yellow. Leaves down. Very still.

Shoko stood in front of them for a long time.

"One moon," she said.

"About a month," said Gojo.

"About a month." She did not look away from the bunches. "And then we see if it bends and doesn't break. And then—" She stopped.

She put her hand in her coat pocket.

Mau knew what was in it. Everyone in the house knew. One bent cigarette. Unlit. Intact. The last one from home. She had carried it since she arrived. Saved.

She did not take it out.

She stood with her hand in her pocket and looked at the six bunches hanging leaves down in the dark.

"*These* are the first ones from here," she said. Quietly. To the bunches. "That one's the last one from home." She took her hand out of her pocket. Empty. "I'm keeping that one. I'm not going to smoke it." She looked at Gojo. "I decided. Just now. I'm going to keep it. Forever, maybe. And I'm going to smoke these instead. When they're ready. The first ones from here."

Gojo looked at her for a long time.

"On the clinic step," he said. "With me beside you."

"On the clinic step," said Shoko. "Like the infirmary." She almost smiled. "Except nobody's coming back hurt."

"Except nobody's coming back hurt," said Gojo.

* * *

Anko came to see at noon.

She stood in the doorway of the small barn and looked at the six bunches hanging leaves down in the dark corner. Lime on her boots. The round still in her face.

She did not say anything for a long time.

"One moon," said Shoko, beside her.

"I heard," said Anko.

She looked at the bunches. Then she looked down the timber yard, toward the house. Toward the corridor where her own box stood outside her door. Where something nobody had planted had been growing since the wind brought it, with four leaves now. Five, as of this morning. Mau had counted when he watered it.

"Mine's got five leaves," she said.

"Mau told me," said Shoko.

"I don't know what it is."

"I know you don't."

Anko was quiet for a moment.

"Yours, you knew what it was," she said. "From the start. You planted it. You knew what you wanted." She looked at the bunches. "Mine, I didn't plant. I don't know what it is. I don't know what I want it to be." She paused. "I'm finding out the slow way."

Shoko looked at her.

"So am I," she said. "I thought I knew what I wanted. The last cigarette. Smoked on the step, for something worth it." She looked at the six bunches hanging leaves down. "And now I don't want that. I want these. The first ones from here. I didn't know that until this morning." She paused. "That's the slow way too."

Anko looked at her for a long moment.

Then she did something Mau had never seen her do. She put her hand on Shoko's shoulder. Briefly. Awkwardly. The way a person puts a hand on someone's shoulder when they have never done it before and are not sure where it goes.

"One moon," she said.

"One moon," said Shoko.

Anko took her hand back and went to bed, because she had been up all night on the round, and a barn full of drying tobacco was not a reason to stop sleeping.

* * *

Mau watered both boxes that evening.

Shoko's first. The plants with the lowest leaves gone now, the next ones up still deep green. Then Anko's, further along the corridor. The thing nobody had planted. Five leaves. Round ones, like hands held out.

He crouched beside it with the empty cup for a while after.

The stem was taller than it had been. As tall as his hand now. At the very top of it, between the two newest leaves, there was something small and tight and green that had not been there yesterday. Round. Closed.

A bud.

He looked at it for a long time.

He did not tell Anko. She wanted to find out the slow way. He would let her.

He put the cup back on the end of Momo's counter, where it would be waiting in the morning.
