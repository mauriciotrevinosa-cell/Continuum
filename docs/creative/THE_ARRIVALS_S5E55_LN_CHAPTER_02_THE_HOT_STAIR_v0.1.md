# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 14 — The Hot Stair

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 14
**Continuity state:** The fourth spending session (level 62, one hundred and sixty-four unspent): one hundred more into Intelligence (two hundred and forty in total). The reserve is roughly nine times its pre-festival depth, present level unchanged; Endurance stays one point, unmeasured; sixty-four are kept. Then floors 61–69 over two days (summarised from Anko's board), ending at a stair that gets hot toward seventy. Gojo looks and says "dragon". Mau reaches level 71 (one hundred and nine unspent)

*When: Day 376, afternoon, and Day 377. Umaru has not nailed up a fourth plank. She has taken the first three sheets down, written everything they said onto one new sheet, very small, and pinned it up in the middle of the first wall. "Consolidation," she said. "The Ministry is running out of wall."*

The new sheet said everything the old three had said, in Umaru's smallest square hand.

At the top: *INTELLIGENCE: 140. CUP: ~5.5x. (Solid.)*

Below: *ENDURANCE: 1. (Something. Too small to see. Broken line.)*

Below that: *REFILLS A LITTLE FASTER? (Ciel. Not sure why. Broken line.)*

And at the bottom, in a box: *164.*

"One hundred and sixty-four," said Umaru, tapping the box. "Since the Warden." She looked at Mau. "You didn't keep the Warden."

"No."

"You chose."

"I chose."

"The Ministry approves," said Umaru. "Of the choosing." She looked at the box. "The Ministry now asks about seventy."

* * *

"Gojo looked," said Mau. "From sixty-nine. Yesterday." He paused. "He said the stair's hot. All the way up. He said there's something at the top that breathes, and when it breathes the stair gets hotter."

Umaru looked at him.

"Breathes fire?"

"He didn't say fire. He said hot." Mau thought about it. "He said *dragon*. And then he said *a real one*. Not like Lantern. Lantern's a dragon, but Lantern was a red-gate dragon. He said this one's older."

Yuka, at the table with the copy book, had stopped writing.

"Older," she said.

"Older," said Mau.

Umaru turned to the wall. She looked at the new sheet for a long time.

"Lantern nearly emptied the cup at three and a half times," she said. "Standing with the bear. On thirty." She tapped *5.5x*. "Beru nearly emptied it at five and a half. Raising him." She turned round. "If seventy is older than Lantern, and you want to keep it—"

"If I want to keep it."

"*If*," said Umaru. "Then you'll need to be able to stand things against it. For a long time. Lantern and Igris and Beru. Maybe all three. And still raise it after." She looked at the box. "One hundred."

"One hundred?"

"Into Intelligence. That makes two hundred and forty." She looked at Yuka. Yuka checked the column in the copy book and nodded. "Ciel says if it still holds, that's about nine times what it was before the festival." She paused. "And then sixty-four kept. For the other words. For when we know."

Mau looked at the box.

"That's a lot," he said.

"Seventy is a dragon," said Umaru. "An old one." She folded her arms inside her sleeves. "The Ministry would rather you had too much than not enough." She paused. "You can always choose not to keep it. Like the Warden. But you can't choose to keep it if the cup says no."

Mau looked at her for a long time.

"You've been thinking about that sentence," he said. "Haven't you."

"Since the Warden," said Umaru. "Out loud. At breakfast." She looked at Yuka.

"She has," said Yuka.

* * *

It took a long time. It always did.

One at a time. Ciel watching. Rimuru at the end of the bench, repeating. Yuka counting in the copy book. Around the thirtieth, Ichika came and stood at the end of the table in her too-long sash, watching. Around the fiftieth, Armin came, with the map of the house under his arm. He stood behind Yuka, reading the copy book over her shoulder, very fast. Then he went to the wall and read the sheet there, very slowly. He did not say anything. But Mau saw him take Anko's pencil from behind his ear and write something on the back of his own map.

Around the eightieth, Milim floated over the table and counted out loud, and got it right, all twenty of the last ones, which nobody had ever seen Milim do.

At one hundred, Yuka put down her pencil.

"One hundred," she said. "Two hundred and forty."

Mau looked at the window.

The cup was enormous now. He could feel the size of it, the way you feel the size of a room in the dark by the sound your voice makes. The fill at the bottom was the same fill. Days of rest since the Warden. It was a good deal of fill. In a cup this size, it looked like very little.

*Roughly nine times,* said Ciel. *Present level unchanged. Still nothing else moved. Only the reserve.* A pause. *And it is refilling faster. I'm surer now. Not a little. Noticeably. I still don't know why.*

Rimuru repeated it.

Umaru turned to the wall. She took the pencil. She looked at *REFILLS A LITTLE FASTER? (Ciel. Not sure why. Broken line.)* for a long moment.

Then she crossed out *A LITTLE*, very neatly, one straight line. She did not make the line solid. She left it broken.

*REFILLS FASTER. (Ciel. Surer. Still not sure why.)*

"Question," she said. "Partly answered. Keep watching."

Then she wrote *240* in the Intelligence box, and *~9x* beside the cup, and *64* in the box at the bottom, with a solid circle round it.

She sat down.

Touka was already in the kitchen doorway with the glass of milk. To the line.

* * *

Armin showed Mau the back of his map afterwards.

On the back of the map of the house, in his small neat hand, a diagram. Not the house. The cup. A tall narrow shape, with a line across it near the bottom for the fill. And beside it, a column of numbers. Thirty. Eighty. One forty. Two forty. And beside those, another column. Two. Three and a half. Five and a half. Nine.

And at the bottom, in very small writing:

*It isn't the points making it fill faster. It's the size. A bigger cup catches more. Like a wider bucket in the rain.*

Mau looked at it for a long time.

"How do you know?" he said.

"I don't," said Armin. "It's a guess." He looked at the diagram. "But Ciel said *the bigger it is, the more it wants to be full*. On the third wall. Yuka wrote it in the copy book." He tapped the line. "If that's true, it's not the points. It's the shape. The points made the shape. The shape does the rest."

Mau looked at him.

"You should tell Umaru."

"I'm going to," said Armin. "Tomorrow. When she's had her milk." He folded the map. "She'll want to draw it as a broken line."

"She will."

"That's fine," said Armin. "It's a guess. Guesses are broken lines." He tucked the map under his arm. "That's the best way I've ever seen anyone draw what they don't know yet."

* * *

Anko's board told the next day plainly.

*Day 377, morning. Mau, Yuta, Maki, Kaneki. From 60. Floor 61: a bridge of bone. Floor 62: a market of lanterns, all lit, all burning nothing. Floor 63: a well going down forever. Kaneki heard the bottom. Cleared, all three.*

*Day 377, afternoon. Mau, Gojo, Frieren, Fern. From 60, walked to 63. Floor 64: ash. Floor 65: more ash, and something under it that moved. Fern found it first. Floor 66: a river of black glass, cooling. Floor 67: a forest, burned. Floor 68: a city, burned. Cleared, all.*

Under that, in Fern's hand, small:

*Everything after sixty-three is burned. Getting hotter.*

And under that, in Gojo's:

*69 is a stair. It's hot. 70 is at the top of it. 70 is a dragon. An old one. —S.G.*

* * *

They stood at the bottom of the stair on sixty-nine at the end of Day 377.

All four of them. Mau, Gojo, Frieren, Fern. They had come through sixty-four to sixty-eight, the ash and the black glass and the burned forest and the burned city, and now they were at the bottom of a long stair. Stone, going up, straight, between two walls. And the walls were warm.

Not warm like the kilns. Warm like an oven when the bread is in.

Mau put his hand flat on the wall. He took it away.

"It's breathing," said Gojo. He had his blindfold up. He was looking up the stair with his bare eyes, toward seventy, which they could not see. "Every few minutes. In. Out. When it breathes out, the whole stair gets hotter."

As he said it, the stair got hotter.

A wave of it, coming down from above, dry and fierce, like opening the door of a forge. Fern took a step back. Frieren did not move. Mau felt it on his face, and in his eyes, behind the filter, which did not like heat.

Then it passed.

"That's it breathing out," said Gojo. "It's asleep. I think. It breathes like something asleep." He lowered the blindfold. "Something very big. Asleep at the top of a stair. In a room full of heat."

*Floor 69 cleared.*

*Level 71. One hundred and nine points unspent.*

Nobody said anything about the points.

Frieren was looking up the stair.

"Tomorrow," she said.

"Tomorrow," said Mau.

"Rest first," said Fern. Very firmly. "And the cup."

"The cup's nine times," said Mau.

"Rest first," said Fern again. "Nine times empty is still empty."

Mau looked at her.

"Anko's going to write that down," he said.

"I'm going to write it down myself," said Fern.

She did. On the board, under Gojo's line, in her small neat hand, when they came home:

*Nine times empty is still empty. Rest first. (Fern.)*

And underlined it twice.
