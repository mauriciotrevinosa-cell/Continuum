# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 16 — Operator Only

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18, DH-124)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 16
**Continuity state:** Day 378, floor 70, continuous. Mau is alone with five shadows against Kamish's true breath, the one it held back while others were there. He survives the breath only by sending every shadow down into his shadow and taking it on the last third of his space, which fails at the edges: burns, his left hand charred, hair singed away on one side. Tusk's blue fire against the dragon's is a candle against a sun. Igris and Beru are broken apart (pieces, not lost); Kaisel is torn out of the air. Mau is down to the cup's dregs, no space left and nothing in the bag that is big enough. Kamish, almost kindly, asks him to show what is under the shadows. Ciel finds something in the walls from the containment in S3: the scale of a True Dragon, half-built by the Noise from Rimuru's memory of the cave, never finished, never let out, and far too big for his body. She tells him it will tear him apart. He says it has to be him. The chapter ends as he lets it out

*When: Day 378, floor 70. The same breath. Outside, at the screen, three people have just come out of the frame onto the dry ground as if thrown, and the frame behind them has gone dark.*

He did the only thing he could think of.

"*Back,*" he said. "*All of you. Back.*"

They went. Igris, from the left foreleg, the long sword pulling out of the joint. Beru, from behind the knee, with a sound of protest that was cut off as he sank. Kaisel, folding down into the black stone. Tusk last, the blue fire in his skull going out as he went.

The drain stopped.

Everything he had left in the cup stayed in the cup. Everything he had left in the other reserve, the third of it that Ciel had counted, went into the space.

Not round two people. Not round anyone. Round him. Small. As small as he could make it and still be inside it. A shell, a hand's breadth from his skin.

Kamish breathed out.

* * *

The first breath had been a tide.

This was the sun.

He did not see it. There was nothing to see. There was only white, all at once, everywhere, so bright it went through his eyelids and his filter and the backs of his eyes and kept going. The black stone under his feet did not melt. It went straight from stone to light. The molten lake from the first breath was not a lake any more; it was gone, risen into the air as a glowing mist and then not even that.

The space took it.

He felt it take it. He felt it go down, the third he had left, the way a candle goes down in a draught. A quarter. A fifth. He held it. He held it with everything Gojo had ever said to him in the yard. *Not a wall. You don't push it back. You let it never arrive.*

It was arriving.

At the edges. Where his attention was thinnest. His left hand, out to the side, where he had held it up without thinking, as if a hand could stop the sun. The space was thin there. The light came through the thin place and went over the back of his hand and he felt it not burn, exactly. He felt it *take*. Skin. More than skin.

The left side of his head. Where the shaman's fire had already been. His hair on that side was there and then it was not.

His ribs. His shin. His ear.

*Hold it,* said Ciel. *It's ending. Mau, hold it, it's ending, it's—*

It ended.

* * *

He was standing.

He did not know how. He was standing on the only piece of black stone left in the front half of the hall, a circle no wider than his own shadow, with glowing air on every side of him, and he was standing.

He looked at his left hand.

He looked away from it.

*The other reserve,* said Ciel. Her voice was not level at all. *There's almost nothing. A sliver. Enough for a breath of space. Not enough for that again. Not a quarter of that.* A pause. *The cup's a third full. You sent them back in time. It's the only reason there's anything.*

Across the glowing air, on its mound, Kamish was looking at him.

Its chest was dark again. Spent. For now. The great flanks heaving, slowly, gathering.

"*Small king*," it said.

There was something in its voice Mau had not heard there before.

"*You stood*," said Kamish. "*In my true fire. You stood.*" It lowered its head a little. "*No one has stood in my true fire.*"

"How long," said Mau. His voice came out of him like something torn. "Before you can do that again."

The gold eyes looked at him for a long time.

"*Not long*," said Kamish. "*I am sorry.*"

* * *

So he did not wait.

"*Igris. Beru. Tusk. Kaisel.*"

The cup went. Not as fast as before. Faster than it could take. They came up out of the tiny circle of black stone round his feet, crowded, crowding, because there was nowhere else to stand, and went.

Igris and Beru at the legs again. Fast this time, both of them, not careful. Kaisel up into the glowing air. Tusk on Mau's left, the staff level, the blue fire pouring out of the skull.

It was a candle against the sun.

Mau saw it. He saw Tusk see it. The blue fire went into the dragon's face and the dragon did not turn away from it this time. It did not need to. The blue fire broke on the red scales like water on a hot stone and was gone.

Kamish moved.

Not much. It did not need to move much. It shifted its weight, and Beru was under the shifting foot, and Beru came apart. Mau felt the hole open in him where Beru had been. *Pieces,* said Ciel, very fast, *not lost, pieces.* Kamish turned its head and caught Kaisel out of the air in its jaws as the wyvern dove at its eye, and closed them, and Kaisel came apart between its teeth and fell in pieces back into the dark.

Igris lasted longest.

He held the left foreleg. He held it with his sword driven into the joint to the hilt, and his armoured shoulder against the scales, and when Kamish lifted the leg to shake him off he did not let go. He went up with it. He hung from the dragon's leg in the glowing air with his sword in its joint and did not let go.

*"My liege,"* said Igris. Very quietly. Across all that distance. *"I cannot hold it."*

"I know," said Mau.

*"I am sorry."*

Kamish brought the leg down.

Igris came apart against the stone.

* * *

Tusk was still standing. Only Tusk.

He stood on Mau's left with the staff level and the blue fire going out of the skull, guttering, and he did not stop pouring it into the dragon's face. Even now. Even knowing.

"*Great one*," said Tusk. Not turning his head. "*Go back. Through the stair. I will burn until it reaches you.*"

"There's no stair," said Mau.

There was not. He had looked. Behind him, where the doorway had been, there was only red stone. Smooth. No opening. The way the frame at the screen had gone dark, the way the companions had gone, the doorway had gone too.

*Operator only.*

"*Then*," said Tusk, "*I will burn until it reaches us both.*"

"Back," said Mau. "Tusk. Back."

The shaman turned his head. For one moment. The cold blue lights in his eyes on Mau.

"*Great one—*"

"*Back.* Please."

Tusk went down into the stone.

* * *

He was alone.

Truly alone. Not a shadow standing. Not a companion. The cup at the bottom. The other reserve a sliver. A tiny circle of black stone in a sea of glowing air. His left hand. His face.

Kamish looked down at him.

It did not breathe. Not yet. Its chest was dark, gathering, slowly, but it did not breathe. It lowered its head instead. All the way down. Down to the edge of the glowing air, its gold eyes level with him, very close, enormous.

"*Small king*," it said. Very quietly. Almost kindly. "*Your little shadows are broken. Your walls are thin. Your companions are gone, and someone up there is watching you be alone.*"

Mau did not say anything.

"*I have been at the top of this stair for longer than your house has stood*," said Kamish. "*I know the smell of a thing that has more in it than it shows. You smell of it.*" The gold eyes did not blink. "*You have smelled of it since the bottom of my stair. Something under the shadows. Something under the walls.*" It was quiet. "*Something old.*"

"I don't know what you mean."

"*No*," said Kamish. "*You do not.*" It drew back a little. Its chest began, faintly, to glow. "*Show me anyway. Show me what is under everything, small king. Before I burn it.*"

* * *

*Ciel.*

*I know.* Her voice was very strange. *I know what it means. Mau, I've known since the containment.*

*Tell me.*

*In the bay. When Rimuru took you in. When you were coming apart inside him.* A long pause. *The Noise read everything in him. His skills. His friends. His enemies. A city's worth of them.* Another pause. *And his memory of a cave. Of his first friend. Of something sealed in light for three hundred years, so vast that standing near it felt like standing in the open mouth of weather.*

Mau did not move.

*A True Dragon,* said Ciel. *The Noise tried to build it. Not the dragon. The scale. The size of it. The pressure. It started building it inside a body that was already coming apart, and the building nearly killed you, and then it stopped. When the walls went up.* A pause. *It never finished. It never threw it away either. It's still there. Under everything. Half-built. Folded so small I almost forgot.*

*Can I use it?*

*No.*

*Ciel.*

*It's not a technique, Mau. It's not a pattern from the kitchen. It's the size of a thing that is to a dragon what a dragon is to you.* Her voice broke. *Your body can't hold it. Not a fraction of it. Last time it was only being built and it tore you open. If you let it out—if you let even part of it out—it will tear you apart from the inside. I don't know if I can put you back. I don't know if anyone can.*

The glow in the dragon's chest was rising. Gold. White.

*Is there anything else?* said Mau.

Ciel did not answer.

*Is there anything else in the bag,* said Mau, *big enough?*

A long silence.

*No,* said Ciel.

* * *

He thought about the frame at the screen. Dark. Gojo on the other side of it. Yuta. Frieren. Standing on the dry ground in front of a dark frame they could not go through, with Anko's board beside them.

He thought about what Frieren would do if he did not come out.

He thought about the road. One second. *She's got it.*

Nobody had it. Nobody was here. *Operator only.* Someone had made sure of that.

"*It has to be me,*" he said aloud.

Kamish's head tilted. Very slightly.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *Please.*

*I'm sorry,* said Mau. *Hold what you can. Tell me when I'm dying.*

*You're always saying that.*

*I know.*

A pause. The longest. The dragon's chest going from gold to white.

*I'll hold what I can,* said Ciel. *I'll tell you.* Very small. *Come back.*

He reached down. Under the shadows. Under the walls. Under everything, into the very bottom of the dark, where something enormous lay folded so small that even Ciel had almost forgotten it.

And let it out.
