# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 16 — Small King

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 16
**Continuity state:** The extraction of Kamish. The cup is nearly dry, so they wait an hour in the cooling hall while the nine-times reserve refills faster, as Armin guessed, and the old dragon's dark waits longer than the others did. Mau raises it with a plain request (keep watch over a house). Kamish speaks, deep, slow and approving, calls Mau "small king" and Frieren "the elf who went through the inside". The floor 70 transfer point is secured. Mau reaches level 72 (one hundred and fourteen unspent). Seven registered shadows, with Lantern broken and awaiting remaking. Home within the agreed time

*When: Day 378, the furnace-hall on seventy. The molten lake has crusted over black. The great red body lies on its mound. Under it, the dark is gathering, deeper than anything. The cup is nearly dry.*

*Can I?* said Mau.

Ciel did not answer for a long time.

He felt her look. At the cup, nine times deep, with almost nothing in the bottom of it after four shadows standing at once for three minutes, and Lantern torn apart. At the other reserve, empty. At the dark under the dragon, which was the deepest dark he had ever felt. Deeper than Beru's. Much deeper. Like standing at the edge of a well and dropping a stone and never hearing it land.

*No,* said Ciel. *Not now.*

Mau sat on the black stone and closed his eyes.

*Not now,* said Ciel again. More gently. *The cup is nearly dry. Raising this would take more than you have. A great deal more.* A pause. *If you tried now, it would fail. And I don't know what happens to an extraction that fails halfway.*

*Then I can't keep it.*

*I didn't say that,* said Ciel.

He opened his eyes.

*The cup is refilling,* said Ciel. *Faster than it ever has. I can feel it. Armin was right, I think. A bigger cup catches more.* A pause. *And this one is waiting. The others waited a minute, two, while you decided. This one is waiting longer. I can feel that too. It's old. Old things are patient.* Another pause. *If you rest here, an hour, maybe a little more, the cup may have enough. And it may still be waiting.*

*May.*

*May,* said Ciel. *I don't know. I've never seen anything this old wait.*

* * *

He told them.

Gojo, sitting on the black stone beside him. Yuta, with his sword across his knees and gold on the blade. Frieren, still kneeling, with her cold hands on his face, which she had not taken away.

"An hour," said Gojo.

"Maybe more."

"In here."

"In here."

Gojo looked round the hall. At the crusted lake, black now, still glowing in the cracks. At the red haze, thinner now that the dragon was not breathing. At the great red body on the mound.

"It's very hot," he said.

"It's cooling," said Yuta.

"It's very hot and cooling," said Gojo. He lay back on the black stone with his hands behind his head. "Fine. An hour. I've lain in worse places. I've lain in a box."

Frieren took her hands off Mau's face.

She sat down beside him. Close. Her shoulder against his.

"An hour," she said. "I'm good at waiting."

"You're very good at waiting," said Mau.

"I've been waiting for a thousand years," said Frieren. "An hour is nothing." She leaned against him. "Rest. Close your eyes. I'll watch the dragon."

"It's dead."

"I'll watch it anyway," said Frieren.

* * *

He did not sleep.

He sat on the black stone with his back against Frieren's shoulder and his eyes closed and felt the cup refill. Slowly. Faster than it ever had. Like water rising in a well after rain. A little. Then a little more.

The hall cooled round them. The cracks in the crusted lake went from orange to red to dark. The haze thinned. Somewhere far above, in the smoke where the roof must be, something dripped.

Gojo, lying on his back on the stone, started to hum. Very badly. Something Mau did not know.

"What's that?" said Yuta.

"A song," said Gojo. "From school. We used to sing it on the way back from missions. Shoko and Geto and me." He was quiet for a moment. "Badly. Shoko always said we sang it badly."

He kept humming.

After a while, Yuta started humming too. He did not know the song. He hummed underneath Gojo's, a low steady note, the way Ryo played bass under Bocchi.

Frieren did not hum. She watched the dragon.

* * *

At fifty minutes, Ciel spoke.

*Mau.*

He opened his eyes.

*The cup,* said Ciel. *It's not full. But there's enough. I think. Barely. For this.* A pause. *And it's still waiting. The dark. It hasn't moved. It's been waiting the whole time.*

Mau looked at the dragon on its mound. At the dark underneath it, deeper than a well.

He stood up.

His legs held. Just. Frieren stood with him, her hand under his arm. Gojo stopped humming and sat up. Yuta stood, with his sword.

"Ciel says enough," said Mau. "Barely."

"Then sit down," said Gojo. "Before you fall down. Maki's rule."

Mau did not sit down.

He walked across the black stone to the foot of the mound. Up the mound, slowly, one step at a time, with Frieren's hand under his arm until the last step, when he let her go. He stopped beside the great red head lying on the stone. One gold eye closed. The other with Yuta's sword-wound in it, closed too.

He put his hand flat on the red scales of its jaw.

They were still warm. Not cold, like Lantern's jaw. Not yet.

* * *

He thought about what he was asking.

It had talked to him. Before it fought. It had called him *small king*. It had watched him come up its stair and smelled the Warden on him and found it *interesting*. It had let Lantern bite its throat and said *little cousin bit well*. At the end, with Frieren's light going out in its throat and Yuta's sword in its eye, it had looked up at him on the wyvern and said *good*.

He did not need to be angrier than it had been. It had not been angry at all.

*You've been at the top of this stair a long time,* he thought, with his hand on the warm red scales. *Watching people come up. Waiting.* He looked at the hall. At the cooling lake, the red haze. *I'm not asking you to wait at the top of a stair any more.*

He thought about the house. Every window lit. The kilns breathing red. The roof with ten people on it. The chalked corner at the end of the west wing, for whoever comes.

*I'm asking you to keep watch,* he thought. *Over a house. Where people sleep. So they can.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the mound.

Slowly. Slower than any of them. Like water rising in a well. Up through the black stone, through the red scales, through the vast curled body. The red went out of it. Not all at once. Scale by scale. From the tail up, along the body, along the folded wings, up the long neck. The gold edges on every scale dimmed and went dark. Until the whole great shape was dark. No shine on it. Like Lantern. Like all of them.

Mau felt the cost.

It took everything. Not almost everything. Everything. The cup went down to the floor. He felt the floor of it. He felt it scrape. His knees went. He put his other hand on the dragon's jaw to stay upright. Frieren's hand was under his arm again before he knew he needed it.

The dark finished rising.

Kamish lifted its head.

* * *

It lifted it slowly, the way it had lifted it when they first came in. Off the stone. Up, on its long neck, higher and higher, until its head was far above them in the thinning haze. Its wings stayed folded. Its body stayed curled on the mound.

It opened its eyes.

Both of them. Whole. The pierced one too. Where they had been gold, there were two cold blue lights. Larger than the others. Much larger. Like two lamps, very high up, very far away.

It looked down at Mau.

"*Small king*," it said.

The voice was the same. Deep. Slow. Laid down like stones. But quieter now. Not filling the hall. Just reaching him.

"*You came back for me.*"

"I didn't leave," said Mau.

"*You rested. In my hall. For an hour. While I waited.*" The cold blue lights looked at him. "*You could have gone. Come back another day. Many small kings would have.*" A pause. "*You sat on my floor and waited for the cup to fill. So that you could take me with you.*"

"I didn't want to leave you at the top of the stair," said Mau.

Kamish was silent for a long time.

"*No*," it said at last. "*I heard. What you asked.*" Its head came down a little. "*A house. Where people sleep. Keep watch.*" It was quiet. "*I have kept watch at the top of a stair for longer than your house has stood. I know how.*"

"Will you?"

Kamish looked at him with its two great cold lights.

"*Small king*," it said. "*I already am.*"

* * *

It turned its head.

Slowly. On its long neck. Away from Mau. Down. Toward the foot of the mound.

Toward Frieren.

She looked up at it. She had her staff in her hand. She did not move.

"*The elf*," said Kamish.

Frieren did not say anything.

"*The elf who went through the inside*," said Kamish. "*While the white-haired one's small blue thing was in my throat. You put your light down after it. You went through the inside of me.*" Its cold blue lights were on her. "*No one has ever gone through the inside of me.*"

"Someone had to," said Frieren.

Kamish was silent.

Then it did something none of them expected. It lowered its head. All the way. Down the mound, past Mau, until its vast dark jaw was resting on the black stone in front of Frieren's feet. Its cold blue lights were level with her face.

It did not bow. Dragons did not bow, Mau thought. It lowered its head and rested it in front of her, the way a very old dog lowers its head in front of someone it has decided is not a stranger.

"*Elf*," it said. Very quietly.

"Frieren," said Frieren.

"*Frieren*," said Kamish, as if filing it. "*I will remember.*"

Frieren looked at the vast dark head resting at her feet.

Then, slowly, she put her hand on it. Flat. On the dark jaw. The way Ori had put her hand on Lantern's.

"It's warm," she said. Surprised.

"*I am a fire dragon*," said Kamish. "*Even now.*"

* * *

*Floor 70 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 or 70.*

*Level 72. One hundred and fourteen points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 7. Places: 20.*

Mau read it out. Sitting on the black stone, because his legs had finally decided, and Frieren had let them.

"Seven," said Yuta.

"Seven. Lantern's in pieces."

"You'll remake him."

"When there's something in the cup," said Mau. "There's nothing in the cup."

"*Back*," he said to Kamish.

The old dragon lifted its head off the stone. It looked at him once more with its two great cold lights.

"*Small king*," it said.

Then it went down into the black stone, slowly, the way it had risen. Like water draining from a well. The cold blue lights went last. Very high up. Then not at all.

* * *

They went home through the small frame. The seal had appeared at the far end of the hall, past the mound, where the haze was thinnest.

Gojo carried him.

He did not ask. He picked Mau up off the black stone, the way you pick up something that cannot stand, and carried him across the hall to the frame. Frieren walked beside them. Yuta walked behind with his sword, with the gold still on the blade.

"I'm not a child," said Mau, into Gojo's shoulder.

"You're nine times empty," said Gojo. "Fern said that's still empty."

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Four hours forty," she said. "From sixty." She looked at the four of them. At Gojo, carrying Mau. At Yuta, with gold on his sword. At Frieren, whose hand was still warm.

"What happened?" she said.

Nobody answered for a moment.

"A dragon," said Gojo at last. "An old one. It talked. It called him small king." He shifted Mau in his arms. "It breathed fire on us once. We didn't let it do it twice. Frieren went through the inside. Yuta got the eye." He paused. "And then we sat in its hall for an hour so the cup could fill. And it waited for us."

Anko looked at him for a long time.

Then she wrote it down.

*Floor 70. Kamish (the notice). The old dragon. Talked. "Small king." Breathed once. Frieren through the inside. Yuta, the eye. Waited an hour. It waited too. Lantern in pieces.*

And under it, smaller:

*Gojo carried him home.*
