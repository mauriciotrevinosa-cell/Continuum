*When: Day 378, floor 70. The same breath. Kamish's chest has gone from gold to white. At the screen, Rimuru has just stopped in the middle of a sentence and put his hand on the frame.*

The pressure came first.

Before the light. Before the shape. Before anything. It came up out of the bottom of the dark the way the sea comes up when the tide turns, all at once and from everywhere, and the hall changed.

The glowing air stopped glowing. Not cooled. Pressed. Held down. The haze of the first breath, the risen stone, the heat itself, flattened against the floor as if a hand had come down on top of all of it. The red smoke where the roof should have been parted and went away to the edges and stayed there, afraid to come back.

Kamish stopped breathing in.

The white in its chest did not go out. It held. Its whole vast body held, the way a deer holds at the edge of a wood when something larger than any wolf has stepped out of the trees behind it.

Its gold eyes were on Mau.

"*…That*," said Kamish. Very low. "*I knew it. I knew I smelled it.*"

* * *

Then the shape.

He did not feel himself become anything. He did not grow. His bones did not change. That was what he would try to explain afterward, to Rimuru, to Frieren, to anyone, and never manage. He did not turn into a dragon.

A dragon turned into him.

It came up out of the bottom of the dark round him. Out of his shadow, out of the tiny circle of black stone, out of the air pressed flat on every side. Dark, and then gold at the edges, the dark-gold of something buried a very long time and seeing light for the first time. Pressure, made into a shape. Weight, made into a body. It rose round him, enormous, and he was inside it, at the heart of it, where a heart would be.

Wings. He felt them open. Not his. Not anyone's. Wider than the hall. They went out to either side into the smoke, and the smoke fled from them, and the walls of the hall groaned where the tips of them pressed.

A neck. A head. Rising above Kamish's head. Above everything. Horns like the roots of mountains. A jaw. Eyes, and he was looking out of them, and he could see the whole hall from up there, the whole furnace of it, small, very small, and the red dragon on its mound below him.

Below him.

It was looking up.

* * *

*Mau.* Ciel. Very far away and very close, both at once. *Five seconds.*

He felt his body.

His real body. Small. At the heart of the vast dark-gold shape. And the shape was too big for it. Ciel had said so. Ciel had been right. The pressure that made the dragon was pressing on him too, from the inside, from everywhere, the way it had pressed on the hall, the way it had once pressed on a slime's memory of a cave.

His skin split.

Along his arms. His chest. His back where Beru's three lines had been, opening again, and then other places opening that had never been opened. Blood. Not running. Leaving. Pulled out of him everywhere at once by the weight.

*Ten seconds,* said Ciel. *Mau. I'll count. I'll tell you every five. When I say forty, you let go, or there won't be anything left to let go with.*

*Forty.*

*Forty. Not one more.*

A rib went. He heard it, inside the vast shape. A tiny sound. A twig.

* * *

Kamish breathed.

It did not wait to see what he would do. It was old. It was a dragon. It knew that you do not wait when something larger than you has stood up in your hall. Its white chest opened, and its jaws opened, and the true fire came out of it, all of it, the sun, straight up at the dark-gold shape above it.

It did not arrive.

Mau did not do anything. He did not put up the space. He had no space. He did not need it.

The fire came up toward the True Dragon's shape and met the weight of it, the pressure, the sheer size of what a True Dragon was, and slowed. And slowed. And hung there in the air between the two of them, a pillar of white, roaring, going nowhere, the way Beru's claws had once hung in front of Frieren's face.

It could not get there.

Nothing that size could get there.

Kamish kept breathing. Its whole body shook with it. The pillar went on and on, and did not get one hand's breadth closer.

Then it ran out.

The fire stopped. Kamish's jaws closed. Its chest was dark.

It looked up at him.

*Fifteen,* said Ciel.

* * *

For a long moment the old dragon did not move.

It lay on its mound with its chest heaving and its gold eyes on the vast dark-gold head above it. And Mau, looking down out of eyes that were not his, saw something happen in its face that he had not seen there before. Not fear. Not surprise.

Recognition.

"*Not borrowed*," said Kamish.

Its voice was very low. Almost wondering.

"*I thought it would be borrowed. Like the rest. A knight's patience. A girl's kitchen.*" Its gold eyes moved over the shape of the True Dragon. Slowly. Taking it in. "*It is not. It was read. Somewhere. From someone. And then it was built.*" A long breath. "*Built, in the dark, by the thing under your walls. Piece by piece. For a year. Without anyone lending it a single scale.*"

It lifted its great head a little off the mound.

"*A thing that builds is its own, small king.*" The gold eyes held his. "*That is yours.*"

Mau did not answer. He could not. His body was coming apart at the heart of a shape that was too big for it, and every breath was a rib.

"*Then I will show you mine*," said Kamish.

* * *

It stood up.

Not the way it had stood before. Before, it had unfolded one wing, slowly, the size of the long room, and the heat had come off it in a wave. Before, it had been an old dragon on a mound, curious, courteous, holding back.

It stopped holding back.

The red scales went gold. Then white. Not the white of its chest before a breath. All of it. Every scale, from its horns to the tip of its tail, white-hot, so bright the haze of the hall lit up round it like a lantern lit inside a fog. The black stone of the mound under its feet did not melt. It went straight to light and was gone, and Kamish stood on nothing, on its own heat.

And its wings.

They opened, and they were not leather any more. They were fire. Open fire, in the shape of wings, wider than they had been, wider than the hall, pouring up into the smoke where the roof should have been. The smoke did not flee from them. The smoke burned.

The heat came up off it, and the hall began to come apart.

The walls ran. The far edges of the floor, the ones the first breath had not reached, glowed and slumped and flowed toward the middle like wax toward a wick. The writing on the doorway stone blistered and was gone.

"*This*," said Kamish, "*is what the borrowed light was needed for.*"

It was not loud. It was very quiet. The quiet of something enormous that has decided.

"*Five of them. On the ash of the last city. With light lent from above the kings.*" Its white-hot head rose, level with the vast dark-gold head of the True Dragon. Eye to eye, at last, over the glass of the melted floor. "*No one has made me show this since.*"

It looked at him.

"*You will not need borrowed light, small king*," said Kamish. "*You have brought your own.*"

*Twenty,* said Ciel.

The two dragons faced each other in the burning hall.
