*When: Day 378, floor 70. The same breath. Kamish's chest has gone from gold to white. At the screen, Rimuru has just stopped in the middle of a sentence and put his hand on the frame.*

The pressure came first.

Before the light. Before the shape. Before anything. It came up out of the bottom of the dark the way the sea comes up when the tide turns, all at once and from everywhere, and the hall changed.

The glowing air stopped glowing. Not cooled. Pressed. Held down. The haze of the first breath, the risen stone, the heat itself, flattened against the floor as if a hand had come down on top of all of it. The red smoke where the roof should have been parted and went away to the edges and stayed there, afraid to come back.

Kamish stopped breathing in.

The white in its chest did not go out. It held. Its whole vast body held, the way a deer holds at the edge of a wood when something larger than any wolf has stepped out of the trees behind it.

Its gold eyes were on Mau.

"*…That*," said Kamish. Very low. "*I knew it. I knew I smelled it.*"

* * *

Then the shape.

He did not feel himself become anything. He did not grow. His bones did not change. That was what he would try to explain afterward, to Rimuru, to Frieren, to anyone, and never manage. He did not turn into a dragon.

A dragon turned into him.

It came up out of the bottom of the dark round him. Out of his shadow, out of the tiny circle of black stone, out of the air pressed flat on every side. Dark, and then gold at the edges, like the dark-gold of something that has been buried for a very long time and is seeing light for the first time. Pressure, made into a shape. Weight, made into a body. It rose round him, enormous, and he was inside it, at the heart of it, where a heart would be.

Wings. He felt them open. Not his. Not anyone's. The size of the hall. Bigger. They went out to either side into the smoke and the smoke fled from them.

A neck. A head. Rising above Kamish's head. Above everything. Horns. A jaw. Eyes, and he was looking out of them, and he could see the whole hall from up there, the whole furnace of it, small, very small, and the red dragon on its mound below him.

Below him.

It was looking up.

* * *

*Mau.* Ciel. Very far away and very close, both at once. *Five seconds.*

He felt his body.

His real body. Small. At the heart of the vast dark-gold shape. And the shape was too big for it. Ciel had said so. Ciel had been right. The pressure that made the dragon was pressing on him too, from the inside, from everywhere, the way it had pressed on the hall, the way it had pressed on Rimuru's memory of a cave.

His skin split.

He felt it go. Along his arms. His chest. His back where Beru's three lines had been, opening again, and then other places opening that had never been opened. Blood. Not running. Leaving. Coming out of him everywhere at once, from his nose and his ears and his eyes and the splits in his skin, pulled out by the weight.

*Ten seconds,* said Ciel. *Mau—*

A rib went. Then another. He heard them, inside the vast shape. Tiny sounds. Twigs.

*Do it,* said Ciel. *Whatever you're going to do. Do it now.*

* * *

Kamish breathed.

It did not wait to see what he would do. It was old. It was a dragon. It knew that you do not wait when something larger than you has stood up in your hall. Its white chest opened, and its jaws opened, and the true fire came out of it, all of it, everything, the sun again, straight up at the dark-gold shape above it.

It did not arrive.

Mau did not do anything. He did not put up the space. He had no space. He did not need it.

The fire came up toward the True Dragon's shape and met the weight of it, the pressure, the sheer size of what a True Dragon was, and slowed. And slowed. And hung there in the air between the two of them, white, roaring, going nowhere, the way Beru's claws had hung in front of Frieren's face.

It could not get there.

Nothing could get there. Nothing was big enough to get there.

Kamish kept breathing. Its whole body shook with it. The fire went on and on, a pillar of white between the mound and the dark-gold head above it, and the pillar did not get one hand's breadth closer.

Then it ran out.

The fire stopped. Kamish's jaws closed. Its head dropped, a little. Its chest was dark.

It looked up at him.

* * *

*Fifteen seconds,* said Ciel. *You're bleeding out. Mau. Mau, finish it or let go.*

He brought the shape down.

Not a strike. Not a bite. Not anything a dragon does with claws or jaws. He brought the weight of it down. All of it. The way the shaman's spell had brought the sky down on the hollow, except that this was not a spell and not the sky. This was the size of a thing that had been sealed in light for three hundred years because nothing in the world could hold it.

It came down on Kamish.

The old dragon went flat.

Onto its mound. Into the black stone. Its great wings pressed out to either side, its long neck down, its head down, its gold eyes still open. Not crushed. Held. Pressed into the floor of its own hall by a weight so much larger than itself that it could not lift one claw.

It did not struggle.

It lay there under the weight and looked up at the dark-gold shape with its gold eyes, and Mau, at the heart of the shape, bleeding out, looked back.

"*Small king*," said Kamish.

Very quietly. Deep. Pressed flat.

"*Not small.*"

A long breath. The last of its heat going out of it.

"*Good.*"

* * *

*Twenty,* said Ciel. *Mau. Now. Let go, or you won't—*

He reached down.

Not with the shape. With his own hand. His right one, the one that was not charred. Down through the dark-gold weight, from the heart of it, to the place under the pressed-flat neck where the scales met the skull. He could barely feel his fingers. He made the smallest point he had ever made. Hardly anything. All he had left.

He pulled.

Kamish's gold eyes closed.

* * *

*LET GO.*

He let go.

The shape went.

Not out. Down. Back into the bottom of the dark, the way it had come, the dark-gold folding in on itself, the wings, the neck, the head, the weight, all of it collapsing back down into his shadow faster than it had risen. And behind it, as it went, Ciel did something he had never felt her do. She *shoved*. The walls came up. One after another. Slammed. Like doors closing down a long corridor in a storm, all of them at once, every one, and the last one went up over the place where the True Dragon's scale lay folded, and Ciel held it there, and held it, and held it.

He fell.

Onto the black stone. Onto his back. In a spreading pool of himself.

* * *

He could not see very much.

Red. Smoke. The great red body on the mound, very still, its eyes closed. The hall going quiet. The heat going out of the air.

*Ciel.*

*I'm here.* She was crying. He did not know Ciel could cry. *I'm here. The walls are up. All of them. It's down. It's folded. I've got it.* A breath. *You're bleeding from—Mau, you're bleeding from everywhere. Your ribs. Your hand. You're—*

*Am I dying?*

A pause.

*Not yet,* said Ciel. *Not if you stay awake. Stay awake. Please.*

The notice came up. Somewhere in the red. He could barely read it.

*Operator: trial passed.*

*Guardian defeated.*

*Shadow extraction available.*

* * *

*No,* said Ciel. *No. Mau. The cup is almost empty. You're almost empty. If you try—*

*It's waiting.*

*Everything's waiting. You'll die.*

*It's alone,* said Mau. *In here. Like me.* He could feel it. Under the great red body. The dark gathering, deeper than anything. Patient. Old. *Nobody's coming in. Operator only.* He breathed. It bubbled. *If I go to sleep, I don't know if I wake up. And then it's just us. In here. Alone.*

*Mau—*

*I'm not leaving it alone in here.*

Ciel did not say anything.

He put his right hand flat on the black stone. Toward the mound. He could not reach it. It did not matter.

*You've been at the top of this stair a long time,* he thought, at the dark. *Watching people come up. Waiting.* The red was going grey at the edges. *I'm not asking you to wait at the top of a stair any more.*

*I'm asking you to keep watch,* he thought. *Over a house. Where people sleep. So they can.*

"Arise," he whispered.

* * *

The dark came up out of the mound.

Slowly. The way old things do everything. Through the great red body. The red going dark. The gold going out of the scales. Two small cold lights opening in the place where the eyes had been.

It rose.

Mau did not see it rise. He saw the edges of it. The dark of it filling the grey at the edges of what he could see. Vast. A wing, unfolding over him. A long neck, coming down.

A head, lowering, very slowly, until two cold blue lights were close above his face.

*"Small king,"* said the voice. The same voice. Deep. Slow. Like stone moving under water. *"You are bleeding."*

"Yes," said Mau.

*"You have no one."*

"They'll come," said Mau. "When the door opens. They'll come."

The cold blue lights looked at him for a long time.

Then the vast dark shape lowered itself. Not on him. Round him. The long body curling round the pool of red on the black stone where he lay, the great dark wings folding down over the curl like a roof, the long neck coming round, the head lying down on the stone beside him, close, between him and the place where the doorway had been.

Between him and anything that came through it.

*"Then I will keep watch,"* said Kamish, *"until they do."*

* * *

*Mau,* said Ciel. *You can sleep now.*

*You said stay awake.*

*I know. I've got you. The bleeding's slowing. Not stopped. Slowing.* A pause. *You're alive. Barely. Stay.*

*Stay,* he thought.

The cold blue lights watched him. The dark wings were a roof over him. The hall was quiet.

He closed his eyes.
