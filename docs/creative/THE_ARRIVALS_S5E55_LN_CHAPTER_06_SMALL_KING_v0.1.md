## Chapter 18 — Small King

*When: Day 378, the screen. The middle of the morning. Anko is at the board with the pencil behind her ear. Rimuru is on the bench, because he always sits on the bench now when Mau is in the tower. Across the yard, Taihei is washing up after the four o'clock breakfast.*

They came out of the frame all at once, three of them, as if they had been thrown.

Gojo landed on his feet. Gojo always landed on his feet. Yuta came down on one knee with his sword still in his hand. Frieren came last, and did not fall, and did not stumble. She was standing on the dry ground in front of the dark frame with her staff up, exactly as she had been standing in the furnace hall a breath ago, inside a space that was not there any more.

She turned round.

The frame was dark.

* * *

"Where is he?" said Anko.

Nobody answered her.

In the middle of the frame, pale, where everyone in the yard could see it, there was a notice.

*Trial in progress.*

*Operator only.*

Gojo walked to the frame. He put his hand flat on it, where the dark was. It was not dark. It was a wall. Smooth. Solid. Cold. He pushed. Nothing moved.

He took the blindfold off.

He looked.

He had looked through walls, through floors, through the inside of a hive. He looked into the frame now with his bare eyes, and his face did something Anko had never seen it do.

"I can't," he said.

"Can't what?" said Yuta.

"See in." Gojo did not take his hand off the frame. "It's not dark. It's not closed. It's—" He stopped. "There's nothing. There's no in. Someone took the in away."

Yuta drew his sword.

He did not ask. He cut at the frame. Once, twice, with his cursed energy up round the blade like a dark film. The blade hit the dark and stopped and rang, the way a blade rings on an anvil. It did not leave a mark.

He cut again.

"Yuta," said Gojo.

He cut again.

"*Yuta.*"

Yuta stopped. He stood in front of the frame with his sword up and his chest heaving.

"He's alone in there," said Yuta. "With that. He's alone."

Gojo did not answer him. He was looking at the frame. When he spoke, it was very quiet, and it was not to Yuta. It was to Anko, at the board, because somebody had to know.

"It breathed once," he said. "While we were there. Before." He swallowed. "The four of us. Two spaces. Mine round Yuta, his round Frieren. It nearly broke both. In one breath." He looked at his own hands. "And it wasn't trying. I could see it. It was holding back. Because there were four of us. It was being *careful*."

Anko's pencil had stopped.

"And now there's one," said Gojo. "And it's got no reason to be careful at all."

Nobody said anything after that.

* * *

Frieren had not moved.

She stood in front of the frame, a pace back from it, with her staff lowered now. She was looking at the notice. *Operator only.* She read it once. She did not read it again.

She did not say anything.

Anko looked at her and understood that she was not going to move from that place until the frame opened. Whether that was an hour or a year.

Anko took the pencil from behind her ear. She went to the board. She did not know what to write. She wrote:

*Day 378. 70. Mau. (Operator only.) (Not by his choice.)*

Then she stood beside the board and did not write anything else.

* * *

Rimuru was on his feet.

Nobody had seen him stand. He was standing on the bench, and then he was standing beside Frieren in front of the frame, with his face turned inward, the way it turned when Ciel was speaking to him. And then it was not turned inward. It was white.

"Ciel," he said aloud. "Ciel. Talk to me."

Nothing. Then something.

His face changed.

"She's there," he said. To nobody. To Gojo, to Yuta, to the back of Frieren's head. "She's in him. The thread's thin, it's so thin, I can barely—" He stopped. "He's alive. He's alone. It's breathing at him. It's—"

He went very still.

Then he sat down. On the dry ground. All at once. As if his legs had been taken away.

"Rimuru," said Gojo.

Rimuru did not answer. He was staring at nothing. At the frame. At the dark. His hands were shaking.

"I know that," he said.

"Know what?"

"That." Rimuru's voice was very small. "I can feel it. Down the thread. Even this thin. I'd know it anywhere. I'd know it if I were dead." He looked up at Gojo. "There was a cave. In my world. The first thing I ever—when I was nothing, when I was a slime in the dark, there was a cave, and something sealed in it. In light. For three hundred years." He swallowed. "Standing near it was like standing in the open mouth of weather."

Gojo did not say anything.

"The Noise read it," said Rimuru. "In the bay. When I took him in. It read my memory of that cave. Ciel told me. It started building it. Not the dragon. The size." He looked at the frame. "It never finished. Ciel said it never finished."

"And?" said Yuta.

"And he's letting it out," said Rimuru. "Right now. In there. Alone." His voice broke. "It's going to tear him apart."

* * *

Nobody said anything for a long time.

The frame stayed dark. The notice stayed pale. *Operator only.*

Frieren did not move.

Twenty minutes. Thirty. The sun moved across the yard. Taihei came out of the kitchen with a cloth in his hands and saw them and stopped and did not come any closer. Ori came to the back door. Maki was behind her, with a hand on her shoulder, and did not let her come out, and Ori did not try.

Forty minutes.

The notice changed.

*Operator: trial passed.*

*Party size restored.*

The frame opened.

* * *

Frieren was through it before the dark had finished becoming a door.

Gojo after her. Yuta. Rimuru, last, on legs that did not want to hold him. Up the stair from sixty-nine, the long straight stone stair between two walls, and the walls were not warm any more. They were cold. The heat had gone out of everything.

The doorway at the top was there again. Wide. Tall. No door.

Frieren went through it.

And stopped.

* * *

The hall was not red any more.

The molten lake was gone. The haze was gone. Half the floor was gone, the front half, where the stone had gone straight to light and not come back; it was a great smooth hollow of glassy black, still faintly warm. The smoke had gone from the roof, and there was a roof, very high, cracked across from wall to wall as if something enormous had pressed up against it from below.

On the mound in the middle, nothing. No red dragon. No body. Nothing.

And on the black stone in front of the mound, something dark and vast, curled.

A dragon. Dark. No shine on it at all. Its long body curled round in a ring on the stone, its great wings folded down over the ring like a roof, its long neck coming round, its head lying on the stone. And inside the ring, under the wings, in a pool of red that had spread out across the black stone much too far, much too far, something small.

The dark dragon lifted its head.

Two cold blue lights opened and looked at the four of them in the doorway.

*"Who comes,"* it said.

Not a question.

* * *

Gojo's hand went up. The marble. Yuta's sword came up.

Frieren put her hand on Gojo's arm without looking at him, and pushed it down.

She stepped forward.

The cold blue lights followed her. The dark head lowered a little, between her and what lay inside the ring. Not threatening. Placed. The way you put yourself between a sleeping child and a door.

Frieren stopped in front of it.

She looked up at the cold blue lights. She did not lift her staff.

"Frieren," she said. "I'm his."

The dragon looked at her for a long time.

*"The elf,"* it said at last. *"Yes. I know you. You stood beside him on my stair, inside his space, before the one above took you away."* The cold lights did not move from her face. *"He said your name. When he slept. Many times."*

Frieren did not say anything.

*"I am Kamish,"* said the dragon. *"He asked me to keep watch. Over a house. Where people sleep."* It lifted its head. *"He did not have a house here. He had only the stone. So I kept watch over him."*

It drew its head back.

It lifted one wing.

* * *

He was very small under it.

That was what Frieren would remember. How small. Lying on his back on the black stone in the middle of a red that went out from him much too far. His clothes were not clothes any more. His skin was split. Not cut. Split, along his arms and his chest and his face, the way bark splits on a tree in a hard frost. His left hand was black. The hair on the left side of his head was gone. His eyes were closed. There was blood dried at the corners of them, and at his nose, and at his ears.

His chest was moving.

Barely. Very, very small.

Frieren was on her knees beside him.

She did not remember crossing the stone. She was there. In the red. Her hands on his face, one on each side, the way she always put them, and his face was cold, and she held it.

"*Mau.*"

He did not open his eyes.

"Mau. I'm here. I'm—it opened. We came. I'm here."

His lips moved.

Nothing came out. Then, very faintly, a sound. Not a word. Not quite.

She bent down until her ear was against his mouth.

"…said they'd come," said Mau. A breath. Hardly anything. "Told him."

Frieren made a sound.

It was not a word. It came out of her the way it had come out of her on the ground outside the clinic after forty, except that this time she did not let it all the way out. She held it. She held it in her teeth. Her hands did not leave his face.

Behind her, Yuta was saying *no, no, no*, very quietly, as he knelt, over and over, the way Mau had said it on the north road. Yuta did not know he was saying it.

Gojo had stopped at the edge of the red. He was standing there with his blindfold up, looking at all of it, at every split and every crack, and his hands were shaking at his sides, and he could not make them stop. He had held Infinity against a dragon's breath an hour ago with those hands. He could not make them stop.

"We barely held its first breath," said Gojo. To nobody. "Four of us." He looked at the glassy hollow where half the floor had been. "He held the real one. Alone."

* * *

Yuta was on his other side.

He put both hands flat on Mau's chest, over the split skin and the cracked ribs, and his cursed energy went into him. Outward. Warm. Not fast. Yuta's face was white and very still and his hands were shaking, and he did not stop.

"Ribs," he said. "Five. Six. His lungs are—" He stopped. "There's so much. Gojo, there's so much. It's everywhere. I don't know where to start."

"Start with the lungs," said Gojo.

Gojo was kneeling at Mau's head. He did not touch him. He had his blindfold up and his bare eyes going over all of it, all of him, every split, every crack, and his face was the face he had worn on the night of the south field, and he was talking to Yuta very quietly, very steadily. *There. And there. That one first. Now that one.* Telling him where.

Rimuru stood at the edge of the red with his arms round himself.

*Ciel,* he said.

*He's alive,* said Ciel. In both of them. Her voice was raw. *I held the walls. I shoved them up behind it. It's down. It's folded. It's—Rimuru, it was so big. It was so much bigger than the cave. He let it out for twenty seconds and it nearly—*

*I know,* said Rimuru. *I felt it.*

*He made it stop. At twenty. When I said.* A pause. *And then he stayed awake to raise the dragon. Because he didn't want to leave it alone in here.*

Rimuru did not say anything. He looked at the vast dark shape coiled on the stone, with its wing lifted over the people kneeling in the red.

* * *

Kamish watched them.

It did not move. It kept its wing lifted. Its head was lowered to the stone, close, its cold blue lights on the small shape in the middle of the people.

*"Small king,"* it said. Very quietly. Not to any of them.

Then, after a long time:

*"Not small."*

* * *

*Floor 70 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 or 70.*

*Level 72. One hundred and fourteen points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 8. Places: 20.*

Gojo read it out. Nobody answered.

"Back," Mau said, much later, when Yuta had done what he could, which was not everything, which was enough to move him. His eyes were still closed. He did not know he had said it.

Kamish heard.

*"Small king,"* it said. And went down into the black stone, slowly, the way the tide goes out, the dark wing last.

Gojo picked him up.

Carefully. So carefully. One arm under his knees, one behind his shoulders, the way you carry someone who is broken in more places than you can count. He did not say anything. He carried him down the long cold stair, and through sixty-nine, and through the seal at the far end of the hall, and through the frame.

* * *

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

She did not say the time.

She looked at what Gojo was carrying. At Frieren beside him, with blood to her elbows, holding Mau's black hand in both of hers. At Yuta, grey, swaying. At Rimuru.

The yard saw too.

It was the middle of the morning and the yard was full, the way it was always full when he was in the tower, everyone pretending to be busy within sight of the screen. They saw Gojo come through with him. Not held up between two people, this time, the way he had come back from forty. Carried. Like a child. Like something broken that had to be held together in the carrying. One arm hanging down, black to the wrist. The left side of his head without its hair. Red everywhere, red on Gojo's shirt and Gojo's arms and Gojo's face where he had wiped it without noticing.

Momo did not drop anything this time. She put the knife down very carefully on the board and put both her hands flat on the counter and stood like that, not moving.

Himmel, at the gate, took two steps toward the screen and stopped, because he did not know what he would do when he got there.

Kaneki came out from the kilns, which he never did in daylight, and stood at the edge of the timber yard with his hands at his sides.

On the back step, Ori stood up.

Maki was not behind her this time. Maki was on her knees in the dry ground beside the screen, where she had been since the frame went dark, because Yuta had been in there and come out and could not go back in, and Maki had sat down beside the frame and not got up.

So it was Touka. Touka, from the kitchen door, with Ichika on her hip, who saw Ori stand up and saw her face and crossed the yard in four strides and put her free arm round her. Ori did not look at Touka. She looked at what Gojo was carrying, and her mouth started to move.

Counting.

Touka bent down to hear. She did not need to. She knew what it was.

Shoko was already there. She had been there since the frame went dark. She did not say anything either. She looked, once, and turned round and walked very fast toward the clinic, and Gojo followed her.

Anko went to the board.

*Day 378. 70. Mau. (Operator only.) (Not by his choice.)*

She wrote under it.

*Kamish. The old dragon. He fought it alone. Somebody made him.*

*He let something out. Rimuru knows what. It was too big for him.*

*He won.*

*Kamish kept watch over him until we came.*

And under that, smaller, the line she always wrote, and had never in all her years written with her hand shaking like this:

*Shoko.*

* * *

Shoko worked for six hours.

Nobody left the yard. Not for lunch. Not for dinner. Taihei made both and brought them out and put them on the bench and nobody ate either, and then at the end of the day Stark ate a bowl, slowly, because Fern told him to, and then Fern ate one, and then, one by one, because Fern had, the others did.

At dusk, Shoko came out.

She stood in the clinic door for a long time with her hands hanging at her sides and blood to her elbows and her coffee somewhere behind her, cold since morning. She looked at the yard. At all of them.

"He's alive," she said.

Nobody moved.

"Six ribs. Both lungs bruised. His left hand—" She stopped. "His left hand will work. I don't know how well. Not soon. The splits will close. They'll scar. All of them." She took a breath. "He lost more blood than I've ever seen anyone lose and keep. Yuta gave him what he had. Rimuru gave him what he had, down the thread, until Diablo made him stop." She looked at Frieren. "He'll live."

Frieren, on the step, did not cry.

She had not cried all day. She had sat on the clinic step with her staff across her knees and her hands red and dried and her face perfectly still, and she had not cried, and she did not cry now.

She stood up. She walked past Shoko into the clinic.

Through the open door, the yard heard the chair scrape. Then nothing.

Shoko stood in the doorway a moment longer. Then she sat down on the step, where Frieren had been. All at once. She put her face in her red hands.

Gojo sat down beside her.

He did not say anything. He did not touch her. He sat there.

* * *

That night nobody went up to the roof.

The roof came down to the clinic. All of them. Gojo and Yuta and Rimuru and Taihei, on the floor of the clinic round the bed, with the lamp low. Frieren in the chair beside the bed with Mau's black hand in hers. Shoko at the table, not writing anything, with her coffee going cold.

Nobody said anything for a long time.

"Someone did that," said Gojo at last. Very quietly. "The party size. It wasn't the dragon. The dragon said so." He was looking at Mau. "Someone up there wanted to see him alone. With nothing. To see what was under everything."

Nobody answered.

"I'm going to find them," said Gojo.

Nobody told him not to.
