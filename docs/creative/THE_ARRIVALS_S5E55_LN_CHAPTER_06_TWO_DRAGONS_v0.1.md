## Chapter 19 — Two Dragons

*When: Day 378, floor 70. Twenty seconds on Ciel's count. The hall is melting. Two dragons are looking at each other across the glass, and one of them has a boy at its heart.*

Kamish moved first.

It always would. It was old, and it was a dragon, and it had burned a coast in a night. It came off its nothing in one long white-hot surge, the fire-wings pouring up behind it, and the heat came ahead of it like a wall.

The True Dragon met it.

Mau did not decide to. The shape did. Or the Noise did, building it, or Ciel, holding it, or the thing at the bottom of all of them that he still did not have a name for. The vast dark-gold body leaned into the charge and the two of them met in the middle of the burning hall, over the glass, chest to chest.

There was no sound. Or there was so much sound that it stopped being sound and became the floor shaking and the walls running and the cracks in the ceiling opening like mouths.

Fire against weight.

The fire-wings wrapped round the True Dragon's shoulders and burned. The wings of weight came down on the fire-wings and pressed. Kamish's claws, white-hot, raked at the dark-gold chest, and where they raked, the dark-gold flickered, and inside it, at the heart of it, something small felt the heat come through.

Mau screamed.

He did not hear it. It went into the shape and was lost.

*Twenty-five,* said Ciel. *Mau, it's getting through. The heat. It's finding the edges of you inside the shape. Your hand. Your left hand. Your face—*

He felt it. The left side of his head. Where the shaman's fire had been, and the true breath. The heat came through the dark-gold the way water comes through a crack in a cup, and he felt his hair go, and his ear, and his left hand, held out to the side the way he always held it out without thinking, go from burned to something past burned.

The True Dragon's claws came up.

Not claws. Pressure, in the shape of claws. They took Kamish under the white-hot jaw and pushed, and the old dragon's head went back, and back, and its fire-wings beat against the weight and could not lift it.

Then its jaws came down.

On the True Dragon's neck.

* * *

They closed.

The dark-gold neck held. Mostly. Pressure is hard to bite. But Kamish's jaws were the jaws of a thing that had bitten through a coast, and they closed, and held, and the white heat poured out of them into the shape, and the shape began, at the neck, to come apart.

Mau felt it come apart.

Not the shape. Himself. Through the shape. As if the jaws were closed on his own neck at the heart of it, and the heat was in his own throat. He could not breathe. He could not think. The weight of the True Dragon was pressing in on him from every side, and the heat of the old dragon was coming in through the bite, and between the two of them, in the middle, small, was a boy who had walked into a tower in the spring with a bag over his shoulder and Frieren beside him.

*Thirty,* said Ciel. Very small. *Mau. Ten more. Ten more and I'm taking it away from you.*

*Not yet.*

*Mau—*

*It's holding back.*

He did not know how he knew. He knew. Even now. Even with its jaws on his neck and its fire-wings round him and its whole white-hot body leaning into the bite. Kamish was holding back. Not much. A breath's worth. The way it had held back the first breath, because there had been four of them. The way an old thing holds back without knowing it does, out of a habit older than the tower.

*It wants to see,* thought Mau. *It said so. Whether there's anything in me that's mine.*

*Then show it.*

* * *

He opened the True Dragon's mouth.

He had not known it could breathe. Ciel had not told him. Ciel had not known. It was pressure, not a dragon; weight, not fire; the scale of a thing, not the thing. It had no breath to breathe.

He breathed anyway.

Not fire. He had no fire. He had been burned by two fires today and the arrow-fire on forty, and he did not want any more fire. What came out of the dark-gold jaws, past Kamish's head where it was clamped on the neck, was nothing anyone in the hall could see.

It was weight.

A roar of it. Pure. The thing that had flattened the hall's heat to the floor, the thing that had held the true breath in the air, poured out of the True Dragon's mouth in a single long silent roar, straight down, onto the old dragon's back.

The fire-wings went flat.

All at once. Pressed out of the air like candle flames under a hand. Kamish's jaws came off the neck. Its head came down. Its whole white-hot body went down under the roar, onto the glass of the melted floor, and through it, into the black stone beneath the glass, and the stone cracked in rings out from under it, and the old dragon was pressed into the floor of its own hall, flat, its wings spread out to either side, its long neck down, its head down, its gold eyes open.

The white went out of its scales.

Red again. Then dark red. Then the deep red it had been on its mound when they came up the stair, curious, courteous, an old dragon with a long memory.

It did not struggle.

* * *

*Thirty-five,* said Ciel.

The hall was quiet.

The walls had stopped running. The fire had gone out of the air. There was only the dark-gold shape, vast, standing over the old dragon pressed into the stone, and the heart of it, small, tearing.

Kamish looked up.

Its gold eyes found the eyes of the True Dragon, and through them, somehow, the boy at its heart. Mau felt them find him. The way he had felt the dark under Roof weigh him, and the dark under Beru.

"*Small king*," said Kamish.

Very quietly. Deep. Pressed flat.

"*Not small.*"

A long breath. The last of its heat going out of it, into the cracked stone.

"*Your own*," it said. "*All of that. Your own.*" Its gold eyes did not leave him. "*No borrowed light.*"

Another breath.

"*Good.*"

* * *

*Forty. Mau. Now. Finish it or let go. Now.*

He reached down.

Not with the shape. With his own hand. His right one, the only one that still answered. Down through the dark-gold weight, from the heart of it, to the place under the pressed-flat neck where the scales met the skull. He could barely feel his fingers. He made the smallest point he had ever made. Hardly anything. All he had left.

He pulled.

Kamish's gold eyes closed.

* * *

*LET GO.*

He let go.

The shape went.

Not out. Down. Back into the bottom of the dark, the way it had come, the dark-gold folding in on itself, the wings, the neck, the horns, the weight, all of it collapsing back into his shadow faster than it had risen. And behind it, as it went, Ciel did the thing he had never felt her do. She *shoved*. The walls came up. One after another. Slammed. Like doors closing down a long corridor in a storm, all of them at once, every one, and the last one went up over the place where the True Dragon lay folded, and Ciel held it there, and held it, and held it.

He fell.

Onto the black stone. Onto his back. In a spreading pool of himself.

* * *

He could not see very much.

Red. Smoke. The great red body pressed into the stone, very still, its eyes closed. The hall going quiet. The heat going out of the air.

*Ciel.*

*I'm here.* She was crying. *I've got it. It's down. It's folded. The walls are up. All of them.* A breath. *You're bleeding from everywhere. Your ribs. Your hand. Your face. You're—*

*Am I dying?*

A pause.

*Not yet,* said Ciel. *Not if you stay awake. Stay awake. Please.*

The notice came up. Somewhere in the red. He could barely read it.

*Operator: trial passed.*

*Guardian defeated.*

*Shadow extraction available.*

* * *

*No,* said Ciel. *No. He's bigger than the cup. Bigger than all of it. The rest will come out of you, and you're almost empty. If you try—*

*It's waiting.*

*Everything's waiting. You'll die.*

*It's alone,* said Mau. *In here. Like me.* He could feel it. Under the great red body. The dark gathering, deeper than anything. Patient. Old. Weighing him. *Nobody's coming in. Operator only.* He breathed. It bubbled. *If I go to sleep, I don't know if I wake up. And then it's just us. In here. Alone.*

*Mau—*

*I'm not leaving it alone in here.*

Ciel did not say anything.

He put his right hand flat on the black stone. Toward the mound. He could not reach it. It did not matter.

*You burned a coast,* he thought, at the dark. *And five people with borrowed light stopped you. And you've hated it ever since.* The red was going grey at the edges. *I'm not asking you to burn anything.*

*I'm asking you to keep watch,* he thought. *Over a house. Where people sleep. So they can.*

"Arise," he whispered.

* * *

The dark came up out of the stone.

Slowly. The way old things do everything. Through the great red body pressed into the floor. The red going dark. The gold going out of the scales. Two small cold lights opening in the place where the eyes had been.

It rose.

Mau did not see it rise. He saw the edges of it. The dark of it filling the grey at the edges of what he could see. Vast. A wing, unfolding over him. A long neck, coming down.

A head, lowering, very slowly, until two cold blue lights were close above his face.

*"Small king,"* said the voice. The same voice. Deep. Slow. Like stone moving under water. *"You are bleeding."*

"Yes," said Mau.

*"You have no one."*

"They'll come," said Mau. "When the door opens. They'll come."

The cold blue lights looked at him for a long time.

Then the vast dark shape lowered itself. Not on him. Round him. The long body curling round the pool of red on the black stone where he lay, the great dark wings folding down over the curl like a roof, the long neck coming round, the head lying down on the stone beside him, close, between him and the place where the doorway had been.

Between him and anything that came through it.

*"Then I will keep watch,"* said Kamish, *"until they do."*

* * *

*Mau,* said Ciel. *You can sleep now.*

*You said stay awake.*

*I know. I've got you. The bleeding's slowing. Not stopped. Slowing.* A pause. *You're alive. Barely. Stay.*

*Stay,* he thought.

The cold blue lights watched him. The dark wings were a roof over him. The hall was quiet.

He closed his eyes.
