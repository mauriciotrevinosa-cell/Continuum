# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 21 — The Cave

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-19, DH-128)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 21
**Continuity state:** The night after seventy (Day 378 into 379), the clinic, the reset hour. Frieren is finally asleep in the chair at Shoko's insistence. Rimuru, still grey from pouring himself down the thread, sits on the end of the clinic bed. For the first time he tells Mau about the cave: a slime in the dark, a voice sealed in light for three hundred years, his first friend, Veldora, a True Dragon, and how standing near him felt. He tells him what it was like to feel that size again, down Ciel's thread, inside the boy he took into himself in the bay and could not save from his own memory. It is grief and fear, not anger. Mau apologises; Rimuru will not take the apology ("It isn't mine to forbid. It's his size, and your Noise built it"). Ciel reports that the scale is folded and walled but no longer asleep: at the reset hour it stirs, the way something stirs in its sleep, which it never did before. Rimuru says Veldora would laugh, and would want to meet him. Seed for S6. No powers used

*When: Day 379, the reset hour. The clinic. One lamp, turned low. Frieren is asleep in the chair beside the bed with her hand on the blanket over Mau's black one, because Shoko stood over her until she slept. Yuta is asleep on the clinic floor again. Rimuru is not asleep.*

He woke the way he always woke.

All at once. Braced. Every muscle he still had ready. The Trial's hour.

It hurt. Everything hurt. Six ribs and a pair of lungs that did not want to fill and a left hand that was a weight at the end of his arm, wrapped in white, and a left side of his head that felt cold, because there was nothing on it any more to keep it warm.

He lay still.

Rimuru was sitting on the end of the bed.

* * *

He was grey. Not the deep grey of the back step, holding the walls down the thread. A paler grey. The grey of somebody who had given more than he had and had not yet got it back. He was sitting with his hands loose in his lap, looking at nothing, and when Mau's breathing changed he turned his head.

"Hey," said Rimuru. Very quietly, so as not to wake Frieren.

"Hey."

"You're not supposed to be awake."

"It's the hour."

"I know," said Rimuru. "That's why I'm here."

He did not say anything else for a while. Mau did not either. The lamp hissed. Yuta, on the floor, turned over in his sleep and said something that might have been *Rika*.

* * *

"There was a cave," said Rimuru.

Mau turned his head on the pillow.

"In my world. When I first woke up there." Rimuru was looking at the lamp. "I wasn't anything. I was a slime in the dark. I couldn't see. I couldn't hear. I didn't know what I was or where. I just—moved. In the dark. For a long time."

He was quiet.

"And then something spoke to me," said Rimuru. "In the dark. Out of a light. A voice so big it didn't fit in the cave. It had been sealed there for three hundred years, in a prison of light, and it was bored out of its mind, and it was lonely, and it was the first thing that ever talked to me." He almost smiled. "It was very rude. And very loud. And it laughed at everything. And it was my first friend."

"Veldora," said Mau. He did not know how he knew the name. Then he did. Ciel. Or the walls. Or something that had been read, in the bay, a long time ago.

"Veldora," said Rimuru. "A True Dragon." He looked at his hands. "Standing near him felt like standing in the open mouth of weather. Like the sky had decided to sit down next to you. You couldn't breathe properly. You didn't want to." He was quiet. "I've never felt anything that size since. Not in my world, after. Not here."

He looked at Mau.

"Until this morning," he said. "Down the thread. On the back step. Coming up out of you."

* * *

Mau did not say anything.

"Ciel told me," said Rimuru. "In the bay. When I took you in, and you were coming apart, and the Noise read everything I had. My skills. My friends. My enemies." His voice went very quiet. "And the cave. It read the cave. It read *him*. Not his power. Not anything he could do. Just—the size of him. The way he felt. And it started building it. Inside you. And it nearly killed you, and then it stopped."

"And today it didn't stop."

"Today you let it out," said Rimuru. "For forty seconds. Alone. In a hall I couldn't get into." He looked at the lamp. "And I sat on the step outside a dark frame and felt my first friend come up out of a boy I couldn't reach. The size of him. The weather. And then I felt it tearing you apart."

He stopped.

He put his hand over his eyes.

"I'm sorry," said Mau.

* * *

Rimuru took his hand away from his eyes.

"No," he said.

"I used him. Without asking. Something of his. It's yours. It's your friend's, it's your memory, I didn't—"

"It isn't mine," said Rimuru. "That's what I've been sitting here working out for four hours." He looked at Mau. "It isn't mine to forbid. It isn't even really his. Your Noise read the size of him off a memory and then spent a year building it, piece by piece, in the dark, with nothing of his in it but the shape." He was quiet. "Kamish said it. Down the thread, I heard. *Not borrowed. Built.*"

"Then why—"

"Because it frightened me," said Rimuru simply. "Not that you had it. That you used it. That it was the only thing big enough. That you were alone, and something up there arranged it so you'd be alone, and the only thing in all of you that was big enough to live was the size of my first friend." His voice did something. "I couldn't get in. I've built a nation. I've stopped wars. And I sat on a step and couldn't get through a door, and the only thing that saved you was him."

Mau did not know what to say.

"I'm not angry," said Rimuru. "I want you to know that. Not even a little." He looked at the lamp. "I'm frightened. And I miss him. And those got mixed up today, on the step, and I've been sitting here letting them come apart again."

* * *

*Rimuru,* said Ciel. In both of them. Very carefully.

He turned his face inward.

*It's folded,* said Ciel. *The scale. I shoved every wall I have up behind it. It's down. It's walled.* A pause. *But it isn't asleep.*

Mau went still.

*Before today, it was asleep. Folded so small I almost forgot it. It never moved.* Another pause. *Tonight, at the reset hour, when Mau woke, it moved. Not much. The way something moves in its sleep, when it's dreaming. It turned over.* A pause. *It's still turning. Very slowly. Under everything.*

Rimuru did not say anything for a long time.

"He used to do that," he said at last. Quietly. "In the cave. When he was bored. You could feel him turn over in the light. The whole cave would shift." He almost laughed. "I used to think it meant he was dreaming about getting out."

"Is it going to get out?" said Mau.

"I don't know," said Rimuru. "I don't think it's him. It's the size of him. Built by your Noise." He looked at Mau's chest, rising and falling under the blanket, very carefully, because of the ribs. "I think it's yours now. And I think it knows it's been let out once." He was quiet. "Things that have been let out once remember the way."

* * *

They sat there for a while.

The lamp hissed. Frieren's hand on the blanket did not move. Yuta said *Rika* again, and turned over, and was quiet.

"What was he like?" said Mau. "Really. Not the size."

Rimuru looked at him. Then, slowly, for the first time that night, he smiled. The real one.

"Loud," he said. "Ridiculous. He read manga. He'd read every one I could find him and then complain about the endings. He wanted to be called *Storm Dragon* at all times, and he'd sulk for a week if you forgot." He shook his head. "He was the most powerful thing I've ever met and he cried at the endings he'd just finished complaining about."

Mau almost laughed. It hurt. He did it anyway.

"He'd laugh at you," said Rimuru. "If he knew. A little human with his size in him, taking down an old fire dragon in a tower. He'd laugh until the cave shook." He was quiet. "And then he'd want to meet you. Immediately. He'd want to see whether you'd do it again. He'd want to fight you, probably. And then he'd want you to read to him."

"I'd like that," said Mau.

"I know," said Rimuru. "That's the bit that frightens me."

He stood up. He put his hand, very lightly, on Mau's good shoulder.

"Go back to sleep," he said. "I'll sit up a bit. Ciel's watching it."

*I'm watching it,* said Ciel.

Mau closed his eyes.

Under everything, very far down, something the size of weather turned over in its sleep.
