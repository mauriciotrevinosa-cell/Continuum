# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 22 — The First Thing He Feels

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 22
**Continuity state:** Ori's own plan (her third this summer; parents accommodate). Lantern is remade at dawn in the north field where she named him, with Ori's hand on the place his jaw will be, so that "the first thing he feels is someone who named him". It costs from the half-refilled cup, under Shoko's eye and two-minute limit. Afterwards: an ordinary family day of games and reading, Ori's choice. No combat

*When: Day 381, just after dawn. The north field, well inside the lime, in the long grass where the fireflies were. The same place Ori named a dragon, forty-two days ago. Dew on the grass. Shoko is on the slope with her watch and her coffee. She has said "two minutes" three times. Shoko has allowed one remaking this week, because the cup has refilled faster than anyone expected and because it is Ori asking. Igris, Beru and Kaisel will wait for theirs, on Days 383 and 384.*

Ori's plan was written out on one page.

She had read it to them on the back step the evening before. Sitting between them. Holding the page in both hands. Reading in her careful voice, one line at a time, the way she read everything that mattered.

*LANTERN.*

*1. He's in pieces. Since the dragon. (Kamish.)*

*2. Mau has to make him again. Ciel says it costs. Not as much as the first time.*

*3. When he comes back, the first thing he feels should be someone who named him.*

*4. That's me.*

*5. So I want to be there. With my hand where his jaw will be. When he comes back.*

*6. In the north field. Where I named him. At dawn. Like the first time.*

*7. Shoko can come. Two minutes.*

She had stopped there and looked at them.

"That's the plan," she said. "It's mine. Not a fort plan."

Frieren had looked at Mau.

Mau had looked at the page. At number three. *The first thing he feels should be someone who named him.*

"Where did you get that idea?" he said.

Ori had thought about it.

"From Ichika," she said. "When Tank came back. She put her hand on his nose. And she said *cold*. And Tank breathed out." She paused. "He liked it. I could tell. He breathed out like Momo does when the bread comes out right." She looked at the page. "Lantern didn't have anyone. When he came back the first time, in the red gate. There was snow and everyone was hurt and there wasn't anyone to put their hand on him. Until I did. Later. In the field." She looked up. "I want to be there. This time. From the start."

* * *

So: the north field. Dawn. Dew on the long grass.

Shoko on the slope with her watch. Frieren standing a little way off with her staff. Yuka at the edge of the lime with the red ribbon, and Milim on the end of it, very still, because she had been told that if she so much as floated during the two minutes she would be sent back to bed.

And Ori.

She stood in the long wet grass in her coat, ten paces out from the slope, where Mau had stood forty-two days ago. Exactly there. She had paced it out. She stood still.

"Where will his jaw be?" she said.

Mau looked at his shadow. Long and thin on the wet grass in the low gold light.

"About there," he said. He pointed. A little to her left. Level with her shoulder.

Ori moved. One step. She held out her hand. Flat. Palm out. At the height of her shoulder. Where there was nothing yet.

"Here?"

"Here," said Mau.

Ori held her hand very still in the air.

"Ready," she said.

* * *

Mau knelt in the wet grass.

He put his hand flat on his shadow. He felt for Lantern in the cup. In pieces. Scattered through the dark, the way Kamish's claws had scattered him over the molten lake. All of them still there. Waiting.

*It'll cost,* said Ciel. *Less than raising him. More than Tank. The cup can afford it. Half full and refilling.* A pause. *Ori's hand is in the right place. I checked.*

*Thank you.*

*I wanted to be sure,* said Ciel. *It's her plan.*

Mau thought about what he was asking.

It was simple. It had been simple the first time too, at the snowline, when the dragon lay in the snow with its eye struck and the blue light running over its scales. He had not needed to be angrier than it had been. He had needed to know what he was asking it to follow.

*Come back,* he thought. *You were torn apart. By something much bigger than you. You bit well. Kamish said so.* He looked at Ori's hand, held out in the air. *Someone's waiting for you. Someone who named you. Come back to her.*

"Arise," he said.

* * *

The dark came up out of the wet grass.

In pieces, the way Beru had come back after the north field. They rose up out of Mau's long shadow like smoke. Like leaves going the wrong way. They gathered in the air beside Ori and drew together: a wing, folded; a long neck; the curve of a head, lowering.

The jaw.

It came together in the air where Ori's hand was. Exactly where her hand was. The dark drew itself into the shape of a jaw, long and smooth and cool, and her palm was resting flat against it before it was finished.

Ori did not move.

The rest of him came together round her hand. The head. The neck. The wings, unfolding behind, dark, enormous, with no shine. The long body. Until Lantern stood in the long wet grass of the north field in the dawn, whole. His head was lowered, his jaw resting against the hand of the girl who had named him.

Two small cold blue lights opened in the dark of his skull.

They were on her.

* * *

Mau felt the cost.

Ciel had been right. Less than raising him. More than Tank. The cup went down, steady and definite. He held the intention. *Come back to her.*

The thread stopped.

Lantern was whole.

He did not move. He stood in the long grass with his head lowered and his jaw against Ori's palm, and his two small cold lights looked at her. Very close. Very faint. Very steady.

"Hello," said Ori.

The dragon did not answer. He never had. Shadows did not, Mau thought. Not this one.

"You came back," said Ori. "You were in pieces. Since Kamish. You bit him. Kamish said you bit well." She did not take her hand away. "I wanted to be here. When you came back. So the first thing you'd feel was someone who named you." She paused. "It's me. Ori. I named you. Lantern. Because of your eyes."

The dragon's two small lights did not move from her face.

Then, very slowly, he breathed out.

Through his dark nose. Long. Slow. Like Momo when the bread comes out right. The breath stirred the wet grass round Ori's feet. It moved her hair. It went on for a long time.

Ori did not move her hand.

"He liked it," she said. Very quietly. Not to anyone. "I can tell."

* * *

"One minute forty," said Shoko, from the slope.

"Twenty seconds," said Ori. She did not look round. "I know. I'm counting too."

She stood with her hand on the dragon's jaw for the twenty seconds. She did not say anything else. She did not need to.

At two minutes, she took her hand away.

She stepped back. One step. Two.

"Goodbye, Lantern," she said. "See you next time."

"Back," said Mau.

Lantern folded down into his shadow. Wings, neck, head. The two small lights went last, still on Ori.

The thread stopped. What he had used stayed used. The cup was lower. Not low. There was a margin. There was always a margin now.

Ori turned round.

She looked at Mau, still kneeling in the wet grass. Then at Frieren, a little way off. Then at Shoko on the slope with her watch.

"That was the plan," she said. "It worked."

"It worked," said Mau.

"He breathed out."

"He breathed out."

Ori nodded. Satisfied. Then she took out the list from her coat pocket. *SHADOWS (NAMED)*. She found line two, and the pencil, and added something at the end of it. She did not show anyone. But Mau saw it later, pinned to Umaru's wall.

*2. Lantern. (Ori.) A light. Torn by Kamish. Bit well. Remade. The first thing he felt was me.*

* * *

The rest of the day was Ori's.

She had planned that too. It was on the back of the page. Mau had not seen it until she turned it over at breakfast.

*AFTER LANTERN:*

*Breakfast. All three of us. (Not at the long table. On the back step.)*

*Cards. Umaru and Yuka and Milim and Ichika. And Mau. (Mau has to lose at least once.)*

*Frieren reads. Near the cards. With her foot against Mau's ankle under the table. (She does that. I've seen.)*

*Lunch.*

*The fort. Mau and Frieren are invited. (Grown-ups, but allowed.)*

*Dinner.*

*Bed. With the connecting door open a crack.*

*NOTHING ELSE. NO TOWER. NO POINTS. NO SHADOWS (AFTER LANTERN).*

Frieren read it over Mau's shoulder at breakfast, on the back step, with Touka's coffee.

She read the line about her foot twice.

"I do not," she said.

"You do," said Ori.

"I don't put my foot against his ankle under the table."

"You do it every time," said Ori. "During cards. I've seen. Yuka's seen. Umaru wrote it in the Ministry record." She ate her porridge. "It's fine. It's allowed."

Frieren's ears went pink.

She did not argue any more.

* * *

So they did it. All of it. Ori's day.

Breakfast on the back step. Cards at the end of the long table, with Umaru dealing and Yuka keeping the sheet. Milim roared at every seven. Ichika played her second game ever, guided by Ori, and won one round by accident, and made a rule. She did not say what it was. She pointed at the sheet and Ori wrote it down for her: *11. Ichika: Everyone gets a turn to hold the cards for someone smaller.* Mau lost three rounds. He did not have to try.

Frieren read the green book at the end of the table. Under the table, Mau felt her foot against his ankle. The whole time.

Lunch. The fort, in the afternoon, in the Summer Annexe under the ink tree. Mau and Frieren sat on the edge of the blanket as invited grown-ups while the fort held a session of the Ministry about whether Kamish could be made an honorary citizen. They ruled he could not, because he would not fit under the awning. Dinner.

And then bed.

Ori went through the connecting door at the usual time. She did not close it. She left it open a crack, the way she always did. Milim floated over her bed on the ribbon.

At the door, before she went through, Ori stopped.

She turned round and looked at the two of them, in the east room. Mau on the edge of the bed. Frieren in the reading chair he had said no to, with the green book.

"Was it a good day?" said Ori.

Mau thought about it.

"It was a very good day," he said.

"Nothing happened," said Ori. "That was the plan. Nothing after Lantern."

"I know."

"Nothing happening is my favourite," said Ori. "I've decided."

She went through the connecting door. A moment later, faintly, they heard her tell Milim, "It was a very good day," and Milim say sleepily, "*ROAR*," for no reason at all.

Frieren closed the green book.

"She's right," she said.

"About nothing happening?"

"About it being her favourite." Frieren looked at the connecting door, open a crack, with the lamplight from Ori's room coming through. "It's mine too. I didn't know until this summer." She was quiet. "I used to think a good day was one where I found a grimoire."

"And now?"

Frieren looked at him.

"Now it's one where nothing happens," she said. "And everyone's here."

* * *

Ori's plan had been on the page called *Days* for two weeks, seven lines in Fern's copy, waiting for the day it would be needed. *In case.*

That night Ori asked Fern for the ledger. Fern gave it to her. Ori found her own seven lines and, with Fern's pencil, very carefully, put a tick beside each one. All seven.

Then she wrote an eighth line underneath, which had not been in the plan.

*8. And then nothing happened all day. And everyone was here.*
