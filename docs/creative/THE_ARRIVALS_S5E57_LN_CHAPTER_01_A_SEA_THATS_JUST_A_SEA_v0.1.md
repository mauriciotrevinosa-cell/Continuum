## Chapter 26 — A Sea That's Just a Sea

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)

*When: Day 382, night, and Days 383 and 384. The roof; then the board. Wakana finished Armin's room on Day 381, with the window facing south and west. Armin slept in it for the first time last night.*

On the seventh night, Armin found his star.

He found it through the telescope, with Senku beside him and Byakuya holding the lantern turned down so low it was nearly out. It took him most of an hour. He went across the sky slowly, star by star, the ones behind the ones, the small faint ones you could only see through the lens. Senku had a list on the back of Umaru's invitation card from the night of first light, in pencil, of every star anyone in the house had ever named. Armin checked each one against it.

At last he stopped.

"That one," he said, with his eye to the eyepiece.

Senku looked.

"Nobody's named that one," he said. "It's not on the card."

"It's south," said Armin. "And west. Low. Over the hills." He lifted his head from the eyepiece and looked with his eyes. You could not see it without the telescope. Just dark, low over the hills, south and west. "Toward the sea."

Nobody said anything.

"*A Sea That's Just a Sea*," said Armin.

Senku looked at him for a long moment.

Then he took the stub of pencil from behind his ear. He wrote it on the back of the invitation card, under all the others. *The Hundred and First. Lillian. The Bird. The Ministry. Taihei (later). The Party. Still Here.* And now, at the bottom, in his own quick hand:

*A Sea That's Just a Sea. (Armin.)*

Umaru, who had been invited to the roof for this specific occasion and was sitting by the hatch in her hood, held out her hand for the card. She took it. She looked at the new line for a long time.

Then she took her own pencil out of her sash and added, very small, underneath, in square Ministry hand:

*(Ratified.)*

* * *

Taihei, by the draught, leaned over to see.

"*Taihei (later)*," he read, a few lines up. Very quietly.

"You've seen it," said Umaru, without looking at him.

"Mau showed me. On the roof. Last week."

Umaru pulled her hood down over her face.

"The Ministry," she said, from inside it, "names stars in advance. It's a function of the Ministry."

"It's a good function," said Taihei.

Umaru did not answer. But she did not give the card back to Senku for a long time. She sat by the hatch with it in her hands, looking at it, until Senku had wrapped the telescope back up in its blanket and Byakuya had gathered the legs.

* * *

Armin slept in his room that night.

Mau saw him go. Down the ladder, along the west wing, past Taihei's room that said *Came*, past the room with two windows where the lamp was still lit. To the very end, to the room Wakana had finished the day before.

There was a card propped against the door. Wakana's. Mau had seen him put it there. In Wakana's square careful hand:

*Armin. (Toward the sea.)*

Armin stopped at the door and looked at the card for a long time.

Then he went in.

And at the very end of the west wing, past his door, on the bare dry ground, there were two white lines of chalk. A corner. Rimuru had drawn them that afternoon, the way he always did when the last empty room was taken.

For whoever comes.

* * *

Shoko had set the terms on the morning of Day 383, in the clinic, with his ribs still bound and his left hand in its splint and the left side of his head growing back pale.

"You can't walk a floor," she said. "You can barely walk to the door. But the tower won't move without you on it." She looked at him for a long time. "So Kaisel carries you. You lie on his back. You don't fight. You don't call anything but him. Your party clears the floors, and you're there because the tower needs its Operator there, and that's all you are for two days." She did not blink. "If I hear you got off that wyvern, you're not going to eighty."

He said yes. He meant it. Mostly.

The board said the next two days plainly.

*Day 383. Mau (on Kaisel; didn't fight; Shoko's terms), Yuta, Maki, Mikasa. From 70. Floor 71: a forest of ice. Floor 72: a long stair with the steps missing. Mikasa jumped them all. Floor 73: a town with nobody in it and every door locked. Yuta found the one unlocked one. Cleared, all three.*

*Day 384, morning. Mau (on Kaisel), Frieren, Fern, Stark (with the plate). From 70, walked to 73. Floor 74: rain that fell upward. Again. Floor 75: a garden that grew while you watched. Floor 76: a library. Not drowned. Not locked. Every book was the same book. Fern read one page and said it was very badly written. Cleared, all three.*

*Day 384, afternoon. Mau (on Kaisel, asleep for most of 78), Gojo, Kaneki, Armin. From 70, walked to 76. Floor 77: a maze. Armin mapped it while walking. Floor 78: a field of standing stones that hummed. Kaneki listened. Floor 79: a long bridge over a dark lake, and on the far side, a stair going up into banners. Cleared, all three.*

Under the last line, in Gojo's hand:

*Armin mapped a maze while walking through it. In pencil. On his knee. He did not get lost once. I got lost twice. —S.G.*

And under that, in Armin's small neat hand:

*He got lost three times. I didn't say anything about the third one.*

* * *

"Mikasa went in," said Yuka, at the board, on the morning of Day 385.

"She asked," said Mau.

"She's never asked."

"Himmel had the north end," said Mau. "Yesterday was his day." He paused. "She came to breakfast and said, *if there's room.* That was all." He looked at the board. "She jumped every missing step on seventy-two. Thirty-one of them. One after another. Without stopping." He paused again. "Yuta said she was smiling."

Yuka looked at the line. *Mikasa jumped them all.*

"Was she?"

"I didn't see her face," said Mau. "She was ahead. But Yuta said."

"And Armin went in," said Yuka. "Yesterday. To seventy-seven." She looked at the line. *Armin mapped it while walking.* "He asked too?"

"He asked Gojo," said Mau. "Not me. He said Gojo would know whether it was safe enough for someone who can't fight." He paused. "Gojo looked at seventy-seven from seventy-six and said, *it's a maze. Nothing in it. Bring a pencil.*"

Yuka almost smiled.

"And he didn't get lost."

"Gojo got lost three times."

"Armin only said two," said Yuka.

"Armin didn't say anything about the third one," said Mau.

* * *

Anko wrote today on the board.

*Day 385. Mau, Gojo, Maki, Yuta. From 70, walked to 79. Floor 80 (guardian).*

Then she looked at Mau.

"Eighty," she said.

"Gojo looked from seventy-nine," said Mau. "Yesterday. At the top of the stair. Into the banners." He paused. "He said there's a lot of them. Not one. A lot."

"A lot of what?"

"Soldiers," said Mau. "He said it looks like an army. Standing in rows. Under banners. Waiting." He looked at the board. "And something at the back. Behind them. He couldn't see it properly. He said it was standing very still. Like Igris on twenty. But—" He stopped.

"But?"

"He said it wasn't the guardian," said Mau. "He said the army was the guardian. And the thing at the back was just—watching." He was quiet. "He said it reminded him of the statues on fifty-six. That turned their heads."

Anko looked at him for a long time.

Then she wrote, under *Floor 80 (guardian)*:

*An army. (Gojo.) And something at the back, watching.*

And underlined *watching* once.

"Level?" she said.

"Eighty-one," said Mau. "One hundred and fifty-nine points."

"Umaru know?"

"Umaru knows," said Mau. "She says the Ministry advises not spending before eighty. She says we don't know what an army costs to keep. She says if I want any of them, I'll have the points after." He paused. "She drew it as a broken line."

"Of course she did," said Anko.

She looked at the board. Four planks. Seventy-nine floors. Eighty lines of parties and floors and margins, in a dozen hands.

"Twenty more," she said.

"Twenty more," said Mau.

"Then what?"

Mau looked at the board.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "Nobody does. The notice on the first floor said a hundred. It didn't say what happens after." He was quiet. "Frieren says the statues on fifty-six were made to watch people walk past. By someone." He looked at the word *watching*, underlined once. "I keep thinking about that."

Anko did not say anything for a while.

Then she took the stub of pencil from behind her ear, and at the very bottom of the fourth plank, below everything, she wrote one word. Small. In her plain hand.

*After?*

She did not underline it. She left it there, a question, at the bottom of the board, where everyone who came to the screen would see it.
