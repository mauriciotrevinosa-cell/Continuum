*When: Day 385, morning outside. Four hours agreed, from seventy, walking to seventy-nine. Taihei made breakfast at four, again. Seven days since seventy. Shoko looked at the healed splits on his arms for a long time this morning and said "not fine, but go", which she has started saying instead of "fine". Anko's board says AFTER? at the very bottom, unanswered.*

The stair from seventy-nine went up into banners.

They saw them from the bottom. Long strips of cloth hanging down the stairwell from somewhere far above. Dark red, dark grey, dark gold, faded, moving very slightly though there was no wind. The stair went up between them. Narrow. Stone. The banners brushed their shoulders as they climbed.

At the top, an opening. Wide. Beyond it, light. Grey.

And sound.

Not loud. A low steady sound, like breathing, but not one breath. Many. Hundreds. All at once. In. Out. Together.

Gojo stopped on the last step.

He had his blindfold up. He looked through the opening with his bare eyes for a long time.

"Still there," he said. "All of them. Same as yesterday. Rows. Standing." He was quiet. "Breathing together. Like one thing."

"And at the back?" said Mau.

Gojo did not answer at once.

"Still there," he said. "Still watching." He lowered the blindfold. "It hasn't moved since yesterday. Not once. It's been standing at the back of that army, looking at this stair, for a whole day." He paused. "Waiting for you."

* * *

They went through.

It was a field. A wide flat field of grey earth under a grey sky that was not a sky, like the inside of a lid. Banners on tall poles, everywhere, in rows, hanging still. And under the banners, in rows, the army.

Soldiers. Hundreds. Mau stopped counting at two hundred. Armoured in dark plate, plain, battered, like soldiers who had marched a long way. Helmets with slits. Spears, all held upright, all at exactly the same angle. Shields on their left arms. Standing in perfect rows, perfectly still, breathing together. In. Out.

Their eyes behind the slits were dull red. All of them.

And at the back of the army, behind the last row, on a low mound, a figure.

Tall. Robed. Hooded. Stone. Grey stone, smooth, like the statues on fifty-six. The robe fell straight to the ground. The hood was deep. Inside it, where a face should be, smooth stone. No eyes. No mouth. Nothing.

It was looking at them.

Mau could tell. Even without eyes. He could feel it, the way you feel a gaze on the back of your neck. It was looking at the four of them in the opening. At him.

The notice came up.

*Floor 80 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the eighth ten.*

*Guardian: the Legion of Eighty.*

Nothing about the figure at the back.

"The Legion," said Mau.

"The whole army," said Maki. She had her spear. The new ash shaft, with the silver flower below the blade. "The whole army is the guardian."

"Not the thing at the back," said Yuta.

"Not the thing at the back," said Gojo. "The thing at the back isn't anything. It's not on the notice." He was looking at the hooded figure. "It's just watching."

* * *

The Legion moved.

All at once. Every spear came down, level, pointing at the opening. Every shield came up. Every foot stepped forward, together, one step, with a sound like a single enormous drum.

Then another step.

Then another.

Coming. Slowly. In perfect rows. Breathing together.

"*Igris. Beru. Tusk.*"

They came up out of the grey earth round him. The knight with his long sword. The ant king with his claws. The shaman, slow, with the blue fire in his skull. Remade, all of them, three days ago and two, on Shoko's schedule, one a day, so the cup could keep up. Mau could still feel where each of them had been broken on seventy, the way you feel a mended bone in the cold.

Igris looked at the army coming toward them.

"My liege," he said. Very calmly. "That is a great many."

"I know."

"It moves as one," said Igris. "It will fight as one. If one falls, the next steps into the gap." He looked at the rows. "It is very well drilled."

"Can we break it?"

Igris was silent for a moment.

"An army that fights as one," he said, "breaks as one. If it is frightened enough. All at once." He looked at Mau. "My liege has something frightening."

Mau looked at him.

Then at the army. Coming. Step. Step. Step.

"*Kamish*," he said.

* * *

The old dragon came up out of the grey field behind them.

Slowly. The way it always came. Like water rising in a well. The dark gathering on the grey earth and rising, and rising, and still rising. Wings first, folded. Then the long neck. Then the vast curled body, black now, no shine, no red. It filled the field behind them like a hill.

Its head rose above them on its long neck. Higher than the banners. Higher than anything.

Two great cold blue lights opened.

Mau felt the cup go. Fast. Kamish standing. Igris and Beru and Tusk standing. The thread pulled out of the cup like water through a hole.

*Short,* said Ciel. *Very short. Thirty seconds of him, no more. You'll need the rest.*

*Thirty seconds.*

The Legion stopped.

All of it. Every soldier. In mid-step. The drum-sound stopped. The breathing went on for one beat, together, and then it stopped too.

Hundreds of dull red eyes looked up. Past the four of them in the opening. Past Igris and Beru and Tusk. Up. At the vast dark dragon rising behind them, higher than the banners, with two great cold lights looking down.

Kamish opened its mouth.

It did not breathe. It roared.

* * *

The sound was not like anything.

It was not loud. It was *deep*. So deep Mau felt it in his bones before he heard it. It went out across the grey field and through the banners and through the rows of the army, and every banner moved, all at once, as if in a great wind.

The Legion broke.

Not all of it. The front rows. They had been stepping forward together, breathing together, one thing. When the roar hit them, one of them flinched. Mau saw it. One soldier, in the front row, on the left. His spear wavered. And the soldier beside him felt the waver, and wavered too. And the next.

A ripple. Down the front row. Then the second. Then the third. Spears coming up out of line. Shields coming down. Feet stepping back instead of forward.

The one thing was not one thing any more.

"*Back*," said Mau, to Kamish. Twenty-eight seconds.

The old dragon sank back into the grey field like water draining from a well.

"*Now*," said Maki.

"No," said Mau.

She looked at him.

"Not you first," said Mau. He was looking at the army. At the wavering rows, the banners, the ripple going down the front line like wind through a field. "Not any of you first. The notice." He did not look away from the army. "It's an army. I've got an army." He took a breath. "Let me be the one who uses it."

Maki looked at him for a long moment. Then she stepped back. One pace. Beside Yuta. Her spear grounded.

"Then use it," she said.

* * *

He had never done it before.

Called them, yes. Two at once. Five at once, on seventy, before the party went away. But never like this. Never standing in front of hundreds of soldiers with all of them in his head at once, every one of his own shadows, and the shape of the field, and the shape of the enemy, and the gaps between.

He thought about Mikasa at the north end. About Igris on the bridge, holding the far end. About Frieren on seven, telling Yuka: *the far end first.*

He thought about Gojo in the yard, a hundred mornings. *You don't look at the thing coming at you. You look at where it's going to be.*

"*Igris,*" he said. "*The left flank. Where the ripple started. Don't kill. Push. Make them step back into the rows behind them.*"

The knight went. Not fast. A man sweeping a floor. Into the wavering left flank, and the long sword went round flat, and the front rank stepped back, and the rank behind them was not ready, and they tangled.

"*Beru. The banners. Every banner-bearer. They breathe on the banner. Take the banners and they stop breathing together.*"

"*MY KING!*" Beru was already gone. A blur of black through the rows, from banner to banner. Each one he passed went down. Each one that went down left a hole in the breathing, and Mau heard it, the great in-and-out of the Legion going ragged, going wrong.

"*Tusk. The right. The rows that haven't broken. Fire between them, not on them. A wall. Cut them off from the middle.*"

The shaman lifted his staff. The blue fire came out of the skull in a long low wall across the field, between the right-hand rows and the centre. The right-hand rows stopped. They could not reach the centre. They could not see it.

"*Kaisel.*" The wyvern rose out of his shadow. "*Up. Stay over me.*"

The wyvern went up into the grey sky over the field and circled there, low, a dark shape, and every soldier in the Legion who looked up saw it and did not know what it meant, and that was the point.

The Legion was not one thing any more.

It was four things. Then six. Then a dozen. Each one cut off from the others, tangled, not breathing together, not seeing the middle.

"*Gojo,*" said Mau. "*The clusters. Where Igris pushes them. Pull them tighter.*"

"I see it," said Gojo. His hand was up. Two fingers. The marbles went out, one after another, into the places where the tangled ranks were thickest, and pulled, and the tangles became knots.

"*Maki. Yuta. The knots. Only the knots. Only where Gojo's pulled.*"

They went.

* * *

It was work. A long grinding hour of it. The army was broken, but each soldier still fought, and there were hundreds of them.

But it was not the hour it would have been.

Mau stood in the middle of the field and did not fight. Not with his hands. He had nothing in his hands. He stood with the space round him thin, and his eyes on everything, every shadow and every knot and every gap, and he talked. All hour. Quietly. Without stopping.

*Igris, left again, they're reforming. Beru, there's one more banner, far back, by the mound. Tusk, let the wall go, let them through, now, into Maki. Kaisel, lower. Yuta, behind you. Gojo, there. And there.*

He made mistakes. He told Tusk to drop the fire wall a breath too soon and forty soldiers came through it at once and Maki had to give ground, and he felt it in his chest like a stone. He told Kaisel to come lower and a spear thrown from the rows took the wyvern through the wing, and he felt the drain lurch and called it back down into the field. He called Igris left when the knight was already going right and the knight hesitated, one step, for the first time, and a sword got in under his guard.

Every mistake, he learned. Every one. He could feel himself learning. The field getting smaller in his head. The shapes getting clearer.

By the end of the hour, he was not making them.

* * *

At the end of the hour, there were ten left.

Ten soldiers. Standing in a ragged line in the middle of the grey field among the fallen banners, with their spears level and their shields up. They were battered. Dented. Breathing hard, together, the ten of them. Still one thing.

They did not attack. They did not retreat.

They stood.

Maki stopped. Yuta stopped. Igris and Beru stopped. Tusk lowered his staff. Gojo's hand came down.

The ten soldiers looked at them with their dull red eyes.

"They won't break," said Maki. She was breathing hard. "The others broke. These won't."

"They're the best ones," said Igris. Very calmly. "In any army, there are ten who will not break. Whatever comes." He looked at them. "Those are the ten."

Mau looked at the ten soldiers in their ragged line.

At their battered armour. Their level spears. Their dull red eyes, looking back at him, not afraid. Just standing. Because they had been told to stand, and they would stand until they fell.

He thought about Mikasa at the north end. *Someone has to stand there.*

He walked toward them.

* * *

"Mau," said Gojo.

He did not stop.

He walked out across the grey field, past Igris, past Beru, past the place where Tusk's wall had been, until he was standing alone in front of the ten. Ten paces from their level spears.

"The notice says it's mine," he said. Not to anyone. "So it's mine."

"*Finish it*," he said.

To Igris. To Beru. To Tusk. To himself.

And went in with them.

* * *

The ten came at him together.

He had known they would. He had seen the shape of them all hour. Ten spears, level, one thing. He went in under the first with the space slanted, the way Maki had taught him on ten, and the second came round the slant, and Igris's sword took it. Beru took the third and the fourth. Tusk's fire took the fifth and sixth, close, a hand's breadth from Mau's shoulder.

The seventh got through.

He saw it coming. He could not get out of its way. Not and keep the space where it needed to be, on his left, where three more were coming.

It went through his right side. Above the hip. In, and through, and out the back.

He did not stop.

He put his hand on the shaft where it went into him, and held it, so the soldier could not pull it back for another thrust. And with his other hand he made the marble and put it in the soldier's chest.

The soldier went down. The spear stayed in him.

Eight. Nine. Igris. Beru.

The tenth was in front of him. The last one. The best one. Its spear level at his chest. Its dull red eyes on his.

It did not thrust.

It looked at him. At the spear through his side. At the blood. At him standing anyway.

It lowered its spear. A hand's breadth.

Mau put his hand flat on its breastplate, and made the smallest point he could, and pulled.

It went down.

He stayed standing. With a spear through his side. In the middle of the grey field among the fallen banners, with his army round him.

Then he sat down. Because his legs said so.

* * *

*Guardian defeated.*

The grey field was very quiet.

Banners lay fallen everywhere, in the grey earth, among the blue light where the soldiers had been. Most of them were gone now. Dissolved. But under the last ten, where they had stood in their ragged line, the dark was gathering. Ten small darks. Not deep. Shallow. Like puddles after rain.

The notice came up.

*Shadow extraction available. (10)*

Mau looked at the ten puddles of dark.

"Mau," said Gojo.

He did not look round.

"Mau," said Gojo again. Differently. "Look at the back."

Mau looked.

* * *

The hooded figure was still there.

On its low mound behind where the army had been. Tall. Robed. Grey stone. The deep hood. Inside it, smooth stone where a face should be.

It was looking at him.

He could feel it. Stronger than before. Not at the four of them. At him. Only at him.

Then it moved.

Mau saw it move. He would remember it afterward, and not be able to explain it. The figure did not walk. It did not lift a hand. It turned its head. Slowly. Inside the stone hood, the smooth stone face turned, a little, to one side. The way you tilt your head when you are looking at something that interests you very much.

The way the statues on fifty-six had turned their heads.

And then it crumbled.

All at once. From the top down. The hood, the robe, the smooth stone face. Into grey dust. It fell in a heap on the low mound and lay there.

Nobody said anything for a long time.

"It wasn't stone," said Gojo.

He had his blindfold up. He was looking at the heap of grey dust with his bare eyes. His face was very white.

"It looked like stone," he said. "It was stone. On the outside." He was quiet. "But something was looking through it. From somewhere else. The whole time. I could see it. Like a thread. Going out of the back of its head. Away. Somewhere I couldn't follow." He lowered the blindfold. "And when the army fell, it let go. And the stone fell down."

"Something was watching me," said Mau.

"Something was watching you," said Gojo. "Through a statue. On the eightieth floor of a tower." He looked at Mau. "And it turned its head. When you said *finish it*. Not before. Not at the dragon. At *finish it*."

Nobody said anything.

Maki was very still, with her spear.

Yuta had his hand on his sword hilt.

"Anko wrote *after?*," said Mau. Very quietly. "At the bottom of the board."

"I saw," said Gojo.

"I think something's waiting. After."

"I think so too," said Gojo.

* * *

He did not raise only the ten.

He sat on the grey earth among the fallen banners with Yuta's hands on his side, where Maki had pulled the spear out, and Yuta's cursed energy going in warm. And he looked at the field.

At all of it.

Where the Legion had fallen, everywhere, in the grey earth among the blue light, the dark was gathering. Not ten small darks. Hundreds. Shallow, like puddles after rain, across the whole field, as far as he could see.

The notice came up.

*Shadow extraction available. (212)*

And under it, a line he had never seen.

*Interface change: soldier-rank shadows may be registered as a Legion. A Legion does not occupy named places.*

He read it twice. Then he showed them.

"Two hundred and twelve," said Yuta. His hands did not stop on Mau's side.

"Not named," said Gojo. He had his blindfold up, looking at the notice, then at the field. "A legion. They don't take places. That's—" He stopped. "That's an army. That's a real army."

*Can I?* said Mau.

*Not all of them,* said Ciel. Very carefully. *They're shallow. Each one is nothing. Two hundred nothings is something.* A pause. *A hundred. A hundred will leave you a little left. More than that and you won't stand up for a day.*

*A hundred,* he thought.

He thought about Igris. *In any army, there are ten who will not break.* He thought about what it would mean to have ten of those, and ninety more behind them.

He thought about the north end. About Mikasa, and Himmel on his days. About a house where people slept.

He thought about a road, and one second.

He put his hand flat on the grey earth.

*You stood,* he thought, at the ten small darks closest to him, and at all the others behind them. *Some of you broke. Some of you didn't. It doesn't matter.* He looked across the field. *I'm asking you to stand for someone. Not because you're told. Because there's someone behind you who's asleep.*

"Arise."

* * *

They rose.

Not all at once. In waves. The ten first, the ones who would not break, straight up out of the earth beside him in a line. Then the rows behind them. Then the rows behind those. Across the grey field among the fallen banners, the dark came up out of a hundred shallow puddles and drew itself into a hundred shapes. Soldiers. In plain dark armour, with spears and shields. Not ragged. Straight. A hundred pairs of small cold blue lights behind their helmet slits.

They did not speak.

They lifted their spears, all of them at once. Upright. At exactly the same angle. The sound of it went across the field like a single footstep.

Then they were still.

Maki was looking at them. Yuta was looking at them. Gojo had his blindfold up and his mouth slightly open.

"A hundred," said Maki.

"A hundred," said Mau. He was very tired. "And a little left."

* * *

*Floor 80 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 or 80.*

*Level 82. One hundred and sixty-four points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 8 named. Places: 20.*

*Legion: 100.*

"A hundred and eight," said Yuta.

"A hundred and eight," said Mau.

Gojo did not say anything for a long time. He looked at the hundred soldiers standing in their rows across the grey field, the ten in front, and the dark knight and the ant king and the shaman and the wyvern circling overhead.

"When I was sixteen," he said at last, very quietly, "I thought I was the strongest thing in the world." He looked at Mau. "I never had a hundred people standing behind me."

"They're not people," said Mau.

"No," said Gojo. "That's what worries me."

He sent them all back. The hundred. Igris. Beru. Tusk. Kaisel. The drain stopped.

At the far end of the grey field, past the low mound with its heap of grey dust, a seal had appeared in the earth. And beside it, a small frame, with home faintly visible.

They walked past the heap of dust to reach it.

Mau did not look at it. He felt it anyway, as he passed. Not a gaze. Not any more. Something that had been a gaze, and had let go, and left a smell behind it, like a room someone has just walked out of.

At the frame, Gojo stopped.

He looked back at the heap of grey dust on the mound.

"Whoever you are," he said, very quietly, to the dust, "I saw you."

Then he went through.

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Three hours fifty," she said. "From seventy." She looked at the four of them. At their faces. "What happened?"

Mau told her. The army. The roar. The hour, and the talking. The ten who would not break. The spear. The hundred. The figure at the back.

Anko listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she went to the board. To the bottom of the fourth plank, where she had written *After?* the night before, small, in her plain hand.

She looked at it for a long time.

Then, under it, she wrote:

*Something watches. Through stone. (Gojo saw.)*

And above it, squeezed in, because she had forgotten and gone back:

*80. He commanded. Spear, right side. Legion: 100. Shoko.*

She did not underline it. She did not need to.
