# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 24 — The Legion

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 24
**Continuity state:** Floor 80 (Day 385), the eighth guardian. The guardian is an army, the Legion of Eighty, in rows under banners. Party: Mau (owner), Gojo, Maki and Yuta, with Igris, Beru and, briefly, Kamish standing (the dragon only looms and roars; no breath). Behind the army a hooded stone figure watches and turns its head to follow Mau; when the Legion falls it crumbles, and Gojo says something was looking *through* it. Mau raises ten common soldiers from the Legion (they do not speak). Seventeen registered shadows, twenty places. Level 82 (one hundred and sixty-four unspent). The floor 80 transfer point is secured

*When: Day 385, morning outside. Four hours agreed, from seventy, walking to seventy-nine. Taihei made breakfast at four, again. Anko's board says AFTER? at the very bottom, unanswered.*

The stair from seventy-nine went up into banners.

They saw them from the bottom. Long strips of cloth hanging down the stairwell from somewhere far above. Dark red, dark grey, dark gold, faded, moving very slightly though there was no wind. The stair went up between them. Narrow. Stone. The banners brushed their shoulders as they climbed.

At the top, an opening. Wide. Beyond it, light. Grey.

And sound.

Not loud. A low steady sound, like breathing, but not one breath. Many. Hundreds. All at once. In. Out. Together.

Gojo stopped on the last step.

He had his blindfold up. He looked through the opening with his bare eyes for a long time.

"Still there," he said. "All of them. Same as yesterday. Rows. Standing." He was quiet. "Breathing together. Like one thing."

"And at the back?" said Mau.

Gojo did not answer at once.

"Still there," he said. "Still watching." He lowered the blindfold. "It hasn't moved since yesterday. Not once. It's been standing at the back of that army, looking at this stair, for a whole day." He paused. "Waiting for you."

* * *

They went through.

It was a field. A wide flat field of grey earth under a grey sky that was not a sky, like the inside of a lid. Banners on tall poles, everywhere, in rows, hanging still. And under the banners, in rows, the army.

Soldiers. Hundreds. Mau stopped counting at two hundred. Armoured in dark plate, plain, battered, like soldiers who had marched a long way. Helmets with slits. Spears, all held upright, all at exactly the same angle. Shields on their left arms. Standing in perfect rows, perfectly still, breathing together. In. Out.

Their eyes behind the slits were dull red. All of them.

And at the back of the army, behind the last row, on a low mound, a figure.

Tall. Robed. Hooded. Stone. Grey stone, smooth, like the statues on fifty-six. The robe fell straight to the ground. The hood was deep. Inside it, where a face should be, smooth stone. No eyes. No mouth. Nothing.

It was looking at them.

Mau could tell. Even without eyes. He could feel it, the way you feel a gaze on the back of your neck. It was looking at the four of them in the opening. At him.

The notice came up.

*Floor 80 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the eighth ten.*

*Guardian: the Legion of Eighty.*

Nothing about the figure at the back.

"The Legion," said Mau.

"The whole army," said Maki. She had her spear. The new ash shaft, with the silver flower below the blade. "The whole army is the guardian."

"Not the thing at the back," said Yuta.

"Not the thing at the back," said Gojo. "The thing at the back isn't anything. It's not on the notice." He was looking at the hooded figure. "It's just watching."

* * *

The Legion moved.

All at once. Every spear came down, level, pointing at the opening. Every shield came up. Every foot stepped forward, together, one step, with a sound like a single enormous drum.

Then another step.

Then another.

Coming. Slowly. In perfect rows. Breathing together.

"*Igris. Beru.*"

They came up out of the grey earth on either side of him. The knight with his long sword. The ant-man with his claws.

Igris looked at the army coming toward them.

"My liege," he said. Very calmly. "That is a great many."

"I know."

"It moves as one," said Igris. "It will fight as one. If one falls, the next steps into the gap." He looked at the rows. "It is very well drilled."

"Can we break it?"

Igris was silent for a moment.

"An army that fights as one," he said, "breaks as one. If it is frightened enough. All at once." He looked at Mau. "My liege has something frightening."

Mau looked at him.

Then at the army. Coming. Step. Step. Step.

"*Kamish*," he said.

* * *

The old dragon came up out of the grey field behind them.

Slowly. The way it always came. Like water rising in a well. The dark gathering on the grey earth and rising, and rising, and still rising. Wings first, folded. Then the long neck. Then the vast curled body, black now, no shine, no red. It filled the field behind them like a hill.

Its head rose above them on its long neck. Higher than the banners. Higher than anything.

Two great cold blue lights opened.

Mau felt the cup go. Fast. Kamish standing. Igris and Beru standing. The thread pulled out of the cup like water through a hole.

*Short,* said Ciel. *Very short. Thirty seconds of him, no more. You'll need the rest.*

*Thirty seconds.*

The Legion stopped.

All of it. Every soldier. In mid-step. The drum-sound stopped. The breathing went on for one beat, together, and then it stopped too.

Hundreds of dull red eyes looked up. Past the four of them in the opening. Past Igris and Beru. Up. At the vast dark dragon rising behind them, higher than the banners, with two great cold lights looking down.

Kamish opened its mouth.

It did not breathe. It roared.

* * *

The sound was not like anything.

It was not loud. It was *deep*. So deep Mau felt it in his bones before he heard it. It went out across the grey field and through the banners and through the rows of the army, and every banner moved, all at once, as if in a great wind.

The Legion broke.

Not all of it. The front rows. They had been stepping forward together, breathing together, one thing. When the roar hit them, one of them flinched. Mau saw it. One soldier, in the front row, on the left. His spear wavered. And the soldier beside him felt the waver, and wavered too. And the next.

A ripple. Down the front row. Then the second. Then the third. Spears coming up out of line. Shields coming down. Feet stepping back instead of forward.

The one thing was not one thing any more.

"*Back*," said Mau, to Kamish. Twenty-eight seconds.

The old dragon sank back into the grey field like water draining from a well.

"*Now*," said Maki.

* * *

They went in.

Maki first. Straight at the wavering front row with her spear, the silver flower flashing below the blade. Yuta beside her, with his sword out and his cursed energy round him like a dark film. Igris on Mau's left, unhurried, a man sweeping a floor. Beru on his right, fast, a blur of black.

Gojo did not go in. He stood in the opening with his hand up and two fingers out. Small blue marbles came off the ends of them, one after another, into the rows of the army. Each one pulled a cluster of soldiers together, tangling their spears, tangling their shields, for the moment it took Maki or Yuta or Igris or Beru to get there.

It was not like Tusk. It was not like Kamish. It was work. A long grinding hour of it. The rows had broken, but each soldier still fought, and there were hundreds of them. They came, and were met, and fell, and dissolved into blue light. More came.

Mau stayed in the middle.

He held the space. Not round himself. Round the others, one at a time, wherever a spear came where they were not looking. A thin flat skin of Infinity at Yuta's back as three spears came at once. At Maki's side as a shield-edge swung at her ribs. Once, at Igris's helm. Igris did not need it. He looked at Mau afterward with his cold blue lights and inclined his head.

The other reserve went down. And down.

* * *

At the end of the hour, there were ten left.

Ten soldiers. Standing in a ragged line in the middle of the grey field among the fallen banners, with their spears level and their shields up. They were battered. Dented. Breathing hard, together, the ten of them. Still one thing.

They did not attack. They did not retreat.

They stood.

Maki stopped. Yuta stopped. Igris and Beru stopped. Gojo's hand came down.

The ten soldiers looked at them with their dull red eyes.

"They won't break," said Maki. She was breathing hard too. "The others broke. These won't."

"They're the best ones," said Igris. Very calmly. "In any army, there are ten who will not break. Whatever comes." He looked at them. "Those are the ten."

Mau looked at the ten soldiers in their ragged line.

At their battered armour. Their level spears. Their dull red eyes, looking back at him, not afraid. Not anything. Just standing. Because they had been told to stand, and they would stand until they fell.

He thought about Mikasa at the north end. *Someone has to stand there.*

"Finish it," he said. Quietly.

They finished it.

* * *

*Guardian defeated.*

The grey field was very quiet.

Banners lay fallen everywhere, in the grey earth, among the blue light where the soldiers had been. Most of them were gone now. Dissolved. But under the last ten, where they had stood in their ragged line, the dark was gathering. Ten small darks. Not deep. Shallow. Like puddles after rain.

The notice came up.

*Shadow extraction available. (10)*

Mau looked at the ten puddles of dark.

"Mau," said Gojo.

He did not look round.

"Mau," said Gojo again. Differently. "Look at the back."

Mau looked.

* * *

The hooded figure was still there.

On its low mound behind where the army had been. Tall. Robed. Grey stone. The deep hood. Inside it, smooth stone where a face should be.

It was looking at him.

He could feel it. Stronger than before. Not at the four of them. At him. Only at him.

Then it moved.

Mau saw it move. He would remember it afterward, and not be able to explain it. The figure did not walk. It did not lift a hand. It turned its head. Slowly. Inside the stone hood, the smooth stone face turned, a little, to one side. The way you tilt your head when you are looking at something that interests you very much.

The way the statues on fifty-six had turned their heads.

And then it crumbled.

All at once. From the top down. The hood, the robe, the smooth stone face. Into grey dust. It fell in a heap on the low mound and lay there.

Nobody said anything for a long time.

"It wasn't stone," said Gojo.

He had his blindfold up. He was looking at the heap of grey dust with his bare eyes. His face was very white.

"It looked like stone," he said. "It was stone. On the outside." He was quiet. "But something was looking through it. From somewhere else. The whole time. I could see it. Like a thread. Going out of the back of its head. Away. Somewhere I couldn't follow." He lowered the blindfold. "And when the army fell, it let go. And the stone fell down."

"Something was watching me," said Mau.

"Something was watching you," said Gojo. "Through a statue. On the eightieth floor of a tower." He looked at Mau. "And it turned its head. When you said *finish it*. Not before. Not at the dragon. At *finish it*."

Nobody said anything.

Maki was very still, with her spear.

Yuta had his hand on his sword hilt.

"Anko wrote *after?*," said Mau. Very quietly. "At the bottom of the board."

"I saw," said Gojo.

"I think something's waiting. After."

"I think so too," said Gojo.

* * *

He raised the ten.

Not because he wanted to keep the army. Because of the ten. The ones who would not break. Because in any army there are ten who will stand whatever comes, Igris had said. Because Mikasa had stood at the north end every day for longer than anyone remembered.

*Can I?*

*Easily,* said Ciel. *They're shallow. All ten together will cost less than Igris did.* A pause. *They won't talk, Mau. They're soldiers. Not knights. Not kings.*

*I know.*

He knelt on the grey earth among the fallen banners. He put his hand flat between the ten small darks.

*You stood,* he thought. *When everyone else broke. You stood because someone told you to stand.* He thought about the north end. About Himmel on his days, and Mikasa on hers. *I'm asking you to stand for someone. Not because you're told. Because there's someone behind you who's asleep.*

"Arise."

They rose.

Ten. All together. The dark came up out of the ten small puddles and drew itself into ten shapes. Soldiers. In plain dark armour, with spears and shields, standing in a line. Not ragged now. Straight. Ten pairs of small cold blue lights behind their helmet slits.

They did not speak.

They lifted their spears, all ten at once. Upright. At exactly the same angle.

Then they were still.

* * *

*Floor 80 cleared.*

*Transfer point secured. Future expeditions may begin at Floor 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 or 80.*

*Level 82. One hundred and sixty-four points unspent.*

*Registered shadows: 17. Places: 20.*

"Seventeen," said Yuta.

"Seventeen," said Mau. "Ten of them are soldiers."

"Three places left."

"Three places left," said Mau. "And twenty floors."

He sent them all back. The ten. Igris. Beru. The drain stopped.

At the far end of the grey field, past the low mound with its heap of grey dust, a seal had appeared in the earth. And beside it, a small frame, with home faintly visible.

They walked past the heap of dust to reach it.

Mau did not look at it. He felt it anyway, as he passed. Not a gaze. Not any more. Something that had been a gaze, and had let go, and left a smell behind it, like a room someone has just walked out of.

At the frame, Gojo stopped.

He looked back at the heap of grey dust on the mound.

"Whoever you are," he said, very quietly, to the dust, "I saw you."

Then he went through.

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch.

"Three hours fifty," she said. "From seventy." She looked at the four of them. At their faces. "What happened?"

Mau told her. The army. The roar. The hour. The ten who would not break. The figure at the back.

Anko listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she went to the board. To the bottom of the fourth plank, where she had written *After?* the night before, small, in her plain hand.

She looked at it for a long time.

Then, under it, she wrote:

*Something watches. Through stone. (Gojo saw.)*

She did not underline it. She did not need to.
