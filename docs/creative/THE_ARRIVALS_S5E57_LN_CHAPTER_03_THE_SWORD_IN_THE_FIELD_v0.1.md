# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 26 — The Sword in the Field

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 26
**Continuity state:** Floors 81–89 summarised (Day 386). On floor 86, a field of swords, the notice lets the owner take one, and Mau takes a plain katana in a black scabbard that looks like Yuta's. The Noise attends to it (Observe), with no technique and no unlock. At home Yuta recognises the shape. A cursed tool carries what its holder puts into it; his own carries Rika. He warns Mau: "be careful what comes out with it". Shoko forbids testing it at home. Gojo looks at ninety and calls it "a general". No fight in this chapter

*When: Day 386, afternoon, then evening. Floors 81 to 89. Then the back step.*

The board said the day plainly, later.

*Day 386, morning. Mau, Yuta, Mikasa, Kaneki. From 80. Floor 81: a lake of ink. Floor 82: a hall of mirrors that showed you behind yourself. Floor 83: a long road through a grey country that never ended until Kaneki heard the end. Floor 84: wind again. Cleared, all four.*

*Day 386, afternoon. Mau, Fern, Stark (with the plate), Armin. From 80, walked to 84. Floor 85: a city of towers, all leaning. Armin found the one that didn't. Floor 86: a field of swords stuck in the ground. Mau took one. Floor 87: a library again. Fern read the last page of every book. Floor 88: a garden of statues. Again. They turned their heads. Floor 89: a stair up into black and violet banners. Cleared, all five.*

The board did not say what the sword looked like.

* * *

Eighty-six was a field.

Flat. Grey grass. A grey sky like the inside of a lid. And swords. Hundreds of them. Stuck point-down in the earth in no order at all, as far as the eye went. Leaning, some of them. Rusted, most. Broadswords and sabres and long straight blades and short curved ones. Some had hilts wrapped in leather gone black with age. Some had no hilts left at all, only the tang sticking up like a bone.

The notice came up.

*Floor 86 of 100.*

*Objective: cross.*

*Items: blades. Eligible to take: one.*

Fern read it over Mau's shoulder.

"Don't touch any of them," she said.

"It says I can take one."

"It says you *can*," said Fern. "It doesn't say you *should*." She looked at the field of swords. "A field of weapons that belonged to someone, stuck in the ground. Where they fell, or where they were left." She was quiet. "That's a graveyard, Mau."

Stark, beside her, had taken his hand off his axe. He was walking through the swords very carefully, with his arms close to his sides, not brushing any of them.

Armin had the board out. He was drawing. Fast. The field, and the swords, and where they stood.

"They're not random," he said, without looking up. "I thought they were. They're not." He turned the board. "Look. They're in rings. Wide ones. Around something. In the middle." He pointed across the field. "There."

Mau looked.

In the middle of the field, where Armin's rings came to their centre, there was one sword that was not leaning.

* * *

It was a katana.

Plain. In a black scabbard, lacquered, worn at the mouth. Its hilt was wrapped in dark cord. Not stuck in the ground like the others. Lying on the grey grass, on its side, as if someone had set it down carefully and walked away.

There was no rust on it. Not anywhere.

Mau stood over it for a long time.

He knew the shape. He did not know why for a moment, and then he did. He had seen it every morning in the yard, at dawn, in the slow practice. He had seen it on the roof, laid across a pair of knees. He had seen it come out in the hive, and on seventy, and on twenty, at the door, where it had been drawn for the whole of the duel with Igris and never used.

Yuta's.

Not Yuta's. Yuta's was at home, in Yuta's room, on the hooks above the bed. This was not it. But it was the same shape. Almost exactly. The length. The curve. The black scabbard worn at the mouth.

"That looks like Yuta's," said Stark.

"It does," said Mau.

"Don't touch it," said Fern.

Mau looked at the katana on the grey grass.

The Noise was looking at it too.

He felt it. Not the way he felt Ciel. Underneath. In the place where the building happened, where the wheel slept and the walls held. Attending. The way it attended to things it had not seen before, or had seen and wanted to see again. *Observe.* It did not do anything else. It looked.

*Ciel,* he said.

*I feel it too,* said Ciel. *The Noise. It's attending.* A pause. *It isn't building anything. It isn't reaching. It's looking. The way it looked at Yuta's sword the first time you saw it.* Another pause. *There's something in the blade. Not a curse. Not a spirit. Room. The way a cup is room. Waiting for whatever's put into it.*

*Is it safe?*

*To carry? Yes,* said Ciel. *To draw? I don't know. It's a tool. Tools do what you put into them.* A pause. *I wouldn't draw it at home.*

Mau looked at the katana for a long moment more.

Then he picked it up.

* * *

Nothing happened.

That was the first thing. He had half expected something. A notice. A light. The Noise reaching. Nothing. It was a sword in a scabbard. Heavier than it looked. Balanced near the hilt. The cord on the hilt was rough under his fingers.

The notice came up, small, at the edge of his sight.

*Item taken: a blade (cursed tool, unnamed).*

That was all.

Fern was looking at him with an expression he had seen on her face when Stark stood in front of the bear.

"You touched it," she said.

"I picked it up."

"That's touching it."

"It's a sword," said Mau. "In a scabbard. I'm not drawing it." He looked at it. "It looks like Yuta's. I want to show him."

Fern looked at the katana. Then at him.

"If it does anything," she said, "anything at all, you put it down. On the ground. And walk away."

"I promise."

"And you show Shoko before you show Yuta."

"I promise."

Armin, with the board, had drawn the katana on his map. In the centre of the rings. A small careful drawing of a sword lying on its side. And under it, in his neat hand:

*The one in the middle. The only one not stuck in the ground. Somebody put it down on purpose.*

* * *

Shoko looked at it on the clinic table that evening.

She did not touch it either. She looked at it for a long time, sitting on her stool, with her arms folded.

"Gojo should see this," she said.

"Gojo's on the roof of the barn," said Mau. "With his blindfold off. Looking at the tower. He said he wanted to see ninety before tomorrow."

Shoko looked at the katana.

"It's a cursed tool," she said at last. "From the shape. Like the ones in our world. Like Yuta's. Like Maki's spear." She was quiet. "A cursed tool isn't a curse. It's a thing that takes what you put into it. Cursed energy. A technique. Whatever you've got." She looked at him. "You've got a lot of things in you that nobody understands."

"I know."

"Don't draw it," said Shoko. "Not here. Not in the house. Not anywhere I can't see you." She unfolded her arms. "If you draw it, I want to know what comes out. And I want to be close enough to deal with it."

"I'm not going to draw it."

"I know you're not going to draw it," said Shoko. "I'm saying it so it's on record."

* * *

Yuta was on the back step.

He was sitting with his own katana across his knees, the way he sat after the slow practice in the mornings. He was not practising. He was looking at the yard in the last of the light. Maki was beside him, close, with her spear leaning against the wall and the silver flower catching the light.

Mau came out with the black scabbard in his hand.

Yuta looked up.

He saw it.

He did not move for a long moment. His face did something Mau had seen it do only a few times. On the floor of the hive, looking at the long cut on his own arm. On the roof, long ago, telling Mau about the scar on his forehead.

"Where did you get that?" said Yuta. Very quietly.

"Eighty-six," said Mau. "A field of swords. Hundreds of them. Stuck in the ground. This one was in the middle. Lying down." He held it out. Not to give. To show. "It looks like yours."

Yuta did not take it.

He looked at it for a long time. At the black scabbard, worn at the mouth. At the dark cord on the hilt. Then at his own katana, across his knees. Then back.

"It's the same shape," he said.

"Almost."

"Almost," said Yuta. He reached out and touched the scabbard, very lightly, with one finger. Just where the lacquer was worn. "Mine's worn there too. Same place. From drawing it." He took his finger away. "Someone drew this one. A lot. Before it was in that field."

Maki was looking at it too. She did not say anything.

* * *

"Shoko says it's a cursed tool," said Mau.

"It is," said Yuta.

"She says it takes what you put into it."

"It does." Yuta looked at his own katana. "Mine does. When I put cursed energy into it, it holds it. Sharpens it. Makes it a blade instead of a cloud." He was quiet. "And when I draw it in a fight, sometimes—" He stopped.

Mau waited.

"Sometimes Rika comes with it," said Yuta.

Nobody said anything.

Mau had seen Rika. Once. On Day 133, in the field, in the worst fight of his life, when he and Gojo had both been preparing Purple and neither of them could stop. Something enormous and pale had come out of nowhere and broken the alignment of his hands. He had not seen her face. He had seen an arm. And felt, for one moment, a terrible, crushing, protective rage. Not at him. *For* someone.

For Yuta.

He had never told anyone that he remembered that feeling.

"She's not in the sword," said Yuta. "She's in the ring. She's in me." He touched the ring on his finger. "But the sword is where she comes out. When I'm fighting. When I need her. It's like—" He thought about it. "It's like the sword is a door. And she uses it."

He looked at the black scabbard in Mau's hand.

"If you ever draw that," he said slowly, "in a fight. When you need it." He looked up at Mau. "Be careful what comes out with it. You've got a lot of doors in you. Ear. The wheel. The shadows. The things the Noise builds." He paused. "A sword like that might be one more door."

"Shoko said the same thing."

"Shoko's usually right," said Yuta. "It's very annoying." He almost smiled. "Satoru says that."

"Satoru's usually right too," said Maki, without looking up. "Also very annoying."

* * *

Gojo came down off the barn roof after dark.

He came to the back step, where the three of them were still sitting, and stood at the bottom of it with his blindfold pushed up. He looked at the katana in Mau's lap.

He did not say anything clever.

"Ninety," he said instead. "I looked."

"And?"

"One," said Gojo. "Not an army. One." He was quiet. "A general."

"Like Igris?"

"Igris is a knight," said Gojo. "This is what knights are afraid of." He looked at the yard. "It's standing in the middle of a field with its sword point-down in the ground and both hands on the hilt. Very still. And the sword's not a sword." He frowned. "It's a sword. And it isn't. It's made of pieces. Joined. I could see the joins." He looked at Mau. "I think it comes apart."

"Into what?"

"Something long," said Gojo. "Something that finds the edges. All at once." He looked at the katana in Mau's lap. Then at Mau. "Anything at the back? Watching? No. I looked very hard. Nothing at the back." He paused. "But it's waiting for *you*, Mau. Not for a party. For you. I could see that much."

Nobody said anything.

"Who's going?" said Gojo.

Mau looked at the katana. At Yuta beside him. At Maki with her spear.

"Yuta," he said. "Maki. Frieren." He looked at Gojo. "Not you."

Gojo's eyebrows went up.

"Not me?"

"You've been to every big one," said Mau. "Twenty. Forty. Seventy. Eighty. You stood at a door on twenty and carried me home from seventy. You've been at all of it." He looked at the yard. "Shoko asked me. Yesterday. Quietly. If one of them could be without you." He paused. "This one."

Gojo looked at him for a long time.

Then he looked toward the clinic, where a lamp was still burning.

"She asked?"

"She asked," said Mau.

Gojo did not say anything for a while.

"Fine," he said at last. "I'll stay home." He pulled the blindfold down. "I'll sit on the clinic step. And be very annoying about it." He turned to go. Then stopped. "Mau."

"Mm."

"The sword," said Gojo. "If you draw it. Up there." He did not turn round. "Draw it like you mean it. Half-drawing a cursed tool is worse than not drawing it at all." He paused. "Yuta knows."

"I know," said Yuta.

Gojo went toward the clinic. The lamp was still lit. After a while, through the clinic window, Mau saw him sit down on the step outside it, with his back against the door frame. A moment later the door opened, and Shoko came out and sat down beside him, and neither of them said anything at all.
