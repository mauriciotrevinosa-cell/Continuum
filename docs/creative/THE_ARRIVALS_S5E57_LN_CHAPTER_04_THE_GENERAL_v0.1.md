# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 29 — The General

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31

*When: Day 387, morning outside. Anko's board: Day 387. Mau, Yuta, Frieren, Maki. From 80. Floor 90 (guardian). And under it, in Fern's hand: Gojo stays home. (Shoko asked.) And under that, in Gojo's: Under protest. —S.G.*

They walked from eighty.

Through the ink lake and the mirrors that showed you behind yourself. Mau did not look at the mirrors. Through the grey road and the wind and the leaning city. Through the field of swords on eighty-six, where Armin's rings still circled an empty place on the grey grass where something had been put down on purpose, and was not there any more.

It was at Mau's hip now. In its black scabbard. Tied with a cord Maki had given him at breakfast.

"If you're going to carry it," she had said, "carry it properly."

Through the library. Through the garden of statues on eighty-eight, where every stone face turned to follow them except one, which looked at a blank wall as if there were a door in it.

Up the stair into black and violet banners.

At the top, Mau stopped.

"Before we go in," he said.

The three of them looked at him.

"Gojo says it's waiting for me," said Mau. "Not for a party. For me." He looked at the opening. "I think it's going to come at me. Whatever we do." He looked at them. "So I want to say it now. Before. I'm going to try to get inside it. Inside its reach. The way Maki does. Because a whip's strongest at the end and nothing at the handle." He paused. "And I'm going to get hurt doing it. I want you to know before it happens. So nobody's surprised."

Frieren looked at him for a long time.

"You're telling us the plan," she said.

"I'm telling you the plan."

"You never tell us the plan," said Frieren. "You just do it."

"I'm learning," said Mau.

Maki leaned on her spear.

"Get inside," she said. "Then what?"

"Then I don't know," said Mau honestly. "Then whatever's there."

"That's half a plan."

"It's half more than I had on twenty," said Mau.

Yuta almost laughed. He put his hand on his katana. His own. The real one.

"Half's enough," he said. "We'll do the other half."

* * *

Ninety was a field.

Dark grass. A sky violet at the edges, like the last of a sunset that never finished setting. Black and violet banners on tall poles round the edge. In the middle, a figure. Tall. Black-violet plate. A crested helm like a crown. Both gauntleted hands on the hilt of a long sword driven point-down into the grass. The sword made of segments, joined, every hand's breadth.

Violet eyes, lifting.

*Guardian: Bellion, Grand Marshal.*

"*You are the one who climbs*," said Bellion. The voice carried across the whole field without being raised. "*Ninety floors. I have been told of you. By the stone that watches.*" Its violet eyes went to the katana at Mau's hip. Stayed there. "*It said you choose. What to keep. What to leave.*" Back to Mau's face. "*It said you are not yet what it needs. But you will be.*"

It pulled its sword out of the ground.

"*I am to see how close.*"

"Who's watching?" said Mau. "Through the stone. Who is it?"

Bellion did not answer.

It held the sword out, level, toward him.

And the sword came apart.

* * *

Twenty paces of segmented steel uncoiled across the dark grass like a snake striking. Mau had the space up before it came. Round himself. Not round the others. He had told them the plan. Frieren went left, floating. Maki went right, low. Yuta stayed behind him, sword drawn.

The whip hit the space.

A dozen strikes in a heartbeat. Every segment from a different angle. High, low, behind, above. The space held. Mau felt each strike like hail on a roof, in the reserve he used for the space and the pull and his eyes.

Then a segment found the edge.

High. At his left shoulder. Where his attention wasn't, because his attention was on the segments coming low. It slipped through the thin place.

It opened his shoulder.

Not a scratch. Not like Igris on twenty. Deep. He felt the steel go in and come out, and then the heat, and then nothing for a moment, and then all of it at once. His left arm went cold to the fingers.

He did not go down.

The whip drew back. Coiled. Bellion's violet eyes were on him. Patient.

*It's learning you,* said Ciel. *Faster than Igris. Every segment is learning a different edge.*

*I know.*

*Your shoulder—*

*I know.*

* * *

"*Soldiers*."

The eight came up out of the dark grass in front of him. Shields locked. Spears up. A wall.

The whip hit the wall. The shields rang like bells. The soldiers held. Again. A segment went through between the third and fourth shield and the fourth soldier came apart into pieces of dark. The fifth stepped into the gap. Again. The seventh. The sixth stepped in.

They held. Barely.

Behind them, Mau breathed and thought.

*Think,* he told himself. *Don't react. Think.* He had reacted on twenty. He had thrown everything at the space and hoped. It had worked because Igris had been one sword and he had been lucky. This was twenty swords at once.

The whip was strongest at its length. All twenty paces. That was where the segments were fast, where they could curve, where they found edges. At the handle it was nothing. A hilt. A man's hand.

He had to be at the handle.

Between him and the handle was twenty paces of steel that could find every edge of the space he could make.

*Ciel. How long can I hold the space if I don't spread it? If I put it only in front. A wall. Not a skin.*

*A flat wall in one direction? Much longer. It doesn't have edges in front of you. Only at the sides.* A pause. *But the sides would be open. And behind.*

*Then the others cover the sides.*

He looked left. Frieren, floating, with her staff up, watching. Right. Maki, low in the dark grass, already moving round the end of the shield wall. Behind. Yuta, sword drawn.

"*Frieren*," he said. "Left side. *Yuta*. Behind me. *Maki*—"

"I know," said Maki. "Right side. Go."

He went.

* * *

He went round the end of the shield wall with the space flat in front of him like a door.

The whip came at him. He did not try to stop every segment. He stopped the ones in front. The ones that curved round the sides, Frieren met. Thin precise lines of light from the left, knocking segments off their arcs. The ones that came round behind, Yuta met. His sword moved, quiet and quick, and the segments rang off it.

Ten paces.

Bellion saw what he was doing.

It did something Mau did not expect. It stepped back. One step. Two. And the whip, which had been twenty paces, became twenty-five. Thirty. Longer. The segments were further apart now. More of them. Coming from more angles.

A segment came round under the flat space, low, from in front, under the bottom edge. It opened his side. Above the hip. Deep, again. He felt it go in.

His legs went.

He went down on one knee in the dark grass, ten paces from the general, with his left arm cold and his side opened and blood running into the grass.

"*Mau*—"

Frieren's voice. From the left.

"*Close*," said Bellion. Very quietly. Its violet eyes were on him. "*Closer than the knight, on twenty. Closer than the ant.*" The whip coiled back. All thirty paces of it. Gathering. "*Not close enough.*"

It struck.

* * *

He did not decide.

His right hand went to his hip. To the black scabbard, tied with Maki's cord. To the hilt, wrapped in dark cord, rough under his fingers.

*Draw it like you mean it,* Gojo had said. *Half-drawing a cursed tool is worse than not drawing it at all.*

He drew it like he meant it.

All the way. In one movement. The blade came out of the black scabbard clean and pale and very sharp. He held it in his one good hand. He did not know how to use a sword. He had watched Yuta every morning in the yard for a year. He held it the way Yuta held his.

And something came out with it.

* * *

He felt the Noise move.

Not attend. *Move.* Underneath, where the building happened. It reached for something. He did not know what. Something it had seen. It had seen it once, on Day 133, in the field, in the worst fight of his life. Something enormous and pale that had broken his hands' alignment. That had been terrible, and crushing, and so full of rage that was not rage at him but *for* someone.

It had seen it. It had kept it. It had been building it ever since, the way it built everything it saw. Slowly. In the dark. Without asking.

The sword was a door.

It came out through the door.

* * *

An arm.

Out of the air beside him. Out of nothing. Pale. Enormous. Longer than he was tall. Thin, too thin, with too many joints, and a hand at the end of it with long pale fingers spread wide.

The whip hit it.

All thirty paces of segmented steel hit the pale arm. The arm caught it. The fingers closed round the steel. Round dozens of segments at once. And *held*.

Bellion's violet eyes went wide.

Behind the arm, in the air, something else was coming through. A shape. Pale. Huge. Half-made, the way a drawing is half-made when someone has only started it. The suggestion of a body, hunched. A head, tilted. And a face. Not a face. The start of a face. Pale. Wide. With something like a mouth, too wide, and something like eyes, too dark.

It made a sound.

Not a word. A shriek. High. Wordless. Full of the same thing Mau had felt on Day 133. Rage that was not rage. Protection. *For* someone.

For him.

* * *

Behind him, Yuta made a sound.

Mau heard it through everything. Through the shriek and the ring of the steel and his own heart. A sound Yuta had never made in front of him. Not a word. A breath going in and stopping.

"That's—" said Yuta.

He did not finish.

*It isn't her,* said Ciel. Very fast. Very level. *Mau. Listen. It isn't Rika. It's a construct. The Noise built it from what it saw. On Day 133. From watching her. It's a pattern. Not a person. Not a curse. Not Yuta's.* A pause. *And it's costing everything. The walls are thinning. I can feel it. Three seconds. Maybe four. Then it comes apart, and if it comes apart while it's still connected to you—*

The pale arm held the whip.

The half-made face turned. Slowly. Not toward Bellion. Toward Yuta.

It looked at him with its too-dark start of eyes.

Yuta did not move.

Then the whip tore free.

* * *

Bellion pulled. All of its strength. The segments ripped through the pale fingers, through the hand, through the arm. The construct shrieked once more, high and wordless, and came apart. Not into pieces of dark, like the shadows. Into nothing. Into light. Pale light, scattering in the violet air like ash. Like the last of a lantern going out.

Gone.

Mau felt it go out of him. Felt something tear, underneath, where the walls were. Not break. Strain. Like a rope taking a weight it was not made for.

The whip came back.

At him. All of it. Thirty paces of steel. And he was on one knee, with a pale katana in his one good hand, and his shoulder open and his side open. The space was gone, because there was nothing left to make it with.

It hit him.

* * *

And the wheel turned.

He did not decide that either.

It was in his shadow. It had always been in his shadow, asleep, sinking deeper every time the walls held. It turned at what hurt him. That was all it did. It did not listen, or want, or watch. It turned at harm, and learned its shape.

The whip hurt him.

*Click.*

He heard it. Not in his ears. Underneath. One eighth of a turn. The sound of something enormous shifting one notch in the dark.

The segments hit him. Across the chest. Across the arms. A dozen of them at once.

And slid off.

Not stopped. Not caught. *Slid.* As if he were wet stone and they were water. The edges turned on his skin and went past. They left long red marks and did not cut. The whip went past him on both sides, and he was still kneeling in the dark grass, bleeding from the shoulder and the side, and not bleeding anywhere new.

Bellion stared at him.

*It adapted,* said Ciel. Her voice had gone very strange. *The wheel. One turn. It learned the whip.* A pause. *Mau. Your eyes.*

He felt it then. Warm. Under the filter. Running down from both eyes, under the edges of the cloth. Not tears. He knew what it was. He had felt it once before, in the clinic, after the bell.

*The walls,* said Ciel. *The construct thinned them. The wheel turning pushed on them. They're holding. Barely.* A pause. *Don't let it turn again. Please. If it turns again—*

Bellion changed the whip.

Mau saw it change. The segments twisted. Turned their edges. A different angle. A different cut. The general was learning too.

It struck.

*Click.*

Two.

* * *

The second turn was worse.

The segments slid off him again. But underneath, where the walls were, something groaned. He felt the whole structure of himself shift, the way a house shifts in a storm. The blood under the filter was running freely now. His vision went red at the edges. Then white.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *Mau. Get up. Now. Get inside. Before it changes again. Before it turns a third time.* Her voice was not level any more. *I can't hold the walls through a third. I don't know what happens after a third.*

He got up.

He did not know how. His legs were not working. His left arm was cold and useless. His side was open. His eyes were bleeding under the filter. He got up anyway. He went forward. Toward the general. Toward the handle of the whip.

The segments came at him and slid off.

Ten paces. Eight.

Bellion drew the whip back. Fast. Coiling. Trying to make it a sword again. A sword would not slide off him the way the whip did. The wheel had learned the whip. It had not learned the sword.

Five paces.

And Maki was already there.

* * *

She had gone round. While Mau drew the katana. While the pale thing shrieked. While the wheel turned twice. She had gone round through the dark grass on the right, low and fast, the way she always went round. She was inside Bellion's reach before the whip finished becoming a sword.

Her spear came up at the hand on the hilt.

Bellion turned it. Barely. The sword was half a sword, half a whip, segments still locking. It met her spear clumsily. The silver flower rang against violet steel.

Yuta was on its other side.

He did not draw Rika. He did not need to. His sword went in at the back of Bellion's knee, where the plates joined, with his cursed energy round it like a dark film. The knee buckled.

Frieren fired.

Zoltraak. From the left. White. Into Bellion's chest, where the black-violet armour was thickest. It went in halfway and stopped, like a nail into hard wood.

Halfway was enough.

Bellion staggered.

"*Igris*," said Mau. It came out as a whisper.

The knight rose out of the grass at the general's back with his long sword already moving. At the neck. At the gap where the crested helm met the plates.

And Mau, three paces away, with the pale katana in his one good hand, did the only thing he had ever seen done with a sword that he could do with one arm, bleeding, half-blind. He drove the point forward. Into the gap under Bellion's arm, where Maki's spear had gone on ten and his own on twenty.

It went in.

* * *

Bellion went down on one knee.

It did not fall. It knelt, in the middle of the dark field, with Maki's spear and Yuta's sword and Frieren's light and Igris's blade and a pale katana all in it at once. Its violet eyes looked past all of them. At Mau.

"*Close*," it said. Very quietly. "*It said you would be close.*" The violet was going out. "*It did not say how close.*" Its eyes held his for one moment more. "*Closer than it thinks.*"

The violet went out.

*Guardian defeated.*

Mau let go of the katana.

It stayed where it was, in the gap under the general's arm.

He did not stay where he was. His legs had decided. He went down onto the dark grass on his back. He lay there looking up at the violet edge of the sky that never finished setting. It was very red and very white at the edges, because of the blood in his eyes. He could hear the wheel. Underneath. Not turning. Settling. Two notches further round than it had been that morning.

Frieren's face appeared above him.

Her hands were on his face. Cold. Shaking.

"You told us the plan," she said. Her voice was not steady.

"Half of it."

"You said you'd get hurt."

"I did."

"You didn't say the wheel," said Frieren. "You didn't say *that*." She meant the thing with the pale arm. Mau could see it in her face. "You didn't say anything would come out of you."

"I didn't know," said Mau. "I swear. I didn't know."

Frieren looked at him for a long time.

Then she put her forehead against his. Very gently. Over the cloth of the filter, over the blood.

"I know you didn't," she said.

Behind her, at the edge of his red-and-white sight, Yuta was standing very still in the dark grass. He was looking at the place in the violet air where the pale half-made thing had come apart into light.

He had not moved since it looked at him.
