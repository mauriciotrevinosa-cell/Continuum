# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 30 — What Came Out With It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 30
**Continuity state:** Aftermath on floor 90, before the extraction. Yuta uses his outward reverse cursed technique on Mau's shoulder and side; it closes them partly, not fully, and costs Yuta. Ciel's account: the walls held, the wheel has turned two notches and adapted to that whip only, the construct was a pattern the Noise built from Day 133 and is gone, and nothing is permanently unlocked. Mau apologises to Yuta; Yuta says the thing looked at him the way Rika looks at him when he is hurt, and that it was protecting Mau. Maki holds Yuta. No new power is granted

*When: Day 387, the dark field on ninety, a few minutes after. Bellion kneels with five weapons in him, the violet gone from his eyes. Mau is on his back in the grass. The wheel has stopped settling. The blood under the filter has stopped running.*

Yuta knelt down beside him.

He did not say anything. He put his sword down on the grass, carefully, the way he always put it down. Then he put both hands on Mau's left shoulder. Over the cut. Over the blood.

Mau felt it.

Warm. Not the warm of the roof, the thin film Yuta laid over the air on cold nights. Deeper. Going in. Into the cut, into the cold arm, into the place where the steel had gone in and come out. It did not hurt. It was the strangest thing he had ever felt. Like being filled with something that was not his and fitted anyway.

"Hold still," said Yuta.

"I'm holding still."

"Hold stiller."

Mau held stiller.

The warmth went in and in. He felt the cut begin to close. Not all at once. Slowly. From the bottom up. The edges finding each other. The cold going out of his fingers.

Yuta's face was very white.

"That's enough," said Frieren, from his other side. "Yuta. That's enough for the shoulder. You're shaking."

"The side," said Yuta.

"Yuta—"

"The side," said Yuta again. Very quietly. Not looking at her. "It's deeper than the shoulder. It's near something that matters." He moved his hands. Down. To Mau's side, above the hip, where the second segment had gone in. "Then I'll stop."

* * *

He did the side.

It took longer. Mau watched his face while he did it. Yuta's eyes were closed. His forehead was wet. The scar across it, Kenjaku's stitches, stood out pale against his skin. His hands did not move. The warmth went in.

The cut closed. Not all the way. Enough. A long pink line where there had been an open wound. Tender. Not bleeding.

Yuta took his hands away.

He sat back on his heels in the dark grass. He did not say anything for a long time. He was breathing as if he had run up all ninety floors.

"It's not finished," he said at last. "Either of them. Shoko will have to stitch the shoulder. The side will hold. For now." He looked at his hands. "I'm not as good as Shoko. I never was. Shoko could put a person back together in a minute." He was quiet. "I can only do what I can do."

"Thank you," said Mau.

Yuta did not answer. He was looking at the place in the violet air, above them, where the pale thing had come apart into light.

* * *

*Ciel,* said Mau.

*I'm here.*

*Tell me.*

Ciel was quiet for a moment. He could feel her gathering it. All of it. In order. The way Frieren told him things when she had decided he should know them exactly.

*The walls held,* she said. *They thinned. A great deal. When the construct came through, and again when the wheel turned. But they held. They're thickening again now. Slowly.* A pause. *The blood in your eyes was from the strain. Not a breach. It's stopped.*

*The wheel.*

*Two notches,* said Ciel. *Two turns. It's further round than it was this morning. It adapted to the whip. That whip. Bellion's, in that form.* A pause. *It hasn't adapted to anything else. It doesn't know swords. It doesn't know fire. It knows that one whip.* Another pause. *And it's asleep again. It sank back down when the hurting stopped. It does that. It isn't watching. It isn't waiting. It's asleep.*

*Can it turn again?*

*Yes,* said Ciel. *If something hurts you enough. Next time, somewhere else, at something else.* She was quiet. *I don't know how many turns the walls can take. I don't want to find out. Two was too many. Please don't make me find out what three is.*

*I won't.*

*You said that about the space on twenty,* said Ciel. Very gently. *You keep saying it, and then you do what you have to.* A pause. *I know you do. I'm just telling you what it costs.*

He did not say anything.

*The other thing,* he said at last.

*The construct,* said Ciel.

*Yes.*

Ciel was quiet for a long time.

*It's gone,* she said. *Completely. It came apart into light, and the light went out. There's nothing left of it in you. Not a piece. Not like the shadows, where the pieces wait.* A pause. *The Noise built it. From what it saw on Day 133. From watching her break your hands apart before the Purple. From watching Yuta, every day, for a year. In the yard. On the roof.* A pause. *It built a pattern of her. The way it builds everything it sees. In the dark. Without asking. And the sword was a door. And when you drew it like you meant it, the pattern came through.*

*Is it Rika?*

*No,* said Ciel. Very firmly. *No. It isn't Rika. It isn't Yuta's. It isn't a curse. It isn't a person. It's a pattern. A shape the Noise made of something it saw once and kept.* She paused. *Rika is in Yuta's ring. In Yuta. She always has been. Nothing happened to her.*

*It looked at him.*

*It looked at him,* said Ciel. *I saw. I don't know why. Patterns don't look at people.* A pause. *But that one did.*

* * *

Mau turned his head.

It hurt. Everything hurt. He turned it anyway, in the dark grass, until he could see Yuta. Sitting on his heels with his hands in his lap, looking at the empty air.

"Yuta," he said.

Yuta did not look at him.

"Ciel says it wasn't her," said Mau. "It wasn't Rika. It was something the Noise built. From watching her. On Day 133. And from watching you." He swallowed. "She says Rika's fine. She's in your ring. Nothing happened to her."

"I know," said Yuta.

"I'm sorry."

Yuta turned his head and looked at him.

"Don't," he said.

"It came out of me. I didn't know it would. I didn't—" Mau stopped. "It looked like her. I didn't see its face. I saw it looking at you. And I heard you." He looked at Yuta. "I'm sorry. I didn't mean for anything that looked like her to come out of me. I know what she is to you."

Yuta was quiet for a long time.

"It didn't look like her," he said at last.

Mau looked at him.

"Not really," said Yuta. "It was too thin. Too pale. Half-finished. Like a drawing someone only started." He looked at the empty air. "Rika isn't half-finished. Rika is—" He stopped. "Rika is all the way there. Always. Too much there."

"Then why did you—"

"Because of how it looked at me," said Yuta.

He looked down at his hands. At the ring on his finger.

"When I'm hurt," he said slowly. "When I'm in a fight, and I get hurt. Really hurt. Rika comes. Even if I haven't called her. Even if I've told her not to." He turned the ring. "And before she does anything, before she fights, she looks at me. Just for a second. To see where I'm hurt." He was quiet. "It's the worst look in the world. It's so angry. Not at me. *For* me. At whatever did it."

Mau did not say anything.

"That thing looked at me like that," said Yuta. "For one second. Before the whip tore it." He looked at the empty air. "Like it wanted to see if *I* was hurt too. Not just you." He was quiet for a long time. "It was protecting you. And it looked at me to check I was all right. The way she does."

He looked at Mau.

"That's not something you need to be sorry for," he said.

* * *

Maki came and sat down beside Yuta.

She had pulled her spear out of Bellion. The silver flower was still below the blade, not a scratch on it. She laid the spear in the grass. She did not say anything. She sat down close beside Yuta, very close, and put her hand on the back of his neck and left it there.

Yuta leaned against her.

Just a little. The way he leaned against her on the roof sometimes, when he thought nobody was looking.

"You never told me," said Maki, very quietly. "That she looks at you like that."

"You've seen it."

"I've seen her come," said Maki. "I've never seen the look. I'm always too busy hitting whatever she's about to hit." She was quiet. "I didn't know there was a look."

"There's a look," said Yuta.

Maki looked at the empty air where the pale thing had been.

"Then whatever that was," she said slowly, "it learned the look from somewhere." She looked at Mau. "From watching her. From watching *you*." Back at the empty air. "That's a strange thing to learn. From watching."

* * *

Frieren had not taken her hands off Mau's face.

She was still kneeling beside him in the grass. She had not said anything since the forehead. She had listened to all of it. Ciel's account, as Mau repeated it. Yuta. Maki.

"Can you sit up?" she said.

"I don't know."

"Try."

He tried. She helped. Her hand under his good shoulder. He sat up in the dark grass and the field swam and came back. His left arm was warm again, and stiff. His side was tight. The cloth of the filter was stiff with dried blood. He could see.

He looked at Bellion.

The general still knelt in the middle of the field, its sword point-down in the grass in front of it, with Yuta's sword gone from its knee and Maki's spear gone from its arm, and the pale katana still in the gap under its other arm, where Mau had driven it.

He looked at the katana.

"I'll need that back," he said.

"You'll need to not draw it again for a very long time," said Frieren.

"I know."

"I mean it, Mau."

"I know," said Mau. "But I'll need it back. It's not something you leave in a field." He looked at it. "Somebody put it down on purpose. In the middle of eighty-six. And I picked it up. And it was a door." He was quiet. "I don't want to leave a door open in a tower where something watches through stone."

Frieren looked at him for a long time.

Then she stood up. She walked across the dark grass to the kneeling general. She took hold of the hilt of the pale katana, wrapped in dark cord, and pulled it out of the gap under Bellion's arm, cleanly. She walked back. She knelt down. She held it out to him.

Not the blade. The hilt.

"Sheathe it," she said. "Now. Before anything else."

He took it in his good hand. He found the black scabbard at his hip, tied with Maki's cord. He slid the blade home. It went in clean. The mouth of the scabbard was worn in exactly the place where Yuta's was worn.

Nothing came out.

He let out a breath.

* * *

Under the kneeling general, between the dark grass and the black-violet armour, the dark was gathering.

The notice came up.

*Shadow extraction available.*

Mau looked at it.

*Can I?* he said. *The cup. Not the walls. The cup.*

*The cup is fine,* said Ciel. *The shadows didn't cost much today. Igris for a moment. The soldiers. The construct didn't come from the cup.* A pause. *Raising him will take most of it. It won't touch the walls.* Another pause. *But you're very hurt, Mau. And you're very tired. And tired makes it harder.*

He looked at the others.

At Yuta, leaning against Maki, white-faced, with his hands in his lap. At Maki, with her hand on the back of Yuta's neck. At Frieren, kneeling beside him, with her cold hands in her own lap now, looking at him.

"Ciel says the cup's fine," he said. "She says I'm very hurt and very tired."

"She's right," said Frieren.

"I know."

"Then sit," said Maki, without looking up from Yuta. "You're already sitting. Stay sitting. Do it from there."

Mau looked at the kneeling general.

*It said you would be close. It did not say how close. Closer than it thinks.*

He put his good hand flat on the dark grass.
