# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 30 — Two Notches

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 30
**Continuity state:** The morning after floor 90 (Day 388), in the clinic. Shoko is calm and devastating about the two wheel turns. Gojo talks about cursed tools and doors from his own experience as a teacher, but does not know what Mau's construct is. Ciel's account to Shoko is relayed aloud by Rimuru. Ori comes through the connecting door and asks whether the wheel is "awake"; it is asleep. Bed rest: the shoulder is stitched, the side holds, the eyes are unfiltered for an hour under Gojo's watch to check them. No new power

*When: Day 388, morning. The clinic. Mau slept fourteen hours. When he woke, his eyes were crusted shut, and Frieren washed them with warm water before she let him try to open them.*

Shoko did not shout.

Mau had thought she might. He had lain awake for a while in the clinic bed in the grey before dawn, after Frieren washed his eyes, and thought about what Shoko would say. He had decided she would shout. Gojo said she had shouted once, a long time ago, in their world, at a first-year who had done something stupid. He said nobody in that school had ever forgotten it.

She did not shout.

She sat on her stool beside the clinic bed with her coffee, which Touka had brought, and her notes, which she had written in the night. She looked at him for a long time without saying anything. Then she put the coffee down.

"Two," she said.

Mau did not say anything.

"Two turns," said Shoko. "Of the thing in your shadow that nearly killed you on the night of the bell. That turned once in the winter, and Yuta had to pull you out before it turned again. That everyone in this house has spent months making sure stays asleep." She looked at her notes. "It turned twice. Yesterday. Because a man with a whip was cutting you to pieces on the ninetieth floor of a tower, and you had already spent everything else."

"Yes."

"Did you decide?"

"No," said Mau. "It turns at what hurts me. I didn't decide. It turned."

"I know it did," said Shoko. "I know you didn't decide. I've read everything Ciel's ever said about it. I know it doesn't listen." She folded her hands on her notes. "I'm asking because I want you to say it out loud. That you didn't decide. That you were hurt that badly. That it was that or the whip."

Mau looked at the ceiling.

"It was that or the whip," he said.

"Thank you," said Shoko.

* * *

Rimuru was sitting on the end of the bed.

He had come in at dawn. He had not said much. He had sat on the end of the clinic bed with his chin on his knees and listened to Ciel, inwardly, and every so often he said something out loud so Shoko could hear it.

"She says the walls are back to where they were before the fight," he said now. "Not stronger. Not weaker. Back." He was quiet for a moment, listening. "She says the wheel is two notches further round than it was yesterday morning. It's adapted to that whip. Bellion's, in that form. Nothing else." Another pause. "She says it's asleep."

"Asleep," said Shoko.

"Asleep," said Rimuru. "She says it sank back down the moment the hurting stopped. It always does. It isn't waiting. It isn't watching."

Shoko wrote it down.

"And the other thing," she said. Without looking up. "The thing that came out of the sword."

Rimuru was quiet for a long time.

"She says it's gone," he said. "Completely. She says it was a pattern. The Noise built it from what it saw of Rika on Day 133, and from watching Yuta, every day, for a year." He looked at Mau. "She says it isn't Rika. It isn't anything. It was a shape, and the shape came apart into light, and there's nothing of it left."

"Was it dangerous?"

"She says it thinned the walls more than the wheel did," said Rimuru. "Both turns of the wheel together thinned them less than four seconds of that." He paused. "She says if it had stayed connected when it came apart, she doesn't know what would have happened."

Shoko put her pen down.

She looked at Mau for a long time.

"Four seconds," she said.

"I know."

"Four seconds of something you didn't know you had, that came out of a sword you picked up in a field, that thinned the walls more than two turns of the wheel." Her voice was very level. "That's what you're carrying at your hip now."

"It's in the scabbard," said Mau. "Frieren made me sheathe it. Before anything else."

"Good," said Shoko. "Where is it now?"

"On the hook. In the east room. Next to Frieren's staff."

Shoko looked at him.

"Leave it there," she said. "For a long time."

* * *

Gojo came in at nine.

He did not knock. He came in with his blindfold on and his hands in his pockets and stood at the foot of the clinic bed and looked at Mau. Then he pushed the blindfold up and looked again, with his bare eyes.

"Eyes," he said to Shoko.

"I was going to ask you," said Shoko.

"I know," said Gojo. "That's why I'm here."

He sat down on the edge of the bed. Very close. Closer than he usually sat. He put two fingers under Mau's chin and tilted his face toward the window.

"Take it off," he said. "The filter. For a minute. I want to see behind it."

"Shoko said—"

"Shoko asked me to," said Gojo. "She can't see behind it. I can." He looked at Mau. "One minute. Then you put it back. I'm right here."

Mau let the filter go.

It was not a cloth. It never had been, really, not since the bell. It was a thing behind his eyes, a habit Gojo had taught him and Ciel had built into the walls. He let it go. The clinic came in all at once. Every grain in the wooden ceiling. Every fibre of the blanket. Every line on Shoko's face, every hair on Gojo's head, the tiny tremor in Gojo's two fingers under his chin.

Too much. All of it.

He breathed.

Gojo's bare eyes were looking into his. Very close. Reading.

"Mm," said Gojo.

"What?" said Shoko.

"The strain's gone," said Gojo. "Mostly. Last night it was all through them. Red, at the edges. Like after the bell." He tilted Mau's face a little more. "Today it's just—tired. Like eyes that stayed open too long." He let go of Mau's chin. "Put it back."

Mau put the filter back.

The clinic went soft again. Bearable.

"They'll be fine," said Gojo, to Shoko. "Two days. Maybe three. No tower. No reading. No looking at anything very bright." He paused. "No drawing that sword."

* * *

He did not leave.

He sat on the edge of the bed with his hands in his lap and looked at the wall for a long time.

"I had students," he said eventually. "Before. In our world. Who carried cursed tools." He did not look at Mau. "Maki. Yuta. Others." He was quiet. "A cursed tool isn't a technique. It doesn't think. It does what you put into it. That's what everyone tells first-years. *It does what you put into it.*"

"Shoko said."

"Shoko was there when I said it to the first-years," said Gojo. "She used to roll her eyes." He almost smiled. "Here's what nobody tells first-years. Because first-years can't hear it yet."

Mau waited.

"You don't always know what you've put into it," said Gojo. "You think you know. You think you're putting in cursed energy, and a technique, and a little bit of fear, and a lot of wanting to win." He looked at his hands. "And then you draw it, in a fight, when you're desperate, like you mean it. And something comes out that you didn't know was in you. Something you'd been carrying for years without looking at it."

Nobody said anything.

"Yuta's tool is a door for Rika," said Gojo. "Because Rika is the thing Yuta carries. Always. More than anything." He looked at Mau. "Yours was a door for something the Noise built from watching her. Because that's what you carry. Everything you've ever watched. Kept. In the dark. Built." He was quiet. "I don't know what that thing was. I wasn't there. I can't see it in you now. Ciel says it's gone." He paused. "But I know what a door is. And I know you know now that you have one."

"I know," said Mau.

"Good," said Gojo. He stood up. He pulled the blindfold back down. "Then you're a second-year."

"What's a second-year?"

"A first-year who's found out he doesn't know what's in him," said Gojo. "It's the worst year. Everyone hates second year." He went to the door. He stopped. "Mau."

"Mm."

"You told them the plan," said Gojo. Without turning round. "Before you went in. Frieren told me. You stood at the top of the stair and said *I'm going to get hurt doing it. I want you to know before it happens.*" He was quiet. "First-years never tell anyone the plan."

He went out.

* * *

Ori came through at noon.

Not through the clinic door. She came down the stair from the east room and along the corridor and stood in the clinic doorway with her list in her hand. She had been told he was resting. She had been told not to come until the afternoon. It was noon.

She stood in the doorway and looked at him.

At his bandaged shoulder. At his side, bound. At the cloth of the filter, clean now, over his eyes.

"Frieren said the wheel turned," she said.

Mau looked at her.

"Twice," said Ori. "She told me this morning. Before breakfast. She said you'd want me to know from someone, and she wanted it to be her." She held the list against her chest. "Is it awake?"

"No," said Mau. "It's asleep."

"Are you sure?"

"Ciel's sure. Rimuru's sure. Shoko's sure." He held out his good hand. "Come here. You can check."

Ori came.

She did not climb onto the bed. She stood beside it and took his hand in both of hers. Then she looked down. At the floor beside the bed. At his shadow, lying across the clinic boards in the noon light from the window.

She looked at it for a long time.

"I can't see it," she said.

"Nobody can. It's very deep down."

"I know," said Ori. "I'm looking anyway." She looked at the shadow. "Lantern's in there. And Corner. And Igris, and Tank, and Beru, and Kaisel, and Tusk, and Kamish. And the soldiers. A hundred of them. And the new one." She paused. "And Ear. And the wheel." She was quiet. "That's a lot of things in one shadow."

"It is."

"Is it crowded?"

Mau thought about it.

"No," he said. "It's very big in there."

Ori looked at the shadow for a while longer. Then at him.

"I'm going to write the new one on the list," she said. "Bellion. Igris says he's the general." She paused. "But I'm not going to write the other thing. The one from the sword." She looked at the list. "Frieren told me about that too. She said it wasn't a shadow. She said it wasn't anything. She said it came apart into light."

"It did."

"Then it doesn't go on the list," said Ori. "The list is for things that stay." She looked at him. "Is that right?"

"That's right."

Ori nodded. Satisfied. Then she did climb onto the bed, carefully, on his good side, and sat with her back against the wall. She held his hand. She did not say anything else. After a while Mau realised she had fallen asleep, sitting up, still holding on.

Shoko, at her desk, saw it. She did not say anything either. She wrote something in her notes. Mau did not see what.

Later, when Frieren came to swap with her, he asked.

Frieren read it over Shoko's shoulder. She did not read it out. But her ears went pink at the tips. She left it on the desk. Mau could see it from the bed, if he squinted through the filter, which Gojo had told him not to do.

*Best treatment available: Ori. (Not in my clinic. In his.)*
