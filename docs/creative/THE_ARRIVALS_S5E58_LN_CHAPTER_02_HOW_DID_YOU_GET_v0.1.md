# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 33 — How Did You Get

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 33
**Continuity state:** The third connection fragment (Day 390, the reset hour), two nights after the wheel turned. Through the shadow branch: "How did you get—", cut off mid-question. Behind it, for the first time, a faint sound like wind, or many voices far away. Mau answers what he can. Ciel says the contact was longer and clearer, but cannot tell from where. He tells Frieren, Rimuru and Yuta; it is not a threat or a demand, so it is not yet the house's. Gojo notices something and is told part of it. No location, army or mentor; Jin-Woo's identity is not confirmed

*When: Day 390, the reset hour. The seventh night was last night, and Mau spent it in the east room again, because Shoko said no ladders. The roof came down to him, five cups on a tray. Seven shadows and eight soldiers are in the cup. The wheel is asleep, two notches further round than it was a week ago.*

He woke the way he always woke.

All at once. Braced. The Trial's hour.

The cold foot on his shin.

"One," said Frieren, into the pillow. "Still here."

"Still here."

He lay in the dark with his bound shoulder and his bound side and his eyes behind the filter, and his heart coming down. He did not close his eyes. He had not closed them at this hour since the first word. He lay and listened, down, toward the branch, where the shapes were.

A hundred and ten of them now. Lantern and Roof and Corner and Igris and Tank and Beru and Kaisel and Tusk and Kamish and Bellion. Ninety-eight soldiers, standing in rows in the dark. Two more, in pieces, waiting to be remade. A great deal of dark, and a great deal of room around it.

Nothing.

He waited.

* * *

*How did you get—*

The same voice.

Low. Male. Tired. From underneath. From the branch.

But clearer. Much clearer than *hello*. Clearer than *who are you*. As if a door that had been open a crack had opened a little further. Four words. And then nothing. Not silence. Cut. The way a voice is cut when someone puts a hand over a mouth, or a door slams, or something much louder happens nearby.

And behind it.

For one moment, behind the voice, he heard something else. Faint. Very far. Like wind across a great open place. Or like many voices, very far off, all at once, shouting.

Then nothing at all.

Mau lay perfectly still.

Frieren's foot had gone rigid against his shin.

"It's back," he said, very quietly.

She put her hand flat on his chest.

* * *

*Ciel.*

*I felt it,* said Ciel. Very fast. Very careful. *The same contact. Something reaching the branch from outside it.* A pause. *Longer than before. Clearer. And it didn't fade. It was cut.*

*There was something behind it.*

*I felt something,* said Ciel. *I don't know what. A pressure. Behind the voice. Like a great deal of something, a long way off.* Another pause. *I can't tell from where. I still can't tell from where.*

*Why is it clearer?*

Ciel was quiet for a long time.

*I don't know,* she said. *I can guess. I don't like guessing about this.* A pause. *The walls thinned on ninety. The wheel turned twice. Something came through a door that you didn't know you had. Maybe the branch is—more open. Than it was. Maybe things come through it more easily now.* Another pause. *Or maybe it's on the other side. Maybe whoever it is is getting better at reaching.*

*Or both.*

*Or both,* said Ciel. *I'm writing it down. The date. The time. The words. The sound behind it.*

* * *

He answered.

He did not say it aloud. Down. Toward the branch. The way he had answered *hello* and *who are you*.

*How did I get what?*

Nothing.

He thought about it. *How did you get—*

How did you get here. How did you get that. How did you get the shadows. How did you get out. How did you get to be someone a voice can reach through a branch in the dark.

He did not know which. He did not know any of the answers, really.

*I don't know,* he said. Down. Toward wherever it was. *I don't know how I got any of it. I woke up in a clearing with nothing. Everything since then, somebody gave me, or I watched someone do it, or it happened to me.* He paused. *The shadows came with a key. And a red gate. And Ciel. And a dragon at a snowline.* He paused again. *If that's what you mean.*

Nothing.

*It sounded like you were somewhere loud,* he said. *Wherever you are. I hope it gets quieter.*

Nothing came back.

* * *

"Tell me," said Frieren.

He told her. The four words. The cut. The sound behind it, like wind, or voices. What Ciel said. What he answered.

Frieren listened.

When he finished, she was quiet for a long time.

"It's getting clearer," she said.

"Ciel thinks the branch might be more open. Since ninety." He looked at the ceiling. "Since the wheel. And the sword."

"Or he's getting better at reaching."

"Or both."

Frieren lifted her head and looked at him in the grey.

"Three times," she said. "*Hello. Who are you. How did you get.*" She was quiet. "A man in a war, reaching into your shadow, asking questions. Each one longer than the last." She looked at him. "He wants to know something."

"I know."

"So do I," said Frieren. "What he wants to know. And why he's asking you."

* * *

He told Rimuru on the back step.

Touka had brought three cups. She heard *voice* and *shadow* again and went back inside without a word, the way she had before. Rimuru sat with his chin on his knees.

"Longer," he said, when Mau finished. "And cut off."

"Cut off."

"With something behind it."

"Wind. Or voices. Far off."

Rimuru turned his cup.

"That's not a threat," he said slowly. "Or a demand. It's a question. That didn't finish." He looked at the yard. "The rule's still the rule. If it says something that sounds like a threat, or wants something, we tell the house. This doesn't." He paused. "Yet."

"Yet."

"Tell Yuta," said Rimuru. "Again. It's more words. And he heard the man's voice."

* * *

Yuta was in the yard at seven with Maki, doing the slow practice. Mau did not interrupt. He sat on the back step with his bound shoulder and watched, until they stopped.

Then he told him.

Yuta listened the way Yuta always listened. Carefully. Without interrupting. When Mau got to the sound behind the voice, he went very still.

"Like voices," he said. "Far away. Shouting."

"Or wind."

Yuta was quiet for a long time.

"When he stood in your place," he said at last. "In the exchange. With his eyes closed. Waiting." He looked at the yard. "I didn't hear anything behind him. Not then. It was very quiet in that room." He was quiet. "But he stood like a man who was going back to something very loud. You could see it in his shoulders. Like Maki before a fight she knows is going to be long."

Neither of them said anything for a while.

"It might be him," said Yuta. "I think it's more likely now. Than it was." He paused. "I still can't be sure. I'd have to hear it."

"I know."

"If it is him," said Yuta slowly, "he's still in it. The war. Whatever it is." He looked at Mau's shadow on the step. "And he's reaching for you in the middle of it. To ask how you got something."

* * *

Gojo found out at lunch.

Nobody told him. He sat down across from Mau at the long table, pushed his blindfold up, and looked at him for a long time with his bare eyes. Not at his face. At his shadow, on the bench beside him.

"Something's different," he said.

Mau looked at him.

"In the shadow," said Gojo. "Not the shapes. I can see the shapes. A hundred and ten, two in pieces. Same as yesterday." He tilted his head. "Something else. Like a draught. Like a window somebody's opened a crack in a room that used to be shut." He looked at Mau. "Did something come through?"

Mau looked at Frieren beside him. Frieren looked back. She did not say anything. It was his to tell.

"A voice," said Mau. "Three times this summer. At the reset hour. Through the branch. The last one was this morning." He paused. "Frieren knows. Rimuru. Ciel. Yuta." He looked at Gojo. "Now you."

Gojo did not say anything for a long moment.

"A voice," he said.

"A man's. Tired. Asking questions." Mau looked at his shadow. "We think it might be the one who stood in my place in the exchange. Yuta isn't sure."

Gojo looked at the shadow for a long time.

Then he pulled the blindfold back down.

"Okay," he said.

"Okay?"

"Okay," said Gojo. "It's yours. You told me. I'm not going to stand at the door of your sleep." He picked up his spoon. "But if that draught gets any bigger, I want to know before it's a window." He ate. "And if anything other than a voice comes through it, I want to know before it's in the room."

"That's fair."

"That's not fair," said Gojo. "That's me being terrified and pretending to eat soup." He ate another spoonful. "Shoko taught me how. It's very effective."

Across the table, Shoko, who had heard every word, did not look up. But she pushed the bread toward him.
