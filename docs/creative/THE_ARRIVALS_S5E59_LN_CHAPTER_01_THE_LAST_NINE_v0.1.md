# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 31 — The Last Nine

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 31
**Continuity state:** The two broken soldiers are remade at dawn (Day 391). Floors 91–99 are cleared over two days, using Armin's sheet of the green book's margin drawings as a map for the first time. One scene, on floor 97 (a door with no wall), shows Mau's growth: one controlled heartbeat of Okarun's borrowed speed instead of a torn-leg sprint, and planning aloud before acting. Mau reaches level 101 (two hundred and fifty-nine unspent). Floor 100 is next. No extraction

*When: Day 391 and Day 392. The cup is full for the first time in a week. Mau's shoulder has come out of the sling. His side holds. Shoko has said "the tower, yes; the sword, no" and written both on the board herself.*

He remade the soldiers at dawn.

The two that Bellion's whip had broken. In pieces in the cup since ninety. He sat on the back step with his hand flat on the dry ground of the yard, and Touka brought him a cup without asking, and he remade them one at a time.

It was easy. Ciel said so. Soldiers were shallow. They wanted to be soldiers. The pieces came up out of the dirt and drew together: plain dark armour, spear and shield, two small cold blue lights behind the helmet slit. Then the next.

Ten again.

They stood in a line in the yard in the grey before dawn, with their spears upright, at exactly the same angle. Ichika had come down early because Touka had. She stood in the kitchen doorway in her too-long sash and looked at them for a long time.

"Different colour?" she said to her mother.

"Different colour," said Touka. "It's allowed."

Ichika considered the ten soldiers. Then she walked across the yard, barefoot, to the end of the line. She looked up at the last soldier. It looked down at her with its cold blue lights. It did not move.

She saluted.

Not properly. Ichika did not know how to salute. She put her hand flat on her forehead, the wrong way round, and held it there very seriously.

The soldier looked at her.

Then, very slowly, it lifted its spear. A hand's breadth. And lowered it again.

Ichika went back to her mother, satisfied.

"Back," said Mau, and the ten went down into the dirt, and he finished his coffee.

* * *

Armin brought the sheet to the screen.

The tower, copied from Anko's board, every floor in his small neat hand. And beside each one, the page from the green book and its margin drawing. Ninety floors that they had seen. Nine that they had not.

*Floor 91. Page 91. A staircase made of light.*

*Floor 92. Page 92. A room full of water standing upright, like a wall.*

*Floor 93. Page 93. Three doors. One with a mark on it.*

*Floor 94. Page 94. A field of grass with a single figure in it, very small.*

*Floor 95. Page 95. A lake with a single boat.*

*Floor 96. Page 96. Fire falling like rain.*

*Floor 97. Page 97. A door with no wall.*

*Floor 98. Page 98. A bridge, and on it, something waiting.*

*Floor 99. Page 99. A stair going up into white.*

He pinned it beside Anko's board.

Anko looked at it for a long time.

"Somebody drew all of that," she said. "Before."

"Someone who climbed it," said Armin. "A long time ago." He looked at the sheet. "It's not much. A drawing in the margin. But it's more than anyone's had going into a floor before." He paused. "It's a map. Of sorts."

Anko took the stub of pencil from behind her ear.

At the top of Armin's sheet, above everything, in her plain hand, she wrote:

*Read this before you go in.*

* * *

The board said the two days plainly.

*Day 391. Mau, Gojo, Kaneki, Armin. From 90. Floor 91: a staircase of light. It moved when you didn't look at it. Armin said look at it the whole way up. We did. Floor 92: water standing like a wall. Gojo walked through it. Nobody else could. He came back for us. Floor 93: three doors. The page said one has a mark. It did. Cleared, all three.*

*Day 391, afternoon. Mau, Yuta, Maki, Mikasa. From 90, walked to 93. Floor 94: a field with one figure in it, very small, very far. It did not move. Mikasa walked to it. It was a statue. It turned its head. Floor 95: a lake, one boat. Yuta rowed. Cleared, both.*

Under *Floor 94*, in Mikasa's hand, which almost nobody had ever seen:

*It turned its head when I was close. Same as 56, 80, 88. I did not like it.*

* * *

Day 392 was Frieren and Fern and Stark.

They walked from ninety. Through the staircase of light, looking at it the whole way up, as Armin had said. Through the water wall, which Gojo had walked through and then held open for the rest. It was open still, because cleared floors stayed cleared. Through the three doors, and the field with the small statue, which turned its head as they passed, and Frieren looked back at it for a long time. Over the lake in the boat, with Stark rowing because Yuta was not there and Stark said he had always wanted to row a boat.

Ninety-six was fire falling like rain.

Fern's defensive magic held over the four of them, a curved shining shell like the inside of a bowl turned upside down. The fire came down on it and ran off its edges like water off a roof. Fern held it for the whole of the floor. At the far end, when they reached the stair, she let it go. She sat down very suddenly on the bottom step.

"That was long," she said.

"You held it the whole way," said Frieren.

"I know." Fern looked at her hands. "I didn't know I could hold it that long."

"I did," said Frieren.

* * *

Ninety-seven was a door.

They came up the stair from ninety-six and stood at the top and looked at it. A door. Wooden, plain, with iron hinges and an iron ring for a handle. Standing on its own in the middle of a wide grey plain. No wall. No frame. Just a door, standing upright on the grey ground, with nothing on either side of it.

*Floor 97 of 100.*

*Objective: go through the door.*

Stark looked at the door. Then at the empty grey on either side of it.

"Can't we just go round?" he said.

Frieren walked round it. All the way. She came back.

"Round is just round," she said. "It's the same grey. There's no stair on the other side." She looked at the door. "You have to go through."

Mau looked at Armin's sheet. He had a copy of it, folded in his pocket, the way Armin had asked.

*Floor 97. Page 97. A door with no wall.*

Under the drawing in the green book, Frieren had said, there were four lines of writing. And one of the forty-one small words was in them. A word that came up on pages with doors.

They did not know what it meant.

He walked up to the door. He did not touch the ring.

"Gojo looked at this one," he said. "Yesterday. From ninety-six. He said there's something on the other side of it. Not behind it. *Through* it." He looked at the door. "He said it's fast. Faster than Beru. And it's waiting for the door to open, so it can come out before anyone can get through."

"So we don't open it," said Stark.

"We have to open it," said Mau. "The objective's to go through."

He looked at the door for a long time.

Then he turned round.

"Here's the plan," he said.

* * *

Fern looked at him.

Frieren looked at him.

Stark, who had been at the drowned library and the iron wood and the bear and the prison and the furnace, and had never once heard Mau say *here's the plan* before going into anything, looked at him with his mouth slightly open.

"It's fast," said Mau. "Faster than Beru. It's going to come out of the door the moment it opens. Straight at whoever opened it." He looked at the iron ring. "So the one who opens it has to not be there when it comes out."

"How?" said Fern.

"Okarun," said Mau.

Frieren went very still.

"Mau," she said. "Your legs. On forty. You couldn't walk for two days."

"On forty I used it for twenty seconds," said Mau. "Running. Round a whole hive. Away from the food." He looked at the door. "I don't need twenty seconds. I need one." He looked at Frieren. "One step. Open the door. And be one step away before it comes through."

Frieren looked at him for a long time.

"One step," she said.

"One step. Then I let it go. Then you're ready on the left, and Fern's on the right, and Stark's in front of the door with the axe, and whatever comes out, comes out into all three of you instead of into me." He paused. "And Igris behind it. Waiting."

Nobody said anything.

"You told us the plan," said Fern. Very quietly.

"I'm learning," said Mau.

* * *

He reached for the ring.

He put his hand on it. Iron. Cold. He did not turn it yet.

*Ciel. Okarun. One heartbeat. Not twenty.*

*It's there,* said Ciel. *Still rough. Still not yours.* A pause. *But one heartbeat isn't twenty seconds. It'll hurt. It won't tear.* Another pause. *If you let it go the moment you've moved.*

*I'll let it go.*

*I know you will,* said Ciel. *This time.*

He looked at Frieren on the left with her staff up. At Fern on the right, her defensive shell already half-formed. At Stark in front of the door with his axe crosswise and the black plate over his heart under Marin's quilted jacket.

"*Igris*," he said, quietly, and the knight came up out of the grey ground behind the door, on the far side, where nobody could see him from the front.

Mau turned the ring.

He pulled.

And reached.

It went into his legs like fire. One heartbeat. And he was not at the door. He was four paces to the right, behind Fern's half-formed shell, on one knee, with his legs burning from hip to ankle. He let it go. All at once. The way Ciel said. The fire stopped. The burning stayed. It did not tear.

The door was open.

Something came through it.

* * *

Mau never saw it clearly.

Grey. Fast. A blur, low to the ground, like a hound but longer. It came out of the open door where he had been standing a heartbeat before. Its jaws closed on the empty air where his arm had been. It found nothing there.

It found Stark's axe.

Stark met it the way he met everything. He did not dodge. The flat of the axe took its head and it went sideways and down. Frieren's line of light came from the left and Fern's from the right. And then Igris came round the side of the door from the far side, unhurried, and it was over.

The grey thing dissolved into blue light on the grey ground.

The door stood open. Beyond it, through it, not the grey plain. A stair. Going up.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Stark lowered his axe and looked at Mau, on one knee behind Fern's shell, rubbing his legs.

"You were there," he said. "At the door. And then you weren't."

"One step," said Mau.

"It went for where you were."

"I know," said Mau. "That was the plan."

Stark looked at him for a long time.

"Fern," he said, without looking away from Mau. "He told us the plan."

"I know," said Fern.

"Before," said Stark. "He told us *before*."

"I was there," said Fern.

Stark shook his head slowly. He put his axe back on his shoulder.

"That's new," he said.

* * *

Ninety-eight was a bridge, and on it, something waiting. A knight, grey, with no colour at all, like a statue that had learned to stand. It fought, and they beat it, without Mau drawing anything. Igris and Stark and Fern's shell and Frieren's light, and Mau holding the space where it was needed and nowhere else.

Ninety-nine was a stair going up into white.

They stood at the bottom of it at the end of Day 392. It went up and up, straight, stone, into a white light so bright that even Mau's eyes behind their filter could not see where it ended.

*Floor 99 cleared.*

*Level 101. Two hundred and fifty-nine points unspent.*

"A hundred and one," said Fern. She had heard him read it.

"Level."

"Level a hundred and one," said Fern. "On ninety-nine." She looked up the white stair. "And a hundred at the top."

"And a hundred and one after that," said Frieren. Very quietly. "In the book."

Nobody said anything.

They looked up the stair into the white for a long time. Then Mau said "Home," and they went home, through the small frame at the bottom of the stair. Nobody was going to climb the last stair at the end of a long day with tired legs.

On the other side, Anko looked at the watch. She looked at them. She went to the board, to Armin's sheet, to the line *Floor 99. Page 99. A stair going up into white.* She wrote beside it, in her plain hand:

*Seen. Not climbed. Tomorrow.*

Then, at the very bottom of the board, beside *After?*, she wrote one more line.

*One more floor.*
