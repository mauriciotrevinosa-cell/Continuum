# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 34 — The Ruler

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18, DH-124)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 34
**Continuity state:** Floor 100 (Day 393, dawn), the tenth guardian. Gojo goes up the white stair first, alone, and comes back down white-faced: not nothing, a figure of light standing in front of a closed garden. Party: Mau (owner), Gojo, Yuta and Frieren. The notice: "Guardian: the Light of the Hundredth (Ruler)". It is a being of white light, faceless, many-handed in a way the eye can't hold, whose weapon is an invisible hand: force that seizes, crushes and throws from any distance, unlike Momo's psychokinesis (a will, not a kitchen). It treats the shadows as a stain. The Legion breaks against nothing, Kamish is pinned from the sky, and Bellion is thrown. Mau gives the plan aloud. He uses the Legion as a hundred decoys so the hand has too many things to hold, Frieren's Zoltraak against the light, Gojo's marble on the place the hand comes from, and Yuta and the katana. Then, with the eyes (a short unfiltered breath, with Ciel's consent and her count), he sees the shape of the hand itself and puts his own space inside its grip so it closes on nothing. He finishes it with the katana and the pull. Dying, the light speaks: "You carry his door… you should not exist." No extraction ("Not eligible"). Instead: "Fragment acquired: the Ruler's Authority (rough)" — the first piece of another kind of power, an invisible hand of his own, wrong-handed and costly, not a shadow. Injuries: left arm broken where the hand seized it, ribs, bleeding eyes again. The garden opens behind it. Level 101 (the completion notice comes on the second visit)

*When: Day 393, before dawn. The white stair on ninety-nine. Gojo climbed it alone in the dark, because nobody could stop him, and came back down an hour later and did not say "garden". Shoko said "go" after a long look. She did not say anything else.*

Gojo came back down the white stair and did not say anything for a long time.

He sat down on the bottom step. He took his blindfold off and put his face in his hands. Mau had seen him do that once, on the night of the south field. Never since.

"What's up there?" said Yuta.

Gojo did not lift his head.

"A garden," he said. "Behind a wall. A gate in the wall, shut." He breathed. "And in front of the gate, something standing."

"What kind of something?"

Gojo lifted his head.

"Light," he said. "It's made of light. Not like Frieren's. Not like a lamp." He looked at the white stair going up into the white. "I looked at it for a long time. With everything. I couldn't find the edge of it." He was quiet. "And it saw me looking. It didn't move. It just—knew I was there." He put the blindfold back on. "It doesn't like what you are, Mau. I could feel that. It doesn't like you at all."

* * *

Anko wrote it on the board.

*Day 393. Mau, Gojo, Yuta, Frieren. From 90. Floor 100 (guardian). (Gojo looked. Light.)*

She did not write anything else. She did not write *Ori*.

* * *

The white stair went up and up into the light.

It was not hot. It was not cold. It was like climbing into the inside of a cloud at noon, and the light got brighter with every step until Mau could see only the next step, and Frieren's back, and Yuta's shoulder. Then the white thinned.

And they were at the top.

A low stone wall, going out on either side into soft white mist. In the wall, a gate. Wooden. Old. Shut. Beyond it, just visible over the top of the wall, the top of a tree. Tall. Straight. Pale. With something gold moving in its leaves.

And in front of the gate, standing on the grass, the light.

* * *

It was the shape of a person. Roughly. Tall. Taller than Bellion. White. Not white like snow or bone. White the way the sun is white when you look at it for one second too long. It had no face. Where a face would be, there was more light, brighter, and Mau's eyes behind their filter watered and stung and he had to look at its chest instead.

It had hands.

He could not count them. That was the strangest thing. When he looked at it, it had two, held loosely at its sides, the way anyone holds their hands. And when he looked away and back, there were more. Or there were not. Or the hands he saw were not the hands that mattered. The air round it moved the way the air moves round a hot stone. Shapes in the air. Fingers that were not there.

The notice came up.

*Floor 100 of 100.*

*Guardian. Objective: defeat the guardian of the last ten.*

*Guardian: the Light of the Hundredth. (Ruler.)*

"Ruler," said Mau.

The light turned toward him.

He felt it look. Not with eyes. The way Gojo had said. *It knew I was there.* It knew he was there. It looked at him, and then down, at his shadow on the grass, long in the white light, and something in the air round it changed.

Not anger.

Disgust.

*"Shadow,"* it said.

The voice was not a voice. It was the sound of light. High and clear and very cold, like a bell rung in a frozen room, and it came from everywhere at once.

*"You carry shadow up my stair."*

* * *

"Separate," said Mau.

He said it before anything moved. He had learned. On eighty, on ninety, on the bench at the screen. *Say the plan first.*

"Frieren, left. Light against light, if you can find its edge. Gojo, the hands. Watch where they come from. Tell me before. Yuta, with me. If I go down, you don't carry me. You keep it busy." He took a breath. "I'm going to throw the Legion at it."

"All of them?" said Yuta.

"All of them. It's got hands I can't see. Let's give them a hundred things to hold."

* * *

He called them.

The Legion first. The hundred. Up out of the grass in rows, spears level, shields up, the ten in front. Then Igris. Beru. Tusk. Kaisel, into the white air above. Bellion, last, tall, black-violet gone to black, the segmented sword already drawn. Kamish behind them all, rising like a hill.

The cup went. Fifteen times deep, and it went like a river.

The light looked at all of it.

*"A stain,"* it said.

And reached.

* * *

The Legion broke against nothing.

That was what it looked like from where Mau stood. The front rank charged, spears level, the ten at the point, and ten paces from the light they stopped. All of them. As if they had run into a wall. Then the wall closed. Mau saw it close. Not a wall. A hand. A hand the size of the front rank, invisible, closing round thirty soldiers at once like a fist round a bundle of sticks.

It squeezed.

Thirty soldiers came apart.

Mau felt them go. Not lost. Pieces. Thirty holes in him at once, small ones, like teeth pulled.

*Pieces,* said Ciel, very fast. *Not unmade. Pieces. But Mau, thirty—*

"*Gojo,*" said Mau.

"Above it," said Gojo. "The hand came from above it. And to the right. Not from its body. It doesn't come from its body. It comes from—" He stopped. "From a place next to it. A place I can't see into."

*"Shadow,"* said the light again. And reached again.

Kamish.

The old dragon, behind everything, rising into the white air on his great dark wings. The invisible hand took him out of the sky. Mau saw the dragon stop. In mid-beat. Held. A thing the size of a hill, held in the air by nothing. Then the hand turned, the way you turn your wrist, and Kamish went down. Into the grass. Pinned. His wings pressed flat. His cold blue lights blazing.

*"Small king,"* said Kamish, from under the hand. Very calm. *"It is strong."*

"I know."

Bellion went at it. Fast. The sword coming apart into the whip, twenty paces of singing steel, at the light's middle. The light did not move. A hand Mau could not see caught the whip in the air, and pulled, and Bellion went with it, off his feet, across the grass, past the light, into the stone wall beside the gate. The wall cracked.

*"Commander,"* said Bellion, getting up. *"I cannot reach it. It holds the air."*

* * *

Frieren fired.

Not Zoltraak. Something else. A line of white light from her staff, straight at the light's chest. Light against light. It went in. Mau saw it go in, a thin bright line into the whiteness.

And go out the other side. Into the gate.

The light did not move. Did not flinch. Frieren's spell had gone through it as if it were not there. Because it was not there. Not in the place it looked like it was.

"It's not where it's standing," said Frieren. Very calmly. Very fast. "It's standing there. But it isn't *there*. It's—"

"Next to itself," said Gojo. "Where the hands come from."

*"Elf,"* said the light. It turned its faceless brightness toward her. *"You walk with a shadow."*

A hand reached for her.

Mau put the space in front of her. Thick. All of it. The hand hit it.

It did not stop.

It did not go through. It *closed round*. The space was a plate in front of her face, and the hand did not push against the plate. It took hold of it. The whole of it. The way you take hold of a dish. And squeezed.

He felt it in his own arm. In the arm that held the space.

His left arm broke.

* * *

He did not scream. He went down on one knee in the grass with his arm folded wrong across his chest and the space in front of Frieren still there, cracked, held, and the invisible hand still round it, squeezing.

"*Mau*—"

"It's holding the space," he said. Through his teeth. "It's not pushing. It's holding. It's—" He gasped. "It's not a wall. It's a hand. It takes hold of things."

The Legion. Seventy left. Igris and Beru and Tusk. Kaisel overhead. Bellion getting up from the wall. Kamish pinned.

Too many things.

*Too many things to hold.*

"*Legion,*" he said. "*Every one. At it. From every side. Now.*"

* * *

They went.

Seventy soldiers. Not in rows. Not as one thing. Scattered. From every direction at once, across the grass, the way a flock of birds goes when something frightens it. Each one alone. Each one a thing the light would have to take hold of separately.

The light reached.

A hand closed on ten. Ten went. Another hand closed on eight. Eight went. Another. Another. Mau felt them go, one handful after another, holes in him. The light was taking them as fast as they came.

But it was taking them.

Its hands were full.

The hand on the space in front of Frieren loosened.

Not much. A breath. The light had too many things to hold.

"*Now,*" said Mau. "*Gojo. Where they come from.*"

* * *

Gojo's marble went.

Not at the light. Next to it. Above and to the right, at nothing, at the place Gojo said the hands came from, the place he could not see into.

It pulled.

The air next to the light bent. Toward the marble. And for one moment, something was there. Mau saw it. The edge of something. Like the edge of a sheet of glass seen from the side. A place next to the light that was not light and not air.

The light made a sound.

High. Cold. Like a bell cracking.

*Ciel,* said Mau. *The eyes.*

*Mau—*

*Not all of it. Not like forty. A breath. Just to see the hand.*

A pause. Very short.

*Five seconds,* said Ciel. *I'm counting. When I say, it goes back on. You don't argue.*

*I don't argue.*

The filter came off.

* * *

He saw the hand.

Not with eyes. With the other thing. He saw the light, and the thing next to the light, the place it really stood. He saw the hands coming out of that place. Not hands. Threads. Thousands of them. Pale, cold, reaching out of the place next to the light into the garden, wrapping round soldiers, round Kamish, round the space in front of Frieren. A will. Not a pattern from a kitchen. Not a spoon turning in a bowl because someone's hands were full. A will that reached out and *held* things because it had decided they should be held.

He saw where they all came from. One point. Above and to the right.

*Two seconds,* said Ciel.

He saw the hand on the space in front of Frieren. Every thread of it. Where they gripped.

He put his own space inside the grip.

Not in front of it. Not against it. *Inside* it. Between the threads and the thing they held. The way he had put the weight of the sky outside himself on sixty. A skin of nothing, inside the closing fist, so the fist closed on a distance that would never end.

The hand closed.

On nothing.

It kept closing. Forever. It could not get there.

*Back on.*

The filter came down. The world went red at the edges. Blood from his eyes again, not much, warm.

But he had seen it.

* * *

"*Yuta,*" he said.

Yuta was already moving. He had been waiting the whole time, beside Mau, his sword out, Rika's presence round him like weather. He went across the grass. Not at the light. At the place next to it. Where the marble had bent the air. Where Mau had seen the edge.

Mau went with him.

With his right hand. With the pale katana from eighty-six, out of its black scabbard, the one that looked like Yuta's. His left arm folded against his chest, broken. Okarun's speed, one heartbeat, into his legs.

Yuta's sword went into the edge.

Into the place next to the light. And something was there, after all. It went in. Rika's hand came down behind it, enormous, and held the edge open.

Mau's katana went in beside it.

And at the point of the katana, he made the smallest point he could. Inside the place next to the light. Where all the threads came from.

And pulled.

* * *

The light came apart.

From the inside. All the threads at once, pulled back into the point, into the place they had come from, and the place folding in on itself. The white went out of the light. Not dark. Just gone. The way a lamp goes out. One moment it was standing in front of the gate, too bright to look at, and the next it was a shape in the grass, faint, flickering, like the last of a candle.

All over the garden, the hands let go.

Kamish rose off the grass with a sound like a mountain sighing. Bellion straightened. The thirty soldiers in pieces, and the ten, and the eight, and all the handfuls, did not come back; they were in pieces in him, waiting. But the ones still standing stood.

Mau knelt in the grass beside the flickering shape.

It turned its faceless head toward him. Very slowly. The light in it was almost gone.

*"You carry his door,"* it said.

The bell-voice was very quiet now. Cracked.

*"I know the shadow on you. I know whose. We put him to rest. A long time ago. And you carry his door up our stair."* The light flickered. *"You should not exist."*

"Whose?" said Mau.

The light did not answer.

It went out.

* * *

*Operator: trial passed.*

*Guardian of the last ten defeated.*

He waited for the next line. *Shadow extraction available.*

It did not come.

*Not eligible.*

And then, after it, a line he had never seen. Not from underneath, the way the Noise's notices came. From the tower. From the same place as all the others.

*Fragment acquired: the Ruler's Authority. (Rough.)*

He read it.

He did not understand it.

*Ciel.*

*I see it,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *It's not a shadow. It's not a pattern the Noise built from watching.* A pause. *It's a piece of what it had. The hand. The will that holds things. The tower gave you a piece of it.* Another pause. *It's very rough. It isn't yours yet. If you used it now, it would hurt worse than Okarun's legs. It would take from the other reserve and the cup at once.* A pause. *It's not Momo's. Momo's is a spoon turning because her hands are full. This is—deciding that a thing will be held.*

He looked at his right hand. Not his left. His left was broken.

He thought about holding something with nothing.

He closed his hand.

* * *

Behind the place where the light had been, the gate in the wall stood open.

Mau did not remember it opening. It was open. Beyond it, soft green grass, and a tree, tall and straight and pale, with gold fruit in its moving leaves, and a still pool at its foot.

A garden.

Nobody went through.

Frieren knelt in the grass beside him. She looked at his left arm, folded wrong. At the blood at the corners of his eyes. She did not say anything. She took his face in her hands, the way she always did, and held it.

"It said you shouldn't exist," she said.

"I heard."

"It said *we put him to rest*." Her thumbs moved under his eyes, wiping. "The Architect—the book said *watches*. Bellion said a voice through stone. Kamish said *someone up there*." She was quiet. "And this one said *we put him to rest*, and you carry his door."

Mau did not say anything.

Gojo was standing at the open gate, looking at the garden. At the tree. At the still pool. His blindfold was up.

"It's a garden," he said. Very quietly. "Just a garden. Nothing in it. Nothing watching." He turned round. "It was behind the light the whole time."

He looked at Mau, kneeling in the grass with his arm broken and his eyes bleeding and his right hand closed round nothing.

"I'm going to say something," said Gojo. "And you're not going to like it."

"Okay."

"Take Ori," said Gojo. "This afternoon. When Shoko's fixed your arm." He looked back at the garden. "Not because it's safe. It is now. I looked very hard." He was quiet. "Because you've climbed a hundred floors for this summer, and she hasn't seen one single good thing at the top of any of them." He looked at Mau. "Let her see this one."
