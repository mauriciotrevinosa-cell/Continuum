# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 36 — What Anko Wanted

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 36
**Continuity state:** Anko's request resolves (V29 "The True Leaf"; V30 "Something in the Box"). After seventy-eight mornings of Mau's cup, the seedling nobody planted flowers: small and white, the same north-field weed as the sixth flower on the east-room shelf, the one nobody agreed on until two people did. Anko finds out the slow way and says what she wanted. Nazuna was right about the wind. Anko keeps her last cigarette in its tin. No threat

*When: Day 394, just after dawn. Seventy-eight mornings since the true leaf. Mau has a cup of water from the end of Momo's counter. Anko is coming in from the night round with lime on her boots. Nazuna is coming down for breakfast before she sleeps.*

The box was outside Anko's door, on the left, where it had always been.

Mau came round the corner of the corridor with the cup and stopped.

He did not need to look closely. He could see it from the corner. The plant in the box had grown all summer. It had grown from two round leaves like hands held out, to four, to five, to a stem as tall as his hand with a tight green bud at the top. Then taller, and more leaves, and the bud getting rounder every morning when he watered it. He had not told Anko about the bud. She wanted to find out the slow way.

The bud had opened.

* * *

It was white.

Small. The size of a fingernail. Five petals, round, very white, with something faintly gold at the centre. On a thin green stem at the top of the plant, in the middle of the plain wooden box, in the dirt Mau had sifted himself on the day after the true leaf.

He stood at the corner of the corridor with the cup in his hand and looked at it for a long time.

He knew it.

He had carried one in his pocket all night under the ink tree at the festival. He had held it between his palm and Frieren's. He had watched her put it on the shelf in the east room, beside the grimoire with the four old flowers in it and the fifth from the date. A weed from the north field. That had got in with the yarrow by accident. That was not on Kusuri's list. That Maomao said did not mean anything, because nobody had agreed on it.

*It means next year,* Frieren had said. *We agreed on it. Just now.*

The sixth flower.

The same one.

* * *

He did not water it.

He stood at the corner with the cup and waited.

Anko came along the corridor from the yard. Lime on her boots. Her coat over her arm. The whole night in her face, the way it always was at this hour. She saw Mau at the corner with the full cup, not moving. She looked at his face.

Then she looked at the box.

She stopped.

She did not say anything for a long time. She stood in the corridor with her coat over her arm and looked at the small white flower at the top of the plant nobody had planted.

Then she came the rest of the way. She crouched down beside the box, very slowly. Her knees cracked. She looked at the flower.

"Huh," she said.

* * *

The door opened behind her.

Nazuna came out, in her coat, on her way down to breakfast. She saw Anko crouched by the box, and Mau at the corner with the cup, and stopped in the doorway.

"Is someone dead?" she said.

"Something's flowering," said Anko.

Nazuna came out and looked over Anko's shoulder.

"Oh," she said. "That."

"You knew?"

"I didn't *know*," said Nazuna. "I said it was from the north field. The wind. Same place as the fireflies." She looked at the flower. "Same as the one in Mau's pocket at the festival. I said that too. On the first day it came up." She shrugged. "Nobody listened."

"I listened," said Anko. "I didn't want to know yet."

"Well," said Nazuna. "Now you know."

She went off toward the kitchen. At the corner she stopped, and looked back.

"It's nice," she said. Not to anyone in particular. "It's a nice one. Small. It waited a long time." She looked at the flower. "Like the fireflies did. Under the grass. Till someone came out to look."

She went round the corner.

* * *

Anko crouched by the box.

Mau came the rest of the way and crouched down on the other side of it. The way they had crouched on the first morning, when there were two round leaves and nothing else.

"Do you know what it is?" said Anko.

"It's a weed," said Mau. "From the north field. Maomao said. At the festival." He paused. "Kusuri didn't have it on her list. It doesn't mean anything. Nobody agreed on it."

"Nobody agreed on it," said Anko.

"Until Frieren and I did," said Mau. "That night. We said it meant *next year*." He looked at the flower. "There's one on the shelf in the east room. The same as this. It's the sixth."

Anko looked at him.

"The sixth flower," she said.

"The four old ones in the grimoire. The fifth from the date. And this one." He looked at it. "The same kind."

Anko did not say anything for a long time.

* * *

"I asked for an empty box," she said at last.

"I know."

"Because I didn't know what I wanted to grow." She looked at the flower. "Everyone in this house had something growing. Shoko's tobacco. Senku's lamps. Wakana's rooms. Nazuna's fireflies." She paused. "I had the board. The round. The register. All of it watching. Writing down what came." She was quiet. "Nothing of mine was growing. So I asked for an empty box, and somebody to water it, in case I worked it out."

"Did you?"

"No," said Anko. "I never worked it out. Something came on the wind and grew instead." She looked at the white flower. "A weed. That nobody planted. That nobody chose. That waited under the dirt for weeks until it was ready, and came up anyway, and grew all summer while somebody watered it every morning without knowing what it was." She was quiet. "And then flowered. White. On the day after the tower was finished."

Mau did not say anything.

"I know what I wanted now," said Anko.

He looked at her.

She did not look at him. She looked at the flower.

"I've been writing down what comes," she said. "For years. Every road. Every sighting. Every bad thing that came out of the hills and every good thing that came down the road with Hollis." She touched the edge of the box. "Things that came. Whether anyone chose them or not. Whether anyone wanted them or not." She was quiet. "That's what I do. I watch for what comes, and I write it down, and I don't decide whether it's allowed."

"And this?"

"This came," said Anko. "Nobody chose it. Nobody planted it. Nobody agreed on what it meant." She looked at it. "And it's the most beautiful thing in this corridor."

She was quiet for a long time.

"I wanted something that came on its own," she said at last. "And grew. And turned out to be good. Without anybody choosing it." She almost smiled. "Like most of the people in this house."

* * *

Mau poured the cup.

Slowly. Round the edge of the box, not on the flower. The dirt went dark. The small white flower at the top of the stem did not move.

Anko watched him.

"Every morning," she said. "Still. Even now it's flowered."

"Every morning," said Mau.

"Until it stops."

"Until it stops."

Anko nodded.

She stood up. Her knees cracked again. She looked down at the box, at the small white flower. Then she reached into her coat pocket and took out a tin. Small. Scratched. Mau knew what was in it. Everyone did. One cigarette. The last of the nine she had come with. Saved, she had always said, for something worth it.

She looked at the tin for a long time.

She did not open it.

She put it back in her pocket.

"Not this," she said. To the tin. Or to herself. "This isn't the thing worth it." She looked at the flower. "This is better than the thing worth it."

She went to her door.

At the door, she stopped.

"Mau."

"Mm."

"The sixth flower," said Anko. Without turning round. "On your shelf. The one you and Frieren agreed on." She was quiet. "Tell her mine's the same. Tell her there's two now. One that means *next year*." She paused. "And one that means *whatever comes*."

She went in.

Mau stayed crouched by the box for a while. Then he took the empty cup back to the end of Momo's counter, where it would be waiting in the morning.

* * *

He told Frieren at breakfast.

On the back step. With Touka's coffee. He told her about the box, and the flower, and what Anko said.

Frieren listened.

When he finished, she did not say anything for a long time.

Then she got up and went in. He heard her go up the stair. A minute later she came back down with something in her hand.

The sixth flower. From the shelf. Very small, a little crushed, white, from the night under the lanterns.

She went along the corridor to Anko's door. Mau followed. She did not knock. Anko was asleep by now. Frieren knelt down by the box.

She held the sixth flower beside the living one. Close. Not touching.

They were the same. The same five round petals. The same faint gold at the centre. One pressed and dry. One living, in the dirt, at the top of a stem nobody had planted.

Frieren looked at them for a long time.

"*Next year*," she said, very quietly, touching the dry one. Then the living one, very lightly, with one finger. "*Whatever comes*."

She stood up.

She took the dry flower back up to the shelf in the east room. Mau did not go with her. He stood by the box in the corridor and looked at the small white flower. A weed. That nobody chose. Growing in a box outside the door of the woman who had been writing down whatever came for years.

Two meanings now. For the same small weed.

That was how meanings started, Kusuri had said. Somebody agrees.
