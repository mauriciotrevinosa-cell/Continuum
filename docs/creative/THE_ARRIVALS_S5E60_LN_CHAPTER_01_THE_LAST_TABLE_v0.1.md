# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 41 — The Last Table

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 41
**Continuity state:** The last spending session before floor 101 (Day 395; level 106, two hundred and eighty-four unspent). Umaru, Yuka and Armin plan from the *After?* line and from Armin's guess that the cup's size is what makes it refill. One hundred more go into Intelligence (four hundred and forty in total), and the reserve is roughly fifteen times its pre-festival depth. Fifty go into Endurance at last, because Ciel can now see a pattern: wounds since Igris have closed a little faster than Shoko expected. One hundred and thirty-four are kept. No mana, no CE, no Black Heart; registration places stay twenty

*When: Day 395, afternoon. The long table. Umaru has taken down every sheet on all three wall sections and replaced them with one, pinned in the middle of the first wall, in her smallest hand. Armin is at the table with the copy of the tower. Yuka has the copy book. Fern is witnessing.*

The one sheet said everything.

Umaru had written it in the smallest square Ministry hand Mau had ever seen. It took him a while to read it all, standing in front of the first wall section with a cup of Touka's coffee.

*INTELLIGENCE: 240. CUP: ~9x. (Solid.)*

*ENDURANCE: 1. (Something. Broken line.)*

*REFILLS FASTER. (Armin: bigger cup catches more. Broken line.)*

*284.*

And at the bottom, below the box, in larger letters than anything else on the sheet:

*101?*

"One hundred and one," said Umaru, tapping it. "Anko wrote *After?*. You wrote *101*. Frieren saw it. In the mist. At the top." She looked at him. "The Ministry is planning for one hundred and one."

"We don't know what's there."

"We know what's drawn," said Armin, from the table. He had the copy of the tower in front of him, every floor and every page. He turned it so Mau could see the bottom. "A seated stone figure. A ring of statues, each holding something. And one word, pressed so hard the pen nearly went through." He looked at the drawing. "*Watches*."

"And we know what the general said," said Yuka, very quietly. "*You are not yet what it needs.*"

"And we know what the stone at the back of the army did," said Armin. "On eighty. It turned its head when you said *finish it*." He looked up. "Whatever's on a hundred and one has been watching you since eighty at least. Maybe since fifty-six."

Nobody said anything.

* * *

"So here's what the Ministry thinks," said Umaru.

She went to the sheet.

"The cup is nine times. That's a lot. That raised Kamish and Bellion." She tapped *~9x*. "But on a hundred and one, you might need all of them. At once. For a long time." She looked at him. "All eighteen. Kamish and Bellion and Beru and Igris and Kaisel and Lantern and Tank and Corner and the ten. Standing. Together." She paused. "Nine times won't hold that. Not for long."

"How long would it hold?"

Umaru looked at Armin.

Armin had done the arithmetic. Of course he had. It was on the back of the tower sheet, in neat columns, next to the diagram of the cup he had drawn on the day of the fourth wall.

"About a minute," he said. "All of them at once, with the Legion. Maybe a little more." He looked at the columns. "Kamish alone is most of it."

"And with a hundred more points in Intelligence?" said Umaru.

Armin ran his finger down the column.

"Fifteen times," he said. "Roughly. If it still holds." He looked up. "Two minutes, all of them, with the Legion. Or a lot longer with fewer."

"Two and a half minutes," said Umaru. She turned back to the wall. "Two and a half minutes of all of them is a lot more than one."

* * *

"And Endurance," said Yuka.

Everyone looked at her.

"We put one point in," said Yuka. "On the day of the second wall. Ciel said something changed. Too small to see." She looked at the copy book. "We said, *you'll get hit again, and we'll find out.*" She looked at Mau. "You got hit again. A lot."

"I know."

"So did we find out?"

Mau looked at Rimuru, at the end of the bench.

Rimuru had his chin on his hand. He had been listening to Ciel for some time. He lifted his head.

"She says yes," he said. "She says she's been watching since the second wall. Every time you got hurt." He counted on his fingers. "Igris was before the point. The arm and the leg and the ribs. Shoko's times for healing were what she expected. Beru, after the point. The back and the legs. Shoko thought three days in bed. It was two, with Yuta." Another finger. "Kamish. You were carried home and walked the next day." Another. "Bellion. The shoulder and the side. Shoko thought a week. It's been eight days and the shoulder's out of the sling." He looked at Mau. "She says it's small. Very small. But it's there. Every time. Things close a little faster than they should. Since the one point."

Yuka was writing very fast in the copy book.

"So Endurance does something," she said, without looking up. "It's not just a word."

"It does something," said Rimuru. "She says one point did all that. A little. She says fifty would do more. She doesn't know how much more."

Umaru turned to the wall. She took the chalk. She found *ENDURANCE: 1. (Something. Broken line.)* and looked at it for a long time.

Then she drew over the broken line.

Slowly. Carefully. Until it was solid.

"Fifty," she said. "Into Endurance." She turned round. "Because if you're going to get hit on a hundred and one, the Ministry would like you to close faster."

* * *

It took a long time. It always did.

One at a time. Ciel watching. Rimuru repeating. Yuka counting. A hundred into Intelligence first. Around the fortieth, Ichika came to the end of the table in her too-long sash. Around the seventieth, Ori came, with her list, and sat beside Ichika and helped her count on her fingers. Around the ninetieth, Milim came and floated over the table and counted out loud, correctly, all ten of the last ones.

At a hundred, Yuka said "Four hundred and forty," and put her pencil down. Then she picked it up again.

"Endurance," she said.

Mau put the first point into Endurance.

It felt different from Intelligence.

He did not know how he knew. Intelligence had always felt like nothing. A number going up. The cup getting deeper, somewhere he could only feel when he went looking. This was not nothing. This was very small, and it was in him. In his body. Not in the cup. Like stretching after sleeping. Something in the way his body held itself together, tightening, a hair.

*There,* said Ciel. *I can see it this time. Not much. But I can see it.* A pause. *It's in how you mend. Not how hard you are. How fast you close.*

"She can see it," said Rimuru. "This time. She says it's about mending."

Yuka wrote it down.

Mau put in the next. And the next. Forty-nine more.

At fifty, he felt it. Not much. His shoulder, under the last of Shoko's bandage, where Bellion's whip had gone in. It had ached all week, a deep dull ache. The ache was a little less. Not gone. Less.

He moved his arm.

"Oh," he said.

Everyone looked at him.

"My shoulder," said Mau. "It's—" He moved it again. "It hurts less. Than it did a minute ago."

*It's closing,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *Faster. Not instantly. Not like Shoko's hands. But faster than it was.* A pause. *That's fifty points of Endurance, Mau. That's what it does. You'll still get hurt. You'll still bleed. You'll close faster afterwards.*

Rimuru repeated it.

Umaru, at the wall, wrote *ENDURANCE: 51. (Mending. Solid.)* She drew a solid circle round it.

* * *

One hundred and thirty-four.

She wrote it in the box at the bottom, very large, with a solid circle round it, and crossed out *284*.

Below it, *101?*, still there.

She sat down.

She did not ask for milk. She looked at the kitchen door. Taihei was already coming out of it with a glass. Filled exactly to the line.

He put it down in front of her.

"I wasn't going to ask," said Umaru.

"I know," said Taihei. "I brought it anyway."

Umaru looked at the glass for a long moment.

"The Ministry," she said, "acknowledges." And drank it.

* * *

Fern closed the ledger.

She had written the whole afternoon on the day's page. Mau saw it as she closed it.

*Mau: 100 Intelligence, 50 Endurance. Cup ~15x. Mending (Ciel can see it now). 134 kept. Witnessed.*

And under it, smaller:

*The last spending before 101. (Umaru drew the last line solid.)*

Armin was still at the table with the copy of the tower.

He had turned it over. On the back, under the diagram of the cup and the columns of numbers, he had drawn something new while they were counting. A small careful drawing. A box, with a solid line in it. And a second line, across, going up.

"What's that?" said Mau.

"Your cup," said Armin. "Before today. And now." He touched the drawing. "And this, beside it." He touched the line going up. "That's how fast you mend. It's not in the cup. It's in you." He looked at Mau. "Two different things. One for the shadows. One for you."

"Umaru said that on the first day," said Mau. "We don't call two things the same just because a window uses a familiar word."

"Umaru was right," said Armin. He folded the sheet. "She usually is."

From inside her hood, over the empty milk glass, Umaru said, "The Ministry heard that."

"I meant it to," said Armin.
