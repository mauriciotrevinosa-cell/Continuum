## Chapter 41 — Four O'Clock

*When: Day 397, four in the morning. The kitchen. Dark outside. Taihei has had the stove lit since half past three. The bread is in.*

Taihei made breakfast at four.

He had said he would. On the roof, in the draught, with his cup of tea that was a crime against tea. *Four o'clock. For the four of you.* He had set no alarm, because there were no alarms. He had simply woken at three, the way he had woken every morning for nine years in his own world to make his sister breakfast before he went to the office. He lit the stove. He started the bread.

By four, the kitchen was warm and smelled of it.

Mau came down first. Then Gojo, with his blindfold on and his hair flat on one side. Then Yuta, with his katana. Then Frieren, last, with the green book under her arm and her staff, and the cold of the corridor still on her.

They sat at the end of the long table. Taihei put bread in front of them. And eggs. And a pot of something hot and thick with vegetables in it that he said was a soup and Momo, coming down at half past four, said was a stew and then ate two bowls of.

Nobody talked much.

They ate.

* * *

Touka came down at a quarter to five.

She came into the kitchen in her apron and looked at the four of them at the end of the long table, eating Taihei's bread in the lamplight. She did not say anything. She went to the stove and made coffee. Properly. The way she had made it every morning on the back step since the day after she arrived.

She brought four cups.

She put one down in front of each of them. Mau. Gojo. Yuta. Frieren.

"I'm not grateful," she said.

"You're open early," said Mau.

"I'm open *very* early," said Touka. She looked at the four of them. At the katana at Mau's hip, in its black scabbard, tied with Maki's cord. "Drink it. All of it. It's the best I've ever made."

"How do you know?"

"Because I made it for people going somewhere," said Touka. "It's always better when it's for people going somewhere." She folded her arms. "In my world, I made coffee for a lot of people going somewhere. Some of them came back." She looked at Mau. "The ones who drank all of it came back more often."

"Is that true?"

"No," said Touka. "But drink it anyway."

They drank it. All of it.

* * *

Ori came down at five.

She came down the stair in her coat, with her shoes on, and her list in her hand. She had not been told to come down at five. She had not been told not to. She came down the stair and across the long room to the end of the table, where the four of them were finishing Touka's coffee, and stood beside Mau's chair.

She did not say anything for a moment.

Then she held out the list.

*SHADOWS (NAMED).*

Folded. Small. Into a square that would fit in a pocket.

Mau looked at it.

"It's all of them," said Ori. "Every one. Ear and Lantern and Roof and Igris and Corner and Tank and Beru and Kaisel and Tusk and Kamish and Bellion." She held it out further. "And the soldiers. I didn't write their names because they don't have names. I wrote *ten*. And *they don't break*."

"Ori—"

"Take it," said Ori. "Up there. To a hundred and one." She looked at him. "Frieren says there's a thing up there that watches. That's been watching you all summer." She swallowed. "I thought, if it's watching, it should know they have names. All of them. That someone named them." She paused. "So they know who they are. If it tries to make them forget."

Mau looked at the folded list for a long time.

Then he took it.

He put it in the inside pocket of his coat, on the left. Where the warm coin had been, for nearly a year, until it went to Kaneki. Beside Mai's bullet, which was still there, cold.

Folded list. Cold bullet.

"Thank you," he said.

Ori nodded.

She did not cry. She looked at him very seriously, and then at Frieren, and then she said the thing she had clearly been practising on the stair.

"Back for dinner. Taihei's making curry. Even though it's not his day."

"Back for dinner," said Mau.

* * *

The house came to the screen.

Not everyone. Not a crowd. Fern had said so the night before, at the long table, and the house had listened. But enough.

Fern, with the ledger. Stark behind her, in Marin's quilted jacket with the black plate over his heart. Anko, at the board, with the stub of pencil. Maki, with her spear and the silver flower, standing beside Yuta, very close, not touching. Shoko, with her kit, which she did not usually bring to the screen, open on the dry ground beside her. Maomao, with hers. Touka, with Ichika on her hip in the too-long sash. Kaneki behind her, awake past his shift.

And at the back of the house, on the back step, Rimuru. Sitting with his chin on his knees, very calm, the way a person is calm when they have decided to be. At the corner of the house, Diablo, standing very straight, pretending not to watch him.

Ori stood beside Frieren and held her hand until the very last moment.

Anko looked at the frame.

* * *

The frame had changed.

It had been a dark doorway all summer. A frame in the dry ground beside the relocated gate, with the key's pattern round its edge, and through it, depending on where you started, the first street or the garden on five or the drowned library or the furnace on seventy. Every floor anyone had ever started from. Every floor secured.

This morning there was a number above it.

Carved. Or burned. Into the top of the frame, where nothing had ever been carved. A mark. Not one of the numbers the tower used on its floors. The mark from the green book. The mark at the top of page 101, after the hook that meant *floor*. The one that was not a number at all.

Frieren looked at it for a long time.

"That's it," she said. "The mark. From the last page." She looked at Mau. "It wasn't there yesterday."

"It's there today," said Anko.

She looked at it. Then at the board. At four planks, a hundred floors. *COMPLETE.* And under it, small, still not crossed out, *After?* And under that, in Mau's hand, *101*.

She took the stub of pencil from behind her ear.

She wrote the line.

*Day 397. Mau, Frieren, Gojo, Yuta. From the frame. 101.*

She looked at it. She did not write a number of hours. Nobody knew how long.

Then she looked at Mau.

"Write it down," she said. "Everything. When you come back."

"I'll tell you everything."

"I didn't say tell me," said Anko. "I said write it down. On the board. Yourself." She held out the stub of pencil. "So it's here. In your hand. For whoever comes after."

Mau looked at the pencil.

He took it. He put it in his inside pocket, with Ori's list and Mai's bullet.

* * *

Shoko did not say *two minutes*. She did not say *be careful*. She looked at the four of them at the frame for a long time, with her kit open on the ground beside her.

Then she looked at Gojo.

Gojo looked back at her. He pushed his blindfold up.

Neither of them said anything.

Shoko took something out of her coat pocket. Small. Bent. White. One cigarette. Unlit. Intact. The last one from home. She had carried it since the day she arrived. She had decided, on the morning of the leaves, to keep it forever.

She held it out to him.

"Hold this," she said. "For me. Up there."

Gojo looked at it.

"It's yours," he said. "The last one from home."

"I know what it is," said Shoko. "I want it to come back. So you have to bring it back." She held it out further. "That's all. I'm not saying anything else."

Gojo took the cigarette.

He looked at it in his palm for a long time. Then he put it in his breast pocket, very carefully, the way you put something away that you have been trusted with.

"I'll bring it back," he said.

"I know you will," said Shoko. "I'm saying it so it's on record."

* * *

Fern stood in front of Mau.

She did not say *be careful* either. She looked at him the way she looked at the ledger when the numbers were right.

"Back for dinner," she said.

"Ori said."

"I'm saying it too," said Fern. "Ori says it because she's a child and she's frightened. I'm saying it because I'm the one who writes down whether people came back." She looked at Frieren beside him. "Mistress Frieren."

"Fern."

"Back for dinner."

"Back for dinner," said Frieren.

Fern stepped back.

* * *

They went to the frame.

Mau first. He was the owner. Then Frieren, with her staff and the green book. Then Yuta, with his katana. Then Gojo, last, with his blindfold up and a bent cigarette in his breast pocket.

At the frame, Mau stopped.

He looked back.

At the house, on the dry ground behind the screen. Fern, and Stark, and Anko at the board, and Maki beside Yuta's empty place. Shoko and Maomao with their kits. Touka, with Ichika on her hip, saluting the wrong way round. Kaneki. And Ori, standing alone now that Frieren had let go of her hand, very straight, in her coat with the top button done up, not crying.

Further back, on the back step of the house, Rimuru, with his chin on his knees.

*I'm here,* said Ciel. Inside. *Both ends. I'll tell him everything.*

*I know.*

*I'll tell you everything too.*

*I know.*

He looked at the mark at the top of the frame. The mark from the last page of a book someone had left on a shelf on fifteen, a long time ago, for whoever came after.

"Here's the plan," he said.

The three of them looked at him.

"I don't know what's through there," said Mau. "So the plan is: we go through. We look. We don't touch anything until Frieren's read the walls." He looked at Frieren. "And then I'll tell you the rest. Before."

"Before," said Yuta.

"Before," said Mau.

Gojo almost smiled.

"Second-year," he said.

They went through.
