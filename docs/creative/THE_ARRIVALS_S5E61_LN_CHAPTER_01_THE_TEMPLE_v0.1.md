# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 40 — The Temple

*When: Day 397, morning outside. Inside, there is no time of day. There is light, grey and even, from nowhere.*

The frame closed behind them.

Mau felt it close. Not heard. Felt. The way you feel a door shut in a house when you are in another room. Air moving that should not. He turned round.

There was no frame.

There was a wall. Stone. Grey. Smooth. Where the frame had been, a heartbeat ago, there was a wall, and nothing in it, and no small door with home faintly visible through it.

Gojo had his blindfold up. He was looking at the wall with his bare eyes.

"Gone," he said. "Not hidden. Gone."

*Ciel,* said Mau.

*I'm here,* said Ciel. At once. *The thread to Rimuru holds. He can hear me. I can hear him.* A pause. *He says the frame on his side is still there. With the mark on it. Dark. He says nobody can go through it now from that side either.*

*So we're shut in.*

*You're shut in,* said Ciel. *But you're not cut off. I'm still here. He's still there. I'll tell him everything.* A pause. *He says, tell Mau: back for dinner.*

Mau almost laughed.

"Rimuru says back for dinner," he said aloud.

"Everyone says back for dinner," said Gojo. He looked at the wall where the frame had been. "Nobody says *and here's the door*."

* * *

They turned round.

It was a temple.

Mau did not know the word until Frieren said it. He had never seen one. There had been a shrine in Gojo's school, Gojo said once; there had been churches in Himmel's world. Mau had never stood in one. He stood in one now.

It was enormous. A great hall of grey stone, so long the far end was dim, so tall the roof was lost in shadow. Pillars. Rows of them, thicker than the oldest trees in the low woods, going up into the dark. A floor of grey stone tiles, each one the size of a room, perfectly smooth, perfectly level, with not a speck of dust on any of them.

And at the far end of the hall, on a dais of a hundred steps, a throne.

Stone. Huge. Higher than the house. And on it, seated, a figure.

Stone too. Robed. Hands resting on the arms of the throne. Head slightly bowed. Inside the deep hood, where a face should be, smooth stone. No eyes. No mouth. Nothing.

It was looking at them anyway.

Mau could feel it. The way he had felt the hooded figure at the back of the army on eighty. The way he had felt the statues on fifty-six and eighty-eight turn their heads. A gaze, without eyes. From the far end of the hall. On him.

Only on him.

* * *

"It's the drawing," said Frieren. Very quietly. "Page one hundred and one."

She had the green book open in one hand. She held it up. The drawing that filled the whole last page. A seated stone figure on a throne. And all round it, in a ring, standing figures.

Mau looked from the book to the hall.

The standing figures were there.

A ring of them, on the floor of the hall, at the foot of the hundred steps, round the dais. Twelve. Mau counted. Statues. Tall. Taller than Igris. Robed and hooded like the seated one, with smooth stone faces. Standing very still, evenly spaced, facing outward, like a guard.

Each one held something.

Frieren looked from the book to the ring. She went round it with her eyes, one statue at a time, the way she went round a page.

"A lamp," she said. "A bell. A star, on a staff." Her eyes moved. "A scale. A key. A coil of rope." She went on. "A spear. A cup. A book. A mask." She was quiet. "A flower. And a coin."

"They're the same," said Yuta. "As the drawing."

"They're the same," said Frieren.

* * *

The notice came up.

Not in the middle of Mau's sight. Not at the edge. It came up the way the Noise's notice had come at the top of a hundred. From underneath. And at the same time, from everywhere. As if the hall itself were writing it.

*Floor 101 of 100.*

He read it twice.

*One hundred and one of one hundred.*

*Objective: ———*

The objective was not a word. It was a line of marks. In a script he did not know. Long. Very long. Longer than any objective he had ever seen.

"I can't read it," he said.

He described it. Frieren listened. When he got to the third mark in the line, she went very still.

"That's one of ours," she said. "One of the forty-one."

"Which?"

"I don't know what it means," said Frieren. "Only that it's common." She looked at the walls.

And Mau looked at the walls too. For the first time properly.

* * *

They were covered.

Every wall. Every pillar. From the floor to as high as he could see. Carved into the grey stone, in long neat rows, small and close and perfect. The same script. The green book's script. The tower's script. The writing on the frame and in the notice and on page 101, pressed so hard the pen had nearly gone through.

There was a great deal of it.

"It's a book," said Frieren. She was looking at the nearest pillar. At the rows of marks going up and up. "The whole hall. It's a book. Written on the walls."

Gojo was looking at the walls with his bare eyes.

"It goes all the way up," he said. "Into the dark. Past where I can see." He was quiet. "There's more writing in this room than in every library I've ever been in."

"Can you read any of it?" said Yuta.

Gojo shook his head.

"Numbers," he said. "Some. I can see numbers. Like on the floors." He looked at the pillar. "The rest, no."

* * *

Frieren walked to the nearest pillar.

She did not touch it. She stood in front of it with the green book open in one hand and Fern's ledger slip in the other. The three columns. Forty-one small words. One that meant *watches*. She looked from the slip to the pillar, and back. She was looking for the ones she knew.

Mau watched her.

He had watched her read all summer. In the chair he said no to. At the river with Fern. At the long table with three columns. He had never seen her read like this. Fast. Not word by word. Shape by shape. Her eyes going up the pillar and down, and across, and back to the slip, and up again. Her lips moving very slightly.

"There," she said.

She did not point. She did not touch. She looked at a place on the pillar, at the height of her shoulder.

"The hook," she said. "That means *floor*. And after it, a number." She looked closer. "Seven."

Mau went to stand beside her.

"Seven was the arcade," he said. "The lamps. Near end first."

"I know," said Frieren. "And there's a drawing. Look. Beside it. Carved very small. Like the margin of the book." She looked. "A lamp."

Mau looked.

Beside the hook and the number, carved into the stone, a tiny drawing. A lamp. The same as the margin of page seven in the green book.

He looked across the hall. At the ring of statues round the dais.

The first one, nearest them, on the left, held a lamp.

* * *

Frieren went to the next pillar.

She found it faster this time. The hook. A number. Nine. And beside it, carved small, a bell.

The second statue in the ring held a bell.

The next pillar. *Floor*. Nineteen. A star.

The third statue held a star on a staff.

Nobody said anything.

Frieren went round. Pillar to pillar. Mau followed her. Gojo and Yuta stood in the middle of the hall, back to back, watching the ring of statues. Not one of them moved.

*Floor*. Ten. A scale.

*Floor*. Sixty. A key.

*Floor*. Forty-nine. A coil of rope.

*Floor*. Twenty. A spear.

*Floor*. Seventy. A cup.

*Floor*. Fifteen. A book.

*Floor*. Eighty. A mask.

*Floor*. Five. A flower.

And the last. The twelfth. On the last pillar, near the foot of the hundred steps. The hook. And after it, not a number. The mark from the frame. From the last page. One hundred and one.

And beside it, carved small, a coin.

* * *

Frieren stood in front of the last pillar for a long time.

"Each one is a floor," she said at last. Very quietly. "Each statue. Each object. Each floor we climbed." She looked at the ring. "The lamp is seven. Near end first. Never dark on both sides." She looked at the second. "The bell is nine. Stand where a teacher stands. Out of the way. Let them go home." The third. "The star is nineteen. Find the one that doesn't move."

"The scale is ten," said Mau. He was looking at it. "The guardian. When I didn't keep it. *I'd rather have a little left.*"

"The key is sixty," said Frieren. "The Warden. When you chose not to keep the thing that locked people in."

"The rope is forty-nine," said Mau. "The cloud stair. Yuka. *Throw at the thing that won't let go.*"

"The spear is twenty," said Frieren. "Igris. Maki's spear. Inside the reach."

"The cup is seventy," said Gojo, from the middle of the hall. He had been listening. "The hour. In Kamish's hall. Waiting for the cup to fill."

"The book is fifteen," said Frieren. "Where Fern found this." She held up the green book.

"The mask is eighty," said Yuta. "The stone at the back of the army. That watched."

"The flower is five," said Mau. Very quietly. "The garden. Where I couldn't breathe. And then could."

They all looked at the twelfth statue.

The coin.

"And the coin is a hundred and one," said Frieren. "Here." She looked at it. "I don't know what the coin means."

Mau put his hand inside his coat. On the left. Where the warm coin had been for nearly a year. Ori's folded list was there now. Anko's stub of pencil. Mai's bullet, cold.

No coin.

"I gave mine away," he said.

* * *

Nobody moved.

The ring of statues stood round the dais, facing out, each holding its object. The seated figure on the throne at the top of the hundred steps, with its head slightly bowed, looked at Mau without eyes.

"It's a test," said Gojo. "Isn't it."

"It's every test," said Frieren. "Every lesson. Every floor we learned something on." She looked at the walls. At the rows of writing going up into the dark. "Somebody built a tower to teach someone a hundred things. And then built this to see if they'd learned them."

"Who?" said Yuta.

Frieren looked up the hundred steps at the seated figure.

She did not answer.

* * *

Mau looked at the hall.

At the twelve statues, each a floor. At the walls, covered in a script he could read twelve words of and a woman beside him could read forty-one. At the seated figure at the top of the steps. It had been watching him since fifty-six at least. Since eighty for certain. It had told a general he was not yet what it needed.

He took a breath.

"Here's the plan," he said.

The three of them looked at him.

"We don't touch anything," said Mau. "We don't go near the ring. Not yet." He looked at Frieren. "You read the walls. As much as you can. Every word we know. Everything that's near a drawing." He looked at Gojo. "You watch the statues. If one of them moves, even a finger, say so. Before it does anything else."

"Before," said Gojo.

"Before," said Mau. He looked at Yuta. "You watch me."

Yuta looked at him.

"If I draw the sword," said Mau, "and something comes out with it, I want you to tell me what it's doing. Whether it's looking at you. Whether it's coming apart. Anything." He paused. "And if the wheel turns, you tell me how many times."

Yuta did not say anything for a long moment.

"I'll tell you," he said.

"And me?" said Mau. He answered himself. "I'm going to stand here. In the middle. Where it can see me." He looked up the hundred steps. "Because it's been watching me all summer. And it built all this for me to walk into." He was quiet. "I want it to see me walk in and not touch anything until I'm ready."

Nobody said anything.

Then Gojo, from behind him, very quietly:

"Second-year."

"Third," said Mau.

Gojo was silent for a moment.

"Yeah," he said. "Maybe third."

* * *

Frieren read.

She went from pillar to pillar, and along the walls, slowly, with the green book in one hand and Fern's slip in the other, her lips moving. She found the small words. She found the hook that meant *floor* and the mark that meant *watches*. She found the drawings carved small beside them. She read for a long time.

Gojo watched the ring. Twelve statues. Not one of them moved.

Yuta watched Mau.

Mau stood in the middle of the hall, on a grey stone tile the size of a room. He looked up the hundred steps at the seated figure, and let it look at him.

Inside his coat, on the left, a folded list. *SHADOWS (NAMED)*. So they knew who they were.

He did not take it out.

He waited.
