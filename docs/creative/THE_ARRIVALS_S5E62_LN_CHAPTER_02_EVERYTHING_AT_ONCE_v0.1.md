## Chapter 45 — Everything at Once

*When: Day 397. Floor 101. Four islands of stone in a sea of grey nothing. Twelve statues on twelve more, standing for the Architect. The Architect at the foot of the hundred steps, hands folded, watching.*

They came up out of the stone all at once.

All of them. Every name on Ori's list. Mau felt the cup tip, the way he had felt it tip on the scale, and pour, and pour.

Lantern first, wings unfolding out of the shadow that lay across the nothing, rising into the grey air of the hall. Kaisel beside him, smaller, lean, fast. Igris, on Frieren's island, a pace behind her and to her left, where he always stood now. Beru on Mau's own island, a pace behind him and to his right. Tank, huge and dark, on nothing, and then on stone, because he had come up where a statue's island met the grey and found footing. Corner, the first of them all, with his long hooked weapon, on the edge of Yuta's island. Bellion, tall, black-violet gone to black, with his segmented sword already drawn. The ten soldiers, in a line, shields locked, across three islands at once.

And Kamish.

The old dragon came up out of the nothing itself. Not out of stone. Out of the grey sea below the islands. He rose and rose and kept rising, the vast dark body uncoiling, the wings unfolding, the long neck lifting. Two great cold blue lights opened higher than the pillars.

*Two and a half minutes,* said Ciel. Very fast. *All of them. Two and a half. Then you're empty. Ration them. Send them back when they're done. Don't wait.*

*I know.*

*Armin did the arithmetic,* said Ciel. *I'm counting.*

* * *

"*Kamish*," said Mau. "*Lie down.*"

The old dragon turned its great head and looked at him.

"*Across*," said Mau. "*Between the islands. Be the floor.*"

Kamish was silent for one heartbeat.

Then it lowered itself.

The whole vast dark body came down out of the air and laid itself across the sea of nothing. From the island where Frieren stood to the island where Yuta stood, and past them, curving, to two of the statues' islands. A road. A bridge. Its back as wide as the long room, black scales with no shine, solid enough to stand on.

"*Small king*," said Kamish. Deep. Very quiet. Approving. "*I have kept watch at the top of a stair. I can be a floor.*"

"*Tank*," said Mau. "*On him. Hold the middle.*"

The great bear went up onto the dragon's back and planted himself there, in the middle of the dark road, between the islands.

The statues came.

* * *

Mau climbed onto Kaisel.

He did not decide to. He had thought about it on the roof the night before, lying awake after Frieren's cold foot. *If I'm on the ground, I see the floor. If I'm up, I see the field.* Armin with his maps. Anko with her board. Bellion on ninety, standing in the middle of his field, seeing all of it.

Kaisel knelt on the island. He climbed on. The wyvern went up.

Not high. A little higher than the pillars' first carvings. High enough.

And he saw it.

The whole hall, from above. The way he had seen the house from above on the seventh night, every window lit. The four islands. The twelve. The dark road of Kamish's back between them. The statues on their islands, each one moving toward its target. The Architect at the foot of the hundred steps, very still, its pale face turned up to him.

He saw where everything was in relation to everything else.

*Like a map,* he thought. *Like Armin's.*

"*Frieren*," he called down. "*Lamp, your left. It's lighting the far end.*"

* * *

The first statue held its lamp high. It burned gold, at the far end of the hall, where the frame had been. The light did not reach the islands. The islands were going dark. Not all at once. From the near end. The near end first.

*It's doing it backwards,* thought Mau. *It's darkening from where we stand.*

"*Near end first*," he called. "*It's taking the near end. Give it back.*"

Frieren lifted her hand.

Her wonky lights came out. Small. Uneven. Not at the far end. Here. At her own island. Then the next, a pace further out over the nothing, hanging in the grey air. Then the next. Lighting the near end first, outward, toward the lamp-statue, one after another, never dark on both sides.

The lamp-statue came at her along Kamish's back with its lamp held like a club.

Igris met it.

He did not hurry. He stepped off Frieren's island onto the dragon's back, in her lights, and the long sword went round once, flat. The lamp went one way and the statue went the other. Off the edge of the dark road, into the grey nothing, falling, falling, and gone.

"*Lamp's out*," said Gojo. "*The thread went slack. One.*"

"*Igris, back*," said Mau. The knight sank into the dragon's back. The thread in the cup eased. A little.

* * *

"*Bell*," said Gojo.

The second statue rang its bell.

And out of the walls, all round the hall, from between the carved rows, came the stone children. Walking. Heads down. Toward the far wall. And the dark road of Kamish's back was in the way, and the soldiers' line was across it.

"*Soldiers*," called Mau. "*Stand aside. Out of the way. Let them go home.*"

The ten soldiers did not understand. He felt them not understand. They were soldiers. They held lines. That was all they knew.

"*Bellion*," said Mau.

The Grand Marshal, on the far island, turned his crowned helm.

"*My liege.*"

"Tell them," said Mau. "Stand where a teacher stands. In the doorway. Out of the way."

Bellion did not ask what a teacher was. He lifted his sword. He spoke one word to the soldiers, in a voice that carried across the whole hall without being raised. Mau did not know the word. The soldiers did.

They broke the line. Smoothly. In order. Five to the left edge of the dragon's back and five to the right. Shields up, spears upright. Out of the way.

The stone children walked down the middle of the road between them. Heads down. Not looking up. One of them bumped a soldier's knee, very lightly, and did not stop.

They reached the far wall. They went into it. They were gone.

The bell-statue stood on its island with its bell, ringing, ringing, and nothing came. Nothing walked. Nobody was in its way.

"*Bellion*," said Mau.

The whip came apart. Twenty paces of segmented steel uncoiled across the nothing, wrapped round the bell-statue's ankles, and *pulled*. The statue went off its island into the grey and was gone.

"*Two*," said Gojo.

"*Soldiers, back. Bellion, stay.*"

The ten sank into the dragon's back. Two of them did not sink. They had been at the edge, nearest the bell-statue, and somewhere in the moment of the pull a stone hand had caught them. They came apart into pieces of dark that fell into the nothing and did not come back to the cup.

*Two soldiers,* said Ciel. *Not in pieces. Unmade.* A pause. *The nothing took them. I'm sorry.*

Mau did not let himself stop.

* * *

"*Star*," said Gojo. "*It's lifting the—*"

The hall began to turn.

The way it had turned on the first test. The islands sliding. The pillars sweeping past. Kamish's dark road curving round. The statues on their islands going round. Everything turning, faster and faster, and the sea of nothing turning with it.

Kaisel faltered in the air.

Mau held on.

*The one that doesn't move.*

He looked down at the turning hall from Kaisel's back. At everything going round. The islands. The pillars. The statues. The four of them.

At the foot of the hundred steps, the Architect.

It did not move.

Everything in the hall swept round it. It stood at the foot of the steps with its long hands folded and its pale face turned up to Mau, perfectly still, while the whole hall turned.

The throne had been north. Now the throne was empty. Now the Architect was north.

"*There*," said Mau. He pointed down. At the Architect. "That's the one that doesn't move."

The hall stopped.

The star-statue, on its island, lowered its staff.

The Architect smiled.

"*Yes*," it said. Very softly. Delighted. "*Yes. You remember.*"

"*Beru*," said Mau. "*The star.*"

Beru went. Off Mau's island, onto the dragon's back, along it, a blur of black, and across the gap between the road and the star-statue's island in one leap. Claws. The star-statue went into the nothing.

"*Three*," said Gojo.

* * *

It went faster after that.

He could not have said how. It was the way the long table went quiet in a wave from one end to the other. Once three statues had gone, the others began to go faster, because he could see the field now, from above. Where everything was in relation to everything else. Which statue was turning toward which island, which shadow was nearest, which lesson the statue knew.

"*Scale. Your right, Yuta.*" The fourth statue held out its scale toward Yuta. Yuta looked at it and did not pour anything into it. He did not have a cup to pour. He went inside its reach instead, along Kamish's back, with his sword. "*Keys*." The fifth statue shook its keys, and stone bars began to grow out of the edges of Frieren's island, reaching for her. "*Corner*." The old sentry, the first shadow, the one that had guarded a street corner on floor one, hooked the bars with his long weapon and tore them away, one after another, guarding the corner of Frieren's island the way he had guarded the corner of the street.

"*Four*," said Gojo, as Yuta's sword went through the scale-bearer.

"*Five*," as Bellion's whip took the key-bearer off its island.

"*Rope*," said Gojo, "*it's throwing*—"

The sixth statue threw its rope. Not at anyone's ankle. At Frieren. At her waist. The coil went out across the nothing, weighted, and wrapped round her, and pulled.

She went off her island.

* * *

Mau did not think.

He was on Kaisel, above, too far. He had one heartbeat.

He reached into the bag.

Not Okarun. Too far for one step. Momo. The kitchen. The kettle lifting itself off the hook. Rough, and wrong-handed, and not his.

He caught her.

Not with his hands. With the thing he had watched in the kitchen for years. It felt like writing with the wrong hand. Like a voice that was not his coming out of his mouth. Frieren stopped in the air over the nothing, a body-length below the edge of her island, with the rope round her waist pulling her down and something invisible holding her up.

His head split.

"*Igris*," he said.

The knight came up out of Kamish's back at the edge of the road and cut the rope.

Frieren hung in the air, held by Momo's hands that were not Momo's. Then she did the thing she had always been able to do, the thing he had forgotten in the heartbeat of panic. She floated. Her own power. She rose back up to her island under her own power, slowly, the way she came down out of anything, and landed.

Mau let Momo's pattern go.

His head kept splitting.

*That cost the other reserve,* said Ciel. *A lot. For one heartbeat.* A pause. *She can float, Mau.*

*I know. I forgot.*

*I know you forgot,* said Ciel. Gently. *Everybody forgets things when the person they love goes off an edge.*

"*Six*," said Gojo. Lantern had come down out of the air on the rope-statue and taken it into the nothing in its jaws.

* * *

Six left. And the cup was going.

Mau could feel it. Fifteen times deep, and the floor of it coming up. Eighteen shadows standing at once, sixteen now, for a minute and a half.

*One minute,* said Ciel. *Less.*

"*Tank, back. Corner, back. Lantern, back.*" They sank. The thread eased. Not enough.

"*Cup*," said Gojo. "*Book. Mask.*"

Three statues. On three islands, close together, on the far side of Kamish's road. The cup-bearer, holding out its bowl, and the hall round it beginning to *wait*. Mau could feel it. A heaviness. A slowness, as if time itself were filling a cup. The book-bearer, with its stone book open, and the carved lines on the walls round it beginning to glow. And the mask-bearer, between them, with its blank stone mask held against its chest.

Its head turned toward Mau.

"*The mask is the one with the thread*," said Gojo. Very fast. His bare eyes were on it. "*Not like the others. His. The Architect's. It goes straight up. He's looking through it.*" He was quiet for half a heartbeat. "*And the other two are tied to it. The cup and the book. All three threads go through the mask.*"

Mau looked at the three statues. Close together. On three islands. Tied.

He looked at Gojo, on his island, with his hand already coming up. Two fingers. A small blue marble at the end of them.

*Gojo's Blue pulls toward a point.*

*So does mine.*

"*Gojo*," he said. "*The mask. Your marble. Now.*"

Gojo flicked it.

The small blue point went across the nothing, into the middle of the three statues. Between them. At the mask.

And Mau reached for the pull.

Not the bag. His own. The small one, the one he had used on twenty against Igris, on forty against Beru. The space turned inside out. He put it at the same point. Exactly. Where Gojo's marble was.

Two pulls.

Meeting.

* * *

He did not know what would happen. Nobody did. He had never seen two of them meet.

It was not a marble any more.

It was bigger. Not much bigger. A fist, maybe. Two. But it *pulled*. Gojo's marble pulled a hand's breadth; his own pulled a hair. Together they pulled everything. The air of the hall rushed toward the point, the grey light bent round it, the carved lines on the nearest pillar smeared toward it like ink in water. And the three statues, the cup and the book and the mask, on their three islands, came off them.

Toward the point. Toward each other. Together.

They met in the middle of the double Blue with a sound like a building coming down.

Stone on stone on stone. Crushed into each other. The cup. The book. The mask. One piece. Then less than one. Then nothing. Falling, a single broken lump, into the grey sea.

The double Blue went out.

The hall was silent.

On his island, Gojo was staring at his own hand. His bare eyes were very wide.

"*Seven*," he said. "*Eight. Nine*." He lowered his hand. "*All three threads. Slack. At once.*" He looked up at Mau on Kaisel's back. "*What was that?*"

"Your Blue," said Mau. "And mine. In the same place."

Gojo stared at him.

"Two Blues," he said. "In the same place." He looked at his hand again. "Nobody's ever—" He stopped. "There's never been two. In my world. There was only ever me."

"There's two now," said Mau.

Gojo did not say anything for a moment.

Then something happened to his face that Mau had never seen. He laughed. Not the startled one. Not the one from the roof. Something underneath both. As if a door had opened somewhere very deep in him, one he had thought was locked for good.

"*Two*," he said. To nobody. "*There's two.*"

* * *

"*Flower*," said Yuta.

The eleventh statue had turned toward Mau.

Toward Kaisel, in the air. It held up its empty hand. The hand that had held the white and blue flowers, that he had taken and put in his coat, beside the list.

It held up its empty hand to him. And something in the hall changed.

Frost.

On the edges of the islands. White. Creeping. On the pillars. On Kamish's dark back. On Kaisel's wings. The cold coming up out of the nothing, and with it, at the corners of his sight, flowers. White and blue. Growing out of the stone. Out of the air. The first clearing, coming up round him in the grey hall of a tower, with a seam—

He felt his chest close.

"*Mau*." Frieren. From below. "*Count it.*"

He put his hand inside his coat.

On the left. He took out the spray of white and blue flowers the statue had given him. He held them up. In the frost. In the grey hall.

*One part. Not the whole thing.*

"I've already counted this one," he said. Aloud. To the statue. To the frost. "I took it from you. I held it. It's in my pocket. With the list." He looked at the white and blue flowers in his hand. "It's just flowers."

The frost stopped.

It did not go away. It stopped spreading. It stayed where it was, white at the edges, and did not come any further.

"*Beru*," said Mau.

The ant-man went. The flower-statue went into the nothing. The frost melted off the edges of the islands like breath off glass.

"*Ten*," said Gojo.

* * *

"*Spear*," said Yuta. "*Mine*."

He was already moving.

The seventh statue had come along the far end of Kamish's road with its spear levelled at Yuta's island. Yuta went to meet it. Along the dragon's back. Inside the reach. The stone point went past his shoulder. His sword went into the place under the statue's arm where Maki's spear had gone on ten, and Mau's on twenty.

"*Eleven*," said Gojo.

* * *

The twelfth statue stood alone.

On its island, at the foot of the hundred steps, beside the Architect. Holding out its empty hand.

The coin.

It did not move toward anyone. It stood with its empty palm held out. Toward Mau.

"*My King*," said Beru. On Mau's island. Very low. "*That one. It asks for the coin.*"

"I know."

"*My King gave his away*," said Beru. "*The little lady with the list told me. To the coffee man. For his wife and his little one. The one with the red eyes.*" His cold blue lights were on the twelfth statue. "*So it has no coin to ask for.*"

"No," said Mau.

"*Then it has nothing*," said Beru. "*It is asking for nothing.*"

He went.

Not fast. Not hungry. He walked along Kamish's dark back to the island at the foot of the steps. The Architect stood there, still, with its long hands folded, watching. He stood in front of the twelfth statue. He looked at its empty stone palm.

"*My King gave it away*," said Beru. To the statue. "*That is all there is to say.*"

He put one long claw on the statue's chest, and pushed.

It went into the nothing without a sound.

"*Twelve*," said Gojo. Very quietly. "*All of them. Every thread.*"

* * *

The cup was nearly dry.

*Send them back,* said Ciel. *All of them. Now. Or you won't have anything left for him.*

"*Beru, Bellion, Kaisel—*" He stopped. He was on Kaisel. "*Everyone back. Except Kamish. Kamish, stay. Be the floor.*"

They sank. Beru. Bellion. Igris, wherever he was. The soldiers. Kaisel knelt on Frieren's island and Mau climbed off, and Kaisel sank into the stone. Only Kamish stayed, lying across the nothing, the dark road between the islands.

The thread eased. The cup stopped draining so fast. It did not stop.

Mau stood on Frieren's island, beside her, with his head splitting and the cup nearly dry and two soldiers unmade.

At the foot of the hundred steps, on the last solid piece of floor in the hall, the Architect unfolded its long hands.

It was smiling. Wider than before.

"*Twelve lessons*," it said. "*Twelve statues. All at once. With names.*" Its pale eyes went to the folded list in Mau's coat. "*And two blues in one place. I did not build that either.*" It took a step forward. Onto the end of Kamish's dark road. "*Now.*"

The light far back in its pale eyes grew brighter.

"*Now show me what you do when I am the one in the way.*"
