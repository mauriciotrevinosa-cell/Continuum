*When: Day 397. Floor 101. The twelve statues are gone into the nothing. Kamish lies across the void as a dark road. The cup is nearly dry. The other reserve is nearly dry. The Architect has stepped onto the end of the dragon's back.*

It walked along Kamish's back toward them.

Unhurried. Each step it was simply on the next part of the dark road, the way it had been on each of the hundred steps. Its long hands hung at its sides now. Not folded. Its pale eyes were on Mau.

Kamish turned his great head.

The old dragon, lying across the nothing as a floor, looked back along his own body at the tall grey thing walking on it. His two great cold blue lights narrowed.

"*Small king*," he said. Deep. Very low. "*It is on me.*"

"I know."

"*Shall I throw it off?*"

Mau looked at the Architect. At the long grey robe. At the pale eyes.

"Yes," he said.

Kamish heaved.

The whole vast dark body rolled. Like a wave. Like a mountain shrugging. The road twisted under the Architect's feet, and the far end of the dragon's body lifted out of the nothing and came down. Tail and wing and the weight of a hill, at the place where the Architect stood.

The Architect lifted one long hand.

And drew a line.

* * *

It was not a cut.

Mau saw it. He would see it again for a long time, at the reset hour, in the dark. A line. Thin. Pale. Like a stroke of chalk on a slate. It came out of the end of the Architect's finger and went through the air, through Kamish's descending wing, through the dark body, and through the nothing beyond.

Where the line went, things were not.

Not cut. Not broken. *Not.* The wing was there, and then a thin pale line went through it, and on one side of the line there was wing and on the other side there was not. As if the wing had been a word on a page and someone had drawn a line through it.

Kamish roared.

The great body shuddered across the nothing. A third of it was gone. The far end, the tail, the wing. Not fallen. Unmade. The rest of him lay across the void still, with a clean pale edge where the rest had been.

*He's not lost,* said Ciel. Very fast. *In pieces. In the cup. Most of him.* A pause. *The part that was unmade is gone. It'll take a great deal to remake what's left.* Another pause. *Mau. That line. It unmade part of a shadow. It can unmake anything.*

The Architect stood on what was left of Kamish's back, unhurt, with its finger still lifted.

"*Unbuilding*," it said. Softly. As if explaining. "*It is the other half of building. Every architect knows both.*" It lowered its hand. "*I built a hundred floors. I can unbuild anything on them.*" Its pale eyes went to Mau. "*Even what you raised on them.*"

"*Kamish, back*," said Mau.

The old dragon sank. What was left of him. Into the nothing, into the cup, in pieces. The dark road between the islands went with him.

The four islands stood in the grey sea, alone.

* * *

The Architect looked at him across the nothing.

"*Before we go on*," it said. Courteously. "*I wish to see one more thing.*"

It lifted its hand. Not toward the islands. Not toward Mau. Toward nothing. Toward a place in the air beside him, at the height of his eyes, where there was nothing at all.

Where, if he opened it, the window would be.

It drew a line through it.

* * *

He felt it before the notice came.

Everything went heavy. Not the way the shaman's sky had gone heavy. From the inside. As if something that had been holding him up for a whole summer, every day, without his noticing, had been taken away all at once.

*Operator interface: suspended.*

The cup.

He felt it go. Not drain. *Shrink.* Fifteen times deep, and then not. The walls of it coming in. Fourteen. Ten. Five. Down, and down, to the size it had been on the morning of the festival, before the first thirty points, before Umaru's first sheet. A small cup. A shallow one. With nearly nothing in it.

Under him, in the dark, his shadows felt it too. He felt them feel it. Nine of them and a hundred, in a cup suddenly far too small to hold them, pressing against its walls like water in a jar that has been squeezed.

*Ciel—*

*I see it.* Her voice was very fast and very strange. *He's cut the window. Not broken it. Cut the line between it and you. Everything you spent. Intelligence. Endurance. All of it. It's not gone, it's—disconnected. The Noise is screaming. It's already trying to put the line back. It doesn't know how fast it can.*

And then his body.

His left arm, where the Ruler's hand had broken it four days ago, splinted, healing faster than it should have because fifty points of Endurance had been mending it every hour. It stopped mending. It started to hurt the way a four-day-old break hurts.

The long splits from seventy, along his arms and his chest, which had closed in a week when they should have taken a month. They did not open. Not yet. He felt them think about it.

Beru's three lines across his back.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *Listen to me. The window was holding you together. All summer. The Endurance. It's been mending you every hour, faster than anything should. Without it, you've got a body that's been broken on every floor for three months and never had time to finish healing.* A pause. *Four minutes. Maybe five. Before it starts to come apart on its own. Before anything even hits you.*

*And the Noise?*

*I don't know,* said Ciel. *It's working. It's never worked this hard.* A pause. *Four minutes.*

* * *

The Architect watched him.

"*There*," it said. Softly. "*Now we see.*" Its pale eyes rested on him, very bright. "*Not what I gave you. What you are.*" It tilted its long head. "*The window was mine. The cup was mine. The army was mine, in a manner of speaking; I built every floor you raised it from.*" It smiled. "*Your eyes are not mine. Your walls are not mine. Your wheel is not mine. The thing that builds in the dark under everything, that read a slime and a dragon and a girl in a kitchen, is not mine.*"

It lifted one finger.

"*Show me those.*"

* * *

"*Everyone back,*" said Mau. "*All of you. Back.*"

They went. The shadows that were still standing, Bellion on his island, Beru beside him, Igris at Frieren's back, sank into the stone and the nothing all at once, into a cup too small for them, and he felt it strain and hold.

He did not have an army.

He had what he had come into the world with. Whatever that was.

*Four minutes,* said Ciel.

* * *

The Architect did not fall.

It stood on nothing. Where Kamish's back had been, there was grey void, and it stood on it, the way it had stood on the steps. It looked at the four islands. Frieren's, with Mau beside her. Yuta's. Gojo's, furthest away.

It lifted one finger toward Gojo's island.

"*No*—" said Mau.

The line went through the island.

Through the stone under Gojo's feet. Clean. Pale. The island was there, and then on one side of the line there was stone and on the other there was not, and Gojo was on the side that was not.

He fell.

* * *

Mau reached into the bag.

He did not think. There was no time to think. He had nothing in the cup, nearly nothing in the other reserve, and Gojo was falling into the grey nothing with his blindfold up and his bare eyes very wide.

Momo's hands.

The kitchen. The kettle lifting itself off the hook. Wrong-handed. Rough. Not his.

He caught him.

Gojo stopped in the grey air a body-length below where his island had been, held by something that was not there. Mau felt the weight of him. Much heavier than Frieren. Much heavier than a kettle. His head split open. Not like before. Worse. Something behind his eyes going white.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *You can't hold him. Not with that. It isn't built for—*

He held him.

He pulled. Toward Frieren's island. Slowly. Gojo came across the grey air, a pace at a time. Mau's eyes behind the filter went warm, then wet, then hot.

Gojo landed on the island beside Frieren.

Mau let go of Momo's pattern.

He went down on one knee. The blood was running from under the filter, both eyes, down his face. His head was a white roar.

"*Mau*." Gojo's hands on his shoulders. "*Mau, your eyes*—"

"I know."

"*You caught me*." Gojo's voice was strange. "*With what? I didn't see anything. There wasn't anything there.*"

"Momo," said Mau. "In the kitchen. The kettle."

Gojo stared at him.

"*You caught me with Momo's kettle*," he said.

"With what the Noise built from watching her," said Mau. "For years. It's rough." He wiped the blood off his face with the back of his hand. "You're heavier than a kettle."

* * *

The Architect was watching.

It stood on the grey nothing between the islands with its finger lowered and its pale eyes very bright.

"*The psychokinetic*," it said. "*The girl in the kitchen. You watched her.*" It was delighted. "*I did not know you had kept that. Not in the floors. Not in the stone.*" It tilted its long narrow head. "*What else have you kept, that I did not see?*"

Mau got up.

He did not know how. His legs did it. He stood on Frieren's island with blood on his face and his head roaring and nothing in the cup. He looked at the tall grey thing standing on nothing.

He put his hand on the hilt at his hip.

* * *

*Mau,* said Ciel. *No.*

*I know.*

*The walls. They're already thin. From Momo's hands. From Gojo's weight.* A pause. *If the pattern comes through again—*

*I know,* said Mau. *Tell Rimuru.*

Ciel was silent for half a heartbeat.

*Tell him what?*

*Tell him I'm going to draw it,* said Mau. *Tell him before.*

He drew it.

Like he meant it. All the way, in one movement. The black scabbard tied with Maki's cord. The pale blade coming out clean and very sharp. He held it in both hands this time. His shoulder had mended enough. He held it the way Yuta held his, every morning, in the yard.

And something came out with it.

* * *

He felt the Noise move.

Not reach. Not build. *Know.* It knew what it was doing this time. It had done it once before, on ninety, half-blind, in four seconds. It had kept the pattern. It had been building it ever since, in the dark, the way it built everything. It knew the shape.

An arm.

Out of the air beside him. Pale. Enormous. Thin, too thin, too many joints. Then another. Two arms this time, not one. Then a body behind them. Hunched. Huge. Pale. And a face. Not half-made. More than half. A wide pale face with a mouth too wide and eyes too dark, and something on the head like a crown of bone.

It looked like her.

More than on ninety. Much more.

It shrieked.

High. Wordless. Full of the same thing. Rage that was not rage. *For* someone.

For him.

* * *

Beside him, on Yuta's island, Yuta made a sound.

Not a word. A breath going in and stopping.

Then he did something Mau had never seen him do in a fight. He lowered his own katana. He looked at the pale shape beside Mau. At its too-dark eyes, at its crown of bone.

It turned its head and looked at him.

The look. The one he had told Mau about on ninety, on the dark grass, with his hands in his lap. *When I'm hurt, before she does anything, she looks at me. To see where I'm hurt. It's the worst look in the world. It's so angry. Not at me. For me.*

The pale shape looked at Yuta like that.

Yuta looked back at it.

"Rika," he said.

Very quietly. Not to the shape.

To the ring on his finger.

* * *

She came.

Out of the air beside Yuta. Out of his katana, as he lifted it again. Out of the ring. Out of him. Not half-made. All the way there. Too much there. Huge and pale and many-toothed, with the crown of bone and the too-wide mouth and the too-dark eyes, and the terrible protective rage coming off her like heat off a forge.

Rika.

The real one.

She stood beside Yuta on his island in the grey hall, enormous. And she looked across the gap of nothing.

At the other one. Beside Mau.

The copy. The pattern. The thing the Noise had built from watching her break Mau's hands apart on Day 133, and from watching Yuta every day for a year.

Nobody moved.

The real Rika looked at the copy for a long time. Her too-dark eyes. Its too-dark eyes. Her crown of bone. Its crown of bone, a little less sure, a little less finished. Her rage, all the way there. Its rage, learned.

Then she screamed.

And the copy screamed with her.

* * *

The sound was not a sound.

It was two of them. The same pitch. The same wordless rage. Going out across the grey hall from two islands at once. The walls shook. The carved writing on the pillars blurred. Gojo put his hands over his ears. Frieren went down on one knee.

And both of them went for the Architect.

From two sides. The real Rika from Yuta's island, the copy from Mau's. Across the nothing. Pale and enormous and screaming. Arms out. Too many joints. Two of them, the same shape, the same rage, the same protection, coming at the tall grey thing standing on nothing between the islands.

The Architect did not move.

It watched them come with its pale eyes very wide.

"*Oh*," it said. Softly. "*Oh, look. A curse born of love. And a copy of a curse born of love.*" It was delighted. "*Made from watching. By something that does not know what love is.*" Its eyes went to Mau. "*And yet.*"

The two Rikas hit it.

* * *

It went back.

One step. On the grey nothing. For the first time since it stepped out of the throne, it went back. Two pale enormous shapes on it at once, tearing at the grey robe, screaming. The real one's hands at its shoulders. The copy's at its long arms. The robe tore.

Under the robe, not stone. Not skin. Something else. Grey. Smooth. With a light far back in it, like the light in its eyes.

The Architect lifted one finger.

And drew a line through the copy.

* * *

It came apart.

Mau felt it. Not like on ninety, where the whip had torn it and it had scattered into light. The line went through it, clean and pale, and on one side of the line there was copy, and on the other there was not.

It did not shriek. It did not have time.

It came apart into light. Pale light, scattering in the grey air like ash. Gone.

Mau felt it go out of him.

Felt the walls, underneath, where the building happened, *bow*. Not strain. Bow. The way a beam bows when a weight lands on it that it was never built for. The blood under his filter came faster. The white roar in his head went whiter.

*Mau,* said Ciel. *Mau, the walls*—

The real Rika screamed.

Alone now. Beside the place where the copy had been. Her too-dark eyes on the empty air. Then on the Architect. The rage coming off her doubled. She tore at it, both hands, alone, and the Architect went back another step.

It lifted its finger toward her.

"*Rika*," said Yuta. Sharp. "*Back.*"

She did not want to.

Mau could see her not want to. Her too-dark eyes on the Architect, her hands still on it. Then she looked at Yuta. Just for a second. The look. To see where he was hurt.

He was not hurt.

She let go.

She went back to him. Into the ring, into the katana, into him. Gone. Before the line could reach her.

* * *

The Architect turned to Mau.

Its robe was torn at the shoulders and down one arm. Under it, the grey smooth not-skin, with a light far back in it. It did not seem hurt. It seemed delighted. Its pale eyes were brighter than they had ever been.

"*More*," it said. "*Show me more.*"

It drew a line.

At him.

* * *

He had nothing.

The cup was dry. The other reserve was dry. The copy was gone. The walls were bowing. He was standing on Frieren's island with a pale katana in both hands, blood on his face, and a thin pale line coming at him through the grey air. It would unmake whatever it went through.

It went through his chest.

* * *

And the wheel turned.

*Click.*

Three.

He heard it. Underneath. One more notch. The sound of something enormous shifting in the dark.

The line went through his chest.

And did not unmake him.

He felt it go through. Cold. Pale. A stroke of chalk across a slate. He felt it try. Felt it try to make him into a word with a line through it. On one side of the line, Mau. On the other, not.

The wheel had learned it.

One turn. It turned at what hurt him, and learned its shape, and the shape of being unmade went across his chest like water across wet stone. It slid. It did not take. It left a long pale mark across his shirt, across his skin, like a burn, and did not take.

He was still standing.

The Architect's pale eyes went very wide.

"*Oh*," it said. "*Oh. The wheel.*"

* * *

But underneath, the walls were going.

He felt them. All of them. The structure of himself, the house that Ciel and Rimuru had built inside him after the bell, the walls that held the eyes and the building and the wheel apart from each other. Bowing. Bowing further. The copy's unmaking had bowed them. The third turn of the wheel was pushing on them from underneath. The blood was pouring from under the filter now. The white roar in his head was everything.

*Mau,* said Ciel. Her voice was not level. Her voice was nothing like level. *I can't hold them. I can't hold them on my own. They're going to*—

And then, from very far away, down the thread, from a back step in a house beyond a closed frame:

*I've got them.*

Rimuru.

Mau felt him. The way he had never felt him before. Not Ciel relaying. Rimuru. His own strength, his own enormous patient strength, the strength of someone who had built a nation once and knew how walls stood up. It came down the thread from the house and into the walls inside Mau. It went where Ciel could not reach on her own, and *held*.

The walls stopped bowing.

They did not straighten. They stopped.

*I told you,* said Rimuru. Down the thread. Inside him. Very tired. *Somebody has to be at the other end.* A pause. *Diablo is watching me pretend to be calm. I am not calm. Finish it, Mau. I can hold this for a while. Not forever.*

And in the space that Rimuru made, holding the walls from a back step in a house beyond a closed frame, the Noise found what it had been looking for.

He felt it. Underneath. Where the building happened. A line, cut clean by a pale finger, and something reaching across the cut from his side, the way it reached for everything, the way it had reached for a slime's memories and a girl's kitchen and a curse born of love. Not asking. Building. A new line, rough, crooked, its own, from the window to him, across the place where the Architect's had been.

*Operator interface: restored.*

And under it, smaller, a line he had never seen:

*(Rebuilt by the Noise.)*

The cup came back. Not all at once. Opening, like a hand. Five. Ten. Fifteen times deep, with almost nothing in it, but deep. In his left arm, the mending started again. Slowly. Like warmth coming back into cold fingers.

*Three minutes and forty seconds,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *It took it back. It took it back from him.* A pause. *Mau. It's the Noise's now. Not his. The line. It built its own.*

Across the nothing, the Architect had gone very still.

Its pale eyes were on the place in the air beside Mau, where the window was.

"*Oh*," it said. Very softly. "*It took it back.*"

For a moment the delight was not there. Something else was. Something that looked, on that long grey ancient face, almost like respect.

"*It built its own*," said the Architect. "*Across my line.*"

* * *

Mau lifted his head.

The blood was on his face, and his chest burned in a long pale line, and the walls were held up by Rimuru from a back step in a house beyond a closed frame. And the Architect was standing on the grey nothing in front of Frieren's island with its robe torn, looking at him with its pale eyes wider than they had ever been.

He looked at Frieren.

She was beside him. On the island. On her feet. Her staff up. She had been on one knee when the two Rikas screamed, and she was not on one knee now. She was standing beside him with her staff levelled at the Architect, and her face was doing the thing it did very rarely.

Not serene. Not calm.

The thing it had done at the wall, in the plaza, for four hundred and thirty-seven deaths, when she could not get in.

She was in.

She fired.

* * *

Zoltraak.

Not halfway. Not stopped. White. Straight across the nothing. Into the torn place in the Architect's robe, at the shoulder, where the grey not-skin showed.

It went through.

All the way. Out the other side, into the far wall of the hall, into the carved rows of the green book's script, and the writing on that pillar blackened and smoked.

The Architect went back.

Three steps. On the grey nothing. With a hole through its shoulder, clean, the light far back in it showing through.

It looked down at the hole.

Then at Frieren.

And laughed.

The way it had laughed inside the cracking stone. Delighted. Old. Very old. The laughter of someone who had been waiting a very long time for something, and had just watched it arrive. Better than they hoped.

"*Yes*," it said.
