# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 50 — He Will Work

**Volume:** 31

*When: Day 397. Floor 101. The Architect stands on grey nothing with a hole through its shoulder, laughing. Mau's cup is dry. The other reserve is dry. The walls inside him are held up from a back step in a house beyond a closed frame.*

"*Yes*," it said.

It put one long hand over the hole in its shoulder. Not to cover it. To feel it. The way you put your hand on something you have built, to see whether it holds.

"*She went through*," it said. Softly. "*The elf. All the way.*" It looked at Frieren with its pale eyes. "*Nothing has gone through me since before this tower stood.*"

Frieren did not lower her staff.

"Then you've been waiting a long time," she said.

"*A very long time*," said the Architect. "*For this.*" It turned its pale eyes to Mau. "*For him.*"

It lifted its finger.

* * *

"*Hand*," said Gojo.

He was already moving.

Mau did not see him go. One moment Gojo was standing beside him on Frieren's island with his bare eyes wide and blood from Mau's face on his sleeve. The next he was off the island. On the grey nothing. Walking on it. Infinity under his feet, Mau understood a moment later. The space between his feet and the nothing so infinite that he never fell. Gojo was beside the Architect, close, closer than anyone had been, with one hand reaching for the long grey finger as it lifted.

He did not grab it.

He put his hand round it. Not touching. A hair away. And between his hand and the Architect's finger, the space opened. Infinity. All of it. Everything Continuum had left him.

The finger kept moving.

It moved toward the end of the line it was drawing. Toward the place where the line would arrive, and unmake whatever it arrived at.

It never got there.

* * *

"*You draw lines*," said Gojo. Very quietly. Very close to the Architect's long narrow face. "*I watched you. Six times now.*" His bare eyes did not move from the finger. "*Lines have to arrive somewhere. A line that doesn't finish isn't a line.*" He tightened the space round the finger. A hair. "*Your finger's going to the end of that line. It's going to keep going. Forever. It's never going to get there.*"

The Architect looked at its own finger.

Moving. Slowly. Toward the end of the line. And between the finger and the end, Infinity. The space getting smaller and smaller and never arriving.

"*Oh*," said the Architect. Softly. "*Oh, that is clever.*"

"I've been told," said Gojo.

The Architect lifted its other hand.

"*Rika*," said Yuta.

* * *

She came out of him.

Out of the ring, out of the katana, out of Yuta, enormous and pale and screaming. She did not look at Yuta first this time. She knew he was not hurt. She went straight across the nothing to the Architect, and took its other arm in both her too-jointed hands, and *held*.

The Architect's other hand stopped, halfway lifted.

It looked at Rika.

"*The real one*," it said. "*Not the copy.*" It tilted its long narrow head. "*She is very angry.*"

"She's always angry," said Yuta. On his island, with his katana up and his face white. "When I'm in the room with something that wants to hurt the people I love."

* * *

The cup was not quite dry.

Mau felt it. Fifteen times deep, and the floor of it bare, and on the floor, a thin layer. Refilling. Faster than it ever had. *A bigger cup catches more.* Armin's broken line.

*Ciel. How much?*

*Seconds,* said Ciel. *Two shadows. Seconds. Then nothing.* A pause. *Rimuru says do it.*

"*Bellion*," said Mau. "*Igris.*"

They came up out of Frieren's island.

The Grand Marshal, with his segmented sword already coming apart. The knight, with his long sword already drawn.

Bellion's whip went out across the nothing. Twenty paces of segmented steel wrapped round the Architect's middle, round the torn grey robe, round the not-skin under it, and pulled tight.

Igris crossed the nothing. Mau did not know how. Shadows did not walk on nothing. Igris did, for three steps, because Mau asked him to and he would not be told no by the void. He stood at the Architect's back with the point of his long sword against the place between its shoulders.

"*Commander*," said Bellion. His whip was singing with the strain.

"*Marshal*," said Igris.

* * *

Mau stepped off the island.

He did not use Okarun. He did not have anything left to use Okarun with. He used the one thing he had not used up. The thing Gojo was standing on. Infinity, under his feet, the small amount he had left, the way Gojo had shown him on the grey nothing a moment before. He did not know it worked until he was standing on it.

He walked across the void.

He stopped in front of the Architect, very close, with the pale katana in both hands. He put the edge of it against the long grey throat.

Frieren came beside him.

She did not walk on Infinity. She floated. Her own power. The thing she had always been able to do. She stopped on the grey air at his left shoulder, inside, beside him, and put the end of her staff against the Architect's long narrow face. Between the pale eyes.

Nobody moved.

* * *

The Architect looked at them.

At Gojo, holding its finger in a space that would never close. At Rika, holding its other arm. At Bellion's whip round its middle and Igris's sword at its back. At Frieren's staff between its eyes, the end of it glowing very faintly, the way it glowed before Zoltraak.

At Mau. With a pale katana at its throat. Blood on his face. A long pale burn across his chest where the line had not taken.

Its pale eyes were very bright.

It did not struggle.

It smiled.

The widest smile yet. So wide its long narrow face seemed too small to hold it.

"*Yes*," it said.

Very softly.

"*Yes. Yes.*" It looked at Mau with something that was not delight any more. Something beyond delight. Something like relief. Like a man who has been building something for longer than he can remember, and has just watched it stand up on its own. "*He will work.*"

* * *

"*What will I work for?*" said Mau.

The katana did not move from its throat.

The Architect looked at him.

"*You learned a hundred lessons*," it said. "*You chose what to keep. You gave away what was yours. You told them the plan before. You raised a dragon and a knight and an ant who was a king, and gave them names, and they came when you called the names.*" Its pale eyes held his. "*You stood two blues in one place. You caught a man with a kettle you watched in a kitchen. You held a copy of a curse born of love, and the real one stood beside it.*" It was quiet for a moment. "*And the wheel learned to stop being unbuilt.*"

"What will I work *for*?"

"*For him*," said the Architect. "*My monarch. Who rests.*"

"I don't know who that is."

"*No*," said the Architect. "*Not yet.*" It smiled. "*But he left his door in you. And you will grow. And when you are grown enough—*"

It stopped.

Its pale eyes moved. Not to Mau. Down. Past him. To the grey nothing beneath them all.

To the place under Frieren.

* * *

It did not use its finger.

Its finger was held in Gojo's space. Its other hand was held in Rika's.

It used its eyes.

Mau saw it. Too late. A line. Thin. Pale. Coming not from a finger but from the light far back in the Architect's eyes. Down. Into the grey nothing under Frieren, where she floated at his left shoulder with her staff between its eyes.

The line did not go through her.

It went through the place under her. Through the air she was floating on. Through whatever it was that let an elf float, that had let her float for a thousand years.

She dropped.

Not fast. The void took her. Pulled. Down into the grey nothing with no bottom. Her staff came away from the Architect's face. Her hand came out, reaching. Her face—

*A finger's width.*

* * *

He did not think.

He did not reach for the bag. There was nothing in the bag he could reach. He did not reach for the space. He let it go. The katana came away from the Architect's throat and fell, turning, into the nothing.

He went after her.

Off the Infinity under his feet. Down. One step into the void. One hand back, catching the edge of the space Gojo was standing on, the edge of the Infinity under Gojo's feet. He did not know he could. He did. His other hand going down, reaching.

He caught her.

Her wrist. Cold. In his hand. He hung from the edge of Gojo's Infinity by one hand and held Frieren by the wrist over the grey nothing with the other. She hung below him, looking up.

Not a finger's width.

*Here.*

* * *

And in that heartbeat, with both his hands full, with the katana falling into the nothing and the space let go and nothing between him and anything at all—

The Architect stepped.

Out of Bellion's whip. The whip went slack, because Mau's attention had gone, and Bellion was a shadow, and shadows stood on the attention of the one who called them. Out from Igris's sword point, for the same reason. Its finger was still in Gojo's space. Its other arm was still in Rika's hands.

It did not need its hands.

It leaned down. Over the edge of the Infinity Mau was hanging from. Its long narrow face very close to his.

And it put its hand through his chest.

* * *

Not its finger. Not a line. Its hand. The one Rika was holding. It drew Rika's hands with it, the way you draw a sleeve, and pushed its long grey hand into the pale burn across Mau's chest. Into the place where the line had gone and the wheel had made it slide.

It did not hurt.

It was cold.

Colder than Frieren's hands. Colder than Lantern's jaw, or Igris's sword laid across his palm, or the stone in shade. Cold like the bottom of the cup when it was dry. Cold like the dark in his shadow where the doors were.

Something went into him.

He felt it go. Small. Dense. Black. Like a stone. Like a seed. It went through the pale burn, through his chest, into the place under his ribs where his heart was. And settled there. Beside his heart.

And beat.

* * *

*Thump.*

His heart.

*Thump.*

Something else.

Not at the same time. A half-beat apart. *Thump*—*thump*. *Thump*—*thump*. His own heart, and another one, beside it. Cold. Black. Beating.

The Architect drew its hand out.

It looked at him, very close, with its pale eyes.

"*A gift*," it said. Softly. "*You will need it.*" It smiled. "*You are still too small. For what is coming.*" It straightened. "*Now you will grow.*"

* * *

*Mau.*

Ciel. Inside. Her voice nothing like level.

*Mau, something's in you. Something's—it's next to your heart, it's beating, it's—I don't know what it is. I don't know what it is. I've never—*

*I know.*

*Rimuru felt it. Down the thread. He's—*

*I know.*

He did not let go of Frieren's wrist.

He did not let go of the edge of Gojo's Infinity.

He hung there over the nothing, with two hearts beating in his chest half a beat apart, and Frieren's wrist in his hand. And he pulled.

He did not know where it came from. The cup was dry. The other reserve was dry. He pulled anyway, with his arms, with nothing but his arms, the way anybody pulls someone they will not let fall. It was enough. Barely.

Frieren came up.

Out of the void. He pulled her up past him, onto the Infinity under Gojo's feet. Gojo's hand came down and took her other wrist and lifted. She was on Gojo's space, beside him, on her knees, breathing.

Mau pulled himself up after her.

He knelt on the edge of Gojo's Infinity over the grey nothing. Two hearts. *Thump*—*thump*. He looked up.

* * *

The Architect was going up the hundred steps.

Not walking. Each step it was simply on the next one. Unhurried. The torn grey robe brushing the stone. It had let go of Rika. Or Rika had let go of it. She was beside Yuta again, on his island, screaming at its back. It had drawn its finger out of Gojo's space while Gojo was lifting Frieren.

It went up all hundred steps.

At the top, it turned. It looked down at them. At the four of them, on the islands and on the nothing, at the foot of the steps.

At Mau.

"*I will be watching*," it said.

It sat down in the throne.

And the throne opened.

* * *

Not broke. Opened. Like a door.

The stone of the seated god's body, the shell the Architect had stepped out of, split down the middle. Behind it, not more stone. Not the back wall of the hall.

Sky.

Open sky. Blue. Real. With clouds in it, moving. And under it, very far down, land. Green and brown. Fields. A river. Hills.

The world. The one outside the tower. The one with the house in it.

The Architect stepped through.

Into the sky. Into the world.

And was gone.

* * *

Gojo moved.

He was off the Infinity and onto the hundred steps before anyone else had breathed. Up them. Fast. His bare eyes on the open door at the top, on the sky, on the place where the Architect had gone.

"*Gojo*," said Mau.

Gojo did not stop.

"*Satoru*."

Gojo stopped.

Halfway up the steps. He turned round. He looked down at Mau, kneeling on the edge of the space over the void, with blood on his face and two hearts in his chest.

"He's *there*," said Gojo. "Right there. Through the door. I can see him. I can still—"

"Don't," said Mau.

Gojo looked at him.

"You promised Shoko," said Mau. "You've got her cigarette in your pocket. You said you'd bring it back." He took a breath. Two hearts. "If you go through that door, you're not bringing anything back. You're going wherever he's going. On your own. With nothing."

Gojo stood on the steps.

Above him, at the top, the open door into the sky. Below him, Mau.

"I saw him," said Gojo. Very quietly. "I *saw* him."

"I know," said Mau. "So did I. We'll see him again." He looked at the door. "He said so. *I will be watching.*" He looked back at Gojo. "Not today. Today you're bringing back a cigarette."

Gojo did not move for a long time.

Then he put his hand on his breast pocket. On the bent cigarette inside it. He looked at the open door at the top of the steps for one moment more.

He came back down.

* * *

The door closed.

The throne. The seated god's broken shell. The sky behind it, the world. It closed like a door in the wind. The split stone came back together and was stone, broken and empty, with nothing behind it but the back wall of the hall.

And the hall settled.

The floor came back. Tile by tile. Grey and smooth and level, under the nothing, over the nothing, until there was no nothing. Only a stone floor, and four islands that were not islands any more, and Rika screaming at the empty throne until Yuta said her name and she went back into him.

The carved writing on the walls stopped glowing.

On the far wall, where the frame had been, a small door. Dark. With home faintly visible through it.

Nobody moved for a long time.

Mau knelt on the grey stone floor.

*Thump*—*thump*.

*Thump*—*thump*.

Frieren knelt down in front of him.

She did not say anything. She put both her cold hands on his face, one on each side, over the blood. She looked at him for a long time.

Then she took one hand away from his face. She put it flat on his chest. Over the pale burn. Over his heart.

She went very still.

Mau watched her face.

Watched her feel it. *Thump*—*thump*. *Thump*—*thump*. His heart, and another one, beside it. Cold. Black. A half-beat behind.

Her eyes went wide.

She did not say anything.

She did not take her hand away.
