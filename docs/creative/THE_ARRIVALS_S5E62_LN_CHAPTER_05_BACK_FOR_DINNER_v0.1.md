# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 49 — Back for Dinner

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 49
**Continuity state:** Return from floor 101 (Day 397, evening). Yuta finds the katana on the restored floor and sheathes it. At the screen: Shoko and Maomao; Rimuru is grey and exhausted on the back step after holding the walls down the thread, with Diablo beside him. Gojo gives Shoko back her cigarette, unlit and intact. *When: Day 397, evening. Eleven hours by Anko's watch. The frame at the screen lit at a quarter past six. Ori has been standing in front of it since noon.*

Yuta found the katana.

It was lying on the grey stone floor of the hall, where the nothing had been, under the place where Mau had dropped it. The floor had come back under it. It lay on the tiles, bare, the pale blade clean, as if someone had set it down carefully and walked away.

Yuta picked it up.

He did not hand it to Mau. He looked at it for a long time, in the grey light, with Rika gone quiet inside him. Then he walked over to where Mau knelt on the floor with Frieren's hand flat on his chest. He took the black scabbard from Mau's hip, very gently, untying Maki's cord. He slid the pale blade home.

It went in clean.

He tied the cord again. He put the scabbard back at Mau's hip.

"You dropped it," he said.

"To catch her."

"I know," said Yuta. "That's the right reason to drop it." He stood up. "Gojo said draw it like you mean it. He didn't say anything about dropping it like you mean it." He looked at Frieren. "But you did."

* * *

They went home through the small door on the far wall.

Mau first, because he was the owner. He walked. He did not know how. Frieren walked beside him with her hand on his back, between his shoulders, not holding him up. Just there. Gojo behind them, with his hand on his breast pocket. Yuta last, looking back once at the empty broken throne at the top of the hundred steps.

They came through the frame at a quarter past six.

Into the evening. Gold light. The dry ground at the screen. Anko's board. Four planks and *COMPLETE* and *101*.

And Ori.

* * *

She had been standing in front of the frame since noon.

She did not run. She stood very straight, in her coat with the top button done up, and looked at them come through. One at a time. Mau. Frieren. Gojo. Yuta. She counted. Four. She looked at Mau's face, the blood dried dark under the filter and down his cheeks. At the long pale burn across his chest, through the torn shirt.

At Frieren's hand on his back.

Ori did not say anything.

She walked across the dry ground to him. She stopped in front of him. She looked up at his face for a long time. Then she put her arms round his middle and held on.

She did not cry.

She held on very tightly, with her face against his torn shirt, against his chest. Then she went very still.

She had heard it.

Mau felt her hear it. Her whole body going still against him. Her ear against his chest, over his heart. *Thump*—*thump*. *Thump*—*thump*.

She did not let go.

She did not say anything about it.

After a long time, she said, into his shirt, very quietly:

"Back for dinner."

"Back for dinner," said Mau.

* * *

Shoko did not ask anything.

She came across the dry ground from where she had been standing all day with her kit open and took his face in both her hands, the way Frieren did. She tilted it to the light. She looked at the blood under the filter. She looked at the burn across his chest. She put two fingers on his wrist.

She held them there for a long time.

Her face changed.

Mau watched it change. The way a doctor's face changes when the pulse under her fingers is not the pulse she expected. She held her fingers on his wrist and counted, and her lips moved. She counted again.

She looked at him.

She did not say anything. She took her fingers off his wrist and put her palm flat on his chest instead. Over the burn. Over his heart. The way Frieren had, and Ori.

She went very still.

"Inside," she said. Very quietly. "Now."

"Shoko—"

"*Inside*," said Shoko. "I'm not asking."

* * *

Rimuru was on the back step.

He was sitting where he had been sitting all day, with his chin on his knees. He was grey. Not pale. Grey, the way a slime goes grey when it has used more than it has. Diablo was standing beside him. Not at the corner of the house, pretending not to watch. Beside him. With one hand on his shoulder.

Rimuru looked up when Mau came round the corner of the house.

He did not say anything.

He did not need to. Mau could hear him. Down the thread, very faint, very tired.

*I held them,* said Rimuru. *The walls. As long as I could.* A pause. *I felt it. When he put it in you. I felt it go in. Through Ciel.* Another pause. *I don't know what it is either.*

"Thank you," said Mau. Aloud.

Rimuru looked at him for a long time.

"I told you," he said. Aloud. His voice was very rough. "Somebody has to be at the other end." He put his chin back on his knees. "Diablo hasn't let go of my shoulder for three hours. It's very embarrassing."

"Rimuru-sama held a structure from a distance he has never held anything," said Diablo. Very quietly. Not letting go. "For a person he cannot see. I am permitted to hold his shoulder."

Rimuru did not argue.

* * *

At the clinic step, Gojo stopped.

Shoko was at the door, waiting to take Mau in. Gojo stood at the bottom of the step and put his hand in his breast pocket.

He took out the cigarette.

Small. Bent. White. Unlit. Intact. The last one from home.

He held it out.

Shoko looked at it.

She did not take it at once. She looked at it in his palm for a long time. Then at Gojo. At his bare eyes, very tired. At the blood on his sleeve that was not his. At his face, which had seen a door into the sky open at the top of a hundred steps, and had turned round and come back down.

"You brought it back," she said.

"I said I would."

"I know you did," said Shoko. "I'm saying it so it's on record."

She took the cigarette. She put it back in her coat pocket, where it had always been.

Then she did something Mau had never seen her do. She put her hand flat on the side of Gojo's face. Briefly. Just for a moment. The way you touch something to see whether it's real.

She took her hand away.

"Inside," she said. To both of them. "Now."

* * *

She kept him in the clinic for two hours.

She cleaned his eyes. She looked at the burn across his chest, the long pale line where the Architect's line had gone through and the wheel had made it slide. She did not put her hands on it the other way. "I don't know what it is," she said. "I'm not putting my technique into something I don't know." She bandaged it, white, from shoulder to hip.

She listened to his chest for a long time.

With her ear. Flat against it. The way Ori had, at the frame. She did not say anything for the whole time.

When she lifted her head, her face was very careful.

"Two," she said.

"I know."

"Two hearts." She sat back on her stool. "One's yours. The one I've been listening to since the bell. The one I know." She looked at his chest. "The other one's—" She stopped. "Slower. Colder. Half a beat behind yours. Every time. Like it's following."

"Ciel doesn't know what it is."

"Neither do I," said Shoko. "Neither does Maomao. Neither does Rimuru." She folded her hands on her notes. She had not written anything. "Nobody in this house knows what it is."

She was quiet for a long time.

"Does it hurt?" she said.

Mau thought about it.

"No," he said. "It's cold. Like something in my chest that's colder than the rest of me." He paused. "It doesn't hurt."

Shoko wrote one line in her notes. He did not see what. Later, when Frieren came, he saw it on the desk.

*Day 397. Two hearts. Unknown. Not painful. Watch.*

* * *

He wrote it on the board himself.

Before dinner. Anko was standing at the board with the stub of pencil behind her ear, waiting. When he came out of the clinic with Frieren beside him, she took the pencil out and held it out to him.

"You said write it down," said Mau.

"I said write it down," said Anko. "Yourself. In your hand. So it's here."

He took the pencil.

He stood in front of the fourth plank. Under *COMPLETE*. Under *After?*, still not crossed out. Under *101*, in his own hand, from the night of the tree.

He wrote.

His hand was not steady. His writing had never been very good. It was crooked, the way his column on Fern's ledger slip was crooked. He wrote anyway. Slowly. One line at a time.

*Day 397. 101. Mau, Frieren, Gojo, Yuta.*

*A temple. Twelve statues. Twelve lessons. We remembered all of them.*

*The Architect. He built the tower. He watches. He says I am a vessel for his monarch, who rests.*

*Two blues in one place (Gojo's and mine). Two Rikas (Yuta's and the copy). Rimuru held the walls from the step.*

*Frieren went through him.*

*He gave me something. In my chest. It beats.*

*He went through the throne into the world. I stopped Gojo following.*

*Back for dinner.*

He stopped.

He looked at what he had written. Crooked. Uneven. Eight lines, on the bottom of the fourth plank, under everything.

Then, under *After?*, which Anko had written so long ago and never crossed out, he drew one line. Not through it. Under it.

And wrote, very small:

*He said he'll be watching.*

He gave Anko back the pencil.

She looked at what he had written for a long time.

She did not add anything. She put the pencil back behind her ear.

* * *

Taihei had made curry.

Even though it was not his day. Momo had let him. The big pot, not the biggest. He had started it at three, the way Umaru had timed it at the festival. It had been ready since six. He had kept it warm on the stove for an hour, because nobody knew when they would come.

The house came to the long table.

All of it. Nobody had said to. Nobody needed to. Fern at the head with the ledger. Stark beside her, in the quilted jacket with the black plate. Seiko in the high seat with Turbo Granny. Kessoku, the five, the witches, Coco and her mother. Marin and Wakana. Momo and Okarun and Jiji and Vamola. Rem and Ram. Eren and Mikasa, with Armin between them. Kaneki and Touka, with Ichika in her too-long sash. Himmel at the far end, with his hair brushed. Maki beside Yuta. Mai beside Maki. Yuka with her rope across her knees and three silver flowers on it now.

Umaru in her hood.

Taihei came out of the kitchen with the pot. He put it down at the end of the long table.

"It's slightly worse," he said. To nobody. To everyone. "I tried."

Momo, at the kitchen door, said nothing. Which was the highest mark she gave.

* * *

Mau told them.

After the first bowl. He put his spoon down. He told them the way he had written it on the board, but longer. The temple. The twelve. The Architect stepping out of the stone. *A vessel. For my monarch, who rests.* The lines that unbuilt things. Kamish. The two blues. The two Rikas. Rimuru holding the walls from the back step. Frieren, going through.

The gift.

He put his hand flat on his own chest, over the bandage, over the burn.

"It's here," he said. "Beside my heart. It beats. Half a beat behind mine." He looked at the table. "Shoko's listened to it. Maomao. Ciel. Rimuru. Nobody knows what it is." He was quiet. "He said I'd need it. He said I'm still too small for what's coming." He looked at his hand on his chest. "He said now I'll grow."

The long table was very quiet.

"I don't know what it means," said Mau. "I don't know what's coming. I don't know who his monarch is, or what a vessel is for, or what this thing in my chest does." He looked down the table. At all of them. "I wanted you to know. From me. Tonight. Not find out later."

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Then Touka put down her cup.

"Does it hurt?" she said.

"No."

"Then eat your curry," said Touka. "It's getting cold." She picked up her cup again. "We'll find out what it is. Later. When there's something to find." She drank. "Tonight you're back for dinner. That's all tonight is."

Nobody argued.

Mau picked up his spoon.

* * *

He ate the curry.

It was not slightly worse. It was very good. Everyone knew it, including Momo. He ate two bowls. Frieren sat beside him with her shoulder against his and ate one, very slowly. Under the table, her foot was against his ankle. Under the table, on his other side, Ori's hand was on his knee. She did not take it away.

At the end of the long table, Gojo was eating his third bowl. Shoko was beside him. When he reached for the bread, she pushed it toward him without looking.

Across from them, Yuta was very quiet. Maki was beside him. She had her hand on the back of his neck, under the table, where nobody could see. Mau could see.

At the far end, Rimuru had come in from the step, still grey. He sat down in his place with Diablo standing behind his chair. Momo put a bowl in front of him without asking.

He looked at it.

"I'm a slime," he said. "I don't need to eat."

"Eat it anyway," said Momo.

Rimuru ate it.

* * *

Later, when the plates were cleared and the house was going up, Umaru stood on the bench at the end of the long table.

She did not have her slate. She had nothing in her hands. She stood on the bench in her hood and sash and looked at the long room, at everyone going up the stairs.

"The Ministry," she said.

Everyone stopped.

"The Ministry declares," said Umaru. Very quietly. Not her proclamation voice. "That today is also Tower Day. A second one. Every year." She paused. "The day they came back."

Nobody said anything.

"That's all," said Umaru. "No amendments. No objections." She got down off the bench. "It's in the constitution now."

She went up the stairs.

Taihei, at the kitchen door with the empty curry pot, watched her go. Then he looked at the pot. Then at Mau, still at the long table with Frieren beside him.

"Back for dinner," he said. Very quietly.

"Back for dinner," said Mau.
