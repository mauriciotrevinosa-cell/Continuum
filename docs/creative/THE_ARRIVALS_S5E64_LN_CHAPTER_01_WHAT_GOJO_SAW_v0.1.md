# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 52 — What Gojo Saw

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 52
**Continuity state:** Day 398, morning, the clinic. A study of the second heart with no answers. Shoko's hands: it is not tissue she knows, and it is not hurting him. Maomao: no poison, no parasite she knows, "and I know a great many". Senku can only write a ledger line: unknown, beating, watch. Rimuru, still grey, felt it arrive down the thread and calls it "a closed door with something warm behind it". Gojo looks with bare eyes for a long time. It is not cursed energy, mana or anything Ciel is made of; it has a root going down into Mau's shadow, and he cannot see past the root, the first thing since the bell he has looked at and not seen through. Gojo is shaken by that and says so to Shoko afterwards. Individual reactions only; nothing is used, explained or decided

*When: Day 398, mid-morning. The clinic door is open for the light. Ori is on the step outside with Shoko's watch, though nobody asked her to time anything. Across the yard, Taihei is washing last night's curry pot for the second time.*

"Breathe in," said Shoko.

Mau breathed in.

Two hearts. *Thump*—*thump*.

Shoko had her hand flat on his chest, below the bandage, the way she had put it there last night. Her eyes were half closed. Something moved under her palm that was not quite heat. The thing she did when she healed, turned the other way. Not putting in. Looking.

She kept her hand there a long time.

"Out," she said.

He breathed out.

She took her hand away. She sat back on the stool and looked at it, her own hand, as if it had told her something she did not like.

"It isn't tissue," she said. "Not any I know. It's not muscle. It's not a growth. It's not attached to anything I can follow." She looked at his chest. "It isn't hurting you. Your chest's healing. The burn's half what it was last night. Your ribs are knitting." She paused. "Faster than I'd like to take credit for."

"Is that it?" said Mau. "Healing faster?"

"I don't know," said Shoko. "That's my answer. I don't know." She picked up her coffee. "I've said it twice now. I'm going to keep saying it until it's not true."

* * *

Maomao went second.

She did not use her hands. She used her nose, first, close to his chest, which made Mau go very still and Frieren, on the bench by the wall, lift her head. Then his wrist. Then the inside of his elbow. Then a drop of blood on a white cloth, which she held up to the light from the door and looked at for a long time.

"No poison," she said.

"You can tell that from a drop?"

"I can tell most things from a drop." She folded the cloth. "No parasite I know. And I know a great many." She looked at him with something that was almost offended. "Whatever it is, it doesn't want anything from your blood. It's not feeding. It's not spreading." She put the cloth in a jar and stoppered it. "It's just beating. Which isn't a thing anything does without a reason."

"Then what's the reason?"

"I don't know," said Maomao. "Write it down."

* * *

Senku wrote it down.

He was at the clinic table with a page from his ledger and a pencil. He had not said *ten billion percent*. He had not said *doable*. He had come in an hour ago with the page already headed, and he had listened to Shoko and Maomao, and he had written nothing until now.

He wrote now.

*Object 1 (internal). Placed Day 397, without consent. Beats, ~half beat behind host heart. Not tissue (Shoko). Not toxin/parasite (Maomao). Not painful. Healing accelerated? (cause unclear).*

He looked at it.

Then, under it:

*Purpose: unknown.*

*Mechanism: unknown.*

*Removal: DO NOT ATTEMPT.*

He underlined the last line twice. He turned the page so Mau could see it.

"That's the whole ledger," he said. "Right now. That's everything science has." He tapped the last line. "And that's the only thing I'm sure of. Nobody cuts anything out of you that they don't understand. Not Maomao. Not Shoko. Not you, at three in the morning, because you can't sleep."

"I wasn't going to."

"Good," said Senku. "Now it's written down, so you definitely aren't."

* * *

Rimuru came in on Diablo's arm.

He was still grey. Not the deep grey of the back step last night, with the thread pouring out of him. A paler grey. Tired grey. He sat down on the end of the clinic bed and looked at Mau for a while without speaking.

"I felt it go in," he said at last.

Mau looked at him.

"Down the thread. I was holding the walls. Everything I had was going down to you. And then something came the other way." Rimuru looked at his own hands. "Not at me. Past me. Into you. Like someone putting a stone in a well." He was quiet. "Ciel screamed. I've never heard her do that."

*I didn't scream,* said Ciel.

"She says she didn't scream," said Mau.

"She screamed," said Rimuru. He almost smiled. Then he did not. "I can feel it now. Through her. Faintly." He closed his eyes. "It's like a closed door. With something warm behind it. Not hot. Warm. Like a room someone's been sitting in." He opened them. "I don't know whose room."

* * *

Gojo went last.

He had been standing in the doorway the whole time, with his shoulder against the frame and his blindfold up, not saying anything. Ori was sitting on the step below him with the watch. He had not made a single joke since he came in. Shoko had noticed. Shoko had looked at him twice.

He came in now. He stood at the foot of the bed.

He took the blindfold off.

He looked.

Mau had seen him look at things before. At the stone of eighty, all the way up the thread to the Architect. At the Architect's pale lines, six times, until he knew where they had to arrive. Gojo's bare eyes went *through* things. That was what they were for. They found the edge of everything and the inside of everything, and he usually said what he saw before anyone asked, because he liked to.

He did not say anything.

He looked for a long time.

"Satoru," said Shoko.

"Give me a second."

He leaned in. Closer. His bare eyes very close to Mau's chest. To the place below the bandage where Shoko's hand had been.

"It's not cursed energy," he said at last. Quietly. "It's not mana. Not Frieren's kind. Not the tower's kind." He tilted his head. "It's not what Ciel's made of either. I can see her. I can see the walls. I can see the wheel, three notches round, asleep." He was quiet. "I can see this. I can see its edge. It's small. Dense. It beats."

"And?" said Mau.

"And there's a root," said Gojo. "Going down. Out of it. Down through you, into your shadow." His eyes went to the floor. To the long dark shape lying across the boards from Mau's feet. "Into the branch. Where your soldiers are."

He followed it with his eyes. Down. Into the shadow.

And stopped.

"I can't see past it," he said.

Nobody in the room moved.

"I can see the root go in," said Gojo. "And then—" He stopped. He looked at the shadow on the floor for a long time. "It's not dark. Dark I can see through. It's—closed. Like it goes somewhere my eyes don't."

He straightened up. He put the blindfold back on. His hands were very steady doing it. Mau noticed that they had to be made steady.

"Huh," said Gojo.

That was all he said.

* * *

Frieren had not moved from the bench.

She had watched all of it. Shoko. Maomao. Senku's page. Rimuru's door. Gojo's eyes. She had not said anything, because she had already said the thing she had to say, last night, in the dark, with her head on his chest.

Now she stood up. She came across to the bed. She sat on the edge of it, by his knee, and put her hand flat on his chest. Over the bandage. Over both.

"So," she said. "Nobody knows."

"Nobody knows," said Shoko.

"Then it's the same as last night." She looked at Mau. "It's following yours. It isn't hurting you. And nobody's taking it out." She looked at Senku's page. At the line underlined twice. "Good."

* * *

Outside, afterwards, Shoko found Gojo behind the clinic, against the back wall, where the barn with the drying leaves threw its shadow.

He was not smoking. He did not smoke. He was leaning against the wall with his hands in his pockets and his blindfold up, looking at nothing.

She leaned on the wall beside him.

"You couldn't see it," she said.

"I couldn't see it."

"When was the last time that happened?"

Gojo thought about it.

"Not since I got here," he said. "Not for a long time before that." He was quiet. "I saw the Architect through a floor of stone, Shoko. All the way up. I looked straight at him." He laughed. A small sound with nothing in it. "And I can't see to the bottom of a kid's shadow."

Shoko did not say anything.

She reached into her coat pocket. She took out the cigarette. Bent. White. Unlit. She held it, the way she held it sometimes, between two fingers, not doing anything with it.

"So you're scared," she said.

"I'm scared," said Gojo.

"Good," said Shoko. "Now you're like the rest of us." She put the cigarette back. "Come and have coffee. Taihei's on his third wash of that pot. Somebody has to tell him it's clean."
