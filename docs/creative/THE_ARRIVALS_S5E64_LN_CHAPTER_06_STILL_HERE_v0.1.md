## Chapter 53 — Still Here

*When: Day 410. The last day of summer, by Nazuna's count, which is the only count in the house that anyone agrees settles it. Three nights ago, for the first time since spring, the dark was longer than the light.*

Nazuna came down off the red stake at first light with the watch in her hand.

She did not go to bed. She walked along the lime line to the north end, where Igris stood with his cloak moving in no wind, and nodded to him. He bowed. She went on, past the dark ridge, where two great blue eyes were closing slowly as the light came up, and in through the back door of the house, and found Anko in the kitchen with her coffee.

She put the watch on the table.

Anko looked at it.

"Done?" she said.

"Done," said Nazuna. She sat down across from her. "The night's longer than the day now. Three nights running. It's past the middle." She was quiet. "From here it only gets longer. All winter. Every night a bit more." She looked at the watch. "It's all mine again."

Anko picked up the watch. She looked at it for a moment, as if it might have changed. Then she put it back on its chain.

"How long was last night?" she said.

Nazuna told her. To the minute. She remembered every number she had ever counted.

Anko did not write it down. She looked at Nazuna across the kitchen table in the grey light, and then, unexpectedly, she reached across and put her hand on top of Nazuna's, once, briefly, the way you touch the arm of a friend at the end of a long road.

"Good summer," said Anko.

"Good summer," said Nazuna. And went to bed.

* * *

Anko went to the board.

Four planks. Every day of the summer, from the first green shoots in the boxes to the tower's last floor. In her plain hand, mostly. In other hands, sometimes. In Mau's crooked one, eight lines at the bottom of the fourth plank. Under it, the days since: *Day 398. Two. Unknown. Watch.* *Day 400. Kamish remade. Two spaces.* *Day 402. The shift (trial).* *Day 403. The shift.*

She took the stub of pencil from behind her ear.

At the very bottom, under everything, she wrote:

*Day 410. Summer, end. (Nazuna's count.)*

She looked at it. Then she added, smaller:

*Everyone home.*

She put the pencil back behind her ear and went to bed. She had slept every night that week. She was still getting used to it.

* * *

At dusk, Mau walked the house.

He did not mean to. He came down after his two minutes in the yard (Shoko had kept the two minutes, shift or no shift, out of what she called principle and Gojo called spite) and found himself walking instead of sitting. Through the long room. Along the corridors. Out the back.

The barn first. The leaves hung in their rows on the poles, brown now. Gold-brown, curled at the edges, the smell of them thick and sweet in the warm dark. Shoko was standing in the door with her coffee, looking at them.

"Ready?" said Mau.

"No," said Shoko. "Dry isn't cured. Curing's longer." She did not look away from the leaves. "Weeks. Maybe more." She paused. "I've waited this long."

She did not take out the cigarette. She did not need to. He could see the shape of it in her coat pocket, where it had always been.

* * *

The workshop. Senku at the bench with a lamp and a lens and the box on the shelf behind him, the one with *NOT YET* on the lid in his blocky capitals. A gold fruit inside, and a flask of water that did not move, and a seed. He did not look up.

"Not yet," he said, before Mau could ask.

"I wasn't going to ask."

"You were going to look at it like you were going to ask." Senku turned the lens. "Ten billion percent not yet. When I know what each one does on its own. When Maomao's done every test she can think of and three she hasn't thought of yet." He looked up at last, briefly. "We've got time. That's the point of a NOT YET box. It means we've got time."

* * *

The kilns. Kaneki raking, early, before the full dark. Three cups on the shelf. Touka on the bench with Ichika asleep against her side under a coat, and Rem beside them, talking quietly about something Mau could not hear. Touka saw him at the edge of the timber yard. She lifted her cup, very slightly. Black. Properly made.

He lifted his hand.

* * *

The west wing. The room at the end with a window toward the sea, where a pencil was moving on paper. Armin, at a desk Wakana had built, with the green book open beside him and his map of the house spread across the whole surface. The house, and the north field, and the river bend, and the lime line. And now, along the low ridge at the north end, in small neat letters, a new mark.

*Kamish (nights).*

Armin saw him in the door.

"Is that right?" he said. "Do you mind?"

"It's right," said Mau.

* * *

The north end.

Himmel's day. He stood at the end of the lime line in the last of the light, the way he did on alternate days, with his hands behind his back and his face toward the north field and the hills beyond it. And beside him, a pace to his left, very straight, Igris. The two of them were not talking. Himmel looked entirely happy about it.

"He's very formal," said Himmel, without turning round. "I said good evening and he bowed for nearly a minute."

*"The hero asked how I was,"* said Igris. *"I told him. At length."*

"He did," said Himmel. "It was wonderful."

Out in the south field, past the corner of the house, two figures were walking slowly through the second flowering of the yellow flowers, not hurrying, not watching anything. Mikasa had her hands empty. Eren was saying something and gesturing at the sky. She was laughing.

Mau watched them for a long time.

* * *

The back step.

Rimuru was sitting on it in human shape with his chin on his hands, watching the dark come down over the yard. Diablo stood behind him. He was not grey any more. He had not been grey for three days. He looked tired in an ordinary way, the way anyone looks at the end of a long summer.

"You're walking around," said Rimuru.

"I'm walking around."

"Ciel says you're counting." Rimuru looked at him. "She says you've been doing it since the stairs."

Mau did not deny it.

"Everyone?" said Rimuru.

"Everyone," said Mau. He sat down on the step beside him. "Everyone's home."

Rimuru was quiet for a while.

"That's a good thing to count," he said.

* * *

Dinner. Taihei's curry, because it was his day of the week for it, and Momo gave him the kitchen at three without a word, which was the highest mark she gave. Umaru on the bench at the end, not standing, eating. Ori between Mau and Frieren, with the list beside her plate, because she had added a line and wanted him to see.

He looked.

At the bottom, under *The soldiers. (Nobody named them.) (Ten.)*, in her careful printing:

*Kamish keeps watch over a house now. Like he asked.*

"It's true," said Ori. "So I wrote it."

"It's true," said Mau.

* * *

He woke the way he always woke.

All at once. Braced. The reset hour.

Two hearts. *Thump*—*thump*. His own, fast, braced against something that was not coming. And the other one, beside it, half a beat behind, cold and slow. Following. Thirteen nights now. It had not missed a beat. It had not done anything at all, except beat, and follow, and learn.

Out of the window, past the lime, the dark ridge at the end of the north field lay long and still, with two great blue eyes open at the front of it. A knight stood at the north end. Somewhere on the far side of the lime line, very faintly, an ant was counting to ten.

Nobody else was outside.

Everyone was asleep.

The foot touched his shin.

Cold. Deliberately cold.

"One," said Frieren. Into the pillow. "Still here."

He waited.

She moved. The way she had every night for thirteen nights. She turned over in the dark and came down the bed a little and put her head on his chest. Her ear flat against him. Over the thin pink line where the burn had been. Over both.

She listened.

*Thump*—*thump*. *Thump*—*thump*.

He felt her count.

"Two," said Frieren.

Very quietly. Not frightened any more. Just counting. The way she counted everything she meant to keep.

He put his hand in her hair.

Through the connecting door, open a crack, Ori was breathing. Milim floated over her bed on the red ribbon, snoring very slightly. On the shelf beside the old grimoire, six flowers. The fifth from the date. The sixth, small and white, that meant *next year*.

Somewhere out in the world, a long way off, the thing that had built a tower was watching. He knew that much. He did not know what else was out there in the dark with it. Something was beating in his chest that nobody understood, and three doors were turning in his shadow, and the summer was over.

He did not know what was coming.

Nobody did.

But the house was asleep. Every one of them. Because three of his were standing in the dark so they could.

"Still here," said Mau.

Two hearts.

One following the other.

Still here.
