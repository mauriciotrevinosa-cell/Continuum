# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 31
## Chapter 55 — The Edge of the Window

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S5 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-18)
**Volume:** 31
**Reading order:** 55
**Continuity state:** Day 407, the long table. Umaru has a new spending sheet ready for the 134 unspent points. Before anything is spent, Ciel (through Rimuru) sees something new at the edges of the window: faint, unfinished outlines beside the stats, as if the Noise were making room. Two of them sit next to where Mau's cursed energy and his mana are. They take no points and cannot be read. They are NOT the Black Heart's empty nameless bar, which is unchanged. Mau chooses, on his own, to hold every unspent point (134) until the Noise finishes. Umaru crosses out her plan and starts a new one ("DON'T TOUCH ANYTHING"). Nobody knows what will happen to the points already spent. Nothing is integrated yet

*When: Day 407, after lunch. The long table. Umaru's newest sheet is pinned in the middle of the first wall section, with a box drawn at the bottom for today's spending. Armin has the tower copy. Yuka has the copy book. Fern is witnessing, because Fern always witnesses. The night shift slept through the morning in Mau's shadow.*

The sheet was ready before he sat down.

*INTELLIGENCE: 440. CUP: ~15x. (Solid.)*

*ENDURANCE: 51. (Mending. Solid.)*

*134.*

And at the bottom, in the box, in Umaru's smallest Ministry hand, a proposal. Three lines of it. Twenty more into Endurance, because the night shift meant the cup was working every night now. Thirty into Intelligence, because Armin had worked out that the refill rate had a curve and they were near a good part of it. The rest kept, because Mau always kept some.

"Twenty and thirty," said Umaru. She tapped the box with her chalk. "Eighty-four kept. The Ministry has reviewed it three times. Armin has reviewed it twice. Yuka has copied it."

"Once," said Yuka. "Neatly."

"Open the window," said Umaru.

Mau opened the window.

* * *

It was the same as it had been every night since the fifth point. The small set of fields. The five words. The numbers. The cup, fifteen times deep, nearly full after a good night. And below it, the second bar. Empty. No name. Exactly as it had been on the night he first saw it.

He looked at it for a moment, the way he did every time. Then he looked at the fields.

*Ciel. Will you measure?*

*Yes,* said Ciel.

He felt her look. Carefully. Every part. The way she always did, before a point went anywhere.

Then she stopped.

*Mau,* she said. *Wait.*

* * *

"She says wait," said Rimuru, from the end of the table.

Umaru's chalk stopped.

*Look at the edges,* said Ciel. *Not the fields. Round them. The places where the window stops.*

Mau looked.

He had never looked at the edges. Nobody looked at the edges of a window. You looked at what was in it.

At the edges, there was something.

Very faint. So faint he would not have seen it without Ciel pointing. Outlines. Thin and grey, like lines drawn in pencil and then rubbed nearly out. Beside the fields. Not in them. Beside them. Shapes the size of a field, with nothing written inside. Two of them sat close to *Intelligence*, one above and one below, the way you would leave room in a list for things you meant to add.

"There's something round the edges," he said aloud. "Outlines. Grey. Empty."

"Like the second bar?" said Armin at once.

"No." Mau looked between them. "The bar's a bar. It's finished. It's just empty. These aren't finished. They're—" He looked for the word. "Drafts."

* * *

*It's the Noise,* said Ciel.

Rimuru said it after her, in her words, so the table could hear.

*"It's the Noise. I can see it working. It's been working on this window since the red gate. Since the first day the window showed only the shadow branch."* A pause. *"I thought it had finished. When the window came up clean. It hadn't. It was just being quiet about it."*

"What's it doing?" said Fern.

*"Making room,"* said Ciel. *"Like a carpenter marking a wall before he cuts it. These are the marks."* Another pause, longer. *"Two of them sit next to his cursed energy. And his mana. Not on them. Next to them. Like it's measuring the distance between the window and the rest of him."*

Nobody at the table said anything.

Umaru put her chalk down.

"The first day," she said. "On the first day, the window only showed the shadow branch. The Ministry wrote it down. *The window doesn't touch the rest of you.*" She looked at the wall. "We built every sheet on that. Every point. *Only the cup.*"

"Every session," said Yuka, very quietly, turning back through the copy book. "Ciel said it every time. *Mana, cursed energy, the eyes, all unchanged.*"

*"They were unchanged,"* said Ciel. *"They still are. Nothing has moved. These outlines don't take points. I tried, gently, just now. Nothing goes in."* A pause. *"They're not ready. I don't know when they will be. I don't know what they'll do when they are."*

* * *

Mau looked at the outlines for a long time.

At the two grey shapes beside *Intelligence*. At the window that had only ever touched one part of him, for a whole summer. At the rest of him. His cursed energy, the part Gojo had measured and Yuta had warmed, that had never once grown from a point. His mana, Frieren's kind, which no point had ever touched either.

He thought about the box at the bottom of Umaru's sheet. Twenty and thirty.

He thought about the floor-ten choice. *I'd rather have a little left.*

"Don't spend anything," he said.

Umaru looked at him.

"Not today," said Mau. "Not the twenty. Not the thirty. Not one." He closed the window. "If the Noise is making room for the rest of me, I don't want to put anything anywhere until it's done. I don't know what a point does if it goes in before the room's finished." He looked at the sheet. "I'll keep all of them. Until we know."

"All hundred and thirty-four," said Armin.

"And whatever comes after."

* * *

Fern had not written anything yet.

She looked at Mau across the long table for a moment. Then she opened the ledger, found the page with his column on it, crooked as ever, and wrote one line underneath, in her small exact hand.

*Day 407. Points held. All. (His choice.)*

She turned the ledger round so he could see it. He nodded.

* * *

Umaru stood up on the bench.

She took the chalk. She went to the first wall section, to her newest sheet, to the box at the bottom with its three careful lines that the Ministry had reviewed three times.

She drew a line through all three.

Then she took the sheet down, folded it once, and put it in her sash. She pinned a blank sheet in its place. At the top, in the largest letters the Ministry had ever used, she wrote:

*DON'T TOUCH ANYTHING.*

And under it, smaller:

*The window is growing. (Ciel.) Two outlines by CE and mana. (Not ready.) (Not the bar.)*

*134. HELD.*

*New plan: pending.*

She looked at it. Then she turned round on the bench and looked at Mau, very seriously, with her hood half down.

"The Ministry is going to need a much bigger sheet," she said.

"How much bigger?"

Umaru thought about it.

"All the walls," she said. "Maybe the ceiling."

* * *

Afterwards, when the table had gone back to its afternoon, Armin stayed.

He had the copy of the tower in front of him, and on the back of it his columns of arithmetic, the ones that had worked out the cup and the curve. He was looking at them with the expression he wore when a sum had grown a question.

"What happens to the ones you've already spent?" he said. "The four hundred and forty. If the window reaches the rest of you." He tapped the columns. "Do they stay in the cup? Or does the window go back and count them again?"

Mau looked at him.

"I don't know," he said.

"Neither does Ciel," said Armin. "I asked." He turned the sheet over, so the tower was face up again. "I'll keep the columns, though. In case."

He went off toward the west wing with his map and his sums.

Mau sat alone at the long table for a while, with the window closed and a hundred and thirty-four points he was not going to spend. Under the table, very faintly, his shadow turned. The cup full. The bar empty. And somewhere at the edges of a window nobody else could see, two grey shapes waiting to be finished.
