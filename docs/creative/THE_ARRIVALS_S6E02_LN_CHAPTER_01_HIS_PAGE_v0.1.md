*When: Day 418, after lunch. Six days since the day with nothing in it. Frost on the north-field grass two mornings running. The long table is covered in Umaru's winter sheets.*

The winter list was long.

Umaru had started it on Day 413 and it had grown every day since, one sheet, then two, then a third pinned beside them on the first wall section with a line drawn under the heading in chalk so firm it had snapped the chalk. *FIRST FROST.* Under it, in the Ministry's square hand, everything the house had not had to think about since spring.

*Firewood: split, stacked, covered. (Stark. Taihei. NOT Milim.)*

*North roof: the corner over the west wing. (Wakana says Thursday.)*

*Barn: the tobacco does not freeze. (Shoko. Non-negotiable.)*

*The fort: insulated. (Ori has a plan. Ori has three plans.)*

Wakana was arguing with Senku about the north roof. Senku wanted to try something with tar. Wakana did not want to try anything with tar on a roof he had built with his own hands. They had been arguing about it since the soup.

Mau sat at the end of the table with his chin on his hand and listened to them. It was the best kind of afternoon. Nothing in it was about him.

"Mau," said Umaru.

He looked up.

She had another sheet in her hands. Not a winter one. He knew it at once, because it had been pinned in the middle of the first wall section for eleven days and he had walked past it every morning. *DON'T TOUCH ANYTHING.* The Ministry's largest letters. And under them, smaller: *The window is growing. (Ciel.) Two outlines by CE and mana. (Not ready.) (Not the bar.)* And under that, *134. HELD.*

"The Ministry needs to check your page," said Umaru.

"Now?"

"It's been eleven days." She put the sheet down on the table in front of him, on top of the north roof. Wakana made a noise. "The Ministry checks every week. That's the rule. The Ministry made the rule." She looked at him. "Are the outlines still grey?"

"I don't know," said Mau. "I haven't looked."

"Then look," said Umaru. "Please."

* * *

He opened the window.

It came up the way it always did, small and clean in the space just in front of his eyes where nobody else could see it. The small set of fields. The five words. *Intelligence: 440.* The cup, fifteen times deep, full. Below it, the second bar, the one with no name, empty, exactly as it had been on the night Frieren said *don't fill it*.

He looked at the edges.

He had looked at the edges every few days since Ciel showed him. He knew what to expect. Faint grey lines, rubbed nearly out, the shapes of fields with nothing written inside them. Two of them sitting close to *Intelligence*, one above and one below. *Drafts.*

They were not grey.

He sat very still.

They were not grey and they were not drafts. They had edges now, sharp ones, the colour of the other fields. And from the edges of the window, from the places where the window stopped, thin lines were running into them. Many lines. Fine as hair. Coming in from every side, the way water comes into a footprint in wet sand, and the two outlines were filling.

One of them had a word in it now. Half a word. He could not read it yet. He knew what it was going to say.

"Mau?" said Umaru.

"They're not grey," said Mau.

* * *

The first thing was heat.

Not a fever. Not yet. A warmth in the middle of him, under the two hearts, low, the way a stove feels from across a room. Pleasant, almost. He noticed it the way you notice the sun come out.

The second thing was that it did not stop.

It rose. Through his chest, his arms, his face. Warm, then hot, then something that was not temperature at all but *pressure*, the feeling of a room filling with people faster than the doors could let them in. He felt his cursed energy move. Not the way it moved when he used it. The way the river moved in spring, when the snow went all at once in the mountains and the water came down past the bend higher than anyone had seen it, carrying things.

At the window, Gojo's head turned.

He had been leaning there all afternoon with his blindfold down, half asleep, listening to Wakana and Senku argue about tar. He was not half asleep now. He pushed the blindfold up and looked across the long room at Mau with his bare eyes, and his face did something it almost never did.

"Hey," said Gojo. Very quietly. "Hey. What are you doing?"

"Nothing," said Mau. "I'm not doing anything. It's—"

The lamps flickered.

All of them. The three on the long table and the two on the wall by the stove. It was the middle of the afternoon and they were not lit; Taihei never lit them before dusk. They flickered anyway, the dead wicks catching a pale blue that was not fire, and went out, and caught again.

Frieren was on her feet.

She had been at the far end of the table with Fern and the green book. She was not there now. She was beside him with her hand on his arm before anyone saw her cross the room, and she was looking at the lamps.

"That's mana," she said. "That's yours."

"I'm not—"

"I know you're not." Her hand tightened. "It's leaking."

* * *

*Ciel.*

*I see it.*

Her voice was very level. It was the voice she had used on forty when she said *I'll tell you when you're dying*, and he hated that he knew it.

*It's the Noise,* said Ciel. *It's finishing. The room it was making. It's not making room any more. It's moving in.* A pause. *All of it. At once.*

*All of what?*

*The points.* Another pause. Longer. *Mau. The four hundred and forty. It's not starting from now. It's going back. It's taking every point you ever spent and putting it into your cursed energy and your mana as well as the cup. All four hundred and forty. Into a body that was built for none of them.*

The heat in his chest went up again.

He put his hand flat on the table to steady himself and the wood under his palm went warm, and then hot, and Wakana said "*Hey*—" and pulled the north roof sheet out from under it just before it scorched.

*It isn't attacking you,* said Ciel. *I want you to know that. Nothing's attacking you. It's yours. It's your own power, arriving. It's just arriving faster than you can hold it.* A pause. *Like pouring a bucket into a cup.*

*How do I stop it?*

*You don't,* said Ciel. *I don't think it can stop now. It's too far in.* Her voice did not change. *But I can talk to it. I think. I've never tried.*

* * *

Around him, the long room moved.

Nobody told anyone anything. That was the thing he would remember afterward, when he could remember anything clearly. A year ago the house would have stood and stared. A year ago somebody would have said *what's happening* and somebody else would have said *I don't know* and they would have stood there in the not-knowing while it got worse.

Nobody stood there.

Fern was already at the door to the yard, saying "Shoko, Maomao, now," not loudly, to whoever was nearest, and Stark was gone through it before she finished. Anko came down the stairs with her coat over her nightclothes because she had been asleep, and looked once at the lamps burning blue in the middle of the afternoon, and turned round and started moving the children out of the long room toward the kitchen without a word, Ichika by the hand, the five in front of her, Ori last, Ori looking back over her shoulder at him the whole way.

Rimuru was on the bench. He had been on the bench. He was not grey any more, and he put both his hands flat on the bench on either side of him and closed his eyes, and Mau felt the thread between them go taut, the way it had gone taut on the hundred and first floor when Rimuru held the walls up from a back step.

Umaru had not moved.

She stood at the end of the table with the DON'T TOUCH ANYTHING sheet in her hands, very pale, and she was writing on it. With the chalk. Small. Fast. Everything.

*Day 418. After lunch. The outlines filled. (Not grey.) Heat. The lamps. Frieren: mana, his, leaking. Gojo: CE. Ciel: the Noise. All at once. Retroactive.*

She underlined the last word twice.

Then she looked up at him across the long table, with her hood half down and her face very white.

"The Ministry is here," said Umaru. "The Ministry is writing it down. Whatever it is." Her voice shook once and steadied. "Somebody will know what to do. Somebody always does."

At the window, Gojo had not taken his eyes off him.

"It's still rising," said Gojo.

*I know,* said Ciel, inside Mau, very quietly. *I'm going to ask it for help.*
