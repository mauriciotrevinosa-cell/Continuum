*When: Day 418, the same afternoon, then the clinic, then night. The lamps in the long room are still burning blue with no oil in them.*

Ciel had never asked the Noise for anything.

She had watched it. For more than a year she had watched it, the way you watch weather from a window. She had watched it build the walls. She had watched it read things through tethers and fold them small and keep them in the dark where even the eyes did not want to look. She had watched it make room in a window, slowly, for weeks, while everyone thought the window was finished. She had told Mau what it was doing, when she could tell. She had told Rimuru. She had never once turned to it and spoken.

She did not know if it could hear her.

*Please,* said Ciel.

Mau heard her say it. Inside him. Not to him.

*I can't hold this. He can't hold this. You're pouring it in faster than he was built for, and it's going to break him, and then there won't be anything to pour it into.* A pause. *I know you're not doing it to hurt him. I've watched you. You've never once done anything to hurt him. You built his walls. You kept the bag. You made the room.* Another pause. *So help me. Slow down. Show me where it's going.*

For a moment, nothing.

The heat went on rising. The lamps went on burning blue. Somewhere very far away Mau heard Shoko's voice in the yard, coming fast.

Then the heat stopped rising.

It did not go down. It stopped. The way water stops at the top of a dam, trembling, held.

*Oh,* said Ciel.

* * *

He did not remember the clinic.

He remembered the long room, and then he remembered the clinic bed under his back, and the ceiling, and Shoko's hand flat on his chest, and Maomao's fingers on his wrist, and in between there was nothing except heat and the sound of Ciel talking very quietly to something that did not answer in words.

*It's showing me,* she said. Not to him. To Rimuru, down the thread, and to him because he was there. *It's letting me see where it's putting things. Look. The cup's taking its share. The cup's fine, the cup was built for it. But the other two—* A pause. *Your cursed energy. Your mana. They're full. They were full ten minutes ago. It's still coming.*

*Where's the rest going?*

*Into you,* said Ciel. *Into your blood. Your skin. Your eyes. Anywhere it fits.* Her voice did not shake. *That's the fever. That's the lamps. It's leaking out of you because there's nowhere left for it to go.*

On the bench by the clinic wall, Rimuru had his eyes shut and both hands flat on his knees.

"There's room," said Rimuru suddenly. Out loud. To nobody. "There's room somewhere. I can feel it. Ciel—"

*I know,* said Ciel.

*Where?* said Mau.

She did not answer at once.

* * *

*The bar,* said Ciel.

He knew which one. There was only one.

Under the cup, in the window nobody else could see. The second bar. The one that had come on the night of the hundred and first floor, the night the cold heart started beating half a step behind his own. Empty. No name. Not even a mark in the tower's script.

*It's big,* said Ciel. *I didn't know how big until now. The Noise showed me. It's bigger than the cup. It's bigger than all three put together.* A pause. *And it's empty. It's the only thing in you that is.*

*Frieren said don't fill it.*

*I know.*

*Not until we know what it's for.*

*I know,* said Ciel. Very gently. *We still don't know. I can't see into it. The Noise can't see into it either, it showed me that too. It's as blind there as Gojo.* A pause. *But it's the only thing big enough, Mau. And if we don't put it somewhere in the next few minutes, it's going to put itself somewhere, and the somewhere is you.*

He opened his eyes.

The clinic swam. Shoko, close, with her coffee nowhere. Maomao at his wrist. Gojo in the doorway with his bare eyes on the middle of Mau's chest, at the place under the two hearts where the root went down. And Frieren, on the stool by the bed, holding his hand. He had not felt her take it.

"Frieren," he said.

She leaned in.

"The bar," he said. His voice was not right. It was too hot. "The empty one. Ciel wants to put it there. The extra. It's the only place." He swallowed. "You said don't fill it."

Frieren looked at him for a long moment.

Then at Rimuru on the bench, who had opened his eyes and was nodding, very slightly, grey round the edges again.

Then back at him.

"I said don't fill it until we knew what it was for," said Frieren. Her hand did not move. "We don't. We might never." She leaned closer. "I would rather it held something we don't understand than lose you to something we do." She held his hand harder. "Fill it. Better that than you."

* * *

*Thank you,* said Ciel. Not to him. To Frieren, though Frieren could not hear her. Then, to the other thing, the one that did not answer in words: *There. The bar. Under the cup. Put it there.*

He felt it go.

It was like the north field after the snow, when the meltwater that had been standing on the grass all morning finds the ditch all at once and goes. A long, sliding, downward rush. Out of his blood and his skin and his eyes and the hot pressure under his ribs, and down, and in, into something cold and very deep that he had never once felt from the inside.

The heat went out of him.

Not all at once. The way a fever goes when it breaks: a sweat, a shudder, and then suddenly the room is the right temperature again and you are lying in a wet shirt wondering when that happened.

In the long room across the yard, the lamps went out.

Taihei, who had been standing in the kitchen doorway watching them burn blue for an hour, said "Oh," and went in and lit them properly, with a taper, because it was getting dark.

* * *

It was nearly midnight when he opened the window again.

Shoko let him. She sat on the end of the bed with her coffee, which somebody had finally brought her, and watched his face while he did it, and said nothing.

The small set of fields. The five words. *Intelligence: 440.* The cup, fifteen times deep, full.

And beside *Intelligence*, above it and below it, where the grey outlines had been, two new fields.

Not drafts. Finished. With words in them now, in the tower's script, that he could read. *Cursed energy.* *Mana.* And numbers. Larger than he had expected. Much larger. He did not know yet what the numbers meant; nobody did. But they were not grey, and they were not empty, and they were his.

Under the cup, the second bar.

Not empty.

Not full, either. Not close. A line of something in the bottom of it, dark, very thin, like the first water in a cistern after a long dry season. Still no name. Still no mark. He looked at it for a long time and could not tell what the dark in it was or what it would do.

*I can't see into it,* said Ciel. *Nobody can.* A pause. *But it took it. All of it. And you're here.*

"Well?" said Shoko.

"Two new fields," said Mau. "Cursed energy. Mana. With numbers." He closed the window. "And the bar's not empty any more."

Shoko looked at him for a long moment. Then she put two fingers on his wrist. Then her palm flat on his chest, for a moment, over the two hearts.

"Not fine," she said. "But calm." She picked up her coffee. "Sleep. Here. Tonight."

* * *

Umaru was waiting outside the clinic door when Frieren came out.

She had been there for three hours, on the step, with the DON'T TOUCH ANYTHING sheet folded in her lap and her chalk in her hand. She stood up very fast.

"Is he—"

"He's asleep," said Frieren. "He's all right."

Umaru let out a breath. Then she unfolded the sheet and looked at it. At everything she had written in the long room, small and fast, while the lamps burned blue.

"Can we spend them now?" she said. "The hundred and thirty-four? The room's finished. The Ministry would like to know."

From inside the clinic, very quietly, through Rimuru, who was still on the bench and still grey round the edges, came Ciel's answer.

*"Not yet,"* said Rimuru. *"Let it settle. A few weeks. Then we'll see."*

Umaru looked at the sheet for a moment longer.

Then she took it back to the long room, and took down the old one from the first wall section, and pinned a blank one in its place, and wrote across the top of it, in the Ministry's largest letters:

*IT HAPPENED. (Day 418.) HE'S FINE. (Not fine. Calm.) (Shoko.)*

*CE: YES. MANA: YES. THE BAR: SOMETHING. (Nobody knows.)*

*134. STILL HELD.*

She stood back and looked at it. Then she added, at the very bottom, smaller:

*Nobody had to be told what to do.*
