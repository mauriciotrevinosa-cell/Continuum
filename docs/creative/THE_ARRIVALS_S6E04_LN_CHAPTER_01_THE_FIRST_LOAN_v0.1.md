# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 32
## Chapter 7 — The First Loan

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-20)
**Volume:** 32
**Reading order:** 7
**Continuity state:** Day 424. Rimuru's idea: the thread that has run between him and Mau since Ciel can carry more than words. Built carefully with Ciel, and with the Noise watching and not objecting, it becomes the first loan contract: Mau lends one thing (the sight of one soldier) to one person (Anko) for one stretch of time, and his body carries the weight. Umaru writes the terms, because terms are the Ministry's (what is lent, to whom, for how long, who carries it, and that either side ends it by saying "return"). Fern witnesses. At dusk on the red stake, with a soldier standing on the far hill, Anko sees past the ridge with her own eyes for the first time. The weight on Mau is a dull ache behind the eyes, nothing worse. Shoko allows it with a limit. This is the first of the new unions

*When: Day 424, afternoon into dusk. Two nights of scouts past the ridge, written on Anko's sheets. The titan is still asleep in its valley. Rimuru has spent both days at the end of the long table with Ciel, talking to nobody anyone else could hear.*

"It's the thread," said Rimuru.

He had a cup of tea in front of him that had gone cold an hour ago. He had not touched it. Around him the long table had filled up slowly through the afternoon, the way it did when people could tell something was about to be explained.

"The one between me and Mau," he said. "Since Ciel. Since the beginning." He turned the cup a little. "Everyone knows it carries words. Ciel talks down it. I say what she says. That's what it's been for." He looked up. "But on a hundred and one, I held his walls up down it. From the back step. That wasn't words."

"No," said Mau quietly.

"So it can carry more than words," said Rimuru. "And if it can carry more than words between me and him—" He stopped. "Ciel thinks it could be made. Between him and anyone. Carefully. One thing at a time."

"What thing?" said Fern.

Rimuru looked down the table. At Anko, at the end, with her sheets in front of her. Three nights of them now.

"The sight," he said. "One soldier's. For a little while."

* * *

It was Umaru who insisted on the terms.

She did not ask whether there would be terms. She took out a fresh sheet and put it in the middle of the table and wrote across the top of it, before anyone could say anything:

*LOAN. (First.)*

"The Ministry will write it," she said. "If it's going to be a contract, it's going to be a *contract*. With lines."

"It's not that kind of—" began Rimuru.

"Everything is that kind," said Umaru. "Ask Echidna."

Down the table, the woman in black who kept every book did not look up from the one she was reading. But the corner of her mouth moved.

So they wrote it. All of them, round the table, arguing over every word, the way the house argued over the wording of everything that mattered.

*What is lent: the sight of one soldier. (Not the soldier. Not a command. Only what it sees.)*

*To whom: Anko.*

*For how long: from dusk until she says return, or until he does. (Not longer than an hour. Shoko.)*

*Who carries it: Mau. (His body. Not hers.)*

*How it ends: either says "return". Then it returns. (Rimuru says it will. Ciel says it will.)*

*What it costs: nothing from the cup. (Ciel.) A weight on him. (Ciel doesn't know how much yet.)*

*Witnessed:*

Fern took the pencil and signed it.

* * *

They tried it on the back step, because Shoko would not have it anywhere she could not see his face.

Rimuru sat on one side of Mau. Anko sat on the other. Ciel, inside him, was very quiet, the kind of quiet that meant she was concentrating on something very small.

*I'm going to lay it down the thread,* she said. *Like a string between two cans. You hold one end. Rimuru hands her the other. Then I let the soldier's sight go down it.* A pause. *The Noise is watching. I can feel it. It isn't stopping me.*

*Is that good?*

*I don't know,* said Ciel. *It's never not stopped me before. I've never asked it anything before.* A pause. *Hold still.*

He held still.

He felt it go. Not out of him. *Through* him. A thin line of something running from the place behind his eyes where he turned the soldiers' pages, out along the thread to Rimuru, and from Rimuru, carefully, the way you pour from a full jug into a thimble, across the step.

Anko made a small sound.

* * *

She had her eyes shut. She had shut them when Rimuru said to, without arguing, which was not like her. Now she sat very straight on the back step with her coat round her and her hands flat on her knees, and her face had gone perfectly still.

"Anko?" said Fern.

"There's a hill," said Anko.

Nobody said anything.

"I'm on a hill," said Anko. "It's—" She stopped. "It's getting dark. There's a valley. There's something in the bottom of it. Big. Lying down." Her voice was very level. Her hands were not. "It's breathing."

"That's the titan," said Mau quietly. "The seventh soldier. It's been on that hill two nights."

Anko did not answer for a long time.

"I've walked the round for years," she said at last. "Every hundred paces. Count of ten. Looking out." Her eyes stayed shut. "I always wondered what was out there. In the dark. Past where the lamp goes." She breathed out. "It's grass. It's hills. It's a big thing asleep." She was quiet. "It's just the world."

On the step beside her, Mau felt it.

The weight. Not pain. A dull pressure behind his eyes, the way your arm feels when you have held something out at arm's length for a long time. Not heavy. Steady. His. He had lent her something, and his body was carrying it, and it was exactly as Ciel had said: nothing from the cup. Only him.

*How is it?* said Ciel.

*Like holding a door open,* said Mau. *For someone carrying something.*

*Good,* said Ciel. *That's what it's meant to feel like.*

* * *

Shoko gave it forty minutes.

At forty minutes she put two fingers on Mau's wrist and looked at his face and said, "Return," as if she had the right to say it, which on the back step of the clinic she did.

"Return," said Anko. At once.

It came back. Up the thread, through Rimuru, into him. The weight lifted off the back of his eyes like a hand taken away.

Anko opened her eyes.

She sat on the step for a moment, looking at the yard. At the lime line. At the north end, where Igris stood. At the ridge, low against the last of the light, with the dark shape of Kamish lying along it.

Then she took her pencil from behind her ear and her sheets out of her coat, and on the bottom of the night's page, under three nights of a soldier's sight in Mau's words, she wrote one line in her own.

*Day 424. Saw it myself.*

* * *

Umaru took the contract back to the long room and pinned it on the first wall section, beside IT HAPPENED, in a space she had cleared for it.

Then she stood back and looked at it. At the lines. At Fern's signature. At the words *First* at the top.

"There'll be more," she said. To nobody. To the wall.

Behind her, at the end of the long table, Rimuru had finally picked up his cold tea.

"There will," he said. "Carefully." He drank it, and made a face, and drank the rest anyway. "One at a time."
