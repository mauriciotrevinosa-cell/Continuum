# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 32
## Chapter 9 — If I Say Go

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-20)
**Volume:** 32
**Reading order:** 9
**Continuity state:** Days 427–429. Ciel finds something new the Noise has been building since the overload: Mau can trade places with any of his shadows (the Shadow Exchange), and the Noise has bent it so he can send the people standing near him to a shadow's place instead of himself. It has a long cooldown, about a day on Ciel's count. Mau does not hide it. At the long table he proposes the emergency plan out loud: the shadows build a shelter under the ridge, where Kamish lies every night; if something ever corners the house, he sends everyone near him there and stays; when the cooldown ends he follows, or he handles it. Frieren hates the "and stays". Gojo: "That's me on the roof again." Mau: "No. That's me telling you before." The house agrees on terms (only in an extreme case; he says it out loud first; he comes after). They test it with Stark and a sack of flour. The shadows dig at night. Nothing is used in anger

*When: Day 427, morning, then two nights. The map on the long-room wall has three pins, none of them moved. Frost every morning now.*

*There's something else,* said Ciel.

He was at the pump, washing his face in water so cold it hurt. He stopped with his hands in it.

*Something new?*

*Something the Noise built.* A pause. *I think since the overload. Maybe before. I didn't see it until the shadows started riding in other things. I was looking at how that works, and it was there. Next to it. Finished.* Another pause. *It's been finished for a while. It was waiting for me to notice.*

*What is it?*

*You can trade places with them,* said Ciel. *Any of your shadows. Wherever it is. You go where it was. It comes where you were. One breath.*

He took his hands out of the water.

*Wherever it is?*

*Wherever. The valley. The hills. The ridge.* She was quiet for a moment. *But that isn't the strange part. The strange part is what the Noise did to it.*

* * *

He told the long table at lunch. All of it.

He did not decide anything first. He did not sit with it for three days and bring them a plan with the edges already sanded. He came in from the yard with his hands still red from the pump and sat down at the end of the table and said, before the soup, "There's something new and I need to tell you before I decide anything about it."

That was how the house knew it mattered.

"I can trade places with a shadow," he said. "Any of them. Wherever they are. I go there, it comes here." He looked down the table. "That's the first part. Ciel thinks it's from the tower. Something I watched or something the Noise read. It doesn't matter."

"What's the second part?" said Fern.

"The Noise changed it." He put his hands flat on the table. "I don't have to be the one who goes. If people are standing near me, I can send them. To where the shadow is. And stay."

The long table was very quiet.

"How many people?" said Armin.

"Ciel doesn't know yet. As many as are close. Close enough to touch, maybe a little more."

"And then?"

"And then it's done for a while. A day, Ciel thinks. Maybe a bit less." He looked at his hands. "Then I can do it again. Or follow them."

* * *

Frieren did not say anything for a long time.

She was sitting beside him. She had been sitting beside him at every meal since the night the lamps burned blue, closer than she used to, her shoulder against his arm. She did not move away now.

"And stay," she said at last.

He knew she would say it. He had known on the way in from the pump.

"And stay," he said.

"Here. With whatever it is. Alone." Her voice did not rise. "While we're somewhere else, and can't come back, because you've sent us somewhere we can't come back from for a day."

"Yes."

"That's me on the roof," said Gojo, from the window. He had his blindfold down and his arms folded. "That's me at the top of everything, so nobody else has to. That's what the tower was teaching. You said yourself, a week ago, at this table."

"No," said Mau.

He turned in his chair so he could see Gojo.

"The tower taught me to go up alone and not tell anyone," he said. "To decide in the dark. To come back in pieces and say *I'm all right*." He did not look away. "This is me telling you. Before. Before anything's happened. Before I've decided. At lunch." He looked down the table. "If I wanted to do it the way the tower taught me, I wouldn't have said anything. I'd have just done it, one day, and you'd have woken up on a ridge."

Gojo looked at him for a long moment.

Then he unfolded his arms.

"Fair," he said quietly.

* * *

They made the terms.

Umaru wrote them, on a fresh sheet, with everyone arguing over every line, the way they had argued over the loan.

*SHADOW EXCHANGE. (Emergency only.)*

*When: only if something comes that could corner the house, or trap us, or take everything. (Not for a titan. Not for a wolf. Not for a bad dream.) (Shoko.)*

*How: he says GO out loud first. Then he sends whoever is near him. (He does not send people without saying it. Ever.) (Frieren.)*

*Where: under the ridge. The shadows build it. (Kamish keeps it.)*

*After: when it's ready again, he comes. Or he handles it and then he comes. (He comes.) (Ori.)*

*If he can run, he runs. (Maki.)*

"I'm not writing that last one," said Umaru. "It's not a term, it's a threat."

"Write it," said Maki.

Umaru wrote it.

* * *

They tested it on the second afternoon, with Stark and a sack of flour.

Stark volunteered. Nobody else was allowed to; Shoko said one person and something that could not get hurt, and Stark said he could not get hurt, and Fern said that was not what Shoko meant, and Stark went anyway.

One soldier stood at the top of the ridge, by Kamish's head. Mau stood in the yard by the pump with Stark on one side of him, holding a sack of flour, looking nervous.

"Go," said Mau. Out loud. First.

Stark was gone.

Not with a sound. Not with a flash. The place beside Mau was empty, and the soldier was standing there instead, dark, faceless, spear upright, and the sack of flour was lying on the frosted ground at its feet where Stark had dropped it, because the flour had not been close enough.

At the top of the ridge, very small, Stark stood beside Kamish's great head with his arms out, looking at his empty hands.

Then he looked round. Down at the yard. At the whole house standing along the lime line staring up at him.

He waved.

Kamish lifted his head and looked at him sideways with one blue eye, the way an old dog looks at a puppy that has been dropped into its basket.

*It worked,* said Ciel. *It took him. Not the flour. Close enough to touch.* A pause. *And it's gone now. The trade. I can feel it. Empty. It's going to take a while to fill.*

"Is he all right?" called Fern.

"I'm fine!" shouted Stark from the ridge. "Kamish is looking at me!"

"Walk back," called Fern.

"It's a long way!"

"Walk," said Fern.

Stark walked back.

* * *

The shadows dug at night.

He did not have to tell anyone. They knew. Everyone had been at the table. But he went out to the north end anyway, after dinner, with Frieren, and stood beside Igris and called them up, the soldiers, forty of them, and Tank, and sent them out across the north field to the ridge, where Kamish lay.

"Under you," he said to the old dragon. "A room. Big enough for everyone. Dry. With a way in that only they know."

Kamish lifted his head.

*"A place to keep a house,"* said Kamish, in his slow voice, like stone under water. *"While the small king stands outside it."*

"Only if I have to."

*"Only if you have to,"* agreed Kamish. He laid his head down again on the ridge. *"I will keep it. As I keep the rest."*

The soldiers dug.

All night, in the dark, under the old dragon's long body, without a sound. By dawn there was nothing to see from the house. The ridge looked exactly as it had. Kamish lay along it, as he always did, with his head on his paws and his two blue eyes half shut.

Under him, a room was beginning.

* * *

At the reset hour Frieren did not put her foot on his shin.

She lay on her side facing him in the dark, very close, and he could feel her looking at him.

"You told us," she said.

"I said I would."

"I know." She was quiet. "I still hate it. The *and stay*."

"I know."

"I'm not going to stop hating it."

"I know," said Mau. "I'd hate it too."

She put her hand flat on his chest. Over the two hearts.

"You told us before," she said again, very quietly, as if she were saying it to herself. As if she wanted to remember it. "Not after."

"Two," she said.

"Two," said Mau.
