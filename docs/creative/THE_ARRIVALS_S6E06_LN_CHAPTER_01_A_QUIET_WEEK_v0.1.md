# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 32
## Chapter 11 — A Quiet Week

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-20)
**Volume:** 32
**Reading order:** 11
**Continuity state:** Days 430–436. A week where nothing happens, and the house knows it is lucky. The first-frost list gets done (firewood stacked, the north roof patched without tar, the fort insulated by Ori's third plan). The room under the ridge is finished; Kamish keeps it. *When: Days 430–436. Cold mornings, clear days. The tobacco is still curing. The soldiers ride in three shadows past the ridge, and every morning Armin checks the pins, and every morning they are where they were.*

Nothing happened all week.

Afterward, the house would remember that. Not because anything was wrong with it. Because it was the last one.

* * *

The firewood went up on Day 430.

Stark and Taihei split it in the yard all morning, and Milim was not allowed near it, and stood at the edge of the yard with her arms folded and her lip out, until Taihei gave her the job of carrying it, one armful at a time, to the stack against the north wall of the barn. She carried it at a speed that made the stack sway. By noon there was enough wood against the barn to last until spring, and a little more, and Stark lay down on top of the pile in the sun and went to sleep.

The north roof went on Day 431.

Wakana did it without tar. Senku watched from the yard with his arms folded, saying nothing, which for Senku was the loudest kind of saying something, and when Wakana came down the ladder at dusk with the last nail in, Senku looked up at the corner over the west wing and said, "Ten billion percent fine," as if it cost him, and Wakana pretended not to hear and was pleased for the rest of the evening.

The fort went on Day 432.

Ori had three plans. She presented all three at breakfast, on three sheets, with drawings. The first involved straw. The second involved Milim. The third involved the old quilts from the cellar, folded double and nailed inside the fort's walls like a second skin, with the curtain doubled too and a flap at the bottom for the cold air, and a small lamp in a jar that Senku would make safe.

The house voted for the third.

Ori had known it would. She had made the first two worse on purpose so the third would win. Umaru saw that, and wrote in the Ministry book, under the date, *Ori: a politician. (Watch her.)*

* * *

The room under the ridge was finished on Day 433.

Mau went out at dusk to see it, with Frieren and Gojo and Shoko, because Shoko would not have him going anywhere with a door in it that she had not seen.

The soldiers had made it well. The way in was a fold in the far side of the ridge, behind a stand of thorn, that you could walk past ten times and not see. Inside, a long low room dug into the hill, dry, with the floor packed hard and the walls shored with timber they had carried out from the yard at night. Big enough for everyone. A little bigger. A shelf along one wall where somebody, at some point, had put three lamps and a jar of oil and a stack of blankets.

"Who put those there?" said Shoko.

Nobody knew.

"Kamish," said Mau, after a moment.

Above them, on top of the ridge, the old dragon lay with his head on his paws.

*"A house should have blankets,"* said Kamish, without lifting his head. *"I remember that much."*

Shoko looked at the blankets for a long time.

Then she nodded once, and went back to the house, and did not say anything else about the room under the ridge, which from Shoko was approval.

* * *

The pins did not move.

Every morning Armin stood at the long-room wall with his coffee and checked them, and every morning Ciel said, through Rimuru, *the valley, the hills, the stream,* and every morning they were where they had been. The titan asleep. The grey pack running the eastern ridges at night and sleeping in the day. The slow blind thing in its hole by the milestone, drinking at dusk.

Ori moved none of them. She checked anyway, every morning, standing in front of the wall with her hands behind her back.

"Still there," she said each time, to nobody, the way she said *two* at the reset hour.

* * *

On Day 434 Shoko took Mau to the barn again.

The leaves hung in their long rows from the rafters. Browner than before. Darker. Smelling deeper.

"Not yet," said Shoko.

"Closer?"

She looked up at them for a long time.

"Closer," she admitted.

* * *

On Day 435, Yuka asked.

She did it at dinner. She did it badly, because she had been practising it all afternoon in her room and had worked it out perfectly, and when the moment came at the long table every word she had practised went out of her head at once.

She put her spoon down. She looked at her bowl.

"My birthday," she said. "Is next week."

The long table went quiet. Not the wave. Just the near end.

"Eight days," said Umaru, who knew everybody's birthday and had them on a sheet.

"Eight days," said Yuka. Her ears were going red. "I'll be eighteen." She stopped. She started again. "And everyone always asks what I want, and I always say nothing, because I don't, because I've got—" She looked round the table. At the house. At all of it. "I've got everything."

Her grandfather, beside her, put his hand very gently on her back.

"But this year I want something," said Yuka. Very fast, before she could stop. "One day. With Oppa." She did not look at Mau. She looked at the table. "Like Frieren's day. On Fern's page. The *Days* page. One day where he isn't anything else. Not training. Not shadows. Not the map." Her face was scarlet now. "Just a day. With my brother." She swallowed. "That's what I want."

* * *

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Mau sat at the end of the table and looked at her. At Yuka, his little sister, probationary, teleport division, Umaru's registry, who had blamed herself for a field once and had never quite stopped, who called him Oppa in a voice that made it sound like the safest word in the world.

He did not get to answer first.

"Yes," said Frieren.

Everyone looked at her.

She was sitting beside Mau, with her shoulder against his arm, the way she sat now. She did not look embarrassed. She looked at Yuka across the long table, steadily, with something in her face that was almost a smile.

"You can have him," said Frieren. "For a day." She paused. "I've had a lot of his days. You should have one."

Yuka stared at her.

"Thank you," she said. In a very small voice.

"Do I get a say?" said Mau.

"No," said Frieren and Fern and Umaru at the same time.

At the far end of the table, Fern had already opened the ledger. She turned to the page that said *Days* at the top, the one that had nine names on it and a line at the bottom about somewhere to come back to, and under the last entry, in her small exact hand, she wrote:

*Day 443. Yuka. (Her eighteenth.) (Her brother.) (The whole day.)*

Nobody objected.

* * *

Later, on the back step, Mau found her.

She was sitting with her knees up and her coat round her, looking at the yard. She did not look round when he sat down beside her.

"Eighteen," said Mau.

"Don't."

"That's so old."

"*Don't*." But she was smiling. She hid it against her knees.

"What do you want to do?" said Mau. "On the day."

Yuka thought about it.

"I don't know," she said. "Something. Not here." She looked up at the sky. Clear and cold and full of strange stars, *The Party* and *The Hundred and First* and the one Umaru had written on the back of the invitation card. "Somewhere I've never been. I don't know where." She paused. "You choose."

"I'll think of something," said Mau.

"You always do," said Yuka.

She leaned against his shoulder. Just for a moment. The way she had leaned against him in the field, a long time ago, when she was small and frightened and he was the only thing in the world that did not frighten her.

Then she sat up and went in, because it was cold.

* * *

Far to the north-east, past the hills on Armin's map, past the valley where the titan slept and the soldier rode in its shadow, past where any of the soldiers had gone, the fires went on growing.

Nobody in the house knew.

It was a quiet week.

It was the last one.
