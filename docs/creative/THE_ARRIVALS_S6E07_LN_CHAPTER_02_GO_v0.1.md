# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 32
## Chapter 13 — Go

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-20)
**Volume:** 32
**Reading order:** 13

*When: Day 438, late morning. Cold and clear. From the top of the ridge, past the hills, a long brown smudge on the sky where the east road is.*

He called them in at eleven.

He did not have to call loudly. Everyone was already near the long room; nobody had gone far from it all morning. They came in from the yard and down the stairs and in from the kitchen, and stood round the long table in their coats, the way they had stood the morning before. Taihei with a cloth still in his hand. Kessoku together. The witches in a row by the wall. The children at the back with Anko. Ori in front of them, with her hands behind her back.

"It's time," said Mau.

Nobody asked time for what.

* * *

"It takes whoever's close enough to touch," he said. "That's what Ciel said. That's what happened with Stark." He looked round the room. At the house. At more than sixty people standing in it. "I can't touch sixty people."

*You don't have to,* said Ciel. Out loud, through Rimuru. *"Close enough to touch might mean close enough to touch someone who's touching him. I think. The Noise built it to take the people near him. If you're all holding on to each other, you might all count as near."*

"Might," said Shoko.

*"Might,"* said Ciel. *"I can't test it on sixty people without sending sixty people."*

The long room was quiet for a moment.

Then Momo, by the kitchen door, put down the bowl she was holding and held out her hand to Okarun beside her.

He took it.

That was how it started.

* * *

It went round the room like a fuse burning.

Okarun took Seiko's hand. Seiko took Jiji's, scowling. Taihei put the cloth in his pocket and took Momo's other hand. Kessoku, all four, in a row. Rem took Ram's hand without looking at her, and Ram allowed it, and took Seika's. The witches, one after another, Echidna at the end of their line holding out her pale hand to Daphne's dirty one, and Daphne taking it with a smile. Senku and Byakuya. Touka with Ichika on her hip and Kaneki's hand in her free one. Mikasa and Eren. Armin with the house map rolled under his other arm. Fern and Stark. Himmel, smiling the easy smile as if this were an ordinary thing to do before lunch. Milim, floating a hand's breadth off the floor, holding Umaru's hood because Umaru's hands were full of chalk. Rimuru and Diablo. Shoko and Maomao. Anko and Nazuna. The five. The witch-hat girls. Yuka, between her grandparents, holding her grandmother's hand so tightly her knuckles were white, and not looking at Mau at all.

The chain went round the long table and back.

Yuta took Maki's hand.

Gojo did not move.

* * *

"No," said Gojo.

He was at the window, where he always was, with his blindfold up and his bare eyes on Mau across the room.

"No," he said again. "I'm staying."

"Gojo—"

"Twenty thousand." Gojo did not raise his voice. "And in front of them, people you can't see clearly yet, and behind them, things nobody has names for. And you, on your own, having promised Rimuru you won't kill a single one of them." He took a step away from the window. "I'm the strongest, Mau. That's not a boast. It's just true. I'm staying."

"If you stay," said Mau, "they go home saying I needed you."

Gojo stopped.

"That's what Rimuru asked for," said Mau. "Not that I win. That they go home and say *we went to fight the monster, and he was alone, and he didn't kill any of us*." He did not look away. "If you're standing next to me, that's not what they'll say. They'll say *the monster had the strongest man in the world beside him, and that's why we lost.* And they'll come back." He paused. "Next year. With more."

Gojo looked at him for a long time.

"That's a good argument," he said at last. Very quietly. "I hate it."

"I know."

"I'm not going to stop hating it."

"I know," said Mau. "Frieren said that too."

Gojo crossed the long room.

He did not take Mau's hand. He took Maki's free one, at the end of the chain, and stood there with his blindfold up, looking at Mau, with his jaw very tight.

"If you can run," said Maki.

"I'll run," said Mau.

* * *

Frieren was last.

She had been standing beside him the whole time. Not holding anyone. Waiting.

The chain went all the way round the room now, from Gojo at one end, round the long table, past the children and the witches and the stove, all the way back to Ori, at the other end, with her small hand out, waiting for someone to take it.

Frieren took Ori's hand.

Then she turned and held out her other hand to him.

He took it.

She held it harder than she had ever held it. Her cold fingers, all the way round his, round the scars and the two fingers that would not close. She did not say anything. She looked at him, steadily, with her face very still, and he understood that she was not going to say anything, because if she started she would not stop.

"Run if you can," she said at last. Very quietly. Just that.

"I will."

"You told us first."

"I told you first."

She did not let go.

He had to be the one to let go. He had known he would have to be.

He held her hand a moment longer. Then he let it go, and stepped back, and the chain closed round the space where he had been. Sixty people and more, holding on to each other, round a long table in a long room, with an empty place in the middle of them where he stood.

He looked at them all once.

"Go," he said.

Out loud. First.

* * *

The long room was empty.

Not all at once. In a breath. One moment it was full of people holding hands, and the next there were sixty soldiers standing in a ring round the long table where they had been, dark and faceless, spears upright, and a cloth on the floor where Taihei's pocket had been, and the house map lying on the boards where Armin's arm had been, and nothing else.

He stood in the middle of the ring of soldiers in the silent long room.

*It took them,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *All of them. Holding hands counts.* A pause. *They're under the ridge. I can feel Kamish. He's got them.*

He let out a breath he had been holding since dawn.

*And it's gone,* said Ciel. *Cooling. A day, maybe less.* A pause. *You can't send them anywhere else now. And you can't go to them.*

"I know," he said aloud, to the empty room.

He sent the soldiers down. All sixty, into the boards, into his shadow.

Then he picked up the house map off the floor, and rolled it carefully, and put it on the long table where Armin would find it.

* * *

Under the ridge, it was dark.

Frieren knew where the shelf was. She had come out with Shoko and Gojo on the day the room was finished. She let go of Ori's hand and felt her way along the wall in the dark, with sixty people breathing round her and a child somewhere starting to cry and Touka's voice saying *shh, shh*, until her fingers found the shelf, and the lamp, and the jar.

She lit it.

The long low room came up out of the dark round them. The packed floor. The timber walls. Sixty faces, blinking. Gojo at the end of the line, still holding Maki's hand, with his bare eyes on the ceiling, on the earth above them, as if he could see through it. Above that, very faintly, through the hill, a slow vast breathing. Kamish.

Under the lamp, on top of the blankets, a folded sheet of paper.

Frieren picked it up.

She knew the hand before she opened it. Crooked. The letters leaning into each other like people in a crowd.

She read it out loud.

*I'm not taking you away.*

*You're on the ridge. Under Kamish. You're safe here. He'll keep you.*

*If I can run, I'll run. I promise. I'm not staying to be brave.*

*The Exchange is cooling. When it's ready, I'll come. Or I'll come walking.*

*Don't come out until I do.*

She stopped.

"There's more," said Ori.

Frieren looked at the bottom of the sheet.

*I told you first. This is only the part I couldn't say out loud.*

She did not read that part out loud.

She folded the note again and held it, and sat down on the packed floor of the room under the ridge with her back against the timber wall, and Ori came and sat beside her, and leaned against her, and after a moment Frieren put her arm round her.

"He'll come," said Ori.

"He'll come," said Frieren.

Above them, through the hill, Kamish breathed, slow and vast, keeping a house.

* * *

At the lime line, at the east side of the yard, where the road came in, Mau stood alone.

Not alone. Igris on his left, a pace behind, long sword sheathed. Beru on his right, very still for once, antennae laid back. The rest of them in his shadow, waiting. Ninety-eight soldiers. The knight and the ant and the wyvern and the shaman and the bear and the grand marshal, and Corner, and Roof, and Lantern. Everything except Kamish, who lay on the ridge with his head on his paws and a house underneath him.

The east road ran out from the yard across the frosted fields and over the low hills toward the bend.

On the far side of the hills, the brown smudge on the sky had grown into a wall of dust.

*"My liege,"* said Igris. *"They are close."*

"I know," said Mau.

*"My King,"* said Beru. Very quietly, for Beru. *"The queen held your hand a very long time."*

"I know," said Mau.

He put his hands in his coat pockets, and stood at the lime line, and waited.
