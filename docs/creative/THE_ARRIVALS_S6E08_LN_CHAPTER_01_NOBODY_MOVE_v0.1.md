# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 32
## Chapter 15 — Nobody Move

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-20)
**Volume:** 32
**Reading order:** 15
**Continuity state:** Day 438, afternoon, the field east of the lime. Everything rises from Mau's shadow except Kamish. The Legion of ninety-eight forms a line of shields between the lime and the wall of twenty thousand. Mau speaks to the ordinary soldiers, not the otherworlders: "Nobody move," as he said once on the north road. Most don't. The first who charge are knocked senseless where they stand by something nobody sees; it is the Ruler's Authority fragment from floor 100, rough and used for the first time, a hand that is not there. Mau never moves. After the seventh man drops in the frost, the front ranks stop. The ordinary army is held without a death. Then the otherworlders move, and the real fight starts

*When: Day 438, the same afternoon. The sun a hand's width above the western hills. The frost on the field has not melted all day.*

They came up out of his shadow all at once.

Not the way they had come up on the hundred and first floor, in a hall of stone over nothing, with Ciel counting what it would cost. Calling them cost nothing now. They came up the way the house came out into the yard when something happened at the north end: because they were his, and he had called, and it was time.

The soldiers first. Ninety-eight of them, up out of the frosted ground in their rows, spears level, shields locked, the ten in front. They came up in a line a hundred paces long between the lime and the field, and stood, and in the last row, where two places had been empty since the hundred and first floor, they left the two places empty.

Then the rest.

Igris on his left. Beru on his right. Tank, huge and dark, on all fours at the end of the line, his breath going up white. Tusk, slow, the blue fire in his skull. Kaisel, up out of the shadow and into the cold air, wings wide, circling. Bellion, tall, black-violet gone to black, the segmented sword already drawn. Corner, at the corner of the line, where Corner always went. Roof, the long blade on its pole upright, planting itself on the frozen ground as if it were a roof it had been told to keep. Lantern, last, rising up out of his shadow behind him, wings unfolding, huge.

Everything except Kamish, who lay on the ridge with his head on his paws and a house underneath him, and did not move.

In the wall of twenty thousand, something went along the front rank. Not a sound. A shiver. The way a field of grass shivers when the wind turns.

* * *

He did not speak to the otherworlders.

He looked past them. Past the herald in its grey hood, past the fair-haired man and the man with a blade for an arm and the woman on the air. Past the boy in grey with the little girl at his sleeve. At the wall.

At the ordinary soldiers. Men and women with spears and shields, from a lot of places, in a lot of colours, who had walked across the country because somebody had told them a story about a monster.

He had said it once before. On the north road, at nightfall, to three demons and a devil, in a voice that was level and empty and almost kind. *Nobody move.*

He said it again now. Not empty. Not level. Just tired.

"Nobody move," said Mau.

His voice carried. It went out across the frosted field and into the wall and through it, rank after rank, to the very back.

"I'm not going to hurt anyone who stands still," he said. "I promised. I'm not going to kill anyone at all. Not today." He looked at the front rank. At a young man with a spear and a too-big helmet, who was shaking. "So stand still. Please. And this'll be over sooner."

* * *

Most of them stood still.

He watched it happen. All along the front rank, men and women who had been gripping their spears so hard their knuckles were white looked at the line of shadows, and at the dragon rising behind him with its wings spread, and at the knight and the ant, and at the boy at the white line with his hands in his coat pockets, saying *please*, and did not move.

Most.

Not all.

A man in the third rank, near the middle, under a red banner, shouted something in a language Mau did not know, and broke out of the line, and ran at him.

Then another, to his left. Then five more, from different places in the wall, the way it always goes, one brave man and then the ones who could not bear to let him be brave alone.

Seven of them. Running across the frosted field at the line of shields, spears down, shouting.

The Legion did not move. He had not told them to.

He did not move either.

* * *

On the hundredth floor, the Ruler had stood in a garden of white light and closed a hand that was not there round thirty of his soldiers at once.

He had felt it. He had felt what it was, in the instant before the Ruler broke his arm with it. A will that held things. A grip with no fingers. And when the Ruler was gone and the garden opened and the notice said *not eligible*, something had stayed behind in him. A piece of it. Rough. Unfinished. The Noise had folded it away with everything else and he had never once touched it.

He touched it now.

Not the whole of it. He could not have lifted thirty of anything. A sliver. The smallest grip he could make. A hand the size of his own, made of nothing, at the end of a reach as long as the field.

The first man was forty paces away, running, his spear down.

Mau did not move.

The man's head snapped sideways as if somebody had cuffed him, hard, with an open palm, at the hinge of the jaw. His legs went on running for two more steps without him. Then he folded up in the frost, spear and all, and lay there, breathing.

The second man, twenty paces to the left, never saw it coming either. Neither did the third. Snap. Fold. Down.

Mau stood at the lime line with his hands in his pockets, and his eyes on the field, and did not lift a finger.

Four. Five. Six.

The seventh had seen the others drop. He slowed. He was ten paces from the line of shields, and he slowed, and stopped, and stood there in the frost with his spear in his hands and his chest heaving, staring at the boy at the white line who had not moved at all.

Mau looked at him.

"Please," said Mau.

The man looked at the six bodies in the frost round him. Breathing, all of them. One of them already groaning, trying to sit up.

He put his spear down on the ground.

Then he sat down beside it, very slowly, and put his hands on his knees, and did not move again.

* * *

*Mau,* said Ciel. Very carefully. *What was that?*

*The hand. From a hundred.*

*You never used it.*

*I know.*

*You never even looked at it.* A pause. *How did you know how?*

He did not answer at once.

He did not know. He had reached for it the way he reached for the space, or the pull, or Momo's kettle, and it had been there, rough and strange and much too big, and he had taken the smallest piece of it he could and done the smallest thing with it he could think of.

*It was easy,* he said slowly. *It shouldn't have been easy.*

Ciel did not answer.

Somewhere at the bottom of the walls, under everything, where something had said *maybe* an hour ago and sounded pleased, he felt the Noise watching.

* * *

The wall of twenty thousand did not move.

Not one more man broke out of it. All along the front rank they stood with their spears grounded, staring at the six men in the frost, getting up now, holding their jaws, staggering back toward their own lines with nobody stopping them, and at the seventh, sitting with his hands on his knees ten paces from the shields.

Twenty thousand people, held still by a boy who had said *please*.

In the little group of otherworlders, the fair-haired man had stopped grinning.

He was looking at Mau with his head a little on one side, the way a man looks at a fighter in a ring he has just realised he has misjudged.

"Huh," he said. Quietly. To himself.

The man with the blade arm had turned to look at the wall behind him. At the soldiers standing still. Then back at Mau.

"He didn't move," he said. Flatly. "I was watching. He didn't move at all."

* * *

The herald lifted its hooded head.

It had not spoken since Mau said *that's not what I said*. It had stood in the frost while the seven ran and fell, and had not turned to watch them, and had not said a word.

It spoke now. Not to Mau. To the little group.

"*The soldiers were never going to be enough,*" said the herald. "*That is why you are here.*"

The woman on the air came down another step. The short wide boy cracked his knuckles. The woman with the crossbow unslung it from her back, and the three women behind her, who moved wrong, spread out on either side of her like wings.

The man with the blade arm sighed.

"Yeah," he said. "Figured."

He pulled something at the side of his chest, under his coat, like a man pulling the cord of an engine. There was a sound like a chainsaw catching. And his whole body changed. The blade at his elbow grew longer; another came out of his other arm; and out of his head, splitting it, out of where his face had been, a third, long and straight and shining, like a sword driven up through a skull.

Beside Mau, Igris drew his sword.

*"My liege,"* said Igris. *"That one is mine."*

The boy in grey had stepped back behind the others with the little girl. His face was white. His lips were moving. He was counting something under his breath.

The fair-haired man rolled his shoulders, and the coat that had been made for someone half his size split down the back.

"Okay, kid," he said. The grin was coming back. Different, now. Interested. "Let's see what else you've got."

Mau took his hands out of his pockets.

Under him, in his shadow, the wheel turned over once more, slowly, and settled, and waited.
