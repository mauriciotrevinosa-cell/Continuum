# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 1 — The Morning After

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-20)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 1

*When: Day 439, morning. Frost. The long-room lamps lit early, because nobody went to bed. The board in the yard still says, in Mau's crooked hand from a week ago, Day 412. Nothing., and under it nothing for any day since.*

He woke in the clinic and did not know why he was there.

Then he did. The field. The army. The cookies. He sat up on the clinic bed, too fast, and his chest hurt where the fair-haired man had hit him, and he laughed, because he had won.

"Lie down," said Shoko.

She was on her stool by the bed with her coffee. She looked as if she had been sitting there all night, which she had.

"I'm fine," said Mau.

"I'll decide that." She put her coffee down. "Lie down."

He lay down.

* * *

She looked at all of him.

Maomao came in halfway through with her sleeves rolled up and looked at all of him too, the way Maomao looked at things, as if he were a plant she had not catalogued yet. Between them they found a bruise on his chest the size and shape of a fist, already going yellow at the edges. A split lip. Grazes on his palms from the frost. Dried blood in both nostrils.

"What else?" said Shoko.

"Nothing," said Maomao.

"Look again."

Maomao looked again. She put her fingers on his temples, his jaw, the back of his skull. She looked in his eyes with a candle. She asked him to follow her finger and he followed it. She asked him his name and he said *Mau*, and what day it was and he said *the day after the army*, and where he was and he said *the clinic*, and she sat back on her heels and looked at Shoko.

"Nothing," she said again. "Nothing I can find."

Shoko did not answer.

"Thank you," said Mau. He meant it. They had both clearly been up all night. "Both of you. Really." He sat up again, slowly this time. "Is Yuka up yet?"

* * *

Rimuru was on the bench by the clinic wall.

He had been there all night too. He had his eyes shut and his hands flat on his knees, the way he sat when Ciel was talking down the thread, and he had sat like that for nine hours, and nothing had come down it.

"Ciel," he said. Quietly. To nobody anyone could see. "Ciel. Please."

Nothing.

Diablo, beside him, said nothing either. He had his hand on Rimuru's shoulder. He had not taken it off since the field.

Mau looked at them. The small grey boy on the bench with his eyes shut, and the tall man in black with his hand on his shoulder.

"Is he all right?" he asked Shoko, quietly, so as not to disturb them.

Shoko looked at him for a long time.

"He's waiting for someone," she said.

* * *

Gojo came in at dawn.

He did not say anything. He stood in the clinic doorway with his blindfold up and looked at Mau with his bare eyes, the way he had looked at the second heart on the morning after the hundred and first floor. For a long time. Not at his chest this time. At his head.

Mau looked back at him and smiled.

"Morning," he said.

Gojo did not smile back.

"What do you see?" said Shoko, very quietly.

Gojo was silent for so long that Mau began to feel uncomfortable, the way you feel when someone in a room is staring at a stain on your shirt.

"Something's been in there," said Gojo at last.

Shoko went still.

"I can't see what," said Gojo. "I can't see what's missing, or if anything is. I can't see in, any more than I can see past the root." His mouth went flat. "But I can see where something went in, and came out again." He paused. "It was tidy. Whatever it was. It put everything back very neatly. Like somebody going through a drawer and closing it after." He lowered the blindfold. "That's all I can see. Tidy."

"Is that bad?" said Mau.

Gojo looked at him.

"I don't know," he said, and went out.

* * *

He ate breakfast at the long table.

It was a strange breakfast. Everyone was there. Nobody was talking. Taihei had made porridge with honey in it, the kind he made only when someone had been ill, and set it down in front of Mau without a word, and stood behind him for a moment after, as if he were going to say something, and then went back to the stove.

"Thank you, Taihei," said Mau. "This is really good."

Taihei did not turn round.

Mau looked down the table. At all of them. He knew all of them. Momo with the pear bread. Okarun beside her. Kessoku at the end, Bocchi not looking at anyone. Seiko in her high seat with her mouth set. The witches in a row. Fern with the ledger shut under her hand. Maki. Yuta. Senku, Byakuya. The five, unusually quiet.

He liked them. They were good people. It was a good house; he was lucky to have been taken in.

He looked for Yuka.

She was halfway down the long table, between her grandparents, looking at her porridge. She had not touched it.

He picked up his bowl and went and sat beside her. He did not think about it. There was a space on the bench next to her grandmother and he slid into it and bumped Yuka's shoulder with his.

"Hey," he said. "You didn't eat your cookie."

Yuka looked at him.

"I'm saving it," she said. Very quietly.

"For what?"

She did not answer.

* * *

Frieren had not slept.

She sat in her place at the long table, beside the empty place where Mau always sat, and watched him across the room, sitting beside Yuka, talking to her, eating his porridge.

She had sat on the back step all night, after the field. Fern had sat with her. Neither of them had said anything for a long time. Then, near dawn, Frieren had said, to the yard: "He was hit."

Fern had looked at her.

"In the head," said Frieren. "Something hit him in the head. Or did something to it. Gojo saw." She looked at her hands. "I've seen it before. A long time ago, in villages, after rockfalls. People pulled out from under things. They forget. For a while. Days. Sometimes longer. Then one morning it comes back, and they're angry that nobody made their breakfast right."

"Always?" said Fern.

Frieren did not answer that. She looked at the clinic door. "So I'll wait."

She was waiting now.

She watched him across the long room, sitting beside Yuka, and she held her cup in both hands, and she waited, and she did not go over to him, because the last time she had gone toward him he had stepped away four paces through nothing and asked if he knew her.

She could wait. She was very good at waiting. She had a thousand years of practice.

She had not expected it to feel like this.

* * *

At the end of the table, Ori watched him too.

She was not eating either. She was sitting with her hands behind her back on the bench, which was not how you sat to eat, and looking at him.

At the way he sat beside Yuka. At the way he bumped her shoulder. At the way, when Momo passed the pear bread down the table, he took a piece and passed the plate on to Seiko without looking at her, the way you pass a plate to someone you don't know very well. At the way he said *thank you, Taihei*.

At the way he had not looked toward the connecting door of the east room once, all morning, or toward Frieren's place, or toward her.

She took the list out of her pocket and looked at it.

Then she put it back, and got up, and went out to the long-room wall, to the map, and stood in front of it with her hands behind her back the way she did every morning, and checked the pins.

Red. Grey. Brown. Where they had been.

"Still there," said Ori, very quietly, to nobody.

Behind her, at the long table, Mau laughed at something Yuka had said.

Ori did not turn round.
