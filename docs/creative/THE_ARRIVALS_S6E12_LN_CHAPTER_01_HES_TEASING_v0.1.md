## Chapter 5 — He's Teasing

*When: Days 441 and 442. Two days to Yuka's birthday. Umaru's sheet for it is pinned on the first wall section beside IT HAPPENED, headed in the Ministry's largest letters: EIGHTEEN.*

The house got ready for a birthday.

That was the strange thing. It was the thing nobody could stop doing, even now. Umaru pinned a sheet on the wall and wrote EIGHTEEN across the top of it, and under it, in the Ministry's square hand, everything that had to happen. *Cake (Momo). Not a surprise (Yuka hates surprises). The long table, dinner. Then the Day (Fern's page) (Oppa).* And people did it, one line at a time, because there was a sheet, and because Yuka was turning eighteen, and because it was easier to do things on a sheet than to think about the other thing.

Mau helped with all of it.

He carried flour for Momo. He climbed the ladder in the long room and hung the paper lanterns Ori and the five had made, crooked, in a row across the beams. He argued with Senku about candles. He laughed at Milim when she tried to help with the lanterns and floated into one and set it swinging.

He was cheerful. He was kind. He was helpful.

He said *thank you* to everyone.

* * *

He did not sleep in the east room.

Nobody said anything about it. Nobody had to. On the night of the field he had slept in the clinic, because Shoko made him. On the night after, Shoko let him go, and he went up the stairs with everyone else, and stopped at the top, and stood in the corridor for a moment looking at the doors as if he were not sure which one was his.

Then he came back down and slept on the bench in the long room, under a blanket Taihei found him, with his coat folded under his head.

He did it again the second night.

Frieren lay in the east room alone and listened to the house settle round her, and to the connecting door, open a crack, where Ori was not asleep either, and at the reset hour she put out her foot, the way she always did, and found nothing there.

"One," she said. Very quietly. To the empty half of the bed.

Nobody answered.

* * *

She did not go down to him.

She had decided. On the back step, on the first night, with Fern beside her: *it's shock. He was hit. I'll wait.* She was very good at waiting. She had told herself that every hour since.

She almost spoke to him once.

On the afternoon of Day 441, after the witches' table had emptied and the boy in grey had gone off with Rem to the river, Mau came into the long room from the yard alone, with a lantern Ori had dropped in the frost, to hang it. Frieren was at the table with the green book. Nobody else was there.

She closed the book.

"Mau," she said.

He looked round. He smiled at her. The friendly smile. The one he gave Taihei.

"Hi," he said.

She stood up. She did not go toward him; she had learned that. She stood by the table with her hand on the closed book.

"You haven't slept upstairs," she said.

He looked a little embarrassed.

"I didn't want to take anybody's room," he said. "It's fine. The bench is comfy." He climbed the ladder and hung the lantern and came down again and grinned at her. "Honestly, you're all so nice. I keep thinking someone's going to tell me I've been taking up too much space." He picked up the ladder. "Don't worry about me."

He went out.

She stood by the table for a long time.

*Don't worry about me.*

He had said that. In that voice. Easy, polite, a little shy, the way you say it to someone you have just met at a market.

He was teasing her. He had to be. Nobody could say *don't worry about me* to her like that, after everything, unless they were doing it on purpose.

She sat down again and opened the green book and did not read a word.

* * *

Ori decided it was a game.

She decided it on the second morning. At breakfast, across the long table, Mau caught her eye and winked.

She knew that wink.

He had done it a hundred times, in the fort, when they were playing spies, when Umaru was the Ministry and Ori was the agent and Mau was the double agent who pretended not to know anything and passed her secret notes under the table. The wink meant: *I'm pretending. Play along.*

She looked at him.

He winked again, and went back to his porridge, and said something to Yuka that made Yuka go red.

Ori sat very still with her hands behind her back.

It was a game. It had to be. He had made up a game where he did not know her, and he was not going to tell her the rules, because that was part of the game. She had to work them out. She was good at working things out.

She took the list out of her pocket. Not the shadow list. A new page. And at the top of it, in her careful hand, she wrote:

*THE GAME. (He doesn't know me.) (He winks.) (Rules: ?)*

She did not feel better. But she felt like she had something to do.

* * *

Armin was the one who heard the story.

He did not mean to. He was at the long-room wall on the morning of Day 442 with his coffee, checking the pins, the way he did every morning, and Mau came and stood beside him with his own cup and looked at the map.

"This is really good," said Mau. "You drew all of this?"

"From what Ciel told me," said Armin. Carefully. "Through Rimuru. Before."

"Ciel?"

Armin looked at him.

Mau was looking at the map. At the valley, the red pin. He did not seem to have noticed what he had said. He did not seem to know the name.

"Who's Ciel?" said Mau, after a moment, politely, the way you ask about someone's cousin.

Armin put his coffee down on the windowsill very carefully.

"Someone who used to talk to us," he said. "How long have you been here, Mau? In the house?"

"Me?" Mau thought about it. "Not that long. A while. Yuka was here before me." He smiled at the map. "The gate found me. That's what Yuka says. It had been looking for me, or something like that. I woke up in the woods and walked around for a while, and some people told me stories about a settlement, and I just kept walking until I got here." He shrugged. "And then the Red Gate. That's where Yuka and I got close." He laughed, a little embarrassed. "Sorry. That's a weird thing to say to you at seven in the morning."

Armin did not say anything for a moment.

"No," he said. "It's not weird."

Mau finished his coffee, and patted Armin on the shoulder, the way you pat a friend you like, and went off toward the kitchen to see if Momo needed more flour.

* * *

Armin stood at the map for a long time.

Then he took the stub of pencil from behind his ear, and turned over the sheet of arithmetic he always carried, and wrote on the back of it, very small:

*Day 442. He asked who Ciel is.*

*He says: Yuka was here first. The gate was looking for him. He woke in the woods, walked, heard stories, came to the settlement. Then the Red Gate. "That's where Yuka and I got close."*

*He doesn't mention Frieren.*

He looked at it for a long time.

He did not show it to anyone. Not yet. It was the day before Yuka's birthday, and the paper lanterns were up, crooked, across the beams, and Momo was making a cake, and Frieren was sitting at the long table with the green book open in front of her, waiting, and Ori was writing the rules of a game in her careful hand.

He folded the sheet and put it in his pocket.

Tomorrow, he thought. After the birthday. Let her have the birthday.

Across the yard, Gojo was standing by the barn with his blindfold up, watching Mau carry a sack of flour into the kitchen, and the look on his face said he was thinking exactly the same thing.
