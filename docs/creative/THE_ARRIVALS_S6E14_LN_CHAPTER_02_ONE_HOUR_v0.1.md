## Chapter 10 — One Hour

*When: Day 446, dawn to mid-morning. Frost again. Anko's board, third plank, in her plain hand: Day 446. Mau, Yuka. Floor 100, the garden. One hour. (He said.)*

He told them the night before.

That was the first thing. He found Yuta and Maki on the back step after dinner, where they sat most evenings now, with their shoulders touching and a blanket over both their knees, and he stood in front of them with his hands in his coat pockets and said it before they could look at him the way they had looked at him at the lime.

"Tomorrow," he said. "Yuka and me. An hour. The tower."

Maki looked up.

"The garden," said Mau. "On a hundred. Yuka heard there's a garden there. Really quiet. Fruit, and still water." He shrugged. "She wants to see it. She asked me to go with her." He paused. "So I'm telling you. Before. Like you said."

Yuta and Maki looked at each other.

"An hour," said Yuta.

"An hour."

"The tower's cleared," said Maki slowly. "Every floor. The transfer point on a hundred's safe. Ori had an afternoon there." She was quiet for a moment. "Shoko checks you first."

"Of course."

"And Anko writes it on the board. With a time."

"Of course."

Maki looked at him for a long moment. Then she looked at Yuta. Then, slowly, she nodded.

"Thank you," she said. "For telling us."

He smiled at her. The warm one. The friendly one.

"You told me to," he said.

* * *

Shoko checked him at dawn.

She looked at all of him, the way she always did. Eyes. Pulse. Chest. The fist-bruise, nearly gone. She put her palm flat on his chest for a moment, over the two hearts. She looked at his face for a long time.

"Fine," she said.

It was the first *fine* she had said to him in a month.

He did not notice. He thanked her and went out.

She sat on her stool in the clinic with her coffee going cold in her hands and looked at the door he had gone through. His body was fine. That was true. That was the only part of him she could look at.

* * *

Anko wrote it on the board.

*Day 446. Mau, Yuka. Floor 100, the garden. One hour.*

She looked at it. Then she added, smaller, in brackets, the way she had once written *(Alone.)* and *(Together.)* and *(No check.)*:

*(He said.)*

* * *

The frame stood at the end of the yard where it always stood, the tall stone arch with the screen beside it, the place every floor of the summer had started from. It had been quiet for a month. Nobody had needed it.

Yuka stood beside him in front of it in her coat, with her hair up in her grandmother's pin.

She had not said much all morning. She had said *good morning* at breakfast and not much else. She had eaten half her porridge. She stood in front of the frame now with her hands in her pockets and looked at it.

"Ready?" said Mau.

"Ready," said Yuka.

He did not know why she sounded like that. Like someone about to jump into cold water.

"It's just a garden," he said. "One hour. Then lunch."

"I know," said Yuka.

They stepped through.

* * *

The light was red.

He knew it at once. Not the grey-white of the transfer, the flat even glow of the frame taking you to a floor you had already cleared. Red. Deep red, like light through a closed eyelid in the sun. It went through him and round him and he felt the frame behind him not be there any more.

He turned.

There was no frame. There was no screen. There was no yard, no Anko at the board, no long-room windows. There was red light, fading, and stone under his feet, and cold air that smelled of ice and old stone, and a sky above that was the wrong colour.

He had felt this once before. Months ago, before any of the floors. A red light, and the door sealing behind him, and time running wrong, and a dragon at a snowline.

A Red Gate.

He looked at Yuka.

She was looking at him. Her face had gone white. Her hands had come out of her pockets.

They stood there in the red-lit cold, on old stone, under a wrong sky, with no way back, and looked at each other.

And at exactly the same moment, they both laughed.

"*What bad luck*," they said. Together. The same words, the same breath.

He laughed harder. He could not help it. Of all the doors in the world. Of all the mornings. *One hour. Then lunch.*

Yuka was laughing too. Too hard. The way you laugh when the other thing you could do is cry. She put her hand over her mouth and laughed into it with her eyes wet, and he did not know why her eyes were wet, and he reached out—

And then he did not laugh.

* * *

He grabbed her.

He did not think. His body did it. One arm round her shoulders and he pulled her in, hard, against his chest, and turned, all in one movement, so that his back was where her head had been a heartbeat before.

She made a sound against his coat.

"*Oppa*—"

"We have to move," said Mau.

He held up his other hand.

There was something in it. Long and thin and clear, like a needle of glass as long as his forearm, so cold it was smoking in the air, so cold his scarred palm had gone white round it.

An arrow. Made of ice.

He had caught it. He had not seen it coming. He had felt it, the way he felt the claws on twenty, a hand's breadth before it arrived, and his hand had gone up and closed on it a finger's width from where Yuka's head had been.

She stared at it. Then up at him.

He was looking past her. Up. At the wall of old stone behind them, at the dark gap between two great blocks high up, where something had moved and gone.

"Something up there," he said. "It doesn't like visitors."

He dropped the arrow. It shattered on the stone like a dropped glass.

He did not let go of her.

* * *

The notice came while they were still standing there.

He saw it the way he saw the window. In the space just in front of his eyes where nobody else could see. But it was not the window. It was the other kind. The kind the tower had used. The kind the first Red Gate had used, months ago, before any of it.

*Red Gate.*

*Party: 2.*

*Notice: within this gate, the Operator has access to inventory and to the shop for its whole duration.*

*This gate has multiple levels.*

*It will not open until every level is cleared.*

*Each level ends with a guardian.*

*Good luck.*

He read it out loud.

Yuka listened with her face against his coat and his arm still round her shoulders. When he finished, she did not say anything for a long time.

"Multiple levels," she said at last.

"Yeah."

"Not one hour."

"No," said Mau. "Not one hour."

She did not move away from him.

* * *

*Ciel,* he said, without thinking.

He did not know why he said it. The name came into his head the way a word you have used a thousand times comes into your mouth before you think. He did not know whose name it was.

*Mau.*

He stopped.

Close. Inside. Not behind anything. A woman's voice, level and careful and very tired, like someone who has been calling through a wall for days and has just found the wall gone.

*Mau. It's me. Can you hear me?*

*Who's—*

*Ciel.* A pause. *I've been with you a long time. Since long before this. I've been here the whole time, behind something I couldn't get through.* Another pause. *It can't reach in here. The gate shut it out, the way it shuts out everything.* Her voice did something. *I can hear you again.*

He thought about that. It should have frightened him, a voice in his head he did not know. It did not. It felt like the knock on Yuka's door. Two and one. Something his body knew before he did.

*Okay,* he said. *Hi, Ciel.*

A sound that might have been a laugh, or might not.

*Mau,* said Ciel. Quickly, as if she were afraid the wall would come back. *Listen. Before anything else. The elf. In the east room. Frieren. She's—*

*Ciel.*

He said it gently. He was looking at the wall above them where the arrow had come from, and at Yuka's face against his coat.

*Not now. Please.* He did not let go of Yuka. *Whatever it is. There's something up there shooting at her, and I have to get her out of here, and I can't think about anything else right now.* A pause. *Can it wait until we're out?*

Ciel did not answer.

*And there's nobody in here to tell anyway,* said Mau. Not unkindly. Just true. *Is there. It's just us.*

She was quiet for a long time.

*It can wait,* she said at last. Very quietly. *For now.*

She did not say it again. Not in that gate.

* * *

But something else answered.

Not behind a wall. Close. In his shadow. Down in the dark under his feet, on the old cold stone of a gate that had sealed behind him, something stirred without being called, the way it had not stirred since a night in a frosted field.

"*My King,*" said Beru.

Very quietly. For Beru.

Mau looked down.

The dark of his shadow on the stone had moved. And out of it, slowly, just the top of a head: the long black chitin, the antennae, two cold blue lights, peering up out of the shadow like something looking out of a hole. Not rising. Just looking.

"*My King,*" said Beru again. His voice was different. Lower. Steadier. Not the voice that had shouted *my King!* across a hive, or apologised to fence posts. Older, somehow. "*You have brought the little rope into a red place.*"

"It wasn't on purpose," said Mau. "It was supposed to be a garden."

The cold blue lights looked at Yuka. At her face against his coat, and his arm round her.

Then back at him.

For a long moment Beru said nothing at all.

"*Yes,*" he said at last. Quietly. "*I see.*"

And he sank back down into the dark, slowly, the blue lights last, and was gone.

Yuka had felt Mau go still when the shadow spoke. She lifted her face off his coat.

"Who was that?" she said.

"Beru," said Mau. He was frowning at his shadow. "He's never come up on his own before."

He did not know that was not true. He did not know that Beru had come up on his own a hundred times, in the yard, on the round, on the hive floor, kneeling, saying *my King* to a boy who had broken him and raised him and been very kind to him afterward.

Yuka knew.

She looked down at the shadow on the stone where the ant king had been, and at the place where he had said *I see*, and she understood, very cold and very clear, that she was not the only one in this gate who knew the truth.
