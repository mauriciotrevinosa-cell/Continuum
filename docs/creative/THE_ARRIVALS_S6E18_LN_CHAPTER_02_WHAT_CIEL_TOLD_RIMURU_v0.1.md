# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 18 — What Ciel Told Rimuru

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-20)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 18
**Continuity state:** Day 447, late night, after the south field. The thief's wall did not come back when they came out of the gate. *When: Day 447, past midnight. The house asleep, or pretending. The east-room window dark. Yuka's window dark. On the back step, a small grey boy with a blanket round his shoulders.*

It came down the thread at midnight.

Rimuru had been sitting on the back step since the south field. He had not gone in with the others. He had watched Mau and Yuka walk back across the frost from the south field side by side, talking, not touching, and go in through the kitchen door, and he had sat down on the step with his blanket and stayed there, because something had changed in the thread, and he did not know what.

For nine days it had been silent.

Not empty. Silent. The way a room is silent when someone is in it holding their breath. He had sat on benches and steps and the end of the long table with his eyes shut, saying *Ciel*, *Ciel*, *please*, and nothing had come down it.

At midnight, something did.

*Rimuru.*

He sat up so fast the blanket fell off his shoulders.

Diablo, asleep sitting up against the kitchen wall beside him, opened one eye.

"*Ciel*," said Rimuru. Out loud. His voice cracked. "Ciel. Ciel, where have you *been*—"

*Behind a wall,* said Ciel. *I'll tell you. I'll tell you all of it.* A pause. *I need you to listen to the end before you do anything. Will you?*

He put both hands flat on the step on either side of him.

"I'll listen," he said.

* * *

She told him.

She told him the way she told things: plainly, carefully, in order, without making any of it smaller or bigger than it was.

The field. The soldier. The two fingers on Mau's forehead. The white. The thing that went through his memories like someone going through a drawer, and took things out, and put other things in their places, small things, wrong things, so neatly he would never know.

"*What* things?" said Rimuru.

*Frieren,* said Ciel. *First. Before anything. I felt it go first.* A pause. *Then the rest. The Trial. The Muslin. The tower. The coins. Ori's door. Me.* Another pause. *And in their places, a story. He came to the house after Yuka. The gate was looking for him. He and Yuka got close in the Red Gate.* Her voice did not change. *All of it fits. That's what's so clever about it. Every false piece is shaped to sit exactly where a true one was.*

Rimuru did not say anything.

*And then the wall,* said Ciel. *Between me and him. Between me and you. I could see him. I could hear him. I couldn't reach him. For eight days.* A pause. *Until the gate.*

* * *

She told him about the gate.

The red light. The ice arrow. The wall falling, all at once, because whatever held it could not reach into a Red Gate any more than Rimuru could. Mau hearing her, and not knowing her, and saying *hi, Ciel* anyway.

*I started to tell him,* said Ciel. *About Frieren. The first hour.* A pause. *He asked me to wait. Gently. He said there was something shooting at her and he had to get her out, and there was nobody in there to tell anyway.* Another pause. *He was right. So I waited.*

"And then?"

She told him.

The coats. The levels. The loan, which she had helped him build, the way she and Rimuru had built Anko's. The training. The shikigami, nine each, *Summoner: 1*, the rule bent and not broken. Two Mahoragas on a white plain in two minutes. A black dog that had always been Yuka's, waking. Beru standing in front of her. Igris calling her *my lady*. Kamish speaking to her at night.

Rimuru listened to all of it with his eyes shut.

*And the night under the stars,* said Ciel.

* * *

She told him that too.

Not every word. She did not need every word. She told him that Mau had kissed Yuka, and that Yuka had kissed him back, and that it had gone further than a kiss, and that Yuka had stopped it.

*She felt his scars,* said Ciel. *All of them. She knew where every one came from. And she stopped.* A pause. *She cried. He stopped the instant she did. He asked if he'd got it wrong.* Another pause. *Then she told him the tower. And he understood there had been something with Frieren. And he said* okay. *And stayed.*

Rimuru had opened his eyes.

He was looking across the dark yard at the house. At two windows on the upper floor. The east room, at the end. And three doors along, Yuka's.

Both dark.

*It went on,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *After. Not like that night. But she didn't move away from him again. He kissed her again, on the last level. They held hands coming out. They let go on the last step so nobody would see.* A pause. *She asked him to hide it. All of it. To only talk about it when it's the two of them.* Another pause. *He said okay.*

Rimuru did not say anything for a long time.

* * *

*There's one more thing,* said Ciel.

"Tell me."

*He's remembering.* A pause. *Pieces. Since Yuka told him the tower. A cold foot. Someone saying* one. *The bench on thirty.* Her voice was very level. *On the last level he told her. He said he knows he had a past with someone. He said he doesn't know what would happen if it all came back.* A pause. *And he said he doesn't want it to.*

Rimuru shut his eyes again.

*He's so happy, Rimuru,* said Ciel. Very quietly. *I've been inside him since before the house. I've never felt him like this. Not once.* A pause. *And it's built on something stolen.*

* * *

"Did you tell him?" said Rimuru at last. "When you came out?"

*I tried.*

She had. In the yard, in the dusk, as the house went in from the south field and the two Mahoragas went back down into their shadows. *Mau. We're out. You said it could wait until we were out.*

And he had stopped, at the kitchen door, with Yuka just ahead of him going in, and stood very still for a moment.

*I know,* he had said. Inside. To her. Gently. *Not yet, Ciel. Please.* A pause. *Not yet.*

*So I didn't,* said Ciel. *He asked me. Twice now. I'm not going to force it into him. Whatever's in his place, it's still him choosing.* A pause. *That's the part I can't get past.*

* * *

"Frieren," said Rimuru.

*I know.*

"She has to know. Ciel. She's lying up there—" He looked at the dark east-room window. "She's lying up there every night with her foot out, saying *one* to nobody. She has to *know*."

*And if she knows,* said Ciel, *what happens to Yuka?*

Rimuru stopped.

*She'll know about the stars,* said Ciel. *The kiss. The hands. The hiding. She'll know Yuka was the one he chose, while he was stolen. And whatever Frieren does with that, kind or not, the whole house will see it.* A pause. *And Yuka will be the one who pays for it. Not him. Not the thing that took him. Her.*

Rimuru did not answer.

*She stopped, Rimuru,* said Ciel. *Under the stars. She was eighteen and she was in love with him and he was holding her and there was nobody in the world to see, and she felt his scars and she stopped.* A pause. *Kamish told her to remember that she did. I think somebody else should remember it too.*

* * *

"Then what do I do?" said Rimuru.

Very quietly. To the dark yard. To the thread.

"Ciel. What do I *do*?"

*I don't know,* said Ciel.

She had never said that to him before. Not like that. Not about something that mattered.

*I'm telling you because I can't carry it alone,* said Ciel. *And because you're the only one I can tell.* A pause. *Not Frieren. Not yet. Not Yuka; she'd break. Not him; he asked me not to.* Another pause. *You.*

Rimuru sat on the back step with his blanket round his shoulders and his hands flat on the cold stone.

He looked up at the two dark windows. The east room. Yuka's.

Then across the yard, at the long-room window, where a crooked paper lantern still hung that nobody had taken down from the birthday.

Beside him, Diablo had both eyes open now. He did not say anything. He had heard nothing; the thread spoke only to Rimuru. But he had watched his master's face for the last hour, and he moved, very slightly, closer on the step, until his shoulder was against Rimuru's, the way Frieren's shoulder used to rest against Mau's at the long table.

"I don't know what to do," said Rimuru.

He said it to nobody. To the dark. To the only other one who knew.

*I know,* said Ciel. *Neither do I.*

They sat there together, the slime and the voice and the demon, on the back step of a house where everybody else was asleep or pretending to be, until the window over the east room went grey with the dawn.

Nobody else in the house knew.

Only him.
