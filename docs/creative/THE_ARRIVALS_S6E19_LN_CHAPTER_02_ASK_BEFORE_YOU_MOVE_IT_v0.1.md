# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 20 — Ask Before You Move It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 20
**Source episode:** S6E19
**Continuity state:** Day 448, breakfast and the washing court. Umaru reconciles the sixty-seven spent and sixty-seven held points without spending more. Ciel proposes a careful survey of the three shadow accesses now that the shikigami prerequisite is met; no merger is performed. Noise agrees to ask for help and warn of overflow, developing their Day418 cooperation. The Black Heart remains only a previously demonstrated overflow destination, not understood or unbounded. Mau asks privately whether Ciel told Rimuru and chooses to seek him out immediately.

*When: Day 448, after the yard. Tank has been sent down again. There is room to carry breakfast through the kitchen door without a bear in it.*

Umaru put the slate beside his bowl.

"Before you say anything," she said, "I'm not asking you to spend them."

Mau lowered his spoon.

"I wasn't going to."

"You sometimes say that immediately after doing it."

"Do I?"

She looked at him. Then she pulled out the chair beside him and sat, turning the slate so he could read it without leaning over the porridge.

"We had a hundred and thirty-four. Before you went in."

"I remember the number."

He remembered it the way he remembered a code on a door, something he had known when he needed it. He did not remember the first time her voice had said it. She was watching him rather than the writing, and he wished he could tell her which parts of what he knew would stay still long enough to explain.

"Sixty-seven spent," he said. "Sixty-seven still there."

"Where did the spent ones go?"

"Endurance. Agility. Some in the new fields. I was trying to stop getting tired before the things trying to kill us got tired."

"A traditional Ministry policy," said Umaru.

He smiled. She had not expected him to, and was a moment late smiling back.

He opened the window for her, selecting the fields she asked for. She copied what she could actually see and left space beside what she could not explain yet. When she came to the dark bar beneath the cup, her chalk stopped.

"Did that go up?"

"There's something in it."

"I know. There was something in it before."

He looked again. Looking at it was not the same as looking into it. The mark was there, a shallow dark line beneath the others, and behind it he felt the second beat in his chest. He could not tell her what filled it or what could be taken back out.

"Ciel?"

*No reliable comparison,* she said. *We didn't measure it the same way in the gate. Please don't try to open it to show her.*

"We can't compare it properly," he told Umaru. "And she says don't poke it."

Umaru wrote *DON'T POKE IT* in a box.

"You can keep that advice without a number."

Across the table, Yuka reached for the bread and Maki slid a second piece onto her plate without interrupting her conversation with Mai. Yuka looked at the bread, looked at her grandmother, and ate it. A rip in her cuff lay open beside the bowl. Mai caught it with one finger as it passed.

"Bring that to me later. Before you pull the whole sleeve off."

"I can mend it."

"I've seen. Bring it to me."

The argument moved along the table with the butter. Umaru went back to her slate. Mau finished his porridge before Momo had to come and tell him to.

At the far end, Rimuru had his hands round a cup he did not seem to be drinking. His eyes were on the steam. Diablo sat beside him with a bowl untouched between his palms.

Mau looked at Rimuru for a moment. He did not look back.

* * *

Kaneki had left a pan soaking in the washing court. Mau offered to do it when he brought his bowl out, and Kaneki handed him the brush without telling him anything about who had done the pan yesterday, or last week, or before the field.

It was a relief.

He worked the brush under the rim while the water warmed his fingers. Through the open kitchen door he could hear Rem asking Ram where she had put something. Ram said exactly where it belonged. Rem said she had looked there. There was a pause, then the sound of a drawer.

"Oh," said Rem.

"Astonishing," said Ram.

Mau grinned down into the pan.

*While you're doing that,* said Ciel, *can we talk about the accesses?*

He turned the pan. *The shadows.*

*The three routes. Yes. What you finished in the gate changes what we can examine.*

He knew the sensation she meant. At the bottom of him, under the walls, three ways that did not sit quite on top of one another. His soldiers. The shikigami. And the other depth, the one he had never been able to give a useful name. Sometimes the overlap felt like a fold in a rug under his foot. Sometimes it felt as though something were standing on the other side, holding the edge.

*Can it join them now?*

*It has finished the part it was waiting for. That doesn't tell me whether joining them is safe.*

He stopped scrubbing. *All right. What's your plan?*

*Ask it.*

He let out a small laugh. *That's the plan?*

*The first part. When it changed the fields, we watched until your body couldn't take what was arriving. I don't want to watch the next change from the same distance.*

He had pieces of that day. Heat in the backs of his eyes. A lamp burning the wrong colour. Someone's hand on his chest, but the hand changed when he tried to remember whose it was.

*I don't want that either,* he said.

The brush floated up against his thumb. He pushed it beneath the water again.

*Then let me speak to it before it starts.*

* * *

He felt her turn.

Not away from him. Toward something else inside him, a change of attention he would have missed a month ago. He kept one hand in the water and the other on the side of the basin so he would know where he was while he listened.

*You have all of them now,* Ciel said.

For a little while nothing answered.

He could hear a knife against the kitchen board. A bird in the bare branches over the washing court. Then, much lower, something in him shifted its weight.

*Yes.*

The word was rough and small. It did not come from the wheel.

*I know you want to bring the routes together,* said Ciel. *Before you move anything, tell me which part you're moving.*

Nothing.

*And if you can't keep it steady, ask me to help. Don't wait until he's the only place left for it to go.*

The silence lasted long enough for the water to settle around his hand.

*Why?*

Ciel did not answer at once.

Mau lifted the brush from the pan. A black scrap of burned porridge clung to it. He picked it off, put it in the little pot for scraps and waited.

*Because I'd rather help you before he's hurt,* she said. *And because you helped me when I asked. We can keep doing that.* A pause. *We can be friends.*

Something moved below the walls, hesitant, as if trying a handle it had never seen turned from this side.

*Friends.*

*Yes.*

Mau held the wet brush very still.

*Okay,* said the Noise.

It was so plain that he almost missed it.

* * *

She did not ask it to begin.

That was the part Mau noticed afterward. He had been waiting for a great shift, a new weight, the dark opening under the floor. Nothing of the sort happened. Ciel asked it to show her the edge of one route, then another, without moving them, and it showed her something he felt only as a fine pressure behind his ribs.

*There,* she said. *That part doesn't meet the other at the same depth.*

The pressure changed. She asked it to stop. It stopped.

He set the clean pan on its edge. Water ran down his wrist.

*Do you remember where the extra went?* she asked the Noise.

*Yes. Sorry.*

*You don't have to apologise for showing me what you were trying to do. I need you to tell me before there's too much.*

He felt a nudge toward the cold reserve under the cup.

*That was where we put the overflow,* Ciel agreed. *It held what we sent that day. We still don't know everything it does. If something starts to spill, warn me. We'll divert what we can there and stop the work. We don't keep pouring just because there's room.*

*Stop,* the Noise repeated.

*Until we can hold it safely.*

Mau dried his hands. *You can stop if I ask too?*

This answer came more quickly.

*Yes.*

He leaned against the basin and let out the breath he had been keeping.

*Then we do that. Not today, not all of it. You look. If there's something you can't look at without moving it, you tell me first.*

The fine pressure withdrew. Nothing new appeared in the yard. The pan stayed a pan, and the kitchen lamp stayed dark in the morning light.

Kaneki came through the door for it.

"You got the bit underneath?"

Mau turned it over. He had not.

Kaneki handed the brush back to him.

* * *

He finished the bottom before asking Ciel the other question.

*You told Rimuru, didn't you?*

She went quiet.

*Yes,* she said at last.

He ran his thumb round the edge of the pan to check for anything sharp. *All of it?*

*What I knew. What happened in the gate. What you said.*

*Okay.*

*Mau—*

*You didn't do anything wrong.*

It was easier to say it inside than he had expected. He could still see Rimuru looking into his tea.

*He was going to have questions. This saves you telling him with me pretending not to know what he means.*

*Do you want me to tell him you want to talk?*

*No. I'll get him.*

*Now?*

He put the brush down, wiped the basin and checked the pan a last time.

*Now. If I leave it until I feel ready, he'll be sitting there all day.*

He carried the pan to Kaneki and found Rimuru at the end of the long table, with the cold cup still between his hands.

"Can we go for a walk?" Mau asked him.

Rimuru looked up.

"Not here," said Mau, gently. "The north field. Just us."
