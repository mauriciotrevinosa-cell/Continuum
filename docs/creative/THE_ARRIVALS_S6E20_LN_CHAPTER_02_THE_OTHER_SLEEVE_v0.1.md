# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 22 — The Other Sleeve

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S6E20
**Continuity state:** Day 448, before lunch. Mai helps repair Yuka's gate coat while Maki examines the damage and asks about the trip. In a short private exchange Maki names the hands she saw; Yuka describes being frightened and getting home, without disclosing the romance or making Maki forget her suspicion. Maki postpones further questions rather than claiming certainty. Yuta hears only technical gate stories later. The women also have ordinary work and humour unrelated to the secret.

*When: Day 448, before lunch. In the workroom, Mai has the coat turned inside out. Yuka is holding a sleeve in the wrong place.*

"If you move," said Mai, "I'll sew you into it."

Yuka stopped moving.

"I mean it."

"I know."

"You say that, but your elbow keeps wandering."

Maki stood on the other side of the table, holding the coat steady with two fingers. She could have held it with one. Yuka had been watching her hands since breakfast, trying not to look as if she was watching them.

Mai had let her keep her own needle for the first few minutes. Then she had examined the loop of thread Yuka was working into the torn cuff, made a sound through her nose and taken the needle away.

"It would have held," Yuka said now.

"A prisoner?"

Maki laughed. Yuka looked at her in surprise, and Maki looked back as if to ask what was surprising about it.

The workroom window was open a hand's width. Outside, somebody was moving the empty crystal crates away from the kitchen step. A soldier walked past carrying four of them stacked so high that only its boots showed. From somewhere behind the crates, Anko told it that she had meant *beside* the wall, not *on* the wall.

Mai cut the thread with a small pair of scissors.

"There. Now try."

Yuka put her arm through the sleeve. She bent it, reached across the table and bent it again. The seam no longer pulled at the place near her thumb.

"That's better," she admitted.

"Tragic," said Mai. "I was hoping you'd tell me I should have asked the dungeon for advice."

* * *

Maki had spread the rest of Yuka's things on the clear end of the table.

Not searching. Yuka had brought them herself, dropping the pack with a noise that made Mai look up from her work. The coat. The rope, cleaned but stained in places the water had not reached. A strip of something pale she had kept because it had held a knot in the gate and she wanted to show Grandpa. The little book with the rules in it.

Maki picked up the rope and worked it through her hands.

"Where did this happen?"

Yuka leaned over. "The glass. It looked smooth. It wasn't."

"You tied it there?"

"Over a lip. I couldn't see the back of it. We were above it."

"Did it hold?"

"Yes. Until we were down. Then it went."

Maki laid that part of the rope aside.

"Mark anything you can't trust. Don't put it back in the coil and hope you remember."

Yuka found a short piece of coloured thread in Mai's scraps and tied it round the worn section. Mai pointed at the thread, then at her own box.

"Ask."

"Can I have this?"

"Yes."

Maki was looking at the next length. Yuka pulled her chair closer.

It helped, doing this. There were questions she could answer. She could show her grandmother where the glass had caught, how far the knot had held, the place on the long stair where she had realised her footwork was wrong. Maki did not look astonished when she said she had changed it. She asked her to stand up and show her.

Yuka did, in the small space between the table and Mai's chair.

"Again."

She did it again.

"You learned that from the knight."

"Some of it."

Maki nodded. "Keep it. Don't do the end on a wet floor."

Yuka did the end deliberately. Her heel caught the table leg.

Mai lifted the scissors without even looking.

"Out."

They went out into the yard to finish.

* * *

There was not much room there either, between the sorting and the crates. Maki found a clear patch and stood at one end of it with her arms folded.

Yuka went through the turn, slowly. Weight back. Blade low, though she had no blade in her hand. The movement Igris had taught her, fitted into the way her grandmother had made her move years before. She could feel the join now. It no longer felt like she was borrowing somebody else's feet.

Maki watched twice. Then she came forward and moved Yuka's shoulder a fraction with her palm.

"Here. You give your elbow away when you look for the next thing."

Yuka tried it.

"That?"

"That."

She tried it faster. The end came round cleanly, her foot landing where she wanted it.

For a second she was back on the black glass with Mau watching from the edge of the light, his coat open and his hand still out where the pull had been. She could hear him laugh when she found it. Not at her. With the pleasure of seeing her do something she had been trying to do for days.

Her next step was late.

Maki caught her sleeve.

"Where did you go?"

"Nowhere. I was thinking."

"Do that after you've put your foot down."

Yuka reset. This time she stayed in the yard.

* * *

They were alone when Maki asked.

Mai had gone to find a matching piece for the lining, and the soldier with the crates was carrying them round the far corner. Yuka was rolling her rope, checking each loop before adding the next.

"I saw your hands," Maki said.

The rope stopped moving.

"When you came through. Before the last step."

Yuka looked up at her.

She had known. Of course she had known. Grandma noticed when a strap had moved a finger's width. She had been standing right in front of them.

"We were coming home," Yuka said.

Maki's face did not change.

"I could see that."

Yuka looked at the rope in her lap. She could tell her about the first arrow. About the day the fire almost went out. About being cold enough to put her hands against her own stomach to get the feeling back before she tied a knot. Those things had happened. There had been so many of them that she could have kept talking until lunch, telling nothing but the truth.

"I was frightened," she said.

"In the gate."

"Coming out too. We'd been there a long time. I didn't know what you'd say."

Maki sat on the step beside her. Not touching her. Close enough for the edge of her sleeve to brush Yuka's coat.

"I spent a day and a half wondering what I should have said before you went in."

Yuka pressed the coil against her knees.

"We were supposed to be an hour."

"I know what you were supposed to be."

She had not raised her voice. That was worse somehow. Yuka could have met anger with a list, with the rules in the little book, with the nine things she had done and the tenth they had brought to heel. There was nowhere useful to put any of that beside her grandmother on the step.

"I'm sorry," she said.

Maki looked across the yard at the sails spread in the weak sun.

"For making me wait?"

Yuka swallowed. "Yes."

"You didn't choose the gate changing."

"I know."

"I'm not asking you to apologise for surviving it."

The rope creaked under her fingers. Maki heard it, looked down and put her own hand over the part of the coil Yuka was crushing.

"Loosen that. You'll put a bend in it."

Yuka let go.

There was another question in the space between them. She could feel her grandmother leaving it there instead of asking it for her. She could have filled the space. She did not.

At last Maki stood.

"Finish the lining with Mai. Then show Grandpa your book. He sat up waiting too."

"Grandma."

Maki turned.

For one terrible moment Yuka thought she would say it. The stars, the scars, *I know, I know, I know*. Her mouth had already opened.

"Can we keep the coat?" she asked instead.

Maki looked at the grey cloth over her granddaughter's knees.

"It's yours."

She went back to the sorting. Yuka sat on the step until Mai came out and asked why she was holding a rope as if it had insulted her.

* * *

The lining took longer than the cuff.

Mai let Yuka do a little of it, with the coat flat on the table this time. She made her undo the first inch. Then the next half inch. By the third attempt Yuka's tongue was between her teeth and Mai had stopped reaching for the needle.

"There," Mai said at last. "That one won't eat your elbow."

Yuka held it up to the light. The stitches were not as small as Mai's. They were close enough together to look like a decision instead of an accident.

"Can you tell?"

"Yes."

Yuka lowered it.

"I can tell you mended it," said Mai. "That isn't a crime."

From the doorway, Yuta asked whether he could come in without being sewn to something. Mai looked at the things spread over her table.

"Depends. How well do you behave?"

"Historically?" said Yuta.

Maki, coming in behind him, made a noise of agreement that answered the question before he could.

Yuka cleared a space for her grandfather. She opened the little book to the drawings of the ritual circles and began explaining where the summoner count sat. Yuta listened, asked her to repeat one part and made her draw it again on a scrap so he could try it with his own fingers.

Maki remained beside the door. She watched their hands over the drawing. Then she picked up the other sleeve of the coat, turned it inside out and found another tear.

"Mai."

Mai looked at it.

"Of course," she said.

Yuka drew the book a little farther away from the scissors.
