# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 24 — The Lamp on the Floor

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 24
**Source episode:** S6E21
**Continuity state:** Day 448, night. Mau tells Yuka about his conversations with Rimuru and Frieren while they lie together on the floor blankets in her room. He explains Rimuru will not lie and that his apology made no promise of restored love. Yuka admits Maki saw their hands. They keep hiding the romance; neither treats that as a permanent solution. Affection and a brief kiss remain nonsexual. Mau has arranged ordinary sword practice with Yuta the following morning.

*When: Day 448, night. Yuka's room. The borrowed blankets are on the floor by the window again; the coat hangs over the chair with both sleeves mended.*

The lamp was too high.

Yuka had put it on the little table because that was where it belonged. From the floor, its light shone straight under the book she was holding and left the page in shadow. She changed the angle twice, held it above her face, complained and finally sat up to move the lamp.

"Not beside the blanket," Mau said.

"I know."

"You were putting it beside the blanket."

"I was considering it."

He folded a corner of the blanket back and cleared the bare board between it and the wall. She put the lamp there. Now the book was lit from below, making her look as if she was about to tell him something terrible about the roof.

"Much better," he said.

She lowered the book and looked at him over it.

He had taken his coat off but not his shirt. She was in her nightclothes with her feet under the spare blanket, and her hair kept catching between the book and her shoulder. He reached up and freed it when she tugged without looking. She waited until his hand came away before turning the page.

"Grandpa got stuck on this bit too," she said. "The count. He says if it really sees a single summoner, it ought to show where the second demand goes."

"Into me."

"He means on the page. Not in your general direction."

Mau looked at the drawing upside down. She turned it round for him, then shifted until she was beside him instead of trying to hold the book between them.

They argued about a line for five minutes. Neither had drawn it particularly well. In the end she crossed the whole thing out and wrote *ask Ciel*, then put the book on the chair beneath the mended coat.

"I spoke to them," he said when she lay down again.

She went still.

"Rimuru. And Frieren."

"I saw you go out with her."

He turned toward her. "I was going to tell you."

"I know."

She did not know, not until he said it. She was glad he could not see the difference in the low light.

* * *

He told her about Rimuru first.

Not every word. The shape of it, the things he had managed to explain and the places where explaining had not helped. Yuka lay with her hands tucked beneath her cheek, facing him, and listened until he said Ciel had told Rimuru about the night under the stars.

"Oh," she said.

He stopped.

"You knew she was there."

"I know. I just—" She looked at his shirt, where her eyes could rest without having to hold his. "I hadn't thought about someone else knowing it. Like that. All of it."

"She didn't do it to hurt you."

"I didn't say she did."

"I know."

Her mouth tightened. After a moment she reached across and put two fingers under his sleeve, just at the wrist, as if checking he was still there.

"Is he angry?"

"Yes. And he isn't sure which part he wants to be angry at first."

She nodded. That sounded like Rimuru. Not the easy smile he gave her at breakfast, when she talked too fast about the dogs, but the person under it who watched doors until everyone came through.

"Did he say he would tell?"

"He said he won't lie. If someone asks him what he knows, he won't tell them they imagined it."

Yuka shut her eyes.

"And you said?"

"That I wasn't going to ask him to."

She did not answer for a while. Her fingers remained under the edge of his sleeve.

"Grandma saw our hands," she said at last.

Mau looked at her.

"When we came through. She asked me about it today."

"What did you tell her?"

"That we were coming home. That I was frightened."

He waited.

"Both of those things are true," she said. Then, before he could agree: "I know that isn't the whole of it. I know."

He moved his hand so he could hold hers properly.

"What did she say?"

"Not much. She let me stop talking. That was worse."

Yuka stared at their hands. She had wanted all day to tell him about the step and the rope and the question she had not answered. Now that he knew, the room had not become safer. It had become the place where both of them knew it was happening outside.

"We can't do this for ever," she said.

"No."

"I don't know what comes after not for ever."

He ran his thumb over the side of her finger, once, without trying to turn it into an answer.

* * *

She moved closer when he told her about Frieren.

It was not something she had planned. She had been beside him, and then her forehead was against his shoulder, and then she was lying partly over him with one arm across his chest and her cheek on his shirt. He shifted the blanket over her feet and made room for her elbow.

"Is this all right?" he asked.

She nodded against him.

He told her about the garden bench. The apology. The pieces he had remembered in the gate. He did not repeat the little word on the bench or the way Frieren's face had changed when he said it. He was not keeping that back to make it less true. He simply found, when he reached it, that he did not want to hand somebody else's face across the room as an explanation.

"I told her I don't know if I can give her what she wants," he said. "And that she can speak to me without having to work out first if I'm going to leave."

Yuka could hear the two hearts beneath her cheek if she stopped breathing to listen. One warm and ordinary. The other a colder beat after it. She had heard both under the strange stars. They did not tell her what he was thinking.

"What did she say?"

"That she hadn't asked me to be different tomorrow."

Yuka looked at the lamp on the floor.

"She wouldn't."

"No."

"Did you want to?"

He was quiet.

She regretted the question almost as soon as she heard it. It had too many things inside it, and she did not know which one she wanted him to answer.

"Be different," she said, trying to make it smaller.

His hand came to rest between her shoulders.

"I wanted to stop being the person who told her to stay away. That was something I could do."

She nodded, slowly.

"And the rest?"

"I don't know how to want a feeling I can't remember. I know how it feels when you aren't in a room and I want you there. That part doesn't need anyone to explain it."

She closed her eyes. The relief was so immediate that it embarrassed her.

"That sounds terrible," she whispered.

"Which part?"

"How happy it made me."

He did not tell her she ought not to be happy. His fingers moved a little against her back, then stopped when she drew a breath as if to speak again.

She had nothing to put after it.

* * *

For a while they talked about other things.

Tank stealing the slate. The archer's empty hand. How Cairn had looked at the gutter as though it had come to challenge him. Yuka insisted that the heron was Bucket and would remain Bucket no matter what the window said. Mau asked whether she meant to tell it that herself.

"I'll write to it."

"It doesn't have a letter box."

"I'll ask Grandpa to make one."

He laughed beneath her cheek. She felt the movement all the way down her side and laughed with him, quietly, because the door was shut and it was late.

Then he told her he had asked Yuta about practising with the sword in the morning.

Yuka lifted her head.

"With Grandpa?"

"He said before breakfast, if I'm awake."

"He means before breakfast. Not when you can smell it."

"Those aren't the same?"

"He'll be there in the dark, looking apologetic about being early. Granny will come and hit both of you if you start making speeches."

"We won't make speeches."

She looked at him.

"I'll make a very small speech," he amended.

She laughed and lay down again. Her arm was beginning to go numb beneath her, but she did not want to move. He noticed when she shook her fingers and shifted so she could bring the arm out without having to get up.

She kissed him then.

Softly. The angle was awkward and their noses met before their mouths did. She laughed against his cheek, tried again, and felt his hand lift to the back of her head. For a moment there was only that, the lamp low beside the wall, his warmth through his shirt, and the small, fierce pleasure of not having to lean away.

When she drew back, he let her.

She stayed with her forehead against his for a few breaths.

"I'm tired," she said.

"Me too."

Neither meant only the gate.

* * *

He got up to put out the lamp.

She rolled onto her back and watched him move the book farther from the edge of the chair before pinching the flame out. He felt along the floor for his cup and set it beside the lamp where neither of them would kick it. Then he came back to the blankets and she shifted over to make room.

"The bird," he said into the dark.

"What bird?"

"In the garden. It stole the straw and kept dropping it. I meant to tell you. It looked furious."

She could not see his face. She could hear the smile.

"Did you help it?"

"I thought I'd let it be furious. It seemed busy."

She laughed once, sleepy now in spite of herself. Her hand found his under the blanket. He turned his palm up to hold it.

The corridor creaked outside. Someone went past toward the far end without stopping. They listened until the steps were gone.

"Mau?"

"Mm?"

"I'm glad you told me."

He squeezed her hand.

She lay beside him until his breathing slowed. Not counting. Not listening for a word at the hour he no longer woke. Just holding his hand in the dark, with the coat mended and hanging over the chair, and the little book closed beneath it, and tomorrow morning already waiting outside the door.
