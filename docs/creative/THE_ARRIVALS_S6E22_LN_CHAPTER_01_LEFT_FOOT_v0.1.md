# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 25 — Left Foot

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 25
**Source episode:** S6E22
**Continuity state:** Day 449, morning. Mau and Yuta practise with wooden swords in the yard, keeping the floor-86 cursed tool out of the exercise. Mau retains his learned movement despite the stolen autobiographical memories; Yuta adjusts a concrete habit rather than teaching him as a beginner. Yuta knows neither the gate romance nor Mau's private conversation with Rimuru. No technique, copy, new power or restoration is produced.

*When: Day 449, morning. The frost is gone from the middle of the yard, but stays white in the shade by the woodpile. Breakfast has not been called.*

Yuta gave him the heavier one.

Mau took it and turned it in his hands. The wood was smooth where other palms had worn it, rougher above the grip. There was a shallow dent on one side, a thumb's width below the point.

"That one?" he said.

"It's closer to the weight of yours."

"That's discrimination."

"You can have the shorter one."

Mau looked at the other sword. Then back at his.

"No, it's fine."

Yuta smiled a little and went to put his sandals under the back step, out of the way. He had already cleared the loose kindling from the practice space. Two baskets stood against the wall, full of it. A third had been left on the step with a pair of gloves inside.

Mau looked at the frost by the woodpile.

"Here?"

"Here," said Yuta. "The ground's dry. Nobody needs to come through this part."

He took up his own wooden sword. No ring of steel. No light coming out of anything. Mau's black scabbard stayed beside his coat on the bench, well beyond the cleared ground.

Yuta looked at it once, to make sure.

Then he looked at Mau's feet.

"Show me the turn you brought back from the gate."

* * *

Mau showed him.

The turn came easily. Weight off the front foot, point passing low, hands coming round together instead of one pulling the other behind it. He did not have to count. His feet knew where the next step was before he looked down.

Yuta stayed out of reach and watched him do it three times.

On the fourth, he put his sword out.

Mau's wooden blade struck it. A dry knock, louder in the morning than either of them expected. A bird went up from the roof.

"Again," said Yuta.

Mau turned. Their swords met.

"Again."

This time Yuta did not meet the cut. He moved his own point aside, barely a hand's width, and the resistance Mau had been expecting wasn't there.

Mau went past him.

Not far. One extra step. Enough for Yuta to touch the loose cloth under his arm with the end of the wooden sword.

"Dead," said Mau.

"Touched."

"It was a very serious touch."

"It was," said Yuta. "Your left foot."

Mau looked down.

"What about it?"

"You leave it where it was."

* * *

Yuta had him stand still.

He did not put Mau's hands anywhere or change the whole shape of him. He went round to the side, bent a little, and pointed with the wooden blade at the patch of earth under Mau's rear foot.

"There. After the turn, you're still pushing from there."

"Because I'm going forward."

"Yes. But you're going forward after I've stopped being in front of you."

Mau considered that.

"Inconvenient of you."

"I thought so too. When Maki did it."

Yuta stepped back into place and turned his wrist.

"Slowly. Don't try to hit anything. Just come through the turn."

Mau did. Half speed, then slower, until he could feel the moment when his shoulder wanted to go on and his left foot wanted to stay. Yuta lifted a hand before the imbalance carried him forward.

"There. Stop there."

Mau stopped. Not comfortably.

"Can you move left?"

He could not. Not without first bringing the foot in.

"Right?"

He tried. The sword dipped.

"I see it," he said.

"Good."

Yuta did the turn himself. It was smaller than Mau's. Less of a sweep. His rear foot came with him, quietly, and when he stopped he could have gone either way.

"I thought you were putting more into it," said Mau.

"Not at the end."

"It looks like more."

Yuta looked down at his own feet, as if checking.

"Maki says that too. Usually just before she knocks it out of my hands."

* * *

The exercise was a sleeve.

Yuta rolled the left one down over his wrist, where he had rolled it up to clear the sword, and held his arm away from his body.

"Touch this."

Mau looked at it.

"That's the whole exercise?"

"After the turn. And stop when you've touched it."

"All right."

"Slowly."

"You keep saying that."

"You keep not doing it."

Mau laughed, and came in slowly.

Yuta gave him the blade to turn round. Mau moved past it, brought his own point up, and touched the sleeve.

Then his left foot dragged forward half a pace.

Yuta looked at the foot.

Mau looked at the foot.

"I touched it."

"You did."

"I'd like that noted."

"Noted. Again."

The second time he thought about the foot so hard that he forgot the point. It went past Yuta's sleeve entirely. The third time he stopped too soon and had to reach with his arms. Yuta stepped away before he could stretch himself into another bad position.

"Don't put the sleeve further away," said Mau.

"I haven't."

"It feels further away."

"Bring yourself with the sword."

Mau stood in place a moment, listening to the words without trying to make them into anything else. He shifted his grip. The little finger of his left hand had been squeezing too hard.

On the next turn the foot came with him.

The point touched the cloth. Stopped.

He was still standing where he meant to stand.

Yuta nodded.

"That one."

Yuta did not move away.

"Stay there a moment."

He pressed two fingers lightly against the wooden blade, near the point. Not enough to force it down. Just a question of weight.

Mau's arms tightened. His rear heel lifted.

"There it goes again," said Yuta.

"I didn't move."

"You nearly did."

He took his fingers away. Mau let out a breath through his nose and brought the heel down. When Yuta touched the blade a second time, he let his elbows soften instead. The sword shifted a little. His feet did not.

"That's better."

Mau looked along the wood to Yuta's sleeve.

"I could have touched you again."

"Yes."

"Without starting over."

"Try."

He did. A short movement, smaller than the first. Yuta lifted his arm and the point went underneath the cloth without catching it. Mau stopped there instead of following the escaping wrist.

"Not that easily," said Yuta.

"You said try."

"I did."

Mau looked at the arm. At Yuta's face, which was almost entirely innocent. Then he took the point back and reset his feet.

"I'm beginning to understand the dents."

Yuta rolled the sleeve down again. It had ridden up while they were talking.

"They weren't all the same morning."

* * *

They did it until Mau stopped being pleased every time it worked.

That took longer than doing it right once.

The morning warmed. Somebody opened the kitchen window and a smell of onions came through it, then steam, then Ram's voice asking who had put an empty bowl back on the clean shelf.

Neither of them answered. Neither of them had been in the kitchen.

"You can change where you're standing now," said Mau.

Yuta lowered his blade.

"Are you asking me to?"

"I'm saying I noticed you haven't."

"Oh."

He moved a pace to the right.

Mau had to reset the whole thing. The light was in a different place on Yuta's sleeve. The bit of earth he had been using to judge the distance was behind him now. His first turn was too wide again.

Yuta touched his side.

"Very serious," said Mau.

"Very."

"You enjoyed that."

"A little."

Mau went back to the start.

He was breathing harder than he had expected to. Not badly. Just enough to make him aware of how much work it was to do a small thing carefully, again and again, without letting the last good one do the next one for him.

Yuta put his sword down.

"Water?"

"I can keep going."

"I asked if you wanted water."

Mau looked at him. Then at the jug on the bench.

"Yes."

* * *

They sat on the back step.

The wood of the practice sword lay across Mau's knees, warm now where he had been holding it. He turned it over and found the old dent again. It was deeper at one end than the other.

"Yours?" he asked.

Yuta leaned over to look.

"Maki's."

"Of course."

"I didn't move my hands."

"Did you learn?"

"Not the next time."

Mau laughed into his cup.

Across the yard a soldier came past with an empty basket in each hand. It stopped beside the woodpile, looked at the three baskets, and picked up the one with the gloves in it.

"Not that one," said Mau.

The soldier put it down. Picked up the other two.

Yuta watched it go round the corner.

"They've been helping since before breakfast."

"I asked them to."

"I know."

Mau drank. The water was cold enough to ache briefly in the back of his teeth.

"I like knowing how to do this," he said after a while. He looked at the wooden sword. "Even when I can't remember learning it."

Yuta did not answer immediately.

He turned his cup between his palms, once.

"You still notice when it feels wrong," he said.

"Usually after you've poked me."

"Before, sometimes. Just now. Your hand."

Mau looked at his fingers.

"I was holding too hard."

"You changed it before I said anything."

They sat for another minute. In the kitchen, somebody found the bowl. Ram said that finding it was not the point. Yuta finished his water.

"One more?"

Mau stood up before he could finish putting the cup down.

* * *

Yuta gave him the sleeve again.

Not exactly the same place. Not exactly the same angle. He turned his shoulder a little between each try, and once he took a small step away, and Mau missed and had to stop himself from chasing the cloth with the point.

"I was going to get there."

"I know."

"Eventually."

"That's the part I was worried about."

On the next try Mau let the miss finish. Brought his feet under him. Started again.

He touched the sleeve.

Yuta moved.

Mau moved with him. Not swinging. Not reaching. The point stayed between them, and his left foot was there when he needed it, and when Yuta lifted his own sword to close the line, Mau could actually step out instead of falling away from it.

He stopped.

Yuta stopped too.

For a moment neither spoke.

Then Mau looked down at the ground between their feet.

"That was different."

"Yes."

"Again."

Yuta smiled.

* * *

Maki found them halfway through again.

She had three bowls balanced along one forearm and a cloth in her other hand. She stopped beside the step and watched Mau turn, miss, gather his feet and try once more.

The point found the sleeve.

"You told him about the left foot," she said.

Yuta lowered his sword.

"I did."

"Did you tell him where you got that?"

"He asked about the dent."

Maki looked at Mau's sword. Then at Yuta.

"Which one?"

Mau turned the wood over.

There were three more on the other side.

He looked at Yuta.

Yuta took the bowls from Maki very carefully.

"Breakfast," he said.

"Is inside," said Maki. "You're carrying those back. Rem wants the shelf clear before she puts anything else on it."

They put the practice swords away by the bench, points down where nobody would catch a sleeve on them. Mau picked up his coat and his own sheathed sword. His left hand wanted to close too hard round the scabbard. He loosened it.

Yuta had two bowls. Maki had kept the third. Mau took the jug and the cups.

At the kitchen door, he waited for Yuta to go through before him.

Yuta stopped at the same moment to let Mau in.

They stood there, both waiting, until Maki went between them with her bowl.

"Try moving your feet," she said.

They followed her in.
