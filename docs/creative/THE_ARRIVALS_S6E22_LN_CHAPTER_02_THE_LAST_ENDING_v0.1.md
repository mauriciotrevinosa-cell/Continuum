# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 26 — The Last Ending

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 26
**Source episode:** S6E22
**Continuity state:** Day449 afternoon. Kessoku rehearse one of their faster songs in the established music room. Bocchi/Kita remain visibly girlfriends; Nijika and Ryo are their bandmates. A disputed ending turns into a practical rehearsal, interruptions and an argument over snacks. No new performance or project is scheduled; no private knowledge of Mau/Yuka is acquired.

*When: Day449, afternoon. The music room. The window is open a little at the top, and Nijika has put her sleeves over her hands between attempts because Ryo keeps saying she is ready when she is not.*

They had finished the song three times and were still playing it.

This was Ryo's fault.

The first ending had been the one on the page. Kita had let the chord ring, Bocchi had lifted her picking hand, and Nijika had brought both sticks down together. Ryo had continued underneath them for another four bars.

"An ending," she had said when they all turned toward her, "is a suggestion."

The second ending had been Ryo's suggestion. Nobody had known how to stop it.

By the third, Nijika had taken the sheet off the stool and written a thick line across the bottom.

"There," she said. "Past this, there is no more song."

Ryo leaned over to inspect it. Her bass rested against her knees; one sleeve had slipped over the heel of her hand.

"That's a short border."

"It's a very firm one."

"I could go round it."

"I will take your strings."

Kita made a small noise into her cuff. Bocchi looked down very quickly at her guitar, because if she saw Kita's face she was going to make the same noise, and once she started she would not be able to play.

She had already laughed into a chord. It had made a dreadful sound. Ryo had liked it.

They were standing close enough that Kita's elbow brushed hers when she turned the page. Kita had moved her stool there at the start, asked, "Am I in your way?" and received a very fast shake of the head. Now Bocchi could see a strand of red hair caught under the guitar strap.

She pointed to it.

Kita looked at the wrong shoulder.

Bocchi put down her pick, reached over and freed the strand. It took longer than it should have because Kita stopped moving and smiled at her halfway through.

"Done?" Nijika asked.

Bocchi snatched her hand back.

"The song," said Nijika. "Are we done discussing the song?"

"Oh," said Bocchi.

"For the moment," said Ryo.

Nijika pointed a stick at her.

* * *

They started four bars before the end.

This was supposed to save time. Bocchi was learning that the last four bars could occupy a whole afternoon if everyone approached them with enough conviction.

The fast songs were different in the music room. At the festival, they had gone straight through them, helped along by people eating and talking and Milim wanting everything played quicker. Here they heard all the places where one of them had been covering for another. Kita came off a chord a little early to reach the next. Bocchi filled the space without thinking. Nijika followed Bocchi, and Ryo followed nobody, although she generally arrived in the right place.

Generally.

"Four," said Nijika.

They played.

Bocchi went up the neck, Kita's rhythm beneath her. The last phrase turned. Nijika gave them the beat they had agreed on.

Ryo stopped.

Everyone else went on.

The room produced a small, ragged collision of guitar strings and a stick striking the rim.

Nijika stared at Ryo.

"I respected the border," Ryo said.

"You stopped before it!"

"With room to spare."

Kita put her hand over her mouth. Bocchi's shoulders started shaking.

"You're both encouraging her," Nijika told them.

"I'm not," Kita said, with almost no voice.

"You are visibly encouraging her."

Ryo rested both hands on her instrument. "I thought we wanted a clean ending."

Nijika set her sticks down very carefully. She stood up and went to the window. For a moment Bocchi thought she was going to leave through it, although it was much too narrow.

She closed the top gap instead.

"Now," she said, coming back, "nobody can hear us. Again."

* * *

On the next attempt, Kita was late.

She knew it before anyone said anything. She had been watching Nijika's right hand, waiting for the finish, and missed the change under her own fingers. Her chord came in thin. Bocchi stopped with her.

"Sorry," Kita said. "I was trying to watch the ending."

"Watch me," Nijika said. "Not the sticks."

"Which part of you?"

"My face."

Ryo looked interested. "How will your face end the song?"

Nijika showed her.

"Very clear," said Ryo.

Bocchi bent over her guitar. She was beginning to hurt around the ribs.

Kita touched her wrist behind the neck of the instrument, out of Nijika's view, which did not help at all.

"Sorry," she whispered.

"Don't," Bocchi whispered back.

"Don't be sorry?"

"Don't make me laugh."

Kita withdrew her hand, looking pleased with herself.

Bocchi looked at the marked sheet. She had written the passage in pencil, with the notes close together where she had run out of room. The last chord sat under a small smear from her thumb. Nijika's border now ran through the bottom of its stem.

"Maybe," she said.

The others waited. That was easier in here than anywhere else. It still took a moment.

"Maybe we don't need to look."

Nijika turned toward her properly.

"If you do the little bit on the rim first," Bocchi said, pointing with the pick, "before the last chord. So we can hear where it is."

Nijika tried it. Two light taps, then the finish.

Bocchi shook her head.

Nijika tried it again, putting the taps closer together.

"That," said Bocchi.

"You want that every time?"

Bocchi nodded.

They went through the four bars. Kita listened instead of staring at Nijika's hands. The small taps came beneath her chord, a quick little step, and all four of them stopped together.

For a second nobody moved.

Then Ryo played one extra note.

Nijika threw a cloth at her.

* * *

There was a knock.

Ryo caught the cloth against her bass and said, "It's open," just as Nijika called, "Wait a second."

The door remained shut.

"Which?" Taihei asked from the other side.

"A second," Nijika said. "She's holding stolen property."

"You threw it at me," Ryo said.

"And I'd like it back."

Bocchi got up because she was nearest the door. She put the pick on her stool where she would not lose it, rested her guitar safely and opened the door.

Taihei had a small covered bowl in one hand and a folded cloth in the other.

"Is this a bad time?"

"No," said Nijika.

"We were almost finished," said Kita.

Ryo looked at her with genuine admiration.

Taihei held out the cloth. "These were cooling on the shelf. I thought you might want them before they get put away."

There were four small pieces of pear bread inside.

Ryo put down her bass.

"That is an excellent thought."

Nijika took the cloth before Ryo could. "Thank you. What's in the bowl?"

"The rest," Taihei said. "For downstairs."

Ryo's hand, which had begun moving toward it, changed direction and tucked her hair behind her ear.

He noticed. He politely did not mention it.

"Can you hear much from the kitchen?" Kita asked.

"The fast part, sometimes. Through the yard."

"The ending?" Nijika asked.

Taihei considered this with the seriousness he brought to questions which might have a correct answer.

"I thought you'd started another song."

Ryo nodded. "A common response to my work."

"Thank you for the bread," Nijika said quickly.

He smiled and went back down the corridor with his bowl. Bocchi shut the door.

The instant it clicked, Nijika turned to Ryo.

"You get one."

"I have two hands."

"You get one piece."

"A quarter of the band. A quarter of the bread. Harsh, but consistent."

* * *

They ate on the floor because the stools had guitars on them.

Bocchi sat beside Kita, their knees touching. She picked a crumb off her trousers and put it in her mouth before Ryo could notice. Ryo had eaten hers so quickly that there was already a debate about whether anyone had actually seen her receive it.

Nijika had.

Nijika had counted.

Kita broke the end off her piece and offered it to Bocchi. Bocchi shook her head, then pointed at the bigger half in Kita's other hand.

Kita stared at her.

Bocchi stared at what she had done.

"I didn't—"

Kita started laughing.

She passed over the larger half, keeping the little end, and leaned her shoulder into Bocchi's until Bocchi stopped trying to explain. Ryo watched this exchange with the expression of someone studying a trade agreement she had not been invited to negotiate.

"Could I also—"

"No," said Nijika.

"You don't know what I was going to say."

"Were you going to offer to sweep?"

Ryo looked down at the crumbs.

"No."

"Then no."

They stayed on the floor longer than the bread lasted. Kita hummed the last phrase, got to Nijika's two taps and made them with her tongue against her teeth. Nijika copied her. Ryo added the extra note.

Bocchi could hear how it should go now, away from the guitar. She took the page from the stool and turned it over on her knee. There was room on the back. She wrote the ending out again, leaving more space between the marks.

"What are you doing?" Kita asked.

"So we all have the same one."

She drew a little arrow over Nijika's cue. Then she wrote, quite small, at the end:

*No more Ryo.*

Kita bent forward to read it. Her hair brushed Bocchi's cheek.

"That's very strict."

"There was room," Bocchi said.

* * *

They played the whole song once before stopping.

At first the afternoon seemed to have made no difference. The same brisk beginning, Kita leaning into the rhythm, Nijika keeping them moving. Bocchi caught herself waiting for the difficult bit and looked down at the strings instead. There was plenty to play before it.

Ryo came in exactly where she should. This was usually what happened when it mattered. Bocchi would never understand how Ryo could be so difficult to rehearse with and so easy to hear beside her.

The change came. Kita reached it on time.

Four bars. Three. Nijika's quick taps.

Bocchi's hand came down across the strings.

Silence.

All four of them, finished.

Nijika held her sticks where they were until she was certain. Then she lowered them.

"Right," she said. "There."

Kita let out a breath and turned to Bocchi with a wide smile. Bocchi found herself smiling back before she remembered to be embarrassed.

Ryo lifted a finger.

"I have a suggestion."

"Tomorrow," Nijika said.

"It concerns the beginning."

Nijika stood up, took the page and folded it carefully in half. Kita put her guitar away. Bocchi went looking for the pick she had left on the stool and found it under the cloth she had already checked twice.

By the time she freed it, Ryo was at the door.

"Where are you going?" Nijika asked.

"To assist Taihei."

Nijika looked at the cloth with its four empty places. Then at Ryo.

She put her sticks down and followed her at a run.
