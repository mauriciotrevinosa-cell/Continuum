# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 27 — Two Small Pulls

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S6E23
**Continuity state:** Day 449, afternoon. In a cleared practice area, Mau, Yuka and Umaru test simultaneous use of the existing loaned Limitless pull under Ciel's monitoring and Gojo's observation. Two reduced outputs can operate within a capped shared budget, with weaker separated effects and additive action on a nearby test object; this is not a second complete technique or a universal exact size-to-force law. Mau still bears the loan's strain. They stop before pursuing Red, large output or other branches. No points are spent, no romance is exposed and no new contract for Umaru is created.

*When: Day 449, afternoon, overlapping the music-room rehearsal. The clear ground beyond the workshops. Two chalk circles, a board across a pair of low trestles and Gojo standing where nobody has asked him to stand.*

"Not there," Umaru said.

Gojo looked down at his feet.

"There isn't a mark."

"You're standing on it."

He moved. The chalk line beneath his shoe had become an opinion rather than a boundary. Umaru knelt, repaired it and pointed to a place beyond the trestles.

"There. Observers."

"I thought I was the expert."

"You can observe expertly."

He went where she pointed. Yuka turned away to laugh, and Mau pretended to find something interesting about the board.

Umaru had measured the distance between the circles with a piece of string. She had made them move the trestles once because the first patch of ground sloped, and a second time because Ear came up to see what the circles were for and lay down on both of them.

He had been sent back down with much regret.

Now the board was level, the ground clear and the small wooden block in the middle was definitely not moving on its own.

Umaru checked it with one finger, just in case.

"First," she said. "One person. The ordinary one."

"Within the limit," Gojo added.

"Within the limit," she repeated, and wrote it at the top of the sheet.

* * *

The question had started over bread.

Yuta had asked whether the contract left Mau without the thing he was lending, or whether he simply chose not to use it while Yuka did. Mau had answered that he could still feel it. Yuka had said she could feel it too. Umaru had stopped chewing.

By the time the bowls were cleared, she had a sheet and three questions. By the time they reached the practice ground, there were eleven.

One had already been crossed out: *Can we try the red one?*

Under it, in Gojo's hand, was *NO*.

Mau stood in the first circle and looked at the block. He built the pull beyond it, small, the knot drawing toward a point that was not quite there. The wood slid a little across the board, leaving a track in the chalk dust Umaru had rubbed over the surface.

"Stop."

He let it go.

She marked the end of the track, moved the block back and asked him to do it again. Three times. Then three more, with a little less, until they had something he could repeat without the dust jumping off the board or the trestles shifting beneath it.

"That one," she said.

Mau held the feeling in mind. "Not the biggest."

"I don't want the biggest. I want the same."

Gojo had stopped smiling. He came nearer, keeping outside the repaired line, and looked at the place the knot had been.

"Can you give her that and nothing else?"

Mau glanced at Yuka.

"That's what we've been doing."

"I know what you did in the gate. I'm asking what you're handing over now. Not the shell. Not the sky. Not whatever else you reach for when this feels small. This."

Mau shut his eyes for a moment.

*The pull only,* said Ciel. *Let me check the boundary.*

He felt the thin route take its shape. Familiar after the days in the gate, but still something that lived across his own body before it reached her. Ciel followed it, paused at one place and asked him to ease his grip.

He did.

"The pull," he said aloud. "Nothing else."

Yuka lifted her hand in the second circle.

* * *

Her first attempt caught the block and spun it.

Not far. Enough to send it off the chalk track. She lowered her hand with an irritated noise.

"I know."

"I haven't said anything," Umaru said.

"You leaned toward the paper."

"That's where the writing goes."

Mau laughed. The knot in his own hand wavered with it, and Gojo looked at him immediately.

"One at a time for now."

He let his go.

Yuka did it again, putting the centre a little lower. The block moved straight. Umaru marked it, set it back and asked for another. Yuka had learnt patience in the gate, though she still made a face at the board whenever it was facing away from the girl with the chalk.

The third track was close to the second.

"There," said Umaru. "Now we know what yours does on its own."

"It's his," said Yuka.

"I know whose it is. I mean your turn."

They switched off, let the route rest and tried once more. Ciel asked Mau about the ache behind his eyes before he thought to mention it.

"A little," he said.

Umaru looked up. "From what?"

"Holding it for her. Same as inside. Not bad."

She wrote that down without changing *a little* into a number.

Gojo crouched to look along the board. "All right. Now you both ask for less."

* * *

The first time, neither of them got anything useful.

Mau felt for the old shape and deliberately made it smaller. Yuka did the same. The wooden block moved, hesitated and turned sideways again. His knot flickered out when he looked at hers.

She dropped her hand.

"You stopped."

"I was watching you."

"Watch yours. I can watch mine."

"That's what I was checking."

"Very kind. Stop it."

They reset the block. Umaru made Gojo get off the corner of her sheet.

The next time they kept the knots apart. Two small centres, on the same side of the wood, one a little farther along the board than the other. Mau could feel the second demand at the edge of his own. When he tried to make his larger, hers thinned.

"There," Gojo said sharply. "You felt that?"

Both lowered their hands.

"Mine went weak," said Yuka.

"I took more," said Mau.

Umaru put down the block she had been about to move. "From what she had?"

"From what we were holding between us. I think."

*At this limit,* Ciel said. *The second outlet did not make a second supply. It divided what you permitted through.*

Mau repeated it.

Gojo nodded. "So don't decide you've found a way to make two of me. One is quite enough."

"Nobody asked for that," said Yuka.

"A hurtful response to excellent supervision."

Umaru put the block back.

* * *

They found the balance slowly.

One knot, then the other. The two together, small enough that neither had to wrestle the other for room. With the centres separated, the tracks were short and uneven. With them nearer, both acting on the same face of the block, the wood slid farther. Once it reached the old mark made by Mau alone.

Yuka looked up, bright.

Umaru lifted her chalk. "Again."

"You saw it."

"I saw one."

They did it again. A little short. A little long. Close enough that Gojo was looking at their hands instead of the board by the fourth attempt.

Mau was beginning to understand how not to think of hers as something to hold in place for her. He made the room. She kept her part where she wanted it. When she shifted the centre, he felt it, but he did not have to reach across and correct it.

The next track was straight.

"Stop," said Ciel inside him.

He let go at once.

Yuka followed a heartbeat later, watching him rather than the block now.

"What?"

"Ciel. The pressure."

It was not a fever. Not even pain, quite. The ache behind his eyes had spread toward his temples while he concentrated on the measurements, and he had stopped noticing it because nothing had gone wrong.

Gojo straightened.

"Then that's enough."

"We can do one—" Yuka began.

She stopped herself. Looked at Mau. Lowered her hand completely.

"Enough," she agreed.

* * *

They sat on the low wall while Umaru copied the tracks before the chalk rubbed away.

Mau drank the water she handed him. The pressure eased with the route closed. Yuka moved as if to rub her own eyes, remembered which of them had been carrying the loan and put her hands flat on her knees instead.

"It worked," she said.

"Small," said Gojo.

"Still worked."

"Yes. Small is where you learn whether you're wrong without removing a workshop."

From the music room came a rapid last phrase, a small collision of notes and a distant burst of laughter. Gojo looked toward it. Then at the trestles, which were still upright.

"They're taking more risks than we are."

Umaru brought the sheet over. She had drawn the board and the tracks with a care she usually reserved for people being difficult about accounts.

"What do we call it?" she asked.

"Two small pulls," said Mau.

"That's not a technique name."

"It's what we did."

She considered that, then wrote it at the top. Under it she added *same permitted budget, split*. Beside it, smaller: *not exact halves just because they look half as big*.

Gojo read over her shoulder. "Keep that line."

"I was going to."

"And no red tomorrow because you've had one successful afternoon."

Yuka looked at Mau.

"I didn't say it," he told her.

"Your face said it," she answered.

They got up to clear the board. Yuka took one trestle and Mau the other; Umaru rescued the sheet from Gojo before he could roll it up with the string. They carried everything back to the workshop, where the sawdust from the morning had gathered against the threshold.

Mau washed the chalk off his hands at the basin. A pale streak stayed near his thumb. He scrubbed it with the edge of the cloth while Yuka went in to find a place for the board.

Inside the house, someone was asking whether birds with legs counted. He looked toward the voice, still rubbing at the chalk, and went to see.
