# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 29 — The Long Piece

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 29
**Source episode:** S6E24
**Continuity state:** Day 449, evening after supper. Umaru helps Taihei prepare fruit while talking about working with Mau. She enjoyed the technical experiment but misses the unconsidered familiarity of their sibling bond. Taihei offers company without treating himself as replaced or promising a cure. They finish an ordinary task together. Neither knows the romance or Mau's private conversations; no reconciliation or restoration occurs by implication.

*When: Day 449, after supper. Most of the pots have been washed. Taihei has kept a small knife, a bowl of apples and one dry patch of the kitchen table.*

Umaru could peel an apple in one piece.

She announced this before Taihei asked her to help, which was the mistake. He put the bowl in front of her, handed her the knife and sat down opposite with a second one.

"How many?" she asked.

"However many you can do before you start explaining why the others don't count."

"They'll all count."

"Good."

She chose the roundest one.

Taihei watched her examine it, turn it over and check the place where the stalk had been. Then he went back to the one in his own hand without saying anything. That was one of his worse habits. It made her explain things he had not asked about.

"You need an even surface."

"I see."

"If it's lumpy, it isn't a fair test."

"Of what?"

She pointed the knife at him.

He pointed at the board, and she lowered it.

The first peel broke after a turn and a half.

"Warm-up," she said.

Taihei put his own apple in the clean bowl. Beside it lay one long curl of peel, unbroken.

She looked at it. Then at his knife.

"Swap."

He swapped.

* * *

They were for the next day.

Momo had found a bag from the last stores check beginning to go soft at one end and asked for it to be used before they lost the rest. Taihei had decided to cook the good parts down. There was no special meal, no birthday, nobody to impress. Just apples that needed doing before they became somebody else's unpleasant surprise.

Umaru liked tasks better when there was a reason to leave them until tomorrow. This one had arrived without that advantage.

She cut a small bruise out with the point of the knife. Taihei inspected it from across the table and nodded toward a second spot she had missed.

"I saw that."

"I know."

"I was coming back to it."

"Before or after you put it in the bowl?"

She removed it and dropped the little brown piece with the peel.

The kitchen was cooling. Through the half-open door came the low noise of the long room after supper, people talking across the space rather than over plates. Someone was trying to remember the beginning of a song and getting stuck on the same three notes. A moment later two people joined in, equally wrong.

Taihei smiled down at his hands.

"They were doing the end all afternoon," he said.

"I heard. Gojo said they were more dangerous than us."

"Were you dangerous?"

"Extremely controlled."

"That wasn't the question."

"The workshop survived."

"Ah."

She showed him the sheet with her dry hand, unfolding only the top so the corner would not go into the apple juice. He leaned over to look.

"Two pulls?"

"One supply. Two places to use it. We think. At the amount we tested."

Taihei followed the tracks she had drawn. He did not understand the technique, but he understood being shown something she had taken trouble over. He asked which mark had come first and waited while she found it for him.

By the time she finished explaining, there were six peeled apples in his bowl and two in hers.

One of hers had a little skin left by the stalk. She picked it back up before he could look.

* * *

"It was fun," she said.

Taihei reached for another apple.

"You sound surprised."

"I'm not. It's fun when it works."

"Even with Gojo standing on your paper?"

"He was doing it on purpose."

"Probably."

She bent over the apple, trying again for a long peel. The knife moved smoothly until she caught herself thinking about whether it would move smoothly. It dug too deep, and a thick piece came away.

Taihei did not mention it.

"Mau asked before he opened the fields for me," she said.

"That's sensible."

"I know."

He waited.

"He let me choose what to look at. Then he asked if he was holding it at the right angle."

"Was he?"

"Yes."

She trimmed the white flesh off the peel and put the good bit back in the bowl.

"And he thanked me after. For the whole afternoon. Not just the sheet. He said I'd made it easier to work out what they were doing."

Taihei stopped peeling for a moment.

"You did."

"I know I did."

She had answered too quickly. He looked at her, and she looked at the apple instead.

"That sounds horrible," she said. "Being annoyed because somebody said thank you."

"Are you annoyed?"

She considered lying. It would be pointless; Taihei could tell from how hard she was holding the knife.

"A bit."

He reached across and turned the board so she had more space.

"Which bit?"

* * *

She told him about the cards.

Not a particular game. The way Mau used to sit down halfway through whatever she was doing and reach for the wrong pile, sometimes deliberately, sometimes because he had not listened to the rules. The way she would slap his hand away, and he would look offended and reach for a different pile, and Milim would decide this meant all the piles were available to everyone.

"He asks now," she said. "Which ones he can touch. Whether we're finished. If there's room."

Taihei's apple turned slowly in his hand.

"There is room," Umaru said. "That's the stupid part. There's always room. I could move. I would move. He doesn't have to stand beside the table until somebody invites him."

Her voice had thickened without permission. She put the knife down before he could ask her to.

"He doesn't know that," Taihei said.

"I know he doesn't know. That's what everyone says. I know."

He nodded.

She pressed the heel of her hand against one eye. It was annoying. She had not cried all afternoon while she was being helpful. Now she was sitting in the kitchen with plenty of reasons not to get apple on her face, and her eyes had decided to become useless.

"I'm glad you're here," she said suddenly.

Taihei looked up.

"I didn't think you weren't."

"I mean it. I wanted you here before any of this. It isn't—"

"Umaru."

She stopped.

He reached for the clean cloth on the back of his chair and put it on the table beside her. Not over her hands. Just there.

"You don't have to explain that part to me."

She took the cloth. It smelled faintly of the shelf where the bread cooled.

For a while he peeled and she watched. He did not ask her to go on. Through the door, the people working on the song finally found the fourth note and immediately disagreed about the fifth.

"I thought if we did the numbers," she said at last, "he'd get irritated with me in the right way."

Taihei put the apple down.

"In the right way?"

"You know. Like I'm being a pain. Not like he's trying not to offend the person who's been very kind to him."

Taihei looked at her for a long moment. Then he picked up the sheet, folded it out of the way of the bowl and set it on the dry end of the table.

"You can be a pain without numbers," he said.

She made a noise that was almost a laugh.

"Years of practice," he added.

"I'd like my contributions recognised."

"They are."

* * *

She went back to the apples after a while.

There were still too many, partly because she kept selecting a promising one and deciding halfway through that it was the wrong shape for an honest attempt. Taihei moved the clean bowl closer to her and put the pieces she had saved into the pan.

"Do I stop trying?" she asked.

He did not answer until he had set the pan down.

"What do you mean by trying?"

"Getting him to—" She stopped. Shook her head. "I don't know. Sit down without asking."

"You could tell him there's a place."

"I have."

"Then you don't have to tell him every time."

She looked at him.

"You can leave the place there," Taihei said. "And you can have a bad evening about it without making a better plan."

She rubbed a damp patch off the board with the corner of the cloth.

"That isn't very productive."

"No."

"The Ministry disapproves."

"The Ministry can finish these apples."

He gave her the next one. It was lopsided, with a dent near the bottom. She turned it over and glared at him.

"Sabotage."

"Use the other side."

She did. The peel went round once, twice, long enough to hang over her hand. Taihei kept his eyes on his own knife, although she could tell he was watching the corner of hers.

She went slower.

The last bit came free without breaking.

For a second she held it up between finger and thumb, delighted in spite of everything. A long green curl almost touching the board.

Taihei put his knife down and inspected it gravely.

"Warm-up over?"

"You witnessed it."

"I did."

She laid it carefully along the table, well away from the scraps. Taihei rose to get the water for the pan. When he came back, she was trying to arrange the peel into a circle without breaking it.

"It won't keep," he said.

"I know."

She left it there until the apples began to soften over the heat. Then they carried the scraps out together, and she let the long piece fall into the bucket last, without making him watch.
