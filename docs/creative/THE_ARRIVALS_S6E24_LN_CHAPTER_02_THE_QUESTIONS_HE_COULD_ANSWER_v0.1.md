# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 30 — The Questions He Could Answer

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 30
**Source episode:** S6E24
**Continuity state:** Day 450, morning. Mau returns to Daphne's request for a clinic hour and consents only to conversation and ordinary observation, not examination inside his mind or alteration. Shoko attends with his agreement. They distinguish the smooth false autobiographical account from retained skills and names without discovering the thief, restoring memories or claiming a cure. Mau chooses to preserve inconsistent details rather than letting others dictate what to feel. The romance and Ciel/Rimuru's private account remain undisclosed.

*When: Day 450, after breakfast. The clinic door is open to let the sun warm the floor. Shoko has a clean cup; Daphne has brought something wrapped in a cloth which she has already eaten.*

Mau found them disagreeing about the cloth.

Daphne had spread it on the little table beside the clinic bed. Shoko wanted it off the table. Daphne pointed out that the food was gone, which seemed to her to settle the matter.

"Then there isn't a reason for the crumbs to stay," said Shoko.

Daphne considered this, gathered the corners of the cloth and held the small bundle in her lap.

"There."

Shoko looked up and saw Mau in the doorway.

"Something wrong?"

"No. I wanted to ask about the hour."

Daphne turned toward him at once.

He remained by the door.

"Talking," he said. "If we can do that. Not looking inside."

Shoko set down her cup.

"We can talk."

"And if I don't want to keep going—"

"We stop," said Daphne.

He looked at her. She had not said it impatiently, as if she had already offered it once and ought not to have to again. She was sitting with the empty cloth in her lap, watching him with the patient interest she sometimes gave a plate she was not yet sure was finished.

"All right," he said.

Shoko pulled the third chair out from under the desk.

"Me here?"

He nodded. "Please."

She moved her cup out of the way before sitting down. The room felt less like a room he had been brought into than one he had walked into himself.

* * *

Daphne asked what he thought had happened.

Mau looked at Shoko, who made a small movement with one hand: he could answer it any way he liked.

"Something happened in the field. Someone did something. I have days in my head that don't fit things you've all got written down."

"Which days?" Daphne asked.

"Coming here. The first gate with Yuka."

"Do you see them? Or do you know about them?"

He started to answer, then stopped.

There was a difference. He had never needed to take it apart before. When he thought of coming to the house, the memory gave him a doorway, the long table, Yuka already there. He could make the light warmer or colder by trying to remember what season it was. That did not feel like making it up. It felt like remembering badly.

"Some of both," he said. "There's a picture. And the feeling that I know what comes before and after it."

"Can you tell us that part?"

He told them the account he had been living with. Not the gate he had just left; the earlier one in the story that had been put where his past belonged. Coming after Yuka. Becoming close. The house finding room for him. It came easily until he reached a date, and then it became less a sequence of days than a sequence of things he was certain had happened.

Shoko did not correct him while he spoke.

That helped. He had not realised how much effort he was putting into preparing for the interruption.

When he finished, she asked whether he wanted water.

He did.

* * *

"I know some of it isn't true," he said, with the cup between his hands. "Before you tell me."

"I wasn't going to read the ledger at you," said Shoko.

"Everyone's got something with a day on it."

"That's because quite a lot of it happened."

"I know."

She waited until he looked at her.

"We don't have to argue with you about the date every time you say a sentence. That doesn't mean we think the other version is true."

He nodded. His fingers loosened on the cup.

Daphne had been turning the corner of her cloth between two fingers. Now she asked, "Do the other pieces arrive when you ask for them?"

"Not usually."

"When, then?"

"When something is like something else. A word. Moving. A thing I already know how to do. Sometimes someone's telling me about what happened, and there's a part I know before they say it. Then I go looking for it and it goes."

"Does it hurt?" Shoko asked.

"Not like the fever. It's more—" He stopped, frustrated. "I keep wanting to explain it by pointing at something behind my head."

Shoko leaned back. "You can say that."

He let out a breath, almost laughing.

"Then that. There's something there. When I turn to look at it, there's something else instead. And the something else feels just as much like mine."

Daphne looked toward the open door.

"A very tidy cupboard," she said softly.

Shoko's eyes moved to her. Daphne did not add anything about who had filled it.

* * *

They did not ask him to close his eyes.

They did not hold up a face and wait for him to say what it meant, or put a date in front of him until he agreed to it. Daphne asked which things he could do without remembering learning them. Mau described the sword practice with Yuta, the turn his feet had known and the mistake they had kept making anyway.

Shoko smiled at that.

"That sounds like sword practice."

"He poked me a lot."

"That also sounds like sword practice."

Daphne asked about reading. About the cups, and whether he knew which door led into the garden. About names.

He knew the names. Some had arrived in him with so little of the person around them that it felt impolite to use them as if they were friends. Others came with impressions he could not date: warmth, a smell of food, the sound of someone laughing from a room he could not find in the house.

"But you know who we are," said Shoko.

"Yes. I don't look at you and wonder what your name is."

"I know. I'm making sure we don't put that on the same line as the rest."

She wrote a few words on a small sheet, then turned it so he could read it. Names. Skills. Events. How close people feel. Four separate lines, not a column marked *remembers* with one answer beneath it.

He read them twice.

"That last one," he said.

"Yes."

"Can it be different from the one above?"

Shoko looked at Daphne, who had gone very still.

"It already is," Shoko said. "You've told us that."

Mau looked down. "I didn't know if you believed me."

"Believing you doesn't mean I think it's harmless."

"I know."

"Do you?"

He ran a finger along the bottom of the sheet without touching the writing.

"I'm beginning to."

* * *

He asked the question near the end.

The sunlight had reached the chair leg. Somebody went past the clinic carrying a basket and called through the open door to ask whether Shoko wanted a cup while the water was hot. She said yes, please, and the footsteps continued.

Mau waited until they were gone.

"Could you put it back?"

Daphne's fingers stopped turning the cloth.

"From what we have done today? No."

"And if we did more?"

Shoko did not move. Daphne answered him herself.

"I don't know where all the pieces are. Or which pieces this thing used to make the ones that fit. I can ask you questions. That is what we agreed to do. It hasn't taught me how to return something to you without taking anything else away."

He sat for a moment with that.

*Nor me,* said Ciel, quietly, inside him.

He had not forgotten she was there. It was the first time she had spoken during the visit, and he was grateful she had waited until he asked the question rather than filling every silence with an answer.

"Thank you," he said aloud.

Shoko looked at him.

"For not telling me yes so I'd let you try."

Daphne tucked the cloth into her sleeve. "Would that have worked?"

He thought about it.

"Maybe," he said. "A bit. If you'd said it the right way."

Shoko put the cap on her pen.

Neither woman used the opening.

* * *

They were not quite an hour.

Mau stood when he had had enough. Shoko stood too, asked whether he was dizzy, and accepted the answer when he said no. Daphne remained in her chair with the sunlight on her hands.

"Can I keep that?" he asked, pointing at the sheet.

Shoko held it out.

"If you want. You can add to it. Or not. It isn't homework."

He folded it once, keeping the writing inside, and put it in his pocket.

"Could we do this again? Talking."

Daphne nodded.

"You could come to tea," she said. "You used to do that without an appointment."

It hurt, just a little, hearing *used to* in the clinic again. But she was not making it into a requirement. She was looking at the sunlight on the floor as if the tea would be there whether he came or not.

"I could," he said.

"You don't have to bring anything."

Shoko looked at the crumbs Daphne had left on her own sleeve.

"You might have to bring something."

Daphne smiled, drowsy again, and went to the door with him.

Outside, the yard had warmed. The ice bow lay under its cover where it had been labelled, and someone had laid a fresh board beneath the last tray from the desert. Mau stopped at the water basin, took out the small sheet and read the four lines once more before folding it away.

Across the yard, Yuka saw him come out and lifted a hand.

He lifted his in answer. Then he went to find her, with something he had chosen to tell her and no promise of a different person waiting at the end of it.
