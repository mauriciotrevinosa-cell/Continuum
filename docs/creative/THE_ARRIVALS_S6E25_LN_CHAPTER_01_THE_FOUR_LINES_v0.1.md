# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 31 — The Four Lines

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 31
**Source episode:** S6E25
**Continuity state:** Day450, continuous from the clinic visit. Mau tells Yuka what he chose to discuss with Daphne and Shoko, including his continued refusal of intervention inside his mind. Yuka feels relief and shame together; Mau asks that future decisions remain speakable between them rather than requiring her permission. They plan lunch together the next day and keep the existing concealment unresolved. No new memory, cure or medical disclosure by Yuka occurs.

*When: Day450, late morning. The table outside the store cupboard. Yuka has been counting ration packets and has reached the same packet three times.*

He found her with one in each hand.

"Are those different?" he asked.

Yuka looked down. The wrappers were the same colour. The small marks on the ends were the same too. She put one in the left-hand pile and one in the right, then frowned and brought the second back.

"They're supposed to be."

Mau pulled out the empty chair. "Can I help?"

She pushed the small list toward him. "The ones that are going to expire first on this side. Not the ones you like most."

"That's a different list?"

"With you, yes."

He picked up a packet and examined it. His fingers knew where the little seam would open. He turned it over instead and found the mark she was using to sort them.

She watched the pocket of his coat.

He noticed.

"The clinic," he said. "I went to talk."

"I saw you come out."

"I know. I wanted to tell you before you spent the afternoon wondering which bit they'd put back."

Her eyes moved to his face at once.

He laid the ration down.

"They didn't put anything back. Or take anything out. We talked. That was all."

Yuka sat down slowly, as if she had forgotten the chair was behind her.

* * *

He put the folded sheet on the table.

Not between the piles. Beside them, on the dry part where somebody had scraped old wax away with the edge of a spoon. She looked at it without reaching for it.

"You can read it," he said.

She unfolded it. Four lines, in Shoko's hand. Names. Skills. Events. How close people feel.

"That last one," she said.

"I asked about it."

Yuka did not look up.

"Whether it could be different. Knowing who somebody is and feeling what everyone says I feel. Shoko said it already was. That she believed me."

"She would."

"She also said believing me didn't make it harmless."

The cupboard door creaked behind them. Rem came out with a jar, apologised for having to squeeze past the chairs and asked Yuka to leave the finished pile where Momo could find it. Yuka nodded, waited until she had gone, and smoothed the sheet against the table with both palms.

"Did you ask Daphne if she could?"

"Put it back? Yes."

"And?"

"She doesn't know how to do that safely. Not from sitting with me asking questions. And I didn't give her permission to try anything else."

Yuka closed her eyes for a moment.

He watched the relief move across her face. It was small and immediate and gone almost as soon as he recognised it. She opened her eyes, looked at the words again and folded the sheet along its old crease.

"I hate that I was glad," she said.

He took the paper when she held it out, but did not put it away.

"You can be glad I'm still here."

"That isn't all of what I meant."

He waited.

"I know what she wants," said Yuka. "Frieren. I know what everybody wants. And then I see you coming out of there and all I can think is whether you've—" She stopped. Pressed her hands together beneath the edge of the table. "I was counting the same packet because I couldn't make myself go and ask."

Mau looked at the two piles. He moved the one he had just placed; it had gone on the wrong side.

"I wasn't going to find out something that important and leave you here guessing."

"You didn't tell me before you went."

"No."

He did not say he was sorry immediately. She watched him deciding whether he should, and wished he would not make this one easy for her by saying it because she had gone quiet.

"I wanted to go," he said. "I knew what I didn't want. I told them. They listened."

She nodded.

"I want to be able to tell you I did something without it meaning I should have asked you first."

The words hurt. Not because he had said them harshly. Because she could hear the little frightened demand inside what she had just asked.

"I don't want you to ask me first," she said.

"I know you don't want to."

She looked up then.

"That's worse."

"A bit," he admitted.

She let out a breath that nearly became a laugh. He looked relieved, though he did not take the words back.

* * *

They did the packets for a while.

Once she had explained which mark mattered, he was quick at it. He held them steady while she separated a pair stuck together, and she took the ones with damaged corners to check rather than making them somebody else's problem on the next expedition. The work made a small, useful noise between them.

"What about if I want to go too?" she asked at last.

"To talk to Daphne?"

"Or Shoko. Or anyone."

He looked up from a wrapper. "Go."

"Even if it's about you?"

He thought for a moment.

"I'd rather know if something's wrong than have you trying to solve it with a pile of rations. But you don't have to give me a transcript."

She turned a packet over twice.

"You didn't tell me every word yesterday. With Frieren."

"No."

"You told me what you said."

"And what she answered when it was something you needed to understand. Not everything about how she looked saying it."

Yuka nodded slowly. She could imagine how much she would have wanted to hear, and how bad she would have felt afterward for wanting it.

"I don't want to become someone who has to know all of it," she said.

Mau put the packet down.

"Then tell me when you notice yourself doing it. I'll try not to make it hard to say."

"You'll have to try very hard."

"I've been told that."

She smiled despite herself. He smiled back and held up the wrapper he had been examining.

"This one's not on the list."

She took it. It was a scrap of cloth she had folded round two needles so Mai would stop finding them loose in her book.

"Don't eat that."

"I wasn't going to."

"You looked as though you were considering it."

He took one of the intact packets and placed it in the right pile, very carefully.

* * *

When the cupboard shelf was clear, they carried the finished stacks inside.

Mau had to turn sideways to pass her. For a moment they were very close in the narrow space, her sleeve brushing his coat, the wooden shelf hard against her back. His hand came up to steady the stack she was holding, and she looked at his mouth before she could stop herself.

He noticed that too.

His eyes went toward the open door. He did not kiss her there. He lifted the top two packets off her stack instead and put them away above hers, so she could move.

She was glad and disappointed at once. She was getting tired of being so many things at once.

"Lunch tomorrow," he said when they came out.

"What about it?"

"With me. Not here. If you want."

She stood with the little list in her hand.

"We ate lunch together today."

"We haven't eaten lunch yet."

"We sorted lunch. That's more committed."

"Then tomorrow we could attempt eating it."

She laughed. "Where?"

"Somewhere we can sit down without being asked to move a crate. I haven't found it yet."

"Very well planned."

"I'm giving myself a day."

She folded the list, checked that both piles were where Rem had asked for them, and put the scraps in the small waste basket. At the door she turned back.

"Not the sea."

He tilted his head. "You didn't like the sea?"

"I loved the sea. I mean not another whole day where we forget to say where we're going."

"No. Lunch. We'll be back before anyone starts looking at the sky."

She studied him for a moment, then nodded.

"I can bring something that isn't rations."

"That sounds like a criticism of our work."

"It is."

He grinned. She wanted to put her hands on his face and kiss him properly until the last of the clinic's fear left her, but someone was coming toward the cupboard with an empty jar. She stepped out of the doorway instead.

"Tomorrow," she said.

He took Shoko's sheet off the table and put it back in his pocket before following her toward the kitchen.
