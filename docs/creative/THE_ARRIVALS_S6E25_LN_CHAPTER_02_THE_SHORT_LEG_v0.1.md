# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 32 — The Short Leg

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 32
**Source episode:** S6E25
**Continuity state:** Day450 afternoon. Mau helps Bocchi and Kita settle a rocking rehearsal stool using an ordinary offcut and repositioning. Their familiar humour briefly returns without restoration of his stolen memories. Bocchi/Kita remain a couple; they do not learn the romance or the contents of the clinic conversation. No instrument, power or technology is invented.

*When: Day450, afternoon. The music room door is open. Kita is on the floor with her chin nearly level with a stool seat, while Bocchi sits on it and tries not to move.*

"Now," said Kita.

Bocchi moved her left foot.

The stool knocked against the floor.

"Again."

She moved it back. Another knock.

Kita reached under the seat, felt along the nearest leg and frowned at it. Bocchi could see the top of her head, the place where her part went crooked because she had tied her hair up without a mirror.

"It's that one," Kita said.

She put a folded scrap of paper beneath it.

Bocchi shifted her weight. The knock came from somewhere else.

Kita looked up at her.

"Are you doing it on purpose?"

"I don't know how."

"Then don't learn."

There was a sound from the doorway, not quite a laugh. Mau was standing outside with a length of dry wood in one hand and a cloth in the other. He had stopped on his way past.

Kita sat back on her heels.

"Do you know anything about stools?"

Mau looked at the wood he was carrying, as though she had caught him impersonating someone qualified.

"Mostly which way up they go."

"We're past that."

"I was afraid you might be."

Bocchi made room without thinking. She stood, carrying the guitar against her chest, and pushed the stool toward him with one foot.

Then she remembered that she had not asked whether he was coming in.

He came in.

* * *

The piece of wood was for Wakana. Mau put it and the cloth down together by the door, out of anybody's path, and crouched beside the stool.

Kita explained the evidence.

It knocked when Bocchi moved her left foot. It did not knock when Kita moved hers, but Kita could not sit on it the same way, which Bocchi thought was a generous way to describe the trial. It had not done this yesterday. Yesterday it had been against the other wall.

Mau took hold of the seat and pressed opposite corners.

Knock.

He turned it halfway round.

Knock.

"That does seem to be its opinion," he said.

Bocchi looked at Kita, who was biting the inside of her cheek.

"We thought the leg was short," Kita said.

"One of them is doing a very good impression."

He checked the joints. None moved under his hand. When he tried to pinch the paper out from under the leg, two fingers of his left hand would not close properly around it, and he changed hands without saying anything.

Bocchi looked at the guitar instead.

She had become practised at looking at something else when his hands did that. It never made her forget why. It only kept her from making him explain it again.

Mau lifted the stool, moved it a little toward the door and set it down.

It sat quietly on all four feet.

Kita put both hands flat on the floor.

"Oh."

The boards were not level beside the window. Not enough to see from standing height. Enough to have occupied twenty minutes of their afternoon.

Bocchi looked at the empty space where she had been sitting.

"But I want to sit there."

The words were out before she had arranged them into a more reasonable request. She nearly took them back.

Mau looked from the window to the stool.

"All right. Then we fix it for there."

Kita's hand found Bocchi's behind the guitar. She squeezed once and let go before either of them could drop anything.

* * *

The paper worked if they folded it enough times.

It also came out every time Bocchi moved her foot. Kita tried turning the fold inward. Mau tried putting it under a different leg. On the third attempt Bocchi sat down, played two quiet chords and heard the paper tearing beneath her.

She stopped.

"That is a very expensive sound," she said.

Mau looked up from the floor.

"Is it?"

"It was part of a song."

Kita bent and rescued the scrap. On its underside, across several creases, there were three little pencilled marks.

"The back of a song," she said.

"Still."

"It was the version you didn't like."

"It might become the version I like."

Mau put his chin on his knee. "Should we be testing furniture with music?"

"We were desperate," Kita said.

They took the stool to the doorway so Bocchi could keep playing while the two of them considered alternatives. For a minute she played with the window behind her instead of beside her, and it was wrong. The light came down across her shoulder differently. She could see their faces too clearly when she lifted her eyes.

Kita noticed.

"We'll put it back," she said.

"I'm not—"

"I know. But we'll put it back."

Mau went to look for an offcut. He asked before taking one from the workshop floor and returned with a short, thin sliver, pale on one side where it had been newly split. It was too thick. Kita held the stool steady while he tried it, then he looked at the sliver, looked at the stool and went back to the workshop.

Bocchi listened to him ask someone a question. Not a request for a new piece of furniture or a better room. Just a thinner bit of wood.

He came back with two.

"Apparently," he said, "I was being optimistic."

* * *

They tested them one at a time.

One was nearly right. The other made the stool lean the wrong way and Kita had to catch Bocchi's elbow, although Bocchi was certain she would not actually have fallen. Almost certain. She had made a noise which did not support her position.

"The first," she said, when she could speak.

Mau put the first one back.

Kita pressed down on the seat while Bocchi sat. Then she moved her hands away.

Bocchi moved her left foot.

Nothing knocked.

She moved it again.

Mau, still crouching, looked up at her. "You could try the right one. If you want a second opinion."

She did. Still nothing.

Kita stood up, brushing dust off her knees, and declared it finished.

Then she moved the stool to sweep underneath it.

Mau and Bocchi both said, "No," at once.

Kita froze with it half an inch off the floor.

They stared at one another. Then at the little sliver of wood, which had stayed exactly where it was supposed to be.

"I was going to put it back," Kita said.

"In the same place?" Bocchi asked.

"Yes."

"How do you know which place?"

Kita looked at the floor.

Mau put his hand over his mouth.

Bocchi started laughing.

She had not meant to. It escaped while Kita was looking from one set of floor marks to another, trying to decide which patch of dust was evidence. Then Kita looked at her and started too, still holding the stool in midair.

Mau took it before she dropped it on anybody.

"All right," he said. "We may need a system."

"No systems," Bocchi managed.

He looked at her in surprise. Then he laughed properly, and put it down.

* * *

There were two small pencil marks on the floor when they finished. Ordinary pencil, no magic. One beside the foot with the sliver beneath it, one beside the foot nearest the wall.

Kita swept round them with an expression of great concentration.

Mau sat on the floor by the door, where he would not get in her way. Bocchi had told him the wood needed testing with a whole song. She had said it so seriously that he had looked at the stool again before agreeing.

She chose a short one. She did not ask him to recognise it.

At first she kept waiting for the knock. Her left foot moved cautiously, the guitar warm against her arm, and she played the change too softly. Then Kita set the broom against the wall and picked up her guitar. She came in beneath the next phrase without asking which version they were doing.

Bocchi knew from her first chord that she had chosen the right one.

Mau leaned against the doorpost and listened. He watched Kita when she sang, the window when neither of them did. His hands lay loose on his knees.

The stool did not knock once.

When they finished, Bocchi let the last chord fade before moving her hand. Kita looked down at the sliver and nodded to it with exaggerated respect.

"It can stay."

"It has passed," Mau agreed.

Bocchi's fingers tightened around the pick. There had been dozens of afternoons when he sat in here and said something foolish while she tried to explain a piece of music. She remembered all of them at once, inconveniently. For him this had been fixing a stool, and then hearing a song because someone had asked him to.

He looked up at her.

"Was it enough?"

For a moment she did not know which question he was asking.

Then his eyes moved to the stool.

"Yes," she said. "I think so."

Kita put down her guitar. She came to stand beside Bocchi, one hand resting lightly at the back of her neck, and did not explain the pause to anyone.

Mau picked up Wakana's wood and cloth on his way out. At the doorway he stopped.

"I didn't use his piece."

"You asked," Kita said.

"I did. I should probably tell him I haven't lost it."

He went to do that.

Bocchi watched the empty door for a moment. Then she looked down at Kita's hand on the back of the stool.

"Don't move it."

"I won't."

"Not even to make the room look nicer."

"Bocchi-chan."

"It's where I like it."

Kita leaned down and kissed her cheek. "I know."

When she straightened, Bocchi's foot missed the floor and hit the leg instead.

The stool knocked.

They both looked at the sliver.
