# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 33 — What the Page Could Say

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 33
**Source episode:** S6E26
**Continuity state:** Day450, late afternoon. Umaru, Gojo, Rimuru and Ciel help Mau distinguish observed patterns, constructed patterns, repeatable uses and uses unsuitable for today's work. The bracelet displays selected fields with his consent; no complete franchise kit, exact recovery percentage or new unlock is declared. His residual hand, eye and hair changes persist. The67 held points remain held. A reconnaissance of the normal E-rank gate that changed on Day448 is planned for Day452: Mau, Yuka, Yuta and Anko, with Rimuru at home.

*When: Day450, late afternoon. The trestles have been put away. Umaru has brought the board inside because the light is better at the end of the long table.*

The first heading was wrong.

Gojo said so before he sat down.

"That's very encouraging," said Umaru.

"You wrote everything he can do."

"I left room."

"That's the wrong bit."

She turned the board toward herself. Across the top, in her careful square hand, she had written *MAU — CAN DO*. Under it she had drawn columns for the name, the reserve and whatever happened afterward. There were already three entries with arrows between them.

Gojo pulled out a chair and sat backwards on it.

"What do you mean by can?"

Umaru looked at him for a long moment. Then she held the chalk out.

"You write it."

He looked at the chalk.

"I didn't say I had a better heading."

Mau sat down before either of them could get up again. He put his cup on the far side of the table, away from the board, where Umaru pointed without looking.

Rimuru arrived with the older sheets under his arm.

"Nobody's spent anything?"

"Not even chalk," said Umaru. "We're still discussing the heading."

* * *

They started with his hands.

Not with a technique. With the left one, flat on the table beside the board, where two fingers did not lie as easily as the others. Mau tried to close it. The familiar gap remained.

"Same?" Rimuru asked.

"Same."

He made the movement again without thinking. Gojo touched the board with one finger.

"Once is enough. We're looking, not persuading it."

Mau stopped. He put the hand back down.

There was a place beside his thumb where the skin had grown smoother than the rest. The white on the left side of his hair had not darkened. When he looked past Gojo at the far window, the left eye still softened the edge of the frame in a way the right did not.

None of it was new. That was part of the problem. He had grown used to making room for it while thinking about other things.

"I can use the deer," he said.

*For some injuries,* said Ciel. *We've seen it close them. That doesn't tell us it will change everything left from seventy.*

He repeated that.

Umaru wrote *body first* in the top corner. Not in the list of powers. Beside it she drew a small box for Shoko's next check.

"And your own healing?" she asked.

"Better than it was."

"That isn't a technique name either."

Rimuru laid one of the old sheets beside her new board. The Endurance entry. The dates of wounds closing. Shoko's small corrections beside two of the lines.

"It doesn't have to be a name," he said. "We can keep what we know."

* * *

The second heading was longer.

*SEEN. BUILT. USED. CAN USE AGAIN.*

Umaru looked at the space it took up. Then at Gojo.

"Happy?"

"Not with the order."

She turned the chalk round and offered it to him a second time.

This time he took it. He added *TODAY* after the last part and gave it back.

Mau leaned over to read.

"I thought this was going to make the page smaller."

"It will," said Gojo. "Eventually."

They put the shadows first because that was the least arguable column. Thirteen registered shapes with names. Ninety-eight soldiers in their own place. Forty named places in total, first checked on the morning after the gate. The shikigami elsewhere.

"Tank," said Umaru.

"Whole."

"Standing?"

"I can call him."

"Cost?"

"Not to call him. To put him back together if he's broken."

She looked at the older sheet, checked the wording and wrote the same thing. Under *new extraction* she left a separate line.

"The remaining things in the trays stay pending," she said.

"Until we know what they are," Mau agreed.

The list did not make the two empty places in the legion go away. It did not fill them either. Rimuru read that entry and moved to the next without asking Mau to demonstrate it.

* * *

The pull took more space.

One small shape, within a tested limit. A short use. A second outlet to Yuka. Shared demand. Weight behind Mau's eyes after repeated trials.

"Not two reserves," Umaru read back.

"No," said Mau.

"Not a new technique for everyone who stands in a circle."

"Definitely not," said Gojo. "I was standing there. I got nothing."

"You weren't in a circle."

"Discrimination."

Rimuru laughed once, quietly. He was bent over the page, his finger resting beside the word *outlet*.

"Can it hold when one of you moves?"

"We did in the gate," said Mau. "The loan. Not both pulls at the same time. Not the way we tried yesterday."

"Then write those separately."

They did. Yuka's walking, fighting and losing control on the first days were one set of observations. Yesterday's block on the trestles was another. The chalk track was not an enemy that could turn round and hit her.

Gojo watched Mau read the two lines.

"That's why the little test mattered," he said. "You know what changed. You don't have to guess afterward."

"I liked it better before we had eleven questions."

"You liked it better because you weren't carrying the paper," said Umaru.

* * *

The hand that wasn't there was a different line.

Mau stopped when she put it under the pull.

"Not that one."

"It pulls things," Umaru said.

"I know. But it doesn't feel like making the knot."

Gojo nodded before he had finished.

"It isn't."

Mau held his right hand open over the table. Nothing moved. He was showing where he meant, not asking the thing under the walls to reach out. The cup stayed beside his other elbow.

"The grip," he said. "The thing I used on the legs in the field."

*Rough,* said Ciel. *You can reach it. That is not the same as holding it steadily alongside everything else.*

Rimuru listened down the thread and wrote her words on a scrap rather than making Mau say them again.

"We don't call it finished," he said.

"No," said Mau. "But it's there."

They made room for that too.

The shell. The little step. The sky-grab that had gone wrong in the desert. The push. Red and Blue meeting where he had put them together in the field and on the white plain. Each had a place where it had happened. Each needed another line for what it had cost and whether anyone would ask him to do it here.

The last column filled with *not in this room*.

Gojo added *not for a list* beside Purple.

Mau read it and did not argue.

* * *

By the time they reached the copied shapes, Umaru had started a second sheet.

"I'm going to need another board."

"Use the back," said Gojo.

"That's where I put the questions."

He looked at the back.

"Oh."

She moved the first sheet aside. Beneath it, in another hand, were old notes about a hand catching space, a beam, a girl, an angle that should not have folded that way. Mau could see the movements more readily than the people they belonged to.

He frowned at one of them.

"Does seeing the whole thing mean it built the whole thing?"

*No,* Ciel said at once.

"Does building it mean I can do it?"

*Not safely. Not necessarily at all, yet.*

Gojo turned his chair so all four legs were on the floor.

"And doing one part doesn't give you every other use somebody spent a life learning."

"I wasn't asking for that."

"Good. I didn't bring enough chalk."

They did not put a domain beside either of the two names from the old fights. What Mau had seen was not proof he had built it. What Noise kept behind a wall was not a move they could put into a practice plan because there was a blank square waiting for it.

The wheel had its own page. Four old notches were not an answer to every possible blow. The tamed shape with a wheel above it was not the same entry as the piece Mau had carried in himself. Umaru drew a line between them so thick the chalk broke.

Everybody looked at the broken bit.

"Now we've spent something," said Mau.

* * *

He opened the points field last.

Sixty-seven. Still there.

Umaru looked at the number for a long time. The plan she had once made had been for a smaller page. That did not make it a bad plan. It made the new fields worth looking at before she picked up the next point.

"Not today," she said.

"Not today," Mau agreed.

They closed the shared fields. Not the things inside him. Just the part of the window they had chosen to show across the table.

Yuka arrived with Anko while Umaru was stacking the sheets. Anko had the new week's entry from the gate board. The frame had changed on the morning they were sorting bodies in the yard; she had written the date down then and waited for somebody to be able to pay attention to it.

"Normal," she said. "E. The screen still says it."

Yuka stood beside Mau's chair. Not touching. She looked at the sheets and then at him.

"Going to look?"

"Not this evening," said Anko.

"I didn't say this evening."

"You looked like this evening."

Yuka folded her arms. Gojo smiled into his cup.

They chose the afternoon, two days later. Day452. A reconnaissance, with time to turn round if the description did not match what they found. Mau and Yuka. Yuta. Anko, with the watch and the route page.

Rimuru would stay home. He said so himself, before anybody could make a joke about two idiots, and Mau nodded.

"Food before," Anko said. "Expected time written before. If it changes, we come out and change it."

"If we can come out," said Yuka.

Anko looked at her.

"Yes," she said. "That's why I'm saying it now."

For a moment the table was quiet.

Then Umaru turned over the last sheet and found Gojo's cup standing in the middle of the questions.

"Move."

He looked down at his chair.

"The cup."

He moved it. A wet ring had gone through three lines.

Umaru stared at it.

Mau gave her the clean cloth from his pocket, and held the corner of the sheet while she blotted the water out.
