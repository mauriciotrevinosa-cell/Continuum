# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 34 — The Same Edge

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 34
**Source episode:** S6E26
**Continuity state:** Day451, morning. During ordinary laundry work in the washing court, Mau, Rimuru, Ciel and Noise resume the agreed survey of the three shadow accesses. They compare the two known routes and establish a small, reversible alignment of their outer entry edges, while their contents remain separate. The third route stays closed and unidentified. Noise warns and waits when asked; Ciel records the changed access sensation. No full merger, new destination, overflow crisis or Black Heart explanation occurs.

*When: Day451, morning. The washing court. One end of the long cloth is in the basin and the other is trying to get back into it.*

"Hold that," Rimuru said.

Mau held it.

"The other that."

He changed hands. The first end slid off the rim and went into the water with a sound like somebody sitting down in mud.

Rimuru looked at it.

Mau looked at him.

"It's already wet," he said.

"I'm aware."

They lifted it out together. A long white tablecloth, folded into something neither of them could now unfold without letting an end fall. Coco's mother had given it to them clean and wrung out. All they had to do was put it on the line.

They had not reached the line.

"We'll need another peg," said Rimuru.

"We haven't used any yet."

"I mean to hold your end to you."

Mau laughed and lost a corner.

* * *

It took them five minutes.

Not because the cloth was particularly large. Because Rimuru had decided he could carry the middle in his slime shape, which was sensible until the middle sagged onto the clean stones, and Mau had said he could lift it a little, and Ciel had said, *You have two hands*, in the tone she used when a bad idea had not yet become a better one.

So they used their hands.

They got the cloth up on the line. Rimuru returned to his small grey human shape, still holding a peg, and put it where Mau pointed. The hem moved in the breeze. A drop fell from it onto his nose.

"Good," he said. "We've done something."

Mau stepped back to check the corners. One had twisted round the line. He untwisted it and moved the peg.

*Now?* Ciel asked him.

He kept a hand on the hem a moment longer.

*The routes?*

*The survey. Not joining them.*

He looked at Rimuru. The little grey boy had stopped under the line, listening to the same voice down the other thread.

"Here?" Mau said aloud.

"If you want," said Rimuru. "We can sit."

* * *

They sat on the low bench under the eaves, where they could still see the laundry.

Mau put the remaining pegs beside his cup. There were seven. He counted them because they were there, and because he wanted something ordinary to look at while Ciel turned her attention downward.

*Will it notice?* he asked.

*It noticed yesterday when you looked at the page. It didn't move anything.*

That was true. He had felt it listen, quietly, as they argued about headings and reserves, the way it listened when he had something in his hands that did not yet make sense.

*We said we'd ask first,* Mau said.

So they asked.

*We want to compare the two entrances we know,* Ciel said. *Not put anything through them. Can you show me the outside edge?*

For a while there was only water dripping from the long cloth.

Then Mau felt the lower dark attend.

*Which?*

*The soldiers first. Without calling them.*

He did not close his eyes. He could feel it with them open now, if he did not try to see too much at once. The dark under his feet, the familiar depth that held the registered shapes. Not empty. Quiet. He knew the difference.

Something gave Ciel a place to look.

*There,* she said. *Keep that still.*

* * *

Rimuru did not describe what he could not see.

He sat with his hands on his knees and waited for Ciel to tell him what she needed. When she asked whether the thread had changed, he listened and said, "No. Not from this side."

Mau looked at him.

"That's useful?"

"It means I haven't lost you while she's looking."

He said it simply. He was watching Mau's face, but not the way Shoko watched it for a fever. He was waiting for a question he could actually answer.

*Now the shikigami,* Ciel said.

The other opening was deeper to the left, though left was not a direction that made much sense inside him. It was the way he found Ear without finding Igris beside him. A different pressure. A different place to mean.

He felt that place answer too.

*They don't start together,* said Ciel.

*I know,* said Mau. *I have to go past one to reach the other sometimes. Not every time.*

*Show me that. Don't correct it.*

He tried to think of the soldier nearest the front of the legion. Then Ear. Then the soldier again. Not bringing either up. Just finding where he would begin.

The sensation snagged on the second change.

He frowned.

Rimuru saw it.

"What?"

"It's like turning a handle after someone's moved the door a little. My hand goes to where it was."

*Good,* Ciel said. *I felt that. Do it once more. Slowly.*

* * *

The Noise shifted something.

Not a wall. Not the things behind it. An edge, so small that Mau would not have known it had moved if he had not been looking for the place a moment before.

*Wait,* Ciel said.

It waited.

He felt it waiting. The pressure held instead of pressing harder. It was an odd feeling, being able to tell the difference, and he reached for the nearest peg with his ordinary hand and turned it between his fingers.

*What are you moving?* he asked.

*Edge.*

*Which one?*

The reply was not a word this time. A small, clear sensation at the soldier entrance. Ciel followed it.

*I can see where you mean,* she said. *You can show me without taking the next part?*

The pressure eased a little, then held again.

*Yes.*

Mau put the peg down.

"I think it's waiting for us to say something," he told Rimuru.

"Then say something."

*That part only,* said Mau. *The bit you showed her. If it pulls at anything else, stop.*

For a moment nothing happened.

Then the catch he had felt between the two places became easier to find.

Not gone. Easier.

* * *

He tried the change again.

Soldier. Ear. Soldier.

The second entrance was still elsewhere. Ear was not sitting in the legion's line. Igris had not moved into the place where the dogs slept. They had not become one room.

But the outside edges met his attention in a more similar way. He did not have to feel past one and correct for the other each time. The place to begin was less crooked.

He breathed out.

"That helps."

*One more,* said Ciel. *Only finding them.*

He did it once more. Then stopped before she had to ask.

Rimuru listened down the thread.

"Still here," he said, then glanced at Mau as if he had not meant to put those words together.

Mau looked at the pegs.

He knew the words. He knew why they mattered to someone. He did not make Rimuru take them back.

"I know," he said.

The hem of the cloth lifted and settled.

* * *

The third place did not move.

They could feel it below the others. The one neither of them had a useful name for. Mau's attention went toward it and the Noise made a short sound, less than a word.

He stopped.

*Not that part?* he asked.

*Not ready.*

Ciel did not ask it to prove why.

*Then we leave it closed.*

Rimuru leaned forward a little. "Everything all right?"

"It said not ready."

"You listened?"

"Yes."

Rimuru sat back.

There was no heat rising through Mau's fingers. No lamp going blue, no feeling that the second heart had become the only place left for something to go. The thin mark in the dark bar stayed where it had been when he opened the selected field to check.

*We aren't filling it,* Ciel said. *We're stopping here.*

*Yes,* the Noise answered.

It let the edges settle.

* * *

They tested the small change once, outside.

Mau asked for a soldier. One of the plain ones, with spear and shield, up beside the bench. It stood at once and waited. He sent it back. Then asked Ear to come, from his own place, and the dog rose in a slow stretch that looked deeply unnecessary for something that had not been asleep in a body.

Rimuru reached down. Ear put his head under the hand.

"Oh, you can find me," said Rimuru.

Ear leaned harder.

Mau let him stay while he found the soldier's entrance again without opening it. There was still a difference. There ought to be. But the awkward catch between them was not in quite the same place.

*I can keep a record of that,* Ciel said. *And we can check whether it stays the next time you use them.*

*So we're done?*

*For today.*

The answer from below came before he asked for it.

*Done.*

Mau smiled down at the dog. Ear was busy trying to persuade Rimuru to use both hands.

*Thank you,* he said, inside.

The Noise did not answer again. It did not need to.

* * *

Coco's mother came round the corner carrying a small basket of socks.

She looked at the cloth. At the hem that had twisted itself back round the line while they were sitting. Then at the two of them and the dog on Rimuru's feet.

"Did it take all three of you?"

"He didn't help," said Rimuru.

Ear looked up.

"The cloth," Mau explained to him.

The dog rested his head on Rimuru's knee again.

They got up to make room. Coco's mother took three pegs from the pile and hung the socks, then gave Mau the rest of the basket. He went to the far end of the line, where the light had finally reached the stones, and started matching the pairs.

Rimuru came with him.

The first two socks were nearly the same colour. Nearly the same length. Mau held them up beside one another, considered them and put one back.

"That's Ichika's," said Coco's mother, from the other end.

Mau looked at the tiny heel.

"Then whose is this?"

"Also Ichika's."

He looked at Rimuru.

"Different days," she explained.

They hung them next to each other anyway, one peg each, where she could sort them when they were dry.
