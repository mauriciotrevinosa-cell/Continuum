# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 36 — The Passenger

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 36
**Source episode:** S6E27
**Continuity state:** Day451 afternoon. Kaneki, Touka and Ichika make paper boats in their established room with two windows. Ordinary reused wrapping paper and a basin only. The parents drink black coffee; Ichika has water in her own wooden cup. No new food permission, ghoul cure, technology or major project. Mau does not appear.

*When: Day451, afternoon. In the room with two windows, Touka has moved the rug away from the basin before anyone has admitted that the rug might need moving.*

The first boat was already under water.

Kaneki looked at it through the clear surface of the basin. Its pointed end had opened out. One side leaned against the bottom as though it had decided to lie down.

"It was floating," he said.

Touka put her cup on the windowsill.

"I saw."

"Before."

"I saw that too."

Ichika leaned forward between his knees and poked the water with one finger.

The boat moved along the bottom.

"It goes," she said.

"Yes," said Kaneki. "It goes."

Touka looked at the wet paper, then at her husband, and did not say the thing she was thinking. This took visible effort.

Kaneki put a hand over the side of the basin and scooped the boat out.

It unfolded in his fingers.

"Maybe not that one," he said.

* * *

The paper had wrapped a parcel from the stores. Nothing valuable. Three pieces, creased and a little thick along one edge, which Touka had kept because Ichika had wanted to fold something after seeing a girl carrying paper shapes through the yard.

Kaneki had said he knew how to make a boat.

Touka had looked at him, interested. She had known him for years and still found that he was full of small claims like this, which came out without warning and had apparently never occurred to him to mention before.

"Do you?" she had asked.

He had been quite sure then.

Now he sat on the floor with the second piece in his lap and tried to remember which corner he had opened too soon.

Ichika sat against his leg, watching his hands. Her small wooden cup stood beside the bed, water exactly to the line. Touka had put the coffee high on the windowsill where nobody could accidentally float anything in it.

"Here," Kaneki said, folding the top corners inward.

Ichika immediately put her hand on the crease.

"Not yet. Let me get it straight."

She kept her hand where it was.

He folded round it.

Touka watched this for a moment before reaching over and moving their daughter's hand a finger's width to the left.

"Now."

Ichika pressed with both palms.

Kaneki ran his thumbnail along the edge. There was a triangular top, two flaps below it and a general air of promise. He turned it over.

"A hat," Ichika said.

"For a moment. Then it's a boat."

She took it out of his hands.

* * *

The hat did not fit.

It perched on the top of her head with the broad paper edge against her hair. She held very still while Kaneki tucked one loose corner out of her eye. Touka stood up to look at it from the front.

"Very grand," she said.

Ichika looked up at her, which made the hat slide down over both eyes.

Kaneki caught it before she could pull it in half.

There was a silence.

Then Ichika started laughing, and Touka made him put it on himself.

He objected. It was too small for Ichika. It was going to be much too small for him. This was not information either of them needed to acquire by experiment.

Touka held it out.

He put it on.

It was much too small.

Ichika laughed so hard she fell sideways against his knee. He kept his hands flat on his thighs, balancing the paper on his white hair while his wife inspected him with her arms folded.

"Should wear it to work," she said.

"It's paper."

"I know what it's made of."

"There's a fire."

"Not that close to your head, I hope."

He looked up at her. The hat fell off.

Ichika caught it with both hands, looked at the squashed side and turned it round. After a moment she offered it to Touka.

Touka shook her head.

"Mama," Ichika said, much more firmly.

Kaneki picked up his cup of black coffee. He had the sense that anything he said now would be kept for later.

* * *

Once they had all worn it, he was allowed to finish the boat.

The paper had become softer. There was a little dent in the top where Touka had put it on too firmly, and one corner had been folded twice in the wrong direction. Kaneki flattened it against his knee, opened the bottom and pulled the points apart.

The shape appeared all at once.

Ichika's hands opened.

"Boat."

"Yes."

He held it above the basin while she got to her feet. She wanted to put it in herself. He could tell because she had taken hold of both his wrists and was moving them toward the water with great purpose.

"Let her," Touka said.

He did.

Ichika put it in bow first, with the certainty of someone launching a much larger vessel. Water came over the folded edge. Kaneki reached, stopped, and watched it rock backward, shed the little mouthful of water and settle.

It floated.

The three of them looked at it.

Ichika breathed on it.

It turned a little.

She looked at Kaneki with enormous suspicion, as though he had been withholding this for a very long time.

He blew gently across the water. The boat moved away from him, stopped against the other side and turned.

Ichika went round the basin on her hands and knees to send it back.

Touka moved the rug another few inches.

* * *

The passenger was a wooden button.

It had come out of the little dish on the shelf, where Touka kept things which would be needed the instant someone threw them away. There were three buttons in there, a short length of cord and a smooth stone which Ichika had apparently contributed without asking.

Ichika selected the largest button.

"That might be too heavy," Kaneki said.

She looked at the boat.

He took the button from her and set it in the hollow. The paper dipped, but not very far. He moved it toward the middle until the boat floated level.

"There."

Ichika bent close to inspect it.

"Papa."

Touka looked at Kaneki.

He looked at the button. Four holes, a rim worn smooth, very pale wood. He was not sure which part of it had suggested him.

"All right," he said.

Ichika went back to the dish.

"One passenger," Touka warned.

She brought the stone.

"That definitely won't work," Kaneki said.

Ichika held it over the basin and considered the problem. Then she put it down beside the water, on the dry floor.

"Mama."

Touka's eyes narrowed.

Kaneki took another drink of coffee.

"She's staying here," he offered.

"Am I?"

"To watch."

"You can stop explaining."

He put down the cup before he laughed into it.

* * *

The boat made several journeys with Papa in the middle and Mama watching from the floor.

Ichika pushed the water with her palm until a small wave came over the side. Kaneki caught the button before it could slide beneath the paper. The boat listed, recovered and went on, much lower in the water.

Touka offered to make a second one.

He turned to look at her.

"You didn't say you knew how."

"You didn't ask."

"You've watched me do two."

"The first one wasn't a boat. It was a damp piece of paper you put in water."

"It had the shape."

"For a moment."

She took the last piece and smoothed it on the floor. Ichika left the basin at once and came to stand over her, interested in whether Mama's version would also be a hat halfway through.

It was.

Touka let her press the folds. She folded more sharply than Kaneki had, checking the edges with her fingers. When she opened the bottom, she paused.

Kaneki recognised the pause.

He waited.

She turned it over. Then back again.

"You said you knew how," he said.

"I do."

"That's what I said."

Touka looked at him. He lowered his eyes to the basin, where the old boat had begun taking water around Papa's left side.

She opened the paper and started again from the last fold she trusted. Ichika crouched beside her and put one hand flat on the floor, copying her posture. Kaneki looked at the two of them for a moment before reaching across for the sinking boat.

He took the button out first.

It seemed important.

* * *

Touka's boat floated better.

Kaneki was prepared for this. He said it before she had to. It had straighter edges and less water coming in, partly because no one had worn it long enough to soften the paper.

Ichika accepted the new boat but refused the new seating arrangement. Papa went in the middle. Mama stayed by the basin. A smaller button, which Touka had not noticed her carrying, was placed beside Mama on the floor.

"Me," Ichika explained.

Touka sat back on her heels.

"Why does he get the boat?"

Ichika picked up the little button, then the stone. She put both carefully into the water beside it.

Kaneki caught his wife's sleeve before she could reach for them. Not to stop her. He had felt the basin tilt under their daughter's knee.

They both caught the rim.

Water went over their hands and onto the bare boards. The boat lurched, the wooden button rolled and Ichika watched the sudden activity with her mouth open.

The boat stayed up.

For a moment Kaneki and Touka knelt opposite one another, each holding half the basin, coffee abandoned on the sill behind them. Then Touka looked down at the stone and the small button lying on the bottom.

"We're swimming," she said.

Ichika nodded.

"Of course."

Kaneki let go of the rim with one hand and offered it across the water.

Touka took it, pushed herself upright and went to find another cloth. She returned with two, handed him one and lifted their daughter clear of the wet patch before Ichika could decide to join the swimmers.

"Papa can carry them now," she said.

Kaneki picked up the stone and both buttons. He dried them one at a time while Ichika supervised, then held his palm out to her.

She took Mama first.
