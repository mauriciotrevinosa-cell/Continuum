# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 37 — Attached

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 37
**Source episode:** S6E28
**Continuity state:** Day452 morning. Coco, Agott, Tetia and Richeh continue their established sewing warm-up with Coco's mother, applying it to worn kitchen apron ties. Qifrey supervises their later private work. All repair uses ordinary thread; no new glyph, power or external disclosure. The ink remains the weekly bottle gathered at the tree's trunk notch, not an unlimited resource or something Coco's mother is suddenly authorised to use.

*When: Day452, after breakfast. The Witch Hat room. Momo has sent back three aprons and a message that a knot is not a permanent repair, even if the person who made it is proud of it.*

Tetia was attached to the apron.

Not emotionally. She had put nine very neat stitches through the end of its tie and the skirt beneath it before discovering that the cloth no longer came up when she lifted it.

Coco's mother looked down at the small patch of blue caught beneath the white.

"Don't pull."

Tetia stopped pulling.

Agott put down her own needle. She leaned across to look, her expression so severe that Tetia held the apron out to her as though presenting evidence.

"The stitches are good," Tetia said.

"Yes."

"They're even."

"Yes."

"I looked where I was going."

Richeh, sitting against the wall, lifted her eyes from her own seam.

"You went there."

Coco covered her mouth. Her mother held out a hand for the little scissors.

Tetia surrendered them.

* * *

They had stopped sewing on scraps that morning because Agott had objected to making a good seam and then putting it in a drawer.

There were already enough practice pieces in the drawer to make a very ugly blanket. Every morning, the curve that went one way and then the other. Thread first, pencil afterward. At the beginning, Coco's mother had watched their hands. Lately she had begun pointing out the places where the cloth was doing something they had not allowed for.

The aprons had arrived at an unfortunate moment for Tetia's suggestion that they had probably learned everything there was to learn from a seam.

One tie had frayed near the edge. Another had been knotted halfway down after something tore. The third was whole but coming away from the cloth it belonged to, a narrow white ladder of loose thread which Coco's mother had examined at breakfast.

"Those," Agott had said. "We can do those."

Momo had looked at the aprons, at the girls and then at their teacher.

"Bring them back before anyone needs to wear a curtain."

Qifrey had agreed to that condition with admirable seriousness.

Now he stood beside the door while Coco's mother cut the first of Tetia's excellent stitches. The morning light came in over his shoulder and caught the tiny blade.

"Should I have checked underneath?" Tetia asked.

Agott took a breath.

"Yes," said Qifrey, before she could speak.

Tetia looked relieved at the brevity of the answer.

* * *

Coco had the tie with the knot.

At first she had thought it would be the easiest. The cloth was all there. If she could undo the knot without making the hole worse, she could put the two ends together and sew them.

The knot had other plans.

She tried to find the end with a needle. Her mother took the needle away and gave her a blunt little tool from the sewing things instead. Coco pushed the point into the narrow gap and lifted.

Nothing.

She turned it over.

Still nothing.

Across the table, Agott's needle went through the cloth with a soft, regular sound. Richeh was working on the loose tie with short motions, turning the whole apron when she reached a difficult part instead of twisting her wrist. Tetia had been freed from her skirt and now sat with both knees well away from the table.

Coco's knot was becoming personal.

"Who did this?" she asked.

"Somebody who needed it to stay on," said her mother.

"It's stayed on."

"Yes."

She went round behind Coco and put one finger beside the place she was trying to open.

"That part is holding the rest tight. Try the other side first. Give it somewhere to move."

Coco followed the folded strip with the tool. On the other side there was a little pocket of slack she had not noticed, flattened beneath the long end.

She lifted it.

The knot opened half an inch.

"Oh."

Her mother went back to Tetia before Coco could thank her, because Tetia had begun threading her needle with both eyes shut.

"It's the light," Tetia explained when they opened. "I can see the hole better if I stop looking at it."

Agott looked toward Qifrey.

He was studying the apron with a thoughtful expression, although Coco was almost sure his shoulders had moved.

* * *

The ink bottle stayed on its shelf.

It was the same small bottle they measured their proper work from, filled from the notch in the tree's trunk on the weekly turn. The room smelled faintly of it even with the stopper in. Nobody reached for it while there were torn ties and pencil exercises on the table.

Qifrey brought over the ordinary paper instead.

He laid one sheet beside each apron. No marks to follow. No ruler until afterward. Coco looked from the paper to the loosened tie and already knew what he was going to ask.

"The path of the thread," he said.

Tetia looked at her seam.

"The successful one?"

"The one you intended."

She took her pencil at once.

Coco began at the edge of the page. Her first line came out too narrow at the turn. She had been thinking about making it look as clean as the needle's path, and had forgotten that she was allowed to use the whole sheet.

Her mother, beside her, turned a length of loose thread between her fingers. There was no pencil in her hand. She was following the seam on Richeh's apron with her eyes.

Richeh watched her watch it.

"Too small?" she asked.

"Very small."

"I like it small."

"I can see that."

Richeh waited, not moving her hand toward the scissors.

Coco's mother touched the cloth on either side of the joined piece and pulled gently. The seam held. The cloth gathered around it, puckering into a little ridge.

"It's strong," she said. "But this part needs to lie flat against someone. Here."

She loosened one stitch, not to make it larger but to let the thread stop pulling the cloth into its own shape. The ridge softened.

Richeh bent nearer.

"I can keep it small."

"Yes."

She took the apron back and began again.

* * *

Agott finished first.

Her tie lay flat. The stitches were small and even, and the end was turned under so there was no raw edge to catch. Coco's mother pulled it once, checked it and nodded.

Agott put it beside the paper and began her lines.

Tetia watched her for a moment.

"You could say something."

"What?"

"About finishing."

"I've finished."

"Something nice."

Agott's pencil stopped halfway to the page.

Coco looked down before Agott could see her smiling. Richeh did not look down. She watched Agott with quiet interest, waiting to find out how much the request would cost.

"Your stitches were even," Agott said at last.

Tetia beamed.

"I know!"

"In your skirt."

Tetia's smile narrowed.

Qifrey turned to the shelf and found something requiring his attention there for several seconds.

* * *

They took the aprons down before lunch.

Qifrey kept their later work in the room. The unfinished drawings were put away, the ink bottle left stoppered until he was ready for the next lesson. Coco's mother carried only the mended cloth. Coco went with her, carrying the third apron because Tetia had decided that hers should be presented personally.

Agott came too. Richeh followed a few paces behind with the scissors, which she had noticed Tetia putting in a pocket and forgetting.

Momo had flour on the back of one hand and no time for a ceremony.

"Those?"

"Done," Agott said.

Momo took the first apron. She tugged the tie, looked at the turned edge and set it down. Then the second. Then Tetia held out the third with both hands.

Momo inspected it.

"Two colours."

Tetia had used a little blue thread to finish the last inch where the white had run out. Coco had told her there was more in the box. Tetia had said she liked this one.

"So you'll know which is mine," she explained.

"It's an apron," said Momo. "I know where it goes."

She put it on.

Tetia watched with her hands clasped beneath her chin. Momo tied it, moved both arms, reached for the bowl on the far side of the counter and went back to what she had been doing.

Tetia stood there for another moment, looking delighted.

Coco's mother caught her hand before it could go into the flour.

"Not that."

"I was only going to look."

"You look with your eyes."

"Usually."

Richeh held out the scissors to her.

They left the kitchen in a loose line, arguing about whether Agott's compliment had counted. Agott said she had only reported what she saw. Tetia said it had counted anyway and she was keeping it.

At the foot of the stair, Coco looked back.

Her mother was still by the kitchen doorway, speaking to Momo about another small repair. She had taken the scissors from Richeh and was pointing with the closed end, listening.

Coco waited until she looked up.

"Mama?"

"Go on," her mother said. "I'll come in a little while."

Coco went up. Inside the Witch Hat room, Tetia was showing Qifrey the blue thread left on the spool, as though this might have become evidence in her favour while they were downstairs.

Qifrey held out his hand.

"Your pencil first."

Tetia looked at her own hand. She was still holding the needle.
