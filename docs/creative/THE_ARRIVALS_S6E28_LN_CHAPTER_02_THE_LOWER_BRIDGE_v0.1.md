## Chapter 38 — The Lower Bridge

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33

A notice specifically enables limited inventory/catalogue access in this gate, extending the previous Red Gate-only observation without implying unrestricted external access or interchangeable currencies. A small encounter uses the tested shared Limitless pull within its capped budget, alongside Yuta's competent sword work and Anko's route decisions. *When: Day452, afternoon. At the frame. Four names on Anko's page. Rimuru has the other copy, and nobody is calling it one hour.*

Anko made them look at the return first.

They had taken six steps into the gate. Yuka had already seen a path between low stone walls, and a line of trees beyond it, and water moving somewhere below the path. Then Anko stopped and pointed back.

Grey-white light. The house yard faintly visible through it.

"Still there," she said.

Nobody laughed.

Mau looked at Yuka. She was looking at the light too, with her hands on the straps of the pack her grandmother had checked twice. She looked back at him and nodded once.

Yuta went to the frame, held his hand through the ordinary light and brought it back. On the other side, Rimuru lifted a hand in answer.

"All right," said Anko.

She turned the page over. At the top, in her plain hand: *E. Normal. Reconnaissance. Home before the next bowl.* Below it, the clock time they had written before crossing. Not a promise about how long a world would let them stay. A time at which the others would start asking.

She drew the return onto the bottom of the blank space.

"Now we can look at the rest."

* * *

The rest was a ditch.

Yuka looked over the low wall, then along it, then at the crooked stone bridge twenty paces ahead.

"It's quite a good ditch."

"Don't sound disappointed," said Anko.

"I wasn't."

"You sounded like someone had promised you a dragon and brought you drainage."

Mau leaned over beside her. The water was shallow and clear, moving over brown pebbles. The ditch widened under the bridge, where roots hung down through a gap in the stones. There was a darker shape beneath one of them.

He did not lean farther.

Yuta had seen it too.

"Wait," he said.

Something knocked once under the bridge. Not water. Hard against hard. Then the dark shape moved, unfolding a leg from what Mau had taken for a stone.

Anko put a small cross on the route page.

"There. Noted. We don't need to climb down to confirm it."

* * *

The window opened before they reached the bridge.

Mau stopped to read it.

*Inventory access: available in this gate.*

*Expedition catalogue: limited access enabled.*

He read it again, this time aloud.

Yuka came nearer. Yuta kept his eyes on the underside of the bridge while Anko looked at Mau rather than the pale words nobody had yet asked to share.

"New?"

"The notice is. It said in that gate before. The red one. I didn't know it would work here."

He opened the selected part through his bracelet. Anko read the same two lines, then wrote the time beside her note.

*It has opened a route,* Ciel said. *Not everything. Look before you buy.*

He looked.

A short list. Rope. Cloth. Lamps. Preserved food. Several little bottles, their prices so much larger that Yuka made a small noise through her teeth when she read them. The inventory beside the list held the expedition stock they had agreed he would carry, separate from things that were his.

"No," Anko said when Mau glanced at her.

"I didn't ask."

"You looked at the bottles."

"I was looking at the price."

"Good. Keep looking at it."

Yuka leaned closer to the accepted-payment line.

"Crystals. These ones. It hasn't turned them into coins."

"Write that too," said Mau.

Anko did. She did not change the house shop's rules because another list had appeared inside a gate. They bought nothing. Mau closed the window, and Yuta, still watching the bridge, said, "There are four now."

* * *

They were like beetles made out of old roof tiles.

Flat-backed, brown, with joints too pale for the rest of them. One came out from beneath the roots and put its front legs on the bank. The others crowded behind it, turning themselves sideways to get through the gap.

The front one's head opened.

Not its mouth. The whole flat plate at the front lifted, and underneath was a row of fine grey points.

"Nobody into the ditch," said Anko.

Yuta drew his sword.

There was nothing spectacular about the movement. Steel out, feet set, enough room on either side for the others. Mau felt Yuka move to his left before she spoke.

"Little ones?"

He knew what she meant.

*The tested route,* Ciel said. *Briefly. Not a larger one because it moves.*

"Little ones," he agreed.

Anko took three steps back along the path to where she could see the bridge, the frame and all three of them. She put the page inside her coat. Both hands free.

The first beetle climbed over the wall.

* * *

Mau set the pull just beyond its front leg.

Small. Not in his palm. In the space where he wanted its next step to go wrong. Yuka set hers on the other side, lower, where the hard body would turn if it tried to face him.

For a moment both knots were there.

He felt the shared demand settle between them. The pressure was familiar now. He did not try to make his larger when the beetle's shell scraped the wall.

It slipped.

Yuta stepped in while the pale joint was exposed and cut once beneath the lifted plate. Then he stepped back, blade clear, before the second thing came over the stone.

Mau let his pull go.

Yuka's vanished with it, half a beat later.

"One," Anko said from behind them.

The second beetle did not follow the first's line. It scuttled sideways along the wall, quick enough to make Yuka turn. She started to put the centre where it had been and stopped herself.

"Not yet," she said.

Mau held his hand low. Waited.

The beetle reached a broken place in the wall, lifted its front half to climb and gave them the angle.

Yuka put her little knot there.

Mau put his beyond it, both on the same side this time. The shell knocked against the broken stone instead of coming through the gap. Yuta's foot moved onto the dry edge. His blade found the place behind the head.

"Two," said Anko.

* * *

The other two stayed under the bridge.

Yuta did not go after them.

He stood beside the wall and watched while Mau and Yuka lowered their hands. A dark shape shifted beneath the roots, then stopped. The ditch went on making its small ordinary noise.

"We could—" Yuka began.

"We could know there are two under that bridge," Anko said. "And put it on the page."

Yuka looked at the opening.

Then at Mau.

He wanted to see what was beyond it too. She could see that. He could see her noticing. The old ease of moving together was there between them, asking for the next step before anybody had said it aloud.

He looked at Yuta instead.

Yuta wiped the blade with the cloth he had brought for it.

"There's a lower crossing," he said. "Ten paces back. We can look from there without standing on top of them."

Anko checked the line of the wall.

"That," she said. "We can do that."

* * *

The lower crossing was three broad stones in shallow water.

They did not need a technique to cross it. Yuta went first, tested each stone with his weight and waited on the far side. Anko came after, looking under them rather than at her feet. Mau followed Yuka, close enough to catch the pack if it shifted, not holding her when she did not need him to.

On the other bank they could see the bridge properly.

Two creatures pressed into the shadow beneath the arch, backs tight against the stone. Beyond them was a little courtyard, empty, with an old turning wheel above the water. A path went on between the trees.

Anko drew the wheel.

"That's farther than the screen showed," she said.

"Not much farther," said Yuka.

"Farther is farther."

Mau turned his head toward the frame. Still visible above the opposite bank. Still the right colour.

*Your eyes?* Ciel asked.

*A little behind them. Not like the first test with the trestles.*

*The route's closed now. Leave it closed while it settles.*

He told Anko. Not because it was an emergency. Because she was writing down what they could do here, and that was part of it.

She nodded and put a short line beside the bridge.

"We've got a route, a crossing and something not to put a hand under. That's a reconnaissance."

Yuka stayed looking down the path another moment.

Then she turned back with them.

* * *

They took the small crystals from the two bodies on their way out.

Not the bodies. Not four new soldiers to make up an old absence. Yuta held each hard plate clear while Mau took what the creature had left, and Anko put the crystals in the sample bag rather than letting them disappear into an uncounted stock.

Mau did not try Arise. He had not come to fill the empty places on a list.

At the frame, Anko stopped once more. Looked back. Counted them, though she could see all three without turning her head.

"Home."

Grey-white light. The yard beneath their feet, colder than the ditch bank, and Rimuru getting up from the bench before the last of them had come through.

He looked at Mau, then Yuka, then Yuta, then Anko.

"Normal?"

"Ditch," said Anko.

"Good ditch," Yuka added.

Rimuru looked at the sample bag.

"That's reassuringly specific."

They gave him the report at the board. Return stayed open. The time on Anko's watch. Two creatures killed, two left under the upper bridge. Lower crossing sound. Limited catalogue newly available inside, no purchase made. Shared pulls worked twice, small and brief; Mau was resting the route now.

Anko drew the bridge larger so the warning would fit beneath it.

Yuka leaned in and added the gap in the wall where the second creature had tried to come through. Mau watched her hand. When she offered him the pencil, he marked the lower stones one by one.

From the kitchen door Momo called that the next bowl was ready.

Anko shut the watch.

"For once," she said, "a useful unit of time."
