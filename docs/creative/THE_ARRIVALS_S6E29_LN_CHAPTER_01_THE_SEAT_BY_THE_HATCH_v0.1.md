# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 39 — The Seat by the Hatch

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 39
**Source episode:** S6E29
**Continuity state:** Day452, after dinner, the established roof-night cycle simultaneous with crackers. Mau joins Rimuru, Yuta, Gojo and Taihei through the east-corridor roof hatch; Taihei is an established participant, not a first invite. Stories and a small absurd argument allow ordinary company. Rimuru retains his private knowledge and unresolved anger, while Yuta/Gojo/Taihei do not learn the romance. Mau is not forced to perform old familiarity or restored memories. Nobody uses a technique on the roof.

*When: Day452, after dinner. The roof ladder at the end of the east corridor. Someone above has decided the person below is taking too long with the cups.*

"I can bring them up myself," Gojo said.

"You can stop leaning over the hole," said Taihei.

"I'm offering assistance."

"Your face is where the tray needs to go."

Mau waited below Taihei with the folded blankets. He could see one slipper, the end of the ladder and, through the square of night above, a white head making the passage smaller than it needed to be.

Yuta reached past Gojo and took the tray. Taihei climbed the last two rungs. Then there was room for the blankets, and for Mau, and finally for the hatch to be lowered against the cold coming up from the corridor.

Rimuru was already sitting near the safe, flatter part of the roof, away from the edge. He had left space between himself and Yuta without putting a cup there to reserve it. Mau looked at the space, then at the folded cloth under Taihei's arm.

"Here?" he asked.

Rimuru looked up. "If you want."

He sat down before the answer could turn into something that needed explaining.

* * *

Taihei distributed the cups in the order that prevented Gojo from reaching over anyone.

It did not work. Gojo reached over Yuta for the small pot, then over Mau for the cloth beneath it, and finally accused Rimuru of having the best view despite sitting in precisely the place Gojo had chosen not to sit.

"You could move," Rimuru said.

"I've settled."

"You've been here four minutes."

"A deep commitment."

Yuta wrapped both hands round his cup. "He gets worse when it's cold."

"I improve the evening."

Taihei looked into the little covered bowl he had brought with the cups. There were small pieces of the bread nobody had finished at dinner, cut so they would not make crumbs everywhere. He lifted the lid just enough for Mau to see.

"Don't tell him yet."

"I can hear you," said Gojo.

"Then don't make me repeat it."

Mau laughed. Gojo put a hand against his own chest as though badly wounded. Yuta turned away to hide the fact that he was laughing too.

Rimuru watched the steam rise from his tea. Then he picked up a piece of bread before Gojo could claim it for having suffered.

* * *

They could hear almost nothing from the room below.

Once, through the hatch, a noise that might have been laughter or someone dropping something. Gojo asked what the crackers were doing tonight. Yuta told him that finding out was how you got assigned a chair downstairs.

"I have sat downstairs."

"And they let you leave," said Rimuru. "Remarkable generosity."

Gojo turned toward him at once. "Was that a joke?"

"No. An observation."

"Good. I was worried about you."

The remark fell more quietly than the others. Rimuru looked at him. For a moment Mau expected another conversation to begin, the kind where people asked what was wrong until there was no answer safe enough to give.

Gojo reached for the bowl instead.

Taihei moved it toward Yuta, just out of reach.

"That's my concern being punished," Gojo said.

"That's the last piece," said Taihei. "You're on three."

"Then somebody has to catch up."

Yuta took it. He did not apologise.

* * *

The argument was about a dragon in a doorway.

Not a real dragon. Taihei had been telling them about trying to get a table through a door with three people giving directions and nobody holding the other end. Gojo said they should have asked Kamish. Yuta said that would only replace the table problem with the dragon problem.

"He wouldn't fit," Taihei agreed.

"Not all at once," said Gojo.

"Which part would you send first?" asked Mau.

Gojo considered this with offensive seriousness.

"The polite part."

"That's most of him," said Rimuru.

"Then we start small."

Yuta looked at the roof beneath his feet. "We aren't testing this."

"Nobody said testing," Gojo said.

"You made the face."

"What face?"

The other four looked at him.

Mau tried not to be the first one to laugh. Failed. Taihei started after him, shaking his head with his cup held well away from his knees, and Rimuru put down his tea before he lost it into the blanket.

Gojo sat among them looking unjustly accused.

"I was contributing."

"We know," said Yuta. "That's what we're worried about."

They spent several minutes deciding which of the shadows would be worst at moving furniture. Beru lost early because he would challenge the furniture. Lantern was disqualified for having claws instead of hands. Cairn was too new for most of them to know, but Mau described the gutter incident, and Taihei took him off the list immediately.

"Igris," Yuta said at last. "Ask Igris."

"He's working," Rimuru reminded them.

Gojo looked over the edge of his own cup. "Then leave the table where it is until morning."

Taihei stared at him.

"We did. That was the story."

Mau began laughing again before anybody could answer.

* * *

The cold found them from underneath.

It always took longer than they expected. The air might be still enough to tolerate, and then the roof would begin taking the warmth from the place where they sat. Taihei shifted the folded blanket beneath him. Yuta pulled another over his knees without interrupting the story he was telling about Maki refusing to acknowledge the usefulness of something until she had already borrowed it twice.

Mau listened. He did not know which version of her Yuta meant at first, the old woman in the world Yuka remembered or the young one downstairs. Yuta noticed the question on his face and answered it in the telling, without stopping to make Mau admit what he did not know.

"At school," he said. "She'd already decided I was useless. So the bag could be useful, but I wasn't allowed any credit for carrying it."

"A sound distinction," said Rimuru.

"You see what I was working against."

Yuta was smiling down at his cup. Not sadly. Not performing a long life as if everyone ought to admire how much of it he had carried. He remembered something funny about the person downstairs, and the memory was his to enjoy.

Mau found himself smiling too.

"Did you tell her?" he asked. "That the bag didn't walk there on its own."

"Eventually."

"How did that go?"

Yuta thought about it.

"I carried it home as well."

Gojo nodded gravely. "A famous victory."

Below them, someone laughed loudly enough for it to reach the hatch. Maki, perhaps, or Mai. The sound was gone before Mau could decide.

He looked down at the roof and tried not to make that into something he had to recognise.

* * *

Taihei and Gojo went down first.

Not because the evening was over. Taihei wanted to put the bowl away before somebody put something wet in it, and Gojo insisted he was helping. Yuta followed them as far as the hatch to make sure the tray went through before Gojo's face did.

For a short while Mau and Rimuru were alone at their end.

The windows across the yard showed a few lights. Nothing was moving fast. A watcher passed at the edge of the visible ground, too far away to hear, and disappeared behind a roof.

"Did you want me not to come?" Mau asked quietly.

Rimuru kept his eyes on the yard.

"No."

"You went quiet when I sat down."

"I was already quiet."

"Yes," Mau said after a moment. "You were."

Rimuru turned the empty cup in his hands.

"I can want you here and still be angry with you."

Mau nodded. He had needed to hear it, though he did not know what to do with it now that he had.

"I talked to her," he said. "Like I said I would."

"I know. She didn't leave the table when you came in tonight."

Neither said which her. There was nobody close enough to hear them, but they kept their voices low all the same.

"I'm not keeping a list of things for you to do so I can stop being angry," Rimuru said. "That isn't what I wanted."

"I didn't think it was."

"Then don't make it one."

Mau looked at him, then down at the cup in his own hands. "All right."

Yuta came back from the hatch with the last blanket, asked which of them had claimed it and let them both hold a side while he sat in the middle. The change of subject was not spoken. He had not heard the subject to change.

They stayed for a little longer. Yuta described the dent in the practice sword; Rimuru asked whether it was the first dent or the one on the other side. Mau told him Maki had asked the same question.

"Of course she did," said Yuta.

When they finally went down, Mau held the cups and Rimuru took the blanket. The seat by the hatch was empty behind them. Nobody folded it away separately from the others.
