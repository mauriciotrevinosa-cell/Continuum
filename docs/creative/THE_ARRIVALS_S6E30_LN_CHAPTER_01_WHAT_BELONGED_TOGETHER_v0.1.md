## Chapter 41 — What Belonged Together

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 41
**Source episode:** S6E30
**Continuity state:** Day453, morning. Anko, Umaru and Mau finish sorting the recovered desert fragments. Sufficient recognisable remains of one glass-serpent guardian are identified among pieces that did not answer while unsorted. Mau extracts one new shadow, named Skein, at a real cost to the shadow reserve; fourteen named registrations of forty, plus98 soldiers, remain separate from shikigami. The two soldiers unmade on101 stay lost. Nonresponsive fragments and other material are catalogued for later craft review, not raised indiscriminately. No67 held stat points are spent. Speaker marks begin here, in accordance with the creator's new reading convention.

*When: Day453, morning. In the yard, the last two trays have finally lost the label ASK. Anko has replaced it with DON'T PUT YOUR HAND IN THIS.*

[U] "It was a perfectly good label."

[A] "It was a question."

[U] "That was the purpose."

[A] "People have been asking it for five days. Nobody's moved the trays."

Anko put the old label into her coat pocket, where it joined a watch, three short pencils and a folded scrap of paper she had been meaning to give to someone since yesterday. She had brought a cart this time. The cart made her intention plain.

Mau came round the corner with two lengths of cloth and the padded boards they had used for the ice bow. The bow had its own covered place now. It did not need both boards.

[M] "Where?"

Anko pointed at the ground beside the trays.

[A] "Clean side here. Anything with an edge, on the cloth. Nothing on my page."

He set the boards down. Umaru had already knelt beside the first tray with her book open. She looked up when the shadow of the longer board went over her writing.

[U] "Not clean enough to put on the book, either."

[M] "I wasn't."

[U] "That was before. You were about to."

He laid it on the ground.

* * *

The pieces looked less alike in daylight.

On the first morning everything from the desert had been black, sharp and wrapped in a layer of sand that turned to mud when they washed it. They had left it drying on the cloths. Now some edges shone green where the light came through. Others did not shine at all.

Anko separated a flat piece with a pair of tongs.

[A] "This one?"

Mau bent to look. A curved rib ran across the underside, dark and smooth as a thumbprint.

[M] "Not with those. That came off the ground."

[U] "Which ground?"

[M] "The bit that folded when it came up. Not the thing itself. We picked it up because it looked the same."

He could remember that part clearly. Yuka's rope over the black glass. The first hard edge coming through it. A whole slab of the desert lifting before the creature beneath it turned.

He did not have to dig for the picture. It was one of the things that had happened after the theft. His own day, in a place he could still put himself inside.

He looked away from it when Anko held up another piece.

[A] "And this?"

That one had a pale seam down its middle.

[M] "Keep that."

[U] "Keep it where?"

He pointed at the second board.

[M] "Not with the ground."

* * *

They found the jaw at the bottom.

Not the whole head. A long lower piece, with hinges at both ends and the small close teeth that Mau remembered noticing just before he got out of their way. Anko lifted it with the tongs and turned it over above the tray.

[A] "That's a mouth."

[M] "That is a mouth."

[U] "Helpful?"

He smiled before he could help it.

[M] "A bit more than last time."

It gave them a direction. Not a complete answer, but a place to begin the curve. The pieces with the pale seams fitted along it in a chain, wider near the head and narrowing toward the other end. They did not lock together like a toy. There were gaps and missing edges. But the turns agreed.

Mau set a hooked piece down near the middle.

[M] "No. That goes underneath. It caught the rope there."

Anko moved it with the tongs.

Umaru leaned over without touching.

[U] "It was one thing."

[M] "This part was. The rest of that tray isn't."

He had stopped seeing a heap of similar pieces. There was a long body now, low to the ground, with its weight in the bend instead of in legs. He could feel something beneath the shape, faint but familiar.

Not a voice asking for anything.

The place where an answer might be.

* * *

Anko saw him go quiet.

[A] "Before you do it."

He looked up.

[M] "I know."

[A] "I'm not saying no. I'm saying I still have the tongs over its head."

She put the jaw down. Then the tongs. Moved her page clear and stepped back to where Umaru was sitting.

Mau crouched beside the board. The piece that had seemed the centre on the first morning had been part of the desert floor. He had been reaching toward the wrong shape. Now he could hold the longer one in mind without forcing a familiar creature into the gaps.

[M] *Ciel?*

[C] *I can feel what you're finding. It doesn't tell us every fragment will answer. This one has a pattern you can follow.*

[M] *The cup?*

[C] *Recovered from the last work. Enough for one careful attempt. Not a reason to try everything on the cart afterward.*

He told them the useful part aloud. Umaru opened a clean page. Anko moved the second board a little farther from the first.

[A] "Room?"

[M] "That's enough."

He made sure it was enough before saying the word.

* * *

[M] "Arise."

The dark began at the hinge of the jaw.

It ran along the pale seams before it filled the broken plates. For a moment nothing rose; Mau felt the shape strain to keep the two curves that belonged together, and loosened his grip on the picture of the broken pieces. He wanted the creature they had been, not a perfect arrangement on a board.

The cup drew lower.

Then the long body lifted from the wood.

Not with the sudden straightening of the archer or the stone weight of Cairn. It gathered itself in a coil, one smooth length passing over another, its dark plates turning a greenish edge to the morning. The broken jaw was whole in the shadow. Narrow. Closed.

It raised its head until it was level with Mau's shoulder.

He stayed crouched. Let it find him there.

[M] "Hello."

The head turned toward his voice.

Anko did not put the tongs back in her hands. Umaru held her pencil above the page, waiting for what he would call it.

Mau looked at the loops on the ground, the length he had spent the morning finding among the wrong pieces.

[M] "Skein."

The coil settled. A small scrape against the earth, then stillness.

* * *

[U] "Fourteen?"

Mau checked. Opened the registration field for her and nothing else.

[M] "Fourteen. Of forty."

[U] "Soldiers?"

[M] "Ninety-eight."

She wrote the second number beside its own heading. There was no arrow carrying Skein across into that line.

[A] "And that cost?"

[M] "New extraction. Yes."

He could feel the difference from asking Tank to stand beside him. A new depth in the registered places, and a smaller amount in the cup than before he had begun. He did not have to sit down before describing it. It had still taken something from him.

Anko looked from him to the serpent.

[A] "Standing there?"

[M] "Same rule as the others once it's raised."

She made a small mark beside the entry. Not a new rule. The same one checked again.

Umaru looked at the edge of the shared field.

[U] "Points?"

He changed the selected view.

Sixty-seven held.

[M] "I haven't touched them."

[U] "Good. You can keep doing that until this afternoon."

[M] "That's very generous."

[U] "I have a whole other page to be generous on."

She looked at the serpent again before closing the shared view.

[U] "What does it do?"

[M] "We know it fits under things. That's where it came from."

[A] "Don't write that as a promise it can go through a wall."

[M] "I wasn't going to."

He moved one empty board aside and asked Skein to come round it. The long body followed the edge, head low, turning back on itself in less room than the broken pieces had seemed to need. It stopped beside his foot without scraping the nearby parcel.

Umaru made a small drawing beneath the name.

[U] "Low. Turns close. Other things pending."

Anko read it and nodded. The list could begin with what they had seen. It did not have to pretend the new name told them everything.

* * *

They did not raise the rest.

Some of it had never been alive. Some had been part of something they could not identify from what remained. Ciel did not turn that uncertainty into a reason to push. Neither did Mau.

They wrapped the sharp pieces in separate cloths and put the matching numbers on the outside. Glass with sand fused through it. A curved plate that would not take a scratch from Anko's old nail. Two pieces that might have been the same material until one rang and the other did not.

Anko tapped the second carefully, once.

[A] "Workshop. Not kitchen."

[U] "I wasn't going to put it in the kitchen."

[A] "I'm writing for the person who sees a bowl-shaped thing and thinks he's found a bowl."

Mau looked at her.

[M] "It does look like a bowl."

She wrote *NOT A BOWL* beneath the number.

He let the joke go. He was looking at the two counts on Umaru's page, and at the entry Ori had made for a hundred soldiers with two spaces left in the line. New names did not change what had been taken away. The empty places did not turn into these new things.

Umaru caught him looking. She did not change either count.

* * *

Skein stayed beside the board while they loaded the cart.

It watched each parcel pass with a slow turn of the head. When Anko lifted the last wrapped length, it moved after it, not threatening, simply following the thing they had spent all morning looking at.

[M] "That part stays here."

The serpent stopped.

Mau held out his hand, palm down. It lowered its head beneath it. He felt the faint cold of the new shadow without putting his stiff fingers round anything sharp.

[M] "Back, for now."

It went down through its own coil. The earth was bare again. The cloths, the parcels and the last few grains of sand stayed where they were.

Anko picked up the empty tray.

It fitted exactly into the space she had cleared at the back of the cart. She looked at it, pleased, for the first time that morning.

[A] "There."

Umaru shut the book. Mau took the handles.

The cart did not move.

Anko looked down. A wedge was still under the wheel.

[A] "Don't." She bent to pull it out before he could try harder. "I've seen what you do to things that won't move."

He waited until her hand was clear.

Then they took the last of the desert to the workshop, slowly enough that nothing on the cart rang against anything else.
