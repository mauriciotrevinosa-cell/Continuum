# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 42 — An Inch Lower

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 42
**Source episode:** S6E30
**Continuity state:** Day453 late morning. Mai adjusts the existing silver flower on Maki's ash-shafted spear, following their agreement at the Day452 crackers table. Ordinary cord and handwork; no new Construction use or weapon effect. Sisterly humour, choice and familiar friction. Maki gains and shares no further knowledge of Mau/Yuka. Dialogue follows the creator's speaker-mark convention.

*When: Day453, late morning. Outside the workshop, in the clear strip beside the woodpile. Maki has brought the spear. Mai has brought three lengths of cord, a cloth and a conviction that this should not take long.*

[Mai] "Show me."

Maki held the spear at an angle, its blade well clear of her sister.

[Mk] "Here. When I bring it across."

She moved slowly. The silver flower turned beneath the blade, brushed her sleeve and caught. A tiny snag, no more than a tug on the cloth. She could have freed it with one finger.

Mai took hold of the sleeve first.

[Mai] "Stop."

Maki stopped.

The silver petal lay under a loose thread at the cuff. Mai lifted the thread away and examined it between finger and thumb.

[Mai] "It isn't the flower."

[Mk] "I didn't accuse it."

[Mai] "You've accused something. You brought the whole spear."

[Mk] "You asked me to."

Mai released her cuff. She had asked, which was inconvenient now that Maki remembered it.

[Mai] "Put it down."

Maki laid it across two low supports by the workshop wall. She had brought an old cloth to put under the blade; Mai folded it twice more before letting the metal rest.

The flower caught the sun. Small, pale, slightly uneven if Mai looked at it long enough. She could find every place where her first intention had become the thing her hands actually made.

Maki leaned against the wall and waited.

* * *

An inch lower looked wrong.

Mai held the flower against the shaft and stood back. Maki looked at it. Neither said anything for a moment.

[Mk] "You said it would."

[Mai] "I know what I said."

[Mk] "I thought you might enjoy being right."

[Mai] "I was enjoying it before you spoke."

Maki's mouth moved at one corner. Mai put the flower back where it had been, untied, and lifted it a little higher instead.

[Mk] "That would be worse."

[Mai] "I know. I'm looking."

She tried it on the other side of the shaft. From there Maki would not brush the edge with the same part of her sleeve, but the tie sat badly under her grip. Maki did not have to lift the weapon to show her. Mai looked at where her hand would go and moved it back.

It had seemed simpler last night, with a cracker in her hand and no spear taking up the entire patch of wall.

Maki waited through two more positions. Then she reached past Mai and turned the flower a little. Not lower. Just round, changing which petal faced the cuff.

Mai stared at it.

[Mk] "What?"

[Mai] "I would have tried that."

[Mk] "I know."

[Mai] "Next."

[Mk] "Probably."

Mai held out her hand for the cord.

* * *

The old tie had worked loose beneath the silver.

She could feel the worn place when she drew it between her fingers. Not broken. Rubbed thin, flattened where it had pressed against the shaft. She put it aside rather than dropping it with the scraps, although she did not know why she was keeping it.

Maki looked at the three pieces she had brought.

[Mk] "Which one?"

[Mai] "The dark one."

[Mk] "They're all dark."

[Mai] "The least ugly one."

Maki considered them with exaggerated attention, then handed her the one nearest her wrist.

It was the one Mai had meant.

She threaded it behind the flower, keeping the cord away from the sharpest edge. One turn round the shaft. Another crossing beneath it. Maki held the silver still while Mai tightened the tie, both their hands close together over the old ash.

Maki's hands had changed. Mai noticed things like that when she was occupied with something else. Small new marks. Older ones which she could remember seeing yesterday, and others she had never asked about. Her sister's body had begun this world young and familiar, but it did not remain exactly as Mai had known it.

Neither did hers.

[Mk] "Too tight?" Maki asked.

[Mai] "No. Keep holding it."

She worked the knot flat with her thumbnail. Maki moved her hand out of the way before Mai had to ask, keeping one finger beneath the lowest petal so the silver would not slide.

Mai looked at that finger.

[Mai] "You're allowed to let go when I tell you."

[Mk] "You haven't told me."

[Mai] "I know."

The answer irritated her because it was accurate. She had been about to explain how to hold it, and Maki had already found the useful place. There was nothing left to correct except the fact that Mai had expected to need a correction.

She took a little longer over the next turn of cord.

[Mk] "There?"

[Mai] "There."

Maki kept it still. She did not lean over to see the knot or put her other hand in the way. Mai could remember quarrelling over much smaller pieces of space, with neither of them willing to be the first to move.

She tucked the loose end beneath the crossing.

[Mai] "Now."

Maki let go. The flower stayed.

Mai smoothed the tie once, then found that she was smiling at it. She turned her face away before her sister could ask why.

[Mk] "You make that face when you're concentrating."

[Mai] "Which face?"

Maki demonstrated.

Mai pulled the cord harder than necessary.

[Mk] "There. That one."

[Mai] "You're going to need the cuff fixed too."

[Mk] "Is that part of the service?"

[Mai] "I'm making a list."

* * *

They tested the tie in the clear ground.

Maki checked behind her before lifting the spear. Mai moved to one side, arms folded, and watched the same slow movement which had caught the sleeve earlier. The silver stayed against the shaft. The cuff brushed past without stopping.

Again. Faster, but not a strike. Maki brought the weapon back and settled it at her side.

[Mk] "Better."

Mai came nearer and looked at the petals. The flower had not shifted. She took hold of the shaft below Maki's hand and turned it a fraction, inspecting the back.

[Mai] "Again."

Maki obliged. Mai could hear the air move around the blade. It was a small sound, easy to miss beneath the workshop noise, and she remembered hearing it long before she had any reason to like the sound.

Her sister stopped in front of her.

[Mk] "That's six."

[Mai] "It might come loose on seven."

Maki looked at her for a moment, then did the seventh.

It did not come loose.

Mai nodded toward the supports, and they put the spear down again.

[Mk] "You could have let me take it to Wakana."

[Mai] "For cord?"

[Mk] "For the tie."

[Mai] "I know how to tie something."

[Mk] "I didn't mean—"

[Mai] "I made it. I can stop it falling off."

Maki said nothing after that. Mai bent over the knot and trimmed the end, leaving enough to undo later. She was not going to be the person who made a knot so permanent that the next repair needed scissors at the wood.

Behind her, Maki picked up the old tie.

[Mk] "Do you want this?"

Mai glanced round.

[Mai] "I put it aside, didn't I?"

Maki laid it on the cloth.

* * *

The cuff took longer than the flower.

It was not really a tear. A small pulled loop, worn pale beside the edge, which looked more dramatic because Maki kept turning it toward the light to see what Mai was doing. Mai slapped her wrist lightly on the third turn.

[Mai] "Still."

[Mk] "I can do it myself."

[Mai] "I haven't said you can't."

Maki sat on the low bench outside the door. Mai sat beside her, the sleeve pulled back enough to keep the loose cloth flat. She used the point of the needle to ease the thread into place rather than stitching over it at once.

There was a hammer tapping inside. Someone shifted a board, and sawdust came drifting across the threshold. Maki moved her boot out of the way. Mai tucked her skirt closer so it would not catch the dust.

[Mk] "It's quieter here in the morning."

[Mai] "People are working."

[Mk] "They're working in the afternoon too."

[Mai] "In the afternoon they explain it more."

Maki laughed once. Mai looked up quickly, annoyed at how pleased the sound made her.

She put in two stitches to keep the thread where she wanted it.

[Mai] "There."

Maki turned her wrist, waited for Mai to move the needle away and lowered her hand.

[Mk] "Thank you."

[Mai] "Don't sound so surprised."

[Mk] "I didn't."

[Mai] "You did a little."

Maki looked at her. Then she shook her head, smiling, and reached for the spear.

Mai stayed seated for a moment, folding the cloth around the spare cord and the old tie. The thing she had made would go on being carried into places she did not go. She knew that. It had been true before the flower existed. Putting it there had not changed which parts of her sister's life she could follow.

Maki had waited beside the bench rather than leaving.

[Mk] "Coming?"

[Mai] "Where?"

[Mk] "To put this away. Then I was going to see whether there's any hot water left."

[Mai] "You could have told me that before I sat in the dust."

[Mk] "You said it wouldn't take long."

Mai stood. She brushed off the back of her skirt, looked at the pale patch remaining and brushed it again.

* * *

They went round the yard instead of through the busiest part of the house.

Maki carried the spear upright where she could, lowering it beneath the branch which always caught people who had forgotten it was there. Mai reached for the flower as it passed her shoulder and touched one petal with a fingertip.

[Mk] "Still there."

[Mai] "I'm checking."

[Mk] "Eight times now."

[Mai] "The tie's new."

Maki waited while she looked at it. No suggestion that she was fussing. No careful praise, either, which Mai would have disliked more.

At the workshop corner a thin trail of sawdust followed them. Mai looked back at it, then at the hem of her skirt, and made an irritated sound.

Maki leaned the spear safely against the wall.

[Mai] "What are you doing?"

She caught Mai gently by the sleeve before she could step away, then brushed the dust from the back of her skirt with two brisk strokes.

[Mai] "I can do that."

[Mk] "I haven't said you can't."

Mai looked at her.

Maki picked up the spear.

They walked the rest of the way with Mai telling her that the third petal was still slightly crooked, and Maki explaining, very patiently, that it had been crooked when she first received it and she knew which way it went.
