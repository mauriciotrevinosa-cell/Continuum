# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 44 — What Might Come After

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 44
**Source episode:** S6E31
**Continuity state:** Day454, late morning into noon, before the weekly reset455. Mau and Yuka revisit the previously surveyed lower crossing and courtyard of the E gate with notice, a return time and Rimuru at home. A registered shadow watches the return out of earshot. Their date includes a tentative conversation about perhaps having a family someday, without sex, pregnancy, a wedding or biological certainty. They acknowledge Ori and the existing family rather than declaring a replacement. Yuka does not disclose her cancer. Their imagined future deepens their present bond and the eventual cost of the deal; Mau's old memories are not restored.

*When: Day454, before noon. The little courtyard beyond the lower crossing. The upper bridge is still marked on the page as a place not to put a hand beneath. Igris has the path back to the frame.*

The waterwheel did not turn.

Yuka had spent the first few minutes trying to work out whether it was meant to. There was water beneath it. There were paddles, or what looked like paddles, and a long wooden shaft disappearing into the wall of the empty building beside it. Something had grown between the spokes and stopped the whole thing at an angle.

She pointed.

[Yk] "If we pulled that out—"

[M] "We aren't pulling things out of a wall we haven't looked behind."

She turned toward him.

[Yk] "You sound like Anko."

[M] "I wrote it down so I wouldn't have to keep making up my own sensible answers."

He unfolded the little route page. The lower stones. The wall. The bridge, with its two crosses underneath. The courtyard, where they had looked first before sitting, and the point beyond which they had told Rimuru they would not go today.

Yuka read the margin.

[Yk] "You put lunch on the map."

[M] "I was told to be specific."

She laughed and sat on the low stone beside him.

They had brought food, water and the ordinary things you brought even when you expected to be back soon. Mau's sword remained sheathed. The two creatures beneath the upper bridge had not followed them; Igris watched the return path, far enough away that they could see the dark angle of his helmet without hearing him move.

It was strange, making a small afternoon inside somewhere that had no reason to care whether it was an afternoon at all.

Yuka liked it.

* * *

There were flowers in the courtyard.

Not beautiful ones. Little stubborn things growing where the stones had cracked, with leaves too large for the pale heads above them. Yuka knelt to look at one without picking it. Mau came over with the cloth they had meant to spread on the stone and held it while she examined the stem.

[Yk] "I don't know what that is."

[M] "We can draw it. Ask when we're home."

[Yk] "Not eat it?"

[M] "I've become very sophisticated."

She smiled up at him. He had said *home* without looking as if he needed to choose which word was polite. She tried not to take too much from it.

They drew the flower badly on the back of the route page, making sure not to put the drawing over the warning about the bridge. Yuka insisted it needed the notch in its leaf. Mau put the notch on the wrong side and had to make another drawing beneath the first.

[Yk] "Now it looks as if there are two kinds."

[M] "One is an interpretation."

She took the pencil away from him and wrote *ONE FLOWER* above both.

They went back to the low stone. She sat facing the courtyard; he sat beside her with the pot between his feet so it would not slide off while they served themselves. It was a plain lunch. They had stopped trying to make it look impressive before leaving the kitchen, when Rem made them choose between a prettier cloth and one that would actually keep the pot upright.

The practical cloth had a little hole in one corner. Yuka kept pushing her smallest finger through it while she ate.

[M] "Mai's going to ask what happened to that."

[Yk] "It was already there."

[M] "It wasn't that big."

She took her finger out.

* * *

The building made her think of a house.

She said so after they had put the food away. Not that it was a house, or that they ought to claim it. The square of the doorway, the place where a window might once have been, the uneven ledge where somebody could have put things they needed near their hands.

[Yk] "Grandma used to leave something everywhere. Not because she lost it. Because she'd decided that was where she'd want it later."

Mau looked at the ledge.

[M] "She still does that."

[Yk] "I know. Grandpa would move it and then have to explain why he was wrong."

[M] "He's had a lot of practice."

She laughed softly.

For a while she told him small things about the house she had known. A door that caught in wet weather. A spot on the floor everyone stepped around without remembering why. Her grandfather pretending not to know which cupboard held what she had come looking for, so she would have to ask him instead of slipping in and out.

Mau listened. Sometimes he asked a question. He did not try to put himself into any of the rooms.

[M] "What would you put there?" he asked when she stopped.

[Yk] "On the ledge?"

[M] "If it was yours."

She looked at it. The question was so simple that she had an answer before she realised what she was answering.

[Yk] "A bowl. For things that don't belong anywhere yet."

[M] "That would get very full."

[Yk] "You'd be responsible for half of it."

[M] "At least."

They smiled at one another. Her hand had found his on the stone without either of them saying anything about it.

[Yk] "Sometimes I think about that," she said.

[M] "A bowl?"

[Yk] "Something that could be ours. Later. Not just where we sit when nobody's watching."

His fingers moved against hers. Not letting go. Making room for what she had said.

* * *

[M] "What sort of later?"

She kept looking at the doorway.

[Yk] "I don't know. A normal one. Breakfast. Something broken that isn't important enough to be an emergency. Having to come home because somebody's waiting."

[M] "We have some of that."

[Yk] "I know."

That was what made it difficult to say. They had a house full of people who had made those things possible, and here she was sitting in a stolen little corner of the day imagining what she could ask for beyond them.

[Yk] "Maybe a family," she said, so quietly that the water nearly covered it.

Mau turned toward her.

[M] "You mean children?"

She went red at once.

[Yk] "Maybe. Not now. I'm not asking you to—"

[M] "I know."

She looked at him.

He was not laughing. That made her face warmer, not less.

[Yk] "I hadn't thought I'd get to want something that far away. Then we're sitting here arguing about a cloth, and it seems like a thing you could go on doing. Arguing about ordinary things. For years."

He looked down at their hands.

[M] "I'd be very good at the arguing."

She laughed despite herself. The sound came out unevenly.

[Yk] "You'd teach them terrible answers."

[M] "You'd teach them to check my answers."

[Yk] "Somebody would have to."

The picture arrived between them, small and uninvited: a child asking a question, both of them answering at once, somebody declaring one answer unfair. Mau could almost hear the argument. Not a recovered memory. Something that had never happened, which he found he wanted to sit with for a little longer.

Then he thought of Ori's paper birds on the shelf at home, and his smile changed.

Yuka saw it.

* * *

[Yk] "Ori," she said.

He nodded.

[M] "I know what everyone tells me. About her. I don't want to be sitting here thinking about being someone's father as if nobody's ever asked me to be that before."

[Yk] "She didn't ask. You chose her."

The words hurt them both. Yuka had not meant to put the hurt into them, but she could not make them less true because the afternoon had been lovely.

Mau sat with it for a while.

[M] "I'm trying to understand how to do something with what I know. Not just stand there wishing the feeling would tell me first."

Yuka pressed her thumb against the side of his hand.

[Yk] "You can play a stupid game with her."

[M] "I did. I liked it."

[Yk] "You can do it again."

[M] "Yes."

He looked at the two flower drawings. One wrong, one less wrong, both waiting to be shown to somebody who knew what they were.

[M] "I don't want a future where we have to pretend everybody else isn't there."

[Yk] "Neither do I."

She meant it. The fact that she did not know how to make one did not change the wanting.

He turned his hand beneath hers and held it properly.

[M] "I'd like to be able to think about it. With you. Without saying I know what a body I don't understand can promise, or when any of it would be possible."

[Yk] "I wasn't asking for a date."

[M] "We're already on one."

She looked at him. Then laughed, unwillingly, and pushed his shoulder with the hand he was not holding.

[Yk] "A date on a calendar."

[M] "Very demanding. Two kinds of date in one afternoon."

She leaned against him, hiding her hot face against his shoulder until he stopped laughing enough to put his arm round her.

* * *

They stayed until the time they had written down.

They did not name children or make a plan for a new house. The one in the courtyard remained an empty building in a gate that would change at dawn. There was no sensible place to leave a bowl on its ledge.

But when she stood, Yuka looked at the doorway once more, and Mau knew she was seeing something other than stone. He could see a little of it too.

She kissed him before they went back to the path. Her hand was warm at the side of his neck; his stayed at her back, not asking for anything beyond what she was giving. When they separated she rested her forehead against his for a moment.

[Yk] "We should go."

[M] "I know."

[Yk] "Igris is pretending not to look."

At the far end of the path the knight's helmet was firmly turned toward the upper bridge. Mau considered him, then looked back at her.

[M] "He's very good at it."

They collected every bowl, every scrap of cloth and the page with the two flowers. Nothing left behind to persuade an empty room that they meant to stay. They took the lower stones back, reported the same two creatures beneath the bridge and came through the ordinary light together, with time to return the pot before the afternoon work began.
