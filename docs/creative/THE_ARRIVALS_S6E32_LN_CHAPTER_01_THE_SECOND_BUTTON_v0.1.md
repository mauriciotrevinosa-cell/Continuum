# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 45 — The Second Button

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 45
**Source episode:** S6E32
**Continuity state:** Day454, night in Yuka's room after their gate picnic. Yuka initiates greater physical closeness, then stops before sex, distressed by the conflict between what she wants and what she knows of Mau's stolen past. Kamish's earlier words recur only as her memory. Mau accepts the stop without pressure; they talk, dress and remain together without treating the conflict as solved. No one else acquires the conversation, no medical history is disclosed and no promise from the date becomes a fixed marriage or pregnancy plan.

*When: Day454, night. Yuka's room. The borrowed blankets are on the floor; the drawing from the courtyard lies face down beneath her book.*

He had missed a button.

Yuka noticed when he came in. The collar sat at an angle, and one side of his shirt hung lower than the other. He had been trying to correct it with the stiff fingers of his left hand while carrying his cup in the right, and had apparently decided the shirt would have to accept being wrong.

[Yk] "Stop."

He stopped in the doorway.

[M] "What did I do?"

She put the cup on the little table, moved the lamp away from the edge and reached for his collar.

[Yk] "You started at the wrong hole."

He looked down.

[M] "It has more holes than it needs."

[Yk] "It has the same number of buttons."

[M] "A poorly considered design."

She undid the two at the top and started again. He stood while she worked, watching her face instead of the buttons. That made it considerably harder to look as if she was doing something practical.

[Yk] "Don't."

[M] "Don't what?"

[Yk] "Look at me like you're waiting for me to get it wrong."

[M] "I'm not waiting for that."

Her fingers stopped.

He smiled, small and entirely unhelpful.

She finished the second button and left the top one undone.

[Yk] "There."

He looked down again, then at her.

[M] "A remarkable improvement."

She pushed him lightly toward the blankets before he could give her a more elaborate review of her work.

* * *

The room was warmer than the courtyard had been.

They sat on the floor at first, backs against the side of the bed, the spare blanket over their knees. He had brought the last of the water because she kept forgetting until she was already comfortable. She drank, passed the cup back and rested her head against his shoulder while he told her about Anko inspecting the flower drawing.

[M] "She said if we wanted a useful answer we should have drawn the bottom."

[Yk] "It was attached to the ground."

[M] "I said that. She looked at me as if I'd gone there specifically to obstruct botany."

Yuka laughed. She could imagine Anko's face very clearly.

He turned the cup between his hands. The marked side was facing him; he ran a thumb over it without seeming to notice.

[Yk] "Did you have a good time?"

He looked at her.

[M] "Yes."

[Yk] "Not just the lunch."

[M] "I know what you meant."

She felt her face warming again. It had been doing that all afternoon, whenever she remembered exactly what she had said on the stone and that he had not laughed at the part she was most afraid to say.

[Yk] "I didn't mean you had to answer anything today."

[M] "You said."

[Yk] "I'm saying it again."

He nodded, accepting it this time without turning it into a joke. The future could remain a future. Neither had brought it back through the frame in a box that needed opening before bed.

She put her hand over his, took the cup from him and set it on the floor beyond the blanket.

Then she turned and kissed him.

* * *

She was the one who moved closer.

He had not tugged at her, or leaned in until it became hard to refuse. She shifted onto her knees so she could reach him properly, put one hand beside his shoulder and kissed him again before he could ask whether she was comfortable there.

He was smiling when she drew back.

[M] "What?"

[Yk] "You were going to ask."

[M] "You keep kneeling on the fold."

She looked down. She was kneeling on the fold.

He eased the blanket flat beneath her knee, carefully, and she began laughing against his cheek. Then the laugh changed and she kissed him again, with both hands at his face this time, feeling him go still beneath her hands before he held her back.

For a while it was easy.

Not thoughtless. Easy in the frightening way the gate had been easy when there was nobody to interrupt the part of them that wanted to stay close. She could feel the ordinary warmth of him, the small adjustment when his left hand could not settle where he wanted it. She moved so it did not have to.

She pulled her own shirt off and put it on the bed, still in her bra. His eyes came back to her face. He waited for her to move again instead of deciding what it meant for her.

She reached for him.

Then her hand stopped at the open collar.

* * *

The scars did not change.

That was what struck her. She had spent the afternoon imagining things that had never happened, and here were the things that had. The marks beneath the shirt she had just corrected. The ones she knew about and the ones she had only been told of. He had carried them into the courtyard, sat beside her and talked about a future with them still against his skin.

*You stopped.*

Kamish's voice was not in the room. It was the one she remembered from the moor, slow and deep beneath the strange stars.

*Remember that you did.*

Her fingers tightened on the edge of Mau's shirt.

[Yk] "Wait."

He stopped at once.

His hands stayed where she could see them, one resting on the blanket, the other loose beside her. He did not draw away as if she had hurt him. He simply waited, breathing a little faster, watching her face.

[M] "Do you want room?"

She nodded before she knew which kind she meant.

He moved back against the bed, giving her space to sit beside him rather than over him. She took it, and reached for the shirt she had put away. Her arm shook once. He saw it and did not try to take the decision out of her hands by dressing her himself.

She put the shirt on. Got one sleeve wrong. Pulled it off that shoulder and tried again.

[Yk] "I wanted to."

[M] "I know."

[Yk] "I still want to. That isn't—"

She stopped because her voice had begun to break.

He looked at her hands, then her face.

[M] "You don't have to prove that part to me."

She drew her knees up under the blanket and pressed the loose cloth of the shirt between them.

* * *

He handed her the water.

She took a little, mostly to give herself something to do. He waited until she had put the cup down before asking whether he had missed something she had tried to tell him.

[Yk] "No. You didn't."

[M] "All right."

[Yk] "I don't know how to explain it without making it sound as though I'm afraid of you."

He was quiet for a moment.

[M] "Are you?"

She shook her head immediately.

[Yk] "Not of you touching me. Not like that. I'm afraid of how easy it is to stop thinking about everything else when you're here."

He looked at the wall, giving her the side of his face rather than making her hold his eyes through every word.

[Yk] "I can know something is complicated," she said, "and then you do something ordinary, and I'm happy. I don't have to make myself happy. It just happens. And then I remember why it feels as if I shouldn't be."

[M] "Because of what you know."

She nodded.

[Yk] "And because of what I don't know. What happens later. What happens to the things we say now."

He reached for the blanket between them and smoothed the edge where she had been crushing it.

[M] "I can't tell you you're wrong to be afraid of that."

She looked at him.

[M] "I wish I could. It would be a convenient answer."

She let out a wet little laugh.

[Yk] "You'd be very good at convenient answers."

[M] "I've been trying. The shirt didn't believe me."

She laughed again, properly enough that he smiled, though the difficult things did not leave the room with it.

[Yk] "I want you," she said. "I just can't do this part. Not now."

He nodded.

[M] "Then we don't."

She had expected relief to feel clean. It did not. There was wanting still, and shame, and the ache of being treated gently while she could think of people who were not getting any gentleness from this at all.

He did not ask for a smaller bargain. He did not make her promise a different night.

After a while she put her head against his shoulder again, and he moved his arm so she could.

* * *

They stayed sitting until her feet went cold.

He noticed when she tucked them under the other leg. She accused him of noticing too much. He said he was prepared to ignore the next thing she dropped, but he would not promise not to laugh.

She caught his wrist before he could reach for the lamp.

[Yk] "You can stay."

He looked at the two blankets already spread on her floor.

[M] "I wasn't packing."

[Yk] "I know. I'm saying it."

He turned his hand beneath hers.

[M] "I'd like to."

She let him put out the light. In the dark they found the edges of their blankets by touch, bumping the cup once and rescuing it before it rolled. He lay down first. She lay beside him, fully dressed now except for the shoes she had never put on, and rested her hand on the uninjured side of his arm.

For a while neither spoke.

Then she felt his shirt shift beneath her fingers and began laughing again, silently, into the blanket.

[M] "What?"

[Yk] "The button."

He reached down to feel it.

There was a pause.

[M] "I maintain that it's a poor design."

She fixed it without turning the light back on.
