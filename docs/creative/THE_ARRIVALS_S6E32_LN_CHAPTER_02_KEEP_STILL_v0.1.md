# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 46 — Keep Still

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 46
**Source episode:** S6E32
**Continuity state:** Day455 morning. An ordinary morning together for Momo and Okarun, followed by Vamola. Portraits on household scrap paper, no new camera or magic. They remain a couple; no reversal of the established memory-loss history or new declarations of recovery. The new weekly gate is not explored in this scene.

*When: Day455, after breakfast. The bench beside the courtyard wall. Momo has borrowed two pencil stubs and brought the wrong kind of confidence.*

Okarun had been sitting still for almost a minute when Momo told him to keep still.

[Ok] "I am."

[Mo] "Your face moved."

[Ok] "I was answering you."

[Mo] "Well, don't do that either."

He shut his mouth.

Across the space between their knees, Momo looked down at the scrap of paper on her thigh. Looked up. Looked down again. Her pencil made a quick, furious sound.

Okarun tried to decide whether he had seen enough pictures in his life to be able to sit like someone in a picture. Most of the people in the pictures he could remember did not appear to be thinking about where their knees went. His knees, by contrast, seemed extremely interested in the question.

He moved one foot.

Momo looked up.

[Ok] "That wasn't my face."

She pressed her lips together and returned to the paper.

It had begun with a pencil.

More precisely, it had begun with Momo saying that people who could draw made it look ridiculously easy, and Okarun making the mistake of agreeing in the tone of someone who might be willing to prove her wrong. He had meant it as encouragement. By the time he had worked out how to explain that, they were outside, and Momo had announced that they would draw each other.

She had finished a bowl of breakfast before anyone found her a task. Somebody else was already carrying the plates. For once she had not gone back to see whether the somebody needed help.

Now she was drawing him with the concentration she normally applied to catching someone in a lie.

He wondered whether that would be visible in the finished portrait.

[Mo] "Stop looking worried."

[Ok] "I'm not worried."

[Mo] "You have worried eyebrows."

He tried to relax them.

[Mo] "Don't do that. Now you look like you owe me money."

He gave up.

* * *

There was a breeze through the courtyard. It lifted one corner of the paper, and Momo put a finger on it without looking away from him.

He liked her like this.

The thought arrived so simply that he almost said it. Her hair had come loose beside one ear. There was a pencil mark on the side of her thumb. She had drawn her legs up onto the bench and was bent over the page, biting the inside of her cheek whenever something annoyed her.

He liked that she had brought him outside to do something she might be bad at. That she had already decided it was partly his fault.

[Mo] "What?"

[Ok] "Nothing."

She looked directly at him now.

[Mo] "You've got that face."

[Ok] "It's the only one I brought."

Her pencil stopped.

For one lovely second he thought he had won something.

Then she laughed, looked away, and said he was getting annoying.

He went warm all the way up to his ears.

[Mo] "There. That's better. Stay like that."

[Ok] "I can't keep being embarrassed on command."

[Mo] "You manage it most of the time."

He wanted to object. She was already drawing again.

After a while she asked him to take off his glasses.

[Ok] "Why?"

[Mo] "The sides are hard."

[Ok] "But I wear them."

[Mo] "I know that. I have met you."

He took them off anyway. The wall behind her softened. Her face remained close enough to see.

She frowned at the paper, then handed his glasses straight back.

[Mo] "No. Put them on."

[Ok] "You just said—"

[Mo] "I was wrong."

He looked at her over the frame.

[Mo] "Don't look so surprised."

He put them on.

She watched him settle them, and this time the pencil moved more slowly.

* * *

His turn was worse.

Momo held out the second stub and sat up as if she were about to have a serious photograph taken. Then she changed her mind, put an elbow on the bench back, rested her chin on her hand and looked at him sideways.

Okarun stared.

[Mo] "Well?"

[Ok] "I haven't started."

[Mo] "I can see that."

He looked down at the paper.

He knew what her eyes looked like. He had spent a humiliating amount of time knowing what her eyes looked like. Putting them on paper turned out to be a completely different occupation.

He drew one.

It was enormous.

He drew the other one smaller to compensate, which made everything worse.

Momo leaned forward.

[Ok] "No. You can't look yet."

[Mo] "I'm making sure you aren't drawing someone else."

[Ok] "Please sit back."

She sat back, looking pleased with herself.

He made a faint mark for her nose and immediately regretted making any decisions at all.

There were things a person could do with a pencil that seemed actively hostile to the person being drawn. He had found several of them already.

[Mo] "You can rub it out."

[Ok] "We don't have anything to rub it out with."

She considered this.

[Mo] "Then commit."

He looked at the two different eyes.

Committing was not going to help.

He turned the page over.

[Mo] "Coward."

[Ok] "It's a second draft."

She was still smiling when he began again.

This time he stopped trying to make individual features. He drew the tilt of her head, the sharp sweep of the hair beside her cheek, the way she had one shoulder higher than the other because of her elbow. The line wandered. He followed it. Her chin ended up too pointed, but at least both her eyes were now on the same general scale.

He had almost got the corner of her mouth when she began to talk.

[Ok] "Keep still."

She closed her mouth.

Then opened it very slowly.

[Mo] "Oh?"

He wished he had used any other words.

* * *

Vamola found them arguing about whether smiling counted as movement.

She stood beside the bench, looked at Momo's page, and made a small, considering sound.

Momo moved it out of reach.

[Mo] "Not finished."

Vamola sat down beside her anyway.

There was room for two people on that half of the bench if both of them were willing to be narrow. Momo shifted without thinking. Vamola took the empty space and inspected Okarun's paper from a distance that did not help him at all.

[V] "Momo."

[Ok] "Yes. It's supposed to be."

Vamola pointed at the page, then at Momo, and smiled.

He decided to accept that as a success.

Momo tried to see around his wrist.

[Mo] "Why did she get to look?"

[Ok] "She didn't ask."

[Mo] "Neither did I."

Vamola reached for the spare scrap.

Momo handed her the pencil she had finished using. Vamola held it, changed her grip, and drew three round shapes beside one another. A few quick lines gave one its glasses. Another received a great sweep of hair. The third had its arms straight out, touching the others.

She turned the page around.

[V] "Us."

Momo looked at it for a moment.

Then she moved her elbow off the bench back and put her shoulder against Vamola's.

[Mo] "That's cheating. Yours is good."

Vamola smiled as if this had been the point all along.

Okarun added the last line to his drawing. He did not know whether it was the right line. It was the line he had.

[Ok] "All right."

Momo held out her hand.

He gave her the paper and discovered that the worst part of drawing someone you liked was the instant when they actually looked at it.

She was quiet.

He began an explanation about the second draft, the pencil, and the fact that he had never claimed to be able to do this.

She put a finger on the little dark curve at the corner of the drawn mouth.

[Mo] "Is that what I do?"

He looked.

[Ok] "Sometimes."

She looked at him, then back at the drawing.

[Mo] "Huh."

He could not tell whether this was a good huh.

Then she folded the paper carefully, once, with the drawing inside.

[Ok] "You don't have to keep—"

[Mo] "It's mine."

She put it in her pocket.

He stopped explaining.

* * *

Momo's portrait of him had excellent glasses.

Everything else appeared to have been constructed in a state of personal disagreement with the glasses.

He had one anxious eyebrow, one furious eyebrow and a mouth much too small to explain either of them. The hair was unexpectedly good. She had found the angle of the loose strands above his forehead, and he kept looking at that before looking away from the rest.

[Mo] "Well?"

He tried very hard.

She watched his face.

He tried harder.

[Mo] "Don't you dare."

He laughed.

She reached for the paper. He lifted it away from her, laughing more now because she was laughing too and furious about it.

[Mo] "Give it here."

[Ok] "It's mine."

That stopped her for a moment.

He folded it as carefully as she had folded his.

She was looking at him differently when he put it away.

Vamola had added a fourth, very small figure to the side of her picture. It had a broad head and a tail.

[Mo] "Don't show her that."

Vamola looked up.

[Mo] "She'll demand a bigger head."

Okarun laughed again. Momo leaned across the narrow space and kissed his cheek, quickly enough that by the time he turned, she was examining Vamola's picture with complete seriousness.

[Mo] "We should draw Jiji next."

[Ok] "Why?"

[Mo] "Your eyebrows deserve revenge."

Vamola turned the scrap over and began another round head.

Momo took the pencil stub Okarun offered her. Their fingers stayed together around it for a second longer than either of them needed.

Then she told him that if he kept looking at her like that, she would draw it.
