# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 47 — From Behind the Line

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 47
**Source episode:** S6E33
**Continuity state:** Day455 noon. Marin interrupts Wakana's tendency to work through a break with an improvised ring-toss game beside the workshop. Rem and Ram join on their own terms. Ordinary rope loops, offcuts and throwing; no borrowed mana, new craft system, power or larger project. No Mau appearance or private knowledge transfer. Dialogue uses stable speaker marks.

*When: Day455, noon. The sun has reached the dry strip beside the workshop. Marin has found three broad loops of cord and five pieces of wood which were, until she moved them, very important scraps.*

[W] "I was using that."

Marin looked at the short block in her hand.

[Mr] "For what?"

[W] "To hold the others off the floor."

She looked at the empty patch where it had been.

The others were on the floor now.

[Mr] "Right. That one can go back."

She returned it, chose another from the little stack against the wall and held it up for inspection. Wakana nodded. She put it on the ground with four more, stepped backward and looked at the arrangement.

[Mr] "You know what this is."

[W] "Five offcuts."

[Mr] "You know what it's going to be."

He looked at the loops on her arm.

[W] "Not until you tell me."

She laughed and caught his hand before he could bend to retrieve the pencil he had put beside the door. He had come out for a drink of water and had somehow brought a measurement with him. Marin had noticed. She was not going to make a speech about it, but she was not going to give him the pencil either.

[Mr] "Come and lose at something."

* * *

The game had one rule.

Throw the ring from the line and get it over a piece of wood.

The first ring hit the nearest block, turned sideways and rolled into the little hollow beside the workshop step. Marin went to get it. The second caught the top of the furthest piece, hung there for one encouraging second and fell off.

She held the third for longer.

Wakana stood behind the line with his hands folded, waiting. He had the patient expression he wore when she tried on something unfinished and began describing what she would do in it once it was done.

[Mr] "You're allowed to say something."

[W] "Which part?"

[Mr] "Anything."

He looked at the targets.

[W] "That one's leaning."

She turned. The middle block was leaning, just enough to make a difference to somebody who noticed things like that. She went to straighten it and came back to find Rem and Ram at the edge of their small playing ground.

Rem had a basket against her hip. Ram had nothing at all except an expression of unfavourable interest.

[Ra] "Is this how the house is being enlarged?"

[Mr] "We're not enlarging anything."

[Ra] "A relief."

Marin threw the third ring.

It caught the middle block neatly, dropped to the ground around it and stayed there.

She put both hands up.

[Mr] "Yes!"

Wakana started smiling before she turned round to look at him.

[Ra] "One in three," Ram observed.

[Mr] "Your turn, then."

* * *

Ram did not put the basket down. That was Rem's basket, and Ram had not been carrying it. Rem put it down herself, on a dry patch where it would not be knocked over, and came to stand beside her sister.

[Re] "Are there points?"

[Mr] "There can be."

[W] "For getting it over."

[Ra] "An unusually clear system."

Marin handed her the rings.

Ram examined the first, turning the knotted part away from her fingers. Then she looked at the nearest piece of wood. She threw.

The ring went much too far.

Nobody spoke.

Wakana went to get it before the silence could grow any larger. When he came back, Ram was inspecting the second ring with considerably more attention.

[Ra] "Poorly balanced."

[Mr] "We can swap."

[Ra] "Ram will manage."

She threw again. This one landed short.

Rem looked at the first landing place, then the second.

[Re] "Between those, Nee-sama."

Ram turned toward her.

Rem smiled with complete sincerity.

[Ra] "Ram had worked that out."

The third ring clipped the side of the nearest block and spun away into the grass.

Marin bit the inside of her cheek. Wakana looked down at his hands. Rem went to fetch the ring, bringing it back carefully as though nothing particularly interesting had happened.

[Ra] "Now Rem."

[Re] "Yes, Nee-sama."

* * *

Rem missed the first.

Her second went over the nearest block. The third went over it too, catching the first ring and making it bounce. She looked pleased, not astonished, and began collecting them before anyone had announced a result.

[Mr] "Two!"

[Ra] "She chose the large one."

[Re] "The rules allow that."

Marin sat down on the workshop step. She had been expecting an easy little distraction before lunch. It had acquired spectators, a score and a family dispute, all without anybody asking her.

Wakana took the rings from Rem.

He stood behind the line, looked at the targets and lifted one hand. Then he paused, moved his foot a little and looked again.

Marin knew this expression. She saw it whenever two things were nearly level and he could not bear the nearly.

[Mr] "You don't get to build a better game."

[W] "I wasn't."

[Mr] "Or measure it."

He lowered his eyes to the line. He had been measuring it with his foot.

Ram noticed.

[Ra] "His toe is over."

Wakana moved it back at once.

[W] "Sorry."

He threw.

The ring went over the middle block and settled on the ground. His second caught the same block. His third hit the top and bounced onto the first two, staying there at a ridiculous angle.

All four of them looked at it.

[Mr] "Does that count?"

[Ra] "No."

[Re] "It has gone over the wood."

[Ra] "It's resting on a previous success."

[Mr] "That isn't against the rule."

There was still only the one rule. Marin had been very proud of this when she made the game. She was beginning to see why people complicated them.

Wakana walked forward and lifted the top ring away from the other two. It dropped the remaining inch to the ground.

[W] "Two is fine."

Ram looked satisfied. Rem looked at her sister. Marin looked at Wakana for a little longer, then held out her hand for another turn.

* * *

The wind arrived while Marin was aiming.

Not much. Just enough to lift a small curl of leaf from the edge of the playing ground and move it past the targets. She waited for it to pass. Ram waited too, with the expression of someone who would remember if Marin blamed the weather afterward.

The ring went low, struck the nearest block and knocked it over.

Marin shut her eyes.

[Mr] "That one was leaning again."

[Ra] "It was standing when the ring arrived."

Rem turned away for a moment. Her shoulders moved.

Wakana set the block upright. He pressed the soil around its base with his thumb, checked it and stood well back.

Marin's next ring went over.

She spun round to find him watching, bright with triumph that was quite out of proportion to the task. He laughed, softly, and she caught hold of the front of his sleeve and pulled him toward the line for her last throw.

[Mr] "Stay there."

[W] "I'm in your way."

[Mr] "No, you're not."

She held the third ring, changed her mind about the furthest block and chose the nearest again. It landed cleanly.

Ram raised one eyebrow.

[Ra] "An abrupt decline in ambition."

[Mr] "Two points is two points."

Rem nodded.

[Re] "That's what Rem thought."

* * *

They should have stopped before Ram's next turn.

That was Marin's opinion afterward. At the time, stopping seemed unreasonable. Ram had been too occupied with the rules to remember that she had not yet scored, and she accepted the rings with the calm of someone who had been studying everyone else's mistakes.

She threw the first one lightly.

It went over the nearest block.

Rem clapped once before catching herself. Ram ignored the clap, but not quite quickly enough.

The second went over the middle block.

Marin looked at Wakana. Wakana looked at the ring, then at the last one in Ram's hand.

Ram took her time.

[Ra] "The line," she said.

[Mr] "You're behind it."

[Ra] "Ram knows."

She was pointing at the scuff from Wakana's shoe, half an inch beyond the edge. She waited while Marin cleared it with the side of her foot.

Then she threw.

The ring fell exactly over the furthest block.

There was a small silence.

Ram held out both empty hands.

[Ra] "Better."

Rem was smiling so widely that Marin could not resent the result. She had been ready to resent it very much. Instead she found herself laughing while Ram began explaining that the nearest block ought to be worth less than the furthest one.

[Mr] "Now you want points?"

[Ra] "Ram wants accurate points."

Wakana bent to retrieve the rings. Marin held out her hand for them, assuming he would want to go back in now that Ram had supplied them with a conclusion.

He kept one.

[W] "Can I try again?"

Marin looked at him, then stepped away from the line.

He aimed at the furthest block. The ring brushed its edge and fell flat on the ground beside it. He looked at the place where it had landed, then at the ring still in his other hand.

Ram folded her arms.

[Ra] "Ambitious."

[W] "A little too far left."

[Mr] "Don't measure it."

He smiled at her without lowering his hand and threw the second. It landed short. Rem collected it, brought it back and stood beside the line, looking at the target with her head tilted.

[Re] "Rem would like another go as well."

Marin gave her the third ring. By then Ram had moved close enough to offer advice which nobody had requested.

* * *

They went in when somebody called that the bowls were on the table.

Rem picked up her basket. She had meant to take it inside before stopping, and she looked into it as though checking that the delay had not altered the contents. Then she took her sister's free hand and went toward the door with her.

Marin stayed to collect the rings while Wakana restored the useful scraps to their stack.

[Mr] "We should keep those five."

[W] "For the game?"

[Mr] "For Ram's rematch. She'll want one."

[W] "She's winning."

[Mr] "Exactly."

He considered this, then set the five offcuts together where they would not be mistaken for anything urgently needed. Marin slipped the loops over her arm, reclaimed his pencil from beside the door and held it out to him.

He looked surprised.

[Mr] "You can have it back. After lunch."

She tucked it behind her own ear and took his hand before he could point out that he needed the other one to close the workshop door.
