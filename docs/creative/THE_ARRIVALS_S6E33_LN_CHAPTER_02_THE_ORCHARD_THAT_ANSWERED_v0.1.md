# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 48 — The Orchard That Answered

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 48
**Source episode:** S6E33
**Continuity state:** Day456, afternoon. Mau, Yuka, Fern and Stark explore the normal E-rank weekly gate that reset on Day455, a different place from the previous week's ditch and lower bridge. Rimuru remains home. Dogs scout; the return stays available. Limited catalogue access is confirmed by the current gate's notice without assuming universal access, cheap healing or interchangeable currencies. A small orchard encounter combines the capped shared Limitless pull with Stark's axe and Fern's Zoltraak; Mau closes the loan when its pressure builds. The party brings samples and a route sketch home in the same chapter. No stat points are spent;57 remain held. No public romance, new shadow, big boss, Red Gate or quantified level jump occurs.

*When: Day456, afternoon. The new week's frame. Stark has the bag. Fern has the map board. Yuka has taken the old page out of it twice.*

[Fe] "It won't be the bridge."

[Yk] "I know."

[Fe] "You keep looking at the bridge."

[Yk] "I liked the lower crossing."

Fern took the page, folded it carefully and gave it back to her instead of putting it on the board.

[Fe] "Then keep it. This is a different place."

The frame had changed yesterday. Anko had written Day455 beside the new description, then beneath it, in letters large enough to read from the back step: *OLD MAP IS OLD.*

Mau looked at the words while Stark tightened the straps on the bag.

[M] "That's aimed at somebody."

[St] "I'm not asking who."

Rimuru stood beside the return page with his hands in his pockets. He had already checked their names and the expected time.

[R] "I'll be here. Or inside. Somewhere a person can find me without going through another world."

[M] "All right."

The four of them went through.

* * *

There were trees on steps.

That was the first thing Mau thought. Wide terraces, one above another, covered in rows of low trees with copper leaves and pale crooked trunks. Old stone steps climbed between them. A warm wind moved down the rows toward the frame and brought a dry rattling noise with it.

Not rain. Not voices.

The leaves knocking together.

Fern turned and looked at the way home before taking the first path. Grey-white. Open. She asked Stark to stand a pace to the side so nobody would block it while she drew.

Yuka called the dogs.

White and black, clean-edged, rising on either side of her. The black one looked up at her before looking toward the trees, and she put her hand briefly between its ears.

[Yk] "Just ahead. Come back if it changes."

They went up the first terrace, separate enough to look at different things. Mau waited with her until they stopped and turned toward the same tree.

Something pale hung from a low branch.

[St] "Fruit?"

[M] "You don't have to sound so hopeful."

[St] "I didn't know there was a rule."

Fern finished the first square on the board.

[Fe] "There usually is, when someone says that."

* * *

The fruit was hollow.

Stark did not find that out by eating it. One had fallen beside the path, split open. He turned it with the end of a stick and found a dry empty chamber inside, ribbed like the underside of a mushroom.

The next gust passed through the split shell.

It made a small, clear note.

Yuka crouched beside him.

[Yk] "Do it again."

[St] "I didn't do it."

She waited. The wind came. A lower note from somewhere above them answered the first, then another from farther up the slope.

The whole orchard was making the sound. Not every branch at once. Different ones as the wind found them, a loose uneven music moving through the terraces.

Mau looked up between the copper leaves.

[M] "That's quite good."

Fern had stopped drawing.

She listened for another moment before putting a mark beside the first row. Then she looked at the dogs, which had gone still again.

[Fe] "Don't stand under that tree."

* * *

One of the trunks was walking.

Slowly enough that Mau had missed it while he listened. The pale shape at the far end of the row had lifted its roots out of the ground, folded them beneath itself and moved toward the path. The copper canopy stayed almost level over it.

Then the canopy shook off.

Leaves went everywhere. Under them was a low, thick body plated with bark, four jointed legs and a split in the front wide enough to take Stark's arm.

The black dog barked once.

Two more shapes lifted under the next row of trees.

[Fe] "Three. Move out of the row."

Stark was already moving. He took the side of the path with the low stone wall behind it, where the first thing could not circle behind him without climbing. Yuka called the dogs back to her feet.

The first creature charged.

Stark met it with the flat of the axe angled across its head. Bark struck metal. His boots slid a little in the dry soil, stopped, and he turned the head aside rather than trying to keep every bit of its weight in front of him.

[St] "Not a tree."

[M] "I noticed."

[St] "Just checking."

* * *

Fern's first shot took the second creature through the shoulder.

It had come round the tree while the first was on Stark. The pale line went past Mau's right side through the clear gap she had waited for, and the bark body twisted under the impact, one leg folding.

Not down. Not yet.

Yuka had reached the wider part of the path. Her rope was already free of the pack.

[Yk] "The little pull?"

Mau looked at the line between them. The third creature was moving behind the damaged second one, hidden each time the branches shook.

[M] "Briefly. Same as before."

[C] *Keep it there. Two small outlets, not a larger one because there are three targets.*

He opened the familiar route. Yuka felt it take and lifted her hand, then waited for his.

The first knot went beyond the damaged leg. The second, hers, lower and nearer the wall. The creature's next step landed badly, pulled toward two nearby points instead of toward Mau.

Its bark plates separated along the turn.

[M] "Stark."

Stark did not need a longer instruction. He let the first creature slide past the axe, stepped inside the second's reaching branch and brought the blade through the opening the turn had made.

The damaged body folded into the soil.

Mau and Yuka let the pulls go.

* * *

The first one came back faster.

It had found the end of the wall. It came over the stone with its front half lifted, the split head opening as it fell toward Stark's back.

Fern's barrier caught the drop.

Stark turned under it. Saw where she was holding it and moved his feet instead of putting his axe through her spell. The pale shield gave him a moment to clear the landing. She let it go as the weight shifted past him, and the bark thing struck the ground sideways.

[Fe] "Now."

He cut across the exposed base.

Mau was looking for the third.

Not toward the trees. Toward the place where the dog was staring, down through the gap between two roots. He saw movement beneath the dry leaves before the pale head came up.

[M] "Below. Left."

Yuka jumped back, hands free, as the thing struck where her boot had been.

She put the little pull to its right.

Mau made his beside it, lower. For one breath they held the same turn, and the head came away from her toward the open path.

Fern had a clear line.

The Zoltraak went through the lifted plates. The creature struck the terrace wall and fell in a tangle of thin roots.

[C] *Close it now.*

Mau closed it.

Yuka lowered her hand, saw his face and did not ask for another.

* * *

They listened.

The wind went down the orchard. A hollow fruit sounded somewhere uphill. Another answered from below. Nothing lifted under the sound.

Stark stayed at the front of the path while Fern checked the fallen bodies without reaching into them. Yuka sent the white dog a short way along the next row. The black one stayed by her knee, watching the places where roots met soil.

Mau put his hand against the wall.

The pressure behind his eyes had stopped growing as soon as the loan closed. It was less than after the long test at the trestles. Still there. Something worth saying instead of waiting until another person saw it first.

[M] "I'm resting that route for a bit."

Fern looked back.

[Fe] "Do you need to go out?"

[M] "Not yet. I want to see if it settles while we walk. Not while I try something else."

She watched him a moment, then nodded.

[Fe] "Tell us if it doesn't."

He said he would.

Stark turned his axe over. A strip of bark was caught near the head. He pulled it loose, inspected the edge and put it aside for cleaning when they were clear.

[St] "Next time, I'd like the tree to stay a tree."

[Yk] "You were hoping it was a pear."

[St] "I can hope for two things."

* * *

The next terrace had a stone shelter at one end.

No door. A roof on four posts, with an old trough beneath it and a wide enough view of the path that they could see what approached. The dogs inspected it before Fern put her board down.

They stopped there to drink.

Mau checked the selected inventory field while Yuka unrolled their food cloth. The notice for the current gate confirmed the same limited catalogue access they had first found at the lower bridge. Rope. Light. Preserved food. The small bottles still had prices he was not going to mistake for a casual afternoon purchase.

[M] "It's still there. This gate too."

Fern read the limited-access line when he offered it through the bracelet.

[Fe] "Accepted stock?"

[M] "The listed dungeon crystals. Not everything in the bag."

[Fe] "Then that goes in the report. We don't need to buy lunch twice."

He closed it. Yuka passed him the food they had brought.

Stark had already taken his portion to the edge of the shelter. Fern looked at the place he had chosen, where he could see the next terrace while sitting down, and moved the water flask to the shade beside him.

[St] "Thanks."

[Fe] "You were going to put it in the sun."

[St] "I was going to hold it."

[Fe] "With your sandwich?"

He looked at the things in his hands.

She sat beside him without adding anything else.

* * *

They did not take the shelter's roof apart for materials.

They took one fallen fruit shell, already split, and three small crystals from the creatures on the path. Fern drew the shelter, the rows and the places where the trunks had moved. Yuka added the root gap the third one had used.

Mau's eyes had eased while they rested. He told them before they stood.

[M] "No more loan today, though."

[St] "We've got these."

He lifted the axe a little, indicating it and Fern's staff rather than making a boast. Mau smiled.

[M] "I noticed."

They went back by the open side of the terraces. The dogs found nothing else moving except leaves, and once the white one stopped to investigate a hollow shell until the wind blew through it and made it jump.

Yuka laughed. Called it gently, and the dog came back pretending nothing had happened.

The frame appeared below them between the low rows. Right colour. Open.

Fern checked the four names on the page before they crossed.

* * *

Rimuru was inside when they came out.

Anko was at the board. She looked at the clock, then at the party, then at the split shell Stark was carrying in one hand like an oddly shaped bowl.

[A] "Useful?"

[St] "It sings."

[A] "That's not an answer."

[Fe] "It's the answer we have."

Anko considered this, then cleared a space beside yesterday's entry. Fern put down the route sketch. Mau gave the report on the shared pulls and the resting interval; Yuka showed the place the dog had found, making the gap wide enough on the drawing that nobody would step over it without looking.

Rimuru came out before they had finished.

[R] "All normal?"

[M] "Normal. Not the bridge."

[Yk] "Definitely not pears."

Stark held up the shell. A little breeze came round the kitchen corner, caught the split at the right angle and made it give one soft, clear note.

Everyone stopped talking.

Anko looked at the empty space on her page, then wrote *singing orchard* above Fern's drawing.

From somewhere inside the house, a chair scraped backward.

[B] "Could you do that again?"

Stark looked helplessly at the air.
