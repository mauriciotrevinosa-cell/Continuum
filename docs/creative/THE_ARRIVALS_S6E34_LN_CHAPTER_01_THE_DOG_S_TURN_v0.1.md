# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 49 — The Dog's Turn

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 49
**Source episode:** S6E34
**Continuity state:** Day457 morning. Ori, Milim and Umaru invent a scent game with Ear at the Fort; Mau joins willingly. Ordinary warmth does not restore his paternal autobiographical recognition. Ori's accumulated hurt remains quiet here, no public break or revealed romance. No new voice, ability or origin is assigned to Ear. A game ribbon is not the Night/Sleep ribbon and has no magic.

*When: Day457, after breakfast. The Fort's curtain is pinned back. Ear has already found the answer, but the humans are still preparing the game.*

Milim looked behind the cushion.

Then under it.

Then behind Ori, who moved out of the way so that she could stop pretending not to look there.

[Mi] "You moved it."

[O] "I didn't."

[Mi] "Then it's invisible."

[U] "Things don't become invisible because you're bad at finding them."

Milim turned toward the voice behind the little curtain.

[Mi] "They might."

Umaru put her hand over her mouth. It was the third time she had done it, and Milim had begun to suspect she was eating something without sharing.

Ear sat beside Ori. His nose moved once.

Ori pressed a finger to her lips.

The dog looked at her, then at Milim, and did nothing useful for the people who were trying to prolong the game.

Milim went back to the first cushion.

There were only four cushions.

She had checked them all twice.

[Mi] "There should be a rule about this."

[U] "There is. You have to find it."

They had started with a strip of ribbon, a knot in the end and an idea that had become much more complicated after Umaru arrived. Ori had wanted to hide it and see whether Ear could find it. Milim had insisted she could find anything a dog could find.

Umaru had asked whether she was allowed to help hide it.

That had been the first mistake.

The second had been agreeing that Milim would close her eyes instead of leaving the room.

The ribbon hung down the back of Milim's collar.

Ori could see the little red end whenever she turned her head.

She had been trying not to laugh for so long that her ribs hurt.

* * *

Mau stopped outside because he could hear someone saying that cushions ought to be searched in a different order.

He looked through the raised edge of the curtain.

Milim was on her knees in the middle of the Fort, accusing the floor.

Ori saw him first.

Her face changed before she said anything, and he felt, briefly, that he had walked into the middle of a surprise meant for him.

[O] "Come here."

He put his cup down on the table outside and crouched to enter.

[M] "What happened to the floor?"

[Mi] "It stole something."

He looked at Umaru. She was sitting with her knees drawn up, pressed against the wall, her whole face red from the effort of not making a noise.

He saw the ribbon.

Ori put a hand on his sleeve.

He looked down at it, then at her.

She shook her head with urgent, shining seriousness.

He sat.

[M] "Have you checked the ceiling?"

Umaru made a terrible sound.

Milim looked up.

Ori folded over against Mau's arm and laughed into his sleeve.

He began laughing too. He had not meant to. It was the combination of Milim's suspicion, Umaru making herself smaller and the dog's absolute certainty that he was the only sensible person in the room.

Milim looked back down at all three of them.

Very slowly, she put a hand behind her neck.

She pulled the ribbon free.

The Fort became too small for the noise.

[Mi] "That wasn't hidden!"

[U] "You couldn't see it."

[Mi] "It was on me!"

[M] "Advanced hiding."

Milim pointed at him.

[Mi] "Your turn."

* * *

He thought they were going to make him find the ribbon.

That was his mistake.

Umaru explained that he would hide it and Ear would find it. Milim would hide it next. They would take turns until somebody made a hiding place the dog could not solve.

[M] "Is Ear allowed to vote on this?"

The dog stood up.

[O] "He likes it."

Ear put his nose against Mau's wrist.

Mau scratched the place behind his ear and watched one back foot start to move.

[M] "You're supposed to be working."

Ori smiled.

She had heard that tone before. Not often lately. It was the voice he used when he already knew he had lost an argument to something smaller than himself.

She sat back to give him room. Her knee touched his. He did not move away.

Umaru held a hand over Ear's eyes while Milim loudly explained that the dog would cheat if he could see.

Ear stepped around the hand.

[U] "Fine. A witness has refused the procedure."

Mau looked at the ribbon, at the cushions and at the bottom of the raised curtain.

He hid it beneath the folded corner of a blanket.

Ear walked straight to it.

[Mi] "Too easy."

[M] "I was learning the rules."

[Mi] "The rule is hide it better."

Her attempt involved folding the ribbon inside one cushion, putting another cushion on top, then sitting on both with the expression of a person who had invented secrecy.

Ear put his nose against her knee.

She looked away.

He put a paw on the cushion.

She looked farther away.

He barked.

[Mi] "You don't know anything."

Ear barked again.

Ori rescued the cushion before Milim could claim she had simply wanted to sit there all along.

* * *

By the fourth round, Umaru had declared that hiding something on someone else without telling them was disallowed, which Milim considered too late.

By the sixth, Mau was lying on his side with his head propped on his good hand, watching Ori try to distract the dog by moving the wrong blanket.

She was very bad at it.

Every time Ear went near the right corner, her eyes followed him.

[M] "Don't look at it."

She looked at him instead.

[O] "I'm not."

Ear put his muzzle under her elbow.

She laughed and gave him the ribbon.

[Mi] "You helped him!"

[O] "He's a dog."

[Mi] "That's why he should do his own work."

Umaru moved the cushions into a low wall. Someone coming past the curtain asked whether a seat had gone missing from the common room. All four people inside said no at slightly different times.

There was a pause outside.

They put one cushion back.

Mau's cup had gone cold. He remembered it when his throat began to ache from laughing and went to get it anyway. Ori moved aside for him, then put her hand on the curtain to keep it clear of his head when he came back.

He thanked her.

She wanted to say that he did not have to.

She let the curtain go instead.

* * *

Umaru said they needed a harder version.

Milim said she was ready for it.

Mau said that, historically, those two things being said together had rarely improved a room.

They all looked at him.

He had said it without thinking.

For a moment he searched the words for an occasion he could put behind them. He knew that there had been several. He could imagine Umaru standing with her hands on her hips and Milim already airborne. It felt plausible, almost easy.

He could not put himself inside any particular one.

Ori had stopped moving.

Mau looked back at the ribbon.

[M] "Two places?"

[U] "Two knots," Umaru said, a beat too quickly. "He has to find both."

She found another scrap in the little collection of string and paper that made up so much of the Fort's useful equipment. Ori took it from her.

It was yellow.

The two strips lay across her palm, small enough that she could close her hand around both.

[O] "I'll hide this one."

She wanted him to watch her choose somewhere. Then to know, without having to be told, where she would have put it. A stupid, tiny wish. She could already feel herself getting angry with it.

Mau watched as she tucked the yellow knot behind the lowest fold of the curtain.

He was not the one finding it. Ear was.

She had forgotten the rules of her own game for a moment.

Milim hid the red one beneath Mau's ankle. He raised his eyebrows at her.

[Mi] "You didn't say no."

[M] "I've been here the whole time."

[Mi] "Then you should have noticed."

Ori laughed before she could stop herself.

Ear found both ribbons in less time than it had taken anyone to hide them.

* * *

There was no prize.

Milim complained about that when they finally stopped. Umaru said it was a research game. Milim said research should have prizes.

Mau got up carefully. His left fingers had gone stiff where he had rested them against his knee; he opened what would open, then stopped trying to force the others. Ori collected the ribbon pieces before anyone could tread on them.

[M] "Do you want help putting it back?"

[O] "We're going to play again."

[M] "Then I'll stop moving the scenery."

He nudged one cushion back into place with his foot.

Ori looked up at him.

[O] "You can come next time."

He smiled.

[M] "I'd like that."

She had wanted *of course*. She had wanted him to say something about his place already being there, or to complain that the Fort had begun charging its own family admission.

He had laughed with them. He had stayed. When she reached up, he let her hug him, and his hand settled between her shoulders with an easy kindness that made her close her eyes.

It was a good morning.

That was what she would have to tell herself later when the rest of it hurt.

Ear pushed between their knees. Mau laughed and let go to scratch his head one more time.

Then Milim put the red ribbon on Ori's shoulder and announced that she had thought of a better rule.

Ori caught the end before it fell.

[O] "I'm not closing my eyes."
