# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 50 — A Small Bundle

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 50
**Source episode:** S6E34
**Continuity state:** Day457 afternoon. Frieren asks Fern for their own shared time. They study and make bounded tests of the existing grimoire for keeping rain off a small bundle, from S5E10–11; no people-sized protection or combat upgrade. Fern's analytical contribution receives specific praise and affection. Household accounting coverage already exists; no new delegation, sister-role, restoration, romance knowledge or power percentage is introduced. Stable speaker marks apply.

*When: Day457, afternoon. The sheltered side of the yard, well inside the lime. Fern has brought the grimoire. Frieren has brought a small cloth bundle, a cup of water and a second book which Fern has not yet agreed they will have time for.*

[Fe] "That isn't small."

Frieren looked at the bundle.

[F] "It isn't large."

[Fe] "It's almost the size of your bag."

[F] "My bag is small."

Fern put the grimoire on the bench and waited.

Frieren untied the bundle. Inside were three books, a folded cloth and a stone which had no clear purpose until she explained that it kept the books flat. Fern took out two books and the stone. Then she folded the cloth around the remaining volume and tied it herself.

Frieren watched her knot.

[F] "I could have done that."

[Fe] "You could have done it before I arrived."

[F] "Then you would have missed the beginning."

Fern turned toward her, prepared to find that this was another excuse. Frieren was smiling a little. Not much. Enough that Fern could see she knew exactly how it sounded.

She sat beside her and opened the grimoire at the third diagram.

* * *

Frieren had come to find her after lunch.

Not with a question about the stores or a reminder that Ram's appointment would need fitting into the week. She had stood by the table while Fern finished a conversation and then asked whether she would come outside.

Fern had thought she meant walking. She had agreed before seeing the book under her arm.

[Fe] "Is this work?" she had asked.

[F] "Only if you don't want to."

Fern had taken the book from her.

She wanted to. That was the part it had taken the household a little time to stop overlooking. Being asked to spend an afternoon with a grimoire did not necessarily mean someone had failed to arrange leisure for her. Sometimes it meant Frieren knew what she liked.

Now she put one finger beside the small note beneath the drawing.

[Fe] "This part. You make the outer boundary first."

[F] "The author does."

[Fe] "And then makes room inside it."

[F] "Yes."

[Fe] "Why?"

Frieren leaned closer to look at the page. A loose strand of pale hair fell between them. Fern caught it before it reached the ink and put it back over Frieren's shoulder.

[F] "A habit, possibly. It makes the next part easier to describe."

[Fe] "It makes the next part harder to do."

Frieren looked at her, interested now.

Fern turned back a page and showed her where the same instruction began. By the end of the paragraph Frieren had put down her cup, and the extra book lay forgotten beside the stone.

* * *

The spell kept rain off a small bundle.

Not off its owner. Not off everything near it. The author had repeated the distinction with an irritation Fern appreciated more each time she read it. It suggested somebody had spent a long time being asked the wrong question.

They had seen what it did before. Today Fern wanted to understand why the recommended order was so awkward when the finished work was simple.

Frieren laid the wrapped book on the dry end of the bench and used the familiar spell in the author's suggested order, keeping the effect close to the cloth. Fern watched the little boundary settle around the folded edges.

[F] "The cup."

Fern held it over the bundle. A little water, then a little more. The drops ran away from the cloth and scattered harmlessly across the bare patch beside the bench.

Frieren looked satisfied.

Fern moved the cup sideways and poured over the knot.

A thin line of water followed the loose end of the tie toward the folded cloth.

Frieren caught it with her fingertip before it could creep underneath.

[Fe] "There."

[F] "That isn't rain."

[Fe] "Rain can reach a knot."

[F] "Rain rarely stops to think about it first."

[Fe] "Then it should find this difficult to repeat."

Frieren looked from her face to the cup, then made a small, resigned sound and undid the spell.

* * *

They dried the end of the tie before trying again.

The book inside remained dry. Fern checked it herself. The point was not to sacrifice a volume to prove that an author had written a poor instruction, however tempting Frieren might find the principle of the argument.

This time Frieren placed the effect around the bundle after checking the shape of the folds. The result looked much the same from a few paces away. Close up, Fern could see where the earlier boundary had left an awkward place by the knot.

She explained what she meant with two fingers over the diagram.

[Fe] "You have to leave room for this before you decide where the edge goes. Otherwise you're making it fit a shape you've already finished."

Frieren did not answer immediately. She studied the drawing, then the bundle.

[F] "Show me the order you would use. Without casting it."

Fern did. At first she tried to explain the whole change in one sentence, lost which part she meant and stopped with her hand above the page.

Frieren waited.

Fern went back to the first instruction. One part at a time. The fold, the tie, the space left beside it. Frieren asked one question, which made her change the second part, then another which did not.

By the time she finished, there were three small marks on the scrap beside the book. No numbers. No accounts. Just the order she meant.

[Fe] "Like that."

Frieren looked at it for a long moment.

[F] "That's clearer."

Fern lowered her hand.

[Fe] "Than the author?"

[F] "Yes. You thought about the thing you were protecting before deciding how to protect it. He thought about the boundary he wanted to hold."

She touched the first mark, then the third.

[F] "You found the part that needed to come first."

Fern looked down at the scrap. She had been certain of her objection when she brought it outside. She had not been certain Frieren would find it interesting enough to stay with for this long.

Frieren was still looking at the order, not reaching for the other book.

* * *

The second test was better.

Fern poured over the middle, then near each folded edge. Frieren moved the little bundle when she asked, giving her the other side. Water ran away without making the cloth dark.

Then Fern tested the knot.

Frieren watched it almost as closely as she did.

Nothing crept under the fold.

Fern set the cup down.

[Fe] "Again?"

[F] "If you like."

They changed the fold, not the size. A different small parcel, the same book. Frieren used the spell again in the order they had agreed, and this time Fern could follow the work without hunting for the moment where it changed direction.

It was still only a little protection against water on a wrapped object. Fern had no intention of calling it anything larger. She liked the fact that they could see the difference with a cup instead of waiting for someone to be in trouble.

When the last test held, Frieren let the spell go and unwrapped the book.

Dry pages. A damp thumbprint on the outer cloth where Fern had picked it up too quickly. She set the cloth open on the bench to air.

[F] "You explain well."

Fern looked at her.

[Fe] "You taught me."

[F] "Not that part. You worked that out."

Fern found that she wanted to look at the scrap again. Instead she put it carefully between the right pages so it would not fall out on the way inside.

Frieren watched her do it with the comfortable attention she gave to things being placed where they belonged.

* * *

They stayed after the testing was finished.

The yard had settled into afternoon sounds. A bucket knocked against a doorframe. Someone called a question toward the workshop and received an answer too quiet for Fern to hear. The sun moved from the end of the bench onto the drying cloth.

Frieren opened the other book.

Fern looked at it.

[Fe] "You were waiting."

[F] "I finished your question first."

[Fe] "It was a good question."

[F] "It was."

There was no resistance in the answer. Fern leaned a little closer, pleased despite herself, and looked at the new page.

It had an illustration which was almost certainly a plant, although the leaves appeared to have been drawn by somebody who had received their description from another room.

[Fe] "What is that?"

[F] "That is what I wanted to ask you."

Fern studied it. Frieren waited with her finger below the caption, neither helpful nor apologetic. After a while Fern turned the book sideways.

[Fe] "It might be upside down."

[F] "The writing isn't."

[Fe] "The writing isn't shaped like that."

Frieren made a small sound. Fern looked at her and saw she was trying not to laugh.

It was difficult to keep an exacting expression after that.

They spent the next part of the afternoon disagreeing about which end of the drawing was supposed to be underground. Fern took the book onto her own knees. Frieren leaned against her shoulder to look, her hair cool against Fern's sleeve.

Neither moved away when they found the roots.

By the time the cloth had dried, the book was still between them, and the water in the cup was gone.

Fern picked up the cup and inspected the patch of wet ground beside the bench.

[Fe] "We should have put a plant there."

Frieren looked down at the illustration again.

[F] "Once we know which way up it goes."
