# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 51 — After the Touch

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 51
**Source episode:** S6E35
**Continuity state:** Day458, morning. Mau resumes wooden-sword practice with Yuta, developing the balanced turn learned in Left Foot into a controlled touch and exit. Mau invites Yuka to demonstrate the rope work and angle changes she knows well. Yuta enjoys teaching and learning with both without knowing their romance. No technique, cursed-tool test, new memory or physical cure occurs. Mau's floor-86 katana stays sheathed off the ground.57 points remain held; fourteen named shadows of forty and98 soldiers remain unchanged.

*When: Day458, morning. The yard. Yuta has drawn two short lines in the earth with the end of the wooden sword.*

[Yt] "Not the sleeve today."

Mau looked at the sleeve.

Yuta had already rolled it up.

[M] "I was getting good at the sleeve."

[Yt] "I noticed."

He put the point of his wooden sword on the farther line. The space between the marks was less than a full step. He stood beyond both, leaving Mau room to come in without finding a foot where he meant to put his own.

[Yt] "Touch the guard. Then get out past the second mark without turning your back."

[M] "Which way?"

[Yt] "You choose."

[M] "I don't trust that."

Yuta smiled and lifted the sword.

Mau's real blade rested in its black scabbard beside his coat on the bench. Nobody had asked him to draw it. The weight in his hands was wood, with an old dent near the point and a familiar patch worn smooth under the grip.

He went in.

* * *

The first part worked.

He turned round Yuta's blade without leaving the rear foot where he had started. The point touched the wooden guard. He felt the contact, clean and brief, and almost stopped there to be pleased about it.

Yuta moved.

Mau stepped back. The other sword followed him, slowly enough that he could see the line coming and not quickly enough that he had a useful excuse when it touched his shirt.

[M] "You said out."

[Yt] "I did."

[M] "I was going out."

[Yt] "Right along the place I was going."

Mau looked at the two marks.

Then at the boots that had gone straight back between them.

[M] "I see."

Yuta lowered the point. Waited while Mau put himself back where he had begun.

The second time Mau went left. Too much left. He cleared the line of the sword but had to turn his whole body back before he could have done anything useful.

Yuta did not touch him that time.

He waited.

Mau finished turning and looked at him.

[M] "You could have."

[Yt] "Yes."

[M] "That's worse."

* * *

They made the movement smaller.

Not all at once. Yuta showed the angle by standing where Mau had stood, letting Mau hold the line of the returning sword while he stepped aside from it. Only enough. His shoulders still faced the person he meant to watch. His feet had not crossed.

Mau tried it with no cut first.

Then the turn, the touch and the little exit, separate enough to feel each part. On the third attempt the end stopped feeling like a jump away from something dangerous. It became a place he could stand.

[Yt] "There. Stay."

He stayed. Let the left hand loosen instead of trying to make the stiff fingers close farther round the wood.

Yuta lifted his own point a little.

Mau did not have to throw himself away from it. He moved the blade, kept the angle and watched the shoulder behind the returning hand.

[M] "That feels less stupid."

[Yt] "Useful test."

[M] "You can write it in the book."

[Yt] "I don't have a book."

[M] "Umaru can fix that."

Yuta looked toward the kitchen door, where Umaru's voice could already be heard telling somebody that carrying the paper did not make it theirs.

[Yt] "I'm all right."

* * *

Yuka came out carrying a piece of rope.

Not because she knew what they were practising. She had been asked to take it to the workshop. The damaged part had been marked with thread, and she was checking the length beyond the mark as she walked, testing each little frayed place between her fingers.

Mau lowered his sword when he saw her.

[M] "Can you show us something?"

She stopped beside the step.

[Yk] "Depends."

[M] "The turn you did on the stair. When you pulled the rope back without stepping into it. I keep trying to do the end with my shoulders."

Yuta looked at her hands.

[Yt] "The one in your drawing?"

She nodded.

[Yt] "I'd like to see it too."

Yuka glanced from one to the other, then down at the worn rope.

[Yk] "Not with this bit. It'll give at the wrong time and you'll both think I'm clever."

She put it on the bench beside Mau's coat and went to get the sound practice length.

When she came back she had taken her coat off and tied her hair out of her face. Her expression had changed. Mau knew that expression. It was the one she wore when she had something to show that was hers.

* * *

She used the post by the woodpile.

Not either of their wrists. She looped the rope loosely round the smooth part, stood back far enough to take the slack and put her feet where she wanted them before moving her hands.

[Yk] "It's not this."

She pulled directly away from the post. The rope tightened. Her shoulders came forward and her heels dug into the ground.

[Yk] "If it's bigger than me, I lose. If it's moving, I go with it."

[M] "You did go with it. Once."

[Yk] "I'm showing the part where I learned not to."

Yuta hid a smile by looking at the knot.

Yuka reset. This time she stepped across the pull instead of backing away from it, keeping one hand lower and letting the other guide the returning length.

The rope came free of the post. Passed close by her hip. Gathered without turning into a loop around her feet.

She did it again, slower, when Yuta asked.

[Yt] "You know where the end is before it comes back."

[Yk] "I have to. Or it hits me."

[M] "Can confirm."

She pointed at him with the end of the rope.

[Yk] "You were in the wrong place."

[M] "You hadn't decided where the wrong place was yet."

* * *

Yuta tried the rope.

That was the part Mau had not expected. He had thought Yuta would watch, find the useful bit and turn it into a sentence about the sword. Instead Yuta put the wooden blade down, took the rope when Yuka offered it and asked her where she wanted him to begin.

She moved his hand lower.

[Yk] "There. Not like the grip. You're guiding it."

He tried.

The loop came back perfectly. The tail hit his ankle.

Yuka pressed her lips together.

Mau looked away into the woodpile.

[Yt] "That bit's harder than it looks."

[Yk] "A little."

[M] "You can laugh. He hit me six times before you came out."

[Yt] "Five."

[M] "One of them was emotional."

Yuka laughed then. Yuta shook his head and did the turn again, giving the tail more room this time. It passed his ankle and went where she had shown him.

She nodded, pleased.

Not because he had failed. Because he had listened.

* * *

They put it together without using the rope on a person.

Yuka took the far post. Yuta gave her a line to work around with his wooden sword, keeping its point well away from her hands. Mau watched the first two turns, then stood where she had stood and tried the same exit with his blade instead of the returning rope.

He went too far again.

[Yk] "You're leaving before you've finished looking."

He stopped.

[M] "At him?"

[Yk] "At what he can still reach. You look at the place you want your foot to go, and then you go there, and you stop watching the thing trying to catch you."

Yuta nodded once.

[Yt] "That's what I was waiting for."

Mau turned the sword in his hands. The grip had started to feel narrow under the parts of his left hand that could hold it easily.

He put it down for a minute and rubbed his palm with his thumb.

Yuka waited. Yuta got the water.

* * *

The next run was better.

He kept his attention on Yuta's shoulder as he touched the guard. The returning blade began. Mau left its line without giving it the whole of his back, and when Yuta turned his wrist to follow, Mau could see where the point would be before he chose the next step.

It touched the edge of his shirt anyway.

[M] "Almost."

[Yt] "Almost."

[Yk] "His left foot was better."

[Yt] "It was."

Mau looked down at the foot.

[M] "I'd like that noted."

[Yk] "I'll tell the Ministry."

[Yt] "Don't."

They laughed. Tried once more, then once each with Yuka doing the turn and Mau holding the slow returning line. She had to make herself stop looking for where the rope would go, because she was holding wood, not rope, and the first exit brought her too close to the point.

Yuta moved it aside.

[Yt] "There. Different tool. Same question."

She went back to the beginning, frowning at it until she could try without hurrying.

* * *

The morning had moved round the yard by the time they stopped.

The lines in the earth were no longer two lines. There were boot marks across both of them, a groove where the rope had dragged and a much smaller mark where Yuta had drawn the angle for Mau with the point of the sword.

Yuka stood over it, eating the piece of bread Yuta had found in the covered bowl by the door.

[Yk] "We should put that on the page. The bit afterward."

[M] "The exit?"

She nodded.

[Yt] "You can draw it. I'll check it."

She looked at him. Then at the rope on the bench, the damaged length she still had to take to the workshop.

[Yk] "After I do that. Or somebody's going to come looking."

She took both lengths, keeping them separate. Mau picked up the wooden swords and laid them beside the wall where they belonged. His real blade stayed sheathed when he lifted it with his coat.

Yuta crouched to smooth the rope-groove and scuff the practice marks out of the path.

Mau watched him do it, then pointed.

[M] "You missed one."

Yuta looked down.

The small mark nearest his left foot.

He rubbed that one out too.
