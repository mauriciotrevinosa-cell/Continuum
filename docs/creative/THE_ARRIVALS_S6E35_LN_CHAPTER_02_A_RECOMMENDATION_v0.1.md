# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 52 — A Recommendation

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 52
**Source episode:** S6E35
**Continuity state:** Day458 afternoon. Mau chooses ordinary tea with Daphne, Echidna and Minerva in the long room, following Daphne's invitation. This is an informal visit, not a moved weekly tea. They argue over a book recommendation and enjoy their own interests; no memory inspection, cure, supernatural reading or private romantic disclosure. Minerva's hands remain bandaged. Speaker marks apply.

*When: Day458, afternoon. A corner of the long table. Rem's teapot, four cups and a plate which Daphne has moved closer to her chair by degrees.*

Mau put the plate back in the middle.

Daphne watched it travel across the table.

[Da] "You can reach it from there."

[M] "So can you."

[Da] "Yes."

She took another piece of dried apple. Mau sat down opposite her, and Minerva pushed a cup toward him with the heel of her bandaged hand.

[Mn] "It's still hot."

[M] "Thank you."

Echidna looked up from her book. She had been reading with one finger resting beneath a line, though Mau had the impression she was keeping the place for someone else rather than for herself.

[Ech] "Have you come with a question?"

[M] "No."

[Ech] "Oh."

He took hold of the warm cup and considered the disappointment in that small word.

[M] "Should I have?"

[Ech] "Certainly not."

Minerva looked at her.

Echidna closed the book.

[Ech] "It would have been welcome. That is all."

Mau found himself smiling. This was not the clinic, and nobody had cleared a room for him. At the other end of the long table, two people were comparing something written on separate sheets. From the yard came the sound of someone being told not to put a bucket there.

He had come because Daphne had said there would be tea. It had seemed a good enough reason while he was walking over, and it still did now that he was sitting down.

* * *

Echidna asked whether he had been reading.

[M] "A little."

[Ech] "What?"

He described the pages he had tried, not particularly well. Some had been practical. One had concerned a journey whose author appeared to have disliked every village he reached. Mau had stopped when the man spent half a page being offended by the arrangement of a stable.

Minerva made a small sound of agreement.

[Mn] "I know that book."

Echidna turned toward her at once.

[Ech] "Do you?"

[Mn] "You read me the stable."

[Ech] "It was an instructive passage."

[Mn] "It was a very long stable."

Daphne reached for the plate again, then looked at Mau and took only the piece nearest her.

[Da] "Was there food in the village?"

[M] "He didn't say."

[Da] "Then it isn't a good book."

Echidna tilted her head, giving this judgement more consideration than Mau had expected.

[Ech] "It may be a very good book about a person who should not travel."

[M] "That's the first thing anyone's said that makes me want to finish it."

She looked pleased.

Minerva shook her head and reached for the pot. Before Mau could offer, she had poured her own cup. She was careful with the handle. He kept his hands where they were.

* * *

The recommendation began innocently.

Mau asked for something to read which did not require another book beside it to explain the first one.

Echidna leaned forward.

[Ech] "What sort of thing?"

[M] "That's what I don't know. Something you like."

For a moment she said nothing. Daphne looked across the table at Minerva, who put her cup down and settled back, as though preparing for weather.

[Ech] "There are several considerations."

[Mn] "One book."

[Ech] "I understood."

[Mn] "Then choose one."

Echidna rested both hands on the closed volume. Mau could see her trying to decide where to begin, which was apparently not the same thing as deciding what to recommend.

[Ech] "There is a collection of accounts which contradict one another in a particularly revealing way."

[M] "Do I need to know what they're contradicting?"

[Ech] "Not initially."

[Mn] "That means yes."

[Ech] "It means the reader acquires the necessary knowledge as they progress."

[Da] "Is there food?"

Echidna looked at her.

[Ech] "At a banquet, briefly."

[Da] "Does anyone eat it?"

[Ech] "That is the subject of the disagreement."

Daphne held out her hand.

* * *

Echidna would not fetch it immediately.

That was surprising. Mau had expected her to produce the collection from somewhere within reach. Instead she began explaining another possibility, with her finger on the table where the book would go if she brought it.

Minerva waited through the first description.

At the second, she took a piece of apple.

By the third, she had moved the plate herself.

[M] "Can I change the question?" Mau asked.

Echidna stopped at once.

[Ech] "Of course."

[M] "Which one did you enjoy? Not which one would be best for me. Which one did you keep wanting to go back to?"

Minerva looked at him over her cup. Then she looked at Echidna.

The answer took a little longer this time.

[Ech] "The one I was reading when you came."

Mau looked at the book beneath her hands.

[M] "That one."

[Ech] "Yes."

[Mn] "You could have started there."

[Ech] "He asked a different question."

[Mn] "He asked for a book you liked."

Echidna turned the volume round so Mau could read the first page. She did not push it into his hands. He leaned over instead, keeping his cup well away from the paper.

It was a story. A visitor had arrived for dinner and refused the cushion because it would crease his trousers. His host offered him a bench. By the bottom of the page, they were arguing about whether the bench was too close to the table.

Mau read to the end of the paragraph.

Then he looked up.

[M] "Does it become more sensible?"

[Ech] "No."

He laughed.

* * *

Minerva had opinions about the chair.

She did not need to read the page to acquire them. Echidna read the next exchange aloud, with a maddeningly calm voice for both people in the argument, and Minerva objected halfway through.

[Mn] "There's a bench. He can sit on the bench."

[Ech] "He dislikes the bench."

[Mn] "Then he can stand."

[M] "They've been standing for three pages."

[Da] "Bring the food to where they're standing."

Echidna lifted one finger, asking to finish the sentence. Daphne considered the request, took the last piece of apple and allowed it.

The sentence made the situation worse. Mau could hear it coming and still found himself annoyed when the visitor refused the other chair on the grounds that it belonged on the far side of the room.

Minerva planted both palms on her knees.

[Mn] "Pick it up."

[M] "He won't."

[Mn] "I know he won't. I'm telling him to."

Echidna looked up from the page with a small smile. It was not her usual expression when she had found something she wanted to understand. She knew this story already. She was waiting to hear what the other three would do with it.

Mau wanted to know what happened next.

* * *

They used the cups to work out the room.

It was Minerva's idea, although she denied this after discovering that her cup had become the disagreeable visitor. Mau's was the host. Daphne's empty plate was the table, and Echidna kept one hand on the teapot to prevent it being enlisted as anything.

[M] "The door is here."

[Mn] "Then there's room."

[Ech] "The table has one leg nailed to the floor. We discover that on the following page."

Minerva stared at her.

[Mn] "Why would you do that?"

[Ech] "The host's grandfather disliked furniture being moved."

[M] "That sounds like the sort of thing you were going to make me read with another book."

She smiled at him over the pot.

Daphne moved her plate toward the middle, apparently following the dispute. When Mau looked again, it was directly in front of her.

[M] "That's the table."

[Da] "It can still be a plate."

[Mn] "There isn't anything on it."

[Da] "Not yet."

Echidna read another few lines. Minerva interrupted with an objection which turned out to be correct two sentences later, and was so indignant about having to wait that Mau missed the beginning of the next paragraph laughing.

He asked Echidna to go back.

She did.

* * *

They stopped when the pot was empty.

Not at the end of the chapter. Minerva had begun a second argument about the window, and Daphne had gone to see whether anything else had been put out on the kitchen shelf. She returned with a small handful of roasted nuts and no explanation of whose they had been.

Mau put one finger beneath the line they had reached.

[M] "May I borrow it?"

Echidna looked at the finger, then at his face.

[Ech] "If you bring it back."

[M] "Before or after you recommend the banquet?"

[Da] "Before. I'm waiting."

Minerva stood and stretched her shoulders. Her knuckles were stiff when she opened her hands; she flexed them gently, without trying to hide it, and picked up the cups two at a time.

Mau took the pot. He carried it to the washing court, rinsed it and brought it back to the clean shelf. When he returned to the table, Echidna had put a scrap between the pages instead of leaving his place to chance.

No questions about what he had remembered. No test waiting inside the book. He knew the difference because she had let him stop at an argument about a window, and because she looked amused when he asked whether the host was ever going to sit down.

[Ech] "Read it."

He took the book under his arm.

At the kitchen door, Daphne was asking who had made the nuts.

Someone answered from inside. Mau could not hear who.

[Da] "More," Daphne said.
