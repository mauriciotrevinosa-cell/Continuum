# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 53 — The Long Way Back

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 53
**Source episode:** S6E36
**Continuity state:** Day459 morning. A short private Mau/Yuka date, using Kaisel's established transport within the protected boundary and returning as reported. Rimuru stays home. The physical birthday bracelet remains separate from the System band; its flower's cultural meaning is not identified by either. They discuss the cost of concealment and what a conversation with her grandparents would involve, without actually confessing or promising a guaranteed solution. No sex, new power, cancer disclosure or full recollection. This flight is not Milim's later discovery.

*When: Day459, morning. Kaisel is waiting in the yard. Yuka has a small bag and the expression of someone who has packed everything except a reason not to go.*

[Mk] "Before lunch."

[Yk] "I wrote it down."

[Mk] "I saw."

Yuka adjusted the strap. It did not need adjusting. Maki watched her do it anyway, then looked past her at the dragon settling its weight beside the clear part of the yard.

Mau was checking the place where Yuka would sit.

[Mk] "No new places."

[Yk] "We aren't going outside."

[Mk] "The boundary."

[Yk] "I know which outside."

Maki let that go.

She had not told her not to go. Yuka knew the difference. It would have been easier, in a small unpleasant way, if she had.

Then her grandmother caught the loose strap, pulled it through its loop properly and let her pass.

[Mk] "Have a good morning."

Yuka's answer came a little too brightly.

* * *

Kaisel took them above the workshops in a smooth climbing turn.

The house shifted below. Roof, courtyard, the tree rising through the spaces people had left for it. A figure on the back step looked up and lifted one hand. Yuka waved before she could see who it was.

She had both arms round Mau's waist.

From the ground, that meant she was staying on the dragon. She knew that too.

Mau looked back when the wind eased.

[M] "All right?"

[Yk] "Yes."

He waited.

[Yk] "She said to have a good morning."

He looked toward the place where Maki had stood.

[M] "I heard."

Yuka pressed her cheek briefly against the back of his shoulder. Then she sat up so that she could see over it.

They went the long way round the inside of the boundary, following the turn they had put on the page rather than finding a new route in the air. Below them, the grass near the wall was pale with the last of the morning damp. Farther out, the trees moved in a different wind.

Mau brought Kaisel down well before the marked line.

There was a broad, dry stone with sunlight on one side and a patch of scrub leaning across the other. Nothing dramatic about it. Yuka had noticed it on a previous walk and decided that a place you could sit without arriving with a job was worth remembering.

Kaisel folded beside the grass. Mau got down first and offered his right hand.

She took it.

Held it after her feet were on the ground.

* * *

The bag contained a flask, two cups and the remains of a very successful attempt to take something from breakfast without appearing to take something for a date.

Mau looked inside.

[M] "You brought four."

[Yk] "They were small."

[M] "They aren't small now."

[Yk] "They grew in the bag."

He sat beside her and accepted one.

The bread had stuck to the cloth where the filling had come out at the edge. She tried to peel it free in one piece. It tore. Mau held the cloth while she rescued the rest, and by the time they had arranged everything on the stone, both of them had sticky fingers.

Yuka licked her thumb, caught him watching and offered it to him in a challenge she regretted almost immediately.

He kissed the clean side of her wrist instead.

She went quiet.

The cord of the birthday bracelet rested against his mouth for an instant. The little flower turned when he let her hand go.

[M] "Better?"

[Yk] "You haven't helped with the bread."

[M] "I held the cloth."

[Yk] "You can hold it again."

He did. She smiled down into the bag where he could not quite see it.

* * *

They had nothing to investigate.

For a while, this caused difficulties.

Mau looked at the stone beneath them, at the slope of the ground, at a little movement in the scrub that turned out to be a bird doing something deeply private with a twig. Yuka watched him notice all of it.

[Yk] "It isn't a gate."

[M] "I know."

[Yk] "You can stop looking for where the stone bites."

He looked at the stone again.

[M] "Now that you've said it—"

She pushed his shoulder. He leaned into her hand instead of away from it, and she ended up with her palm flat against his chest.

Under the cloth there was the familiar warmth, the ordinary rise of his breathing. She kept her hand there.

He stopped making the joke.

The dragon's wing shifted behind them. Grass rasped over stone. Yuka could hear the far-off sound of something being unloaded by the house, too distant to tell what it was.

They were not very far away at all.

She was glad of that until she wasn't.

* * *

[Yk] "Do you think about telling them?"

Mau's hand, which had been resting beside hers, stopped moving.

He did not ask what she meant.

[M] "Yes."

[Yk] "When?"

He watched the little bird disappear behind the scrub.

[M] "I don't have an answer for that."

She nodded. Looked at the flower on her wrist, then deliberately set both hands on her knees.

[Yk] "I keep thinking there's a way to say it where it doesn't hurt anyone. Then I start putting the words together, and there isn't."

[M] "No."

[Yk] "You're supposed to be better at this."

[M] "Why?"

She thought about it.

[Yk] "Because I want you to be."

He let out a breath that almost became a laugh.

[M] "I want that too."

She leaned against him. His arm came round her without searching for where it belonged.

Yesterday she had drawn the little angle after the touch for Yuta. He had looked at her work properly. Asked her to change one line so that somebody copying it would know which foot moved first. She had been proud when he nodded at the finished version.

Then he had asked whether she wanted to practise again.

She had said yes.

Now she could not think of his face without hearing how ordinary the question had been.

[Yk] "He likes being with us."

Mau looked down at her.

[Yk] "Grandpa. Yesterday. I could tell."

[M] "I like being with him."

She closed her eyes.

[Yk] "I know. That's the problem."

* * *

Mau did not tell her that Yuta would understand.

He remembered the practice ground, Yuta waiting while he put his feet back where they belonged. The water appearing beside his hand. He could imagine disappointing that quiet face with painful clarity, even without being able to call up all the years other people insisted they had shared.

He wanted the lesson again.

He wanted the arm around Yuka to stay where it was.

Neither wanting made the other disappear.

[M] "If I start trying to guess exactly what he'll say, I'll spend the whole morning arguing with someone who isn't here."

She opened her eyes.

[Yk] "He'll go quiet."

[M] "Yuka."

[Yk] "First. Then he'll ask questions."

He held her a little closer.

[M] "Then he should get to ask them himself."

She looked at him for a long time.

[Yk] "Not today."

It came out smaller than she had meant it to.

Mau did not answer at once. His thumb moved once against the seam of her sleeve.

[M] "I didn't bring you here to make you do that today."

She put her forehead against his shoulder.

Relief came first. She hated how quickly it came.

* * *

After a while, he asked her what she would do with an entire week that contained no emergency.

She lifted her head.

[Yk] "An entire week?"

[M] "I'm being generous."

[Yk] "I'd sleep."

[M] "For the whole week?"

[Yk] "The first part. Then I'd wake up and make you do something."

[M] "You could fit that into the sleeping. You've got practice."

She pinched the loose part of his sleeve. He laughed and caught her hand before she could do it again.

She considered the question more seriously.

[Yk] "I'd want one day where we went somewhere and didn't bring back a map."

[M] "No map."

[Yk] "You can know how to get home. You just can't spend half the time drawing how."

[M] "That's a difficult distinction."

[Yk] "I'm giving you a week to learn it."

He looked out across the small stretch of grass between their stone and the dragon.

[M] "What would we bring back?"

[Yk] "Nothing."

She waited.

[Yk] "Maybe a terrible drawing of you. Momo showed me hers."

He turned back.

[M] "That's why you were looking at me at breakfast."

[Yk] "I was wondering where she'd found the second eyebrow."

He laughed hard enough that she had to take the cup out of his hand.

* * *

She kissed him when he had stopped laughing.

Not to end the conversation. She had been looking at his mouth while he tried to catch his breath, and wanted to. He set his cup down safely before leaning toward her, which made her smile into the first kiss.

He felt it.

[M] "What?"

[Yk] "Nothing."

[M] "You've got that face."

She kissed him again rather than tell him she liked the little movement with the cup. That he could want her and still remember not to spill hot tea down her leg.

His right hand came up behind her head. She settled closer, one knee on the stone, until the other side of him had nowhere to lean except the warm slope of her shoulder.

They stayed like that for a while.

When she drew back, he let her.

She rested her cheek against his and looked down at their hands.

[Yk] "We should go home."

[M] "We've got time."

[Yk] "I know."

They sat through another small change in the light, then packed the cloth with the crumbs folded inside.

* * *

Kaisel took them straight back.

Mau felt Yuka move her face out of the wind behind his shoulder. A strand of her hair caught against his collar. She pulled it free, laughed at something he could not hear and held his waist more tightly for the turn.

From above, the house still had all its ordinary spaces.

The tree. The clear yard. Someone coming out of the workshop carrying an awkward length of wood, with someone else behind them saying that they had the wrong end.

Yuta looked up as Kaisel came down.

He lifted a hand.

Yuka waved back.

Mau gave her his right hand to get down, then took the little bag while she checked the time on the board. Before lunch, as they had said.

[Yt] "Good morning?"

She looked at her grandfather.

[Yk] "Yes."

He smiled. Asked Mau whether the left hand had been troublesome in the wind. Mau said it had been all right and described the place they had sat.

Yuka carried the empty cups inside.

At the washing shelf she stood for a moment with one in each hand, listening to the two voices continue outside.

Then she put the cups down and went to ask what needed doing before lunch.
