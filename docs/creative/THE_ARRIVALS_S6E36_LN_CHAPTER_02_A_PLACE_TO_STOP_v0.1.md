# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 54 — A Place to Stop

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-22)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 54
**Source episode:** S6E36
**Continuity state:** Day459, afternoon. Mau, Rimuru, Umaru, Ciel and Noise prepare and conduct a limited, reversible access survey following the small outer-edge alignment in The Same Edge. They check known accesses separately, then hold the two outer boundaries in attention briefly without transferring contents or merging routes. Noise warns of pressure and obeys the agreed stop. They leave the third access closed and unidentified. The Black Heart remains a finite, poorly understood overflow destination, not a licence to continue; no overflow is deliberately created and no new kit, crisis, points expenditure or full fusion occurs. The orchard's hollow shell is fitted for hanging during ordinary domestic work.

*When: Day459, afternoon. The back step. A hollow shell from the orchard is on Mau's knees, and nobody can agree where to put the hole.*

[U] "At the top."

[M] "Which one's the top?"

Umaru pointed. Mau turned the shell over.

[M] "Now?"

[U] "You're doing that on purpose."

Rimuru held it up to the light. The split where the wind had found it ran along one side, wider near the old stem and narrowing at the other end. It had made a note when Stark brought it home. It had made several more when Bocchi tried it at different angles by the music-room window.

None of those angles had been upright.

[R] "If we hang it there, the split points down."

[U] "Then at the side."

[M] "Which one's the side?"

She took it off him.

* * *

They used a loop instead.

Two lengths of narrow cloth round the shell, leaving the split clear, joined above it. Mau held the crossing while Rimuru tied it and Umaru checked whether the shell would fall out if the wind turned it backward.

It would.

They changed the loop.

The second arrangement held. The cloth came round the broad part, caught beneath a little ridge of the dried shell and left the opening facing across the step instead of toward the ground.

Mau lifted it by the knot.

[M] "That?"

[U] "That's a shape that stays in the string. Progress."

Rimuru leaned closer to look at the ridge.

[R] "Don't leave it outside in the rain until we know what happens to the shell."

[M] "Under the eaves, then."

They put it on the step while he went for a peg. When he came back, Umaru had moved it out of reach of the door and laid her other page beside it.

The one with the two entrances drawn separately. The third was only an outline at the bottom, with nothing written inside it.

[U] "If you're still doing the other thing, can I stay?"

* * *

Mau looked at Rimuru first.

Not for permission. To see whether they had the same afternoon in mind. Rimuru had come to help listen down the thread. He had not brought the shell as an excuse to persuade Mau to begin.

[R] "If you want."

[M] "A little of it."

[U] "That's what I brought. A page for a little of it."

She turned it so he could read the short lines beneath the drawings.

One entrance. Close. Other entrance. Close. Look for both edges, without moving anything through them. Stop.

[M] "That last bit's very large."

[U] "You sometimes miss the last bit."

[C] *We can leave it there today. We don't need the next step to prove this one was useful.*

Mau set the peg on the shell. Sat with his back against the post, feet on the dry ground and both hands empty.

[M] *All right. Let's ask.*

* * *

The first answer came through what Noise showed them.

The place where Mau found the legion. Familiar now, less crooked at the outside edge than before Ciel had asked it to move the little part it could show her. He did not have to search past the dog to know where the soldier was.

[C] *Still there,* Ciel said. *The same edge. Don't adjust it for me yet.*

It stayed.

Rimuru listened with his eyes open. He had put one hand on the post behind Mau, not touching him, just keeping a place to look while the voice reached him through the thread.

[R] "I can still hear you. Same from here."

Umaru made a small mark. Not a number pretending to measure what she could not see. *Held since451.*

Mau felt for the opening, let one plain soldier rise beside the step and sent it back when Ciel had finished looking. Spear and shield. The same place behind the slit where the eyes were. Nothing borrowed from the shikigami.

Then the other entrance.

Ear came up, looked at the three of them and put his nose on the shell.

[M] "Not a toy."

Ear breathed through the split. The shell gave a short, rude note.

Rimuru had to look away.

* * *

The separate openings worked.

That was not surprising. Mau had used them since the earlier change. What Ciel wanted to know was whether the change stayed where they had put it when he stopped thinking about it.

It had.

She said so, and Mau told Umaru. Umaru wrote the exact part he could describe, crossed out a word that made it sound as if they had joined the entrances and wrote *outer edge* above it.

Ear went back down with the faint air of someone unfairly excluded from an interesting noise.

Mau settled his hands on his knees again.

[C] *Now both edges. Only finding them. If anything draws farther, we stop.*

[M] *Can you show us before that happens?*

He was asking the thing below, not Ciel.

For a few moments nothing answered.

Then a small pressure at the legion's edge, and a different one where he found the dogs. Clearer than when he had tried to describe them in the washing court. Noise was showing him what it meant instead of asking him to guess where it had been working.

[N] *Here.*

[M] *I feel it.*

* * *

It was awkward to keep both in mind.

Not like holding two pulls over the board. There were no outward knots, no second person's hand making a demand across the loan. He was trying not to make a change while attending to a place that had been built to open when he meant it.

He thought of Ear. The dog entrance wanted to answer.

[M] *Not calling him.*

The pressure eased.

Rimuru watched him exhale.

[R] "Too much?"

[M] "Not yet. I nearly asked for something by thinking about it."

[R] "You can stop there."

[M] "I know. Give me a moment."

Umaru put the pencil down so she would not make him feel she was waiting for another result. Outside the low yard wall, someone was rolling an empty cart over the stones. Its wheel rattled at the same bad join, then went quiet.

Mau used the sound to find where his ordinary body was sitting. Post against his back. Hands on knees. The peg and the shell on the step beside him.

Then he looked for the edges again, without putting a shape in either opening.

* * *

They held for a breath.

Two distinct places. An easier beginning. No soldier standing among the dogs. No dog walking through the ranks of the legion. The things inside had not been poured together because their thresholds were easier to find.

Mau felt a faint weight move toward the point between them.

[N] *Heavy.*

[C] *Stop there.*

It stopped.

Mau let his attention come away. First from one edge, then the other. Ciel followed rather than pulling him out through a place he had not chosen.

The weight diminished.

He opened his eyes wider. He had not shut them, but the yard had come back into focus as if he had.

[M] "There. That's enough."

Rimuru nodded.

Umaru waited until he reached for the peg before picking up her pencil.

[U] "What happened?"

* * *

He described the warning Umaru hadn't been able to hear, and the weight that had diminished when he stopped. She put a little mark between the two drawings, with *warned here* beside it.

[U] "And no overflow."

[M] "Nothing we had to send anywhere."

[C] *That's important,* Ciel said. *The place that held the last excess isn't a reason to create more. We don't know its remaining limits. We stop before we need an answer from it.*

Rimuru passed that on, in her words. Umaru wrote *did not use the bar* beneath the trial.

The third outline stayed blank.

Mau looked at it. He could feel the other depth without going toward it. It had not become a new destination because they had made progress elsewhere.

[M] "Leave that one where it is."

[R] "We are."

There was no argument from below.

* * *

They did not try the breath again.

Mau stood, waited until he was sure his balance was ordinary and picked up the shell by its cloth loop. The peg had left a little dent in the wood of the step where it had been sitting. He moved it away from the edge before somebody could catch it with a heel.

Rimuru held the loop while he worked the peg into the old empty hole under the eaves. The hook there had lost its nail weeks ago. They used the remaining hole rather than making another.

Umaru folded the page and came to look.

[U] "Too low."

[M] "You said at the side."

[U] "The shell. Not your head. Somebody's going to walk into it."

He shortened the loop.

The shell hung above the door line, split facing the courtyard. It turned once, slowly, and found the little breath of air coming round the corner.

A low note. Uneven, but clear.

They all looked up.

[R] "There."

Umaru watched it turn back. It made no sound on the second pass.

[U] "It isn't going to do it when somebody asks."

[M] "Neither do you."

She pushed his shoulder with the folded paper, lightly, and went inside before he could say anything else.

Rimuru stayed on the step beside him while they waited for the wind to come back.
