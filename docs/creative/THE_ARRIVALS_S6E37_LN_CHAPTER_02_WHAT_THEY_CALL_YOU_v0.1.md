# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 56 — What They Call You

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 56
**Source episode:** S6E37
**Continuity state:** Day459 night, concurrent with roof55. Seiko, Turbo Granny, Maki, Mai and Frieren at their regular crackers table. Maki remains suspicious from the hands on return but has not acquired knowledge of the kisses/family date. Frieren can enjoy her own relationship with Fern and ask about ordinary speech with Mau without recovered romance. No future attack, disclosure or promise is enacted. Mai's own history remains untouched.

*When: Day459, after dinner. Seiko's room. This time the tin opens. The difficulty is the portrait beside it.*

[Se] "That's not my chin."

Mai turned the scrap toward the light.

[Mai] "I didn't say it was."

[Se] "You've looked at me three times."

[Mai] "You're sitting in front of the light."

Maki leaned over her sister's shoulder.

[Mk] "It is her chin."

Mai moved the paper away before Maki could get a better look. The drawing had begun as a remark about Momo. Momo had been going about with an air of having done something clever, and Seiko had found out what it was when she asked to see the paper in her pocket.

She had not been shown that paper.

This had annoyed her enough to tell the story twice.

Now Mai had a pencil, and Seiko was discovering that she had objections to being drawn by someone who had not spent any time flattering her first.

Frieren looked at the angled scrap.

[F] "The chin is quite good."

[Se] "Whose side are you on?"

[F] "The light's."

Turbo Granny made a pleased noise from her little cushion.

[TG] "Do the neck next."

Seiko put a cracker in the cat's mouth.

* * *

Mai was not trying to make a portrait.

She said this several times, which would have been more convincing if she had stopped improving it. At first there had only been a line, a nose and an eye. Now there was the angle of Seiko's head, the looseness of her mouth when she was preparing to disagree and a little stroke beside the cheek that Mai kept rubbing with her thumb.

Maki watched her work without announcing that she was watching.

The pencil had gone blunt. Mai turned it to find a narrower edge.

[Mk] "There are more downstairs."

[Mai] "This one's fine."

[Mk] "All right."

Mai drew the stroke again.

Seiko had settled into a deliberate stillness which nobody had asked her to maintain. She sat with one elbow on the table, chin slightly raised, looking past the far corner of the room.

[TG] "She's doing her young face."

[Se] "It's my face."

[TG] "Parts of it."

Maki had to put her cup down.

Frieren watched Seiko's expression arrive on the page, then watched the woman change it by becoming offended. Mai sighed and returned to the earlier line.

[Mai] "I can't draw both at once."

[Se] "Then keep the better one."

The cat opened her mouth.

Seiko put another cracker in it without looking.

* * *

They were talking about names by the time Mai put the pencil down.

It began because Maki said she had heard someone calling for Grandma from the bottom of the stair and had nearly answered before remembering that it might not mean her.

Seiko looked personally injured.

[Se] "It certainly didn't mean me."

[Mk] "Momo was calling."

[Se] "Then she should know better."

[Mai] "What does she normally call you?"

Seiko gave her a look.

[Se] "Not the point."

Maki smiled into her cup. Frieren took a cracker from the side of the plate which had fewer broken pieces. She had found that nobody else wanted the broken ones at first, and then everybody wanted them when they were all that remained.

[F] "It must be confusing."

[Se] "Having a granddaughter with no manners?"

[F] "Answering to the same name."

[Mk] "Yuka normally says more than that."

She did not elaborate. Mai gave her a quick, familiar look, then reached for the plate.

The room was warm. Somebody downstairs was laughing loudly enough that the sound came up through the corridor before a door closed on it.

Maki tilted her head toward the sound.

[Mk] "They're enjoying themselves."

[Se] "They should. It took them long enough to get up there with one pot."

[Mai] "Five men and a ladder."

[TG] "The ladder did most of it."

* * *

Frieren had not meant to talk about Fern.

She had meant to ask Maki something, and had kept waiting for a space in which it would not sound larger than she wanted. In the waiting, Seiko asked what she had been reading, and the bundle spell came out instead.

[F] "The diagram was badly arranged."

[Se] "That happens in books."

[F] "Fern noticed before I did."

Maki looked up.

Frieren turned her cup between her hands. She could still see Fern leaning over the page, one finger following the line that seemed to lead into the wrong part of the knot. The careful way she had said she thought the author had added a step he did not need.

Not a challenge. An invitation to look again.

[F] "She waited until I had finished trying it his way."

[Mai] "Polite of her."

[F] "Yes. I told her she should have interrupted."

[Se] "Would you have listened?"

Frieren considered this seriously.

[F] "Eventually."

Maki laughed. Frieren smiled at her, then looked back down at the cup.

[F] "She's very good."

There was something in the way she said it that made Mai leave the obvious joke alone.

[Mk] "Did you tell her?"

[F] "Yes."

[Mk] "Good. Tell her again when she does something else."

Frieren looked mildly offended.

[F] "I do."

[Se] "Then she can bear hearing it."

The room went on being warm. Frieren found she wanted another afternoon like that. A different book, perhaps. Fern had mentioned one with an illustration she disliked so much that Frieren was beginning to want to see it again.

She was still thinking about it when Maki asked whether Mau had joined them.

[F] "No."

She looked up, surprised by how quickly the answer had come.

[F] "It was just us."

Maki nodded.

Frieren let the sentence stay as she had said it.

* * *

Later she found the space she had been waiting for.

Mai had moved the drawing to the side of her cushion. Seiko was pouring the water Maki had fetched before coming, and Turbo Granny had settled into an argument about whether crumbs counted as a whole cracker if there were enough of them.

[F] "What do you call someone when you're trying not to make them remember being called something else?"

The question was too long.

She knew that before she reached the end of it.

Maki's hand stopped beside the plate.

[Mk] "Mau?"

[F] "Yes."

[Se] "You can call him that."

[F] "I know. I do."

She set the cup down because her hands were making too much of it.

[F] "Sometimes I begin something before I think. Something I used to say. Then I stop. And he notices that I've stopped, which is worse than whatever I might have said."

Nobody answered immediately.

She had expected one of them to tell her that she was making it complicated. She almost wanted someone to.

[F] "I don't want him to feel that every word is a question he has to get right."

Mai leaned forward, elbows on her knees.

[Mai] "Has he said that?"

[F] "No."

[Mai] "Then you don't know whether that part's happening."

Frieren looked at her.

[Mai] "I do it too. I decide how something sounded, and by the time the other person answers, I'm already arguing with the answer I made up."

Maki looked down at her own hands.

Mai noticed. Her mouth moved as if she might say something to her sister, then she reached for a broken cracker instead.

[F] "I don't want to hurt him."

[Mk] "I know."

[F] "I want—"

She stopped.

She had too many ways to finish that sentence. A room full of them. A whole ordinary morning, years of them, waiting behind a word she did not want to put on this table.

Maki waited.

[F] "I'd like to stop choosing every word before I say it."

Seiko put the pot down.

[Se] "You're doing it now."

Frieren looked at her, then laughed once, helplessly.

[F] "Yes."

* * *

Maki told them about the rope.

Not as an answer. Frieren had stopped speaking and taken a drink, and after a while Maki said that Yuta had come back from practice with a mark on his ankle and a very elaborate account of how he had acquired it.

[Mk] "He said Yuka was teaching him."

[Mai] "Was she?"

[Mk] "Apparently. There were diagrams."

[Se] "Of the ankle?"

[Mk] "Of the part he got wrong."

Frieren smiled.

She could imagine Yuta standing with the rope in his hands, listening properly to somebody smaller than himself. There were things about this house she had learned to rely on without noticing when the reliance began.

[F] "Mau practised too."

[Mk] "Yes."

[F] "Was it all right?"

[Mk] "They enjoyed it. I think Yuta enjoyed being the one getting corrected."

Frieren looked at the plate. At a cracker broken into three unequal pieces.

[F] "I'm glad."

She meant it.

Wanting him to laugh was easy. Hearing that he had, in a place where she had not been, sometimes left her sitting with another feeling she did not know what to call.

She was tired of wishing that a good thing came with no difficult part attached to it.

Maki moved the plate toward her.

Frieren took the smallest piece and asked whether Yuta was going to try the rope again.

* * *

The portrait was no better after tea.

Seiko said so. Mai said she had not been improving it during tea. Turbo Granny said that explained a great deal.

Maki reached over, turned the scrap round and looked at it properly.

[Mk] "It's good."

[Mai] "Don't sound so surprised."

[Mk] "I'm not."

Mai tried to take it back. Maki held it just beyond the first reach, then gave it to Seiko instead.

Seiko inspected the eye. The little angle beside the cheek. The stubborn lift of the chin she had objected to at the beginning.

She was quieter about it now.

[Se] "You can leave it."

[Mai] "Where?"

[Se] "Here."

Mai looked at the shelf, then at the space beside the tin.

[Mai] "It'll get bent."

[Se] "Then stop bending it."

Mai flattened the scrap between the cover of Frieren's closed book and the table. Frieren held the book still for her. When Mai lifted it again, the little curl at the edge had eased.

Seiko set the drawing on the shelf with the painted tin in front of its lower corner.

Turbo Granny stared at it.

[TG] "Now there are two."

Seiko looked at the cat.

[Se] "You can sleep facing the other way."

They left the drawing there.

In the corridor, Maki waited while Frieren settled the book against her side. Below them, a cup rang against something and a man's voice said it had not been his fault.

Mai leaned over the rail.

[Mai] "Then whose was it?"

A pause.

[G] "The acoustics are terrible in here."

Frieren laughed before she could choose whether to.
