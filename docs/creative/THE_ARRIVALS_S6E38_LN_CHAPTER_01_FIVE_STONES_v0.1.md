# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 57 — Five Stones

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 57
**Source episode:** S6E38
**Continuity state:** Day461 afternoon. Momo, Okarun, Jiji, Vamola and Seiko spend an ordinary afternoon playing a hand game with five small stones in the courtyard. No powers or new equipment. Momo and Okarun remain a couple; Vamola belongs to their household and shares Momo's room. The scene neither repeats the Day455 portraits nor changes anyone's memory or private knowledge.

*When: Day461, afternoon. In the courtyard shade, where Seiko has found a flat enough place for a game and Momo has found a reason to dispute it.*

[Mo] "That one bounced."

[Se] "It came back down. That's what they do."

[Mo] "After it hit the ground."

Seiko opened her hand. Five small stones lay against her palm, white and brown and an unremarkable grey. She tipped them onto the flat paving stone between her knees.

[Se] "Then you do it."

Momo held out her hand.

Okarun had thought they were going for a walk. He still had the water bottle across his knees. Now Momo was sitting on her heels opposite her grandmother, examining the stones as if one of them might confess.

[Ok] "Are we staying here?"

[Mo] "For a minute."

She chose the whitest stone, tossed it, snatched at another and caught nothing.

The white one struck the back of her wrist.

Seiko watched it roll away.

[Se] "That one bounced."

Momo looked at her.

Vamola reached beneath the bench and brought it back.

By the time Jiji found them, the walk had been postponed indefinitely.

He stood behind Okarun, bent at the waist, watching Momo's hand.

[Ji] "What's the record?"

[Mo] "You have to know what we're doing before you can beat it."

[Ji] "I can learn on the way."

[Ok] "You throw one up. Pick up another before you catch it. Then keep going."

[Se] "One at a time first. Then two. Your other hand stays where I can see it."

[Mo] "That means you," Momo told Jiji.

[Ji] "I've barely arrived."

Jiji dropped to his knees beside Okarun.

[Ji] "Show me."

Momo threw the white stone too high.

Everyone looked up.

She collected two from the ground, realised she only needed one and tried to put the second back. The white stone hit her shoulder.

[Ji] "Strong opening."

[Mo] "You distracted me."

[Ji] "I was watching with respect."

[Mo] "You were breathing on me."

He moved back an inch.

[Ji] "Better?"

Okarun turned his laugh into a cough. Momo pointed at him without looking.

[Mo] "You're next."

He put down the bottle.

The stones looked larger in her hand than they did in his. He rolled the white one between finger and thumb, trying to judge how far it would go. Not very high. Just enough time to reach down, close his fingers and come back up.

He threw it.

His hand went down. The grey stone caught beneath his smallest finger. He scooped it up, came back and caught the white one with a little sound of surprise.

[Mo] "There."

He looked at her.

[Mo] "Don't look at me. You've got three more."

He looked down too late.

The next throw landed on his knee.

Jiji collapsed sideways with a groan that suggested he had personally lost a fortune.

[Ji] "I believed in you."

[Ok] "You could believe more quietly."

[Ji] "That's not how belief works."

Vamola took the stones when he passed them over. She spread them with the tips of her fingers, leaving the pale one by her knee.

Seiko leaned forward.

[Se] "No moving them after you start. Put them where you want them now."

Vamola shifted one brown stone slightly to the left. She considered the result, nodded and picked up the white one.

Her first catch was clean.

So was the second.

Jiji stopped leaning sideways.

She finished the singles with all five tucked in her hand. Then she put them down and looked at Seiko.

[V] "Two now?"

[Se] "Two now."

Momo drew her knees closer to make room.

[Mo] "Wait. How are you doing that?"

Vamola demonstrated the small throw.

[V] "Not so high."

Okarun pressed his lips together.

[Mo] "I know that."

[Ji] "We all saw you know it. Very high up."

She kicked his foot.

Vamola's first pair went into her hand. The second did not. One of the brown stones rolled beneath her fingers, and the white one came down before she could turn her hand over.

She looked at the two on the ground, then held out the ones she had caught to Jiji.

[V] "Your turn."

He straightened. Rolled his shoulders. Shook out his hand.

Seiko picked up the bottle and drank while he prepared.

[Mo] "It's not a fight."

[Ji] "I respect my opponent."

[Mo] "Your opponent's gravel."

He gave the gravel a serious nod.

Then, to everyone's annoyance, he got through the first round.

It was not elegant. His hand travelled far further than it needed to, and once he caught the white stone against the heel of his palm with a sound that made Okarun wince. But it stayed caught.

Jiji spread his fingers to show them the result.

[Ji] "Natural ability."

[Se] "Two at once."

He dropped the stones. Tossed the white one, reached down for a pair and brought his hand up too fast.

The pale stone vanished.

Nobody moved.

Jiji looked at his palm. Then at the ground.

[Ji] "Caught it."

[Mo] "Where?"

[Ji] "Still establishing that."

Okarun heard it slide.

[Ok] "Your sleeve."

Jiji lifted his arm. The stone travelled to his elbow and stopped.

[Ji] "Part of me."

[Mo] "Your hand. You have to catch it in your hand."

[Ji] "That's a narrow interpretation."

He shook his arm. Nothing happened. He shook it harder, with the fixed cheerful face of someone determined not to require assistance.

Momo began to laugh.

[Ji] "The fabric's helping."

[Ok] "I think it's keeping it."

Seiko held the bottle out of the way. Jiji put his arm straight above his head, and the stone dropped out at his shoulder, fell down his back and struck the paving behind him.

Vamola retrieved it again.

[V] "Not your hand."

Momo had folded over her own knees. Jiji pointed at the stone in Vamola's fingers.

[Ji] "We knew where it was the whole time."

Seiko made them move two feet to the left when the sun reached the game.

Jiji carried the five stones cupped in both hands. Momo took the bottle. Okarun stood, discovered that one leg had gone numb and had to wait beside the bench while everyone else settled.

[Mo] "What are you doing?"

[Ok] "Nothing. Give me a second."

She watched him put weight on the foot and immediately regret it.

[Mo] "Oh."

She came back, put the bottle on the bench and offered her shoulder. He took her arm instead.

[Ok] "It's coming back."

[Mo] "You say that like it's good news."

He made a small, strangled noise as the pins and needles reached his toes.

She laughed into his shoulder. He would have been more offended if she had let go.

They sat on the bench for a minute first. Momo lifted the bottle and drank, then handed it to him. He took a longer drink than he had meant to and stopped to catch his breath.

[Mo] "Save me some."

He tilted it to show her what remained.

[Mo] "I didn't say I was dying."

She took another mouthful anyway. Across the courtyard, Jiji was demonstrating how he would have caught the stone if his sleeve had been shorter. Vamola listened, then rolled his sleeve back for him.

He looked down at his bare wrist.

[V] "Now you can."

Momo laughed, nearly swallowed wrong, and had to lean forward until she could breathe. Okarun took the bottle before it tipped over.

[Ok] "You're going to lose your place."

[Mo] "He'll still be explaining when I get there."

He was. By the time they joined the others, Vamola had chosen the new playing place and Seiko was in the middle of explaining why nobody ought to put their knees on the stones.

Jiji moved his knee.

Seiko took her turn.

Her throws were so small that Momo had to lean forward to see them. One stone rose, four fingers moved, and there it was again. The first four pickups passed without a wasted movement.

[Ji] "She's done this before."

[Mo] "She was showing me when you got here."

[Ji] "I mean before today. Possibly before stones."

Seiko's hand stopped.

The white stone fell between her knees.

Momo stared at it. Then at Jiji.

He sat very still.

[Ji] "That was admiration."

[Se] "You'll need something better."

Vamola covered her mouth with both hands. Okarun was trying not to look directly at anyone.

Momo reached across for the stones before her grandmother could decide which kind of something better she wanted.

This time, the throw stayed below her chin.

She caught the first. Then the second. The grey one was awkward, longer than the rest; she turned her fingers round it instead of trying to pinch it. It worked.

At the last pickup, Jiji inhaled.

[Mo] "Don't."

He held his breath.

The white stone landed neatly on the other four.

Momo sat back, smiling before she could stop herself.

Okarun smiled at her.

[Mo] "See?"

[Ok] "I saw."

Jiji let his breath out all at once, which spoiled the effect slightly.

They played until the patch of shade had moved again.

Okarun got through the pairs after three attempts. The third time, Momo put a hand over Jiji's mouth before he could announce anything. Jiji finished his own round with the white stone caught against two fingers and insisted on holding it up until everybody agreed that those were part of his hand.

Vamola won the first full game.

She did it while Seiko was telling Momo that arguing did not count as a turn. Four stones went into her hand, the white one followed, and she sat with all five closed in her fist until they noticed.

[V] "Finished."

Seiko looked down. Vamola opened her hand.

[Se] "So you are."

Jiji demanded a rematch. Momo demanded that he stop saying rematch when he had not won the first one. Okarun reached for the bottle and found it empty.

His stomach made a noise.

They all looked at him.

[Ok] "I had lunch."

[Mo] "Nobody said you didn't."

[Ji] "Your opponent's hungry."

[Mo] "His opponent's still gravel."

Vamola closed her hand around the stones again.

[V] "Can we eat?"

Momo was already getting up.

[Mo] "We can find out what's there."

Okarun stood beside her. This time both legs worked. She tucked two fingers into his hand as they started toward the door, then stopped to wait for Vamola, who had gone back for the white stone after putting the others on the bench.

Jiji was still talking to Seiko.

[Ji] "I could have won that last round."

[Se] "Then why didn't you?"

[Ji] "A respectful finish."

Seiko held out her hand.

Vamola gave her all five stones. She counted them, put them in her pocket and looked at Jiji.

[Se] "Tomorrow you can disrespect me properly."

He followed her inside, explaining that she would regret saying that.

Momo waited until the door had shut behind them.

[Mo] "She's going to make him practise."

Okarun glanced back at the cleared patch of paving.

[Ok] "He will, too."

Inside, Jiji asked whether there was a smaller stone for training. Seiko told him to find a larger hand.
