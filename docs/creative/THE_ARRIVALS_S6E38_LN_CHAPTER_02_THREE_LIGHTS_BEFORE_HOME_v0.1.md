*When: Day462, afternoon. A new page on the gate board. Anko has underlined the date and left the rest blank.*

[A] "If it's another orchard, I'm not bringing home a tree."

Yuka looked at the empty sample bag.

[Yk] "It wouldn't fit."

[A] "That's never stopped him."

Mau turned from the frame.

[M] "I've never brought home a tree."

Anko considered him for a moment, then put her pencil behind her ear.

[A] "Give it time."

Rimuru had taken the second copy of their intended route time. He was staying at the house. Yuta checked the straps on his own pack, then asked Yuka whether the line she was carrying was the sound one.

She held up the unmarked rope.

[Yk] "I didn't bring the bit Grandma condemned."

[Yt] "Good."

Grey-white light. The yard fell away behind them, and the wind changed.

* * *

The new place smelled of wet reeds.

Not rotten. Wet, cold and very still. Low water lay on either side of a raised wooden path, with pale seed heads standing above it and a sky the colour of old tin. Farther along, three tall posts stood at intervals beside the boards. Each had a little iron-roofed lamp beneath its crosspiece.

All dark.

The return waited behind them, open and ordinary.

Mau read the notice aloud, then shared the selected lines so Anko could check them.

*Route task: restore the three way lamps.*

*Reward: materials cache.*

*Return available.*

Anko looked from the words to the posts.

[A] "An actual job."

[Yk] "We've had jobs."

[A] "We've had things trying to bite us. This one wants maintenance."

Yuta put one foot on the first board beyond the frame and tested its weight before putting the other down. The path held. A little water stirred underneath, then settled.

[Yt] "We can look at the first one from here."

Yuka called the white dog. It went ahead along the boards, nose low, and stopped beside the first lamp without finding anything underneath it.

They followed.

* * *

The lamp was not broken where Mau expected.

He had brought the small fire kit they used outside. He had been thinking about the wick and whether they could get it dry. Yuta looked at the open side of the iron casing and found the little pin on the floor beneath it.

[Yt] "The shutter's come off."

Anko held the loose shutter while he tried the pin. It passed through the lower hinge and stopped before the upper one.

[A] "Bent?"

[Yt] "The hinge. Hold it a little higher."

She did. He eased the pin through with the flat of the small tool from his pack. No great force. A change of angle and a quiet scrape, and the shutter moved where it was supposed to move.

Inside, the wick was dry beneath its metal hood. The little reservoir was not empty. Mau checked it before putting fire anywhere near it.

Yuka sheltered the opening with her coat while he struck the spark.

The flame took on the second try.

He moved back, and she closed the shutter so the wind could not blow straight through. Light fell in a small square on the boards at their feet.

*Way lamps restored:1 of3.*

Anko wrote it down.

[A] "There. We fixed something without hitting it."

Something clicked beneath the path.

She looked down.

[A] "I should stop saying things."

* * *

The first creature came up over the side of the boards.

Flat. Winged. A brown shape the size of a serving tray, with long jointed forelegs that caught the edge and pulled it up without using the wings at all. The wings were folded close, fringed like the dry ends of the reeds.

Its head turned toward the lamp.

Then toward the hand Yuka had just taken away from it.

[Yt] "Back."

Yuka was already moving. The white dog barked and the creature struck toward the sound, forelegs opening like a pair of shears.

Yuta's sword came down between them and the dog.

One clean cut. The creature tipped off the board and fell into the shallow water with its wings open.

Another head appeared farther along the path.

Anko took the long walking pole out of the side loops on her pack and braced it across the gap. Not trying to fight the thing's whole body. Keeping the sharp front limbs off the boards until Yuta could turn.

Mau put the smallest pull beyond the creature's reaching leg. It missed the pole, turned toward the water and gave Yuta the angle.

Steel flashed low.

The path was clear again.

[M] "Two."

[A] "Seen. Don't lean over to count them twice."

They listened. The reeds moved. Nothing else climbed up.

* * *

The second lamp stood beyond a missing board.

Not a chasm. A gap wide enough to put an inattentive foot through, with dark water just below it and two of the narrow supports showing where the plank had been.

Anko marked the gap before anybody stepped over.

[A] "That comes first. On the way back it'll be behind us, and somebody will be looking at the light instead of down."

There was a sound board lying crosswise beneath the next post. Yuta lifted it, tested both ends and handed one to Mau. They laid it across the exposed supports and found it was a little too long.

[M] "We could move it farther."

[Yt] "Then the end sticks up where we walk."

Yuka crouched beside the support.

[Yk] "Or tie it. Here and here. So it can't lift when somebody steps on the end."

They did that. The knot sat off the walking surface, tight against the support, and when Yuta tested the board it stayed down. Anko crossed last and looked back to make sure the rope could not catch a boot.

The second lamp needed cleaning.

Soot lay over the little window so thickly that they had thought it was a black plate. Anko scraped a corner with a folded strip of cloth and held it up for Mau to see.

[A] "It's glass."

[M] "That's promising."

[A] "It's filthy."

[Yk] "Also promising. We can do filthy."

They laughed and cleaned it. Fire took. The next square of light fell onto the path.

*Way lamps restored:2 of3.*

* * *

The third post had something wrapped round its base.

They saw the movement before reaching it. A thick, low body the same colour as the boards, folded into the narrow space under the lamp platform. Two armoured limbs rested on the wood as if they were part of the support. Between them, a mouth opened and shut very slowly.

Yuka called the dog back.

[Yk] "It's waiting."

[Yt] "It has a good place."

He did not walk straight into the place to prove he could. He stood where the path widened beside a post, with room to move his sword and somewhere firm to put both feet.

Mau looked along the iron casing. One side of the lamp was open. A shutter hung down against the platform by a single hinge, and the creature's shell pressed over it.

[M] "We need it off the post."

[A] "We don't need the post off the path."

[M] "I wasn't going to."

Anko gave him a look.

He looked at Yuka instead.

[M] "The little pair. One turn."

She studied the platform and shook her head.

[Yk] "Not toward us. There's nowhere for it to land except on Grandpa. Sideways. Toward that bit of water."

Yuta followed her line.

[Yt] "I can use that."

They told Anko where they meant to put it. She moved back clear of the short gap in the rail and kept the pole ready in case anything smaller tried to climb up behind them.

* * *

The creature unfolded when they came into its reach.

Faster than its slow mouth had suggested. A long front limb struck the edge of the board, hooked and pulled, trying to bring the whole of its plated back over the platform in one movement.

Yuta stepped aside from the hook.

Mau set the first small knot beyond the right shoulder. Yuka's came lower, near the place where the creature had been braced against the post.

[C] *There. Keep the shared limit. Don't chase its whole weight.*

He did not.

They were not lifting it out of the world. They were asking a badly placed step to go a little farther wrong.

The armoured body turned. One limb lost its brace against the post. The low back slid across the loosened shutter and over the side of the platform.

Yuta went in as the joint under the shell opened.

His sword found it. He moved with the cut, not past it, kept his feet on the sound boards and drew back before the hooked limbs could close round the place he had been.

The creature struck the water beside the path.

For a moment it tried to get its front half up again. Anko put the end of the pole against the hard plate, held it away from the rail and let Yuta finish the cut without anybody leaning into the water.

Mau closed the loan.

The knots went out. Yuka lowered her hand.

The lamp post was still standing.

[A] "Good. Now we can go back to maintenance."

* * *

The bent shutter had jammed under the edge of the platform.

That took longer than the fight.

Yuta could reach it, but not while holding the casing clear. Mau could hold the casing, but his left hand did not have the grip for the fine turning at that angle. Yuka could get the rope round it and hold the loose piece steady while Anko worked the edge free with the end of the small tool.

They tried it one way. Then another.

The shutter came loose so suddenly that Yuka sat down on the boards with the rope in her hands.

She looked up at them.

[Yk] "I'm all right."

[A] "I was going to ask whether you had the shutter."

Yuka lifted it.

Anko nodded.

[A] "Good. You can get up now."

Mau laughed and held the lamp casing steady while she stood.

The last wick lit. They closed the repaired side, checked that the wind did not put it out and stepped back to look along the route.

Three little lights above dark water.

All still burning.

*Route task complete.*

*Materials cache released.*

* * *

The cache was underneath the last post.

A little iron-bound box rested on a shelf they could not have reached while the plated thing sat over it. The clasp lifted easily now.

Inside was a roll of closely woven cloth with a waxy surface, a short spool of copper wire and three small crystals wrapped separately so they would not knock against one another.

Anko looked at the cloth.

Then at the damp edge of her route page.

[A] "Mine."

[Yt] "The house's."

[A] "The part of the house that writes outside."

He smiled and let her wrap the page in it.

Mau put the wire and the crystals in the sample bag. He did not raise the creatures. They had come to do the job on the notice, not to turn every dead thing beside it into another name.

They had enough daylight left in the strange sky to eat where they could see the route. Bread, water and something Yuka had wrapped too carefully for it to have survived the bag.

She opened it. A flat, broken biscuit.

[Yk] "It was whole."

[M] "Still one biscuit. Just a different arrangement."

She gave him the smaller half.

* * *

They walked back through the squares of light.

Anko checked the new board as they crossed it. Yuta checked the two earlier lamps. The first shutter still moved on its repaired hinge; the second window shone through the clean part instead of the soot.

The frame waited where they had left it.

Four through, one after another. The colder yard beneath their feet. House smoke instead of reeds.

Rimuru came out of the kitchen before Anko had unfolded the page.

[R] "How was it?"

[A] "Three lamps. All lit."

[R] "That sounds almost reasonable."

[Yk] "One objected."

Yuta put the bag on the bench. Mau gave the wire and the wrapped crystals to Anko to record while Yuka drew the repaired plank where the path had been incomplete.

Anko cut a small rectangle from the new cloth, folded it over the top of her page and held the board beneath the drip from the eaves.

Water ran off the waxed surface. The writing stayed dry.

She looked at the others, deeply satisfied.

[A] "Useful."

Rimuru looked into the empty sample bag.

[R] "No tree?"

Mau looked at Anko.

She put the pencil behind her ear.

[A] "I told you. Give it time."
