# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 33
## Chapter 60 — Being Good at Waiting

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 33
**Reading order:** 60
**Source episode:** S6E39
**Continuity state:** Day465 night. The intervening days contain ordinary routines, no unrecorded technique or revelation. Ori speaks privately to Frieren instead of devising another game to recover Mau's affection. Her outline briefly flickers without injury or a new origin claim. Frieren affirms their independent mother/daughter bond, does not promise a cure or answer for Mau, and offers support if Ori wants to ask him herself. Ori has not yet made the public question; no one here knows the hidden romance. Milim sleeps in the connecting room as established.

*When: Day465, night. The east room. Ori has come through the connecting door with something she has been trying not to ask.*

Frieren heard her before she looked up.

The handle moved carefully, then the door opened just far enough for a face. Ori stood behind it with one hand still round the edge. Her hair was loose. One sock had worked halfway off her heel.

[F] "Come in."

Ori came in.

She did not sit on the bed. That was the first thing Frieren noticed. Usually she had got there before the invitation was finished, pulling her knees beneath herself or turning round to complain that somebody had taken the good corner of the blanket.

Tonight she stood beside the shelf.

[F] "Is Milim awake?"

[O] "No."

[F] "Is she still in the room?"

Ori looked back through the door, as if she had only just thought to check.

[O] "Yes."

[F] "Good."

There was a small pause in which the joke had somewhere to go. Ori's mouth moved, but the smile did not quite arrive.

Frieren put her marker between the pages.

* * *

They had had ordinary days.

Ori had carried cups, argued with Umaru about something which had become a Fort matter without anyone being certain how, and spent part of an afternoon holding a book for Coco while Coco tried something with a perfectly normal length of thread. Milim had fallen asleep before finishing a story and woken up insisting that everyone else had interrupted the ending.

Mau had been there too.

At breakfast. Coming back with the things from the gate. Laughing when the hollow shell under the eaves refused to make a sound for someone who had asked it very politely.

He did not disappear every time Ori entered a room.

That was what made the rest of it difficult to say.

[O] "I'm trying."

Frieren looked at her properly.

[F] "I know."

Ori's eyes went toward the book.

[O] "I can come back."

[F] "Come here."

This time Ori sat.

Only on the edge at first. Frieren moved the book to the other side and made room without saying anything about how much room there had always been. After a while Ori drew up her bare heel and pulled the sock back on.

Her fingers were not quite steady.

* * *

[O] "He had fun with Ear."

[F] "He told me."

Ori looked up quickly.

[O] "He did?"

[F] "About Milim's collar."

For a moment the whole scene returned. The hidden ribbon, Milim glaring at the ceiling, Mau laughing into his sleeve because Ori was already laughing against the other one.

She smiled.

Then the smile hurt.

[O] "I thought that would help."

Frieren waited. Ori rubbed one thumb against the heel of her other hand, trying to remove a little mark that was only a shadow from the lamp.

[O] "Not the ribbon. Being there. Him staying. I thought if he did it enough times, he'd stop asking if he could."

[F] "You can tell him he doesn't need to ask."

[O] "I have."

Frieren looked down.

[O] "I have," Ori said again, quieter. "He says thank you."

The lamp moved when someone passed the door outside. A brief interruption of the air, nothing else. Ori's outline caught the light strangely along one wrist. It blurred, returned and held.

Frieren put her hand over it.

Ori watched both their hands.

[O] "I'm not doing it."

[F] "I know."

[O] "I'm not trying to make you look."

[F] "I'm looking because you're here."

Ori turned her wrist beneath the warm palm and held on.

* * *

There were questions she had decided not to ask him.

At first that had been easy to understand. He had come home with something missing. People needed time. She knew about being tired and frightened and having too many voices telling you what happened next.

So she had not stood in front of him and asked for the old greeting.

She had played. Helped. Sat near enough that he could choose her without anybody putting her there.

She could remember every time he had.

She could remember how each one ended.

[O] "How long am I supposed to be good at this?"

Frieren's hand tightened before she could stop it.

[F] "Ori."

[O] "I'm not saying he has to. I know he can't just—"

She stopped, frustrated with the sentence she had learned so well.

[O] "I know. Everyone keeps saying it because I know."

Frieren moved closer on the bed.

There was enough of her own voice in those words that she had to take care with the answer. She could not bear another patient explanation about a thing being true when the truth did nothing to make it hurt less.

[F] "You don't have to be good for me."

Ori stared at the space where their hands joined.

[O] "What if I get angry?"

[F] "Then you can tell me you're angry."

[O] "At him."

It was almost a whisper.

Frieren put her other arm round her.

[F] "You can tell me."

* * *

Ori did not cry.

There was a tightness in her throat and a hot pressure behind her eyes which she refused to mistake for anything going wrong with the way she was made. She put her forehead against Frieren's shoulder and let herself breathe until the tightness loosened a little.

On the far side of the connecting door, Milim made a sleepy, offended sound.

Ori lifted her head.

[O] "She does that when she turns over."

[F] "I've heard."

[O] "She doesn't know she's doing it."

[F] "I'm sure she'll tell us who caused it in the morning."

Ori laughed once. It caught awkwardly, but it was a laugh.

Frieren brushed a strand of hair out of her mouth. Ori let her, then stayed close when the hand moved away.

[O] "Did you choose me too?"

Frieren looked at her.

[O] "Or did you get me because he—"

[F] "I chose you."

The answer came before she had finished.

Ori's mouth stayed open for a moment.

[F] "I didn't need him to give you to me."

She watched the face beside hers. The little shift in it as the words arrived somewhere Ori had been keeping guarded.

[F] "You're my daughter. You can be angry in my room. You can be bored here. You can come in and tell me you've done something foolish, which is a possibility I haven't dismissed."

Ori made a sound into her sleeve.

[F] "You don't have to arrange a good afternoon first."

She had not known that was the sentence she needed until she heard herself say it.

Ori moved into her lap sideways, awkwardly, too much leg for the position she had chosen. Frieren made room for that too.

* * *

After a while Ori asked whether it would hurt him if she asked.

Frieren knew which question she meant this time.

She looked over Ori's head toward the shelf. A few paper wings showed above the edge of the little parcel. Mau's bad bird had not improved while nobody was watching it.

She could have told Ori to wait another day.

The suggestion was already there, prepared and familiar. There might be a better morning. Mau might remember something tonight. Another gentle day might put something back where they all wanted it.

She was very tired of being frightened of the question herself.

[F] "I can't promise it won't hurt."

Ori stayed still.

[F] "I can't tell you what he'll say."

[O] "I know."

[F] "But you aren't doing something wrong by needing to ask."

The room was quiet enough that Frieren could hear the little click of a cup being set down in the corridor beyond the closed door.

[F] "If you want us to find somewhere quiet, I'll help you. If you want to speak to him yourself, you can. You don't have to make it into a game."

Ori turned her face into the cloth at Frieren's shoulder.

[O] "I don't want to make him do it."

[F] "I know."

[O] "I want him to know he does."

Frieren closed her eyes.

She held her for a little longer before answering.

[F] "I know that too."

* * *

They checked on Milim together.

She had turned almost upside down in her sleep, one hand loose above her head and a foot moving in a slow, indignant circle. The ribbon still kept her from drifting away from where she was supposed to be.

Ori stopped in the doorway.

[O] "See?"

Frieren looked at the sleeping girl, then at the course of the ribbon.

[F] "Impressive."

[O] "She'll say she was comfortable."

[F] "She might have been."

They adjusted the blanket without trying to improve Milim's entire understanding of a bed. Ori slipped into her own place and pulled the corner to her chin.

Frieren sat beside her until the hand that had caught her sleeve began to loosen.

[O] "Tomorrow?"

[F] "If you want."

Ori nodded without opening her eyes.

She had not decided what words she would use. She had stopped trying to invent another reason to deserve the answer.

Frieren stayed until the room had settled. Then she went through the connecting door, leaving it open the small amount Ori liked.

Her book was where she had left it.

She picked it up, opened it at the marker and read the first line several times before realising that she had not taken in a word.
