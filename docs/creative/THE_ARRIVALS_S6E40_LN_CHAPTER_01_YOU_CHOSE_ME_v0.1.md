*When: Day466, after breakfast. The common room. A bowl is still moving from one end of the table to the other when Ori decides not to wait for a better moment.*

She had wanted to ask him alone.

That was what she had thought while coming down the stair. She would find a place beside him after breakfast, and when the bowls had gone away and people had finished asking him for the small things a morning needed, she would ask whether he had time.

He would say yes. He nearly always did.

Then she would have to say the rest.

She had held that plan all the way through the meal. It was small enough to keep in mind while Milim argued with Umaru about whether something dropped into a cup could still be eaten. Ori passed the cloth when it was needed. Answered a question from Rem. Watched Frieren put her book down and actually eat while Fern was sitting beside her.

Mau asked for the bowl.

Ori passed it.

[M] "Thank you."

He smiled at her. Warmly. He put some food into it, turned to answer something Yuka had said and laughed before the bowl reached the table.

Ori sat with her hand still where the rim had been.

She could not work out why that had done it.

Nothing bad had happened. That was the part she would keep thinking about later. Nobody had been cruel. Nobody had told her to go away.

She got up.

* * *

Mau looked toward her when she reached his chair.

He knew that face. He could have picked it out in any room. The way the hair sat, the little intensity when she meant to tell him something important, the habit of looking at his mouth as if she were checking that he had heard before he answered.

There was a place in him where all of that should have arrived.

He could feel himself trying to make it arrive there.

[M] "What is it?"

Ori looked down at the space between his shoulder and the back of the chair.

[O] "Can I ask you something?"

[M] "Of course."

Frieren heard. She was several places away, with Fern beside her and a cup in her hand. She looked up but did not come over. Ori had not asked her to.

Yuka moved her own cup out of the space between them.

Ori saw that. Saw him make room. All the little kindnesses which proved she was allowed to be there.

[O] "Do you love me?"

The question was quiet enough that not everyone at the table heard it.

Mau did.

He put his spoon down.

* * *

At first Ori thought the silence was him choosing how to say yes.

She knew how carefully he chose things now. He liked to make sure he understood what a question meant. She could wait that long. She had been doing it for days.

He looked at her with an expression she could not bear to recognise.

The same care he gave a fragile thing someone had put in his hands without telling him where it belonged.

[M] "Ori—"

She shook her head.

He stopped.

She could hear a spoon touching a bowl farther down the table. Someone had not heard the question. Someone was still having breakfast.

[O] "You do."

Mau moved his chair back enough to turn toward her properly.

[M] "I'm trying to—"

[O] "You love me."

It came out louder.

The spoon stopped.

Mau looked at the hand she had put against his sleeve. He could hold it. He wanted to hold it. He wanted her not to have the look she had now, as if his next word could take a whole wall out of the room.

He could not find the word she was asking for behind the things he remembered.

He could find the word itself.

She would know the difference.

* * *

Ori had told herself that she was not going to cry.

Now she could not remember deciding that, or why it had seemed like a useful thing to decide. Her eyes had begun to hurt. The room had gone too clear and too far away around Mau's face.

[O] "You love me."

She said it again because the first time had not done anything.

Her hand closed on his sleeve. She pulled once, as if he had simply failed to turn far enough toward her.

[O] "You do."

Frieren was getting up.

Mau saw her at the edge of his vision and wanted, terribly, for someone to tell him what to do without telling him to feel something he could not reach.

[M] "I don't want to lie to you."

Ori made a sound he had never heard from her.

Not loud. A little broken intake of breath, as if the words had found a place under her ribs where she had been trying to keep the rest of herself together.

[O] "Then remember."

She hit his shoulder with the flat of her hand.

Not a blow meant to injure. A helpless, angry movement, too small to make anything change. She did it again before he could speak.

[O] "Remember. You love me. You love me."

He stood, slowly, so she would not have to keep reaching down to where he was sitting.

That was the wrong thing too. His face moved farther away. She caught the front of his shirt and hit him once more, crying now so hard that she could not get the next words out in order.

He let her hands stay there.

[M] "I'm here."

[O] "You aren't."

* * *

Frieren reached them before Ori said the rest.

She put one hand against Ori's back, not pulling, giving her something solid behind her. Fern moved the chair out of the way. Yuta was already on his feet at the other side of the table.

[F] "Ori. Breathe with me."

Ori tried.

It came in wrong. Caught halfway. She hated that too, the indignity of not being able to do something so simple when everyone could see her failing at it.

Mau's hand was open beside hers.

She looked at it.

[O] "You chose me."

His face changed.

For an instant she thought she had found it.

[O] "You made me your daughter. You didn't have to. You chose me."

Her voice had gone low again, rough from everything she had forced through it.

[O] "You went through the door because she said she'd kill me. You told me first. You promised you'd tell me first."

Mau saw a door.

Not clearly. An edge of light where there should have been ordinary space, a girl in front of him with a look that was almost this one. He could reach the beginning of something he had said. Could feel the effort of wanting to keep her alive.

Then the picture broke apart before the feeling found a name.

He put his hand against his own chest, just below the place Ori had been holding.

She watched him do it.

[O] "You love me. Why won't you remember?"

The last word was a plea.

He had no answer to it.

* * *

Yuka was crying.

She had not noticed the first tear. Her hands were pressed against the edge of her chair, holding her there while every part of her wanted to go to him and every part knew what that would mean in this room.

Ori had let go of his shirt. Frieren had her now, one arm round her shoulders, and Ori was trying to turn back toward him without leaving the support.

Mau looked at Yuka for a fraction of a second.

Milim saw the movement.

She had been standing near the end of the table, very still, as if the whole scene had been something she could stop by finding the thing that had hurt Ori and making it move away.

Now she moved.

[Mi] "Why did you make her cry?"

Yuta turned.

Mau saw the fist coming past the corner of the table. Saw Milim's face behind it, wet and furious and far more hurt than the movement could explain.

He raised the route before he found a sentence.

Her hand stopped short of his chest.

The space held.

For one suspended breath Milim was leaning into the place where the distance refused to finish. Her shoulder shook. There was no impact, no overturned table, only the sharp sound of Yuta saying her name as he reached her from the side.

[Yt] "Milim. Stop."

Gojo was at the clear end of the table. His hand was already up. He lowered it when Milim's arm began to come back.

[Mi] "You're my brother."

She was crying openly now.

[Mi] "You don't remember?"

Mau looked from the fist she had drawn against her own chest to Ori, held against Frieren's shoulder.

Then to all the other faces in the room.

[M] "I'm sorry."

* * *

He could not get enough air.

That was absurd. Nothing was wrong with the air. The room had the same doors and windows it had had when he sat down. His body could do things none of the people staring at him should have needed it to do, and now he could not work out how to stand there and finish one honest sentence.

[R] "Mau."

Rimuru had come round the table. His voice was low, trying to give him one thing to listen to instead of the whole room.

Mau looked toward it.

[R] "Stay a moment."

Ori had hidden her face against Frieren. Milim had both hands over her mouth. Shoko was coming from the kitchen side, moving the last chair out of her way with her foot.

He could stay. He knew that.

He could stand there while they found a better way to tell him that he had just hurt people who needed him to be someone he could almost, almost see.

Yuka had stood up.

He reached toward her.

[M] "I need to go outside."

She looked at him through the wet blur on her lashes. At his open right hand. She put her hand in it and nodded.

[C] *Mau, leaving now—*

[M] *Tell him where. Just the yard. I need a minute.*

He let Infinity drop once Milim had stepped back with Yuta, then took the short step toward the clear space he could see through the open door.

The room was gone.

* * *

The yard was cold enough to make his breath catch properly.

Yuka's hand stayed in his. She had come with the step, stumbled once on the change from floor to earth and caught her balance before he could tighten his grip.

Behind them, someone called his name.

He could hear it. He could still turn.

He asked Kaisel to rise in the clear part of the yard.

The dragon unfolded out of the shadow, broad back lowering for them. Mau helped Yuka up with his right hand, then climbed into his place in front of her. Her arms came round him as soon as he was seated.

[Yk] "Where?"

[M] "Inside the line. Just somewhere I can hear myself."

[C] *Rimuru knows you're outside. He needs to know whether you're coming back.*

Mau shut his eyes for a moment.

[M] *Yes. Tell him yes. No search. We're staying inside the boundary.*

Kaisel lifted.

The back door moved below them. Rimuru stood in it, one hand against the frame. Yuta had reached the open yard and stopped when he saw which way the dragon was turning.

Mau knew that he should have said more aloud.

He felt Yuka's forehead against his back. Felt her trying to stop crying where nobody else could see.

The dragon banked toward the sheltered ground inside the western boundary.

Behind them, the door remained open.
