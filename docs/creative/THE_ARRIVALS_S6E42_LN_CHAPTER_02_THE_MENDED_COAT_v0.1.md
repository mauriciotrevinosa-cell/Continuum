# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 2 — The Mended Coat

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S6E42
**Continuity state:** Day466 after midday, following the public confirmation. Yuka speaks privately with Maki and Yuta in her room. They know the romance and that she knew the real history; they do not learn the private family conversation, cancer history, sexual detail or an unestablished date of the first kiss. Their anger and concern coexist with her adult autonomy and continuing family membership. No attack, reconciliation by embrace, restored memory or new intervention occurs.

*When: Day466, after midday. Yuka's room. Her gate coat is hanging over the chair, with the mended cuff turned inward.*

Yuta waited for her to decide about the door.

She had stood beside it after they came in, holding the edge, unable to work out whether closing it would make the room kinder or simply smaller. Voices moved along the corridor. None stopped.

She closed it.

Maki was looking at the coat.

Yuka had taken it off when they came in. She had started toward the hook, then changed her mind and left it over the chair. One sleeve had slipped between the seat and the back.

Her grandmother freed it.

[Yk] "You can sit."

Maki laid the coat across the chair instead of sitting in it. Yuta took the little stool beside the window. Yuka sat on the edge of the bed.

For a while her grandmother stayed standing.

[Yk] "What do you want me to say?"

[Mk] "Don't start with that."

Yuka's fingers closed on the coverlet.

[Mk] "I haven't asked you anything yet."

[Yk] "Everyone's been asking."

[Mk] "I'm not everyone."

Yuka looked down. Maki turned the sleeve back to the right side. The stitches held where Mai had made them small, and where Yuka had finally made hers close enough together to stop being taken out.

[Mk] "I asked about your hands."

Yuka knew which hands she meant.

[Mk] "In the yard. After we brought your things out of the workroom. You told me you were frightened. That you were coming home."

[Yk] "I was."

[Mk] "I know you were."

The answer struck harder than if Maki had called it a lie.

She held the cuff between finger and thumb.

[Mk] "Mai had the needle. I was holding the rest of this out of her way. You were showing me where the glass had cut the rope. I asked what happened, and you told me enough true things that I let you stop."

Yuka pressed her hands flatter against the bed.

[Mk] "Then you asked if you could keep the coat."

She remembered the open place before that question. Her grandmother waiting beside the step. Her own mouth already beginning something she could have said.

[Yk] "I nearly told you."

Maki looked at her.

[Yk] "That morning. I did."

[Mk] "I know. I was there."

Yuka's throat tightened. She had not thought her grandmother could have seen the thing she had not done as clearly as the thing she had.

[Yk] "I was afraid."

[Mk] "Of what I'd do?"

Yuka tried to answer quickly and could not make it honest.

Yuta shifted on the stool. The wood made a small complaining sound beneath him.

[Yt] "Take a moment."

Maki sat at last. She had to pick the coat up to do it. It stayed across her knees, one sleeve hanging nearly to the floor.

[Yk] "Not that you'd hurt him. Or me. Not that."

She rubbed her thumb over a loose thread in the coverlet until it caught under her nail.

[Yk] "I thought if I said it, we'd have to stop being what we were. And I didn't want to."

[Mk] "You thought we'd tell you to stop seeing him."

[Yk] "Yes."

[Mk] "So you made sure we didn't get to say anything."

Yuka looked up sharply.

[Yk] "That isn't how I thought about it."

[Mk] "It's how it happened."

She had raised her voice. Not very far, but far enough for Yuka to stop trying to answer over the end of it.

Maki looked down at the coat. She loosened her hold on the sleeve.

Yuta did not tell either of them to calm down. He waited until Maki had looked up again.

[Yt] "What were you thinking about? Not what you think you should have thought. What was it?"

Yuka looked toward the window. From the bed she could see a narrow piece of the courtyard, the place where the workshop roof cut across the sky.

[Yk] "One more day."

Neither answered.

[Yk] "That's what it kept being. We'd come home, and I'd think we needed to tell you. Then there would be breakfast, or training, or someone asking him something, and I could wait until after. We could have the morning first."

She stopped pulling at the thread.

[Yk] "Then we'd had the morning, and I didn't want the afternoon to be when it ended."

Maki's mouth tightened.

[Yk] "I know how that sounds."

[Mk] "Then don't try to make it sound better."

[Yk] "I'm not."

Her own voice had risen now. She swallowed, looked at Yuta, then back at her grandmother.

[Yk] "I knew about Frieren. I saw her. Every day. I don't need you to ask whether I noticed her sitting there."

Maki looked as if she had been going to ask exactly that.

[Yk] "And I still wanted to go with him. I still wanted him to look at me the way he did. I don't have another answer underneath that which makes it less awful."

The sleeve slipped off Maki's knee. She caught it before it reached the floor.

Yuka watched the little movement and began crying again, silently this time. She wiped her face with her palm, angry that there was still more to come after everything downstairs.

Yuta reached for the folded cloth beside the washing things. He held it out. She took it without looking at him.

[Yt] "The morning with the rope."

She looked up then.

[Yt] "When you showed us the turn. I was pleased about that all day."

She could see it. Him putting the wooden sword down to try what she knew. The tail striking his ankle. Mau looking into the woodpile so that he could laugh without being caught laughing.

[Yt] "You both seemed happy."

[Yk] "We were."

Yuta nodded, but not as though that had answered everything.

[Yt] "I wasn't imagining that."

[Yk] "No. Grandpa. No."

She moved forward on the bed.

[Yk] "I liked showing you. I was proud you asked. When you tried again because I'd moved your hand, I—"

Her breath failed. She waited until it came back instead of trying to push all the words through at once.

[Yk] "I wasn't doing that to keep you from finding out."

He looked at the little book on the table. The corner of a separate sheet showed beneath it, the one where she had drawn the angle after the touch.

[Yt] "I liked learning it. I liked knowing we could do that again."

[Yk] "We can."

The answer came too quickly.

She saw what it did to his face and sat back.

[Yk] "I don't mean you have to."

He put his hands together between his knees. His ring was turned slightly to one side. He straightened it, an ordinary movement she had seen him make while thinking since before she came to this world.

[Yt] "I'm not taking the morning away from you. It happened. I enjoyed it."

She nodded.

His eyes had gone back to the page beneath the book. She could remember being careful over the drawing because he had said someone else ought to be able to follow it. She had wanted it to be worth keeping. Now she wanted to cover it, though there was nothing written there that he did not already know.

[Yt] "Now I keep wondering what would have happened if I'd asked the right question. Whether you'd have told me or found a different thing that was true."

Yuka pressed the cloth against her mouth.

He did not ask her to give him a reassuring answer. That made it worse. She could have promised to tell him anything now. She knew he would hear the ease of promising it after he had already found out.

[Yk] "I don't know."

Yuta looked down.

[Yk] "I want to tell you I would have. But I don't know."

Maki folded the other sleeve over the first.

[Mk] "You let us keep taking care of you both."

[Yk] "That was real too."

[Mk] "I didn't say it wasn't."

[Yk] "It sounds like you want me to say everything we did was only so we could keep hiding. It wasn't. I wasn't pretending you were my grandparents for the useful parts."

Maki's head came up.

[Mk] "I know who I am to you."

[Yk] "Then don't talk like I stopped loving you when I started loving him."

The sentence stayed between them.

Yuka could hear someone stop outside the door, then continue down the corridor. She kept her eyes on her grandmother. Her hands were shaking beneath the cloth, but she did not take back what she had said.

Maki looked away first.

[Mk] "You didn't stop. You shut us out of something that was going to hurt."

[Yk] "Yes."

[Mk] "I had to hear Milim say it."

Yuka nodded once, very small.

[Yt] "What do you think happens when he remembers?"

She looked at him.

There was no anger put around the question to make it easier to fight. He was asking because he wanted to know, and because he was afraid she had found a way not to ask it herself.

[Yk] "I don't know."

[Yt] "Have you talked about it?"

[Yk] "About not knowing. Yes. He hasn't promised me that he'll wake up one morning with all of it back and still want exactly this."

Maki looked at her again.

[Yk] "He can't."

[Yt] "No."

Yuka rubbed the damp cloth between her fingers.

[Yk] "I know he might love her again. I know he might remember being with me before and wonder why I let any of this happen."

[Mk] "He knows you knew."

[Yk] "He doesn't remember knowing me. That's different."

Maki sat back.

For a while none of them spoke. The coat lay neatly now, which was not something any of them had needed it to do.

[Yk] "I miss Oppa."

Yuta's eyes lifted.

[Yk] "I do. When he didn't know what I meant at breakfast, when he thought I was talking about Tsurugi—"

She stopped. She could still feel the spoon halfway to her mouth, the absurdity of having to explain the safest word she knew.

[Yk] "I miss that. And I love him now. I don't know how to want one back without being afraid of losing the other."

Maki drew a slow breath.

[Mk] "So what are you going to do?"

[Yk] "I don't have a clever thing."

[Mk] "I didn't ask for clever."

Yuka looked down at the cloth.

[Yk] "Tell him what I know when he asks. Not only the parts I think he can hear without wanting to go away. If something comes back and he wants to talk about it, let him."

Yuta listened until she had finished.

[Yk] "And I still want to be with him. I don't want to say something different because you're both looking at me."

Maki's jaw moved. For a moment Yuka thought she would stand, take the coat with her and go.

[Mk] "I didn't come up here to choose who you love."

Yuka's eyes filled again.

[Mk] "Don't make that into me saying this is all right."

[Yk] "I'm not asking you to like it."

[Mk] "Good. Because I don't."

Yuka nodded into the cloth. It hurt. She found she was almost grateful for how plainly it hurt, without a question hidden inside it.

Yuta moved the stool a little. One leg had been resting at the edge of the rug and he had been keeping it level with his foot.

[Yt] "We need to speak to him as well. Not through you."

[Yk] "I know."

[Yt] "He has things to answer for himself. I keep remembering putting his sword back in the sheath after the tower. He was on the floor with Frieren beside him. Now I look at what's happened today, and I don't know what I'm going to say first."

He looked tired when he said it. Yuka had known him old enough to have an answer for her almost every time she asked. She had forgotten that she used to wait for some of them.

[Yk] "Are you going to make me leave?"

Maki stared at her.

[Mk] "No."

The answer came so sharply that Yuka flinched.

Maki saw it and put the coat down beside her.

[Mk] "This is your room. This is your home. I'm angry with you. I am not putting you out of it."

Yuka bent forward over the cloth. She had not meant to ask. She had not known until her grandmother answered how long she had been trying not to hear the answer.

Yuta's hand came onto the bed beside her. He did not pull her in. She put her fingertips against his and left them there.

[Yt] "We aren't finished talking."

[Yk] "I know."

Her voice was very small now. Maki stayed in the chair, looking at her granddaughter, at the face she knew through all the ages in which she had been allowed to see it.

Yuka wiped her eyes. Tried to fold the cloth and found that she had twisted it so tightly she had to work one corner free first.

[Yk] "Could you stay a little longer?"

Neither answered at once. Yuka waited, holding the cloth loosely now instead of pulling its corners apart.

Then Yuta shifted his hand enough for her fingers to rest in it. Maki drew one palm over her face, then settled both hands on her knees.

[Mk] "Move your foot. It's in the way of my chair."

Yuka moved it. Maki brought the chair nearer the bed and sat again, leaving the coat folded where she had put it.

The cuff had turned inward once more. Yuka reached over, worked it gently back through the sleeve and smoothed the seam with her thumb.
