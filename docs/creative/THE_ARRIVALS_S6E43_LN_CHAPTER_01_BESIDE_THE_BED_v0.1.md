# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 3 — Beside the Bed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S6E43
**Continuity state:** Day466 early afternoon, overlapping the private conversations following chapter1. Frieren, Ori and Fern return to the east room. Shoko joins after settling Milim downstairs. Ori is still cared for and keeps her own voice; she does not blame herself successfully into responsibility for the romance. Frieren begins processing the difference between the hope she had and the fact now known, without an attack, reconciliation or medical cure. Fern is hurt in her own right as well as protective; she was present in the orchard but did not know the romance. *When: Day466, early afternoon. The east room again. Ori has climbed the same stair twice in one morning, and this time nobody says that things downstairs might be quieter when she comes back.*

She took the place she had sat in before.

The edge of the bed, a little to the right, where she could see the door without having to turn. The cup was still on the small table. Someone had left the cloth folded beside it. For a moment she was annoyed at the room for having kept everything exactly where it had been.

Frieren sat beside her.

Fern went to the window and pulled it almost closed. The air coming through had begun to lift the corner of the page on the reading table. She put the page flat before she noticed she was doing it.

[O] "Did they know too?"

Frieren looked at her.

[O] "Everyone."

[F] "I don't think so."

Ori looked toward Fern.

[Fe] "I didn't."

The answer seemed to take something out of the room. Not the thing they wanted removed. Just one possibility Ori had begun turning over before she had reached the stair.

She nodded and looked at her hands.

* * *

Frieren could still hear Rimuru saying yes.

The first yes had belonged to Mau. The next one to a different question. They had begun to sound alike in her head, answers to something she should have known to ask sooner.

She watched Fern move the cup half an inch so that Ori would not knock it over with her elbow.

They knew how to do this part. All three of them. A room with Mau elsewhere, somebody keeping track of water, somebody telling the person who was frightened that they were staying.

It made her angrier than she expected.

She had no useful place to put the anger. Rimuru was downstairs, and if he came up now she did not know whether she would ask him to explain himself or tell him to leave before he spoke.

Mau had stayed.

That should have been something she could be glad about.

[O] "I was going to ask him later."

Frieren turned back.

[O] "Just him. Like you said we could. I wasn't going to do it with everyone there."

[F] "I know."

[O] "Then he was laughing, and I thought—"

She stopped, frowning.

She could see the bowl passing between their hands. It was such an ordinary thing to be trying to explain. A smile. The way he had turned back to Yuka without having to think about it first.

[O] "I didn't know that was what I was looking at."

Frieren put her hand beside Ori's on the blanket.

[F] "Neither did I."

Ori caught it.

* * *

Shoko knocked against the open doorframe.

She had one cup in her hand and nothing that looked like a bag of things she intended to do to someone. Fern moved away from the window to give her room.

[Sh] "Can I come in?"

Ori nodded first. Frieren looked up and did the same.

Shoko sat on the chair near the bed, close enough to reach a hand if it was offered, not close enough to make one disappear beneath her examination before anybody had decided whether they wanted it.

[Sh] "How are the fingers?"

Ori opened them. Closed them. Opened them again.

[O] "They're fine."

Shoko looked at the movement and nodded.

[Sh] "Good."

That was all. She did not turn the hand into an explanation of what had happened downstairs.

Ori looked toward the corridor.

[O] "Milim?"

[Sh] "With Gojo for a moment. She needed to wash her face."

Ori tried to imagine that. Gojo handing someone a cloth without making a joke or finding something he wanted to improve first.

She could imagine it.

That made her eyes hurt again.

[O] "She shouldn't have hit him."

[Sh] "She didn't. She tried."

[O] "I did."

Shoko waited.

[O] "I know. I won't do it again."

There was a stubbornness in the words which belonged to Ori, not to somebody repeating what a doctor had told her. Shoko let it remain hers.

[Sh] "All right."

* * *

Fern had not sat down yet.

She stood beside the reading table with one hand against its edge, looking at a book she had no intention of opening. Frieren noticed that the little finger on that hand was shaking.

[F] "Fern."

Fern looked up.

[F] "Come here."

There was room on the other side of Ori. Fern took it, sitting carefully so she would not disturb the girl's grip on Frieren's hand.

For a while nobody spoke.

Then Frieren asked the question she had been trying not to bring into the room.

[F] "Were they happy? When you went out with them?"

Fern's face changed.

The orchard came back in pieces. The dry fruit making a note in the wind. Stark with the bag. Yuka asking about the little pull and Mau listening before opening it. Them laughing about pears on the way home.

[Fe] "We were all having a good afternoon."

She did not know whether Frieren wanted more than that. She could not give her what she did not know.

[Fe] "I saw them work together. I didn't know about the rest."

[F] "I know."

Frieren looked toward the nearly closed window.

[F] "I'm not asking why you didn't see it."

Fern lowered her eyes.

She had been preparing an answer to that anyway.

Mau had told her when he needed to rest the route. Yuka had not asked for more. They had come back when they said they would. Fern had felt pleased, quietly, that an ordinary afternoon could end with something brought home to make Bocchi come running.

It was still an afternoon she had enjoyed.

She did not know how to fit that beside the thing she had learned.

[Fe] "I thought it was good that he wasn't alone with it."

Frieren closed her eyes.

[F] "Yes."

* * *

Ori had been looking at Fern while she spoke.

Now she pulled her feet up, turned sideways and pressed her shoulder against Frieren's. There was too much of her to fit neatly into the small space. Fern shifted, giving her knees somewhere to go without moving away entirely.

[O] "Was it because I kept asking?"

[F] "No."

The answer came so quickly that Ori looked up at her.

[O] "I mean before today. If I kept making him—"

[F] "You didn't make him choose her."

Ori's mouth tightened.

[O] "But maybe he didn't want to come here because every time he did I wanted—"

[F] "Ori."

She waited until the girl looked at her again.

[F] "You didn't take something away from him by wanting your father."

Ori stared at her. Her eyes had gone bright, furious with themselves.

[O] "Then why does it feel like I did?"

Frieren did not have an answer she could hand over without also having to believe it for herself.

She brought her closer instead.

[F] "I don't know. But you didn't."

Ori turned her face into the place between Frieren's shoulder and neck. The first breath shook. The next one did too.

Fern put her hand against Ori's back, below Frieren's arm.

No one tried to make the crying quieter.

* * *

After some time Ori slept.

Not suddenly. She kept lifting her head at sounds from the corridor, and once opened her eyes to ask whether someone had said Mau's name. Fern said no, it had been a chair. Ori looked annoyed, as if she might go and ask the chair what it had wanted.

Then she settled against Frieren again.

Shoko helped them change the angle when Ori's neck began to bend uncomfortably. There was a pillow within reach. Fern lifted it, Frieren moved slowly and Ori murmured something into the blanket without waking properly.

They laid her on her side with her hand still holding the edge of Frieren's sleeve.

Frieren stayed beside the bed.

She had not cried.

Shoko noticed that without deciding what it meant. She had seen too many different ways a person could remain upright to mistake one of them for being all right.

[Sh] "Do you want me to bring something to eat?"

Frieren shook her head.

[Sh] "Later, then."

[F] "I don't know."

Shoko nodded. She had a cup in her hands again. At some point she had picked it up from the small table, and now she put it down so that Frieren would not have to watch somebody else holding water she did not want.

Fern's hand rested on the blanket near Ori's back.

[Fe] "I'll find something small. You don't have to eat it now."

She stood, waited until Ori's fingers had loosened enough that Frieren could move her arm, then slipped into the corridor.

The door stayed open.

* * *

Frieren looked toward the shelf.

The birds were there. One of the wings stuck out past the folded cloth beneath them. She could see the uneven place where Mau had tried to correct a fold after discovering it did not work the way he expected.

He had laughed that afternoon.

He had played with his daughter, and she had been there, and it had been something true.

She had spent the days since arranging small true things into a direction.

The direction had not belonged to them.

[F] "He wasn't lying today."

Shoko looked at her.

[F] "When he said it. About her."

Her voice remained quiet. Ori's breathing moved through the space beside it.

[F] "I could hear that he meant it."

Shoko did not ask her to say any more.

Frieren watched the paper wing.

[F] "I wish I hadn't been able to."

The sentence seemed to surprise her after she said it. Her mouth moved, almost an attempt to take the words back before they became something someone could repeat.

Shoko kept her hands still.

[Sh] "I heard you."

That was all.

Frieren bowed her head. One strand of hair slipped across her face and remained there. She could have moved it with the hand Ori was not holding.

She did not.

* * *

Fern returned with Stark behind her.

He stopped short of the doorway, holding the little plate she had asked him to carry. He could see Ori asleep. The two women beside the bed. Fern's face when she turned to take the plate from him.

He handed it over without looking past her again.

[St] "Do you want me to stay nearby?"

Fern nodded.

[Fe] "For a little while."

He sat in the corridor, clear of the door and of anyone who might need to pass. Fern went back inside with the plate and set it on the table beside the unread book.

Frieren looked up at her.

There were things she wanted to say to Fern which would have been easier if Ori were not sleeping beside them. There were things she was grateful not to have to say while somebody needed her hand to remain where it was.

[F] "Sit with us."

Fern did.

Shoko stayed in the chair. Beyond the door, Stark shifted his feet out of the way for someone passing and answered a quiet question too softly for the words to come through.

The plate remained on the table.

After a while Frieren reached for one piece and broke it in half so it would make less noise to eat.
