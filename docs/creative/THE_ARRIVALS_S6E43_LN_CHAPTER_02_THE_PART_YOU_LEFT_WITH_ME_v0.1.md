## Chapter 4 — The Part You Left With Me

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)

**Reading order:** 4

*When: Day466, early afternoon. The long table has begun to clear around the two men who have not found a way to leave it.*

Mau could still see the mark his hand had made.

It was near the end of the table, brown in the small lines of the wood. The earth had dried on his palm by now. When he rubbed his thumb against it, a little more came loose and fell on his knee.

Rimuru watched him notice it.

[R] "There's a basin."

[M] "I know."

He did not get up.

Someone came to take the last breakfast bowls. Rem, moving quietly without turning the quiet into a performance. She reached for the one near Mau, then paused until he had moved his elbow out of her way.

[M] "Sorry."

[Re] "The bowl, Mau. Just the bowl."

He moved it toward her.

She took it and went back to the kitchen with the others.

For a while he listened to the ordinary business of putting them away. Water. A cupboard. Somebody asking where the spoons had gone and someone else answering, tiredly, that they were back in the right drawer.

Rimuru's mouth moved as if a laugh had started and found the room unsuitable.

Mau looked down at the mark again.

* * *

[M] "I didn't want you to get put in the middle of it."

Rimuru looked at him.

[R] "You weren't the only person who put me there."

[M] "I know. You chose not to say anything."

[R] "Don't use my answer to Maki to get to the end of yours."

Mau closed his mouth.

Rimuru put both hands flat on the table, then took them away when he found that he had put one beside the dirt.

[R] "I decided. I know I did. I'm not asking you to tell me I had no choice."

[M] "Then what are you asking?"

[R] "What did you think was going to happen?"

Mau looked toward the stair.

It was not an answer he could reach by arranging the things he already knew in a more flattering order.

[M] "I thought I'd have to say it. At some point."

[R] "And until then?"

[M] "We could have what we had."

Rimuru nodded once. He had expected the answer. Hearing Mau say it did not make it easier.

[M] "That sounds worse when I say it here."

[R] "It sounded like that in the field too. I understood why you wanted it. That wasn't the same as thinking it would stop hurting people if we found a good enough day to tell them."

Mau looked at his hands.

[M] "I didn't find one."

[R] "No."

The word was neither a punishment nor an invitation to keep explaining until Rimuru supplied a gentler one.

* * *

Ciel had been quiet since they sat down.

Mau could feel the difference between that and her being absent. She was there, paying attention, letting a sentence end without reaching into the pause to help him make it safer.

[M] *You can say it.*

[C] *Which part?*

He had expected her to know.

That was an unpleasant thing to discover about himself in the middle of a conversation about what he had left other people to carry.

[M] *The part you're waiting to say.*

Rimuru looked at him. The voice reached both of them when she answered; the room outside their thread remained full of cupboards and running water.

[C] *I warned you that the people waiting did not have the same picture you had. I couldn't choose the conversation for you. I also couldn't make not choosing it harmless.*

Mau looked toward Rimuru.

[M] "You heard that."

[R] "Yes."

[M] *I wasn't angry with you for telling him.*

[C] *I know. You told me. That wasn't the part I was unsure about.*

He stayed quiet.

[C] *You asked me to tell him where you had gone this morning. I did. It meant he knew how to find you. It didn't give Ori the words she hadn't heard before you left.*

Mau turned his hands over on his knees.

There was more dirt along the edge of the right one than he had thought.

* * *

Rimuru got up to fetch the water.

Mau almost asked him not to. Then he saw the cup being left by the basin, the cloth picked up, and understood that nobody was walking out on him. Rimuru returned with both and put them in the clear space between the two chairs.

[R] "There. You can stop grinding it into your knee."

Mau wet the cloth. Wiped his palm slowly, paying attention to the places where the earth had settled into old lines.

It stung a little where the skin was dry. Nothing new had split. He turned the cloth and tried the clean side.

[M] "When I left, I thought I was stopping it getting worse."

Rimuru sat again.

[M] "There were too many people. Every face was another thing I was failing at. I could see myself saying something just to make somebody stop looking like that."

[R] "You could have said you needed some air."

[M] "I did."

[R] "You said you were going outside and then you were gone. We heard the dragon before half the room knew where you were standing."

Mau looked toward the open door.

He had thought of that distance as the small part. The one he could do safely, without hurting anyone, without making a wall or forcing a technique through a hand that would not close.

Rimuru had been standing at the other end of it.

[M] "I knew I'd come back."

[R] "We didn't. Not until Ciel said."

Mau set the cloth down.

[M] "I'll say where. To the people I'm leaving, not only through you."

[R] "And give them time to hear it."

He nodded.

That was something he could do without promising to become a person he could not yet remember being.

* * *

Rimuru's anger was not as loud as Yuta's had been.

Mau found it harder to move around.

There was a chair on the roof which had been warm beneath a blanket, a game about voices, Rimuru laughing before he could stop the second laugh from following. All of it was still in Mau's memory. It had happened in the days he could actually find from the inside.

He did not know whether it was unfair to want to keep those things while the man opposite him looked like this.

[M] "Did I make you regret coming up there?"

Rimuru frowned.

[M] "The roof."

The answer took longer than Mau wanted.

[R] "No."

He breathed out.

[R] "That isn't me saying I was fine. I wasn't. I wanted an evening where I could listen to you make a stupid joke without deciding what letting myself laugh would mean."

[M] "You can still be angry."

[R] "I am."

Mau nodded.

Rimuru looked past him at the empty bench near the stair, where Frieren had sat with Ori. He had not looked straight at it since she went up.

[R] "I wanted you to have the chance to tell her. I let wanting that become another reason to leave her waiting."

[M] "I should have done it."

[R] "Yes."

He looked back.

[R] "And I should have talked to her myself. Those are both true. You don't get to take mine off me because yours is bigger."

Mau's mouth moved once, trying to find a reply which was not an apology for apologising.

He gave up and nodded.

* * *

The mark on the table came off more slowly than the dirt on his skin.

Mau worked at it while they sat. Not hard enough to damage the grain. Enough to darken the cloth and make him turn it twice.

Rimuru watched the patch spread, then watched Mau notice that he had used too much water.

[R] "Dry side."

[M] "There isn't one left."

Rimuru fetched another cloth from the shelf by the kitchen door. He handed it over without making a point of having done two trips.

Mau pressed it flat over the wet patch.

[M] "I don't want you to talk to her as if you can explain me into being less painful."

Rimuru looked at him.

[M] "I don't mean don't tell her the truth. I mean the part where you say you understand why I did it, and she has to sit there and understand me too."

He waited until he had the next words straight.

[M] "She doesn't have to do that for me."

Rimuru let a breath go slowly.

[R] "I wasn't planning to defend you."

[M] "I know. I keep being afraid of the wrong thing first."

[R] "Then don't turn it into something else I have to promise. I'll speak for what I did. You can speak for yours."

Mau nodded again.

He was beginning to understand how much of the conversation consisted of being given back things he had thought he could leave in the middle of the table.

* * *

Yuta did not come down while they were there.

Neither did Yuka. Mau heard a door farther along the upper corridor and looked toward the stair before he could stop himself.

Rimuru saw.

[R] "Do you need to go?"

[M] "She said she'd see me after. I should let her have the conversation."

He looked at the clean place on the table.

[M] "Yuta will want one with me."

[R] "I imagine so."

[M] "I don't know how I'm going to answer him."

[R] "One question at a time. You don't have to prepare all of it while he's upstairs."

Mau looked at him, and for a moment an old shape almost returned around the present words. A person who had said something like that before. A roof which might have been another roof. The feeling of knowing where the next joke would come from.

It did not become a memory.

He did not tell Rimuru to wait while he tried to make it one.

[M] "Can we talk about something else for a minute?"

Rimuru leaned back.

[R] "Probably. I don't know whether you'll enjoy it."

[M] "Try."

[R] "You've made that bit of the table cleaner than the rest. Rem's going to notice."

Mau looked down.

Rimuru looked at the clean rectangle and then at the long, very long remaining table.

[M] "Don't tell her."

Rimuru raised an eyebrow.

Mau heard himself.

For one shocked second neither of them moved.

Then Mau covered his face with his clean right hand, and Rimuru let out a short, helpless laugh which did not last long enough to become comfortable.

[M] "That was terrible."

[R] "Yes."

They sat until the laugh was gone.

Mau took the damp cloths to the basin. He rinsed the dirt out carefully and hung them on the line where the kitchen air could reach them.

When he came back, Rimuru was still in the chair.
