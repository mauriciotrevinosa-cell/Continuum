# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 5 — At the Curtain

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S6E44
**Continuity state:** Day467 morning. Ori, Umaru and Milim play the existing Fallow dice game with Rem in the Fort extension. Yuka approaches voluntarily and meets a real refusal of company. Rem, an established Fort member, distinguishes their right to distance from an attempted claim that Yuka has lost her place in the house. No forced friendship or forgiveness. No cushion, register, gift or other possession is destroyed or erased. Yuka's cushion ends on a common-room chair outside the Fort curtain, still hers; her membership is not formally revoked. The scene adds no knowledge of the private future-family talk, intimacy or cancer, and neither Mau nor Frieren appears.

*When: Day467, morning. The Fort in the common-room extension. The little bone dice from Fallow are making more noise than the people playing with them.*

Yuka heard the cup first.

That was why she stopped. She had come downstairs late, found that someone had kept breakfast covered for her, and eaten enough of it to avoid being asked whether she intended to. Now she stood with the empty bowl in her hands, listening to the familiar rattle beyond the curtain.

[U] "You're keeping that?"

[Re] "I am."

[U] "It's a one."

[Re] "I have four more chances to improve its company."

[U] "You don't. You have two throws."

A short silence.

[Re] "Then this is going to be difficult."

Yuka almost smiled.

She took the bowl to the washing place, rinsed it and left it where the clean ones went. By the time she returned, she had decided that she would only ask. She would not come in as if yesterday had been an unfortunate interruption in a game they were all ready to resume.

The curtain was drawn back at one side.

Umaru sat with the slate across her knees. Ori was opposite her, holding the dice cup in both hands. Milim lay on her stomach between two cushions, her chin propped on her wrists. Rem sat nearer the entrance with her legs folded to one side.

There was room.

Yuka wished she had not noticed that first.

[Yk] "Morning."

Ori's hands stopped.

One die was still rolling. It struck the edge of Milim's cushion, turned once more and settled with a six showing.

Nobody counted it.

[Re] "Morning, Yuka."

Umaru looked down at the slate. There was already a tally on it, though not a very long one. The marks were pressed harder into the surface than they needed to be.

Yuka rested her fingers against the curtain edge.

[Yk] "Are you playing the Fallow one?"

[U] "Yes."

[Yk] "I thought so."

Ori put the cup down over the die nearest her.

Yuka waited for a question. For the familiar argument about whether she had brought a toll. For Umaru to tell her she was in the light, which would have meant she could move farther in.

Milim lifted her head.

[Mi] "Is Mau coming?"

[Yk] "No. I came to see you."

Milim looked toward Ori before lowering her chin again.

Yuka's cushion was propped against the wall beyond them. She could see the corner with the small uneven stitch she always found beneath her thumb. She had sat on it often enough that one side had become flatter than the other.

[Yk] "Could I play?"

[O] "No."

The word was quiet and complete.

Yuka nodded before she had quite taken it in.

[Yk] "All right."

Ori looked down at the cup again. Her knuckles had begun to show white around its rim.

Yuka stayed at the curtain.

[Yk] "Could I just sit for a minute? I don't have to—"

[O] "I don't want you to sit with me."

Yuka stopped.

Umaru rubbed out a mark on the slate with the heel of her hand. The little scrape went on much longer than the mark needed.

[U] "We already started."

Rem looked toward her. Umaru kept rubbing.

It would have been easier if there had been a missing place, a limit on the dice, something Yuka could put right by sitting farther back or waiting for the next turn. There was only Ori looking at her, exhausted by the fact that she had needed to say it twice.

[Yk] "I'm sorry. I heard you."

She took her fingers off the curtain.

Umaru put the slate down.

[U] "You don't get to do that."

Yuka looked back.

[U] "Come here and sound like that and make us be the ones who—"

She stopped. The end of the sentence had arrived somewhere she did not want to follow it.

[Yk] "I didn't mean to make you do anything."

[U] "You didn't mean any of it, apparently."

Rem turned toward her.

[Re] "Umaru."

[U] "What?"

[Re] "Let her answer what you actually asked."

Umaru's mouth tightened. She pulled the slate back onto her knees but did not pick up the chalk.

Yuka could have gone then. The curtain was beside her hand. Behind her, the common room had chairs and a door into the yard and several ordinary places to stand where nobody had said they did not want her.

She looked at Ori instead.

[Yk] "I meant being sorry. I know that doesn't mean you have to let me in."

Ori watched her face.

[O] "You went with him."

Yuka nodded.

[O] "I was still there."

The cup made a small sound against the floor as she set it down too hard. Milim sat up. Not toward Yuka. Toward Ori, close enough that her knee touched the other girl's cushion.

[Yk] "I know."

[O] "You saw me. You knew what I was asking."

[Yk] "Yes."

[O] "And you knew the other thing."

She did not say the kiss. She did not have to.

Yuka held her own wrist. The bracelet shifted under her fingers; she moved her hand away from it at once.

[Yk] "I wanted to help him get some air. And I wanted to get away too."

Ori's face changed.

[Yk] "I should have thought about you before I went. I didn't do enough of that."

[O] "You were both looking for somewhere you could be happy."

Yuka did not answer quickly enough.

Ori looked down.

[O] "And I was in here."

Rem put her own dice back beside the cup, one at a time. She did not reach across the game for Ori's hands. Milim had already found one of them.

[Mi] "She asked you not to play."

[Yk] "I know."

[Mi] "Then don't make her keep saying it."

Yuka looked at her. Milim's face was still marked by the way she had rubbed it yesterday. There was no triumph in it. She looked as though she wished the person in the doorway were somebody she could bring closer without looking at Ori first.

[Yk] "I won't."

She stepped back.

Umaru stood so suddenly that the slate slid down her legs. She caught it against one knee, put it aside and reached for the cushion by the wall.

Yuka knew which one before she lifted it.

[U] "This can go with you."

She brought it toward the entrance, holding it in both arms. The familiar corner pointed at the floor.

Yuka did not put out her hands.

[Yk] "Do you want me to take it?"

[U] "It isn't your place any more."

Rem got up.

Not between them as if she expected a blow. Beside Umaru, where she could see the girl's face instead of speaking over her head.

[Re] "What isn't?"

Umaru stared at the cushion.

[Re] "The game this morning, or her place here?"

[U] "She doesn't get to come back and pretend—"

[Re] "You can tell her you don't want to play with her. You already have. You cannot tell her she's lost her home."

[U] "I'm talking about the Fort."

[Re] "You said it wasn't her place any more."

Umaru's grip tightened. For a moment Rem thought she might throw the cushion. She did not. She held it so hard that the middle bent against her stomach.

[U] "It was supposed to be us."

The words came out without any of the office she had tried to put round them.

[U] "We had things. She'd come in and we'd talk about the numbers, or play, or she would tell me he'd done something stupid and—"

She looked at Yuka.

[U] "You could tell me when he needed a different number on a page. You couldn't tell me this."

[Yk] "I was afraid you'd hate me."

[U] "Well, I don't feel better because you were afraid."

Yuka's eyes stung.

[Yk] "I know."

Rem stayed beside Umaru until the cushion stopped bending. Then she held out her hands for it.

Umaru looked at her, looked away and passed it over.

[Re] "This belongs to Yuka. It can stay in the Fort, or she can take it somewhere else. Neither means her name has been taken out."

[O] "Are you going to make us have her here?"

Rem turned.

Ori had got up too. Her hand had slipped out of Milim's. She stood behind the low part of the frame, looking at Rem with a fear which had begun as anger and had not quite managed to remain it.

[Re] "Not in your game. Not sitting beside you when you've asked her not to."

[O] "Then why—"

[Re] "Because she lives here too. She'll still come in for dinner. She'll still walk down the corridor. You'll be around her sometimes, even when you don't want to play."

Ori looked at Yuka.

Yuka wished she had been looking at anybody else.

[O] "I don't want to be nice about it."

Rem considered her answer before giving it.

[Re] "You don't have to tell her you're glad she came. You don't have to be glad."

Ori's mouth tightened.

[Re] "I'm not going to help you send her away from the house."

Yuka wiped one eye with the heel of her hand. She had not wanted an adult to have to stand beside her and tell her friends that she was still allowed a cushion. It felt very young. It felt worse because nobody was treating it as a joke she could complain about until it passed.

[Yk] "I can go and sit somewhere else. I wasn't going to stay after you said no."

Umaru looked toward the curtain. Yuka had already moved outside it.

For a moment the two of them looked at the same small fact.

Then Umaru looked away.

[U] "Not this morning."

Yuka nodded.

[Yk] "All right."

[U] "I don't know about tomorrow. Don't ask me that now."

[Yk] "I wasn't going to."

She wanted to. She wanted a day she could wait for, something to put on a page, an answer she could follow correctly and come back with as proof that she had done enough.

Ori bent down and collected the dice Milim had knocked aside when sitting up. One had gone beneath the edge of Rem's cushion. She put it with the others without looking toward Yuka again.

[Yk] "Do you want me to stop coming to find you for a while?"

Ori held the dice in her palm.

[O] "I want to stop having to work out what to say when you do."

Yuka looked at her for a moment.

[Yk] "Okay."

She did not ask her to make it smaller.

Rem went through the curtain with her, carrying the cushion by two opposite corners.

In the common room she stopped beside a chair and looked at Yuka.

[Re] "Here?"

Yuka nodded.

Rem set it on the seat.

Yuka stood behind the chair, with both hands on its back. Somebody was chopping something in the kitchen. The even little sounds kept going through a pause in the voices behind the Fort curtain.

[Yk] "You don't have to stay with me."

[Re] "I know."

Rem sat on the edge of the bench nearby anyway. Yuka pulled the chair out and sat down on the cushion.

For a while she looked toward the yard.

[Yk] "I thought if I only asked, it would be all right. Even if they said no."

Rem rested her hands in her lap.

[Re] "It still hurts when they do."

Yuka nodded. She pressed one thumb into the uneven stitch at the cushion's corner and found exactly the place it had always been.

[Yk] "Are you angry too?"

[Re] "Yes."

Yuka kept looking at the yard.

[Re] "I was there yesterday. I heard Ori. I'm not going to pretend I didn't."

Yuka nodded again.

Rem did not add another question. She sat until Yuka had stopped gripping the cushion, then stood when her name came from beyond the curtain.

[Mi] "Rem? Is it your turn?"

Rem looked back at Yuka.

[Re] "I haven't finished losing yet."

Yuka's mouth moved. Not enough to become a smile, but enough that she could breathe without the same sharpness in her throat.

[Yk] "Don't keep the one."

[Re] "I may have committed myself."

She went back through the curtain. It settled behind her, leaving the same opening as before.

Inside, Umaru asked where they had stopped. Milim said it had been her turn. Ori said no, it had been Rem's, and picked up the cup to show her the dice she had kept.

Yuka could hear all of it from the chair.

She did not go back in.
