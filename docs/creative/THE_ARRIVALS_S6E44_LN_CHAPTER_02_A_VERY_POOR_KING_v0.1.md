# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 6 — A Very Poor King

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S6E44
**Continuity state:** Day467 late morning into noon, following5 and before7. Yuka tells Mau only the present result at the Fort, not the full private conversation with grandparents. They take a consensual walk inside the western boundary after announcing route and one-hour return directly. Their relationship continues after the public revelation, with laughter, a shared invented story and a private kiss; no sex, new promises of family, cure or restored memories. *When: Day467, late morning. The common room. Mau finds Yuka standing beside a chair which has acquired a cushion from somewhere she had wanted to stay.*

He recognised the cushion before he understood why it was there.

Yuka had one hand against its corner. She was looking at the little curtain, pinned back at the edge where the voices inside could come through. Bone dice clicked in the cup. Milim said something about a rule being unfair.

Yuka took her hand away.

[M] "Do you want to sit?"

She shook her head.

He looked at the chair, then at her.

[Yk] "Not there."

He did not ask again.

For a moment she looked as if she might explain all of it. Instead she said Ori did not want her to keep coming over for a while.

[M] "Did she say for how long?"

[Yk] "No."

He nodded.

[Yk] "Don't go in and ask her."

Mau looked at her properly.

[M] "I wasn't going to."

She let a breath out. It had been sitting high in her chest, ready to turn into the wrong answer to anything he said.

[Yk] "I know. Sorry."

He held out his right hand, low between them.

She looked at it for a long time before putting hers in it.

* * *

The roof had stayed empty the night before.

Nobody had fetched the pot. Upstairs, the hour for crackers had come and gone without the women gathering round Seiko's table. There had been people going to rooms with small plates, others coming back with them almost untouched, and a great deal of care taken over ordinary doors.

This morning the house had begun the day anyway.

Mau could hear someone carrying wood outside. The chopping board in the kitchen had a rhythm to it. He had thought more than once about going to ask for a job, then wondered whether he wanted to help or merely to be given something he could finish.

Yuka's hand was still in his.

[Yk] "Can we go for a walk?"

[M] "Yes."

He waited while she found her coat. It was on the hook this time, both sleeves where they belonged. She put it on without looking at the chair beside the Fort again.

Rimuru was by the kitchen door when they were ready.

Mau stopped where he could see both their faces.

[M] "The western path. Inside the line. An hour, and we'll come back through here."

Rimuru glanced toward Yuka. She nodded.

[R] "All right."

He did not look pleased about it. He also did not make Mau stand there until he found a better expression to leave with.

They went out on foot.

* * *

It was cold in the shade and almost warm in the sun.

Yuka kept choosing the sunny side of the path, even when it meant walking round a patch of scrub rather than taking the clear way beside it. Mau noticed on the third turn and began choosing it first.

She looked at him.

[Yk] "You don't have to follow all the silly things I do."

[M] "I wanted the sun."

[Yk] "You were walking in the other bit."

[M] "I hadn't tried this bit."

She smiled, almost against her will.

He felt it arrive through the small movement of her hand before he saw it properly.

There was no place they needed to reach. They kept to the path they had reported, slowing where the ground rose and turning back while the house still showed through the gaps behind them. The boundary lay farther out. Mau did not go looking for the farthest part of the hour.

Yuka stopped beside a flat stone with a warm edge.

[Yk] "Here?"

He sat first so that she could decide where she wanted to be beside him.

She chose close.

* * *

For a while she told him about the game.

Not the part he had already guessed. The ordinary part. The die Milim had wanted to roll again, Rem waiting to see whether anyone else would notice, Umaru already preparing an argument before it went back into the cup.

She caught herself smiling and stopped.

[M] "What?"

[Yk] "Nothing."

He looked at her.

[Yk] "I can still think it's funny."

[M] "Yes."

[Yk] "They didn't want me there."

He let the two sentences stay beside one another.

Yuka looked out over the grass. A few leaves had caught along the low edge of the stone. She moved one with the side of her boot, then moved it back when she realised she was arranging them.

[Yk] "I don't want you to make her let me in."

[M] "I heard you."

[Yk] "I just don't know what to do with the time where I'd have gone there."

Mau looked down at their joined hands.

[M] "We can sit here for some of it."

She nodded and leaned into him.

* * *

The story began because he asked her whether the bird beside the path looked offended.

Yuka considered it.

[Yk] "That's just its face."

[M] "You've met it before?"

[Yk] "We're very close."

The bird turned, pecked at something neither could see and hopped out of sight behind the scrub.

[M] "It didn't say goodbye."

[Yk] "It's a king. It doesn't have to."

He looked at her.

[M] "Is it."

[Yk] "A very important one."

He shifted on the stone, putting his back against the part which had kept more of the morning warmth.

[M] "What does it rule?"

Yuka looked at the empty place where the bird had been. At the leaf she had moved twice.

[Yk] "That."

[M] "The leaf?"

[Yk] "The whole area round the leaf."

[M] "Ambitious."

She pressed her lips together.

[Yk] "He inherited it."

Mau nodded with the solemnity this required.

[M] "Difficult to live up to the previous king."

[Yk] "The previous king was much bigger."

[M] "How did they lose the territory?"

She looked toward the scrub, where a little shower of dry leaves had begun falling over the bird's head.

[Yk] "Weather."

He laughed.

She leaned against his shoulder and began the next part before he had finished.

* * *

The kingdom acquired a bridge because Mau would not accept that a king could have nothing to complain about except weather.

Yuka made the bridge a twig. Mau made it a very badly built twig. She objected that he had not seen the plans, and he said the plans had been eaten by the previous king.

[Yk] "He was a good ruler."

[M] "He ate the public works."

[Yk] "He was hungry."

[M] "Then it should have been in the budget."

She put her hand over her mouth.

He looked pleased with himself, which made it harder not to laugh.

They went on until neither could remember which of them had invented the war with the second leaf. It had begun as a misunderstanding about a boundary and ended with the king sitting on the bridge while everyone else waited for him to get off it.

[Yk] "He's thinking."

[M] "He should think somewhere people aren't trying to cross."

[Yk] "You don't understand leadership."

[M] "I'm learning from a bird you've already admitted doesn't like us."

She laughed properly then. Leaned forward with her forehead against his shoulder and held the front of his coat while it passed through her.

Mau stopped trying to add another line.

He liked the weight of her there. The warmth finding its way through both coats. The absurd pleasure of having said something that made her lose her grip on the sentence she had been preparing.

She lifted her head, still smiling.

[Yk] "That was terrible."

[M] "The bridge?"

[Yk] "All of it."

[M] "You made the king."

[Yk] "I didn't say I was innocent."

* * *

He kissed her while she was still close enough to decide whether she wanted it.

She did.

Her hand loosened in his coat and settled flat against it instead. When he drew back, she followed a little, then stopped with her forehead against his.

[Yk] "I wanted this."

[M] "The story?"

[Yk] "This morning. You. Somewhere I didn't have to keep trying to find the right thing to say."

He brushed his thumb once over the side of her hand.

[M] "I wanted to see you smile."

Her mouth moved into one for a moment, then faltered.

[Yk] "I keep thinking I'm not supposed to feel better."

He waited.

[Yk] "Like if I stop being miserable for a minute, it means I didn't understand what we did."

Mau looked at her, then at the hands she had begun to draw back into her lap.

[M] "I don't know how to make it fair. I don't want you to have to prove you know it hurts by hurting all the time."

She took a breath. Held it. Let it go.

[Yk] "I don't want that for you either."

He nodded.

They sat together until the sunny edge had moved off one of her boots.

* * *

They were back before the hour ended.

Mau stopped by the kitchen door and told Rimuru so directly. Rimuru looked at the clock, nodded and asked whether the path had been muddy.

[M] "A little at the low part. We went round."

[R] "Good. Someone put clean things out near the door. I'd rather not identify them by your footprints."

Yuka glanced down at her boots and wiped the edge of one more carefully.

Her cushion remained on the chair outside the Fort.

She looked at it, then at the curtain. Someone inside asked for the cup to be passed back. A game had continued while they were outside, with no empty turn waiting for her.

Mau's hand loosened so she could choose what to do with hers.

She kept it in his for another moment.

Then she went to the chair, picked up the cushion and laid it on the clean side of its seat, where it would not fall when someone brushed past.

[Yk] "I'm going to find something to eat."

[M] "I'll come with you."

She looked back at him.

[Yk] "If there's bread, the king gets first choice."

[M] "The king can get his own."

She laughed once and went through the kitchen door.

Mau followed after checking that neither of them had left a footprint on the clean cloth by the step.
