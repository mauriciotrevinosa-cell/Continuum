# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 7 — Put It Down First

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S6E45
**Continuity state:** Day467, afternoon, after Mau and Yuka's brief outing and return in chapter6. Mau and Yuta attempt wooden-sword practice, developing the touch-and-exit work of V33#51. Yuta pauses the exercise rather than use it to vent his anger. They address what Mau concealed and what Yuta was allowed to believe, without making love, memory restoration or ending the relationship a condition of family membership. Yuta retains affection for Mau and protection of Yuka/Frieren; this conversation does not resolve the fracture. The floor-86 katana remains sheathed. No new technique, cure, injury, memory recovery, stat spending or change to shadow counts occurs. Frieren's future attack has not happened.

*When: Day467, afternoon. The yard, after Mau has come back inside to say that he is home. Yuta has brought out two wooden swords.*

The dent was still there.

Mau put his thumb against it, halfway up the wooden blade. He had found it before nearly every practice, as if checking that somebody had not replaced the sword while it was leaning against the wall.

Yuta waited beside the bench.

[Yt] "You don't have to."

Mau looked up.

[M] "Do you want to?"

Yuta turned the second sword in his hands. Once, with the grip passing from one palm to the other.

[Yt] "I thought I did when I picked these up."

It would have been easier if he had made that sound like an answer.

Mau set his real blade on the bench, still in its black scabbard, where neither of them would step on it. He put his coat beside it.

[M] "We can try."

Yuta nodded. He went to the clear part of the yard and waited for Mau to choose his distance.

No lines today. Mau could still find the space between the old ones, though the ground no longer showed where they had been.

[Yt] "Same beginning. Touch, then leave the return."

[M] "Which return?"

[Yt] "Look."

Mau raised the wooden sword.

* * *

He got the touch.

That part had become something he could do without losing track of the man behind the blade. A small turn, pressure against the guard, his rear foot coming with him instead of being left to drag the rest of his body out afterward.

Then Yuta changed his wrist.

Mau was already going left. The return had been coming straight toward the place he had left, until it wasn't. Yuta let the point turn across the new line and stopped it short of Mau's sleeve.

Mau stopped too.

[M] "That one's different."

[Yt] "It can be."

He lowered the point. There had been no force behind it which needed somewhere else to go.

Mau looked at his own feet.

[Yt] "You chose before I did."

[M] "You told me not to wait for it to arrive."

[Yt] "You don't wait. You watch while you move."

Mau went back to the beginning. Adjusted the grip so that the wood rested against the parts of his left hand which could hold it, without trying to close the other two fingers farther than they would go.

The second time he made the exit smaller.

Too small at first. He felt himself about to lean out of the return instead of moving his feet, corrected it, and brought the sword across his own sight for half a moment.

Yuta's blade stopped again.

Mau lowered his hands.

[M] "I saw that."

Yuta did not answer.

He was looking at the place where the two wooden guards had met. His mouth had tightened. Mau waited for the correction, then realized Yuta had not been watching the end of the movement.

[M] "Yuta?"

Yuta stepped back.

[Yt] "Put it down for a minute."

Mau let the point fall toward the ground.

[Yt] "On the bench. Mine too."

He went first. Laid his sword beside the coat, with nothing left in his hands when he turned back.

* * *

Mau put his wooden sword next to it.

They stood close enough to the bench that sitting would have been easy. Neither sat.

[Yt] "I keep looking for what I missed."

Mau looked toward the post by the woodpile.

The rope was not there. It had not stayed there after the morning they used it. Nothing in the yard had been arranged to keep that morning waiting for them.

[M] "When we practised?"

[Yt] "Then. At the table. When she brought that drawing to show me."

He rubbed one palm against the other, stopping when he noticed the movement.

[Yt] "I asked her yesterday whether she would have told me if I'd asked the right thing. I didn't put it that way. I think that's what she heard."

[M] "Would I have?"

Yuta looked at him.

[M] "Is that what you want to ask me?"

[Yt] "Yes."

Mau had been thinking about Yuta asking since the previous afternoon. Several possible conversations had become very clear while Yuta was not there to take his own part in them.

None helped now.

[M] "I don't know."

Yuta's eyes dropped to the swords.

[M] "If you had asked whether we were together, I think I'd have said yes. But I let you ask things near it. Whether she was all right. Whether we were going out. And I gave you answers that were true without telling you."

[Yt] "You knew what I thought those answers meant."

[M] "Most of the time. Yes."

The qualifier sounded very small. He did not try to use it again.

Yuta sat on the end of the bench. The wood gave a dry creak beneath him. Mau remained beside the coat, turning the loose edge of his own sleeve between his fingers.

[Yt] "I was there when you came out of the tower."

[M] "I know."

[Yt] "You know because someone told you."

Mau stopped moving the sleeve.

[M] "Yes."

Yuta looked out across the yard.

[Yt] "I put your sword back in its sheath. You were on the floor. Frieren was beside you. I had to look at where my hands were going because I couldn't stop looking at you."

Mau did not turn toward the black scabbard. He kept looking at Yuta.

[Yt] "I was glad when you could hold a wooden one again. I was glad there could be something we did which wasn't somebody trying to find out how much of you had broken."

[M] "So was I."

Yuta nodded once. He did not look pleased to have found something they agreed on.

[Yt] "I know you were. I don't want to spend the next month turning every good hour into a trick you played on me."

Mau's throat tightened.

[M] "I wasn't doing that."

[Yt] "I know."

The answer came quickly enough to leave the rest exposed.

[Yt] "I know you weren't picking up a sword so I'd be too happy to ask. That isn't what I'm angry about."

* * *

A door opened near the kitchen.

They both turned. Someone needed the path beside the woodpile; they passed through it with an empty basket and continued toward the workshop. Mau moved his boot clear of the handle of the water jug.

Yuta watched him do it.

[Yt] "I thought we could do this and talk afterward. I can't keep holding the sword while I decide which question I'm asking."

[M] "You stopped it. Both times."

[Yt] "That's why I put it down. Before that stopped being enough."

Mau looked at the old dent again.

Yuta followed his glance.

[Yt] "I don't mean I was going to hit you. I mean you shouldn't have to work out whether a correction is about your feet or about my granddaughter."

Mau nodded. Sat at the other end of the bench, leaving the swords between them.

For a little while there was only the jug to move. Its base had settled crookedly in a patch of earth beside the bench. Mau set it flat.

[Yt] "You were frightened of us telling you what to do."

[M] "Yes."

[Yt] "And while you were frightened, you left Frieren trying to work out what she could ask without frightening you more."

Mau looked down.

[M] "I know that now."

[Yt] "You knew she was waiting before yesterday."

He could remember the garden clearly. Not because it had returned from somewhere lost. The book on her lap, the room she had given his questions, the careful way she had not asked him for the thing she wanted most.

[M] "Yes."

Yuta waited.

[M] "I wanted to be able to talk to her without the rest of it arriving first. I thought if I told her, we wouldn't get that afternoon."

[Yt] "You got it."

Mau pressed his right hand against his knee.

[Yt] "She went home with a different one."

He stayed quiet until he could answer without asking Yuta to make that gentler.

[M] "I should have told her before we sat there. Not tried to find out whether I could give her something good enough to make the other part hurt less."

Yuta drew a slow breath. His shoulders had not relaxed.

[Yt] "You don't have to tell me what you and Yuka say when you're alone. I haven't asked her for that either. I needed to know what we were all living beside. She needed to know."

Mau nodded.

[M] "I kept calling all of it private. Even the part that wasn't only ours."

* * *

Yuta leaned forward, elbows against his knees.

[Yt] "What are you expecting me to do now?"

Mau looked at him. He had been preparing for what Yuta might demand, not what he himself had already begun asking without words.

[M] "I don't know."

[Yt] "You looked at me when you came out, like you were waiting to find out whether you could still stand here."

Mau almost denied it. He could remember exactly where he had stopped in the doorway.

[M] "Yes."

Yuta rubbed his thumb over the side of his ring.

[Yt] "I'm not going to pretend I don't know you because I'm angry with you."

Mau let out a breath which had begun to hurt at the top of his chest.

[Yt] "But I can't make it feel the same by taking these outside. I thought I might manage the practice. I can't today."

[M] "Okay."

[Yt] "And if Yuka asks how this went, don't tell her I'm all right with it because we sat here."

[M] "I won't."

Yuta looked at him until he looked back.

[Yt] "I love her. I don't know what she needs from me in every part of this. I'm trying to listen without becoming so afraid for her that I stop hearing her."

[M] "She knows you love her."

[Yt] "She asked whether we'd make her leave."

Mau's hand lifted from his knee.

Yuta shook his head before he spoke.

[Yt] "We said no. Maki said it very clearly. I'm telling you because this is what I'm doing while you're trying to decide whether I'm going to take her away from you."

Mau swallowed.

[M] "I wasn't going to ask you to talk her out of being scared."

[Yt] "I haven't finished."

Mau stopped.

[Yt] "I'm frightened too. I don't think she knows how much. Not only of you remembering and choosing differently. Of her deciding she has to prove she wasn't wrong every time something hurts."

Mau looked toward the house.

He knew the expression Yuta meant, though the sentence belonged to someone who had known her longer. The chin lifting. The answer coming before the question had finished because she could hear the one she expected beneath it.

[M] "I don't want that for her."

[Yt] "Then let her say when it hurts. Even if you can't fix what she's telling you."

Mau looked back.

[M] "I can listen. I can't promise she'll never be hurt."

[Yt] "I know."

Yuta sat back. He looked tired now, and the tiredness did not replace the anger. Both remained in his face.

[Yt] "I don't want a promise you can't keep. I've heard enough of those."

* * *

They did not pick the swords up again to use them.

Mau turned his over to check the grip before putting it away. One narrow curl of wood had lifted near the lower end, where the wrapping had worn back. He had been rubbing his thumb against it without noticing.

Yuta held his hand out.

Mau gave him the sword.

[Yt] "Not that one next time. We'll smooth this first."

The word reached Mau before he could decide not to hold on to it.

[M] "Next time?"

Yuta looked at the curl. Worked it gently free with his thumbnail rather than leaving it to catch somebody's palm.

[Yt] "I'll tell you when I can. Don't come out with it every morning and wait for me to be ready."

[M] "Okay."

[Yt] "You can use the other one for your footwork. Leave this until we've fixed it."

He laid the damaged sword on top of the bench, separate from the one going back against the wall.

Mau lifted his coat and the sheathed blade. The black scabbard caught briefly against the sleeve. He freed it, took the coat under his other arm and looked toward the door.

[M] "Do you want me to leave you here?"

[Yt] "I'm going in."

Yuta picked up the water jug. Some had spilled while they talked; the bottom of it had left a dark, uneven circle in the earth.

[M] "I'll take that."

[Yt] "You have enough in your hands."

Mau looked down at them.

He did.

Yuta took the jug. Mau put the sound wooden sword back in its place, point down, and left the other flat on the bench where nobody would mistake it for one ready to use.

They went through the door a few steps apart.

At the basin Yuta began rinsing the jug. Mau stopped to move a bowl out of the place where he meant to set it. Yuta put the jug there when it was clean, then went upstairs to find his wife.

Mau stayed long enough to dry the water he had left along the edge.
