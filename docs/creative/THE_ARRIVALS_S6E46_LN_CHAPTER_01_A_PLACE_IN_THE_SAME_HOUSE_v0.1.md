# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 9 — A Place in the Same House

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S6E46
**Continuity state:** Day468 morning. Frieren chooses the garden bench for her book and brings her staff from the east room; it remains an ordinary established possession, not a newly obtained weapon. Maki joins by invitation, without her spear. They speak as friends and family who are hurt in different ways. Maki acknowledges Yuka's concealment without expelling or deciding for her; Frieren does not receive intimate details of the romance. No attack or threat is made here. *When: Day468, morning. The bench beside the garden beds. Frieren brought a book, the folded cloth to keep it off the damp wood and the staff which had been standing beside the east-room wall.*

She had chosen to come down.

That was something she was trying to remember while people took care not to seem surprised to see her. She had got up, found her shoes, put the book beneath her arm and walked through a house where everyone had acquired a reason not to ask her how she had slept.

Fern had asked whether she wanted company.

Frieren had said not yet.

So the bench was empty beside her now. The book lay open on the cloth. Her staff leaned against the post where the old length of garden string had been gathered clear of the seat.

She read the same instruction twice, understood it both times and made no attempt to try it.

At the kitchen corner Maki stopped carrying an empty basket.

Frieren could feel her decide whether to keep walking.

[F] "You can sit."

Maki looked across at her.

[Mk] "I didn't say anything."

[F] "You stopped very loudly."

Maki looked down at the basket, then back. A small, tired smile reached her mouth.

She came over.

* * *

The basket had one handle which folded farther than the other.

Maki put it on the ground, saw the low handle fall and lifted it so it would not get trodden on. It fell again. She left it there.

Frieren had seen the whole thing.

[F] "It prefers that."

[Mk] "I'm beginning to understand."

They sat with the book open between them. From the kitchen came the sound of something being scraped into a bowl. A voice objected to the size of the bowl and was answered by somebody who seemed tired of having that explained to them.

Maki looked toward the sound.

[Mk] "Rem says the little pan is missing again."

[F] "It might be in the little cupboard."

[Mk] "Apparently that would be unreasonable."

Frieren looked down at the printed instruction.

She could remember mornings in which this would have been enough of a conversation to begin the day. The missing pan. An argument carried through the kitchen door. Waiting to tell Mau something about a spell he would pretend to understand less of than he did.

Maki's hands had settled on her knees.

[Mk] "You don't have to talk about it."

[F] "I know."

She closed the book with one finger holding her place.

[F] "That's becoming an odd thing to say when it's the only thing I can think about."

Maki looked at her.

Frieren kept her finger between the pages.

* * *

[F] "Have you spoken to him?"

[Mk] "Yuta did yesterday."

[F] "And?"

[Mk] "They talked. They didn't finish practising."

Frieren waited.

Maki did not give her the private sentences Yuta had brought home. He had told her enough to say that he was still angry, still worried, and not sure when holding a sword opposite Mau would become a thing he could enjoy again. That had been his evening, not a message for the rest of the house.

[Mk] "He isn't pretending he isn't hurt."

[F] "Yuta."

[Mk] "Either of them."

Frieren looked at the straw over the garden bed.

[F] "She is your granddaughter."

Maki's head moved a little.

[Mk] "Yes."

[F] "I know that. I keep thinking I should say something differently because of it."

[Mk] "What would you say?"

The question was quiet. Frieren did not mistake it for an invitation to be cruel without consequence.

[F] "That she knew."

Maki looked down at her hands.

[F] "He didn't know what he was losing. Not properly. I can be angry with him for what he chose after, but when he looks at me, I can see the place he can't reach."

She pressed her finger farther into the book until the pressure hurt.

[F] "She could reach it. She knew where I was."

[Mk] "Yes."

Frieren looked at her then.

She had been preparing for a defence. Something about fear or youth or a thing being difficult, as if any of those would make the other woman's face easier to look at.

Maki gave her none of them.

[Mk] "I asked her about that."

[F] "What did she say?"

Maki took a breath before answering.

[Mk] "That she saw you. That knowing didn't stop her wanting the next day."

Frieren's mouth tightened.

The words had made a shape she could understand. She almost wished they hadn't.

* * *

[F] "What do you want me to do with that?"

[Mk] "Nothing for her."

Maki turned toward her on the bench.

[Mk] "You asked me. I didn't want to give you something cleaner than what she said."

Frieren looked at the book again.

[F] "I don't have anything generous to say."

[Mk] "Then don't make something up for me."

She had heard those words from other people in other rooms. With Maki they did not feel like an instruction to produce the correct hurt before anyone would let her keep it.

She could be ungenerous here for a moment.

She could want what she wanted and still be sitting beside someone who loved the person she wanted it taken back from.

[F] "I want him to remember."

[Mk] "So do I."

[F] "Not only the things that happened. I want him to remember wanting to come upstairs. I want him to stop looking at his place as if taking it would be rude to somebody."

Maki watched her hand in the book.

[F] "I want to say his name without thinking about whether he's waiting for the rest of the question."

The wind moved through the bare part of the bed. A loose straw caught on the post, twisted and stayed.

[F] "I want that morning to have been something I misunderstood."

Maki's eyes had gone to the same place in the garden.

[Mk] "I wanted that when Milim came through the door."

[F] "I know."

They sat without making either wish into an answer.

* * *

Frieren asked whether Yuka would stay in the house.

The question came out too carefully. Maki heard everything arranged around the edges of it.

[Mk] "It's her home."

[F] "I didn't mean—"

[Mk] "I know."

She did not sound certain about what Frieren had meant. She sounded as if she wanted to let her finish explaining it herself.

Frieren looked toward the windows above the yard.

[F] "I mean I don't know how to be in the same room yet. I know she will come down for dinner. I know he will look up when she does. I sit there thinking about where I'll put my eyes before anyone has even brought the food."

Maki moved one hand off her knee, then put it back.

[Mk] "You don't have to sit through every meal to prove you can."

[F] "Then she gets to be there, and I—"

She stopped.

That was not what she had wanted to say. It was much closer to what she had been thinking.

Maki let the silence last long enough that Frieren could hear her own sentence without somebody changing it for her.

[Mk] "Your place doesn't become hers because you need to leave a room."

Frieren looked at the empty space at the end of the bench.

[F] "It feels as if it does."

[Mk] "I know."

This time Frieren believed she did.

* * *

She moved her finger out of the book.

There was a red line along the side of it. She rubbed it with her thumb until the sensation returned to an ordinary part of her hand.

Maki noticed but did not take the book away.

[F] "Are we going to stop being able to do this?"

[Mk] "Sit outside?"

[F] "Talk."

Maki looked at her.

[F] "I don't want every word about her to become something you have to stand in front of."

[Mk] "Some words might."

Frieren lowered her eyes.

[Mk] "I'm not going to pretend she isn't mine to make this easier. I can't do that to you either."

Frieren looked up again.

Maki was watching the little line on her finger, then her face. She looked tired in the way she did after a long morning with the spear, although there was no spear beside the bench and nobody had asked her to lift anything heavier than a basket.

[Mk] "I'm angry with people I love. I don't know how to do that neatly."

Frieren closed the book properly.

[F] "You've lived a whole lifetime with Yuta. I thought that might make it simpler."

[Mk] "It makes me very good at knowing when something is wrong. That's different from knowing what to say about it."

Frieren made a small sound. Almost a laugh.

[Mk] "Don't tell him I admitted that."

[F] "I think he's noticed."

Maki's smile came and went.

* * *

The basket was still there when Maki stood.

She bent to lift it and found that Frieren had put one foot in the space where the handle needed to rise.

[Mk] "Your foot."

Frieren moved it.

Maki waited instead of walking away immediately.

[Mk] "Do you want me to stay?"

Frieren looked at the book, the garden and the space she had chosen to bring herself into that morning.

[F] "Not now."

Maki nodded.

Frieren reached for the basket's folded handle and lifted it clear of the ground before Maki could catch it against the bench.

[F] "I'm glad you stopped."

Maki took the weight.

[Mk] "All right."

She went toward the workshop side of the yard. Frieren watched the basket swing, one handle still lower, and wondered how a person could feel grateful and abandoned by the same small departure.

Then she opened the book again.

The instruction was still the one she had understood twice and not attempted.

She read it for a third time. Put her hand on the staff beside her and let it rest there without lifting it.
