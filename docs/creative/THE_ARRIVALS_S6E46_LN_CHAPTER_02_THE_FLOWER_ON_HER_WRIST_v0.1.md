## Chapter 10 — The Flower on Her Wrist

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S6E46
**Continuity state:** Day468 afternoon. Yuka attempts another apology without knowing the private conversation9. Frieren recognises the mirrored lotus on the birthday bracelet as eternal love in her source world; Mau and Yuka did not know that meaning when he bought it, as explicitly established in S6E12 CH02. The recognition intensifies Frieren's grief but does not justify what she chooses. She intentionally casts a single narrow Zoltraak at Yuka's upper arm/shoulder. Mau reacts to the lift and gathering, takes one short step onto clear visible ground in the line and blocks the brief beam with his own Infinity. Maki is already closing from the workshop side and immediately prevents the staff following the target; Frieren ceases the supply, the beam dies before Mau closes Infinity. No reflection, house damage, second attack, new technique, stat spending or bodily wound. Head pressure is transient strain, not cured residual damage. Maki's threat is explicit; their family relationship is ruptured, not reconciled. The aftermath continues next chapter.

*When: Day468, afternoon. The garden bench is in shadow now. Frieren has moved the book to her other knee and left the staff leaning against the same post.*

Yuka stopped at the edge of the path.

She had not come with a speech this time. The first apology had been two words in a room full of people who had just found out what she had been keeping from them. She could remember every one of their faces and almost none of the things she had thought she ought to say next.

Now Frieren was alone.

The book was open. One hand rested across the page. She was looking at the beds rather than reading, and Yuka could see the place she would have to enter to make the older woman look up.

She waited until Frieren did.

[Yk] "Can I say something?"

Frieren's face became very still.

[F] "What?"

It was not an invitation Yuka could mistake for kindness.

She took one step nearer, still several paces from the bench, and stopped.

Across the yard Mau was carrying the small roll of wire from the gate to the workshop. Anko had asked him to leave it on the clear shelf inside, with its label still attached. He looked toward Yuka before passing the corner.

She knew he had seen where she was going.

He did not come over to answer for her.

* * *

[Yk] "I'm not asking you to tell me it's all right."

Frieren's hand moved on the page.

[F] "Then don't begin by telling me what I don't have to say."

Yuka looked down, swallowed and looked up again.

[Yk] "I knew you were waiting. I knew what it meant when you wanted to see him come back. I kept deciding that I could have a little longer before I had to say anything."

The words were easier in her room, with her grandmother asking them out of her. They sounded small here, in front of someone who had lived the other side of every day she was describing.

[Yk] "I hurt you. I don't have a way to explain that which makes it something else."

Frieren closed the book.

The quiet click of its cover made Yuka's shoulders move.

[F] "And now?"

[Yk] "I don't want to keep pretending I don't understand what I did."

[F] "You understood before."

Yuka nodded.

[F] "You just told me."

[Yk] "Yes."

For a while neither spoke.

Yuka could hear the wire shift against the shelf as Mau put it down inside the workshop. Somebody asked whether he had brought the label. He answered, close enough that the ordinary sound of his voice reached the path.

Frieren heard it too.

Her eyes moved past Yuka, then came back.

* * *

[F] "What do you want?"

Yuka drew a breath.

There were several answers she could have given which would have made the question less dangerous. That she wanted to help. That she wanted Frieren not to be hurt. All of them were true, and all of them had become ways to get near the answer she was afraid to say.

[Yk] "I want to be with him."

Frieren looked at her.

Yuka's hands had come up before she knew what to do with them. One caught the end of her other cuff. Pulled it clear of the little cord which had turned beneath it.

[Yk] "I also want him to be able to remember. I'm not going to stop him because I'm afraid of what he'll feel when he does."

Frieren did not hear the end of it properly.

The pale flower had turned into the light.

Small. Metal. Two mirrored shapes meeting in the centre, carried on an ordinary dark cord against a wrist she had known without ever looking at this part of it.

A mirrored lotus.

Her fingers tightened on the closed book.

* * *

[F] "Where did you get that?"

Yuka followed her eyes.

She looked down at the bracelet and, for an instant, felt the sea wall beneath her legs again. The little packet opened between them. Mau concentrating on the fastening with the fingers which would not quite do what he wanted.

[Yk] "My birthday."

Frieren stood.

The book slid from her knee onto the bench. It stayed there, one corner on the cloth, the other pressed against the post.

[F] "He gave it to you."

Yuka nodded.

There was no useful way to hide her wrist now. She lowered her hands instead, trying not to make the movement look like taking something away.

[F] "Do you know what it means?"

[Yk] "What?"

[F] "The flower."

Yuka looked down again.

[Yk] "I don't know its name. He said he thought of me."

Frieren's face changed.

Not very much. A small movement near the mouth, as if something had been held there and had finally come loose.

[F] "Eternal love."

Yuka went still.

[F] "In my world. That's what it means."

The little cord felt suddenly too tight, although nothing about it had changed.

[Yk] "I didn't know."

Frieren was looking at the flower rather than her face.

[Yk] "He didn't ask the seller. I don't think he—"

[F] "Don't tell me what he thought."

Yuka stopped.

* * *

There had been a room upstairs.

A shelf which never stayed where either of them put it. A foot under the blanket, colder than it had any right to be. A ridiculous morning he would have filled with complaints until she let him help her get up.

Longer.

She had been afraid of the word even while she loved it. Afraid of what he would do to make it true, of how much of himself he might spend trying to give her a life he did not know whether he could live.

Now the word seemed to be lying on somebody else's wrist.

It was a flower. A purchased object. Yuka had said she did not know.

Frieren could understand those things and still feel the terrible movement beneath them, the part of her which had stopped looking for somewhere to put the hurt and begun looking for something it could make stop.

[F] "You had somewhere to stand. With us."

[Yk] "I know."

[F] "You didn't have to take mine."

Yuka's eyes filled.

[Yk] "I didn't want you to have nowhere."

She had meant it as a protest against the thing she had done. It reached Frieren as another person explaining what they had wanted while she stood in the place left over.

Her hand closed on the staff.

* * *

Mau had come out of the workshop.

He saw Frieren standing, the staff lifting from the post. Saw Yuka's shoulders drawn in and the distance between them, clear path with nobody crossing it.

The gathering at the end of the staff was very small.

It was also pointed at Yuka.

He moved before the light left it.

One short step, into the clear stretch he could see between the two women. His right foot landed first. He turned his shoulder into the line and opened the familiar interval in front of his own body.

Frieren had chosen the place below Yuka's shoulder. Had gathered the spell to strike flesh, to make the girl draw back and stop reaching toward a life Frieren could no longer bear seeing her touch.

Mau was there when she released it.

The Zoltraak met the distance before it met him.

* * *

The light did not finish crossing.

For less than a breath it hung in the place where the interval would not end. Mau felt the demand arrive through his head, sharp at the left edge, the world trying to become too detailed in one narrow line.

Behind him Yuka made a sound.

He could not turn to her yet.

[C] *Hold the line. Don't move it.*

Maki reached Frieren from the workshop side.

She had been coming toward the path since the staff began to rise. Her hand closed round Frieren's wrist and kept it exactly where it was, not letting the tip follow Yuka or swing across the house.

[Mk] "Stop."

Frieren saw Mau.

The spell ceased under her own hand. The thin place of light dwindled, lost its length and went out without striking anything beyond it.

Only then did Mau let Infinity close.

He had to put his weight back over his feet. The pressure at the side of his head stayed for a moment after the light was gone.

There was no hole in the wall. No mark through Yuka's coat. The book lay on the bench where it had fallen.

Yuka was shaking.

* * *

Mau turned toward her.

She was staring past his shoulder. Her right hand had gone to the upper part of her other arm, not because anything had hit it, because she had understood where it had been meant to go.

[M] "Yuka."

She looked at him.

[M] "Did it reach you?"

She shook her head.

He waited until she did it again, clearly, before looking back.

Maki had not released Frieren's wrist.

The two women stood so close now that the staff lay between them rather than pointing at either of the people on the path.

[Mk] "Put it down."

Frieren looked at her hand as though it belonged to somebody she had just discovered in the room.

She loosened her fingers.

Maki took the staff. Set it on the empty end of the bench with the tip clear of the book, and kept herself between Frieren and Yuka.

[F] "I—"

[Mk] "Don't. Not yet."

Her voice was so controlled that Mau knew she had not reached the end of what she wanted to say.

* * *

The kitchen door opened.

Gojo came out first. He looked at the space between them, at the staff on the bench, at Mau's hand pressed briefly against the side of his head. He did not need an explanation before moving people out of the line that had just existed.

[G] "Yuka. This way. With me."

She did not move.

Mau put his right hand where she could see it.

[M] "Come on."

She took it. Her grip was painfully tight. He let it be tight and moved with her until they were beside the kitchen end of the path, with Gojo standing nearer the bench.

Then Maki spoke.

[Mk] "I love you. You know I do."

Frieren had raised her eyes to the sound of her voice.

[Mk] "But if you try to hurt my granddaughter again, I will kill you."

Mau stopped walking.

Maki did not look at him.

[Mk] "I won't spend time deciding whether you meant it. You just meant it. I saw where you aimed."

Frieren's mouth opened.

No answer came.

[Mk] "Whatever she did. Whatever you feel. You don't get to do that to her."

She took the staff off the bench and held it beside her own leg, pointed down.

The book remained behind.

* * *

Yuka had begun to cry without making a sound.

Mau could feel it in the hand holding his, the tiny repeated contractions which came before he heard the breath. He turned toward her again, getting himself out of the place where she had to look past him at Frieren.

[Yk] "I didn't know."

[M] "About what?"

She looked down at her wrist.

He saw the little flower.

[Yk] "Eternal love. She said that's what it means."

The rest tangled in a breath she could not finish.

Mau had bought it because he liked its shape. Because it was something he could give her while they sat with their feet above the sea, a little object she could take home from a day which had seemed uncomplicated until they came back.

He looked across at Frieren.

She was staring at his face now.

[M] "I didn't know what it meant. It was a birthday present."

Frieren looked down at the place the staff had left empty beside her hand.

[M] "That doesn't make what just happened a misunderstanding."

He had not known he was going to say it until the words came out.

She looked up.

[M] "You were going to hurt her."

The wind moved the loose straw at the post.

Frieren said nothing.

* * *

Shoko was coming across the yard from the house.

Fern was behind her, looking first for Frieren and then at Yuka's face. Neither had been on the path when the spell was cast. They could see Maki holding the staff, Gojo standing in the gap and Mau's hand still joined to Yuka's.

Shoko stopped beside them.

[Sh] "Who was hit?"

[M] "Nobody. I blocked it."

She looked at the way he was holding his head.

[M] "Pressure. Left side. It's settling. She wasn't hit."

He did not turn that into an answer for Yuka.

Shoko asked her too. Yuka shook her head, then managed to say no.

Maki came over.

She kept the staff in the hand farthest from her granddaughter. Her other hand settled against Yuka's back, and Yuka turned into the touch with a suddenness which made Maki's expression change.

[Mk] "Inside. With me."

Yuka looked at Mau.

He nodded toward the doorway, staying beside her instead of letting the gesture mean she had to go there alone.

Fern reached Frieren by the bench.

[Fe] "Frieren?"

Frieren looked at the younger woman's face.

She had never wanted Fern to look at her like that.

She had not imagined it would be because of something she had chosen to do.

* * *

Mau went into the house with Yuka and Maki.

He wanted to look back. The impulse was there even now, tied to the fragmentary shape of a woman he had once been willing to climb a hundred floors to come home to.

Yuka stumbled at the change from path to stone.

He looked at her instead.

Maki took the step slowly enough for both of them. The staff went through the doorway beside her leg, harmless wood now, and somehow more frightening for looking exactly as it had before.

Outside, Gojo remained near the bench. Shoko had turned back toward Frieren after making sure Yuka could walk inside.

Fern put her hand on the back of the bench and waited for Frieren to decide whether she could sit.

The book was still there, open where the wind had turned it.
