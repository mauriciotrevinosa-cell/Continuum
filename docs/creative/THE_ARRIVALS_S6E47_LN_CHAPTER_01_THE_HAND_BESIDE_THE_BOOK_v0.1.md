# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 11 — The Hand Beside the Book

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S6E47
**Continuity state:** Day468 afternoon, immediately after chapter10. Frieren remains by the garden bench with Fern, Gojo and Shoko nearby. Maki took the staff inside with Yuka and Mau; it is not returned or magically sealed here. Fern and Shoko arrived after the cast and learn its intention from Frieren herself. She acknowledges choosing to strike Yuka rather than calling the spell a warning, accident or outside control. Care does not erase the act. Immediate limits are agreed: she will not follow Yuka/Mau or retrieve the staff now, and remains with company while Shoko checks inside. No one assigns Ori eyewitness knowledge. No new injury, cure, forced forgiveness, ultimatum or Goddess intervention.

*When: Day468, afternoon. The bench beside the garden beds. The book is open where the wind has turned it, and Frieren is still standing.*

Fern had her hand on the back of the bench.

She had put it there because Frieren looked as though she might sit. Now she did not know whether moving it would make the other woman think she was going away.

The doorway closed behind Maki.

Frieren looked toward the sound. Her fingers moved beside her leg, closing on the place where the staff had been.

Gojo saw the movement. He stayed by the clear part of the path without coming closer.

[Fe] "Sit down."

Frieren looked at her.

[Fe] "If you can. There's room."

She moved the book. Not far; just enough to clear the place beneath it. Frieren sat with both feet flat on the ground and her hands empty on her knees.

Fern put the book on the cloth at the other end.

For a moment she wanted to open it herself. Find the page, ask which line Frieren had been reading, do something which belonged to a morning they had known how to get through.

She left it closed.

[Fe] "What happened?"

Frieren looked at her hands.

[Fe] "I came out after. I need you to tell me."

Shoko stopped beside the bench. She had been looking toward the house too. Now she stood where Frieren could see her without turning.

[F] "Yuka came to speak to me."

Fern waited.

[F] "She wanted to apologise."

The sound of the word seemed to stop her. She looked toward the door again.

[Fe] "And then?"

Frieren's hands pressed against her knees.

[F] "I cast at her."

Fern did not move.

[F] "Zoltraak."

[Fe] "At her."

Frieren nodded.

It had been the answer Fern expected. Hearing it from her was worse than having to guess from the staff in Maki's hand.

[Fe] "Where were you aiming?"

Frieren lifted one hand, touched the place below her own shoulder and put it down again.

[F] "There."

Fern looked at the movement. At the fingers which could still place it so exactly.

[F] "Mau stepped in front of her."

Shoko looked at the post where the staff had been. Then she looked back at Frieren, leaving her to answer for herself.

[Fe] "You meant to hit her."

There was a long enough silence for Frieren to hear how easy it would be to put another word into the space. Frighten. Stop. Make her understand.

She had wanted all of those things.

She had gathered the spell to hurt flesh.

[F] "Yes."

Fern sat down beside her, leaving a little space. She had not known until she bent her knees how badly she had needed to stop standing.

Shoko remained on the path in front of them.

[Sh] "Mau told me he blocked it. Yuka told me it didn't reach her. That's the part I'm thankful for."

Frieren looked up.

[Sh] "It doesn't mean there was nothing to stop."

[F] "I know."

[Sh] "When I asked her whether it reached her, she had trouble getting the word out. Mau had already told me no. I still needed to hear her say it."

Shoko looked at her own hands. She had come out expecting to need something which was still inside: a bandage, a needle, someone to carry a person who could not walk. Part of her was still looking for the place to begin.

[Sh] "I'm glad I didn't have to treat a wound. I'm going to sit with her anyway. I don't think she's finished being frightened."

Frieren looked at Fern. She had been trying not to. The younger woman's mouth was held very still, and one thumb kept pressing against the other in her lap.

[F] "She would have been struck. If he hadn't moved."

Fern closed her hands.

[F] "I meant her to be."

Nobody answered at once.

Gojo looked toward the kitchen door, then back to Shoko. She gave him a small nod. He moved nearer the house, keeping the path in view, and left the three women by the bench.

Frieren watched him stop near the doorway.

[F] "Is Mau hurt?"

[Sh] "He said the pressure was settling. I'll check him properly inside."

Frieren's hand rose toward the side of her own head and stopped before reaching it.

[F] "I could have hit him."

Fern turned toward her.

[Fe] "You were trying to hit Yuka."

Frieren's face changed.

She looked down at the hand she had lifted.

[F] "Yes."

Fern did not take the correction back. She sat with it between them, hating that it had been necessary.

For a while they listened to the house. A door opening farther inside. Footsteps which did not come out toward them. The small sound of Gojo answering somebody too softly for the words to reach the bench.

Frieren tried to put her hand beside the book. There was no staff there now. Only the cloth, rucked beneath the cover at one corner.

She smoothed it.

[F] "The flower."

Fern looked at her.

[F] "On her wrist. It was a mirrored lotus. He gave it to her."

She could still see the pale metal against the cord. The small, innocent movement with which Yuka had freed it from her cuff.

[F] "Eternal love."

Shoko waited.

[F] "She didn't know what it meant. He said he didn't either."

Fern's eyes moved to the closed book, then back.

[Fe] "Did you believe that?"

Frieren was quiet.

There had been something she wanted the answer to have been. Something deliberately taken, deliberately handed over. A word she could point at and say she had watched it happen.

Yuka had looked surprised.

Mau had looked surprised too, when she told him.

[F] "Yes."

Fern let a breath go slowly.

[F] "I could hear her. I heard her say it. I wasn't unable to understand."

She stared at the cloth beneath the book.

[F] "I still wanted her to stop being in front of me with it."

[Fe] "You could have asked her to leave."

Frieren's finger stopped against the cloth.

The possibility had been there. A path with room for Yuka to turn. A voice which would have carried a refusal perfectly well. She had used it for smaller things without thinking.

[F] "I didn't want only that."

Fern looked away.

That had been the answer to the question she had not wanted to ask next.

[F] "I wanted her hurt. For a moment I wanted that more than I wanted to be someone who wouldn't."

Shoko sat on the other end of the bench, with the book between her and Frieren. The three of them had to make room for their knees. It was a small awkwardness. Frieren almost apologised for it and could not make the word come out at that size.

[Sh] "Do you want to go after her now?"

Frieren looked toward the door.

[F] "I don't know what I'd say."

[Sh] "That isn't what I asked."

She drew her gaze back.

[F] "I want to tell her—"

She stopped. The words had almost become an apology, and underneath the apology there was still a wish to reach the place where Mau had gone and make him look at her differently.

Fern could see her trying to finish.

[Fe] "Don't go to them now."

Frieren looked at her.

[Fe] "She just had to leave with Maki because you aimed at her. She doesn't need to see you coming through the door before she's had time to sit down."

Frieren's hand came off the cloth.

[F] "All right."

[Sh] "The staff stays apart for now too."

Frieren looked at the empty place beside the post.

[F] "Maki has it."

[Sh] "Yes. You aren't going to get it from her."

There was no promise of safety in simply moving a piece of wood. Fern knew that. Frieren knew that she knew.

[F] "I won't cast at her again."

Shoko watched her for a moment.

[Sh] "Then stay with us while we work out the next part. Don't go and try to put the conversation back together by yourself."

Frieren nodded. The movement felt very small beside what she had agreed not to do.

Fern's hands had begun to hurt from how tightly she was holding them. She opened them one at a time.

[Fe] "I was angry with her too."

Frieren turned toward her.

[Fe] "About knowing. About being there with all of us and not saying it. I thought about what I might say if she came and asked me to listen. I didn't have anything kind prepared."

She rubbed the place her thumbnail had pressed into her palm.

[Fe] "I wasn't waiting for you to do this."

[F] "I know."

[Fe] "Do you?"

The question came out harder than she intended. She did not soften it when Frieren looked up.

[Fe] "I need to know what you thought was going to happen afterward. Was she going to go away? Was he going to stop loving her?"

Frieren looked at her.

[F] "I didn't think that far."

Fern shook her head once, looking down.

[F] "I knew she was there. I knew what I was doing to the spell. I didn't think past making it reach her."

Fern sat very still.

She had wanted an explanation she could put beside the woman who had asked her to lead through a hollow of frozen grass. Who had listened to her change the order of a small rain spell and praised the part she had actually worked out.

Those afternoons had not vanished. The person beside her had done this too.

[Fe] "I don't know what to say to you."

Frieren's mouth moved.

[F] "You don't have to find something for me."

It was a familiar kind of permission. Fern found that she did not want it from her just then.

[Fe] "I'm not trying to make you feel better. I'm trying to understand what happened to someone I trust."

Frieren bent forward slightly, hands still on her knees.

[F] "Where is Ori?"

[Fe] "I left her with Rem. She wasn't out here."

Frieren looked toward the house. The little window above the courtyard had caught the afternoon light; she could not see through it from the bench.

Fern waited. She had no intention of going to fetch Ori to fill the silence for either of them.

When Frieren looked back at her, she seemed to be trying to begin something. Fern waited, hands open on her knees, while the other woman's breathing changed.

[F] "I was afraid of what he'd do to keep it."

Shoko looked at her.

[F] "The promise. A life together. Longer than I live. I used to be afraid he was hurting himself trying to give me something he didn't know he could give."

She could hear the absurdity of telling them this with the staff inside the house and the girl she had tried to hurt on the other side of a door.

[F] "Then I saw that word on her wrist, and I wanted it taken away from her. Even when I knew she hadn't chosen it."

Her voice had gone very thin.

[F] "I knew better."

Fern waited. This time she was not waiting for the rest of an explanation she could argue with.

Frieren lifted a hand to her face, found nothing useful to do there and let it fall.

[F] "I don't know how to go back inside."

[Sh] "We don't have to do it this minute."

The answer was so ordinary that Frieren looked at her.

[Sh] "We're staying out here for now. When you're ready to move, we arrange where. We don't walk you through the people who have just had to get away."

Frieren nodded.

[F] "She meant it."

[F] "Maki. She said she'd kill me if I tried to hurt her granddaughter again."

Shoko looked toward the doorway.

[Sh] "Yes."

Frieren's eyes closed.

Maki's hand had been on the basket handle that morning. She had sat on this bench and said she was angry with people she loved. Frieren had been glad she stopped. Then she had aimed a spell at the person Maki would always stand in front of.

When she opened her eyes again, she could not see the edge of the garden clearly.

Fern saw the first tear reach her cheek. She did not reach for it before Frieren could decide what to do with it herself.

Frieren wiped it away badly. Another followed.

[F] "I can't ask you to make that smaller."

Fern shook her head.

[Fe] "No."

She moved her hand along the bench until it lay beside Frieren's. For a while there was a narrow strip of wood between them.

Then Frieren put two fingers against her wrist.

Fern turned her hand, slowly, and held them.

Shoko got up when Gojo looked over from the door. She stopped beside Fern before going inside.

[Sh] "I need to check the others. I'll tell you before we move anyone. Stay here together."

Fern nodded. Frieren did too, looking at the ground.

Gojo waited until Shoko had reached him, spoke with her quietly and remained by the path when she went in.

The book's cover had begun to lift in the breeze. Fern laid it flat and put the folded edge of the cloth over it, leaving her other hand where Frieren held it.

They stayed on the bench. Frieren cried without making any more sentences out of it. Fern sat beside her, watching the path and the garden by turns, until the hand in hers began to loosen.
