# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 14 — Three in a Row

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S6E48
**Continuity state:** Day469 morning. Mau and Yuka take a short walk on the known protected western path, after announcing route and return to Rimuru, who remains home. They play an ordinary game scratched into bare ground, enjoy each other and share a chosen brief kiss; Yuka's fear after the attack remains without a bodily wound or magical cure. The birthday lotus remains on her wrist. The normal gate reset this morning, but they do not enter or gain knowledge of its contents. No invented private conversation from chapter13, sex, cancer disclosure, final farewell, memory restoration or technique use. They return before lunch and announce the return aloud.

*When: Day469, morning. The long room, then the path inside the western boundary. Yuka has put her coat on and checked the fastening twice.*

Mau waited while she checked it a third time.

The first fastening was right. The next one had caught a fold beneath it. She freed the fold, looked at the first again and let both hands fall.

[Yk] "Ready."

He did not ask whether she was sure.

He had already seen Shoko for the usual morning check. She had asked about his head first. The pressure had settled; she still wanted to hear if it returned. When he said they meant to walk, she asked how far, listened to the answer and told him not to turn it into something longer on the way.

Rimuru was at the long table with something open in front of him. Mau stopped beside the end instead of speaking over his shoulder while already going through the door.

[M] "We're walking along the inside west path. As far as the broad stone. Back before lunch."

Rimuru looked up.

[R] "The known path."

[M] "Yes. Not the gate."

It had changed that morning. For once the date did not mean they had come downstairs to prepare bags and find out what was on the other side.

Yuka stood beside Mau until Rimuru had heard her too.

[Yk] "I'll be with him."

[R] "All right. Tell me when you're back."

He looked between them once, then down at the page. They left without asking him to make the answer warmer.

Outside, the air was cold enough to feel clean against her face.

They took the way past the workshops. Somebody inside was turning a piece of wood which did not want to turn, and a second voice suggested putting the other end down first. Yuka heard the small thump which followed and looked round.

Only at the doorway. Only for a moment.

Mau had stopped beside her.

[Yk] "I'm coming."

He fell into step again when she did.

The path was familiar. She knew the place where it narrowed round the roots, the shallow dip which kept water after rain and the bend from which the house disappeared behind the trees without actually being very far away.

She took his right hand before they reached it.

He looked down, then moved his fingers to hold hers properly.

For a while they walked without talking. Yuka found herself looking at their hands more often than the path. When she nearly put her foot in the wet part, Mau moved his elbow just enough to bring her round it.

[Yk] "I saw it."

[M] "I know. I was saving the water."

She looked at him.

[M] "It was having a quiet morning."

Her laugh came out as a short, unwilling breath. He did not look pleased with himself until she had made it properly.

[Yk] "You're very annoying."

[M] "I've heard that."

The broad stone had sunlight on one side again.

They sat at the dry end, leaving room for their feet below. The ground beside it was bare in a patch where water had washed the loose bits away. Yuka looked at it, then at a short fallen twig caught against the scrub.

She picked up the twig.

Mau watched her draw two lines down and two across.

[M] "Is this what we came out for?"

[Yk] "It is now."

She put a cross in the corner nearest her.

He held out his hand for the twig.

[M] "You've taken the best corner."

[Yk] "There are three more."

[M] "I liked that one."

[Yk] "You can admire it."

He put a circle in the middle.

They drew the first game in less than a minute. Mau began the second, and they drew that too. By the third, Yuka was sitting with her feet braced against the stone and her coat gathered out of the way, frowning at nine small spaces as if the correct way to live might finally be written in one of them.

Mau turned the twig between his right fingers.

[M] "We may be equally gifted."

[Yk] "That's a generous description of you not winning."

[M] "It's also you not winning."

She took the twig and drew a fresh grid.

[Yk] "Different rule. If you get three, you lose."

He looked at the marks.

[M] "You should have told me that two games ago. I was doing very well."

She nudged his knee with hers.

The first game under the new rule lasted longer, mostly because he kept looking for a line he was no longer supposed to want. She watched his hand hover over a space, stop and move somewhere else.

[Yk] "You can put it there."

[M] "You sound much too helpful."

[Yk] "I'm naturally kind."

He examined the other spaces.

[M] "That's been difficult to detect during this activity."

She laughed. Then she made a cross in the wrong place and gave herself a complete line.

For a moment they both stared at it.

Mau leaned back against the stone.

[M] "Your kindness overwhelms me."

[Yk] "I forgot which game we were playing."

[M] "You invented the rule."

[Yk] "I was distracted."

[M] "By my losing?"

She pushed his knee again, more firmly. He caught the twig before it slipped out of her hand and began another grid beneath the first.

There was no skill in the part he liked best. A line scratched into ordinary earth. Her shoulder touching his when she leaned over to see what he had done. Enough time to wait for her to decide whether he was being foolish on purpose.

This time, he lost.

He made the third circle and saw the line an instant after it was finished.

Yuka took the twig out of his hand with great care.

[Yk] "Thank you."

He looked at the three circles.

[M] "I thought I was being clever."

[Yk] "I could tell."

The wind moved through the scrub.

A dry branch caught against another, bent and snapped free with a sharp little crack.

Yuka turned so quickly that the twig slipped from her fingers.

Mau looked toward the sound. Only the branches, moving separately now. He turned back without reaching across her face to make her look at him.

She had drawn her arm in against her body.

[M] "Do you want to head back?"

She shook her head.

Her breath had gone shallow. She could see the branch. She knew where the sound had come from. Knowing did not immediately persuade her shoulders to come down.

He waited.

[Yk] "Not yet."

Mau nodded. He rested his right hand on the stone between them, palm up, and left it there.

After a little while she put her hand in it.

The small flower turned against the dark cord when she moved her wrist. He did not cover it. Neither of them said its meaning again.

[Yk] "I liked this place."

[M] "I still do."

She looked at the grids beside their feet. At the sun on the dry end of the stone and the twig she had dropped when she turned.

[Yk] "Me too."

She had said it quietly, as if she did not want to be caught promising that she would like every part of the next hour as well.

Mau moved his thumb once along the side of her hand.

[M] "We can just sit."

For a while they did.

Yuka leaned against his shoulder. He shifted enough for her not to have the edge of his coat fastening against her cheek. It was a clumsy adjustment, and she had to lift her head while he freed the fold.

[Yk] "You make this look very difficult."

[M] "I didn't design the coat."

[Yk] "I'll put that in the complaint."

He settled again. This time she could lean where she wanted.

She watched the ground until her eyes stopped going back to the scrub whenever it moved. The next small rustle remained a rustle. She did not test whether she could make that true of a louder one.

After a while she lifted her head.

Mau turned toward her, expecting a word. She put her hand against his cheek instead. Waited until he looked at her properly.

Then she kissed him.

His hand came to rest at the back of her shoulder. He kissed her back, softly, staying still enough that she could decide when to draw away.

She stayed close for another breath afterward.

[Yk] "That's why I was distracted."

He looked at her.

[M] "Several games ago?"

[Yk] "I was thinking ahead."

He laughed against her cheek. She leaned into it, smiling for a moment without having to watch herself make the smile.

They played one more game before leaving.

Yuka lost because she had begun trying too hard to arrange a clever trap. Mau pointed to the line, then kept his mouth closed with an expression which made her want to throw the twig at him.

[Yk] "You can say it."

[M] "I wouldn't."

[Yk] "You're saying it with your whole face."

He got up and offered her his right hand. She took it, keeping the twig in the other until she had found her balance. Then she put it down beside the scrub where she had found it.

They took the same path home.

At the bend, Yuka looked toward the part of the house she could see through the trees. Her hand tightened around his.

He did not say nobody would be there. Did not guess which room anybody had chosen that morning.

[M] "I'm with you."

She nodded and kept walking.

They were back before the long table had been set for lunch. Rimuru had moved to the office doorway; Mau stopped where he could see them both.

[M] "We're back."

[R] "I see."

Yuka lifted her free hand a little. Rimuru nodded to her too, then turned to answer someone inside the office.

Mau waited until she was ready to go farther into the room.

She looked toward the washing shelf. There was dirt on his right thumb and a faint streak on her own palm where the twig had rubbed.

[Yk] "You need to wash."

He looked at her hand.

[M] "Naturally kind."

She laughed under her breath and went with him to the basin.
