# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 15 — All of Them Count

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S6E49
**Continuity state:** Day469 afternoon. Kaneki, Touka and Ichika play a new ordinary game with smooth discarded wooden blocks and a rag ball in their established room with two windows. An uneven floor and an unhelpfully light ball create a concrete problem to solve. Yuta has told Kaneki that morning only the attack/block/no-hit facts he learned in12; Kaneki relayed that privately to Touka, not Ichika. Their chosen room and quieter voices reflect the continuing household pressure without making the activity therapy, forgiveness or a cast report. Parents have black coffee, Ichika water. No new food clearance, ghoul power, technology or major construction occurs; Mau does not appear.

*When: Day469, afternoon. The room with two windows. Kaneki is holding six pieces of wood as if one of them has failed to explain itself.*

Touka looked at the pieces. Then at him.

[Tk] "What are they?"

[Kn] "For a game."

Ichika reached up from beside his knee.

He gave her the shortest one. She turned it over, looked at the smooth ends and put it against her cheek.

[I] "Cold."

[Kn] "A little. They were outside."

[Tk] "You brought our daughter cold wood."

[Kn] "I brought six."

Touka took one from the top before he dropped it.

They were offcuts from the workshop, already smoothed along the sides. Nobody had needed the short lengths. Kaneki had asked, received a look which suggested he could have taken twice as many without creating a shortage, and brought back exactly the number he had gone to ask for.

One piece was longer than the others. One had an end cut on a slant.

Touka stood that one on the windowsill.

It fell over.

She looked at him again.

[Kn] "The other end."

She turned it over. It stood.

[Tk] "Promising."

* * *

The ball had been a cleaning cloth before Touka tied it.

A clean one, worn soft enough to have a thin place in the middle. She folded that part inside, pulled the ends round and bound the bundle with spare cord. Ichika watched every turn, one hand still holding the short block against her cheek.

Kaneki put the other pieces upright on the floor.

Two at the back. Two in front. One nearest the bed.

He moved the nearest one farther away so that there would be space to roll the ball without immediately putting a hand against the target.

Ichika put her block down beside it.

There were now two nearest ones.

[Kn] "That goes at the back."

She looked at the empty place in the last row. At the block in front of her. Moved it half an inch closer to herself.

[I] "Here."

Touka finished the knot.

[Tk] "She's arranged it."

[Kn] "I can see."

[Tk] "Then stop rearranging it."

He sat back on his heels. Ichika accepted the ball and put both hands round it.

* * *

Yuta had stopped him that morning near the stair.

Not to ask for help with a weapon or an extra shift. He had told him what had happened in the garden, plainly enough that Kaneki had not needed to ask whether fired meant threatened to fire. One spell. Mau in front of it. Nobody struck.

Kaneki had told Touka when Ichika was not in the room.

They had spoken quietly about where to spend the afternoon. Not the garden bench. Here, where they could keep the door open if they wanted and close it if they didn't.

Now footsteps passed in the corridor. Touka looked toward them. Kaneki did too.

Ichika rolled the ball while neither was watching.

It missed every block.

[I] "Papa."

He turned back.

She pointed after it. It had gone beneath the bed and stopped just beyond the place his arm could comfortably reach.

[Tk] "Your game."

Kaneki lowered himself onto one elbow.

* * *

The first proper roll did not work either.

Ichika sat between Touka's knees and pushed the ball forward with both hands. It travelled straight for a little way, caught a ridge between two boards and turned left.

She looked left after it.

Then at Kaneki.

He fetched it.

The second roll did exactly the same thing, though Ichika pushed much harder. It went left faster and struck the leg of the chair.

[I] "Again."

Touka took the ball before Kaneki could pass it back.

[Tk] "Wait."

She rolled it herself, from the same place. More slowly. The bundle turned across the ridge, wobbled, chose the other direction for a moment and settled on left.

Kaneki had been careful not to smile too soon.

She saw him being careful.

[Tk] "You try."

He sat where they had sat. Put the ball beside his knee, looked at the boards and changed its angle by a small amount.

Touka folded her arms.

[Tk] "It's six pieces of wood."

[Kn] "I know."

[Tk] "You look as if you're being examined."

He rolled.

The ball crossed the first ridge. Touka watched it miss the left side of the nearest block, brush the second and head under the chair.

One piece of wood rocked. Did not fall.

Ichika clapped anyway.

Kaneki looked at his wife.

[Kn] "I touched one."

[Tk] "Very good."

She made the words sound so exactly like praise for a small child that he reached for the ball before he could laugh.

* * *

The tray was Touka's idea.

Not the coffee tray. That had two cups of black coffee safely out of reach on the high sill, and she refused to have its use changed in the middle of the afternoon.

The shallow wooden one under the shelf, which had held folded cloths.

She emptied it onto the bed, turned it over and put it across the ridges in the floor. Its underside made a broad, nearly flat surface. Kaneki set the blocks on one end and rolled the ball from the other.

Two fell.

Ichika came forward on her knees at once.

[I] "Me."

[Kn] "Yes. Let me put them back."

He stood the two pieces upright.

Touka rolled the ball between her palms, feeling the loose bundle move inside its tie.

[Tk] "This is too light."

[Kn] "She has to be able to push it."

[Tk] "She can push it. Look."

Ichika had taken the long block out of the back row and was pushing it across the tray with considerable purpose.

Kaneki moved it back. Touka found another scrap of cloth, untied her first knot and folded the second piece into the ball before gathering it again. The old tie was too short now. She left it on the chair and found a longer one.

Ichika watched the whole operation with growing impatience.

Then she reached for the cord.

[Tk] "Not finished."

She made one last knot. Ichika caught the free end and gave it a sharp pull.

Touka looked down at her hands.

[Kn] "Now it's finished."

* * *

The ball rolled straight.

It reached the first block and knocked it backward into the second. The third went sideways. One of the back pieces fell off the tray and lay on the floor, which seemed to make the event larger than the small clatter had any right to be.

Ichika opened both hands.

[I] "All!"

[Kn] "Four."

Two were still standing. She looked at them. Leaned forward and pushed both over with the flat of her hand.

[I] "All."

Touka picked up her coffee.

[Tk] "She's right."

Kaneki looked from the blocks to his wife.

[Kn] "Then I'm going to need the rules."

[Tk] "You brought the game."

She drank. It was good coffee. He knew because she had made it and because she was not looking at him as if he had done something to the pot.

Ichika rebuilt the rows while they talked. Not in the same arrangement. She lay the short block across two upright pieces, made a little entrance beneath it and tried to put the slanted block on top.

It fell.

She caught it against the tray with both hands.

Kaneki reached to help. Touka touched his wrist.

[Tk] "Wait."

Ichika tried the other end. It stood.

She looked up at her mother.

Touka nodded, very seriously.

* * *

The game changed after that.

The thing they were trying to knock down had a bridge, then a second storey, then a block which nobody was allowed to touch because Ichika had decided it was sleeping.

Kaneki rolled the ball past the sleeper and brought down the bridge.

Ichika laughed. Demanded that he put it back immediately.

Touka got three with one roll and pointed at the tray before anyone could change the count by hand. Kaneki admitted three. Ichika gave her four.

[Tk] "I'll take it."

[Kn] "You said we were counting."

[Tk] "We are. Four."

He looked at his daughter.

She nodded.

The next turn was his. He rolled too softly and the ball came to rest against the sleeper without moving it. Ichika crawled over, bent very close and whispered something to the block.

Touka leaned forward.

[Tk] "What did it say?"

Ichika put one finger against her lips.

Both parents lowered their voices.

They played the next two turns in a whisper. The wooden clatter sounded outrageously loud. Kaneki caught a falling piece before it could hit the bare floor, held it up for approval and was told by Touka that catching it was probably cheating.

[Kn] "You're concerned about that now?"

She looked at Ichika, who had put the sleeper on the bed and covered it with a corner of the folded cloths.

[Tk] "Someone has to be."

* * *

They stopped when Ichika began picking the blocks up instead of setting them down.

Not because she had finished. Because she was trying to hold all six at once, and the short one kept slipping between her palms. Kaneki put the tray right side up and offered it.

She considered it.

Put five pieces inside. Kept the sleeper.

Touka fetched her small wooden cup of water. Ichika drank, paused to look inside and drank again until the surface was below the line she liked to check.

Kaneki moved the chair away from the wall. Found the first ball's loose cord beneath one leg, along with a little clot of dust which had no need to become part of their toy.

He brought the cord to Touka.

[Kn] "For the next one."

[Tk] "There isn't going to be a next one. This one works."

She took it anyway. Put it with the spare lengths, not in the bin.

Ichika wanted the tray under the bed. Kaneki set it where she pointed, then had to move it closer because she could no longer see whether the blocks were still there.

The sleeper stayed on the blanket.

Touka reached for her coffee and found that it had gone cold. She looked into the cup, then at Kaneki's, which had scarcely been touched.

[Tk] "You could have reminded me."

[Kn] "I thought you knew."

[Tk] "That I hadn't drunk it?"

He looked at the covered block. At their daughter sitting beside it, suddenly much less determined to remain upright.

[Kn] "I was busy."

Touka followed his eyes.

She put both cups on the tray's old shelf, came back to the bed and pulled the folded cloths clear of Ichika's feet.

[Tk] "So was I."

Kaneki sat on the floor beside them. Ichika put the ball into his lap, pressed one finger against her lips again and leaned against her mother's side.

He stayed very quiet.
