# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 16 — The First Step

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S6E49
**Continuity state:** Day470 morning. Mau, Rimuru, Umaru and privately relayed Ciel/Noise develop the two outer edges surveyed on Days451/459. A short common outer threshold is built for the monarch and Ten Shadows routes, with distinct inner branches, contents and reserves. One registered soldier and Ear are called separately through it; no new extraction, reconstruction, capacity or power is gained. After the second call, Noise warns of load and they stop rather than repeat or touch the closed third access. The adjustment is real but not yet shown persistent across days. Shoko's follow-up has found the brief Day468 pressure settled; baseline eye/hand/hair sequelae remain. Household rupture and the pause with Yuta are not repaired by technical cooperation. Points remain57;14 named/40 and98 soldiers.

*When: Day470, morning. The back step. Umaru has laid two drawings over each other and cannot keep them from sliding apart.*

The stone was doing a better job of holding the paper than Mau.

He had moved his hand twice to help, and twice Umaru had moved it back.

[U] "Here. That corner."

[M] "That was where I had it."

[U] "Before you decided the other corner needed supervision."

He put one finger on the place she indicated. The upper page stayed where she wanted it.

Rimuru crouched beside the step and looked over the drawings. One had been folded through the middle so many times that it wanted to stand up along the crease. The other was new, with two dark strokes where Umaru had decided the first attempt at showing an entrance was too much like showing a passage between the things inside.

[R] "I understood the old one."

[U] "You told me there was no passage between them."

[R] "I understood what was wrong with it."

She gave him the stone.

* * *

They had not tried the next step the day before.

Mau had asked Shoko about the pressure at the left side of his head when she came to see him again. It had settled. The thin edge of his sight had not changed; neither had the fingers which refused to close all the way when he put his palm on his knee.

She had asked him to tell her which part he meant by *better* before he used the word again.

This morning Ciel asked the same question.

[C] *The ordinary sensation first. Without opening anything.*

[M] *Same as before the garden. No pressure now.*

Rimuru looked up when Ciel relayed the answer to him with Mau's agreement. His expression did not become pleased. It became ready to listen to the next one.

[R] "We can leave it another day."

[M] "I'd like to try. The small part we talked about."

Umaru looked at him. He could see the question she was working out whether to say.

[U] "Does it have anything to do with fixing your memory?"

[M] "No."

She waited.

[M] "It's the entrances. I don't think making them easier to find will tell me what I felt about anybody."

Umaru nodded once. Put the two pages beside each other instead of trying to make them lie together.

[U] "Then don't do more because you think everybody's waiting for something else."

He took a moment before answering.

[M] "Okay."

* * *

Ciel did not begin by changing anything.

She asked Noise to show the place which had become heavy on their last attempt. It took a little time. Mau watched the step, the grain in the wood beside his foot, while something below his attention found the two familiar edges.

One where he reached the legion. One where Ear waited without being called.

The third depth remained farther away. Closed. There was no reason to go toward it simply because he knew it was there.

[N] *Here.*

Mau felt the weight before it became a demand.

[M] *I feel it.*

[C] *Show us the beginning only. Keep everything behind it where it is.*

Nothing moved for a while.

Rimuru shifted his feet so that he could sit instead of keeping his knees bent. Umaru looked at him, then did the same. Nobody asked Mau whether he was finished yet.

[N] *Two.*

[C] *Yes. Two branches. We aren't asking you to put them into each other.*

Mau tried to explain the difference aloud.

[M] "One place to begin. Then I choose which way. Not a path from Ear to the soldiers."

Umaru looked at her new drawing.

[U] "Like standing at the bottom of two stairs."

[M] "Maybe."

[R] "Don't build stairs because she said stairs."

Mau nearly laughed. It broke his concentration enough that both edges eased away.

Below, Noise waited.

* * *

They began again more slowly.

Mau had been finding each entrance by thinking toward what lived behind it. Ciel asked him to stop before that part. To notice the little turn in his attention which came before he reached for a soldier, rather than the soldier itself.

It was much harder than she made it sound.

He thought he had found it. Ear's head came up beside his knee.

The dog looked at him.

[M] "Sorry. Not yet."

Ear sniffed his hand before going back.

[U] "That one looked very successful from here."

[M] "Wrong sort of successful."

Umaru's pencil rested against the paper. She did not write a failure mark. There would have been no way to explain what it measured.

Ciel waited until the ordinary yard had become easy to notice again.

[C] *You can ask Noise to hold the first place for you. You don't have to keep finding it alone while we work.*

[M] *Can you?*

He asked the thing below.

[N] *Small.*

[M] *Small is fine.*

* * *

This time the beginning stayed still.

Mau did not have to keep following it. There was a shallow place beneath his attention, held open without pulling the contents of either route toward it.

He could feel the two ways beyond.

Not see them. There were no rooms or ranks in front of his ordinary eyes. The shadow on the step remained a shadow, broken by Rimuru's foot and the leg of the little bench behind them.

But when Ciel asked him which way led to Ear, he knew before he pictured the dog.

When she asked for the legion, he did not have to lose the first place to find it.

[M] *There.*

[C] *Hold the beginning. Don't call yet.*

Noise kept the shallow place where it was.

Mau let himself breathe all the way out.

[M] "I think that's different."

Umaru looked up quickly.

[U] "Different how?"

[M] "I don't have to go looking for the second from the start. The start is still there."

She turned the new page so he could see it without leaning over.

[U] "This?"

He looked at her two lines. At the short piece which they shared before going in different directions.

[M] "That part. I think."

She did not write *done*.

She put her pencil beside the short piece and waited.

* * *

The soldier came first.

An ordinary one, already registered. It rose beside the step with its spear held upright and stopped where Mau meant it to stop.

Rimuru leaned back enough to look past it at the yard. Nothing else was coming up with it.

[R] "That's one."

Mau could have kept it standing. There was nothing new about doing that. He sent it back because today he wanted to know what happened when the route closed, not how long a soldier could wait while they argued over a drawing.

The shallow beginning stayed.

He found Ear's branch without hunting for the other edge first.

Ear appeared with more confidence than the experiment deserved. He put his head on Mau's knee and immediately looked at Umaru, as if she had arranged an appointment which Mau was neglecting.

[U] "I didn't bring anything."

The dog waited.

[U] "Don't look at me like that."

Mau rested his right hand between Ear's ears.

For a moment he was simply pleased. Not because the dog had become stronger or a secret place had opened, but because the little turn inside him had gone where he meant it to go.

Then the edge beneath it grew heavier.

[N] *Heavy.*

[C] *Enough. Close it.*

Mau sent Ear back.

The shared beginning eased out of his attention. He let the two branches go separately after it, following Ciel's voice rather than trying to hold the result in place to prove he could.

His hands remained on his knees.

Nothing needed somewhere to overflow.

* * *

Umaru did not speak until he moved his right foot.

It had gone slightly numb against the edge of the step. He straightened it, winced at the ordinary pins and needles and looked up to find all of them watching him.

[M] "My foot."

[R] "Which part of your foot?"

[M] "The part I sat on badly."

Umaru let out a breath which almost became a laugh and wrote something down.

[M] "Don't put that in."

[U] "I'm putting in that you stopped."

He looked at the page.

She had drawn a little line across the end of the common piece. Not a wall. A mark showing where the warning had arrived.

[U] "You called one. Sent it back. Called the other. Then stopped when it got heavy."

[M] "Yes."

[U] "And the first accidental dog."

[M] "Apparently that's going in."

[U] "That was the best bit."

She wrote it smaller beside the first attempt.

* * *

Ciel described what she had been able to follow. Mau passed it on when she asked, stopping twice to find words which did not turn a place he could feel into a new physical tunnel under the house.

The two routes still led to different things. Nothing had changed their reserves. Calling the same soldier and the same dog had not extracted new ones, rebuilt damaged ones or enlarged a limit.

The third access was untouched.

[C] *And we haven't checked whether it stays when we leave it. We won't call it stable because we found it once.*

Rimuru repeated that part exactly.

Umaru put the date beside the drawing.

[U] "Then we look another day."

Mau nodded.

He wanted another try. Not badly enough to miss what had happened when the voice below warned them. Noise had felt the weight, told them and stopped holding the place when he asked.

[M] *Thank you.*

There was a pause.

[N] *Small.*

[M] *It worked small.*

Rimuru stood and offered him a hand. Mau took it, waited for the foot to become completely his again and helped Umaru collect the pages before the wind could fold the two entrances into each other.

At the kitchen door she handed him the stone.

[U] "You can carry that. It doesn't need a diagram."

He looked at the small, completely ordinary thing in his palm.

[M] "I was good at this bit."

[U] "Eventually."

She went inside before he could find out whether she was going to smile at him.

He put the stone back beside the step.
