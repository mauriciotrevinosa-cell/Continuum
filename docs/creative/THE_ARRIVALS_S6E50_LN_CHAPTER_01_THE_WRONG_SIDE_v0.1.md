# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 17 — The Wrong Side

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S6E50
**Continuity state:** Day470 after dinner. Seiko and Mai visit Frieren in the east room after Fern has told them the public attack/block/no-hit facts and asked whether Frieren wants company. Neither speaks for Maki or knows Yuka's private aftermath. The visit is not the weekly crackers meeting, next due473. A repair to Seiko's own cushion gives the women something ordinary to do; no magic, reconciliation or compulsory confession. Frieren remains deeply withdrawn, with care and accountability both present. Fern takes time elsewhere. The staff remains in Rimuru's office. No new injury or change to the room.

*When: Day470, after dinner. The east room. Seiko has brought something which is losing its insides.*

The first scrap fell out in the corridor.

Mai picked it up. Seiko stopped, looked at the soft, misshapen parcel under her arm and tightened her grip until a second scrap began working toward the same opening.

[Mai] "You're doing that."

[Se] "I noticed."

[Mai] "You could stop squeezing it."

[Se] "Then it falls down."

Mai put the scrap into her pocket. She had a small sewing roll in the other hand, and no intention of carrying the cushion as well merely because Seiko had made the task look difficult.

Fern opened the door before they knocked a second time.

[Fe] "She said yes."

She looked down at what Seiko was holding.

[Fe] "Is that supposed to be open?"

[Se] "It was."

* * *

Frieren was in the chair by the window.

She looked up when they entered. Her gaze moved first to their faces, then to the cushion, and finally to the little piece of filling which had escaped onto Seiko's sleeve.

[F] "It's coming out."

[Se] "Thank goodness we brought it to an expert."

Seiko put it on the bed before it could lose anything else. Mai laid the sewing roll beside it and opened the folded cloth beneath the cover to catch the loose scraps.

Fern stood near the door for a moment.

[Fe] "I'm going downstairs. I'll be with Stark for a while."

Frieren nodded.

Fern waited until she was sure the movement had been an answer. Then she went, closing the door enough to leave only the little strip of corridor light at its edge.

Seiko sat on the bed. Mai took the stool and turned it toward the lamp.

The room had become so quiet after Fern left that the small sound of unrolling thread seemed inconsiderate.

[Se] "I thought the middle needed more filling. Then I found all of it in the corners."

[Mai] "After you opened it."

[Se] "It was difficult to examine before."

Mai lifted the cover by its two upper corners. Something inside it sagged into a long, very unconvincing middle.

[Mai] "You folded the inner cloth."

Seiko leaned forward.

[Se] "I unfolded it."

[Mai] "You folded a different part."

Frieren watched them without moving from the chair.

* * *

The cushion belonged in Seiko's room.

It was not a present. She had said so before they came, while Fern was still looking at the sewing roll as if she needed to know what the visit was going to ask of Frieren.

Fern had told them what happened in the garden. One spell, deliberately aimed. Mau had stopped it. Nobody struck.

Seiko had asked whether Frieren wanted company. She had not asked Fern to explain the whole thing a second time, or to produce a version in which it would be easier to cross the corridor.

Now Mai pulled the inner cloth free and found the twisted part.

She laid it flat on the bed. Seiko began gathering the loose pieces into a pile.

[Se] "This used to be much smaller."

[Mai] "You were sitting on it."

[Se] "I'm aware of what a cushion does."

Mai looked at the open cover.

Seiko looked at her.

Frieren's mouth moved a little. Not quite a smile. The expression disappeared before either visitor could decide what to do with it.

Mai kept working.

* * *

They filled the first corner too tightly.

Seiko pushed a handful in. Mai spread it out. Seiko added a second handful to the place Mai had just cleared, and Mai sat back on the stool.

[Mai] "Do you want it soft or do you want to win?"

[Se] "Both."

Frieren's hand moved on the chair arm.

[F] "The other side."

Both women looked at her.

She pointed with two fingers, not lifting her arm very far.

[F] "You're putting it through the folded part again."

Mai looked down. Seiko leaned across the bed to see.

There was a pause.

[Se] "Well."

Mai took the handful out.

[Mai] "You can say it."

[F] "I already did."

Seiko gave a short, delighted sound and immediately had to catch another scrap which the movement shook off her knee.

Frieren looked back at the window.

Neither asked her to repeat the moment.

* * *

When the filling lay flat, Mai began to close the inner cloth.

She used a long ordinary needle and a thread which kept catching against a rough place in the seam. After the third catch she cut the end, drew it through again and moved the work nearer the light.

Frieren watched the empty space at the side of the shelf.

The staff was still downstairs. She had agreed not to go and get it. Nobody had come to the room to announce that holding her hands empty had made everybody safe.

[F] "Does Maki know you're here?"

Mai stopped sewing.

[Mai] "No. I didn't go and ask her."

Frieren turned toward her.

[Mai] "I'm not carrying an answer from her."

Seiko put the cushion cover on her lap. The cloth made a soft sound against her skirt.

[F] "I wasn't going to ask you for one."

Mai looked down at the needle.

[Mai] "Good. I wouldn't know how to give you one."

For a while she did not begin the next stitch.

Frieren's fingers pressed into the chair arm.

[F] "I don't know how to see her."

[Mai] "Then don't go looking for her tonight."

The answer had no anger in it. That made it harder to mistake for something they would take back once she had cried enough.

Frieren looked toward the door.

[F] "Is Yuka—"

She stopped.

Seiko watched her wait for a sentence which would not become a request to be told the girl was better.

[F] "I don't know what I'm asking."

[Se] "Then you can leave it until you do."

Mai began to sew again.

* * *

The first corner of the cover was easy.

The second took both of them. The third required Seiko to hold the filling together while Mai pulled the fabric over it, and the fourth refused to accept that the rest of the cushion had already made a decision.

Frieren watched them turn it over twice.

[Se] "I'm not sitting on it like this."

[Mai] "You said you wanted it firmer."

[Se] "I wanted a place to sit. Not something which fights back."

Mai let go. The corner sprang up.

They both looked at it.

Frieren shifted in the chair.

[F] "There's too much on that side."

[Se] "Can you hold this?"

Seiko had lifted the folded edge toward her before stopping to look properly.

Frieren's hands had come off the armrests. She left them suspended for a moment, as if she had answered a different question from the one she heard.

Then she took the edge.

Seiko moved the cushion nearer without making her leave the chair. Mai knelt beside the stool, eased the corner back out and pushed a little of the filling toward the empty side.

Frieren held the cover while they worked.

Her fingers were perfectly steady. She hated noticing it.

* * *

They got the fourth corner in.

Mai put one last row of stitches along the opening. Seiko asked whether they could make that part easier to open next time, and Mai asked whether the point of having a needle in her hand was not to discourage a next time.

Frieren let go when Mai needed the edge.

For a while she watched the little movements. Thread through cloth. Thumb following the seam. A knot made without using anything which had ever frightened anyone.

[F] "I can do all of this."

Mai looked up.

[F] "Hold something. Eat when someone brings it. Answer if they wait long enough."

Seiko's expression changed.

[F] "Then nobody's talking, and I don't know what I'm meant to do next."

Mai laid the needle on the sewing roll.

Seiko did not tell Frieren there would be another day when the answer became easy. She looked at the chair, the closed book, the bed which still had room for somebody to choose to sit on it.

[Se] "We can stay a little longer."

Frieren stared at the place where she had been holding the cloth.

[F] "You have things to do."

[Se] "So far you've found two mistakes in the thing I came to do. I'd call that useful company."

Mai picked up the needle.

[Mai] "Three. She saw the wrong side before you did."

[Se] "I said two I was admitting to."

Frieren looked at her.

For a moment she seemed on the verge of another answer. Then she shook her head very slightly and let herself sit back.

* * *

Seiko tried the cushion on the stool.

She sat. Shifted her weight. Made an expression of deep suspicion, then stood and turned it over.

Mai folded the sewing roll before Seiko could suggest opening it again.

[Se] "That's better."

[Mai] "It had better be."

Seiko held it out toward the chair. Frieren did not reach for it.

[F] "It's yours."

[Se] "I'm aware. I want an opinion which isn't hers."

Mai looked insulted on purpose.

Frieren took the cushion. Held it in her lap, pressing one hand against the middle as if she had forgotten there was a less careful way to touch an ordinary thing.

[F] "It's softer."

[Se] "There."

[F] "You took some out."

Mai laughed before she could stop herself.

Seiko looked at the little heap still resting on the catching cloth.

[Se] "I did wonder why that was there."

Frieren gave the cushion back. Her mouth had moved again, and this time it stayed a little longer before she looked toward the window.

When Fern returned, Mai had the spare scraps in a small bundle and Seiko was still on the bed, telling a story about a chair which had broken under someone who insisted he had only leaned on it.

Frieren was listening.

She was still in the same chair when they left.
