## Chapter 19 — After the Second Verse

*When: Day471, after dinner. Bocchi has asked Kita to come as far as the corner, and is now considering whether the corner might be moved.*

Mau was nearer than she had expected.

He stood beside the washing shelf, drying his right hand while trying to persuade the cloth to remain where his left fingers had put it. When he saw them, he moved out of the way as if both girls had come to wash something more important.

Kita stopped at the corner.

Bocchi stopped too.

They had agreed on this part. The agreement had been very clear while the corridor was empty.

[Kt] "I'll be in there."

Bocchi nodded.

Kita looked at Mau, smiled a little and went toward the music room. Not fast. Not so slowly that Bocchi could accuse her of being available for rescue.

Mau watched her go, then looked back.

[M] "Everything all right?"

[B] "Yes. No. The music."

He waited.

[B] "There is music."

[M] "I've heard a rumour."

She looked down at the clean cloth in his hand.

[B] "We changed something. In one of the quieter ones. I wanted to ask if you could listen."

He looked toward the music-room door.

[M] "Now?"

The speed with which the question became practical nearly made her answer no.

[B] "If you want."

[M] "I do."

She stepped back before he had finished folding the cloth, then made herself stop leaving without him.

* * *

She had asked Fern that morning.

Not about the song. About the other thing she had heard in pieces, each one seeming a little worse because nobody knew where the piece before it had come from.

Fern had looked tired. She had told her what Frieren said she had done and what Mau stopped. No one had a wound.

Bocchi had thanked her, then wished she knew a less small word for having been told something so terrible without being made to guess.

Now Mau came down the corridor beside her.

He had not asked why she had taken several days to find him. She had not explained that she had spent some of those days being angry with him and some being frightened of what she might be agreeing to by asking him into a room she liked.

At the door she stopped.

[B] "It isn't because I think it's all right."

Mau turned toward her.

The words had come out without the rest of their preparation. Bocchi held the edge of her sleeve between two fingers.

[B] "Keeping it from them. I don't think that was all right."

[M] "I know."

She looked up.

[M] "You can ask me to listen without taking that back."

For a moment she could not tell whether he was saying it because he meant it or because he had become very practised at making space for things people said about him.

[B] "I don't want it to be a test either."

[M] "The song?"

She nodded.

[B] "Of whether you remember. Or feel something you're supposed to feel."

Mau looked through the open door. Ryo was holding one note for so long that Nijika had begun counting it out loud with an expression which did not suggest admiration.

[M] "What should I listen for?"

Bocchi let go of her sleeve.

[B] "After the second verse."

* * *

Nijika had put a chair near the wall.

Not the stool. Bocchi needed that, and its small wooden shim remained exactly where the two pencil marks said it belonged. One mark had been darkened again. Mau noticed it when he came in and put his heel carefully outside it.

[Ry] "We have an audience."

[Nj] "One person."

[Ry] "A selective audience."

[M] "I was at the washing shelf."

[Ry] "Exclusively recruited."

Kita lifted her guitar before Nijika could answer. She had tied her hair out of the way, not especially neatly, and her smile changed when Bocchi took her place beside her. A small private question. Bocchi nodded once.

[Nj] "From the start?"

[B] "Yes."

Ryo stopped holding the note.

[Ry] "Then I should begin the important part."

Nijika raised a stick.

[Nj] "One. Two—"

They began.

* * *

Mau did not know where the changed part had been before.

He knew the sound of their playing. Not as a recovered afternoon with a date and a place attached to it, but the way he still knew how to put his feet when somebody passed him a practice sword. Some things stayed familiar without telling him when he had learned them.

This song was quiet enough that he could hear Kita's breath between the lines.

Bocchi played a little phrase above the first verse. It came back near the end of the second, different enough that he looked up from his hands to follow where she meant it to go.

Then she did not answer it.

Ryo's two notes came underneath. The second arrived later than he expected. Kita took the small space between them, made the change with her hand and brought the voice back without hurrying after it.

Mau found himself waiting for a missing thing.

By the next line he was no longer waiting.

Bocchi did not look at him while she played. She was watching Kita's hand and listening to the place where Nijika kept the rhythm from becoming too busy around the change.

The ending came softly. Ryo let one note settle all the way instead of beginning to say something clever over it.

For a moment nobody spoke.

Mau had liked that too.

* * *

[Kt] "Well?"

The question came out brighter than she meant it to. Bocchi looked at her, then at Mau, who seemed to have been surprised that the person in the chair had become part of the work.

[M] "I waited for you."

Bocchi's hand stopped against the strings.

[M] "After the second verse. I thought you were going to do the little bit again."

Ryo looked at her.

[Ry] "My opportunity."

[Nj] "Let him finish."

Mau searched for the words.

[M] "At first I thought something was missing. Then her voice came back and I didn't want it in the way anymore."

Bocchi looked at the page on the stand.

That was the thing she had stopped herself putting there.

Not because she could not play it. Because she had played it, and liked it, and needed to learn not to keep every good thing simply because it belonged to her hands.

[B] "Did it feel too empty?"

[M] "For a moment. Not after the voice."

Kita looked at Bocchi properly now. Ryo shifted her instrument against her knee with the satisfaction of somebody whose small contribution had finally received the recognition it deserved.

[Ry] "Two excellent notes."

[Nj] "We remember."

Mau leaned forward.

[M] "Did I say something wrong?"

Bocchi shook her head quickly.

[B] "No. That's what I wanted to know."

She put her hand back where it belonged.

* * *

They tried it again.

Not the whole song. Nijika brought them in before the change, and Kita made the first line too quickly because she was now listening to herself take a breath.

Ryo stopped.

[Ry] "You've become aware of breathing."

[Kt] "That was your suggestion."

[Nj] "Don't turn it into another song."

Bocchi looked down.

Mau saw her shoulders begin to move before he heard the laugh. Kita saw them too, and abandoned the explanation she had been preparing.

[Kt] "No. We are doing the real version."

[Ry] "The educational version has merit."

[Nj] "The real version."

Mau looked from one to the other.

[M] "Now I want to hear the other one."

Kita pointed at him with the hand which was supposed to be getting ready to play.

[Kt] "Don't encourage them."

They had to wait for Bocchi to stop laughing before Nijika could count.

* * *

He stayed until they began putting things away.

Not a performance with a proper audience or an evening arranged to mean that everybody had chosen a side. One chair occupied while four people worked out whether a small space belonged in a song.

Ryo asked his opinion about extending the ending. Nijika told him to be careful because he would be personally responsible if the answer became six more minutes. He suggested three, which made Kita put her face in her hands.

At the door Bocchi came after him.

Kita was gathering the pages from the stand. She did not follow, though she looked toward them once before turning the little stack so its edges matched.

[B] "Thank you."

[M] "I liked it."

Bocchi nodded. The sentence she had thought would be easier now still had several wrong beginnings.

[B] "I still want you here."

He looked at her.

[B] "I don't mean in the chair. I mean—"

Her hand caught his sleeve before she had finished deciding whether touching him would make it worse.

[B] "You don't have to say it back. I just didn't want to keep waiting until I wasn't angry to tell you."

Mau looked down at the fingers in his sleeve.

He did not remember the first time she had done it. The wanting in her face was not unfamiliar, but he could not find the old place from which he was meant to know how to answer.

He put his right hand over hers.

[M] "I'm glad you asked me."

She breathed out.

It was not the answer she had once been able to expect without asking. It was an answer he had given her while standing here.

[B] "You can come again."

[M] "If Ryo promises a shorter ending."

From inside the room, without looking up:

[Ry] "No."

Bocchi laughed once. Let go of his sleeve.

Mau went down the corridor, and she went back to help Kita with the pages.
