# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 21 — You Can Sit There

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 21
**Source episode:** S6E52
**Continuity state:** Day473 morning. Mau asks to spend ordinary time with Ori while she and Rem shell dried beans for the kitchen. Ori already knows the attack from Fern's report in13. He acknowledges wanting to rebuild their relationship without claiming recovered fatherly feeling or promising a memory deadline. Her love for her mother does not make her responsible for Frieren's act, and she need not choose which adult to protect. A short shared task is allowed, not reconciliation or approval of the romance. Yuka is elsewhere and no one expels her from home. No new family status, magic, cure or power. Roof/crackers are due tonight; no gathering has yet happened.

*When: Day473, morning. The kitchen end of the long room. Ori has decided that the empty pods need a bigger bowl than the beans.*

Rem had told her otherwise twice.

Now she let Ori finish pouring the shells from one bowl to the other. They filled the larger one halfway, rose above the edge at the back and slid down into the space Ori had just cleared in front.

[O] "See?"

[Re] "I see the beans are in the smaller one."

[O] "There are fewer."

[Re] "There will be more when we finish."

Ori looked at the heap still waiting on the cloth.

[O] "Then we'll change it again."

Rem considered the logic. Took another pod.

That was when Mau stopped at the table.

He had not intended to make a secret approach. Ori could see the place where he slowed and the way he looked at the chair before he looked at her. She had become very good at watching the little preparations people made when they were about to be careful with her.

[M] "Can I help?"

Rem glanced at Ori.

Ori looked down at the pod in her hand.

[O] "You can ask her. It's her beans."

Mau rested his right hand on the chair back.

[M] "I was asking whether you wanted me here."

Rem did not find a reason to speak over the question.

For a while Ori picked at a dry thread along the pod. It came free in a long piece. She put it into the shell bowl.

[O] "You can sit there."

* * *

He sat where she pointed.

Not beside her. Across the corner, where both could reach the cloth without their elbows meeting. Rem showed him which end to split, then watched him make much more work of the first pod than it required.

[Re] "You don't have to keep the outside whole."

[M] "I wasn't sure."

[O] "We're not putting them back."

He looked at the half-open shell in his hand.

[M] "That would explain the bowls."

Ori put three beans into the smaller one.

Mau tried another pod. His left fingers did not pinch the dry edge well, so he settled it across his palm and used his right thumb to open the seam.

One bean sprang out, hit the table and rolled toward Ori.

She stopped it with the side of her hand.

[M] "Thank you."

She dropped it into the bowl with more force than the bean deserved.

He looked down at the next pod rather than thanking her again for not throwing it at him.

* * *

Rem had plenty to do without leaving the room.

She moved to the preparation shelf when the pan needed her, keeping the bean cloth in sight while she measured something into a different bowl. Ori knew she was giving them space. She could tell the difference between that and going away to make them solve it.

Mau opened four pods before speaking again.

[M] "I heard you helped her in the room."

Ori's hand stopped.

[O] "Who told you?"

[M] "Fern said you were together. Not what you said."

Ori put the empty shell down.

[O] "She's my mama."

Mau nodded.

[O] "I know what she did."

[M] "I wasn't asking you to stop being with her."

Ori looked at him properly for the first time since he sat down.

[O] "Are you angry with her?"

He let the next pod rest on the cloth.

[M] "Yes."

She looked at his right hand. The one which had gone into the line where the spell was going, according to Fern. She had not seen it, but she had spent a long time imagining the part before it stopped.

[O] "And me?"

[M] "I'm not angry with you for loving her."

[O] "I was angry with Yuka. I still am."

He waited.

[O] "Does that mean I have to stop being angry now?"

[M] "No."

The answer was quick enough that she looked back up.

[M] "You don't have to make yourself feel differently because somebody else did something worse."

Ori watched him for another moment. Then she picked up the pod again.

* * *

The smaller bowl began to fill.

Ori moved it into the space between them, where Mau could reach without leaning. He changed the direction of his next handful and did not make a sentence out of the help.

Rem came back to check how much was left. She put another cloth beneath the shell bowl to keep it from scraping the table when Ori pushed it away from her elbow.

[Re] "This one is winning."

[O] "I told you."

Mau looked at the two bowls.

[M] "I wasn't here for the vote."

[O] "It wasn't a vote."

[M] "More of a declaration."

Rem smiled at the cloth while Ori tried not to.

Then a voice in the corridor laughed at something, and the little movement left Ori's face.

[O] "You laughed with Bocchi."

Mau looked toward the corridor.

[O] "Last night. I heard."

[M] "She asked me to listen."

[O] "I know."

There was no accusation prepared after it. That made him take more care with the answer he had almost offered.

[M] "It was nice. I liked being there."

Ori traced a split in the empty pod with one nail.

[O] "Do you like being here?"

[M] "With you?"

She nodded without looking up.

[M] "Yes."

Her finger stopped moving.

* * *

[O] "Do you want to?"

Mau did not know which part she meant until she added it.

[O] "Remember. Love me. Do you want that?"

Rem set down what she was holding at the shelf.

Mau looked at the girl across the corner of the table. The hand which had hit him that morning in front of everybody had a dry bean skin caught beside the thumb now. Her face was trying to be ready before he answered.

[M] "Yes."

Ori's mouth opened a little.

[M] "I don't know how to make the feeling come back. I don't want to tell you it has when you can hear it hasn't. But I want to know you. I want to be able to sit here without making you wonder whether I'm waiting to leave."

She looked at the chair beneath him.

[O] "You do leave."

[M] "I have."

He had no useful excuse to put after that. She had been there when he panicked. Had been left to watch the place where he stopped being there.

[M] "I'm sorry. You needed an answer and I couldn't get one out. Then I went away before you could say the rest."

[O] "I said a lot."

[M] "You did. I heard you."

Ori pressed the pod flat between her palms.

[O] "I don't want a new person who feels sorry for me."

The sentence hurt him without giving him a memory to explain the hurt.

[M] "I'm not trying to give you somebody else."

She shook her head, frustrated.

[O] "I know you're you. That's why it's worse."

He looked down.

There was nothing on the cloth he could finish quickly enough to make that sentence easier.

* * *

Rem came back to the table.

She did not sit between them. Put the last handful of unopened pods nearer Ori and took the full shell bowl to empty it into the larger container by the preparation shelf.

The smaller bowl had become crowded with beans.

Ori watched Rem return the empty one.

[O] "Now we change them."

[Re] "Now we change them."

They poured the beans into the larger bowl. One caught in a split in the wood and needed a fingertip to persuade it out. Mau let Ori do it.

Afterward Rem went back to the pan.

Ori reached for another pod. Put one within Mau's reach too.

[O] "You can stay until we're done."

He nodded.

[M] "I'd like that."

She did not say she would like it. Not yet.

They worked quietly for a while. The bigger bowl began making sense of what it held, and the empty shells stopped threatening to fall off the table.

When the last bean went in, Ori found the dry thread still caught against her thumb. Mau had seen it several times. He had not tried to take it off for her.

She picked it free and laid it with the shells.

[O] "Don't tell everybody I was better."

Mau looked up.

[O] "Because we did something. I don't want them looking at me like it worked."

[M] "I won't."

Rem came over to lift the bowl.

Ori stood before Mau could mistake staying until they finished for permission to follow wherever she went next. She took the empty shells to the preparation shelf and asked Rem where they belonged.

Mau gathered the cloth.

For now, he had sat where she asked him to sit. He had stayed until the work was done.
