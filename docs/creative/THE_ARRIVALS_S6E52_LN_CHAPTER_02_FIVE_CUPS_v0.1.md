# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 22 — Five Cups

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 22
**Source episode:** S6E52
**Continuity state:** Day473 after dinner, the next weekly roof date after466 was not held. Mau/Rimuru/Yuta/Gojo/Taihei choose to meet using the east-corridor roof hatch, not the sleeping loft ladder. Their continuing gathering does not restore trust or approve the romance. Yuta's practice with Mau remains paused. Taihei knows Umaru's hurt through his own relationship with her, without repeating her private words to the group. No private content passes from crackers by cross-cut, and no new power, medical result or household arrangement is decided on the roof.

*When: Day473, after dinner. The roof hatch. Yuta is passing up a tray which Gojo has declared unnecessarily heavy.*

[Yt] "Then take one cup."

[G] "That would make the tray uneven."

[R] "You can take two."

Gojo looked at the man beside him, then at Yuta below the hatch. He took two cups and held them with the expression of somebody performing work which would otherwise have taken an entire department.

Taihei took the other side of the tray.

Yuta came up after it. Sat near enough to the hatch that nobody would have to step over his legs on the way back down.

There were five cups.

Rimuru looked at the last one without moving it. From below came a foot on the ladder, a pause, then the careful sound of somebody finding the edge above his head before putting a hand through.

Mau came up.

Nobody had to ask why he used the ladder.

* * *

The wind was not especially strong.

Gojo complained about it anyway. Rimuru told him which place had less of it, and Gojo looked across the roof with the suspicion of someone who remembered that a better position for one man generally required somebody else to sit where the better-positioned man had been.

[G] "I'm comfortable."

[R] "You just said you weren't."

[G] "I was describing conditions."

Taihei set his cup between his feet until he could arrange the cloth beneath him without threatening anybody else's tea.

[T] "That explains a lot of the complaints at home."

[G] "I'm invaluable."

Mau sat on the clear part of the ridge. His cup was still on the tray. Yuta moved it nearer with two fingers, leaving enough room beside it for him to choose how to pick it up.

[M] "Thank you."

Yuta nodded.

The silence after the small exchange did not belong to the tea.

* * *

They had missed the last one.

There was no need to decide whether it counted as cancelled when nobody had been able to think about coming up. The evening had passed downstairs and behind different doors. By the time somebody remembered which day it was, the ordinary hour had gone.

Tonight Rimuru had asked.

Not announced that they were going back to normal. He had found each of the others separately and said he intended to go up after dinner if anybody wanted to come.

Yuta had taken longer to answer than the others.

Now he sat with the cup held between both hands, looking past the roof toward the dark line of trees.

Mau could see him without looking straight at him.

He had been able to remember that much of how to share a place. It did not tell him what Yuta wanted from him tonight.

[G] "How's your sister's latest plan?"

Taihei looked up.

[T] "Which one?"

[G] "The one she was explaining with most of the table."

[T] "That doesn't narrow it down."

Rimuru smiled into his cup.

[R] "The food containers."

Taihei let out a long breath.

[T] "She wants three sizes. She says two creates false choices."

[G] "Does it?"

[T] "It created an argument."

* * *

The argument had apparently begun with a very reasonable wish to carry enough food without having the last portion crushed beneath everything else.

From there Umaru had discovered that almost any object could be made to fit a container if a person was willing to make the container large enough to become a different problem.

Taihei had refused to carry one merely to demonstrate that it was technically portable.

[T] "She said Mau could."

He stopped after saying it.

Mau looked down at the cup.

[M] "I probably could."

[G] "Don't encourage the development of a lunch box which needs a carrier."

[R] "You'd be first on her list."

[G] "Then I support all reasonable research."

Taihei laughed. It lasted just long enough for the story to become the story again instead of a thing he ought to have been more careful about mentioning.

Yuta's mouth moved slightly.

Mau noticed because he had been trying not to watch for it.

* * *

For a while they talked about things which did not need settling.

Whether carrying food up a ladder counted as work after dinner. How long Gojo could make a perfectly ordinary chore sound like a contribution to the household's survival. The difference between knowing where a thing belonged and repeatedly finding that somebody else had put it there first.

Yuta said less than usual. He did not sit as if he were waiting for an excuse to leave.

Mau drank his tea too quickly and had nothing left to do with the cup.

He set it on the tray. Then picked it up again when the wind moved the loose edge of the cloth beneath it.

[Yt] "It won't fall."

Mau looked at him.

Yuta moved the tray farther from the slight slope.

[Yt] "There."

[M] "I know. I just—"

He stopped.

Yuta let him stop instead of finding a kind ending for the sentence.

Across the ridge Gojo had begun telling Taihei about the two bags which had briefly become his most demanding pupils. He got as far as describing their excellent attendance before Rimuru told him bags could not leave.

[G] "Exactly. Ideal students."

Mau almost laughed. Looked at Yuta first.

Yuta saw him do it.

* * *

[Yt] "You don't have to check whether I'm letting you."

The others went quiet.

Mau held the empty cup against his knee.

[M] "I wasn't sure."

[Yt] "I know."

Yuta looked down at his own hands.

[Yt] "I didn't come up here because I've stopped being angry. I came because I wanted to sit up here."

Mau nodded.

[Yt] "I'm not going to make every time you laugh into something you did to me."

For a moment there was nothing Mau could say which would not sound as if he had been given a larger permission than the one Yuta meant.

[M] "All right."

Yuta looked back toward the trees.

[Yt] "You can be here. I'm still here too."

Rimuru let a little time pass before reaching for the pot.

[R] "There's more, if anybody wants it."

Mau held out his cup.

* * *

Taihei waited until Gojo had taken the second pour.

He did not have a speech. Mau could tell by the way he began, as if he had decided he was tired of needing the right opening before saying anything difficult.

[T] "She still wants to show you things."

Mau looked at him.

[T] "Umaru. She'll complain about you for half an hour and then find something and start looking for where you've gone."

Mau's fingers tightened on the cup.

[T] "I'm not asking you to make that into a different feeling. I just don't want you to decide she only helps because she wants a result out of you."

[M] "I don't think that."

Taihei studied his face for a moment.

[T] "Good."

He leaned back enough to get comfortable on the cloth at last.

[T] "Because she can make the result very loud. That isn't all that's there."

Mau thought of Umaru holding the paper down with her elbow, writing down what had actually happened instead of leaving his name beside a mistake two people had made.

[M] "I know."

This time he had an afternoon to put behind the words.

* * *

Nobody asked him to settle the rest of it from the roof.

There were questions which would have turned the five cups into a meeting they had not agreed to hold. What would happen if the memories did not return. How many days they could keep arranging themselves around the places people were afraid to meet. Whether ordinary invitations helped when the things behind them had become so difficult.

The questions did not go away because no one spoke them.

For a while the wind eased. Taihei stretched one leg, made a quiet, painful sound and accused the roof of having become harder since last time.

[G] "You're getting old."

[T] "You're older."

[G] "A vicious rumour."

[R] "A number."

Yuta laughed once.

It was short. He did not turn toward Mau to explain whether it meant anything else.

Mau laughed too.

* * *

They went down before it became too cold.

Not in a rush. Rimuru took the pot, Taihei folded the cloth and Gojo offered excellent advice to the people moving things while moving one cup of his own.

Yuta waited near the hatch until Mau had both feet set properly.

[Yt] "I'll pass the tray when you're down."

Mau looked at him.

[M] "Okay."

He took the ladder slowly. The thin edge of his sight made the shadowed space below the hatch less easy to judge, and he used the rungs rather than trusting how far away the floor appeared.

When he reached it, he looked up.

Yuta lowered the tray. Mau took its weight with his right hand, steadied the edge against his forearm and put it on the clear part of the corridor floor before moving away from the ladder.

Five cups. All of them intact.

Yuta came down after it.

He still had not asked Mau to bring the practice sword tomorrow.
