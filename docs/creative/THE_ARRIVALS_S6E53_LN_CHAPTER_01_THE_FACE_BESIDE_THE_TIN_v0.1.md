# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 23 — The Face Beside the Tin

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S6E53
**Continuity state:** Day473 evening, parallel to the roof. Seiko/Mai/Shoko/Turbo Granny meet in Seiko's room. Maki declines Mai's invitation to remain with her own family; Frieren declines through Fern. Neither is secretly present or represented by a proxy. Seiko has explicitly told Turbo Granny the public attack/block/no-hit facts before tonight. Shoko knows the pledge not to hurt Yuka from11; she does not relay private confessions or Ori's conversation13. The portrait Mai made on459 remains beside the tin, providing an ordinary comic activity. This partial gathering is not reconciliation, an agreement to forgive or knowledge of roof dialogue. No power, injury, new household policy or use of the staff.

*When: Day473, evening. Seiko's room. There are four people and a portrait whose subject has begun disputing the evidence.*

[Se] "My chin doesn't do that."

Mai looked at the paper.

Then at Seiko.

[Mai] "It does when you say that."

Turbo Granny made a small, delighted noise from the cushion.

Seiko moved the drawing farther from her face. It had been beside the biscuit tin since the evening Mai made it. For several days she had accepted it as an affectionate likeness. Tonight, with the lamp on the other side, she had discovered something which required an explanation.

[TG] "More light. Let her compare."

[Se] "You can be next."

[TG] "Already perfect. Waste of paper."

Shoko sat on the clear end of the bed with her cup. She had taken off her shoes and placed them where nobody would trip over them. Seiko noticed the care she had taken, remembered what the last few evenings had asked of all of them, and moved the tin within her reach without commenting.

* * *

There should have been more people.

Not because the room needed filling. There was less knee against knee tonight, more space to put a cup down without negotiating the edge of somebody's skirt.

Mai had asked Maki that afternoon.

Her sister had looked toward the corridor before answering. She was staying with Yuta and Yuka. Not a message for Seiko to interpret, not a promise that she would feel differently after a cup of tea.

Frieren had said no when Fern asked her.

Fern had brought back the answer exactly that large. Seiko had not sent her upstairs to ask again.

Now Turbo Granny pushed the tin a little toward herself.

[TG] "You can stop looking at the door."

Mai looked away from it.

[TG] "She said she wasn't coming."

[Mai] "I know."

Seiko had told the cat what happened before tonight. Enough to stop the garden becoming one more thing she had to assemble from people being careful when she entered a room.

Turbo Granny had listened without interrupting.

It was one of the reasons Seiko had not asked her to be more tactful now.

* * *

Shoko took one biscuit.

She broke it in half over her palm and caught a loose crumb with her thumb before it could fall onto the blanket. The small piece gave her something to finish before answering Mai's question.

[Mai] "Have you seen her today?"

[Sh] "Yes."

[Mai] "Is she still in that chair?"

[Sh] "When I went in. She ate a little. Ori was in and out. Fern was there for part of it."

Mai looked at the cup between her hands.

[Mai] "Yesterday she told us where we'd put the cushion wrong. For a moment it was just her being annoying."

Seiko snorted.

[Se] "Accurately annoying."

[Mai] "Then I looked at her hands and remembered."

Shoko set the biscuit half on the cloth beside her.

[Sh] "She told us she meant it."

The room went quiet.

[Sh] "Fern asked. She answered. I don't want that turning into a different story because she's barely speaking now."

Mai shook her head.

[Mai] "I'm not trying to make it different."

[Sh] "I know."

Turbo Granny looked toward the little space where Frieren usually put her feet when somebody complained about how much room she occupied.

[TG] "And the girl?"

[Sh] "Frightened. Nobody was hit. Those aren't the same answer."

Mai put her cup down before it could begin rattling against her fingers.

* * *

Seiko passed the tin again.

Not to end the conversation. It was there, and people could be hungry while discussing a thing which had frightened them. She refused to let the whole room wait for permission to be ordinary.

Mai took one, then looked at the piece in her hand as if she had accepted something more difficult.

[Mai] "I want to see my sister without every sentence becoming about this."

[Se] "Did you tell her that?"

[Mai] "I asked whether she wanted to come tonight."

[Se] "That's a different question."

Mai looked up.

[Se] "You can ask her to walk somewhere with you. Just you. She can still say no."

Mai turned the biscuit between her fingers.

[Mai] "I don't want her to think I'm going to ask her to go and sit with Frieren."

[Sh] "Then tell her before you ask."

It sounded easy in the room where Maki wasn't.

Mai knew that. So did the other women. None of them added a promise that her sister would understand every part of the invitation the way she intended it.

[Mai] "I might."

Seiko nodded and let that be enough of a plan for tonight.

* * *

The portrait had slipped behind the tin.

Turbo Granny pulled it out by one corner with a paw and looked at it sideways. Seiko watched her decide how much damage could be done with a paper she had not drawn herself.

[TG] "No. She's right."

Mai looked suspicious.

[TG] "The chin's wrong."

[Se] "Thank you."

[TG] "You made it too kind."

Shoko laughed into her cup.

Seiko took the portrait back before the cat could develop the criticism into a lecture. Mai reached for it too, then stopped when Seiko laid it carefully on the little table instead of crumpling it.

[Se] "Go on, then. Show me what you meant."

[Mai] "I meant your face."

[Se] "A serious allegation."

Mai laughed despite herself. Reached into the little pocket of her sewing roll, where she had put a short pencil after using it to mark a length of cloth.

[Mai] "I'm not drawing another whole one."

[TG] "Just the offending section."

Seiko held still with great theatrical patience.

It lasted less than a minute.

* * *

Mai tried to sketch the line on a spare piece of paper.

Not over the old portrait. Seiko had moved that out of reach while asking for the correction, which made her motives less difficult to interpret than she seemed to think.

Shoko leaned forward to watch.

Mai drew one curve. Looked up. Seiko changed her expression.

[Mai] "Stop doing that."

[Se] "Doing what?"

[Mai] "That."

Turbo Granny fell sideways against the cushion with an unpleasant little laugh.

Shoko took the pencil when Mai held it out in surrender. Put two marks beside the curve and turned the paper round.

[Sh] "There."

Mai looked.

[Mai] "That's worse."

[Se] "Who is that supposed to be?"

[Sh] "Someone very patient about having her chin examined."

Seiko stared at the drawing. Then at Shoko, who had remained far too composed.

[Se] "You've been waiting to do that."

[Sh] "Not long."

Mai began laughing properly. She bent forward over her knees and had to put the biscuit back in the tin because she could no longer trust herself to keep it out of her mouth while breathing.

* * *

The laughter did not last all evening.

It ended without anyone apologising for having done it. Mai sat back and wiped beneath one eye with the side of her finger. Shoko put the pencil on the table, and Seiko looked at both drawings for a while before choosing where to put them.

The old portrait went beside the tin again.

The new attempt stayed under her hand.

[Mai] "You can throw that one away."

[Se] "I might need it as evidence."

Mai smiled. Then looked at the door once more.

[Mai] "I wish they'd both come."

Nobody asked whether she meant tonight or a different evening on which that wish could become true without making somebody afraid.

[Sh] "So do I."

The agreement was quiet enough not to turn into a plan for going to fetch them.

* * *

They stayed until the tea had gone cool.

Turbo Granny claimed the last biscuit had been placed closer to her by natural law. Seiko challenged the interpretation. Mai ate half before the dispute could be settled and offered the other half to Shoko, who took it with the shamelessness of a woman who had learned something from spending time in this room.

When Mai stood, she put the pencil back in the sewing roll and hesitated beside the little table.

[Mai] "I can ask Maki tomorrow. Just for a walk."

[Se] "You can."

Shoko put her shoes on. Checked beneath the bed for the second one after Turbo Granny had managed to move it without anyone noticing.

[Sh] "A demanding room."

[TG] "Ideal company."

Mai went out smiling at that. The smile faded before she reached the corridor turn, but it had been there when she said good night.

Seiko stayed behind to stack the cups.

She put four on the tray. Left the other two on the shelf where they had been before anyone came.

The portrait remained beside the tin.
