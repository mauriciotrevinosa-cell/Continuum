## Chapter 25 — What Was Taken

*When: Day474, afternoon. The west path inside the protection. Mau has told Rimuru where he is walking and when he will be back.*

He had meant to ask from the house.

The thought had been there while he washed the dust of the mill out of the cloth bag. While he put the two crystals where the short account of the trip could remain with them instead of becoming a story somebody had to ask him to repeat later.

He could have looked up at the ceiling and asked.

There would have been people at the table. Someone would have stopped eating. Somebody would have wanted to know whom he meant before he knew whether there was anybody there to answer.

So he had told Rimuru he wanted a short walk. The known western path, no farther than the broad stone. Back before the late tea.

Now the house was behind the bend, and he stood in the cold sunlight with both hands empty.

[M] "Are you there?"

The leaves moved.

He looked down at the path. Felt very foolish. Waited anyway.

* * *

Ciel did not answer in place of the voice he meant.

She had asked him, before he left, whether he was looking for an ordinary quiet place or trying to contact someone. He had told her the second. She had wanted him to know that asking the Goddess was not the same as knowing she would help, or what her help would mean.

He had said he knew.

It had sounded prepared then. Standing here, it felt like something he ought to check more carefully before saying again.

[M] "Not the thing in my shadow. You. The cookies."

Another little movement in the scrub.

Then:

[Dio] *You remember the cookies.*

He did.

The field. The ridiculous wager. His own delight, intact in a place where the people he came home to had become difficult to recognise properly.

[M] "I remember that part very well."

For a moment he almost asked whether that was deliberate too.

He made himself ask the question he had come here with first.

[M] "Can you give me back what somebody took?"

* * *

There was no change in the light.

No wall lifting at the edge of his mind. No sudden rush of a room which became his because a word had finally been spoken in the right place.

The answer came into the same ordinary afternoon.

[Dio] *Yes.*

Mau sat on the stone.

He had not intended to. His knees bent before he had decided what kind of answer that was. Both feet stayed on the path, his right hand flat on the sun-warmed patch beside him.

[M] "Yes?"

[Dio] *It was not meant to be taken.*

Ciel said his name.

He realised she had only heard the things he said out loud. The other voice had reached him without becoming a sound he could point to in the trees.

[M] *She says yes. That she can give it back. That it wasn't meant to be taken.*

He passed the answer on deliberately. Ciel waited a moment before speaking.

[C] *Ask what she means by give it back. Before you agree to anything.*

He had not agreed.

He became grateful for that fact only after she said it.

* * *

[M] "What would you do?"

The Goddess did not answer at once.

There was an old anger waiting beneath the question. The knowledge that she could make a Trial and put him through it, make a door and put a child's life against it, give him a cookie because he had managed not to kill people who came to kill him.

He could remember the facts of those things even when the people inside them felt farther away than they should.

He did not need the missing feeling to know that being able to do something was not the same as understanding what doing it would cost.

[Dio] *Put back the history that belongs to you. The things you felt in it.*

[M] "Not just tell me what happened."

[Dio] *You have people doing that.*

He looked at the hand on the stone.

[M] "They have tried."

[Dio] *I know.*

He relayed the answer to Ciel. It took longer this time. He did not want *the things you felt* becoming a promise he had improved while repeating it.

Then he asked the other question.

[M] "Why didn't you?"

* * *

The afternoon continued around him while he waited.

A leaf worked free of a branch, turned over twice and landed where the path dipped. He watched it settle. There was a little line of grit on the stone beneath his hand which began to hurt after he had pressed it there long enough.

He lifted his palm.

[Dio] *Would you have wanted me to?*

He nearly answered yes.

Not because it was true of every day since the field. Because it was the answer he had come to a quiet place to ask her to make possible now.

He thought about the gate. The room where Ciel could reach him again, and what he had said when she told him about the woman waiting outside it.

[M] "I was afraid."

The Goddess waited.

[M] "Inside the last Red Gate. Ciel told me things I couldn't remember feeling. Yuka told me what was true before the field. I believed them. I still didn't want somebody to put me back if it meant what I had then stopped being mine."

He had not expected that part to be difficult to say when the woman he loved was not here to hear it.

[M] "I was happy with her. I am happy with her. That part happened after. Nobody made me kiss her by changing a story about what happened before. I chose it."

The voice became quieter.

[Dio] *Tell me.*

So he did.

* * *

He did not give her every private word.

He told her enough to make the choice his rather than a blank place she might decide to fill. The fear of losing Yuka. The relief of being near someone whose presence felt right. The knowledge that Frieren had been waiting and that he had let Yuka help keep the truth from the people who would be hurt by it.

The Goddess did not quote something he had said inside the gate before he told her himself.

He became aware of that while speaking. Of how easy it would have been to assume she had heard everything simply because she was the voice which could answer from nowhere out here.

[M] "You didn't hear it there?"

[Dio] *Not this conversation. You're telling me now.*

He looked toward the part of the path which led home.

The place where Ciel could find him again had not also been a place where the whole outside world got to listen.

[M] "Then why wait? You could see the rest."

Another pause.

[Dio] *I saw you happy. You were choosing. I wanted to see what you would do.*

It was not a comforting answer.

He was glad she had not made one merely because he needed it.

* * *

[M] "People are being hurt."

[Dio] *Yes.*

[M] "Ori thinks if she tries enough different ways I'll feel the thing I'm supposed to. Frieren can barely move some days. Yuka's frightened in the house where she lives. None of that stops because I can do one thing a little better in a field."

He rubbed the little mark the grit had left in his palm.

[M] "I don't want you to wait until I do something interesting enough."

The leaves moved behind him. He looked round before he could stop himself, though the voice had not come from there.

[Dio] *That is not why I answered.*

He waited.

[Dio] *You asked for help. I can help.*

[M] "Can you put it back without taking her away from me?"

For a long time there was no answer at all.

* * *

He understood, eventually, that the silence was part of what he needed to hear.

He had asked two different things and tried to make them sound like one simple repair. Return the stolen history. Keep the life he had built while he did not feel it.

He wanted both to arrive without anybody discovering how much of one had been made while the other was waiting in pain.

The Goddess spoke at last.

[Dio] *Giving back the old feeling does not mean the new one never happened.*

Mau shut his eyes.

Only for a moment. The old fear had expected an answer in which one of them became a mistake he could stop caring about by learning the correct version of the story.

[Dio] *But what you are asking is larger than returning a memory.*

He opened his eyes again.

[M] "I know."

This time he was not sure he had known until she said it.

He repeated the answer to Ciel. She listened, and he could feel the care with which she kept her next words from becoming relief.

[C] *We should speak to Rimuru. To the people who can help you think through what restoring the history means. An offer isn't a plan yet.*

[M] *I haven't said yes.*

[C] *I know. Please keep that distinction.*

* * *

[M] "Is there a price?"

The Goddess gave a small sound. Not quite a laugh. He could remember hearing it before and hating how close it could stand to something which would ruin him.

[Dio] *There are things we have to discuss.*

[M] "That isn't the same answer."

[Dio] *No.*

He let the silence remain instead of filling it with a bargain she had not offered yet.

There would be a shape to this. Conditions. Something which did not become harmless because the first word had been yes.

[M] "Then not now. I need to understand what I'm asking."

[Dio] *You may ask again.*

[M] "And you won't change something while I'm trying to work it out?"

[Dio] *Not because you asked me this afternoon.*

It was a narrow answer. He made himself hear exactly how narrow, and passed it on to Ciel without making it larger.

* * *

He stood when the stone began to feel cold beneath him.

The ordinary part of his body had been waiting through a conversation about things which might change every day he remembered living in it. His left hand was stiff. His foot had found a little hollow in the path and begun to ache from remaining there.

He moved both carefully.

[M] "Can I ask one thing that isn't a deal?"

The voice waited.

[M] "Do you want me to be happy? Or do you want to see what happens if I am?"

The answer took longer than he wanted.

[Dio] *I am learning the difference.*

He looked back toward the bend.

He could not decide whether that made him more or less frightened of asking again.

The voice was gone before he reached the place where the house became visible.

* * *

Ciel stayed with him on the walk back.

She did not speak as if they had found a treatment which could be announced to the table before supper. He was grateful for that. A part of him already wanted to run upstairs and tell every person who had waited that there was something real at the end of the waiting.

It was not real in that way yet.

[C] *What are you going to tell them?*

[M] *That I asked whether she could return what was taken. That she said yes. That I don't know the conditions.*

He paused beside the workshop wall, where somebody had left a clean length of wood out of the path.

[M] *Not that everything's going to be all right.*

Ciel did not answer immediately.

[C] *Start there.*

Rimuru was coming out of the office when Mau went in.

He stopped where the other man could see he had returned, before trying to look past him toward anybody else.

[M] "I'm back. I need to tell you what I asked."
