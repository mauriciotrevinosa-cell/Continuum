# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 26 — Only Yes So Far

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 26
**Source episode:** S6E54
**Continuity state:** Day474 late afternoon, after25. Mau reports the Goddess contact to Rimuru, then permits Shoko to join their discussion. They learn only the stated possibility of restoring the stolen history/feelings, the distinction between that and erasing what happened after, and that conditions are still unknown. Ciel remains private to the hosts, with deliberate spoken relay for Shoko. No one knows future two-heart terms, a rewrite proposal or second price. Mau agrees to bring back the actual terms for discussion before making a bargain affecting the house; this commitment matters if later broken. Yuka receives his direct, limited report afterward and reiterates not wanting to obstruct remembering. No cure, acceptance, deadline or public broadcast yet. Staff remains in Rimuru's office.

*When: Day474, late afternoon. The downstairs office. Rimuru has shut the door because Mau asked for a conversation, not because either man intends to make the room secret forever.*

Mau remained standing until Rimuru pointed at the chair.

Then he sat too quickly, put both hands against his knees and began with the part which had made him sit down outside.

[M] "She says she can give it back."

Rimuru did not smile.

He put the page he had been reading face down on the desk and waited for Mau to keep going.

[M] "The history. What I felt in it. Not just a list of things I did."

[R] "What did she ask for?"

[M] "Nothing yet."

The silence after that was different from the silence at the stone.

Mau looked at the edge of the desk.

[R] "Yet."

[M] "I asked whether there was a price. She said there were things we had to discuss. I said that wasn't an answer to the same question. She agreed."

Rimuru looked toward the shut door.

For a moment he seemed to be measuring the distance between this room and every person who would have stopped what they were doing to hear the first sentence.

[R] "All right. Start before the yes."

* * *

Mau told him about going to the stone.

Calling out. Waiting. Being certain he was going to have to come home and explain that he had spent an afternoon asking a voice to appear and watching a leaf fall down instead.

He told him what the Goddess answered. Which parts she had not answered. That he had told her about his own choices in the Red Gate, and she had not claimed to have heard the private conversations while they were there.

Rimuru listened without turning the account into a search for words he wished Mau had used instead.

When Mau reached the question about keeping the new feeling, he stopped.

[R] "What did you ask? Exactly."

[M] "Whether she could put it back without taking Yuka away from me."

Rimuru looked down.

[M] "She said giving back the old feeling didn't mean the new one never happened. Then she said what I was asking was bigger than returning a memory."

Ciel spoke on the thread both men shared by Mau's choice for this conversation.

[C] *He repeated those answers to me while he was there. No change to the memory has followed the contact that I can establish. We do not have a procedure to evaluate yet.*

Rimuru nodded. Not because he had suddenly stopped worrying.

[R] "Would you like Shoko here?"

Mau looked at him.

[R] "She's been doing the ordinary work of checking what this has and hasn't done to you. I don't want her trying to help with half an account because we got excited about the word yes."

Mau nodded.

[M] "Ask her."

* * *

Shoko came with the expression of someone who had expected a new symptom.

She looked at Mau first. His hands. The way he held his head. Only when he told her it was not that sort of visit did she take the other chair.

[Sh] "That is an improvement on your usual opening."

He almost smiled.

Rimuru let him tell her himself.

It was harder the second time. The first answer had begun losing the warmth of being a voice saying something he had wanted to hear. Now it was a statement which needed details before it could be given to people who had already waited through too many explanations without an end.

Shoko heard him out.

[Sh] "Has she told you whether she'd be restoring something which is still there, or making you feel something based on the history?"

Mau looked at Rimuru.

Then back at her.

[M] "She said put back what belongs to me. I didn't ask it in those words."

[Sh] "Then that's one question."

He nodded slowly.

[Sh] "I'm not saying I think she meant the second. I'm saying those are different things, and I don't want us improving her answer because the first sounds kinder."

Rimuru's thumb moved against the edge of his page.

[R] "We should write the questions. Not write that we have a treatment."

* * *

He found a clean part of an ordinary office sheet.

There was no new medical form. No heading which made the Goddess into a provider they could book and hold responsible for explaining an unwanted effect afterward.

Rimuru wrote one short line about what *returning* meant.

Then another about the conditions.

Shoko asked whether Mau had been told what the change would leave him able to remember about the days since the field. He answered that she had said the new feeling happened, not that she had explained exactly what he would experience if both histories came back.

Rimuru wrote that too.

Mau watched the questions become separate on the paper.

It should have made the conversation less frightening. Instead he could see how many things he had been willing to hide inside the first yes.

[M] "I wanted to tell them she could fix it."

[Sh] "I know."

[M] "I nearly went upstairs before I came here."

Rimuru stopped writing.

[R] "I'm glad you didn't."

He did not add that Frieren might have needed the hope. All three of them knew who would be left carrying it if the next answer changed what the first meant.

* * *

Ciel asked to have one part repeated to Shoko.

Mau listened first. Told her when the words were not becoming clear enough in his head to say them properly. She made the distinction smaller without making it softer.

[M] "She says I can choose to ask for my own memories. That doesn't mean any other part of a bargain is only about me. If she wants something which changes what the house has to live with, that needs its own question."

Shoko looked at him.

[Sh] "Yes."

Rimuru leaned back from the page.

[R] "Did she say there was going to be something like that?"

[M] "No. She didn't tell me the terms."

[R] "Then we don't invent them. But we don't agree to them blind either."

Mau looked at the staff behind the desk.

He had known it was there. Maki had put it there in front of him. Seeing it from the chair made it look like something left for a visitor to collect, as if the six days between taking it out of Frieren's hand and sitting here had been an inconvenient delay in returning an ordinary object.

He looked away.

[M] "People can't stay like this while we make a better list."

[R] "I know."

This time the answer was not gentle.

* * *

[R] "I'm not asking you to wait because the paper matters more than they do. I'm asking because if you tell us tomorrow that she offered you something and you took it before anyone could ask what it does, we will be living with your answer before we've heard the question."

Mau looked at him.

Rimuru kept looking back.

[R] "You've done that before. Sometimes it saved us. Sometimes we spent months trying to get you back from the part you thought only you should have to carry."

Mau's fingers moved against his knees.

He knew the events. Could attach the words to a door, to a field, to an old letter which Frieren had kept. The feeling behind all the choices remained partly hidden from him, but he did not need it to understand the accusation.

[M] "I haven't made a deal."

[R] "I believe you. I'm asking what you do when there is one."

Mau took time with the answer.

[M] "I bring back the words. We talk before I say yes to something which changes what happens to the house."

Shoko watched him while Rimuru nodded.

[Sh] "That includes if the answer looks very kind."

Mau looked down at the questions.

[M] "Yes."

* * *

They did not call everybody into the room.

Rimuru put the sheet beneath the page he had been reading, separate from the account of two crystals and an unexplored path inside the gate. Questions to ask, not a report of a cure already agreed.

Shoko said they could give the limited account to people who needed to hear what Mau was considering. No announcement that tomorrow would be different. No date beside a recovery nobody knew how to describe yet.

Mau wanted to tell Yuka himself.

Rimuru nodded.

[R] "And Frieren and Ori need the same care with the words. We can talk about how to do that. Don't let a piece of this reach them as a promise somebody else thinks you made."

[M] "I won't."

He stood slowly, letting his legs remember that he had been sitting for most of the afternoon in two different places.

Shoko went with him as far as the office door.

[Sh] "I'm glad you asked for help."

He looked back at her.

[Sh] "I mean that. It's allowed to stay true while we worry about the answer."

* * *

Yuka was in her room when he knocked.

She opened the door with a scrap of rope in one hand, looked at his face and put it down before asking what happened.

He told her the simple version. Then she asked, and he gave her the part which made it less simple: he did not know the conditions. He had not agreed. The Goddess had not said the life after the field would become something which had never happened.

Yuka sat on the edge of the bed.

The birthday bracelet moved when she put both hands in her lap. Neither of them looked at it for long.

[Yk] "You asked if you could keep this."

[M] "Yes."

She breathed in carefully.

[Yk] "I'm glad you want to."

Mau came nearer when she held out her hand.

[Yk] "I don't want to be the reason you don't remember. I meant that when I said it."

[M] "I know."

She looked at their joined hands.

[Yk] "It doesn't make me less frightened."

He sat beside her instead of telling her the Goddess had answered enough of the right questions to make fear unnecessary.

For a while they stayed there with the door partly open.

Downstairs, three questions lay beneath a page on Rimuru's desk.
