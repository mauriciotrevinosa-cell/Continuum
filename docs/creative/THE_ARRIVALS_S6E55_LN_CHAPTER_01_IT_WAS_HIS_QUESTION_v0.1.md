# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 27 — It Was His Question

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S6E55
**Continuity state:** Day475 morning. After confirming with Mau that she can give the limited account, Shoko tells Frieren and Fern about his voluntary question to the Goddess, possibility of restoration, unknown conditions and no accepted deal. No one is promised tomorrow or given the later two-heart/rewrite terms. Frieren recognises that restoring the old history would not erase what she did while he lacked it. Her care and regret do not oblige Yuka to see her. Ori receives the same bounded account directly from Shoko, not by secretly hearing an adult conversation. The staff remains downstairs; Frieren rises for ordinary washing, not a recovery montage or return to normal. No new magic, injury or memories.

*When: Day475, morning. The east room. Fern has opened the window a little and is trying to free the curtain from the latch without tearing it.*

Frieren watched her for a long time before speaking.

[F] "Lift it first."

Fern looked round.

Then back at the latch.

She lifted the cloth. The caught corner came loose at once.

[Fe] "I was doing that."

[F] "Not that corner."

Fern kept her face turned toward the window until she could make it ordinary again. It would have been too easy to turn round with relief large enough to make Frieren stop saying small things which sounded like herself.

She finished opening the window. Folded the loosened corner where it would not catch again.

[Fe] "Shoko asked if she could come in."

Frieren looked toward the door.

[F] "Is something wrong?"

[Fe] "She wants to tell us something Mau asked yesterday."

Frieren's hand moved against the chair arm.

Fern waited. She would not make the decision out of that movement.

After a little while, Frieren nodded.

* * *

Shoko did not stand over her.

She took the stool, moved it far enough from the window for Frieren not to have the light behind her face, and began before the room could manufacture something worse than what she had come to say.

[Sh] "Mau went for a walk yesterday. He called to the Goddess and asked whether she could return the history and feelings which were taken from him."

Frieren stared at her.

Fern sat down on the bed.

[Sh] "She said she could. He hasn't agreed to anything. We don't know what conditions she'd put on it."

Frieren looked at Fern.

The younger woman's face had changed before she could stop it. There was the same first, terrible movement in it that Frieren had felt in her own body when the word *return* reached her.

Shoko waited until they looked back at her.

[Sh] "I confirmed with him this morning that I could tell you that part. Not that we have a date. Not that he has been promised an answer which costs nothing."

[F] "He asked?"

[Sh] "Yes."

[F] "He wasn't told to?"

[Sh] "He chose to go."

Frieren closed her eyes.

* * *

For a moment she was somewhere much nearer the field.

The small movement with which he stepped away from her. His politeness afterward. Not cruelty she could argue with, not anger she could answer with the things she should have said before he left. A space where the old recognition ought to have been.

She opened her eyes before the room could become that place entirely.

[F] "What would she give back?"

[Sh] "What belongs to him. That's the answer he was given. We're asking what it means before treating it as a procedure we understand."

[Fe] "Both the things he did and what he felt?"

[Sh] "That's what he asked about. That's what he says she meant. We're keeping the questions separate because we don't want to turn one answer into several better ones."

Fern nodded slowly.

Frieren looked at the book beside the chair. The one she had carried into the garden. Its cover had begun to curl a little at the corner which had lain under the cloth.

[F] "And everything since?"

Shoko watched her.

[F] "He'd remember that too."

[Sh] "He wasn't told these days would stop having happened."

Frieren's hand came off the armrest.

* * *

She had been thinking of remembering as a road which ended before the worst part.

Not consciously. She knew the days had gone on. Had lived them, spoken in them, found little ways to hope until every ordinary movement began needing hope to explain why she could still make it.

But somewhere inside the wish was a morning when he woke in the old place and knew her hand.

Now she could see a different part of that morning.

Mau knowing the woman he loved. Knowing where that woman had aimed while he stood between her and somebody else.

[F] "Then he won't come back to the person I was before."

Fern looked at her.

[F] "I'm here too. This one."

She looked at her empty hands.

Shoko did not tell her the other person had merely been pain wearing her face. Frieren knew who had held the staff. She had been trying to keep the answer that clear even when there were smaller words she could put in front of it.

[F] "What if he remembers loving me and can't bear to look at me?"

The question came out quietly.

Fern's fingers tightened against the edge of the blanket.

* * *

[Sh] "I don't know how he'll feel about everything together."

Frieren looked up.

[Sh] "I won't promise it for him. He doesn't know yet either."

The room became quiet enough for the little movement of the curtain to sound like something asking to be noticed.

[F] "I could tell myself I hadn't meant—"

She stopped before the sentence reached the easy word.

Fern sat very still.

[F] "I know I meant it. I keep coming to a place where I could make it different in the telling. I know where I was aiming."

Shoko let the silence remain after that.

Frieren had begun to hate being thanked for telling the truth about what she had done, though nobody in this room had asked her to be dishonest. It felt like being handed back some small piece of the person she wanted to be before she had worked out what to do with the person who had hurt someone.

[F] "I want him back. That doesn't make any of this go away."

[Fe] "No."

Fern's answer was quiet and completely steady.

Frieren nodded once.

* * *

After a while Fern asked whether Mau was going back to speak to the Goddess today.

Shoko told her he had not given her another time. He had agreed to bring back actual terms before making a bargain affecting the house. There were questions waiting downstairs, not a treatment already arranged.

Frieren looked toward the door.

[F] "Does Yuka know?"

[Sh] "He told her himself yesterday."

Frieren closed her hands.

There was a part of her which wanted the sentence to be another thing taken from her. The first telling. The first frightened face he had chosen to sit beside while he explained what might happen next.

She saw where that thought would go if she let it turn into a reason.

[F] "I don't want you to bring her here."

Shoko waited.

[F] "Even if I ask you later because I think I know what to say. She doesn't have to be ready because I am."

[Sh] "I won't bring her."

Fern let out a slow breath.

Frieren looked at her.

[F] "I should have been able to understand that before."

Fern did not argue.

* * *

Ori was in the connecting room with Milim.

She came through when Fern went to ask whether she wanted the limited news Shoko had brought. Not a secret overheard through a door, not something important which had happened while everybody decided how much she could stand.

She sat on the bed rather than in Frieren's lap.

Shoko told her the same thing.

Mau had asked. The Goddess said returning the stolen part was possible. No agreement yet. No day they could promise it would happen.

Ori listened without interrupting.

Then:

[O] "Does he want her to?"

Shoko took care with the answer.

[Sh] "He wants to understand what saying yes would do. He went to ask because he wants help."

Ori looked at Frieren.

Neither of them smiled for the other.

[O] "So we still wait."

[Sh] "For the next answer. Not because you have to do something better to make him ask it."

Ori put her hands beneath her knees. Watched the curtain for a while.

[O] "Okay."

It was not relief. Shoko did not call it that.

* * *

When Shoko left, Fern remained on the bed.

Ori went back through the connecting door after telling Milim she would. She had left something in the middle of a game; the other girl had been trying very hard not to move it while waiting.

Frieren looked at the book again.

[F] "Could you read something?"

Fern leaned forward.

[Fe] "This one?"

[F] "If you want. Something you've been doing."

Fern rested one hand on the cover. For a moment she thought of the little changes she had wanted to show her teacher, the questions which kept being saved for an afternoon when there was more of Frieren available to answer.

[Fe] "I can bring mine."

Frieren nodded.

Fern began to get up.

[F] "After I wash."

The words made her stop.

Frieren put both feet down and stood. Slowly, using the chair arm for the first part because she had been sitting too long, not because standing had become a new injury no one understood.

Fern moved the stool out of her way.

* * *

The water was cold.

Frieren touched it, drew her hand back and looked at the basin as if it had made an unnecessary contribution to the morning.

Fern almost laughed.

She waited until Frieren looked at her.

[Fe] "It won't become warmer because you disapprove."

[F] "It might."

Neither reached for magic.

Frieren washed her face. Took the cloth Fern held out and dried it without arranging the result into someone who was ready to go downstairs and resume a life nobody else had agreed to return to.

When she sat again, Fern brought her own book.

She read a little. Stopped to explain a change she had made in the margin. Frieren asked her to go back to the line before it.

Fern did.

The second time, Frieren followed far enough to ask a question.

There was no promise in the room that tomorrow would be different.

Fern answered anyway.
