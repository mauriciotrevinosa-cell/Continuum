*When: Day475, afternoon. The air above the settlement. Kaisel has just lifted clear of the trees, and Yuka is trying to keep her scarf out of Mau's mouth.*

She caught the end on the third try.

By then he had turned his face away twice and made a noise which she felt through his back before she understood he was laughing.

[Yk] "Hold still."

[M] "I'm not the part moving."

She tucked the end inside her coat. Waited to make certain it stayed. Then put her arms round his waist again and rested her forehead between his shoulders for a moment.

Below them the yard became smaller.

The workshop roof. The broad tree. A pale stretch of path where somebody was walking toward the kitchen with something held in both arms. Yuka could no longer tell who, and let herself stop trying.

Kaisel turned north.

They crossed the edge of the protected ground without a flash or a sound. Mau watched the known line fall behind them and asked him to keep the course they had described before leaving.

No frame. No change of sky.

Just the country opening beneath the wings.

* * *

The wind was colder above the trees.

Not bitter enough to make breathing hurt, but it found the place where Mau's collar had folded inward and stayed there with great determination. Yuka reached round him to put it right.

[M] "Better."

[Yk] "You're welcome."

[M] "I was talking to the dragon."

She pressed her forehead against his back again.

He laughed and caught her wrist gently before she could make the correction physical. The little flower on its cord touched the edge of his sleeve. He let go so she could put her hand somewhere warmer.

Ahead, the low ridges lay one behind another, blue-grey where the sun did not reach them. Their chosen slope was nearer than the tallest one. A broad shoulder of grass and rock, with a lower rise behind it and enough open ground to see from the air where the wings could settle.

Mau pointed with his right hand.

[M] "That side?"

Yuka leaned far enough to see past him without shifting her weight off the middle of Kaisel's back.

[Yk] "The pale bit?"

[M] "Beyond it. Behind that little rise. It looks less windy."

She watched the long grass bend in one place and remain almost still in another.

[Yk] "Try it slowly."

He asked Kaisel to circle once before descending.

* * *

The first place was not the place they kept.

It looked dry until they were close. Then the light changed on the flat stone and showed a wet skin running down one edge, very thin, fed by something higher up the slope.

Mau saw it clearly on his right. He kept his head turned until Kaisel had passed, rather than trusting the softened edge on the left to become more useful because they were arriving somewhere beautiful.

[M] "Not there."

Kaisel banked away without hurrying.

They settled farther along the same shoulder, on grass lying below a shelf of rock. The dragon folded his wings while both stayed seated.

Mau called Igris onto the clear ground beside them and asked him to check the footing. The knight walked the short strip between the landing and the first outcrop, looking down before turning toward the slope.

[Ig] "Dry here, my liege. The water is farther along."

Only then did Mau get down.

He reached up for the bag.

Yuka passed it to him, then found the lower hold with her boot and climbed down herself. He kept one hand near her elbow until both her feet were on the grass.

She looked up.

The view had changed while she was concentrating on the way down.

The house was a small interruption in the woods. Beyond it were folds of land which had looked almost flat from the yard. Light lay across the tops of them and left the hollows dark.

[Yk] "Oh."

Mau followed her eyes.

He did not add anything to it.

* * *

Igris waited beside the landing place, looking first toward the open slope and then toward his liege. Mau indicated the short path round the lower rise, the dry ground they could already see from where they stood.

[M] "We're going a little way along there. Keep watch from this side. Call if something comes near."

[Ig] "Yes, my liege."

The knight took a place where he could see the landing and the turn of the path. Kaisel remained on the broader shoulder, wings folded clear of the rock.

[M] *Can you tell Rimuru we've landed? The nearer slope. We're looking for a dry place behind the rise.*

[C] *Yes. The return plan remains the same?*

[M] *Before midnight. Earlier if this stops being sensible.*

[C] *I'll tell him where you are.*

Yuka had taken the bag while he spoke. She shifted its strap off the fold of her coat, which the wind had begun lifting round her knees.

[Yk] "Are we eating here?"

[M] "Would you like to?"

A gust came round the rock and slapped the loose cloth against the side of the bag.

They both looked at it.

[Yk] "No."

* * *

The path was short enough that they could still see Igris when they reached the turn.

It was not a made path. Grass worn thin between two outcrops, loose little stones collected where the ground dipped. Yuka went first at the narrow part, put one foot down, felt the gravel move and took it back.

Mau stopped behind her.

[Yk] "Wait."

She crouched and pushed the loose pieces aside with her glove. Under them was flat rock, dry in the middle, dark where the trickle from above had found its way across.

She stood. Chose the dry part.

Mau followed, choosing the same dry footing. His left hand stayed on the rough side of the outcrop; the stiff fingers did not need to close round it to tell him where the stone was.

On the other side the wind dropped at once.

They stood there, surprised by how much of the noise had belonged to the air moving past their ears.

There was a shallow hollow behind the rise, with grass, a broad dry stone and a little channel slipping down into a long pool in the rock. The water was clear enough to see the brown bottom. A thin pale edge remained where the shadow had kept one side cold.

Yuka put the bag down on the grass.

[Yk] "Here."

Mau looked back toward the landing. Igris stood within sight at the turn, dark and still against the light.

[M] "Here."

* * *

They left the food wrapped.

Yuka wanted to see where the water came from first. Mau went with her along the safe side of the little pool, where the stone was rough rather than polished by the trickle.

The channel narrowed into a crack between two rocks. Beyond that there was only a dark seam and the sound of more water than they could see.

[Yk] "It's hiding."

[M] "Badly. We found it."

She put one gloved hand above the seam, where a very small amount of spray had begun wetting the stone.

[Yk] "Don't tell it."

They stayed there long enough to watch a loose blade of grass enter the channel, catch sideways and turn until the water took it through. Then they followed it back toward the pool and lost it against the brown bottom.

Mau found the first flat stone beside his boot.

He turned it in his hand, looked at the water and threw.

It landed with one small, complete plunk.

Yuka looked at the circle spreading from it.

Then at him.

[M] "It was supposed to do something else."

[Yk] "I hope so."

* * *

She chose a smaller stone.

Held it low. Tried the angle once without letting go, then sent it across the long part of the pool.

It touched the surface, lifted, touched again and struck the far bank.

Mau stared at it.

[Yk] "Two."

[M] "One."

She turned toward him.

[Yk] "I saw two."

[M] "It hit the bank."

[Yk] "After two."

He took another stone. A little heavier than hers, which seemed reasonable until it made an even louder sound going straight down.

Yuka laughed.

He looked at the water as though it might explain the objection.

[Yk] "You're throwing it down."

[M] "It's water. Down is where it is."

She took the next stone from his palm and replaced it with a thinner one.

[Yk] "Across. Like this."

She showed him the movement with an empty hand. Low, nearly flat, the turn coming from her wrist rather than from an attempt to get the throw over with by force.

He tried.

The stone touched, jumped and fell back into the pool.

Both looked at the spreading rings.

[M] "Two."

[Yk] "One."

* * *

They disagreed about the count for several minutes.

Not loudly enough to need the hill to answer. Just enough for Yuka to begin laughing before Mau finished explaining why a stone briefly leaving the water had surely earned some consideration.

She threw one which went sideways and struck the rock near their own feet. Stared at it as though it had changed sides in the middle of the game.

Mau picked it up.

[M] "Shall I count that?"

[Yk] "Don't."

He set it well away from the others.

The next one worked. Two little touches and a third which they both heard but only Yuka was certain she saw.

[M] "Nearly two."

She turned to protest and found him smiling.

The wind had worked a strand of hair across her mouth. He reached to move it; she caught his wrist, laughing, because the glove he had forgotten he was wearing was doing the job very badly.

[Yk] "Wait."

She freed the strand herself. Kept his hand.

For a moment they stood close enough that he could see the colour the cold had brought into her cheeks, and the little place near one eye where laughing had made it wet.

He kissed her.

She leaned into it, one hand still holding his wrist, the other finding the front of his coat. There was no need to get the words right before either could move nearer.

When they parted, she looked down at the stones left on the dry rock.

[Yk] "I still won."

[M] "I didn't say you hadn't."

[Yk] "You were about to."

He kissed her once more instead.

* * *

By the time they returned to the bag, Yuka was hungry.

She announced it without making an apology for interrupting the view. Mau opened the bag, lifted the food parcel onto the broad dry stone and took out the blanket beneath it. He spread it on the grass beside the stone, well back from the wet edge.

The sheltered place was not windless. One corner rose, folded over itself and caught the other before he had finished spreading it.

Yuka stepped on the nearest edge.

[Yk] "I'm helping."

[M] "I noticed."

He went round the other side, set the bag on one corner and put two dry stones over the edges where they would not be sitting. Yuka lifted her boot only after he nodded.

The blanket stayed.

She sat down, tested the ground with both palms and shifted nearer the warm side of the rock. Mau sat beside her with his right shoulder toward the open view.

They could see Igris at the turn. Beyond him, a folded wing moved once as Kaisel changed his position on the broad shoulder. The sky remained clear enough for the way home.

Mau took out the flask and unwrapped the two cups. Yuka lifted the cloth parcel down from the rock into the space between their knees and worked at the fastening.

One loop had tightened against the other.

He held it still while she loosened it. The corners fell apart, showing the wrapped cakes and the fruit they had brought from the house.

Yuka put a hand on his knee to stop the nearest cup tipping as he moved.

[Yk] "There."

He set it flat.

She turned the parcel so that both could reach.
