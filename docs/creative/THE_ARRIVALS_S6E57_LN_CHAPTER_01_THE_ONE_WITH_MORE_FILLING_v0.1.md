# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 31 — The One With More Filling

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 31
**Source episode:** S6E57
**Continuity state:** Day475 afternoon at the sheltered hollow30, in the real northern mountains. Mau/Yuka eat their packed savoury cakes/apples and share ordinary questions, humour and tentative wishes. Tsurugi is Yuka's absent brother; Mau's existing coin promise does not become a new arrival or a deadline. They want a future without claiming to know how the Goddess answer will affect it. No cancer disclosure, sex, bargain, restored memory or new technique. Igris remains at the visible path turn and Kaisel near the landing; neither overhears private low speech by narrative assumption. Arrival has been relayed, return before midnight/earlier for weather remains. Ends with a short local walk in sight of the sheltered place; bag/cups/blanket remain together on dry ground.

*When: Day475, afternoon. The blanket in the hollow. Yuka has opened the food and discovered a question of justice.*

[Yk] "That one's bigger."

Mau looked at the cake in his hand. He had slipped off his right glove to handle the food and left it beside the bag.

Then at hers.

[M] "It might be the way I'm holding it."

She put hers beside it. Carefully enough that neither could claim the comparison was unfair.

[Yk] "It isn't."

He offered his.

She looked offended.

[Yk] "I wasn't asking you to give it to me."

[M] "You sounded like the investigation had reached a conclusion."

She laughed. Took it before he could withdraw the evidence, then broke both cakes and put a half from each into his hand.

[Yk] "There."

He examined the result.

[M] "You have the one with more filling."

[Yk] "The investigation is closed."

* * *

The food tasted like the kitchen.

Not because it needed to become a reminder of home before they were allowed to enjoy it. The familiar savoury centre was still good after being carried in a cloth through a flight and an argument about stones. Yuka ate her first half before saying anything else.

Mau poured water into both cups. The flask had been against the blanket inside the bag; the water was cold, but not as cold as the pool had looked.

She took the cup with both hands.

[Yk] "Do you think he knew we were taking it up a mountain?"

[M] "Who?"

[Yk] "Whoever made this."

He looked at the cake.

[M] "I told them it was going somewhere outside. I don't think I explained the altitude."

[Yk] "That's an important ingredient."

[M] "Next time I'll ask."

The ordinary words stayed between them for a moment.

Next time.

Yuka looked down at her food instead of making him defend a promise he had not meant to turn into one.

Then she took another bite.

* * *

The wind passed above the hollow.

They could hear it in the grass along the rise without having it between every word. Igris remained at the path's turn, visible when Mau looked back. Farther out, part of Kaisel's folded wing rose against the sky and settled again.

They had not gone anywhere which made the way home a mystery.

Yuka finished her water. Held the empty cup for a while before putting it on the flat part of the rock.

[Yk] "Tsurugi would say he could throw farther."

Mau followed her eyes toward the pool.

[M] "He probably could. I demonstrated a very low standard."

[Yk] "He'd say that before seeing you try."

[M] "Efficient."

She smiled at the water.

[Yk] "Then he'd stand here until he could. Even if he said it was a stupid game."

It was the first time his absence had come into the afternoon without needing to explain all the things they could not fix about it today.

Mau waited while she chose whether to keep speaking.

[Yk] "I wish he could see this."

[M] "Me too."

He did not reach for a pocket which had no new coin in it. The old promise still belonged to a thing he wanted to do, not a resource which became available because saying it at the right moment would be kind.

* * *

[M] "Would he like me?"

Yuka turned toward him.

Her expression became so carefully thoughtful that he began regretting the question before she answered.

[Yk] "You want the first answer or the answer after he gets used to you?"

[M] "Those sound alarmingly different."

[Yk] "He likes making things difficult."

[M] "Runs in the family."

She pressed her knee against his. He moved his cup before the correction could knock it off the stone.

[Yk] "I was going to say something nice."

[M] "I'd like to return to that part."

She looked at his face properly.

The answer which came next had lost the little game in it.

[Yk] "I think he'd see you trying. Not just what people tell him you are."

Mau looked down at the cloth parcel.

[M] "I hope so."

Yuka moved nearer on the blanket. Their coats touched before she put her hand on his knee.

[Yk] "I don't mean you have to win him. He can be difficult and still want good things for me."

He nodded.

Neither needed to name the people at home whose faces had become harder to read because that was true of them too.

* * *

They shared one apple before opening the other.

Yuka bit first, looked at the pale part and declared it better than she expected. Mau asked whether she had been expecting a different fruit beneath the skin. She gave him the apple with the bite turned toward him, which he decided was probably an answer.

When the core was small enough to become awkward, she wrapped it in the corner of the food cloth instead of throwing it into the pool.

[Yk] "Don't put that with the cakes."

He had not moved his hand.

[M] "My reputation is suffering."

[Yk] "You put the flask on them."

[M] "One decision."

[Yk] "A memorable one."

She stopped smiling before she meant to.

Mau saw it happen. Waited until she looked back rather than pretending the word had not reached either of them.

[M] "It was."

She breathed out slowly.

[Yk] "I didn't mean—"

[M] "I know. You're allowed to remember something funny without checking the word first."

Yuka's eyes moved to his mouth.

[Yk] "Can you say something stupid, then?"

[M] "Immediately?"

[Yk] "You seemed prepared."

He looked toward the pool with great seriousness.

[M] "I think I won the stone game."

She laughed hard enough that he had to catch the empty cup she had forgotten was beside her elbow.

* * *

After eating, they lay back against the dry rise.

Not flat on the cold ground. The lower folds of their coats drawn beneath their hips, blanket held by the bag and stones, the rock taking the worst of the wind. Mau put his right arm where Yuka could lean into it without having her head against a fastening.

She settled there after making one small adjustment of her own.

[Yk] "Better."

[M] "I was doing very well until you arrived."

[Yk] "You were sitting with your shoulder against a rock."

[M] "A peaceful arrangement."

She moved her cheek against his coat. He felt the smile before he saw it.

Above them, a thin cloud came across the open blue. Not a formation they needed to identify, not the first sign of something coming for the settlement. Just weather moving slowly enough to be watched.

Mau let himself look at it without opening more of his sight.

[Yk] "Do you think we'd have done this?"

He turned his head.

[Yk] "If none of the bad part happened. A day up here. Eating things we argued about packing."

He took time with the answer.

[M] "I don't know."

She nodded, though he could feel the little change in her breathing.

[M] "I don't want to tell you it was meant to happen like this. What happened to them wasn't a way of giving us a date."

Yuka closed her eyes.

[Yk] "I know."

[M] "I still want this part."

Her hand found the front of his coat.

* * *

They stayed quiet long enough for the cloud to reach the far side of the view.

Mau could feel where her fingers rested. The ordinary weight of her beside him, the little movement when she swallowed. He had been trying to work out what he could say which did not become a promise the next answer might make impossible to keep.

There were simpler things he had not stopped wanting while he did that.

[M] "I'd like to see you arguing with your brother about the stones. I'd like to be there when you show him something you've learned. I'd like another day where choosing what to eat is the difficult part."

Yuka opened her eyes.

[M] "I don't know what comes back with the rest of my history. I know what I want sitting here."

She lifted her head. Looked at him for a long moment, as if she needed to make sure she had heard a wish rather than a careful way to avoid one.

[Yk] "I want more time with you too."

He touched the loose hair beside her cheek with his right hand. This time there was no glove between his fingers and the strand, and she let him move it before leaning nearer.

The kiss was slow enough that neither had to turn it into something else to keep it from ending.

When she drew away, she remained close.

[Yk] "I'd still win."

He smiled against her forehead.

[M] "I was prepared for that part of the future."

* * *

They packed the food before walking again.

Not the whole bag. The cups stood together inside its open mouth, wrapped so a careless foot would not find their rims. The remaining food stayed under the cloth. The core had its own folded corner, about which Yuka gave a final instruction Mau was able to repeat without assistance.

He put his glove back on before they went toward the dry edge of the pool.

Igris turned when they stood. Mau indicated the little stretch of slope he could already see, no farther than the next outcrop. The knight nodded from the path.

Yuka took Mau's right hand.

They walked where the grass was short enough to see the stones beneath it. Slowly, not because either needed the afternoon to become an expedition worth reporting, but because they had come to a place where there was time to stop when something caught their attention.

Behind them the blanket remained sheltered against the rise. Ahead, a patch of late sunlight lay on the next rock.

Yuka reached it first and looked back to see whether he was coming.
