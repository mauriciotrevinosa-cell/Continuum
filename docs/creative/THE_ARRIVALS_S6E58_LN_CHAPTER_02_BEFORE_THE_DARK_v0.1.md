*When: Day475, early evening. The sheltered hollow. The first stars have appeared, but the dry line across the slope is still plain enough to follow.*

Mau put the first stone down on the bare ground beside the rock, well clear of his boot.

Yuka counted the cups before closing the bag.

Not out loud. She pressed a hand against one wrapped rim, found the other and lifted the food parcel clear so Mau could put the flask where it would not settle on the cakes again.

[M] "I've learned."

[Yk] "I'm making certain."

He took the parcel from her. The corner with the apple core had folded inward. He worked it out before the two parts could be pressed together, then tucked that corner separately against the outside of the wrapping.

Yuka watched him finish.

[Yk] "You have."

He looked at her.

She was smiling. Cold in the cheeks now, coat drawn close, the little flower mostly hidden beneath her cuff.

He wanted to keep standing there.

Instead he put the food in the bag and checked the fastening.

* * *

The remaining stone came off the blanket once the bag was closed.

Mau put it beside the first. Yuka held the other end of the blanket while he shook the dry blades off his side.

The wind tried to take it as soon as they lifted it above the shelter.

She lowered her hands.

[Yk] "Not up there."

[M] "I see that."

They folded it low between them. Once along the length, then again, bringing the corners together without letting it become a sail.

Mau's left grip loosened at the awkward part. He caught the folded weight against his coat with his forearm while Yuka worked the edge back into place.

[Yk] "There. Under my arm until we're over the path."

He let her take it. Lifted the bag and looked over the dry stone where they had eaten.

Nothing beside it. Nothing at the pool except the stones which had been there before them and a pale ring of water along the bank.

Yuka walked a few steps to check the place they had stood throwing. Came back with empty hands.

[Yk] "Ready."

He looked toward the view once more before turning.

The house had begun to hold small points of light.

* * *

Igris came to meet them at the turn.

Mau asked him to go first across the short stretch where the gravel covered the wet rock. The knight checked it with a careful step, then indicated the dry middle they had used on the way in.

[Ig] "Here, my liege. The edge is wet."

Yuka kept the blanket close beneath her arm instead of letting it hang against the outcrop. Mau followed, choosing the same footholds with his right side toward the clearer view.

There was still enough light to see exactly where they were putting their boots.

At the landing Kaisel lifted his head.

The wide shoulder was empty around him. Igris walked the approach before moving clear of the wings, and Mau set the bag down while Yuka folded the blanket to fit inside it again.

[M] *We're leaving now. Same way back. The ground's still clear.*

[C] *I'll tell Rimuru.*

Mau waited until Yuka had tightened the bag. He checked that nothing remained on the grass, then turned toward Igris.

[M] "Thank you. Come back in."

The knight inclined his head. His outline darkened and drew away into its place when Mau called him back, leaving the landing clear.

* * *

Getting on was less graceful with cold fingers.

Yuka held the bag while Mau found the first secure place for his foot. He used his right hand for the pull, settled, then took the weight from her before she climbed.

She put one knee down too near the folded edge of his coat.

[M] "Again."

She looked down.

[Yk] "Move your coat."

[M] "I'm wearing it."

She laughed and lifted her knee long enough for him to pull the cloth free.

Then she settled behind him. Her arms came round his middle, the bag fastened where neither would have to keep it from sliding all the way down the hill.

Mau asked Kaisel to rise slowly.

The wings opened. Air moved through the grass, and the little hollow went out of sight behind the low rise before the whole slope fell gently below them.

Yuka tightened her hold for the first turn.

He covered one of her hands with his.

* * *

The stars were clearer away from the rock.

Only the first few. The western sky still held colour, and the land beneath them remained a set of ridges and woods rather than one dark thing they had to guess their way across.

Mau kept them on the course home. No detour to find a better last view. No climb toward the higher bare rock which had never been part of the outing.

Yuka leaned to see past his shoulder.

[Yk] "I can see the path."

He followed her pointing hand. The pale curve below the nearest trees, then the place it disappeared behind the bend.

[M] "Yes."

She drew the hand back inside his coat for warmth. He moved his elbow enough to give her room without losing his hold.

For a little while they did not talk.

He could feel the part of the day which she was carrying home in the way her forehead came against his back. Not asleep. Resting there while the cold air went past and the lights ahead became a house again.

He wished there could be another hollow between this one and the door. Not because he wanted to stay out until somebody had to come for them. Because the afternoon had been easy in places he did not know how to make easy again once they were inside.

Yuka lifted her head.

[Yk] "Nearly home."

He nodded.

Her arms stayed where they were.

* * *

They crossed the protection while there was still light along the tree tops.

Kaisel descended over the clear ground they had left from. Mau looked before asking him to settle. The path was empty. There was room for both wings and for the slow final movement which brought his feet onto the earth.

They waited until he had folded them.

Yuka passed the bag forward, got down and took it back while Mau climbed after her. His boots reached the ground without a jump.

He put a hand against Kaisel's side.

[M] "Thank you."

Yuka touched him too. Then stepped clear, and Mau sent the dragon back into his place only after both of them and the bag were out of the way.

The yard seemed very quiet without the wings.

From the kitchen came the sound of someone scraping the bottom of a pot. A door closed farther along the ground floor. Yuka looked toward the stair window, then toward the open way inside.

She put her hand in Mau's.

They went in together.

* * *

Rimuru was beside the office door when they found him.

[M] "We're home."

He looked from Mau to Yuka, then at the bag.

[R] "I can see that."

Mau smiled a little.

[M] "I was asked to say it."

[R] "You were. I'm glad you did."

Yuka took her hand out of his long enough to free the strap from her shoulder.

[Yk] "We stayed on the lower part. It was dry behind the rise. The edge of the path was wet, so we came round the same way."

[R] "And the cold?"

[Yk] "Enough to want to come in. Not enough to make it a bad idea before we left."

Rimuru nodded. He did not ask them to turn the date into an account of everything they had said while they were there.

[R] "Maki and Yuta are at the far end. They ate."

Yuka looked relieved by that last part.

[Yk] "Good."

Mau followed her toward the table.

* * *

Maki saw them before Yuka spoke.

Her hand stopped beside her cup. Yuta turned, then stood far enough to move the chair out of the way rather than making them go round it.

[Yk] "We're back."

[Mk] "Early."

[Yk] "The light was going. We said we'd come before it got difficult."

Maki nodded. Her eyes moved over the coat and came back to Yuka's face.

[Yt] "Did you like it?"

Yuka looked at him.

For a moment the answer had to get past the habit of checking what they would feel about hearing it.

[Yk] "Yes."

Maki's fingers moved away from the cup.

[Yk] "There was a little pool. We threw stones. He thinks he won."

[M] "I haven't committed to a number."

[Yk] "He lost."

Yuta's mouth softened. Maki looked at the bag.

[Mk] "The cups?"

[Yk] "Both. Whole. Which reminds me. Three?"

Yuta looked at his wife.

She leaned back.

[Mk] "I said you'd tell her."

* * *

He had carried three empty cups by their handles.

One hand. A pot in the other. He had thought he could get past a partly open door without setting anything down.

Yuka listened, holding the bag by its strap. Mau stood beside her and tried not to begin laughing before the story reached the part which justified it.

[Yt] "The door caught the first one. I tried to catch that."

[Mk] "With the hand holding the others."

[Yt] "Yes."

[Yk] "All three?"

[Yt] "All three."

[Mk] "There was a table beside him."

[Yt] "I know where the table was."

Yuka laughed then, a small surprised sound, and looked down at the wrapped cups as if they had managed something more impressive than travelling up a hill without being broken.

[Yk] "Did you save the pot?"

Yuta looked pleased to be asked.

[Yt] "I did."

Maki shook her head, but she was smiling at him.

Yuka stood a moment longer with them. Nobody asked her to explain why a good afternoon had not made her ready to sit down for the rest of the evening.

* * *

She took the blanket out of the bag before giving it to Mau.

[Yk] "I'm going up to my room. I'd like to stay there tonight."

Maki nodded.

[Mk] "All right."

Mau looked at the bag, then at her.

[M] "Can I come and see you when I've put these away?"

She held the blanket closer beneath her arm.

[Yk] "Yes. Knock."

He nodded.

At the stair she waited for her grandparents. Yuta picked up the cups from their table, took them to the washing place and came back. Maki went up beside Yuka, talking quietly about whether the blanket was damp.

[Yk] "Only cold. We checked."

Mau watched until they had gone round the turn.

* * *

The picnic cups were exactly where Yuka had said they were.

He unwrapped them at the basin, rinsed them and set them to drain. Emptied what remained in the flask, rinsed that too and put it back with the things they could ask to borrow next time.

The food cloth needed more care. He put the remaining cakes and fruit with the covered kitchen food before opening the separate corner over the scraps bucket. The apple core went in without touching anything they meant to keep.

He washed the cloth. Hung it where it could dry.

Then he opened the bag wide and checked the bottom with his right hand. No cup left to strike a floor when somebody next picked it up. No loose food wrapped against the lining.

Mau folded the separate cup wrappings and put them away. Turned the empty bag out once more, shook a dry blade of grass free over the floor and picked it up before hanging the bag on its hook.

His coat was still cold across the shoulders.

He left the cups draining and went to wash his hands.
