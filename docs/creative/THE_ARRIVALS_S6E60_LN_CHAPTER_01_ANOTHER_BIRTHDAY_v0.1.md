*When: Day475, night. Yuka's room. The footsteps outside have gone, and her cheek is still against Mau's shoulder.*

He thought she might have begun to fall asleep.

Her breathing had become slower. The hand holding his shirt had loosened, though it had not moved away. He kept his arm where it was and looked at the fold of blanket across her knees.

Then she spoke.

[Yk] "There was something before I came here."

Mau turned his head enough to look down at her.

She did not lift hers yet.

[Yk] "I haven't told you. Not properly."

[M] "Okay."

She drew a breath. Let it out without finding the next part. Her fingers gathered a little of the shirt again, not pulling him nearer, making somewhere to keep her hand while she decided.

[Yk] "I had cancer."

He stopped moving.

Yuka felt the change through his arm. She lifted her head then, before the word could become a different conversation in his face.

[Yk] "Before. Not now. It was already gone before I came here."

His mouth had begun a question. He closed it long enough to hear the whole answer.

[M] "You aren't telling me you're ill now."

[Yk] "No."

He nodded slowly.

She watched him do it. Then sat a little straighter, still close enough that his arm remained round her.

* * *

[Yk] "It was in my head. A malignant tumour. I went for another opinion because I wanted somebody else to tell me something different."

Her hand left his shirt. She put it flat on the blanket and looked at it.

[Yk] "There was a picture. Of the inside of my head. I could look at it and understand which part they meant. That didn't make it feel as if they were talking about me."

Mau kept his hand beside hers.

[M] "What did they tell you?"

She turned her face toward him.

[Yk] "Six months."

The words reached him with the plainness she had given them. No great change in her voice. A number somebody had once put beside all the days she expected to have.

[Yk] "At the time. That's what I was told then."

[M] "I heard you."

She looked down again.

[Yk] "I took someone with me. Cross. I didn't want to go alone. Then I didn't want him to look at me as if bringing him meant he had to be sad the right way."

A small, impatient movement crossed her mouth.

[Yk] "I was very easy to spend time with."

Mau's thumb moved once against the edge of her hand. She let him take it.

[M] "Was he someone you could talk to?"

[Yk] "Sometimes. We argued."

She thought about the water beyond the hospital, the stupid things a person could say when he thought losing something made him able to explain what everybody else ought to do with their loss.

[Yk] "He'd lost things too. That didn't mean we agreed about what came afterward."

* * *

Mau wanted to ask several questions at once.

Who had been there. Who she had told. How she had got through the stretch of time between hearing that number and being able to say, here, that it belonged to the past.

She had begun speaking again before he chose one.

[Yk] "People would say something about next year. Or a thing they were going to do later. I'd be listening, and then I'd realise I was trying to work out whether I should be in the sentence."

He looked at her.

[Yk] "I didn't think about it every second. I could still get annoyed about ordinary things. Tsurugi being Tsurugi. Somebody saying something stupid. I'd have a whole minute where that was all I was thinking about."

She pressed her lips together.

[Yk] "Then I'd remember, and I'd be angry that I had to remember. As if I'd been careless by enjoying the minute."

Mau felt her fingers tighten in his.

He did not tell her he knew exactly how that felt.

There were things he knew about losing time. There were other things he had only just been allowed to hear from the person sitting beside him. He let her finish without turning them into the same story.

[Yk] "I hated being pitied. I still wanted somebody to come with me. I couldn't make those two things easy for anyone."

[M] "You could want company without wanting to become only that."

Yuka looked at him for a long moment.

Then nodded.

* * *

[Yk] "When I went to fight Dabura, I was still carrying that."

Mau looked at her, waiting.

[Yk] "The duel. When I called Mahoraga."

His gaze moved to her hands. The one resting on the blanket, the one in his. Both ordinary hands now, the little cord lying loose against her wrist.

[M] "You thought you might not come back."

She nodded.

[Yk] "I didn't have a safe way of doing it. Not one I could stand there and tell myself would definitely work. And part of me kept thinking about what I had left anyway."

She stopped. Her mouth tightened around the answer she did not want him to take from that.

[Yk] "That doesn't mean I wanted to die."

[M] "I wasn't going to say that."

[Yk] "I know. I need to say it."

He nodded. Waited.

[Yk] "I wanted to live. I didn't want my brother watching me get worse in a bed. I didn't want another life to be the only place where I could think of being with him differently."

Her voice had gone quieter. She looked toward the wall rather than the door.

[Yk] "I wanted this one. Even when I was going out there acting as if I knew what I was giving up."

Mau moved nearer when she leaned toward him. Not enough to end the telling inside a hug before she was ready.

[Yk] "I was frightened. I could do the thing I went to do and still be frightened of what happened next."

[M] "Did you let him see? Your brother."

Yuka's eyes filled.

[Yk] "Not enough. I kept wanting him not to have to look at it. Then I wanted him to understand without making me say it."

Her thumb stopped moving against Mau's hand.

[Yk] "I wasn't being very fair."

He did not correct every part of the memory until it became one in which she had handled dying well.

She had not died. She was here.

She had still lived the part where she thought she would.

* * *

For a while she cried without trying to find the next sentence.

Mau drew her closer when her shoulder came against him. Her forehead pressed into the side of his neck, and he shifted the blanket up where it had slipped from her arm.

She was warm. Breathing. The hand in his had begun relaxing, then tightening again when something reached her which she did not have words for yet.

He stayed with that instead of asking for the rest of the outcome. She had already told him the part he needed to know about now.

Ciel remained quiet within reach.

[M] *Please don't carry this to Rimuru.*

[C] *I won't relay her confidence.*

There was no second voice in the room. No report leaving it through his mouth. He turned his attention back to Yuka when she lifted her head.

[Yk] "I did come back."

[M] "Yes."

[Yk] "I was well before I came here. I'm not trying to make you scared that there's something still happening which you haven't noticed."

He shook his head.

[M] "I'm listening to what happened."

She wiped beneath one eye with her free hand. The movement left a damp place against her sleeve.

[Yk] "It happened, and then there was time after it. I didn't know how much of it I'd ever get. Then suddenly I had things I could put in it."

* * *

She looked at the blanket.

At the little loose place where their hands had left a fold between their knees. The same blanket they had spread beside the pool, with their food on it and the stones holding the wind out of the corners.

[Yk] "I had another birthday."

Mau looked down at the flower on her wrist.

He could remember that day from inside it. Not a description someone had given him afterward. Yuka opening the little packet. Her face when she saw what he had bought. The sea below them, and the small difficulty of getting the fastening to do what his fingers wanted.

[Yk] "You took me out. I got to be embarrassed about how much I liked the present. I got to want you to think I'd had a good day."

She laughed once, wetly.

[Yk] "It was such a stupid thing to be worried about. Whether I looked too pleased."

His fingers moved beneath hers.

[M] "You looked pleased."

[Yk] "I know. I was terrible at it."

He smiled. It did not stay long enough to make the rest of her face stop hurting, but she saw it.

[Yk] "I wanted more of those. Days where I could worry about something small. Where later wasn't a word I had to argue with before I used it."

She looked up at him.

[Yk] "The dinner I was making plans for today. Tsurugi coming here. Things with you I didn't even know I wanted until we started talking about them."

He could remember the lower courtyard. A future which had begun as a question neither of them knew how to answer properly, then become something they could imagine while the way home remained open behind them.

He did not ask her to say all of it again.

[Yk] "I know I have other things. I love them. My grandparents. My brother. Everyone I'm still trying to work out how to sit beside. I haven't forgotten all of that because I want you too."

[M] "I know."

She searched his face.

[Yk] "I don't want you to hear this and think you have to stay because somebody once told me I'd run out of time."

Mau took a breath before answering.

[M] "I wanted to stay before you told me."

Her eyes closed for a moment.

[M] "You asked me to sit here. I wanted to. I still do."

* * *

Yuka moved nearer.

This time she did not turn into his shoulder at once. She sat close enough that their knees touched beneath the blanket and looked at him while the tears cooled on her face.

[Yk] "I wanted you to know."

He nodded.

[Yk] "Not because you can do something with it. I know what happens in your face when you're trying to find a thing you could fix."

Mau looked down before he could deny something she had seen too often to need his help identifying.

[M] "I don't know how to fix this part."

[Yk] "You don't have to. It's already happened."

Her hand came against the front of his shirt again, above the crease she had left there before.

[Yk] "Just know it was me too. The girl who was scared in the hospital. The one who went out anyway. The one who wanted to see her brother again. I don't want you only knowing the bit where I'm frightened in this house."

Mau raised his eyes.

[M] "I want to know you."

She watched him for another moment before the effort of keeping her head up became too much.

He brought her in. Let her settle where her cheek could rest against his shoulder without having to keep her neck bent. Her hand lay open on his chest, no longer trying to hold the shirt together between its fingers.

For a while neither spoke.

The room had not changed. The coats hung beside the door. His boots remained beneath the stool. Behind the closed window the night had gone on while she told him about a time which had once seemed to contain all the night she would ever have.

Yuka moved one knee, trying to free the blanket caught beneath it.

Mau helped with his right hand. Pulled the loose part over her feet and left the other end where it would still cover him.

[Yk] "There."

[M] "Better?"

She nodded against him.

Then lifted her face just enough to look up.

[Yk] "Don't be different with me tomorrow. Because I told you."

He thought of the mountain, the sound she had made for the small creature on the rock, the dinner she had begun arranging before she knew what he might remember when it came.

[M] "Am I still allowed to say I won?"

She stared at him.

Then a little laugh escaped before she could decide whether she was ready for it.

[Yk] "You didn't."

He rested his cheek against her hair.

Her hand moved once against his shirt. She remained awake, listening to the ordinary sounds beyond the door while he held her.
