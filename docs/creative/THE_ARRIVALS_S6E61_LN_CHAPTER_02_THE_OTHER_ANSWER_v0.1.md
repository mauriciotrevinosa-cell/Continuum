# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 40 — The Other Answer

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 40
**Source episode:** S6E61
**Continuity state:** Day475 late night, directly after39. The Goddess privately offers a different remembered history for the affected household, without time travel, bodily healing or disappearance of matter; Mau keeps both lived histories and two literal loves as described39, and his shadows retain only witnessed/learned truth. Ciel is included in the rewrite but her technical model and non-romantic relationships are not reset. Yuka would keep her medical past but lose having entrusted it to Mau tonight; she does not consent to this. A second future obligation is explicitly unknown, not paid or specified now. At Mau's first audible disclosure about others changing, Ciel objects and immediately warns Rimuru; he receives/replies and physically comes to the room. Mau lets him in rather than leaving Yuka without warning. Discussion starts, not consent/acceptance/execution. No closed communications, third access, frozen time or sleeping Ciel.

*When: Day475, late night. The Goddess has said there is another part they could discuss. Yuka sleeps beside Mau, unaware of the answer he is about to hear.*

[Dio] *The days could remain, and cease to be the story which is hurting them.*

Mau did not speak.

He heard the phrase first as a wish. Something he had been putting into questions about where people could sit, how many meals could pass before somebody had to meet somebody else in a corridor.

Then she told him what she meant.

[Dio] *I can change how the people affected remember those days. The birthday. The gate. Coming home. What followed in this house. They would still have lived the time. They would remember being in those places, doing the work, bringing home what they brought home. But the relationship would be the one she misses being able to have with you.*

Her hand lay across his.

Oppa. Someone she could go and bother. Someone whose face did not have to be read before she could decide whether to sit beside him.

[Dio] *The others would remember a house in which that was true. Their memories of the hurt which grew around the secret would change with it.*

He looked at the window without seeing the coat reflected in it.

* * *

The Goddess went on while he remained silent.

[Dio] *I would return what was stolen from you. You would also keep what happened after it. The two loves would remain yours, as I told you. In the two hearts. Nobody else would have to keep loving or hating someone because of a story they no longer remembered living.*

He could hear her clearly. That was part of what frightened him.

The answer was not a confused version of something he had hoped she might say. It was larger than the thing he had asked her to return.

[Dio] *You would remember. Your shadows would keep what they have seen and heard. I would not give them knowledge of things they never witnessed.*

A slow breath pressed Yuka's shoulder against him.

[Dio] *Ciel would receive the changed history too. Her work would remain. The things she has learnt about your body, what you have built, her work with Rimuru and with the voice you call Noise. The life around that work would be different where it needed to be different.*

He heard Ciel ask whether he was all right.

He had not passed the answer on.

He knew that she was waiting because the last thing he had told her concerned what could be returned to him. He had let the conversation move beyond that while keeping the other voice to himself.

* * *

[Dio] *There is something you have just told me which belongs in that answer.*

Mau looked down at Yuka.

He understood before the Goddess finished, and wanted not to have understood soon enough to let her say it.

[Dio] *She would still know she was ill in her world. That she survived, and was well before she came here. I would not take that history from her. But she would not remember giving it to you tonight.*

The warm place against his shirt seemed to become very distinct.

He could feel the little crease her fingers had made earlier. The dampness had dried almost entirely out of it.

[Dio] *You would keep that confidence. She would no longer know she had trusted you with it.*

He had wanted to give the Goddess a reason to understand.

Now the thing he had told her had a place in the offer.

There was no way to ask Yuka whether the cost was acceptable without waking her and telling her what was being proposed. No way to pretend that asking him to find out an answer had already answered that question for her.

* * *

[Dio] *There would be another thing I would ask of you later.*

He listened.

[Dio] *I have not decided what. Another trial, perhaps. Or a conversation.*

He thought of the word *perhaps* in the mouth of the person who had made the first Trial.

The life he could not yet remember feeling had happened under its consequences. He knew enough of the facts to know that a small word would not protect him from what came after it.

[Dio] *I am not asking you to pay that part tonight. I am telling you it would be owed.*

He had heard her.

It would not be a surprise he could claim had been hidden from him afterward.

[C] *Mau. What is she saying?*

He looked at the hand in his.

Then answered aloud, softly enough that the sleeping girl did not move.

[M] "You'd change what they remember. All of them."

* * *

Ciel did not wait for the next question.

[C] *Whose memories?*

The Goddess's voice remained private.

[Dio] *The people whose memories of this belong to the life around you. This house and its relationships. I am not describing a different history for the whole world.*

Mau passed the answer to Ciel.

Then the rest of it. The false beginning becoming recognisably false for him. The life afterward remaining his. The others remembering different versions of those days. Ciel included.

He did not repeat Yuka's medical history. It was not needed to make the danger of the proposal clear.

[C] *No.*

The word came before he had found a defence for it.

[M] *I haven't accepted.*

[C] *I'm warning Rimuru.*

He felt no interruption in the thread. No wall closing because he had become frightened of what she was going to say.

For a moment he wanted to ask her to wait.

She had already spoken.

* * *

Rimuru was downstairs when Ciel reached him.

He had put one page over the questions without putting them away, then gone back to another piece of work which did not need an answer from the Goddess to be finished.

The warning brought his hand to a stop above the desk.

[C] *She's offering to restore his history and change the household's memories of what happened after it. Mine too. He hasn't accepted. He's with Yuka.*

Rimuru stood.

[R] *Tell him to wait. I'm coming.*

The message reached Mau while Yuka was still asleep with her hand on his.

He could hear what was coming without pretending it was only a possibility Ciel had failed to make real.

[M] *I heard him.*

[C] *Then wait for him.*

Mau did.

* * *

There were footsteps beyond the door.

He had time to take his hand gently from beneath Yuka's and draw the blanket back over the place where moving had let in the cold.

Rimuru knocked quietly.

Mau sat up. The movement made her turn her face into the pillow without waking. He put his feet on the floor and crossed the small room in his socks.

Opened the door.

Rimuru looked at him, then past him to the bed.

[R] "Can I come in?"

Mau moved aside.

There was no sudden attempt to drag him into the corridor. No audience arriving behind Rimuru with a decision already made about the people inside.

He came far enough in for Mau to close the door softly. Took the stool when Mau indicated it, then waited until he was sitting on the edge of the bed again.

[R] "Tell me what she offered."

Mau began with what was his to tell.

* * *

It took several minutes.

He had to stop once when Rimuru asked a question which had not yet been answered. Ask the Goddess, then repeat her words rather than the reassuring version of them he had begun to build in his head.

No time going backward. No scars disappearing. No objects evaporating because a different account had been put around them.

What happened in the gate had happened. The people who had fought there would keep what they learnt and brought back. Their bodies would not become younger by the days they no longer remembered in the same way.

Rimuru listened without making the stool creak beneath a change of position.

[R] "And the others?"

[M] "They'd remember being there. Different things between us."

[R] "Do you know which things?"

Mau looked down.

[M] "Not all of them yet."

[R] "Then ask."

* * *

He did not have to wonder whether Ciel had told Rimuru enough for him to object.

Rimuru was here. The objection had a face, and a hand resting against a knee, and the tired patience of somebody who had asked him to bring back the actual words before trying to live inside them.

Mau looked toward the hooks.

[M] "What about things that are still here? The questions on his desk. Her staff."

The Goddess answered him alone.

[Dio] *Nothing about changing a memory makes a piece of paper disappear.*

He repeated that.

Rimuru looked at him for a long moment.

[R] "I know."

There was no anger in the first part of the answer. That came after it.

[R] "You're asking how to make us believe the thing in front of us has a reason which won't make us ask you what happened."

Mau did not look away.

[M] "Yes."

The word sounded worse when somebody else was there to hear it.

Yuka breathed behind him. One foot moved under the blanket and came to rest.

Rimuru put both hands on his knees.

[R] "Then we need to talk about what you're asking her to do."
