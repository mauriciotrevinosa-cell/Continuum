# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 34
## Chapter 41 — The People Who Would Not Know

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-23)
**Volume:** 34
**Reading order:** 41
**Source episode:** S6E62
**Continuity state:** Day475 late night, continuing40. Rimuru physically present; Yuka sleeps ordinarily. Ciel and Rimuru explicitly refuse the proposed memory intervention. Mau accepts anyway, intending the stated memory operation, not requesting a replacement reality. The operation malfunctions at the second heart; a black cutoff and OVERWRITE close this arc. The new branch follows in V35. No successful original-house restoration, breakfast coin reward or reunion from superseded42–45 occurs here. The unknown second obligation remains.

*When: Day475, late night. Rimuru sits opposite Mau with the sleeping girl between the conversation and the wall. Nothing has been accepted.*

[R] "Start with her."

Mau looked over his shoulder.

Yuka had one hand close to her mouth now. He had left the other where he had set it beneath the blanket before getting up to open the door.

[R] "Does she know what's being offered?"

[M] "No."

[R] "Has she asked for it?"

He could have told him what she had said about being able to say Oppa again. The way she missed the ease of it. Her wish to keep the afternoon without having to put everyone else's hurt beside it.

He had heard those things. They mattered.

They were not an answer to the question Rimuru had asked.

[M] "Not this."

Rimuru nodded once, without relief.

* * *

[R] "And Frieren? Ori? Maki?"

[M] "They don't know."

[R] "Then before you ask her how to arrange my office, you need to understand what those answers mean."

Mau looked back at him.

[M] "I do."

[R] "Tell me."

He sat with his hands open on his knees. The rigid fingers would not straighten or close much farther for any answer he gave here.

[M] "They wouldn't get to choose."

Rimuru kept looking at him.

[M] "What they remembered. What they did about it afterward. They'd wake up and the reason they might have said no wouldn't be there."

[R] "Yes."

He had not wanted Rimuru to say something which let him stop understanding it.

He wished he had anyway.

* * *

[C] *I don't agree to being changed.*

Mau heard her with the same clarity he had heard her offer to stay while he asked.

[C] *My work staying intact doesn't make the rest of me expendable.*

[M] *I know.*

[C] *Then don't describe it as keeping everything important.*

He looked down.

[M] "Ciel says no too."

[R] "She told me."

The reply left no private agreement for him to imagine between them.

Rimuru moved his hands from his knees and rested them loosely together.

[R] "You brought the answer to me. I'm here. This isn't you failing to find a way to tell me."

Mau nodded.

[R] "So listen to the answer you're getting back. We can wake them. We can take longer. Tomorrow's arrangements weren't a sentence to stop loving someone."

He looked toward the bed, then away, giving the sleeping girl the small privacy he could still give her while discussing a choice about her life.

[R] "They were a way to live until we knew what came next."

* * *

Mau remembered the table that afternoon.

Maki's hands. The care taken over saying that a room would remain Yuka's room. People trying to give separate meals and separate time a shape which did not sound like exile.

The Goddess had not made those arrangements impossible.

He could stay in the world where they happened. Return the love which had been stolen, keep the love he had found, and face what that meant with people who were still able to answer him.

He would not be choosing between doing this and letting somebody die before morning.

[M] "I haven't forgotten what you offered."

[R] "Then don't make it smaller so this is the only thing left."

He shook his head.

[M] "I'm not trying to."

Rimuru's face changed a little.

[R] "I think you are."

* * *

For a time Mau could not answer.

He looked at the bed beneath the blanket. A blade of grass still clung to one seam. They had missed it twice, once while packing and once while making room to sit together.

He wanted the girl who had spent the afternoon making a terrible dragon to be able to go downstairs without listening first.

Wanted Ori to stop having to arrange her anger around the possibility of losing somebody else. Wanted the woman in the east room to have a life which had not brought her to the thing she had done in the garden.

He could know those were desires and still find them stronger than the answers he was being given.

[M] "I need to know exactly what would stay."

Rimuru let out a breath.

[R] "Knowing the details doesn't make it agreed."

[M] "I know."

He turned his face toward the window.

[M] "Answer that part. Please."

* * *

The Goddess did not claim that memory could make the objects stop being real.

Her answer was quieter than the argument which had brought it here.

[Dio] *The books, drawings, gifts and accounts would stay. What was made would remain made. What was spent would remain spent. You would still have been ill. They would still have helped you. You would have gone on the expeditions and learnt what you learnt.*

He repeated it.

[M] "Her staff is behind his desk."

[Dio] *Then they would remember her setting it aside there, by agreement, while she spent that time resting and caring for Ori. Not an attack. Not somebody taking her magic away. She could ask for it back.*

He relayed the words without trying to make the invented account sound like an event which had already happened.

[M] "Yuka's cushion is outside the Fort."

[Dio] *It would be the place it was left after an ordinary afternoon together. She could carry it back in.*

The blade of grass moved when his hand tightened on the blanket.

[M] "Yuta and I stopped practising. There's a repaired grip."

[Dio] *He would remember setting the practice aside to deal with the damaged wood. Finishing the repair. You would still have to choose when to practise again.*

Rimuru's face had become very still.

* * *

The questions on his desk would remain questions about returning a stolen history.

Mau asked that too. Heard the answer and passed it on. The papers which said his beginning was wrong would still say so. The medical pages would not become blank. Nobody would be made to remember a different total of crystals because he could not bear a conversation.

The birthday gift would remain on Yuka's wrist.

They could remember learning what the flower meant, and that he had bought it without knowing. An awkward gift from someone she called her older brother, not a declaration he had knowingly made.

The sea would still have been there. They would remember a birthday outing. The places in which they had nearly kissed, or kissed, or talked about the life they might make together would have other words and other endings in the minds which were changed.

No childhood would be fabricated to make them actual siblings. No blood changed.

The girl who had arrived here would remain Yuka.

[M] "And what she told me tonight."

He did not say the contents again.

[Dio] *You would keep it. She would not remember entrusting it to you. Her own past would remain hers.*

He told Rimuru only that a private confidence was included. That he had been warned it would be lost between them, not lost from him.

Rimuru looked past him once more.

[R] "She's asleep."

[M] "I know."

* * *

[R] "Wake her."

Mau did not move.

[R] "If you think the answer is something she would choose, let her choose it."

He could picture her sitting up against the pillow, trying to understand why the two of them were looking at her. The time it would take to explain. Her grandparents coming in. Frieren hearing the part about both loves. Ori listening from somewhere she had been told to wait.

People could say no. They could say something he had not thought of yet.

They could choose the hard mornings instead of the mornings being offered.

He could not call that a flaw in the question.

[M] "If I do this, she won't know why it was hard."

Rimuru looked at him.

[R] "You don't get to use that as her answer."

Mau lowered his eyes.

For a while the room held nothing but the breath of the person who was not being allowed into the conversation.

* * *

He asked about the other obligation again.

Not because he thought repeating the question would make it smaller. Because he needed to hear whether anything had changed while they had been talking.

Nothing had.

The Goddess had not decided what she would ask. He would owe it if he accepted. There was no second bargain already paid by the hurts he had collected before tonight.

[Dio] *You could refuse this answer, Mau.*

He closed his eyes.

She had not put Ori's life behind the refusal this time. Had not made a door in the room which would close on somebody if he took too long.

That made it harder to tell himself why he was still considering it.

[M] "And the shadows keep what they know."

[Dio] *Yes. Not what they do not know.*

[M] "Ciel doesn't."

[Dio] *Not this history.*

He passed that answer on too, though she already knew enough to have refused it.

[M] "Then include her. I don't want to make her keep it from Rimuru for me."

Rimuru stared at him.

[C] *You are not being asked to save me from knowing. I can tell him the truth. I already have.*

Mau looked at the floor.

He had taken the fact that she would not carry his secret and made it a reason to take the choice away from her. Saying he did not want to burden her had not changed what he was asking for.

* * *

Rimuru stood when Mau looked up.

He did not reach for Yuka. Did not use her sleeping body to make the argument harder or put his hand over Mau's mouth as if the only thing between a thought and a choice were the shape of a spoken word.

He moved nearer, into the small space beside the stool.

[R] "We have time to ask them. We have time to make you a bed somewhere nobody needs to pretend anything tonight. I'm asking you to stay with us while it's difficult."

Mau looked at his friend.

The fear in him was different from anger. It had been there beneath the patient questions, beneath the effort to make an ordinary table large enough for people who did not know how to sit together.

[M] "I'm sorry."

Rimuru's hand closed at his side.

[R] "Don't do that instead of listening."

[C] *Mau. I am saying no.*

He heard them both.

He had the answer. He knew the first cost. He knew that the second had no shape yet. Knew whose lives he would be making simpler by taking the harder choices out of their reach.

Yuka had offered to remember for both of them.

He turned toward her without moving his hand to wake her.

[M] "I accept."

Rimuru said his name.

The last sound of it caught in his chest.

Not the first beat.

The other one.

For an instant he could feel the space between them as distinctly as the edge of the mattress beneath his hand. Something had answered where there should only have been something to fill.

[Dio] *Wait.*

Rimuru caught his shoulder.

Mau saw the stool tip. Saw Yuka's hand move against the blanket as though the noise might wake her. He tried to say her name.

The room went black before he could tell whether he had made a sound.

Then came a noise like a word being dragged through a broken speaker.

`OVER—`

It caught, repeated half a syllable, and tore cleanly through the dark.

`OVERWRITE`
