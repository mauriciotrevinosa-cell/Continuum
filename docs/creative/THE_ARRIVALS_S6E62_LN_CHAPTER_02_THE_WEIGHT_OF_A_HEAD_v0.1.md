*When: Day475, late night. Rimuru has said Mau's name. Mau reaches him before he falls.*

His right hand caught the upper arm.

The left found the edge of the stool rather than trying to close fingers which would not close. He drew it beneath Rimuru as the weight came down, then guided the shoulder forward until folded arms could rest against the side of the bed.

Rimuru's head settled on them.

No cry. No impact against the floor. A breath came, slow and ordinary, while Mau kept his hand there long enough to be certain the position would hold.

Behind him Yuka moved toward the little hollow he had left in the bedding.

Her eyes stayed closed.

The room had not vanished.

He found the edge of the blanket with his foot and stepped clear of it. Put his hand flat on the bed.

Then he remembered the trees.

* * *

White hair first.

Green eyes. The long ears becoming ears instead of part of a dream he had not quite escaped. A face near his, and then the sky and the trees beyond it coming into focus when he blinked.

He knew where he had been lying.

Not the room placed inside the beginning he had been carrying. Not a doorway behind which Yuka had already been waiting for him to arrive.

The clearing.

It belonged to him with a force which made his hand press harder into the mattress.

The false beginning did not disappear into a blank patch. He could still remember being certain of it. Answering people from inside it, feeling impatient when they put another version in front of him which fitted less easily than the one he already had.

Now he knew what it had been replacing.

He could feel the difference without somebody pointing to the right name on a page.

* * *

There was sunlight against water.

A hand letting go of his arm very slowly, after she had held the place where the scratch had been. A shoulder finding his shoulder. The surprised moment before he understood she meant to rest her head there.

He remembered staying still.

Remembered how glad he had been when she did not move away.

It was not something a helpful person had explained. Not an account which could be made more convincing by adding another detail he could recognise.

He had wanted to be beside her.

He wanted it now.

The feeling reached the present body before he had found a way of preparing for it. His chest hurt with the effort of taking a breath too carefully.

He sat down on the edge of the bed because he could not keep asking his knees to decide whether they were going to hold him.

Yuka was behind his shoulder.

He looked back at her.

His hand moved toward her face before he had thought of why.

* * *

He stopped short of waking her.

Her hair had caught beneath her cheek. One piece lay against the corner of her mouth, in almost the same place he had moved it when she had been trying to tell him about the next dinner.

He loved her.

Not as an idea he could thank for having kept him company until something more important arrived. Not with the thin relief of a mistake finally being identified.

He remembered the stars inside the Red Gate. Her face after the first kiss. The warmth of the hand which had stayed on his shirt while she found the courage to tell him what had once been said in a hospital.

Those things were not becoming a false history because another life had returned.

He had lived them.

He had wanted them.

His hand touched the blanket beside hers.

The beat in his own heart was no longer the only place the feeling seemed to arrive from. The other rhythm held its own warmth, deep in the body which had already been carrying it.

Yuka.

Then the name which had returned with the weight of the head beside the lake.

Frieren.

He loved them both.

* * *

The room blurred.

Mau brought his right hand to his face before the first tear reached his mouth. The left side of what he could see stayed less distinct than the right, even when he blinked hard enough to make his eye hurt.

The damage had not been part of the lie.

Neither had the body which had survived the things he was remembering feeling again.

There were memories he did not want to look at for long. The Trial returned with the knowledge of having lived inside it, not a new wound which could be separated from the person on the bed. The old fear knew where to settle.

He put his feet flat on the floor.

Found the bed under his palm. The blanket. The real sound of two people breathing close enough for him to count if he needed to.

He was in Yuka's room.

It was night.

He had chosen this after hearing what it would cost.

The next breath did not come easily. It came.

* * *

She had corrected the place in the clearing.

Several feet. He remembered how certain she had been, how the ordinary precision of it had made him smile when there were larger things he was trying to find the words for.

Her hand had come to his face.

He remembered the kiss after they had chosen each other aloud, and the peculiar astonishment of being happy enough that he could not make it dignified.

Then an old silver case beneath his thumb. The cracked face. His turning it twice, because the needle continuing to find her seemed impossible in a way he had wanted to watch happen again.

Her hand behind his neck when he crouched beside her chair.

He remembered moving his own chair closer afterward so that she would not have to reach so far.

Small things.

They came back with their full size.

His mouth moved before any sound came out of it.

He had stood in front of her in the garden and known that this person wanted him to feel something. He had been sorry, careful, frightened of making another promise because the previous ones belonged to somebody he could not quite reach.

Now the wanting was his.

So was the memory of not having it when she needed him.

* * *

Ori had fallen asleep on the cart.

The weight against his shoulder had made his arm go numb. A bucket lay on her knees; a shell moved inside it when the cart shifted, a little dry sound beside the larger sound of the wheels.

Dad.

He remembered hearing it from a girl too far inside sleep to be checking whether it would hurt him.

Frieren's hand came across the space between them and rested on his.

The memory did not ask him to decide how close people felt.

His daughter.

He bent forward until his forehead almost touched the hand resting on the blanket.

It had not been possible to tell her yes the way she had needed. He remembered the question now with everything which should have been inside the answer. The little fists, the tears, the lights, the frightened hope that it might still be a game if only he would admit it.

There was no way to reach back into that moment and hold her differently.

Even the people who had watched it happen were being given a different memory of why she had cried.

He kept the one in which he had failed to answer.

* * *

Ciel said his name.

He raised his head.

For an instant he expected the rest of what she had been saying. The refusal. The exact, clear objection to being treated as the part of herself which could be removed as long as the useful work stayed.

[C] *Are you with me?*

He could not answer at once.

[C] *Take a moment. You don't have to describe all of it now.*

Her voice was awake.

It had the patience he knew, the attention which had stayed while he tried to move two small outlets without turning them into an excuse to do too much. No part of her competence sounded far away.

The thing she had said to stop him was gone from her next question.

[C] *Has the original beginning come back?*

Mau looked at Rimuru, asleep on his folded arms.

[M] *Yes.*

[C] *And you know which parts were put there?*

[M] *Yes.*

He could hear her trying to help him fit the answer into the work they had already done together. Names. Skills. Events. How close people felt.

She had not stopped being Ciel.

He had taken something from her anyway.

* * *

[C] *Is Rimuru comfortable?*

He looked at the angle of the sleeping head. Adjusted the fold of an arm carefully, supporting the shoulder while he made room for it against the bed edge.

[M] *He's asleep. He didn't hit anything.*

[C] *All right. Keep breathing normally. If something changes in your eye or your hand, tell me. Remembering isn't the same as repairing those.*

He looked at his fingers.

They were his. Present. Still limited.

[M] *I know.*

Yuka made a small sound in the bedding behind him.

He turned. Drew the blanket up where his movement had pulled it from her shoulder. Her right hand lay open beside his, no longer holding it.

He put his right palm under hers again.

Could remember her face on the mountain with exactly the feeling which had made him want another day there.

Could remember the woman in the east room with a love which did not become smaller to make that possible.

The quiet house could not hear either beat as a name.

He sat beside her until the tears stopped falling onto the blanket. Then stayed when there were no more tears to give him something to do.
