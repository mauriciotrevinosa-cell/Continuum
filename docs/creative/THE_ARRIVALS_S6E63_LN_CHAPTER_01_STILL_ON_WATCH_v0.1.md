*When: Day475, late night. Yuka's room. Rimuru's head rests above his forearms at the edge of the bed. Yuka's hand lies over Mau's.*

When Mau could move again, he eased his hand out from under hers.

He left it resting on the blanket. Got up slowly, keeping clear of Rimuru's feet, and took hold of the stool.

Moved it a little nearer.

Not far. Enough that Rimuru's weight would stay over the seat instead of drawing him forward when Mau took his hand away.

One of the feet caught the edge of the rug.

Mau stopped, lifted that side and put it down flat. Watched the hand resting beside Rimuru's face. It did not move.

The last thing that hand had done while its owner was awake had been to close at his side.

Mau put his own hand against the wood again.

[C] *He's breathing normally.*

He nodded before remembering that she did not need him to.

[M] *I can see.*

Rimuru's sleeve had drawn back above the wrist. Mau caught the edge and brought it down without changing the position of his arm.

Then there was nothing else to adjust.

* * *

[C] *Mau?*

He looked toward Yuka.

She had turned onto her side. Her hand lay near the place where he had been sitting before Rimuru came in. The blanket still covered her feet.

His mouth had gone dry.

At the hook beside the door, his coat hung by its collar. Hers had slipped slightly beneath it. He remembered helping somebody else get an arm into a sleeve, patiently, while she pretended that the morning was too cold for the task to be completed.

He had always known Frieren disliked getting up.

Now he could remember how long he had been willing to stand there.

The small movement of Yuka's mouth drew his eyes back to her before he had decided what to say.

* * *

[C] *Any new pain?*

He paid attention before answering. Head. Chest. The places he had learnt to check when a feeling arrived too quickly to be mistaken for an ordinary one.

His left eye still softened the edge of the stool. His fingers would not close all the way when he tried them against his palm.

[M] *Not new.*

[C] *Don't keep testing the hand.*

He opened it.

There was no need to demonstrate that part again.

She gave him time to look at the window before speaking again.

[C] *Do you feel like yourself?*

He looked at Rimuru's sleeping face.

[M] *Yes.*

The answer was quiet and complete. It did not make the room easier to sit in.

* * *

Mau crossed to the window in his socks.

The latch was cold. He took it in his right hand and eased it open, keeping the frame from striking the wall.

Air reached his face. He had forgotten how warm the little room had become with three people in it.

Outside, the house had not gone out of existence while he had been unable to hear it. Leaves moved. A branch touched another branch and sprang free. Somewhere below, water fell from an edge in separate drops.

He kept one hand on the sill.

The first time he had come home from somewhere terrible, there had been people waiting to find out what they could do for him.

He could remember them now.

He could remember Rimuru doing it tonight.

The hand on the sill tightened. He loosened it before the stiff fingers began to hurt.

* * *

At the stake, Nazuna slept with her arms folded.

Her head had tipped to one side. From time to time the wind lifted a strand of hair from her cheek and laid it down again.

The shadow keeping watch with her did not sleep.

It remained turned toward the ground it had been told to watch. Nothing had crossed it. Nothing required pursuit, a warning or the waking of the person beside it.

Farther along, the watch continued.

No new soldier had been called to replace the people who had closed their eyes. The ones already there kept their places.

* * *

Mau turned back before the cold could reach Yuka's bed.

He closed the window to a narrow opening and held the latch while the wood settled. When he looked down, his shadow lay between him and the stool.

He called Beru.

The dark rose, then folded itself into the space Mau had left near the window. Beru lowered his head to keep clear of the frame. His first glance found the bed; the second found Rimuru.

He spoke very softly.

[Br] "My King."

[M] "Quietly."

Beru lowered himself farther, with considerable care for somebody who could have put a claw through the floor by being careless.

[Br] "I am being quiet."

[M] "I know."

It was the sort of answer he would once have given without stopping to think about the person who would hear it.

Beru looked at him for a moment.

[Br] "You look tired."

[M] "I am."

The shadow's eyes went toward Yuka again.

* * *

[Br] "The little rope—"

[M] "She's asleep."

[Br] "I can see that, my King."

Mau waited.

Beru had made himself very still. There had been times in the gate when he had done that before choosing a word. Mau remembered the black glass beneath their feet and Igris saying a name sharply enough to make Beru disappear for a day.

He had laughed then.

[Br] "And the Queen?"

[M] "Asleep too."

Beru's gaze returned to him.

The question had not been about whether he should make less noise.

Mau put his hand on the sill behind him. The rough edge gave him something to feel while Beru waited for an answer he had not quite asked for.

[M] "You can still call Yuka the little rope. If she lets you."

[Br] "She has let me."

[M] "Then keep asking her, not me."

Beru lowered his eyes.

[Br] "And the Queen?"

He had asked it exactly the same way.

[M] "Frieren."

The name caught in Mau's throat after he had said it. Beru heard that too.

* * *

For a little while neither spoke.

Yuka moved one hand beneath the blanket. Mau looked toward her before he could stop himself.

Beru watched him look.

There was no uncertainty about what the shadow remembered of the gate. Only about what he should do with it when he came up into this room.

Mau could have made an order of the silence.

Instead he brought his eyes back.

[M] "If there's something you don't know how to say, come and talk to me first. Before you try to say it to somebody else."

[Br] "To you."

[M] "Yes."

Beru considered that. One claw moved against another without making a sound.

[Br] "Even if you are busy?"

[M] "Ask. I'll tell you if I need a moment."

The answer did not please him. Mau knew him well enough again to see it.

Beru looked at the bed once more, then at the space beside the stool.

[Br] "Will you call if you need me?"

[M] "I will."

He dipped his head. Went back into the shadow more slowly than he had come out of it.

Mau remained by the window until the floor was only floor again.

* * *

[C] *You don't have to settle everything tonight.*

He closed his eyes.

She was trying to give him time with what he had asked to recover. It was the sort of thing she had said while staying with him through a difficult attempt, when the work was over and he still could not stop measuring himself against it.

He remembered how she had stayed.

[M] *I know.*

[C] *Would you like me to stay?*

He took a breath. Let it out without speaking the answer he could not give her.

[M] *Please.*

There was no change in the thread which joined them. No sign in her voice that a word had been taken out of the conversation before she could say it.

He went back to the bed.

* * *

Rimuru's hand had slipped a little toward the edge.

Mau put it back above the blanket. Checked that the stool was still flat, then sat on the narrow place left between his friend and Yuka's feet.

He could not reach her hand without leaning across her.

He left it where it was.

The blade of grass remained caught in the seam of the picnic blanket. He picked it free and rolled it between his fingers. Carried it to the window ledge rather than letting it fall into the bed again.

An ordinary thing which needed doing.

When he sat down again, the Goddess spoke.

[Dio] *This is going to be fun.*

He looked toward the window.

[M] "For who?"

Ciel waited a moment.

[C] *Has she said something else?*

[M] *That she thinks this will be fun.*

The answer did not make him laugh.

Neither did Ciel.

[C] *That wasn't what you asked to have back.*

[M] *No.*

The Goddess did not tell him whom she had meant.

* * *

He stayed where he was.

At one point his right foot went numb beneath the bed's edge. He moved it. At another, Yuka pulled the blanket toward herself and uncovered part of Rimuru's wrist. Mau drew a little of the unused fold over it without waking either of them.

The room did not become quiet all at once. It had small sounds in it, and things which needed moving before they caught or fell. Breath. Cloth. The stool creaking when he rested his hand on it and took the hand away again.

He knew where Frieren was.

Knew where Ori slept.

He could have put on his boots and crossed the corridor. The thought came with the love he had been unable to reach, immediate enough that he nearly stood.

Yuka had asked him to tell her before he left.

He looked at the coat on the hook, then sat back.

For a while longer, she could wake and find it there.

Outside, the same shadows kept watch. Inside, Rimuru slept with one hand close to Mau's knee, and Yuka's breathing continued beneath the blanket they had brought home.

Mau waited for the room to grow light.
