*When: Day476, dawn. Yuka's room. The narrow opening at the window has become a pale line, and Mau is still sitting between the stool and the foot of the bed.*

The first movement came from Rimuru.

His fingers drew back beneath his sleeve. Then his head lifted far enough for him to look at the blanket from a distance of a few inches.

He stayed like that for a moment.

Mau leaned forward.

[M] "Slowly. The stool's close."

Rimuru looked sideways at him. Sat back with his hands against the edge of the bed until his weight was properly over the seat.

The wood creaked.

Both looked at Yuka.

She moved one foot beneath the blanket and remained asleep.

Rimuru lowered his voice.

[R] "That's a terrible place to put a bed."

Mau's mouth moved before he could stop it.

[M] "It was here first."

Rimuru looked at the stool. At his own sleeve, where the blanket had left a folded line in the cloth.

[R] "I remember coming up. You were going to ask her."

Mau nodded.

[R] "Did she do it?"

* * *

For a moment he could hear the other question in exactly the same voice.

Whether the sleeping girl had asked for what was being offered. Whether they could wake her. Whether he would stay with them while it was difficult.

Rimuru was looking at his face now, waiting for a different answer.

[M] "Yes."

His friend became very still.

[M] "The beginning. What happened. What it felt like. I know the part which was put there wasn't my life before this."

He could say it clearly without making the room into a place where everything had become easy to look at.

Rimuru let out a breath.

[R] "All right."

He looked down at his hands, then back up.

[R] "How are you?"

Mau had several answers. The right one for a person who remembered sitting with him while he asked to recover his own stolen life was among them.

[M] "I remember you properly."

Rimuru's expression changed.

[M] "Not just where you live. Or what we worked on. I know why I want you there."

He could feel all the ordinary times which belonged to the answer without having to choose one to prove it.

Rimuru looked away first. Rubbed one hand over his face, then put it back on his knee.

[R] "You could have started with that instead of making me ask."

The little complaint was quiet enough not to wake her.

Mau wanted to laugh. Could not quite manage it.

* * *

Rimuru asked about his head before trying to get up.

Mau took a breath and paid attention. The room stayed where it was. On the left, the edge of the stool softened sooner than it did when he turned enough to look with his right.

He opened his left hand against his knee. Two fingers stopped where they had been stopping before the night began.

[M] "The same. Nothing new."

Ciel gave the brief answer to them both.

[C] *The old limitations are still there. Don't make a stronger attempt just to prove it.*

He let the hand rest.

Rimuru nodded slightly, hearing her too.

[R] "Shoko will still want to see you."

[M] "I know."

Rimuru shifted the stool back only after looking down to find the rug. Lifted the caught foot clear, set it flat and stood without scraping it across the boards.

Mau got up enough to give him room.

[R] "Did I hit anything?"

[M] "No. I caught you. Got you back on the stool."

Rimuru looked at the bed edge where his arms had been.

[R] "Thank you."

Mau put his hand on the wood.

[M] "Thank you for coming."

Rimuru looked at him for another moment. There was no sign that the words had found the part of the night Mau meant them for.

[R] "Do you want me to fetch anyone?"

Mau looked toward the door.

[M] "Not everybody. I'd like to go and see them myself."

Rimuru nodded. Took his hand off the stool, checked that it would stay where he had put it and looked back.

[R] "What about Shoko?"

[M] "You can tell her. That it came back. No new pain. I'll come for the check."

[R] "I'll tell her those parts."

He paused at the bed's corner.

[R] "You don't have to give us a demonstration before breakfast."

Mau looked at him.

[R] "I know there will be questions. They can wait until you've had something to eat."

The familiar practicality reached him more sharply than an embrace would have. He could remember being annoyed by it. Could feel why he had kept allowing the same person to ask.

[M] "I haven't even seen what's for breakfast."

[R] "I'm hoping someone else knows."

[R] "I'll be downstairs. The office, for a bit. Come and tell me if anything changes."

Mau nodded.

He watched his friend go to the door, open it just enough and step through. Rimuru closed it with his fingers still on the latch so the sound would not carry.

* * *

Yuka opened her eyes after the door shut.

Not at once. She drew her arm out from under the blanket, put her hand against the pillow and looked at the empty place where the stool had been closer.

Then she saw him.

[Yk] "Oppa?"

The word came through sleep, ordinary and a little rough.

Mau did not answer quickly enough.

She raised her head.

[Yk] "Did you sleep there?"

[M] "I stayed here."

[Yk] "All night?"

He nodded.

She looked at the narrow bit of bed beneath him.

[Yk] "You should have moved the stool."

[M] "It was occupied."

Yuka blinked. Looked toward the door.

[Yk] "Rimuru?"

[M] "He just went down."

She put her head back for a moment, considering the arrangements with the expression of somebody discovering that other people had made a task unnecessarily difficult while she was asleep.

Then she sat up.

The stool had left very little room beside the bed.

She pushed it with her foot. Stopped when Mau reached beneath it.

[Yk] "What?"

[M] "My boots."

He brought them out by their tops. She moved the stool clear, then looked at the marks in the rug where it had stood.

[Yk] "Now you can get up."

He set the boots beside his feet.

[M] "That was the difficulty."

She was already looking down at the blanket.

The blanket slid to her knees. She caught it, looked at one corner and began searching along the seam.

[Yk] "I keep finding bits of mountain in this."

Mau looked toward the window.

[M] "One's on the ledge."

She followed his eyes. Saw the little pale blade and laughed.

[Yk] "We brought half the hill home."

* * *

She remembered the water.

The difficult footing. Kaisel bringing them back while they could still see. The cakes, the game by the pool, the noise she had made for a small dragon which had only been hands and sunlight.

She said enough of it while folding the loose corner that he could hear how easily those things fitted into her morning.

He could remember the same places.

The hand she had kept holding after the game. The way she had turned toward him. What she had meant when she put a table into a possible next time.

Yuka looked up.

[Yk] "Did you get your answer?"

[M] "Yes."

She stopped smoothing the blanket.

[Yk] "You can remember before? Properly?"

He nodded.

[M] "Properly."

Her face brightened before she tried to make it quieter for the room.

[Yk] "Have you told Frieren?"

The name reached the love which had returned with the old silver case and the weight beside the lake.

The girl asking still reached the other one.

[M] "Not yet."

Yuka looked toward the pale window.

[Yk] "It's morning."

She said it as if that explained a useful part of what he could do next.

* * *

Mau wanted to ask her something.

The first word rose with the memory of her telling him what a birthday had once meant. He could have asked whether she remembered sitting beneath this blanket and deciding to trust him with it.

She was pulling her sleeve straight. The flower on its cord had caught at the cuff; she freed it without looking frightened of what it meant to somebody else.

He let the question go unspoken.

Yuka had not misplaced a thing which he could help her find by making her say it again.

[Yk] "Are we eating downstairs?"

[M] "I think so."

[Yk] "I'm going to find Grandma and Grandpa. Before breakfast."

She pushed the blanket down to make room for her feet. Looked at the floor before getting out of bed, then went to the window and touched the narrow gap.

[Yk] "Did you open it?"

[M] "It was warm. I left it a little."

She nodded. Closed it against the cooler air which had begun reaching the bed now she was upright.

The blade of grass remained on the ledge.

* * *

Mau picked up the boots beside his feet.

He sat to put them on, working the first one into place with his right hand. The tongue of the second folded inward. He took it off again rather than forcing his foot past the fold, put it straight and tried once more.

Yuka had begun folding the blanket at the other end of the bed.

[M] "I'm going to the east room. To see Frieren and Ori."

She looked over.

[M] "I said I'd tell you before I left."

[Yk] "I know."

She held the two corners together and laid one over the other.

[Yk] "Go on. I'll get ready."

He stood after checking both boots were secure. Took his coat from the hook without bringing hers down with it.

His gloves were still in the pocket, one tucked into the other. He felt them there, left them there and put his arms into the sleeves.

Yuka set the blanket across the end of the bed.

[Yk] "Your collar."

He reached for the wrong part.

She came over and put the folded edge straight, an impatient little correction made with the same ease as her first word that morning.

[Yk] "There."

He looked at her face.

[M] "Thank you."

She had already turned back toward the bed to find what she wanted before washing.

[Yk] "See you at breakfast, Oppa."

* * *

He closed her door softly.

Stayed beside it long enough to hear her moving about inside. A drawer, a small sound of annoyance, the ordinary business of getting up.

Then he walked.

The house had begun making morning noises. Feet on the stair. Somebody speaking below. A smell from the kitchen which was not strong enough yet to tell him what had been put on first.

He kept his hands at his sides and went round the turn toward the east room.

The door was where it had been when he could not find the thing waiting behind it inside himself.

He remembered going through it on ordinary nights. Leaving the compass beside the bed. Waiting while Frieren tried to find a place for a book which would, inevitably, turn out to be somewhere he needed to stand.

The love did not arrive politely.

He stopped outside.

Put his fingers against the wood, took them away and knocked softly.
