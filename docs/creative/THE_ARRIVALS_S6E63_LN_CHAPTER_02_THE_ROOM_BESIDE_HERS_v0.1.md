# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 35
## Chapter 2 — The Room Beside Hers

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S6E63
**Continuity state:** A lived jump to the G2 reunion in early S1. Bocchi, Yuta and Rimuru already arrived in S1E3/4/6 order; Fern, Anko, Maomao and Momo arrive together after one road night. Frieren initiates the reunion and discloses seven days of searching in her origin world. In this branch Mau/Frieren have always slept separately; Fern can share Frieren's room without displacing him. Rimuru has Raphael, not Ciel; no mental link with Mau is assumed. Gray perception continues.

*When: Footsteps outside the inn. Mau has a bowl of nails balanced on his knee.*

The smallest nail was caught in the seam of his trousers.

Mau had almost freed it when Frieren put down the hinge she was holding and walked past him. He looked up with the nail between his fingers.

"Did you find the—" ᴹ

She had already crossed the room.

Through the open doorway he saw four people coming out of the trees. A woman at the front stopped, and Frieren went to her so quickly that Mau set the bowl on the floor.

The woman had time to say her name before Frieren put her arms around her.

Mau stayed where he was.

For several evenings he had watched Frieren lift her head at a sound outside. Once he had gone to the window himself, hoping to find whatever she wanted before she could see that it was another animal crossing the yard. He had never known what to say afterward.

Now there was somebody to hold.

The dark-haired woman stood uncertainly in the embrace, then brought a hand to Frieren's back.

Something small lay in the dirt beside their shoes.

Mau went far enough to pick it up. An ornament, warm from somebody's hair. He placed it on the low wall within reach and went to greet the other three strangers.

"Hi. I'm Mau." ᴹ

One of them looked past him toward the inn.

"Are all the people here like that?" she asked. ᴬ

He glanced at Frieren. Her face was hidden against the other woman's shoulder.

"I don't know yet." ᴹ

The woman with the long hair gave him a quick, assessing look. The girl beside her appeared relieved to find a building. The last stranger had stopped to inspect something growing near the wall.

"We have water," he said. "I can get cups." ᴹ

"That would be great," said the girl. ᴹᵒ

He was happy to have a job he understood.

* * *

Yuta came downstairs with clean cloths draped over one shoulder. Bocchi followed carrying two blankets so high that she had to look around them to see the steps.

Rimuru took a blanket from the top.

"You can make more than one trip." ᴿ

"I know. I just thought if I did it all at once I wouldn't have to decide when to come back in." ᴮ

Mau took the other blanket and freed her hands.

Outside, Frieren was talking.

He could not hear every word from the table, but he heard seven days. Then the other woman repeating it, very quietly.

Yuta stopped folding a cloth.

Mau looked at him. Yuta returned the look, and neither went outside.

Rimuru put another pot on the hearth. Bocchi collected cups which were already on the table and moved them two places farther along. Nobody objected to the unnecessary task.

When the two women finally came in, Frieren kept a hand around the other's sleeve.

"This is Fern," she said. ᶠ

They made room.

Fern thanked them for the water and, a little later, for looking after Frieren. That made Frieren turn toward her.

"I was looking after them." ᶠ

Yuta looked at the hinge on the table. Mau looked at the nail still in his hand.

"Mostly," Mau said. ᴹ

Bocchi made a noise into her cup and then looked horrified at herself.

Fern sat beside Frieren. Her shoulders gradually lowered while she listened to the introductions. Anko asked about the road. Momo wanted to know how many people had arrived before them. Maomao asked where the clean water came from and whether anyone had been sick.

There were useful answers to some of it. For the rest, they could only show what they had found.

Mau took the empty cups to the kitchen. By the time he came back, Fern had noticed the dusty blankets.

"We'll need rooms." ᶠᵉ

"There are rooms upstairs," he said. ᴹ

"With roofs?" ᴬ

"Several." ᴹ

Anko smiled at the answer. He could not tell whether she thought he was joking.

* * *

Fern looked into Frieren's room first.

Mau waited in the corridor with a spare blanket. He could see the travel case beside the bed, the book left open on top of it, one stocking hanging from a chair because Frieren had decided it was still a little damp after everyone else had stopped caring.

Fern's gaze went from the single bed to the available patch of floor.

"I could sleep here tonight, if you don't mind." ᶠᵉ

"You can stay here," Frieren said. ᶠ

Fern turned toward Mau.

"Will you need anything moved?" ᶠᵉ

"From where?" ᴹ

She glanced into the room again.

He understood.

"Oh. No. I'm over there." ᴹ

He pointed at the door beside it.

Fern looked toward his room. His jersey was visible through the gap, still hanging over the chair. He had washed it twice since the clearing and had begun to dislike how long it took to dry.

"You can borrow that chair if you need one," he offered. ᴹ

"Thank you. I think we're all right." ᶠᵉ

Frieren took the blanket from him.

"We can put another bed here later." ᶠ

Fern's mouth moved as though she had several practical concerns to raise. Then she looked at Frieren's hand, still resting on the folded blanket.

"Tomorrow," she said. ᶠᵉ

Mau left them to it.

Halfway down the stairs he stopped because there was a new pair of shoes at the bottom. Someone had set them where he usually stepped when he was carrying things. He moved them against the wall and made a mental note to tell their owner.

Yuta was at the long table making a list on the back of a scrap of paper.

"How many?" he asked. ʸᵗ

"People or beds?" ᴹ

"Beds first. I was hoping the people would stay still while we counted." ʸᵗ

Mau sat down across from him. Rimuru pushed a cup toward the empty place between them with an elbow.

"The rooms at the back need checking," Rimuru said. "And I don't mean opening the door and deciding it looks fine." ᴿ

"That was one time." ᴹ

"You put your foot through the floor." ᴿ

"Which was a very effective check." ᴹ

Yuta started to laugh. He turned it into a cough when Fern and Frieren appeared at the top of the stairs, though there was no reason for them to mind.

Fern came down alone a moment later.

"She's lying down," she told them. ᶠᵉ

Mau nodded.

She stayed beside the table instead of immediately joining them.

"Was she—" ᶠᵉ

Fern stopped. Mau waited, keeping his hands around his cup.

"She listened for you," he said. ᴹ

Fern looked toward the staircase.

"A lot?" ᶠᵉ

"Yes." ᴹ

The answer sat between them. He wanted to make it easier to receive, and could not think of a truthful way.

Fern pulled out the chair at the end.

"Then I should tell you what she's like when she doesn't want to get up." ᶠᵉ

Yuta put down his pencil.

Rimuru leaned back.

"There are more techniques?" he asked. ᴿ

Fern looked at him with the expression of somebody discovering that her particular difficulty was a shared field of study.

"What have you tried?" ᶠᵉ

Mau began with knocking. Yuta contributed what happened when one announced breakfast too far ahead of breakfast. Rimuru admitted he had threatened to eat her portion and had then discovered the problem of being taken at his word.

By the time Fern laughed, the light in the common room had begun to fail.

Mau got up to fetch the lamp. When he returned, she had taken the paper and was drawing the shape of a second bed beside their list.

He set the lamp where all four could see.

Upstairs, a board creaked. A door opened and closed. Frieren had apparently decided that lying down required the book she had left near the hearth.

"Five minutes," Fern called without turning. ᶠᵉ

There was a pause on the stairs.

"You just got here," Frieren said. ᶠ

"I remember how long your five minutes are." ᶠᵉ

Mau looked down before she could catch him smiling.
