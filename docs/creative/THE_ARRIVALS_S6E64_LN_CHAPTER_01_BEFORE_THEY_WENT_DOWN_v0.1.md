*When: Day476, morning. The east room. Mau has come along the corridor from Yuka's room and opened the door quietly.*

Frieren had one arm outside the cover.

Her hair lay across it, caught in the fold at the elbow. The pillow had been pushed toward the edge of the bed in the night. A strip of light came past the curtain and stopped short of her face.

Mau stayed by the door until he remembered he could close it.

The small sound brought her eyes open.

For a moment she looked at him without lifting her head. Then at the light, as if it might explain why he had come in wearing a coat when the bed was still warm enough to make leaving it unreasonable.

[F] "Morning?"

[M] "Yes."

She moved her arm. Found the hair caught beneath it and pulled it free before trying to sit up.

He wanted to help her.

The wanting was so ordinary that he had to look down to keep it from showing all at once.

* * *

She noticed anyway.

[F] "Were you able to ask?"

He nodded.

Frieren stopped moving the cover.

For a little while she looked only at his face. He could see the questions she had learnt to leave small enough for him to answer, the effort of not turning a nod into the thing she had been waiting to hear.

[M] "I remember."

Her hand tightened on the cloth.

[F] "You remember what happened?"

[M] "I remember being there."

He took one step nearer.

[M] "With you."

She watched him cross the rest of the space.

He sat on the edge of the bed, slowly enough that she could move her knee before his weight reached it. Left his hands on the cover while she looked at them, then back at him.

[F] "The clearing?"

He smiled, and felt the attempt turn unsteady before he could keep it neat.

[M] "You wouldn't let me stand in the wrong place."

Her mouth opened a little.

Then closed again without the answer she had meant to give.

* * *

She touched his face.

The fingers came to rest beside his cheekbone and stayed there, as if she had been allowed to find out something she did not want to make him prove by speaking any more.

Mau turned into her hand.

He had known that she was beautiful while he could not remember loving her. Could have described the long ears, the white hair, the precise irritation which changed her face when somebody disturbed the order of a shelf.

The description had not contained this.

He put his right hand over hers.

[M] "I love you."

Her breath caught.

He had not meant the words as a test. She had heard too many careful answers for him to take back the plain one because it had hurt on the way out.

[M] "I do."

She bent toward him before he could say anything else.

The kiss was awkward at first. He was still sitting too far from the pillow; she had one knee trapped beneath the cover and did not seem willing to stop to free it.

He moved closer.

Her hand went behind his neck.

* * *

When they drew apart she kept her forehead against his.

He could feel her breathing. The small, uneven effort of keeping it quiet. Her other hand found his shirt and stayed against the place where the cloth had been wrinkled the night before.

He remembered who had left the crease.

The love he felt for Frieren did not remove that knowledge. Neither did it fail because the knowledge remained.

He had accepted both.

[F] "Are you in pain?"

He took a moment before answering.

[M] "Tired. The old things are still the old things."

She drew back enough to look at his left eye, then at the hand resting on the cover.

[F] "Shoko should see you."

[M] "She can."

There was no argument in it. He did not want the first morning back beside her to become another occasion for proving he could stand up.

Frieren touched his cheek again.

[F] "You can stay here until breakfast."

He nodded.

For a little while they did.

* * *

The connecting door moved.

Ori stood behind it with sleep still making one side of her hair flat. She had come far enough through to see the bed before remembering to look at the floor.

Mau knew what she was avoiding.

The shelf edge.

He had walked past it while arranging an entire false beginning around another part of the house. Had been told enough of his real life to know where this room was, and still needed people to explain what belonging here had felt like.

Ori looked up.

[O] "Dad?"

He answered before she could turn the word into a question she regretted asking.

[M] "Hey."

Her eyes went to her mother.

Frieren nodded once.

[F] "He remembers."

Ori did not run at him.

She came over slowly, looking at his face as she had looked while deciding whether to ask him to sit beside the beans.

He moved his hand from Frieren's and left it open on the bed.

* * *

[O] "Me?"

Mau could hear the shell in the bucket.

Not here. On the cart, in the life which had belonged to him before somebody had taken it. Her head against his shoulder, the numb arm he had not moved because she had fallen asleep there.

[M] "You too."

She stopped beside his knee.

[O] "The beach?"

[M] "The bucket on the way home."

Her fingers caught the cover.

[O] "Can I sit with you?"

[M] "Of course."

He waited.

Ori came into his arms while he was still trying to find the next thing he could say without asking her to look after how much it hurt.

He held her.

No new lights filled the room. She pressed her face against his shoulder and stayed there, with the full weight of a girl who did not have to stay small enough to be a question.

His left hand could not close all the way against her back.

He held her with it anyway.

* * *

Frieren moved nearer.

Put her hand between Ori's shoulders, over the part of Mau's sleeve which had folded beneath the small arm. For a while none of them tried to improve the position.

Mau looked past his daughter's hair at the book beside Frieren's chair.

The edge had a little bend in it.

He knew why it was there in the history he had kept. The book on the bench after the spell. A hand beside it which had begun to shake.

Here the hand rested gently on his daughter's back.

He did not mistake the gentleness for something the spell had proved impossible.

Nor could he tell himself that seeing it made him want to let go.

He lowered his face into Ori's hair.

Stayed until she began moving because she had got too warm, rather than because she was uncertain she could remain.

* * *

[O] "You have a coat on."

He looked down at himself.

[M] "I noticed."

[O] "It's indoors."

Frieren's mouth moved.

[F] "Very observant."

Ori looked at her suspiciously, then at the arm Mau was trying to free without putting her on the floor.

She helped. Stood between his knees while he took the coat off, then accepted it with both hands as if this part had been assigned to her from the beginning.

He showed her where to put it.

She hung it badly enough that one sleeve nearly touched the floor. Frieren looked at the sleeve. At Mau.

He shook his head very slightly.

Frieren left it.

* * *

He took off his boots before going farther into the room.

Put them where they belonged. Went to wash while Ori told her mother that she had not put the coat on the wrong hook, because nobody had said there was a wrong one.

Water ran over his hands.

He watched it move around the two fingers which would not follow the rest. Dried them without forcing them flat, then changed the shirt he had worn up the mountain and through the night.

The crease remained in the cloth when he folded it for washing.

He put it with the things which needed to be cleaned.

When he came back Frieren was sitting with Ori's hand caught in her hair. A knot had been found. Both of them seemed to believe the other had made it.

[F] "Wait."

[O] "I'm waiting."

[F] "You're pulling."

[O] "That's because it's stuck."

Mau came over.

They let him help without first explaining which of them was right.

* * *

Afterward Frieren began getting ready properly.

Slowly. With the reluctance of somebody who had good reasons to want breakfast and better reasons to make breakfast come a little later.

Ori went back through the connecting door for the things she had left in the wrong room.

Mau sat beside Frieren while she found the fastening at her cuff.

[F] "My staff is downstairs with Rimuru."

He looked at her.

She remembered putting it aside. An agreed place, a quiet interval, a thing which would be there when she wanted it again.

[F] "Would you bring it up? Before we go down."

[M] "Yes."

Her hand found his before he stood.

Kept him there long enough to touch his fingers with her mouth.

[F] "Don't take too long."

He nodded.

It was an ordinary thing to be asked, and he wanted to come back to her before she had to ask it again.
