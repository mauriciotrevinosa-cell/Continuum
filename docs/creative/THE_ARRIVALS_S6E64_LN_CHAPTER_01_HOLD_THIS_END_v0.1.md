# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 35
## Chapter 3 — Hold This End

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S6E64
**Continuity state:** A jump into S2 domestic expansion before Sukuna. Yuta, Maki and Rimuru are present; Mau/Frieren remain teacher/friend, with separate rooms. Physical teaching is modest, not a System/Monarch upgrade. Rimuru still has Raphael. No host link with Mau or public mental speech. Gray perception remains subjective to Mau. The work scene establishes practiced companionship and help between the three friends.

*When: A morning with enough dry weather to work outside. Yuta has made the mistake of saying the repair will be easy.*

The plank landed in the mud.

Mau kept holding his end for a moment, out of loyalty to an arrangement which no longer existed. Across from him, Rimuru looked down at the fallen end and then at Yuta.

"Easy," Rimuru repeated. ᴿ

"I said it should be easy." ʸᵗ

"Does that usually help?" ᴹ

Yuta bent to pick up the plank. It was wet all along the side they had spent the morning smoothing.

"Usually I stop talking before that part." ʸᵗ

Mau lifted his own end and they carried it to the trestles. The object was meant to become a bench, though it had been several other things in conversation before anyone had started cutting. Every time somebody asked whether it could be made a little longer, Rimuru pointed toward the tree and asked whether they were planning to seat the roots too.

The work had settled around the place people already used. There was a patch of shade which stayed cool through the late morning and a route across the yard which nobody could bear to block, because everyone had been walking it before they admitted there would be a house here.

Mau liked being able to imagine where a thing would go.

He had spent a long time putting his bag down near exits. Now he could leave it upstairs and come outside without first checking that it was closed.

Rimuru wiped mud off the plank with a rag.

"We should let that dry before we do the next side." ᴿ

"Then I have time to beat him," Mau said. ᴹ

Yuta looked at him with mild interest.

"At what?" ʸᵗ

"I was hoping you'd choose something." ᴹ

Rimuru stopped wiping.

"That might be the worst challenge I've heard." ᴿ

"I'm keeping my options open." ᴹ

Yuta put the tools in the shallow box they had agreed would be used for tools, after three mornings of finding them on shelves in the kitchen. Then he went to fetch the wooden practice swords.

Mau shook his arms loose while they waited. He had bruises on two fingers from gripping too hard the day before. They worked perfectly well; they just objected to some of his decisions.

Rimuru noticed him flexing them.

"You can practice without trying to win." ᴿ

"Can I?" ᴹ

"It would be new for you. Exciting." ᴿ

Yuta came back with the swords and handed Mau the smoother one. He had sanded a rough patch from the handle after Mau had raised a blister and attempted to hide it by holding his cup in his other hand at dinner.

Mau ran his thumb over the repair.

"Thanks." ᴹ

Yuta nodded and stepped onto the bare ground beside the path.

"Same thing as yesterday. Don't chase the blade." ʸᵗ

Mau set his feet.

For the first few passes it worked. He watched Yuta's shoulders instead of the approaching wood, remembered to breathe, and even found himself in the right place to make a touch.

Then Yuta changed the timing.

Mau tried to follow it with his hands. His feet remained committed to the previous idea. He took a light strike across the ribs and discovered that an instructor saying light was still describing a strike.

"You chased it," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"I had almost forgotten what it felt like." ᴹ

From the trestles, Rimuru made an unhelpful noise.

They started again.

By the sixth pass Mau had stopped trying to solve the whole exercise. He concentrated on staying balanced through the first movement. It left him enough time to see the second, and when he stepped away from that he was surprised to find that he had room to move in a third direction.

Yuta caught the practice blade on his own and lowered both.

"There." ʸᵗ

Mau glanced toward the place where his foot had landed.

"Was that it?" ᴹ

"That was something you can do again." ʸᵗ

It was a more useful answer than being told he was talented. He could remember where he had been standing. He could attempt it with his own feet.

They repeated it until Mau lost it, found it once more, and then lost it for good because he was tired.

Yuta put out a hand before he could insist on another round.

"Stop there." ʸᵗ

Mau wanted to ask for one more. The thought was already at the front of his mouth when he saw Yuta rotating his own shoulder.

"Your arm?" ᴹ

"A little stiff." ʸᵗ

Mau lowered the sword.

"You could have said." ᴹ

Yuta smiled, caught by his own lesson.

Rimuru brought them water.

* * *

They were sitting on the unfinished bench when Maki came through the yard.

It had one leg on a stone and a temporary brace which Rimuru had described as perfectly safe if nobody behaved like an idiot. Mau and Yuta had sat down very carefully after hearing that.

Maki looked at the swords against the tree.

"He still trying to win?" ᴹᵏ

"Less often," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"I got a good one." ᴹ

"Did he let you?" ᴹᵏ

Mau looked at Yuta.

Yuta drank his water.

Maki shook her head, but she was smiling when she went to inspect the crooked brace. She pressed it once with the toe of her shoe.

"Finish this before Milim finds it." ᴹᵏ

Mau looked up. Rimuru looked up at the same time.

"She's not here," Yuta reminded them. ʸᵗ

"Then we have a chance," Rimuru said. ᴿ

The name belonged to stories Rimuru had told them. Mau had begun to recognize the particular expression he wore when speaking of somebody powerful enough to have turned a bad afternoon into an event requiring repairs. It was usually followed by a small, helpless smile.

Maki left them with the warning and a cloth she had brought out for Yuta. He took it without asking what it was for and wiped the sweat off his neck.

Mau looked away before it could become staring.

The ease between them was old. Much older than either face suggested. He knew enough of Yuta's history now to understand that, and still found himself startled by it in little movements. The hand receiving a cloth. Maki continuing through the yard because she knew he would be there when she returned.

Rimuru sat on the remaining strip of bench.

The brace made a sound.

All three froze.

For several seconds none of them breathed properly.

Then Rimuru got up, very slowly, and took his cup with him.

"We're finishing it now." ᴿ

* * *

Frieren found them after lunch with the bench on its side.

Fern had given Mau a plate to bring out when he failed to arrive for the second call. He had eaten most of it with one hand while holding a brace in place with the other. Yuta had taken over after discovering what he was doing.

Frieren stepped around the tools and crouched to look at the joint.

"You could use magic." ᶠ

Mau glanced at her staff.

"To make a bench?" ᴹ

"To hold it while you fix it." ᶠ

Rimuru closed his eyes.

Yuta looked at the brace. Then at the tree. Then at the brace again.

"We could have asked," he said. ʸᵗ

"I walked past you twice," Frieren told them. ᶠ

Mau started laughing. Once he began, he found it difficult to stop. Rimuru tried to blame Yuta. Yuta said he had not been the one insisting that the angle was wrong. Frieren held the bench perfectly still while all three developed accounts of the morning which would never have survived being compared.

When it was upright and safe, she sat on it to test it. Her feet did not quite lie flat on the ground.

"It's a little high." ᶠ

Rimuru made a sound which made Yuta turn away.

Mau sat beside her and looked at the yard from the new height. There was the house, the well-worn path, the tools they would have to put away. On the far side of the tree, someone called for Maki.

Frieren stood again.

"Mana practice tomorrow," she told him. "You were going to show me whether you could hold the same amount twice." ᶠ

"After breakfast?" ᴹ

"After I wake up." ᶠ

"So after lunch," Rimuru said. ᴿ

She looked at him for a long moment, then walked toward the house without giving him the satisfaction of an answer.

Yuta collected the practice swords. Mau reached for the toolbox.

"Tomorrow too?" he asked. ᴹ

Yuta glanced back.

"After she wakes up?" ʸᵗ

"Before. I have a lot of time to lose another fight." ᴹ

"You could help with the chairs," Rimuru suggested. ᴿ

Mau looked at Yuta.

Yuta gave a small nod toward the field.

They left the tools neatly in the box before Rimuru could assign them anything else.
