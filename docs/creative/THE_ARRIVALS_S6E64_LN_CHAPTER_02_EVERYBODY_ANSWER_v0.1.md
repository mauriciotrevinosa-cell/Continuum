# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 35
## Chapter 4 — Everybody Answer

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S6E64
**Continuity state:** Branch S2, immediately after its confrontation with Sukuna. Mau has survived without the original impalement, death, merger or fatal Purple sequence. The group held him off together and he withdrew; the chapter shows the aftermath rather than rewriting an entire combat in summary. Mau's stake was the household. No prime-legion powers or millennia are imported. Yuta/Frieren/Rimuru are present, Maki and Maomao coordinate immediate care. Gray perception remains Mau's subjective experience. This is the first lived consequence of the changed S2 outcome, not a claim that the original events were unreal.

*When: After the shouting. The ground is wet, and somebody is asking Mau a question he has already answered.*

"How many fingers?" ᴹᵏ

"Two." ᴹ

"You said that before I held them up." ᴹᵏ

Mau looked harder.

Maki had three fingers raised in front of his face. He could see all three. He was fairly sure she had changed them while he was blinking.

"Three." ᴹ

"Better." ᴹᵏ

He wanted to get up. Maki had a hand on his shoulder and a way of leaving it there which made the argument feel unlikely to succeed. From where he sat, the house was partly hidden by people crossing between the field and the path.

There was still a roof on it.

He kept looking to make sure.

"Where's Yuta?" ᴹ

"There." ᴹᵏ

He turned too fast. The ground tilted and Maki tightened her grip.

Yuta was kneeling a few paces away with his sword laid beside him. Somebody had wrapped his forearm. The cloth was dark through the middle. He looked up when he heard his name and lifted his uninjured hand.

Mau breathed out.

"Rimuru?" ᴹ

"Still here." ᴿ

The answer came from behind him. Rimuru walked into view with dirt down one side of his coat and an expression which made Mau stop preparing his next question.

"I wasn't going to do it," Mau said. ᴹ

"Stand up?" ᴿ

"I was thinking about it." ᴹ

Rimuru crouched beside him. His hands were steady when he checked the torn sleeve, although he had taken a little longer than usual to make his face look ordinary.

Mau remembered a stretch of ground he had wanted to cross. The distance had seemed very short when the alternative was letting Sukuna get farther toward the others. He had started forward. Yuta had shouted. Somebody else had struck from an angle Mau could not see, and the space he meant to enter had vanished under force.

The next thing he remembered clearly was Maki pulling him out by the back of his jacket.

He had bruised ribs. An arm which stung from elbow to wrist. Mud in his mouth from landing on his face.

He could still swallow.

"He left?" ᴹ

"He's gone from here," Rimuru said. "We're watching. Don't turn that into a reason to get up." ᴿ

Mau looked down at his hands.

They were dirty and shaking. All the fingers moved when he asked them to.

"I can help carry—" ᴹ

"You can answer her questions," Maki said. ᴹᵏ

Maomao had arrived with a bag and a second person to fetch what she needed. She put the bag down in the dry place Rimuru cleared with his coat and told Mau to tell her where it hurt.

He started with his arm.

She looked at the careful way he was breathing and waited.

"And here," he admitted, touching his side. ᴹ

"Good. Continue being useful like that." ᴹᵐ

* * *

Frieren came from the edge of the field carrying her staff.

Mau tried to read her face while she was still too far away for it to tell him anything. She had been up there with Fern. He remembered bright force crossing the sky and a shout he could not place.

He could see Fern behind her now, walking under her own power. Stark was coming with her. Someone had put a hand over the back of his neck and steered him away from trying to retrieve something he had dropped.

Mau shut his eyes briefly.

When he opened them, Frieren was beside him.

"You're hurt." ᶠ

"A little." ᴹ

Maomao made a mark on the scrap of paper in her lap.

"He's still trying that answer," she said. ᴹᵐ

Frieren looked at the ripped sleeve and then at his face.

"Don't." ᶠ

It was said in the same voice she used when he tried to force more mana through an exercise than he could keep steady. He let his head rest against the bundle somebody had put behind him.

"Are you okay?" ᴹ

"Tired." ᶠ

"Fern?" ᴹ

"Tired and angry." ᶠ

"At him?" ᴹ

Frieren looked toward the place where Sukuna had been.

"Yes." ᶠ

He watched her while she watched the field.

Sukuna had been a person, in the way a person could stand on a path and speak. Mau found that harder to fit into his head than anything else. There were creatures in the woods, unknown systems in their bodies, things in this world which nobody had named. But that man had looked at them, understood that they lived here, and chosen to make it an interesting place to hurt.

Mau could still hear his voice.

"I thought he'd keep going," he said. ᴹ

Frieren turned back.

"Toward the house." ᴹ

"I know." ᶠ

"I couldn't think of anything else." ᴹ

She sat on the grass because Maomao had not left her a clean place on the coat.

"Next time, look for us too," she said. ᶠ

Mau stared at the broken earth beyond her shoulder. He had known they were there. He had heard them. Somehow in the moment when he decided to move, knowing had not kept him from behaving as though the distance belonged only to him.

"Okay." ᴹ

Frieren waited.

He looked at her properly.

"I mean it. I'll try." ᴹ

She gave a small nod. Then Fern reached them and discovered that Frieren had sat down in wet grass with a torn place in her own sleeve which she had not mentioned.

For a little while, Mau was relieved to be somebody else's second problem.

* * *

Getting him back indoors required an argument about whether he needed help and a second argument about what counted as help.

Yuta offered a shoulder. Mau looked at the bandage on his arm.

"Other side," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Rimuru took that side before either could attempt to arrange it.

"You both get to be annoying after she checks you." ᴿ

They moved slowly. Mau could walk once the first few steps persuaded his ribs that it was possible. He disliked how much of his weight Rimuru was carrying. He disliked the way the others kept looking over their shoulders more.

At the door, Bocchi was holding it open with both hands.

Her knuckles were pale. Mau could not tell whether she had been crying. She looked at the blood on the cloth around his arm and then up at his face.

"Hi," he said. ᴹ

Her mouth moved once.

"You should come inside," she said at last. ᴮ

He nodded, and she moved with the door so they could pass.

There were bowls on the table. Somebody had put a kettle on. The place smelled of hot water and smoke and food which had cooked too long while everybody was outside. It hit him with an unexpected force. He had been looking at the roof from the field, trying to make sure the shape was still there. He had not thought about coming back into a room where dinner had been interrupted.

Rimuru steered him toward a chair.

Mau sat. The shaking in his hands became worse as soon as nobody needed him to hold on.

Yuta lowered himself onto the next chair with a careful breath.

"Don't laugh," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"I wasn't going to." ᴹ

"I know. I'm telling myself." ʸᵗ

Mau looked at him. Yuta looked back. Between them, in the solemn effort to avoid hurting their ribs, something went wrong.

It was a terrible laugh. Small and breathless and painful. Rimuru put both palms on the table and told them he was absolutely leaving the next medical conversation to someone else.

Maki came in behind him.

"No, you're not." ᴹᵏ

The laugh broke. Mau put a hand over his face, still smiling a little, and realized that his eyes were wet.

Yuta did not make him take the hand away.

He moved the water nearer instead.
