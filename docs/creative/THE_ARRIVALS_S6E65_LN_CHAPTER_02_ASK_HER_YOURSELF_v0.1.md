# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 35
## Chapter 6 — Ask Her Yourself

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S6E65
**Continuity state:** Branch S3, after Mau's return and the stabilization of Noise's construction pressure. Raphael's separation during that work led to Ciel, named by Rimuru as in the original causal family; the branch does not import the original catastrophic injuries as an unseen event. Ciel is now linked to both hosts, privately. Consent and limits persist; no cured Noise, source identity diagnosis or S6 theft attribution. Frieren is caring for Ori, who is becoming attached to her as mother. Mau has his own bed and no romantic partnership with Frieren. Gray perception continues.

*When: A cup of tea has gone cold while Mau waits to hear whether the voice will come back.*

"Don't keep holding your breath," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Mau let it out.

The air made the surface of his tea tremble. He looked down at it, a little embarrassed.

"I didn't know I was." ᴹ

"I noticed." ᴿ

Rimuru sat on the other side of the small table. The room had been left quiet at his request, although quiet in the house meant that somebody outside the door was trying to move furniture without dragging it and failing by short intervals.

Mau listened to a scrape, two whispered instructions, and the unmistakable thump of the same piece of furniture being set down.

"Is that my bed?" ᴹ

"I don't know." ᴿ

"They said they were changing the mattress." ᴹ

"Then it might be." ᴿ

Mau thought about that until another scrape answered the question.

He could remember his bed. The one he had been using before he left, with the chair near enough that he could drop a shirt onto it without getting up. This morning he had remembered which stair complained under his left foot. Yesterday he had known where to look for clean cups without anyone telling him.

Some other memories remained slippery. When he reached for them too quickly, he could still produce an answer before he knew whether it was true.

Rimuru had asked him to say when that happened.

He had done it twice today.

"Can you hear me?" ᶜ

Mau straightened.

The voice had arrived without using the air between the chairs.

"Yes," he said aloud. ᴹ

Rimuru smiled. His eyes stayed on Mau's face, watching for the part the smile could not settle.

"Good. You don't have to answer me out loud." ᶜ

Mau tried to formulate a response without moving his mouth. The attempt made him frown.

*Like this?*

"Yes." ᶜ

He looked at Rimuru.

"Did you hear that?" ᴹ

"Which part?" ᴿ

"Me." ᴹ

Rimuru listened for a moment before answering.

"No. She can tell me what you ask her to tell me. It isn't all one conversation." ᴿ

Mau nodded slowly.

"What should I call you?" he asked the voice. ᴹ

There was a small pause.

"Ciel." ᶜ

He had been told the name. He remembered being told, and had wanted to ask her anyway. The last time he had heard Raphael through Rimuru's spoken replies, the answers had come with an exactness which made a question seem like an object being placed into a machine.

This voice waited for him.

"Okay. Ciel." ᴹ

"Yes." ᶜ

Something in the answer made him smile before he knew why.

Rimuru leaned forward and picked up his own cup.

"She's been working very hard," he said. ᴿ

"You have also been working," Ciel told him. ᶜ

Mau looked at Rimuru, who was suddenly interested in the cup.

"She tell you the same thing?" ᴿ

"Not yet." ᴹ

"Good. You're still new." ᴿ

* * *

They spent most of the hour learning the difference between asking and reaching.

Ciel asked Mau to describe the pressure when it changed. He told her what he could feel, and she told him which observations were useful without pretending to know what all of them meant. Rimuru repeated the practical parts for the paper on the table: stop when the sensation spread; speak before attempting to push it back alone; do not treat a quiet interval as permission to test the largest thing he could imagine.

The Noise was there throughout. It crowded some thoughts and receded from others. Now and then Mau felt the impulse to follow a shape to its end, to learn why it fitted and what he could make from it. The table leg. The cup. Rimuru's fingers around the handle.

"That one," he said once. ᴹ

Rimuru set down the cup.

Ciel asked him to keep his attention on the feeling instead of supplying it with more things to inspect. Mau closed his eyes. The movement inside him continued for a while, then settled far enough back that he could hear the voices outside the door again.

He opened his eyes, tired.

"Was that bad?" ᴹ

"That was something we could notice before you tried it," Ciel said. ᶜ

Rimuru waited for the reply and then made a mark on the page when Mau explained.

"We can use that," he said. ᴿ

Mau looked at the few lines they had managed in an hour.

"I wish there was more." ᴹ

Rimuru turned the paper so he could read it.

The handwriting was messy where they had corrected a sentence. There were crossed-out words, one question at the bottom, and room for things they did not know yet.

Mau read it again.

He had helped make every line.

* * *

Ori was sitting under the table in the common room when they came downstairs.

Frieren sat beside it with a book on her knee and a small pair of socks in her lap. One of Ori's feet stuck out beneath the bench. The other had disappeared somewhere among the chair legs.

"The first one was easy," Frieren said when Mau looked at the socks. ᶠ

"What happened?" ᴹ

"She discovered there was a second." ᶠ

From under the table, Ori objected.

"It twists." ᴼ

"It won't if you let me put it on." ᶠ

"It did last time." ᴼ

Frieren closed the book with one finger keeping her place. She leaned down far enough to look beneath the table, and Ori's foot withdrew.

Mau sat on the floor a little way from the bench. It was the sort of thing he could still do without someone telling him to be careful.

"Hello," he said to the small face between the chair legs. ᴹ

"You had a meeting." ᴼ

"I did." ᴹ

"Did you get in trouble?" ᴼ

He considered this.

"Not this time." ᴹ

Rimuru made a thoughtful sound above him and went to fetch fresh tea.

Ori crawled one chair nearer. Mau waited. She looked past him toward the place Rimuru had gone, then back at Frieren's socks.

"Is she going to do it again?" ᴼ

Mau glanced up.

Frieren held out the second sock.

"Eventually," she said. ᶠ

He took it. Ori watched the handoff as though he had accepted a weapon.

"Can I see your foot?" he asked. ᴹ

She shook her head.

"Then I'll put it here." ᴹ

He laid the sock on the floor between them. Frieren watched with patient skepticism.

Ori stared at it.

After a while, she pushed the sock toward him with one finger.

"You first." ᴼ

Mau picked it up. He looked at his own foot, then at the opening.

"This might be difficult." ᴹ

Ori laughed.

Frieren's expression softened. She kept the book closed while Mau attempted to put a child's sock over his hand and argued, with great seriousness, that this was the only part of him small enough.

Ori came out from under the table to supervise.

By the time Rimuru returned, she was sitting in Frieren's lap with both socks on, explaining why Mau had done it wrong.

"He only has one thumb," she said. ᴼ

"On that hand," Mau pointed out. ᴹ

"It wasn't enough." ᴼ

Frieren smoothed the heel of the troublesome sock. Ori leaned back against her and caught the edge of the book with her fingers.

"Read?" ᴼ

"This one's about a spell." ᶠ

"Read that." ᴼ

Frieren looked at the page she had been keeping and then turned to the beginning. She adjusted Ori on her knee and started with the introduction, simplifying the first sentence when the child's forehead wrinkled.

Mau stayed on the floor and listened.

Rimuru set his tea on the bench where he could reach it. Ciel asked privately whether his head hurt after the exercise. He answered that it was tired, and she told him to say if tired changed into something else.

At the table, Frieren demonstrated the first motion with a bare finger. Ori copied it with her whole hand. Nothing happened, but Frieren nodded with such quiet approval that the child tried again.

Mau drank his tea while it was warm.
