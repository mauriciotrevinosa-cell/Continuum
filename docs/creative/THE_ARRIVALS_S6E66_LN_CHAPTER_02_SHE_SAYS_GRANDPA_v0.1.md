*When: The coin brought her in the morning. Mau reaches the intake hall after somebody has already begun shouting.*

"You look like children!" ʸᵏ

Mau stopped at the threshold.

The girl stood between Maki and Yuta with one hand around the ring hanging at her chest. She looked from one grandparent to the other as though each face required a different argument.

Maki was laughing.

Mau had heard her laugh before, but never like this. It broke in the middle. She put her hand against her mouth, took it away, and reached for the girl instead.

"Granny," the girl said, suddenly much quieter. ʸᵏ

Yuta turned his head for a moment. When he looked back his expression was so open that Mau stepped out of the doorway, giving them the little privacy the crowded hall could offer.

He could hear the girl saying that they had been gone. That she had been strong. Her voice caught on the effort to say it properly.

Mau found himself looking at the register girl's table.

There was a sheet waiting for a name. A cup of water stood beside it. Somebody had brought bread wrapped in a cloth, and the cloth had slipped enough to show the edge of the loaf.

All of it was ordinary.

He kept looking because something had changed and he could not make his mind settle on what.

"Mau?" ᴿ

Rimuru had come up beside him.

"You okay?" ᴿ

"Yes." ᴹ

Rimuru waited a fraction longer.

Mau looked back into the hall.

The girl had turned her head on Maki's shoulder. Her hair, coming loose from its tie, lay dark against the pale coat. There was warmth in her cheek. A small raw patch near the base of a finger where the cord had rubbed. The flush around her eyes had a quality he could see with a clarity which made the rest of the room seem farther away.

He blinked.

It remained.

The ring caught the light when her hand moved. He could follow the difference between the metal and the skin around it. Her mouth moved against Maki's shoulder, forming another embarrassed objection he could not hear.

Then Yuta put an arm around both of them and she stopped trying to make words.

Mau looked down at his own hand.

The room was the room again.

"Ciel," he said privately, trying to keep the question from becoming alarm. ᴹ

"What is it?" ᶜ

He looked up.

"Nothing hurts." ᴹ

"That was not my question." ᶜ

He knew it had not been. He could not explain why it was the first answer he had wanted to give.

"Can we talk later?" ᴹ

"Yes. Tell me if that changes." ᶜ

Rimuru was still beside him. Mau gave him a small nod, and they went to help carry the things which needed carrying.

* * *

Yuka ate late, after the first rush of introductions had exhausted everyone involved.

Mau knew her name by then. Yuta had said it several times, each with a little hesitation before it, as though he wanted to keep discovering that it was a name he could use in the room.

Maki sat close enough to reach her. Mai sat on the other side with a look of suspicion directed at the whole concept of being an aunt by way of a life she had not lived.

Yuka looked at her over a spoon.

"You're really her sister?" ʸᵏ

"Unfortunately." ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki did not even turn.

"She means yes." ᴹᵏ

"I said what I meant." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka smiled into her bowl. Mau watched the smile reach her face and had to look away before it became obvious that he was watching.

He put the water jug near Yuta.

"Do you need anything else?" ᴹ

Yuta looked up. He seemed briefly surprised that other practical needs still existed.

"Another spoon, actually. I dropped mine." ʸᵗ

Mau looked under the table, found it near Yuta's shoe, and went to get a clean one.

When he came back, Yuka was staring at him.

"What?" he asked. ᴹ

"You're Mau." ʸᵏ

"Usually." ᴹ

Yuta made a quiet, warning noise. It was intended for Mau, but Yuka looked at her grandfather instead.

"You talked about him." ʸᵏ

Yuta blinked.

"Here," Maki supplied. ᴹᵏ

Yuka looked down, caught in the difficulty of which grandfather knew what.

"Right. Here. You just did." ʸᵏ

The pause hurt enough that Mau put the clean spoon in Yuta's hand rather than on the table.

"Only good things, I hope." ᴹ

Yuka looked up again.

"He said you helped make the bench." ʸᵏ

"That's very generous. I helped drop it first." ᴹ

Yuta started smiling.

"It's a good bench," he said. ʸᵗ

"Now." ᴹ

Yuka laughed. The sound surprised her. She pressed the back of her hand against her mouth, then gave up hiding it.

Mau sat at the far end of the bench when Maki moved her coat to make room. There was enough distance for Yuta between them and enough conversation that nobody needed to perform being all right.

Frieren came through with Ori a little later. The child was carrying a small wooden cup and refusing assistance on the grounds that she knew which part was the handle.

Yuka watched her climb onto the seat beside Frieren.

"Your daughter?" she asked. ʸᵏ

Ori turned to look at her.

Frieren put a steadying hand behind the child's back.

"This is Ori," she said. ᶠ

"Hi, Ori." ʸᵏ

Ori studied her hair, the ring, the coat, and finally her face.

"You're new." ᴼ

"Very." ʸᵏ

"Did you bring clothes?" ᴼ

Yuka glanced down at herself.

"These." ʸᵏ

Ori considered this a serious problem and turned toward Frieren to discuss cupboards. Mai immediately offered to help, with an enthusiasm which made Maki look at her before nodding.

The conversation moved on around the plates.

Mau listened to Yuka explaining the fit of her coat, then correcting herself when she called Yuta Grandpa for the fourth time and realized he was still catching on the word.

He did not tell her he could see the warmth in her face when she smiled.

He was still not sure what there was to tell.

* * *

He found Yuta outside after the meal.

The bench they had built was empty except for him. A pair of shoes stood beside it where somebody had left them to dry. Yuta had his hands folded loosely between his knees.

Mau sat at the other end.

For a while they watched the yard.

"She says it the same," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Mau turned toward him.

"Grandpa. When she's annoyed with me." ʸᵗ

Yuta looked at his hands. They were young hands holding a history which had become visible in somebody else's face.

"I thought I'd be ready for that part." ʸᵗ

Mau let his shoulder rest against the back of the bench.

"You don't have to do it all today." ᴹ

Yuta laughed once, quietly.

"I've told you that." ʸᵗ

"It was a good idea. I kept it." ᴹ

They sat until Maki came to the door and said Yuka wanted to see where they slept, because she did not believe there was a world in which her grandparents were allowed to have stairs.

Yuta got up so quickly that Mau took the coat he nearly left behind.

From the doorway, Yuka called for him.

Mau saw her over Yuta's shoulder, and the whole gray yard seemed to fall back around that one bright, impatient face.
