## Chapter 9 — The First Watch

*When: The fourth night inside. Frieren has assigned the watch, and Yuka is trying to make less noise with the tin cup.*

The spoon struck the rim again.

Yuka looked over her shoulder.

Milim slept on her back near the rear wall, one hand thrown above her head. Stark had rolled toward the heat and left one foot outside his blanket. Fern lay between him and Frieren with the neatness of someone who had arranged a sleeping place after everybody else's opinions became too much work.

Nobody moved.

Mau held out his hand for the spoon.

"I can take it." ᴹ

Yuka gave it to him, offended by the object.

"It's louder than it looks." ʸᵏ

"Most things are when you don't want them to be." ᴹ

She lifted the cup in both hands. Steam passed over her face and disappeared in the cold at the mouth of the shelter.

The camp had become familiar by degrees. A flat stone for putting hot things down. A place where they had stopped leaving boots because snow slid from the roof there. The side of the opening from which you could see the approach without putting yourself against the sky.

Frieren had chosen it all, and had made them test what they thought they understood.

On the first night, Mau had set his bag in a place she said would get wet. On the second, he had been pleased with himself for remembering until she pointed out that the wind had changed.

Now the bag stood near his knee, beneath a fold of waterproof cloth.

"Is your hand better?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

He glanced down.

"Yes." ᴹ

"That was quick." ʸᵏ

"I meant the answer." ᴹ

She set the cup on the stone and held out her palm.

Mau gave her his hand because refusing would make the conversation longer. The knuckles were sore where he had struck the thing at the pond. Ciel had helped him keep the attempt small enough to finish, but finishing had still put his hand against something much harder than his hand.

Yuka turned it carefully by the wrist.

"You should tell Fern before she asks." ʸᵏ

"I told her." ᴹ

"Everything?" ʸᵏ

"Enough that she made the same face you're making." ᴹ

Yuka tried to remove the face. He watched the effort and smiled.

"It's a good face," he assured her. ᴹ

"I'm telling Grandpa you said that." ʸᵏ

"He's made it too. I have evidence." ᴹ

She let his hand go and tucked her own fingers around the cup again.

They listened to the dark.

Something moved far beyond the shelter. A scrape, then loose snow. Mau watched the place until the sound became part of the wind again. Ciel asked whether he had noticed it; he answered privately that he had, and kept his attention outward without reaching for the restless possibilities inside him.

Yuka saw his focus shift.

"Something?" ʸᵏ

"Not coming closer. I'll tell you if that changes." ᴹ

She nodded and adjusted where she was sitting so that she had room to stand.

It was a small movement, practiced. He liked that she did not ask him to be certain of things he could only keep watching.

"Oppa," she said after a while. ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

"Did you ever get used to all this?" ʸᵏ

He considered asking which part. The cold, the creatures, the house beyond the door they could not open. But she was looking at the edge of her cup, and he thought the question might be easier to ask without having to defend its shape.

"Some of it," he said. ᴹ

"Like what?" ʸᵏ

"People asking whether I've eaten. The stairs. Knowing whose footsteps are outside." ᴹ

"You know mine?" ʸᵏ

"You stop outside doors to decide if you're going to knock." ᴹ

Yuka looked up sharply.

"You do," he said. ᴹ

"Maybe I'm just being polite." ʸᵏ

"I didn't say it was bad." ᴹ

She looked back toward the sleeping group. The fire had settled into the shallow pit Stark had dug. Light climbed the side of her cheek whenever a piece shifted.

Mau watched a moment longer than he meant to. There was a warmth there which seemed to belong to her rather than the fire.

He picked up the spoon and wrapped it in a cloth so it would not hit anything else.

"What about you?" he asked. ᴹ

Yuka rested her chin just above the cup.

"Granny being able to carry everything. I keep forgetting. I offer to take something and she looks at me like I've insulted her ancestors." ʸᵏ

"You are one of the consequences of her ancestors. She should be more understanding." ᴹ

Yuka made a sound and immediately pressed her lips together.

At the back of the shelter, Milim shifted.

They waited in guilty silence until she settled.

Then Yuka leaned forward and whispered with great seriousness.

"You tell her that." ʸᵏ

Mau shook his head.

"I want to get home." ᴹ

* * *

On the seventh day, Frieren made everybody pack before they went to look at the dragon's remains.

Stark had to be reminded twice because he wanted to inspect the mark his axe had left. Milim had to be reminded once, much more firmly, because she wanted to see whether the teeth could be kept.

Mau checked the straps on his bag while Fern counted the food left.

There had been enough. He felt disproportionately glad of that.

His part in the fight had ended early: one short opening, a force he could hold for less time than he wanted, Ciel telling him to close it, and then Stark moving past him to use what it had made possible. He had spent the rest watching where the dragon turned, shouting when he saw its weight change, and getting himself out of the way when Frieren told him to.

He could remember being frightened. He could also remember doing the things he had agreed to do before they went in.

That felt worth keeping.

Yuka came around the dead creature with a little collection of useful things and a much longer complaint about how far Milim had scattered the rest.

"Did you find the book?" Mau asked. ᴹ

"She found the book." ʸᵏ

Frieren stood farther up the slope with a grimoire open in her hands. Fern was beside her, pointing out that weather still existed even after one had defeated the boss.

Mau went to take the book bag before that conversation became a lecture aimed at both of them.

They left with crystals, materials, a careful record of what they had used, and more dirty clothes than anyone had expected to own.

The red surface at the exit opened when they approached it.

Mau had meant to be dignified. He had imagined walking out, putting down the bags, and saying they were fine before anyone could panic.

Rimuru was standing on the other side.

Mau dropped the first bag at his feet and hugged him.

Rimuru made a startled sound. Then both his hands closed on Mau's back.

"Where were you?" ᴿ

"In there." ᴹ

"I know that part." ᴿ

Mau started to laugh, then stopped when he realized how hard Rimuru was holding him.

Behind them, Fern was explaining that a week had passed. Outside, barely an hour. Questions broke over the group before anyone had finished taking off a coat.

Yuka went straight into Maki's arms with a sound she would later deny making.

Mau saw it over Rimuru's shoulder. The pale coat was dirty now. Her hair had been tied back badly with a piece of cord after the proper tie disappeared on the fifth day. Her face had windburn along one cheek.

She was smiling into the embrace.

Mau closed his eyes for a moment and let his friend hold on.
