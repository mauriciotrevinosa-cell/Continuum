# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 35
## Chapter 10 — Two Names in One Hand

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S6E67
**Continuity state:** Branch later S5, after the first Red Gate but without Jin-Woo exchange, tower, Architect or Monarch legion. Mau gives his own branch Trial/Stay coin to Kaneki, who has one and needs two for Touka/Ichika. Kaneki decides whether to petition; willing arrival is required. This coin's finite Trial provenance differs from the original extra long-run gift. Eren knows Touka's name and accompanies Kaneki later; no universal disclosure of private family history. Ghoul coffee restriction respected; no food/child diet approval invented. Yuka remains a household friend; no romantic/sexual scene. Ori is bonded to Frieren as mother. Mau perceives the surrounding world gray. This chapter secures the creator-preserved family arrival cause in the gentler branch.

*When: The kilns have gone quiet. Kaneki has poured a second cup which nobody is drinking.*

Mau had come to return a bowl.

He found Kaneki outside the kilns with the coffee pot beside his foot and a book open on his knee. The page had not turned by the time Mau crossed the last part of the yard.

"Am I interrupting?" ᴹ

Kaneki looked up.

"No." ᴷⁿ

Mau held out the bowl. It had contained something which needed to go to Maomao, and she had washed it before sending it back because she knew exactly where Kaneki would otherwise put a dirty bowl while concentrating on somebody else's problem.

He took it with the air of a person receiving evidence against himself.

"She said you can stop keeping these in your room." ᴹ

Kaneki looked down at it.

"It's useful." ᴷⁿ

"She said you'd say that." ᴹ

He laughed softly, and set it beside the coffee.

Mau sat when Kaneki moved the book. They listened to the cooling sounds from the kilns. Inside, somebody was putting away the last tools. A door shut, footsteps went toward the house, and the evening settled around the two cups.

"Who's the other one for?" Mau asked. ᴹ

Kaneki looked at the empty place.

"Someone I know." ᴷⁿ

The answer closed gently enough that Mau left it alone.

He sat with his hands between his knees. The work that afternoon had put grit into the cuffs of his sleeves. He picked a grain out, then stopped because it seemed a poor thing to do next to somebody's book.

Kaneki noticed.

"It's all right. It gets worse than that." ᴷⁿ

"Books?" ᴹ

"This one. I've been carrying it everywhere." ᴷⁿ

He ran a thumb along the softened edge. There was affection in the gesture, and wear, and the ease of an object whose continued presence no longer required checking.

Mau looked toward the second cup again.

"You don't have to tell me," he said. ᴹ

Kaneki watched the yard for a while.

"My wife," he said at last. ᴷⁿ

Mau stayed still.

"Touka." ᴷⁿ

The name was said quietly. Mau repeated it once to make sure he had heard, and Kaneki nodded.

"You have a coin." ᴹ

"Yes." ᴷⁿ

Mau waited for the rest.

Kaneki rested his hand on the closed book.

"We have a daughter." ᴷⁿ

The two cups changed shape in Mau's understanding without moving at all.

He looked down at the empty one.

"You need two." ᴹ

"I don't know what they'd want. It's been—" ᴷⁿ

Kaneki stopped. Whatever length of time he had intended to name, it could not tell them what was happening in a world they could not see.

Mau felt the coin through the inside of his coat.

He had been keeping it there for months. Sometimes he took it out and turned it over while wondering what name he would say. There were gaps in his own past he still did not know how to cross. People could tell him to take his time, but time did not supply a person at the other side of a request.

He had also thought about giving it away.

Now there was somebody sitting beside him with two names and one hand.

"You can have mine," he said. ᴹ

Kaneki looked at him immediately.

"No." ᴷⁿ

Mau took it out anyway, then stopped before putting it in Kaneki's lap. He held it in his own palm where it was visible.

"I'm offering. You can think about it." ᴹ

"It's yours." ᴷⁿ

"Yes." ᴹ

"You might need it." ᴷⁿ

"So might you." ᴹ

Kaneki's mouth tightened. He looked toward the path Eren used when he came back from the outer walk, then away again.

Mau turned the coin once. It was warm from his coat.

"You don't have to ask them tonight. Or at all. But if the reason is that you'd have to choose one, then—" ᴹ

He held it a little nearer.

Kaneki looked at it for a long time.

"Why?" ᴷⁿ

Mau considered the answer. He could have made it a complicated thing about the people who had helped him, or the work they had done, or what it meant to have room in a house. All of those things existed. None was the first reason he had offered.

"Because you have two people you want to ask." ᴹ

Kaneki took a breath and did not seem able to finish it.

Mau kept his hand steady.

* * *

He told Fern before dinner.

She wrote it down because the coin had changed hands and, if somebody eventually came, a name would have to connect to an actual request.

She did not ask him to justify giving it away. She asked whether Kaneki understood that having the coin was not a deadline.

"I told him." ᴹ

"And you?" ᶠᵉ

"I know." ᴹ

She looked at him over the page.

"I mean that you don't need to wait for it to make you feel different before you decide whether it was something you wanted to do." ᶠᵉ

Mau rubbed the empty place through his coat.

"It does feel different." ᴹ

Fern nodded, accepting that.

"Then take a minute." ᶠᵉ

He did.

In the kitchen doorway, Rem was showing Ram where something had been moved. Momo was asking whether anybody had seen the large spoon. Ori sat on a chair beside Frieren with a piece of string around a small compass, trying to make a knot which would not open when she pulled it.

Mau watched Frieren take the string, loosen the part Ori had tightened in the wrong direction, and return it without finishing the job for her.

"Through there," Frieren said. ᶠ

Ori pushed the end through with a triumphant effort.

The compass lay against her chest when she lifted it. A small, cracked silver object, its needle attending to Frieren even as Ori turned to show Fern what she had done.

Mau smiled at the pride on her face.

He had no reason to think the compass had ever belonged anywhere else.

* * *

Kaneki found him by the back door after the meal.

He had the coin in his hand, and Mau's first thought was that he was bringing it back. He made himself wait instead of trying to refuse the refusal before Kaneki could speak.

"Her name is Ichika," Kaneki said. ᴷⁿ

Mau looked up.

"My daughter." ᴷⁿ

"Ichika," Mau repeated. ᴹ

Kaneki nodded.

He looked down at the coin.

"I'm going to ask them. Eren said he'd come with me." ᴷⁿ

Mau glanced toward the kilns. It was nearly dark now. There would be somewhere to put the cups and the voices, a small space in which somebody could ask for a life without having the whole house watching his face.

"Do you need anything?" ᴹ

Kaneki shook his head. Then he reconsidered.

"If they say yes. Tomorrow. Could you make sure there's room at the table?" ᴷⁿ

Mau thought of the long table, the chairs shifted every time somebody wanted to sit beside someone new, the way a place could be made before a person believed it was theirs.

"Yes," he said. ᴹ

Kaneki closed his hand around the coin.

Mau watched him go as far as the first turn in the path. Eren was waiting there, keeping his hands in his pockets. He did not wave them over or call out a question. He simply fell into step when Kaneki reached him.

After they disappeared behind the building, Mau went back inside.

He moved a chair.
