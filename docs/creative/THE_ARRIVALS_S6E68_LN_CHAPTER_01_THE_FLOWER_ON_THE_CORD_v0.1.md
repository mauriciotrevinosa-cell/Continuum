The cake arrived before the eggs.

Yuka looked at Momo. Momo looked at the cake with the satisfaction of a woman whose argument had survived every objection.

"You said breakfast," she reminded her. ᴹᵒ

"I know. I meant—" ʸᵏ

"Birthday girl gets what she asked for." ᴹᵒ

Umaru leaned across the table.

"I support this rule." ᵁ

"You've already had one this year," Taihei said. ᵀ

"Next year exists." ᵁ

Mau took the plates being passed along the bench and put one in front of Yuka. She had been red around the ears since the first person wished her a happy birthday, and the house had made a point of giving everybody an individual opportunity.

Yuta had managed it three times.

Maki had told him to leave the girl alone, then adjusted the collar of Yuka's shirt without asking.

Mai's parcel lay beside the plate. There were useful things in it, and one carefully chosen thing which had made Yuka go quiet before she reached across to hug her. Mau had looked down at his own plate for that part. It seemed like something they should be allowed to have without an audience.

Now Yuka was looking at the portion of cake Momo had put in front of her.

"You're all going to make me eat this before I can go anywhere." ʸᵏ

"No," Maki said. "I'm going to make you eat breakfast too." ᴹᵏ

Yuka pointed at the eggs when they finally arrived.

"I see what you're doing." ʸᵏ

"Good. Save us the speech." ᴹᵏ

Mau laughed into his tea. Yuka turned on him immediately.

"You could help." ʸᵏ

He put a piece of bread on her plate.

"I'm helping." ᴹ

Across from them, Ori was supervising a parcel of her own. Frieren had helped wrap it with a length of string, and Ori appeared to suspect that accepting help had reduced her authority over the finished knot.

She passed it across with both hands.

"You have to untie it. Don't rip it." ᴼ

Yuka set down her fork and obeyed.

Inside was a bookmark with a pressed leaf fixed between two carefully cut pieces of paper. Ori had drawn a line down the edge and a face at the top which looked startled to have been included.

"That's you," Ori explained. ᴼ

Yuka looked at the face.

"I can tell." ʸᵏ

Frieren's mouth moved at one corner.

Ori accepted the answer and immediately began telling her mother where she had found the leaf, although Frieren had been there for the entire expedition.

Mau watched Yuka smooth the bookmark with a fingertip. He had a small parcel in his coat, downstairs on the hook, and suddenly wondered whether he had chosen badly. It had seemed a simple object when he bought it. At the table, among things people had made with their own hands, simplicity felt harder to defend.

Yuka looked up and caught him watching.

"What?" ʸᵏ

"Nothing." ᴹ

She narrowed her eyes.

He gave her the jam and hoped that would count as an answer.

* * *

They left after the washing-up had been disputed and reassigned twice.

Maki knew the path. Yuta knew when they intended to return. Fern had the two names written beside the outing, and Mau had checked the weather before saying they could eat by the lake. None of this stopped Yuta from coming to the door with an extra scarf.

Yuka stared at it.

"Grandpa." ʸᵏ

"It's colder by the water." ʸᵗ

"I'm wearing one." ʸᵏ

"Then give it to him." ʸᵗ

Mau took it. Yuka looked betrayed.

He put it around his neck while Yuta was watching, because he knew that would let the man go back inside more easily.

They carried the food between them. There were other people near the path at first, finishing jobs before midday. Yuka stopped to answer each birthday wish, growing more elaborate in her complaints when anyone repeated it. By the time they reached the trees, Mau could predict the turn she would make toward him whenever she thought he was enjoying it too much.

He enjoyed it anyway.

At the lake, they found a dry place out of the wind. Mau spread the blanket while Yuka took out the containers and discovered that someone had packed another piece of cake.

She held it up.

"This is a coordinated attack." ʸᵏ

"We can defeat it together." ᴹ

"You just want half." ʸᵏ

"I'm willing to make sacrifices." ᴹ

She handed him a fork, smiling.

For a while they ate and watched the water. He knew this lake in the practical way one knew a place reached on several ordinary walks. The roots by the bank were slippery. There was a patch which stayed sheltered until the wind changed. If they left the food behind them unattended, something small and bold would attempt to take advantage.

Yuka kept looking over her shoulder to check the bags.

"What?" he asked on the third time. ᴹ

"I don't trust that bush." ʸᵏ

He looked. A leaf moved.

"A sensible position." ᴹ

She laughed and leaned back on her hands. Sunlight reached the edge of her cheek. The warmth of it was visible to him, the slight color where the cold had touched her nose, the small distinctions of skin and hair which his eyes seemed willing to find only around her.

He had tried describing it to Ciel. She had listened, asked when it happened and whether he had pain, and accepted that they did not yet have an answer. It had not worsened. He could see the path, judge distance, do everything he ordinarily did.

He had stopped checking every few minutes.

Now Yuka was watching the water with her eyes half closed, and he had forgotten to check at all.

"Did you have plans?" he asked. ᴹ

"For today?" ʸᵏ

"Before. Back there. For turning eighteen." ᴹ

She drew her knees up a little.

"I thought I'd know more." ʸᵏ

Mau nodded.

"You?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"I don't remember what I expected at eighteen." ᴹ

Her face changed.

"Sorry." ʸᵏ

"It's okay. I can tell you what I expected this morning." ᴹ

"What?" ʸᵏ

"That I'd pack the food badly and you'd notice before we got here." ᴹ

She looked at the containers.

"You did. Rem fixed it." ʸᵏ

"Then I'm doing very well at predicting the future." ᴹ

Yuka laughed, but when she looked back at the water her expression was quieter.

"I like being here," she said. ʸᵏ

"At the lake?" ᴹ

"Here. All of it." ʸᵏ

He waited.

"I miss things. And people. I don't want that to sound like I don't." ʸᵏ

"It doesn't." ᴹ

She looked at him to make sure he meant it.

Then she leaned her shoulder lightly against his.

They stayed that way until the wind changed and both reached for the same container lid.

* * *

He gave her the parcel after they packed.

Yuka looked at it with the sudden, unguarded delight of being given something after she thought the giving was finished.

"You got me something?" ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"You didn't have to." ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

She sat down again to unwrap it properly.

The bracelet lay against the paper, a dark braided cord with a pale-metal flower at its center. Two little lotus shapes faced outward from the same small joining, mirrored closely enough that she turned it over before understanding how it was made.

Nothing lit. No window opened.

She ran her thumb along one petal.

"It's pretty." ʸᵏ

His shoulders loosened.

"Hollis had it. I thought the cord would be less annoying than something hard if you were training." ᴹ

Yuka looked from it to him.

"You thought about that?" ʸᵏ

"A little." ᴹ

She held it toward him.

"Will you?" ʸᵏ

He sat beside her and took the two ends. She held her wrist steady while he worked out the knot. It was easier than Ori's string and harder than he wanted it to be with Yuka watching his fingers.

"Too tight?" ᴹ

She moved her hand, testing it.

"No." ʸᵏ

He let go.

She kept looking at the flower. Then, without putting the wrist down, she leaned sideways and hugged him.

Mau caught her carefully. He had expected a quick squeeze, the sort of thanks one could give without changing the rest of the afternoon. She stayed. Her forehead rested against the side of his neck, warm above Yuta's extra scarf.

He put an arm around her back.

"Happy birthday," he said. ᴹ

"Thank you, Oppa." ʸᵏ

They began the walk home a little later than they intended. Yuka kept lifting her wrist as they went, checking that the flower lay the way she wanted it. Twice she reached for a bag he was already carrying, then appeared to forget why she had reached.

At the edge of the trees she took his hand instead.

Neither spoke about it.

When they came within sight of the yard, she let go to wave at someone calling from the house.

Mau flexed his fingers once, feeling the place her hand had been, and followed her inside.
