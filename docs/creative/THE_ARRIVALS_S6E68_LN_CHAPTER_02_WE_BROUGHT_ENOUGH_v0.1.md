# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 35
## Chapter 12 — We Brought Enough

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S6E68
**Continuity state:** Branch Day447, entry to the smaller second Red Gate. Yuka is eighteen. Planned short paired resource/scouting outing is logged with Fern/Yuta/Maki; both carry reserve food and equipment. The entrance changes from blue to red behind them, unexpected; Ciel remains privately reachable here, unlike the later endless-army gate. Only one major boss is established, not the original desert legion/guardian sequence. Mau has no Monarch System, army, inventory or Kaisel. His bounded learned CE/space assistance has costs and cannot open an exit. Exterior time is not yet known. Bracelet remains on Yuka's wrist.

*When: Day447 outside. Yuka stops halfway through telling him that he has packed the rope wrong.*

The last length of blue closed behind Mau's shoulder.

He turned because Yuka had stopped speaking.

The surface filled the doorway from side to side, thick and dark in the broken stone frame. He knew its color from the word Yuka said, very softly, before he had finished reaching for it.

"Red." ʸᵏ

His fingers met resistance.

He left them there long enough to be sure, then took his hand away.

"Don't push it," Ciel said. ᶜ

"I wasn't going to." ᴹ

He had been going to. He looked toward Yuka before the thought could become an argument with himself.

She stood beside the stairs leading down from the entrance, her hand on the strap at her shoulder. Her other hand had gone to the ring on its cord. The bracelet lay against her cuff, the little flower caught sideways beneath the fabric.

"Same as before?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"The door, yes. I don't know about the rest." ᴹ

She nodded once.

They had talked about what to do if an outing stopped being the outing they had planned. It had seemed a useful, dull conversation at the table. Fern had made them keep the extra food when both tried to explain that they expected to be home by the next meal.

Mau set his bag down.

"Count it now?" ᴹ

Yuka looked toward the stairs, then back at the bag.

"Somewhere we can see the door and the approach." ʸᵏ

He picked it up again.

The landing below the entrance had a low wall on one side and enough open space that nothing could stand near them without coming into view. They checked the narrow recess behind it, the broken railing, and the stairs beneath before putting down their things.

Only then did Mau ask Ciel the question he had been keeping behind the practical ones.

"Can Rimuru hear from you?" ᴹ

"I can reach him. He knows the entrance has changed. He cannot use that to open it." ᶜ

Mau shut his eyes briefly.

"Tell him we're together. We haven't gone farther in. We're checking supplies." ᴹ

"I will." ᶜ

Yuka had watched his face during the pause.

He repeated the information aloud. Her shoulders lowered a fraction when he said that somebody outside knew what had happened.

"Okay," she said. "Food first." ʸᵏ

* * *

There was enough for several days if they were sensible.

They counted it twice, not because either doubted the arithmetic but because doing it gave their hands something useful to finish. Water was the more immediate concern. They had what they carried and no reason yet to trust the channel running beneath the stairs.

Mau took out the empty container they had brought for gathering. Yuka put it beside the full ones instead of allowing him to fill it immediately.

"Check it first," she said. ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

"You were looking at it." ʸᵏ

"I can look at water without drinking it." ᴹ

She gave him Maki's expression.

He put the lid back on the empty container.

They found their first shelter before the light changed. The place had been a gatehouse, or something enough like one that the name was useful. A narrow room above the channel, stone steps down to a yard, one doorway they could watch. There were holes where shutters ought to have been. Yuka helped him cover the worst one with the cloth from his bag.

Beyond the yard lay more broken walls, a higher building with a collapsed corner, and a road half hidden beneath coarse grass. Something moved once at the far end. They waited, watching.

It did not come toward them.

They decided to stay near the shelter until they understood the ground.

The first meal was cold. Mau had expected to make a joke about it, but the taste of the bread put him suddenly back at the table where Rem had packed it, and he had to take a moment before speaking.

Yuka noticed.

"Do you want the other half?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He looked at the bread in her hand.

"No. Eat yours." ᴹ

"I didn't mean I'm giving mine up. I meant yours is in the cloth." ʸᵏ

He laughed, grateful that she had made him do it.

She handed him the second piece.

* * *

They learned the first creature by listening to it.

It scraped when it turned. Three short contacts, a pause, then a heavy drag. They had gone only as far as the nearest wall, leaving their larger bags in the shelter with the food tied clear of the floor. When they heard the noise, both stopped.

Yuka pointed toward the opening ahead.

Mau nodded and took a step to his left, where he could see around the angle without putting his whole body through it.

The creature had too many hard surfaces for an animal. Its front half was low, its back hunched beneath plates which met like overlapping pieces of a broken roof. One foreleg ended in a blunt, splintered point. Each time it moved, that leg caught on the stone.

It looked slow until it heard them.

The first rush took it across the open ground so quickly that Mau had to throw himself sideways. The plate at its shoulder struck the wall where he had been. Yuka moved behind it before it could turn, blade cutting at the softer place between two segments.

The sound changed.

Mau saw the injured foreleg catch when it tried to pivot. He called to her. She stepped clear of the point and used the opening to strike again.

Then it lay still, and they stood on opposite sides of it trying to hear whether there were more.

Mau could feel every place his own body had nearly been.

Yuka swallowed, then looked at him.

"Your shoulder?" ʸᵏ

He moved it carefully.

"Caught the wall. Yours?" ᴹ

"Fine." ʸᵏ

He waited.

She made an irritated face and checked her arm properly.

"Fine," she repeated. ʸᵏ

He nodded, accepting it this time.

They returned to the shelter before investigating anything else.

* * *

That night he heard her wake.

They had put their bedrolls on opposite sides of the room with a clear path to the door. Mau had the first watch. Yuka had insisted on the second before he could offer to stay up longer.

She pushed herself onto an elbow and looked toward the covered window.

"What time?" ʸᵏ

"Not yours yet." ᴹ

She lay back down, then sat up instead.

"I thought the room was moving." ʸᵏ

Mau looked at the cloth over the hole. Wind pressed it inward by small degrees.

"The cover's loose on that side. I'll fix it before I sleep." ᴹ

Yuka watched it for a while.

"I know it's not the same place as last time," she said. ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"I just keep expecting to hear the others." ʸᵏ

He turned a little so he could see both her and the door.

"Me too." ᴹ

She rubbed her face with both hands, embarrassed by having admitted it.

Mau took the extra blanket off the top of the bag and passed it to her. She looked at it as though she intended to refuse, then put it over her knees.

"We brought enough," he said. ᴹ

Yuka looked up.

"For tonight. Tomorrow we check what we know before we decide the rest." ᴹ

She pulled the blanket closer.

"And you sleep when it's my turn." ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Not one more thing first." ʸᵏ

He glanced toward the loose cover.

Yuka followed his eyes and sighed.

"That one thing," she conceded. ʸᵏ

He smiled.

After a while she lay down again, still facing the door. He listened until her breathing settled and then returned his attention to the yard.

There were creatures somewhere among the walls. There was a way out they had not found yet. Outside, somebody knew that both of them were in here and would be waiting when they reached it.

He let those facts stay separate.

When Yuka woke for her watch, he told her exactly what he had heard, drank a little water, and lay down where they had agreed he would.
