*When: The next day inside. Yuka has drawn the courtyard so badly that Mau is afraid to ask which shape is meant to be him.*

"That's the fountain," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at the lopsided circle.

"Good." ᴹ

"What did you think it was?" ʸᵏ

"The fountain." ᴹ

She put the piece of charcoal down and looked at him.

He pointed at the smaller mark near it.

"Then that's the broken stair?" ᴹ

"That's you." ʸᵏ

He nodded with the solemnity the discovery deserved.

"I was going to say it's very accurate." ᴹ

Yuka threw a scrap of cloth at him.

It landed against his chest, soft enough that he caught it without moving from the floor. The ordinariness of the gesture settled something between them. They had eaten, found a safe way to collect and treat water, and slept enough in turns that both could think without every choice feeling like the only choice they would get.

Now they had a map. A poor map, but one they could improve.

Mau drew a line along the edge of the courtyard and marked the place where the ground had given slightly beneath his foot.

"We shouldn't run across that." ᴹ

Yuka bent closer.

"Can we get around the other side?" ʸᵏ

"If this passage comes out where we think it does." ᴹ

She tapped the next mark.

"Then we check that first. We don't find out while something's chasing us." ʸᵏ

They agreed, packed the small supplies they needed, and put the rest where they could reach them if they had to return quickly.

At the door Yuka pulled the flower bracelet farther under her sleeve. The charm had caught on the edge of her glove the day before. Mau noticed the adjustment and looked away before his attention could make it awkward.

"Ready?" he asked. ᴹ

"Come back this way," she said, touching the little line on the map. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"Both of us." ᴹ

* * *

They found the guardian beyond the collapsed stair.

It stood beneath an arch too small for it to pass through without lowering its head. At first Mau thought the shape beside it was another creature. Then it moved, and he understood that it was carrying a shield made from several thick plates bound together.

Yuka touched his wrist before he leaned farther around the corner.

They backed away without speaking.

At the turn, he stopped long enough to check whether anything had followed. There was a scrape beneath the arch. A heavy shift of weight. Then silence.

They continued to the shelter.

Yuka put her blade down carefully. It was only when she tried to take off the glove that Mau noticed her hands were shaking.

"Wait," he said. ᴹ

She looked up, ready to say she could do it herself.

He held up the water container instead.

"Drink first. Then tell me what you saw." ᴹ

She accepted it, took two small mouthfuls, and handed it back.

"The right side of the shield. It drags when he lifts it. There's a gap below the shoulder." ʸᵏ

Mau sat opposite her.

He had seen the drag too. He had also seen the reach of the weapon in its other hand, the space beneath the arch, the length of ground between cover and the place where a person might make a useful strike.

There was enough information to frighten him properly.

"We don't have to do it today," he said. ᴹ

She looked at him.

He kept his voice level, for both of them.

"We know where it is. We can check the passage, see if it leaves the arch, work out where we'd have to stand. Going now doesn't make the door open sooner if we get hurt before we know what we're doing." ᴹ

Yuka rested the container against her knee.

"I wasn't going to ask you to." ʸᵏ

"I know. I'm telling myself too." ᴹ

She let out a breath through her nose. After a moment, she smiled.

"You sound like Grandpa." ʸᵏ

"He's been working on me." ᴹ

The smile stayed while she picked up the charcoal.

They drew the arch.

* * *

By evening they had a better map and a bad meal.

The meal was Mau's responsibility. He had tried to improve something which was already edible, and the result had the texture of a lesson.

Yuka took another bite while watching him.

"You don't have to finish it," he said. ᴹ

"We counted the food." ʸᵏ

"I know. I can have that one." ᴹ

"It's the same food." ʸᵏ

He looked at his bowl. She looked at hers. They were both trying hard enough that she began laughing first.

He joined her, leaning back against the wall with his bowl held carefully between his knees.

For a while they ate it as a shared grievance. Yuka described exactly how Momo would react to the method. Mau offered to explain the method as something she had suggested. She told him she had a blade and witnesses would understand.

The laughter made the shelter feel inhabited.

Afterward they washed what needed washing and checked the covering over the window. The wind had shifted again. They fixed the loose edge together, one holding the cloth while the other tied it, until the sound stopped reminding them of footsteps.

Yuka stood close while he finished the last knot.

Mau could see the fine mark the glove had left around her wrist. A little soot near her sleeve. Her hair had come loose beside her cheek and she kept trying to push it back with a hand which was holding the cloth.

He reached to take the cloth from her.

She gave it to him, and their fingers stayed together a fraction too long.

Neither moved immediately.

Outside, something called from farther among the ruins. They listened until it ended. Mau tied the last knot, tested it, and let his hands fall.

Yuka was still there.

"What?" he asked, very quietly. ᴹ

She shook her head once.

"You keep asking that." ʸᵏ

"Because I don't know." ᴹ

She looked at his mouth and then back at his eyes.

He knew a little more.

The knowledge made him still. He could hear the water below the room, the wind on the other side of the cloth, his own breath stopping before it should. She was close enough that he could see the small changes in her expression while she tried to decide whether she had been understood.

"Yuka." ᴹ

"I know," she said quickly. Then stopped. "I don't know what you were going to say." ʸᵏ

He laughed once, softly, because the alternative was to keep standing there with his heart making a fool of him.

"I want to kiss you." ᴹ

Her face changed.

"Oh." ʸᵏ

He waited.

She looked down for a moment, then reached for his hand.

"Yes," she said. ʸᵏ

He moved slowly enough for her to meet him.

The first touch was brief. He had thought, in the moment before it happened, that he would know what to do once the question had an answer. Instead he found himself more aware of every small thing: the warmth of her mouth, the place her fingers tightened, the way she breathed in when he began to draw away.

She stayed close.

"Was that—" ᴹ

"Don't ask me if it was okay like you do after food." ʸᵏ

He closed his eyes, mortified.

Then she laughed against his cheek, and he felt her free hand settle lightly at the side of his neck.

"It was," she said. ʸᵏ

He opened his eyes.

Yuka was smiling now, flushed and uncertain and pleased in a way she did not seem able to hide. She looked toward the blankets, then at the door, remembering that they were still in a place which needed watching.

"We should sit down," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded and moved aside so she could get her cup.

They sat near the entrance, close enough that their shoulders touched. The map lay between Mau's feet. Beyond the courtyard there was a guardian they had not beaten, a way out they still had to earn, a third morning which would need both of them rested.

Yuka put her hand over his on the floor.

He turned his palm up to meet it.

When they changed watches later, they kissed once more. Then Mau lay down, as promised, and let her keep the door.
