# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 35
## Chapter 14 — After We Get Home

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S6E69
**Continuity state:** Branch second Red Gate, third/fourth internal days, return on outside Day447 after several hours. One major guardian defeated cooperatively through preparation and limited learned force, with no extraction/army. Mau/Yuka have kissed, no sex. Homecoming includes medical checks and ordinary concern from grandparents, not revelation of romance. Rimuru learns from Mau privately the following evening; he will respect privacy but not lie, and advises telling Yuta/Maki. Mau does not yet do so. This unresolved choice must persist. Ciel's private communication remains distinct from spoken relay. No second heart or original memories yet.

*When: The third day inside. They make one attempt at the route without going near the guardian.*

Yuka reached the corner before the little stone stopped rolling.

Mau counted the beats under his breath and stopped when she held up a hand. Too fast. The sound would still be drawing attention toward the wrong opening when she moved.

They walked back.

"Again?" he asked. ᴹ

She nodded.

They did it again.

By midday both were tired of rehearsing a plan which would have to survive being frightening. They ate, rested, and checked the weak patch above the arch one more time. The guardian remained beyond the same stretch of broken wall. It moved when a sound reached it and returned when there was nothing to strike.

They had watched long enough to know that much.

Not enough to make it safe.

Mau went through the part Ciel could help him bound: one short pull, the direction of it, the feeling which meant he had to stop. He could not keep it available while doing everything else. Yuka would have to make the first opening without expecting him to solve the whole ground at once.

He told her that again.

She looked at him over the bread.

"I heard you yesterday." ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

"And this morning." ʸᵏ

He looked down.

Yuka touched his uninjured hand.

"I know which part is mine." ʸᵏ

He turned the hand so he could hold hers briefly.

"Okay." ᴹ

They waited until morning to do it.

* * *

The guardian reached the stone sooner than they wanted.

Mau saw its foot strike the mark they had chosen. Saw the shield lift, heard Yuka move behind the broken pier. He had been counting; he lost the count when the weapon hit close enough to throw grit into his face.

He ducked and held the direction in his head.

"Now," Ciel said. ᶜ

The pull caught the support they had loosened. It moved an inch, stuck, then came free with a grinding sound which went straight through his teeth.

Mau let it go when she told him to.

The slab above the side of the arch dropped. It struck the raised shield and drove one edge of it down into the broken paving. The guardian twisted to free it.

Yuka came through the opening.

Her first strike reached the gap beneath its arm. It recoiled hard enough to throw her off line. Mau saw her shoulder turn, saw the weapon beginning to follow, and pushed from his open palm with the small, violent force he had been saving for this.

It cost him the skin across two knuckles and the breath he had meant to take next.

The weapon missed her.

Yuka hit the ground, rolled under the dragging shield, and came up on the side they had checked three times. Her blade went through the damaged join at the back of its knee.

The guardian fell to one side.

Mau moved toward her before he could decide whether that was useful. She shouted for him to stay clear. He stopped, caught himself against the pier, and watched her find the second opening on her own.

The last strike was shorter than he expected.

Afterward the yard contained a fallen body, the slab, and both of them breathing as if they had run all the way from home.

Mau could not stop looking at her.

Yuka braced a hand on her knee and lifted her head.

"I'm here," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded too quickly.

She came to him instead of making him cross the ground.

He put his good hand at the back of her head and held her against his shoulder. Neither spoke for a while. Her blade lay within reach against the pier. His hand throbbed. Above them, the remaining stone of the arch stayed where it was.

When she drew back, he checked her face.

She touched his sleeve.

"We have to look at that." ʸᵏ

"After—" ᴹ

"Now." ʸᵏ

He let her.

* * *

They went through the exit carrying less than they could have taken.

Yuka had wanted to gather more of the material by the wall. Mau had agreed until Ciel asked which injury he meant to put the extra weight on. They kept the crystals they could carry safely, a few pieces worth showing the others, and the record of the route. The rest stayed in a neat pile where the next people through might find it, if the place ever opened the same way again.

Mau took one last look at the shelter before they left it.

The cover over the window still held. There were marks in the dust where the bedrolls had lain. Yuka had left the charcoal map rolled into a crack above the door rather than carry something which might be useless on the other side.

It was strange to leave a place they had worked so hard to make bearable.

Then the exit opened and he stopped caring about the room behind them.

Maki was on the step outside.

Yuka said her name. Maki moved before the second syllable had finished, and Mau had just enough time to let go of the bag they were carrying together.

Yuta reached him next.

"How long?" ʸᵗ

Mau looked at the sky. The light was different, but still belonged to the same day.

"Four days. We slept three nights." ᴹ

Yuta shut his eyes for a moment.

"Come inside." ʸᵗ

There were questions after that. Shoko inspected Mau's hand and told him what she thought of people who made a bandage so tidy that it hid what was swelling beneath it. Yuka had a bruise coming along her shoulder and a cut she had not noticed at all. Both were fed before they could give a full account of the guardian.

They gave the account together, correcting one another's distances, making sure the things each had done were included.

Neither mentioned the kiss.

Once, under the table, Yuka's foot found his.

Mau kept looking at the map Fern had put in front of them.

* * *

The next evening he found Rimuru outside with a cup of tea.

He had intended to ask about a supply list. He had the list in his hand. Rimuru glanced at it and made room on the step.

"You look like you're going to confess to breaking something." ᴿ

Mau sat.

"I haven't broken anything." ᴹ

Rimuru waited.

Mau looked at the paper. There were six things on it, all easy to discuss. He could say the words, receive the answers, and leave with the more difficult subject still private.

He put the list down.

"Yuka and I kissed." ᴹ

Rimuru's cup stopped halfway to his mouth.

He lowered it carefully.

"Inside?" ᴿ

Mau nodded.

"You both wanted to?" ᴿ

"Yes." ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him for a long moment. Then he leaned back against the doorframe.

"Okay." ᴿ

Mau had prepared for enough possible answers that this one left him without his next sentence.

"Okay?" ᴹ

"I didn't say there weren't things to talk about." ᴿ

Mau nodded, looking down.

Rimuru let the quiet sit before asking.

"Does Yuta know?" ᴿ

"No." ᴹ

"Maki?" ᴿ

"No." ᴹ

"You should tell them." ᴿ

Mau rubbed the edge of the paper with his thumb.

"We just got back. Everybody's still—" ᴹ

"I know. You don't have to start a family argument while Shoko's unwrapping your hand. But don't wait until telling them feels impossible." ᴿ

Mau looked toward the yard. Yuka had gone to help Mai with something after dinner. He could hear them speaking through an open window, the words indistinct, the tones familiar.

"I want to know what it is first," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru turned the cup between his hands.

"That's fair. Wanting a little time is fair. They'll still have feelings about being the last to know." ᴿ

Mau nodded.

"Will you—" ᴹ

He stopped before finishing the request.

Rimuru understood it anyway.

"I'm not going to announce your private life for you. I'm not going to lie for you either." ᴿ

"I know." ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him until he looked back.

"He's your friend too, Mau." ᴿ

That was the part which made his stomach tighten. He could face the idea of Maki being angry with an effort. Thinking of Yuta looking at him without understanding why he had not been told was harder.

"I'll talk to her," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru nodded once. He did not make the promise larger than it was.

After a while he picked up the supply list and asked about the first item. Mau answered. They found themselves disagreeing about how many containers the house actually needed, and for a little while the step was only a step again.

When Mau went inside, he took the paper with him.

He found Yuka in the corridor and asked whether they could walk outside before bed.
