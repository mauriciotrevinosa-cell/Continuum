*When: Day457. Ichika has been asked to hold one end of the string and has interpreted this as a position of command.*

"Don't move," she told Mau. ᴵ

He stopped moving.

Ori looked at the little bundle they were trying to tie.

"He has to turn it." ᴼ

"Then I move." ᴵ

They considered this solution together while Mau held both hands very still around the rolled cloth. Touka watched from the doorway with a cup of coffee. She had offered advice once, been told that the children were doing it, and retired from active involvement with admirable speed.

"You're enjoying this," Mau told her. ᴹ

"Yes." ᵀᵏ

Ichika objected to the conversation. He looked back at the knot immediately.

The bundle contained two cloths and a small flat box Yuka wanted to take. Ori had suggested adding another string because the first one looked lonely. Ichika had decided the second string required someone at each end, which was how Mau had lost most of the morning to a parcel he could have put in his bag without tying at all.

Frieren came through the room with a book under her arm.

Ori looked up.

"Mum, does this go over or under?" ᴼ

Frieren crouched beside the table and inspected it.

"Which part do you want to open later?" ᶠ

Ori pointed.

"Then under. If you do it the other way, he'll need a knife." ᶠ

Ichika looked at Mau as if he might already have been planning to betray the entire enterprise with a blade.

"Under," he agreed. ᴹ

The knot was completed after several more instructions. Both children tested it by pulling. It held. Mau was allowed to put the bundle down.

Yuka arrived in time to see them inspecting the result.

"Is that for us?" ʸᵏ

"Don't lose the strings," Ori said. ᴼ

Yuka promised. She put the parcel into the bag with a care which pleased both supervisors, then helped Mau collect the things he had scattered while trying to remain still.

Touka waited until the children had gone to show Frieren a drawing before speaking.

"Bring yourselves back too," she said. ᵀᵏ

Mau looked up.

The words were quiet and perfectly ordinary. He nodded.

"We will." ᴹ

* * *

The campsite took them less time to reach than to pack for.

It was near enough that the house had agreed where they would be without pretending the woods were an extension of the kitchen. There was a path, a clear patch, and a place to put a fire where they could see the ground around it. Maki had given them the same look she gave anybody who called a manageable trip an easy one.

They set up before doing anything else.

Yuka drove the last peg while Mau held the fabric clear. When she stood, she turned toward him with the expression of someone deciding whether to mention an unhelpful comment from five minutes earlier.

"It is straight," he said before she could ask. ᴹ

She stepped back to look.

"It's not." ʸᵏ

"We can both be comfortable on a slight angle." ᴹ

She laughed and pulled out the peg.

They made it straight.

The afternoon belonged to the small things they had brought it for. Walking as far as the low bank. Sitting where their feet could nearly reach the water. Talking without somebody needing a hand with the table, the laundry, a spar, a question which had to be answered before the person forgot why they came upstairs.

Mau liked all those interruptions when he was at home. Out here, the empty space between them felt extravagant.

Yuka noticed him looking at her and raised an eyebrow.

"What?" ʸᵏ

He smiled.

"I was going to ask you that." ᴹ

She reached over and put her hand on his cheek.

He stopped preparing a joke.

Her thumb moved once, a light touch near the corner of his mouth. He turned enough to kiss the heel of her hand. She looked down, smiling in a way which still seemed new each time he caused it.

They had kissed since the gate. In brief moments, behind a door or at the far end of a walk, always with the awareness of somewhere they ought to be next. This afternoon nobody was waiting for them until tomorrow.

Yuka leaned toward him.

He met her halfway.

* * *

After dinner they argued about whether the food had been better than in the ruins.

The answer was obviously yes. Yuka maintained that setting the comparison so low was an insult to the food. Mau pointed out that this time nothing in the bowl had frightened either of them.

They packed away what was left and banked the fire carefully.

Inside, Yuka took off the outer coat and folded it over the bag. Mau pulled the small lamp nearer so she could find a tie which had disappeared into the bedding. She found it under his knee and gave him a look as though he had been keeping it on purpose.

He caught her hand when she reached past him.

She turned.

The next kiss lasted longer than the ones outside. She moved close enough that he put a hand behind her back to keep her from leaning awkwardly. The bedding shifted under them. After a moment they were lying down, still kissing, his hand at her waist and hers against his shoulder.

Mau stopped once to look at her.

"Okay?" he asked. ᴹ

She nodded and drew him back.

He could feel how unsure they both were beneath the wanting. The effort to read each movement, to give a little room, to discover that closeness could keep becoming closer. Yuka's fingers caught at the hem of her shirt.

Then they stilled.

Mau felt the change before he understood it. He lifted his head.

Her eyes were open, looking past his shoulder.

"Yuka?" ᴹ

She blinked and looked at him.

"I want to," she said. ʸᵏ

He waited.

She swallowed.

"I thought—I'm not sure I'm ready." ʸᵏ

Mau took his hand from her waist and braced it beside her so there was no weight keeping her where she was.

"Then we stop." ᴹ

Her mouth tightened. He could see her trying to supply an explanation quickly enough that he would not have time to feel rejected.

"I wasn't just—" ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

He sat back slowly, giving her room to sit too.

Yuka pulled the loose edge of the blanket over her legs. She looked at the lamp, then at the folded coat, anywhere except his face.

He wanted to make it easy, and was afraid that saying too much would make her responsible for reassuring him. So he reached for the water instead.

"Do you want some?" ᴹ

She nodded.

He gave her the cup and waited until she had taken a drink.

"We can still stay," he said. "Or go back in the morning like we planned. Nothing has to change tonight." ᴹ

She held the cup against the blanket.

"You're not upset?" ʸᵏ

"I'm glad you told me." ᴹ

Yuka looked at him properly then.

The answer did not solve everything on her face. It gave her something steady enough to stop trying to hide the rest.

"I want to stay," she said. ʸᵏ

"Okay." ᴹ

She moved nearer, slowly, leaving him time to understand what she was asking now.

"Like this?" ʸᵏ

He put an arm around her shoulders.

"Like this." ᴹ

She rested against him until the cup became awkward to hold. He took it and put it down. After a while she started talking about something Mai had said while they were packing. A small, ridiculous complaint. He answered, and she laughed, and the night found another way to go on.

They slept with their clothes on and the lamp out, close enough that when Yuka woke once she could find his hand without looking.

* * *

In the morning she helped make breakfast.

It was a deliberate choice. Mau saw her waiting for him to become strange about the night before, and did his best to remain the man who could burn something while insisting he was watching it.

She took over before the food was lost.

"You're watching it from too far away." ʸᵏ

"I was giving it room." ᴹ

"It doesn't need privacy." ʸᵏ

He laughed, and the last stiffness in her shoulders eased.

Before they left, she checked that they had kept Ori's strings. They rolled the cloths without tying them into the original impossible bundle, and she tucked both lengths of string safely into a pocket.

At the path she took his hand.

"We should do this again," she said. ʸᵏ

"The part where you saved breakfast?" ᴹ

She pushed against his shoulder.

"The going away." ʸᵏ

He squeezed her hand once.

"Yes." ᴹ

They went home with the bags lighter, the food mostly finished, and something between them more settled for having been allowed to stop.
