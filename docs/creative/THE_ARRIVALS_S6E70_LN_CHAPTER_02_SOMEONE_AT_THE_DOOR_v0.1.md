"You hit the sleeve," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"The sleeve was on you." ᴹ

"After I stopped." ʸᵗ

Mau looked at the patch of ground where it had happened. There were footprints which could be interpreted in his favor if nobody remembered the actual sequence.

Unfortunately, Yuta remembered the actual sequence.

"I'll give you the first half," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"Of a touch?" ᴹ

"The movement. You kept your balance." ʸᵗ

Mau considered the offer.

"I'll take it." ᴹ

They carried the swords back together. The weather had given the yard a dry surface which held dust in the footprints instead of mud. Mau liked training in it. He could see where he had put his feet after the correction, could compare one attempt with the next without having to decide whether he was remembering the same mistake differently.

Yuta stored the blades on the rack and checked one of the handles.

"Need sanding again?" Mau asked. ᴹ

"Soon." ʸᵗ

"I can do it." ᴹ

Yuta handed it back to him immediately.

The ease of being believed warmed Mau before the thought behind it caught up. He took the blade and the little piece of abrasive cloth and sat on the step to work.

Yuta stayed to oil the other handles.

They spoke about ordinary things first. A repair which had gone well. A practice Fern and Stark had arranged which would require part of the field. Whether the weather would hold for the house outing people kept moving from one day to another because somebody always had a job they could not leave.

"Maki wants to go," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Mau looked up from the handle.

"On the outing?" ᴹ

"She says she doesn't care. Then she asks whether the others picked a day." ʸᵗ

Mau smiled.

"You could ask her." ᴹ

"I did. She said I ask too many questions." ʸᵗ

"That sounds promising." ᴹ

Yuta laughed quietly, rubbing the oil into the wood.

Mau looked at his friend's hands. There had been so many mornings like this. The last exercise already finished, the rest of the day not yet demanding to begin. He could have turned almost any of them into a conversation if he had been willing to let the words make a difference.

He stopped sanding.

Yuta noticed.

"Something wrong?" ʸᵗ

Mau looked toward the house.

He imagined saying Yuka's name. The questions which would follow it. The way Yuta would have to rearrange a person he trusted beside the granddaughter he had been given back. Mau was not afraid of being struck. He was afraid of the small stillness before Yuta decided what to say.

"No," he answered. ᴹ

It was not an answer he could feel good about.

Yuta kept looking at him for another moment.

"If it's the hand, let Shoko see it before practice. Not afterward." ʸᵗ

Mau flexed the hand. The injury from the gate had healed. All the fingers closed.

"It's fine." ᴹ

He hated that the true part made the rest easier to leave unsaid.

Yuta passed him the other blade when he was done. Mau finished both handles properly and stayed until everything was put away.

* * *

That evening Yuka found him outside the kitchen with a tray he had been asked to return.

She was carrying a book Mai had lent her and a short length of cord which had been given to her for reasons Mau could not immediately understand.

"Come upstairs," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at the tray.

"I have to—" ᴹ

"After." ʸᵏ

She went ahead before he could ask whether she needed help with anything. He returned the tray, said good night to the people still in the kitchen, and followed her up.

Yuka's door was open. He knocked on the frame anyway.

"You're doing that on purpose now," she said from inside. ʸᵏ

"What?" ᴹ

"Being polite outside my door." ʸᵏ

He leaned against the frame.

"I was taught very well." ᴹ

"Come in." ʸᵏ

She closed the book when he sat beside her. The cord lay on the cover. It turned out to be a replacement for a fastening on her coat, and she had brought him up partly because she wanted another pair of hands while she checked the length.

They finished that in a few minutes.

Afterward he stayed.

Yuka put the coat aside, shifted nearer, and rested her forehead lightly against his shoulder. He turned to kiss the top of her head. She lifted her face, catching him halfway, and the kiss changed direction.

He smiled against her mouth. She made a small, annoyed sound at the smile and put her hand at his neck to keep him there.

The lamp had been placed low beside the bed. When they moved, his sleeve brushed it and both stopped at once.

"Move that first," he said. ᴹ

Yuka laughed under her breath, waited while he put it safely on the table, and pulled him back.

This time she unfastened the outer shirt herself, slowly enough that he could see the uncertainty and the decision both. He touched her hand instead of helping.

"You can change your mind," he said. ᴹ

"I know." ʸᵏ

She kissed him again. He let the conversation rest there rather than asking her to prove she knew.

Then somebody knocked.

Both went still.

"Yuka? Have you got a minute?" ʸᵗ

Her grandfather's voice.

Mau drew back so quickly that his elbow struck the wall. Yuka clutched the open edges of the shirt together and stared at him, eyes enormous.

For one absurd second neither remembered that Yuta could still hear.

"A minute," she called. ʸᵏ

"All right." ʸᵗ

Mau stood, stepped on the edge of a blanket, and nearly took it with him. Yuka pressed a hand over her mouth. He could not tell whether she was about to laugh or die of embarrassment.

He picked up the coat and sat on the chair.

She refastened the shirt, checked the lamp, checked herself, and opened the door.

Yuta stood outside holding a small parcel from Maki.

He looked at Mau.

"Oh. Hi." ʸᵗ

"Hi." ᴹ

Mau held up the coat, an answer to a question nobody had asked.

Yuta nodded at it.

"The cord?" ʸᵗ

Yuka nodded too quickly.

"We fixed it." ʸᵏ

"Good. Maki said to give you this before she forgets where she put it again." ʸᵗ

Yuka took the parcel. Yuta asked whether she wanted to join the morning practice or take the later one. They arranged a time. Mau sat very still, listening to a perfectly ordinary conversation which had become difficult only because of what neither of them was telling him.

At the door, Yuta looked back.

"Don't stay up too late. Both of you." ʸᵗ

"We won't," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

He went away.

She closed the door and leaned against it.

Mau looked down at the coat in his hands.

"He knows something," she whispered. ʸᵏ

Mau shook his head.

"I don't think so." ᴹ

Yuka watched him. The fright began to leave her face, and something harder to laugh at took its place.

"That almost makes it worse," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

She came to sit at the edge of the bed. There was space between them now, enough to hold a conversation instead of simply returning to where the knock had interrupted them.

"I almost told him this morning," Mau said. ᴹ

Yuka looked up.

"What stopped you?" ʸᵏ

He thought about offering the reasons which made it sound accidental. Tools to put away, somebody walking past, a better time later.

"I was afraid he'd look at me differently." ᴹ

Yuka put the parcel in her lap and worked a finger under its fold.

"He might." ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

They were quiet.

Downstairs somebody laughed loudly enough that the sound came through the floor. A cupboard closed. The house was proceeding through an evening in which Yuta had delivered his granddaughter's parcel and assumed his friend would look after the coat.

"I don't want to stop this," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Mau put the coat beside him.

"Neither do I." ᴹ

"I don't want telling them to mean asking whether we're allowed to feel it." ʸᵏ

"It doesn't have to." ᴹ

She looked at him until he admitted the rest.

"They're still allowed to be upset we kept it from them." ᴹ

Yuka nodded, slowly.

He went to sit beside her. They did not try to recover the moment from before the knock. She put her head on his shoulder, and he stayed until the noises below them grew fewer.

Before leaving, he kissed her once by the door.

Yuta's parcel was still unopened on the bed.
