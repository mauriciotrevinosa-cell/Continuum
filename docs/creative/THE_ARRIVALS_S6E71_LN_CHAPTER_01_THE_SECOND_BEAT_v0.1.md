## Chapter 17 — The Second Beat

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35

*When: Day474. Mau is putting a stack of plates away when Ciel tells him to stop.*

He stopped with the top plate halfway to the shelf.

"Put that down first," she said. ᶜ

He set it on the table.

The other plates stayed where he had stacked them. One tilted slightly against the next, making a little white edge in the shade. He watched it while he waited for her to say the rest.

"Mau. Are you in pain?" ᶜ

He looked down at himself.

"No." ᴹ

"Dizzy? Short of breath?" ᶜ

"No. What is it?" ᴹ

Her answer took longer than he wanted.

"I am finding a second rhythm. It was not there before." ᶜ

He put a hand against his chest.

At first he felt nothing beyond the small pressure of his own palm. Then his heartbeat made itself noticeable through the shirt. A familiar spacing, made stranger because he was waiting for something to interrupt it.

Something did.

It was faint. So faint he could have blamed the movement of his hand. He held still, listened inward, and felt it again.

"What does that mean?" ᴹ

"I don't know yet. Rimuru knows I found it. I want Shoko to examine you." ᶜ

Mau looked toward the doorway. He could walk to the clinic. He knew that before moving, and was unexpectedly glad of having one simple thing to establish.

"Okay. I'm going there." ᴹ

He told Rem he was leaving the plates because something needed checking.

She looked at his face, set down what she was holding, and asked whether he wanted her to come.

"I'm not hurt. I think I can get there." ᴹ

Rem did not argue. She walked as far as the corridor with him, where Rimuru was already coming toward them.

"There," she said, and waited until he reached his friend before returning to the kitchen. ᴿᵉ

* * *

Shoko listened twice.

The second time she made him change position, wait, breathe normally, and tell her whether anything felt different. He answered each question. Rimuru sat on the stool near the wall with both hands on his knees, resisting the urge to fill the pauses.

When Shoko took the instrument away, she looked at the place she had been listening to for another moment.

"You felt this today?" ˢʰ

"Ciel noticed it. Then I could feel it when I paid attention." ᴹ

"Yesterday?" ˢʰ

He searched the ordinary day behind him: breakfast, work outside, a practice he had cut short because he wanted to help finish something before lunch. Nothing had felt like this.

"No." ᴹ

Shoko asked permission to check with her technique. He said yes.

Her hand was warm against his chest. He looked toward the shelf rather than watch her expression so closely that every small change would start to mean something. There were cloths stacked there, a bottle he recognized, a small box which had never stayed in the same place long because people kept returning it to where they thought she preferred it.

He could hear his own breathing.

The new beat was easier to feel now that he knew where to wait for it.

Shoko withdrew her hand.

"I want Gojo to look too. You can say no." ˢʰ

Mau turned back.

"Would it help?" ᴹ

"It might give us another observation. It won't automatically give us an answer." ˢʰ

He nodded.

"Then yes." ᴹ

Rimuru stood to fetch him.

Mau watched the door close and made himself ask the question before it got larger in his head.

"Is it two hearts?" ᴹ

Shoko put down the cloth she was folding.

"There's a second structure doing something I didn't find when I examined you before. It has a rhythm. It isn't replacing the one I know how to follow." ˢʰ

He looked at her.

"I'm being careful with the words because that's what I can tell you," she added. ˢʰ

Mau nodded. It was better than watching her choose something reassuring and having to wonder which part she meant.

"I feel normal," he said. ᴹ

"Good. Keep telling me if that changes." ˢʰ

* * *

Gojo did not joke when he first came in.

That frightened Mau more than it would have if he had tried to be funny and failed. He sat very still while Gojo took off the blindfold and looked.

Shoko made him describe what he could actually see before attempting to name it.

He leaned back after a while and rubbed a finger along the edge of the blindfold.

"There's more than there was," he said. ᴳ

"Very helpful," Shoko said. ˢʰ

"You told me to start there." ᴳ

Mau found himself smiling a little. Shoko saw it and let the exchange last one more sentence before asking the next question.

Gojo could distinguish something new from the patterns he had seen before. He could not tell them who had put it there, why it had appeared, or whether its presence meant that a function would soon become available. Nothing in the look gave him permission to call it a second person.

Ciel asked questions through Rimuru where a comparison might help. Mau listened, answering when the question concerned a sensation instead of a shape someone else could observe.

At the end they had several descriptions and no cause.

Shoko wrote them down anyway.

"Can I ask her?" Mau said. ᴹ

The room quieted.

He looked at Rimuru, then Shoko.

"The Goddess." ᴹ

Rimuru's expression changed in a way he could not entirely read.

"You can ask. I don't know if she'll answer." ᴿ

Mau tried.

He spoke her name, then explained plainly what had happened. He waited long enough to feel foolish, then long enough that the foolishness no longer mattered.

There was no reply.

Shoko did not treat the silence as medical information.

She put the page beside the others and asked what he had planned for tomorrow.

* * *

The outing had finally found a day when enough people could leave their jobs covered.

It was not the whole house. Some people were looking forward to having the place quieter. Others had work they wanted to finish. The plan involved a walk, food, a long stretch outdoors, and enough company that changing one's mind about the pace would not leave everyone waiting.

Mau had meant to go.

He looked down at his shirt, where nothing visible had changed.

"You'd rather I stayed," he said. ᴹ

"I'd rather we didn't learn what this does halfway up a path with you trying to make sure you don't spoil everyone else's afternoon," Shoko said. ˢʰ

He thought about the walk. The nearest place they could turn back. How long it would take to reach the house if he began feeling something he could not explain.

"Yes. That makes sense." ᴹ

Shoko watched his face.

"I'm agreeing," he said, a little more firmly. "I'm not waiting for you to leave so I can argue with Rimuru." ᴹ

"Good. Saves us both time." ˢʰ

He looked toward the doorway.

"Do I have to stay in here?" ᴹ

"No. You're not presently ill. I'd like a check tonight and another in the morning. After that, ordinary room, ordinary people. No testing it because you're bored." ˢʰ

"There goes my whole plan." ᴹ

She gave him the look which had ended many better jokes.

He stayed for the instructions, repeated the changes he should report, and went downstairs with Rimuru when they were finished.

* * *

Yuka heard because Mau told the table what they knew.

He did not turn it into a dramatic announcement. He was already tired of watching people look at his chest. He explained the second beat, the checks, the agreement to stay tomorrow. Shoko, arriving behind him, corrected one phrase and confirmed the rest.

Yuka put down her cup.

"I'll stay too," she said. ʸᵏ

Maki turned toward her.

"You were looking forward to going." ᴹᵏ

"I was looking forward to not doing things for a day. I can not do things here." ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

She lifted her eyebrows, daring him to make it difficult.

Shoko made clear that she would still be in and out of the clinic, and that staying in the house did not make Yuka responsible for a diagnosis. Rimuru would be nearby for part of the day too. There were other people at home. Nobody had to turn it into a bedside watch.

Yuka nodded through the explanation and reached for her cup again.

Mau felt the new beat once while somebody asked about the food.

It did not hurt. It continued in the quiet behind his ordinary heartbeat, patient and unfamiliar.

Across the table, Yuta was explaining the route to someone who had arrived halfway through the plan.

Mau watched him for a moment, then looked down before he could be caught doing it.
