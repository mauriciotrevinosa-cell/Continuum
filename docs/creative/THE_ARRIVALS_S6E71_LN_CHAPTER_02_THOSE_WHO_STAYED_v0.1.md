*When: Day475, breakfast. The people leaving have brought enough things to make staying home look like the efficient choice.*

Umaru had put her bag on the bench and was attempting to persuade Taihei that none of it weighed very much individually.

"Individually isn't how you carry it," he said. ᵀ

"I can rearrange it." ᵁ

"Will that remove anything?" ᵀ

She considered him over the top of the bag.

Mau passed her the cloth she had dropped and went to get his cup. Shoko caught him before he could take tea to the far end of the table.

"First," she said, pointing toward the clinic. ˢʰ

"I was coming." ᴹ

"You've gone past twice." ˢʰ

"Those were preparation." ᴹ

She held out her hand for the cup. He surrendered it and followed her.

The examination was short because nothing had changed. She checked the things she had checked last night, asked the questions without supplying his answers, and listened to the two rhythms. Mau watched her face more openly this time. She did not pretend not to notice.

"Still there," she said when she finished. ˢʰ

"I know. I felt it when I woke up." ᴹ

"Anything else?" ˢʰ

He considered the actual morning instead of trying to guess which answer would get him back to breakfast sooner.

"No. Slept normally. Hungry. Mostly annoyed I'm missing the walk." ᴹ

"That's a very ordinary collection of symptoms." ˢʰ

He smiled.

Shoko gave him back the cup, then stopped him with a hand before he could stand.

"You can be annoyed and still think staying is sensible. You don't need to convince me you like it." ˢʰ

Mau looked at the cup between his hands.

"I know. It's the part where I feel fine." ᴹ

"Yes. That part is inconvenient for both of us." ˢʰ

She stood and reached for the next page on the desk. It was a clear dismissal, free of the pity he had been preparing to endure. He thanked her and went back to the table.

Someone had kept his portion covered.

* * *

Yuka was sitting where he had left his coat.

She lifted it when he approached, then folded it beside her instead of moving. The place next to her remained available. He took it with an attention to the ordinary movement which made it feel much less ordinary.

"How was it?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"The same." ᴹ

"Good." ʸᵏ

She pushed the plate nearer. He began eating while she listened to the discussion about when the group meant to return.

Fern had the route, food, and rough timing settled. She was going on the walk herself. Jinshi would handle the small list of things which might need recording while she was gone, and Rem had firmly declined three different offers to leave instructions for lunch.

"We'll eat without you," Rem said. ᴿᵉ

Fern looked at the remaining page.

Frieren took it from her hand and passed it to Jinshi.

"Come with us." ᶠ

It was said quietly enough that the words belonged to Fern before anyone else could make them a joke. Fern looked at her, then at Ori, who was already leaning against Frieren with a bag small enough that nobody had yet argued about it.

"I am coming," Fern said. ᶠᵉ

"Then stop leaving pieces of yourself here first." ᶠ

Fern's face softened despite her obvious effort to maintain the practical part of the conversation.

Mau looked down at his breakfast and gave her the moment.

At the other end, Maki was checking that Milim had understood which things they were not turning into a competition. Milim had agreed to everything so rapidly that Yuta was asking follow-up questions.

Yuka watched them and smiled.

"You can still go," Mau said softly. ᴹ

She turned.

"I know." ʸᵏ

"You don't need to—" ᴹ

"Mau. I know." ʸᵏ

She said it without smiling, so he would hear the decision instead of converting it into kindness he had to refuse.

He nodded.

"Okay." ᴹ

Under the table, her knee touched his once. She kept listening to her grandfather ask Milim whether she intended to carry half the forest home if Ori found something interesting.

Milim looked offended that the answer was supposed to be no.

* * *

Getting the group out of the house took longer than breakfast.

Bocchi had forgotten something, then remembered it was already in her bag, then offered to check whether anybody else had forgotten something because she was standing nearest the stairs anyway. Nijika put a hand on the back of her shoulder and steered her toward the door. Kita carried a folded cloth, Ryo carried suspiciously little, and the argument about the division had clearly begun before they entered the room.

Umaru's bag had become lighter at some point. Taihei was pretending not to notice the extra thing she put in his.

Ori came to Mau carrying the compass.

"It works outside too," she told him. ᴼ

"I believe you." ᴹ

"You said the needle might get stuck." ᴼ

"When you put it under the cushion and sat on it." ᴹ

She considered the distinction.

"It's not stuck." ᴼ

He crouched far enough to see. The needle turned when Frieren moved across the room. Ori held it still with both hands and watched the adjustment with evident satisfaction.

"That's very good," he said. ᴹ

"I'm going to see if it points at her through a tree." ᴼ

"Ask before you go behind the tree." ᴹ

Ori looked toward her mother, measuring the likelihood of agreement. Frieren had heard the entire exchange.

"A small tree," she said. ᶠ

Ori went to her at once, already proposing candidates.

Yuta came to the bench while Mau was getting up.

"We'll be back before dinner," he said. ʸᵗ

"You don't need to hurry for me." ᴹ

"I was thinking of dinner." ʸᵗ

Mau laughed.

Yuta looked toward Yuka, who was collecting the cups they had used.

"Thank you for staying." ʸᵗ

She turned, a little too quickly.

"It's fine. I wanted a quiet day." ʸᵏ

"I know. Still." ʸᵗ

He put a hand briefly on her shoulder and went to help Maki with the last bag.

Mau watched Yuka's face after he turned away.

She set the cups down before coming back to him.

Neither said anything about the words they had not found in the days since her grandfather knocked.

* * *

The house did not become silent when the door closed.

Mai went toward the workshop carrying something Wakana had asked her to bring. There were voices from the kitchen, a door opening at the back, somebody upstairs remembering a job they meant to finish before the light changed. The place felt differently occupied, with fewer collisions between people trying to pass one another.

Rimuru stopped beside Mau on his way out.

"I'll be near the growing beds for a while, then the workshop. Shoko knows where. You can call." ᴿ

Mau nodded.

Rimuru looked at him, then at Yuka.

He did not say anything about the secret in front of her or turn an ordinary day into a warning delivered for witnesses. He simply waited until Mau looked back and then gave him a small nod before leaving.

Mau understood it.

Yuka went to find the box of cards.

By the time she came back, Mau had moved a cushion to the end of the sofa and was trying to decide whether lying down would make everybody who passed ask whether he felt sick.

She put the box on the low table.

"Sit however you want," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked up.

"You're doing the face." ʸᵏ

"What face?" ᴹ

"The one where you're arranging yourself for someone who hasn't come in yet." ʸᵏ

He let his shoulders fall against the cushion.

Yuka sat on the other end and began taking out the cards. She counted them into piles, stopped when one was missing, and found it stuck to the inside of the box.

"What are the rules?" Mau asked. ᴹ

She smiled without looking up.

"I'll tell you after you lose." ʸᵏ

He reached for the nearest pile.

"That sounds like Umaru taught you." ᴹ

"She did." ʸᵏ

Outside, the last of the departing voices faded along the path. In the kitchen someone set down a pan. Yuka shuffled badly, dropped two cards, and blamed the box.

Mau settled in to defend himself.
