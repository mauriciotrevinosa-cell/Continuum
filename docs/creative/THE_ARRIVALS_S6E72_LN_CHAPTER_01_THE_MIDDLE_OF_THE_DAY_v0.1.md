*When: Day475, late morning. Mau has lost three games and is beginning to suspect the rules are stable after all.*

Yuka laid down the last card and folded her hands.

Mau looked at the table.

"That doesn't look right." ᴹ

"It looks very right from here." ʸᵏ

He counted the cards in the pile, checked the one in his hand, and discovered that the argument he meant to make had already been dealt with by the rule she explained ten minutes earlier.

Yuka waited with a patience he found offensive.

"Again," he said. ᴹ

"We can stop if you're tired." ʸᵏ

He looked up.

She smiled so broadly that he could not even pretend to take it as concern.

They played again.

By the middle of the fourth game he understood what she was doing well enough to make her hesitate. That was satisfying in a way winning by accident would not have been. He watched her look between the two remaining piles and tried to keep his own face empty.

"You have it," she accused. ʸᵏ

"Have what?" ᴹ

"That was too quick." ʸᵏ

He made his next answer slower on purpose.

Yuka threw a cushion at him.

He caught it against his chest, laughing, and she stopped so abruptly that both knew what she had thought.

"I'm okay," he said before she could ask. ᴹ

She looked at the cushion, then at him.

"I didn't mean—" ʸᵏ

"It's a cushion." ᴹ

"I know." ʸᵏ

He put it behind his shoulder and settled against it.

"A useful cushion. Thank you." ᴹ

She took a breath, then picked up the cards she had dropped in the movement.

The game continued, a little quieter for a few turns and then normally. Mau did not make her apologize again for something which had not hurt him. When he finally won, she disputed the result with enough energy to convince him she had stopped checking whether he was fragile.

He liked that better than the victory.

* * *

Rem found them putting the cards back in the wrong compartments.

She inspected the box, said nothing, and asked whether they could set out bowls before lunch.

Mau got up at once. Yuka caught the last cards before they slid from the table and followed him into the kitchen.

The meal was already underway. Ram stood near the work surface with the knife she preferred, cutting at a pace which suggested that any assistance ought to make itself useful before speaking. Rem had a pot to attend to and a plate which needed to be taken into the common room.

Mau took the plate.

Yuka took the bowls.

They passed one another twice in the doorway, each stepping to the same side. On the third attempt Ram looked up.

"Could one of you choose a direction and remain loyal to it?" ᴿᵃ

Yuka turned away, laughing. Mau stood still and let her pass.

There were fewer places to set than usual. He counted the people staying, checked with Rem before deciding which absent person had simply gone to wash, and put water where someone would not have to stand up to reach it.

The task was small enough that he felt foolish enjoying the completion.

From the kitchen, Rem asked for two more bowls.

He fetched them without needing to revise his opinion of the first count. Somebody else had come in to eat. That happened in a house.

Yuka watched him put them down.

"What?" he asked. ᴹ

"You looked pleased with yourself." ʸᵏ

"I found the bowls." ᴹ

"A great achievement." ʸᵏ

"I'm having a quiet day." ᴹ

She leaned against the back of the nearest chair, smiling at him.

He could have kissed her. The desire arrived so simply that he nearly moved before the open kitchen door reminded him where they were.

He took the water jug to the other end of the table instead.

When he returned, she was looking down at her hands.

* * *

Rimuru came back with dirt along the bottom of one trouser leg and a complaint about something which had looked level until they put it down.

Mau moved a bowl nearer.

"You could have asked Frieren to hold it." ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him.

The memory arrived between them without anyone needing to explain the bench. Mau began laughing before Rimuru put a hand over his face.

"I hate that you remember useful things at the wrong time." ᴿ

"I'm recovering a skill." ᴹ

Yuka looked from one to the other. She demanded the story, and they told two incompatible versions until Ram, listening from the doorway, said that both versions seemed to leave Rimuru responsible for the angle.

He defended himself. Rem put food in front of him while he was distracted.

Mau ate with the comfortable appetite of a day spent doing very little. Nobody rushed. At the far end, Mai came in halfway through, took the place which had been left for her, and began explaining why a measurement sent from the workshop could not mean what the person sending it believed it meant.

Yuka asked a question. Mai put down her spoon to answer it properly.

Mau listened to the two of them work it out. There was room for the conversation without anyone having to schedule it. He liked the house like this, with fewer people and the same tendency to turn one practical question into three other subjects.

Shoko came in near the end.

She looked at his bowl before looking at him.

"Finished?" ˢʰ

He lifted the spoon.

"Almost." ᴹ

"Then finish. I'll check you after." ˢʰ

He gave her a mock-suspicious look.

"That sounded generous." ᴹ

"I want the table when you're done." ˢʰ

Rimuru laughed. Mau finished his food.

* * *

The check gave them the same answer as the morning.

Mau could feel the second rhythm if he paid attention. He could also forget it for whole stretches, which he told Shoko because it seemed relevant. She wrote that down, asked whether he had tried provoking it, and accepted his no after one long look.

Yuka waited at the other end of the common room with the book she had brought down.

He returned to the sofa afterward. Shoko took her papers back to the clinic, Rimuru went to finish the work he had abandoned for lunch, and Mai collected the object which had caused the measurement argument. Rem and Ram went through the washing-up together, their voices drifting in and out through the kitchen door.

It was afternoon before the room became quiet enough for Yuka to hear him sit down.

She looked over the book.

"Anything different?" ʸᵏ

"No." ᴹ

"Good." ʸᵏ

He looked at the cover.

"Any good?" ᴹ

"It was until I tried to remember where I stopped." ʸᵏ

He moved the cushion to make space near him.

She came over and sat with her legs folded to one side. For a while she read, and he rested with his head back, listening to the occasional turn of the page.

The warmth of her arm against his was enough to keep drawing his attention away from the sound.

After a few pages she closed the book around one finger.

"We should tell them," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau opened his eyes.

"Yes." ᴹ

She kept looking at the cover.

"Not because you nearly kissed me in the kitchen." ʸᵏ

He felt his face warm.

"You noticed." ᴹ

"Mau." ʸᵏ

He smiled, then let the smile go when he saw she needed the answer to remain serious.

"When they get back," he said. "We can ask for a little time. Somewhere quiet." ᴹ

Yuka rubbed a thumb along the edge of the page.

"Together?" ʸᵏ

"If you want." ᴹ

"I do." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

The decision did not make him feel brave. He could already imagine the first questions. But the room seemed less crowded by things he had to avoid thinking about once they had said when.

Yuka set the book down.

"I had a nice day," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau looked around at the cards, the empty cups, the cushion which had been thrown at him before lunch.

"You beat me four times." ᴹ

"That was part of it." ʸᵏ

He took her hand, turning the little flower gently where it had slipped to the inside of her wrist.

"Me too," he said. ᴹ

She rested her head against his shoulder. He kept his hand around hers and let his eyes close again.

Outside, the afternoon continued. There would be people home before dinner, food to prepare, a conversation they could no longer leave waiting for a better time.

For a little while, they sat where they were.
