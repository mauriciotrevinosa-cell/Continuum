*When: Day475, late afternoon. Yuka has taken his book because he has been pretending to read the same page.*

"You haven't moved it," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at the page.

"There's a lot on it." ᴹ

"You've been watching me." ʸᵏ

He considered denying it, then looked at her instead.

"Yes." ᴹ

She put the book on the low table. It slid onto the one she had finished with earlier, leaving both of them without an object to pretend was the reason they were sitting there.

He had stretched out along the sofa after the cards were put away, one knee bent, his shoulder against the cushion she had thrown at him. Yuka had been sitting near his waist. When she leaned to kiss him, he put a hand at her back to steady her.

She stayed near when the kiss ended.

"What?" he asked, smiling. ᴹ

"Nothing." ʸᵏ

"Now you're doing it." ᴹ

She kissed him again instead of defending herself. He laughed softly into it, then forgot the joke when she moved closer. Her knee settled on the cushion beside him. Both hands found his shoulders, and for a little while there was no reason to look anywhere else.

The front door opened.

They heard it too late.

"We're—" ᴹⁱ

Milim stopped in the entrance to the room.

Yuka sat back so quickly that Mau caught her by the elbow before she could lose her balance. She was still over him, one hand gripping his sleeve, when all three understood the scene at the same time.

For a moment nobody spoke.

Then Milim pointed.

"You were kissing." ᴹⁱ

Mau sat up carefully, giving Yuka room to move off his lap. She went to the other side of the sofa with the expression of someone who had discovered that moving did not erase where she had been.

"Yes," he said. ᴹ

Milim looked between them.

"Since when?" ᴹⁱ

Yuka put a hand over her face.

Outside, somebody called for Milim to stop leaving her bag in the middle of the entrance.

"Wait," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Milim turned toward the voice before the word reached her.

"Mau and Yuka were kissing!" ᴹⁱ

The words went straight through the open door.

Yuka's hand dropped.

Mau stood. He could see Maki at the far end of the entrance, one strap still over her shoulder. Yuta was behind her, taking a bag from Ori so Frieren could help the child with her coat.

Yuta looked up.

The expression on his face was not the one Mau had been preparing to see.

It was simple incomprehension.

"What?" Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Milim looked back at the sofa, then at the faces in the doorway. The delight of having found something surprising began to leave her.

"They were," she said, quieter. ᴹⁱ

Mau could have asked for a minute. Could have turned the moment into something which needed a better explanation before anybody was allowed to react.

Instead he looked at his friend.

"She's right." ᴹ

* * *

Maki put down her bag.

It was a small, controlled movement. Mau watched her set it clear of the doorway because watching her face had become difficult.

Yuka came to stand beside him.

"We were going to tell you tonight," she said. ʸᵏ

Maki looked at her.

"Tonight." ᴹᵏ

"We talked about it today. We were going to ask if we could talk somewhere—" ʸᵏ

"How long?" ᴹᵏ

Yuka stopped.

Mau looked at her, then back at Maki.

"Since the gate. The second one." ᴹ

Yuta was still holding Ori's bag.

Frieren took it gently from his hand. He let go without looking at her.

"When you came back," he said. ʸᵗ

Mau nodded.

Yuta's attention moved to Yuka. Then back to him.

"And when I came to her room." ʸᵗ

Yuka drew in a breath.

Mau did not let her answer for him.

"Yes." ᴹ

Yuta looked down for a moment. When he lifted his head, the bewilderment had begun to become something else.

The rest of the group was arriving behind him. Voices stopped when people saw the room. Nobody who came in after Milim had heard the private day, the conversation about telling, or the kiss itself. They saw a couple standing too close to be mistaken for accidental company, and two grandparents trying to understand the answer they had just received.

Ori looked from Yuta to Frieren.

"Are we in trouble?" she asked. ᴼ

"No," Frieren said. ᶠ

She lifted the child's bag and took her toward the stairs, pausing long enough to ask Fern whether she could bring the other coat. Fern nodded and followed.

Mau watched them go without expecting Frieren to turn back and make the room easier for him. She had a child to settle after a long walk. This conversation did not need Ori standing in the middle of it.

At the foot of the stairs, Frieren did look back once.

Her face held concern and a question. He had no answer he could offer her from across the room.

Then she went up.

* * *

"I'm not a child," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Maki turned toward her immediately.

"I know how old you are." ᴹᵏ

"Then—" ʸᵏ

"Then let me finish having a thought before you decide which argument I'm making." ᴹᵏ

Yuka closed her mouth.

Mau looked at Maki properly. She was not reaching for a weapon. Her hands were empty. He could see that she was working to keep them that way, to remain a person speaking to two adults while the older life in her remembered a much smaller girl.

"We kept it from you," he said. ᴹ

"You did." ᴹᵏ

"I know being allowed to choose doesn't make that part disappear." ᴹ

She watched him for several seconds.

Then she looked at Yuta.

He had gone very quiet.

Mau had spent so much time afraid of how his friend would look at him that he had not imagined what it would feel like if Yuta could not quite manage to look at him at all.

Mai came in from the back of the house carrying the thing she had been working on. She stopped when she saw Maki's expression.

"What happened?" ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka turned toward her.

"Mau and I—" ʸᵏ

"I can see that part," Mai said, looking at the space between their hands. "What happened?" ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka told her. There was no version of the explanation which could leave Mai believing she had been part of something she had not known existed.

Mai put down the object she was carrying.

She did not supply the joke Yuka seemed to be hoping for.

Rimuru arrived while they were still finding words. He looked at the returned bags, at the sofa, at Mau's face, and understood enough to stop asking the first question.

Yuta noticed.

"You knew." ʸᵗ

Rimuru looked at him.

"Yes." ᴿ

The room changed again.

Rimuru did not hide behind Ciel or say he had only guessed. He explained that Mau had told him after the gate. That he had advised them to speak to their grandparents.

Yuta listened without moving.

"You and I can talk too," he said at the end. ʸᵗ

Rimuru nodded.

"Yes." ᴿ

* * *

They did not finish it in the common room.

There were people returning with bags, food to put away, a child upstairs asking why the adults were upset, and too many listeners who did not need every answer merely because the doorway was open.

Yuta set the last bag down and asked Mau whether they could talk after the meal.

"You, me, Maki." ʸᵗ

Yuka moved nearer.

"I should be there." ʸᵏ

"You and I are going to talk," Maki told her. "I'm not putting all of this on him. There are things Yuta needs to ask his friend." ᴹᵏ

Yuka looked at Mau.

He nodded, although he would have liked her beside him. That seemed like another comfort he could not require everyone else to provide before he would answer.

"Okay," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru offered the downstairs office. Yuta accepted the room without making the offer into reconciliation.

Milim was standing near the doorway with the bag she had finally picked up.

"I didn't know it was a secret," she said. ᴹⁱ

Yuka looked at her. The anger which could have made this somebody else's fault was available for a second, easy and useless.

She let it go.

"I know," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau sat down on the end of the sofa while people began to move again.

The books remained on the low table. The cushion was where his shoulder had been. A cup stood beside the cards, with a little tea left at the bottom.

Nothing in the room had prepared itself to remember a different day.
