*When: Day475, evening. Rimuru has moved the spare papers off the two chairs and left his office to them.*

Mau sat nearest the door.

He had not chosen the place as an escape route. It was the chair left after Maki sat beside the desk and Yuta pulled the other one away from the wall. Knowing that did not stop him noticing how little of the room lay between him and the corridor.

Yuta closed the door without locking it.

For a moment all three listened to the ordinary sounds beyond it. A plate being carried away. Someone calling upstairs. The house was still there, full of people who knew more than they had known before the walk.

"I don't need you to begin by saying you're sorry," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Mau looked up.

"I will have things to apologize for," he said. ᴹ

"Then tell me what you think they are." ʸᵗ

It was harder than being given an accusation to answer.

Mau looked at his hands. The old training bruises had faded. The burn from the gate had healed cleanly enough that he had stopped checking it every morning.

"We started something. I knew you'd want to know. I kept finding reasons not to tell you." ᴹ

Yuta watched him.

"You let me keep thinking I understood what was happening in my granddaughter's life when you knew I didn't." ʸᵗ

"Yes." ᴹ

Maki leaned back slightly.

"Don't give every answer so quickly that you can get through it before you feel it," she said. ᴹᵏ

Mau stopped.

She was right about the speed. He had prepared for this room by trying to get ahead of every question, to admit enough that nobody would need to work to make him admit it. It was another way of asking them to finish being hurt in a shape he could tolerate.

He looked at Yuta properly.

"When you came to her door," he said, "we'd been kissing. We stopped because you knocked. The coat really needed fixing, but I let you believe that was all we'd been doing." ᴹ

Yuta looked down at the space between the chairs.

Mau left the silence alone.

"I remember thinking it was good you were spending time with her," Yuta said after a while. "That she'd have people here who weren't just us. That she could ask you things she might not want to ask me." ʸᵗ

Mau could hear the generosity he had accepted without examining what it cost to leave it uninformed.

"That part was real," he said. ᴹ

Yuta looked up.

"I know. That's why this isn't as simple as you lying about where you were." ʸᵗ

Maki put one hand on the edge of the desk.

"She gets to choose who she wants. We didn't come in here to pretend she doesn't." ᴹᵏ

Mau nodded slowly.

"She's eighteen," Maki continued. "I know that in this body, in this room. I also remember being old and having her stand at the foot of my bed. I don't stop being that person because I can see she's grown." ᴹᵏ

Her voice remained level. It made the words heavier.

"I need time to put those things next to each other. I should have had it from you. From her. Not while Milim was standing in the doorway asking since when." ᴹᵏ

"I know," Mau said. Then stopped himself and tried again. "You're right. We took that time for ourselves and left you to find out." ᴹ

Maki kept looking at him. There was no approval in the look, but the conversation could continue inside it.

Yuta rubbed a thumb along the side of his hand.

"Why Rimuru?" he asked. ʸᵗ

Mau had been expecting the question. He still wanted to evade the answer.

"Because it was easier," he said. ᴹ

Yuta's expression tightened.

"He doesn't love her the way you do. I thought he'd listen without—" ᴹ

Mau stopped.

"Without what?" Yuta asked. ʸᵗ

"Without looking at me the way you're looking at me now." ᴹ

The room became very quiet.

Yuta sat back. For a moment the hurt was so plain on his face that Mau wanted to withdraw the words, replace them with a better version which left his friend's care out of the reason he had been avoided.

He did not.

"I trusted you," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"I know." ᴹ

"I'm not saying you can never be someone she loves. I'm saying you were already someone I loved. You kept coming to practice. Sitting with me. Letting me talk about her. You had chances to tell me what those conversations meant to you now." ʸᵗ

Mau looked down.

He remembered the step, the practice blade, the moment he could have spoken. He could no longer make that morning smaller than it had been.

"I was afraid of losing that," he said. ᴹ

Yuta let out a short breath.

"Then why did you leave me outside it?" ʸᵗ

There was no answer which would make fear into a thoughtful way to treat somebody.

Mau sat with that until Maki asked the next question.

"Does she know you want this after it's difficult?" ᴹᵏ

He looked at her.

"She knows I want to be with her. I don't think we've understood all of what being with each other will be. I don't want to say I have because it'll sound better here." ᴹ

Maki nodded once, barely.

"Then that's a conversation you still owe each other. Not a speech about how much you're willing to endure for her. A conversation." ᴹᵏ

Mau began to answer.

The left edge of the desk disappeared.

He blinked. It came back as a pale smear beside the darker shape of Maki's sleeve. He shifted his eyes toward it, trying to make the edge settle, and lost the position of Yuta's hand in the same movement.

"Mau?" Yuta said. ʸᵗ

He looked at his friend.

The middle of the face was clear. The rest would not stay where he expected it.

"Sorry. I—" ᴹ

"Don't stand up," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Mau had not realized he was trying to.

There was something wrong with the distance between his hand and the chair. He could feel the wood under his palm, yet looking at it failed to confirm that it belonged to him.

The second beat moved through his chest.

"Something is changing," Ciel said. ᶜ

He tried to answer her. He could not decide which part to tell first.

Yuta got out of his chair.

Mau saw the movement, the whiteness of a sleeve, Maki's hand coming away from the desk. He heard somebody say his name once more.

He did not remember what happened after that.

* * *

Yuta caught him before his head reached the side of the chair.

The movement was instinctive. He went down with him, one arm behind Mau's shoulders and the other keeping his head clear of the floor. Maki moved the chair out of the way and called for Shoko so loudly that every sound outside the door stopped.

Mau's face had gone white.

Yuta said his name. There was no answer. He shifted just enough to see whether Mau was breathing and hated how much he had to work to keep his own breath from becoming the loudest thing in the room.

The door opened.

Yuka stopped on the threshold.

She saw Mau in his arms, Maki crouching beside them, the chair tipped away from where somebody ought to be sitting.

"What happened?" she said. ʸᵏ

Yuta looked up.

"What did you do to Mau?" ʸᵏ

He could not make his face do anything which might help her.

He was too frightened.
