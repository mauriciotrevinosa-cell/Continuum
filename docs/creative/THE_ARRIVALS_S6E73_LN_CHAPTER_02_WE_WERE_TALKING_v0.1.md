*When: Day475. Shoko reaches the office while Yuta is still holding Mau off the floor.*

"Move the chair," she said. ˢʰ

Maki moved it.

Yuka stepped forward and Shoko put a hand out, not looking away from Mau.

"Give me the space first." ˢʰ

Yuka stopped with both hands open at her sides. She had come running from the other room. Her mind had arrived before her body, full of the last thing she knew: her grandfather and grandmother wanted to talk to Mau, and now he was on the floor in Yuta's arms.

There had to be something between those facts.

She could not find it.

Shoko crouched. She checked Mau, told Yuta how to lower him without letting his head move, and asked the question without changing her tone.

"Tell me exactly what happened." ˢʰ

"We were talking," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

His voice sounded wrong to Yuka. Too thin, as though the rest of it were being used to keep his hands steady.

"He stopped looking at me properly. Went pale. I told him not to stand. He was gone before I could—" ʸᵗ

"He didn't hit the floor?" ˢʰ

"No." ʸᵗ

Maki answered the next questions. No technique. No blow. Nothing given to eat or drink in the room. They had been sitting and speaking until he tried to focus on something at the side of the desk.

Rimuru came through the doorway, stopped where Shoko could still work, and listened inward.

Yuka watched his face.

"Ciel noticed a change before he fell," he said. "Something inside him. She doesn't know the cause yet." ᴿ

Yuka looked at Yuta.

He was staring at Mau. There was no anger left available in his face, no argument he wanted to win. Only a fear so bare that the question she had thrown at him became unbearable to remember.

"Grandpa—" ʸᵏ

Shoko asked Rimuru to help her move Mau.

Yuka stepped back because it was the useful thing she could do.

* * *

The distance to the clinic had never seemed long before.

They moved carefully. Shoko kept speaking, short instructions to people who could follow them without having to work out the whole situation first. Rem cleared the path. Someone went for Maomao. Maki stayed close enough to help but far enough not to become another obstacle.

Yuka followed until the clinic door, where she nearly walked into Yuta when he stopped.

He let the others pass him.

His hands were empty now. He looked at them as if he could still feel the weight he had been holding.

"I'm sorry," she said. ʸᵏ

He turned toward her.

She looked at his face and found that she could not make the apology neat.

"I saw him and I thought—I didn't know what happened." ʸᵏ

Yuta swallowed.

"Neither did I." ʸᵗ

It was not forgiveness for the secret. It was an answer to what she had just said. She heard the difference and made herself stay with it.

Inside, Shoko asked for the lamp to be moved.

Both turned at once.

* * *

Frieren had Ori in her arms when the child saw them.

She had come to the top of the stairs after hearing her mother's name called from below. Frieren had reached her before she could follow the movement toward the clinic.

"Mau fell," Ori said. ᴼ

"Yes." ᶠ

"Did he hurt his head?" ᴼ

"Yuta caught him." ᶠ

Ori looked past Frieren's shoulder. She could see only the corridor and the backs of people trying to decide whether standing farther away would make them less in the way.

"Why won't he wake up?" ᴼ

Frieren adjusted the child's weight on her hip.

"Shoko is finding out." ᶠ

Ori did not like the answer. Frieren could feel it in the hand tightening around her collar. She stayed with her rather than giving her to somebody else so she could join the people at the door.

Fern came to stand beside them with the coat Ori had dropped.

"I'll bring you what they know," she told Frieren quietly. ᶠᵉ

Frieren nodded.

She carried Ori back upstairs, stopping at the turn when the child asked one more question.

"Can we be quiet for him?" ᴼ

Frieren looked down at her.

"We can." ᶠ

Ori pressed her cheek against her mother's shoulder and let herself be taken to the room.

* * *

Mau's shirt was open at the chest so Shoko could examine him.

The skin was ordinary. No wound beneath the place Yuka had imagined someone might have struck him, no blood hidden by the fabric. The fact should have helped. It only moved the fear somewhere she could not point to.

Maomao worked beside Shoko. Rimuru repeated Ciel's observations, careful to say when she was describing something she had measured and when she was uncertain.

The new rhythm had changed during the conversation. It had not stopped when Mau lost awareness. His breathing continued. His body was becoming warmer, slowly enough that each examination mattered more than the last one alone.

Shoko did what she could establish a reason to do. She did not pour more power into the unknown part merely because everyone in the room wanted something to happen.

Gojo arrived and stood near the wall until she asked him to look.

He removed the blindfold.

Yuka watched the little changes in his face and understood why Mau had said it was exhausting to be examined by people who could see something they could not explain.

"Different from this morning," Gojo said. ᴳ

Shoko made him be specific.

He was. The specifics did not become a name.

Ciel asked Rimuru for a moment of silence while she compared what she had observed before and after. He repeated the request, and the room quieted at once.

Yuka could hear Mau breathing.

She put her own hand against the flower at her wrist and waited.

* * *

It was later when Shoko let her sit beside him.

Not because the problem had been solved. Because there was a chair which did not block anybody's work and Mau's hand lay where somebody could hold it without interfering with anything they were checking.

Yuka sat carefully.

She had held that hand all afternoon. While he turned a card over, while they watched the room grow quiet, while they agreed to tell her grandparents together. It was difficult to understand why doing the same thing now required asking where the chair should go.

She closed her fingers lightly around his.

"I'm here," she said. ʸᵏ

His hand did not move.

Rimuru sat on the other side. He was listening in a way which made her aware of another person working where she could not see.

"Can Ciel talk to him?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

Rimuru took a moment before answering.

"She's calling him. He isn't answering. She's still with him." ᴿ

Yuka nodded. She wanted to ask whether that meant he was safe. The question would have forced Rimuru to choose between an answer he could not know and one she did not want.

She looked back at Mau instead.

His hair lay against his forehead in the untidy way it always did after he had been lying on the sofa. She moved one strand back before it could touch his eye.

Near the door, Yuta spoke to Shoko in a voice meant to stay there.

"Should we not have had the conversation?" ʸᵗ

Shoko looked at him.

"You didn't know this was going to happen. I don't know why it happened. I'm not going to turn an argument into a diagnosis because it was the last thing in the room." ˢʰ

Yuta stayed very still.

"You caught him," she added. "That helped." ˢʰ

He nodded once, with difficulty.

Yuka looked down before he could see she had heard.

In her hand, Mau's fingers twitched.

She leaned forward at once.

"Mau?" ʸᵏ

His eyelids moved. His mouth opened a little.

The word he tried to say was too quiet to understand.

Shoko came back to the bedside. Yuka made room without letting go, and waited for him to try again.
