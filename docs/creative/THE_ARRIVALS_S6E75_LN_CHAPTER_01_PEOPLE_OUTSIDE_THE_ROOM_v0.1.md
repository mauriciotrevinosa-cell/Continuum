*When: Day477. Yuta finds Rimuru washing a cup he has already washed.*

He stood beside the kitchen doorway for a moment before saying anything.

Rimuru looked down at the cup, realized what he was doing, and set it on the cloth with the others.

"How is he?" Yuta asked. ʸᵗ

"Same fever. A few more words. Shoko's comparing the times." ᴿ

Yuta nodded.

Neither moved to leave.

In the common room somebody was putting the chairs back after breakfast. The sound gave them small places to pause without making every pause an accusation.

"You should have told me," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Rimuru dried his hands slowly.

"I told him to talk to you." ᴿ

"I heard you say that." ʸᵗ

Rimuru looked up.

"I didn't come here to have you repeat what you said in the room. I want to know what you thought you were doing." ʸᵗ

Rimuru put the cloth down.

He had imagined this conversation often enough since the outing returned. In most versions he began by explaining why a private confidence could not simply be turned into someone else's announcement. It was an argument he still believed. It was also an easier place to stand than the one Yuta had asked him to occupy.

"I thought they needed a little time," he said. ᴿ

"They had it." ʸᵗ

"Yes." ᴿ

Yuta looked at the cup on the cloth.

"I don't need you to bring me every private thing someone says about my family. I'm not asking to be the last person everybody has to approve their feelings through." ʸᵗ

Rimuru waited.

"You saw me talking to him. All those times. You knew what I didn't know. At some point that stopped being only his conversation." ʸᵗ

Rimuru looked toward the empty doorway, then back.

"I should have pressed him about when, instead of letting soon keep meaning something," he said. ᴿ

Yuta's expression did not soften much. The answer had reached the part he asked about, and that was different from making it all right.

"Would you have told me if they hadn't?" ʸᵗ

Rimuru took a breath.

"I don't have an honest answer that makes me look good. I hadn't decided what I'd do if he kept putting it off. I should have decided before I kept standing beside you with it." ᴿ

Yuta watched him for a long moment.

"I wish you'd said that first." ʸᵗ

Rimuru nodded.

They stood in the kitchen until the scrape of the chairs stopped.

Then Yuta asked which time Shoko wanted the next cloths brought in, and Rimuru answered. They could do that together while the rest remained difficult.

* * *

Mai found Yuka at the back step with her hair still wet from washing.

She wore Mau's black-and-orange McLaren hoodie. Maki had brought it clean from his things, and Yuka had finally changed after Shoko told her she would be more useful with some sleep and less resentment toward the existence of water. The sleeves came past her knuckles; she had folded them back twice before giving up.

Mai looked at the name across her chest, then at the sleeves gathered in her hands.

"Granny brought you that one?" ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka nodded. She smoothed the front before noticing what she was doing.

"It was in his things. I didn't ask her for it." ʸᵏ

Mai caught the edge of a cuff and turned it back once more. This fold stayed.

"There. Now you can hold something without wearing it afterward." ᴹᵃⁱ

She held out a cup.

Yuka took it, both hands tucked round the warmth.

"He'll want it back," she said after a moment. ʸᵏ

Mai looked toward the clinic door.

"Then he can ask you for it." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka looked that way too. She tried to imagine him awake enough to complain about his sleeves, and held on to the little picture until she could look back down at the cup.

"Thanks." ʸᵏ

Mai sat one step below, leaving room for her legs without requiring her to move.

They watched someone cross the yard carrying a basket. A door opened at the workshop. Further away, a laugh rose and broke off in conversation. The sound was strange to Yuka, not because anybody had done something wrong by laughing, but because it belonged to a part of the day which had no bed in it.

"You can laugh at something," Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka turned.

"I wasn't—" ʸᵏ

"You look guilty every time someone remembers there are other rooms." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka stared into the cup.

"I don't know what to do when I'm not in there." ʸᵏ

"Drink that. Start small." ᴹᵃⁱ

She did.

Mai stayed beside her without immediately asking the question Yuka had been expecting.

That made Yuka ask it herself.

"Are you angry too?" ʸᵏ

Mai looked toward the yard.

"A bit." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka nodded.

"I know you don't have to tell me everything," Mai said. "You don't owe me the inside of your head because I'm there when your coat needs fixing." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka's grip tightened around the cup.

"But I liked being somebody you came to. I thought I was. Then everyone was standing in that room, and I found out I was behind a door I didn't even know was there." ᴹᵃⁱ

"It wasn't because I didn't trust you." ʸᵏ

Mai looked up at her.

"Then why?" ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka wanted an answer which was kinder than the truth.

"I thought you'd tell Granny." ʸᵏ

Mai let the words settle.

"So it was about what you trusted me to do." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka looked down.

She could have argued that every person who knew made it harder to choose the telling. She had made that argument to herself more than once. Sitting beside Mai, it sounded like turning the people who might care into hazards on the way to being happy.

"I'm sorry," she said. ʸᵏ

Mai rubbed a thumb along the edge of the step.

"I'm not saying I'd have agreed with everything. You didn't give me a chance to find out what I'd say." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka nodded.

For a while they drank in silence.

"Are you going to be like this forever?" Yuka asked at last. ʸᵏ

Mai looked back at her.

"Sitting on steps? Probably. It's convenient." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka laughed before she could stop herself.

Mai's mouth moved at one corner.

"No," she said. "I'm not planning to be angry forever. Let me finish being it before you ask me to promise the exact afternoon." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka looked down, smiling a little through the sting behind her eyes.

"Okay." ʸᵏ

Mai stayed until the cup was empty.

When they went back inside, she took it from Yuka and said she would put it away. It was a small, familiar act. Yuka let it remain small instead of making it stand for forgiveness Mai had not offered yet.

Mau had moved in his sleep.

There was light caught near his left temple.

She changed the angle of her hand, expecting the pale strand to darken when her shadow crossed it.

A few strands lay against the rest, white from where they met the skin. She separated them gently with the tip of a finger. There were more underneath.

"Shoko." ʸᵏ

Shoko came to stand beside her. She looked where Yuka pointed, moved the surrounding hair carefully, and stopped writing the answer into her face before she knew what the answer was.

"Was that there this morning?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

"I didn't see it." ˢʰ

"Neither did I." ʸᵏ

Yuka put her hand down beside his, very carefully, and wondered how many times a person could discover a new way to be frightened by a face she knew.
