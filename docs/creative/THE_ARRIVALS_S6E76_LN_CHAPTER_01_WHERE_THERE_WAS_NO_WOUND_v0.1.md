## Chapter 27 — Where There Was No Wound

*When: Day478, morning. Maomao stops while helping Shoko change his shirt.*

She did not exclaim.

She put one hand against the fabric to keep it from falling back over the place she had seen, then looked at Shoko.

Shoko looked down.

A closed mark crossed the skin of Mau's torso.

It was irregular, unlike the clean little lines left by an ordinary cut. The skin had drawn itself into the shape of something which had healed after a much deeper injury. Around it there was no fresh blood, no open edge, no warmth different enough from the fever to tell them when it had happened.

Shoko had examined that place on the first night.

It had not been there.

She looked once toward the page where she had written the earlier findings, then back at the scar.

"Leave the shirt a moment," she said. ˢʰ

Maomao held it clear while Shoko checked what she could. Mau shifted at the touch and made a small, unhappy sound without waking properly.

They stopped the part which seemed to distress him.

Maomao kept the clean cloth ready while Shoko looked at the rest of the visible skin. There were other lines now, some pale, some less even, all closed. The forearm marks from yesterday no longer looked like an isolated change.

Shoko could have named them scars and still known almost nothing useful about how they came to be on this body.

She covered him again when she had finished.

"Get Rimuru," she said. ˢʰ

* * *

Yuka was eating when they told her.

Maki had sat with her long enough to make the meal a meal rather than something taken between waiting for footsteps. Neither had tried to finish the conversation about Mau while his condition kept changing down the corridor.

When Rimuru came, Yuka put the spoon down.

"He's breathing. Shoko is with him," he said first. ᴿ

She watched his face.

"What changed?" ʸᵏ

He told her.

Maki stood, then stopped before making Yuka choose between finishing the meal and following her immediately.

Yuka pushed the plate back just far enough that it would not tip when she got up. She was learning too many small movements which belonged to bad news.

They went together.

* * *

Frieren came while Shoko was comparing the histories they actually had.

There had been injuries over the years. Bruises, burns, the things one could receive learning to use a body in a world which kept asking more of it. There had been a frightening confrontation with Sukuna. There had been the search, the memory instability, the work which led to Ciel.

Mau had not been opened through the torso in any of those events here.

Maki described where she had pulled him out of danger. Yuta described the wounds he remembered seeing afterward. Maomao had treated him. Frieren had stood beside the same bed and watched the same recovery.

Their accounts did not acquire a hole merely because the skin now carried the shape of one.

"I don't remember this," Frieren said. ᶠ

"Neither do I," Shoko answered. ˢʰ

Frieren looked at Mau's face. The white area near the left temple had become large enough that she could see it without moving the surrounding hair. His eyes were closed. He looked both younger than the marks and older than he had at breakfast three days ago, though she knew that was an impression made from worry, not a measurement.

"Can it be something he remembers?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

Shoko looked toward her.

"A memory putting a scar there?" ʸᵏ

"I can't rule things out just because I don't know how they'd work in this world. I also can't tell you yes because it would give the change a name." ˢʰ

Yuka nodded, frustrated but listening.

Rimuru took a moment with Ciel before speaking.

"She can tell it is part of what has been changing. She can't date a history she hasn't seen. She isn't finding an answer by looking through his thoughts." ᴿ

Frieren rested her hands in her lap.

"Then we wait for him to tell us what he can." ᶠ

Shoko nodded.

Waiting was not the same as doing nothing. There were signs to follow, water to offer when he could swallow, discomfort to reduce, people to keep from spending every hour standing in the way of the people doing those things.

It was simply the part of the work nobody could make faster by being clever.

* * *

He woke near midday.

Yuka had taken the chair again. Maki sat at the table outside, close enough to be reached, and Shoko was folding the clean shirt they had not managed to put on him earlier.

Mau's eyes opened without the first violent flinch from the light.

He looked at the ceiling.

Then at the curtain.

His right hand moved toward his chest.

Shoko set the shirt down.

"Mau. Does that hurt?" ˢʰ

His fingers stopped on the blanket.

He looked at her, taking a moment to connect the face with the voice.

"Shoko." ᴹ

"Yes." ˢʰ

Yuka sat forward, holding herself back from saying his name over the top of hers.

"Your chest," Shoko said. "Tell me what you're feeling." ˢʰ

Mau looked down as though he could see through the cloth.

His face changed.

It was not the expression Yuka expected from discovering something new. There was a small, weary recognition in it, followed by confusion when he looked at the room again.

"Old," he said. ᴹ

Shoko stayed still.

"What is old?" ˢʰ

He did not answer immediately. His fingers moved against the blanket, then stopped.

"It's not—" ᴹ

The sentence failed.

He closed his eyes and drew in a breath too shallow to satisfy either person watching him.

Shoko gave him time, then asked one more simple question. He did not reach it. Sleep took the effort from his face by slow degrees, leaving his hand where he had tried to find the mark.

Yuka looked up at Shoko.

"He knew it." ʸᵏ

Shoko watched the hand settle.

"He recognized something. I want him to tell us what before we decide what it means." ˢʰ

Yuka nodded.

On the table outside, Maki heard the voices quiet and waited rather than coming in to make them repeat the attempt.

Inside, Shoko wrote down the word exactly as he had said it.

Old.
