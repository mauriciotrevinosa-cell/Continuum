## Chapter 28 — The Fort Has Room

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Codex, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 35

*When: Day478, afternoon. Umaru has moved one cushion and considers this sufficient preparation for an international reception.*

"Here," she said. ᵁ

Yuka looked at the space.

The Fort contained more space than it looked capable of containing from outside. There were cushions, a place people used as a booth, little boundaries which mattered intensely until somebody fell asleep across them. Light came through a gap Ori called a window. Someone had left a pencil on a folded page, marking a dispute which had apparently been suspended rather than settled.

Yuka knew all of it. She had sat there, argued there, helped rescue things from beneath the cushions. Today the familiar opening seemed to require a decision she had forgotten how to make.

Umaru patted the cushion again.

"You can sit without becoming a permanent citizen of the floor." ᵁ

Yuka went in.

Shizuka moved her book so there was room for Yuka's feet. Nano adjusted the cup on the low surface between them with a precision which made it unlikely anyone would knock it over by mistake. Bocchi had a notebook near her knee and the anxious look of somebody who had prepared three possible greetings and lost all of them when the guest arrived.

"Hi," Yuka said, saving her the choice. ʸᵏ

"Hi." ᴮ

Umaru gave her a folded blanket.

"You don't have to use it. It's just there." ᵁ

Yuka took it and put it over her knees.

For a few moments nobody asked about the clinic.

The absence of the question made her eyes sting.

* * *

They were trying to decide what to play later in the week.

Bocchi had a suggestion which needed her to sing a line under her breath, stop, correct the way she had written something, and begin again. Umaru listened with the expression of someone who did not know the technical names but knew exactly which version she preferred.

Nano asked a question about the change in timing. Bocchi answered it more easily once the question belonged to something she understood.

Yuka listened.

The little piece of music had a turn in it which seemed as if it ought to go one way and then went somewhere warmer. She noticed it the second time, waited for it the third, and smiled before she remembered to check whether smiling was permitted today.

Umaru caught the smile and did not comment on it.

Bocchi wrote something down.

"Mau would get that part wrong," she said, almost to herself. ᴮ

Umaru looked over.

"He'd insist it was a different version." ᵁ

Bocchi laughed quietly.

Yuka looked between them. They spoke with the ease of people who had a place for him in conversations which happened when he was not present. It was not the version of him she had beside the lake, or the one Yuta knew from years carried in a younger face. It belonged to them.

"He does that with cards too," she said. ʸᵏ

Umaru turned toward her with immediate interest.

They compared stories until Nano pointed out that, based on the evidence provided, Mau seemed capable of losing under several rule sets simultaneously.

Yuka laughed.

This time she let it finish.

* * *

Milim arrived carrying something which had to be placed carefully outside the opening before she could come in.

Yuka looked up.

Milim stopped.

The hesitation was so unlike her that everybody noticed, though nobody made her stand inside the notice longer than necessary. Umaru moved her own foot out of the way.

"There's room," she said. ᵁ

Milim came in and sat cross-legged, tall enough that the Fort's height mattered. She looked at Yuka, then at the blanket.

"How is he?" ᴹⁱ

Yuka told her what Shoko had said when she stepped out. Fever lower at the last check. Still not awake properly. They were watching the changes. It was the honest report, short enough that she could give it without having to begin the fear again from the first night.

Milim nodded, looking down at her own hands.

"I didn't mean to make it bad," she said. ᴹⁱ

Yuka shifted on the cushion.

"You didn't make him sick." ʸᵏ

"I mean the other part." ᴹⁱ

Umaru let the notebook fall closed without making the movement sound important.

Yuka looked at Milim. She could still remember the exact force of the words through the open doorway, the absurd wish that she could catch them before they reached her grandparents.

She could also remember having weeks in which to say the same thing herself.

"You saw something you weren't expecting. You didn't know we hadn't told them." ʸᵏ

Milim looked up.

"I know things can be private," she said. ᴹⁱ

"I know you do." ʸᵏ

The answer mattered. Milim was not merely a child to be told she could not understand what the grown people were doing. She had made a mistake, and Yuka could let it be a mistake without giving it the whole weight of what followed.

"I'm still embarrassed," Yuka admitted. ʸᵏ

Milim's face shifted.

"You were very close." ᴹⁱ

Yuka covered her face.

Umaru made a strangled sound. Bocchi looked at the notebook as if hiding inside a song remained a possibility.

Nano moved the cup a little farther from Yuka's elbow.

After a moment, Yuka started laughing into her hands. It hurt less than trying to keep the afternoon solemn enough to deserve being beside a sick person later.

Milim smiled, cautiously at first.

Then she reached for the cards someone had left near the booth and asked whether they were playing or merely using them as furniture.

* * *

Yuka fell asleep before the game finished.

She had meant to sit for a few minutes. Shoko knew where she was; Maki knew; someone would come if Mau woke enough to ask for her. None of those facts had felt convincing until her body decided that the cushion under her shoulder was safe enough.

The last thing she heard was Umaru disputing a rule.

When she woke, the game had moved to the other side so nobody would have to climb over her. The blanket lay higher than she remembered putting it. Bocchi sat nearby with the notebook open again, writing in the little quiet they had made.

"How long?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

Bocchi looked up.

"Not that long. You needed it." ᴮ

Yuka pushed herself onto an elbow.

"You don't have to make it sound short." ʸᵏ

Bocchi smiled a little.

"I know. People do that for me." ᴮ

Yuka sat with the blanket around her shoulders.

"You and Mau are close," she said. ʸᵏ

Bocchi looked down at the page.

"He's like—" ᴮ

She stopped, frowned at the difficulty of choosing a word which would not sound too large once it was outside her, and tried again.

"Like my brother. In the way he waits when I'm trying to say something. I don't have to make the first attempt the one that counts." ᴮ

Yuka nodded.

Bocchi looked toward the opening.

"I want him to wake up so I can show him the new bit. He won't get it right. But he always tries." ᴮ

Yuka held the blanket closer for a moment.

Then she folded it, carefully, and put it where Umaru had found it.

When she went back to the clinic, the music followed her as a half-remembered turn she could wait for next time.

Mau was sleeping. His temperature was a little lower. Shoko gave the news without making it a promise, and Yuka sat in the quiet chair knowing that a room full of other people still had a place for her if she needed to leave this one again.
