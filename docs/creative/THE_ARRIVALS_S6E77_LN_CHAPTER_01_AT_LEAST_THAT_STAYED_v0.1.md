*When: Day479, morning. The curtain is still gray to him.*

Mau looked at it for a long time.

Brown. He remembered the brown of that curtain. Remembered sunlight warming the wood beside it, green leaves moving through the gap by the window. The colors were there when he thought of the room in another life.

He opened his eyes wider.

Gray cloth. Pale scratch on a gray table. A darker patch where someone had set something wet.

He blinked, shut one eye, then the other. The soft edge on the left was familiar. This was something else. He remembered living with it here, but he remembered living without it too, and for several breaths he waited for his sight to catch up with the rest of him.

Nothing changed.

"What the fuck?" he whispered. ᴹ

He turned his head.

Yuka was asleep in the chair beside the bed.

Her head had fallen toward one shoulder. A strand of hair lay across her cheek, moving a little with each breath. There was color in her face, warmth he could actually see instead of remembering. Just her, against the gray chair and the gray room. She had changed her shirt. The blanket over her knees had been tucked around the side of the chair to keep it from slipping.

The flower at her wrist lay against her hand.

Mau looked at the cord.

He remembered buying it in a market by the sea. Remembered the purse, the paper, the effort of choosing something small enough that she could wear it without having to arrange herself around a gift.

He remembered buying it from Hollis.

The memory was just as clear. The parcel in his coat through breakfast. The lake. Her wrist held out while he tried to make the knot work and she watched his fingers.

Both had happened to him.

He lay very still.

The last room he had chosen to leave was behind his eyes too. Yuka asleep beneath a dry blanket. Rimuru close enough to catch his shoulder. Ciel saying no, clearly, with no possible way to mistake her meaning.

He remembered saying yes.

The breath he took afterward would not finish.

He tried again, slowly.

There were days in him which had been made to feel like the beginning. Faces he had known for years offered as people he had met recently. A voice inside him introduced as if he had not heard her through everything which came before. He could remember believing it. Could feel the kindness he had offered while the lie determined how far he thought he belonged.

There were other days which had never needed to be a lie. A leaking bucket. Yuta handing him a practice blade. A child letting go of his trousers so Frieren could pick her up. The smaller Trial, the coins, the first watch in the snow.

The water at the lake.

Yuka saying she was not ready.

Him moving back because she had told him.

He did not find somebody else's life sitting beside his own. He remembered being the person who had done those things. The frightened man in the chair, trying to keep his friend from looking at him differently, had been him too.

The memories would not fit into the same past.

They were all there.

His left hand moved against the blanket.

Two fingers stopped where he expected them to stop.

The familiarity of the limit nearly made him laugh. He knew that hand. Knew how to take a cup so he would not ask it to do what it could not do. Knew the softened edge at the left of his sight and the habit which kept him turning his head a little farther than other people needed to.

He knew how the mark beneath the blanket had been made.

He could remember a hand going through him.

He could remember getting up after the fight with bruised ribs and mud in his mouth, all his fingers working, Yuta laughing beside him because stopping seemed harder than laughing.

Mau shut his eyes.

"Mau?" ᶜ

The voice was careful.

He had heard it call him through days he had not managed to hold. Some of the attempts came back now as a sound behind a door he could not find the handle for.

"Mau. Can you hear me?" ᶜ

He opened his eyes again.

"Yes," he answered privately. ᴹ

The silence which followed was full of her.

Then she asked him to say it again, plainly, and he did.

"I hear you, Ciel." ᴹ

"You are answering coherently. Please don't try to sit up yet. I am telling Rimuru and Shoko that you are awake." ᶜ

Mau looked at Yuka.

The voice was hers. Ciel's. The person who had helped him count small attempts, learned how to ask questions with room for an answer, and waited on the other side of this sleep.

She had not said no beside that bed.

She had not heard the things he remembered putting another Ciel through.

For a moment the wish to explain everything rose so violently that he pressed his right hand into the blanket to keep from reaching for more than the link they actually shared.

"I'm here," he told her instead. ᴹ

"I know," she said. Then, after a pause: "Thank you for answering." ᶜ

His eyes burned.

The room sharpened all at once. Grain in the ceiling, threads in the curtain, the little place where Yuka's pulse moved beneath the cord. Too much arrived with too little distance between noticing and understanding.

He knew that problem too.

He closed the familiar part of the opening by careful degrees, letting detail settle back into ordinary things. The room stayed gray. Yuka did not. It cost attention he did not have much of. By the time he finished, he was breathing harder.

"What did you do?" Ciel asked. ᶜ

"Made it quieter. I'll explain. I need a minute." ᴹ

He looked at the curtain again. He could remember colors he could no longer find in it. All that information, and his own sight would not give him this.

"The colors haven't come back," he told her privately. ᴹ

"It looks the way you described it before?" ᶜ

"Yes. Except I remember it being different. From before this life. I thought—" He stopped and looked at Yuka. "She's still the only one." ᴹ

"I don't know why," Ciel said. "I'll ask Rimuru to tell Shoko the color problem is still there. I can't tell them what caused it." ᶜ

"Neither can I." ᴹ

She did not reach through the minute and take the explanation from him.

"I'm watching the changes I can measure," she said. "Tell me if it hurts." ᶜ

He nodded before remembering she did not need the movement.

Yuka woke at the sound of his breath.

* * *

She looked at him and sat up so quickly that the blanket slid from her knees.

"Mau?" ʸᵏ

He tried to say her name. The first attempt failed.

She was already leaning forward, one hand reaching for him and stopping just short of the blanket as though she had spent several days being told where it was safe to put it.

"Yuka," he said at last. ᴹ

Her face changed.

It was such an ordinary gift, recognizing somebody in a room. He could see what it did to her now and remember the many times he had been unable to give it.

"You're here," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

She took his right hand.

"Shoko said not to pull you up. Are you in pain? Is the light—" ʸᵏ

"Wait." ᴹ

She stopped immediately.

He held her hand as well as he could and looked at her face. This face. The girl who had spent the afternoon losing and winning at cards with him. The young woman who had told him she wanted him and could not go farther, then trusted him enough to remain.

He remembered another night too. Tears drying on a sleeve. Her asking him for children, for a life which would continue after everything they did not know how to repair.

The yes he had given still lived in him.

She had not asked him that question here.

He could not put it into her hand just because he remembered offering it.

Yuka watched him, fear beginning to mix with the relief.

"You're crying," she said. ʸᵏ

He had not noticed.

She reached for a cloth and wiped the side of his face gently, with the frown she made when trying to do something well enough that he would not have to help her through it.

The flower brushed his wrist.

He looked down.

"At least that stayed," he whispered. ᴹ

Yuka followed his gaze to the bracelet.

"What?" ʸᵏ

He swallowed. The answer was too large for the few words he could make steady.

"I need to tell you things. I don't know how to begin yet." ᴹ

She looked back at him.

He wanted to keep talking before anyone else arrived, to spend the relief quickly enough that she would not have time to decide whether the man looking at her was different from the one she had been waiting for.

He recognized the desire.

It was not a good reason to rush her.

"I'm glad you're here," he said. ᴹ

Yuka's mouth trembled.

"I said I would be." ʸᵏ

He nodded, and let her put the cloth down.

* * *

Shoko came in with Rimuru before they had found a way to say more.

She asked questions. Mau answered the ones he could answer simply: his name, the date Yuka had given him, where he was, whether he could follow her hand without forcing his eyes. When she asked about the left edge of his sight, he turned his head the little extra distance which made the object whole.

She noticed.

He saw her notice.

There would be another conversation about that. Several, probably.

Rimuru stayed near the bed without crowding him. Mau looked at him and remembered two different men in two different moments, one catching him before sleep took a room, the other holding a cup while saying he did not know how long he could keep standing beside a friend with somebody else's secret.

Both were Rimuru.

"Hi," Mau said. ᴹ

It was not enough. It was what he could manage without turning the morning into another apology someone would have to understand before he understood it himself.

Rimuru sat down.

"Hi." ᴿ

Shoko made him wait before drinking. Then she helped arrange the cup, watched him use the right hand and support it differently with the left, and asked how he knew which grip would work.

Mau looked down at his fingers.

"I remember," he said. ᴹ

She did not accept it as the whole answer.

She did let him finish the water.

Beyond the half-open door, a child called for her mother. Frieren answered at once, quiet and near. Footsteps passed, then stopped while the child explained something which apparently could not wait until they reached the stairs.

Mau held the cup very carefully.

He remembered a small voice calling for him.

He remembered this same child calling for Frieren through months in which he had never been her father.

He had loved her in both lives.

The cup remained in his hands until Shoko took it gently away.

Outside the curtain, the leaves moved in shades of gray. He remembered their green. Remembering did not put it back.

Yuka reached to straighten the blanket, and he followed the color in her face before looking back at the window.

His body had brought so much back with it. He did not understand why it had kept this too.
