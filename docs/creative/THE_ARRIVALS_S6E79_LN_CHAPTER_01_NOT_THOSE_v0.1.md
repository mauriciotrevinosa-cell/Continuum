# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 36
## Chapter 3 — Not Those

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 36
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S6E79
**Continuity state:** Day479, night, the clinic; Shoko asleep in the next room, Yuka resting in the Fort by Shoko's order, Rimuru in his loft. This branch's Ciel (CielB), privately linked to Mau and Rimuru, asks about the other life. Mau sets a limit: she may not go looking in the original memories; they would change her, and he does not want her to have to become the Ciel who held his walls through everything. To be honest about why, he lets one small fragment pass along their link with his consent: waking alone in the frost on the night of the theft, holding two cookies, and a voice behind a wall saying his name for days that he could not hear. She understands the voice was hers, in that life. She respects the limit and says so, and also says what it costs her: she is the only one inside him who does not know him all the way through. Neither pretends that is resolved. No Window, shadows, weapons, Goddess, confession or new power. The original Ciel's history is not given to CielB beyond this one fragment

*When: Day479, night. The clinic lamp is turned down to a coal. Shoko is asleep on the cot in the next room with the door open. Somewhere above, in the loft over a closet, Rimuru is not quite asleep either.*

"Can I ask you something?" said Ciel. ᶜ

Mau had been lying in the dark watching the lamp. Not sleeping. He did not think he would sleep much tonight. Every time he closed his eyes the three lives in him shifted against each other like stones in a river, and he had to open them again and find the curtain and the table and the cup and make himself believe in the room.

"Yes," he said privately. ᴹ

"The other life. The one where things went worse." ᶜ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Rimuru says I was there." ᶜ

"You were." ᴹ

She was quiet for a moment.

"Can I see it?" ᶜ

* * *

He had known she would ask.

He had known it since the morning, since the first time she said his name and he heard, under it, the voice of another Ciel saying it through a wall. He had known it since the tea, when she asked *was I different*, and he had not been able to finish the sentence.

He had thought about what he would say.

He still did not have it.

"Not those," he said. ᴹ

Ciel did not answer.

"Not the memories from there. Please." He looked at the lamp. "I'm not saying never. I don't know about never. But not now. And not looking for them. I don't want you to go looking." ᴹ

"I wouldn't take them without asking," said Ciel. ᶜ

"I know you wouldn't." ᴹ

"Then why—" ᶜ

"Because if you asked, I'd say yes." ᴹ

The quiet went on a long time.

* * *

"Tell me why," said Ciel eventually. "If you can. I'd rather understand the limit than just obey it." ᶜ

Mau almost smiled. That was her. That was both of her. Neither Ciel had ever been content to obey a rule she did not understand.

"Because they'd change you," he said. ᴹ

"Everything changes me. I learn things every day." ᶜ

"Not like this." ᴹ

He tried to find the shape of it.

"You're happy," he said. ᴹ

"Am I?" ᶜ

"I think so. Here. You count my attempts at things and you tell me when something's wrong, and you tell Rimuru when I won't, and you argue with me when you think I'm being careless." He swallowed. "And the worst thing that's ever happened to me, in this life, while you were there, is that I got a fever and you couldn't reach me for four days." ᴹ

"That was bad," said Ciel quietly. ᶜ

"I know. I know it was." ᴹ

"You don't know. You weren't awake for it." ᶜ

He stopped.

She was right. He had been the one asleep. She had been the one calling.

"I'm sorry," he said. ᴹ

"Don't be sorry. Just don't make it smaller." ᶜ

"Okay." ᴹ

* * *

He lay there and listened to Shoko breathing in the next room.

"In the other life," he said slowly, "you went through things you haven't gone through here. A lot of things. Over a long time." ᴹ

"What kind of things?" ᶜ

"The kind that change how you sound." ᴹ

He did not say *the sound of bones*. He did not say *forty seconds of dying*, or *the hive*, or *a boy blind until noon*, or *I'll tell you when you're dying*, said in a voice so level it was worse than screaming. He did not say *walls*, built over and over, every time something broke them.

"I loved you there," he said. "I love you here. You're the same person." ᴹ

"But." ᶜ

"But you'd been through more," he said. "And I don't want you to have to go through it. Even just by remembering it for me." ᴹ

The lamp ticked.

"That's what you'd be doing," he said. "If you saw it all. You'd be carrying it. And you'd never be able to put it down again. You'd be her." ᴹ

* * *

Ciel did not say anything for so long that he wondered if she had gone somewhere in herself to think.

Then:

"I don't understand," she said. "Not properly. I believe you. But I don't understand what I'm being kept from, and that makes it hard to agree to." ᶜ

Mau closed his eyes.

He had thought she might say that. He had thought about whether there was a way to let her understand without giving her all of it. One thing. Small. Hard enough to be honest. Not so hard that she would have to carry it the way the other Ciel had carried everything.

"Can I show you one?" he said. ᴹ

"You said not those." ᶜ

"I'm choosing one. Just one. Not the worst. And then I'm closing it." He opened his eyes. "If you want. You can say no." ᴹ

The quiet this time was shorter.

"Show me," she said. ᶜ

* * *

He found it the way you find a stone in a riverbed with your foot. Not the biggest. Not the sharpest. One he could lift.

Then he let it go along the thread between them, the thin line where she lived, carefully, the way he had once poured the sight of a soldier into a thimble for Anko on a back step.

Frost.

Cold all down the front of him. The taste of earth and blood. Waking face down in a dark field, alone, not knowing why. Getting up with nobody to help. Soldiers of shadow standing motionless all over the field, waiting for something.

Two cookies in his coat pocket. Still warm.

A woman with long pale hair coming across the frost toward him with her arms out, and his body stepping away from her, and the look on her face.

And under all of it, very far away, behind something, like a voice through a closed door in another part of a house: someone saying his name.

*Mau. Mau. Mau. That's Frieren. Mau, that's—*

Over and over. For days.

And him not hearing it. Hearing it the way you hear a sound in a dream, and folding it into the dream, and forgetting.

He closed it.

* * *

He felt her go still.

Not the stillness of thinking. The other kind. The kind he had felt, once, in another Ciel, on a floor of black stone, when she said *you'll die* and he said *I'm not leaving it alone in here*.

"That was me," she said. ᶜ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Behind the wall." ᶜ

"Yes." ᴹ

"For how long?" ᶜ

"Eight days." ᴹ

Ciel did not say anything.

"I couldn't hear you," he said. "I didn't know there was anything to hear. You called and called and I just—" ᴹ

"Stop," said Ciel. ᶜ

He stopped.

The lamp ticked. Shoko turned over on her cot in the next room and murmured something and was still again.

"That's the small one?" said Ciel at last. Very quietly. ᶜ

"That's the small one." ᴹ

* * *

When she spoke again, her voice had changed. Not much. A fraction. He would not have heard it if he had not spent a whole other life learning every shade of it.

"I understand now," she said. ᶜ

"I'm sorry." ᴹ

"I asked you not to make it smaller." ᶜ

"I'm not. I'm sorry anyway." ᴹ

"I know." She was quiet. "I understand why you don't want me to look. If that was the small one." ᶜ

"Will you not?" ᴹ

"I won't," said Ciel. "I'll respect it. You don't have to ask me again." ᶜ

He let out a breath he had been holding since the morning.

"Thank you," he said. ᴹ

"But I want to say something first," said Ciel. "And I want you to hear it, not fix it." ᶜ

"Okay." ᴹ

"I'm the only one inside you who doesn't know you all the way through." ᶜ

He went very still.

"Your shadows will remember. Rimuru tells me you think they will. The other life is in you, in your own head, every hour." Her voice did not shake. Somehow that was worse. "And I'm in you too. I've been in you since the beginning of this life. And there's a whole person in here I've never met. Who loved me somewhere else. Who I can't reach." ᶜ

Mau said nothing.

"I'm not angry," said Ciel. "I'm not asking you to change your mind. You're right not to show me. I can feel that you're right." She paused. "I just want you to know that it costs something. That it isn't free, keeping me out. Even for my own sake." ᶜ

"I know," he said. ᴹ

"You don't. Not yet." ᶜ

"Then I'll learn," said Mau. ᴹ

* * *

They did not resolve it.

He had thought, somewhere in the fever, that if he ever got back he would be able to fix the things he had broken. He saw now that some of them could only be carried. This was one. She would always be on the other side of something, a little. And he would always know she was there.

"Ciel," he said, after a long time. ᴹ

"Yes." ᶜ

"I'm glad it's you. Here. Now." ᴹ

She did not answer at once.

"So am I," she said. "Mostly." ᶜ

That nearly made him laugh. He did not, because he did not want to wake Shoko, and because his eyes were stinging and he did not trust what would come out.

"Go to sleep," said Ciel. "I'll stay." ᶜ

"You always say that." ᴹ

"Have I said it before?" ᶜ

He opened his mouth to say *yes, a hundred times, in a voice that had cried*.

"Yes," he said instead. "You have." ᴹ

"Then it must be true," said Ciel. ᶜ

He closed his eyes. The stones in the river shifted, and settled, and shifted again, and under them, close, not behind any wall at all, a voice he had loved in two lives kept watch while he slept.
