# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 36
## Chapter 4 — The Ones Who Remember

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (Claude, 2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 36
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S6E79
**Continuity state:** Day480, before dawn, the clinic; Shoko asleep in the next room. By habit Mau opens the private window and it is there again, the original System's fields at their last original values (level 106, 57 held, Intelligence 440, cup about fifteen-fold, 14/40 named registrations and 98 soldiers), with nothing new demonstrated and no new use. In his shadow, his shadows stir. They cannot come out uncalled, but they can look and speak. Igris, Beru (older and steadier in his speech now) and Kamish (an old, wise voice) remember everything they witnessed in the original life, up to the dark room; they do not know the branch life, because in it they never existed. They tell him they slept in the dark. Beru does not know what to call Yuka now, since there is no queen in this life; Mau tells him there is no queen, there is Yuka, and Beru decides to use her name. Kamish tells him he will have to tell the house, because his shadows cannot be the only ones who remember. CielB meets the speaking shadows for the first time through Mau. Nothing is extracted, summoned or used; Shoko does not wake

*When: Day480, the hour before dawn. Mau's old hour. He woke at it without knowing why, the way he woke at it in another life, and lay listening for a cold foot that was not there.*

He opened the window without thinking.

That was the strange part. He did not decide to. His hand did it, in the dark, the way it had done it on a hundred nights in another life when he could not sleep and wanted to look at something that made sense. A small movement of attention, the way you reach for a light switch in a room you know.

In this life he had never once done it. There had been no window to open.

It opened.

* * *

He lay very still.

The small set of fields hung in the dark in front of his eyes where nobody else could see them, faint and clean, the tower's script, exactly as he remembered. The five words. The numbers. *Intelligence: 440.* The cup below it, fifteen times deep, nearly full. Below that, the other bar, the one with no name, with its thin dark line at the bottom.

*Level 106.*

*Points held: 57.*

*Registered shadows: 14 named. Places: 40. Legion: 98.*

He read it twice.

It was all there. Every number. As if he had closed it the night before in a dark room with a girl asleep beside him, and opened it again now, and nothing had happened in between.

Something had happened in between. A whole life had happened in between.

The window did not know that. Or if it knew, it did not say.

"Mau?" Ciel's voice. Careful. ᶜ

"I'm here." ᴹ

"Something just opened. In you. I felt it. I've never felt it before." ᶜ

"I know." He swallowed. "It's mine. From before. From there." ᴹ

Ciel was quiet.

"It's very orderly," she said at last. ᶜ

He almost laughed.

"Yes," he said. "It always was." ᴹ

* * *

Then his shadow moved.

He felt it before he saw it. Not the window. Lower. Under him. The place under the bed where the dark lay thickest, where in another life a knight and an ant and an old dragon and a hundred others had lived in rows, waiting. He felt it shift, the way water shifts when something comes up from underneath.

He turned his head on the pillow.

On the floor beside the bed, in the faint coal-light of the turned-down lamp, the dark had gathered. And out of it, slowly, came the top of a helm.

Just the top. Dark iron. Then two small cold blue lights, low, looking up at him over the edge of the shadow like a man looking over a wall.

Mau stopped breathing.

"*My liege,*" said Igris. ᴵᵍ

Very quietly. Very formally. As if they had last spoken at dusk at the north end yesterday.

* * *

Mau could not say anything for a long moment.

"You remember," he said at last. It came out as barely a whisper. ᴹ

"*We are yours,*" said Igris. "*We remember what you remember of us. We were in the dark room. We were there when the world went black.*" The cold blue lights did not waver. "*And then we were in the dark for a long time.*" ᴵᵍ

"How long?" ᴹ

"*We do not know, my liege. Long. The dark has no hours.*" ᴵᵍ

Beside the helm, the shadow moved again.

A second head came up. Long black chitin, swept back. Two antennae, laid flat. Two cold blue lights, a little lower than Igris's, a little brighter.

"*My King,*" said Beru. ᴮᵉʳᵘ

His voice was different.

Mau heard it at once. It was still Beru, still that strange high timbre that had once filled a hive with *my King!* and apologised to every fence post on Anko's round. But it was quieter now. Steadier. As if something in the long dark had made him older, the way waiting makes some people older.

"*We waited,*" said Beru. "*In the dark. I did not know if you would call again.*" A pause. "*I am glad you are home.*" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

Mau put his hand over his eyes.

* * *

The third one did not come up.

He would not have fit. Not in a clinic, not out of the shadow of a narrow bed. But Mau felt him all the same, under the others, vast and slow and very old, lying in the deepest part of the dark like a hill lies in the earth.

"*Small king,*" said Kamish. ᴷᵃ

His voice came up through the floor and the frame of the bed and Mau's ribs, slow and deep, like stone moving under water.

"*You took the long way home.*" ᴷᵃ

"I know," said Mau. "I'm sorry." ᴹ

"*Do not be sorry to me. I have taken longer ways than that.*" A long pause, the length of a breath for something that size. "*Tell us where we are.*" ᴷᵃ

* * *

So he told them.

Quietly, in the dark, with Shoko asleep in the next room and the lamp ticking down to nothing. Not everything. The shape of it. A choice in a dark room. The black. A life, here, in this same house, with these same people, where none of it had happened. No tower. No floors. No Arise, no Legion, no window. No them.

"*We did not exist,*" said Igris slowly. ᴵᵍ

"Not here. Not until I came back." ᴹ

"*And in this life,*" said Kamish, "*what were you?*" ᴷᵃ

Mau thought about it.

"Quieter," he said. "Weaker. Happier, I think. Some of it." He looked at the ceiling. "I learned to hold a sword from Yuta. I never died. Nobody I loved ever had to watch me bleed out on a floor." ᴹ

The shadows were silent.

"And I fell in love with Yuka," said Mau. "Here. Properly. Slowly. Without anyone stealing anything." ᴹ

* * *

Beru had gone very still.

His antennae had come up off his skull, a little, the way they did when he was confused.

"*My King,*" he said. Carefully. ᴮᵉʳᵘ

"Yes." ᴹ

"*The little rope.*" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

Mau closed his eyes.

"*In the red gate,*" said Beru. "*In the dark. I did not know what to call her. You looked at her the way a king looks at a queen. But there was a queen. She was at home. She put the white light through me in the north field, and afterward I knelt to her.*" A long pause. "*So I called her the little rope. Because she had a rope. Because I did not know what else she was.*" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

"I remember," said Mau. ᴹ

"*And now?*" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

Mau opened his eyes and looked at the two cold blue lights at the edge of his shadow.

"There isn't a queen, Beru," he said. "Not in this life." He swallowed. "There's Yuka." ᴹ

Beru looked at him for a long time.

"*There is no queen,*" he repeated. Slowly. As if fitting the words into a place inside himself where something else had been. "*There is Yuka.*" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

"Yes." ᴹ

Beru was quiet.

Then the antennae settled, flat and calm against his skull.

"*Then I will call her by her name,*" said Beru. "*It is a good name. She has earned it.*" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

Something in Mau's chest that had been held tight since the morning let go a little.

* * *

"*There is another,*" said Igris. ᴵᵍ

Mau looked at him.

"*In you. Close.*" The cold blue lights shifted, as if looking at something none of them could see. "*Not one of us. We can feel her listening.*" ᴵᵍ

"Ciel," said Mau. ᴹ

There was a pause in his head. Then, very carefully, the way you speak to someone you have just met at a door:

"Hello," said Ciel. ᶜ

He did not know whether they could hear her. He relayed it anyway, aloud, softly.

"She says hello." ᴹ

Igris bowed his helm. Not deeply. The bow of one sentry to another.

"*Lady Ciel,*" he said. "*We know you. Or we knew someone with your name.*" A pause. "*She held the walls. Many times. When we could not.*" ᴵᵍ

Ciel was silent for a long moment.

"Tell him," she said, very quietly, "that I'm glad she had them." ᶜ

Mau told him.

Igris bowed again, a little deeper, and said nothing.

* * *

The window had gone back down while they talked. Mau had not closed it; it had simply folded itself away, the way it always had when his attention left it.

The hour was nearly over. The square of the window had begun to go from black to grey. In the next room, Shoko turned over on her cot.

"*Small king,*" said Kamish. ᴷᵃ

"Yes." ᴹ

"*You will have to tell them.*" ᴷᵃ

Mau did not answer.

"*We remember,*" said the old dragon. "*Every floor. Every name. The dark room, and the girl asleep, and the slime saying no.*" The deep voice did not hurry. "*We cannot be the only ones who remember. A house where only the shadows know the truth is not a house. It is a tomb with windows.*" ᴷᵃ

"I know," said Mau. ᴹ

"*I know you know. I am old. Old things say what the young already know, so that the young can hear it said aloud.*" ᴷᵃ

Despite everything, Mau smiled.

"Thank you, Kamish." ᴹ

"*Sleep a little. The doctor will wake soon, and she will want to look at you again, and she will not like what she sees, and you will have to say* for me." ᴷᵃ

Mau laughed. Quietly. It hurt his ribs.

"You heard that?" ᴹ

"*We hear everything you hear,*" said Igris. ᴵᵍ

"*We are very proud of you for saying* for me," said Beru, solemnly. ᴮᵉʳᵘ

"Go back down," said Mau. "All of you. Before she wakes up and finds a knight's head on her clinic floor." ᴹ

"*My liege.*" ᴵᵍ

"*My King.*" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

"*Small king.*" ᴷᵃ

They went. The helm, the antennae, the cold blue lights last, sinking back into the dark under the bed like stones into still water, and the shadow on the floor was only a shadow again.

* * *

He lay in the grey light and did not sleep.

"They remember," said Ciel, after a while. ᶜ

"Yes." ᴹ

"All of it." ᶜ

"All of what they saw." ᴹ

She was quiet.

"The knight was kind," she said. ᶜ

"He always was." ᴹ

"The insect cried." ᶜ

"He does that." ᴹ

"The big one is very old." ᶜ

"He is." ᴹ

Another pause.

"I'm glad you have them," said Ciel. "The ones who remember." ᶜ

He heard what was under it. *Since I can't.*

"I'm glad I have you," he said. "The one who doesn't have to." ᴹ

Ciel did not answer that.

But in the next room Shoko sat up on her cot, and yawned, and reached for the cold cup of coffee she had left on the floor beside it the night before, and in the clinic the day began.
