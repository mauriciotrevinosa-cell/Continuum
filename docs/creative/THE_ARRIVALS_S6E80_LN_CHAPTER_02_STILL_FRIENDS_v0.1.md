**Continuity state:** Day480, night, the clinic; Mau awake, the katana in its corner, the coin in his left shirt pocket. The Noise has returned with the original Mau. She tells it the truth: she didn't know they had been friends. It answers, "Maybe," the word Mau once heard it say pleased. She says, "Then yes. Still." Mau hears both and does not intervene. *When: Day480, night. Shoko has gone to bed in the next room, after moving the sword one hand's width further into its corner and looking at it as if daring it to move back.*

He was nearly asleep when it happened.

Not asleep. That place just before it, where the edges of the room go soft and the three lives in him stop pushing against each other for a while and simply lie side by side, like stones that have finally found the bottom of a river.

Ciel was there. She was always there, now. Not speaking. Watching his heart rate, the way she had said she would, and the slow tide of the fever's last heat going out of him, and the two rhythms in his chest, the warm one and the cold one half a step behind.

And under her, deeper, at the very bottom of everything, something else stirred.

* * *

He knew what it was before it did anything.

He had known it all his life. Both lives. The thing in the dark at the bottom of his walls that watched everything and kept everything and built things out of what it watched, quietly, without asking, for years. The thing that had folded a hundred patterns small and kept them in a bag for him. The thing that had made room in a window, slowly, while everyone thought the window was finished.

The Noise.

In this life it had been quieter. It had come loose once, in the third season, and taken his name and his memories with it for a while, and then settled. Ciel had come out of that settling. She had helped it. Small things, careful things, with Rimuru's help. Pressure here, a warning there. It had never said anything to her. It had never said anything to anyone.

In the other life, it had said one word.

*Maybe.*

To him, in a frosted field in front of twenty thousand people, when he asked whether it was going to have a lot of things after this. One word. Not a voice, exactly. A shape that meant a word. And it had sounded pleased.

* * *

He felt it rise.

Not toward him. That was the strange part. He waited for it to come up the way it had in the field, to the place behind his eyes where he could hear it, and it did not.

It went to Ciel.

He felt her notice. A small sharpening of attention, the way a person turns their head when someone comes into a room behind them.

*Noise?* she said. Not to him. To it. ᶜ

Silence.

Then, very slowly, the way old things do everything, something that was not a voice made a shape in the dark that was a word.

Three words.

*Still friends?* ᴺ

* * *

Mau did not move.

He lay in the dark with his eyes closed and his hands on the blanket and did not move, because he understood, all at once and completely, what he was hearing.

The Noise remembered.

It had come back with him. It had been there in the dark room. It had been there for everything before it. And in that other life, on an afternoon in autumn when the lamps in the long room had burned blue with no oil in them, a Ciel who had watched it for more than a year without ever speaking to it had turned to it at last and said *please*.

*Help me. Slow down. Show me where it's going.*

And it had.

It had slowed where she asked. It had shown her where it was putting things. And afterward, for weeks, they had worked together. Surveys of the three accesses. Warnings when the load got too heavy. A small reversible threshold built between two doors, carefully, with Rimuru and Umaru and a hollow shell from an orchard. Ciel asking, and the Noise answering, not in words, in what it did.

That had been a friendship. Mau had not had a word for it at the time. He had one now.

And the Ciel it had been friends with was gone.

Not dead. Not anywhere. Just not here. Overwritten. A whole person in a life that did not happen.

And this Ciel had never once asked it for help with lamps burning blue.

* * *

Ciel did not answer for a long time.

Mau could feel her thinking. Carefully, the way she thought about everything. Fitting the question against what she knew, and finding that it did not fit anywhere, and turning it over to see if there was a side she had missed.

*I don't know what we were,* she said at last. ᶜ

Very gently. Not unkindly. The truth.

*In this life you came loose, and I helped you settle. We worked together a little. You never said anything to me.* A pause. *I didn't know we were friends. I didn't know you could ask.* ᶜ

The Noise was silent.

Mau felt it waiting. The way it had waited in the field. The way it waited for everything. Patient, and old, and very quiet, at the bottom of the dark.

Then, the shape of one word.

*Maybe.* ᴺ

* * *

Mau's eyes were stinging.

He knew that word. He had heard it once, in a frosted field, and it had sounded pleased.

It did not sound pleased now.

It sounded like a question. Like something holding out its hand in the dark, not sure whether anyone would take it. Like a thing that had lost the only friend it ever made, and had found someone wearing her name, and did not know whether it was allowed to ask.

*Maybe* we were. *Maybe* you were. *Maybe* you could be.

He did not say anything. He could not have, if he had wanted to. This was not his.

* * *

Ciel was quiet for a very long time.

Long enough that Mau wondered if she had gone somewhere deep inside herself to decide. Long enough that the lamp in the corner ticked down and the light on the sword's dark sheath faded to nothing.

When she spoke, her voice had changed. Only a fraction. He heard it.

*Rimuru says there was another one of me,* she said. ᶜ

Silence.

*In the other life. Mau won't let me see her. He says she'd been through more.* A pause. *I think she must have been your friend.* ᶜ

Silence. Then, faint, the shape of a word, low and unfinished, as if it did not know how to make the next one.

*Yes.* ᴺ

Ciel was quiet.

*I'm not her,* she said. ᶜ

*I know.* ᴺ

Two words. Mau had never heard it say two words in a row.

*But I'm here,* said Ciel. *And you're here. And you asked.* ᶜ

A pause, long and careful.

*So yes,* she said. *Still.* ᶜ

* * *

Mau put his hand over his eyes.

He felt it, under everything. The Noise settling. Not going quiet the way it went quiet when it was working, when the room was being made, when the lamps were burning blue. Settling the way a dog settles when it has been let back in from the cold. Turning once in the dark, slowly, and lying down.

And close to it, closer than she had ever been to it in this life, Ciel.

Not touching. There was nothing in him that could touch. But near. The way two people sit on a back step at night, not talking, close enough that their shoulders could meet if either of them moved.

Neither of them moved.

* * *

After a long time, Ciel spoke to him.

"You heard that," she said. Not a question. ᶜ

"Yes." ᴹ

"You didn't say anything." ᶜ

"It wasn't mine." ᴹ

She was quiet.

"Was she its friend? The other me?" ᶜ

He thought about the long room, and the lamps, and a voice in his head saying *please* to something that had never been asked anything in its life.

"She was the first person it ever had," he said. ᴹ

Ciel did not answer.

"And it was the first thing she ever asked for help," he said. "She'd watched it for a year. She'd never once spoken to it. And then one day she had to, because I was burning up, and she didn't know if it could hear her." He swallowed. "It could." ᴹ

"And it helped." ᶜ

"It helped." ᴹ

The quiet went on.

"I'm glad it asked," said Ciel at last. ᶜ

"So am I." ᴹ

"I'm glad I said yes." ᶜ

Mau could not answer that. He lay in the dark with his hand over his eyes and his face wet and the two hearts going in his chest, the warm one and the cold one, and under them, settled in the dark at the bottom of everything, an old thing that had been very lonely, and was a little less lonely now.

"Ciel," he said eventually. ᴹ

"Yes." ᶜ

"I think it was crying." ᴹ

She was quiet for a while.

"Maybe," said Ciel. ᶜ

He laughed. It came out broken and wet and much too loud for a clinic at night, and in the next room Shoko said something sleepy and furious and turned over, and Mau pressed his face into the pillow and laughed and cried at the same time until he could not tell which was which.

Down in the dark, very faintly, something that was not a voice made a shape that might have been a word, and might have been nothing at all.

It sounded pleased.
