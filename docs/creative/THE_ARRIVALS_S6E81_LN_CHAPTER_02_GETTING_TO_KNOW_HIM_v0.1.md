*When: Day481, afternoon. The clinic. Yuka has brought a deck, two apples, and a problem with the chair.*

The chair would not go where she wanted it.

One leg caught the edge of the rug; another met the bed. Yuka lifted it, turned it sideways, scraped the wall, and looked at Mau when he started to get up.

"Don't you dare." ʸᵏ

He sat back against the pillows.

"I wasn't going to." ᴹ

"You already had one foot out." ʸᵏ

He drew it under the blanket.

She put the chair against the bed at last, sat, and discovered she had left the cards on the other side of the room.

Mau looked at them. Then at her.

"Don't," she said. ʸᵏ

"I haven't said anything." ᴹ

"You're doing it with your face." ʸᵏ

He watched her get up again. She took the cards, the apples, the little board someone had lent her for their last game, and the knife she had left beside them. On her way back she kicked the rug flat with a satisfaction disproportionate to its offence.

"There." ʸᵏ

"Very efficient." ᴹ

She laid the board across his knees and dealt him seven cards.

"You start with six," he said. ᴹ

"Not anymore." ʸᵏ

"When did that change?" ᴹ

"When I found out you could remember what I had in my hand from two rounds ago." ʸᵏ

He picked up the cards. His left thumb could hold the fan if his right arranged it first; he had to turn the outside card farther than usual to see it properly. Yuka watched him do that, then looked quickly at her own.

He wished she had not looked away so quickly. He wished he knew what to do when she didn't.

"Do I get a new rule too?" he asked. ᴹ

"You get to lose." ʸᵏ

"That one's been available for a while." ᴹ

She put down her first card.

* * *

They played three rounds before touching the apples.

Yuka won the first, lost the second, and accused him of inventing the third. He reminded her that she had explained the rules to him herself, here in this house, and she said being right about where a terrible rule came from did not make it less terrible.

He laughed. She stayed looking at him a moment too long and missed her turn.

"Your go," he said. ᴹ

"I know." ʸᵏ

She put down the wrong card, took it back, and glared when his mouth moved.

"Don't." ʸᵏ

He held up his free hand.

She cut one of the apples on the edge of the board. It was a little soft on one side. She cut that part out and began dividing the rest into slices, which would have been easier if the deck had not been in the way.

"Let me hold that," he said. ᴹ

She passed him the cards before he could reach for the knife.

"The apple," he said. ᴹ

"You can have that when I've finished." ʸᵏ

"I'm allowed an apple." ᴹ

"I'm aware." ʸᵏ

She put a slice in his mouth.

He had an objection ready, and had to eat it first.

Yuka smiled into the board. For a moment she could do this without seeing the clinic bed under him: give him something because she wanted to, watch him make a face because she had won a small argument, take a piece herself before he could steal it. It had been easy before. Not always, but often enough that she had stopped noticing when it was.

She wanted to stop noticing again.

He took another slice. With his right hand, this time.

"I can do that part," he said. ᴹ

"Fine. Be independent. Starve." ʸᵏ

"You're holding the apple." ᴹ

"Consequences." ʸᵏ

He leaned across the board and took one from between her fingers. Not quickly. He gave her time to pull it away. She didn't, and when he sat back she had forgotten her next remark.

He was still smiling when he noticed.

"What?" ᴹ

"Nothing." ʸᵏ

She put down the knife.

"Not nothing. I just—your eyes." ʸᵏ

He waited.

"I keep expecting them to be brown when you look up." She picked up an apple pip and rolled it under her thumb. "I know they're not going to be. I watched them change. Then you look at me and I get surprised again." ʸᵏ

Mau looked down at his cards.

"You don't have to like everything that happened to me." ᴹ

"I didn't say I disliked them." ʸᵏ

"No. You didn't." ᴹ

She wanted him to look up again. She was also grateful for the moment to decide what she meant before those unfamiliar eyes were on her.

"They're very pretty," she said, with more annoyance than the compliment required. "That's not helping." ʸᵏ

He looked up.

"Sorry." ᴹ

"Don't apologise for being pretty. It's unbearable." ʸᵏ

That got him. He laughed with his head turned toward the pillow, and she watched his shoulders move and found herself smiling too.

Then he stopped laughing, caught by a pain under the old mark, and shifted carefully until it passed.

She did not ask whether he was fine. She waited until his breath settled and handed him the water.

He drank.

"I was going to ask something," she said. ʸᵏ

"You still can." ᴹ

She moved the empty plate to the little table. There was room between them now for the cards and his hands.

"Did you know me there?" ʸᵏ

* * *

He had been expecting a different question.

He did not know which. About the sword, perhaps, or how his hand knew where it belonged on a hilt that had appeared in front of her. About the scar. There were so many questions he had been rehearsing answers to that the simple one reached him without any answer prepared.

"Yes," he said. ᴹ

Yuka looked at the flower on her wrist.

"You looked at this when you woke up. You said, 'At least that stayed.'" ʸᵏ

"I remember." ᴹ

"I thought you meant it hadn't fallen off while I was asleep." ʸᵏ

He put his cards face down.

"I bought you one there too," he said. "The same flower. The same sort of cord. On your birthday." ᴹ

Her thumb found the little metal petals.

"Hollis?" ʸᵏ

"A market by the sea." ᴹ

She looked up.

He could see her putting it beside the day she remembered: the lake, the cold patch under the trees, his inability to make a convincing excuse while hiding the parcel behind his back. He remembered that day too. He could remember how pleased he had been with himself, and how quickly she had seen through him.

"Ours happened," he said. "The lake. You said I was bad at surprises and then pretended to be surprised anyway." ᴹ

"I was being generous." ʸᵏ

"You were terrible at it." ᴹ

She smiled, but it did not quite settle.

"Did we—" She stopped, then made herself finish. "Were we together? There?" ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

Yuka let out a breath.

For a little while he watched her look at the bracelet. He wanted to tell her that he knew what she was thinking, and could not, because he didn't. That was the most useful thing he could remember just now. He did not know.

"Was I different?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Some things happened differently." ᴹ

"That's what everyone says about everything." ʸᵏ

"You're right." He rubbed the edge of a card with his thumb. "You were there for things you haven't seen here. We made choices we didn't make here. There are things I need to explain properly. About me, about Frieren, about how I ended up with both lives. I don't want to give you a nice little version and let you find the rest somewhere else." ᴹ

She looked at him sharply at Frieren's name. He did not look away.

"But you don't want to tell me now." ʸᵏ

"I don't think I can tell it properly yet. I keep starting in the middle." ᴹ

She considered that. The cards lay between them in untidy little heaps. He had managed the game perfectly well. The story he was trying to tell her seemed to require something he had not got back with the rest.

"Were you happy with me?" she asked. ʸᵏ

His answer came before he could make it careful.

"Yes." ᴹ

Her face moved. A small relief, followed almost immediately by something harder.

"And now?" ʸᵏ

He reached out his right hand and stopped beside hers on the board.

"Yes," he said. "When I can stop being frightened long enough to notice where I am. Yes." ᴹ

Yuka put her hand in his.

He had remembered the weight of it. The actual thing was warmer.

"You can ask me," she said. ʸᵏ

"What?" ᴹ

"If you don't know what I'm thinking. You keep doing this thing where you look as if you've already decided I must be upset." ʸᵏ

"Are you?" ᴹ

"A bit." ʸᵏ

"Right." ᴹ

"That doesn't mean I want to leave." ʸᵏ

She tightened her fingers. He looked at their hands, then back at her.

"I don't know how much of you I know anymore," she said. "I liked knowing what would make you laugh. I liked being able to sneak up on you. Now you turn before I reach the door." ʸᵏ

"I can stop doing that." ᴹ

"No, you can't. You'd have to stand there pretending you hadn't heard me, and I'd know." Her mouth turned up a little. "You're not good enough at acting." ʸᵏ

"I was convincing about the lake." ᴹ

"You were holding a parcel behind your back." ʸᵏ

He conceded that with a small movement of his head.

"I want to know the rest," she said. "I might not like all of it. I'm not promising I will." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"But you can't keep sending me off to rest every time I look confused. Sometimes I'm just confused. Let me sit here while I am." ʸᵏ

He looked at the chair she had fought into place.

"Okay." ᴹ

"And don't make a face if I ask something stupid." ʸᵏ

"I won't." ᴹ

"You did when I asked if the sword was dangerous." ʸᵏ

"I was thinking about how to answer." ᴹ

"You looked like I'd asked if water was wet." ʸᵏ

He winced.

"Sorry. That one I can stop." ᴹ

"Good." ʸᵏ

She shifted the chair closer. This time the leg cleared the rug.

* * *

They returned to the game badly.

Neither could remember whose turn it was. Yuka proposed hers. Mau pointed out that she had won every disputed turn since arriving, and she said she had also moved the chair, which had to count for something.

He gave her the turn.

Halfway through it he said her name.

She waited with a card between her fingers.

"You told me something important once," he said. "In that other life. I'm still digesting everything new. All the things I remember at once." ᴹ

The card lowered.

"What did I tell you?" ʸᵏ

"Something that was yours to tell. I don't want to make you say it because I already know. Or make you wonder what else you owe me because somebody who was you trusted me with it." ᴹ

Yuka watched him. Then her gaze dropped, briefly, to her own lap.

He did not follow it with another question.

"And if I don't?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Then you don't." ᴹ

"If I don't want to talk about it?" ʸᵏ

"We can play cards." ᴹ

She held his gaze a moment longer, making sure he meant the ordinary thing too.

Then she put down the card.

"You're losing," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at the board.

He was, comprehensively.

"You gave yourself an extra turn." ᴹ

"You agreed." ʸᵏ

"Under considerable pressure." ᴹ

She collected the little pile that settled it and put it proudly beside her elbow.

"Ask me something," she said. ʸᵏ

He thought about it.

He could have asked whether she was frightened of him now. He could have asked whether she still wanted the same things, and ruined the whole afternoon trying to make her answer for a future she had not had time to imagine.

Instead he looked at the apple pip stuck to her sleeve.

"Would you rather have a terrible trip with good food, or a wonderful trip where everything tastes like that spell book?" ᴹ

Yuka considered the problem with the gravity it deserved.

"How terrible?" ʸᵏ

"Wet socks." ᴹ

"How good is the food?" ʸᵏ

"Better than my soup." ᴹ

"That's not a standard." ʸᵏ

He put a hand over his chest. She ignored it.

"Wonderful trip," she decided. "I'll bring something to eat." ʸᵏ

"That's cheating." ᴹ

"You gave me time to pack." ʸᵏ

He laughed. She took the second apple, held it up so he could decide which half he wanted, and began to cut.

"The sea," he said after a while. "Would you like to see it?" ᴹ

"With you?" ʸᵏ

"With me." ᴹ

She glanced toward the door. Nobody had called her; the house was carrying on without needing this answer.

"Yes," she said. "Not today. You'd fall over on the beach and Grandma would kill us both." ʸᵏ

"Not today." ᴹ

"And we tell them." ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

She handed him his half of the apple.

"Good. I want to see you explain the transport." ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

"I haven't told you the transport." ᴹ

"You said a market by the sea. You said the same house. You have a sword that appeared out of nowhere. I don't think we're walking." ʸᵏ

He looked down at the floor beside the bed.

Yuka leaned over to see what he was looking at. There was only the ordinary dark under the bed, and one of his slippers where Shoko had pushed it out of her way.

"A slipper?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Very fast slipper." ᴹ

She hit him with the empty card packet.
