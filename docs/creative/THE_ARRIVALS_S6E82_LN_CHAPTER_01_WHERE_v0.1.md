# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 36
## Chapter 9 — Where

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 36
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S6E82
**Continuity state:** Day482, afternoon. Mau and Yuka resume the interrupted family conversation with Yuta and Maki in Rimuru's downstairs office, with Rimuru's prior agreement to use it. Both take responsibility for the long concealment without treating the collapse as forgiveness. They propose a Day485 coast outing with Kaisel, whose return Mau now discloses to both grandparents. They give the approximate flight route from his remembered original trip, planned stops and return before the evening kitchen cleanup; a test, medical clearance and fresh morning check remain necessary. No outing today, no full house confession, blanket forgiveness, compulsory chaperone or new power demonstrated

*When: Day482, afternoon. Rimuru's office downstairs. The ink has not dried on his note asking people not to put cups on the large map.*

Maki put her cup on the small map.

Rimuru looked at it, opened his mouth, and met her eye.

"That one too," he said. ᴿ

She moved it onto the saucer beside Yuta's elbow.

"You need a bigger table." ᴹᵏ

"I need people to use the empty bit of this one." ᴿ

There was, in fairness, very little empty bit. He cleared a stack of copied figures from the nearest chair, carried it outside, and left them the room as agreed. His last look at Mau was a question. Mau nodded. Rimuru closed the door behind him.

For a moment none of the four sat down.

The last time they had come in here together, Mau had still had brown eyes. Yuta could remember the exact place beside the chair where his knees had started to go.

Mau pulled that chair out and sat in it before somebody could move it for him.

Yuka took the one beside him.

"We wanted to finish talking," she said. ʸᵏ

Maki looked at Yuta. He nodded, and they sat opposite.

* * *

Mau had prepared an opening. He had tried it privately, twice, before coming downstairs.

Looking at Yuta, he could not use it.

"I remember what you said," he began. "Before I fell. About going out to train with me, and thinking we were talking about everything." ᴹ

Yuta laid his hands on the table.

"We weren't talking about everything," Mau said. "I let you think we were. You didn't get a chance to finish being angry about it. Then you had to catch me." ᴹ

The fingers on the table moved against each other.

"I didn't have to choose," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"I know." ᴹ

"Do you?" ʸᵗ

Mau looked at him properly.

Yuta had come without his sword. They were in his house; there was no reason to bring it. Still, Mau noticed the absence with an ache he did not know how to explain. In another life Yuta had stood beside him with a blade ready when things went wrong. Here he had come with tea and the intention of listening to someone who had lied to him.

"I don't think you're only taking care of me because you've forgotten," Mau said. "I just don't want to use being ill to get out of what I did." ᴹ

Yuta let his hands settle.

"All right." ʸᵗ

"We kept saying we'd tell you," said Yuka. "Then we'd decide the day wasn't right, and somehow that meant we could have another whole day without telling you. I did that too." ʸᵏ

Maki's gaze stayed on her.

"Yes," she said. ᴹᵏ

Yuka swallowed. Mau wanted to put a hand over hers under the table. He kept it on his own knee until she found it herself.

"I was scared you'd say we couldn't," she said. ʸᵏ

"And you decided for us what we were going to say." ᴹᵏ

"Yes." ʸᵏ

"You gave us the worst version of it anyway." Maki leaned back. "Milim shouting across the house, everybody turning round, you two looking as if we'd arrived to arrest you. I would have liked one evening where I didn't have an audience for finding out my granddaughter's been lying to me." ᴹᵏ

Yuka looked down.

"I'm sorry." ʸᵏ

"I heard you the first time," said Maki. "I haven't decided what to do with it yet." ᴹᵏ

There was a small sound from outside, somebody laughing too near the door and then being shushed. The office was not soundproof. The whole house had never been as private as they thought it was.

Yuta turned his cup by the handle.

"I'm still angry," he said to Mau. ʸᵗ

"Okay." ᴹ

"You don't have to say okay to make it easier." ʸᵗ

Mau closed his mouth on the second one.

"I don't want you to stop coming to find me," Yuta said. "That's the part I don't know how to say without making the rest sound smaller. I can be angry with you and still want to practise in the yard. When you're able to." ʸᵗ

Mau's right hand tightened on his knee.

"I'd like that." ᴹ

"Then don't make me come and fetch you because you think you ought to stay away." ʸᵗ

Mau nodded. He could do that. He could at least start by not turning the hurt he had caused into an excuse to disappear from the person he had hurt.

Yuta watched him a moment, then reached for the cup and found Maki's beside it.

"Which one's mine?" ʸᵗ

"The one you were turning," she said. ᴹᵏ

"I've been turning both of them." ʸᵗ

Yuka made a sound, caught it, and looked mortified.

Maki gave her a flat look.

"You can laugh. Your grandfather's been doing that for years." ᴹᵏ

Yuta looked at both cups as if one might identify itself.

Mau let himself smile.

* * *

It took time to move to the other reason they had come.

Yuka asked whether Mai had gone to the workshop. Maki said yes, and that the coat Yuka had left on her bed still needed its fastening looked at. Yuka said she would take it over after this. Nobody called it a punishment. The fastening had needed looking at before any of them were angry.

Then Mau asked whether they could borrow the smaller map.

Maki moved her saucer well away from it.

"We're thinking of going out," he said. "Not today. In three days, if Shoko agrees. I want you to know where before we make arrangements." ᴹ

Yuta sat back a fraction.

Mau took the pencil. His left hand held the edge of the paper down while his right marked the river they knew. He did not draw a precise coastline. He could remember the journey, but he had followed water and hills, not surveyed them from above with instruments.

"South, following this. There's a rise where we can stop, then the river opens out. The sea's beyond it. A town with boats at one end of a long beach. I went there before." ᴹ

Maki looked from his pencil to his face.

"Before." ᴹᵏ

"In the life the scars came from." ᴹ

Yuta's attention sharpened.

Mau had intended to explain a day out. He could feel the enormous shape of what he had not told them yet taking its place at the table.

"I know that isn't enough," he said. "I'm trying to put it in order. Rimuru knows some of it. Shoko knows the injuries are remembered. I'm not asking you to take that as the whole explanation." ᴹ

"Are we waiting because you can't tell it yet," Yuta asked, "or because you're afraid of what we'll say?" ʸᵗ

Mau looked at the pencil.

"Both." ᴹ

Yuta did not seem pleased by the answer. He did seem less far away than he had when Mau started explaining.

"Then tell us when one of those is all that's left," he said. ʸᵗ

Mau nodded.

Maki tapped the unfinished line.

"How are you getting there?" ᴹᵏ

"That's the next part." ᴹ

* * *

Kaisel took more explaining than the map.

Mau gave them the shape of it first: a wyvern, one of the shadows that had returned with his memories, already his before the world changed. Not something he meant to catch for the trip. Not an untested living animal he was proposing to climb onto with their granddaughter.

He stopped there long enough for Maki to put her cup down.

"Shadows," she repeated. ᴹᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"How many?" ᴹᵏ

He told her the actual numbers. Named and soldiers, separately. He did not pretend Kaisel was the only one because a wyvern was easier to explain than an army.

Yuka turned toward him. She had seen him look under the clinic bed. She had not imagined quite so much could be under it.

"They're stored," Mau said. "They're not walking round the house. I haven't called any of them out here. Some can look out and speak to me. They remember the other life too." ᴹ

Yuta's eyes moved to the ordinary patch of shadow beside Mau's chair.

Nothing rose from it. Mau had no intention of making a demonstration in Rimuru's office.

"And you were going to tell Shoko this before asking to fly?" Maki asked. ᴹᵏ

"Yes. Rimuru too. I want to call Kaisel in the field first. See that he responds the way I remember. See how I manage riding now, while someone's here who can help." ᴹ

"Not with Yuka on him for the first try," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"No." ᴹ

"And not over the house." ᴹᵏ

"No." ᴹ

Maki looked at Yuka.

"You want to go?" ᴹᵏ

"Yes." ʸᵏ

"You haven't seen this thing." ᴹᵏ

"I'll see him before we go. If I hate it, we don't go." She glanced at Mau. "That's still an option, right?" ʸᵏ

"Of course." ᴹ

She looked back at her grandmother.

"I want a day with him where we're doing something other than watching him sleep. I'm not trying to get him to prove he's well. We can sit down on a beach as easily as here. And if he isn't well enough, we stay." ʸᵏ

Maki listened to the end. It was harder than responding to the first sentence.

Yuta took the pencil from Mau and put a small mark beside the rise.

"Here?" ʸᵗ

"Roughly." ᴹ

"Then when you land, you rest. Both ways. If you change where you're going, you come home first." ʸᵗ

"Yes." ᴹ

Maki frowned at the page.

"When?" ᴹᵏ

"After breakfast. Back before the evening kitchen's cleared. Earlier if either of us wants to come back. We'll leave the route here and tell Fern, so it doesn't become somebody's missing-person emergency." ᴹ

"If you're late, it becomes one anyway," she said. ᴹᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

"I'm telling you what will happen. Not threatening you with it." ᴹᵏ

Mau met her eye.

"I know," he said again. "That's why I'm here before we're on the beach." ᴹ

She studied him for a moment, and then the approximate route on the map, and then Yuka, who was holding his hand under the edge of the table without making any attempt to hide it.

"Show us the wyvern first," she said. ᴹᵏ

* * *

They had not made everyone happy.

Mau could see that when they stood to leave. Yuta still took too long over certain words. Maki still looked at their joined hands as if finding them there was something she had to get used to each time. An afternoon in an office did not remove the weeks they had spent excluding these two people from their lives.

But Maki asked Yuka to bring the coat before Mai left the workshop, and Yuka said she would.

At the door Yuta put a hand briefly on Mau's shoulder.

"Not tomorrow morning," he said. "The practice. We'll start with sitting down and complaining about your grip." ʸᵗ

"My grip's fine." ᴹ

Yuta looked at him.

"I've seen it," he said. ʸᵗ

Mau found he could bear the look. There was still hurt in it. There was also Yuta.

"Then I'll find something to complain about too." ᴹ

"I'm sure you will." ʸᵗ

In the corridor Yuka stopped and let go of his hand.

"Coat," she said. "I'll come find you after." ʸᵏ

He nodded. She started toward the stairs, turned back, and kissed his cheek in the corridor where anyone could see.

Then she went, very quickly, before she could find out whether anyone had.

Mau stood by the office door with the pencil still in his hand.

Rimuru came back, carrying his stack of copied figures.

"Is that mine?" he asked. ᴿ

Mau gave it to him.

Rimuru looked at his face.

"That bad?" ᴿ

"No." Mau glanced at the stairs. "No. Not that bad." ᴹ

Rimuru went into the office, put the figures down, and saw the rings on the map.

"Both of them," he said, with genuine despair. ᴿ
