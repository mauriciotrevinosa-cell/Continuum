## Chapter 10 — Let Him Land

*When: Day483, late morning. The clear field beyond the washing. Yuta has carried out a bench. Maki has brought it back because they were about to put it where the wings would go.*

"How big?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

Mau looked at the space they had left.

"Bigger than that." ᴹ

Yuta picked up the bench again.

"You could have said." ʸᵗ

"I thought you were bringing it over here." ᴹ

"You were standing over here." ʸᵗ

Maki took the other end without comment. They carried it another few paces, put it down, and looked at Mau until he nodded.

Shoko sat on it at once.

"Good. Before somebody moves it again." ˢʰ

The field was dry on top and soft underneath. Mau had walked out under his own power, slowly enough to annoy himself. He could feel where the strength of the body he remembered ended and the fatigue of this week began. Having one did not remove the other.

Rimuru stood beside him, coat lifting a little in the breeze.

"Ready?" he asked. ᴿ

Mau looked down.

His shadow lay beside his boots, entirely ordinary. For a moment he remembered a narrow bed and cold lights looking over the edge of the dark, and Beru saying he had not known whether he would be called again.

He let his attention find the one he wanted.

"Kaisel." ᴹ

* * *

The head came first.

It rose slowly enough that Maki could take a step toward Yuta before the great folded wings followed it. Shadow spread across the grass without darkening the sky. Claws found the ground, then the long body, then the tail settled clear of the washing line.

Kaisel lowered his neck.

Mau had meant to stand where he was and let everyone see. He found himself walking forward instead.

The broad dark head turned toward him. Close now, close enough to put a hand against, with all the patient attention he remembered from a hundred landings. He laid his palm on the ridge above the nostril.

Kaisel pushed into it.

Only a little. He was large enough that a little mattered. Mau put his other hand up and stood with both hands against the dark scales, head bowed, while the wyvern waited.

Behind him, no one spoke.

He had carried two people away from the shore when neither of them knew how to face the people waiting at home. He had brought them out of cold places and down from impossible heights. He had lowered this same shoulder for a boy whose hands were wrapped and stayed still while everybody argued about whether the boy could get down.

Mau had remembered all of that in bed.

Remembering had not prepared him for the weight leaning into his hands.

"Hello," he said, too softly for the bench. ᴹ

Kaisel tipped his head. Mau scratched the place beside the ridge with the fingers that could move.

"I know. It's been a while." ᴹ

* * *

Rimuru came closer when Mau beckoned him.

He stopped out of reach of the head, not from fear but because he was trying to find where all of the creature joined up. The dark body was solid to the eye and not quite solid to his other senses. Something lived in it that he had not met in this world before.

"He's beautiful," he said. ᴿ

Mau looked back, surprised and plainly pleased.

"He is." ᴹ

"Can he understand me?" ᴿ

"Try." ᴹ

Rimuru considered the head.

"Hello. I'm Rimuru. I'm supposed to stop him doing anything stupid." ᴿ

Kaisel turned one eye toward Mau.

Yuta made a small noise behind them.

"Don't encourage him," Mau said. ᴹ

"Which one?" Yuta asked. ʸᵗ

Kaisel lowered a shoulder. Mau went to it, checked where to set his foot, and caught himself reaching for a grip with the wrong two fingers. He changed hands. It was a minor adjustment, quickly done. Yuta watched it more closely than he watched the wings.

Once settled, Mau leaned forward and let the familiar balance come back.

It was not riding an animal he had to persuade. It was asking someone who had done this with him before to do it once more.

"Just up and down," he said. ᴹ

The wings opened.

Shoko put a hand over her cup.

Grass flattened in two broad sweeps. Kaisel rose above the field, steady, a few lengths and no more, and held there while Mau found the rhythm of the movement under him.

The house looked wrong from here.

Not its shape. The tree stood where it belonged, with the house around the roots, and the yard, and the smoking chimney. The sight was so familiar that his body expected other things: Frieren beside him, Ori calling from below, a list that needed a name crossed out before they could leave.

He looked down at the bench instead.

Shoko's cup. Maki's folded arms. Yuta shading his eyes. Rimuru waiting with his head tipped back.

Here.

"Down," he told Kaisel. ᴹ

The landing barely shook him.

* * *

They did it twice.

Then Mau got off, sat on the bench, and discovered that Shoko had put his water where he would have to take it before he could sit comfortably.

"Subtle," he said. ᴹ

"I gave up on subtle before I met you." ˢʰ

He drank.

She checked what she needed to check without making a performance of it. He had not become paler. The two rhythms had not changed with the flight. His hand was warm when she took it, and he answered properly when she asked what he had felt.

Maki stood a few paces from Kaisel's head.

"Does he bite?" she asked. ᴹᵏ

"Not you." ᴹ

She looked over her shoulder.

"That's an answer with too much room in it." ᴹᵏ

"He won't bite you," Mau corrected. "He knows I don't want anyone hurt." ᴹ

She studied the creature another moment, then stepped closer and put her hand against his neck. Kaisel stayed still. He had been given no reason to do anything else.

Yuta joined her, holding out his hand first. Maki murmured something Mau could not hear. Yuta glanced at her, then at him, and the two of them stood with a hand each on the dark scales.

Mau looked away before they could see what that did to him.

* * *

The longer circuit went out over the field and came back along the river.

Rimuru flew beside them, far enough from the wings to keep his hair. For the first minute he talked too much, mostly about how much easier this would be if Mau were smaller and portable. Mau asked whether this was professional medical advice. Rimuru said no, it was a longstanding personal complaint.

Then the wind took the easy conversation apart and they flew quietly.

The river turned under them.

For a few breaths Mau enjoyed it without having to work at enjoying it. There was cold air on his cheeks and Kaisel's back under him, a line of dark trees, and Rimuru looking absurdly pleased at being above his own office.

Then Kaisel's wing shadow passed over a white patch of bank.

Smoke.

Not a thought of smoke. Smoke in his mouth, thick and hot, and his left hand slipping because the new skin had opened again. A voice calling that he needed to land. A whole other body remembering a flight home after something terrible.

He gripped the ridge.

The river was under him. Not smoke. River.

"Slower," he said. ᴹ

Kaisel eased at once.

Rimuru moved nearer, saw his face, and did not touch him in the air.

"Field?" he asked. ᴿ

Mau nodded. Pointed to the one they had left.

He stayed with the shape of Kaisel's neck until they landed. Counted the wingbeats without meaning to, then stopped counting because the grass was moving beneath them and he knew where he was.

Rimuru touched down first.

"Let him land," he said when Yuta started forward. "He's coming down." ᴿ

Mau climbed off. Slowly. His foot found the ground in the right place.

He stood a moment with a hand on Kaisel's shoulder, then went to the bench.

Shoko was already moving over.

* * *

"What happened?" she asked. ˢʰ

"I remembered something. Flying when I was hurt." ᴹ

"Are you hurt now?" ˢʰ

"No new hurt. I thought I was there for a second." ᴹ

She listened, then took his wrist.

Rimuru crouched beside the bench so Mau did not have to keep looking up.

"You asked him to slow down," he said. "You picked the field. You landed." ᴿ

"Yes." ᴹ

"I'm saying what I saw. I'm not telling you it wasn't bad." ᴿ

Mau nodded.

Yuta brought the water. Maki stayed by Kaisel, one hand on the lowered neck, keeping a space clear without seeming to do anything at all.

In his head, Ciel asked whether he wanted to stop. She had felt the change in his breathing. She did not go looking for the picture that made it happen.

*For today,* he told her. ᴹ

*I'll tell Rimuru you said that,* she said. ᶜ

He was glad she said it before she did. He was glad he did not want to argue.

*Tell him.* ᴹ

Rimuru's eyes shifted toward him, then softened.

"That's enough for today," he said aloud. ᴿ

* * *

Yuka found them on the way back from taking her coat to the workshop.

She came round the end of the house, saw the wings, and stopped so abruptly that the coat fell off her arm.

Kaisel turned his head.

Mau had been thinking about smoke. Now he watched Yuka pick up the coat without taking her eyes off the wyvern, miss the sleeve she wanted, and make a second attempt.

"That's not a slipper," she said. ʸᵏ

"I was going to tell you." ᴹ

"Were you." ʸᵏ

She came closer. Carefully at first, then with the particular determination she used when she was not going to let anyone see that she was nervous.

"Kaisel," Mau said. "Yuka." ᴹ

The narrow head lowered toward her.

She stepped back, looked at Mau, and found him waiting instead of laughing. She moved forward again and put her palm where his was.

Kaisel held still.

"He's warm," she said, surprised. ʸᵏ

"He's here," Mau said. "You can touch him." ᴹ

She ran her hand a little way along the neck. The dark scales caught no sun, but she could see the edges clearly, the great structure folded under the stillness. He was watching her with the patience of something that had carried her before.

She did not know that. She knew only that he seemed to have decided she could take her time.

"Hello," she said. ʸᵏ

One wing adjusted. She looked up at it and laughed under her breath.

"We're going on him?" ʸᵏ

"If you want to." ᴹ

"Yes," she said, before he had finished. ʸᵏ

Then she looked back at Mau and the water in his hand and the way Rimuru had stayed beside the bench.

"Are you all right?" ʸᵏ

He told her what had happened. The brief version, but the actual thing, without calling it nothing.

She listened with her hand still against Kaisel.

"We can wait longer," she said. ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

Shoko put the cup back into his hand before he could stand.

"Tomorrow is quiet," she said. "We see how you are the morning after. Then we talk about the coast again. I want him able to ask for a stop before he needs one, and I want both of you willing to come home with half a day left." ˢʰ

"We can do that," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Shoko looked at Mau.

"We can," he said. ᴹ

Kaisel stayed while they argued about lunch.

It was his fault. He was lying across the shortest path to the kitchen, and Milim had come out to see why everyone was taking so long and immediately tried to climb onto him. Maki caught her by the back of her clothes before she reached the wing.

"Later." ᴹᵏ

"He's a dragon." ᴹⁱ

"I can see him." ᴹᵏ

"I know dragons." ᴹⁱ

"Then you know he can wait until you've washed your hands." ᴹᵏ

Milim looked down at her hands. She appeared to discover dirt there for the first time.

Rimuru laughed. That made her furious with him, which took care of getting her back toward the house.

Yuka stayed beside Mau while the others carried in the bench. He watched them and did not offer to take an end.

"Are you coming?" she asked Kaisel. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at the kitchen door, then at the span of the folded wings.

"I'd like to hear you explain that to Momo." ᴹ

Yuka gave the dark neck one last stroke.

"Another time," she told him. ʸᵏ

Kaisel lowered his head to Mau's hand once more before he went down. The tail disappeared last, pulling out of the grass without flattening it further. The way to the kitchen was only a path again.

Yuka looked at the place where he had been, then slipped her hand into Mau's right.

They went in slowly. From the doorway Milim announced that her hands were clean and therefore she was ready to go back outside. Momo said that was wonderful, she could carry the plates.

Mau sat down before Yuka could point at the chair.

She pointed at it anyway.
