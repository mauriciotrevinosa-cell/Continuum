## Chapter 11 — A Bag for One Day

*When: Day484, afternoon. Mau's east bedroom. His boots are where he left them before he fell. One has been cleaned. The other has not.*

Yuka held them up by their tops.

"Which one of these did you get bored during?" ʸᵏ

Mau looked from one boot to the other.

"The second one, apparently." ᴹ

"That narrows it down." ʸᵏ

She put them beside the chair, clean one first, as if that might shame the other into improving. The bag lay open on the bed. So far it contained two folded cloths, an empty bottle, a little pouch for coins, and the wrong shirt.

Mau had put the shirt in. Yuka had taken it out. He had put it in again while she was considering the boots.

"It's warm," he said when she found it. ᴹ

"Exactly. We're going somewhere warm." ʸᵏ

"It'll be cold on the way." ᴹ

"That's why we're taking coats." ʸᵏ

He took the shirt from her, held it up, and judged it afresh. It had looked entirely reasonable in his hands a minute ago. It now looked as if it belonged on a much longer expedition.

"One day," she reminded him. ʸᵏ

He put it on the chair.

Yuka watched him fold it. The fingers still surprised her. Less than yesterday, but she noticed how he trapped the cloth against his thigh to make a neat edge instead of asking her to do it for him.

He saw her watching and held the folded shirt out.

"Cupboard?" ᴹ

She put it away.

The room smelled of wood and the clothes he had left in it. After the clinic it felt oddly large. There was no curtain to pull round the bed, no shelf of jars, no lamp positioned for someone to look into his eyes. He had come upstairs after lunch, slowly, with Shoko behind him and Rimuru at the top doing a very poor imitation of someone who happened to be passing.

Now he could hear Rimuru moving in the loft. A drawer opened, shut, opened again. Something fell over.

"Found it," Rimuru announced to nobody downstairs. ᴿ

"Were you looking for it?" Mau asked. ᴹ

There was a pause above the closet.

"Eventually." ᴿ

Yuka bit the inside of her cheek.

"He's leaving in a minute," Mau told her quietly. ᴹ

"I heard that." ᴿ

"I know." ᴹ

Rimuru came down the ladder with an armful of papers, looked at the shirt on the chair, and added another folded cloth to their bag.

"For the sand. It goes everywhere." ᴿ

Then he left them the room. He took the papers with him. One remained on the ladder until Yuka pointed it out, and he returned for it with injured dignity.

* * *

Umaru came up when they had finally agreed on the shirts.

She carried the purse in both hands, with a little folded page tucked under the cord. Her expression suggested a state visit had gone badly and she had been sent to recover the silver.

"This is for the day," she said. ᵁ

Mau held out his hand. She did not give it to him yet.

"Fern and I agreed the amount. Not the house supplies. Not anything earmarked for winter. If you see a fascinating, once-in-a-lifetime implement that you absolutely must have, you bring home a drawing and we all suffer together." ᵁ

"What if it's a very small implement?" ᴹ

"I know how your brain works." ᵁ

Yuka put her hand out too.

Umaru gave her the purse.

"Thank you," she said. "Someone responsible." ᵁ

"She changed the rules while we were playing cards." ᴹ

"Financially responsible." ᵁ

The page inside listed the amount and left a generous blank space for what they bought. Yuka read it over, then turned it sideways.

"Why does it say no unannounced pets?" ʸᵏ

"Experience." ᵁ

Mau took the pencil from behind Umaru's ear before she could forget it there.

"Does the one we leave on count?" ᴹ

"The enormous one that came out of your shadow? I'm still deciding what form he needs." ᵁ

"A large form," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Umaru gave her an approving look, which made Mau feel hopelessly outnumbered.

"Bring back the change," she said. "And something nice to tell me that isn't about you being injured." ᵁ

He stopped playing with the pencil.

She saw it. She looked away first, toward the bag, and picked up the bottle.

"You need water in this." ᵁ

"Tomorrow morning." ᴹ

"Just checking." ᵁ

He caught the edge of her sleeve when she turned to go.

"I'll find something nice." ᴹ

She wrinkled her nose at him, embarrassed by having been taken seriously, and left before he could do it again.

* * *

Yuka put the purse in an inside pocket of the bag. Then moved it to another, tried the fastening, and moved it back.

Mau sat on the bed and waited. The walk upstairs had been enough for the afternoon. He disliked that, but he could sit and fold things and be of some use without pretending he needed to check the view from the window every few minutes.

He took the warm coin out of his shirt pocket before it could get mixed in with the outing money. Yuka watched him wrap it in a clean scrap and put it at the back of his drawer.

"Not that one?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Definitely not that one." ᴹ

She checked that the drawer shut properly, then gave him the cloths.

He folded one. She picked the other up again before he could begin.

"Can I ask about Frieren?" ʸᵏ

He laid the cloth down.

He had thought the question might come this morning, at breakfast, when Frieren carried Ori's cup to the table and put it where she could reach. Or yesterday, when he mentioned the other life in front of her grandparents. He had thought of too many possible answers and trusted none of them now.

"Yes." ᴹ

Yuka stayed standing by the bed.

"You said there were things about you and her. Things you needed to explain. I don't want to go tomorrow wondering whether I'm supposed to know what that means." ʸᵏ

He nodded. Her question was fair. He could hear it without turning it into an accusation she had not made.

"We were together," he said. "In that life. For a long time." ᴹ

She looked at the cloth in her hands.

"And when you remembered—" ʸᵏ

"I remembered loving her too." ᴹ

The drawer Rimuru had not quite closed in the loft slid shut with a soft click. Neither looked up.

"Do you still?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He did not use the version of the answer that would make tomorrow easier.

"I remember being in love with her. I remember what it felt like. That hasn't come back as wanting to be with her here. I'm still working out how to carry a whole life without asking it to choose this one for me. And she hasn't lived it with me. I don't get to put it back on her and ask her to be the person I remember." ᴹ

Yuka sat in the chair. She moved the thick shirt out of the way without looking at it.

"Were you together when we were?" ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

"It became a terrible mess," he said. "Someone took my memories. Took what I felt, too. I thought I only loved you. I did things that hurt her. And when I got a chance to have my memories back, I agreed to something I shouldn't have. I didn't know it would change all this. That doesn't make agreeing right." ᴹ

Her fingers loosened on the cloth.

"Is that why you won't tell it?" ʸᵏ

"It's one reason I'm frightened to. You deserve to know it properly, though. Not find out one piece at a time because I keep making excuses." ᴹ

He stopped there. He wanted to explain the two hearts and the dark room and the woman who had told him to wait. He could feel himself reaching for those things as if a complicated enough account might make him easier to forgive.

Yuka watched him stop.

"And she knows?" ʸᵏ

"Not this Frieren. Not yet." ᴹ

Yuka looked toward the door.

"She was laughing with Ori at lunch." ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

"I don't want that to be something we take away from her." ʸᵏ

"Neither do I." ᴹ

For a while she pulled at a loose thread on the cloth. Then she realised she was making it worse, laid it on her knee, and pressed the thread flat.

"I don't want to be the easier answer because she doesn't remember." ʸᵏ

Mau moved to the edge of the bed, stopping short of reaching for her.

"You're not easy," he said. ᴹ

Her head came up.

He winced.

"That came out wrong." ᴹ

She almost smiled. It hurt a little to see it fail.

"I loved you before I fell," he said. "Here. I remember choosing that. I remember the lake, and that awful patch of ground where we put the tent, and you trying to tell me the blanket wasn't wet when I was sitting on it." ᴹ

"It was only one corner." ʸᵏ

"My corner." ᴹ

The smile stayed for a moment this time.

"I remember that life because I lived it too," he said. "I'm not looking at you and trying to get back to somebody else. I'm here. I want to be with you. And I know saying that doesn't answer everything." ᴹ

She considered him for a long time. He could see questions he had not answered yet. He could see the beginnings of ones neither of them would be able to answer in a bedroom with a half-packed bag between them.

"I still want to go tomorrow," she said at last. "If we're going because we want a day together. I don't want it to be a test of whether I can make you happy enough." ʸᵏ

"It isn't." ᴹ

"I might ask about things. Even at the beach." ʸᵏ

"Ask." ᴹ

She looked at his outstretched hand, and put the badly folded cloth in it.

"Then finish this. You've been holding the bag open for twenty minutes and calling it helping." ʸᵏ

He laughed, unexpectedly enough that his face crumpled a little with it. She stood, came to the bed, and sat beside him while he folded.

Her shoulder touched his. For the rest of the cloth, she left it there.

* * *

They went downstairs for supper before anybody came to find them.

Mau took the stairs slowly. Yuka walked ahead with the empty bottle. She did not offer her arm until he reached the last three steps, where there was less room to pretend his legs were enjoying themselves.

In the common room someone had moved a chair into the path from the stairs. Frieren was in it, with a book in one hand and Ori leaning against her other arm, watching Fern adjust something in the little girl's sleeve.

"It catches," Ori was saying. ᴼ

"It catches because you keep pushing it into the buckle," Fern said. ᶠᵉ

"The buckle's in the way." ᴼ

"That is where buckles go." ᶠᵉ

Frieren looked up at Mau and Yuka.

"You can go round," she said, before either asked. ᶠ

"I was admiring the position," Mau said. ᴹ

"It's a very good position. People bring things past it." ᶠ

Yuka lifted the empty bottle.

"Disappointing," Frieren said. ᶠ

They went round.

At the table Yuka sat beside Mau instead of waiting to see who looked. Maki saw it, and passed her the bread. Yuta made room for the water. Further down the table Umaru leaned across somebody's plate to remind them about the change, though they had not spent anything yet.

Yuka put a piece of bread on Mau's plate. He broke it and gave half back.

"No wet corners tomorrow," he said quietly. ᴹ

"It's a beach, Oppa." ʸᵏ

He considered that while she ate his half of the bread too.
