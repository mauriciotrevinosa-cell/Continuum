# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 36
## Chapter 13 — The Wrong Side of the Castle

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (2026-10-24)
**Volume:** 36
**Reading order:** 13
**Source episode:** S6E84
**Continuity state:** Day485, afternoon, coast. A full ordinary beach afternoon for Mau/Yuka: shallow water, sand building, conversation and rest. Yuka sees his closed scars outside a medical examination; neither new injury nor cure. Mau admits he was often happy in his original life too, rather than erasing it to flatter this relationship. *When: Day485, afternoon. The beach beyond the boats. Yuka has discovered the water is warmer than the air and is being extremely smug about it.*

"You said it would be cold." ʸᵏ

"I said it might be cold." ᴹ

"That's what you said after I said it wasn't." ʸᵏ

Mau stood at the edge in the plain swimming clothes they had bought, shirt folded on the bag behind him. The sea came in, covered his feet, and withdrew before he could decide whether to concede the argument.

It was warm.

He looked at Yuka. She had put her hands on her hips. She looked insufferably pleased.

"You can say it," she told him. ʸᵏ

"I'm thinking." ᴹ

She splashed him.

Not much. Enough that he blinked and the next wave reached his shins while she was laughing.

"Well," he said. ᴹ

She backed away. Too late. He sent a little water after her with his good hand, and she made a noise that suggested he had committed a much larger offence than she had.

"You started it," he pointed out. ᴹ

"You were supposed to admit I was right." ʸᵏ

"You were right." ᴹ

"Not now. Now it doesn't count." ʸᵏ

He waded after her, laughing, and stopped when the water reached his knees. She noticed him stop and came back toward him without being asked, pretending to be interested in something under the surface.

He noticed that too.

Neither spoiled it by thanking the other.

* * *

She found a shell with a broken edge.

It was small, pale on the outside, rosy inside where the sea had opened it. She washed sand out of the hollow and held it up. Mau put out a hand to take it, then stopped when she put it against his ear instead.

"Well?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Water." ᴹ

"You're supposed to say you can hear the sea." ʸᵏ

"I can hear the actual sea. It's right there." ᴹ

She turned him by the shoulder to face the other way.

"Try harder." ʸᵏ

He stood with the little shell against his ear and the whole ocean behind him, trying very hard not to laugh.

"Much better," he said. ᴹ

"There." ʸᵏ

She took it back and listened herself. The broken edge caught a strand of her hair; he freed it with one finger before she could pull.

Her hand paused against the shell.

He was looking at the hair, making sure it was loose, with an attention she had always liked. It was harder to remember how frightened she had been of his eyes when he used them for something this small.

"Thank you," she said. ʸᵏ

He pushed the freed strand behind her ear. His fingers touched her cheek on the way back.

She let herself lean into that for a moment.

Then a wave struck them both from behind and settled the question of whether his clothes were going to stay dry.

* * *

They built too close to the water.

Yuka said this at the beginning. Mau said the damp sand would hold better. Both statements proved correct in a way neither found satisfying.

The first tower held beautifully until the sea took half its base away. The second went the same way. Mau dug a trench, then another, then found himself on the wrong side of both while Yuka sat comfortably beyond the destruction and offered suggestions.

"Higher wall," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at the pile of sand between him and the tide.

"Against the sea?" ᴹ

"You're the one who put us here." ʸᵏ

He tried to move round. She put a hand on the remaining tower.

"No. You'll step on it." ʸᵏ

"I'm trapped." ᴹ

"Your castle. Your decisions." ʸᵏ

He looked past her at the bag. At the cloth spread on dry sand. At the perfectly sensible place where they could have started.

Yuka followed his gaze.

"I did say." ʸᵏ

"You've been remarkably restrained about that." ᴹ

"I'm saving it." ʸᵏ

Another wave reached the trench. He cupped both hands to reinforce the wall, felt the sand slide through the two fingers that would not close, and swore under his breath.

Yuka stopped laughing.

She came round the castle and crouched beside him.

"Other side," she said. ʸᵏ

"What?" ᴹ

"Use your right. I'll hold it here." ʸᵏ

She put both hands against the leaking place. He packed sand into the gap with his right palm while she held it. The next wash of water stopped a little farther out.

They looked at their successful repair.

Then the tower behind them fell over.

Yuka sat back so abruptly that she nearly landed in the moat.

Mau began to laugh.

"Don't," she said. ʸᵏ

He could not stop. He tried, which made it worse, and she watched the effort break his face apart until she was laughing too.

The next wave came while neither was doing anything useful.

They let it have the castle.

* * *

He sat down on the cloth afterward and knew at once he had waited a little too long to do it.

Nothing terrible. A hollow feeling in his legs, his shoulders tired from holding himself in the same position. He accepted the bottle Yuka handed him and stayed sitting while she rescued the shell from the ruins.

She put it beside the food.

"Next one goes up here," she said. ʸᵏ

"We'll need to transport the damp sand." ᴹ

"You have an army." ʸᵏ

He looked up.

She held his gaze for a moment. Then her mouth moved.

"I'm joking. Before you ask a hundred people to build us another terrible castle." ʸᵏ

He laughed into the bottle.

She sat beside him and drew the cloth out from under her thigh. The new blue fabric had dried in little patches. Her hair was coming loose again. There was sand on the bridge of her nose and the inside of her elbow, and he thought he had never seen anyone he wanted so badly to spend a completely uneventful afternoon with.

He took the spare cloth and held it up.

"Nose." ᴹ

"Yours?" ʸᵏ

"Yours." ᴹ

She tipped her face toward him. He wiped the sand away carefully, then held the cloth where she could see the result.

"That was part of my outfit," she said. ʸᵏ

"It was excellent. Very local." ᴹ

She took the cloth away and brushed his shoulder with it. Most of the sand stayed where it was.

"Yours is more committed," she said. ʸᵏ

* * *

After a while she lay back with her head on his folded shirt.

He reached for the bag to make her a better pillow. She caught his wrist.

"I'm comfortable." ʸᵏ

He put the bag down.

"You don't have to improve every single thing," she said. ʸᵏ

"It was a very minor improvement." ᴹ

"Rejected." ʸᵏ

She closed her eyes. Mau leaned on his good arm and watched the boats beyond the wall.

For several minutes neither spoke. He thought she might sleep. He could feel the sun warming the old mark on his torso and the fine scars down his arms, places this Yuka had first seen in a clinic while they appeared without explanation. Out here they were simply part of him. He was grateful for that until she opened her eyes and looked at them.

He kept still.

"Can I?" she asked, lifting one finger toward his forearm. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

She touched a pale line lightly. It was closed and old; it did not hurt. Her finger moved a little way along it, then stopped.

"Do they all have stories?" ʸᵏ

"Most of them." ᴹ

She looked at the mark under his ribs. He saw her decide not to touch that one. He did not take her hand and make the choice for her.

"Were you ever just happy there?" she asked. ʸᵏ

The question surprised him more than the touch.

"Yes," he said. ᴹ

Yuka waited.

He looked out over the water.

"A lot. There were awful things. I keep talking about those because they're what everybody can see on me. But there were mornings when the worst thing that happened was somebody took my breakfast. There were games. Music. People who found each other after thinking they never would." He paused. "I can't make that life nothing but the bad parts. It wasn't." ᴹ

Her finger rested against his wrist.

"I think I was doing that," she said. "When you told me." ʸᵏ

"I haven't given you much else to work with." ᴹ

She considered that without trying to make it easier for him.

"Tell me one good thing," she said. "Something small." ʸᵏ

He could have chosen a hundred. He found himself remembering one he had not thought about since waking.

"Milim gave you a rock for your birthday." ᴹ

Yuka blinked.

"She gave me a rock here." ʸᵏ

"Was it a very good rock?" ᴹ

"She said it was. Repeatedly." ʸᵏ

"Then some things are reliable." ᴹ

Yuka laughed. He smiled down at her, and for a moment he could remember the two mornings without trying to decide which he was allowed to keep.

"I still have it," she said. "In the box with the hairpin. Grandma thinks that's a terrible place for a rock." ʸᵏ

"Grandma's probably right." ᴹ

"It's a very good rock, Oppa." ʸᵏ

He bowed his head in concession.

* * *

They ate the rest of the bread later, when the shadows on the beach had grown longer.

Yuka had half an idea for a new castle and spent most of the food explaining why it would succeed. Mau asked questions until she realised he was trying to keep her talking so she would not make him build it.

"Lazy," she said. ʸᵏ

"Resting. Doctor's orders." ᴹ

"Convenient." ʸᵏ

She took his hand and lay it on her hair.

He looked down at her.

"This doesn't count as work," she said. ʸᵏ

"Doesn't it?" ᴹ

"Very restful." ʸᵏ

He began with the strands beside her temple. Slowly, picking out a little sand first, then smoothing them back. She shut her eyes and made a satisfied sound that he would have liked to hear every day for a very long time.

He let himself want that without asking her to promise it.

The boats moved. Somebody near the sea wall was calling to someone else to bring a basket. The afternoon went on with no urgent message arriving to claim either of them.

After a while Yuka opened one eye.

"You stopped." ʸᵏ

He resumed immediately.

She closed it again.
