*When: Day485, sunset. They have dressed for the ride home. Yuka is carrying her boots because putting them on near the water has proved an argument she cannot win.*

Mau found the missing cloth inside his coat.

They had spent several minutes looking for it. Yuka had said she remembered handing it to him. He had said he remembered putting it in the bag. Both had searched the bag while it sat, perfectly folded, in his pocket.

He held it up.

Yuka looked at it, then at his face.

"I won't say it," she said generously. ʸᵏ

"Thank you." ᴹ

"You know what it is, though." ʸᵏ

"I do." ᴹ

She took the cloth and dried one foot. By the time she had finished the other, the first was sandy again.

Mau watched her discover this.

"You could put your boots on farther up," he offered. ᴹ

"I could do a lot of things." ʸᵏ

She gave up and put both boots under her arm. The breeze had cooled. Out on the water the boats were coming toward the wall, little dark shapes with long reflections. The town's first lamps had begun to catch above them.

"Do we have time?" she asked. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at the sky, then at the way back to the clear sand beyond the boats.

"A little. Then we go." ᴹ

He had asked Ciel before they began packing up. She had told him how long they had been on the ground, how his pulse had settled, and that she would tell him if it changed. He had not needed to ask her to turn a pleasant afternoon into an exception to the agreement.

Yuka put the boots down again and went to wash the last sand from her feet. He followed.

* * *

The first wave broke white round her ankles.

The next came in blue-green.

She looked down.

Mau stood a little way beside her, one hand lowered toward the water. The light was small at first, a faint patch following the fold of a wave. Then the fold unrolled and carried it farther, round both her feet and out along the shore.

The water withdrew. Little sparks remained in the wet sand and faded.

Yuka forgot to move when the next wave reached her.

"You're doing that." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"How?" ʸᵏ

"Light. An old pattern. Nothing in the water changes." ᴹ

She crouched, touched it with one finger, and brought her hand up to see whether any would stay.

It would not. She put it down again anyway.

Mau watched her try to catch a little of the shining sea. He knew the shape of this moment from another evening, but the memory had not contained this: Yuka crouching, almost losing her balance, laughing at herself before he could put a hand under her elbow.

She rose and wiped her wet fingertips on his sleeve.

"Very nice," he said. ᴹ

"You're welcome." ʸᵏ

Another strip of light came in with the sea.

She turned to watch it reach farther along the beach. He lowered his hand and let the little working settle, held to the water nearest them, where he could feel its size without opening his eyes to anything more.

Then he came up behind her.

He did not put his arms round her at once. She heard him step closer, felt the warmth of him behind her coat, and leaned back before his right hand reached her waist.

He rested the other beside it. She covered both with hers.

They stood there while the water shone.

* * *

"Was this in the other day?" she asked. ʸᵏ

His chin moved a little above her hair.

"Yes." ᴹ

"This part?" ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

She watched the sparks go out between her toes.

"You can tell me," she said. "I asked." ʸᵏ

He drew a breath. She felt it where her shoulders rested against his chest.

"I made the sea light up. I didn't remember learning how. You asked me, and I couldn't tell you." ᴹ

"But you remember now." ʸᵏ

"I do." ᴹ

"Frieren?" ʸᵏ

"She showed me once. A long time before that day." ᴹ

Yuka turned his hand slightly under hers. The little metal lotus touched a pale scar.

"It's beautiful," she said. ʸᵏ

He closed his eyes for a moment. Opened them on the water.

"You were beautiful then too," he said. "I spent half the day trying to find things that would make you stay looking at me for another minute." ᴹ

She laughed softly.

"That sounds exhausting. For both of us." ʸᵏ

"You were very polite about it." ᴹ

"Doesn't sound like me." ʸᵏ

He laughed against her hair. She held his hands while he did, and then for a little longer, until there was no sound but the water.

"Sometimes I don't know which thing to answer first," he said. "I remember us here. I remember us at the lake. I remember things that someone put in my head that I know didn't happen. And I keep thinking, if I say one part badly, you'll think I don't know who's standing in front of me." ᴹ

She turned her head a little, but not far enough to see his face.

"Who is?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He looked down at her.

"Yuka. Who built a castle below the tide line after correctly telling me not to." ᴹ

"You needed supervision." ʸᵏ

"Who wanted to know which colour I liked today. Who won't let me pretend I didn't hear her coming. Who keeps coming back to my room with things I didn't know I wanted until she's there." ᴹ

She had been going to make another joke. It would have been easy, and he would have let her. She stayed quiet instead.

His thumb moved against the side of her hand.

"In two timelines, I love you," he said. "I know that much. Even when I can't find where to begin the rest." ᴹ

She stood very still.

There were things she still wanted to ask him. About the years he had not explained, about how much of him looked for a different room when he woke. About the woman whose name had been difficult to say yesterday, and the choices he said he needed to own. One day at the sea had not used up those questions.

But he was warm against her back. His hands had not tightened as if she owed him an answer. He had told her something and let it stay between them.

She turned inside his arms.

* * *

For a long second she only looked at him.

His hair had dried badly in the wind. The white at the left temple had escaped every effort he had made to flatten it, and the blue of his eyes was darker now that the sun had gone. He was watching her mouth, then trying to look at her eyes instead. She found that so reassuringly familiar she nearly laughed.

"What happened then?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"We remembered we hadn't told your grandparents where we were. Or when we'd be home." ᴹ

Her eyebrows lifted.

"We spent the entire day here without telling them?" ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Idiots." ʸᵏ

"Very." ᴹ

She looked past him once, toward their bag and boots, then back.

"They know this time." ʸᵏ

"They know." ᴹ

"And we're not late." ʸᵏ

"Not yet." ᴹ

Her fingers found the front of his coat. She held it there, not pulling, just keeping the place while she decided how she wanted to do this.

Then she kissed him.

He had time for one surprised breath before his hand settled at her back and he kissed her too. She had expected it to feel strange, after hearing about another version of herself here. It felt like his mouth, warm in the cool air, a little clumsy at first because she had come closer than he expected.

She moved nearer.

His left hand loosened where it had been trying to hold the coat. She caught it between them and let his right arm do the work instead. He made a soft sound against her mouth, almost a laugh, and she liked that enough to kiss him again before he could say anything.

The wave reached their ankles.

Neither moved away.

When they stopped, his forehead rested against hers. She kept a hand on his cheek. She had wanted to do that when she sat in the clinic and he slept through everything she could think to say. Now he turned toward her palm, awake and looking at her, and she could take her time.

"I love you," she said. "I still want to know you." ʸᵏ

His eyes closed.

She brushed her thumb under one before he could turn away.

"You can keep being pretty," she added. "I'll manage." ʸᵏ

He laughed then, properly, and she kissed the corner of his mouth because she had made him do it and was pleased with herself.

* * *

They had to return to the boots eventually.

It was less graceful than either would have liked. The sand had got into everything while they were being distracted. Yuka tried to shake out one boot, got the water wrong, and had to empty it again. Mau held the bag open for her with an expression of such determined helpfulness that she accused him of laughing internally.

"Nothing to prove that," he said. ᴹ

She put the boot on the wrong foot.

That finished them both.

By the time Kaisel rose beyond the boats, they had packed the damp things separately, counted the purse twice, and left the cloth no chance of being forgotten in a pocket. Mau made sure the little working had faded from the shore before they climbed on.

Yuka turned back from her place between the wings.

"I'm glad you told me," she said. ʸᵏ

He knew she did not mean the tide alone.

"So am I." ᴹ

She took his offered hand for the last adjustment, then let it rest at her waist for the ride.

"Slowly," Mau told Kaisel, and the wings opened over the darkening sand. ᴹ

* * *

They stopped on the rise on the way home.

Neither argued for passing it. The cold had returned, and they welcomed the chance to get down, drink, and walk a little before sitting close on the grass. Yuka's hair smelled of salt when she leaned against him. He was tired now. A real day's tiredness, with an ache in his legs and sand still working its way out of a sleeve, not the fever pulling the room away.

Ciel asked him about it privately. He answered without saying he was fine.

The remaining bread was cold. They ate it anyway.

Then Kaisel took them up again and followed the river home.

* * *

Yuta was drying plates when they came in.

Maki sat near the kitchen doorway with a cup and the knife she had been cleaning. She looked up at their faces before she looked at the bag.

"Well?" she asked. ᴹᵏ

Yuka began explaining the sea before Mau had put anything down.

It was bigger than he had said. Much bigger. The clothes had been reasonably priced. They had eaten something she had written down for Momo. They had stopped both ways, yes, properly. There had been a castle, but it had suffered from poor leadership.

"My leadership?" Mau asked. ᴹ

"Who else's?" ʸᵏ

Yuta put down a dry plate.

"Sounds familiar." ʸᵗ

Mau looked at him. Yuta's mouth moved at one corner, then he picked up the next plate.

Yuka handed Maki the purse, the page and the change. Maki checked them with the brisk attention of someone unwilling to let relief make her careless about money. Then she gave back the shell Yuka had accidentally put in the same pocket.

"That one's yours." ᴹᵏ

"Oh. Thank you." ʸᵏ

Yuka held it out to Yuta. He dried his hands first and took it as carefully as if she had brought back something much finer.

Mau was still watching when Maki pointed at the chair.

"You can sit down. I can hear the rest from there." ᴹᵏ

He sat. Yuka pulled another chair beside his.

* * *

Umaru came for the page later and stayed for the food description.

She had several objections to the handwriting and none to the story, though she did ask whether a photograph of Mau losing to the ocean might become available. Yuka said she could draw it. Mau asked why nobody wanted an accurate account.

Momo took the page to look at the ingredients. The kitchen went on clearing around them, and someone gave them something hot to drink without making a ceremony of their return.

They had come back when they said they would. There was room for everything else.

At the stairs Yuka gave him the bag and kept the shell.

"Tomorrow," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked up from the buckle.

"I'll come find you. After breakfast. Unless you're asleep." ʸᵏ

"Then what?" ᴹ

She considered him with a complete lack of sympathy.

"I'll be quiet for almost a minute." ʸᵏ

He laughed. She touched his cheek once more before she went upstairs ahead of him.

When he reached his room, he set the bag down, took off his coat, and looked at the pair of boots beside the chair.

He would have to clean both now.
