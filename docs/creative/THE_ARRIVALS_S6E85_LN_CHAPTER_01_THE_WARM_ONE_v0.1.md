## Chapter 15 — The Warm One

*When: Day486, breakfast. Umaru has drawn a castle on the back of the coast receipts. Its foundations have several arrows pointing at them.*

"That's not what it looked like," Mau said. ᴹ

"I used the witness statement." ᵁ

Yuka took the page from her. The drawing contained three people, although only two had gone to the sea, and a wave with a face.

"Who's that?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"The ocean. He's winning." ᵁ

"The other one." ʸᵏ

"An independent observer." ᵁ

Bocchi looked down at her plate.

"I thought there should be someone to explain the structural problem," she said. ᴮ

Mau turned toward her.

"You helped?" ᴹ

She made the mistake of looking up. His expression was so wounded she nearly apologised before seeing the corner of his mouth move.

"Only with the wall," she said. ᴮ

"The part that fell down." ᴹ

"It had to be accurate." ᴮ

Umaru took the paper back and drew another arrow.

Mau stole a piece of her toast.

"Financial records," she said, protecting the page. ᵁ

"Then stop illustrating them." ᴹ

"Historical records too." ᵁ

Yuka laughed into her cup. She had come to find him after breakfast as promised, then found him already downstairs and decided this counted. Her shell stood beside the bowl, clean now. Every time somebody reached past it she moved it a little farther out of harm's way.

Mau watched her move it for the fourth time.

"Pocket," he suggested. ᴹ

"Then nobody can see it." ʸᵏ

He had no answer to that. He gave her the last unclaimed piece of bread instead.

* * *

Afterward Bocchi stayed to show him something on her guitar.

She did not make an announcement. She sat at the end of the bench while the table cleared and played four notes, stopped, changed the last one, and looked annoyed in a small, private way. Mau had heard her practise enough to recognise when the problem was not playing the note but deciding which note she wanted.

"Again?" he asked. ᴹ

She looked at him, then played it again.

Yuka sat on the table's other side with the empty plates stacked at her elbow. She listened to three versions and chose the one Bocchi had already begun giving up on.

"That one," she said. ʸᵏ

"It's the same as the first." ᴮ

"I liked the first." ʸᵏ

Bocchi looked at Mau.

"I liked that one too," he said. "But she's going to accuse me of agreeing with her because she's here." ᴹ

"Were you?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

"I was trying not to." ᴹ

Bocchi laughed, caught it as if it had escaped without permission, and played the first version once more.

Mau tapped its rhythm with his right fingers. His left rested on the table beside them, not asked to do anything it could no longer do. Bocchi's eyes went there once. Then she shifted the guitar so he could see the hand that made the change.

"This is the bit," she said, and showed him slowly. ᴮ

He leaned closer. Yuka leaned too, from the wrong side, until Bocchi turned the guitar a little farther and all three could see.

They stayed until Momo asked whether the plates were enjoying the music or intended to return to the kitchen.

* * *

Mau took Yuka upstairs before lunch.

He told her he had something to give her, which immediately made her ask whether he had bought another thing at the market without putting it on Umaru's page. He said no. She asked whether it was alive. He said no to that too, and she looked relieved enough to concern him.

In his room he opened the drawer.

The cloth lay where he had put it before the coast. He brought it to the bed and unfolded it between them.

Yuka recognised the coin at once.

"The one from the clinic." ʸᵏ

"Yes. The new one." ᴹ

She looked at him instead of reaching for it.

He had rehearsed this too. He was getting tired of finding that rehearsal did not help when the person he meant to speak to was there.

"I offered you a coin once," he said. "For Tsurugi. In the other life." ᴹ

Her hand went to the ring at her chest.

"I thought I still had my old one. I didn't remember giving it to Kaneki. I kept looking for it, in front of everybody, while his family was sitting there." He rubbed the cloth's edge. "You told me you weren't ready yet. I said I'd find it." ᴹ

Yuka looked down at the warm metal.

"I didn't tell you that here." ʸᵏ

"I know. I'm not asking you to be ready because I remember you saying it somewhere else." He held the coin on his open palm. "But I still want to help you bring your brother. If you want to. I can keep that part of what I said." ᴹ

She sat beside him, leaving the coin between their hands.

For a while she turned the ring on its cord. Mau waited. He could hear someone below the window calling for a basket, and another voice saying it was where baskets went, which did not seem to help.

"I'd want to talk to them first," she said. ʸᵏ

"Your grandparents?" ᴹ

She nodded.

"Not ask whether I'm allowed to miss him. Just—if we're going to ask, I want us all to know what we're asking. And he gets to decide." ʸᵏ

"He does." ᴹ

Her finger touched the coin.

"You don't want to keep it?" ʸᵏ

"I want you to have it." ᴹ

"That's a different answer." ʸᵏ

"It's the one I mean." ᴹ

She took it at last. The warmth surprised her even though she had watched it arrive. She held it in both hands, then laid it carefully on the cloth and folded the corners over it.

"I'll keep it until we talk," she said. "I don't want to do something this big just because today feels good." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

"That's a very good idea." ᴹ

Something in his voice made her glance up. He did not turn it into another story before she had had time to hold her own.

She tucked the bundle into the inner pocket of her coat, fastened it, checked it once, and took his hand.

"Thank you," she said. "For remembering. And for asking me again." ʸᵏ

He bowed over their joined hands with exaggerated solemnity.

"I was promised a better castle next time." ᴹ

"You were promised supervision." ʸᵏ

* * *

That night he woke reaching for a door that was not there.

His hand struck the bedside wall.

For a moment he knew exactly where he was: the room beneath the ridge, Yuka asleep beyond the reach of his arm, Rimuru saying no. Then he knew he had never had that room, that he had arrived here after the girl, that the beginning was a field with two warm cookies and a name he had been given by someone else's mouth.

No.

He sat up.

The east room. His east room. It had been his alone in this life. There had been two of them in it in the other. The loft was above the closet. Rimuru was there. He could hear him breathe.

He knew which history was false.

His body did not seem interested in the distinction.

His heart was going so fast he could feel the second rhythm trying to fit between it. His breath stopped short under the old mark. He put a hand there, found closed skin, and remembered a hand going through him with such certainty that the closed skin felt like the thing he must have imagined.

*Mau.* ᶜ

He did not answer.

He was on a bench. He was in a bed. He was in a room he had left on purpose. Yuka was seventeen. Yuka was eighteen. Frieren was coming toward him across frost and he was stepping away because he did not know her, except he did, he knew her, he had known her for—

*Your feet are on the floor,* Ciel said. *You put them there. Can you feel it?* ᶜ

He could not tell her. He pressed harder with his heels.

Floorboards. A seam under the right foot. A cold patch where the blanket had fallen away.

*Yes,* he managed. ᴹ

*Rimuru is above you. Shall I wake him?* ᶜ

He almost said no.

He could hear himself preparing to make this small for her. He knew that habit too.

*Yes.* ᴹ

The sound above changed. Cloth, a movement, then the careful quickness of someone getting out of a loft without wanting to arrive by falling through the ladder.

Mau kept his feet on the floor. He tried to count the seams. There were too many things he knew, and none of them would agree to stay behind him.

Rimuru came down in his nightshirt and stopped beside the bed.

"Can I sit?" he asked. ᴿ

Mau nodded.

The mattress moved under the actual weight of him.

"I know which one isn't real," Mau said. His voice shook so badly he had to begin again. "The life they put in me. I know that one isn't real. I know." ᴹ

"All right." ᴿ

"Then why can't I—" ᴹ

He could not finish. His hand had begun to shake against his chest. Rimuru did not pull it away or tell him the scars were gone. They weren't.

"I don't know," he said. "I'm here, though." ᴿ

Mau looked at the door. Closed. Not the door beneath the ridge. Not the one with Frieren on the other side, waiting for a goodbye he had not got to give. His bedroom door, with a familiar scrape near the bottom where somebody had once carried something through badly.

He breathed enough to hurt.

Rimuru stayed beside him until the next breath came, then the next. Ciel asked only for things he could answer. Was the pain new? No. Could he feel the cup when Rimuru put it in his hand? Yes. Did he want the door open?

"Yes," Mau said aloud. ᴹ

Rimuru got up and opened it.

The corridor outside was ordinary and dark. Somebody had left a pair of slippers in it. Mau could see the toes pointing the wrong way.

He looked at them until he could breathe without deciding to.

When Rimuru sat down again, Mau moved enough to leave him room.
