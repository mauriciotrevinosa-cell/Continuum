*When: Day487, late morning. The field. Gojo is inspecting a piece of iron with the offended expression of a man who has been given equipment without being consulted about its appearance.*

"Couldn't we have found a nicer one?" he asked. ᴳ

"Are you training the iron?" Shoko asked. ˢʰ

"Presentation matters." ᴳ

"Then stand somewhere else. You're in the light." ˢʰ

He moved half a step. She looked at him until he moved the rest.

Mau sat on the low support beside the test load, feet planted, with Umaru's page on his knees. The night before last had left him tired in a way he could not explain by pointing to a muscle. Yesterday he had rested, eaten, played two unsuccessful rounds of cards and talked to Rimuru without pretending the bad night had been a bad dream.

This morning he had asked to try something.

Shoko had said they could look first. He had agreed to that before asking Gojo to come.

"Open it," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Mau did.

* * *

The numbers had not moved.

One hundred and six. Fifty-seven held. Four hundred and forty in the field he had once thought of as mostly a way to deepen the cup.

Under the familiar physical fields was a line he had not seen on the morning he woke to Igris at the side of the bed.

*Physical application: reconciled.*

Below it, something could be opened.

He did not open it immediately.

*Ciel. You can look at this,* he said. *The page. I want you to see what it does when I touch it.* ᴹ

*Not the things behind it?* ᶜ

*Not those.* ᴹ

He felt her come closer to the part he offered. Not reach past it. The permission made him aware, again, that she had been keeping her distance because he asked, not because she could feel nothing there.

*It has changed how it applies what you already have,* she said after a while. *I can see that in its instructions. I can't tell you who changed them.* ᶜ

He read the line aloud.

Umaru wrote it down exactly, then underlined *reconciled* twice.

"It means the body caught up?" she asked. ᵁ

"Maybe," Mau said. "I don't know what it thinks it's reconciling." ᴹ

Gojo crouched in front of him.

"Then we start with something you can put down." ᴳ

* * *

The first load was ordinary.

It had a rope round it and enough clearance beneath that he would not have to trap his fingers to lift. Yuta had checked the knot. Mau took it in his right hand and raised it a little way from its rest.

Heavy. Familiar. He could do it, but his shoulder knew he was doing it.

He put it down.

Then he accepted the small opening beneath the new line.

He had expected heat. Or the feeling of CE gathering under his skin, the beginning of something he would have to measure by the pressure behind his eyes.

Neither happened.

He picked up the rope again.

The load left its rest so quickly that he stopped halfway to standing, startled by how close he had come to swinging it into his own leg.

Yuta stepped forward. Mau had already lowered it.

"Again," Gojo said. "Half the movement. Slowly." ᴳ

This time Mau thought about where the load would stop before lifting.

It rose. Held. Came down without a sound.

His shoulder had barely noticed.

Umaru put her pencil down.

* * *

They increased the load by steps.

Not because the small one had become meaningless. Because it had become dangerous in a new way: the amount of effort he expected and the movement he got no longer agreed. He had to relearn the difference while there were people beside him who could catch what he mishandled.

Rimuru did not stand under any of it. Neither did anybody else.

Gojo watched with his eyes uncovered for the first comparisons. Then he covered them again and asked Mau to close the physical application.

The next lift demanded his whole shoulder.

He put it down before the rope dragged against the stiff part of his other hand.

"There," Gojo said. "That's the difference." ᴳ

"Reinforcement?" Yuta asked. ʸᵗ

"Not the usual kind. His CE didn't rise with the weight. I was watching." Gojo tipped his head toward Mau. "You didn't push anything else into it?" ᴳ

"No." ᴹ

*No other outlet opened,* Ciel confirmed privately. *The demand is going through the physical application. It still has a demand.* ᶜ

Mau passed that on.

Shoko made him sit while she took his pulse. He was beginning to feel a pull along the old scar, a strain from holding himself braced too long rather than anything tearing. She wanted to know which before letting him stand again.

He told her.

Umaru drew a line between the unchanged numbers and the description of what he had lifted.

"Same account," she said. "Different thing coming out of it." ᵁ

"Or different amount," Rimuru said. ᴿ

She added that, less happily.

* * *

The iron was supposed to be the last comparison.

Yuta passed it hilt-first in the cloth they had wrapped round one end, a thick offcut from work at the house, too short to be useful as a tool and too substantial for an ordinary hand to reshape. Mau knew the kind. He had carried pieces like it without thinking of them as something that might fail before his fingers did.

"Only if the hand feels all right," Shoko said. ˢʰ

He flexed the right one. The left stayed resting on his knee.

He opened the new application, set his thumb against the iron, and began to press.

The metal gave.

Not a spell taking it apart. Not the outline of a technique. The actual surface dented under his thumb, then began to fold where he had expected resistance. He stopped with it crooked across his palm.

The field was silent.

He remembered the first practice blade Yuta had put in his hand. How much work it had taken to keep his wrist steady. That man had been him, and this hand had been his, and he had almost no idea how to place one beside the other now.

"Mau," Shoko said. ˢʰ

He looked up.

"Put it down." ˢʰ

He did.

Gojo picked the piece up. Turned it once, then passed it to Rimuru without a joke.

"And we're not putting that much through a person to see what happens," he said. ᴳ

"No," Mau said at once. ᴹ

* * *

The warning came after he had closed it.

Not a dramatic thing. He reached for the bottle and found his hand shaking. There was a heaviness under the second rhythm, and the sense that keeping the body so precisely arranged had used something he could not simply refill by drinking water.

Ciel asked him not to open it again today.

He told the others she had asked.

Shoko wrote down the delay between stopping and noticing the cost. Umaru wrote it too, on her page, with a box round it. They were recording different things. Mau was glad they both wanted it there.

"It doesn't hurt while you're doing it," he said. "That doesn't mean there's nothing happening." ᴹ

"That's a sentence I would like to hang over your bed," Shoko said. ˢʰ

He smiled. Tried to. The thought of all that strength inside the same skin as the handprint on his torso was making it difficult.

Rimuru sat beside him on the support.

"The fingers?" he asked. ᴿ

Mau tried to close the left hand.

The same two stopped. He turned his head to bring the bottle out of the soft edge of his sight. Looked beyond it at gray grass and the gray house. Nothing in those things had changed.

"Still me," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru nodded. He did not say that meant everything was fine.

* * *

They carried the equipment in without him.

Mau sat until standing no longer felt like something he needed to prove. Then he went to the clinic to collect the sword he had left there.

Shoko watched him stop at the corner.

"It can come upstairs now," she said. "Sheathed. Somewhere nobody sits on it." ˢʰ

He picked it up with the hilt covered. The weight was familiar in a way the iron had not been. He had not used it in the field. He did not draw it in the clinic.

"Thank you," he said. ᴹ

Shoko looked at his face, then the hand carrying the sword.

"Tell us before the next new thing," she said. ˢʰ

"I will." ᴹ

He meant it when he said it.

Upstairs he laid the sheathed blade on the shelf along his wall, clear of the bed and the route to the loft. The drawer beneath it was empty where the coin had been. He knew where the coin was now, who was holding it, and what choice she would get to make with it.

He kept his hand on the scabbard a little longer than he needed to.

When Ciel asked if he wanted to lie down, he said yes before she had to ask again.
