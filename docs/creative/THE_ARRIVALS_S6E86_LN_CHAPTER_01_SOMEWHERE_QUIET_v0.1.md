# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 36
## Chapter 17 — Somewhere Quiet

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (2026-10-25)
**Volume:** 36
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S6E86
**Continuity state:** Day488, ordinary morning into afternoon. *When: Day488, morning. Someone has put the wrong sheets in the wrong pile. Mau has been confidently folding them for ten minutes.*

"That's the small one," Rem said. ᴿᵉ

He looked at the sheet spread between his hands.

"It doesn't feel small." ᴹ

"It will when you put it on a large bed." ᴿᵉ

Ram reached past him and moved three of the folded pieces from his pile to hers.

"A remarkable discovery. We should tell someone." ᴿᵃ

Mau surrendered the fourth before she had to find it herself.

He had wanted something to do that morning. An actual job, with an end someone could point to. Rem had given him linen to fold, then remained close enough to correct his work without correcting the fact that he wanted to do it.

Yuka had gone to find Maki with the coin. They had agreed she would tell Mau afterward how much she wanted to share of that conversation. He had not asked to attend it. Her brother did not need to become another thing that happened only while Mau watched.

He folded the correct sheet at last and laid it where Rem pointed.

"See?" he said. ᴹ

Ram looked at it.

"We may save the household yet." ᴿᵃ

He threw the loose corner over her hand. She did not move it until he folded it properly.

* * *

Frieren was in the corridor when he took a smaller stack upstairs.

Ori stood beside her with a piece of string round her wrist, which she was insisting was a measuring device. Frieren had apparently failed to recognise the seriousness of the device and was holding a book open with one thumb while the child explained.

"For the hiding game," Ori told Mau. ᴼ

"Does it stop her floating?" he asked. ᴹ

"No. It says how high is allowed." ᴼ

Frieren looked up from the page.

"She keeps making it shorter." ᶠ

"You keep going higher." ᴼ

"Reasonable complaint," Mau said. ᴹ

Ori looked satisfied. Frieren looked less so, although not enough to stop reading.

He made to go round them. The linen shifted against his chest.

For a moment he expected Frieren to take the top half of the stack. Not because he could not carry it. Because in another life she would take it, complain that he had brought too much, and walk beside him to the east room while trying to finish the sentence in her book.

This Frieren moved her daughter clear of his path.

"You can get through," she said. ᶠ

"Thanks." ᴹ

She had done nothing cruel. Nothing wrong.

That made the ache harder to put anywhere.

Ori asked whether he would play later. He said he hoped so. Did not say he promised.

* * *

It began when he opened the cupboard.

There was a smell of clean linen and old wood. A folded blanket had been pushed behind something else, leaving a narrow dark space at the back. Mau put the new sheets down and reached to straighten it.

White stone under his knees.

A reset he could not stop.

He took his hand out of the cupboard.

He had spent an afternoon answering three small tests. He had spent lifetimes trying to get the answer right. He had never taken the first steps to this house with the people everyone said had been waiting for him, except he could remember those steps as clearly as either Trial, and remembered believing them.

He knew. He knew which things had happened.

His chest tightened anyway.

*Mau?* ᶜ

He braced his right hand on the cupboard frame. Wood. Not stone. He could feel the old burn marks against the edge.

*I'm here.* ᴹ

*Shall I ask Rimuru to come?* ᶜ

He closed the cupboard carefully.

*Not upstairs. I want to move a little.* ᴹ

*Tell me where.* ᶜ

He looked at the door of his room. Then at the sheathed sword along the wall.

He was going to tell them. Tonight, perhaps. Tomorrow morning, if everyone needed to sit and there was too much happening tonight. He could start with the thing Rimuru already knew and go forward from there. He could do that.

He could also hear how easily *tomorrow* had arrived in the thought.

He picked up the sword.

* * *

The house was busy at that hour.

There were people in the kitchen, voices beyond the common-room door, somebody walking overhead with more speed than Fern would approve of. Mau went through all of it carrying a blade in its sheath and did not stop anyone long enough to tell them why.

Nobody was refusing him a place to speak.

He did not speak.

Outside, the air was cold enough to make him notice his breathing. He walked the familiar path toward the frame. The sword lay along his leg. He had carried it through much worse places than this one, and knowing that made a quiet gate seem, for a few dangerous minutes, like a reasonable place to put his feet.

*This isn't the field,* Ciel said. ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*You agreed to rest after yesterday's test.* ᶜ

*I rested yesterday. I want somewhere quiet now.* ᴹ

She was silent for several paces.

*You can tell Rimuru that. He'll listen.* ᶜ

Mau looked down at the path. He could feel the familiar shame beginning to arrange itself into annoyance, as if being reminded of the people who would help him were the thing making this hard.

He stopped walking.

*Tell him where I am,* he said. *Please.* ᴹ

*I am telling him.* ᶜ

He nodded. Waited one breath. Then went on before the answer came.

* * *

The frame stood open.

Beyond it, stone steps descended into shadow. He could see a wall far inside, a strip of empty floor, light coming from no window he could find. The outside panel showed a simple floor plan, an outer hall and an inner chamber, with a line he read without taking it in properly.

*Inner encounter seals upon challenge. Challenger release after clear. Outer passage remains open.*

He had seen stranger rules. He had been inside worse places.

Those were not good reasons. He used them anyway.

Igris came first when he called.

He rose beside the path, bowed, and waited. Beru followed, antennae lifting at the sight of open ground. Kamish unfolded farther back where there was room, the great dark head lifting beyond the smaller shapes.

Mau looked at the three of them. For one moment he could imagine they were all he needed to bring out of that other life. People who remembered. People he would not have to introduce himself to before explaining why he could not breathe.

Then he found the other access.

Separate. Familiar. The old subdued pattern, not a name on the Window's list.

Mahoraga rose behind him.

Pale mass, the immense arm and the blade, the wheel held above the head. Not the open ritual that had once come straight for its summoner. This one stood still because Mau had not asked him to move.

His edges had the slight roughness Mau remembered from his copy. The control was his too. He did not have to earn it again because the world around him had forgotten the earning.

Ciel went very quiet.

*You have this as well,* she said. ᶜ

*Yes.* ᴹ

*Mau. Wait for Rimuru.* ᶜ

He put his hand on the cold rim of the frame.

"No one from the house gets hurt," he told the four of them. "Keep the entrance clear. If Rimuru comes, let him through. The others wait outside. Tell them I asked. Don't fight them." ᴹ

Igris's helm lifted.

"*My liege. What shall I tell them you are doing?*" ᴵᵍ

Mau opened his mouth.

"I'm trying to get quiet," he said. ᴹ

* * *

He crossed before Ciel could answer.

The familiar thread drew thin, then stopped.

Not gone. He could feel the place where it waited outside, just as he could feel the doorway behind him. He turned his head and saw daylight framed by stone, Igris beyond it with the helm still raised.

*Ciel?* ᴹ

No answer.

He took a step back toward the threshold and heard the beginning of his name. A frightened, sharp beginning. He stopped, close enough to know she was there, not close enough to answer properly.

He knew what he should do.

For several breaths he stood with his hand on the sheath, feeling the room behind him wait.

Then he turned inward.
