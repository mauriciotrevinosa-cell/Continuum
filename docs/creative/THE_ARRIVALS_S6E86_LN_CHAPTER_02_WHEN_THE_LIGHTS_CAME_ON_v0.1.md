*When: Day488, afternoon. Rimuru leaves an unfinished sentence on his office page.*

*He crossed,* Ciel said. *I can't reach him.* ᶜ

Rimuru was already standing.

He had heard her first warning a little earlier, while Mau was still on the path. Had put down the page and gone to find him instead of answering through somebody else. He had meant to reach the frame before there was anything else to warn him about.

He had not.

*Did it close?* he asked, moving faster. ᴿ

*The entrance is open. He came back close enough that I could almost hear him. Then he moved away again.* ᶜ

Rimuru went cold.

Anko was near the turn in the path. She looked at his face before he said anything.

"Shoko," he said. "Please. Tell her Mau went in. Ciel's connection stopped." ᴿ

She did not waste time asking whether he meant now.

He went on.

* * *

The dragon was visible first.

Rimuru slowed despite himself. The huge dark head stood beyond the frame, turned toward the house. One folded wing made a wall of the space behind it. Closer to the entrance was a knight, and beside the knight a tall black insect with its arms held very still.

Then the pale shape moved and Rimuru saw the wheel.

He knew, abruptly, that the list Mau had read out in the office was not a list of names he could postpone understanding. These things had come from somewhere. They belonged to the man who had been spilling tea with him in the clinic.

Mahoraga turned its head toward him.

Only its head. The blade did not rise.

Igris stepped aside.

"*Lord Rimuru. You may pass.*" ᴵᵍ

The voice came from inside the helm. Formal. Calm.

"Where is he?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

The knight looked toward the frame.

"*Within. He asked that no one from the house be hurt. He asked that you be allowed through.*" ᴵᵍ

"And that one?" Rimuru looked at the wheel. ᴿ

"*His. Subdued before we came here.*" ᴵᵍ

Rimuru wanted to ask what *before* meant for a thing that large. Wanted to ask why a boy who could command it had been unable to ask somebody to walk beside him down this path.

Neither question would get him inside sooner.

He read the outer panel once. Open passage. Sealed inner encounter. Release after clear.

*I'm going in,* he told Ciel. ᴿ

*Call him before you go farther than the first hall,* she said. *Please.* ᶜ

He promised he would.

Then he crossed and her voice went out of reach.

* * *

At first there was almost no light.

A great enclosed space. Stone underfoot. Something high overhead, too far above him to make out. He could smell metal and wet earth and a sweetness under both which his body understood before he did.

He took three steps.

The lamps lit.

One row along the wall, then another. Small flames caught behind glass bowls and threw their light up the pillars. The dark lifted by pieces.

Rimuru stopped walking.

There was a head in the ceiling.

It had gone through a hanging ironwork frame and lodged above it. The helmet was still on, split across the brow. Beneath the torn opening of the neck hung a length of spine, white against the dark, swinging almost imperceptibly in the air disturbed by the opening door.

It was not human. The jaw had too many joints, the mouth a hard plated thing. Rimuru saw that. Understood it. The understanding did not change the way his own jaw locked when he followed the vertebrae down and found where the rest had ended.

Below it, a pillar had a handprint in it.

Not on it.

In it.

Stone pressed inward round five separate marks, and beneath those marks the remains of another armored shape were spread so thin that he had first taken them for damage to the pillar itself.

*Mau.*

He had watched a thumb bend iron yesterday. Watched the boy put it down when Shoko asked. He looked at the pillar and understood how little of that new strength they had asked to see.

* * *

He made himself move.

Not toward the head. Toward the far end, where the lamps were still catching, where a central passage might lead to another room. There were things underfoot he did not look down at until he felt one shift.

A wrist guard. Empty.

The hand lay farther on, fingers still closed round the broken part of a weapon. Beside it, two boots were upright beneath a body folded so sharply that the shape no longer looked as if it could ever have stood.

Rimuru stepped round it.

He had seen death. Had ordered death. There were choices in his own past he would not pretend belonged to somebody softer because he wanted to be sick now.

This was not frightening because he had never known what a battlefield could contain.

He knew whose hands had done this.

Hands that steadied a cup with the fingers that still moved. Hands Ori had passed a compass to. A hand Yuka had been holding at supper while they argued about sand.

He could not fit that knowledge into the shape against the pillar. He could not stop trying.

"Mau?" he called. ᴿ

His voice went too far. Came back from somewhere above the lamps.

No answer.

* * *

The bodies were not in a single heap.

That would have been easier, somehow. A place the eye could turn away from. These had ended where they had been caught. Against steps. Halfway through an arch. In the grooves between paving stones, where something much heavier had been driven across them and taken the upper layer of stone with it.

A long black soldier came through the far arch carrying a crystal in each hand.

It stopped when it saw Rimuru. Bowed its head a fraction. Went on past him toward the outside.

Behind it came another with a broken shape over its shoulder. The body dripped onto the floor. The soldier walked with a care which made Rimuru think of somebody carrying a heavy sack through a kitchen without wanting to knock into a table.

His stomach turned.

He put a hand on the wall and waited for it to pass.

It did not pass quickly enough.

"Where is he?" he asked the next soldier. ᴿ

It turned and pointed inward.

He followed the direction.

* * *

The inner doors were closed.

They were enormous, dark, banded at the edges with the same light as the outer frame. A shallow inscription above them repeated the condition outside. The encounter was active. The challenger had not cleared it.

There were no hinges for him to pull out of stone.

Rimuru put his palm against the centre. The surface met him as a boundary, not timber or iron. He tried a small, careful opening at one side, enough to see whether it was something he could separate without bringing the room down.

The opening filled at once with the frame's light.

Behind it there was a sound.

One low impact. A delay. Then another, far enough away that he could not tell how much of what he heard was the building and how much was something inside it.

He did not see Mau.

"Mau. It's me." ᴿ

He stayed close, trying to hear an answer.

Something moved at the base of the sealed doors. A dark soldier came through carrying the remains of a creature twice its width, as if the barrier knew whose hands it belonged to and let it pass.

Rimuru stepped back for it.

The handprint on the pillar was behind him. Above it, out of sight now, the spine still hung from the ironwork.

He bent suddenly.

Nothing came up. His throat worked, and his borrowed human shape remembered a helpless motion he had not asked it to remember, and for several seconds all he could do was brace both hands on his knees.

He hated that it was happening here. Hated that Mau was somewhere beyond the door and he was using time to get air into a body that should not have needed it this badly.

He straightened too quickly.

The room tipped in a way no room had any right to tip.

He needed the others. Needed to tell them what the door was doing. Needed to get somebody to ask through those dark soldiers, because they were reaching a place he could not.

He went back.

* * *

At the threshold he had to step aside for more of the cargo.

One soldier carried a severed arm under its own, almost companionably. Another dragged a sheet loaded with dull crystals, each one clicking softly against the next. The sounds followed him up the steps.

Daylight struck his eyes.

He came out with one hand already over his mouth.

Anko was there. Shoko behind her. Gojo coming down the path with his blindfold up.

Rimuru tried to tell them to wait, and could not get the first word out.

*Rimuru?* Ciel said, back all at once. *Where is he?* ᶜ

He turned from her voice as if that would help him answer it, bent again at the side of the path, and heard Shoko tell everyone to give him room.

Behind him, the soldiers kept coming out.
