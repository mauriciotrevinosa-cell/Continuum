*When: Day488, dusk. The lamps inside the castle have begun to go out behind the departing loads.*

Rimuru kept his eyes on the route.

He knew it now. The first pillar. The broken arch. The step where something had caught under his foot. He could cross the hall without discovering it all again, though the knowledge did not keep him from seeing what had changed.

Some of the bodies were gone.

Their absence left shapes on the floor. Clean patches where the stone had been covered, darker ones where it had not. Near the pillar, a soldier knelt to collect a crystal from a crack with the care of someone retrieving a dropped button.

The inner doors stood open.

Rimuru went through.

* * *

It was a throne room.

Or had meant to be one. The high seat remained, tilted on a split base. On either side of it stood frames in the shape of bodies, empty molds with the same jointed jaws and plated limbs as the things in the hall. Channels ran beneath them toward a hollow altar.

The channels were dry.

Something had been built here, over and over, out of what the room could give it. Now the room had nothing moving in it except the departing shadows and a strip of torn cloth lifting in the air above the broken seat.

Mau stood beside the altar.

One hand rested on the sheathed sword. His clothes were dark with what had happened in the rooms behind him. There was a mark on the side of his face, not deep, and his left hand hung with the same two fingers held out from the rest, as if the whole impossible day had been unable to alter that familiar small damage.

Beside him stood a single new shape.

Tall, narrow, crowned with a broken-looking ridge. A dark staff held in both hands. It had the outline of the seated thing painted on the wall above the throne, except there was no paint left on the crown and no throne it seemed to want to sit in.

It bowed when Rimuru stopped.

One.

Not the garrison. Not all the bodies carried past him. One new servant standing quietly at Mau's side.

"Mau," Rimuru said. ᴿ

No answer.

* * *

He came closer.

"It's me. I'm coming up beside you." ᴿ

Mau's eyes did not follow him. They were open, fixed a little above the altar on something Rimuru could not see. His breathing was shallow. Not the measured breathing of someone holding a technique. The other kind, the kind Rimuru had listened to beside a bed two nights ago.

"You can come out now," he said. "It's finished." ᴿ

The new shadow lowered its staff. Took one step back, leaving the space beside its king clear.

Rimuru lifted his hand where Mau should have been able to see it.

"I'm going to touch your shoulder." ᴿ

He waited.

Then put his hand down.

* * *

Steel moved between them.

Rimuru saw the beginning of it, then the edge was already close enough that his own body had begun to give way from where the blade might go. Mau had turned with the sword half drawn, right hand locked on the hilt, a movement so quick and complete that the person speaking to him had not seemed to belong to it at all.

Then Mau saw his face.

The blade stopped.

It did not touch skin.

For a heartbeat they stayed there: Rimuru's hand still on the shoulder, steel between their bodies, Mau looking at him as if he had arrived at the very last possible moment.

"Rimuru?" ᴹ

"Yes." ᴿ

Mau looked down.

At the sword. At where he had put it.

His face changed.

"I'm sorry." ᴹ

* * *

He tried to put the sword away too quickly.

Rimuru caught his wrist before the point could miss the scabbard. Not to hold him prisoner. To keep the two things in the same line while Mau's hand began to shake.

"Slowly," he said. ᴿ

"I'm sorry. I'm sorry, I didn't—I thought—" ᴹ

"Slowly. I've got the sheath. You move your hand." ᴿ

Mau looked at what they were doing. Managed the last small movement. The guard met the mouth of the scabbard with an ordinary click that sounded far too loud in the room.

Then he could not stop saying it.

"Sorry. I'm sorry. I saw you. I didn't see you. I don't know why I—" ᴹ

Rimuru moved the sheathed blade clear and set it against the altar, where neither would have to hold it.

"You stopped." ᴿ

"I nearly—" ᴹ

"I know. You stopped." ᴿ

Mau shook his head. Once. Again. The tears arrived without the rest of his face seeming to have decided what to do about them.

Rimuru had thought the sight of the hall was the thing he would not be able to carry out of here.

Then Mau bent against him, trying not to put weight on the shoulder Rimuru had touched, and began to cry as if he had been holding it behind his teeth for hours.

He put both arms round him.

* * *

"I promised," Mau said into his coat. ᴹ

"What?" ᴿ

"I promised I'd tell you. What happened. If something went wrong. I promised all of you. After the floors. I said I'd—" His breath caught. "You don't remember. You don't—" ᴹ

Rimuru closed his eyes.

There had been no floors for him. No tower with Mau coming back one piece at a time, no agreement after it for him to hold the boy to. The promise was somewhere else. The person trying to keep it was in his arms now.

"You can tell me," he said. "You don't have to do it standing here." ᴿ

"I have to tell everybody. I said I'd do it. I keep saying—" ᴹ

"Then we go home. We get you somewhere you can sit. Then you tell us." ᴿ

Mau tried to draw back. His knees changed his mind for him.

Rimuru caught more of his weight and felt how much of it had been held upright by sheer refusal to stop.

"There," he said. "You don't have to fall first." ᴿ

* * *

It took time to get moving.

Not because Mau resisted him now. Because every instruction had to be small enough to do. Put the good hand here. Leave the sword until Rimuru could take it. Look at the next step, not all the way across the room.

The new shadow followed without coming too close.

"Who is that?" Rimuru asked when they had reached the open doors. ᴿ

Mau turned his head as if noticing it again.

"Muster," he said. ᴹ

"Yours?" ᴿ

He nodded.

"Only him. I didn't—" He swallowed. "Only him." ᴹ

Rimuru accepted that answer for now.

In the hall Igris came to meet them. He did not ask whether he might help. He took the sheathed katana from Rimuru, bowed once to Mau, and went ahead to clear the route.

Mau looked at the black shoulders moving before them.

"I didn't want anybody hurt," he said. ᴹ

"I know what you told them." ᴿ

"I could have come out." ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him.

Mau's mouth was trembling too much for the rest of the sentence.

"We'll talk about that too," Rimuru said. ᴿ

* * *

At the first steps Mau stopped.

He looked at the daylight and the shapes moving through it. His breathing changed again.

"They're there?" he asked. ᴹ

"Yes." ᴿ

"All of them?" ᴹ

"Enough of them to help. The rest are making somewhere ready." ᴿ

Mau looked down at his clothes. At his hands. He seemed to see for the first time what he would carry into the house if he simply walked through it like this.

Rimuru did not tell him nobody would mind.

"Shoko's waiting. We start there." ᴿ

Mau nodded.

They went up.

* * *

Ciel reached him at the threshold.

*Mau?* ᶜ

He made a sound aloud before finding the private answer.

*I'm here.* ᴹ

Her silence shook. He could not have explained how a silence could do that. He knew it anyway.

*Answer me again,* she said. ᶜ

*I'm here, Ciel.* ᴹ

Rimuru heard his own name on the other thread. Heard her try to arrange what she could feel now against all the time she had been unable to feel anything.

*He's exhausted,* she told him. *I'm not losing his answers the way I did during the fever. He's here. Please keep him sitting while she looks.* ᶜ

Rimuru passed that to Shoko aloud.

She had already come to meet them.

* * *

Mau saw Yuka over Shoko's shoulder.

Color in her face. The rest of the path in gray. He had forgotten the absence inside the castle, where brightness and dark had seemed sufficient for everything he wanted to do.

Now he could see how pale she looked within the color he still had, and the sight almost folded him again.

She did not ask why before he was sitting. Maki kept a hand against her back without holding her away.

Shoko knelt in front of him.

"Look at me," she said. ˢʰ

He tried.

"New pain?" ˢʰ

"Tired." ᴹ

"I heard that part. Anything sharp? Anything you can't feel?" ˢʰ

He worked through the questions. The little cut on his face. His scraped knuckles. The old hand which would not close and had not been made better by the things he had done with the other one. The ache under his ribs, familiar and worse with holding himself upright.

Shoko looked at Rimuru once.

Then she took a clean cloth from Maki and began with the part she could actually clean.

Behind them, Mahoraga stayed still, the blade lowered, while a small black soldier carried the last of that particular load past his feet.
