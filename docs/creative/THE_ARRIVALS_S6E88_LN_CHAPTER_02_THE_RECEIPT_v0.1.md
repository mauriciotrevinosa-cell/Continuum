*When: Day489, morning. Anko's page has been folded and unfolded so often that the crease cuts through the word missing.*

She had reported it in the night.

Nazuna had seen the first tagged crystal lot go. Not carried away. Gone between one look and the next, with its little numbered slip falling flat where the weight had been. Anko had checked the neighbouring lot, then the next, and come straight to Rimuru.

He had been beside the Fort with Shoko and two empty hands where the daggers had appeared.

They had compared the times.

Now the page lay on a tray beside Mau while he drank something warm and tried to remember making a purchase.

He could not.

* * *

"I remember picking up the weight," he said. "In the dream. Trying not to drop it." ᴹ

Yuka sat opposite, beyond the tray. She had slept after the second time Shoko checked his hands. Not well, but some, and her grandmother had made her eat before she returned.

"You said sorry," she told him. "Then they went away." ʸᵏ

He looked at his palms.

"I know how I put them away. I don't know how I got them." ᴹ

"Start with what you can actually look at," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Mau opened the Window.

He asked Ciel to read the current page with him. Only that. She waited for the permission and he felt ashamed, briefly, of how grateful he was that she still waited.

*Equipment exchange,* she said. *That's the heading. I haven't seen you open it awake.* ᶜ

*Neither have I.* ᴹ

He opened it.

There was a receipt.

* * *

The name was *Dragon's Wrath*.

Two blades. S. Their little images stood beside a record of the purchase, the familiar right-handed motion preserved as a confirmation beside the price.

He recognised the motion. Had made it a thousand times to accept something ordinary in a menu. Had apparently made it in his sleep because a dream asked him not to let go.

He read the important parts aloud.

No one looked pleased.

"What paid for them?" Anko asked. ᴬ

He followed the line under the price.

The collection manifest. His name. The tagged crystals and the value they had represented when his soldiers put them through the gate. He remembered accepting that page in the throne room because it looked like an instruction for getting things out.

It had also registered where those things went.

"The salvage," he said. "From yesterday. The listed lots. It took them from the collection claim." ᴹ

Anko turned the page so he could see the marks where she had checked each empty space.

"Almost all of it," she said. ᴬ

He looked at the list.

She was right.

* * *

Not everything had vanished.

There were the unregistered samples Anko had set apart. Some exhausted material, useful enough to study but not accepted by the exchange at the same value as a live crystal. The slips were still there. The things with the greatest value on the manifest were not.

Mau checked the other accounts twice before saying the next part aloud.

"Not the house supplies. Not the food money. Nothing we had before the gate." ᴹ

"And the coin?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

Yuka put a hand to the inside of her coat.

"Here," she said. "Still here." ʸᵏ

Mau let out a breath. It did not make the page in front of Anko smaller.

"I accepted the collection," he said. "I didn't know it meant it could take the things back out of the pile." ᴹ

"Now we do," Anko said. ᴬ

She did not add that it was all right because he had not meant to buy them. It was not all right. She had spent the evening bringing that weight out of the dark and somebody would need to know what it had become.

"Show us where the weapons are," Shoko said. ˢʰ

* * *

They cleared the tray first.

Mau did not call the blades into hands held near another person. He laid his right palm over the bare board, asked for one, and let it arrive low enough that he could set it down at once.

It was heavier than the little image suggested. A swept edge, a pale inner curve, ridges along the back like the remains of something once alive. The grip fitted him too well. He disliked that at once.

The other joined it.

Yuka looked at them without moving closer.

"Those are the ones," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

There were claims on the page about cutting resistance and carrying power along an edge. He told the others they were claims. He had not tried them. He was not trying them in the Fort.

Shoko put a cloth beside the tray and made him store them again before anybody passed it.

They vanished.

"Not under the blanket tonight," she said. ˢʰ

"I don't want them under the blanket ever." ᴹ

He closed the equipment page. Asked Ciel to wake him if the movement began again, to tell Rimuru too. She reminded him that she had tried last night, and the acceptance had been faster than waking.

He repeated that aloud rather than pretending they had found a solution.

"Then somebody stays clear of his hands," Shoko said. "And we find out how to keep that page closed properly. We don't call asking him to remember not to dream a precaution." ˢʰ

Yuka nodded without looking at him. He knew she was remembering the points above the blanket.

* * *

The next page had changed too.

His level was still one hundred and six. Fifty-seven points still held. The same Intelligence field. A short record beside the garrison entries identified reconstruction cycles rather than new opponents to be paid for again and again.

He had kept fighting something the page had already counted.

He read that part silently first. Then aloud.

Rimuru looked down at his own hands.

Below it, the named register said fifteen. The soldiers still ninety-eight. The new name, *Muster*, lay below the fourteen he remembered. A cost had been taken from the cup for raising him; storing the others had returned no fuel.

The ceiling of forty was gone.

Not a larger number. A line stating that fixed registration places no longer limited the list.

Mau showed Ciel where he meant. She read it twice before replying.

*Places,* she said. *Not the cost of filling them. Not the cost of rebuilding what breaks. It doesn't give us a reason to pretend either has disappeared.* ᶜ

He passed that on too.

"And the new one?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

"He doesn't have the altar," Mau said. "The molds. The rooms that made the army. His page says construction needs material and his own reserve. He can't make that castle happen here because I brought him out." ᴹ

Anko made a separate note. Her pencil sounded very loud against the board.

Nobody asked Mau to prove the new ceiling by raising something else.

* * *

When the pages were closed, the Fort was only the Fort again.

Blankets. Morning sounds. Someone outside the opening trying to decide where to put a cup without making the tray everybody's problem. Mau could hear life continuing beyond the conversation and knew he had been using that sound as an excuse for too long.

He put the cup down.

"This afternoon," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru looked up.

"I want to tell them this afternoon. All of it. The part I know, the part I don't. I keep waiting to find a way to say it that won't hurt anybody. There isn't one." ᴹ

Shoko studied his face.

"After you sleep some more and eat. Sitting down. We stop if you stop being able to answer." ˢʰ

"Yes." ᴹ

"And you don't buy anything." ˢʰ

He nearly laughed. Couldn't quite.

"Yes." ᴹ

* * *

He spoke to Frieren before lying down again.

Not the whole story. The question he should have asked before deciding who would hear what.

"Ori," he said. "Some of this is about her. I don't want her to get it from someone talking outside her door. I also don't want to tell her things just because I need to say them." ᴹ

Frieren sat beside the opening, hands quiet in her lap.

"Then tell her the part she needs first," she said. "Without the hurt. I'll sit with her. If she wants to leave, we leave." ᶠ

He nodded.

"And if I don't say it well—" ᴹ

"I'll ask you to say it again." ᶠ

There was nothing cruel in her voice. No hidden grievance from a life she had not lived. A mother deciding how to help her child hear something difficult.

Mau looked at the blanket over his knees.

"Thank you." ᴹ

Frieren rose, then paused.

"You can tell me something difficult too," she said. "I'm older than you think." ᶠ

He almost smiled.

"That's a complicated subject." ᴹ

"Apparently most subjects are, with you." ᶠ

She went to find Ori. He lay down while Rimuru went to ask the others when they could sit, and did not use the time she had given him to think of another place to go.
