*When: Day489, late afternoon. The chairs are the house's own chairs. Nobody has arranged them into rows.*

Mau was grateful for that until he saw how many people could fit round an ordinary table when everybody meant to listen.

Shoko had put his chair where he could rest his back. Rimuru sat close enough to pass him the cup without getting up. Yuka had chosen the place on his other side, not tucked behind him, not put there by anyone else's decision.

Further down the room Nazuna leaned against the wall beside Anko. Gojo had found a place near Shoko and was not making an occasion of being quiet. The people who had been at the frame and the people who had stayed home looked at Mau with different questions.

He could not answer all of them first.

Ori sat on Frieren's lap with the compass held between both hands.

He began there.

* * *

"I remember something about you," he told her. "Something that didn't happen the way you remember it here." ᴹ

Ori looked at her mother, then back.

"The game?" ᴼ

His throat tightened.

"Before the game. A lot of days before it." He put his good hand flat on his knee. "In another life I remember, you called me Dad." ᴹ

The compass moved between her hands.

She did not reach toward him. Did not suddenly recover something hidden. She looked confused, and then worried in a way he had been afraid of making her.

"Was Mum there?" she asked. ᴼ

"Yes." ᴹ

"But she's Mum." ᴼ

"She is. That isn't changing. I'm not asking to take you anywhere, or make you call me anything different." He looked at Frieren, then back at Ori. "I wanted you to know why I sometimes look at you like I'm remembering more than you are. It isn't something you've forgotten to do for me." ᴹ

Ori considered him very seriously.

"Do I have to remember?" ᴼ

"No." ᴹ

"Will you be sad if I can't?" ᴼ

He had promised himself he would not lie to her to make the room easier.

"I miss things," he said. "But you don't have to make that better. I still like playing your game. I'd like to keep doing that, if you want me there." ᴹ

She looked down at the compass. Turned it, watched the needle find Frieren although she was sitting on her, and seemed to take some comfort from the pointlessness of that familiar result.

"You can play," she said. "You have to follow the rules." ᴼ

He managed a smile.

"Even the new ones?" ᴹ

"Especially the new ones." ᴼ

Frieren put a hand over the little fingers round the compass.

"Do you want to hear more now?" she asked her. ᶠ

Ori thought about it, then shook her head.

"Can I go do the drawing?" ᴼ

"Yes." ᶠ

Coco's mother was waiting by the doorway with the paper she had already offered. Frieren took Ori over, crouched long enough to hear the question the child asked quietly, and answered just as quietly before returning.

Mau waited until she sat down.

* * *

He looked at the cup Rimuru had put within reach.

"There are three histories in my head," he said. "Two happened. One was put there. I know which one was put there. Knowing doesn't stop it arriving like something I lived." ᴹ

He took a breath.

"The life you remember with me happened. The clearing. Fern arriving. Me learning how not to fall over while Yuta hit me with a practice sword. The lake. All of it. I'm not telling you the person you knew was somebody else, or that I woke up and got rid of him." ᴹ

Yuta had gone very still.

"I remember being him," Mau said. "Being me, here. I know why I said the things I said to you. I don't get to say another man hid the relationship and made you catch him when he fell." ᴹ

Yuka looked at his hand on his knee. Kept her own where he could find it without having to interrupt himself.

"And the other real one?" Anko asked. ᴬ

"The same people. Most of the same house. A lot of the same ordinary days. Some things went very differently. I remember them just as clearly." ᴹ

"Before this one?" ᴬ

"That's how I came back to it. I don't know if before is the right word for everything it did to the world." ᴹ

Anko accepted the limit of that answer without writing it down as a conclusion.

* * *

"When you look at us," Subaru asked, "which one are you seeing?" ˢᵘ

He was sitting forward, elbows on his knees. Beatrice had not moved from the place beside him.

Mau looked around the room.

At faces whose differences he knew in detail. At people who had held him while dying somewhere else and carried a blanket to him here without ever knowing they had done it before.

"You," he said. "That's the part I'm trying to get better at. I know things about another you. Sometimes I expect you to know them too. Rimuru asked how old I was and I laughed at a joke he never told me." ᴹ

Rimuru's fingers tightened round his own cup.

"You didn't suddenly become somebody I don't know," Mau went on. "But you aren't obliged to be the person who remembers the thing I expect. I have to stop handing you half a conversation from another life." ᴹ

Frieren had laid her book on the table without opening it.

"Ori isn't the only one," she said. ᶠ

Mau looked at her.

"No." ᴹ

* * *

He did not make Yuka let go of his hand before answering.

He did not hide it from Frieren either.

"We were together," he said. "You and me. We chose it, in that life. We had good days. Stupid arguments. Dates that were mostly walking somewhere for food and coming back later than we meant to. I loved you." ᴹ

The room did not move.

"I remember loving you. It isn't something I can put down because I found a different room when I woke up. But you didn't live that with me here. I know that. I'm not asking you to feel as if you did." ᴹ

Frieren watched him for a long moment.

Fern had moved a hand onto the table beside hers. Frieren noticed, touched it once, and kept looking at Mau.

"Is that why you knew where I'd read?" she asked. ᶠ

The question was so small beside what he had tried to give her that he almost could not answer it.

"Yes. Some of it. And because I've known you here too." ᴹ

She nodded slowly.

He could not tell what she thought of the rest. He did not try to invent it for her.

* * *

Shoko asked about the scar before he found a way to move on.

Not bluntly. She touched the same place under her own ribs, as she had in the clinic, and waited for him to nod.

"Sukuna," he said. "In that life, the fight didn't end with us getting up together and laughing because we were alive. He put his hand through me." ᴹ

Yuta's eyes went to the place Mau had not touched.

"Here," Mau said, "I remember you laughing beside me. I remember getting up. I remember all my fingers working. Those things happened too. That's why I knew what you meant when you said they'd been fine before I fell." ᴹ

Shoko looked down at the notes she had brought and did not open them.

"And the rest?" she asked. ˢʰ

"Different days. Different places. A tower we didn't climb here. The way I got the people in my shadow. The reason they remember when you don't." He looked at Rimuru. "I'll tell you what I can. I don't need to make you watch it to prove I remember." ᴹ

*Thank you,* Ciel said privately. ᶜ

He did not repeat her answer to the room. It was hers to give him.

* * *

"You said the Trial was different," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Mau nodded.

"You told me longer. That you died." ᴿ

Somewhere at the back of the room a chair creaked. Nobody had moved much; the room had simply become quiet enough for the small sound to reach them all.

"The afternoon you remember was real," Mau said. "The choice after it was real. The coins came after people decided to stay. I gave mine to Kaneki. His family is here because of that choice, not because I woke up and brought a different family with me." ᴹ

Kaneki's hand covered Touka's on the table.

"But there was another Trial," Mau said. "In the other life. The Goddess made that one too. I kept getting a chance to try again. That's what the people outside thought was happening. What Ciel could count." ᴹ

He looked down at his own hands.

"She didn't know at first what she was counting." ᴹ

Rimuru set his cup down very carefully.

"How many?" he asked. ᴿ

Mau had known the question would come. He had thought knowing would make the answer a little easier.

"Four hundred and thirty-seven," he said. "Times I died. Some of the lives between them were long." ᴹ

He waited while the number reached the people who had only known an afternoon.
