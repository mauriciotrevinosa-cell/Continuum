*When: Day489, the same late afternoon. Rimuru has put water in front of him again. This time Mau remembers to drink it.*

Milim looked at him across the table.

"How long?" she asked. ᴹⁱ

He swallowed the water before answering.

"I can't give you an honest number of years. I lived long enough in some of them to think I might have found a life that would stay. In others I didn't get that far." ᴹ

"Older than me?" ᴹⁱ

It did not sound like a challenge. She was trying to put him somewhere she understood and finding all the usual places occupied by a person she had not known existed.

"The other Rimuru thought so," Mau said. "He tried to make me laugh about it." ᴹ

Milim looked at Rimuru. He did not pretend to remember making the joke.

Then she looked back at Mau and folded her hands tightly under the table.

* * *

He told them enough of the Trial to explain why ordinary things could come back sharp.

A room in which he had felt safe for years. A person he had thought he would grow old beside. A morning which turned out to have been waiting for the part where it could take that away. He did not describe every death. He did not show Ciel the pictures which pressed behind the words, though he could feel her listening and knew how near they were.

"The other Ciel counted returns," he said. "She didn't live all the time between them. Nobody standing outside did. So when I came back, there were things I knew in my bones which nobody could have seen happen." ᴹ

Shoko looked at the hand he had put round the cup.

"That's part of last night," he said. "Not an excuse for the rest. I knew where I was. I knew the cupboard wasn't that place. I still couldn't make my body believe me." ᴹ

"And the castle?" Anko asked. ᴬ

He looked at her.

"I went because I wanted it to stop. The noise of everything arriving at once. I thought if I did something simple enough, something I knew how to do, I could get quiet." ᴹ

"You could have come out," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Mau nodded.

"I could have. Before the inner room. After it started, I could have finished. I kept choosing another wave instead. I heard you through Igris. I knew you were there." ᴹ

Rimuru's face changed. Not surprise. The hurt of receiving a thing he had suspected and could no longer hope was different.

"I'm sorry," Mau said. "For keeping you outside. For letting you think I might be trapped when I was the one keeping it going." ᴹ

Rimuru did not answer quickly enough for Mau to mistake it for acceptance.

He let the silence stay.

* * *

The tower took fewer words than the scars.

Not because it mattered less. Because he did not have to make them walk every floor to tell them what it had given him and what it had taken. A borrowed seed. A hundred floors, and the one after them which had not been what they were promised. Igris, Beru, the dragon, the others whose names he could say without looking at the page. A thing which had put a second heart in him before he understood what it wanted to use him for.

He told them the two losses stayed lost. That remaking a shadow had costs, that standing one beside him was not the same thing as making one for free. He did not ask the room to admire the list.

Gojo listened with his hands still.

"The eyes," he said when Mau stopped. ᴳ

"Your pattern. A copy. The filter came from work Ciel and Rimuru did with me. The other ones. They built things round it because I couldn't live with it all open." ᴹ

Gojo looked at Rimuru, then back.

"And now it arrives in you already built." ᴳ

"Yes." ᴹ

"That doesn't explain the gray." ᴳ

"No. I thought it would go away when I remembered how everything used to look. It didn't." ᴹ

Yuka's fingers moved beside his. He found them, held them without tightening, and looked back at the room.

* * *

He told them about the field.

The original one. The point at which somebody outside him had taken his history and the love which belonged to it, and left him a different beginning to believe in.

"Noise didn't do it," he said. "It wasn't the loss of memory you remember before the Trial, when I didn't know my own name. That was different. The one in the field was done to me." ᴹ

Echidna lifted her head at that distinction. She had not mistaken the two, but she was watching the rest of the room hear that they must not either.

"I remember thinking I arrived after Yuka," Mau said. "Thinking the way I slept on her floor was something we'd always done. People tried to tell me I had a different life. I thought they were being kind to somebody I couldn't feel I was." ᴹ

Yuka looked down.

"But the things I chose afterward were real. I went to the coast with her. I kissed her. I took her into a gate, and we came back with things we'd done together. Loving her wasn't one more memory somebody wrote for me. It happened after the theft." ᴹ

"While you couldn't remember Frieren," Maki said. ᴹᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

Frieren sat very still.

Mau looked at her instead of speaking around her.

"I hurt you there. You were waiting for someone who could know what you meant when you spoke to him. I kept seeing you hurt and choosing not to ask for all of what remembering would change." ᴹ

He did not lower his eyes until the next sentence was ready.

"It got bad enough that, in that life, you tried to hurt Yuka." ᴹ

* * *

Yuka's hand moved in his.

He let her go at once. She did not take it away, only laid it flat on the table while she looked at Frieren.

The elf looked back, then at Mau.

"I did?" she said. ᶠ

"There. Not here. I'm not telling you what you secretly want. I'm telling you what I remember happening to people whose whole lives with each other had gone differently." ᴹ

Fern's hand was still beside Frieren's. Frieren turned hers into it without taking her eyes off him.

"And did she know?" she asked. "The other one. Did she understand what she'd done?" ᶠ

"Yes." ᴹ

Mau heard the breath which went out of her. He wanted to say he was sorry for telling her, and knew that would not be the right apology either.

"There were plans for how we'd live while we worked it out," he said. "Separate places to sleep. People making room without sending either of us away. I wasn't being told the only way I could stay was to stop loving somebody before morning." ᴹ

He looked at Yuta. Then Rimuru.

"I had help. I need you to know that before I tell you what I chose." ᴹ

* * *

Yuka drew a breath beside him.

"This is the night," she said. "The one you keep starting in the middle of." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"You told me something private. Something important about your life before here. I'm not going to say what in this room." He kept his hands still. "I told part of it to the Goddess because I wanted her to understand why I was frightened for you. I hadn't asked you if I could." ᴹ

Yuka's fingers went to the ring at her chest.

"You told her?" ʸᵏ

"Yes. That was wrong too." ᴹ

He did not reach for her hand while she was deciding where to put it.

"She offered to return what had been taken from me. The history. What I felt. I'd know which part had been put there and which part we'd actually lived afterward. That was the answer I asked for." ᴹ

"And the other one?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

"She offered to change what the house remembered around it. Ciel too. Yuka too. Work would stay. Things we'd learned would stay. The life round those things would be different where it needed to be." ᴹ

The room seemed to become smaller round that last sentence.

* * *

"Was I awake?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

Mau shook his head.

"Did you ask me?" ʸᵏ

"No." ᴹ

She looked at the table. He could see how much it cost her not to speak again before he finished.

"The other Rimuru came," Mau said. "Ciel warned him. They both told me no. He asked me if you knew, if Frieren knew, if Maki knew. I said you didn't. He made me say what that meant. That you wouldn't get to choose. You'd wake up without the part which might have made you refuse." ᴹ

Rimuru had gone very pale.

"Ciel said her work wasn't all of her. That keeping the things she'd built didn't make the rest of her something I could throw away." Mau swallowed. "I heard her. I understood what she was saying." ᴹ

He looked at the place near his shoulder where Rimuru had caught him in the castle. Remembered another room, another reach, the sound of a stool beginning to fall.

"They asked me to wait. To wake the others. To stay while it was difficult." ᴹ

Nobody spoke into the space after that.

"I accepted anyway," he said. "I knew what they were saying no to. I wasn't agreeing to another world. I didn't know that could happen. But I did agree to change what they could remember and choose. I don't get to use the accident to say I hadn't already made that choice." ᴹ

He took his hand off the cup before they could hear it shake against the saucer.
