*When: Day489, the same room. The light at the windows has lowered while they have been talking.*

Yuka had not taken her hand away from the table.

Mau noticed small things because the large one had become too difficult to look at all at once: the ring against her shirt, the cuff Mai had repaired, the place her finger rested beside a scratch in the wood.

"The things that came back," she said. "The sword. The coin. That was her?" ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Then the knives—" ʸᵏ

He shook his head, then stopped himself.

"I bought them. I know that part now. I don't know who made the place I bought them from. Or why I can open it." ᴹ

He looked at the empty place between the cups. He did not expect a face to appear there.

"Are you still here?" he asked. ᴹ

* * *

"Here." ᴰⁱᵒ

The word belonged to the room.

Not to a thread behind Mau's eyes. Not something he would have to repeat while the others waited for the version he chose to give them.

Fern's head lifted at once. Anko went still. At the far end of the table several people looked toward the same empty place without agreeing on where the sound had come from.

Those who had stood through the afternoon Trial knew the voice.

The others learned something from their faces before anyone told them who had answered.

Rimuru looked at Mau.

"I heard that," he said. ᴿ

"So did I," Shoko said. ˢʰ

*Ciel?* Mau asked privately. ᴹ

*Yes,* she said. *This time.* ᶜ

Mau put both hands on the table where everyone could see that he was not doing anything to make the voice.

"Was it you?" he asked. "The shop. The purchase. Did you change it? Or was it Noise?" ᴹ

"I returned the sword," the Goddess said. "And the coin. I did not put those blades in your hands." ᴰⁱᵒ

He waited.

"I think the second heart is involved in what your system is becoming. I don't know enough to tell you that thought is the answer." ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

Shoko leaned forward.

"You think," she repeated. ˢʰ

"Yes." ᴰⁱᵒ

"That's the body I'm trying to keep alive. If you don't know, say you don't know before you do anything else to it." ˢʰ

Mau looked at her.

She was not looking at him. She was looking into the room with the expression she had worn when he told her his scars were normal.

"I don't know," the Goddess said. ᴰⁱᵒ

The simplicity of it did not make Shoko sit back.

Gojo's eyes were uncovered. They had found no body to put their attention on, no face to read. He kept them open anyway.

"What did you actually know?" he asked. ᴳ

"The heart was not an ordinary heart. It could hold what his first one could not. I believed I understood the part of it I needed to touch." ᴰⁱᵒ

"And you didn't," Gojo said. ᴳ

"No." ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

Mau looked at Yuka before asking the next thing.

She had been looking at him already. He did not know whether she wanted him to reach for her, and did not make deciding that another thing she had to do while everyone watched.

"Tell them about the hearts," he said. "The part you offered. Not the things I told you that weren't yours to repeat." ᴹ

"The loves already existed," the Goddess said. "I did not make either one. What had been taken could return to the heart it began in. What he had lived afterward could be held in the other. Both his. The same man, able to keep both without one destroying the place the other occupied." ᴰⁱᵒ

Yuka's eyes went to Mau's chest.

"Literally," she said. ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau nodded when Yuka looked up at him.

"That doesn't make one of them somebody else choosing for me," he said. "I loved you before anybody offered to put it somewhere. I made the choices after that too." ᴹ

Frieren looked down briefly at her own hands.

"And returning that feeling was part of what you asked for," she said. ᶠ

"Yes," he told her. "Remembering wasn't only getting the facts back. I asked for what I felt too." ᴹ

She held his gaze long enough that he stopped looking for an easier way to have said it.

* * *

The Goddess spoke again before he asked.

"The connection was not as closed as I believed. There was something beyond the store I was measuring. When I reached for the place to anchor what he wanted to keep, it answered somewhere I had not measured." ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau remembered the second beat catching. The word *wait* arriving too late.

"Jinwoo," he said. "The voice that had tried to reach me?" ᴹ

"A foreign connection. I did not understand its remaining reach. I know that I touched it. I will not tell you I have mapped everything beyond it." ᴰⁱᵒ

Senku had not interrupted before now.

"Then separate what you did from what you think followed," he said. ˢⁿ

There was a pause.

"I attempted the anchoring and the change of remembered history," the Goddess said. "The effect reached farther. A life developed along a different course. He lived it. His original state and that lived course met. That is the man sitting here, remembering both. I closed the interaction I had opened. I have not undone what followed by closing it." ᴰⁱᵒ

Senku stayed still. He had questions. Mau could see him choosing the one that mattered first.

"This life happened," he said. "It isn't an explanation you planted in a room full of people." ˢⁿ

"It happened." ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

Rimuru looked toward the corridor, where Ori had gone.

"And the other one?" he asked. ᴿ

"It happened too," the Goddess said. ᴰⁱᵒ

"Where?" ᴿ

"I cannot give you a map of where every consequence continues. I can tell you these people are real, and so was the life he remembers. I am not asking you to treat one as a story I made to comfort him." ᴰⁱᵒ

It was not enough.

Mau could feel the room discovering that someone could answer in the voice of a goddess and still fail to give them enough.

"I don't want anyone calling you the replacement," he said to Rimuru. To all of them. "I remember choosing things here too. Your lives don't become less real because something hurt more in the other one." ᴹ

Rimuru looked back at him.

"Then you don't get to hide from this one behind how much the other hurt," he said. ᴿ

"I know." ᴹ

This time he did not use the words to stop the question.

* * *

The room stayed quiet for long enough that Mau thought the Goddess might have gone.

He had another question ready. The gray. The new physical line. Whether the thing he had bought in his sleep would let him do it again. They crowded up behind his teeth, practical enough that he almost welcomed them as something he could ask instead of looking at Yuka.

Then the voice came back.

"I'm sorry." ᴰⁱᵒ

No light changed.

Nothing on the table became whole. Nobody remembered a life they had not lived. Shoko's hand did not leave the cup she had been holding against her palm for warmth.

The apology stayed in the room with what it could not repair.

"I believed I could make the answer fit what I understood of him," the Goddess said. "I did not measure enough before offering it. He did not ask for this consequence. I caused the intervention which reached it." ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau stared at the table.

He had imagined this moment while he was feverish, or half awake, or trying to find a first sentence in the clinic. He had imagined he would know exactly what to say when she finally said the words.

He did not.

* * *

"It wasn't just that night," he said after a while. ᴹ

"No," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

He looked up.

"Two cookies. I could help, not kill anybody, and prove I could do the thing without paying the price everybody was frightened of. I thought I was being careful because I had a plan." His mouth moved without making a smile. "Then I thought asking you enough questions meant I understood the answer I was accepting." ᴹ

The Goddess did not improve his account for him.

"Someone else took things from me," he said. "That was done to me. I don't have to say I deserved it because I got confident. But what I asked you to do afterward was mine." ᴹ

He looked at Ciel's absence from the visible room, the place he always had to remember was not an absence at all.

"I heard no. Twice. I kept making it into something I could be sorry about later instead of something I had to stop doing." ᴹ

Rimuru looked away for a moment.

When he looked back, Mau was still there. Still seated. Still letting the words remain between them without asking to be released from them.
