*When: Day489, evening beginning. Someone has lit a lamp near the far end of the room. Mau does not know who stood to do it.*

He had been watching the tabletop too closely.

The new light made the little scratches stand out. He could remember different hands making some of them in a life the people opposite him had not lived. Could remember the same table before those marks in this life. Neither memory was useful for answering Yuta when he finally spoke.

"You knew they were saying no," he said. ʸᵗ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Not because they didn't understand what you were asking." ʸᵗ

"No. They understood it. They were trying to make sure I did." ᴹ

Yuta looked toward Rimuru.

Mau wanted to say that this Rimuru had not been there, should not have to carry the look the other one had worn. He stopped himself. Rimuru could hear the question without Mau deciding in advance what it would cost him.

"I told him before the gate," Rimuru said. "That I'd stand with him while he told you. That wasn't agreeing before I heard what he had to say." ᴿ

Mau nodded.

"I know." ᴹ

"I need you to keep knowing it after tonight." ᴿ

"I will." ᴹ

He did not add anything about all the things he would do to make that answer safe. Another enormous promise would not improve the small one he had just been given.

* * *

Rimuru listened to something the others could not hear.

Then he looked at Mau.

"Ciel wants to ask you something. She asked me to say it." ᴿ

Mau sat a little straighter.

"All right." ᴹ

"She says she understands the difference between telling her what happened and making her remember it. She doesn't want you to open all those memories. But if she asks for words later, she wants you to let her ask without assuming that keeping her happy means keeping her outside everything difficult." ᴿ

Mau closed his eyes briefly.

He could hear her listening. Not hiding behind Rimuru. Choosing him to carry the question because she was tired of the whole of it arriving as something Mau arranged for her protection.

"Yes," he said aloud. "Words. If you ask. I might need time to find them, but I won't tell you that not knowing is the price of getting to stay yourself." ᴹ

He stopped before it could become too clean.

"Some of keeping you out is for me. I don't want to hear you sound the way she did. That isn't only protecting you. I know that." ᴹ

Ciel stayed quiet for a while.

Then, privately:

*Thank you for saying the other part.* ᶜ

He nodded. Did not mistake her thanks for the end of the distance.

* * *

Gojo asked the practical question Mau had been avoiding.

"Can any of this happen again while he's asleep?" ᴳ

The Goddess did not answer at once.

"I have closed the interaction I opened," she said. "I will not claim that accounts for every new function in him. It does not explain the equipment purchase." ᴰⁱᵒ

"Then we still have that problem," Shoko said. ˢʰ

"Yes," Mau answered. "The receipt says I accepted it. It doesn't tell me how to be certain I won't make the movement again." ᴹ

He described what they knew. The collection he had accepted. The material the page could claim back from its tagged lots. The two weapons bought with nearly all the value they had brought out. The things still held outside that account, untouched.

He did not call the weapons a reward for what he had done in the castle.

Anko's face told him she had heard that distinction.

"And the colors?" Gojo asked into the room. "Did you account for that when you returned the rest?" ᴳ

"No," the Goddess said. "I don't know why that remains." ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau looked at the lamp. A bright shape. At Yuka's face beside it, the color which had never spread into the rest of the room just because she was there.

"Neither do I," he said. ᴹ

It was a small answer to give after everything else. He was beginning to understand how many small answers they would need to live with.

* * *

He thought she had gone again.

Then remembered there was something else he must not leave sitting silently underneath the account.

"I haven't forgotten the other request," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him at once.

"I know," the Goddess replied. "I wasn't going to bring it up while I was apologising." ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau laughed.

One short, startled sound. It escaped before he could decide whether he was allowed to find anything funny in the room they had made. It hurt his ribs, and he stopped with a hand against them.

Shoko's eyes narrowed. He lowered the hand before she could ask whether the pain was new.

"Another request?" Anko said. ᴬ

The laugh was gone when he answered.

"Something I owe. She hadn't chosen what when I accepted. She told me that part before I said yes." ᴹ

"And you still owe it." ᴬ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Not paid by what happened afterward?" ᴬ

Mau shook his head.

"No. Not the fever. Not the castle. Not the coin. None of that was a way of paying something I haven't been told yet." ᴹ

There was no answer for Anko to improve by writing it more neatly.

She left the page where it was.

* * *

Yuka touched the inner pocket of her coat.

"The coin you gave me," she said. "That's why I asked about the other things. I wanted to know if you were giving away something you'd need for this." ʸᵏ

"It isn't a payment token," the Goddess said. "It was the new coin I gave him for my mistake. The sword was something of his I could return." ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau looked at Yuka.

"I meant what I said when I gave it to you. About Tsurugi. I offered you a coin in the other life and couldn't find it because I'd already given the old one away. That offer was mine even while I was wrong about where my life began." ᴹ

Her hand stayed over the pocket.

"This time I asked you again. You chose to take it. You get to decide when to talk to your grandparents and when to ask for him. He gets to choose whether he comes. Nobody's coming through a door tonight because I want to prove I kept a promise." ᴹ

Yuka nodded slowly.

"Good," she said. "Because I want to know him here. I don't want him to arrive in the middle of us not knowing how to talk to each other." ʸᵏ

Maki looked at her granddaughter. The look held things she had not said yet. She did not say them now just because everybody was waiting to see whether she would.

"The coin can wait," she said. ᴹᵏ

Yuka kept her hand over it a moment longer, then let it rest on the table.

* * *

Frieren had not asked him to make the story kinder.

She asked something harder.

"What are you asking me to do with this?" ᶠ

Mau looked at her.

"Know it. Ask me things when you want to. I'm not asking you to become the woman I remember because some part of me still turns toward the place she stood." ᴹ

"And Ori?" ᶠ

"Is your daughter. The compass is hers. Remembering another place those things had in my life doesn't make them something I can take back from either of you." ᴹ

He swallowed.

"I'm sorry for what I did to the other you. For letting the waiting go on while I kept choosing not to face what came after remembering. That doesn't make what happened to Yuka right. And it doesn't make you responsible for it here." ᴹ

Frieren looked at Fern's hand beside hers, then back at him.

"I need to think," she said. ᶠ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Without you deciding what I must be thinking." ᶠ

His mouth moved. Not quite a smile.

"I'm trying to learn that one." ᴹ

She did not give him a better answer to make the effort easier.

* * *

Yuka's question came after another drink of water.

"There were other things we said," she said quietly. "There. Things you haven't asked me for here." ʸᵏ

He looked at her hands, then at her face.

"Yes." ᴹ

"You don't have to say them in front of everybody." ʸᵏ

"I wasn't going to. Some of them were promises about a future. I remember making them. I don't get to tell you what you want now because somebody who was you asked me once." ᴹ

She nodded. The small movement did not tell him whether it helped.

"I heard what you said about telling her something private," she added. "I want to ask you about that. Later. Not like this." ʸᵏ

"Whenever you want." ᴹ

"And you answer." ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

She took the cup which had been sitting between them, turned it so he could get the handle with his good hand, and put it closer to him.

He accepted it without trying to make the gesture mean more than she had given.

* * *

The Goddess did not speak again.

After a while Mau asked whether there was anything in the account she needed to correct. Waited. Received no new answer he could honestly call one.

He looked around the room instead.

At people who had been trying to understand him for days, at people who had only learned this afternoon how much there was to understand. He could see anger. Fear. The concentration of someone holding a question until it would fit into words. None of it belonged to a verdict he could ask them to give before getting up.

"That's what I know," he said. "The parts I remember doing. The parts I was told. Where those stop being the same thing. There will be things I haven't explained well. You can ask me again." ᴹ

He held the cup in both hands, the left arranged so it would not fail him.

"You don't have to decide what happens next while I'm sitting here waiting for you to make it easier." ᴹ

Rimuru stayed beside him. Shoko moved the water within reach. Across the table Yuta unfolded the hands he had kept closed for most of the conversation.

Mau had no last sentence that could put the house back the way it had been before he started.

He finished the water.

Then he put the cup down and let the telling end.
