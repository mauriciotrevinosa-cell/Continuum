# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 36
## Chapter 27 — After He Went Upstairs

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (2026-10-25)
**Volume:** 36
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S6E91
**Continuity state:** Day489 evening after26. Mau goes to his east room with Rimuru, by choice, for a meal and rest; no exile. Household has its first discussion without him and distinguishes original life from their own. Initial fear is acknowledged, but the movement is toward understanding the same person who chose them twice. Yuka speaks for herself; no new permission tribunal, private medical disclosure or imposed memories. Rimuru returns after Mau is settled; Ciel stays privately available to Mau, not secretly broadcasting family deliberation. Frieren and Fern have their own brief exchange. At supper household affection is made concrete without pretending all questions are settled. One day/night of adjustment begins, not instant universal comprehension.

*When: Day489, after the telling. The water in Mau's cup is gone. Dinner has been kept for him.*

Getting out of the chair took less strength than not apologising for needing to.

Rimuru moved the chair back. Mau stood, steadied himself with his good hand and looked at the people still sitting there.

Nobody was asking him to stay. Nobody was asking him to go.

That ought to have made it easier.

"I'll take something upstairs," he said. ᴹ

"You will take dinner upstairs," Shoko said. "Something is what people say before they try to call three bites a meal." ˢʰ

Rem had already put a bowl on a tray. She added bread, considered the distance to the east room, and took the cup off again.

"I'll bring the drink," she said. "This will spill if we argue about who can carry everything." ᴿᵉ

Mau looked at the tray, then at her.

"Thanks." ᴹ

Yuka stood too. His eyes went to her at once, and she saw the question he did not ask.

"I'll come up in a bit," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded carefully.

"Okay." ᴹ

She touched the back of his hand before letting him take the tray.

"I mean that." ʸᵏ

This nod was different.

He went with Rimuru. Rem followed with the drink, telling them to stop making the stairs into a complicated expedition. At the turn, Mau looked back once. Yuka was still standing beside his chair.

Then he was upstairs, and the room discovered how much of its silence had been the effort of choosing words he could bear to hear.

* * *

Maki put both palms flat on the table.

"I keep thinking about the years," she said. ᴹᵏ

Yuta looked at her.

"The things he remembers doing," she added. "Growing used to a place. People. Thinking he'd get to keep them. Then having to begin again." ᴹᵏ

"And coming downstairs here," Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

The answer made Maki look toward the stair.

Mai rubbed at a dried mark on the table. It did not come off.

"I'm trying to put that beside him asking whether I'd seen his coat. Or standing still while I fixed something because he didn't want to tear it worse. He was carrying all that, and I was telling him to stop moving his elbow." ᴹᵃⁱ

"He probably needed to stop moving his elbow," Shoko said. ˢʰ

Mai gave a small, helpless laugh.

"He did." ᴹᵃⁱ

Across the table Bocchi had her hands tucked beneath her thighs. Kita moved her chair closer without asking her to speak.

Bocchi spoke anyway.

"Was he thinking we wouldn't want him here?" ᴮᵒ

Nobody had a quick answer which felt fair to him.

"I think he didn't know what wanting him would look like after we knew," Yuta said at last. ʸᵗ

Bocchi looked down at his empty chair.

"I still want to play the song with him." ᴮᵒ

It was such an ordinary thing to bring into that room that several people turned toward her.

She went red.

"Not tonight. I mean—he was learning the bit. Before. I don't want him to think that's gone." ᴮᵒ

Kita took her hand out from under her leg and held it.

"Then tell him tomorrow," she said. ᴷⁱ

* * *

Anko leaned back, looking up at the darkening beams.

"I was frightened when Rimuru came out of that gate," she said. "I'm not going to pretend I wasn't." ᴬ

Yuka's hand tightened around the sleeve at her wrist.

"So was I." ʸᵏ

"I kept thinking we hadn't got there in time. And then we had him out, and I thought I'd be able to stop thinking it." Anko looked down. "Now I know he was trying to get away from something we couldn't see." ᴬ

Shoko nodded slowly.

"It helps me understand the running. It doesn't make running alone a good way through it." ˢʰ

"He knows," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Her voice came out more sharply than she meant. She took a breath before continuing.

"I know you're not saying he doesn't. I'm just—he looked so tired when he said it. Like he thought understanding why would make us more tired of him." ʸᵏ

Kaneki had stayed near the doorway, where he could hear if Ichika woke. Touka sat beside him with one knee touching his.

"I know that look," he said. ᴷⁿ

He did not explain where he had learned it.

"Being glad someone came home and wishing they'd asked for help can be the same evening." ᴷⁿ

Yuka looked at him. After a moment she nodded.

* * *

Rimuru returned without the tray.

"Eating," he said before anyone asked. "Rem is making sure he doesn't put the bowl somewhere ridiculous." ᴿ

He took his place again. The chair beside it remained empty, but nobody treated it like something that had to be left untouched.

"Do we know what happens now?" Fern asked. ᶠᵉ

Rimuru shook his head.

"Not all of it." ᴿ

"I don't mean what the Goddess will do. I mean what we do tomorrow." ᶠᵉ

He looked around the table.

Gojo had been turning a spoon between his fingers. He stopped.

"Breakfast," he said. ᴳ

Shoko looked at him, expecting the next part.

He put the spoon down.

"He shouldn't have to find out whether we're still his family by walking into a room full of people who stop talking." ᴳ

The plainness of it held her gaze a little longer.

"We can have questions," Rimuru said. "We can need a day to work out which ones we're really asking. But he needs to hear that part while we're working it out." ᴿ

"He picked this place again," Yuta said quietly. ʸᵗ

Maki looked at him.

"Different things happened. Different reasons to stay. He still gave the coin away. Still stood beside us. He didn't have to remember the other life to choose us here." ʸᵗ

Rimuru let out a breath which had been sitting high in his chest.

"Twice," he said. ᴿ

Yuka looked toward the stair again.

She did not think Mau had counted it that way.

* * *

Frieren stayed while the others began moving dishes.

Fern waited beside her.

"You don't have to ask whether I'm all right," Frieren said. ᶠ

"I wasn't going to ask it like that." ᶠᵉ

Frieren looked up.

Fern sat where there was room.

"I was going to ask whether you'd like me to sit here." ᶠᵉ

For a little while Frieren said nothing.

"Yes," she said at last. ᶠ

They watched Yuka help Rem with a pot which did not need three people to carry it. Yuka knew it did not. Rem let her take a handle anyway.

"I keep trying to imagine her," Frieren said. "The woman he remembers." ᶠ

Fern did not supply an answer for that woman.

"There are things I would want to ask her," Frieren added. ᶠ

"You can take your time." ᶠᵉ

Frieren rested her hand over Fern's.

"I know. I want him to be able to take his too." ᶠ

Fern's fingers turned beneath hers.

They stayed until the table was clear.

* * *

By the time Yuka went upstairs, Mau had finished most of the bowl.

The spoon bent very slightly where he had been holding it too tightly. He had laid it beside the tray as if putting down an apology he had decided not to make.

Rimuru came behind her with the water.

Mau looked from one to the other.

"Did I—" he began. ᴹ

"We talked," Rimuru said. "We're going to talk more. There's a lot to understand." ᴿ

Mau's right hand moved against the blanket.

"But you live here," Rimuru continued. "You are wanted here. Don't spend tonight trying to work out how much of yourself you have to leave outside the door." ᴿ

Mau stared at him.

Yuka sat on the bed's edge, close enough that their knees touched.

Downstairs somebody laughed at something which had gone wrong with the washing-up. Rem's voice rose in protest. A second laugh followed.

Mau listened to it until he could breathe normally.

"Okay," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru put down the cup.

"Also, Shoko says the bread counts. You can't just eat around it." ᴿ

Yuka passed it to Mau before he could argue.

He took it.
