*When: Day490, late morning. Mau is holding a cup with a handle he does not trust himself to squeeze.*

It had been an ordinary breakfast.

That was not quite true. Two people had begun a question, stopped, and said they could ask it later. Gojo had looked at Mau for a moment longer than usual before stealing the bread nearest him. Shoko had made him put it back because it was Mau's bread, not because anybody was in danger of going hungry.

But people had eaten. Ori had come to show Frieren a drawing and stayed to tell Mau that one of his trees looked like a hand.

"It might be a hand," he had said. ᴹ

She had considered him with deep disappointment.

"It has apples." ᴼ

"Then it's probably a tree." ᴹ

That had been the end of the inquiry.

Afterward he had offered to help clear the table and found himself slowing every movement around breakable things. The strength stayed whether or not he looked at its numbers. He carried cups with the attention he used to give something hot enough to hurt.

Yuka took three off his tray.

"Leave some for the rest of us." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

"Cups or work?" ᴹ

"Yes." ʸᵏ

She went to the kitchen before he could improve the question.

* * *

She found him later at the step facing the tree.

He had chosen shade. The filtered light made it easier to look up without the left side of the world pulling at his eye. There was still a little dressed place on one knuckle. His fingers rested open on his knee.

Yuka stood in front of him for a moment.

"Can I sit?" ʸᵏ

"You don't have to ask." ᴹ

"I know. I want you to know I'm asking to talk." ʸᵏ

He moved a little, making room.

She sat close enough to share the shade without touching him yet.

The hoodie lay loose at her shoulders. She had turned the cuffs back more neatly today. One side was beginning to come undone.

"I got scared," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked down at his hands.

"I know." ᴹ

"You don't. Let me say which part." ʸᵏ

He looked back at her.

She drew one foot under the step, searching for a comfortable place and finding that the search gave her a few seconds to begin properly.

"I saw the way Rimuru looked when he came out. And then you came back, and I couldn't make you look at anything. I thought the next thing someone told me was going to be worse." ʸᵏ

Mau held still.

"Then yesterday I listened, and it kept getting bigger. How long you'd been alive. What was in you. The things you could do before we ever went to the water here." She pressed her thumb against the step. "You were sitting right beside me, and for a second I felt as if I was looking up at something enormous and couldn't see where it ended." ʸᵏ

He swallowed.

"I'm sorry." ᴹ

"I'm not finished." ʸᵏ

He closed his mouth.

She looked at him then. Directly, though he could see it took effort to do it with wet eyes.

"You still checked whether my chair was catching on the rug. You remembered which apples I liked. You spent half that day trying to find out whether I'd rather stay by the sea. All of that was you too." ʸᵏ

He did not know what his face had done. Her hand moved toward him, then stopped so that he could see she meant it to be his choice whether to take it.

He did.

* * *

"I love you," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at their hands as if the words had changed what he was holding.

"Yuka—" ᴹ

"I do. I'm not saying it because you're sad. I'm saying it because you keep waiting for me to finish being frightened and discover I want to leave." She tightened her fingers. "I'm staying." ʸᵏ

He took a breath. It did not arrive smoothly.

She waited for the next one with him.

"You don't have to know everything about me by tomorrow," he said. ᴹ

"Good. Because you took a very long time making the problem." ʸᵏ

The laugh hurt a little coming out of him.

She leaned her shoulder against his.

"I want to keep finding out. I'll have questions. I'll probably say something badly. You can tell me when I do. But don't start packing my side of the room in your head every time I go quiet." ʸᵏ

"We don't have a side of the room." ᴹ

"Then stop inventing one so you can be tragic about it." ʸᵏ

He put his head down, laughing into a breath he had thought he would lose.

She stayed against him until he could look at her again.

"I love you too," he said. ᴹ

"I know." ʸᵏ

She was smiling now. A little. Enough that he could see how much she had wanted to say the first part without needing him to lead her there.

* * *

They watched Fern cross the yard with a folded cloth over her arm. Frieren followed carrying Ori's drawing. Ori had the compass out again and was walking sideways to see how long she could make its needle argue with the direction she wanted to go.

Frieren caught her by the shoulder before she met a post.

"Forward first," she said. ᶠ

Ori looked accusingly at the compass.

"It doesn't care." ᴼ

Yuka smiled. Mau's hand changed in hers, not enough to let go.

She gave him time.

"The private thing," she said when the three had gone inside. ʸᵏ

He turned toward her at once.

"You said you told her because you were frightened for me." ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Did I ask you to?" ʸᵏ

"No." ᴹ

She looked down at their hands.

"Then I need you to understand that caring about me doesn't mean you get every decision about what other people hear." ʸᵏ

"I understand." ᴹ

She looked up sharply, and he stopped before the words could be the whole answer.

"I wanted her to know how much there was to lose," he said. "I used something you'd trusted me with to make my reason stronger. I didn't ask you. That's the part I have to change, even when I think the reason is good." ᴹ

Yuka sat with that for a while.

"I'm not asking you to tell me what it was," she said. "Not today. I might want to tell you something myself one day. I want it to be mine when I do." ʸᵏ

"It will be." ᴹ

She rubbed her thumb over his.

"Okay." ʸᵏ

He did not ask whether the word meant everything was repaired. She had given him something he could do, which was more useful than making her name a feeling she was still finding.

* * *

After a while she put her arms round him.

He moved slowly enough that she could feel where his hands were going before they settled against her back. For a few breaths the extraordinary body everybody had been trying to understand was occupied with the ordinary difficulty of holding someone gently enough.

"This part," she murmured. ʸᵏ

"What?" ᴹ

"You can keep doing this part." ʸᵏ

He held her closer.

Inside him Ciel stayed quiet. He could feel that she was there. He did not reach for an old image to explain why the quiet mattered.

*Thank you,* he told her privately. ᴹ

*I'm here,* she answered. ᶜ

Yuka drew back only far enough to look at his face.

"I want to talk to them," she said. "Granny. Grandpa. Mai." ʸᵏ

The old tension went through him before he could stop it.

She touched his cheek.

"About us. About what I want. You don't have to rehearse what I'll say while I'm gone." ʸᵏ

He tried to smile.

"Will you tell me if it's terrible?" ᴹ

"I'll tell you if Grandpa makes a terrible joke." ʸᵏ

"That's not quite—" ᴹ

She kissed him before he could finish.

It was brief, warm, and entirely her idea.

"I'm staying," she said again, close enough that he felt the words. ʸᵏ

Then she stood, fixed the cuff which had finally come undone, and went to find her family.
