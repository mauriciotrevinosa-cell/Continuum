Mau could feel the difference before he took the first step.

The weapons were in his hands, low and pointed away from the people watching. Their weight had settled into a balance which made the stiff fingers on the left less awkward without making them move any farther. He could still feel where the grip had to avoid them.

Beyond his hands, the same field waited.

His body expected to reach the far marker sooner.

"Don't run yet," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Mau stopped leaning toward it.

"I wasn't—" ᴹ

Yuka looked at him.

"You were doing it with your shoulders." ʸᵏ

He straightened.

* * *

The current item page had opened without asking for another payment.

*Dragon's Wrath — paired daggers. S.*

Below the familiar name, the equipment entries were plain enough for him to read them twice without finding a hidden instruction between them.

Attack increased while equipped.

Movement speed increased while equipped.

No number of kills to collect. No injury to wait for. No requirement to prove that he was angry enough. The weapons had been bought. The effect came with carrying them as equipment.

He read that aloud.

Umaru wrote it beside the old page.

"So these aren't more points," she said. ᵁ

"No. These are on top of what the points already did." ᴹ

He set the pair on the cloth and took his hands away.

The added readiness withdrew. He could feel where it stopped.

His own strength remained, enormous and inconvenient and present in the care he had to take when turning back toward the others. He closed the Window. Nothing about that body went away.

"And that's still you," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Mau nodded.

"That part stays." ᴹ

* * *

They began without the weapons.

Yuta stood to the side with the timing cord. Rimuru watched the markers. Shoko had agreed to a short comparison, and was now making sure short continued to mean something to the people who had proposed it.

Mau crossed the distance once, stopped well clear of the end marker, and returned at a walk.

He picked up the pair.

The second crossing was faster with the same intention behind his feet. He reached the stopping point sooner than his old judgement expected, corrected without going beyond it, and came back looking at the ground he had covered.

Umaru compared the marks.

"Again without them," she said. ᵁ

He put the weapons down.

The result returned to the first one. Still far beyond the boy who had once needed Yuta to show him how to stand. The daggers had added something to that person. They had not been lending him his own points.

"No extra outlet," Rimuru said after listening to Ciel. "She can see the equipment effect changing. He isn't secretly reinforcing the second run." ᴿ

Mau waited while Umaru finished the line. Nobody asked for a hundred more crossings to make the difference more spectacular.

* * *

The target was layered material from the workshop's unusable offcuts, mounted over clear earth.

First they used an ordinary practice edge, and stopped before Mau's force could turn the comparison into a broken handle. The cut was clean. The depth was measurable.

Then he took Dragon's Wrath in his right hand while keeping its partner low in the left.

He did not swing harder.

The point passed the mark they had made for the first cut. It went through the next layer with resistance he could feel and an ease which made him stop before the movement was finished.

He looked at the blade.

The page offered one more description beneath the equipment entries.

"Sharp enough to cut through a True Dragon," he read. ᴹ

Rimuru's head lifted.

Mau looked toward him before anybody could turn the sentence into a story about something he had never done.

"I remember the shape I made against Kamish. The one I told you about. If this is describing that kind of scale—" ᴹ

He stopped.

He could remember what it had cost to stand in that shape. Remember the fight around it. He had not brought the memory out to be admired beside a target post.

"We're not finding a dragon to check," Shoko said. ˢʰ

"Agreed," Rimuru said promptly. ᴿ

Yuka let out a small breath through her nose. Almost a laugh.

Mau looked down at the two hilts.

"They're staying away from people," he said. ᴹ

* * *

He set them on the cloth again.

Without the equipment the extra speed withdrew, as clearly as it had arrived. The body beneath it stayed his. The left fingers still needed arranging before he could release the second grip neatly.

Yuka waited until his hands were empty before coming closer.

"You felt it when you picked them up?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"And you don't have to make the page stay open?" ʸᵏ

"No." ᴹ

"Expensive," she said. ʸᵏ

"Very." ᴹ

"I'm glad you didn't buy one for each hand separately." ʸᵏ

"Don't suggest things to it." ᴹ

Umaru covered the bottom of her page with her hand, as if protecting the household from the idea entering an official record.

Yuta laughed first. Rimuru followed. Shoko let them finish before telling Mau to put the equipment away and drink his water.

* * *

There were things to carry home, and Mau was allowed one of them.

An ordinary basket. Nothing under examination. He had asked whether anybody needed it moved, and Ram had put it in his hands with a list of where it belonged.

He carried it through the kitchen door and found Rem making room at the counter.

"Here," she said. ᴿᵉ

He set it down too carefully.

"It won't complain," Ram said. ᴿᵃ

"I'm giving it time." ᴹ

Ram looked at the basket as if considering whether it would be more useful than him if encouraged.

Rem handed him a cloth before the discussion could develop.

They worked for a little while without asking him to explain another life.

At the doorway Bocchi appeared with a pick held so tightly between finger and thumb that he could see the little pale place it made.

"The bit," she said. "If you still want to try it." ᴮᵒ

"I do." ᴹ

"Later. When you're done." ᴮᵒ

"He's done." ᴿᵃ

"I can—" ᴹ

He played the difficult bit badly twice and slightly less badly the third time.

Bocchi corrected his right hand. His left could not do everything the position wanted, so they found a smaller part which would sound right beside hers. She tried it slowly, listened, then nodded with the serious relief of someone who had solved a real problem.

He was glad it was a real problem.

When Yuka came looking for him, he was still trying to get back to the right place after a pause. She sat on the floor beside Kita and waited until they finished.

"Tomorrow morning," she said afterward. ʸᵏ

"You're ready?" ᴹ

"I think he's doing fine," Mau said. ᴹ

"Will you come?" ʸᵏ

"Of course." ᴹ
