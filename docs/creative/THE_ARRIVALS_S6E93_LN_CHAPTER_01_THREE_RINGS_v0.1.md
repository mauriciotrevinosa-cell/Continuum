*When: Day492, morning. The cloth lies open between Yuka's hands.*

She had thought the room would be quieter.

Instead she could hear Mai turning a cup on its saucer, the faint complaint of Yuta's chair, Maki telling him to leave the water where somebody could actually reach it. Mau sat beside the window, making himself small in a room where nobody had asked him to.

Yuka looked at him.

He stopped trying quite so hard.

"Ready?" he asked. ᴹ

She nodded, then shook her head.

"Yes. No. Yes." ʸᵏ

"A family trait," Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki glanced at her.

"We know which family." ᴹᵏ

Yuka laughed. It helped her close her hands without holding the coin too tightly.

She thought of her brother.

Not a name on a page of people who might be useful. The sound of his voice when he was trying not to let her hear concern. His shoulder ahead of her on a road. All the things they had understood too late and some they were still learning how to say when she left.

She wanted him to have this too.

The voice came into the room.

*You are sure? Tsurugi Okkotsu.* ᴰⁱᵒ

Yuta's hands went still.

"Yes," Yuka said. "I'm sure." ʸᵏ

Warm. Warmer.

She opened her fingers at the sudden heat and found only the small pink place on her palm.

For a moment she could not look up.

Maki put a hand against the back of her neck.

"We've got somewhere for him," she said. ᴹᵏ

Yuka nodded at the empty cloth.

Mau said nothing about having kept a promise. This moment was hers to wait through.

* * *

Jinshi had the old key.

He fetched it when Fern told him the request had been made, opened the intake hall and checked the space with the brisk care of someone doing a job which needed doing today. There was no register girl waiting for arrivals who never came. The ordinary reception work had ended; the door could still be opened for a person who needed it.

Mau helped move a bench away from the centre.

"There?" he asked. ᴹ

Jinshi nodded.

"Thank you. Leave the doorway clear." ᴶ

They waited near it with water and a clean cloth. Yuka sat, stood, sat again, and told Mai she was not nervous.

Mai looked at her moving knee.

"Of course not." ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Tsurugi came in the afternoon.

There was no trumpet or tearing sound to warn Yuka to be ready. One moment the cleared space was empty. The next he was standing in it, turning at a sound which had not followed him here.

His hand went toward his weapon.

"Tsurugi." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

The movement stopped.

He said her name with none of the composure she had spent the morning expecting him to pretend to have.

She crossed the room before he could decide whether to ask a question first.

His arms closed around her so hard she made an offended sound into his shoulder.

"I'm here," she said, laughing badly. "You don't have to test whether I come apart." ʸᵏ

He loosened his hold without letting go.

"Where have you—" ᵀˢ

Then he saw the people behind her.

* * *

Yuta had stood up.

Maki remained a little to his side, her eyes fixed on the young man looking over Yuka's shoulder. Mai watched him discover both faces. She did not try to make what they meant smaller by supplying a joke too soon.

"Grandpa?" Tsurugi said. ᵀˢ

Yuta swallowed.

"Hi." ʸᵗ

It was an absurdly small word. He seemed to know it, and could not find a larger one before Tsurugi looked at Maki.

"You—" ᵀˢ

"Yes," Maki said, with a smile which was beginning to fail at the edges. "I know." ᴹᵏ

Yuka stepped aside. Her brother did not seem to know which of them he was allowed to go to.

Maki solved it by going to him.

She put a hand against his cheek, the way she had once done when his head had been much lower, and looked at how far he had grown beyond the boy she remembered.

"Look at you," she said. ᴹᵏ

He caught her hand.

For a while nobody explained the world.

Mau stood beside the bench with the unused cup in his hands. When Yuta went to them, he put it down and moved to the open doorway, far enough away to give them room and near enough to fetch anything they needed.

Yuka saw him go. She smiled at him over her brother's shoulder before he turned away.

* * *

The first explanation took a cup of water and most of another.

Tsurugi listened to the broad facts. Continuum. People from different points in lives which did not arrange themselves neatly beside one another. His sister had been here long enough to have routines, arguments, people she went looking for at breakfast.

He asked the questions twice when the first answers would not stay still in his head. Nobody called that difficult.

"And you?" he asked Mai at last. ᵀˢ

"I'm your grandmother's sister. Mai." ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked between them.

"You look—" ᵀˢ

"Careful," Maki said. ᴹᵏ

"Related," he finished. ᵀˢ

Mai smiled with genuine appreciation.

"A promising start." ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

The rings were the first thing none of them could explain by looking younger.

Tsurugi had noticed the cord at Yuka's neck. His fingers went to his own ring before he could stop them.

Yuta set his hand on the table.

Three points in the same family history sat in the light together.

"Continuum brought mine with me," Yuka said. "It wasn't on my hand when I came. It came anyway." ʸᵏ

Tsurugi looked at her, then at Yuta's hand.

"And that one's yours." ᵀˢ

"The one I arrived with," Yuta said. "Not the one taken off either of you." ʸᵗ

Maki let him look.

Nobody called three souls into the room to make the objects easier to explain. There were rings, reserves, the extraordinary persistence of something Yuta had left behind. What they could do beside each other would take care and testing.

Tsurugi covered his own ring for a moment.

"She helped me," he said. ᵀˢ

Yuta nodded, very slowly.

"I'm glad." ʸᵗ

The answer seemed to land somewhere Tsurugi had not known he was waiting to hear it.

* * *

It was Mai who brought another plate.

Mau had given her the tray at the doorway and stepped back again. Tsurugi watched him go across the yard to help somebody with a basket, then looked at Yuka.

"Who is he?" ᵀˢ

She had known the question was coming.

It still made her heart move hard enough that she put her cup down.

"Mau," she said. "My boyfriend." ʸᵏ

Tsurugi looked back toward the yard.

"Your what?" ᵀˢ

"The person I love," she said, less helpfully. ʸᵏ

Mai hid her mouth behind the cup. Yuta looked at the plate with the concentration of a man who had suddenly found it full of delicate work.

Maki did not look away from Tsurugi.

"You can ask him about himself after you've eaten," she said. "You don't have to work out the whole family before the food gets cold." ᴹᵏ

"I wasn't—" ᵀˢ

Yuka raised her eyebrows.

He picked up the food.

* * *

They took him home before evening.

He saw the tree, the wings around it, the people who greeted his sister without needing her to explain who she was. A girl passed carrying folded laundry and told Yuka she had saved her place. A small child announced that there was someone new and was gently told that he could probably hear her.

At the threshold, Tsurugi stopped.

Yuka turned back.

"You don't have to do everything today," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at her. At Maki waiting beside the door. At Yuta carrying a cup he had forgotten to put down in the intake hall.

"I'd like to stay with you tonight," he said. ᵀˢ

Yuka's smile lost all its teasing.

"There's a bed." ʸᵏ

Mau came to the doorway from inside. He had flour on the heel of one hand and had not noticed.

"Welcome," he said. ᴹ

Tsurugi looked him over more carefully this time.

"You train?" ᵀˢ

Yuka sighed.

Mai took Yuta's forgotten cup.

"He managed almost an entire afternoon," she said. ᴹᵃⁱ
