*When: Day493, after breakfast. Tsurugi has watched his sister take the last piece of fruit from Mau's plate and offer him half of it back as if she has done it all her life.*

"You should fight me," he said. ᵀˢ

Yuka stopped chewing.

Mau looked up from the half she had left him.

"I should?" ᴹ

"A match." Tsurugi's gaze moved briefly over the hand holding the fruit. "If you're going to be with my sister, I'd like to know what you can do." ᵀˢ

"You could ask," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

"I'm asking." ᵀˢ

Mai put her cup down.

"You have an interesting accent." ᴹᵃⁱ

Tsurugi ignored that with some effort.

"If I win, you break up with her." ᵀˢ

"Excuse me?" Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Maki closed her eyes for a brief, patient moment. Yuta looked at the ceiling as if searching for a family member he could blame.

Mau set the fruit down.

"If I have to beat you for you to accept our relationship, okay." ᴹ

Yuka turned on him.

"I'm not the prize." ʸᵏ

"I know." He met her eyes before looking back at Tsurugi. "I'll fight you. What happens between me and her still comes from talking to her. You don't get her answer by hitting me." ᴹ

"And neither do you," Yuka added. ʸᵏ

"Understood." ᴹ

Tsurugi leaned back, irritated that the part he had meant as a condition had become something everybody was looking at him for saying.

"Then show me." ᵀˢ

Maki opened her eyes.

"After we decide where. And what you're both using." ᴹᵏ

* * *

Yuta made Mau put the katana down first.

They stood at the cleared practice ground well away from the house. There was open earth beyond the marked space and nobody in the line behind either fighter. Maki had checked the ordinary steel blade she was lending Tsurugi. No soul-cutting tool. No surprise edge taken out of a ring halfway through the match.

Mau laid his sheathed katana on the cloth beside Yuta.

"The daggers too," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"They're in—" ᴹ

"Out. Here. If something startles you, I don't want finding a blade to be the first thing your hands remember." ʸᵗ

Mau looked at him, then nodded.

Dragon's Wrath arrived low over the cloth, one in each hand. The equipment's extra readiness entered his body at once. He set both down and released the grips.

It left him at once too.

The strength beneath it remained.

Yuta covered the blades, then moved the whole cloth farther behind the watching line. Mau let his empty hands hang where everyone could see them.

Tsurugi watched with a small expression of confidence.

"All that for a practice match?" ᵀˢ

"Yes," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

His tone made Maki glance at him. She did not add anything.

* * *

*Before we start,* Mau said privately, *can you do the timing? If I need the Void to stop it. The shortest pulse.* ᴹ

Ciel did not answer immediately.

He waited instead of reaching for the pattern without her.

*I'm comparing the structure you can hold with the release interval. Gojo's old example is not a table of what will happen to every person.* ᶜ

*I want a few seconds. Enough to take the sword. Not a hospital.* ᴹ

*A ten-thousandth of a second is a candidate exposure, not a promise of five seconds afterward. I can prepare the release. You still have to form it cleanly. The boundary must exclude the people watching.* ᶜ

He looked over the marked ground. Counted the space between it and the others.

*And if it isn't clean?* ᴹ

*Don't use it. Stop the match.* ᶜ

He let the answer stand. Then asked her to pass the plan to Rimuru so the people supervising would know what he was preparing.

Rimuru listened, went to Shoko and Gojo, and spoke quietly. Shoko's eyes came to Mau at once.

"One attempt," she said. "You don't keep increasing it to see what the number means." ˢʰ

"I won't." ᴹ

Gojo watched the brief outline Mau formed over empty ground, without opening its information flood. The shape held away from the spectators. Mau let it go.

"That close," Gojo said. "Keep it that close." ᴳ

Tsurugi had watched the preparation without knowing what every quiet exchange meant. Maki told him there might be a brief immobilising technique, that he could refuse the match, and that her stop ended it whether either fighter liked the result or not.

He looked toward Mau's nearly empty hands, the small amount of CE he could feel, and the wrapped weapons behind Yuta.

"I'm ready," he said. ᵀˢ

* * *

Beru asked before Mau stepped onto the earth.

The voice came from the shadow at his feet, rich with an emotion entirely out of proportion to the occasion.

"My king. May I witness this?" ᴮᵉʳᵘ

Mau looked down.

"You're not fighting him." ᴹ

"I shall be exemplary." ᴮᵉʳᵘ

"You're not threatening him afterward either." ᴹ

A small silence.

"I shall be exemplary," Beru repeated, with greater care. ᴮᵉʳᵘ

Another voice followed.

"I would also see it, if you permit," Igris said. ᴵᵍ

"As would I," Kamish said. ᴷᵃ

Mau closed his eyes briefly.

Yuka covered her mouth.

"You are enjoying this," he told her. ᴹ

"I haven't done anything." ʸᵏ

He let the three rise in the space behind the watching line: the knight, the insect, the dragon keeping his great shape folded well clear of the trees and the people. The reserve registered the calls. No new shadow joined them.

Tsurugi looked from one to the next.

"They're spectators," Mau said. "They won't attack. They won't help. Witnesses." ᴹ

Beru bowed as if the word described a sacred office.

Igris inclined his head.

Kamish settled on the earth with his attention fixed on the two young men.

"Begin when Maki says," Yuta told them. ʸᵗ

* * *

Tsurugi was fast.

The first step erased the easy distance Mau had left between them. Steel rose from low on the right, changed its path when he moved, and came back toward the shoulder instead of chasing the place his chest had been.

Mau turned.

The blade passed his shirt.

Tsurugi was already moving into the recovery, using the motion to take the space Mau had yielded. His feet were good. The economy in the second cut made Yuta lean forward a little, recognising training which had continued after his own life ended.

Mau gave another step.

"You're giving him the line," Tsurugi said. ᵀˢ

Mau did not answer.

He could see the line. He could also see Yuka's attention moving between their hands, worried for both of them in ways which would not fit into a demand to win.

The next cut came lower.

He stopped its arrival at the narrow space beside his hip, Infinity present for the contact and gone when the blade withdrew. Tsurugi felt the refusal and changed his angle instead of wasting strength pushing through it.

That was good too.

Mau moved again.

* * *

"He's retreating," Tsurugi said under his breath. ᵀˢ

From where Yuka stood, she could see why he believed it.

Mau had made no great display. He had not struck her brother. The brief distortions stopped steel at places so small that, if she had not known what she was looking for, she might have thought Tsurugi had checked his own hand.

Her brother began to press harder.

Mau changed the distance, let a controlled shallow pass brush the outside of his right forearm, and drew back before the edge could travel.

A thin line opened. Nothing deep.

Shoko stood straighter.

Above Mau, the wheel appeared.

Yuka heard its first movement and felt the sound in the space between her shoulders.

Mau kept the marked arm where he could see it. The blade had given the turning shape one thing to understand. It had not given it every weapon in the world.

Tsurugi came in again.

Mau caught the line with distance, let a short Infinity contact turn the point aside, and heard the wheel working through what had reached him.

"You can stop giving ground," Tsurugi said. "Or is this what she thinks protecting her looks like?" ᵀˢ

Mau's expression changed very little.

"She can hear you." ᴹ

"Then she can hear that she's letting a pretty face do her thinking." ᵀˢ

"Tsurugi," Yuka said sharply. ʸᵏ

Her brother turned the complaint into the beginning of another advance.

Mau stopped moving backward.

The wheel clicked.

Steel struck the place it had opened before.

The skin did not part.

The force moved through Mau's braced arm and into the earth beneath his feet. He felt it. The edge which had known how to cut that place no longer found the same answer.

He looked at Tsurugi across the length of the blade.

"You can say what you want about me," he said. "Don't speak to Yuka like that again." ᴹ

For the first time since the match began, her brother's confidence did not find a quick reply.
