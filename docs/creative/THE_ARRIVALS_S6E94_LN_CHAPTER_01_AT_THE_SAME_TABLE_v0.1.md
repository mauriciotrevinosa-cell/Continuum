*When: Day493, on the practice ground. The sword is still against Mau's arm, and neither man has moved.*

Tsurugi felt the change through the grip.

He had put that edge into the same line twice. The first time it had found skin. The second time the resistance was wrong in a way no ordinary hardening explained. The blade had not become blunt. The man behind it had changed his answer.

Mau lowered his arm.

"She makes her own decisions," he said. ᴹ

Tsurugi pulled the sword back.

His eyes went to Yuka. She was furious, but she was also watching his hands with the worry he knew too well to mistake.

That should have been enough to make him stop talking.

It was not quite enough to make him stop fighting.

He touched the ring.

* * *

The reserve arrived like a door opening behind him.

What stood around his shoulders was not a second fighter taking his place. The form fitted over him, white and terrible, its strange head rising above his own, the mane lifting in a wind the people watching could not feel. Rika's power ran along his arms and into the steel he still held.

Yuta stood very still.

He could recognise where something began and still be unprepared to see where it had gone after him.

Maki moved one foot back to make room for the recovery if it went wrong.

"Tsurugi. Control your line," she said. ᴹᵏ

He shifted it. Away from the watchers. Toward the empty run of earth beyond Mau.

She saw that he had heard her. She also saw the way he looked at Mau's empty hands as if he could still force them to prove what he wanted to believe.

"This is different," he said. ᵀˢ

Mau looked at the shape over him.

"I can see that." ᴹ

The wheel remained above Mau's head, turned to the one cut it had learned. It had not learned all the power now filling the blade. He knew the difference. So did Ciel.

*The body is coupled to the active reserve,* she said privately. *Your boundary can include both without the audience. The prepared interval is ready.* ᶜ

Mau set his feet.

*One pulse,* he said. ᴹ

*One.* ᶜ

* * *

Tsurugi moved with the whole borrowed weight behind him.

The ground broke beneath the first drive. His sword came through a line which would have forced Mau toward the second step, then changed before that step existed. The form over his shoulders moved with him. Its attention was his, its enormous reserve made available through the ring he had once found so difficult to accept.

For an instant Yuta could see what the boy had learned to do with help.

Mau did not retreat into the line.

He raised his right hand.

The old shape was there, familiar in the nerves which remembered forming it long before this body had become so strong. Ciel held the prepared release beside his intent. He gave her the moment they had agreed on.

"Domain Expansion." ᴹ

Gojo's uncovered eyes narrowed.

"Unlimited Void." ᴹ

* * *

The watching line stayed outside.

The earth inside the small boundary disappeared from Tsurugi's experience before his sword could finish travelling. There was distance, and the idea of distance, and everything which could have been between one and the next arriving without the space required to understand it.

Then it stopped.

Not because he had understood it. Because Mau and Ciel closed the exposure where they had prepared to close it.

One ten-thousandth of a second.

The field returned to the people who had never left it.

Tsurugi remained in the beginning of his strike, eyes open, the command which would complete it unable to find its way through him.

Mau stepped inside the sword's path.

The copied technique had gone empty. Infinity did not rise when the old reflex reached for it. He let that reach go. His own body was still his, strengthened by points which did not depend on a technique answering.

He turned the flat with his good hand, took the grip out of fingers which had not yet received the instruction to resist, and set the sword on the earth behind him.

Rika's form loosened around Tsurugi as the control sustaining the move faltered.

Maki's voice cut across the ground.

"Over." ᴹᵏ

Mau took a step back, empty-handed.

One breath. Another.

Tsurugi's eyes moved.

His knees followed a little too quickly.

Mau caught him before he struck the ground.

* * *

"Where—" Tsurugi said. ᵀˢ

He blinked hard. His hands tried to find the sword. Mau guided one toward his own shoulder instead.

"Here. Stay here a second." ᴹ

Shoko was already coming across the cleared line. Yuka reached them at the same time, knelt, and caught the sleeve her brother was trying to pull away from his face.

"Don't rub your eyes," she said. ʸᵏ

"I can see." ᵀˢ

"Good. Then look at her." ʸᵏ

Shoko had begun asking questions.

Name. Where he had been before the match. Whether the ground was moving. Whether he could follow her hand without turning his whole head. He answered the first too sharply, the second with a pause, and the next with his teeth clenched because his head hurt.

She made him sit properly instead of attempting to prove he could stand.

Mau stayed within reach until Yuta took the place beside him.

*Four to five seconds before useful response,* Ciel said privately. *For this exposure. For him. I cannot turn one result into a promise for another person.* ᶜ

Mau closed his eyes briefly.

*I know. Record it. No second attempt.* ᴹ

He passed the result to Rimuru in words when his friend came over. No one called for a longer demonstration.

* * *

Behind the watching line Beru had drawn himself so upright that the stillness looked painful.

Igris looked at him once.

"I am behaving," Beru whispered. ᴮᵉʳᵘ

"Continue," Igris said. ᴵᵍ

Kamish watched Mau stand while keeping his attention on the young man Shoko was examining.

"There," the dragon said. "Watch that part too." ᴷᵃ

Beru's gaze followed.

Mau had noticed Yuka kneeling on a loose stone. He moved it out from under her before taking his hand away.

The insect went quiet.

* * *

Tsurugi could sit without support after a little while.

He did not like the time it took him to find the first sentence. Mau stood far enough away to let him have it without somebody towering over his shoulder.

"I lost," Tsurugi said. ᵀˢ

Yuta nodded once. He did not improve the sentence into an explanation of how badly.

Tsurugi looked at his sister.

The headache had not made her less angry. He could see that, which was almost a relief. Her face was one thing in the field he understood how to read.

"I shouldn't have said that about you." ᵀˢ

"No," Yuka said. "You shouldn't." ʸᵏ

He looked down.

"I got here, and everyone had a place with you already. He had one. I didn't know what I'd missed." ᵀˢ

Her anger changed shape. It did not vanish.

"Then ask me. You're my brother. You don't have to beat someone to have a place." ʸᵏ

He put his hand over the ring again. The form around him had withdrawn, leaving him looking far younger than the power he had just carried.

"You really love him?" ᵀˢ

Yuka looked at Mau.

He was watching them with his hands open, a thin closed line on his forearm and the expression of a man trying very hard to let a family have its conversation.

"Yes," she said. "I told you that before you lost." ʸᵏ

Mai made a small sound behind her hand.

Maki did not bother hiding her smile.

* * *

Mau came closer when Tsurugi looked up at him.

"I'm not very good at finding out I don't know things," Tsurugi said. ᵀˢ

Mau thought about a room in another life. Two voices telling him no, and all the ways he had tried to make knowing better mean choosing better without doing the choosing.

"Neither am I," he said. ᴹ

Tsurugi looked at him, unsure whether he was being mocked.

Mau held out his right hand.

"You can ask me things too. Next time." ᴹ

The hand remained there long enough that Tsurugi had to decide what to do with it.

He took it.

Mau helped him up without pulling him off his feet.

"Not another round today," Shoko said immediately. ˢʰ

Both men said yes.

That made Yuka laugh at last.

* * *

Yuta returned the covered weapons after Shoko had finished looking at Mau's arm and told him exactly what she thought of offering a blade somewhere to learn from.

He listened. He did not call the shallow cut proof that nothing could go wrong.

The two daggers came back into his inventory without being equipped again. The sheathed katana went with Yuta to the house. The three witnesses returned to Mau's shadow when he thanked them; none had moved to attack Tsurugi, during or after the match.

He did not try another copied technique to see whether the burnout was over. Ciel would keep watching while he rested. His strength remained when he helped carry the water back, and he used a very small part of it.

* * *

Tsurugi sat between his sister and Mai at dinner.

Shoko asked about the headache again before he touched the food. It had eased, not disappeared. She told him where to find her if it changed, and made him repeat the instruction instead of nodding because he wanted the conversation finished.

Yuka waited until she had gone before passing him the bread.

"You could have just eaten with us yesterday," she said. ʸᵏ

"I did eat with you yesterday." ᵀˢ

"Yes. You should have kept doing that." ʸᵏ

Mai laughed into her cup.

Across the table Maki was telling Yuta that a person could recognise his own bad habits in a grandchild without looking quite so personally betrayed by them.

"I wasn't like that," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Maki looked at him.

He took a drink.

Mau sat beside Yuka. When she reached under the table, he gave her his hand without first checking whether anybody was looking.

Tsurugi noticed. He let the bread reach his plate before deciding he did not need to comment.

"Good start," Mai murmured. ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Later, when there were dishes between all the things they still wanted to ask, Yuka leaned her shoulder against Mau's.

"He'll get easier," she said quietly. ʸᵏ

Mau glanced at her brother arguing with Mai about who had misunderstood the directions to his room.

"Will he?" ᴹ

She considered it.

"No. But you'll get better at it." ʸᵏ

He laughed.

Her fingers stayed in his. The house moved around them, making room for one more voice.

For tonight there was nowhere either of them needed to go.
