*When: Day494, morning. Rimuru is still asleep above the closet. Mau is trying to get his sleeve over the hand which never quite closes.*

He had put the wrong arm in first.

That was the whole problem. It ought to have been a small enough problem that a person who had done all the things he remembered doing could solve it without sitting on the bed to think.

He sat on the bed to think.

*Other sleeve first,* Ciel said. ᶜ

*I know.* ᴹ

*You have been knowing it for some time.* ᶜ

He looked up toward the loft as if Rimuru might have heard her.

Nothing moved above him.

*You didn't tell him?* ᴹ

*About the sleeve? No.* ᶜ

Mau pulled his good hand out, rearranged the shirt and tried again. It went on without argument this time. The fingers still stopped where they always stopped. He smoothed the cloth over them before finding the cuff.

*Thank you.* ᴹ

*You're welcome.* ᶜ

He remained sitting for a moment, listening to the small movements of the house getting ready for breakfast.

*How are you?* he asked. ᴹ

*There is no change in the measurements you asked me to watch.* ᶜ

*Not those.* ᴹ

She was quiet.

He let her be.

* * *

They went downstairs before Rimuru woke.

Mau took water, found a seat near the kitchen and watched Rem sort a little stack of spoons. She set one aside, gave him a look, then began using the rest.

It was his bent one.

He looked down into the cup.

*I thought you meant the duel,* Ciel said at last. ᶜ

*I can ask about that afterward. I wanted to know how you were.* ᴹ

*I'm still deciding what I felt when you asked me to prepare the timing.* ᶜ

Mau put the cup down.

She continued before he could decide which apology belonged first.

*You waited. You let me finish. You told Rimuru what we were considering before doing it. Those were things I had asked you for.* ᶜ

He remembered her voice at the side of the field. Not a voice which knew every life inside him. The one who had been here, with him, deciding what she could help him do.

*I didn't want asking for those things to become something you had to keep doing forever without anything changing,* he said. ᴹ

*I noticed.* ᶜ

He let the small answer reach him.

* * *

There was a sound from the kitchen, a bowl put down too sharply, then Ram telling somebody that winning an argument did not change where the bowls went.

Mau smiled into his water.

*You keep thinking I need to meet the other Ciel to understand you,* she said. ᶜ

The smile faded.

*Sometimes.* ᴹ

*You don't say it. But when I ask something, you stop as if you're working out whether the answer would make me more like her.* ᶜ

He could have explained why the thought frightened him. She knew enough of why already.

*I do that,* he said. ᴹ

*I don't want you to give me everything she endured. I haven't changed my mind about the pictures. But I'm here. I can be sad about something you tell me and still be myself afterward.* ᶜ

He rubbed his thumb along the cup's rim.

*What would you like me to do?* ᴹ

She took a little time with that answer too.

It struck him how often he had asked what she could measure, how often what she could hold, and how rarely the question had been left open like this.

*Tell me things when you think of them. You can say you aren't ready for a question. Let me ask it anyway.* ᶜ

*Okay.* ᴹ

*And tell me ordinary things too.* ᶜ

He looked toward the kitchen doorway.

*Ordinary?* ᴹ

*I don't want every way of knowing you better to begin with finding a smaller terrible thing.* ᶜ

He closed his eyes briefly.

*No. I don't want that either.* ᴹ

* * *

The memory he offered was from the afternoon with Bocchi.

Not the field. The room where Bocchi had put an instrument into his hands and shown him where the smaller part would fit beside hers. The first wrong note. The patient silence while she worked out what his left hand could actually do. Kita on the floor with her knees up, trying not to laugh when he found the wrong note again in an entirely new place.

*I was there,* Ciel said. ᶜ

*I know. Would you like to see how I remember it?* ᴹ

She considered.

*Yes. Just that.* ᶜ

He let her see the small piece he chose.

The note sounded wrong in him too. Under it there was something she had not needed to measure at the time: the relief of having one problem he could hear, one adjustment he could make, a person beside him who wanted him to try again because the song would sound better with him in it.

He closed the piece when it ended.

Ciel stayed quiet for a while.

Then she said, *You were happy.* ᶜ

*Yes.* ᴹ

*I knew. I didn't know that part of it.* ᶜ

He opened his eyes.

Rem had put breakfast beside him without interrupting the conversation she could not hear.

*We could have more of those,* he said. ᴹ

*We already do. Let me know when you find one.* ᶜ

*I will.* ᴹ

Deep in the part of him which had once answered every difficult thing by building, there was a familiar shift of attention.

*Maybe,* Noise said. ᴺ

Ciel was still for half a breath.

*Yes,* she answered. ᶜ

No new door opened. Nothing in Mau's body became easier to carry by magic. He ate while the two presences stayed close, each in the place he was learning not to confuse with what they had been somewhere else.

* * *

Ori found him after breakfast.

She had a folded paper in one hand and the compass in the other. The paper had been folded badly enough that one corner refused to stay down.

"I made a better map," she announced. ᴼ

Mau put down his cup.

"Better than whose?" ᴹ

"Yours." ᴼ

"A low bar." ᴹ

She put it in front of him with the air of someone who did not need encouragement from the defeated party.

The tree was recognisable. So were the kitchen door and the long step beside it. There were marks for places a person could hide, and one large cross over the roof of a building which appeared to be his contribution to the old game.

"Mum says we can play after lunch." ᴼ

"You and her?" ᴹ

Ori looked at him as if the question had been left unfinished by mistake.

"And you. If you want." ᴼ

He looked down at the paper again.

In another life an invitation from her would have belonged to a different set of ordinary things. He could remember them without making this one smaller.

"I'd like to," he said. ᴹ

She sat beside him and began explaining which marks were clues and which were places he was definitely not allowed to suggest this time.

He listened carefully.

* * *

They played after lunch.

Frieren hid first. Ori used the compass openly because it was her game and she saw no reason to make it needlessly difficult. Mau carried the paper and was told, twice, that holding it upside down did not mean the house had moved.

They found Frieren behind a hanging cloth beside the laundry.

"You weren't very hard to find," Ori told her. ᴼ

Frieren looked at the compass.

"I wonder why." ᶠ

Mau bent his head over the map to hide his smile.

On the next round Ori hid while Frieren and Mau waited together near the tree. There were footsteps from the far side of the yard, then the exaggerated quiet of someone believing herself unobserved.

Frieren listened.

"Don't look yet," she said. ᶠ

"I'm looking at the map." ᴹ

"It's upside down." ᶠ

He turned it over.

When they went to look, Ori was exactly where the paper said a person ought to be.

Mau took long enough finding her that she finally had to complain.

That was the part he had been waiting for.
