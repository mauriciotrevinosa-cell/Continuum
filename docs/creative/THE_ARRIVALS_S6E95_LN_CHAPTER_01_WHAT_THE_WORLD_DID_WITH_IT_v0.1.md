## Chapter 35 — What the World Did with It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S6 v0.1 (2026-10-25)

*When: Day494, afternoon. Ori has gone to find Milim, taking the map with her. Frieren is collecting the book she left beside the tree.*

"Could we talk?" Mau asked. ᴹ

Frieren looked up from the place she had marked.

"We have been talking." ᶠ

He smiled a little.

"Properly. Just us, if you have time." ᴹ

She put the bookmark back in and closed the cover.

"I have time." ᶠ

They took the bench on the quieter side of the yard. From there the house was close enough to hear in small pieces, dishes and a voice calling a name, without every word passing between them becoming part of somebody else's afternoon.

Frieren set the book beside her.

Mau looked at it for a moment.

He knew how she would rest her hand on the cover. Knew the little movement she made when a page wanted turning and she was trying not to interrupt a person who might have something worth finishing.

He had loved those things once.

He could remember how it had felt to love them without the feeling reaching forward to ask this woman to come back to him.

That difference had taken longer to say than it had to notice.

* * *

"I owe you a better conversation than what I managed at the table," he said. ᴹ

"You managed quite a lot at the table." ᶠ

"I know. Some of it was about you without being yours." ᴹ

She considered that.

"What did you want to tell me?" ᶠ

He looked toward the tree before answering.

"That it was good." ᴹ

Frieren did not move.

"There was so much hurt in what I told you that I don't think I left enough room for that. We were happy. You were funny. You were impossible about where books belonged. We had mornings where the most difficult thing I had to do was get you downstairs before breakfast stopped being breakfast." ᴹ

Her mouth moved at one corner.

"People become unreasonable about breakfast." ᶠ

"You said that there too." ᴹ

He laughed softly, and was glad the laugh could be true without turning into another thing he had to swallow.

"I loved you. I remember it. I don't want remembering it to turn into telling myself it was all a mistake because of how it ended." ᴹ

Frieren folded her hands over her lap.

"But you aren't asking for it now." ᶠ

"No." He took a breath. "The memories came back. Remembering how I loved you came back. That hasn't become being in love with you here. I chose Yuka when so much was missing. I know more now. I still choose her." ᴹ

Frieren looked at him for a little while, then nodded.

The nod did not need to be sad to have weight.

* * *

"I kept thinking about what you told me," she said. "The waiting. The way she asked you for more time." ᶠ

He looked up quickly.

"That wasn't your fault." ᴹ

"I haven't finished." ᶠ

He closed his mouth. After a moment she gave him the smallest approving look, which made him want to laugh again.

"I know I didn't live it. But I can understand wanting someone to stay. I can understand how wanting it badly enough might make it difficult to hear what they were really promising." ᶠ

Mau let his hands rest open on his knees.

"I wasn't lying about wanting to," he said. "There. I wanted a life with you. I wanted it to be longer than anybody could tell me it would be. The love wasn't something you did to me." ᴹ

She looked toward the house.

"Then why does it sound as if it hurt you so much?" ᶠ

He followed her gaze. Somewhere inside, Ori had begun explaining something at a speed which suggested Milim was not agreeing quickly enough.

"Because everything learned where to press," he said. ᴹ

* * *

Frieren waited.

He was grateful she did not make him turn the answer into a list of names to prove it.

"A life could be built where losing you was the worst thing I could imagine. Then something made me live it. Again. A promise could mean choosing to stay, and somebody could turn it into a door I had to walk through. Someone could take loving you out of me and leave the people who knew me standing there trying to get me to understand what I'd lost." ᴹ

His left fingers drew in as far as they could and stopped.

"I keep wanting one word for it. Beautiful doesn't explain what it cost. A curse doesn't explain why I was happy." ᴹ

Frieren looked at his hand, then back at his face.

"The world used it," she said. ᶠ

He nodded.

"It used what I loved. That doesn't make you the person who cursed me." ᴹ

For a while there was only the sound of the house.

He could feel his own breathing beginning to settle. The memories had not become gentler. He had found a way to put a few of them down between two people without making either person carry all of them alone.

* * *

"I'm sorry," he said. ᴹ

Frieren looked back from the tree.

"For the things I did when I had choices and kept pretending I only had one. For making her wait while I tried to find an answer which wouldn't ask anything difficult of me. I don't get to blame all of that on what was done to me." ᴹ

He did not ask her to forgive him on behalf of another woman. He did not make what the other Frieren had done to Yuka disappear inside his apology.

"I wanted you to hear the difference from me. And thank you. For what you were to me there. For what you've done here without knowing any of it." ᴹ

She looked down at her book.

"You helped me here too," she said. ᶠ

"I know." ᴹ

"Then don't turn all of this into one person being grateful and the other being useful." ᶠ

He smiled, properly this time.

"I'll try." ᴹ

She rested her hand against the cover.

"Don't let it do that to you again." ᶠ

The smile left him slowly.

"The world?" ᴹ

"Use someone you love until you can't tell which decisions are still yours." ᶠ

He thought about the voice saying no. The many ordinary ways a person could refuse to listen before the refusal became one terrible night.

"I know," he said. "I have an advantage this time. I remember things the world around me hasn't done yet. I can recognise some of the questions before I answer them." ᴹ

Frieren watched him.

He added, before she had to:

"And I can ask somebody else what I'm missing. Knowing more doesn't mean knowing everything." ᴹ

She nodded once.

"That would be a useful advantage." ᶠ

* * *

They sat until the shade had moved off one end of the bench.

It was Frieren who opened the book again, then closed it without reading. Mau recognised the thought which had caught her by the way she kept her finger between the pages.

"Himmel is here," he said. ᴹ

She looked at him.

"I have noticed." ᶠ

"I mean—" He stopped. She waited with a face which made it clear she would let him finish digging if he preferred. "You don't have to spend this life only looking at the things the other one lost." ᴹ

She took her finger out of the book.

"Neither does he," she said. ᶠ

"No. You're right." ᴹ

The answer made her look at him more carefully.

He leaned forward, elbows on his knees.

"There's something I could try to understand. Later. If you wanted me to. The elf route. Whether any part of it could be shared safely with him, instead of me guessing what it could give someone else." ᴹ

Frieren's eyes changed, attention sharpening at once.

"Could it?" ᶠ

"I don't know. I know what it did to me. That's not enough to promise him years. I could look. With help. And only if he wanted us to." ᴹ

He watched her think about it. A possibility was not a gift he could put in her hands and ask her to unwrap with the right expression. He could not repay a life by choosing somebody else's length.

"Thank you," she said after a while. ᶠ

He nodded.

"But first I want to understand Himmel," she continued. "This Himmel. What he wants. What being here has meant to him. I know so many things about him that I sometimes forget to ask him anything new." ᶠ

Mau sat back.

"I think he'd like that." ᴹ

"I'll ask him," she said. ᶠ

He laughed at the correction, and let it be one.

* * *

When they stood, Frieren picked up her book and looked toward the kitchen.

"What time is it?" she asked. ᶠ

Mau checked the light, then decided he did not trust himself to give it a number.

"Close enough that you'll say everyone is unreasonable again." ᴹ

She made a small, thoughtful sound.

"They should be more consistent." ᶠ

They walked back together.

At the door Ori called for her mother. Frieren answered, already turning toward the voice. Mau held the door while she went through, watched her stop to listen to something important about the map, and then followed them inside.

He did not feel a life returning to its old place.

He felt the afternoon continuing.
