*When: Day494, after dinner. Upstairs, two different doors have been left open for people who already know the way.*

Taihei had the cups.

Yuta had told him he could carry half, and Taihei had looked at the stack, looked at the hatch, and said he would carry them to the ladder before they invented a better way to spill everything.

It had become a small system.

Mau went up first, turned, and took each cup as it came through. Rimuru followed with the pot. Gojo arrived last, discovered the best place had already been taken, and sat in it anyway until Taihei reminded him that the person there had legs.

"I noticed," Gojo said. ᴳ

"Then stop sitting on them." ᵀ

He moved, taking his dignity a few inches sideways.

Below them a window stood open. From another part of the upper floor came the sound of Seiko telling someone that the chair had been there longer than their complaint.

"Both tables," Yuta said, passing the last cup. ʸᵗ

Mau settled against the support and looked out across the darkening yard.

"Good," he said. ᴹ

* * *

Yuka had stopped in Seiko's doorway.

Mai had told her to come up. She had assumed that meant something needed carrying, which was why she held a little dish of crackers in both hands and was beginning to suspect she had been tricked.

"Sit down," Seiko said. ˢᵉ

Yuka looked at the people already there.

Maki made room beside her. Shoko had a cup near the window; Frieren sat with her book closed on her knee. Turbo Granny occupied a place which looked chosen to inconvenience the largest possible number of people.

"I brought these." Yuka held up the dish. ʸᵏ

"Useful qualification," Seiko said. ˢᵉ

Mai took it from her and put it within reach.

"You're visiting. You don't have to stand in the door until somebody gives you a duty." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka sat beside Maki. Her grandmother put a hand briefly against her shoulder, then went on listening to Shoko finish a story about Gojo's opinion of the practice equipment.

"He wanted a nicer piece of iron," Shoko said. ˢʰ

Frieren looked up.

"Did the iron care?" ᶠ

"That's approximately what I asked." ˢʰ

Yuka began to laugh before she had decided whether she was allowed to.

Nobody made anything of it. Mai passed her the dish.

* * *

Kaneki came through the roof hatch carrying his own cup.

Coffee. Black. Mau caught the scent before he saw what it held.

"For a little while," Kaneki said. "Touka's with Ichika." ᴷⁿ

Yuta moved a knee to leave space. Nobody asked him to explain whether he had earned the evening off.

Gojo was describing the moment Tsurugi discovered three armed spectators were interested in his practice match.

"The dragon was very restrained," he said. ᴳ

"He was," Mau said. ᴹ

"I meant spatially. He took up most of the idea of there being room left." ᴳ

Rimuru made a sound into his cup.

Taihei looked toward Mau.

"How is he today?" ᵀ

"Tsurugi? Better. Shoko checked him again. He says he's never going to let Yuka tell the story." ᴹ

"That seems unlikely to work," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"She already told it twice at breakfast." ᴹ

Yuta smiled into the dark.

"Yes. That sounds like them." ʸᵗ

For a while they talked about the young man still discovering the house: the wrong turn after the stairs, the surprise of Mai being exactly as young as she looked, how quickly he and Yuka could become the same argument they had been before either of them came here.

Mau listened with the pleasant relief of not being the most difficult thing to explain that evening.

* * *

"He's trying," Yuka said downstairs. ʸᵏ

They had arrived at Tsurugi by way of his attempt to carry three things while refusing to admit he did not know where any of them belonged.

"He is," Maki agreed. ᴹᵏ

"He gets proud when he's worried. Then he has to keep being proud because otherwise someone will notice he was worried." ʸᵏ

Mai looked at her sister.

"How unfamiliar." ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki gave her a look.

"You could try worrying quietly," Turbo Granny suggested. ᵀᴳ

Seiko reached for another cracker.

"An example would be welcome." ˢᵉ

The argument took several minutes and acquired three subjects unrelated to where it had begun. Yuka found herself listening with her knees tucked comfortably beside the chair, the borrowed hoodie loose around her arms.

Frieren laughed at something Maki said.

It was a small laugh. Familiar, unguarded. Yuka looked at her before remembering she did not need to check whether seeing it was allowed to make her happy.

Frieren noticed the glance.

"What?" she asked. ᶠ

"Nothing. I like being here." ʸᵏ

Frieren considered the dish.

"The food isn't very substantial." ᶠ

Yuka laughed properly this time.

Maki smiled beside her.

* * *

On the roof, the pot had begun to empty.

Gojo went through the hatch for more water. Taihei stretched his legs and told him to look before coming back. Kaneki watched the lit kitchen doorway, where a person passed carrying a folded cloth and disappeared again.

Rimuru turned his cup between both hands.

"You look different tonight," he said to Mau. ᴿ

Mau looked down at himself.

"Same shirt." ᴹ

"I can see that." ᴿ

The answer was patient enough that he stopped trying to make the question smaller.

"I talked to Frieren," he said. ᴹ

Yuta looked toward him without interrupting.

"I think I'd been waiting to have to make it hurt in the right way. As if getting through it easily would mean I'd forgotten something important." ᴹ

"Was it easy?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

"No. But there were bits where we laughed." ᴹ

He looked out over the yard again.

"I remembered a whole life. I didn't come back to the same place in it. I don't think I have to keep standing where it ended to prove it happened." ᴹ

Kaneki set his cup against his knee.

"You can remember a place without living there," he said. ᴷⁿ

Mau nodded slowly.

"I want to live here." ᴹ

Nobody answered too quickly. The words belonged to a person who had already chosen them in more than one way, and was learning that choosing could include an evening which required nothing extraordinary from him.

Then Taihei passed him the dish he had been keeping out of Gojo's reach.

"Eat while you can," he said. ᵀ

Mau took something from it.

* * *

Yuka's idea came while Mai was arguing that people should mend their clothes before the damage could be described as structural.

"I want to take him somewhere," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

The room paused just enough to make her regret saying it in a room full of people.

"Mau," she added unnecessarily. ʸᵏ

"We gathered," Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

"Not far. Just something we can do together without it turning into the whole house trying to make it perfect." ʸᵏ

Shoko took a drink before replying.

"You don't need a medical committee to sit down to dinner." ˢʰ

"I know." ʸᵏ

Maki leaned closer.

"What did you have in mind?" ᴹᵏ

Yuka told them.

They were very bad at not helping.

By the time she finished, Mai had an opinion about the cloth, Seiko had offered something and then forbidden her to call it special, and Frieren was asking a practical question which nobody else had considered.

Yuka looked from one to the next, smiling despite herself.

"I said without the whole house." ʸᵏ

"This is one room," Seiko said. ˢᵉ

* * *

When the two gatherings ended, the corridor became briefly crowded with people going different ways and trying not to knock cups out of one another's hands.

Gojo stopped near Shoko. She looked at the extra cup he had brought down and took it from him without asking whether he had remembered which one was hers.

Rimuru went toward the east room and the ladder waiting above its closet. Taihei took the empty dish downstairs. Kaneki went to find Touka.

Yuka found Mau beside the hatch.

"Tomorrow evening," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at her face.

"What about it?" ᴹ

"Would you go on a date with me?" ʸᵏ

His answer came before she had finished enjoying the question.

"Yes." ᴹ

She smiled.

"You don't know where." ʸᵏ

"I know who." ᴹ

Mai made a quiet, appalled sound from farther along the corridor.

Yuka turned pink.

"I'll find you before dinner," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau took the last cup from her so she could put her hand in his.

They went downstairs together to return it.
