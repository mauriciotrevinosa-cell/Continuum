*When: Day495, morning. Yuka has a list with four things on it and has been told, by three different people, that four is enough.*

The fourth thing was a table.

That had caused the most discussion. A table sounded simple until the house began offering tables, each with an explanation of why it was the correct one. One was too heavy. One wobbled. One was in use for something which had apparently been almost finished since yesterday.

Yuka went to look at the little one beside the kitchen store and put her hand on its surface.

"This one." ʸᵏ

Ram looked at it.

"Excellent. It's a table." ᴿᵃ

"Can we use it?" ʸᵏ

"For its intended purpose? A radical proposal." ᴿᵃ

Rem hid her smile behind the cloth she was folding.

Yuka crossed the fourth thing off before somebody could think of a reason to offer another.

* * *

Momo had a question about the food.

"Do you want something he hasn't had, or something he likes?" ᴹᵒ

Yuka stopped beside the counter.

She had spent enough of the night imagining the evening to have confused those two ideas in several expensive ways. There had been a point at which she thought about asking Hollis for something from a place whose name she did not know.

"Something he likes," she said. ʸᵏ

"That makes it easier." Momo set a bowl beside her. "You can help with these." ᴹᵒ

Yuka took the knife and the little pile of vegetables. Rem moved over to show her where the second board belonged. Ram took one look at the arrangement, put a cloth under the slipping board and went back to what she had been doing.

They worked together until Yuka could see the dinner in the things they were making: bread warmed at the right time, the food Mau always took a second spoon of when he thought nobody was keeping track, a little sweet thing afterward which Momo said did not need to be announced with its own speech.

"I'm not going to give a speech." ʸᵏ

"Good. Then you can stop practising it while you cut." ᴹᵒ

Yuka looked down at the uneven pieces.

Momo gave her another vegetable.

* * *

Tsurugi found her carrying two cups across the yard.

He took one before asking whether she wanted help, then checked her face as if remembering this was one of the things he had been told to ask about.

"Thanks," she said. ʸᵏ

He relaxed a fraction.

"Where?" ᵀˢ

She pointed with the cup she still held.

At the quieter edge of the yard, beyond the path people used to the kitchen, the table stood where the light would stay for the first part of the evening. There was room to sit without putting a chair among the tree's roots. From it they could hear the house without needing to speak over it.

Tsurugi put the cup down.

"All this for dinner?" ᵀˢ

Yuka looked at him.

He corrected himself.

"It looks nice." ᵀˢ

She smiled.

"You're learning." ʸᵏ

He examined the table more seriously, pressed one corner, and crouched to check that all four legs were on firm ground.

When he stood, he looked toward the house.

"I don't want to make it difficult," he said. ᵀˢ

Yuka set her own cup down beside his.

"Then keep helping me with the nice part." ʸᵏ

He nodded. After a moment she nudged his shoulder with hers.

"And stop looking as if someone stole your last normal afternoon. You're allowed to have one here too." ʸᵏ

"You sound like Granny." ᵀˢ

"I had an excellent teacher." ʸᵏ

Maki, passing with a folded cloth, smiled without stopping to collect the compliment.

* * *

Mau spent the early afternoon doing something very ordinary with Himmel.

They had a loose fastening to put right, an awkward place to reach, and several opinions from people who were quite sure it would take five minutes if everybody else moved aside.

It took longer than five minutes.

Himmel held the piece steady while Mau arranged the cord with his good hand. They changed positions when the stiff fingers made the angle unhelpful. Neither made a lesson out of it.

"There," Himmel said when it held. ᴴ

Mau let go carefully.

It stayed.

"Now we can pretend it took five minutes." ᴹ

"A generous estimate," Himmel said. ᴴ

They put the unused cord back together. Mau could feel the old knowledge waiting beside this small task, all the things he might have said to a man whose future he had just discussed with somebody else.

He left those things where they belonged.

When Frieren came across the yard with her book under one arm, she found them trying to decide who had borrowed the piece they had not used.

"Himmel," she said. ᶠ

He looked up at once.

"Would you like to walk with me tomorrow?" ᶠ

Mau bent to pick up the loose end of cord before either of them could find him looking.

Himmel's answer was quieter than Mau expected.

"Yes. Where?" ᴴ

"We can decide when we get outside." Frieren paused. "I'd like to hear what you've been doing." ᶠ

Himmel smiled.

"Then I'll think of something more interesting than this fastening." ᴴ

"You don't have to make it interesting," she said. ᶠ

Mau gathered the cord, told them he would put it away, and went inside without turning a walk into a future they had not chosen.

* * *

He was very bad at getting ready for his own date.

There were fewer practical decisions than there had been on the morning of the sea trip, which left him too much room to consider each one. He put a shirt on, decided it was wrong, took it off and realised he could not explain what was wrong with it.

Rimuru leaned over the edge of the loft.

"You're going downstairs, aren't you?" ᴿ

"Outside." ᴹ

"The tremendous journey past the kitchen." ᴿ

Mau looked at the two shirts on the bed.

"Helpful." ᴹ

"The clean one," Rimuru said. ᴿ

"They're both clean." ᴹ

"Then you've done very well already." ᴿ

Mau sat on the bed, laughing despite himself, and put the first one back on.

He left the last cuff open while he worked it over the fingers. The body carrying all those points still required the same patience in little places. He fastened it with his right hand and stopped when it sat comfortably instead of trying to make the left hand prove something.

*You could ask her to help,* Ciel said. ᶜ

*With the cuff?* ᴹ

*With deciding whether it looks all right. She might like that.* ᶜ

He looked at the reflection in the small glass.

*I hadn't thought of that.* ᴹ

*I know.* ᶜ

He smiled.

* * *

Mai found Yuka in the corridor just before she was supposed to go looking for him.

The girl had already changed, already decided twice where the bracelet should sit and whether she ought to put the ring over or under the collar. Now she was holding the McLaren hoodie as if it required one more decision before she could leave.

"Take it," Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

"I don't want to hide in it all evening." ʸᵏ

"Then put it on the chair until you're cold." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka looked at the folded cloth.

"Does everything get this complicated because I'm making it complicated?" ʸᵏ

Mai thought about being kind enough to lie.

"Frequently." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka laughed, then stepped forward and hugged her before she could think about whether it needed explaining.

Mai's hand came up against her back.

"Go on," she said after a moment. "Before he changes into the third clean shirt." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka drew back.

"You saw?" ʸᵏ

"Rimuru came down for water looking exhausted." ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Mau was near the stairs when she found him.

He stopped when he saw her. The expression was so immediate that she forgot the carefully ordinary thing she had meant to say.

"Hi," she said instead. ʸᵏ

"Hi." ᴹ

He looked down at his cuff, remembered something, and held out the hand.

"Does this look all right?" ᴹ

Yuka stepped closer and turned the cloth back once, just enough that it would not rub the awkward place.

"Now it does." ʸᵏ

He watched her finish, then took her hand before she could draw it back to inspect her work.

Behind them someone called for a plate. There was a sound from the common room, a few notes being tried and corrected, the beginning of somebody else's evening.

Yuka squeezed his fingers gently.

"Come with me." ʸᵏ

He did.
