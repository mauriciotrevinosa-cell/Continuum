*When: Day495, evening. Yuka has asked everyone to leave the table alone. Miraculously, they have.*

For the first few steps she watched Mau instead of where she was going.

He saw the cloth first, then the two cups, then the place she had chosen at the edge of the yard. He slowed without stopping. She could feel the question beginning in the hand holding hers and waited for him to find it.

"You did this?" he asked. ᴹ

"Some of it. I had to borrow several opinions." ʸᵏ

He looked toward the kitchen.

"How many?" ᴹ

"Don't start. Sit down before someone comes out to improve it again." ʸᵏ

He pulled her chair back first, then hesitated as if trying to work out whether helping was the opposite of doing what she had asked.

She sat before he could make it harder.

"Thank you." ʸᵏ

He took the chair opposite.

* * *

The light was still in his face.

It caught the blue of his eyes and the pale strands at his temple, left the closed marks visible where the collar did not cover them. She had spent enough days being afraid of those changes that seeing them in an ordinary evening felt like learning a second set of meanings for the same face.

Here was Mau looking at a little table as if someone had done something impossibly kind.

Here were his hands waiting for a cup.

She put it within reach of the right one.

"I thought you might like having someone ask you out without needing you to plan the journey," she said. ʸᵏ

"I liked the asking part very much." ᴹ

"You answered too quickly. I was supposed to have time to be nervous." ʸᵏ

"Would you like me to consider it now?" ᴹ

She looked at him.

"Don't you dare." ʸᵏ

He laughed, and the last of the speech she had been trying not to prepare went away.

* * *

They got the food together.

It only required a short trip to the kitchen, but she went with him because part of the evening she had imagined involved not leaving him sitting alone while she tried to make the rest happen around him.

Rem had put the dishes where they could take them. Momo looked up from her own plate, pointed at the covered one, and went on eating before Yuka could apologise for taking up space.

Mau took the heavier dish.

"Carefully," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

He looked at the food.

"I thought you trusted me." ᴹ

"With my life. I'm considering the bread separately." ʸᵏ

Ram passed behind them with a stack of bowls.

"Sensible." ᴿᵃ

They carried everything back without spilling. That felt like a good beginning to a dinner which she had worried about far more than the meal itself deserved.

* * *

At first they ate too politely.

Yuka noticed it when Mau cut a piece much smaller than the one he would have taken inside. He was sitting straighter too, listening with the attention he gave to something he did not want to damage.

She put down her fork.

"You're allowed to be hungry." ʸᵏ

He looked up.

"I am hungry." ᴹ

"Then why are you eating like Grandpa when Granny says the food took a long time?" ʸᵏ

He glanced toward the lit doorway.

"Because it looks as if the food took a long time." ᴹ

She laughed so suddenly that he began laughing too, before she could tell him about the uneven vegetables and the way Momo had finally taken one from her and put another in its place.

"I thought something should be different," she admitted. "Nicer. More complicated." ʸᵏ

"This is nice." ᴹ

"I know that now. I spent a whole morning trying to make it impossible first." ʸᵏ

He took a proper mouthful.

She followed his example.

After that the dinner began to feel like theirs.

* * *

She told him about Tsurugi as a boy.

Not the brave story. Not the difficult one. The time he had been sure a task could not possibly take as long as everyone said and had spent the rest of the afternoon trying to conceal how much of it he still had left to do.

"He looked exactly like he did yesterday," she said. "I recognised the face as soon as he decided he knew where the room was." ʸᵏ

"He asked me afterward." ᴹ

"He told me he hadn't." ʸᵏ

Mau stopped with the cup halfway to his mouth.

Yuka pointed at him.

"There. You have it too." ʸᵏ

He tried to look innocent and failed so thoroughly that she had to put down her cup before she lost it.

From inside came a short run of music, then a laugh and the same notes tried again. Someone had found a better place for the harmony. Nobody called outside to see whether the two of them were ready to become part of it.

Mau listened until the passage ended.

"That was the bit," he said. ᴹ

Yuka knew which one he meant.

"It's getting better." ʸᵏ

"Their part is." ᴹ

"Then you'll have to keep going back." ʸᵏ

He looked at her, smiling.

"I intend to." ᴹ

* * *

When the light began to change, they lit the little lamp Seiko had lent her.

There had been a suggestion of candles. Frieren had asked what she planned to do when the wind reached the table, and the discussion had become much simpler after that.

Mau turned the lamp so neither of them had to look into it.

"Better?" he asked. ᴹ

Yuka nodded.

She had put the hoodie over the back of her chair. He noticed her glance at it and reached before she could ask, lifting it by the shoulders so the sleeves would fall the right way.

She stood and let him help her into it.

His fingers paused at the collar, a small adjustment made with care. The cloth settled warm against the back of her neck.

"You're getting it back one sleeve at a time," she said. ʸᵏ

"An excellent rate of progress." ᴹ

She sat sideways instead of taking her old position, closer to the edge of the table where he could reach her hand.

He left his there.

* * *

"What do you want to do tomorrow?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He thought about it.

She liked that he thought. Liked, after everything, that the question could be small enough to answer without solving the rest of their lives.

"Get breakfast before somebody takes the part I want." ᴹ

"Gojo?" ʸᵏ

"Several suspects." ᴹ

"A difficult case." ʸᵏ

"Then maybe finish learning the song. Help with whatever needs carrying. Find you." ᴹ

Her thumb moved against his hand.

"You can do that part first." ʸᵏ

He looked at her with such simple warmth that she had to look down for a moment.

When she looked up again, he had not mistaken it for the beginning of her leaving.

"What about you?" he asked. ᴹ

"Breakfast. Tell my brother what actually happened today so he can't invent anything. Maybe be very lazy afterward." ʸᵏ

"Ambitious." ᴹ

"I've planned carefully." ʸᵏ

They made room for the sweet thing Momo had told her not to announce. Mau took a bite, stopped, and asked whether there was more. Yuka had to admit she had asked for exactly enough, and he looked at her portion with an expression which made her pull the plate closer.

He laughed.

She gave him one bite anyway.

* * *

For a while they ate without trying to make the evening do anything else.

Then Yuka looked from his hands to his face.

"What can you actually do?" she asked. ʸᵏ

Mau glanced at the plate.

"Apparently not get a second helping of that." ᴹ

"You know what I mean." She smiled. "I keep hearing about enormous things. Is there anything you can show me which doesn't need a field and three people telling us where to stand?" ʸᵏ

He considered it. A little expression came into his face, almost embarrassed by the idea.

"Possibly something very stupid." ᴹ

"Promising." ʸᵏ

He put down the cup before asking Ciel privately.

*Rimuru's body pattern. Just that one. Could we hold it, instead of letting it answer a hit?* ᴹ

There was a pause while she looked at the shape he meant, rather than reaching for all the things beside it.

*The pattern is there. Noise, can you leave the others closed?* ᶜ

The attention below them moved to the one outline.

*One,* Noise answered. ᴺ

*Then try it seated,* Ciel said. *A short change first. And back.* ᶜ

Mau moved his chair a little away from the table.

"Don't panic," he told Yuka. ᴹ

She put her cup down at once.

"That is an extremely bad beginning." ʸᵏ

* * *

His outline softened.

Yuka saw the shoulders settle, the shape within the shirt become blue and translucent, then round itself into something which yielded gently into the clothes left on the chair. No hard shape pushed out through it. No other body tried to arrive at the same time.

A small slime sat where Mau had been.

For a moment she could only stare.

Then his familiar voice came from the quivering little body.

"Still me." ᴹ

"Mau." ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

She rose so quickly that he rocked back into the folded edge of his shirt.

"Wait, what are you—" ᴹ

She scooped him up with both hands.

"A little warning," he protested. "I have a perfectly good chair." ᴹ

He settled into her arms, soft and astonishingly easy to hold. She felt a small ripple travel through him as he found where his weight would rest. The faint marks he made for a face turned upward toward hers.

She hugged him closer before she could decide how to explain herself.

"This is perfect," she said. ʸᵏ

Then she realised she had said it aloud.

Heat climbed into her face. Mau looked up at her for long enough that she could not pretend he had missed it.

"Perfect," he repeated. ᴹ

"For holding," she said, which did not make her face any cooler. ʸᵏ

"I see. An assessment of the design." ᴹ

She laughed into the top of him.

He gave a little indignant wobble, then rested more comfortably against her.

"You could ask before picking me up next time." ᴹ

"Next time?" ʸᵏ

There was a small pause.

"That was careless of me." ᴹ

She held him until he asked for the chair back. Ciel was still watching the one shape, the two familiar rhythms steady within it. The other outlines stayed where they belonged.

* * *

Yuka set him down among his clothes and turned aside while he found his ordinary shape again.

He took a moment to arrange the sleeve, then cleared his throat.

She looked back.

Mau. The same face. The pale strands by his temple. He flexed the left hand; the same two fingers stopped at their familiar limit.

His shirt was crooked and one shoe had escaped under the table.

Yuka put a hand over her mouth.

"Don't," he said. ᴹ

"I haven't said anything." ʸᵏ

"You're doing it with your face." ᴹ

That finished her.

He laughed too, took the shoe she nudged back toward him, and let Ciel finish checking the return before standing. The body had changed its outline and come back. It had not become somebody else's life, or lost the things he still needed to take care of.

Yuka reached across to straighten his collar.

"Both are good," she said softly. ʸᵏ

He caught her hand before she drew it back.

"Thank you for the technical review." ᴹ

She kissed his knuckles.

* * *

Afterward they cleared the table together.

Nobody tried to rescue them from that part of the date. Yuka liked folding the cloth with him, each holding an end, trying to make the corners agree while they were still talking. Liked the ordinary crossing of their hands when they both reached for the same cup.

They left the cups and dishes in the kitchen and the folded cloth beside the lamp. Those could wait until they went inside for good.

Mau held out his hand.

"Walk with me?" ᴹ

She took it.

They went round the quiet part of the yard, no farther than they wanted to go. The windows shone behind them. Somewhere near the door Tsurugi was talking to Yuta; Mai's voice entered the conversation, and her brother made the affronted sound which meant she had found something accurate to say about him.

Yuka leaned into Mau's arm.

"He'll survive her," he said. ᴹ

"We all hope so." ʸᵏ

* * *

They stopped beside the tree, clear of the roots, where a little of the kitchen light still reached the path.

Yuka turned toward him.

He stopped turning with her, understanding slowly that she had meant to put herself in front of him.

"Thank you for coming," she said. ʸᵏ

His eyes moved over her face.

"Thank you for asking." ᴹ

She put her free hand against his chest. Felt the warmth through the cloth, the familiar, complicated rhythm of a body she was still getting to know.

"I had a very good time." ʸᵏ

"Me too." ᴹ

He sounded almost shy. It undid her more than any of the extraordinary things she had heard about him.

She rose toward him and kissed him.

He met her carefully, then more surely when her hand moved to the back of his neck. There was nothing to hurry past. When they drew apart, she stayed close enough to feel his next breath before he laughed softly against her forehead.

"What?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Nothing. I'm happy." ᴹ

She smiled up at him.

"Good. That was the idea." ʸᵏ

* * *

They went back for the lamp.

Mau carried it. Yuka carried the folded cloth. At the doorway he waited for her to straighten the last corner, then shifted the lamp to give her a free hand to take.

Inside, someone was asking who wanted the remaining tea. They put the borrowed things away and stayed for a cup while the house began the small work of ending its day.

Yuka found room on the sofa afterward.

Mau sat beside her. She leaned against his shoulder, watched the last people carrying dishes pass the doorway, and waited until he looked down to find out why she kept smiling.

"Could you do it again?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"I knew this would happen." ᴹ

"You did say next time." ʸᵏ

He considered that argument with a seriousness it did not deserve, then checked privately with Ciel. The one pattern had held. Its brief use had asked for none of the other bodies. She could keep watching while he rested without opening anything further.

Yuka lifted the folded blanket from the end of the sofa to give him some privacy with the clothes. When the small blue shape settled into the space beside her, she put the bundle neatly over the chair and held out her hands.

"May I?" ʸᵏ

Mau looked from them to her face.

"Much better." ᴹ

She gathered him against her and sat back, warm inside his hoodie.

"Comfortable?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He made a small adjustment beneath her hand.

"Very." ᴹ

* * *

Rimuru found them a little later.

He had come down for the cup he had left somewhere and stopped in the common-room doorway with a hand still on the frame.

Yuka was asleep on the sofa, cheek against the cushion. Her arms curved around a blue slime nestled comfortably against her. The blanket covered her knees; one of her hands rested over the little rounded body as if making sure he knew he was wanted there.

Rimuru looked down at his own hands.

Then back at the sofa.

*Mau,* Ciel confirmed privately, before he could finish deciding how offended to be. *One pattern. The others are closed. He's asleep.* ᶜ

Rimuru stared at his friend.

"You stole my form," he whispered. "And got the better seat." ᴿ

The sleeping slime gave one faint, contented wobble.

Yuka's hand moved with it. Her arms tightened a little, and both of them settled again.

Rimuru stood there another moment, trying to maintain an expression suitable to the theft.

Then he smiled, turned down the lamp beside them and went to find his cup.
