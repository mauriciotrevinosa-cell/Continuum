*When: Day496, morning. In the common room, Maki has found the two people who did not make it upstairs.*

"Very cute. Come and have breakfast." ᴹᵏ

Mau woke against Yuka's arm.

For a moment he could not work out why the room was so low. Then he remembered the sofa, the blanket and the particular shape he had gone to sleep in.

Yuka opened one eye.

Her grandmother stood beside the chair holding his clothes.

"Grandma." ʸᵏ

"That's right." ᴹᵏ

Yuka looked down at the small body gathered against her stomach. Her hand settled over him before she seemed to remember there was an audience.

"We fell asleep." ʸᵏ

"I worked that out." ᴹᵏ

*Good morning,* Ciel said privately. *Still the same one. Tell her you need the chair.* ᶜ

"Could I have the chair?" Mau asked. ᴹ

Maki set the bundle down.

"You can have breakfast too, if you get there before Milim finishes finding it." ᴹᵏ

She went toward the dining room. Yuka waited until she had gone before burying her face briefly in the cushion.

Mau shifted under her hand.

"I think that went quite well." ᴹ

She lifted her head. There was a crease on her cheek.

"You didn't have to look at her." ʸᵏ

"I was there." ᴹ

"You were a cushion." ʸᵏ

He made the indignant little movement she had begun to recognize. She laughed and loosened her arms.

"Sorry. May I put you down?" ʸᵏ

"On the blanket. Near the clothes." ᴹ

She did, then turned her back and held up the other end while he changed. Getting his left hand through the sleeve took longer than the rest. When he stood, she was waiting with his missing shoe.

He took it from her.

"Thank you." ᴹ

"You're welcome." ʸᵏ

They folded the blanket together and went to breakfast.

* * *

Ori waited until Mau had taken his first mouthful.

Umaru did not.

"We have received a declaration of war." ᵁ

Mau stopped chewing.

Across the table, Maki looked up. Yuka put down her cup.

Ori hurried to lay a sheet of paper beside his plate.

At the top was a dragon. Its wings were uneven. Beneath one foot sat something square, with a lid, and beside it Umaru had drawn a much smaller figure wearing a crown.

Mau swallowed.

"From whom?" ᴹ

"Kamish," Umaru said. "He has awakened and intends to reclaim his treasure." ᵁ

Yuka leaned over his shoulder to inspect the drawing.

"Is that your snack box?" ʸᵏ

"The royal reserve." ᵁ

"It has your name on the lid." ʸᵏ

"Royal property usually does." ᵁ

Maki went back to her breakfast.

Ori put a second sheet beside the first. This one was mostly writing. She had squeezed a final line along the bottom, then turned the paper and continued up the side.

"We need to ask you some things." ᴼ

Mau looked at the two sheets.

Then at the serious faces waiting for him.

"Apparently Kamish has been very busy while I was asleep." ᴹ

"He's a dragon," Umaru said. "They do this." ᵁ

* * *

The first rule was that Kamish could not attack anyone.

The second was that Mau could not help him attack anyone.

The third covered biting, swallowing, burning, stepping on, knocking over and anything Umaru might have forgotten to name.

"Then what can he do?" Mau asked. ᴹ

"Defend his treasure." ᵁ

"Without attacking." ᴹ

"Exactly." ᵁ

Ori moved his cup away before the rules could acquire a wet ring.

"He can roar. But he has to warn us first." ᴼ

"And nobody uses real weapons," Maki said. ᴹᵏ

Umaru turned the paper toward her.

"We already put that one." ᵁ

Maki read it and nodded.

That pleased Umaru more than Mau thought she had intended to show.

"We need workers," Ori said. "And people to guard the roads. And a castle." ᴼ

"We have a Fort." ᴹ

"That's our headquarters. The castle is his." ᴼ

Her finger landed on Kamish.

Momo paused on her way past with the pot.

"Headquarters still has to let people get to the cupboard." ᴹᵒ

"We can leave a corridor," Ori said at once. ᴼ

"And the cushions come back indoors before it gets damp." ᴹᵒ

Umaru made a note. The war was beginning to require another sheet.

"What do I do?" Mau asked. ᴹ

"Observe," Umaru said. ᵁ

"Only observe?" ᴹ

"You can't fight. You can't move the treasure. You can't disappear and take it with you." ᵁ

"Can I choose where I observe from?" ᴹ

Umaru considered that. Ori considered it with her.

"Yes," Ori said. "As long as you don't interfere." ᴼ

Mau nodded.

"Then I'll sit on Kamish." ᴹ

Yuka's hand came down over her mouth.

Umaru looked at the rules. There was nothing there about where an observer could sit. She consulted Ori in a whisper that could be heard three places down the table.

Ori looked back at Mau.

"All right." ᴼ

Yuka began to laugh.

"Would you like to join me?" he asked her. ᴹ

"She's not an observer," Umaru said quickly. ᵁ

Yuka thought about that.

Then she took the pencil.

"Then it's our treasure." ʸᵏ

Beside the crowned figure she drew another, seated on the dragon.

"Kamish's and mine." ʸᵏ

Ori studied the addition. Umaru pointed at the second figure, then at Mau.

"He still can't help you." ᵁ

"I'll manage." ʸᵏ

Mau looked down at the two figures beside the enormous dragon.

Breakfast had become rather more complicated than Maki had promised.

* * *

By the time they carried the papers outside, people were asking what they could bring.

Marin offered cloth. Wakana asked which cloth before she could offer any more of it. Fern agreed to let them use the low carrying baskets, provided the baskets were empty first and came back empty afterward.

Ori wrote that down too.

The Fort stayed where it was, at the north end of the common room. They carried out only what they needed for its forward position. Beyond the tree's root circle, Umaru set down the royal reserve and pointed out where the enemy castle would go.

It was an excellent position. It had room for a dragon, a good view of the approaching army and a patch of shade in which the approaching army could have a drink.

Mau called Igris and Bellion first.

The two shadows rose beside him. Bellion turned toward the open ground. Igris waited, straight and still.

Umaru held up the plan.

"We need your help with a war." ᵁ

Bellion inclined his head.

"Against whom?" ᴮˡ

"Kamish." ᵁ

His gaze moved to Mau.

"He's agreed," Mau said. "There are rules." ᴹ

Bellion took the papers.

He read the first. Then the second. When Ori pointed out the writing up the side, he turned it without comment and read that too.

"A defended treasure," he said. "Restricted engagement. An assault from this direction." ᴮˡ

"And a secret passage," Ori said. ᴼ

Bellion looked at her.

She pointed to a narrow space behind two baskets.

"It has to look like we found it ourselves." ᴼ

"Understood." ᴮˡ

Igris lowered his head over the plan.

Mau watched the two of them begin discussing where to put an entirely unnecessary defensive wall.

They were going to do this properly.

* * *

Four ordinary soldiers carried the first poles into position. They waited while Wakana showed them where to rest the crosspiece, then lifted together.

Nobody had to struggle with the weight. The argument was about whether the entrance looked enough like a castle.

"Higher," Umaru said. ᵁ

Bellion raised one hand. The soldiers stopped.

"The opening?" ᴮˡ

"The flag." ᵁ

He looked up.

Marin had fixed a square of cloth to the top pole. On it was a hamster with round cheeks, small paws and an expression of considerable authority.

"The standard is secure," Bellion said. ᴮˡ

"It's tiny." ᵁ

Bellion looked at the flag again.

Then down at the girl inspecting his work with her hands on her hips.

"You won't be able to see the hamster from the enemy castle," she told him. "What's the point of having a flag if the enemy can't see the hamster?" ᵁ

For a moment he said nothing.

Then a short laugh came from inside the armor.

Mau turned toward him.

Bellion bowed to Umaru with complete seriousness.

"My apologies, Commander. I underestimated the required scale." ᴮˡ

Umaru nodded, accepting the apology.

"At least twice as big." ᵁ

Bellion glanced at the distance to the enemy position.

"Three times, I think." ᴮˡ

She looked delighted.

Marin was already unfolding another piece of cloth.

* * *

Beru had watched the preparations from beside Mau.

He had watched the wall go up. Watched Igris take a basket from Ori with the care he might have given a weapon. Watched Bellion order a larger hamster.

Now he looked from the enemy castle to his king.

"My king." ᴮᵉ

"Yes?" ᴹ

"May I serve on your side?" ᴮᵉ

Mau looked at him.

There was a bright, unmistakably miserable shine in the ant's eyes.

"I will obey every restriction. I will not attack you." ᴮᵉ

"I wasn't planning on being attacked." ᴹ

"Then I ask permission to—" ᴮᵉ

"You're defending Yuka," Umaru said, coming past with the pencil behind her ear. "He's only watching. You don't fight him." ᵁ

Beru went still.

Yuka, carrying the snack tin toward the castle, stopped too.

"I'm getting a guard?" ʸᵏ

"The treasure needs defending," Umaru said. ᵁ

Beru turned back to Mau.

The misery was gone.

"My king, I will protect her as if the world were ending. Little Rope shall not be scratched." ᴮᵉ

Yuka looked at Mau over the tin.

He was trying very hard to keep his face suitable to the occasion.

"No actual fighting," he reminded Beru. ᴹ

"Of course, my king." ᴮᵉ

Ori added a line to the rules before anybody could change their mind.

* * *

Kamish settled on the far side of the field with a dignity which made the low basket wall seem even lower.

Mau read him the rules.

All of them.

The dragon listened. When Mau reached the prohibition against swallowing anyone, the old head lowered a little toward Umaru.

She looked up at him.

"It's in there for everyone." ᵁ

Kamish made a low sound.

Ori lifted her pencil.

"Was that your warning?" ᴼ

He stopped.

Yuka had to put the snack tin down.

Mau waited for her to stop laughing, then pointed out his place between the ridges behind the dragon's head. Kamish lowered himself enough for him to climb up without anyone inventing a ladder. Yuka followed and sat beside him, the tin in her lap.

"You're not being a very neutral observer," she said. ʸᵏ

"I haven't done anything." ᴹ

"You chose the enemy castle." ʸᵏ

"It has a good view." ᴹ

Down below, Beru took up his position. Igris stood at the secret passage, which everyone had now inspected. Bellion was overseeing the raising of the second flag.

It took two soldiers to hold the cloth clear while Marin fixed the corner.

The hamster looked magnificent.

Umaru stood beneath it with Ori, checking the last page.

"After lunch," Ori called. "We start after lunch." ᴼ

"Both armies have to eat," Umaru added. ᵁ

Bellion brought a hand to his chest and acknowledged the order.

Mau looked across the field: the borrowed baskets, the solemn guards, the great white cheeks of the hamster moving in the breeze. From the house someone called that the extra cups were ready.

Yuka leaned against him.

"Are you enjoying being outnumbered?" ʸᵏ

Bellion turned to receive another instruction from his small commander.

Mau settled back against the broad curve of the dragon.

"Very much." ᴹ
