Mau had put his name down for the sweets before he knew he would be serving them as a prisoner.

Rem found that very funny.

She did not say so. She put another little piece of dough beside his hand and waited while he tried to persuade its edges to meet.

His left fingers did not make a good pinch. He turned the piece with them and used his right hand to close it.

"Better?" he asked. ᴹ

Rem inspected it.

"That one may keep its filling." ᴿᵉ

"A promising development." ᴹ

She laughed and moved it onto the tray.

At the other end of the table, Yuka was supposed to be taking clean cups outside.

She had been supposed to do that for several minutes.

"They're for the victors," Mau told her. ᴹ

"I know." ʸᵏ

"You defended the enemy treasure." ᴹ

"And lost very graciously." ʸᵏ

Rem put the next tray between them before their legal discussion could reach the cooling ones.

Yuka took her cups. As she passed Mau, her fingers rested briefly against his shoulder.

"I'll negotiate." ʸᵏ

* * *

The flag took longest to bring down.

Umaru did not want it folded where the hamster's face would crease. Bellion received that instruction, looked at the cloth and directed the soldiers to start from the opposite edge.

Ori carried the papers indoors.

The secret passages became baskets again. The castle poles went back to Wakana. Lantern lowered his head for a touch before Mau let him rest; Igris checked the field with the soldiers for anything small left in the grass.

Kamish had recovered sufficiently from friendship to watch the removal of his kingdom with interest.

"A worthy army," he told Umaru. ᴷᵃ

She nodded solemnly.

"You were a good dragon." ᵁ

The old head tilted.

"I am a dragon." ᴷᵃ

"You were particularly good today." ᵁ

Beru turned away. Bellion did not manage it quickly enough to hide his laugh.

Mau thanked them, one by one. Sent the workers to rest first, then the knight and the general. Beru waited until Yuka had come back from the kitchen before accepting that the defense of Little Rope was finished for the day.

When Kamish sank out of sight, there was a broad patch of flattened grass where the castle had been.

Ori put the last basket on it.

"We should do it again sometime." ᴼ

Umaru looked at the flag in her arms.

"Next time he'll know our attack." ᵁ

They went inside to discuss this problem.

* * *

Frieren found Himmel taking a borrowed stool back toward the house.

"You said you would walk with me." ᶠ

He stopped.

"I did." ᴴ

She looked at the stool.

"Does that need to come?" ᶠ

Himmel set it by the door.

"It can wait here." ᴴ

They took the path around the yard. Behind them somebody was asking where the flag would be stored; a much smaller voice replied that it could not go anywhere damp.

Frieren glanced back once.

"She was pleased." ᶠ

"Which one?" ᴴ

She considered the question.

"Both." ᶠ

Himmel smiled.

For a little while they walked without talking. Then she asked about the things he did when she was elsewhere. He told her about repairs, carrying supplies, a morning when someone had asked him to help without first asking what he could fight.

"Did you mind?" ᶠ

"Being asked to help?" ᴴ

"That it was something small." ᶠ

He looked back toward the house.

"I liked that they expected me to be here tomorrow." ᴴ

Frieren walked a few more steps before answering.

"Tell me about that morning." ᶠ

He did.

They took the longer way back.

* * *

By dinner, the common room contained a flag, an army's worth of opinions and a plate of sweets which Mau had been forbidden to touch until the commanders arrived.

Momo called for the food to be passed instead of defended.

That helped.

People made room. Cups changed hands. From the end of one table, Gojo was explaining that he had always believed in friendship; Shoko asked whether friendship also took credit for the dust on his trousers.

He looked down.

Mai laughed into her cup.

Umaru took the first sweet with great ceremony. Ori waited beside her until Mau said they could have one each without agreeing on which one was the prize.

She chose the crooked one.

"That was mine," he said. ᴹ

"I know." ᴼ

She put it on her plate carefully.

Yuka sat down beside him. Across from them, Tsurugi was explaining to Maki how he would have approached the secret passage.

"You'd have hit your head," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She reached for the dish between them.

"It was very low." ʸᵏ

Maki nodded as though that settled the military question.

Mau passed Yuka a sweet before she had to ask anyone to reconsider her status as an enemy combatant.

She smiled at him.

"Corruption," Umaru said immediately. ᵁ

"Dinner," Momo corrected from farther down the table. ᴹᵒ

The decision stood.

* * *

The prisoner helped clear his plate.

Umaru watched him take it to the kitchen, return, and start to sit beside Yuka again.

"He hasn't been released." ᵁ

Mau remained standing.

"What else do I owe?" ᴹ

She thought about it. Ori came over with the empty cup she had been collecting from beside the Fort.

Yuka looked from one commander to the other.

Then she raised her hand.

"I'll take responsibility for the prisoner of war." ʸᵏ

Mau turned toward her.

She was trying to sound sober. Her smile kept interfering.

"You were on his side," Umaru said. ᵁ

"I know him very well." ʸᵏ

"That's the problem." ᵁ

Ori looked at the two of them for a moment.

"He helped with the sweets." ᴼ

"Which is why we're considering it." ᵁ

Yuka folded her hands.

"I accept the responsibility." ʸᵏ

Umaru pointed at her.

"No running away with the prisoner." ᵁ

"I can't promise that." ʸᵏ

Mau laughed before he could help it.

That made both commanders look at him.

"Sorry," he said. ᴹ

He did not look sorry enough to be convincing.

* * *

They went outside while people were still carrying cups to the steps.

Only a little way. Yuka chose a place with a view of the sky and helped Mau spread a blanket over the grass. The kitchen light reached the path behind them; if someone wanted either of them, there would be no difficulty finding them.

She sat first. He sat beside her.

"A very eventful captivity," he said. ᴹ

Yuka tucked the edge of the blanket around her feet.

"Are you complaining?" ʸᵏ

"Not at all." ᴹ

She leaned against him, laughing softly when he tried to make the blanket reach both their shoulders without pulling it off her knees.

They settled the problem by moving closer.

Above them the first stars were beginning to show. Mau listened to the voices near the steps, the clink of a cup being set down, Yuka's breathing beside his ear.

"Your grandmother is going to find us again," he said. ᴹ

"Let her." ʸᵏ

He turned toward her.

Yuka kissed him, brief and warm, then rested her head against his shoulder again.

For a while they talked about the game. About Bellion's apology. About how Beru had looked when he realized he had been tricked, and whether Igris would fall for it next time.

Eventually Yuka's answers grew shorter.

Mau let his cheek rest against her hair.

* * *

Ori found them when she came out to ask whether anyone had seen the little dish from the kitchen.

Umaru came with her.

They stopped beside the blanket.

The prisoner and his guard were asleep, shoulder to shoulder, close enough to the rest of the house that they could still hear people laughing if they woke.

Umaru opened her mouth.

Ori held up one finger.

She bent and drew the loose end of the blanket over Mau's feet. Yuka stirred, tightened her hand round his sleeve and settled again.

Umaru waited.

Then she leaned close enough to whisper.

"We'll question him tomorrow." ᵁ

Ori nodded.

They went to look for the dish.
