Mau was doing that when Anko asked Stark whether he wanted to go.

Stark had his axe across his knees. He looked up from the fastening he was checking.

"Today?" ˢᵗ

"Unless you'd prefer me to ask tomorrow and mean yesterday." ᴬⁿ

Mau put the cups down.

"The gate?" ᴹ

Anko nodded.

The night before, Maki had found them while there were still people outside. She had brought the dish Ori wanted, waited for Yuka to wake properly and sent them back in with the blanket. Yuka had gone to her room. Mau had climbed the stairs to his, where Rimuru complained sleepily about somebody folding cloth loudly enough to qualify as a workshop.

This morning he had expected to help clear breakfast and see what happened next.

He looked at Anko's bag.

"Can I come?" ᴹ

She stopped checking the strap.

"Do you want to look, or do you have something in mind?" ᴬⁿ

He considered the difference.

"Something ordinary. Bring back things we can use. See how it feels when the point isn't to finish everything before I can leave." ᴹ

Anko looked at him for a moment, then moved her bag off the bench.

"Sit down. We were going to choose where before we chose how far." ᴬⁿ

* * *

Yuka came over before Mau had finished explaining the idea to Stark.

"I want to come too." ʸᵏ

"I was going to ask you." ᴹ

"I saved you time." ʸᵏ

She sat beside him. There was a little grass caught in the edge of her sleeve; she found it, looked at him and flicked it into his empty cup.

Anko put the map between them.

It showed approaches and rooms people had actually checked, with marks where someone had turned back or found the shape different from what they expected. It was not a promise that the next door would behave like the last one.

"Useful things," Anko said. "Not the deepest room. Not a boss because we happen to find one. If the route stops matching what we're prepared for, we come back while that's still a choice." ᴬⁿ

Stark traced one bend without touching the paper.

"Materials?" ˢᵗ

"Materials, crystals if they're there. Anything damaged that tells Wakana something useful. We mark it before deciding whether to carry it." ᴬⁿ

He glanced at his own axe.

"I can help with that." ˢᵗ

Mau nodded toward the narrow section near the entrance.

"Four of us? I can bring Igris for the way back." ᴹ

"Provided we still look behind us ourselves," Anko said. ᴬⁿ

"Yes." ᴹ

She took out her pencil.

* * *

Rimuru found them over the map.

He listened before taking a place at the end of the bench. Mau told him the route, the party and when they meant to return.

"I'll stay," Rimuru said. "We can check the thread at the entrance rather than assuming it still reaches because the last one did." ᴿ

"You don't mind?" Mau asked. ᴹ

"Going to a few rooms without me? No. Bringing back three incompatible descriptions of the same object? I'll reserve judgement." ᴿ

Stark looked at Anko.

"That's why she comes," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Shoko stopped beside them on her way through. Mau told her too, without waiting for the map to explain on his behalf.

She asked how his hand had felt that morning and whether the strain from the last field test had returned. He answered both questions properly. She watched him turn the cup, then set it down.

"Tell them if anything changes," she said. "While it's changing." ˢʰ

"I will." ᴹ

"And take food." ˢʰ

Anko pointed to the bag.

Shoko looked inside it, seemed satisfied, and went on to the medical room.

Nobody had taken the map away from him. Nobody had needed to pretend that wanting an ordinary outing made him ordinary in every other respect.

He looked at Yuka.

She was checking her rope.

* * *

Outside, she made the familiar shadow with her hands.

Her white dog rose beside her and waited.

Mau watched her touch its head. This was hers: the technique she had brought, the shape which already knew her. She did not need anything from him to call it.

"I want to get better at this," she said. ʸᵏ

"The dog?" ᴹ

"The rest too. What I can call. What I can do once I've called it. I keep knowing there's more without knowing how to reach it." ʸᵏ

He crouched to check the knot on his bag.

"I might be able to help with some of that. Not by doing it instead of you." ᴹ

She looked down at him.

"How?" ʸᵏ

He straightened.

"There's something I remember making before. A connection. I'd need to explain it properly, and ask Ciel and Rimuru whether we can build it here." ᴹ

Her expression changed. Interest first; then a more careful attention.

"Then explain it when we get back," she said. "Don't start making it while I'm still guessing what you mean." ʸᵏ

"All right." ᴹ

She picked up her bag herself before he could reach for it.

"Today I use what I've got." ʸᵏ

He smiled.

"So do I." ᴹ

* * *

They told Fern where they were going.

She asked which containers were for the findings and which held their food. Anko answered; Stark discovered he had picked up the wrong empty bag and exchanged it before she could make him unpack it in front of everyone.

Mau took the water. Yuka checked the spare cloth and gave him the sort of look which meant she was remembering a particular packing mistake.

He decided not to defend himself before anyone had accused him.

At the marker, Rimuru waited while the four of them checked their equipment one last time.

The gate was blue.

That was what they knew about it from this side.

Mau looked at the others, then at the entrance.

"Ready?" he asked. ᴹ

Anko adjusted the strap on her shoulder. Stark took his place where the passage would give him room to turn. Yuka stood beside Mau with her dog waiting at her heel.

They went in together.
