*When: Day497, evening. Hollis has a folded paper in his coat and something wrapped so that nobody will put a hand through it by mistake.*

He kept both beside his chair while he ate.

Mau noticed them because Hollis moved the wrapped thing before Yuka could put a clean cup on it.

"Sharp?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"It was," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

He did not say more until Momo had taken the bowl and he had washed the mud from his hands.

Then he asked whether Wakana was still working.

They found him putting away the metal from the outing.

* * *

The thing in the wrapping had been a cutting tool.

Part of the edge was missing. Another part had folded back on itself, leaving an ugly, blunt ridge. Wakana turned it without touching that section.

"What did this meet?" ᵂ

"That's the question," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

He unfolded the paper.

A workshop near Fallow had been asked to cut and work material brought back by people who said they had gone through one of the strange openings farther along the road. Hide, plates, things ordinary tools were supposed to bite into. Some of the tools had chipped. One had broken. This one had come back looking as though the owner had tried to use it against a wall.

Hollis had seen the tool. He had not seen the creature.

"So I can't tell you which part of the story grew on the way to me," he said. "But the edge is there for you to look at." ᴴᵒ

Wakana laid it down carefully.

Fern came to see what had brought them back to the table. Rimuru followed with a cup, and Anko moved the bar from the outing away from the broken blade before anyone could start calling them the same sort of metal.

"Why ask us?" Mau said. ᴹ

"They asked me whether I knew anyone using material which didn't behave like theirs. I said I knew people who might be able to answer a question." ᴴᵒ

"Might," Fern repeated. ᶠᵉ

"Very firmly." ᴴᵒ

* * *

The drawings were requests for shapes, not orders.

Wakana recognized enough of them to stop at the question underneath: could they make an edge which would keep doing its job after it met the material?

He looked at the small bar.

"I don't know yet." ᵂ

Hollis nodded as though that were a useful answer.

Mau had expected him to look disappointed.

"You've come all this way with it," he said. ᴹ

"I came with your supplies and three other people's errands. This is something I thought was worth asking about while I was here." ᴴᵒ

He touched the paper.

"It isn't a cart full of weapons I promised somebody." ᴴᵒ

Fern pulled a chair out.

"Then let's keep it that way until we know what we'd be promising." ᶠᵉ

* * *

They began with what the workshop actually wanted to learn.

What material had broken the tools? Had it been wet or dry? Had the edge failed at the first contact, or after repeated use? Was the handle wrong, the fastening wrong, or the metal wrong?

Hollis answered what he could and marked the rest for the return journey.

There was no price attached to an answer nobody had found yet.

"Could they look at a sample?" Jinshi asked when he came over to collect Fern's loose notes. ᴶ

Wakana looked at him, then at the bar.

"Perhaps. After I know what condition it leaves in. I don't want a test done there to become something everyone here thinks we've already proved." ᵂ

"Borrowed, then," Fern said. "If we decide to send it. Returned, with an account of what they did to it." ᶠᵉ

"I can carry that," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

Mau looked at the damaged tool again.

There was a whole piece of the outside world in it which he had not seen: somebody working, expecting the edge to hold, having to stop when it did not.

"If they do want to buy something later," he said, "we'd have to know we can make another one." ᴹ

"And what it costs to get the material," Anko said. ᴬⁿ

"And what we need here first," Fern added. ᶠᵉ

Hollis listened without trying to turn any of those things into a promise he could sell.

* * *

They kept the broken tool beside the questions.

The little bar went back to Wakana for the local examination. The two crystals remained wrapped; nobody decided the color of a glint was a price. Jinshi wrote down which things belonged to the house and which belonged to somebody waiting near Fallow.

Yuka helped gather the cups when they finished.

Mau reached for the last one at the same time she did. Their hands met. She let him take it, then stayed beside him instead of going ahead to the kitchen.

"Tomorrow?" she asked quietly. ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She nodded toward his hand.

"The connection you said you could explain." ʸᵏ

"Tomorrow morning," he said. "Before we try anything." ᴹ

She nodded, satisfied that the question had not been lost among all the others on the table.
