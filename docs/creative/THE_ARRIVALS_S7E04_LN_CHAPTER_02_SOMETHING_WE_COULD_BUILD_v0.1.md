*When: Day498, morning. Yuka has made sure the cups are out of the way before asking him to begin.*

"You said you might be able to help," she said. "What did you mean?" ʸᵏ

Mau sat opposite her.

He had thought about how to explain it before coming down. Now that she was waiting, he put the explanation he had prepared aside and started with the part she actually needed.

"In the other life, we found a way for someone else to use an ability I'd built. Not just stand beside me while I did it. They could choose to use it themselves." ᴹ

Her hands grew still around her cup.

"Who?" ʸᵏ

"You." ᴹ

She looked at him for a moment.

"Oh." ʸᵏ

He waited.

"Then you remember me already being good at it," she said. ʸᵏ

"I remember you learning. And mistakes we made while we were figuring it out. That's useful, but it doesn't mean you've made an agreement here, or know something nobody's taught you." ᴹ

She glanced toward the rope lying beside her chair.

"Would I have to learn it the same way?" ʸᵏ

"No." ᴹ

He leaned forward a little.

"I'd rather explain what I remember and find out how you want to do it. If you want to at all." ᴹ

Yuka took a slow breath.

"Explain the rest." ʸᵏ

* * *

He used the two cups because they were there.

One for him, one for her, a little distance between them. He stopped before pouring anything and smiled when she looked suspiciously at the tea.

"I'm not going to make you drink the demonstration." ᴹ

"Good." ʸᵏ

He touched his cup.

"The pattern is built in me. The supply and the load come through me. We'd make a connection that lets you reach that pattern, form the operation and decide where it goes." ᴹ

Her eyes moved to the space between the cups.

"And you wouldn't be doing it for me?" ʸᵏ

"No. I'd feel the demand, but you'd be the one trying to make it behave." ᴹ

"Could you move my hand?" ʸᵏ

"That's not part of what I'm offering." ᴹ

"Could you see what I'm thinking?" ʸᵏ

"No. We don't need that." ᴹ

She nodded, slowly.

"So you lend me something you can do. I still have to learn to do it. And it costs you when I use it." ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"That sounds like something we should ask Rimuru about before we decide it's simple." ʸᵏ

Mau laughed.

"Yes. Very much." ᴹ

* * *

Rimuru listened in his office, downstairs beside the common room.

Yuka did the first part of the explaining. Mau filled in what she asked him to, then waited while Rimuru considered the proposed connection rather than the confidence with which they had described it.

Ciel asked Mau privately whether she could look at the small operation he remembered lending.

*The operation,* she said. *I don't need the fight around it.* ᶜ

He agreed to that.

After a while Rimuru drew a line between two marks on the page.

"We can work on a path like this," he said. "But the agreement comes first. What can go through it, who chooses, when it stops. That's the contract, not just a way to make the first attempt happen." ᴿ

Yuka rested her elbows on her knees.

"What would we start with?" ʸᵏ

"The small pull," Mau said. "Blue. I can make it and release it deliberately. I wouldn't start by offering something I've only survived doing once." ᴹ

She thought about the doorway in the gate, the weight of the creature Stark had stopped, the places where a little change of direction would have mattered.

"Can I try it without asking you to switch it on each time?" ʸᵏ

Rimuru nodded.

"That's the point of making a usable path. You agree to the access. After that, within what you've agreed, she initiates it." ᴿ

Mau looked at her.

"You don't have to ask permission for every hand movement." ᴹ

"And if you need it?" ʸᵏ

Rimuru tapped the single line.

"We aren't building a second full operation today. You're sharing access to what he has. We need a signal that means pause, and we need to learn how the demand feels before you both try to do everything at once." ᴿ

She looked at the line for a long time.

Then she asked what would happen if either of them wanted the agreement to end.

* * *

There was no reason to attach a punishment to leaving.

Rimuru said that first, plainly. They would stop the active operation, close future access and check the path was clear. It did not need to become a promise about the rest of their lives.

Yuka turned toward Mau.

"You'd tell me if it hurt." ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Before it was something you thought I might blame myself for." ʸᵏ

He looked down at the page, then back at her.

"Yes. And you'd stop if I asked. Even if you thought you almost had it." ᴹ

"Yes." ʸᵏ

She folded her hands together.

"And you don't take it away because we disagree about something else." ʸᵏ

"No," he said. "That isn't what this is for." ᴹ

Rimuru let the answer stay between them before asking the next practical question.

* * *

When they had gone through the questions, Yuka took the pencil.

She wrote down what they were agreeing to try, checked the words with both of them and added her own sentence at the bottom. It was a list of what the agreement would mean, not a signature which made it happen before they were ready.

*This does not replace learning my technique.*

Mau read it.

"I want the rest of my shikigamis," she said. "I want to understand what I'm doing with them. If this helps us work on that, good. But I don't want to get stronger only when you hand me something." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"Then we work on both. This is something we can try first, not everything you get to become." ᴹ

She put the pencil down.

"All right. I'd like to try it." ʸᵏ

Rimuru looked at Mau.

He gave the same answer.

"After lunch," Rimuru said. "We make the connection before anyone attempts a pull. Then one small thing, with people who can help you understand what you felt." ᴿ

Yuka picked up her cup on the way out.

Mau reached for the other one.

She waited beside the door until he had it.
