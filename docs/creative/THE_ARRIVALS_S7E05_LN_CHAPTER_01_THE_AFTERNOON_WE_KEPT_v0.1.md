*When: Day506, afternoon. A week has passed, and Yuka has decided that they are keeping this part of the day.*

The crossing was clear now.

Anko had taken the returning scouts back with the right tools. The four soldiers had learned the difficult turn by the workshop. Jinshi had a receipt for the little metal bar Hollis had borrowed to show the workshop near Fallow; nobody had called it a sale.

Yuka had put three small marks beside the same exercise in her notes and crossed out one of them after Gojo asked where she had meant to place the pull.

The hoodie was drying.

Mau noticed that last thing when he found her waiting by the door.

"What?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Nothing." ᴹ

She followed his glance toward the clothes drying farther along the yard.

"It needs washing sometimes." ʸᵏ

"I wasn't objecting." ᴹ

"Good. You're still not getting it back." ʸᵏ

He took the cloth-wrapped food from her before she could hand him the cups as well.

"I gathered." ᴹ

* * *

They had chosen a place where the ground rose a little beyond the busier part of the yard. Enough distance to hear less of the work, not enough to make a journey of it.

Yuka spread the blanket. Mau set the food where neither of them would sit on it.

"A lesson learned," she said. ʸᵏ

"One time." ᴹ

"You say that about a lot of things." ʸᵏ

She sat down, drawing her coat round her knees. The sun had reached the wall behind them; the ground beyond the blanket was still cool.

Mau poured the tea while she opened the food. There were two little filled cakes, something savory tucked in a separate cloth and enough bread that nobody would have to pretend they were not hungry.

Yuka chose one cake and handed the other to him.

"This isn't for afterward?" he asked. ᴹ

"It can be for now." ʸᵏ

He considered the argument and took a bite.

She looked pleased.

* * *

The cards were hers to bring and his mistake to agree to.

He discovered that after losing the first hand.

"You knew I had that one." ᴹ

"You kept looking at it." ʸᵏ

"I was looking at all of them." ᴹ

"Especially that one." ʸᵏ

He inspected his remaining cards as though they might explain her behavior.

She drank her tea.

For the second hand he tried looking at none of them. That proved less useful than he had hoped.

Yuka laughed when he admitted it.

"You're allowed to look. You just don't have to announce everything you notice with your eyebrows." ʸᵏ

He looked up.

She pointed at his face.

"That." ʸᵏ

He laughed too, and put the card down.

* * *

A breeze took one of the cards while they were gathering the hand.

Yuka reached.

Mau felt the familiar operation answer before her hand could catch the corner.

The card slid toward the little pull she had placed beside the blanket. So did the loose edge of the food cloth.

She released it at once and caught both with her ordinary hand.

They looked at the small mess.

"The card," she said. "I meant the card." ʸᵏ

"It was coming." ᴹ

"So was lunch." ʸᵏ

She put the cloth back, smoothed the card against her knee and checked his face.

He nodded before she had to ask.

"Brief enough. I'm fine." ᴹ

She let out a breath and set the whole stack under the cup.

"Ordinary solutions," she said. ʸᵏ

"Occasionally useful." ᴹ

* * *

After the food they stayed where they were.

Mau had expected the afternoon to feel borrowed from the other things he ought to be doing. For a while it did. He heard a cart move and wondered whether somebody needed help. Then Yuka asked him a question about the game, and when he looked back she was watching him rather than the cards.

"Do you want to go?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"No." ᴹ

She waited.

"I keep thinking somebody might ask for something." ᴹ

"They know where we are." ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

She rested her cheek against his shoulder.

"Then let them ask when they need to." ʸᵏ

He settled beside her.

There was a little crease near the corner of her mouth where she was trying not to smile at his face again. He touched it with his thumb.

"What?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"You're enjoying being right." ᴹ

"I try to appreciate it when it happens." ʸᵏ

He bent toward her. She met him halfway, hand warm against his jaw, and kissed him without either of them having to decide where the next thought was meant to go.

When they drew apart, she stayed close.

"I'm glad you came," she said. ʸᵏ

"You asked." ᴹ

"That isn't always the same thing." ʸᵏ

He looked at her for a moment.

Then he put his arm round her and drew her comfortably against him.

"I'm glad I came too." ᴹ

* * *

They talked until the tea was gone.

About her practice. About Bocchi playing the little unfinished piece again and getting farther this time. About the particular satisfaction of watching a soldier carry something past the workshop corner without anyone shouting a correction.

Yuka asked whether he would come to her next lesson even if he only watched.

"If you want me there." ᴹ

"I do. I also want you to tell me when you're tired instead of waiting for me to guess." ʸᵏ

"All right." ᴹ

She took his hand. Let the two stiff fingers rest where they naturally stopped rather than pulling them straight to fit hers.

"And sometimes," she said, "we do this instead." ʸᵏ

He turned their hands together.

"The cards?" ᴹ

"The afternoon." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

* * *

They packed slowly.

Yuka checked beneath the blanket for the card which had escaped. Mau held both cups while she folded the cloth, then gave one back so he could take her hand on the way home.

At the door she stopped to look toward the drying clothes.

He followed her glance.

"Still there." ᴹ

"I'm checking." ʸᵏ

"I haven't had time to steal it back." ᴹ

She leaned close enough to kiss his cheek.

"I know where you live." ʸᵏ

Then she went inside with his cup as well as hers, leaving him to bring the blanket.
