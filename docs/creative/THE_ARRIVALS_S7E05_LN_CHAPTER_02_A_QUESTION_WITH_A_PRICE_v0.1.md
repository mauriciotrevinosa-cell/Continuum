*When: Day507, afternoon. Hollis has brought the metal back, which is the first thing Fern checks.*

He let her compare the marks to Jinshi's note before unfolding the other paper.

The bar had acquired a shallow notch near one end and several scratches where someone had tried its surface. It was still the same piece.

Wakana turned it under the light.

"What did they do?" ᵂ

Hollis told him. Then told him which part he had seen and which part the workshop owner had said happened before he arrived.

Wakana asked again about the tool used on the notch.

That answer interested him more than the offer.

Fern waited until he had put the bar down before taking the paper.

"For one test piece?" ᶠᵉ

"If we agree to make one," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

"And if it does what they're asking." ᶠᵉ

"I put that part in before I let them finish telling me how many they could sell." ᴴᵒ

Anko smiled from the other end of the table.

* * *

It was not a fortune.

Mau had not expected one. He had also not expected the price to become so much smaller when they began setting things beside it.

Fuel. Time. A handle. The chance that making the edge would ruin the only piece they had brought home.

The journey to show it.

The people who would walk that journey.

Fern read the final line again.

"It isn't all money we get to spend." ᶠᵉ

"Never was," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

Jinshi moved the offer to the middle of a fresh sheet and left space on both sides.

"Then we should put what it costs where we can see it." ᴶ

Wakana was still looking at the notch.

"I want to test it here before we promise anyone an edge." ᵂ

"Yes," Mau said. ᴹ

Rimuru nodded beside him.

Anko put a finger on the road.

"And no new division because somebody thinks a shadow can replace the people walking it." ᴬⁿ

"It won't," Mau said. "If we offer help on a run, that's something we agree before the run. Not a reason to forget the guards afterward." ᴹ

Hollis looked at him for a moment, then toward Fern.

She kept the old four-three-three note where it was.

"The people going still decide whether they go," she said. ᶠᵉ

"Good," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

* * *

The workshop wanted to know how reliable the supply was.

That was the part nobody could answer with the small bar on the table.

"I can get more things," Mau said. ᴹ

"The same things?" Wakana asked. ᵂ

He stopped.

"Not a promise I can make from one room." ᴹ

Wakana nodded, relieved that the distinction had landed before he had to explain it badly.

Rimuru leaned over the drawing.

"Then start with what we have. A test. A question about what failed in their old tools. We don't agree to outfit every person who might walk into a gate." ᴿ

"They'll ask anyway," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

"They can." ᴿ

Mau looked from the offer to the list of things the house had wanted only days before.

Good containers. Better fastenings. Cold storage. Food they did not have to hope someone else could deliver in bad weather.

"If it works," he said, "I'd like it to buy more than the next load of supplies." ᴹ

Fern looked up.

He took a moment to find the ordinary way to say it.

"We make the things. Some of us should be able to go and talk to the people using them without somebody deciding we're a problem before we get there." ᴹ

The room went quiet enough that Hollis put his cup down.

"That isn't mine to grant," he said. ᴴᵒ

"I know. I'm not asking you to promise for everyone. But it should be part of what we ask for." ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him, then at the paper.

"Start with the workshop," he said. "Can their people meet ours? What would they need for that to happen?" ᴿ

Hollis rubbed a thumb along the softened fold.

"That's a question I can carry." ᴴᵒ

* * *

Jinshi wrote the reply once, then read it back before making a cleaner copy.

It asked what had broken, what had been tried against it and whether the workshop would be willing to discuss a test with the people doing the work.

It did not promise a delivery date.

It did not give the shop away.

It did not call anyone a king.

Mau listened to the last part, smiled at something Hollis had crossed out and asked whether the question about the visit sounded too much like a condition they had already imposed.

Hollis considered it.

"A little." ᴴᵒ

They changed that sentence.

Fern checked the quantities, then handed the page back to Jinshi rather than rewriting it herself.

Wakana took the bar with him when he went to the workshop. The offer remained on the table until Hollis folded it into the copy of his own notes.

Mau watched the two halves of the conversation go in different directions.

There was still no money to spend.

There was work they could choose to try, and someone outside waiting to hear what they said.

He helped Jinshi clear a dry space for the reply.
