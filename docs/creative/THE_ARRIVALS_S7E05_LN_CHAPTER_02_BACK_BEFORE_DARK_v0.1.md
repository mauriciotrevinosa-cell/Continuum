*When: Day499, morning. Mau has asked for the map without asking anyone to stand guard over him.*

Anko put it on the table.

"Which part?" ᴬⁿ

"The roads we actually know," Mau said. ᴹ

She laid two smaller pages beside it.

"Then not all of this one." ᴬⁿ

Rimuru moved his cup before the corners reached it. Mikasa came round the table and turned the nearest sheet so the route faced the way she remembered walking it.

Mau looked at the three of them.

"I want to use more of the army," he said. "For looking. Carrying. The work we keep stopping other work to do." ᴹ

Anko sat down.

"How much more?" ᴬⁿ

He thought about the register. About the four soldiers who had obeyed Umaru's directions for most of an afternoon, then waited just as patiently to bring everything back.

"Start with the ones I already have. Find out what they can do without all of us standing beside them. Then decide what else we need." ᴹ

Rimuru nodded.

"That's a better first number than as many as possible." ᴿ

"Eventually I do want more," Mau said. ᴹ

"I know." ᴿ

* * *

Mikasa asked about the reports.

Anko asked about the return.

Mau had an answer to one and only part of an answer to the other. He knew the soldiers belonged to him. He could call them, send them, bring them to rest. That did not tell him what a stranger might mean by waving at one from the far side of a road.

"They observe first," he said. "If they find someone, that's something to tell us. Not someone to pick up and bring here because they think we want people." ᴹ

"And if someone's being attacked?" Mikasa asked. ᴹⁱᵏ

He looked down at the crossing on the map.

"Then we help. But we still have to find out who they are and what they want afterward." ᴹ

She nodded once.

Anko tapped the short route between two familiar markers.

"This first. I can show them the turn. They come back, show us what they saw, and we see whether we understood the same thing." ᴬⁿ

"In pairs," Mikasa said. ᴹⁱᵏ

Mau agreed.

They chose four soldiers.

* * *

Igris stood beside the marker while Anko walked the first pair as far as the turn.

The other two carried empty crates along the route from the work area. Wakana waited at the far end to show them where to set them without blocking the door.

Mau watched them pass.

One crate had an awkward handle. The soldier carrying it stopped, adjusted its grip and continued. Behind him, a person who had been about to carry a bundle of poles put it down and pointed out a space beside the wall.

That was how it began.

Not with a thousand shapes covering the road. With somebody discovering they could finish one job before beginning another.

Rimuru came to stand beside him.

"You could have asked yesterday," he said. ᴿ

"Yesterday we were making sure Yuka didn't pull the ground out from under herself." ᴹ

"Fair." ᴿ

They watched the second crate go past.

"And the scouts?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

"Farther, when this works. If it works. Roads, settlements, people asking about someone we know." ᴹ

Rimuru looked down the path.

"Sometimes people arrive where we're not looking." ᴿ

"I know." ᴹ

Neither spoke for a while.

Then Rimuru went back inside to find another sheet.

* * *

Yuka found Mau near the steps with Igris beside him.

She had finished practice. A few strands of hair had worked loose near her face; she was eating the piece of fruit she had brought out with her and watching the road between bites.

"How far?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He showed her.

She leaned over the page.

"That's close." ʸᵏ

"It's the first one." ᴹ

"I wasn't complaining." ʸᵏ

She sat beside him and offered the fruit.

He took a bite.

"What are you hoping they find?" ʸᵏ

"Today? A way there and back that means the same thing to everyone." ᴹ

"And after today?" ʸᵏ

He looked toward the path.

"People we haven't found yet. Places we can go without arriving in the middle of trouble. Work we don't have to keep doing the hard way." ᴹ

She waited.

"And enough help that going out doesn't always mean somebody at home has to give something up." ᴹ

Yuka took the fruit back.

"Then put that part on the page too." ʸᵏ

* * *

The first pair returned while Anko was arguing with one of the carriers about a narrow corner.

There had been nothing wrong with the crate. The soldier had gone exactly where she pointed. Unfortunately, her pointing had not included the width of the crate.

Mikasa moved the marker half a pace.

"Again from here," she said. ᴹⁱᵏ

The scout waited until she had finished before approaching Igris.

It pointed down the road, made a low gesture with one hand, then held both hands apart.

Mau looked at it.

"The crossing?" ᴹ

The soldier repeated the gesture.

Anko crouched beside the map.

"Blocked?" ᴬⁿ

It nodded.

They took the report far enough to know what needed checking, then Anko went to look with the pair. She came back with wet mud on one boot and a small branch in her hand.

"They're right. It's caught underneath. Water's backing up over the path." ᴬⁿ

Mikasa marked it.

Mau watched the line appear.

He had not seen the crossing through someone else's eyes. He knew about it because somebody had gone, noticed, returned and been understood.

He could work with that.

"Tomorrow we clear it," Anko said. "Today we know to bring the right things." ᴬⁿ

The soldier stood beside her, waiting.

She looked up at it.

"Good work." ᴬⁿ

It straightened.

Mau smiled before he could decide whether the movement meant quite what it looked like.
