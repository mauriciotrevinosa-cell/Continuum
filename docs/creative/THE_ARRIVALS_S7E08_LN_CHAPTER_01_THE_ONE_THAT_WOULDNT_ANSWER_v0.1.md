## Chapter 15 — The One That Wouldn't Answer

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-26)
**Volume:** 37
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S7E08
**Continuity state:** Day509 morning. Before a new subjugation ritual, Mau explains the original branch's recovery of Yuka's dormant black Divine Dog (S6E17CH02), which was hers already, not dead, resurrected, another tame, Ear or a Monarch soldier. Yuka describes her own failed calls. She asks to investigate. Rimuru/Ciel distinguish restoring native access from lending another creature and request specific consent before inspecting her technique through the new contract; no general memory access or automatic permission from Blue. Maki contributes the expected starting pair. Yuka chooses the examination; it has not yet occurred at chapter exit. No new shikigami, summon, reserve, stat or roster change yet.

*When: Day509, morning. Yuka has brought her rope out, but Mau asks about the dog before they put down the markers.*

"There was something else," he said. ᴹ

She turned toward him.

"In the other life. Before we decide which ritual to try, I should tell you about the black one." ᴹ

The rope stopped uncoiling between her hands.

"Mine?" ʸᵏ

He nodded.

For a moment she only looked at him. Then she set the rope down rather carefully.

"You saw him?" ʸᵏ

"You called him." ᴹ

Yuka glanced at the ground beside her foot.

The white dog had not come up yet. There was only her ordinary shadow, falling across the short grass, and a place she had reached toward so often that she no longer expected an answer.

"What did I do?" she asked. ʸᵏ

* * *

He sat with her on the step.

Mau had thought they would be talking about rabbits that morning. He had been going over the things that could invalidate a ritual, the places where remembering the outcome was not the same as being able to promise it again.

This came before any of those things.

"You felt him there after we finished fighting," he said. "Asleep. You knew him before you brought him out." ᴹ

She listened without interrupting.

"You didn't have to beat him. He wasn't one of mine. Something in your technique had opened while the contract was there, and he could answer you." ᴹ

"The thing in you?" ʸᵏ

"The Noise. That's what I thought had done it. We hadn't asked it to work on that part. This time, we can ask before anything changes." ᴹ

She rubbed her thumb against the end of the rope.

"So I wasn't doing the sign wrong." ʸᵏ

Mau turned toward her.

"Is that what you thought?" ᴹ

"Sometimes." ʸᵏ

She looked down again.

"Sometimes I thought I was trying too hard. Then not hard enough. Grandma showed me, and I knew what it was supposed to feel like because I could call her." ʸᵏ

She made the familiar shape with her hands.

The white dog rose, shook herself once and pushed her nose against Yuka's wrist. Yuka moved her hand beneath the warm jaw.

"I stopped asking in front of people," she said. "It was easier when I didn't have to explain that it hadn't worked again." ʸᵏ

He let her finish stroking the dog before speaking.

"We could look for what's keeping him from answering. If you want us to." ᴹ

She lifted her head immediately.

"Yes." ʸᵏ

Then she caught his sleeve.

"But tell me what that means first." ʸᵏ

* * *

Rimuru was coming downstairs when they found him.

He had a folded shirt over one arm and the expression of somebody who had been asked where an object was while still carrying it. Yuka waited until he had put the shirt away before asking whether he had time.

He listened at the bottom of the stairs, then took them outside again. It would be easier, he said, if she could show them without somebody having to step over the dog on the way to breakfast.

Maki caught the last part.

"The black one?" she asked. ᴹᵃ

Yuka nodded.

Her grandmother looked at Mau.

"He answered her before," he said. "In the life I remember. He was there all along." ᴹ

Maki sat on the low step rather than asking the question while standing over Yuka.

"Show us where it stops," she said. ᴹᵃ

Yuka hesitated.

The white dog looked between them.

"It still might not work," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

"I know," Maki said. ᴹᵃ

She stayed where she was.

* * *

Yuka dismissed the white dog first.

Then she put her hands together again, slowly enough that nobody could mistake one part of the sign for another. Mau watched the shadow deepen beneath her feet.

She held it.

Nothing rose.

Her shoulders moved with a breath. She tried once more, changing the pressure of her fingers by so little he might have missed it without knowing where to look.

The shadow remained dark and empty.

"There," she said, lowering her hands. "It feels as if I'm reaching the right place. Then I can't find the rest of the way." ʸᵏ

Maki nodded.

"The pair is the beginning of the technique. You haven't skipped a fight you were supposed to win for him." ᴹᵃ

Yuka looked at her hands.

Mau waited for Ciel.

*I would need to follow the operation farther than that,* she said privately. *What I can see through you isn't enough to tell her what is wrong.* ᶜ

He passed that on. Then he explained what she was proposing: using the connection they had made to look at the part Yuka had just tried to call.

"We haven't done that with this contract," he said. "Blue doesn't give us permission to start changing yours." ᴹ

"You can look," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Rimuru rested his forearms on his knees.

"And if we find something we can work on, we tell you before we do it." ᴿ

She looked between them.

"Would he still be mine?" ʸᵏ

Mau understood which part she meant.

"I'm not offering you a copy of Ear. We look for the dog you were reaching for. If that's not what we find, we stop and talk about it." ᴹ

She nodded.

Maki reached for the rope Yuka had put beside her and moved it out from under a passing foot.

"You can sit down for this, can't you?" she asked. ᴹᵃ

Rimuru looked at Mau. Ciel answered him before he asked aloud.

"Yes," he said. "Sitting is fine." ᴿ

Yuka sat beside her grandmother, leaving room on her other side.

Mau took it.

* * *

The small pull was quiet between them.

He left it that way. What Yuka was offering now was a different part of her technique, and he waited while Ciel helped them distinguish the familiar reach from the new one.

Yuka described it herself. Where she began. Where she lost the feeling. Rimuru asked her to do it once without trying to force the end.

This time she did not have to keep attempting the call while everybody watched for a result.

She put her hands down when she had finished showing them.

"Like that," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau felt the attention beneath his own patterns turn toward what she had offered.

He told her before allowing it farther.

"Ready?" he asked. ᴹ

Yuka looked at the place where the white dog had sat, then at her grandmother beside her.

"Ready." ʸᵏ
