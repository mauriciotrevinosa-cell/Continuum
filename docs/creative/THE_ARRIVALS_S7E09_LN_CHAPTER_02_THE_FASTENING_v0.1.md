## Chapter 18 — The Fastening

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-26)
**Volume:** 37
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S7E09
**Continuity state:** Day510 afternoon. Household workshop separates the unknown returned bar's question from the tested strap/fastening problem. Wakana/Marin with Stark and Senku develop and load-test one padded carrier fitting using a recovered strap and existing materials; first positioning slips, they revise it. *When: Day510, afternoon. The first fastening slips before Stark has taken three steps.*

Wakana asked him to stop.

Stark stopped so abruptly that Marin caught the loose end before it swung into the table.

"I can carry it with my hand," he said. ˢᵗ

"We know," she said. "That isn't the part we're fixing." ᴹᵃʳ

He stood while Wakana moved the padded strip higher, then lower, then back to the place he had first rejected.

The recovered metal strap held. The cloth beneath it stayed where he had sewn it. The whole arrangement had simply decided it belonged somewhere other than Stark's shoulder.

Marin held up one side.

"Look at it when he moves." ᴹᵃʳ

Stark moved.

The box tilted.

Wakana made a small sound of recognition and took it off him.

* * *

Senku had come for the bar.

It lay on a separate bench with Hollis's notes, the shallow notch and the marks left by somebody whose hands none of them had seen at work.

Wakana showed him what he had tried since it came back. A little filing, no attempt to turn the entire piece into something they could no longer compare.

Senku looked at the dust on the scrap beneath it.

"So it can be worked. That gets us to the next question." ˢᵉ

"Whether it will hold an edge?" Wakana asked. ᵂ

"Among other things. What edge, doing what job, against what?" ˢᵉ

He picked up the drawing Hollis had brought. There was an outline of the damaged tool, then a space for the material which had damaged it.

The space had words in it. It still had no material.

Stark leaned over to see.

"Could we bring something back from the gate?" ˢᵗ

"We could. Then we'd learn whether it works on the thing we brought back. That's useful. It won't tell us what happened to their tool unless it's the same problem." ˢᵉ

Wakana glanced toward his cutting table.

He knew that part. Somebody could send measurements and still leave out the one thing which made a sleeve impossible to move in.

"I'd like to speak to whoever used it," he said. ᵂ

* * *

They left the bar where it was and returned to the fastening.

Marin had folded the padding differently. Stark put the box on again. This time Wakana could see the moment when the weight began pulling it outward.

He marked the place with his thumb.

"The second point needs to be here." ᵂ

"There you go," Marin said. ᴹᵃʳ

He looked up.

She was smiling at him over Stark's shoulder.

"You're doing the face." ᴹᵃʳ

"What face?" ᵂ

"The one where you've already taken it apart in your head and we're all waiting for your hands to catch up." ᴹᵃʳ

Stark tried to turn far enough to see it.

The box slipped again.

Wakana caught it. Marin laughed, and Senku moved the bar to a safer part of the other bench without waiting for a prediction.

* * *

The revised fitting had two attachment points.

It took the recovered strap, an ordinary second loop and more stitching than Wakana had wanted to admit before beginning. Marin made him leave enough room for the padding instead of pretending a strong thing could not be uncomfortable.

Stark carried the box to the door and back.

Then round the corner.

Then round the corner with somebody standing where a person ordinarily stood rather than where a test wanted them to stand.

The box stayed level.

He came back looking disproportionately pleased.

"That's better." ˢᵗ

Wakana checked the stitching before agreeing.

* * *

Mau arrived with one of the carriers while they were putting the tools away.

He listened to the explanation, then asked the soldier to hold still while Wakana fitted the arrangement to its frame. The first adjustment did not fit the same way it had on Stark.

This time Wakana expected it.

The soldier waited. Marin passed the loose end back through the loop; Stark showed it the route he had just walked.

Mau stayed beside the door while they made the second trip.

"You don't have to walk with it?" Wakana asked. ᵂ

"Not once it knows what we're asking. Tell it if it gets the corner wrong." ᴹ

The carrier came back, stopped at the bench and lowered the box where Stark pointed.

Nothing in it had fallen over.

Momo, who had come looking for a box nobody wanted to use for metal, watched the next trip.

"If that can keep a lid upright, I have a use for it." ᴹᵒ

Wakana looked at the unfinished strip still lying on his table.

"Tell me what you want to put inside before I sew it." ᵂ

She stayed to tell him.

* * *

Jinshi found Mau still there when he brought the letter for Hollis.

It asked the workshop's toolmaker whether they would meet the people working on the material. On the road, at a place both parties could agree to reach safely. They could bring the damaged piece and a sample of what it had failed against; nobody had to bring a payment for a product which did not exist yet.

"Would you come?" Jinshi asked Wakana. ᴶ

Wakana read the question twice before answering.

"If they agree. I'd rather ask badly in person than make something beautiful for the wrong job." ᵂ

"You'll ask fine," Marin said. ᴹᵃʳ

He glanced at her.

"I'll come too. Somebody has to remember what he looks like when he figures it out." ᴹᵃʳ

Wakana's ears went pink.

Jinshi waited until the laughter settled before asking which days they could actually leave their work.

Mau looked at the carrier making one last trip with Momo's empty box. Stark walked beside it, showing it how much room to leave near the kitchen door.

The bar had not become an arsenal that afternoon.

The box had become easier to carry.

He asked Jinshi to say he would come too, then passed the letter back so Wakana could check the questions he would be carrying.
