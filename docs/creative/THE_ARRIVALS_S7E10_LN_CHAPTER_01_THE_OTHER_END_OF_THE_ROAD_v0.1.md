*When: Day514, afternoon. Hollis has delivered the salt before going to the workshop, and the woman inside recognizes his knock.*

"You came back with more questions," Tamsin said. ᵀᵃ

"I was hoping you'd appreciate that." ᴴᵒ

She put down the handle she had been fitting.

Her workbench had a pale patch where his borrowed bar had lain on the previous visit. Hollis noticed because everything else had been used since: a knife moved, a wedge split, the thin shavings beneath her stool swept into a different corner.

He had spent a week carrying marks on a piece of metal between people who wanted to know what the other person had meant by them.

He put the letter down.

"They'd rather speak to you." ᴴᵒ

* * *

Tamsin read the first page standing up.

She sat for the second, mostly because she wanted to lay it beside the damaged blade without moving the handle out of its clamp.

Hollis let her finish.

"They haven't made it," she said. ᵀᵃ

"They didn't tell me they had." ᴴᵒ

"Your last customer wanted a price before I knew whether his door opened inward." ᵀᵃ

"That's why I thought this might be refreshing." ᴴᵒ

She gave him a look, then tapped the question about the material which had damaged the edge.

"I can answer that better with the thing in front of them." ᵀᵃ

"So can they." ᴴᵒ

* * *

It was under the bench, wrapped separately from the tools.

Tamsin put it on a board before removing the cloth. A curved plate, little bigger than her hand, with a tough strip still attached along one edge.

"Off something they brought out of an opening. The men wanted the plates fixed onto a coat. Easy enough to ask for." ᵀᵃ

She turned it.

Hollis could see where the tool had skated before biting into the edge at the wrong angle.

"And then?" ᴴᵒ

"Then I stopped before I ruined another blade on what they assured me was just hide." ᵀᵃ

She took the damaged cutter from its place and laid it beside the plate.

The missing part of the edge matched the little bright mark she had been describing. Hollis had not seen the cutting. He could at least carry both pieces of the question now.

"If they can give me an edge that works on this, I have uses for it," she said. "If they can only give me a harder bar, we should find that out before I order six." ᵀᵃ

He nodded.

She wrapped the plate again.

* * *

The proposed place was a clearing off the road Hollis already used.

Not the house. Not the market in Fallow where a seller had once hidden his purchases under the table until somebody else went home.

Tamsin read that part twice.

"Is that their condition or yours?" ᵀᵃ

"We talked about a place where you can ask your questions without needing to walk into somebody's home first." ᴴᵒ

"And afterward?" ᵀᵃ

"Afterward, we know whether we want another conversation." ᴴᵒ

She rubbed a thumb along the uninjured part of the blade.

"I've heard things." ᵀᵃ

"So have I." ᴴᵒ

"You go there." ᵀᵃ

"Yes." ᴴᵒ

She looked at him then, away from the tool and the page.

"And?" ᵀᵃ

Hollis thought about Momo making him sit down while the salt was still on his cart. About Fern checking the returns before deciding which loss belonged in whose column. About Anko asking his guards whether they wanted another run rather than telling them they had already agreed to it.

"I go back," he said. ᴴᵒ

* * *

Tamsin did not ask him to settle every story she had heard.

She asked who would be at the clearing. Whether they expected her to bring a finished design. Whether somebody would be doing anything with the material while she was standing close enough to regret it.

Hollis answered the first. The other two went onto the return page where he would not forget to ask them properly.

"No demonstrations sprung on anybody," she said. ᵀᵃ

He wrote that down without arguing.

She looked at the date.

"I can make time for that. Not tomorrow. I've promised these handles and I'm keeping the promise." ᵀᵃ

"Four days from now," he said. ᴴᵒ

"The day you've marked there. And if the road goes bad, we send word instead of deciding the other lot has betrayed us." ᵀᵃ

He laughed.

"I'll put that in too." ᴴᵒ

* * *

She kept the invitation.

Hollis took her reply, the dimensions she wanted checked and permission to tell them exactly how the blade had failed. The actual plate would travel with her.

At the door she stopped him.

"They put their names on it." ᵀᵃ

He looked back.

She held up the page. Wakana. The people who would come with him. Written plainly, with the questions they wanted to ask.

"It's easier when there are names," she said. ᵀᵃ

Hollis put on his hat.

"Usually." ᴴᵒ

Outside, Dunmore was waiting by the cart. He had found the place where a strap was beginning to wear and was holding it up for Hollis to see.

"Before the next run," he said. ᴰᵘ

Hollis looked at the strap, then back at the workshop door.

"Yes," he said. "Before the next run." ᴴᵒ
