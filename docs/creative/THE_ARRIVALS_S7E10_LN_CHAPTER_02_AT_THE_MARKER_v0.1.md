*When: Day518, morning. Hollis has brought the answer back, and the people named in the letter are waiting where they agreed to meet.*

Tamsin arrived with the damaged tool under one arm and a pack she was glad to put down.

Mau stepped forward to take it, stopped when her hand tightened on the strap and pointed to the clear place beside the bench instead.

"There?" he asked. ᴹ

She looked at the bench, then at him.

"There is fine." ᵀᵃ

He moved out of the way.

Hollis introduced them before anybody touched the bundle. Tamsin knew Wakana's name from the letter. She knew Senku's from the questions beside it. She looked at Marin, who showed her the rolled measurements and said she had come to make sure the people holding the tools were comfortable enough to use them.

Tamsin put down her pack.

"That's worth remembering." ᵀᵃ

* * *

The cutting tool was the same one.

The material beside it changed the conversation.

Wakana bent to look at the curved plate, then waited until Tamsin turned it over for him. There was a strip of tough tissue still attached. The place where the tool had broken was not where he had expected it to be.

"You were trying to trim this?" he asked. ᵂ

"Here. To get a fastening through without splitting the whole plate." ᵀᵃ

She showed him the angle.

He looked down at the outline they had brought.

Then he put a line through part of it.

Senku grinned.

"Glad we came?" he asked. ˢᵉ

"Very." ᵂ

Tamsin's mouth moved a little at one corner.

* * *

They tried an ordinary edge first.

Not the broken tool. Tamsin had brought a shorter blade with a defect in the handle, something she could afford to use for the comparison. She showed Senku what she meant to do before putting any weight behind it.

The soft strip resisted, bent and moved against the plate.

She stopped.

"There," she said. "If I keep pushing, the edge meets the hard part before the cut finishes." ᵀᵃ

Wakana crouched so he could see where her wrist had gone.

"The first drawing would make you turn farther." ᵂ

"Which is why I asked for that curve." ᵀᵃ

He nodded and changed another line.

Marin asked Tamsin to hold the tool as though she had been working for an hour instead of demonstrating the correct way to begin. Tamsin looked at her, then shifted her hand lower on the handle.

The angle changed again.

Marin pointed to it.

"That. He needs to see that too." ᴹᵃʳ

* * *

Mau had brought the bar wrapped separately.

He unwrapped it when Senku asked, laid it on the board and kept the cloth away from the tool Tamsin was still holding.

She recognized her own shallow notch.

"What have you learned?" ᵀᵃ

Senku showed her the little worked surface near one end. What they had managed to cut, what had not told them anything useful yet. He did not turn it into the blade in the drawing by describing it enthusiastically enough.

Tamsin took it when he offered.

She felt the weight, looked at the surface and set it beside the plate.

"It might do the job," she said. ᵀᵃ

"That's what we'd like to test," Wakana said. ᵂ

"One edge first. Not six because I said I could use six." ᵀᵃ

He looked relieved.

"One." ᵂ

* * *

The sample she left was small enough to fit in Wakana's palm.

It had broken away when the larger plate was taken off the creature, and still had a little of the same tough strip attached. She had brought it wrapped separately. Now she watched them label it with the tool and the direction of the failed cut.

Hollis made a note of what had changed hands.

"Do you want that paid for now?" he asked her. ᴴᵒ

"I want to see whether they can make the cutter. This is for testing it. If we get further than that, we'll count the rest." ᵀᵃ

Fern had sent questions about the next stage rather than a price she expected them to agree to on the road. Jinshi had left room beside each one.

They used the room.

Material. Work. What happened if the piece failed the first test. Who kept it afterward. How much of the bar they could afford to lose before deciding it was the wrong answer.

Mau listened while Tamsin asked about the journey back.

"We'll have to bring it to you," he said. ᴹ

"Or I come this way again." ᵀᵃ

"I'd like the people making it to see how it's used." ᴹ

She looked toward Wakana.

He had put down the sample so he would not damage its edge while thinking about it.

"They can come to my workshop," she said. ᵀᵃ

Hollis did not write immediately.

"Who?" he asked. ᴴᵒ

Tamsin looked at him.

Then she understood why he was waiting.

"The people here. For this job. I'm not offering to speak for every shop between there and Fallow." ᵀᵃ

"I wasn't asking you to," Mau said. ᴹ

He let himself smile.

"This is a good place to start." ᴹ

* * *

They ate what they had brought before parting.

Tamsin asked Marin about the padding in the carrying strap. Marin asked what happened to the handles Tamsin had stayed to finish before making the journey. Wakana listened until he had a question about the grip, then forgot the bread beside his hand while explaining the change he wanted to make.

Marin put it back in his hand without looking away from Tamsin.

Senku laughed.

Mau moved the tool drawing out of the way of a cup and found Hollis watching him.

"What?" he asked quietly. ᴹ

"You wanted them to meet," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

Across the bench, Tamsin had reached for Wakana's pencil. She asked before using it, then drew the place where the handle met the blade.

"Yes," Mau said. ᴹ

He stayed quiet while she explained it.

* * *

On the way home, the sample rode in Wakana's bag.

Beside it lay the drawing, changed in three places by somebody who had actually tried to use the tool. Wakana kept taking it out to look at the curve.

Marin walked with Wakana, discussing what to change in the padding now that somebody who used a blade all day had shown them her hand.

Senku had already begun counting the tests they could do with the small piece of plate. Hollis stopped him before the number exceeded the material.

Mau carried the empty cups.

When the house came into view, he could see one of the ordinary carriers waiting near the kitchen door with Momo's box. The new fitting was still doing its very small job.

He went to hold the door while it passed.
