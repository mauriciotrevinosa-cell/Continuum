One rabbit spread out beneath Maki's chair.

Another took the instruction as permission to investigate Yuka's bag.

The third stayed beside her foot.

"That one is listening," Maki said. ᴹᵃ

"They're all listening." ʸᵏ

The rabbit at the bag found the cloth round her food.

Yuka dismissed it before her grandmother had to say anything.

Mau put the containers down with the lids beside them. He was still smiling when Yuka came over.

"You saw nothing," she told him. ʸᵏ

"Very promising obedience." ᴹ

She took one container and began fitting the lid.

They meant to look farther into the ordinary gate the next morning. Something like the plate Tamsin had brought would help the workshop's tests; other materials might answer different questions. They had agreed not to call an object the same merely because it looked familiar.

Yuka checked the second lid too.

"We should take the rope with the new wrapping," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

Then he looked toward the table where Rimuru was sitting with Yuta.

"Before we finish packing," he said, "there's something I should explain." ᴹ

* * *

He did not ask everybody in the house to come.

Rimuru stayed. Yuta put down what he was doing. Maki brought her chair over, and Yuka sat beside Mau instead of carrying the containers away.

Gojo was at the end of the table when Shoko came for her cup. She listened to the first question and took a seat.

"You know there were places where they took the others out," Mau said. "Where the tower decided I had to be the only one fighting." ᴹ

Yuta nodded.

"You told us." ʸᵗ

"I didn't tell you what standing outside it meant." ᴹ

His hand stopped on the loose lid.

Yuka waited. He could feel her beside him without having to look.

"With Beru, they couldn't come in. They could see what was happening. I couldn't make the rule let them help." ᴹ

Rimuru moved his cup away from the middle of the table.

"Do you want him here?" he asked. ᴿ

Mau considered it.

"Yes. I think I do." ᴹ

* * *

Beru rose beside the table and bowed.

He looked from Mau to the others, pleased to have been called before any work had been mentioned.

"My king." ᴮᵉ

"We're talking about our fight," Mau said. "What the others could and couldn't do." ᴹ

Beru straightened.

"A battle worthy of—" ᴮᵉ

He stopped when Mau lifted his eyes.

Mau had not raised his hand. Had not asked him to be quiet.

The ant waited.

"He was getting hurt," Mau told the table. "I kept thinking if I could make the next cut count, he wouldn't have time to recover. Then he took it from me." ᴹ

Shoko's fingers stopped beside her cup.

"Took what?" she asked. ˢʰ

Mau looked down at his forearm.

"Pieces. He ate them. It helped him heal while he was still cutting me." ᴹ

Nobody moved for a moment.

Beru's hands had lowered to his sides.

"The people outside could see that," Mau said. "They were there. They wanted to help. There wasn't a useful thing for them to do with how much they wanted it." ᴹ

Yuka had gone very still beside him.

He put the lid down.

* * *

He could have continued through every injury.

He remembered them. His body had remembered some before he was able to explain why they were there.

He did not need to put all of them on this table.

"That's the part I want us to understand before we go farther," he said. "Going with me doesn't always mean a gate will let you stay with me. Standing outside doesn't always mean you get to pull me out." ᴹ

Gojo leaned forward.

"And a blue entrance doesn't tell us whether it will stay blue." ᴳ

Mau nodded.

Yuka looked at Beru.

The ant met her eyes, then lowered his head toward Mau.

"I remember what you overcame," he said. "I have spoken of the victory." ᴮᵉ

Mau waited.

"I did not ask whether you wanted the rest made small by it." ᴮᵉ

Yuta looked down at his hands.

"I don't want to make you pretend it wasn't your fight too," Mau said. "But I don't want it again." ᴹ

Beru bowed, slowly this time.

"Then I will remember that when I speak of it." ᴮᵉ

* * *

Yuka asked the first practical question.

"If someone comes out without you, how do we know whether to wait or go in?" ʸᵏ

"If the entrance still allows it, we check what happened. We don't assume I sent them out because everything's fine." ᴹ

"And if it doesn't allow it?" ʸᵏ

He rubbed the heel of his hand against his knee.

"We use whatever contact still works. If Ciel can answer, ask her. If she can't, don't turn that into a promise that she's keeping something from you." ᴹ

Rimuru nodded.

"We check the actual connection. We don't invent an answer because we need one." ᴿ

"Sometimes there are conditions for opening it," he said. "I don't have a method I can promise will ignore all of them." ᴹ

"And tomorrow?" ᴹᵃ

She looked at him, then back at her grandmother.

"I still want to go," she said. "But we're taking what we need if it lasts longer than we meant. Not one bag because we'll be home for lunch." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"Two." ᴹ

* * *

They changed the packing before going to dinner.

Food divided between them. Water. Cloth that had not already been used to wrap metal. The route people actually knew, and the places beyond it which they were choosing to look at rather than pretend had been checked.

Rimuru would remain home. Ciel would answer at the entrance before they went farther. If the familiar passage was different, they would start by looking rather than calling it safe from the color of the doorway.

None of that made Mau feel as though they had put the next morning in a box and closed it.

It made him feel less alone at the table.

Beru stayed while the others stood. Mau looked up at him.

"You'll come if I need you," he said. ᴹ

The ant's head rose.

"Always, my king." ᴮᵉ

Mau touched his arm before letting him return to the dark.

* * *

Yuka found him fastening his bag after the meal.

She sat beside him and took the second bag into her lap. Her fingers followed the place where he had redone the knot.

"Are you all right?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He thought about the answer before giving it.

"I'm glad I told you. I don't particularly like having told you." ᴹ

She nodded as though that made sense.

For a little while they checked the bags in silence.

Then she rested her head against his shoulder.

"I still want to go," she said again. ʸᵏ

"I heard you." ᴹ

"Good." ʸᵏ

She stayed until he had finished the last knot. When she stood, she took her own bag with her.
