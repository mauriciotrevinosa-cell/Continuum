*When: Day520, morning. Mau has taken two steps into the gate when Yuka stops at the threshold to lift the bottom of her bag clear of the stone.*

He turned to help her.

Behind her, the light changed.

There was no time to tell her what he saw. The edge of the entrance drew inward; the patch of daylight beside her shoulder narrowed to a line.

Mau caught the strap with one hand and put the other against her shoulder.

He pushed.

Yuka went backward through the opening, bag and all. She heard him take a step after her.

Then Rimuru caught her arm and the entrance went red.

* * *

She turned before her feet had settled.

Her hand met a surface where there had been room to step.

"Mau?" ʸᵏ

Nothing moved behind the red.

Rimuru put a hand beneath her elbow. She had struck one palm against the ground; there was dirt in the scrape and no useful place to put the anger which had arrived before she understood what she was angry at.

She pressed her clean hand against the entrance.

It remained closed.

Rimuru was listening to something she could not hear.

"Ciel?" he asked. ᴿ

Yuka stopped moving.

He looked at her.

"She can hear him." ᴿ

* * *

Mau had hit the inside of the entrance with his forearm.

It had not given.

He stepped back, looked at the empty place where Yuka had stood and made himself ask the question before trying to tear through something he did not understand.

*Is she out?* ᴹ

*Yes,* Ciel said. *Rimuru has her. She's scraped a hand. She's speaking to him.* ᶜ

He closed his eyes briefly.

*Tell her I tried to follow.* ᴹ

*She heard you.* ᶜ

Mau let his breath go.

The window opened beside the sealed entrance.

*Exit condition: defeat the Warden.*

The transfer he tried would not find a path beyond the boundary. His own dark answered when he reached toward it, but there was no answering place on the other side for him to step through.

Ciel checked the refusal with him.

They had contact. They did not have a doorway.

He turned to look at where he was.

* * *

The court was enormous.

An open circle of stone beneath a low, moving sky. Four pylons stood around it, each joined to a raised place farther north by thick seams in the paving. Broken galleries ran along the outer wall. There were no people in them.

Shapes stood between the columns.

Too still to be waiting casually. Too evenly spaced to be the remains of a crowd which had fled.

Mau watched the first head turn.

The figure beneath it stepped free of the wall.

Stone plates over a darker body. Long arms. An edge where a hand ought to be. Behind it, another figure moved, then three together.

He looked across the empty room they would have had to defend with Yuka beside him.

Then down at his hands.

"Good," he said softly. ᴹ

Ciel waited.

"She's out. There's nobody here I have to keep behind me." ᴹ

The daggers settled into his hands.

"I can actually let go." ᴹ

*Use the room,* Ciel said. *You still have to come back.* ᶜ

He smiled.

"That's the plan." ᴹ

* * *

Yuka sat on the low stone beside the marker while Rimuru cleaned her palm.

Anko had come when somebody called from the path. She looked at the entrance first and asked what had happened before looking at the blood.

"He pushed me out," Yuka said. "It was already closing." ʸᵏ

Rimuru held the cloth where she could reach it.

"He says he tried to come after you." ᴿ

"I know. I heard him." ʸᵏ

She looked at the red surface again.

"Is he hurt?" ʸᵏ

Rimuru listened.

"Not so far. There's something he has to defeat to open it. He's checking the room." ᴿ

Yuka drew a slow breath.

She could still feel the quiet place where the borrowed operation waited.

"Tell him I won't reach for Blue while he needs it. Not until we talk again." ʸᵏ

Rimuru nodded and passed that on.

Anko stayed beside them, looking at the sealed entrance rather than pretending she could see through it.

* * *

The first guard reached Mau at a run.

He stepped inside the blade.

It was the movement Yuta had made him repeat until he stopped reaching for the opponent's weapon instead of the space beside it. His body covered the distance before the guard could bring its second arm around.

The right dagger went beneath the plate.

Mau turned and pulled.

The guard came apart against the stone.

He kept moving.

The second one had already begun its swing. Mau met its wrist with the flat of his left blade, drove his shoulder into the plated chest and let the strength he ordinarily measured out go through the movement.

The body crossed the court in pieces.

He stared after it for half a breath.

Then he laughed once, breathlessly, and went to meet the others.

* * *

They came in groups.

Mau learned where the plates overlapped. Where the darker joints opened when the arms rose. The daggers gave him the extra speed he could feel at the edge of each turn; the body beneath it was his, strong even when he reached for no cursed energy at all.

He did not have to stop the blade short of somebody standing behind the target.

Stone burst from a column where the third guard landed. A fourth struck the place he had left; he kicked it sideways into the fifth and cut the nearer joint before either could rise.

The shadows beneath his feet stirred with interest.

"Wait," he told them. ᴹ

The paving pulsed.

All four pylons answered.

Mau stopped laughing.

North of him, the raised platform opened along its middle. Something enormous unfolded from beneath it, lifting six armored limbs out of the dark.

The remaining guards stepped aside.

The Warden put one hand on the court.

The stone under Mau's feet dropped.
