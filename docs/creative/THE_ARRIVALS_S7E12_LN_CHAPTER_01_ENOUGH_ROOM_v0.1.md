*When: Inside the third Red Gate, continuous. The stone has dropped out from under his feet.*

Mau jumped before the second section fell.

He cleared the first gap. The slab he aimed for turned on its edge, leaving empty air where he had meant to land.

"Kaisel." ᴹ

A wing opened beneath him.

He caught the familiar ridge behind the dragon's neck, bent his knees against the landing and felt Kaisel carry them over the pieces of floor still dropping into the dark.

Below, the Warden lifted its hand.

The fallen paving began to rise.

*Not a fall,* Ciel said. *It's moving the ground.* ᶜ

Mau looked at the seams reaching toward the four pylons.

"Then we stop standing where it wants." ᴹ

He opened his shadow.

* * *

He left the four assigned at home where they were. The rest answered.

The soldiers came out across the court.

They filled the broken gaps between columns, caught the guards still trying to reach him and drove them away from the center. Mau felt the weight of calling them, deep and familiar, separate from the work he was about to ask of his own cursed energy.

Igris took the western gallery.

Bellion's blade lengthened across the eastern steps, caught two guards at their joints and pulled them into the path of the soldiers behind him.

Beru landed on the court hard enough to break the paving which had survived the first collapse.

He looked up.

"My king?" ᴮᵉ

Mau pointed at the Warden.

"Legs. Don't let it turn everyone into a pile in the middle." ᴹ

The ant smiled.

* * *

Mau dropped from Kaisel as the dragon banked.

The Warden met him with its upper right arm.

He put both daggers into the opening beneath the plate, braced a foot against the wrist and turned with all the force his body could give him.

The limb struck the court before the rest of the creature understood where he had gone.

Stone flew.

Beru went through the space below it, taking a lower joint at speed. Igris's soldiers struck the guards driven out of the way; Bellion caught the edge of the falling arm before it could bury them.

Mau landed, bent low and cut again.

Something dark opened beneath the armor.

The Warden pulled back.

He grinned at it.

Then the western pylon lit.

New plates closed over the wound.

* * *

He hit it a second time to make sure.

The cutting pattern opened when he let the defensive space go. A line passed through the half-formed wrist; the limb split and struck the ground beside him.

The pylon answered again.

Mau saw the movement beneath the stone before the arm began rebuilding.

*There,* Ciel said. ᶜ

"I see it." ᴹ

The Warden drove another hand toward him.

He shut the cutting pattern, formed the pull beyond its shoulder and dragged the creature off the angle it wanted. The hand missed him by more than its own width.

Mau released before planting the opposite force against its chest.

Red struck.

The Warden left the dais.

For a moment its entire plated body crossed the court without touching it. Then it hit the northern wall and drove through the gallery behind it.

The sound rolled back over Mau.

He lowered his hand slowly.

No house behind the wall. No person sheltering where the gallery had stood.

He could still feel the push in his arm.

"That," he said, "was better." ᴹ

* * *

The Warden rose beneath a rain of its own masonry.

All four pylons answered this time.

Mau felt the change before he saw what it meant. The pressure did not come toward his skin like a blade. It travelled through the court, lifting one edge of each broken slab and turning it toward him.

The soldiers nearest the center were crushed between them.

Darkness broke, gathered and rose again where he called it. He felt the reserve answer each rebuilding rather than pretending that being his made it free.

"Back to the galleries," he told them. ᴹ

The second wave came higher.

He stored the daggers and drew the katana. The change of balance mattered; he adjusted the next step instead of expecting the weapons' extra speed to remain in his empty hands.

His body was still fast enough to take him out of the first line.

The second was already there.

Mau caught the surface in front of him and turned it.

The rising stone travelled along the fold of sky rather than straight through his chest. Not as far as he wanted. The rough edge shuddered in his grip, slipped and threw the last fragment against his shoulder.

He landed on one knee.

Pain ran down his arm.

He let the borrowed sky fall flat before trying to put anything else in its place.

*Shoulder,* Ciel said. ᶜ

"I felt it." ᴹ

*Then don't make me ask twice.* ᶜ

He checked the arm. It moved. It hurt. He could work with both facts.

* * *

Kamish rose behind the southern half of the court.

The old dragon looked at the moving floor and then at the pylons.

"A shell with four places to hide its life," he said. ᴷᵃ

Mau glanced up.

"Can you break one?" ᴹ

"One?" ᴷᵃ

He smiled despite the shoulder.

"Wait for me to give you the others." ᴹ

The dragon settled his weight without arguing.

Another wave built beneath the Warden's hands.

Mau put the katana away.

Something much larger than a soldier rose beside him, pale mass and blade, the wheel above its head turning as it found the direction of the pressure.

"Take that one," Mau said. ᴹ

Mahoraga stepped forward.

* * *

The first impact drove him back.

His arm turned with it, blade scraping through the stone. Mau felt how the pressure had entered the block: low first, then upward, catching whatever braced against it.

The wheel moved.

The Warden struck again.

This time Mahoraga set his foot differently. The rising slab broke against his lower arm instead of carrying it straight into his chest.

The blow still moved him.

It moved him less.

Mau waited for the second turn, felt the changed response begin to settle into the familiar shared rhythm, and called him back before a third strike could demand a different answer.

The wheel remained above Mau.

He lowered his stance as the pressure reached him.

It scraped his boots across the paving. His shoulder protested. But it did not lift his feet out from under him as it had before.

"Enough," he said through his teeth. ᴹ

He moved.

* * *

Igris reached the western pylon first.

His blade found the seam Mau had pointed out. The soldiers held the guards away while he opened it, taking small, precise pieces rather than throwing himself against the whole column.

Across the court, Bellion wound his weapon round the eastern support and pulled.

Kamish's breath struck the southern pylon.

Beru went over the Warden's back and drove both hands into the northern one.

For an instant, every line on the floor showed.

Then three went dark.

The fourth brightened.

Fresh stone closed over Igris's cut. Bellion's split support drew itself together. The Warden caught Beru by one arm and threw him into the broken gallery.

Mau was already reaching.

The Ruler's grip caught the largest falling piece before it could strike the soldiers beneath him. He shoved it aside and let it crash where nobody stood.

Beru came out of the dust, furious and intact.

*They feed each other,* Ciel said. *The fourth one stayed open long enough to restore the other three.* ᶜ

Mau looked at the four points around the court.

"Then none of them gets that long." ᴹ

* * *

The Warden put all six hands on the ground.

Mau felt the court change beneath him.

The circle split into separate platforms. Galleries tore loose from the wall. Each pylon rose with its own island of stone, pulling the four lines taut between them and the creature standing in the middle.

Kaisel wheeled above the opening.

The soldiers spread over the pieces still large enough to hold them.

Mau looked at the distance between his four captains.

The Warden had made it harder for them to reach together.

It had also shown him exactly where the connections ran.

He called Kaisel down.

"All right," he told Ciel. "Now we do it properly." ᴹ
