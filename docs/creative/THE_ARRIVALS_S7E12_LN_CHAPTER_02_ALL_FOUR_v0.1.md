*When: Inside the third Red Gate. The four pylon-platforms are rising away from the center, and Mau can see the lines holding them together.*

"Igris, west. Bellion, east. Stay with the seams you already opened." ᴹ

Both knights looked up.

"Beru, north. Kamish, south." ᴹ

The Warden moved between them.

Mau pointed at the lines beneath its body.

"Don't break yours early. It will use the one left standing to put the others back. Wait until I make it reach." ᴹ

Beru's claws stopped just short of the northern stone.

"As you command." ᴮᵉ

Kaisel carried Mau toward the center.

The Warden raised its two upper arms to meet him.

* * *

He left the dragon before the hands closed.

For an instant he was above the creature's shoulder with nothing beneath his feet. Then he caught a plate, drove the katana into the exposed join and used it to swing himself down beneath the arm.

The blade held.

His shoulder did not like the movement at all.

Mau braced against the creature's ribs, pulled the katana free and let the next hand pass between him and the place he had been.

The pressure wave caught him anyway.

The wheel answered the familiar part. It did nothing about the upper arm striking the plate beside his head.

He slid, lost his foothold and landed hard on the inner platform.

Blood ran along his forearm where the edge had found him.

He looked at it once.

Then at the Warden above him.

"Still learning," he said. ᴹ

It brought three hands down.

* * *

Mau put the katana away and moved between the first two impacts.

The third would have caught him against the stone if he had tried to outrun the whole hand. He did not. He drew its lowest finger aside with the Ruler's grip, barely enough to change where the force landed, and went through the gap it made.

The grip shook under the weight.

He let it go before trying to hold something that size still.

Ciel had followed the pulse in the floor beneath him.

*It repairs the body before the outer supports when both are damaged.* ᶜ

"Then give it a body to repair." ᴹ

The daggers came back.

He went up the creature's folded arm.

* * *

The first blade opened the join.

The second went beneath it.

Mau kicked the plate away, changed hands and drove the right dagger into the darker structure which had been hidden underneath. The Warden turned with him, trying to bring the damaged place behind its other limbs.

He stayed on it.

The equipment's extra speed carried him to the next opening. His own strength pulled him through the turn when the creature tried to shake him loose.

Below him, the central lines brightened.

One. Two.

He could see the third feeding toward the shoulder he was opening.

*The south line is holding back,* Ciel said. ᶜ

Kamish saw the change too.

The old dragon lowered his head, waiting beside the unbroken pylon.

The Warden struck its own side to get Mau off.

He let go before it landed.

* * *

Kaisel caught him low over the central platform.

Mau stayed crouched against the dragon's neck while a slab crossed the air where his head had been. He reached for its surface, felt the rough sky-grab take, and turned it just enough to send it past the eastern pylon instead of into Bellion's soldiers.

The fold tore out of his grasp.

He released it and breathed through the moment of pressure behind his eyes.

*Choose what you look at,* Ciel said. *You don't need every falling stone.* ᶜ

He shut out the fragments.

Four supports. The lines between them. The creature choosing which damaged part to close first.

That he could follow.

"Tell Rimuru I'm still here," he said. ᴹ

*He knows. Yuka wants you to stop answering questions while something is trying to hit you.* ᶜ

He laughed despite the breath he needed.

"She makes a good point." ᴹ

* * *

They came round behind the Warden.

Mau released Kaisel's ridge, dropped to the platform and planted the pull beneath the creature's damaged side. It fought the change of balance, turning two hands down to keep itself upright.

He let Blue end.

The daggers were gone when he raised his right hand.

A cutting line passed through the upper join he had already opened.

The plate fell away.

Inside, a many-sided core turned toward him, and the fourth line began to feed.

"Now," Mau said. ᴹ

* * *

Igris struck the exposed western seam.

Bellion's weapon tightened around the eastern support and pulled its two halves apart.

Beru went through the northern plate at the place he had already weakened, both hands buried until the stone broke around his wrists.

Kamish breathed into the southern column.

Mau caught the lowest stone collar around the central connection with the Ruler's grip and held it out of alignment.

Only for a moment.

Enough to keep the first breaking support from drawing the others closed again.

The collar tore free of his grasp.

All four lines went dark.

* * *

The platforms began to fall.

Mau called the soldiers away from the collapsing edges. Some jumped to the wider pieces. Others broke in the fall and answered when he called them back into shape where they could stand.

Bellion cleared the eastern landing.

Igris took his people with him.

The Warden pulled its upper arms inward.

For the first time, the open place in its side stayed open.

Then the core began to turn faster.

*It's gathering what remains,* Ciel said. ᶜ

Mau watched the broken stones lift toward it.

Not all of them. The pieces nearest the torn connections first.

It was trying to make another way to the supports.

He put his hand on Kaisel when the dragon came down beside him.

"Get me above it." ᴹ

* * *

They climbed through falling stone.

Mau looked down once, found the clear line past the creature and the gap already torn in the northern gallery. Nothing behind it needed him to stop the shot before it was finished.

"Everyone away from the center," he said. ᴹ

The captains moved.

He felt the army withdrawing from the lane before he began the next operation. The grip on the stone was gone. The cutting pattern was closed.

Only the two forces he needed.

One drew the drifting grit toward his palm.

The other pressed so densely against the air that the dust would not approach it at all.

Mau kept them apart.

The Warden lifted its head.

* * *

He knew what it had felt like when they met in the wrong place.

That memory came without his asking for it: something through his chest, nowhere left to pull away, his hands becoming the only distance he could still control.

He was not there.

Kaisel was beneath him. The sky was open. He could set the meeting point beyond his palms and still choose whether to let it happen.

Ciel felt the hitch in his breathing.

*Here,* she said. *You are here.* ᶜ

"I know." ᴹ

He waited until he meant it.

Then he moved.

* * *

Mau jumped from the dragon as the Warden's core faced him.

Kaisel banked out of the line.

For a moment the only thing holding Mau in place was the shape of the leap he had already made. He had the shot, the two forces and the space between his hands.

He brought them together beyond it.

"Hollow Purple." ᴹ

* * *

The shot crossed the court.

The Warden tried to put its upper limbs between the core and the approaching force. Mau saw the plates meet it, saw the first section come apart, then lost the center of the creature behind the crossing light.

The northern gallery vanished into the same line.

Something in the court gave a deep, single report.

The stones which had been rising stopped.

Mau was falling.

He closed the operation before reaching for anything else, but his hand would not quite close on Kaisel's ridge when the dragon came beneath him.

Kaisel lifted into the catch.

Mau struck the broad neck with his chest, caught with his right forearm and stayed there while the dragon carried them away from the falling platform.

For several breaths, he did nothing but breathe.

* * *

The Warden hit the court in pieces.

Enough of the body remained to make the impact enormous. The lower limbs collapsed beneath the broken shell. The opened core struck the stone, rolled once and went still.

No pylon answered.

No new plate closed over it.

Mau watched until the dust began to settle.

Ciel checked the operation he had closed. The spent force. His breathing, which was ugly but becoming less so.

*Done,* she said at last. ᶜ

He let his forehead rest against Kaisel.

"Done," he repeated. ᴹ

Behind them, at the southern edge of the court, daylight returned to the entrance.
