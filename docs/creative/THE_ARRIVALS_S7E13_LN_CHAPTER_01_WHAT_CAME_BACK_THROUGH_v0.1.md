His legs held.

That was useful. His shoulder hurt, his forearm was bleeding and the part of him which had put the two forces together wanted him to stop asking it for things.

He sat on a piece of stone which was no longer moving.

*Rimuru knows it's finished,* Ciel said. ᶜ

"Tell him I'm sitting down. Before he decides the silence is interesting." ᴹ

He looked across the court.

Beru was standing on the northern wreckage. Bellion had soldiers moving away from an unstable edge. Igris came back along the route to the entrance, checking where the paving had settled rather than trusting that a victory made every stone safe.

Mau let the dragon rest.

* * *

Yuka came through with Anko behind her.

She stopped at the sight of the court.

The northern wall had a hole in it. Pieces of gallery lay where the floor ought to be. Something enormous and defeated occupied most of the space before the raised platform.

Then she saw him.

Mau stood when she came closer.

She got both arms around him before he had worked out which explanation to give first.

He caught her carefully, leaving the injured forearm out of the pressure.

For a little while, neither of them explained anything.

Anko went past them to ask Igris about the loose stones.

* * *

"You tried to follow me," Yuka said against his shoulder. ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

She drew back enough to see his face.

"Good. I didn't imagine it." ʸᵏ

"How's your hand?" ᴹ

She showed him the cloth.

He looked at it longer than the small scrape justified, then let her turn his wrist to see his own.

"You first," she said. ʸᵏ

He sat again.

Ciel helped him check the little line along his forearm before he used the familiar, narrow work of repair. The fresh edges drew together slowly. His two stiff fingers stayed where they always stopped.

Yuka watched until he was done, then gave him water instead of another question.

* * *

The notices had been waiting.

Mau looked at them after the first drink.

One hundred and nine. Seventy-two points waiting where fifty-seven had been. He did not press anything beside the number.

"Umaru gets more arithmetic," he said. ᴹ

Yuka looked at him.

"Levels. Three. The points are still there." ᴹ

"Good," she said. "She'd be unbearable if you spent them before she got to look." ʸᵏ

Something settled into his open hand.

They both stopped smiling for a moment.

* * *

The key was metal, cool against his palm.

Marks ran along the broad head of it. When Yuka bent closer, the words stood clear enough for her to read.

*Meet the Monarch.*

Below them, smaller:

*Owner: Mau.*

He turned it.

A countdown moved beneath the name. Days, hours, minutes; seconds changing while they watched. There was enough time on it that he read the opening condition twice rather than assume a smaller number was hiding underneath.

"Not today," he said. ᴹ

Anko came over.

He handed it to her when she asked. The countdown continued in her palm.

"Owner required," she read. "Companions permitted." ᴬⁿ

Yuka looked at Mau.

"So nobody walks in just because they're holding it." ʸᵏ

He nodded toward the words.

"And it doesn't say I have to go alone." ᴹ

Anko turned it once more before giving it back.

"We'll read the rest at a table that isn't falling apart." ᴬⁿ

He put it away separately from the crystals.

* * *

There were things he wanted to bring back which would not fit into the sample containers.

Mau asked Ciel about the remaining demand before getting up. She checked the reserve which answered his shadows, then the different strain in his hands and chest.

*You can call them. You are not making another shot like that while we collect the pieces.* ᶜ

"Wasn't planning to." ᴹ

Yuka waited while he went to the Warden.

He stopped at the edge of the broken body. The core which had moved the court was gone. Enough of the thing beneath the armor remained for the shadow to lie deep under it.

Mau looked at the quiet shape.

"Arise." ᴹ

* * *

The first movement was a hand against the stone.

Then the plated body gathered above it, dark where the Warden had been pale, its broken limbs whole again within the shape answering him.

Mau felt the cost when it rose. He waited for the movement to settle rather than calling the next one over it.

The new guardian bowed its head.

"Rampart," he said. ᴹ

It lowered itself farther.

The court did not rebuild around it. No four pylons rose out of the floor to join the ones which had been destroyed.

There would be things to learn about what it could do away from this place.

For now, Mau let it rest.

* * *

He chose four of the guards near the southern wall.

Whole enough to answer. Different enough in the way they had moved that he wanted to see what they could do when they were not trying to cut his legs out from under him.

They rose one at a time.

Anko waited until he had finished before asking him to count them with her. Four new ordinary soldiers. One new captain. The old soldiers which had broken during the fight had already returned; he had left the four working at home where they belonged.

He called the remaining fighters back as Igris finished arranging the load.

* * *

Thirty-two small crystals went into the wrapping.

Four larger ones had fallen from the broken pylon supports. Another, heavier than the rest, lay among the rewards beside the defeated body, distinct from the core Mau had destroyed.

Anko counted those separately.

Wakana's sample containers took smaller pieces; the four broad sections of plate needed soldiers to carry them. Yuka inspected the labels before anyone could call the new material the same as Tamsin's on the strength of its shape.

Mau watched her cross out the first description and write a better one.

"Different creature," she said. ʸᵏ

"Different question," he agreed. ᴹ

She looked up.

He smiled.

* * *

They left the ruined court by the route Igris had checked.

Mau carried his own bag. The key rested in an inner pocket. Yuka walked beside him with her uninjured hand in his, not pulling him toward the exit, just keeping the place they had meant to walk together.

At the entrance he stopped.

Daylight lay beyond it. Rimuru was standing by the marker, looking toward them rather than at the first load of crystals going past.

Mau tightened his hand gently around Yuka's.

This time they took the step together.
