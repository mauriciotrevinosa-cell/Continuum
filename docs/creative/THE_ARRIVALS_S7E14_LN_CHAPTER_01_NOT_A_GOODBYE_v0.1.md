Mau sat down when they asked him to.

He had expected more questions about the fight. Instead, Fern pointed to the counter and asked him to read the largest unit aloud.

He did.

She compared it with the number she had written before he arrived.

"More than a year," she said. ᶠᵉ

The words changed the shape of the thing on the table.

Yesterday it had belonged to the wrecked court. To a creature large enough to unfold out of the floor. To what would happen if he put it in a door he had not seen yet.

More than a year belonged to other things too.

He looked at Yuka across the room. She was helping Rem find the cloth somebody had put away in the wrong place, sleeves pushed back from her hands.

"And here," Fern said. ᶠᵉ

He looked back.

Her finger rested beside the birthday they had chosen for him.

"After this one." ᶠᵉ

* * *

Rimuru remembered the day they had chosen it.

He told Fern that, smiling, and she gave him a look which suggested she remembered him making the decision sound like the easiest one in the world.

"You said tomorrow," she reminded him. ᶠᵉ

"You wanted him to have a birthday. We needed a day." ᴿ

Mau touched the edge of the sheet.

Different lives had done terrible things to the places where his memories belonged. The date had stayed on the paper. People had still wanted to prepare something for him before he knew he ought to expect it.

He looked at the key again.

"It doesn't say I have to enter when it opens." ᴹ

Rimuru turned it toward the light.

"No penalty here. No threat. A time when the lock becomes available." ᴿ

"Not a promise about what's on the other side," Fern said. ᶠᵉ

"No," Mau agreed. ᴹ

He waited for the fear to finish making its first lap around that thought.

Then he put his hand beside the key.

"We can prepare without treating every day before it like a goodbye." ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him for a moment.

"I'd like that." ᴿ

* * *

They wrote down what they could actually read.

Who needed to be there to enter. What the key said about companions. Where the counter put the opening against the dates they kept, with room to check it again instead of trusting a note they might remember differently later.

They did not give the unknown Monarch a face.

When Jinshi came to collect the return notes, Fern passed him the copy rather than beginning a third version herself. He checked which part needed to stay private before taking it.

The key went back to Mau.

"Don't put it with the loose fastenings," she said. ᶠᵉ

"You saw me think about it." ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him.

Mau put it in the inner pocket and closed the fastening properly.

* * *

Ori arrived looking for Frieren.

The compass was open in her hand. She followed the needle past the table, turned too soon and had to come back when it pointed through the wall instead of down the passage she had chosen.

Mau leaned aside so she could see the next doorway.

"That way?" he asked. ᴹ

Ori inspected the needle, then him.

"You have to look," she said. "Not guess." ᴼ

He nodded gravely.

"Good rule." ᴹ

She went to find Mum.

Fern watched her reach the reading room. Frieren looked up when Ori appeared, then toward Fern still sitting beside the papers.

She moved the book off the other chair.

Fern hesitated, looked at the copy Jinshi had already taken and stood.

Mau stayed to carry the cups with Rimuru.

The key rested against his chest while they cleared the table. Beyond the door, Frieren was asking Fern which part of the chapter she wanted to argue about first.
