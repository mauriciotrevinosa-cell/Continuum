*When: Day522, afternoon. Yuka has come to Rimuru's office carrying the rope, and Mau is sitting where he can see both the window and the table.*

She put the rope beside his chair.

"We paused it for the fight," she said. "I'd like to use it again." ʸᵏ

He looked up.

"Me too." ᴹ

"Small?" ʸᵏ

"What we know. Tell me when you begin." ᴹ

She reached for the familiar path. The rope end moved toward the place she had chosen, held for a breath and fell still when she released it.

Mau felt the beginning and the end.

He nodded.

Yuka picked up the rope herself instead of using the pull to put every part away.

"All right. What are we looking for?" ʸᵏ

* * *

Ciel had asked him the same question that morning.

He had begun by saying less noise. She had asked what part he meant.

He had thought about the court. About trying to follow the four lines while every broken edge in the room insisted on being another thing he could notice.

*I don't want to be unable to see them,* he had said. *I want to be able to hear someone talking without carrying all of it at once.* ᴹ

Now Gojo stood by the window, watching the light fall across the table.

"You already choose," he said. "The question is what you have to lose before the choice becomes bearable." ᴳ

Mau looked toward Rimuru.

He was listening to Ciel.

"She wants to try keeping the work somewhere you don't have to read every answer," Rimuru said. "Then you ask for the part you need." ᴿ

"And the rest?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

"Still processed. Not pushed at him because it's finished." ᴿ

Mau smiled.

"That last part sounds important." ᴹ

* * *

They began with things he could already see.

The rope on the floor. The cup on the table. The edge of a leaf in the window and the little pull Yuka knew how to place beside a marker. Nothing behind a wall. Nothing somebody else remembered.

Ciel helped him show the Noise what she wanted kept apart.

Not the eye's receiving.

The labor of sorting what arrived from it.

For a while, Mau could feel only the familiar activity beyond his attention. Then a little of it gathered around the task instead of continuing past it toward the next unfinished thing.

*Keep this,* Ciel said. *Let him ask for it.* ᶜ

The Noise answered after a pause.

*Keep.*

Mau rested his hands on his knees.

"Ready," he told the room. ᴹ

* * *

Yuka began the pull.

The rope moved.

Mau opened a part of the intake he usually kept narrowed. Ciel took the offered flow and guided it toward the new work.

At first it was easier.

The little operation stood apart from the floor around it. He could follow its beginning without mistaking every shifting fiber for another event he needed to manage.

Then the rest arrived.

Not a jumble. That would almost have been kinder.

Each thing had been separated neatly enough that he could notice how much there was to notice. The leaf. The water against the cup. The grain of the table, changing under the light from the window. The rope and the space around it and the pressure of his own attention trying to decide which answer to take first.

"Mau?" Yuka said. ʸᵏ

He heard her.

Finding the part of the room from which to answer took too long.

*Stop,* Ciel said. ᶜ

He closed the opening. Yuka released the pull when he lifted his hand.

The room became a room again.

* * *

Rimuru gave him time to describe it before asking another question.

Mau looked at Yuka, then down at his hands.

"It worked very hard," he said. "And gave me an extremely organized flood." ᴹ

Gojo laughed once, quietly.

Rimuru had the expression of somebody listening to an objection he did not intend to repeat word for word.

*An accurate description,* Ciel said at last. *Unnecessarily pleased with its phrasing.* ᶜ

Mau smiled despite himself.

*You asked what it felt like.* ᴹ

She considered the part they had kept.

*It returned the work as though completing it meant you wanted it. We can change that.* ᶜ

He passed the explanation on.

Yuka looked at the rope.

"Like asking someone to tell you when dinner's ready, and getting the entire recipe instead?" ʸᵏ

Rimuru looked at her.

Then he smiled.

"She likes that one better." ᴿ

* * *

They did not open it again that afternoon.

Ciel wanted to work on how the answer waited. Gojo asked Mau to describe the first moment, the part before the flood, while it was still easy to tell from the rest.

He could do that without making himself experience it again.

Yuka sat beside him, rolling the rope while he talked. When he had finished, she put it in her bag and asked whether he still wanted to go outside.

Mau blinked.

"You asked me that?" ᴹ

"Before the table tried to explain itself." ʸᵏ

"Yes. I do." ᴹ

"Umaru wants partners for the cards. Milim has decided you're on our side unless you win too often." ʸᵏ

"Does she know how often I win?" ᴹ

"I offered no guarantees." ʸᵏ

* * *

They found the game outside, on the table Umaru had claimed before anybody could put work on it.

Milim looked up when Mau sat beside Yuka.

"You can't ask Ciel what to play." ᴹᶦ

"Wasn't planning to." ᴹ

Umaru dealt.

Yuka looked at her first cards, looked at Mau and smiled with the confidence of somebody who had already decided how much trouble he would be.

He leaned closer when she explained the opening move.

The rope, the room and the leaf stayed where they were. He was back on the familiar setting while Ciel worked, able to listen to one ordinary sentence without asking it to become a test.

He played the wrong card anyway.

Milim put her head on the table.

Yuka laughed so hard she had to put her cards down.
