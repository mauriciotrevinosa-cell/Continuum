*When: Day526, afternoon. The first cutter works until Marin holds it the way Tamsin held hers.*

Then the edge catches.

"Stop," Wakana said. ᵂ

Marin stopped.

Mai looked over his shoulder at the little piece fixed to the board.

"It's still the wrist," she said. ᴹᵃᶦ

Wakana lowered himself until he could see the angle without asking Marin to make it comfortable for him.

The blade had entered the soft strip cleanly. That was already more than the ordinary comparison had managed. But the end of the movement brought it against the plate before the cut could finish.

They had copied the curve.

The handle still expected a different hand.

* * *

The metal had taken work.

Wakana could point to where Senku had tested it before they committed the small end of the bar. He could point to the pieces which were no longer useful for a blade but still worth keeping, and to the first shape which had taught him why a drawing did not become an edge just because somebody wanted it to.

Now there was an edge.

He wrapped it before taking the handle apart.

Mai moved a strip of cloth out from under his elbow.

"I can hold that end," she said. ᴹᵃᶦ

"Thank you." ᵂ

She held it while he found the place where the grip had turned the blade too far.

* * *

Marin showed the movement again without the cutter.

The late, tired grip. The one Tamsin had used after being asked to stop demonstrating the ideal first cut of the morning.

Mai put her own hand beside it.

"I wouldn't want this digging in all day either." ᴹᵃᶦ

Wakana marked the place.

"If I take more off here, she'll have room to turn without pulling the edge toward herself." ᵂ

"Then let her turn it," Marin said. ᴹᵃʳ

They changed the handle.

Not the metal they had already spent hours persuading into the right shape. The part somebody would actually have to hold while making the blade do its job.

* * *

Senku came back while they were fitting the new grip.

He looked at the shortened bar, then at the separate tray where the Warden samples waited.

"Same test piece?" he asked. ˢᵉ

Wakana nodded toward the board.

"Hers. We haven't cut into the new plate." ᵂ

"Good. One answer at a time." ˢᵉ

Mai looked at him.

"From you?" ᴹᵃᶦ

Marin laughed.

Senku grinned and stayed to watch.

* * *

The second try went through.

Marin moved slowly enough that all three could see the end of the cut. The tough strip separated without the edge being dragged sideways against the plate.

She put the cutter down where nobody would reach across it by mistake.

For a moment they only looked at the little piece which had come away.

Then Wakana picked up the blade and inspected it.

The edge had held.

Mai turned the board toward the light.

"Again somewhere that wasn't already weakened," she said. ᴹᵃᶦ

They did that too.

* * *

The handle was ugly by the time it worked properly.

Different marks where they had changed their minds. A place which needed smoothing. Cloth where the final covering would go.

Wakana looked at it for longer than the problem required.

Marin leaned against the bench.

"You get to finish that part," she said. ᴹᵃʳ

"After she tries it." ᵂ

"You can make it comfortable before she tries it." ᴹᵃʳ

He considered that.

Mai took up the little piece of padding and held it against the grip.

"Make the one she'll bring back for another handle because she actually used it," she said. "Not the one nobody touches because it looks expensive." ᴹᵃᶦ

Wakana smiled.

He began smoothing the place beneath the thumb.

* * *

They packed the cutter with its drawing and the notes Tamsin had asked them to keep.

One working piece. The small fragment they had tested, marked to show the new cuts. A handle shaped by four sets of hands and one absent woman who had described what her day felt like when the tool did not work.

The rest of the bar stayed on the shelf, visibly shorter.

Marin asked Mai whether she wanted to come when they took it back.

Mai looked down at her own clean hands.

"Yes," she said. "I'd like to see what she does with it." ᴹᵃᶦ

They left room for her name on the list of people going.
