*When: Day531, morning. Tamsin holds the finished cutter, and Wakana has run out of useful things to do with his empty hands.*

Marin moved half a pace nearer without taking his arm.

Mai stood on his other side, looking at the grip as though she could persuade the tool to remember all their work by watching closely enough.

Hollis had checked the extra name before adding her to the visit. Tamsin had wanted to meet the person who kept asking where the handle would hurt after an hour.

Now that person was saying nothing.

Tamsin turned the cutter once, set the plate on its support and began.

* * *

The first cut was slow.

She watched the edge enter. Watched the place where her old tool had forced her wrist into the wrong angle. Wakana felt his own hand trying to make the movement with her and put it against his leg until it stopped.

The strip separated.

Tamsin did not look up.

She moved to another part of the plate and tried again.

This time she worked at the speed of somebody who actually needed to finish a job.

The cutter went through.

"Here," she said. ᵀᵃ

Wakana stepped forward before remembering that she had not asked him to take it.

She showed him the place beneath her thumb.

"A little more room there. Not enough to make it turn in my hand." ᵀᵃ

He nodded.

"I can do that." ᵂ

* * *

They made the small change at her bench.

Tamsin showed them where her tools were instead of leaving them to guess which ones were for the demonstration and which ones she trusted every day. Mai held the grip while Wakana smoothed the marked place. Marin checked it against the position Tamsin had shown.

Then they gave it back.

Tamsin worked for a while.

Long enough for Wakana to stop holding his breath between each cut. Long enough for Marin to ask about the fastenings on the work already hanging from the wall, and for Tamsin to answer without putting the cutter down to make the conversation easier.

When she finally inspected the edge, she held it where they could see.

It had kept its shape.

* * *

"I'd like this one," she said. ᵀᵃ

Wakana looked at the cutter.

Then at Hollis.

Hollis waited for him to answer.

"That's what we brought it for," Wakana said. ᵂ

They put the figures on the clear part of the bench.

Material used. Time. Fuel. The journey which had brought them there, and the people who made the journey possible. What they would keep for replacing the things they had used to make one working tool.

Tamsin read the total before speaking.

"I couldn't pay that six times and make the coats worth selling." ᵀᵃ

Wakana glanced at the one cutter.

"I don't know what six would cost yet. This one took the attempts before it. The next might be easier. It might also need something we haven't learned to repeat." ᵂ

She looked at him for a moment.

"Then don't let me order six because I'm pleased with the first." ᵀᵃ

He smiled, surprised by how relieved it made him.

"All right." ᵂ

* * *

Hollis counted the money when she put it down.

He counted it where everybody could see, compared it with the line they had agreed and put the note beside it before closing the small pouch.

The cutter stayed on Tamsin's bench.

For a moment Wakana felt the odd urge to tell her how to put it away.

He stopped himself.

It was hers now.

Mai noticed him looking and touched his sleeve.

"She knows which side is sharp," she murmured. ᴹᵃᶦ

Marin heard and had to turn away.

* * *

They returned the little test fragment too.

The new cuts were marked. The pieces which had come away were wrapped with it. Tamsin looked at the notes, asked one last question about the curve and kept the page beside the tool rather than filing it somewhere she would never reach while working.

"If you find more of the material, tell me," she said. "And if somebody brings me another thing the ordinary edges won't touch, I'll ask before letting them assume you've already solved it." ᵀᵃ

"Good," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

She glanced toward the door.

"Same way next time?" ᵀᵃ

"With notice. And guards who know what trip they're agreeing to." ᴴᵒ

She nodded.

Nobody had opened the whole world to them that morning.

One woman had made room at her bench and paid for something their hands had made.

* * *

Outside, Dunmore was checking the load before the return journey.

Hollis asked about the strap he had replaced. Dunmore showed him the new piece and the old one kept for comparison, then helped Mai find a place where the rolled drawings would not be crushed.

Marin stood beside Wakana while he looked back at the workshop door.

"You can look happy," she said. ᴹᵃʳ

"I am." ᵂ

"I know. I'm waiting for your face to find out." ᴹᵃʳ

He laughed.

She took his hand for the short walk back to the cart.

Behind them, from the open workshop, they could hear Tamsin begin another cut.
