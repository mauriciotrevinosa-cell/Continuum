*When: Day541, late morning. Frieren has asked him to show the landing before she lets him call the beginning a success.*

Mau looked at the grass.

"I'm still on it." ᴹ

"Then this part should be easy." ᶠ

Fern hid a smile by looking at the place they had marked beside him.

He remembered a cold gate, a carrying frame and the brief little hover he had managed before everybody sensibly took part of the load away. Remembering how that felt did not make this body's next attempt happen by itself.

He let the small flow gather.

The grass moved away from his boots.

Only a little.

"Now stop," Fern said. ᶠᵉ

He stopped.

The ground arrived sooner than he had expected.

* * *

"You let the whole shape go," she said. ᶠᵉ

Mau looked down at his feet.

"I was trying to stop rising." ᴹ

"Then stop rising. You don't have to stop everything which was holding you there." ᶠᵉ

She showed him with one hand, a slow change instead of a release.

Frieren watched him follow the motion.

"The part you keep fixing is the part you're letting become untidy first," she said. ᶠ

He could see it now.

Not because he suddenly had a vast amount of personal mana where the little flow had been. Because the work in his perception could keep the shape clear while he listened to what Fern was telling him to change.

He tried again.

This time he came down quietly.

* * *

On the next attempt, he stayed.

The space beneath his boots was small enough that the grass still looked close. He resisted the urge to measure success by making it farther away.

Fern asked him a question.

He answered without dropping.

Frieren walked round to see the angle from the side.

"Less correction," she said. ᶠ

Mau let the flow settle.

The little strain he had been bracing against eased.

For a moment he was simply there, above the ground, with enough attention left to notice that Fern was smiling now.

"That's better," she said. ᶠᵉ

He smiled back.

* * *

They let him rise a little higher after he had landed and rested.

About a meter. Enough to change where he looked at the world from, not enough to make falling an ambitious new activity.

He moved toward the marker.

Too quickly at first. He caught the error before trying to correct it with another equally large one. The next movement was smaller.

Fern stood beside the place he meant to stop.

He stopped there.

Then he came down, slow enough to feel the first blade of grass brush his boot.

Nobody caught him.

He looked at his teachers.

"That," he said. ᴹ

Frieren nodded.

"That." ᶠ

* * *

Ori arrived while he was resting.

Her compass was in her pocket this time. She had already found Mum and was more interested in the space Mau had left above the grass.

"Were you flying?" she asked. ᴼ

"A little." ᴹ

She looked at Frieren.

Then back at him.

"Not for hiding," she said. ᴼ

Mau glanced toward Frieren.

She was looking at a perfectly ordinary part of the sky.

"Is there a rule I should know?" he asked. ᴹ

"Yes." ᴼ

Ori explained it. Firmly.

He accepted the terms before attempting another lift.

* * *

Yuka came out to find them before the last try.

She watched from beside Fern rather than calling to him while he was holding the shape. Mau moved to the marker, paused and came down where he had meant to.

Yuka smiled.

"That looks considerably more useful than falling onto Kaisel." ʸᵏ

"Less dramatic." ᴹ

"We can survive that." ʸᵏ

He laughed.

Fern passed him the water. Frieren looked at her over the cup he had taken.

"That was a good way to explain the stopping," she said. ᶠ

Fern's fingers paused on the cloth.

"He understood it." ᶠᵉ

"Yes," Frieren said. ᶠ

She let the praise remain that simple.

Fern looked pleased for longer than she meant to show it.

* * *

Yuka asked about carrying someone while they put the markers away.

Mau looked at the space she had chosen beside his arm.

"Not before we've tried a load that won't complain if I put it down badly." ᴹ

"I will complain." ʸᵏ

"I was counting on it." ᴹ

She helped him gather the cloth.

Ori had got Frieren to confirm the rule about hiding. Fern was asking what part of the next lesson would be useful to practise before they tried distance.

Mau listened while they walked toward the house.

He had crossed enormous spaces on borrowed wings. Broken things he could not have imagined lifting when he first came here.

The little clear space beneath his own feet stayed in his thoughts all the way back.
