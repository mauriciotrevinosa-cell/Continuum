The table still smelled faintly of worked wood.

Hollis put the wrapped return on one side and the papers on the other. Maomao looked at the knot before touching it.

"Anything leaking?" she asked. ᴹᵃᵒ

"Not that one." ᴴᵒ

She looked up.

He pointed at the ordinary supply jar in the cart.

"Different problem. Lid's loose. I kept it upright." ᴴᵒ

"Then let's keep doing that." ᴹᵃᵒ

Jinshi made a place for the jar too.

The table had already begun paying for the space it occupied.

* * *

The return was Tamsin's drawing, changed in two places where using the cutter had given her another question.

She still had the tool. It was still doing the work she had bought it for.

Hollis put that sentence before the rest of the report.

Jinshi read it twice, then reached for a clean page.

"Another one?" he asked. ᴶ

"A question about another one. She knows somebody who saw hers work. They want to know what we'd need before saying yes." ᴴᵒ

Maomao leaned over the little sketch.

"So now they know which problem they want solved." ᴹᵃᵒ

"That part helps," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

They set the request apart from anything already ordered.

Wakana would need to see it. So would the people deciding what material they could spare without counting an unvisited gate as a cupboard they owned.

* * *

The second paper was about seeds.

Hollis had carried their questions onward during the trip, along with the ordinary errands which had kept his route alive before anybody had thought of selling a gate-made cutter.

Someone with a farm along that route was willing to talk.

Not everything in the list grew there. Some things had a season. Some could be stored. Two of the names meant different plants depending on whom Hollis asked, which was why he had written down the description instead of returning with whichever packet looked most convincing.

Maomao read that part first.

"Good." ᴹᵃᵒ

"I was hoping you'd say that." ᴴᵒ

"I was going to ask what you thought you were buying." ᴹᵃᵒ

He smiled.

"So was I." ᴴᵒ

* * *

Mau came past on his way back from the tea.

He stopped when he saw the two papers, waited while Jinshi finished the line he was writing and asked which part wanted an answer from him.

"The next visit," Jinshi said. "After the people who need the information decide what they want to ask." ᴶ

Mau read the farm note.

He could hear Daphne asking about the season, about what had failed, about the ground nobody had thought to describe beneath the picture of the fruit.

"I'd like to go," he said. "Daphne might too. I'll ask her, and we can bring the questions instead of making you carry another version of them back and forth." ᴹ

Hollis nodded toward the road.

"Not today. I'd like to tell them when to expect us, and the guards haven't agreed to an extra journey because the afternoon suddenly looks free." ᴴᵒ

"Not today," Mau agreed. ᴹ

* * *

Jinshi kept the two requests separate.

One belonged to the workshop first. One needed Daphne, the kitchen and somebody who would ask about the plants rather than only the price of a packet.

Maomao took that one to copy the names which might otherwise become three different crops by the time they reached the garden.

Mau stayed to help Hollis take the jar down.

It was still upright. Still not leaking.

They carried it to the place Momo had asked them to use, leaving the papers dry on the table made for them.

When they came back, Jinshi was talking to Wakana rather than waiting for somebody to tell him whether he was allowed to begin.

Mau went to find Daphne.
