*When: Day553, midday. Daphne has found the thing which does not fit the field.*

Ilen made them walk around the planted bed.

Not through it, although the space between the rows was wide enough for Mau's feet.

She put her basket down by the fence and handed Daphne a little fork with a worn wooden handle.

"There. Beside the end one. Don't pull it." ᴵˡ

Daphne knelt.

The top layer came apart easily. Beneath it, the pale soil held together in lumps which looked very little like the dark earth inside their seedling trays at home.

She loosened the edge, lifted it with two fingers and pressed it.

"You didn't replace the bed." ᴰᵃ

"Couldn't afford to. And this wasn't where I wanted it." ᴵˡ

Ilen pointed toward the slope beyond the house. From here, Mau could see an old line of stakes at the edge of a rougher patch.

"That was better ground. The water took it." ᴵˡ

"So you put this here." ᴰᵃ

"I put three rows here. Two died. This is what I planted after I remembered I wasn't fourteen anymore." ᴵˡ

She took back the fork and laid the soil down carefully.

Yuka crouched beside Daphne, keeping her sleeve clear of the plants.

"What did you remember?" ʸᵏ

"Something my aunt was very tired of teaching me." ᴵˡ

* * *

There was no buried stone beneath the plant. No crystal. Ilen let Daphne check the shallow edge, then showed her where the roots had entered the earth below.

Mau could see another movement there.

It was faint. Without looking for it he might have mistaken it for part of the field, the old, small work which remained after somebody stopped standing over a spell.

He did not follow it into Ilen.

"Is that yours?" he asked. ᴹ

She looked at him.

"You can see it?" ᴵˡ

"Some of it. I'm not sure what you're doing." ᴹ

"Now? Nothing. That would be a poor way to spend my days." ᴵˡ

She stood up and brushed the soil from her knees.

"There's a pot I haven't done yet. You can look at that, if watching is how you learn." ᴵˡ

He thanked her before moving.

* * *

The pot waited in the shade of the house with a drooping transplant in it.

Ilen showed them the roots first. A little soil clung to them, dark and fine beside the heavier earth she had brought for the pot.

Daphne asked how long it had been out of its bed. Then about the water. Then whether Ilen meant to leave it in that pot.

"No. But I can move a pot into the shade without asking my nephew to pick up half a field." ᴵˡ

Yuka put a hand over her mouth.

Mau did not look at her.

He was listening to the roots.

Ilen lowered them into place and tucked the earth around them with both hands. She spoke a few words under her breath, once, and held her fingers still.

The little movement of mana changed.

It did not pour into the leaves or force the drooping stem upright. It spread around the place where the old soil met the new, briefly steady enough for Mau to make out where it ended.

Then she took her hands away.

The plant still drooped.

Yuka leaned closer.

"Did it work?" ʸᵏ

"Ask me tomorrow." ᴵˡ

Mau laughed before he could help it.

Ilen looked at him, then at his hands.

"What were you expecting?" ᴵˡ

"I don't know. Something less patient." ᴹ

"Try growing onions." ᴵˡ

* * *

They ate at the little table beside the back door.

Hollis's guards took turns rather than both sitting down. Ilen passed bread, a dish of cooked roots and a small pot of something she told Yuka to try before putting it on everything.

Yuka looked at Mau.

"We learned that one." ʸᵏ

"He did," Mau said. "Mostly me." ᴹ

Daphne was still asking questions.

Not whether there would be food. That question had filled too many tables already. She wanted to know whether this would let them use the patches which had not been worth asking people to dig again.

"Clay?" she asked. ᴰᵃ

"That field." ᴵˡ

"Stony ground?" ᴰᵃ

"My aunt grew beans where I couldn't put a stake in without hitting something. She still carried out the stones she could lift." ᴵˡ

"And ground you haven't tried?" ᴰᵃ

Ilen tore her bread.

"Then I haven't tried it." ᴵˡ

Daphne nodded, as if that answer pleased her more than a promise would have.

Ilen called it settling. Her aunt had used another name, but she had been particular about all sorts of things, including which niece was allowed to carry which knife.

"We don't make the soil into different soil," she said. "We help the plant take to what is there. Enough to begin. After that it still has to live in it." ᴵˡ

"Water," Daphne said. ᴰᵃ

"Water. Food for the ground. Something to keep the goats out. If you know a word that handles all three, you can teach me." ᴵˡ

"Winter?" ᴰᵃ

Ilen shook her head.

"I can make a plant take root in the wrong place. I can't persuade it that the sun stayed up." ᴵˡ

Daphne looked down at the page beside her plate.

She crossed out nothing.

Instead she wrote *small glass first* beside one of the descriptions, and moved another farther down the list.

Mau thought of the greenhouse against their barn. Of Daphne looking at something so small it would have vanished beneath his shoe if he had not stopped outside the door.

Now she was dividing a page into what could be tried and what needed to wait.

He wanted to bring this home.

* * *

"Would you teach me?" he asked. ᴹ

Ilen looked at the bags beside Hollis's chair.

Then she looked at him again.

"Do you garden?" ᴵˡ

"Badly, compared to her." ᴹ

Daphne did not disagree.

"I can work with mana," he added. "Not very much of my own. Enough to start learning, I hope. If you'd rather not, that's all right." ᴹ

Ilen set her bread down.

"My aunt taught three of us. Only two could do anything useful with it. The third had more sense and made baskets." ᴵˡ

"I could ask about baskets too," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Ilen smiled properly this time.

Then she asked Daphne what grew beneath the glass at their place.

Daphne answered with names and quantities, not a picture of an enormous garden they did not have. There were things she had kept back for seed. A little of one could be spared if Ilen wanted to try it.

"I'd like to see that," Ilen said. ᴵˡ

"We can bring a small amount. I won't promise the whole bed before I've counted it." ᴰᵃ

"Good. Neither will I." ᴵˡ

Mau reached toward the money Hollis had helped them set aside for supplies.

Ilen held up a hand.

"The seeds have a price. We'll count those when we choose them. You can pay me for showing you this by helping me move the pots and not telling the next person that I taught you to grow dinner out of a stone." ᴵˡ

He stopped reaching.

"I won't." ᴹ

"And if you manage it," she added, "you tell me what you did." ᴵˡ

He grinned.

"That too." ᴹ

* * *

After the plates were cleared, Ilen brought out another pot.

She did not put it in front of him immediately.

"Show me how you start," she said. ᴵˡ

Mau rested his fingers against the earth.

He knew how to float a body now. How to hold a force still enough for somebody else to put a hand near it. None of that told him what a root needed to feel.

He let a small flow gather.

The earth shifted against his fingertips.

Ilen caught his wrist.

Not frightened. Simply stopping a hand before it tipped over a cup.

"No. You're trying to pick it up." ᴵˡ

Yuka made a sound behind him.

He looked back.

She was doing a very poor job of looking innocent.

"Don't," he said. ᴹ

"I didn't say anything." ʸᵏ

Ilen moved his fingers closer to the stem.

"We can begin with leaving the pot on the table." ᴵˡ

He looked down at the little plant and began again.
