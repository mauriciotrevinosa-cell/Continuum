*When: Day558, afternoon; then Day563, morning. Daphne has let the new plant remain a plant for five whole days.*

It had not died.

That was a useful beginning, although not the same as a crop.

Daphne moved the pot from the shaded place where she had kept it after the journey and put it beside the shallow tray waiting for Mau.

He bent to look.

"That's better, isn't it?" ᴹ

"It is alive," she said. ᴰᵃ

"I was hoping for a little more enthusiasm." ᴹ

"Then keep it alive." ᴰᵃ

She gave him the smaller pot.

* * *

There were two young plants from the same tray, close enough in size for Daphne to choose them without having to make an excuse for either one.

She had prepared the soil from a patch they knew at home. Not the best of the three mixtures under the greenhouse glass. The ground she wanted to know whether they could use without carrying the best of somewhere else to it, basket after basket.

Mau put his fingers where Ilen had shown him.

Daphne stopped his other hand before he pressed too much earth against the stem.

He looked at her.

"That isn't the spell," she said. "That's you burying it." ᴰᵃ

He loosened the soil again.

"Sorry." ᴹ

"Now." ᴰᵃ

The little flow gathered more quietly than it had on Ilen's table. He knew what picking up the pot felt like now, which was a helpful thing not to do.

For a moment the trace thinned at the edge.

He waited, adjusted where he was feeding it and stopped trying to hold the whole shape rigid.

It settled against the roots.

Mau took his hands away.

Daphne waited with him until he was certain he had stopped supplying it.

Then she put a marked stake beside that pot and a plain one beside the other.

"Same place," she said. "Same water. Leave them." ᴰᵃ

He looked at the two small plants.

"How long?" ᴹ

"Long enough for you to do something else." ᴰᵃ

* * *

On the fifth morning, she called him back.

Mau arrived with Yuka, who had been standing beside him when Daphne found him. Neither tried to make the greenhouse door wider than it was by going through at the same time.

Daphne put the pots on the low work surface and tipped the first one into her hand.

Yuka made an involuntary movement toward it.

"I'm holding it," Daphne said. ᴰᵃ

"I know. Sorry." ʸᵏ

The little root mass rested in Daphne's palm. She turned it just enough to show them the edge, then put it back before either could decide the interesting part was permission to keep it in the air.

The second came out more reluctantly.

Mau saw where the new pale threads had reached beyond the dark earth which had begun in the tray. More of the heavier soil came away with them.

He leaned closer.

"There," he said. ᴹ

Daphne let him look, then returned that one too.

"Yes." ᴰᵃ

He looked at her face.

She was smiling.

* * *

Yuka held the marked stake while Daphne checked that the root ball had gone back without folding underneath itself.

"Can we plant the rest?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"A small bed," Daphne said. "Not everything I want to eat." ᴰᵃ

"How small?" Mau asked. ᴹ

Daphne led them outside.

She showed them a strip near the existing work, far enough from the tree's roots to leave that boundary out of the argument altogether. It had light. It had a path beside it where somebody carrying water would not have to tread on the thing they were keeping alive.

It was considerably smaller than Mau had pictured.

He looked at Daphne.

"We can do more than that." ᴹ

"If this works, we will." ᴰᵃ

She put the little pots down.

"I know how much work I have before I ask for a new one." ᴰᵃ

Mau looked along the narrow strip again.

Enough plants to notice a failure. Few enough to move if they had misunderstood something important.

"All right," he said. "Show me where you want the first one." ᴹ

* * *

By lunch, people had already asked twice whether the new vegetables were ready.

Daphne took her bowl to the table and sat down before answering the third person.

"No." ᴰᵃ

Milim looked disappointed.

"Then what's that?" she asked, pointing. ᴹⁱ

"Something we bought. From a woman who did not grow it since breakfast." ᴰᵃ

Momo set down the dish between them.

The roots Ilen had sold them had cooked slowly enough to take on the flavor around them. Rem had cut the pieces small without losing all their shape. There was another bowl for the people who wanted the stronger seasoning after they had tried the first.

Yuka reached toward it.

Then she looked at Mau.

He shook his head.

"I learned something on that trip." ᴹ

She put a little on her own food and did not offer him a spoonful to prove that she had been braver.

Across the table, Ram said something to Rem about the size of the pieces. Rem answered without looking away from the person asking for more.

Daphne ate while her food was warm.

The morning's barn count could wait where she had left it with Fern and Jinshi. Neither of them needed her to stand beside the sacks in order for an onion to stay where it had been put.

Mau turned toward her.

"What should we tell Ilen?" ᴹ

"What happened," Daphne said. "And send back the cloth." ᴰᵃ

Yuka smiled into her bowl.

Outside, the little plants remained exactly where they had put them.

There would be something else to do before dinner.
