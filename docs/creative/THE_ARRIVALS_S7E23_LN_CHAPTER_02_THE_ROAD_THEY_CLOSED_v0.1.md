*When: Day564, afternoon. Hollis has been told there is trouble at the cutting, which usually means he will need another wheel.*

There were three carts before his.

None had a broken wheel.

That was the first thing he disliked.

The second was the rope across the road. It had been tied to a post on one side and a fence on the other, at a height which could stop a tired horse before the driver had time to pretend he had not seen it.

Hollis drew up behind the last cart.

One guard stayed with the load. The other went with him to ask.

"Road's closed," said the man beside the rope. ᵍ

"I saw. How far?" ᴴᵒ

The man pointed down the cutting.

Hollis looked past him.

At first he saw only the line of exposed stone where the road narrowed. Then the space between two darker faces moved, and blue ran across it without lighting the ground in front of it.

He stopped trying to see around the guard.

"How long has that been there?" ᴴᵒ

"Since this morning. Far as anybody will tell me." ᵍ

"Did you see it arrive?" ᴴᵒ

"No." ᵍ

The answer helped Hollis decide what not to repeat as something the man had watched.

* * *

The first cart belonged to somebody with a delivery which was supposed to have been unloaded before noon.

He explained that several times.

The rope did not move.

One woman said there was another way around. The driver beside her said the cart would never make the slope. Someone suggested taking off half the load, carrying it past and bringing the cart back empty, which was the sort of suggestion people made while looking at somebody else's sacks.

Hollis went back to his own horse.

He could not see the blue from here. He knew where it was now, though, and kept finding the place every time the wind moved something in front of it.

His guard waited beside the cart.

"What is it?" he asked. ᵍ

"I don't know enough yet." ᴴᵒ

"You know somebody who might." ᵍ

Hollis looked at him.

"That doesn't make this road open." ᴴᵒ

* * *

He saw one man come back from nearer the cutting.

The man wore the same rough badge as the guard at the rope. He walked carefully, watching the road behind him. There was dirt on one knee but no blood Hollis could see.

The waiting drivers began talking before he reached them.

The guard at the rope made them stop.

"Nothing came out while I was there," the returning man said. ᵍ

"Can we go round it?" asked the first driver. ᵈ

The man looked at him for a long moment.

"Not with the cart." ᵍ

That started the argument again.

Hollis let it pass him. When there was room to ask something which had not already been answered, he asked whether anyone had gone into it.

The guard shook his head.

"Not from here." ᵍ

Hollis wrote that down as carefully as the rest.

* * *

He took out the page for the journey and drew the bend, the fence and the cutting.

Not a fine map. Enough to distinguish this road from the one a frightened person might describe after being told twice that the light was somewhere else.

He put a mark where he had stood.

Then he put the time beside it, as closely as he could tell.

One of the other drivers watched him.

"Who are you writing to?" ᵈ

"People who will ask where I was when I saw it." ᴴᵒ

"Somebody who can get rid of it?" ᵈ

Hollis looked toward the cutting again.

"Somebody I mean to ask. That's all I've promised." ᴴᵒ

* * *

They turned back before the light changed.

One of the waiting drivers called after him, asking whether that meant he knew something the rest did not.

Hollis tightened the strap over the load which had not been delivered.

"It means I know how long it takes this horse to get to the next place we can stop." ᴴᵒ

The driver looked at the road.

Then at the sky.

He began checking his own harness.

Hollis's guard walked on the side of the cart nearest the cutting until they passed the bend and could no longer see it.

The page was inside Hollis's coat. The delivery was still behind him. He would have to speak to the person expecting it before deciding how much of this journey he could change.

For the first time that afternoon, he was glad he had not told anyone the road would be clear tomorrow.
