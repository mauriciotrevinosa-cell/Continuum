The guard kept looking at it.

Mau saw the question coming before the man found a way to ask it.

"It's still there," the guard said. ᵍ

"We found them in the first part," Mau said. "We haven't seen where it ends." ᴹ

"But you could—" ᵍ

Anko came back from the cart, wiping her hands on a cloth.

"We could do a great many things badly this afternoon. That doesn't mean we agreed to." ᴬⁿ

The guard looked toward the injured man.

His leg was still supported. His friends were still beside him. None of those facts required the blue to disappear for them to matter.

"What do I tell the next cart?" he asked. ᵍ

"Keep them back," Mikasa said. "The first room isn't the whole place." ᴹⁱᵏ

* * *

Mau wanted to return to the chamber before they left.

He told Anko and Mikasa why before asking Igris to go with him. Eight creatures. A part of the route already checked. No going farther because something interesting might be around the next corner.

Mikasa considered the passage he pointed toward.

"I'll stay at the turn," she said. ᴹⁱᵏ

Anko stayed outside with the people who still needed her.

Ciel carried the short plan to Rimuru. Hollis would take the injured man to the local place arranged by the guards before bringing the cart back toward home; their return would be later than the first estimate. Mau waited for the acknowledgement before stepping through.

Igris went first.

The chamber remained quiet.

* * *

The bodies lay where the fight had left them.

Mau counted before doing anything with the shadows beneath them. The one beside the shield. The two near the low opening. The others along the places Igris and Mikasa had kept clear of the men they had found.

Eight.

He had once imagined *more* as something so large that a number would barely seem worth keeping.

Now he looked at the way the two old soldiers had carried a man without making Anko leave his leg unsupported.

Eight could be a road watched while somebody slept.

Or a cart pulled out before the wheel broke.

He drew on the reserve beneath the call, enough to begin the work of making these shadows his rather than merely summoning something which already belonged there.

"Arise." ᴹ

* * *

They came up differently from Igris.

No old dignity waiting for a place to stand. No voice making a speech about the honor of the room.

Eight shapes found the space above their own feet and turned toward him.

One still held its shield at the angle it had used to force the gap. Mau pointed toward the clear floor instead.

"There." ᴹ

It lowered the shield and moved.

The others followed when he asked them to.

He let himself finish that first instruction before checking the number.

One hundred and ten ordinary units now. Sixteen named shadows beside them. Mahoraga belonged to a different part of him and had not suddenly become the next number on that line.

He closed the display without opening the points he was still keeping.

"Help us carry what we're taking," he said. "Then you come home." ᴹ

* * *

They collected eight small crystals from the cleared room.

Not a reward chest. Not the thing which ended the gate. Small pieces which had to be picked up, checked and wrapped rather than assumed to have arrived somewhere in Mau's inventory because he had won a fight.

He kept them in a separate bag from the food.

Igris watched the darker passage while the last of the new soldiers joined the line toward the opening.

Mau looked once more at the low space where the men had held the tool.

Then he went back to the road.

* * *

The guard had not been expecting eight more soldiers.

Mau stopped them clear of the cart and explained what he had brought out.

The woman beside her brother watched one lower its shield to pass a narrow space without scraping the wheel.

"Those were in there?" she asked. ʷ

"They were attacking the room where we found them. They aren't doing that now." ᴹ

She looked at the soldier.

Then she returned to making her brother drink the water he had been holding without touching.

Mau did not ask her to decide what else she thought of him before she had finished doing that.

* * *

Hollis came over while the injured man was being made comfortable for the short journey to care.

"The roadkeeper wants a proper account," he said. ᴴᵒ

Mau looked at the man by the rope.

"He's not the one?" ᴹ

"He has the rope. Someone else gets to hear why he put it there." ᴴᵒ

"Do they want me to speak to them?" ᴹ

"They'll be asked. I won't put another day's work in your mouth while you're still here doing this one." ᴴᵒ

Mau smiled.

"Tell them I'd like to. With someone from home when we've agreed on what we can offer. They should hear from the people they'd be asking to walk through it." ᴹ

Hollis nodded.

"And the road?" ᴴᵒ

"If they want a visit, they agree we can arrive for it. People, bags, questions. No one deciding halfway through that the work is welcome but the person isn't." ᴹ

Hollis looked toward the guard.

The man was holding the cart steady while a soldier waited for Anko to finish moving the splint.

"That part may be easier than it was this morning," Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

"Good." ᴹ

"It still isn't everyone." ᴴᵒ

Mau looked beyond the bend to the ordinary stretch of road they would take home.

"I know." ᴹ

* * *

Before the cart moved, the woman came to find him.

Her brother was sitting with both feet on the ground. The hand she had described lay against his knee where she could see it.

"Thank you," she said. ʷ

Mau had been preparing an answer to a question about the opening.

For a moment he did not have one for that.

"You're welcome," he said at last. ᴹ

She nodded once and went back to her brother.

Mikasa took the wrapped crystals from where he had set them down and checked the knot.

Anko handed him the bag with the part of lunch he had not eaten.

"Now," she said. ᴬⁿ

He accepted both instructions.

Behind them, the rope stayed across the road. The guard stood beside it, watching the light instead of watching the people who had come to help.
