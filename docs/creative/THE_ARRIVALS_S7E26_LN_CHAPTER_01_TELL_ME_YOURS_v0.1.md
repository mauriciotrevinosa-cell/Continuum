He saw her first.

She stood beside the table, one hand resting on the back of a chair, and waited until he came close enough to put the road down for a moment.

"Late," she said. ʸᵏ

"I know. Did you get the message?" ᴹ

She nodded and came into his arms.

He held her without trying to keep one hand free for the strap on his shoulder.

After a while she reached past him and took it off herself.

"Eat." ʸᵏ

He looked at the bowl.

"That sounds familiar." ᴹ

"People keep having good ideas around you." ʸᵏ

* * *

He told her about the cup.

Not the eight bodies first. Not the different way the light had looked from inside the road. A man tapping metal against stone because he had nothing left which might reach someone beyond the bend.

Yuka pulled the other chair closer.

"He could hear you?" ʸᵏ

"I don't know. He kept doing it until we got there." ᴹ

She was quiet for a moment.

"All three got out?" ʸᵏ

"All three. One couldn't walk. Anko kept his leg still, and we took him to people who could look after it." ᴹ

"Not here?" ʸᵏ

"His own people. The woman who was waiting had already found somebody before we reached the cart." ᴹ

Yuka nodded.

He ate another spoonful and found that he was hungry enough to need the next one before finishing the story.

She waited.

* * *

He told her about the first room and where Igris had stood. About Mikasa finding an opening he had not seen quickly enough, and the man who thought his sister might be the worst part of coming back through the blue.

Yuka laughed at that, then asked whether the man had been right.

"He was still alive when we left." ᴹ

"That isn't an answer." ʸᵏ

"She had a lot of questions." ᴹ

Yuka looked at the wrapped bag by the chair.

"So did I." ʸᵏ

He put down the spoon.

She touched his wrist before he could begin preparing for a different conversation.

"Not that kind. I wanted to hear how it went. You told me." ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her hand.

Then he turned his own over and held it.

* * *

"Now yours," he said. ᴹ

She looked pleased enough that he knew she had been saving part of it.

The turn had been wider. Yuta had put the second mark farther away without telling her to reach it in one try. She had come down short on the first pass and landed before Nue had finished folding its near wing.

"He caught the strap?" Mau asked. ᴹ

"No. He told me to stop grabbing the side I wanted to turn toward." ʸᵏ

Mau pictured it.

Yuka saw his mouth move.

"Don't." ʸᵏ

"I haven't said anything." ᴹ

"You're thinking it very loudly." ʸᵏ

He laughed.

She tried to keep her own face stern and failed.

"The next one worked. I let it finish the turn before asking it to come down." ʸᵏ

"All the way to the second mark?" ᴹ

She nodded.

He leaned over and kissed her.

Yuka kept his hand when he moved back.

"You still have to come and see it." ʸᵏ

"I want to." ᴹ

* * *

Rimuru came down while Mau was finishing the bowl.

He looked at the two of them, took the seat which did not require either to let go and put Hollis's drawing beside the now-empty plate.

"We shouldn't leave this waiting on a long conversation about who owes somebody a visit," he said. ᴿ

Mau nodded.

They knew enough about gates to be careful of the part they had not seen. Nothing had followed the men out today. That did not make the opening a permanent, harmless addition to the road.

"Tomorrow," Mau said. "If we can get the people and things together. We go back ready to look farther, not just hope it stays like this while the messages travel." ᴹ

"That's what I wanted to ask," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Yuka looked from him to Mau.

"Then ask me too. I know what I want to practise tomorrow, and I can tell you whether I want to change it." ʸᵏ

Rimuru smiled.

"All right. Would you like to come help us plan what goes back to the road?" ᴿ

She stood, taking Mau's empty bowl.

"After he puts this away." ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

She handed it to him.

"I brought it here. You can take it that far." ʸᵏ

He rose with her, warm food behind him and her hand finding his free one again before they reached the kitchen.
