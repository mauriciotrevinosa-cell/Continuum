The head was wrapped separately from the handle.

Not because the axe was broken. The edge had been fitted into a shaped cover, dark against the folded cloth beneath it. Stark lifted the handle first and felt where the balance would settle when the whole thing left the box.

He put it down again.

Yuta looked at him.

"You can pick it up." ʸᵗ

"I know. I wanted to see what I was picking up." ˢᵗ

Yuka smiled.

Mau had stopped beside the open lid.

"There's a description," he said. ᴹ

* * *

He asked Ciel to look at the item with him.

Not the exchange. Not a collection claim. Nothing which would turn an object left on a table into money he had accidentally agreed could be spent.

The field which answered belonged to the axe.

*Reinforced Battle Axe. C.*

Below it, a line about striking damage while wielded. Twelve percent above the user's corresponding strike without that added effect.

Mau read the words aloud.

Stark looked at the weapon again.

"While I'm holding it?" ˢᵗ

"While you're using it. It doesn't say your body changes when you put it away." ᴹ

Yuta kept his hand off the edge.

"Then we check what it does before trusting the number near somebody." ʸᵗ

Mau nodded.

"It isn't the same as testing it." ᴹ

Stark lifted it clear of the box.

The balance was different from the axe lying beside his pack. He turned his wrists a little, stopped before the head reached anything and lowered it.

He wanted a place where he could try the next movement without pretending a narrow chamber was a practice field.

* * *

The return route still needed checking.

Igris went with two soldiers while the others brought the cart forward. Yuka rested Nue and let her dog watch the open passage. Yuta checked the loose stone above the doorway which Mau had kept from blocking their way.

Nothing followed the sound of the wheels.

Mau waited for Igris's return before beginning the extraction.

Twelve from the two passages. The larger body in the chamber, its opened shell no longer moving with a breath.

The call took work. New shapes did not become old soldiers merely because he could count how many there would be if it succeeded.

He let the reserve answer, held the pattern where it needed holding and gave the word.

* * *

The burrower came up last.

Yuka took one step back before deciding she had meant to do it.

Mau looked at the creature, then at the passage they had to use.

"Not through there," he told it. "You wait with me until I call." ᴹ

The great head lowered.

Its shadow went away without taking the floor beneath it along.

Yuka looked at the place where it had stood.

"That one's not coming into the kitchen." ʸᵏ

"Agreed," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Mau closed the count after making sure the twelve smaller shapes had returned with it.

One hundred and twenty-three ordinary units. Sixteen named shadows. No new name given because the largest thing in a room had looked as if it expected one.

The level and the points he was holding had not moved.

He put the Window away and helped Stark settle the axe into the cart.

* * *

They carried twelve small crystals and one larger one from the cleared part of the gate, wrapped apart from the pieces brought out yesterday.

Two sound pieces of shell went with the samples. Mau left the shattered material rather than spending the rest of the afternoon persuading every fragment to become something somebody might want at home.

Yuka checked the bag beneath the axle before Igris began moving the cart.

"Lunch first," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

She pointed at the place he had put the food instead of the treasure.

"Before we carry all of this up the road and you remember you had something to eat." ʸᵏ

Stark sat down immediately.

Yuta followed him.

Mau looked at Igris.

The knight moved the cart out of the way and waited.

Mau sat.

* * *

The guard saw them come out with the same people who had gone in.

He counted anyway.

Yuka noticed and waited until he finished before telling him nobody else was behind the cart.

Igris had brought the last of the soldiers across. Mau checked the threshold once more, then called them to rest where they would no longer crowd the cutting.

The blue thinned.

For a moment Stark could see the stone behind it without being certain whether the light was still there.

Then the road was only a road.

The guard did not move the rope immediately.

He walked to the place the opening had occupied, stopped short and looked at the rock from two sides.

Only then did he wave the waiting cart forward.

* * *

It went slowly.

One wheel caught in an old rut which had nothing to do with the gate. The driver swore. His companion got down, shifted a stone beneath it and told him to stop pulling before he broke something they could not replace today.

Stark watched them finish arguing and continue.

Yuka laughed softly beside him.

"What?" he asked. ˢᵗ

"Nothing. I like that that's the problem again." ʸᵏ

Mau looked at the ordinary wheel marks crossing the place where the blue had been.

Then he looked at the guard.

"If another one appears, don't send three people through with a rope and hope it tells you everything." ᴹ

The man gave him a tired look.

"I'll be saying that to the next person who suggests it." ᵍ

He asked what to put in the report.

Mau gave him the names of the people who had gone through together. Not only his own.

* * *

At home, Stark carried both axes to the workshop.

Wakana looked at the one he had rebound, then at the covered head beside it.

Stark took a breath.

"The binding held," he said. ˢᵗ

Wakana waited.

"I got the blade stuck. Had to change the whole angle to pull it free. It didn't shift under my hand once." ˢᵗ

Wakana's shoulders loosened.

"Good." ᵂ

Stark set the new axe down where it would not damage the surface.

"Can you help me check this one too?" ˢᵗ

Wakana took a clean cloth and cleared room beside the old handle rather than putting it away as if the arrival of something better had made yesterday's work embarrassing.

"Tomorrow," he said. "Tell me what the problem was first." ᵂ

Stark pulled up a chair.

Outside, Yuka was telling Fern the names Mau had given the guard. Rimuru had the new crystal bag apart from the other lots, with no money spent merely because it had reached the table.

The common room smelled of dinner.

Stark stayed long enough to show Wakana where the shell had caught his axe, then went to wash his hands before they put the food out.
