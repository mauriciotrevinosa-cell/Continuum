"Again," he said. ˢᵉⁿ

Stark lowered the new axe.

"Was something wrong with that one?" ˢᵗ

"Nothing. That's why I want another." ˢᵉⁿ

Wakana gave him the old axe while Byakuya checked that the next piece sat at the same angle. They had stopped using the largest cuts. Stark could have broken those with either weapon, which was satisfying and not especially useful to the question.

Now he was repeating a shorter movement, trying to give both handles the same beginning.

The old binding lay comfortably beneath his palms.

He struck.

Senku bent over the mark left by the support, then looked at Mau.

"What did you see?" ˢᵉⁿ

* * *

Mau told him where Stark's shoulder had moved differently.

Not a verdict. An observation they could use on the next try.

Mini Ciel kept the earlier movement available while he watched the new one. He did not have to leave the field to remember it. He could see the small difference in the wrists and still hear Wakana asking whether the grip wanted another turn of cloth.

Stark switched weapons again.

This time the beginning matched more closely.

The new head reached the wood with something added to the strike. Not a secret change in the body holding it. A brief work of energy in the weapon itself, completing where the edge met resistance.

Mau pointed it out.

Senku leaned closer without putting his hand anywhere near the next piece.

"That part again." ˢᵉⁿ

Stark sighed.

Byakuya grinned at him.

"He's enjoying himself. That's usually when you get to do it six more times." ᴮʸ

* * *

They did not prove the inscription down to its last fraction that morning.

They proved that something happened at contact, that it stopped when the weapon was put aside, and that Stark could not treat the two balances as the same because a line had promised him a better number.

Senku was pleased enough to write *useful* and displeased enough to put a large mark beside *twelve*.

Stark read both.

"So I can use it?" ˢᵗ

"You can practise with it," Senku said. "Which is what you wanted to do before I improved your morning." ˢᵉⁿ

Wakana offered the adjusted grip.

Stark took it, tested where his thumb sat and smiled.

Mau watched him.

Better equipment. A movement corrected rather than a wound paid for. The right kind of growing.

*Now you will grow.*

* * *

The words arrived with a room around them.

Grey stone. A long hand which had not asked. The cold, second beat finding a place inside his chest as if there had always been space waiting for somebody else.

Mau looked down.

Both hands were empty.

His feet remained on the grass. He could hear Stark asking Wakana whether he could keep the old cover for the old axe.

The voice had not spoken here.

He knew that.

His body took longer.

*Mau?*

Ciel's voice came from the present.

He took a breath which stopped short and tried another.

*A memory. Give me a moment.*

She did not open it to see which one.

*I'm here.*

He put one hand against the side of the bench until the cold beneath his breastbone stopped being the only thing he could feel.

* * *

Stark brought the axe over.

He did not notice at first. He was showing Mau the place where the new wrapping would need to sit if he changed from the longer swing to the shorter one.

Then he stopped.

"You all right?" ˢᵗ

Mau looked up.

He could have answered too quickly. He had done that often enough to know what it would sound like.

"Something caught up with me. Not the axe." ᴹ

Stark looked toward the bench.

"Sit?" ˢᵗ

Mau nodded.

Stark sat beside him, leaving the covered blade on the far side, and went back to explaining the grip without making the explanation require an answer.

The second beat continued beneath Mau's ribs.

He did not know whether getting better was bringing him closer to something waiting for him.

He knew the question was not an answer.

For now, he watched Stark's thumb find the improved place on the handle and let his own hand remain on the bench.
