*When: Day588. The roadkeeper has asked for Mau and has been told who is coming instead.*

She read the names again after Rimuru sat down.

"He's not coming?" ʳᵏ

"Not today. One of us stays home when the other goes out." ᴿ

"And you can speak for him?" ʳᵏ

Rimuru smiled.

"For the things we agreed before I left. For the others, I can bring you an answer." ᴿ

Jinshi put their copy beside hers. Hollis remained where he could see the man whose cart had waited behind his at the rope.

That man had been asked to tell the roadkeeper what he had seen.

He began with the light, not with the people who had removed it.

Rimuru listened.

* * *

By the time the account reached the cart crossing, the roadkeeper had stopped looking at the names.

"And what did they ask for?" she said. ʳᵏ

The driver looked at Hollis.

"For us not to stand in the way. Before they went in. Afterward, he wanted the names written properly." ᵈ

"Payment?" ʳᵏ

"Nobody asked me." ᵈ

Jinshi waited until the man finished before turning their page around.

"That was a rescue," he said. "It isn't a price list for every journey we might be asked to make." ᴶ

The roadkeeper looked at the first line.

Rimuru watched her finish it.

"You want your people to come here." ʳᵏ

"For an agreed visit, yes. To trade. To discuss the things they make. To get where they told the guards they were going without being made to prove at every post that they aren't the story someone heard last week." ᴿ

She rested a finger on the page.

"I have three posts. Not a country." ʳᵏ

"Then write the three posts." ᴿ

* * *

That was the first useful change.

Three names instead of a promise about the road. A person at each who could receive notice of a visit, and a copy carried by the visitors if the message arrived late or a shift changed.

Not a paper which made every stranger safe. Not a demand that the house open its rooms to anyone carrying one back.

The roadkeeper wanted to know whether she could send someone to look at the equipment before buying it.

Jinshi described the receiving place, the advance notice and the parts of the house which were not a market.

"We're not inviting people to walk through bedrooms to find the person they want to ask about a blade," he said. ᴶ

"Sensible," she said. ʳᵏ

Hollis smiled at his hands.

He had spent enough journeys carrying questions toward people who had not thought that part needed an answer.

* * *

She put a damaged blade on the table.

Not the one Tamsin had shown them. The metal had bent near the point, where someone had used it for a job it had not been shaped to do.

"Can yours avoid this?" she asked. ʳᵏ

Rimuru did not touch it before asking.

Then he turned it toward the light.

"What were they hitting?" ᴿ

"I can ask." ʳᵏ

"Start there. If they want it to do a different job, the people making it need to hear that before promising a stronger version of the wrong thing." ᴿ

She gave a short laugh.

"That's what the smith said." ʳᵏ

"Then perhaps he's worth listening to." ᴿ

They agreed on an inquiry. One tool to examine, its owner's account, no order for a dozen replacements until the workshop knew what was being asked.

Rimuru did not give away a method of making crystals, a private shop, or the knowledge which had never belonged to him to sell.

* * *

The second light came up while the roadkeeper was putting away the blade.

Not outside the window.

A report from a different post, farther from the cutting than Hollis usually went. Its runner had seen people turning carts back. He had not stood close enough to describe what lay beyond the light, and the roadkeeper had already sent the question back with someone less pleased to repeat a frightening story.

Rimuru asked where the runner had stopped.

She showed him.

Jinshi marked the point on a separate sheet rather than extending the old drawing until two gates became one.

"If another appears," she said, "who do I send to?" ʳᵏ

"Hollis knows where our receiving place is. Give him what someone actually saw, when, and who can answer questions. If it's urgent, say what makes it urgent." ᴿ

She looked at him.

"You won't promise to come." ʳᵏ

"I won't promise that nobody at home can ever need us more." ᴿ

For a moment her face grew still.

Then she put the second report beside the first.

"I can understand that." ʳᵏ

* * *

Jinshi carried the finished letters on the return journey.

Three posts. Specific visits. A trial which could be checked against what people actually did with it.

Rimuru watched the track ahead while Hollis explained which of the turns would become mud after two days of rain.

"How was it?" Jinshi asked after a while. ᴶ

"Smaller than fixing the world." ᴿ

"Disappointing?" ᴶ

Rimuru looked at the letter in his hand.

"No. I think I can use this." ᴿ
