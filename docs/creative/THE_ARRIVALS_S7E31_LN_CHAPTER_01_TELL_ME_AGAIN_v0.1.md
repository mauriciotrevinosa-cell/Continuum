*When: Day625, late afternoon. The place Yuka chooses has room for two cups and no reason for anybody else to come looking for a tool.*

Mau set the second cup down beside her.

She was sitting with her knees drawn up, the bracelet turned a little on her wrist. He knew the shape of her hand well enough now to notice when she kept finding the same small place with her thumb.

He sat down without asking what was wrong before there was room for her to answer.

For a while she talked about practice.

Toad had finally brought the empty bag back slowly enough not to throw it at her. The black dog had discovered that being told to wait beside her was not always an instruction to put its head in her lap.

Mau smiled at that.

Yuka smiled too, then looked at the cup she had not picked up.

"Can I ask you something about the other time?" ʸᵏ

He turned toward her.

"Yes." ᴹ

* * *

"You said I told you something. Something important. You didn't say what." ʸᵏ

"I remember." ᴹ

"I keep wondering whether I'm supposed to ask." ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her wrist, then back at her face.

"You aren't supposed to do anything because I remember it. But you can ask." ᴹ

She breathed out through her nose.

"What was it about?" ʸᵏ

"Your health. Something before you came here. And the things you wanted afterward." ᴹ

Her thumb stopped against the bracelet.

He waited.

"Did I tell you everything?" ʸᵏ

"I don't know what everything would be. I know what you trusted me with." ᴹ

Yuka looked away.

From here, the house sounded far enough away to belong to another afternoon. Someone called a name. A reply came from the wrong side of the yard, followed by laughter which did not need either of them.

"Then I'd like to tell you," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau moved his hand closer to hers.

"I'm listening." ᴹ

* * *

She began with the picture.

Not the word. The inside of her head displayed where she could look at it, a part of herself made into a shape somebody else could point to with a finger.

"I thought if I understood what I was looking at, I'd understand the rest," she said. "I didn't." ʸᵏ

He did not tell her where the next sentence would go.

She told him she had had cancer. A malignant tumor. Another opinion, because sometimes the only thing a person could do with an answer was try to find a different one.

Then the number.

"Six months. That was what they told me then." ʸᵏ

Mau's hand closed gently around hers.

"And now?" he asked. ᴹ

She looked at him quickly.

"Gone. Before I came here. I'm not telling you it's happening again." ʸᵏ

He took that answer from her, here, instead of taking it from another night in his head.

"Okay." ᴹ

Yuka watched his face for a moment longer.

Then she began again.

* * *

Cross had gone with her. She had not always made being with her easy.

She said that with a little irritation at herself, then became irritated at the possibility that the irritation would make the whole thing sound smaller.

Mau let both parts stay.

"Was it easier because he knew?" he asked. ᴹ

She considered it.

"Sometimes. Sometimes I wanted him to forget for a minute, and then I wanted him to notice the part I wasn't saying." ʸᵏ

She gave a short laugh.

"Very reasonable." ʸᵏ

"Very easy to understand from here," Mau said. "Probably harder while it was happening." ᴹ

Yuka leaned against his shoulder.

"I hated thinking about next year. Not all the time. I'd forget to hate it. Then somebody would say something ordinary, and I'd find myself trying to work out if they were being kind by including me." ʸᵏ

He looked at the hands joined on her knee.

The second heart beat beneath his ribs.

He let her have the silence instead of filling it with his own reason to understand.

* * *

When she spoke about the duel, she sat straighter.

"I called Mahoraga knowing I couldn't stand there and promise myself I'd survive it." ʸᵏ

Mau nodded.

He remembered the wheel. Remembered what it meant to hear a body being asked to learn quickly enough not to end.

He did not make that her story.

"Did you want someone to stop you?" he asked. ᴹ

Yuka looked past the edge of the yard for a long time.

"I don't know. I wanted to win. I wanted my brother not to watch me disappear a little at a time. I wanted there to be a thing I could still do." ʸᵏ

She swallowed.

"I wanted to live. I need you to know that part." ʸᵏ

"I do." ᴹ

"Not because I said it the other time." ʸᵏ

He met her eyes.

"Because you're telling me." ᴹ

Her mouth shook before she managed the next breath.

Mau stayed where she could choose how close to come.

She came all the way.

* * *

For a while there were no more questions.

Yuka pressed her face against his neck and held his shirt as if she had found the place where she could stop arranging the words properly. He put one hand against her back and kept the other around hers.

The house continued beyond them.

Someone carried a stack of bowls across the yard. A door closed. The ordinary, thoughtless sounds of people who expected to have somewhere to put things tomorrow.

She began to breathe more evenly.

"You aren't going to become strange about every headache?" she asked into his shoulder. ʸᵏ

Mau let out a small, surprised laugh.

"I'm going to try very hard not to." ᴹ

She drew back enough to look at him.

"You can ask if I'm all right. I just don't want that to be the only thing you see." ʸᵏ

He brushed a loose strand away from her cheek.

The only color in the whole afternoon looked back at him with damp eyes and an expression which dared him to become unbearable about it.

"It isn't." ᴹ

* * *

She drank the tea after it had lost most of its heat.

Then she asked what the other telling had been like.

Mau gave her the part which belonged to the question. She had trusted him. He had listened. Later, he had done something wrong with a little of that trust, telling someone who had not been given it by her.

He did not ask her to forgive that on behalf of a person who had been real and was not sitting here to answer.

Yuka turned the cup in her hands.

"You didn't tell them this time." ʸᵏ

"No." ᴹ

"Not even before I asked?" ʸᵏ

"Not even then." ᴹ

She put the cup down and took his face between her hands.

"Then keep doing that." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

She kissed him, slowly enough that the afternoon did not become something he had already finished remembering.

When she rested against him again, he held the woman who had chosen to tell him here.

* * *

The cups stayed where they were.

Yuka had stopped trying to find the next difficult thing to say. She sat against him, listening to the yard, until Mau brushed his thumb lightly beneath her cheekbone.

"You're going red again." ᴹ

She caught his hand.

"You can see that?" ʸᵏ

"On you, yes." ᴹ

Yuka lifted her head.

"What do you mean, on me?" ʸᵏ

He looked at her, then past her at the grass.

It took him a moment to understand the question.

"The rest is gray. You aren't." ᴹ

Her fingers grew still around his.

"Since when?" ʸᵏ

"Since you arrived." ᴹ

She searched his face for the joke. There wasn't one.

"You never told me that." ʸᵏ

Mau began to answer, then stopped. The clinic came back to him: Ciel asking what had changed, Shoko's lamp, the same leaves beyond the window. So many questions about his eyes that he had stopped separating the things he had explained from the things he simply lived with.

"I told Ciel. We talked about the color problem in the checkups. I thought—" He looked back at her. "I think I just assumed you knew. It became normal to look at you and see you." ᴹ

Yuka lowered his hand into her lap without letting go.

"Why me?" ʸᵏ

"I don't know." ᴹ

He had wondered. More often in the first days, before knowing what she was going to say had become less interesting than hearing her say it.

"Sometimes I wonder if it's something I held on to when everything went dark. The last bit of color." His thumb moved against hers. "But that's me guessing. I could already see you before the other memories came back. I don't know how those things would fit." ᴹ

She looked toward the house. Then at her own hand in his, as though she might find the difference there if she looked carefully enough.

"And you just got used to it?" ʸᵏ

"I got used to not understanding it." ᴹ

Yuka breathed out a small, unsteady laugh.

He waited while she wiped beneath one eye with her free hand.

"You could have mentioned it," she said. ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Preferably before using it to tell me I'm blushing." ʸᵏ

"I'll remember that for the next inexplicable thing." ᴹ

She laughed properly then, still close enough for him to feel it.

For a little while she watched him looking at her. He let her, without trying to supply a reason which would make the mystery easier to carry.

Then she leaned in and kissed him once more.

The tea was cold. Someone near the kitchen was calling for the bowls to come back.

Yuka took one cup, left him the other and held out her free hand.

"Come on. We can take the long way." ʸᵏ
