*When: Day626, late morning. Yuka asks for the conversation before anyone finds a bag beside a different bed.*

Maki looked at Yuta.

He looked back with the exhausted innocence of somebody who had already answered the wrong question last night.

Mai began smiling before either of them spoke.

"Not a baby," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Mau closed his eyes briefly.

"Excellent opening," Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

Rimuru put down his cup.

"Should I have brought a different one?" ᴿ

Yuka sat straighter.

"We were thinking about living in the same room. At some point. We wanted to talk before making it everybody else's surprise." ʸᵏ

* * *

Maki asked which room.

That was a better beginning than Mau had been expecting.

He told her the obvious possibility, then looked at Rimuru before saying anything which would turn the loft into a place someone else had generously decided he could leave.

Rimuru watched him.

"I'm still there," he said. ᴿ

"I know. That's why I wanted you here for the conversation." ᴹ

"Good. Because my office hasn't developed a bed while none of you were looking." ᴿ

Mai laughed.

Yuta rubbed a hand over his face.

Yuka reached for Mau's hand beneath the edge of the table.

* * *

The questions became practical quickly enough to be uncomfortable in an entirely different way.

Where her clothes would go. Whether she wanted to give up the room she already had. What visiting somebody meant when neither person could leave the conversation simply by saying good night and going to their own bed.

Mai asked that last one.

Yuka stopped turning the spoon beside her cup.

"We can still ask for space." ʸᵏ

"You can," Mai said. "I want you both to remember it while you're excited about not needing to walk down the corridor." ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau nodded.

"And Rimuru gets to want the same thing," Mai added. ᴹᵃⁱ

Rimuru lifted his cup toward her.

"I was beginning to wonder when we got to that part." ᴿ

* * *

Maki sat back.

"If you're thinking about that room now, Rimuru stays. We're not moving him out to make this easy." ᴹᵃ

Yuka looked at her.

"With Rimuru?" ʸᵏ

"With Rimuru." ᴹᵃ

Mau glanced at him.

Rimuru held up one hand.

"I will not sit on the stairs counting how long either of you has been smiling. I live there. I also sleep." ᴿ

Yuta made a noise which might have become a laugh if he had allowed it another second.

"I'd keep an eye on them," Rimuru added. "In the normal way one keeps an eye on two people who can make a perfectly ordinary afternoon very complicated." ᴿ

"That's fair," Mau said. ᴹ

Yuka looked at him, then began laughing herself.

* * *

They went outside before answering.

Not to make a secret plan which would undo the one they had just discussed. Yuka wanted to be able to think without watching four people pretend not to see her doing it.

Mau stood beside her beneath the gallery.

"I'd like to," she said. "I do. But I think I'd rather wait than make the first thing we do together a room where everybody is deciding how much room anybody else gets." ʸᵏ

He nodded slowly.

"I don't want to push Rimuru out." ᴹ

"Neither do I. I like him." ʸᵏ

She looked up at him.

"I also like you without him." ʸᵏ

Mau laughed.

"I'll tell him carefully." ᴹ

"Don't you dare." ʸᵏ

She held his shirt and kissed him before the joke could take the whole answer away.

* * *

When they returned, Yuka gave them the decision herself.

Not now. They still wanted a future room, and would think about it while the house thought about the new spaces people had asked for. In the meantime, no bags moving without the people who used the room agreeing what would happen to them.

Maki nodded.

Yuta looked relieved by the conversation having actually happened rather than by the answer being the one he might have preferred.

Mai reached across to straighten the spoon Yuka had left crooked.

"Good. Now you can stop whispering about it like you're planning a robbery." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka opened her mouth to protest.

Rimuru spoke first.

"Nobody take my blanket." ᴿ

Mau put a hand over his heart with great solemnity.

"Your blanket is safe." ᴹ

From the look Rimuru gave him, that would remain a matter of observation rather than faith.
