"I'm waiting for the report." ᴹ

"You're watching the corner as if the report will come sooner if you look annoyed at it." ᴬⁿ

He looked away from the corner.

Beru stood beside him with a room's worth of eagerness contained in the posture of a creature trying very hard to obey a small order.

"You too," Mau said. ᴹ

"My King, I am being patient." ᴮᵉ

Anko put a mark beside the first room.

"Then there's hope for both of you." ᴬⁿ

* * *

They had chosen this gate for the return route, not because Mau had promised to make every dangerous place a workplace by lunchtime.

One connected passage first. Igris with the first group, the soldiers returning in pairs, the cart staying behind until somebody had actually checked that it would fit.

Mau and Anko remained close enough to the entrance to end the trial without needing a second search to find the people running it.

The first pair returned with one cracked shield and a gesture toward a low turning.

Anko took the account, asked the same question another way and sent one back with her correction.

The shield's damage had already begun to draw on the work which rebuilt it.

Mau could feel that cost. He could also feel the difference between repairing something and paying merely for it to stand beside him.

He let the repair finish before giving the next instruction.

* * *

Igris brought the route to them rather than bringing the whole fight.

Three rooms. Then a fourth beyond a drop too awkward for the cart. More creatures gathered below it, carrying shields which made the first turn expensive if somebody tried to win by pouring bodies through it.

Beru looked at Mau.

Mau looked at the narrow line Anko had drawn.

"You and Igris. Different sides. Don't make the soldiers pay for fitting where you don't need to fit." ᴹ

Beru bowed low enough to make the answer a ceremony.

Then he was gone.

* * *

Mau went as far as the cleared turn when the report came back.

Below him, Igris held the lower passage while Beru moved above the shield line.

The fight did not look like two powerful creatures competing to reach the same enemy first. Igris forced the front rank to turn. Beru struck where that turn uncovered a back, then left the place before the weapons beneath it could become a trap for the soldiers following.

The old units had learned to wait for a gap.

The newer ones needed a second order before following it.

Mau gave that order once. Not louder because it had taken them longer to understand.

A shape larger than the others struck the far wall hard enough to shake dust from the ceiling.

Beru turned toward it with a delighted movement of the head.

Igris lifted his sword.

This time Mau kept himself from reaching for the thing he could have done fastest.

He watched the two routes meet beneath the larger body, the shield line break and the space beyond it become somewhere a person could stand.

* * *

They checked every passage before calling it finished.

Anko walked the route herself when Igris said it was clear. She found the narrow place, agreed the cart should stay above it and made the soldiers bring the salvage up in smaller loads instead of discovering what a broken wheel looked like inside a gate.

Mau counted the bodies after that.

Sixty-four which could answer the extraction. The larger one among them. Not a hundred because he had wanted a rounder number, and not only the ones close enough to look impressive beside him.

He brought them up in groups.

The reserve answered without the drop he had once waited for. The work still required a route, a form kept intact and his attention on what he was asking to become his.

When he finished, the ordinary line read one hundred and eighty-seven.

The named shadows remained sixteen.

Mau looked at the new total for a long moment.

*Now you will grow.*

He closed the display.

Anko waited beside the cart.

"A memory," he told her before she had to ask why his hand had stopped. ᴹ

She nodded toward the clear part of the floor.

"We'll count those from here. Come sit down." ᴬⁿ

* * *

The levels had moved twice during the fight.

One hundred and eleven now. Eighty-two points held, the older allocations still part of the body which had not needed to enter the shield line itself.

Anko wrote the changes beside the gate and the date. The kills belonged to the summons he had sent into the rooms, not to somebody quietly fighting outside a dungeon while the Window invented another reason to reward him.

Sixty-three small crystals. One larger reward. Separate from the lots at home, with the cracked and exhausted pieces kept out of the count which somebody might later mistake for ready fuel.

Beru watched the cart being loaded.

"We could do more," he said. ᴮᵉ

"We will," Mau said. "When we know what part of this worked without making everybody come back tomorrow to repair it." ᴹ

Beru considered that.

Then he turned and corrected the newest soldier's grip on a sack instead of taking the whole sack away.

Anko watched him.

"Useful," she said quietly. ᴬⁿ

Mau smiled.

* * *

At home, Jinshi did not send all the new crystals toward a buyer.

The workshop wanted a selection to compare. The house wanted fuel it could keep. A possible sale needed a price which remembered where the material came from, what had to remain and who would carry it next time.

Mau left them that conversation instead of standing over the table until the army's work became his choice of everybody else's afternoon.

Yuka was waiting by the practice space with a bag which Toad had finally learned to return without throwing.

"Show me," he said. ᴹ

She did.

For once, the thing coming back to him was exactly the weight he expected.
