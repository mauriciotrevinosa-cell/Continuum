She noticed anyway.

"Whatever it is, wait." ʸᵏ

He looked at the broad stone floor, then the lower channel along its edge.

"Good room." ᴹ

"I know." ʸᵏ

Yuta checked the place where they had marked the ritual's boundary. He would remain beyond it. Mau had agreed to the part Yuka wanted from him and no more, as he had when they had subdued Nue together.

She wanted the weight turned when it committed.

Not the whole fight ended before she could find the opening herself.

Mau took his place.

* * *

The elephant arrived larger than the picture Yuka had successfully held in her mind.

Her first thought was that the floor looked less generous now.

Her second was the water.

It struck before she had finished putting the rabbits where she wanted the head to turn. The first wave broke them apart and took the rope out from beside her boot.

Yuka ran diagonally rather than trying to outrun it straight back.

Mau caught her at the far edge of the rush, turned enough to keep her feet beneath her and let go as soon as she had the ground again.

The elephant came through its own falling water.

Yuka sent Nue upward.

"Not the floor," she said. ʸᵏ

The lightning held above them.

* * *

The next wave went toward the bird.

Yuka had asked Nue to draw the head, not to meet the water. It banked hard enough to make her feel the effort of keeping the call together while she moved below.

Mau reached the flank when the elephant turned.

He did not lift it out of the ritual.

He put his weight where the next forward step needed its balance, made the shoulder correct and left Yuka the side of the head which had been too high to reach a moment earlier.

She caught the rope.

Not around a place she meant to hurt. Across the line she needed to keep facing away while her dog came low from the other side.

The elephant pushed.

Yuka went with it before the rope could pull her flat.

"Now," she said. ʸᵏ

* * *

Mau changed the angle instead of adding another great force in the opposite direction.

The forefoot came down where the wet floor sloped.

The elephant corrected with its trunk. Yuka had been waiting for that part. She let the line run, stepped beneath the space it opened and held the new position while Nue's wingbeat carried the loose water away from her face.

Her native energy shook through the calls.

She dismissed the dog before holding it became the reason she lost the thing in front of her.

The weight came lower.

Mau left her the last movement.

Yuka put her palm against the great head, the rope braced across the position she had fought to reach, and kept her own technique where it was beginning to answer something which no longer wanted only to throw her off.

For a moment the whole room seemed to wait on her next breath.

Then the shadow took the weight back.

* * *

She sat down in the shallow water because standing had become a more ambitious plan than she wanted immediately after winning.

Mau sat beside her.

From beyond the line, Yuta looked at both of them and reconsidered the usefulness of the dry cloth he was carrying.

"I thought you chose the drain," Mau said. ᴹ

Yuka turned her wet face toward him.

"It hasn't finished." ʸᵏ

He began laughing.

She splashed him with considerably more satisfaction than force.

* * *

The independent call came after she had rested.

Mau stood with Yuta this time. Yuka made the sign alone, felt the greater demand arrive and let the elephant stand only long enough to know it was hers.

Then she dismissed it before excitement made her ask her body for the whole afternoon twice.

Five families now.

Not five things she could keep out at once merely because they answered separately.

She leaned against Mau on the way back.

"You waited for me," she said. ʸᵏ

"You had the line." ᴹ

"At the end. Before that I had a very wet plan." ʸᵏ

He laughed and took the bag from her shoulder.

"Still yours." ᴹ
