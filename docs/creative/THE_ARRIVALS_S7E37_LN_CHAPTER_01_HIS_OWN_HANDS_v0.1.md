*When: The night of Day734. In the dream, the hand moves before Mau decides to move it.*

He recognized the fingers.

The old white line at the knuckle. The two which would not close properly. Every small proof that the body belonged to a life he had survived.

The hand rose.

He had not raised it.

Across the stone room, the Architect watched with the pleased attention of a teacher seeing the lesson finally understood.

*Now you will grow.*

Mau tried to answer.

His mouth opened.

He woke before anything came out of it.

* * *

The room was dark.

Not the stone room. He knew the angle of the window, the blanket against his knees, the place his shoes waited by the chair.

His hand was against his chest.

He could not remember putting it there.

That was enough.

He sat up too quickly. The next breath stopped halfway, and the harder he tried to finish it, the less room there seemed to be for it to go.

Two beats.

One he had been born with, perhaps. One somebody had put inside him while smiling.

He could not make himself decide which he had just felt beneath his palm.

*Mau.*

Ciel's voice reached him as if from the wrong side of the room.

He could not answer quickly enough.

* * *

*I can hear you,* he managed at last.

*Would you like Rimuru?*

He wanted to say no. To put the whole thing away before it became somebody else's night too.

Then his own hand twitched against his shirt and he was back in the dream for an instant, watching the first movement become something he had not chosen.

*Yes.*

Rimuru came down from the loft without asking why the light was still out.

He lit the lamp, stopped far enough away not to put his body between Mau and the door, and said his name.

Mau stared at him.

"A dream," he got out. ᴹ

"All right. I'm here." ᴿ

* * *

Shoko came when they asked her to.

Mau let Rimuru call. Let her look at his hands and listen where both rhythms continued beneath the shirt he had twisted in his fist.

She asked about pain.

He told her what he could tell. Tightness. Cold. The feeling of being unable to make the next breath belong to the room he was actually in.

She stayed beside that answer long enough to check it rather than deciding a frightened person had no body worth examining.

"Nothing new that I can find here," she said at last. "Tell me if that changes." ˢʰ

Mau nodded.

His hands would still not become ordinary just because she had not found the thing he feared in them.

* * *

He looked at Ciel's quiet presence without asking her to reach into the dream and prove it false.

*Did I do anything? Before I woke?*

*No technique opened. Nothing came out. I felt you wake.*

*Before I asked?*

*Before you could answer me.*

He closed his eyes.

She did not claim to know what a voice in another room had meant by a future it had never finished describing.

She told him what she had been there to observe.

He held on to that.

* * *

Rimuru brought the water after Mau could take it without feeling as if lifting a cup would test whether his own arm still answered him.

He drank a little.

Shoko put another blanket beside him rather than wrapping it around him before he had noticed her reaching.

"Do you want to tell us?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

Mau looked toward the window.

There were words. An unfinished sentence. A purpose somebody had named while the people he loved had not been able to make that hand stop.

He could tell them.

He could not yet bear to watch the words move from his mouth into their faces.

"Not the whole thing," he said. ᴹ

Rimuru nodded.

"Then don't make it the whole thing tonight." ᴿ

* * *

He stayed where they could find him.

Not in the gate. Not on the road with a plan which would become a missing person before it became an answer.

In his room, with the lamp on and Rimuru's cup beside his own.

Shoko left after he could speak without fighting every breath. She asked him to come by in the morning and waited until he answered rather than accepting a nod given to end the question.

Rimuru remained below the loft.

Mau stared at the lines in his hands until they became hands which could hold a cup again.

* * *

Yuka came before breakfast.

He had asked them to tell her in the morning. Not the dream's contents. That the night had been bad, that he had asked for company, that he was here.

She sat beside him, took his hand and waited until he looked at her.

"No practice today if you don't want it," she said. ʸᵏ

"Gojo will be devastated." ᴹ

"I'll let him survive it." ʸᵏ

Her thumb moved once against his knuckle.

"You told them." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

She leaned close enough for him to rest his forehead against hers without having to find a better answer.

"Come eat," she said. "We can decide the next part after that." ʸᵏ
