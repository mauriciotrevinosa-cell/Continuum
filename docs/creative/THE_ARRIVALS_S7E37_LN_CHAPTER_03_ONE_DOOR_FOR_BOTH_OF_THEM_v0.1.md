*When: Day746, after dinner. Yuka says good night and then stays where she is.*

Mau had already turned toward the corridor.

He stopped when her hand stayed in his.

"What?" he asked. ᴹ

"Nothing." ʸᵏ

He looked at their hands.

She looked at them too, then gave a little laugh without letting go.

"I don't want to say that every night." ʸᵏ

"Good night?" ᴹ

"The part after it. Where you go one way and I go the other." ʸᵏ

Somebody below them was trying to persuade Milim that carrying the whole basket did not count as helping put away the bread. The argument moved toward the kitchen, leaving the stairs briefly quiet.

Yuka turned to face him.

"Do you still want to live with me?" ʸᵏ

Mau answered before remembering all the careful ways they had found to discuss it last time.

"Yes." ᴹ

Her shoulders eased.

"Good. Because I think I'm finished waiting for the perfect room." ʸᵏ

* * *

They sat on the top step rather than filling the passage while people tried to get past.

Yuka leaned her shoulder against his.

"I meant it when I said not yet," she said. "I liked having a place that was mine. After the clinic and everything, I didn't want to make another enormous thing out of us." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"I know." ᴹ

"I still like having it. I just keep finding myself wishing you were there." ʸᵏ

He turned toward her.

"Your room?" ᴹ

"Unless you've found a third one while carrying things for Kaneki." ʸᵏ

"He hasn't offered me a room." ᴹ

"Then mine." ʸᵏ

She said it with a small, nervous decisiveness. Mau could see the warmth in her face against the dim wood behind her.

"Not just visiting," she added. "Your clothes. Your books. Your terrible arrangement of things you're definitely going to put away later." ʸᵏ

"That's an established system." ᴹ

"I've seen the system. I want a shelf between it and my pillow." ʸᵏ

He began smiling.

She watched him for a moment, then caught the part he had not answered.

"You don't have to say yes because I've finally asked." ʸᵏ

"I said yes before you told me where." ᴹ

Her hand tightened around his.

* * *

He thought about Rimuru.

About the familiar movement overhead, the voice arriving before a face, the evenings when neither of them needed a reason to remain in the room. Leaving that arrangement would be a change even though he was not leaving the house.

Yuka followed his glance toward the corridor.

"He keeps the loft," she said. ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"And everything he wants below it. We aren't taking his things apart to make ours fit." ʸᵏ

Mau looked back at her.

"I'd like him to hear it from us." ᴹ

"So would I." ʸᵏ

She stood and held out her hand to help him up, though both of them knew who would have to do the lifting if he decided to stay where he was.

He let her make the gesture.

They went downstairs together.

* * *

Rimuru was at the table with Yuta. Maki had brought back the cloth Mai was trying to fold around something which did not appear interested in being rectangular.

Yuka waited until Mai put it down.

"We want to talk about the room again." ʸᵏ

Yuta looked from her to Mau.

Mau sat before the silence could convince either of them they had accidentally announced something else.

"I'm not asking him to leave the loft," he said to Rimuru. "I'd move into Yuka's room." ᴹ

Rimuru put his cup down.

"You can say that without looking like you're resigning from being my friend." ᴿ

Mau let out the breath he had been holding.

"I wasn't—" ᴹ

"You were beginning a very formal face." ᴿ

Mai leaned back to inspect it.

"He was." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka laughed, and Mau looked at the table until he could trust his own mouth again.

* * *

Maki asked whether they had actually looked at what would fit.

"Not all of it," Yuka said. "But we don't need all of it tonight." ʸᵏ

"Tonight?" Yuta asked. ʸᵗ

She met his eyes.

"If we can make the space. I want him there. We both do." ʸᵏ

Yuta's gaze moved to Mau.

"Yes," Mau said. ᴹ

It felt strange to answer such a small word when there were so many ways to make it more complicated. He wanted to wake beside her. He wanted somewhere to put the book down without first deciding whether he was staying too late. He wanted to learn which ordinary things annoyed them before they had become enormous things neither of them knew how to say.

He let the word mean that without delivering a speech about it.

Maki looked toward Rimuru.

"Last time, we were talking about that room," she said. "And making sure he wasn't moved out of it for you." ᴹᵃ

"I remember," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

"That still stands. It doesn't mean he has to follow you into every room you ever choose." ᴹᵃ

Rimuru looked deeply relieved.

"Thank you. I'd begun to worry my duties were becoming architectural." ᴿ

Yuta laughed despite himself.

* * *

There were a few more questions.

Whether Yuka wanted to clear the whole shelf or only part of it. She said part. Whether Mau was expecting to move a chair through a doorway without checking the width. He said he would check it, and Mai asked him to say that again while she was listening.

Yuta was quiet long enough for Yuka to turn back to him.

"Grandpa?" ʸᵏ

He looked at their hands beneath the table.

"You've thought about what happens when you don't agree on something?" ʸᵗ

"We won't stop having a whole house to walk around in," she said. "I can want an hour by myself without deciding I don't want him there afterward." ʸᵏ

Mau nodded.

"And I won't turn taking a walk into leaving." ᴹ

Yuta looked at him a little longer. Then he nodded too.

"All right." ʸᵗ

Yuka smiled before she could help it.

Mai picked up the troublesome cloth.

"Then go and discover whose side has the good light." ᴹᵃⁱ

"Mine," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

"Very promising," Rimuru said. ᴿ

* * *

He caught Mau before they went upstairs.

"I'm keeping the blanket." ᴿ

Mau laughed.

"We've discussed this." ᴹ

"I'm updating the discussion. Also, knock when you come in. You can't make the lower half your cupboard and leave me the stairs." ᴿ

Mau's smile softened.

"I know." ᴹ

Rimuru held his gaze for a moment, then nudged his arm.

"You can still come over without bringing a problem." ᴿ

"Tea?" ᴹ

"Sometimes something less terrible than tea." ᴿ

Mau promised no particular improvement to the drinks.

When he turned, Yuka was waiting with her hand on the stair rail. She had heard enough to give Rimuru a little smile of her own.

Then she held out her hand again.

This time, they did not stop at the top to say good night.
