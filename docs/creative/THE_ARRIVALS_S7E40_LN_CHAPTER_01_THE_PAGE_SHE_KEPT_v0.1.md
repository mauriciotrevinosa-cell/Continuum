"I haven't spent anything." ᴹ

"I know. I'm preserving a good habit." ᵁ

Yuka looked over the back of the chair, saw the arrows and numbers, and wisely continued to the place where Milim was demanding someone decide whether a rule could be unfair even if she had won by using it.

Mau opened the Window for the part he had agreed to show Umaru.

One hundred and fifteen.

One hundred and two held.

The old physical allocations remained old physical allocations, not something he could close with the display when he wanted the next test to look less difficult.

Umaru checked the figures, then uncovered the second page.

* * *

The new annotation beneath Intelligence was not a finished reward.

Ciel read it with him before he repeated the relevant part aloud. The mapping which had once concerned the shadow reserve now had separate work beneath it: his own mana, cursed-energy handling, the places where a wider supply would not automatically teach his body to use it well.

Some lines were still marked incomplete.

Noise had not made them disappear merely because giving him a simpler answer would be pleasant.

Umaru pointed at the unfinished part.

"So if you put everything there now, you don't even know which version of the thing you're making larger." ᵁ

"Not well enough." ᴹ

"Good. That's why the third page says wait." ᵁ

He lifted the third page.

It did.

* * *

"The gift won't get better because you panic and make yourself more expensive," she said. ᵁ

Mau looked at her.

"I'm not panicking." ᴹ

She looked at the empty point field, then at him.

"At this exact moment. Excellent. Let's keep it." ᵁ

He laughed despite himself.

Ciel sounded pleased in the quiet space behind the laughter.

*I like her page.*

*You would.*

Umaru pushed the blank one toward him at last.

"What can you actually do with less effort now? That's the part I want before deciding what more should mean." ᵁ

Mau took the pencil.

He began with the two small pulls.

* * *

Taihei came to see whether either of them remembered that the afternoon had originally included a game.

Umaru put one hand over the work.

"Important planning." ᵁ

"You said that about the last game too." ᵀᵃ

"It was. I was investigating why the rules hated me." ᵁ

Mau put the pencil down.

Umaru looked at him.

"We haven't finished." ᵁ

"We've decided not to spend the points. That's a very efficient amount of work." ᴹ

Taihei began smiling.

Umaru looked betrayed by both of them.

* * *

She beat Mau in the first round.

He accused her of putting the rules beneath three other rules until nobody could reach them.

She said a brother ought to be proud of initiative.

Taihei, already acquainted with the cost of that principle, passed the next hand in silence.

Mau looked at the neat page waiting beside the game.

For once, something unfinished had stayed unfinished because the people who cared about him wanted another hour of his company.

He took the cards she gave him.
