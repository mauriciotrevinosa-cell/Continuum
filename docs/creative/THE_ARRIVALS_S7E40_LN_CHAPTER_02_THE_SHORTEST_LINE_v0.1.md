*When: Day782. Yuka draws the line the ox must not be allowed to finish.*

Mau looked from one end to the other, then at the three shorter paths she had put beside it.

"You want it to keep turning." ᴹ

"I want it not to keep getting the same run." ʸᵏ

Yuta checked the mark beyond which he would stand. Mau remained inside with her, the part of the help agreed before the call.

Yuka had learned enough now to ask for something more precise than *don't let it hit me*.

When she made the sign, she was still afraid of how quickly a straight line could become a body arriving at its end.

She made it anyway.

* * *

The first charge carried past the rabbits.

Not through the place Yuka had stood. Through the empty space she had made interesting enough for the head to choose it before the body finished gathering speed.

She moved while it turned.

The second line was shorter.

The third began too close.

Yuka's boot slipped against the stone, and the horns lowered before she could get her next foot under her.

Toad's tongue caught the loop at her waist.

She had practised how quickly it could bring her back.

This time she asked for all of it.

* * *

Mau caught the ox at the edge of the turn.

Not by standing at the end of the longest run and making the ritual a contest between its growing impact and the body the System had already changed.

He met the side, gave the head enough ground to continue in the wrong direction and left the center clear for Yuka's next step.

She landed, released Toad and made the sign she had been practising for months.

The domain closed around the place she could actually hold.

Darkness under the hooves.

A different place for the serpent to begin.

* * *

It rose beneath the ox's next correction, not at the far end of another straight line.

The coils turned the head. The charge became weight pushing against its own lost balance instead of speed gathered across the room.

Mau held that turn long enough for Yuka to find the second opening in her own shadow.

Max Elephant came down where she had placed it.

Not on Mau. Not beyond the boundary she was already struggling to keep closed.

The impact broke the ox's unbound form before its forefeet could find another run.

Yuka released the calls and opened the boundary with what attention remained.

The practice room came back all at once.

* * *

She sat before her legs made the decision without her.

Mau crouched beside her, keeping his hands clear until she reached for one.

Yuta came in with the water.

"How long?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"About nine," he said. ʸᵗ

She nodded, breathing too hard to look pleased immediately.

The native technique was quiet with the exhaustion which followed a domain. She knew not to call into it until quiet became readiness again.

Mau took the loose loop from her shoulder.

"That wasn't a short plan," he said. ᴹ

She laughed weakly.

"Shortest line I could find." ʸᵏ

* * *

The independent call waited until the next morning.

Yuka stood alone with room ahead of her. The ox emerged and stopped before the place she had asked it not to cross.

She did not make it charge simply because she could.

She dismissed it, then looked toward Mau with seven families answering where there had once been a dog she had not even been able to bring herself to call properly.

He smiled before she asked.

She took his hand on the way to breakfast, tired in muscles which had had a night to complain and pleased in a way which did not need the whole house to watch another demonstration before eating.
