*When: Day798. Taihei has brought enough cups for the people who said they were coming and one for the person who always arrives after saying he will only stay a minute.*

Kaneki took the last one, checked it and looked relieved to find his coffee still black.

Gojo objected to the implication about arriving late.

Yuta pointed out that nobody had named him.

Rimuru sat down before they improved the argument into something which required another chair.

Mau looked across the yard. The new covered stretch held light from the common room, and somebody had remembered to take the spare cloth off the bench before the damp reached it.

Small work. Still there the next evening.

He wanted to be able to look at the rest of himself that way.

* * *

The conversation began with the birthday.

Food. Where they would move the tables if the weather changed. Whether Gojo's offer to make the occasion unforgettable was something which should be accepted without further questions.

"It should not," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"You haven't heard the offer." ᴳ

"I know who's offering." ʸᵗ

Taihei passed another cup and saved Gojo from defending himself without something to do with his hands.

Mau laughed.

Then, when the laughter had finished, he asked the question which had been waiting beneath several other ones.

"Does being good at something make it what you're for?" ᴹ

* * *

No one answered quickly enough to turn it into a joke he could escape behind.

Gojo rested the cup against his knee.

"Depends who's doing the telling." ᴳ

Mau looked at him.

"If it's somebody who needs a thing done and has found the person who can do it, they'll be very confident about the answer." ᴳ

Taihei looked toward the common room.

"Umaru has a lot of confidence in what I'm for when she wants something," he said. ᵀᵃ

Mau smiled a little.

Taihei went on without making the smile the end of it.

"She still wants me there when she isn't asking. That's the part I trust more." ᵀᵃ

* * *

Rimuru waited until Mau looked at him.

"You aren't an empty place because somebody found a way to put something in it," he said. ᴿ

The second beat struck once beneath Mau's ribs.

The words were not the secret he had kept. They were close enough to the question that he had to make himself remain beside them instead of looking away.

Yuta put his cup down.

"She asked you for an afternoon," he said. "Not a stronger version of you." ʸᵗ

"I know." ᴹ

"I thought you might like hearing somebody else say it." ʸᵗ

Mau looked back across the yard.

"I do." ᴹ

* * *

They did not make him finish the larger question that night.

He did not tell them the sentence which stopped at *grown enough*, or the purpose he feared filling the missing end of it.

He stayed while Kaneki spoke about the new shelf having finally earned its place, and Taihei explained why a household could apparently move seventy pieces of furniture before finding one spoon nobody admitted to having moved.

When Gojo made another offer about the birthday, Yuta rejected it before he finished the first word.

Mau began laughing properly this time.

Later, going down, he took the empty cups with him.

He had not been built for that.

He had simply seen they needed carrying and chosen to do it.
