*When: Day808, evening. Mau remembers the seasoning because Rem has put a small mark beside the bowl he is not supposed to forget.*

Yuka waited while he carried the last plate out.

Not hidden miles from the house. A little table beyond the busiest part of the common room, with enough light to see what they were eating and enough distance for the conversations behind them to remain somebody else's.

She looked at the food, then at him.

"You made this?" ʸᵏ

"With instructions. Some of them repeated." ᴹ

She smiled.

"It smells good." ʸᵏ

"You can wait until you taste it to decide whether the instructions worked." ᴹ

She did.

Mau discovered that being watched while somebody took the first bite could require a ridiculous amount of courage from a person who had fought things larger than the whole table.

Yuka closed her eyes briefly.

Then opened them on his face.

"They worked." ʸᵏ

* * *

The stronger seasoning had its own place.

She noticed that too.

"You remembered." ʸᵏ

"I remember learning it." ᴹ

Yuka laughed, and the afternoon at Ilen's table came back without a scar or an older voice asking it to become something else first.

He told her about Rem taking away the spoon the day before. She told him about Mai's opinion of the first mountain she had drawn.

He looked at the packet beside her chair.

She put a hand on it.

"After we eat." ʸᵏ

"I wasn't asking." ᴹ

"You were looking very politely." ʸᵏ

He began laughing.

For a while, the only thing he had to finish was the food on his own plate.

* * *

When she gave him the packet, he held it before opening it.

Ordinary paper. A binding made to survive being folded and unfolded by somebody who would not always remember to keep the corner clear of his knee.

On the front, in her own writing: *Our Year*.

He looked up at her.

Yuka had lost some of the confident expression which had carried her through a morning of people watching her receive things.

"It isn't a proper map," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau opened it.

* * *

There were two hands at the first mark.

Not beautifully drawn. One thumb had been corrected and corrected again, until she had left the small dark line beside it rather than making the paper thinner by trying once more.

He knew the hands anyway.

The day from which they counted.

Below it, she had written the thing she remembered thinking before he took hers: that she would pretend not to be disappointed if he did not understand what she was offering.

Mau stopped with his fingers beside the sentence.

"You didn't tell me that." ᴹ

"I'm telling you now." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She smiled a little.

"Keep going." ʸᵏ

* * *

The coast occupied far more space than its distance from the house deserved.

A dragon above the route. The little sandcastle with the door which had been so important at the time. A mark beside the edge of the water, where she had written that telling her grandparents had apparently been the most effective romantic preparation either of them could have made.

He laughed before the warmth behind his eyes made the next part harder to see.

Yuka laughed with him, relieved enough to let her shoulder touch his.

The ordinary world remained grey beyond her wrist.

The marks on the paper were clear. She had made them so he would not need a color he could not see to find the path she meant him to follow.

He touched the edge of the page with his good thumb.

"You made all this." ᴹ

"I had help keeping it from folding through the words. The bad drawings are mine." ʸᵏ

* * *

The Fort had an enormous hamster flag.

That, she explained before he could comment on its size, was historically accurate.

He conceded the point.

Below the flag, somebody appeared to be carrying a prisoner while another small figure was trying to keep the victory sweets from becoming tribute to Milim.

At the farm, his pot sat firmly on a table.

Under it: *Eventually.*

Mau covered his mouth with one hand.

Yuka looked delighted with herself.

He turned to the meadow she had chosen.

Two figures hung beneath a bird whose wings seemed almost too large for the paper. Her note did not say that he could have gone farther on another dragon. It said she had been able to feel him trusting her to take him somewhere.

He did not turn that page immediately.

* * *

There were smaller places between the larger ones.

A chair where she had kept his place. A bowl he had forgotten after saying he would put it away. The mark on the field where her first landing had stopped short and the later one where he had been waiting to see her finish it.

Nothing from a life she had not lived in order to make the one she had lived more impressive.

He remembered these things.

From here.

The hand he had held. The person who had chosen him after being frightened by how much of him she had not known. The mornings which had not become less real because another memory could hurt more loudly.

Yuka watched him reach the last page.

It had room left on it.

* * *

"I didn't know what to put next," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at the blank space.

"We haven't done it yet." ᴹ

"I know. I thought you might like a part nobody had decided for you." ʸᵏ

For a moment he could not answer.

The second beat continued beneath his ribs. The old sentence remained unfinished. A room of stone still existed in the things he remembered, and being loved did not make him know what its maker had meant.

Here was another thing he did not know.

An empty part of a page, offered by somebody who wanted to see what he chose to put beside her.

He set the map down carefully and drew her into his arms.

* * *

Yuka held him without asking what part had made his breath change.

When he moved back, she touched his cheek with the side of her hand.

"Too much?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He shook his head.

"A lot," he said. "Not too much." ᴹ

She smiled, then blinked quickly when his own smile became something she had not needed to prepare him to give her.

"I love you," he said. ᴹ

"I love you too." ʸᵏ

He kissed her slowly, with the map resting safely beside the empty plates and no one needing to interrupt them because the place they had chosen was something the people inside already knew about.

* * *

They stayed until the room behind them began finding its quieter evening sounds.

The last chairs moving. Someone laughing at a story which had already been told once during the meal. The band putting things away without being expected to supply the whole night with another song.

Yuka leaned against Mau and opened the map once more on both their knees.

"A whole year," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at the first pair of hands.

Then took hers.

"A whole year." ᴹ

* * *

They took their own plates back.

Put the seasoning where Rem had asked for it. Left the cups with the washing rather than beside a beautiful little table somebody else would have to remember in the morning.

At the foot of the stairs, Yuka held the map while Mau made sure its wrapping would not bend the corner.

She waited, then kissed his cheek.

"You can put it somewhere you actually use," she said. "It doesn't have to stay perfect." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

"I will." ᴹ

She went to her room with the book they had made together.

He went to his with the year she had made visible.

Before putting out the lamp, he opened the last page once more.

There was still room.
