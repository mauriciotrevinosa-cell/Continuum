## Chapter 4 — The Returned Handle

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-26)
**Volume:** 40
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S7E47
**Continuity state:** Day812 afternoon. Hollis returns one of Tamsin's two previously sold cutters for grip repair; ownership retained, no duplicate sale or refund yet. First bounded follow-up inquiry is not a sale/order guarantee. Workshop identifies fit/fastening and use problem before promising warranty. No fuel spent, new crystal sale, stock or shadow change. Receiving protocol retained; no new visitor admitted.

*When: Day812. Hollis puts a familiar wrapped shape on the receiving table and keeps his hand on it until Jinshi has looked up.*

"Before the other things," he said. ᴴ

Jinshi moved the papers away.

The cloth opened on one of the two cutters they had sold to Tamsin. Mau recognized the mark before he recognized the slight movement of the grip beneath Hollis's thumb.

"Did it break?" he asked. ᴹ

"Not the edge." ᴴ

Hollis laid down the small note which had travelled with it. The fold was dark where someone's fingers had held it too often.

Jinshi read it, then passed it across.

Tamsin had tried tightening the grip. It had held for a while, then begun moving again. She wanted it repaired and wanted to know whether the second one would do the same.

Mau turned the cutter without lifting it from the table.

"She was cutting something different?" ᴹ

"She wrote what she used it on. She also wrote that sharp things should stay in their handles." ᴴ

Mau looked down at the note again.

That part was difficult to argue with.

* * *

Wakana took the cutter to the workshop, leaving the cloth and the note on a clear part of the bench.

Mau followed with Jinshi. Hollis accepted the offered water before going after them, his boots making a small scrape at the entrance. He had been on the road long enough to keep looking for the next place to put his weight down.

Wakana tried the grip by hand and stopped before increasing the force.

"Don't make it worse while we're finding out," he told Mau. ᵂ

Mau withdrew the hand which had been about to help.

"I wasn't." ᴹ

Wakana looked at him.

"Yet," Mau added. ᴹ

They removed the fastening carefully. The binding had worn unevenly. When they set the grip apart, the polished line beneath it showed where the load had been travelling.

Jinshi brought the note close enough to compare with what Tamsin had described.

"Twisting," he said. ᴶ

"The late turn," Mau said. ᴹ

Wakana turned the grip to show him where the fastening had worn.

He took out the second drawing they had used while making the pair. Mau remembered how awkward the first grip had been. They had changed and tested it before Tamsin ever bought the tool. She had used this one for months since. The bright line beneath the binding belonged to work the successful first cuts had not been enough to predict.

Hollis sat down.

"She didn't ask whether the drawing was good," he said. "She asked whether the tool could come back." ᴴ

* * *

The repair needed time.

Jinshi wrote that before writing a price. They could change the fit, rebind and test it; they could not truthfully promise an answer on the second cutter before they had examined it or reproduced the problem.

Mau looked at the line for return transport.

"We should cover this journey," he said. ᴹ

Hollis turned the cup in his hands.

"My journey had other reasons." ᴴ

"The part for this." ᴹ

Jinshi tapped the page.

"And the repair, if it's our fitting. We need to say what we're covering before this becomes every repair for every tool somebody has used as a lever." ᴶ

Hollis nodded.

Wakana, still looking at the grip, said nothing until Mau had stopped waiting for a quick answer.

"Let me make the new piece. Then we'll load it in the direction she actually used." ᵂ

The cutter stayed in the workshop. They gave Hollis a receipt stating whose it was, what had come back and what they had agreed to examine. He read it before putting it away.

* * *

Only then did he take out the second page.

Eight tools, divided between two kinds of work. A request for prices and time, with enough blank space beside each line to make clear that neither was already decided.

Mau read the quantities.

"From Tamsin?" ᴹ

"Two people who saw hers. She told them to wait until she knew about the handle." ᴴ

Jinshi began smiling.

"Sensible woman." ᴶ

"Very inconvenient," Hollis said. "She keeps making sense before I can sell her anything." ᴴ

Mau handed the page to Wakana rather than answering it himself.

Eight meant materials and workshop time. It meant testing what would happen to the next eight handles. It meant the household carriers they still needed to fix would be competing with work for people outside.

Wakana read the request and put it beside the cutter.

"After this," he said. ᵂ

Hollis accepted that answer without trying to turn it into a date.

* * *

On the way back to the receiving space, he asked whether their covered table had become larger.

Mau looked at it.

"No." ᴹ

"Feels smaller with three people waiting to put papers on it." ᴴ

Jinshi moved an empty box from the visitor's side to the place it belonged. The table became larger again.

Hollis laughed, then stood while his knees were still willing to cooperate.

He had another stop before the road turned bad enough to make it unwise. They agreed when he would ask for the repair update. He would send word before coming, and they would give him something more useful than a promise that the cutter would be excellent this time.

When he left, Mau carried Tamsin's note back to the workshop.

Wakana had already begun measuring the part he intended to change.

Mau set the note where both of them could see it.

"Tell me which direction to push," he said. ᴹ

Wakana looked at his hands, then at the cutter.

"Gently." ᵂ
