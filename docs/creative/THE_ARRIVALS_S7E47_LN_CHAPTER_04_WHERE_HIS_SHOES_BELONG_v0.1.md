*When: The same evening. Yuka clears half a shelf and then takes one book back.*

Mau stood in the doorway with an armful of clothes.

She looked at the book. At the space she had just made.

"That one goes there," she said. ʸᵏ

"Then leave it there." ᴹ

"I said half." ʸᵏ

"I don't own half a shelf of necessary things." ᴹ

She looked at the clothes in his arms.

"You brought that many sleeves without bringing the rest of the shirt?" ʸᵏ

He looked down.

One of the cuffs he had been trying to catch had dragged another shirt halfway out of the pile.

Yuka took the top two before the rest could make their own way onto the floor.

* * *

His things arrived in several journeys.

The first was mostly clothing. The second included the map, which he carried flat because she had already made enough remarks about its mountains. His notes went beside her books rather than on top of them. The weapons stayed properly stored; there was no reason to turn finding a place for a shirt into a display of everything which could kill someone.

At the old room, he stopped beside the chair.

Rimuru came down from the loft and found him considering the width.

"You're actually measuring." ᴿ

"Mai was listening." ᴹ

Rimuru held the door while he turned it through.

The chair caught the frame anyway.

For a moment they looked at the place where it had stopped.

"You've fought very large things," Rimuru said. ᴿ

"This one is using the house." ᴹ

They turned it back, changed the angle and made it through without asking the wall to lose.

Rimuru walked with him as far as Yuka's door. She moved the small basket which had been occupying the only sensible place to stand.

The chair fit beside her table.

All three inspected it.

"Now you need a place for the basket," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Yuka pointed at the space Mau had just vacated.

He put it there and tried not to look as though the answer had been difficult.

* * *

Mai came past with a second pillow under her arm.

"Linen cupboard," she said, before either could ask. "I checked. You aren't stealing somebody else's night." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka took it, suddenly pink.

Mai's expression became unbearable.

"What? You intended to share that one too?" ᴹᵃⁱ

"Thank you," Mau said quickly. ᴹ

"Very sensible answer." ᴹᵃⁱ

She looked at the chair, the crooked pile of clothes and Yuka holding the pillow as if it had become far more personal on its journey down the corridor.

Then her smile changed.

"It already looks like you both live here." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka glanced around the room.

Mau followed her look. Nothing had been replaced to make room for him. Her things were still where she wanted them. His had begun finding places between them, inconvenient in a few ordinary ways they would have to solve tomorrow.

Mai left them to it.

* * *

The first place Mau put his shoes was wrong.

Yuka told him when she nearly stepped into one.

He moved them.

The first place she put his cup was beyond the part of his left sight which would catch it easily from the bed. He turned his head to find it. She noticed and asked rather than taking every object in the room away from that side.

"Here?" ʸᵏ

"A little closer." ᴹ

She moved it.

He put *Our Year* beside the chair. *Two Beginnings* lay on her table, with the page she had last been using still marked. A cuff had escaped the stack again.

Yuka folded it and handed the shirt back.

"Your system." ʸᵏ

"Needs refinement." ᴹ

She laughed.

* * *

When the last footsteps had passed, Mau stood beside the bed with the particular uncertainty of somebody who had been invited and still wanted to check he was not using more of the invitation than intended.

Yuka sat down and looked up at him.

"You don't need to wait for me to fall asleep in a chair first." ʸᵏ

He smiled, a little embarrassed.

"I know." ᴹ

"Do you?" ʸᵏ

He sat beside her.

The bed dipped. They both adjusted at once and bumped shoulders.

Yuka started laughing.

"We may need to practise that." ʸᵏ

"I was hoping to improve through repetition." ᴹ

She put her hand on his cheek and kissed him before he could say anything else about practice.

* * *

They took too long deciding where the second pillow belonged.

Once they lay down, Yuka decided she had wanted his shoulder instead. Mau moved his arm so the old stiff fingers would not end up folded beneath him. She let him find the position before coming close again.

The room was familiar to her in ways it was not to him.

A floorboard answered someone in the corridor. The curtain shifted, then settled. Mau looked toward the place where he would usually have heard Rimuru moving above him and found an ordinary ceiling instead.

"Strange?" Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

"A little." ᴹ

She lifted her head enough to see him.

"Bad strange?" ʸᵏ

He ran his thumb lightly along the back of her hand.

"I'm trying to understand why I was preparing to say good night and go home when you were asking me to come home." ᴹ

Yuka stayed very still for a moment.

Then she put her face back against his shoulder.

"You can keep your shoes here," she said. ʸᵏ

He laughed softly.

"You've already moved them once." ᴹ

"There are conditions." ʸᵏ

* * *

He did not fall asleep at once.

Some nights were still like that. Being wanted did not reorganize every old memory into something which would stay politely quiet after the lamp went out.

Yuka did not ask him to explain a whole life because his breathing changed.

"Still awake?" she murmured. ʸᵏ

"Yes." ᴹ

"Me too." ʸᵏ

He looked down at the dim shape of her face.

"I'm happy," he said. "I'm just awake." ᴹ

Her fingers moved once against his shirt.

"You can be both." ʸᵏ

They stayed there while the house settled. At some point she began telling him about the book she had taken back from his half of the shelf. He asked a question. She answered it, then another, until neither remembered exactly which part of the story she had been trying to explain.

He slept before she finished.

* * *

Yuka woke because his hand had left hers.

She reached across the sheet, still half asleep, and found the warm hollow where he had been lying.

Then she looked up.

Mau was above it.

Not sitting. Not halfway out of bed. He lay on his back a little more than a hand's breadth above the mattress, one arm loose beside him, breathing with the unhurried contentment of somebody to whom this arrangement made perfect sense.

Yuka sat up very slowly.

"Mau?" ʸᵏ

He smiled in his sleep.

She stared at him.

"That's not an answer." ʸᵏ

* * *

Rimuru answered his door after the second quiet knock.

He looked at Yuka, then past her toward the corridor.

"What's happened?" ᴿ

"He's asleep." ʸᵏ

Rimuru waited.

"Above the bed." ʸᵏ

His expression changed in a way she found insufficiently concerned.

"Oh." ᴿ

"Is that normal?" ʸᵏ

"Yes. When he's having a good dream." ᴿ

Yuka looked back toward her door.

"He does that?" ʸᵏ

"A few times while we were sharing. I used to wake up and find him getting rather ambitious about the distance to my stairs." Rimuru rubbed one eye. "He usually comes down smiling. I don't ask for the whole dream before breakfast." ᴿ

She put a hand over her mouth.

Rimuru looked more awake now.

"You thought I was joking?" ᴿ

"I was deciding whether to be worried." ʸᵏ

"About the floating? No. About what else you haven't discovered yet, I make no promises." ᴿ

Her laugh came out too loudly. She caught the rest behind her hand.

Rimuru smiled.

"Go back to bed. You're allowed to enjoy finding something harmless." ᴿ

* * *

Mau had turned a little by the time she returned.

He was still over the mattress. Still smiling faintly. Whatever he was dreaming had not required the room to change around him or anybody to come running.

Yuka sat on the edge and watched him for a moment.

Then she took his dangling hand.

"You could have warned me about this one too," she whispered. ʸᵏ

His fingers curled around hers without waking.

She laughed under her breath and lay down beneath the ordinary ceiling of the room they had finally decided to share.

He drifted lower as she settled. By the time his shoulder touched the sheet, her eyes were closing again.

She kept his hand.

For once, she did not need to know what he had been dreaming. She was glad something in it had made him this light.

* * *

In the morning, Yuka tried to turn over and found his arm where the rest of the bed had used to be.

Mau woke to her looking at him.

For a moment neither said anything.

Then she smiled with her whole face, still soft with sleep.

"Good morning." ʸᵏ

He drew her closer.

"Good morning." ᴹ

It was an ordinary thing to say. He had said it to her at tables, on paths, after waiting for her to answer a door.

He liked this one best.

* * *

Rimuru knocked before they had decided who would get up first.

"I've come to establish that I can also visit at inconvenient times." ᴿ

Yuka pressed her face into Mau's shoulder to stop laughing too loudly.

"We're awake," Mau called. ᴹ

"That isn't the question breakfast is asking." ᴿ

They heard him move away.

Mau sat up. Yuka caught his hand before he stood, kissed his knuckles and then released him to find the shirt he had put away himself.

She watched him look for it.

"Second shelf." ʸᵏ

"I knew that." ᴹ

"Of course." ʸᵏ

They went downstairs with the cups. Yuta glanced up when they arrived together, then moved the bread within reach of both their places. Maki asked whether the chair had survived the doorway. Mai asked about the wall.

Rimuru was already eating.

Yuka sat beside Mau, reached for the bread and began answering for herself.
