# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 40
## Chapter 5 — Before We Promise

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-27)
**Volume:** 40
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S7E48
**Continuity state:** Day813 afternoon. Household/workshop choose repair-first capacity and separate receiving/work spaces; no order accepted or construction completed. Cutter grip repair and torsion test pass an initial check, not unlimited warranty. No charged crystal use, sale, new staff/resident or boundary expansion. Mai contributes without returning to combat.

*When: Day813. Momo puts a serving tray on top of the proposed delivery schedule.*

Jinshi moved his hand before she caught his fingers beneath it.

"That's when we use this table," she said. ᴹᵒ

He looked at the tray, then crossed out the time.

Mau had come expecting a conversation about eight tools. The first argument concerned where people would put their cups if the table became a place for counting them.

Hollis was gone. His papers remained, with the cutter receipt copied beside them and Wakana's proposed times written on a separate sheet. Eight tools had become enough pages to occupy a considerable part of the afternoon before anyone made one.

Fern pulled a chair into the space Momo had left.

"Receiving stays outside," she said. "But the work shouldn't keep coming through here every time someone wants a signature." ᶠ

"The workshop doesn't need a signature for every grip," Wakana said. ᵂ

"It needs one when someone promises the grip before asking you." ᶠ

Mau found that several people were looking at him.

"I said we'd ask." ᴹ

"Good," Maki said. "Keep doing that." ᴹᵃ

* * *

Wakana put the repaired cutter on the cloth.

The grip had been fitted again and bound with the direction of the load in mind. Mau had helped test it. When the fastening held, he had been asked to stop before helping became an investigation into how much force would destroy the bench.

It had passed the first sideways-load check. Wakana wanted to repeat it after use, not send it out on the authority of one successful afternoon.

"So we tell her what changed," Jinshi said. ᴶ

"And ask her to show us if the other handle has moved," Mau said. ᴹ

Anko looked over the page containing the eight-tool inquiry.

"What happens if they all come back at once?" ᴬ

The room became quieter.

Senku leaned back in his chair.

"We find out that making eight things and supporting eight things are different jobs." ˢᵉⁿ

"I'd prefer to find that out while they're still asking," Fern said. ᶠ

* * *

Mai arrived with the repaired household strap Mau had forgotten was still waiting to be collected.

She laid it across his shoulder as she passed. He looked down at it, then up at her.

"Thank you." ᴹ

"You're welcome. That one was yours before it was a trade policy." ᴹᵃⁱ

Rimuru laughed into his cup.

They made room for her at the edge of the table. When Mau explained the inquiry, she asked how many hours they had left for their own things.

No one had a satisfactory answer until Wakana turned his page over and wrote those things down first.

A carrier. Two fastenings. An adjustment someone had been waiting to bring him because visitors kept arriving with something that looked more urgent.

"I don't mind work," he said. "I need to know which work I'm supposed to finish." ᵂ

Mau took the strap from his shoulder and set it on his knee.

He had been pleased that people wanted what they could make. It had been easy to keep feeling pleased while the waiting remained somebody else's problem.

"Household repairs keep their place," he said. ᴹ

"They need a place first," Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

Fern handed Wakana the clean page.

* * *

By the time they finished, they had agreed to considerably less than eight tools.

First, finish Tamsin's repair and send the findings through Hollis. Then give the two interested buyers a price inquiry response, with the material and time still subject to confirmation. A first small batch could follow if they accepted the limits and the workshop had completed its checks.

No promise of an open-ended supply. No need for Wakana to have every future afternoon decided for him by whoever wrote the largest number.

Jinshi read the wording aloud. Anko changed the part which sounded like they would accept any tool returned in any condition. Momo asked again when the papers would leave the meal table.

This time they had an answer.

The covered receiving space would keep visitor receipts. The workshop would keep its own measurements. The common room would have a place to leave a request instead of making every request a meeting.

"And the tray?" Momo asked. ᴹᵒ

Jinshi lifted it off the page and offered it back.

She looked at the empty tray.

"You can bring it when you come for food." ᴹᵒ

* * *

Mau stayed to put the chairs back.

Rimuru collected the last cups while Fern and Jinshi checked the times they had agreed to share. Maki had gone to find Yuta. Mai remained beside the window, turning the old piece of binding over between her fingers.

"We could make room for more people," Mau said, looking at the place where chairs had been touching backs. ᴹ

She followed his gaze.

"People or work?" ᴹᵃⁱ

He considered that.

"Both, eventually." ᴹ

"Then don't put them all in the same room and hope they sort themselves out." ᴹᵃⁱ

He laughed.

She put the worn binding beside the workshop measurements. There would be a drawing to make before there was a wall to build. Paths, meals, quiet, places for children and people who wanted to go to bed before the last conversation ended.

Mau picked up the serving tray.

For today, they could begin by giving Momo her table back.
