*When: Day814, after lunch. Mai turns the little cupboard catch over and asks who has been opening the door with their whole body.*

Mau looked at it.

"It was like that when I got here." ᴹ

"You live here." ᴹᵃⁱ

"Before lunch." ᴹ

She gave him a look which suggested he had improved the answer without making it useful.

The catch needed a pin. Wakana had already put a temporary piece through it; Mai had brought it back because the rough end kept finding sleeves. She could reshape that end with the ordinary tools, or make the small smooth piece the fitting actually wanted.

Her hand trembled as she set it down.

Mau noticed before she tucked the fingers beneath the cloth.

"This morning?" he asked. ᴹ

"The bullet." ᴹᵃⁱ

He sat on the other side of the bench.

* * *

She knew what making the pin herself would take.

That had never been the part anybody needed to explain to Mai. She knew how long her hand would shake. Knew how much of the afternoon she could still expect to keep if she waited. The smallness of the finished object did not make the work cheap.

"I'd like to try something," Mau said. ᴹ

She looked at him.

"With your technique. If you want to." ᴹ

"That is a very large beginning for a cupboard." ᴹᵃⁱ

He smiled, then put his hand on the bench where she could see it.

"You know how Yuka can draw from me. A connection like that, but for what you already do. My cursed energy supplies it. The strain goes through me instead of asking your body to carry it." ᴹ

Mai stopped turning the cloth.

"Instead of mine?" ᴹᵃⁱ

"That's what I want to test. Not tell you it's done because it sounds useful." ᴹ

She looked at the pin again.

"And what does yours do with it?" ᴹᵃⁱ

He did not say nothing.

"We'll find out with something smaller than the thing which already made your hands shake." ᴹ

* * *

Mai made him explain which part would still be hers.

The shape. The material she understood how to produce. The decision to make it at all. He was not asking to replace her technique with something which would wait for his instructions.

Nor did a supply of his energy give her all the things she had never learned to make.

"I still have to know what I'm doing," she said. ᴹᵃⁱ

"Yes." ᴹ

"How disappointing. I was hoping to become unbearable immediately." ᴹᵃⁱ

"You could try." ᴹ

She almost smiled.

Then held out her hand, palm up.

"Look at the work. Not everything else." ᴹᵃⁱ

He nodded.

Ciel answered him privately. The channel they had built before gave them somewhere to begin; Mai's technique still needed its own route. Noise worked on the accessible pattern she had allowed them to observe. Mau repeated the useful part rather than leaving her outside a conversation about her own hand.

The first connection would carry only the small demand they were about to try.

Mai listened, asked a question about stopping and waited for the answer before saying yes.

* * *

Shoko found them sitting with both their hands on the table.

"That's either very encouraging or the beginning of an explanation I won't enjoy," she said. ˢʰ

Mai told her what they were trying. Mau let her.

Shoko pulled up a chair where she could see both of them.

The first attempt gave Mai a faint sense of pressure at the start and no object at the end. She let it go. Ciel checked the point at which the supply had met the new route; Noise corrected the join rather than asking Mai to force it with what little she had left from the morning.

On the second attempt, a tiny metal washer appeared in her palm.

Mau felt a brief warmth across his wrist.

Mai stared at the washer.

Then at him.

"Again?" she asked. ᴹᵃⁱ

He checked the answer Ciel was giving him before nodding.

"The pin this time." ᴹ

* * *

She took her time with its shape.

The little rounded end mattered. The length mattered. Giving her more energy did not decide what the cupboard needed.

When the pin settled in her hand, Mau felt the weight of the demand more clearly: a pull from the cursed reserve, followed by a dull heaviness along his forearm. Enough to notice. Not enough to spoil his grip when Shoko put a cup into that hand.

Mai reached for her own cup and stopped.

Her fingers were still a little unsteady from the morning. The new work had not made them worse.

"That hasn't gone," Shoko said, watching her. "Don't confuse not adding to it with undoing it." ˢʰ

Mai nodded without looking away from Mau's hand.

"How much?" she asked him. ᴹᵃⁱ

"I felt it." ᴹ

"That wasn't the question." ᴹᵃⁱ

He flexed his fingers. The old two remained stiff. The others answered normally.

"Much less than it costs you. I can still hold this. I don't need to lie down. Ciel says the demand stayed where we meant it to." ᴹ

Shoko made him repeat the ordinary grip, then let him put the cup down.

* * *

His body had been changing for a long time.

The permanent strength was only the most obvious part of it. The paths which could deliver energy, the tissues which had learned to survive its passage, the work Noise kept refining behind the things he could already use—all of it made the same little demand land differently in him.

The energy was still spent. The load had still arrived.

Mai ran a finger across the pin's smooth end.

"So you aren't making it cost less," she said. "You're making the person paying better able to afford it." ᴹᵃⁱ

"For this. For things this small. We don't know the other end yet." ᴹ

She nodded.

Mau looked at the cupboard catch.

"I'd like you to have the choice. It doesn't have to be for things everyone else needs." ᴹ

"I know." ᴹᵃⁱ

"And it doesn't mean you have to—" ᴹ

"Fight. Yes. I was going to tell you that part." ᴹᵃⁱ

He smiled.

"I wasn't asking." ᴹ

"Good." ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

They opened the little standing connection only after she chose it.

Small work she understood could use the route without Mau having to approve every beginning. If he could not carry it, he would tell her or close the supply. If she did not want it, she could stop using it. A larger unfamiliar object would need a conversation, not faith in the small washer.

Mai fitted the pin into the catch.

The door closed without the little scrape which had started the whole afternoon.

She opened it once. Closed it again.

Mau watched her hands.

"You don't need to ask me for every little thing," he said. ᴹ

"I heard you." ᴹᵃⁱ

She took the washer too. It was real, small and ordinary, and she was unwilling to leave it behind merely because the pin had been the point of the exercise.

At the doorway she turned.

"Tomorrow. A hook for the cloth beside the sink. Plain metal, about this long." She showed him with two fingers. ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau looked at her.

"You don't have to warn me." ᴹ

"I wasn't asking permission." ᴹᵃⁱ

She went to find Maki before he could offer another version of the same argument.
