*When: Day815, morning. Mai opens the box and leaves the empty place empty.*

There were enough bullets in it.

She knew how many without counting. For a long time the daily addition had been a way to make something certain: whatever else happened, one more would exist because she had sat down and paid for it.

This morning the little place beside the others remained clear.

She closed the lid.

The hook she meant to make belonged beside the sink, where the cloth kept ending up on the edge of a bowl. She had measured the space last night. Deciding to make it felt oddly less automatic than making something which might never be fired.

She took the measurement downstairs.

* * *

Mau was carrying cups when she found him.

"The hook," she said. "For the sink. Same size I showed you." ᴹᵃⁱ

He stopped with the cups still in his hands.

"Good morning to you too." ᴹ

"Good morning. The hook." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka, beside him, began smiling.

"You don't need to tell me each time," he said. ᴹ

Mai waited until he had put the cups down. Then she made it.

He felt the small demand join the work already running inside him. His hand grew warm for a moment. It passed.

Mai turned the finished hook over.

Her fingers were steady.

She looked at them long enough for Yuka to stop smiling at the joke and understand what else had happened.

* * *

Maki came through while Mai was fitting it.

She looked at her sister's hand.

"Today?" ᴹᵃ

Mai nodded.

Maki stayed where she was while the cloth found its new place.

"And you?" she asked Mau. ᴹᵃ

He opened the hand which had felt the work. Closed it again.

"Fine. A little warmth. Nothing like yesterday's longer attempt." ᴹ

Mai glanced at him.

"Yesterday was two successful pieces and a failed beginning. Today's one hook." ᴹᵃⁱ

"She's keeping notes," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

"Someone should." ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau looked toward Maki for support and received the expression of a woman who had no intention of rescuing him from that particular accusation.

* * *

Mai made a smaller fastening later, after eating and doing something which had nothing to do with making things.

She walked out to the practice ground to tell him first.

Mau stood beside Yuka and Yuta. Two light targets waited on the ground; Yuka was checking the place from which her own pull would begin.

Mai held up a finger before anyone started.

"A fastening for the cupboard cloth. Smaller than the hook." ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau lowered his hand.

"I said I'd tell you if something was too much." ᴹ

"And I am telling you what the something is." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuta looked away, smiling.

* * *

They tried the overlapping work small.

Yuka moved her target with Blue. Mau moved the other with his own. Each effect had its own beginning; both demands returned to him through the source they already shared.

Mai made the fastening.

For a moment Mau felt three distinct arrivals: the familiar demand of his own operation, Yuka's draw beside it and the new little weight running down his forearm.

He kept the target moving.

Ciel gave him the useful distinction quietly. Mai was making her own object. Yuka was controlling her own technique. Supplying both had not put three different techniques in Mau's hands for him to perform at once.

The load still met in the same body.

He let his target settle before the last mark.

"I noticed that more," he said. ᴹ

Mai looked at the fastening.

"Pain?" ᴹᵃⁱ

"No. Like remembering I'm carrying a bag when someone puts one more thing in it." ᴹ

Yuka released her pull.

The difference was immediate enough for him to point it out without being asked.

* * *

Maki had come to see whether they meant to return for the next meal.

She listened while he explained the three demands, then looked at the targets.

"And how is the wear? Afterward, not just while you're pleased it worked." ᴹᵃ

Mau considered it.

"I'm not as fresh as before. I can still do ordinary things. I wouldn't judge a bigger fight by whether I could carry this much." ᴹ

Mai put the fastening into her pocket.

"Then we don't add another one right now." ᴹᵃⁱ

"You don't have to make every small thing a—" ᴹ

"I'm not. I'm going to eat." ᴹᵃⁱ

She took Maki with her.

Yuta followed, still smiling.

* * *

Umaru caught Mau before he put the targets away.

She had heard enough of the last exchange to be carrying a question instead of the lunch message she had originally been sent with.

"How much do you actually have?" ᵁ

He looked at her.

"Lunch?" ᴹ

"Energy. Compared with people. Mai. Yuta. Gojo. Not compared with how worried everyone looks when you say you're fine." ᵁ

Yuka folded her arms, interested now.

Mau looked down at his hands.

He had enough answers to make three things work and not enough to tell her where the far end of the supply really lay.

"That's more than one question," he said. ᴹ

"Then bring more than one person." ᵁ

She took one of the light targets before he could turn carrying everything into an alternative to coming inside.
