## Chapter 12 — By the Road

*When: Day823. Yuka checks that the repaired cutter is tied down before asking Mau whether he has remembered his own bag.*

He looked over his shoulder.

She waited.

"It's here." ᴹ

"I can see it. I was asking whether you remembered it." ʸᵏ

Anko passed them with the note containing the meeting time.

"If you've finished packing each other, we can go." ᴬ

Mikasa had already checked the route message. Hollis had carried the reply before they set the day; Tamsin would meet them at the familiar clearing with the second cutter and a sample of the work she had been doing. Wakana wanted the tools in front of him when they spoke.

Rimuru stayed by the door until Mau looked back.

"I'll be here," he said. ᴿ

Mau raised a hand.

Yuka caught the loose end of the tie before it could strike his sleeve and tucked it away. Then they followed the others onto the path.

* * *

Beyond the protected grounds, the road made its own demands.

Roots had lifted one section since the last time the rain had left it soft. A cart had gone around the worst place and made another, which Mikasa examined before choosing the firmer side for them.

Igris moved ahead with the two ordinary soldiers Mau had called for the route. They kept enough distance from the clearing that the people waiting there would not find an armored figure standing silently behind their backs.

Mau had given the order before leaving the house. Anko checked it with him where the path divided, then continued beside Wakana.

Yuka walked on Mau's right.

She could have asked Nue to take them over the broken section. Instead she helped him find where the cart rut ended, and they both stepped badly into the same patch of mud.

He looked down at their boots.

She looked at him.

"You first," she said. ʸᵏ

"I thought you were showing me." ᴹ

They heard Anko laugh without turning around.

* * *

Tamsin had put her bundle on the old marker before they arrived.

She greeted Wakana first, then looked at the repaired cutter as he began untying it. Hollis stood beside a stack of things whose arrangement suggested he had already used the waiting time to conduct another piece of business.

"You brought the whole committee," he said. ᴴᵒ

"They wanted to see me leave the house without promising you eight tools," Mau said. ᴹ

"A reasonable concern." ᴴᵒ

Wakana laid the cutter on the cloth and showed Tamsin what he had changed. She checked the grip, found the repaired fastening and asked which load they had repeated.

He demonstrated slowly with empty hands.

She copied it, then changed the angle near the end.

"There. Once the cut has already started." ᵀᵃ

Wakana watched her wrist. Yuka could see him remembering it rather than pretending he had expected the exact movement.

Tamsin unwrapped the sample she had brought: recovered material held against a board so the tool could reach the tough part near the edge without her other hand doing something foolish behind it.

"May I?" she asked. ᵀᵃ

He moved back and gave her room.

* * *

The first cut went straight.

The second included the late turn. Tamsin stopped, checked the grip and repeated the movement with less help from her first, careful posture.

Mau watched the handle, resisting the urge to ask whether it had moved.

She put it down and picked up the second cutter.

This one had not loosened. The binding showed wear, and she let Wakana examine it where it rested rather than holding the blade while he tried the fastening.

"Same warning signs?" she asked. ᵀᵃ

"This one hasn't begun to turn," he said. "If it does, don't keep tightening over it. Send it back before you need both hands to hold one handle still." ᵂ

She looked at the repaired one again.

"You could put that in the note you send with them." ᵀᵃ

Wakana glanced at Mau.

Mau was already reaching for the folded page in his bag.

* * *

Tamsin accepted the repaired cutter after making the condition plain: she would tell them if it moved during ordinary work, not wait until the entire grip had become a separate object.

They recorded the return and the second cutter's inspection. Hollis checked the transport share they had agreed to cover. That part belonged to the repair, and Jinshi had included it in the envelope instead of leaving Mau to improvise another agreement on the road.

Anko watched the last signatures, then looked toward the waiting travellers beyond Hollis's things.

One man had been looking at the gap between the trees where Igris had passed. He noticed her noticing and turned to the wrapped goods too quickly.

Mau saw it as well.

He did not call the soldier closer to demonstrate that there was nothing to fear. He held the receipt while Tamsin found a flat place to write.

The man waited until she finished before asking Hollis whether those were the tools.

Tamsin picked up her cutter.

"These are mine," she said. "You can ask about yours after I've put them away." ᵀᵃ

Yuka's fingers touched Mau's wrist.

He looked at her.

She was trying not to laugh at his expression. He had been preparing for a very different sort of conversation.

For the moment, she only needed him to hold the bag open.
