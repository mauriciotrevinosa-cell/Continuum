## Chapter 13 — What the Letter Can Say

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-27)

Specifications/capacity/final price await household confirmation; Hollis remains intermediary. A single scheduled road-keeper contact proposed, not blanket town permit. Tamsin cutter returned, inspection/repair transport share settled from household money, no new proceeds/crystals/units. Party returns with no new residents.

*When: Day823, later. The first interested buyer asks when he can collect four, and Wakana turns the page so that the smaller number faces him.*

"One first," he said. ᵂ

The man read it. His companion leaned over to read with him.

Hollis waited beside the marker, leaving the makers to explain the parts which belonged to their work. The route had enough merchants already willing to promise what somebody else would have to make.

Mau stood at the other end of the cloth. Yuka had taken out the notebook she used for practical things which did not fit beside a domain diagram. She copied the first man's description of what he needed to cut, then asked him to show her the shape with his hands.

The two descriptions were not the same.

Wakana looked from one man to the other.

"Then they won't be the same handle." ᵂ

* * *

They had brought an answer, not eight finished cutters.

Mau had been glad of that on the walk. He was glad of it again when the second man changed his request after seeing how Tamsin held her tool.

Wakana drew the new position. Yuka repeated the question about use after an hour rather than the first neat cut. The man shifted his hand, looked at what his wrist was doing and became more interested in the answer.

It took longer than being told the edge was excellent.

By the end, they had two sets of measurements to take back, with one first piece for each man subject to the household confirming the final terms. The larger quantities stayed on the inquiry page. No payment changed hands for tools which had not been made or assigned a final price.

The first man asked if a good first piece meant the rest could come quickly.

Wakana looked at his own hands.

"It means I know whether the first piece was good." ᵂ

Tamsin made a satisfied sound from where she was packing.

* * *

Mikasa came back from the edge of the clearing when the road was ready for them again.

Anko had spent the last part of the conversation with one of the local men responsible for the next stretch. The letters they already exchanged dealt with notice and visits; they did not give every person from the house the same right to walk through every gate along the road.

He wanted to know whether there would be more deliveries.

"If there are, we'd like to stop explaining the same route at every marker," she said. ᴬ

The man looked toward Mau.

Mau waited until he was addressed rather than treating the glance as a summons to negotiate for everybody.

"Names in advance," the man said. "So the people at the next post know who they've been told to expect." ˡ

"And know what we've actually agreed," Anko said. ᴬ

He hesitated, then nodded.

It was a beginning of a letter. It was not permission for the whole house to visit the whole world.

Hollis offered to carry it when the road-keeper had written what he was willing to stand behind. Anko gave him the name of the person at home who would receive it. No one announced that the matter had been settled before either side had read it.

* * *

On the way back, Yuka walked beside Wakana until he had finished explaining the difference between the two new grips.

Then she drifted back to Mau.

"You didn't promise them anything bigger." ʸᵏ

"Everyone keeps saying that like they're surprised." ᴹ

"I'm enjoying it." ʸᵏ

He offered her the lighter side of the bag. She looked inside before taking it.

The cutter had stayed with Tamsin. In its place were ordinary return papers, the cloth and the little thing Yuka had kept moving away from the knot each time he tried to close it.

He had not asked about it yet.

"You bought something?" ᴹ

She pushed the cloth over it.

"You can wait until we're home." ʸᵏ

He began smiling.

"I wasn't asking." ᴹ

"You were looking very politely." ʸᵏ

The memory of her anniversary voice caught him so precisely that he laughed out loud.

Ahead of them, Anko told Mikasa they were going to spend the entire walk listening to this.

Mikasa looked back once, then took the easier side of the path so Wakana would not have to carry the rolled drawings through the branches.

* * *

Rimuru was in the common room when they arrived.

Mau put the delivery copy into Jinshi's hands before taking his own bag upstairs. Anko gave the route conversation separately, making it clear which part still needed a letter from the road-keeper.

Wakana set the new measurements beside the household work he had promised to finish first.

Nothing needed to be decided over the meal.

When Yuka came back from their room, she found Mau waiting by the stairs and put a small paper-wrapped package in his hand.

"For tomorrow," she said. ʸᵏ

He turned it over.

Something ordinary from the road stalls. Something she had chosen while he was listening to the practical limits of a handle.

"Mysterious," he said. ᴹ

"I'm trying it." ʸᵏ
