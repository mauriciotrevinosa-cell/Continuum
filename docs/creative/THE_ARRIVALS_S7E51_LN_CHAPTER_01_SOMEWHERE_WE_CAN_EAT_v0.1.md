There were two little sweet cakes.

One had been pressed against the paper during the walk, and she looked at it with the expression of somebody considering whether there was a way to pretend it had been made like that.

Mau took that one.

"You don't have to," she said. ʸᵏ

"I want to know how it tastes without structural integrity." ᴹ

She laughed and pulled the paper open between them.

They sat beyond the busiest part of the house, close enough to hear someone calling for a missing cup and far enough away not to be the first people asked to find it. The path was dry. Mau set the map on his knee with a clean piece of wrapping beneath it.

Yuka looked at the blank space after the bird.

"We could put the marker there," she said. ʸᵏ

He considered it.

"Would it need the handle?" ᴹ

"Everything important in our lives appears to need a handle." ʸᵏ

She drew while he held the edge of the page still.

* * *

The drawing was small.

A post, the bend of the road and two marks beside a patch she insisted was mud. Mau suggested that could describe most of the world. She gave one of the small figures boots large enough to make the intended memory unmistakable.

He wrote the day beside it after asking where she wanted the number.

Then she took the pencil back and added a tiny bundle between them.

"The mysterious package," he said. ᴹ

"An important landmark." ʸᵏ

He looked at the cake already disappearing from her hand.

"Rapidly becoming historic." ᴹ

She moved the other half out of his reach and ate it before he could finish smiling.

* * *

They remained after the food was gone.

Yuka read the first page again. He had imagined that receiving the map would make it his to read while she watched, but she kept finding her way back through it too. Sometimes she laughed at the same sentence before he reached it.

"I thought about the space downstairs," she said after a while. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

She kept her finger beside the drawing of the chair.

"Now that we have our room. I wondered what we'd still want downstairs, when everyone is there." ʸᵏ

He waited.

"Somewhere we could sit downstairs without someone asking if we were waiting for food." ʸᵏ

"We are usually waiting for food." ᴹ

"That's why it's difficult." ʸᵏ

He leaned his shoulder against hers. She let the map close over her finger.

"I like having everyone there," she said. "I don't want every meal to become us carrying two plates away while people pretend they aren't looking." ʸᵏ

He looked back toward the common room. Someone had found the cup. A different voice wanted to know why it had been put there in the first place.

"A place we can use when we want to," he said. ᴹ

She nodded.

"And come out of when we want to." ʸᵏ

Their room had already begun collecting the small habits neither had known to ask about before living together. Mau had learned which book Yuka wanted within reach at night; she had learned where he would leave his shoes if she did not move the basket there first. Rimuru still had his loft in the old room. A quiet place downstairs could belong to an afternoon with anyone, without making the bedroom the only alternative to a crowded table.

"We could tell them that when there's a drawing," Mau said. ᴹ

Yuka turned her head.

"You won't decide it's built before I see it?" ʸᵏ

"I've become very difficult to sell eight rooms to." ᴹ

She laughed against his shoulder.

* * *

He felt her looking at him before she spoke again.

"Did the company help? The other night." ʸᵏ

Mau looked down at the folded paper in their hands.

"Yes." ᴹ

"You don't always tell me when you've had a bad part of the day." ʸᵏ

He began to answer, then stopped. She noticed the stop and stayed where she was.

He could say he did not want to spoil this. He had said versions of that before. The cakes were gone, the drawing was done and she was asking him a question rather than waiting for him to choose the perfect time.

"I don't know how much to bring with me when I come to you," he said. ᴹ

Yuka lowered the map to their knees.

"You already bring it." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She seemed nervous now, but continued.

"I can tell when you stop hearing a joke. I don't always know why. You can tell me you've had a bad day without deciding you're going to make mine bad too." ʸᵏ

The breeze lifted the loose wrapping. He caught it before it could cross the page.

"I can try," he said. ᴹ

She accepted the answer without making him enlarge it.

"You can also tell me when you had a good one. I enjoy taking credit." ʸᵏ

He laughed, a little unsteadily.

"This one was good." ᴹ

"Obviously." ʸᵏ

* * *

When he kissed her, she put the map safely beside them first.

She came closer without needing him to ask a second time, one hand holding his shoulder while the other settled at the back of his neck. He could feel the warmth of her thumb move just below his hair.

After a moment she smiled against his mouth.

He moved back far enough to see her face.

"What?" ᴹ

"You taste like the broken cake." ʸᵏ

He stared at her, then began laughing so hard that she had to hold his sleeve to keep him from moving entirely away.

"I was being very romantic," he said. ᴹ

"I liked it." ʸᵏ

"You called me structurally unsound." ᴹ

She drew him back by the sleeve.

"Come here." ʸᵏ

He did.

* * *

By the time they went inside, Yuta had started looking for an extra bowl.

Yuka took it from him, pointed at the places they wanted and began setting them out. Mau put the map safely away before helping with the rest.

They ate with the family.

Her knee touched his beneath the table, and when he glanced at her she was listening to something Tsurugi had begun explaining far too confidently to stop now.

She found his hand without turning away from her brother.

Mau let the conversation go on around them and kept his fingers where she had put them.
