Rimuru leaned over to read the page. Mau remained on the other side of the bench, keeping the lamp housing upright while the two of them decided which part of the afternoon deserved to be recorded first.

Shoko pulled her chair clear of the route people needed to use.

Senku did, with enough force on the pencil to make clear that he was being extremely reasonable about it.

* * *

The light used the method they already knew.

The first path test had given them a real end time rather than the length of an evening everyone wished it would last. This afternoon's question concerned the housing and the demand they put through the insert, not whether they could make a light come out of a crystal at all.

Jinshi had marked one further small piece out of usable stock before handing it over. Two hundred and forty-eight remained. The older pylon pieces and the larger rewards stayed where they were; nobody had decided a better lamp justified experimenting with all of them.

Mau held the housing while Senku checked the fitting. Byakuya made him move his thumb away from the part which would grow warm.

"It won't hurt him," Senku said. ˢᵉⁿ

"Then he'll make it look safe for someone else." ᴮʸ

Mau moved his hand.

Shoko looked pleased enough to make him suspect she intended to use that sentence again.

* * *

At first they asked for too much light.

It was easy to enjoy the result. The corner of the work area became clear, the writing on the far page legible without carrying an ordinary lamp across the bench.

It was also making one part of the housing warmer faster than Senku wanted.

He lowered the demand before anyone had to pull a hand away.

Rimuru checked the change through the quiet work he and Ciel could share, then told the others what belonged in the practical result. Mau heard her own more detailed answer privately and kept the extra information from becoming five new subjects at once.

"Can it hold this setting?" Byakuya asked. ᴮʸ

"That's what we're waiting to find out," Senku said. ˢᵉⁿ

"Excellent. I have a chair." ᴮʸ

Shoko moved hers far enough to give him space.

* * *

Waiting turned out to contain several small jobs.

The housing needed to remain clear of loose cloth. The page needed a place where nobody could set a cup on it. Somebody had to check the light without staring at it until ordinary dimness seemed like failure.

Mau offered to hold everything.

Senku gave him the piece which actually needed holding and told him to leave the rest on the bench.

After a while, Shoko asked him to relax his hand.

He looked at it. The stiff fingers had been working harder than he needed them to.

He shifted the housing to the other hand. She watched him do it, then went back to the comparison notes without declaring the experiment over.

"Mai's work?" Rimuru asked quietly. ᴿ

Mau shook his head.

"Holding this badly. She told me she's making a fastening after supper." ᴹ

Shoko looked up.

"And that arrangement, after a few weeks?" ˢʰ

"Still small things. I notice the longer ones. She still tells me first." ᴹ

Rimuru smiled at the particular resignation in the last sentence. Shoko let him keep the housing, with the better grip.

Rimuru caught the look on his face.

"You can be useful sitting down," he said. ᴿ

"I'm being exceptionally useful sitting down." ᴹ

"We know. Senku would like his housing back eventually." ᴿ

* * *

By the later part of the test, people had begun using the light without stopping to ask about it.

A label was read. A dropped fastening was found. The person carrying it went away grateful for the fastening rather than impressed by the five people beside the lamp.

Mau watched that happen twice before he stopped expecting someone to come over and admire the result.

Byakuya wrote down when he first needed to lean closer to the page. Senku checked the insert instead of immediately demanding the earlier brightness back. The light was fading at a lower demand than the first setting, and the housing had remained within the limits they had marked.

That was a result. It was not a promise that the next crystal would last forever.

They let the test finish.

* * *

When the insert went quiet, Mau kept his hand in place until Senku told him which part could be moved.

Byakuya carried the page to the ordinary lamp so the last entry would not be written by guesswork. The spent piece went into its separate container. Shoko put the cloths away; Rimuru took the cups before somebody used the clean part of the table to begin another problem.

Senku looked at the notes.

"We can try a better fitting next time." ˢᵉⁿ

"After dinner," Shoko said. ˢʰ

He looked at her.

She pointed toward the common room, where someone had already called once.

Byakuya stood with the stiffness of a person who had successfully spent too much of an afternoon in the chair he had been so pleased to possess.

Mau offered a hand. He took it without turning the moment into a competition over who needed less help.

Rimuru waited while Mau put the housing down.

They went inside together, and Senku brought the page because he was going to explain the new fitting whether or not anyone had asked him to.
