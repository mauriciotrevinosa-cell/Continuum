# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 40
## Chapter 19 — The Hand He Was Watching

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-27)
**Volume:** 40
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S7E54
**Continuity state:** Day850. Mau/Yuta wooden-sword drill, permanent physique retained and no technique/weapon-stat addition. Existing katana and daggers stored, not surrendered or disabled. Follow-up household proposal for limited route scouts, no new shadow units or universal surveillance. Exact vessel words remain private; repaired Yuta/Mau friendship intact.

*When: Day850. Yuta touches the wooden blade to Mau's wrist for the third time and waits for him to notice which hand he is watching.*

Mau lowered the practice sword.

"I saw it that time." ᴹ

"You saw the blade." ʸᵗ

Yuta stepped back. Mau had enough reach to make the same exchange a very different problem if he committed his strength to it. That was not the exercise. They had agreed on pace and contact before beginning, and the wooden swords had survived because both of them remembered.

He reset his feet. Yuta let him begin.

The first movement drew Mau's attention toward the point. The second came before he had finished deciding where the first would end.

Wood touched his wrist again.

He looked at Yuta's other hand.

Yuta smiled.

"There." ʸᵗ

* * *

They slowed it down.

Yuta made the turn without bringing the blade forward. Mau followed the change through the wrist, shoulder and foot behind it. He could see each piece of the movement very clearly. His own body still wanted to answer the part which looked most like an attack.

"You keep expecting the important thing to announce itself," Yuta said. ʸᵗ

"It would be polite." ᴹ

Yuta laughed and gave him the next movement slowly enough that he could choose a different answer.

Mau moved less. His blade met Yuta's before the point had drawn him off the line.

It happened once, then failed twice, then happened again when he stopped trying to reproduce the successful exchange exactly.

By the end, his shoulders were warm and his good hand had begun gripping too hard.

Yuta noticed. He lowered his sword first.

"Enough?" ʸᵗ

Mau opened his hand.

"Enough." ᴹ

* * *

They sat where the edge of the practice ground met the path. Yuta laid both wooden swords beside the water, safely beyond the place someone would put a foot down without looking.

Mau rubbed the mark on his wrist.

"Yuka will ask." ᴹ

"Tell her I won." ʸᵗ

"You want me to lie?" ᴹ

Yuta looked at the mark.

Mau drank his water before continuing the argument.

It was good to have one. Something small enough that Yuta could smile through it without either of them checking whether the other was still angry about an old room, an old secret or the person Mau loved.

Yuta leaned back on one hand.

"You're thinking about the road reports." ʸᵗ

Mau looked at him.

"You asked about them before picking up the sword." ʸᵗ

"One party hadn't been confirmed back. The other report might not even be the same kind of opening." ᴹ

"Hollis said he'd find out." ʸᵗ

"I know. I keep wondering how many places we're waiting to hear about because we only have one person travelling that road." ᴹ

Yuta looked toward the house. A soldier carried an empty crate across the far path without being followed by someone whose whole morning depended on the crate.

"Then ask about more eyes on the road," he said. "Not more mornings where you go and become all of them yourself." ʸᵗ

* * *

Mau brought the question to Rimuru after lunch.

Rimuru asked him to bring it to the people who would be receiving the reports. By the time they sat down, Mikasa had a route sheet, Anko had three objections and Fern wanted to know whether anyone intended to put another permanent duty on the same two people.

Mau put his proposed patrol marks on the table.

Existing shadows. Short, defined routes beyond the places they already checked. Observe the road and dangerous openings, keep clear of travellers unless help was needed, return at a known time. Recognition of someone connected to the household would be a report to verify, not permission to take a person out of a life they might want to keep.

Anko moved one of his marks.

"That's someone's house." ᴬ

"I meant the crossing beside it." ᴹ

"Then mark the crossing." ᴬ

He did.

* * *

They worked out what a scout could do when the person it found was in trouble.

Immediate danger called for immediate help. Afterward, the person got to choose whether to come with them, walk with an escort or refuse. A shadow exchange could save a life; it did not become the default way to collect relatives who had not yet agreed to move.

For an uncertain report, watch from a respectful distance and bring back what could actually be seen. For someone hostile, disengage and report rather than lead them toward the house. For someone known and safe, send word before arriving with them at a bedroom door.

Fern wrote down who would review the first reports and who would cover when they were out.

"And nothing inside private rooms," Rimuru said. ᴿ

Mau nodded.

"I want to know what's happening beyond us. I don't want everybody here to feel watched." ᴹ

He looked at Yuta, who had stayed at the end of the table after bringing their cups.

Yuta tapped the route, just where Mau had moved his first mark.

"Watch the thing you mean to watch." ʸᵗ

Mau began laughing before anyone else knew why.

* * *

Yuka found him putting the wooden swords away later.

She took his wrist gently and examined the mark.

"Grandpa?" ʸᵏ

"He asked me to say he won." ᴹ

"Did he?" ʸᵏ

Mau looked down at the neat line across his skin.

"Repeatedly." ᴹ

She kissed the unmarked side, then let him finish putting the equipment where it belonged.

"You can tell him I was very impressed." ʸᵏ
