*When: Day856. The two cutters lie beside each other, and Mau reaches for the wrong one.*

Marin caught his sleeve before his hand reached the cloth.

"That one isn't for you." ᴹᵃʳ

"Neither of them is for me." ᴹ

"Then this is going to be easy to remember." ᴹᵃʳ

Wakana smiled without looking up from the second fastening. Mai held the drawing where he could compare it with the hand guard. The two pieces looked close enough that someone standing at the receiving table might ask why making both had taken more time than making one twice.

Mau could see the difference once Marin put the first grip into his hand.

It expected a different wrist position. He tried the movement at an ordinary pace while Wakana watched the fit rather than the edge.

"Stop there," Wakana said. ᵂ

Mau stopped.

Mai made a small mark on the drawing.

* * *

The cutting tests used the material they had retained for comparison.

The new plates from the last dungeon waited on another shelf, labelled by source. Nobody had used their promising appearance as a reason to change the two tools after the price was agreed.

Marin took the first turn with the better-fitting grip. Mai checked the movement from beside the board. Mau held what needed holding and kept his strength out of the answer.

The edge went through.

They inspected it, changed the angle within the intended work and repeated the test. Then Wakana checked the fastening before they tried the next piece.

It was slower than a demonstration in front of a buyer.

By the time they finished, the successful cuts were only part of what had been written down.

* * *

Mai took a step back from the table and rolled her shoulders.

Mau moved her chair closer with his foot.

She sat without pretending she had not wanted it.

"You could have asked earlier," he said. ᴹ

"You could have stopped hovering earlier." ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked down at himself.

"I'm on the floor." ᴹ

"Emotionally." ᴹᵃⁱ

Marin laughed. Wakana put the cutter down before joining her.

Mau sat on the spare stool and accepted that he had walked into this one.

The work had asked for Mai's eye and hands, for the part she wanted to do. No one had suggested that helping a blade exist meant she owed them a return to carrying one into a fight.

She glanced at the second cutter, then at Wakana.

"That guard looks less like a drawing now." ᴹᵃⁱ

He turned it toward the light.

"I think he'll know where his hand goes." ᵂ

"Good. We can stop explaining his hand to him." ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

The delivery notice went out before the tools did.

Names, day, meeting place. Anko checked the three-post arrangement and sent through the contact they had been given. The return answer set the meeting for Day862, leaving time for the people at the far end to be told rather than discovering the plan when the party arrived.

Jinshi wrote the expected payment on the delivery page, then left the received column empty.

Mau watched him separate the test notes from the part which would travel with the cutters.

"They'll actually use these," he said. ᴹ

Jinshi looked up.

"That's the hope." ᴶ

Mau touched the worn cloth they had used to carry Tamsin's return.

Wakana wrapped the first new tool in a clean one and folded the instructions over it.

Then he wrapped the second.
