## Chapter 24 — The Turn Before the Charge

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-27)
**Volume:** 40

*When: Day868. The deer steps across the first mark without lowering its head, and Yuka has to make herself wait.*

It was larger than her drawing had made it feel.

The antlers changed the space above the body. The hooves placed themselves quietly. Behind that stillness, she could feel the output beginning to move, something unlike the native calls which had been familiar to her for months.

Mau stood where they had agreed. Beyond the working boundary, Yuta and Maki remained still.

Yuka moved the rope into her right hand.

The deer turned toward the movement.

* * *

She did not begin with the ox.

She wanted to see the turn, the place where it changed its weight and the opening which would let a larger impact meet the body instead of the antlers. The first approach gave her none of those things.

It moved sooner than she expected.

She stepped clear. The rope brushed one antler and slipped away before she could put her own weight behind it.

Mau shifted to keep the retreat open. His shoulder met the side of the body long enough to turn the rush past her rather than into her.

The deer struck the stone with one hoof and corrected.

Yuka had time to draw breath.

She had not yet earned the charge.

* * *

The next exchange left a small injury along the deer's flank, where the turning body met the hard edge of the room.

Yuka saw it begin to close.

The pressure around the creature altered the feel of the cursed energy she had been gathering into a reinforced hold. She let that idea go before it could become the part of the plan on which her balance depended.

Ordinary rope remained ordinary rope.

She threw the loop lower, let it reach the place it could catch and moved before the deer had finished deciding whether to follow her.

"Now," she said. ʸᵏ

Mau came in at the side they had marked. He did not use Blue to make the answer neat. He took the short physical turn, allowing the forward weight to continue while changing where the front of the body could put its next hoof.

The loop held for one breath.

Yuka used it.

* * *

The ox's line began well behind her.

She had left that ground clear instead of trying to make the call fit into whatever space remained. Its first steps came heavy and straight. She kept herself out of the run and watched the deer rather than the creature she had just summoned.

The loop began to slip.

Mau changed his footing. His hand scraped the rough stone as he caught the last part of the turn.

Yuka almost spoke too early.

She waited until his other hand opened, the signal that he was leaving the line.

Then released the rope and committed the charge.

* * *

The impact arrived before the deer could regain the angle it wanted.

The larger unbound form broke under the force she had made room to gather. The antlers vanished with it; the presence which had been pressing against her next move withdrew into the technique she was struggling to keep organized.

Yuka recalled the ox.

For a moment she remained standing with an empty loop in her hand and no creature at the end of it.

Mau was out of the line.

She checked that before sitting down.

* * *

Maki came in only after Yuka signalled that the ritual had ended.

Yuta brought the water. Mau sat beside the place Yuka had chosen, keeping his scraped hand away from the rope so she could see what had happened to it.

"Stone," he said before she asked. ᴹ

She looked at the mark, then at the empty ground where he had been holding the turn.

"You waited for me," she said. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

"You had a plan." ᴹ

She drank, lowered the cup and laughed once because she had too much relief in her chest to decide whether she wanted to sound pleased.

Maki crouched far enough away to leave her room.

"How much have you got left?" ᴹᵃ

Yuka listened to her own body before answering.

"Enough to walk. Not enough to prove everything else." ʸᵏ

"Good answer." ᴹᵃ

* * *

The controlled call would wait until morning.

She said so herself when Mau began gathering the markers. He looked pleased by the decision, then more pleased when she asked him to carry the heavier bag while she kept the rope.

At the door, Yuta asked what she would change if she had to make that turn again.

Yuka looked back at the room.

"The first mark. I wanted it where I could see it, not where the deer would put its foot." ʸᵏ

He nodded.

Mau waited while she moved the mark on the drawing.

Then they went home without asking the room for a second victory.
