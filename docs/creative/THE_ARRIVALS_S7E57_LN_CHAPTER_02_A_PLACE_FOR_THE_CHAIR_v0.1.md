# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 40
## Chapter 24 — A Place for the Chair

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-27)
**Volume:** 40
**Reading order:** 24
**Source episode:** S7E57
**Continuity state:** Day875 afternoon–evening. First agreed portion of two-tool proceeds buys ordinary timber/fastenings/cloth, recorded without new currency invention or crystal trade. Existing common-room edge reorganized as quiet shared alcove, no exterior expansion/root intrusion/bedroom moves. New scouts first route trial deployed with six existing ordinary and Igris coordination, all return; no new units or transport consent violation. Couple shares practical work and affection; small future space not exclusive property.

*When: Day875. Yuka moves the chair two hand-widths, sits down and tells Mau that the door still hits her knee.*

He looked at the door.

The chair.

Her knee, which she presented as decisive evidence.

"We could move the door," he said. ᴹ

Wakana looked up from the fastening he was about to fit.

"We could move the chair," he said. ᵂ

Yuka covered her mouth.

Mau pulled the chair farther into the little space they were making at the edge of the common room. It had been occupied by things nobody meant to store there permanently. Moving them had revealed a corner large enough to sit in without becoming the first person to answer every question at the meal table.

It had also revealed how many people had intended to use the word *temporary* for the rest of their lives.

Mai carried the last empty box out.

"Look. A wall," she said. "We had one all along." ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

The new timber, fastenings and cloth had come through an ordinary purchase from the first agreed part of the tool income. Jinshi had kept the replacement-material money separate before anybody chose the nice-looking part.

This was not a new wing. Nothing crossed the roots or changed the walls which kept the sleeping rooms quiet upstairs. Wakana fitted a narrow shelf; Marin checked how the cloth would hang without trailing through the path. The small table remained movable so the space could hold more than one sort of afternoon.

Fern wanted it clear by meals if someone needed the route. Bocchi wanted to know whether talking there would still be heard from the music-room door. Maomao asked which side people intended to set hot cups on.

By the time they finished answering, the chair had moved again.

Yuka sat in it a third time and pushed the door through its full opening with one foot.

Her knee survived.

* * *

Mau went to receive the first route-trial report while the cloth was being adjusted.

Six ordinary scouts, already part of the legion. Igris had coordinated the two short routes they agreed to try rather than turning one success into an expedition beyond every mark on the page.

All six returned.

One had waited at a crossing until a cart passed instead of following it toward a private yard. Another brought the position of a damaged stretch of path. No familiar face found, no request for rescue, no new portal close enough to the checked route to identify.

Mau kept the empty parts of the report empty. He asked for a route correction where the ground had made the return slower, then recalled the scouts rather than sending them straight out again to make the afternoon feel productive.

When he went back, Yuka had a piece of cloth over one shoulder and a very serious expression.

"Marin says you're needed." ʸᵏ

"For what?" ᴹ

"Holding this where she can see it." ʸᵏ

He took the cloth.

It required less authority than commanding six soldiers and considerably more attention to where his hands were.

* * *

They finished before dinner.

Umaru came to inspect the corner and immediately objected to its distance from the Fort. Ori sat on the chair to see whether she could read without needing to reach the table. Frieren examined the shelf with the interest of somebody who saw an unfinished argument about books wherever an empty surface appeared.

"No claiming it," Fern said. ᶠ

Frieren looked up.

"I was only looking." ᶠʳ

Yuka leaned toward Mau.

"Very politely," she whispered. ʸᵏ

He had to turn away before Frieren could decide whether they were laughing at her.

* * *

The first people to use the corner after they had put the tools away were not Mau and Yuka.

Coco's mother sat with a little mending she wanted to finish. Richeh took the other chair and asked whether the new cloth would move much when somebody passed. The answer became an experiment involving the doorway and several ordinary steps.

Mau found Yuka watching from the edge of the table.

"We built somewhere to sit and now we can't sit there," he said. ᴹ

"We can wait." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She touched his sleeve.

"I like that they wanted it too." ʸᵏ

* * *

Later, after the meal, they found the chairs empty.

Yuka brought her book. Mau brought the map, still wrapped to keep the edge from catching against his bag. She watched him put it on the new shelf, then looked pleased when he took it down again to show her where he intended to add the corner.

"No more drawings of my boots," he said. ᴹ

"I make no promises." ʸᵏ

He sat beside her and let her examine the blank part of the page.

When he kissed her, she caught his cuff so he would stay near after it ended.

"This was a good idea," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at the door, which opened without touching either of them.

"We could still move it." ᴹ

She laughed against his shoulder.

Across the room, Wakana looked up with the expression of a man who had heard enough to be worried.
