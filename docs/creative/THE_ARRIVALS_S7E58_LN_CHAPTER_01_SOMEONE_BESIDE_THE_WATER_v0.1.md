# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 40
## Chapter 27 — Someone Beside the Water

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-27)
**Volume:** 40
**Reading order:** 27
**Source episode:** S7E58
**Continuity state:** Day882 afternoon. One existing route scout reports wounded woman outside protected perimeter, observes without abduction. Mau/Anko/Mikasa respond, Rimuru remains home; no attack crosses protection, no shadow destroyed/new enemy defeated. DIO arrives as same Goddess in vulnerable body, identity recognized by voice and personal reference, no avatar distinction. Initial assistance/consent, complete account still pending.

*When: Day882. The report reaches Mau while Yuka is explaining why the smaller drawing is supposed to be the bigger deer.*

He had been trying not to smile at the page.

Then the road was there instead: the angle a scout had been told to observe, water below a broken stretch of bank, a woman holding one hand against her side. The image carried no explanation of who she was.

Mau put the cup down.

Yuka stopped speaking.

"Someone's hurt," he said. ᴹ

He gave the report where Rimuru could hear it. Anko asked for the position. Mikasa was already looking for the route sheet when he repeated the place the scout had actually seen, keeping the things it had not seen out of the answer.

The woman had noticed it. She had asked whether somebody could come.

The scout had not touched her.

* * *

Rimuru stayed home.

Yuka took Mau's hand once before he went, checked that he was looking at her and let go when he said he would send word before changing the route. This was a response to a person asking for help, not a reason for him to vanish toward every new patch of dark ground.

He went with Anko and Mikasa. The scout waited near the water, keeping enough distance that the woman could see where it was without needing to turn every time it moved.

She had reached a firmer part of the bank.

Mau saw the blood first. A torn sleeve. Mud dried along the hem. The hand at her side was shaking with the effort of staying there.

She looked up when they approached.

"You came," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

He stopped.

* * *

Anko noticed before asking what had happened.

Mau knew the voice. It belonged above a table where he had once thought he could understand a bargain if he asked clever enough questions. It belonged in a room where everyone had heard an apology arrive from someone they could not see.

It did not belong to a woman struggling to take her weight off an injured leg beside a stream.

She tried to smile and failed halfway through.

"I'd prefer not to discuss cookies until I'm standing," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

Mikasa looked at Mau.

He crouched where the woman could see his hands.

"Is this you?" ᴹ

The answer came without the usual play in her voice.

"Yes. Me. Not something I can leave here instead." ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

Mau did not pick her up before asking.

He checked what she could move. Anko looked beyond the bank. Mikasa found the route back which would not make them carry her through the loose ground they had just crossed.

The woman gave him permission to help her stand. Her breath caught when he took the weight carefully, and that sound made the hand on his shoulder feel much more real than the words she had said.

"Who did this?" he asked. ᴹ

She closed her eyes for a moment.

"I'll tell you. Somewhere it can't follow me." ᴰⁱᵒ

"There's something following?" Anko asked. ᴬ

"There was. I don't know how far." ᴰⁱᵒ

Anko signalled Mikasa. They changed the way they watched the route without pretending an uncertain pursuit was an attacker already inside the grounds.

Mau sent word home: the person was alive, hurt and asking to come; he recognized her voice. He asked Rimuru to bring Shoko to the receiving side and told him who he thought they were helping.

Then he lifted her only as far as she had agreed.

* * *

The protected edge did not move toward them.

They had to reach it.

The Goddess stayed quiet against his shoulder while he crossed the last uneven ground. Once, she said his name, and he looked down to find her watching the familiar trees.

"Still there," she whispered. ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau did not know whether she meant the house or what kept it safe.

At the boundary, Mikasa checked the route behind them. Anko made sure the scout's report and their own return were both accounted for.

No enemy came through with them. No weapon crossed the edge in pursuit.

The woman let out the breath she had been holding.

Beyond the receiving space, Rimuru had stopped beside Shoko with an expression Mau had not seen him wear in a long time.

Mau carried her the remaining distance.

For once, the person they needed to put down carefully was the one who had always seemed able to watch from somewhere else.
