Mau stayed until Shoko had taken the weight of the injured side onto the padded bench. Then he moved back when she asked him to.

Rimuru stood beside the door. Anko went out to make sure the route report did not become three different stories before anyone had finished treating the person it concerned.

The Goddess watched Shoko examine the wound.

"You can stop watching me as if I'm waiting for you to do a trick," Shoko said. ˢʰ

The woman looked up.

"I don't know what this face is doing yet." ᴰⁱᵒ

Shoko paused, then continued with the cloth.

"Then let it hurt. I need you to tell me where." ˢʰ

* * *

It was a body which could be supported, cleaned and examined.

That did not make it a body Shoko knew everything about. She checked what she could check and did not let someone else's title turn the rest into certainty. Ciel observed through the people who could hear her, giving Mau and Rimuru the difference between a finding and a guess.

The bleeding slowed. The wounded woman could speak without using the words to keep herself upright.

Mau found his hand still closed around the cloth she had held against her side on the way home.

He put it down.

She noticed.

"I didn't plan that entrance," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

Rimuru's expression did not change.

"Good to know." ᴿ

* * *

Yuka came to the doorway only after Shoko said the immediate work was done.

Her gaze moved from the woman to Mau. He went to her rather than calling across the room. She took his wrist and checked the place where the blood had reached his sleeve.

"Hers," he said quietly. ᴹ

She nodded, then looked past him.

The woman on the bench was watching the exchange. For once she did not turn it into a comment before anyone could decide whether it had been offered to her.

Frieren waited farther along the passage with Ori beside her. Maki and Yuta had come down. The house had not stopped being a house while the news travelled through it, but people had begun choosing where they wanted to stand when the explanation came.

Mau looked back at the bench.

"DIO," he said. ᴹ

She raised her head.

He needed her looking at him for the next question.

"Are they outside?" ᴹ

"I don't know. Not inside." ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

She answered the immediate question before anyone had to ask it a second time.

What protected the place was still there. It was tied to her life and the land she had marked, not to whether this new body stood at the boundary or lay on a bench. It had not moved out to cover the stream. It would not grow around every road they wanted to use.

"If you die?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

DIO's hand tightened against the cloth Shoko had given her.

"Then it falls." ᴰⁱᵒ

The room went quiet enough for Mau to hear somebody moving a chair outside.

"And while you're alive?" he asked. ᴹ

"An attack doesn't earn an exception because it cuts space instead of wood. It doesn't come through." ᴰⁱᵒ

She looked toward the passage, where the others were waiting.

"That was a promise about this place. It still is." ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

Shoko ended the conversation when DIO's voice began costing too much breath.

They made a place for her to rest in the medical area, with enough privacy that being watched did not become the price of being helped. No one erased a resident's name from a bedroom door to produce a guest room which had always existed.

Mau stayed until the others had gone far enough that she could ask him without an audience.

"May I ask for something now?" ᴰⁱᵒ

He looked at her.

"You can ask." ᴹ

"A place to stay. Somewhere I don't have to keep listening for the next thing trying to make me answer." ᴰⁱᵒ

He remembered a second request waiting behind a bargain he had once been confident enough to make.

"That's the other one?" ᴹ

She nodded.

"Refuge. Not a test." ᴰⁱᵒ

He sat on the chair Shoko had left clear.

"You're safe here tonight. For the rest, we talk when you can stay awake through your own answers." ᴹ

Her mouth moved almost into a smile.

"A sensible condition." ᴰⁱᵒ

He stayed until she no longer needed to be sensible at him.
