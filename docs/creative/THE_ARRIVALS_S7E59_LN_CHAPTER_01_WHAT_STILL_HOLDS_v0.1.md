She sat.

The common room held the people who wanted answers and the people who would rather hear the answers than receive them through someone else's anger. Not every resident needed to crowd the table. The doorway remained clear, and the questions would be written down for those who came later.

Mau sat beside Yuka. Rimuru had the route sheet, not because a map could explain what happened to a god but because it could establish where the woman had actually been found.

DIO looked at the mark by the water.

"They brought me into a body outside it," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

She had not chosen that arrival.

There had been a calling worked through a fixed place, made to catch the person being called without waiting for an answer. She could describe what she experienced: the pressure, the incomplete opening, the moment a body became something she could not simply decline to feel.

She could not give them a complete diagram of the machinery from inside it.

"One of the Rulers was there," she said. "The one I saw tried to kill me." ᴰⁱᵒ

Yuta leaned forward.

"One?" ʸᵗ

"One that I saw. Don't make the rest into that one because it gives the sentence a cleaner ending." ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau looked at the map.

"What did it want?" ᴹ

She was quiet for a moment.

"It called me a jailer. I can tell you what it said. I don't know everyone who taught it to say it." ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

She had broken enough of the calling's hold to get away before it finished deciding where her body could go.

That had not returned her to the place from which she used to speak. It had left her on ground outside the protection, hurt, with one remaining choice she understood well enough to use.

Walk toward the house.

"The coins?" Fern asked. ᶠ

DIO shook her head.

"Not your ordinary use of them. Someone finding a way to call without an answer doesn't mean every promise about asking disappeared. I need to understand what they made before I tell you how far they can repeat it." ᴰⁱᵒ

Anko wrote the distinction down.

Mau could feel Yuka's fingers beside his on the table. He moved his hand so they touched.

* * *

The protection came next because the children lived here, because the gardens were here, because every quiet evening they had begun believing in had been built inside a border they had never been able to move.

It stayed fixed to the place already marked.

DIO did not need to stay awake to maintain it. Rest was not death. The injury did not reduce the line to whatever strength she could put into standing up this morning.

It did not follow her skin outside. A new building beyond the known edge would not become protected merely because someone wanted to call it part of the house.

"And we don't take her outside to prove any of that," Shoko said. ˢʰ

No one argued.

Senku asked whether the anchor could be studied without being altered. DIO agreed to observations they discussed first. She did not agree to have the promise dismantled so they could see which piece made it work.

He wrote down a different question.

* * *

Frieren had said little until the discussion returned to refuge.

"Will you ask him to pay for something else once he has done this?" she asked. ᶠʳ

DIO looked toward her.

Mau felt the question reach something he had been leaving politely beside the bargain.

"Not for giving me a place to rest," DIO said. "This is the request. There isn't a test hidden behind it." ᴰⁱᵒ

Rimuru did not lower his eyes.

"You've been certain before." ᴿ

"Yes." ᴰⁱᵒ

She let the word stay without asking them to turn it into trust.

"Then we ask about the actual place," he said. "What you can enter. What you can leave. What nobody gets to take from anybody." ᴿ

* * *

Mau listened before answering the part which belonged to him.

He could offer a room made with Noise's help, separate from the things they had kept closed. They would need to build it, test it and make sure the person staying there could reach him and leave without needing to earn permission every time.

DIO would not receive his body, his memories or a right to rearrange the things under his guardrails. He would not receive her memories, authority or answers as rent.

Yuka looked at him when he said the last part.

He had meant it.

"You can rest here while we find out whether that room can be what we're offering," he told DIO. ᴹ

She looked at the family around the table.

Then at the door which nobody had closed behind her.

"I'd like that," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

Afterward, Maki asked who would be told if the next road scout found the calling site.

They agreed on a report and observation from safety. No lone rush to break a machine whose construction they had not yet seen. No assumption that the person who had already been injured needed to go back and identify it in the field.

The new problem did not erase the two roads they had been watching before it arrived.

Jinshi placed the notes where people could read them. Fern took the next shift of questions so the same people did not have to stay at the table until dark.

DIO went back to the place Shoko had prepared.

She took the offered arm on the way, and Mau noticed she waited for Shoko to set the pace.
