Mau looked at her.

She smiled.

"You make it very easy," she said. ᴱ

He sat down.

Daphne had something to eat and an opinion about the plants someone had moved too close to a path. Minerva asked about the wrist he had been rubbing, then accepted that the old stiffness had not become a new injury simply because she disliked it.

The seven had found their own arrangement around the afternoon. Sekhmet looked as though the effort of making room had been sufficient work. Carmilla moved a cup where someone could reach it. Satella waited until Mau had stopped looking for the question before asking it.

"Do you want to give her the room?" ˢᵃ

"Yes." ᴹ

* * *

Pride watched him over the edge of her cup.

"Good. That's the question the bargain doesn't answer." ᴾʳ

He gave a small laugh.

"I did remember there was a bargain." ᴹ

"So did she. You still get to know whether you want the door open." ᴾʳ

Echidna asked about the difference between the room and the things Noise had already made. Mau described the part he could describe without opening somebody else's private space or pretending the parts they had not built were finished.

There were places they could work from. There was an access they had not opened and would not use as a convenient empty room.

"You don't know what's in it," Echidna said. ᴱ

"That's a very good reason not to put a guest in it." ᴹ

She looked pleased with him, which remained an unreliable guide to whether the next question would be pleasant.

* * *

They did not spend the whole visit discussing a god.

Daphne made him describe what had come out of the last dungeon because the word *plate* had become too broad to be useful. He admitted the material had not been tested. She told him how much the garden paths mattered when somebody insisted a moving crate could simply go around the bed.

He promised to keep the soldiers on the marked routes.

Minerva wanted to know why people still brought her questions after deciding in advance to ignore the answer. Carmilla said something so quietly that he missed the first part, then smiled when he asked her to repeat it instead of pretending he had heard.

By the time the tea cooled, Mau had laughed twice without remembering why he had come prepared to make an explanation.

Satella touched the side of the pot.

"Would you like more?" ˢᵃ

He did.

* * *

That evening Ciel helped him separate the room from the idea of a room.

An entrance was not enough. Somewhere to put a bed was not enough. DIO would need air, an ordinary way to reach them, a return she could initiate and privacy which did not depend on Mau remembering not to listen.

Noise began from a place they could already reach. It did not open the unknown access when another part of the construction looked more difficult.

*Here?* Mau asked.

The answer came with the outline settling farther from the closed work.

*Here.*

Ciel checked the difference between the boundary they were building and the systems they were deliberately leaving alone.

*We test it empty first,* she said.

*I know.*

*You say that after an impressive number of ideas.*

He began laughing.

* * *

Yuka found him sitting in the new corner with his eyes open and no book in his hands.

"Occupied?" she asked. ʸᵏ

"Working. I can stop." ᴹ

She took the other chair.

"You can finish the bit you're doing. I brought mine." ʸᵏ

He looked at the notes she opened on her knees. A new deer stood awkwardly between two old diagrams. She had begun correcting the size of its antlers without correcting the little mark where she had celebrated making the call.

He let Ciel close the last part of the check before turning toward her.

"How was the practice?" ᴹ

She looked up.

Then started telling him.
