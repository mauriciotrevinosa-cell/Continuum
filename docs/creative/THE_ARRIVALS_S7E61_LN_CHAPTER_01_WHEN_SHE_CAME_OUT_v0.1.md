*When: Day890. DIO comes out while Mau is trying to keep a piece of bread from falling butter-side down.*

He caught it.

The small movement of the return caught his attention a moment later, and he turned to find her standing where the clear space had been left for precisely that reason.

"Morning," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

He looked at the bread in his hand, then at her.

"Morning." ᴹ

She had returned because she wanted to. Nobody had called her to prove the room worked.

Mau set the bread on a plate and realized she was waiting to ask where she should sit.

* * *

Shoko checked her before letting the morning become an excuse to ignore the wound.

DIO submitted to the examination with more patience than she had shown yesterday, then accepted that being better did not make the next walk unlimited.

By the time they reached the common room, Ori had put a small folded blanket on the end of the bench.

Frieren stood beside her.

"For if you get cold," Ori said. ᴼ

DIO looked at the blanket.

"Is it yours?" ᴰⁱᵒ

"I'm lending it." ᴼ

Frieren watched without telling her what the gesture ought to mean.

DIO sat down carefully and put the blanket across her knees.

"Then I'll bring it back," she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

Ori nodded, satisfied by an agreement whose terms made sense to her.

* * *

Mau left them to find her something to eat.

In the kitchen, Rem asked what Shoko had said she could manage. He went back to ask rather than deciding that a goddess should be able to eat anything put in front of her.

By the time he returned with the answer, Ram had found the bowl he needed.

"How many journeys will this breakfast take?" she asked. ᴿᵃ

"I'm becoming very informed." ᴹ

"You'll be late and informed." ᴿᵃ

He took the tray.

Yuka opened the door with her elbow while holding two cups. They reached the table together and discovered that Ori had begun explaining which part of the house belonged to the Fort and which part was merely on the way to it.

DIO was listening with the attention of someone who knew better than to offer a border revision.

* * *

The harder conversation happened after the children had gone to find the people they had promised to meet.

Rimuru asked whether DIO could tell if the calling was still trying to reach her. She could describe what she felt now. She could not certify that nobody elsewhere was building another attempt.

Anko wanted a description of the site. Mikasa asked where the escape route had begun. They separated those answers from the places the scouts could safely check.

No one asked Mau to take her back outside for a better look.

When the next patrol brought a glimpse of disturbed ground beyond the route, they would compare it with the description, keep their distance and decide together what to do. A report was not an order to put a body in danger because the information would be useful.

DIO looked at Mau when he said so.

He did not explain whether the rule had been easy to learn.

* * *

Later, Frieren stopped beside him while he put the empty tray away.

"You're still allowed to be angry," she said. ᶠʳ

He looked at her.

She had not lived the memories which made the question difficult. She knew what he had told the house. She knew enough not to pretend the chair and the blanket had made all of it harmless.

"I know," he said. ᴹ

"Good." ᶠʳ

Ori called for her from the other side of the room.

Frieren turned toward the voice, then looked back once.

"You don't have to finish feeling everything before dinner." ᶠʳ

He laughed quietly.

She went to Ori, and Mau put the last bowl where it belonged.

* * *

DIO returned the blanket before going back to her room.

Ori was not there to receive it, so she gave it to Frieren and asked her to say it had been useful.

At the return point, she knocked rather than trying to make the room a door into every part of Mau's attention.

He answered. She said she would rest a little longer, and the exchange ended without either of them asking for more.

Yuka found him by the new corner and sat beside him until the others called them to eat.
