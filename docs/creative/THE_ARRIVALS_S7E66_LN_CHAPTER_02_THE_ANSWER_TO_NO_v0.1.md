# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 41
## Chapter 10 — The Answer to No

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-27)
**Volume:** 41
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S7E66
**Continuity state:** B late night→B+1 dawn. Explicit defer/reject participation request observable triggers local network sanctions and revised Operator-only admission. Logs preserve oldterms. Local dungeon access/Shop newtransactions/newcreditsettlement suspended, not stored crystals/money/points/inventory/foreign skill construction/Yuka gift or all worldgates. All people outside beforetest. House builds alternatives, no instantaneous hunger. Architect identity still inferred until confrontation.

*When: Before dawn. The request to defer participation is the first thing Mau sends through the key after choosing to stay.*

Everyone needed to be outside the ordinary dungeon first. They checked. The last expected return had already been recorded; Fern had the names, and Rimuru asked again before they touched the connected procedure.

Ciel helped Mau read what the request actually did.

It did not offer his body, his memories or another bargain. It told the visible system that the operator was declining entry for now and asked that the opening remain available under the conditions it had already displayed.

The acknowledgment appeared.

Then changed.

* * *

**Normal dungeon access suspended.**

**Store unavailable.**

**New credit settlement paused.**

**Pending procedure: Meet the Monarch.**

**Admission revised: Operator only.**

Yuka read the lines before Mau could turn toward her.

Gojo looked at the earlier copy, then at the live message.

"Keep both," he said. ᴳ

Mau felt something cold find the space beneath his ribs.

Maki was already asking who had received the command and which marker had answered it.

* * *

The link matched the one they had found before the birthday.

It was not proof that every private word had been heard. They had sent an explicit refusal into a system which could see the request. The response had followed that action, and the logs showed the new conditions replacing the old ones.

Mau repeated Ciel's finding aloud.

"So he can change this installation," Rimuru said. ᴿ

"Whoever sent that order can," Mau said. "I think it's him." ᴹ

They tested the parts they could test safely.

The ordinary gate would no longer admit a traveller. New Shop transactions failed. The crystals on the shelf remained crystals; money earned from the road remained where Jinshi had put it. Mau's held points did not disappear when he opened the Window.

Yuka made a small pull from her own pattern and closed it herself.

The gift had not become something the administrator could repossess because she asked the person she loved to stay alive.

* * *

For a while Mau could hear almost nothing beyond the lines on the message.

Then Fern asked what they would actually lose by leaving it closed for a week.

The question brought the room back.

Food already existed. The garden had not vanished. Hollis was not inside the Shop. Tools, seed and the work they could do with ordinary hands did not wait for this permission to continue.

They made an alternative serious enough to use.

Separate the installation from the tasks which could continue without it. Keep the money and fuel reserves clear. Observe the connection without sending another accidental acceptance. Find out what another ordinary gate could offer if this one remained closed.

No one said that a shop was worth Mau.

* * *

He listened until the shape of staying had become possible again.

Then asked the other question.

"What do we do about the part that can keep answering me like this?" ᴹ

Rimuru looked at the marker.

"We investigate it." ᴿ

"From here, or from where it expects me to go?" ᴹ

The room did not answer quickly.

Gojo turned the copy of the old terms toward the live message.

Maki asked whether going in would actually let him reach the administrator. They could not promise that. Ciel could distinguish the present link from a root she could control; she had not found a switch which made the threat stop being real.

Yuka looked at Mau with the fear returning differently this time.

He kept his hand on the table.

He had not taken back the fact that he wanted to stay.

* * *

By daylight the new gate had opened where the key had always said it would.

It carried the revised conditions.

The ordinary one remained closed.

Mau stood beside Yuka and watched the space beyond the first opening. There was no clock threatening a child's life, no reason to pretend an extra hour of thinking was forbidden.

There was an adversary waiting at a place made for him, who had just answered a refusal by putting a hand on the house's work.

Mau turned back toward the others.

"I want to talk through entering it," he said. "Not because we're out of food. Because I want to reach the thing doing this before it decides what the next answer costs." ᴹ
