*When: Mau crosses the threshold. The first thing he notices is that nobody has bothered to make the room look kind.*

Stone ran beneath his feet toward a figure too large for the seat which held it. The face looked down with a fixed smile. Twelve wardens stood along the sides, six to each row; four seated figures held instruments which had never needed breath.

The way behind him closed.

Mau counted again.

Seventeen bodies linked through the marks in the floor.

*Ciel?*

*Here.*

He breathed more easily for that word.

*Rimuru?*

The return did not come.

*The relay isn't crossing this instance,* she told him. *That is not the same as being alone.*

* * *

The inscription appeared before any statue moved.

**Assessment: embodied combat.**

**External manifestations and active technique outputs restricted until assessment concludes.**

Mau tested the beginning of a small operation, short of trying to force it into existence.

The room answered at the point the pattern would have become output. A prepared containment field, linked to the constructs and the admission he had crossed, caught the new command. It did not empty his reserves. It did not make his muscles forget what the points had built into them.

Ciel followed the rejection.

*The room is prepared to intercept the work as you begin it. Here. It isn't reaching out to erase everything Noise has made.*

*Repair?*

*An active output too. So is the layer you used against Gojo.*

He flexed his hands.

His body answered.

* * *

A voice reached him from somewhere beyond the central figure.

"You have grown." ᴬʳ

Mau went still.

Then looked past the smile.

"You should have asked what I wanted to grow for." ᴹ

One of the wardens stepped down.

Its weapon struck where his head had been.

* * *

He moved under the next swing and hit the wrist with the strength already inside his arm.

Stone cracked. The weapon turned. A second warden entered the space he would have used if he had followed the first body too far.

Mau stepped back instead.

He could feel the difference between fighting the room's field to begin a technique and using the body it had already accepted. The first would take work while seventeen prepared things decided where he could stand. The second was available now.

He chose it.

The next strike drove through the damaged joint. A stone hand broke away and hit the floor.

The warden did not stop.

* * *

Mau gave it enough room to make the mistake larger.

It raised the remaining arm. He crossed inside the reach, planted his foot against the lower support and struck the glowing point beneath the neck as the body bent toward him.

The anchor broke.

The figure collapsed into a weight he left behind rather than trying to carry.

Sixteen linked signatures remained.

The floor's restriction stayed.

Mau looked at the other wardens.

The first one had not been ordinary carved stone, and the next eleven had seen what he could do to it.

He rolled his aching wrist once.

"All right," he said. ᴹ

The seated figures began moving their instruments.
