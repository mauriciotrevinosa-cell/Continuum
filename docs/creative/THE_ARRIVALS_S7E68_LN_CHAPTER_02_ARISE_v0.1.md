*When: The light strikes the place Mau has just left. The floor opens into heat behind him, and the sound of it nearly hides the next note.*

Nearly.

He saw the marks answer one of the musicians before the central face turned again. A supply change, then a direction. A room made to force its subject into the open.

Mau ran toward the musician instead.

The first reaching stone hand struck the edge of the instrument. The other tried to put him back where the face could see him. He crossed under it, took the impact against his upper arm and drove his own hand into the anchor beneath the instrument's base.

The note broke.

Three remained.

* * *

He could not make the entire room miss him.

The next sweep clipped his leg. He stumbled, caught himself with the hand he should have kept clear and lost enough breath that Ciel gave him a shorter answer when he asked about the side.

*You can still move. Stop making the same landing.*

He changed it.

The second musician tried to catch him at its usual height. He stayed low, struck the supporting joint and used the collapse to reach the anchor before the instrument could answer the next command.

Two.

The central figure began to rise.

* * *

It did not become slow because it was large.

Mau had to leave the third musician unfinished to avoid a hand whose weight would have made the damage he had already taken look like a polite warning. He moved with the falling arm and drove its arrival toward the weakened side of the seat.

Stone broke against stone.

He came out beneath the reach, changed direction before the eyes found the end of his run and finished the anchor he had left.

One musician.

One great figure.

The room had begun telling itself where he would go next before he got there.

He stopped completing the route it expected.

* * *

He turned back across the space he had just crossed.

The last musician reached into a place where his shoulder should have been. Mau met the arm near the hinge rather than the hand, broke the joint and struck through the opened base before the body could settle onto it.

The music ended.

The great figure's next step came without the same support.

Mau heard the change through his feet.

* * *

He did not try to win a contest with the entire chest.

The right leg was carrying the damaged side of the seat and most of the correcting turn. He hit it where the supply passed through the narrowing joint, then had to throw himself clear when the knee failed and the hand came down to replace it.

The fall opened the next point.

He reached it with everything his body already had, pain and permanent strength arriving together in the same decision.

The last anchor broke.

For a moment the face remained above him, still smiling as the structure behind it failed.

Then it fell away.

* * *

**Assessment complete.**

**Manifestation restriction released.**

Ciel confirmed the change before he began the next command. The room's interception had ended with the prepared links which sustained it.

Mau stood among seventeen animated bodies whose traces remained bound to the fragments. There was something there to call. Not the bare stone of any wall he happened to dislike.

He looked toward the place the voice had come from.

"Arise." ᴹ

* * *

The shadows lifted.

Twelve wardens. Four figures carrying instruments in the memory of their shapes. The great form gathered lower beneath the room's broken height.

Seventeen ordinary additions, not names he needed to give while his hands were still bleeding. One thousand and thirteen in the legion now.

He ordered them to hold the room and protect the place he occupied. Not pursue through an unknown opening.

Only then did he let the prepared repair begin on the fresh damage. The small torn places started closing; his side and wrist would need more than the first breath he could give them.

Ahead, the Architect stepped into view.

Mau started toward him.

The space between them changed before he could finish the movement.
