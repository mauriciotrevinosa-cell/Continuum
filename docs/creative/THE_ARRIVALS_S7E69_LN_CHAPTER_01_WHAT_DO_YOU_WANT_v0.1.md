*When: Mau reaches for a man he wants to hit and finds himself looking at someone who does not seem to be waiting for a fight.*

The figure stood in a quiet which did not belong to the broken temple.

Armor. A presence which made the space feel occupied before he had moved within it. Mau felt the two beats inside his chest and hated that he had checked them before asking anything else.

"Was this for you?" he said. ᴹ

The figure looked at him.

Mau took a step forward.

"The tower. The body. The heart. Did he do all of that so you could have somewhere to stand when he was finished with me?" ᴹ

* * *

"He has not understood my answer," the figure said. ᴬˢ

Mau stayed where he was.

"Then give me yours." ᴹ

For a moment the presence seemed larger, not as a threat but as though the quiet had acquired the weight of everything which had been asked of it before.

"I chose a successor. I chose what I would leave him. I did not ask for you to be emptied." ᴬˢ

Mau could not let the first sentence turn the others into something easy.

"Ashborn?" ᴹ

The figure inclined his head.

* * *

Ciel remained with Mau.

He could feel her attention becoming very precise, careful about the difference between the presence before them and a description someone had given it. Noise was already trying to understand what had reached their boundaries.

Ashborn turned slightly.

"Something is studying me." ᴬˢ

Mau stopped moving.

Then answered what he could answer honestly.

"Noise. It's part of how I survived coming here. It builds. Learns. Sometimes it begins before I know what question it's trying to answer." ᴹ

He drew his attention back inside.

*Not without asking.*

The movement slowed.

Ciel checked the boundary he meant rather than taking the request as permission to close every sense which could help him stay safe.

Ashborn waited.

* * *

"You can understand what is needed to speak safely," Ashborn said. "You cannot take my rest as an invitation to open everything I am." ᴬˢ

Mau nodded.

He made the limit clear to Noise. The answer came quietly, with the construction drawing back to the edge they had named.

*Ask.*

"We will," Mau said aloud. ᴹ

Ashborn looked at him differently after that. Mau could not decide what the difference meant, and for once he did not let uncertainty become an excuse to pretend the other person had agreed to more.

* * *

The rage had not left him.

His side still hurt in the place his attention returned to whenever the quiet gave it room. The body waiting back in the temple had not become uninjured because somebody important had denied intending the damage.

But the man before him had not claimed it.

Mau looked at the armor, the still hands and the space which seemed to offer no task except another conversation about what its owner should be used for.

"What do you want?" he asked. ᴹ

Ashborn did not answer at once.

* * *

"To stop," he said finally. ᴬˢ

Mau waited.

"The choice is made. The war is not mine to keep returning to whenever someone else refuses to accept its ending." ᴬˢ

"You want to rest." ᴹ

"Yes." ᴬˢ

The word was so plain that Mau heard what the Architect's story had never allowed it to contain.

Not another title. Not a more suitable body. Not gratitude for someone who had found a way to make the same duty last longer.

A person who wanted his own part of the fighting to end.

* * *

Mau thought of DIO moving a chair before deciding whether to stay.

Of the first time they had tested the return from inside. Of Yuka asking whether the guest could leave without making her desire acceptable first.

He could offer a place.

That did not mean offering his body as the empty part of it.

"I have somewhere you could look at," he said. "A room. A way out. No army you're expected to lead for me." ᴹ

Ashborn remained still.

Mau held his gaze.

"You can say no." ᴹ
