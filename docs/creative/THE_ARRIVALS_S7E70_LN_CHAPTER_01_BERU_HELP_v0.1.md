*When: Mau sees the Architect again. The man is looking at him as though the answer has finally become simple.*

The signature was there.

Not an imitation of the monarch's power. The presence itself, settled within the place the operator carried.

The Architect had not heard the agreement. He did not need to hear it to believe the conclusion he had wanted before the meeting began.

"Good," he said. "Now it only needs to grow." ᴬʳ

Mau's side tightened when he drew breath.

"He chose to rest," he said. ᴹ

The Architect's expression did not change.

* * *

Ciel asked him to gather the room before he tried to reach the man again.

He recalled the seventeen constructs under the order he had given them. The separate gift stayed with the place which had accepted it. His original weapons remained stored, not scattered among the broken floor.

The Architect was speaking to a plan more than to him.

Mau caught *everything*. Then *the vessel*. Then enough of *give up* to make the floor under his feet feel suddenly less reliable.

The man beyond him smiled.

"My monarch will awaken fully." ᴬʳ

Mau started to answer.

The floor opened.

* * *

Outside, Yuka saw the entry disappear.

No message replaced it.

She went forward before she could stop the first movement and found Yuta already beside her. Gojo checked the empty place. Rimuru asked for the time, and Maki answered because somebody had to keep recording what they actually saw.

Then the ordinary gate returned.

Its access procedure answered again. The Shop no longer carried the closure. None of those things said where Mau was.

Yuka looked at the empty place until Frieren's head turned toward the sky.

* * *

The new opening was enormous.

For one breath, everybody prepared for the shape its size suggested. Gojo moved so the people closest to the return ground had clear space behind them. Maki called the others back rather than letting fear become a crowd under whatever was coming.

Then a small figure fell through.

"Mau," Gojo said. ᴳ

Yuka forgot the size of the gate.

* * *

Air struck Mau's face.

The world was grey and turning, with a patch of sky where the room should have been. He had the operations which had returned when the field fell, but not a clear upright world in which to spend a second making a complicated choice.

He chose the help he trusted.

"Beru. Help." ᴹ

The shadow moved.

* * *

Beru reached him at speed.

He did not place himself in the path as a wall. He matched the fall, caught the body against his own movement and took the descent out of it before turning toward the clear ground.

Mau felt the change before he understood where the earth had gone.

Beru held him with a care which left no room for a performance about how magnificent the request had been.

"I have you, my king." ᴮᵉ

He brought him to the others slowly enough for Shoko to tell him where to put him down.

* * *

Yuka was there when Mau found the ground again.

He tried to lift his head toward her. The movement cost more than the words he had meant to say.

She put a hand where he could feel it.

"I'm here," she said. ʸᵏ

Rimuru looked up sharply as Ciel's relay reached him again. She had observations to give, no need to manufacture a voice everyone else could suddenly hear.

"The injuries are from the fight with the statues," he repeated for Shoko. "Exertion too. She saw what happened. There is a guest who entered by agreement; she isn't detecting an attack on him from inside." ᴿ

Shoko asked the next practical question before anyone could turn the word *guest* into a reason to move the patient around.

Mau heard Yuka say his name again.

He kept his fingers around hers until the effort of staying awake finally slipped away.
