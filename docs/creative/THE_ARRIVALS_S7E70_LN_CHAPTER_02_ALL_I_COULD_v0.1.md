*When: Mau rests. The offered record reaches him without pretending to be another voice calling from the road.*

He had accepted it in the room.

He knew the source Ciel had kept separate from the Architect's messages. What unfolded was a fragment offered for him to understand, not an order which had learned to use the shape of a memory.

Jinwoo.

A body brought to stillness. A life which did not end with the choices other people had made about what could occupy it.

Mau wanted to turn away from the point where everything stopped moving.

He stayed long enough to see the choice after it.

* * *

The record did not make him live the man's whole war.

It gave him the part Ashborn had chosen to leave: power transferred rather than a person erased; the difference between a successor accepting what was offered and a maker deciding that a vessel should be empty.

The man who had died returned with his own will still belonging to him.

Mau felt the danger in the shape the Architect had been trying to copy.

Not a prophecy. Not proof that killing him or taking Yuka would make the real guest obey. An explanation of why a man who could not accept another person's answer might decide that sufficient loss would produce the answer he wanted.

Mau thought of the half-heard words before the floor opened.

He needed to tell them.

* * *

He woke slowly enough to hear ordinary sounds first.

A chair shifting. Water being moved away from the edge of a table. Someone outside asking a question in a voice which had been lowered badly.

His side hurt when he tried the deeper breath. Less than it had in the temple. His wrist was supported where Shoko had wanted it still; the newly repaired skin had not persuaded her that every strain underneath ought to be ignored.

There were still two beats.

The space inside held the guest who had accepted it, not a hand waiting for the body to fall asleep.

Mau turned his head.

Yuka was beside him.

* * *

She had the scarf over her knees.

One hand held the raised line near its end. The other was close to his, waiting without making his sleep a test of whether he could answer her.

Her head lifted when he moved.

For a moment neither of them spoke.

Mau looked at the thing she had made him because she wanted him warm. At the place on her sleeve where the color remained when the rest of the room came back grey.

"I did all I could to get back," he said. ᴹ

* * *

Her face crumpled before the laugh arrived.

She tried to make a word out of the breath and had to try again.

"I can see." ʸᵏ

He moved his good hand. She caught it and pressed it between both of hers.

"I can see," she said again, laughing through the tears now. ʸᵏ

He wanted to tell her every part of the choice. The room, the other man, the thing he had understood only after returning.

She leaned close and kissed him carefully enough that he knew Shoko had explained where she could put her weight.

He let the first thing he did after waking be to kiss her back.

* * *

When they moved apart, she stayed near.

"You scared me," she said. ʸᵏ

"I know." ᴹ

"Don't say you know like that makes it something you have to apologize your way out of before you can rest." ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She wiped her face with the heel of one hand and kept the other around his.

"I wanted you here. You're here." ʸᵏ

His breath changed, and she waited while he found the next one.

"I have things I need to tell everyone," he said. ᴹ

"When you can. I won't help you hide them, but you don't have to bleed through the telling tonight." ʸᵏ

* * *

Shoko came in before the conversation became longer than his wakefulness could hold.

She checked him, listened to his answers and told him which new pain he should report instead of quietly deciding it belonged to the rest.

Yuka moved the scarf so it lay where it would warm him without pressing against the bandaged place.

He watched her hands.

The record remained his to explain. The room remained theirs to speak in.

For a little while, all he needed to do was let someone stay beside him while he slept.
