*When: The afternoon after his return. Shoko lets him sit with the others on the condition that the chair remains a chair, not the first stage of walking around to prove he is better.*

Mau accepted.

Yuka took the place beside him. Rimuru had brought the notes from outside, including the time the entry vanished and the moment the ordinary installation answered again.

Mau began with what he had heard before the floor opened.

"He thinks Ashborn is inside me to become me," he said. ᴹ

The room became very still.

He looked at them before continuing.

"He is inside as a guest. He didn't ask for that. He told me he doesn't want it. The Architect doesn't know the agreement." ᴹ

* * *

He gave them the words in pieces because that was how he had received them.

Grow. The vessel. Give up. Everything.

The last clear statement about the monarch awakening fully.

He did not join them into a speech he had not heard. Ciel gave Rimuru the parts she could corroborate and the places the audio had been lost beneath the transition.

"I think he intends to take the things which make me want my own life," Mau said. "I can't tell you the whole plan. I can tell you I heard enough to stop keeping the possibility to myself." ᴹ

Yuka stayed beside him.

Her hand pressed his once, then loosened enough for him not to mistake being heard for being held in place.

* * *

They asked about the guest.

Mau explained the room he had offered, the refusal Ashborn had been allowed to give, the fact that his successor had already been chosen elsewhere. The presence was not a new heart or a body waiting behind Mau's eyes.

The separate sentinel was a gift, not the real man compelled to fight whenever somebody asked. It remained distinct from the seventeen animated constructs he had raised after the field ended.

He gave the total plainly: one thousand and fourteen with the gift counted apart, sixteen named, nine hundred and ninety-seven ordinary. Mahoraga still a different call.

No one asked Ashborn to come out so the house could inspect his rest.

DIO listened from the chair Shoko had let her use and said only what she could say about the kind of consent which had been described. She did not claim that knowing one god's story made every foreign power hers to explain.

* * *

The offered fragment mattered because it explained the enemy's possible misunderstanding.

Mau told them what he understood from it without pouring another person's memories into the room. Death and awakening had been part of Jinwoo's story. That did not make them instructions for what Mau should endure next.

Gojo leaned forward.

"If the one he wants has already said no, then taking things from you doesn't change that answer." ᴳ

"I know. I don't think he knows." ᴹ

Maki looked at the ordinary household page beside the gate notes.

"Then we prepare for someone who believes the wrong thing and can still hurt people trying to make it true." ᴹᵃ

* * *

They did not solve the threat by agreeing that Mau would never leave his room.

They changed who knew about the interfaces, how an unexpected order would be reported and which routes would be checked before somebody brought a visitor toward the house. They kept the fixed protection distinct from the places outside it where people could be threatened.

Rimuru asked Mau to stop assuming that telling them the frightening part required him to arrive with a complete solution.

Mau looked at Yuka.

"I'm trying," he said. ᴹ

"Today you're doing it," she said. ʸᵏ

* * *

The conversation ended when Shoko saw him begin holding himself differently to hide the strain.

He objected once, quietly.

She raised an eyebrow.

He let Yuta take the page he had been about to lift.

Maki kept the practical work at the table with the people who could continue it. Yuka helped him stand without pretending she could take all his weight if he suddenly stopped carrying it.

At the doorway he looked back.

The frightening part had stayed in the room without becoming only his job again.
