*When: The third day after the gate. Rimuru finds Mau staring at an empty chair and asks whether it has done something wrong.*

"I'm deciding whether I can get up without Shoko hearing it," Mau said. ᴹ

Rimuru looked toward the open door.

"No." ᴿ

Mau leaned back.

"I appreciate the optimism." ᴹ

Rimuru put a cup on the table and took the chair himself.

* * *

The house had begun deciding which parts of the story belonged to its next ordinary day.

The people who needed the threat information had it. The people who needed to know where a guest was resting had a way to ask without crowding around Mau's ribs as though a king had taken a bedroom there.

DIO had spoken to him about returning to her own room, and they had agreed on the timing with the same practical care as before. Ashborn remained in the second chamber. There was no demand to introduce himself at breakfast before his rest counted as accepted.

The sentinel waited with the stored shapes. Mau had not promised the house a combat demonstration to celebrate receiving it.

"How much do they talk?" Rimuru asked. ᴿ

"Less than you." ᴹ

"A troubling standard for a host to set." ᴿ

Mau laughed and immediately regretted the particular breath he had used.

Rimuru moved the cup closer without making a speech.

* * *

Yuka came with food she had brought from the shared kitchen, not a new responsibility she intended to take away from everyone else forever.

She set it where he could reach and told him the one piece of information Rem had insisted travel with it.

Wait until it cooled.

Mau looked at the bowl.

"I'm being told to wait by an extraordinary range of people." ᴹ

"You keep giving us opportunities," Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Rimuru got up before the two of them could decide the chair would fit everybody if somebody became small enough.

"I'm going to find food nobody has assigned me a lesson with," he said. ᴿ

Yuka took his place.

* * *

Mau did wait.

She looked pleased when he tasted it without needing to pretend the temperature had been his preferred one all along.

Her book remained closed beside her. She had brought it in case he wanted quiet. He had a question instead.

"Do you ever think a good thing can feel frightening because you remember something which looked like it?" ᴹ

Yuka watched his hand on the spoon.

"Yes," she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She waited until he put the spoon down.

"Which good thing?" ʸᵏ

He had meant to find a perfect beginning for this conversation too.

Instead he answered the one she had given him.

"A wedding." ᴹ
