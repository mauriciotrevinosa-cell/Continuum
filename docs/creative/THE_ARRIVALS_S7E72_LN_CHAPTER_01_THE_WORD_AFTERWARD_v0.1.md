*When: Yuka hears the word wedding. She puts her book beside the food instead of opening it to give him somewhere to hide the next sentence.*

"The one from the Trial?" she asked. ʸᵏ

Mau nodded.

He had told the house what that place had done to him. Telling was not the same as being ready for the word to sit beside a future he wanted.

"It wasn't the fear that I didn't want the person waiting there," he said. "I was happy. That's part of what it used." ᴹ

Yuka stayed quiet.

He watched her fingers on the edge of the book until he could continue.

"At breakfast, when I thought about what we could do one day, it came back before I knew I was remembering it." ᴹ

* * *

She asked what part he wanted to tell her.

Not what part she had a right to inspect before believing he loved her. He heard the difference and found himself able to answer.

The clearing. The flowers he had spent years learning to make in that life. White and blue. Everyone arriving because he asked. The seam in the sky, and Infinity becoming the thing which would not let him reach the people it was supposed to protect him for.

Frieren's hand almost touching him.

He stopped before the last remembered words.

Yuka did not ask him to earn the pause.

"That isn't something she did here," he said. ᴹ

"I know." ʸᵏ

"I don't want to make the Frieren downstairs carry another ending she never lived." ᴹ

"Then don't. You can tell me what you need without turning her into the person in the memory." ʸᵏ

* * *

The next question frightened him more because it was gentle.

"Does wanting that kind of day with me feel wrong?" she asked. ʸᵏ

He looked up immediately.

"No." ᴹ

She waited for the rest.

"It feels possible. Then the memory makes possible feel dangerous. Those aren't the same thing." ᴹ

Yuka nodded slowly.

"I don't need you to pretend none of it mattered. I don't want to be where you put it so you never have to feel it again." ʸᵏ

His good hand tightened around the edge of the chair.

She touched it.

"And I don't want us to make every good choice as an argument against something that happened before me." ʸᵏ

* * *

Mau looked at her for a long time.

She was not the woman in the clearing. She had her own way of waiting when he needed a moment, her own impatience when he tried to decide that loving her meant deciding alone what would frighten her least.

He wanted the future with this person.

Not as a corrected version of an older scene.

"I want it to belong to us," he said. ᴹ

"Then we'll have to leave room for what I want too." ʸᵏ

He laughed quietly.

"You keep bringing that up." ᴹ

"I intend to be very inconvenient about it." ʸᵏ

* * *

Later he asked Rimuru, Yuta and Gojo to sit outside with him for a while.

He did not need the dungeon to be the only place where he could admit something was too loud. The bench was within the protected grounds, and Shoko had agreed to the short walk provided he stopped treating standing as a moral victory.

They brought cups.

Mau told them which memory had returned. The shape was already known; he did not make them endure the whole ending to justify wanting company.

Yuta stayed beside him. Rimuru asked whether he wanted to talk or simply sit. Gojo complained about the quality of the entertainment and remained in the grass long after the complaint had stopped being useful.

Mau let them.

When he went inside, Yuka was where she had said she would be, reading the book she had waited to open.
