*When: Six days after his return. Mai looks at the drawing in Mau's hand and asks whether it is supposed to be a ring or a small bridge.*

He looked down.

"A ring." ᴹ

She took the page and turned it.

"Unfortunate." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuta, who had come to see why Mau had asked for a quiet minute, made a small noise which he disguised by moving a chair.

Maki did not disguise hers.

Mau put his head down and waited for them to finish enjoying themselves.

* * *

"You said I might need help," he told Mai. ᴹ

"I was being generous about might." ᴹᵃⁱ

She set the drawing on the table and asked what he actually wanted it to do.

That was a better question than the one he had spent the morning trying to answer with lines.

"Stay on her hand without catching the rope. Be something she wants. Not another thing she has to check a cost for before using." ᴹ

Mai looked at him.

"So a ring." ᴹᵃⁱ

He began smiling.

"Yes." ᴹ

* * *

She could help.

They had already learned how to put the supply and the bodily cost through him. Mai had spent months announcing hooks, pins and small things for the house before making them, despite his repeated assurance that she did not have to warn him about every one. The agreement belonged to this life. It had begun with a cupboard catch, not a memory she was expected to honour.

A ring would still need her attention and a design she understood. It did not have to take the afternoon from her hands the way her old daily bullet had.

"When we know the shape, I'll tell you what I'm making," she said. ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau smiled.

"I know." ᴹ

"And I'm not going back to fighting because you found a way to make things easier," she said. ᴹᵃⁱ

"I didn't come to ask you for a weapon." ᴹ

She watched him answer.

Then tapped the drawing.

"This might become one if we let you design it alone." ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuta lost the fight with his laughter.

* * *

They began with ordinary questions.

What she liked wearing. How she used her hands. Which materials the makers actually had and which would need to be bought or worked first. Somebody with better drawing skills would help before a shape became a commitment nobody wanted to insult.

Mai suggested Marin, then asked Mau before involving another person in the surprise. He agreed.

Maki asked whether he intended to make the proposal a public event.

"No room full of people waiting for the answer," he said. ᴹ

She nodded.

Yuta put a hand on his shoulder, the same simple contact as on his birthday.

"You came back," he said. ʸᵗ

Mau looked up.

"I still wanted to ask." ᴹ

"Good." ʸᵗ

* * *

Yuka found Yuta later, studying the safe side of a page which apparently became fascinating whenever she approached.

She stood in front of him until he looked up.

"Do you know what he's doing?" ʸᵏ

Yuta looked toward the doorway.

Maki stood there with a cup.

She took one look at him and began laughing.

"That isn't helping," he said. ʸᵗ

"You haven't tried answering yet." ᴹᵃ

Yuka put her hands on the table.

"Grandpa." ʸᵏ

He set the page down carefully.

"It isn't another gate. It isn't something dangerous we're helping him keep from you." ʸᵗ

Her face changed before she could hide how much she had needed that part to be plain.

Maki came over.

"Ask him about the surprise. Let him give you the answer he can give without showing you the whole thing before it's ready." ᴹᵃ

Yuka looked from one to the other.

Then at the page, still safely turned away.

"You are both being very suspicious." ʸᵏ

Yuta glanced at Maki again.

She laughed harder.
