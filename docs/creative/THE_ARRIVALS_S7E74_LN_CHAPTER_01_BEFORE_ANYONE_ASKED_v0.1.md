## Chapter 1 — Before Anyone Asked

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-28)

*When: Yuka wakes to the sound of a drawer being opened very carefully.*

Mau had made it halfway through shutting it when she spoke.

“You know I can hear you doing that.” ʸᵏ

He stopped with two fingers on the wooden edge. The drawer contained his shirts, a belt and a piece of paper which was too large to disappear convincingly beneath either.

“Doing what?” ᴹ

“Trying to be quiet.” ʸᵏ

She rolled onto her side. The blanket followed her as far as her hip and stayed there. Morning light lay across the room in a pale rectangle. Inside it, her wrist was warm brown and the little flower in her bracelet kept its colour. Beyond her, the chair, the scarf hanging over it and the wall were shades he had stopped expecting to change overnight.

He pushed the drawer closed.

“I was doing quite well until you started talking.” ᴹ

“Come here.” ʸᵏ

He looked toward the window.

Yuka lifted the blanket without repeating herself.

He put his shoes down.

The bed dipped as he sat, and she caught his hand before he could decide whether sitting counted. He lay back. Her head fitted under his chin with the small adjustment they had learned during months of sharing a pillow neither of them admitted stealing.

For a while she listened to his chest.

Both beats were familiar to her now. He could tell which she was following from the light pressure of her fingers. The second rhythm no longer made her lift her head every time it fell out of step with the first.

“Did you sleep?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“All the way through?” ʸᵏ

He put his hand over hers.

“I woke up once.” ᴹ

“And stayed here?” ʸᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She relaxed against him. “Good.” ʸᵏ

He had stayed. He had known the room, known where she was, known that the man resting beyond the second interior doorway had already given him an answer. None of those facts had prevented him from hearing another man's voice in the dark.

*It just needs to grow.*

He had counted Yuka's breaths until he could hear them without the words underneath.

“I want to train today,” he said. ᴹ

“With Grandpa?” ʸᵏ

“Some of it. I want him and Gojo there.” ᴹ

She tipped her face up.

“What are you doing?” ʸᵏ

He had already put a version of the answer into a sentence. Now, with her watching him from so close, it sounded smaller than the thing he wanted.

“I want to fight the army.” ᴹ

Her fingers stopped moving.

“Which part?” ʸᵏ

“All of it.” ᴹ

Yuka stared at him for a second.

“That is a very annoying way to answer that question.” ʸᵏ

* * *

He waited while she sat up and found the sleeve of the hoodie twisted inside itself.

The McLaren lettering appeared across her chest as she pulled it straight. He had started noticing a particular thread beside the pocket. Each time it looked a little longer. Each time he decided against pointing it out because she would tell him it had survived worse than being worn by her.

“Does Beru know?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Not yet.” ᴹ

“He'll think he's done something wrong.” ʸᵏ

“I'll explain.” ᴹ

“Before he starts apologising for things he hasn't done.” ʸᵏ

“Before.” ᴹ

She looked toward the drawer.

“Does this have anything to do with the surprise?” ʸᵏ

“No.” ᴹ

The answer came quickly enough that he heard it himself.

Yuka watched him.

He bent to pick up a shoe, then put it back down.

“It has to do with what happened in there,” he said. “I keep thinking of ways somebody could stop me using things. Then I think of another one.” ᴹ

“So you want to see what happens when a thousand things try.” ʸᵏ

“When things that know how I fight try.” ᴹ

She rested her palms on her knees.

“Tell them that part too.” ʸᵏ

He nodded.

Yuka leaned forward and kissed him. Then she stayed close enough that he could not mistake the next sentence for something she had forgotten to say.

“And come back for lunch.” ʸᵏ

“The fight might be after lunch.” ᴹ

“Then come back for breakfast first.” ʸᵏ

“I haven't left.” ᴹ

“You're doing it in your head.” ʸᵏ

He opened his mouth, found the answer waiting in it and let it go. She smiled a little when he reached for the other shoe.

* * *

Downstairs, Ori was holding a spoon upright in a piece of bread.

Milim leaned across the table to inspect it.

“It needs a flag.” ᴹⁱ

“It's breakfast.” ᶠ

Frieren said this without looking up from the page Fern had put in front of her. There was a second piece of bread beside her own hand, already missing its corner.

Ori moved her bowl out of Milim's reach.

“You can't take the foundation.” ᴼ

Mau sat beside Yuka and accepted the bowl Rem handed him. A small plate followed it. Ram set the plate down with more precision than the plate required.

“We are trying a new system,” she said. “People who turn up while the food is hot receive hot food.” ᴿᵃ

“Radical,” Mau said. ᴹ

“We don't expect everyone to adapt immediately.” ᴿᵃ

Yuka kicked his ankle lightly under the table.

He ate.

The first mouthful had more pepper than he expected. He reached for water and found Yuka already pushing the cup toward him. Across the room, Yuta was listening to Tsurugi describe a grip with two fingers held an inch apart. Maki corrected the angle without looking away from the bread she was cutting.

Mai came through the door with Marin. Between them they carried a sheet of paper face down.

Mau looked at it.

Mai looked at him.

He looked at his breakfast.

Yuka began to smile into her cup.

“Very subtle,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I thought so,” Mai replied, and went past them without stopping. ᴹᵃⁱ

Mau followed her with his eyes until she reached the workshop door. The paper bent as Marin adjusted her grip. A black line showed through the back: a small curve, then another one, as if two people who could actually draw had found somewhere to begin.

He wanted to follow them.

He also wanted to be in the field, moving fast enough that no thought could keep up.

Yuka nudged his bowl.

“Breakfast,” she reminded him. ʸᵏ

He took another mouthful.

When Yuta came over, Mau made room on the bench instead of standing before his friend had even spoken.

“Can we look at something after this?” he asked. ᴹ

Yuta glanced at Yuka, then at the drawer key Mau had absentmindedly brought down in his hand.

“If it looks like a bridge again, Mai has asked me to stop encouraging you.” ʸᵗ

Mau laughed. It surprised him how much of a breath the sound took.

“The sword,” he said. “Something I should have shown you.” ᴹ
