*When: Yuta lays his own sword beside Mau's and asks which one he wants to talk about.*

The scabbards were almost the same length.

That had bothered Mau the first time. He remembered the grey field, Fern telling him not to touch anything, and a blade laid down carefully in the middle of hundreds which had been left standing. His hand remembered picking it up more readily than his mouth remembered how to explain the day.

Yuta sat back on his heels.

Maki stood beside him. Gojo had taken the place on the low fence from which Shoko could reach his collar without getting up. Rimuru leaned against the post, listening. Yuka sat on the cloth she had brought, with her knees drawn up and the white dog's head resting across her shoes.

They were in the practice ground beyond the house's ordinary walks. Nobody had brought a breakfast bowl out with them.

“Mine,” Mau said. ᴹ

Yuta touched his own scabbard and moved it a little farther away.

“Go on.” ʸᵗ

“On the ninetieth floor, before Bellion was with us, this brought something out.” ᴹ

He saw Yuta begin to place the account in the history he had already heard. Mau waited for him to finish that small, necessary movement.

“In the other line,” Yuta said. ʸᵗ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“I was there?” ʸᵗ

“You and Maki. Frieren. You helped get me out.” ᴹ

Maki's hand rested on her knee. She was looking at the sword rather than trying to manufacture a memory to accompany the words.

Mau drew the blade just far enough for light to catch its edge.

“Noise had seen Rika. Seen you with her. It built a shape from that, and the sword was where it came through.” ᴹ

Yuta's fingers closed once against his own palm.

“Rika?” ʸᵗ

“Something made from the pattern. Yours stayed with you. The thing that held Bellion's weapon came apart. That body was gone.” ᴹ

“But the pattern wasn't,” Gojo said. ᴳ

Mau looked at him.

“No.” ᴹ

* * *

He had asked Noise that question before bringing anyone out.

There had been a quiet movement along a structure he recognised, a door which had not needed to become a new room to remain available. Ciel had checked the route with him. The sword was still the sword the Goddess had returned. His memories had returned with their unfinished work.

*You can give it a smaller body,* Ciel had said. *A defined task. You don't have to begin by asking it to hold a general.*

Now he put the edge back into the scabbard and stood.

“I'd like to show you. Briefly. Then put it away.” ᴹ

Yuta looked toward Shoko.

“How briefly?” she asked. ˢʰ

“One action,” Mau said. “A catch. Something I can feel before it has to fight.” ᴹ

Shoko considered him, then pointed to a patch of level ground clear of everyone else.

“There. And tell me if that changes.” ˢʰ

Maki picked up a practice pole. She weighed it once, looked at Mau, and waited for him to take his position.

He stood where Shoko had pointed. The old stiffness in his left hand made him loosen and settle his grip rather than force the fingers closed. He drew the katana.

The metal came free with an ordinary sound.

For a moment there was nothing else.

Then the shadow of his arm acquired another edge.

Something pale reached past his shoulder. It had the suggestion of a wrist before the fingers became distinct, thick and articulated, held open in the empty air. The shoulder behind it took shape more slowly. A curve of a head emerged above him, turned toward the people standing by the fence.

Yuta rose.

Mau kept the blade low.

The shape was clearer than the one in his memory. Its surface still changed where he had not given it enough work to define an edge. He could feel the demand as it gathered, separate from the deeper, steadier reserve which carried his soldiers.

*There,* Ciel said. *That's the route. Keep the hand. You don't need the rest for this.*

Mau let the unfinished shoulder diminish. The hand remained beside him.

“Ready,” he told Maki. ᴹ

She stepped in and brought the pole down.

The pale hand closed around it.

Wood struck the palm with a sharp crack. Maki drove a little more weight into the line, testing the hold. Mau felt the change behind his eyes and adjusted the supply. The fingers stayed shut.

“Release,” he said. ᴹ

The hand opened.

Maki took the pole back. Mau drew the shape inward before looking at anyone.

He sheathed the katana.

* * *

Yuka got up from the cloth.

“Are you all right?” ʸᵏ

“Yes. It took more to make the hand than to hold that.” ᴹ

Shoko came close enough to examine his eyes. She held up a finger, moved it and asked him to follow without turning his head. He told her where the old blurred edge began. It began where it had before.

“And the head?” Gojo asked. ᴳ

“I wasn't trying to make all of it.” ᴹ

“I know. It started anyway.” ᴳ

Mau nodded.

He could feel Noise keeping the route available, waiting for the next task rather than insisting on finishing the body in front of everyone. He asked it to leave the work there. The answer was a small settling behind the closed door.

Yuta stopped beside him.

For a second Mau wanted to apologise before the other man had decided what he felt.

Yuta looked at the scabbard.

“Does she always look for me?” ʸᵗ

Mau remembered the pale, half-finished face turning in the dark field. Remembered the man beside him, older and younger in ways no number could put in order, watching a thing which resembled someone he had never stopped carrying.

“She did then,” Mau said. “I don't know what this version knows. I don't want to pretend I do.” ᴹ

Yuta absorbed that.

Then he touched the ring on his own hand.

“Show me again another day,” he said. “When you've got the shape steadier. I'd like to be there.” ʸᵗ

“All right.” ᴹ

“And don't call it stealing from me. You haven't.” ʸᵗ

Mau's fingers eased on the scabbard.

Yuka reached for his empty hand. The dog got up and stretched at her feet, uninterested in the delicate silence the humans had been arranging around a hand which had caught a stick.

Maki set the pole against the fence.

“That was the quiet part?” she asked. ᴹᵃ

Mau looked toward the empty ground beyond them.

“I want the army to fight me this afternoon.” ᴹ

“All of them?” Gojo asked. ᴳ

“All of them.” ᴹ

Gojo's expression changed. He pushed himself off the fence.

“Then we're choosing somewhere much farther from the kitchen.” ᴳ
