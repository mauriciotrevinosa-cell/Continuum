* * *

They left after lunch.

Mau had eaten while the others argued about the ground, and gone back for the piece of bread Yuka pushed toward him. He told both guests where he intended to go. DIO chose to remain in the house's ordinary rooms; Ashborn preferred the quiet of his own chamber and agreed to the journey. Neither offered to settle the argument about whether a dragon's breath counted as reaching the far ridge.

Gojo made it count.

“If he can hit it, it's part of your field,” he said. “Pick another place for us to stand.” ᴳ

They used the empty basin where the rematch had stripped the old terraces down to stone. Time and rain had made shallow pools in the lower scars. Nothing lived in the largest one yet. Maki and Mikasa checked the approaches while Rimuru remained with the house, the medical preparations and the route back.

At the eastern lip, Yuta drove a marker into a crack and looked across the wide space below.

“Here?” he asked. ʸᵗ

Gojo shook his head and pointed higher.

Yuta pulled the marker out again.

Yuka made a noise very like Maki's.

“I can walk up a hill,” she said. ʸᵏ

“And I'd like you to keep enjoying the privilege,” Gojo answered. ᴳ

* * *

Mau went down alone.

His shadow lengthened across the broken stone. At first it only seemed to pass beneath the edge of the next ledge. Then the dark spread uphill, against the sun, and the first soldiers rose.

Ants with narrow jointed limbs. Armoured infantry. Heavy creatures which used their forelimbs as readily as weapons. The seventeen figures from the temple found their places among older companies without becoming the people whose shapes they had once been made to resemble.

One thousand and fourteen.

The number had fitted on Umaru's page without changing the size of the page. Here it climbed the terraces and filled the gullies between them. Rows became ranks. Ranks became a moving dark country broken by the violet light of eyes.

All the usual duty units had returned before he left. The house knew how to keep its own afternoon for the duration. Nothing remained behind to be counted as present while standing beside a cupboard at home.

Mau felt each connection settle.

The reserve beneath them was deep. The work of restoring them would still have to pass through him.

*Keep that separate from the cursed-energy work,* Ciel said. *Tell me what you're trying to do before you make the change.*

*I will.*

Above him, Kamish opened his wings.

The basin darkened beneath them. Kaisel took the opposite side, leaner, quicker, and Lantern rose between the two with his head low and his eyes turned toward the man in the centre.

Tank struck the ground once.

The sound reached the spectators as a dull movement through their shoes.

* * *

Bellion walked down the centre of the formation.

He did not give an inspiring speech. He moved a group of archers away from Fang's line, sent a heavy company into a dry channel and told Igris where he wanted the second approach to meet the first.

Igris changed two positions.

Bellion studied them and let the changes stand.

Beru remained close to Mau for a moment longer.

“Would you prefer I begin slowly?” ᴮʳ

“No.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴮʳ

Mau felt a small, involuntary thrill. He had watched that face turn toward other people. It was different to have earned its full attention.

He produced the paired daggers and let their weight settle into his hands. The familiar increase accompanied the equipment. His legs had already been his own before he drew them; now their added advantage changed how far he could trust the first step.

On the ridge, Yuka had both hands on the marker Yuta had finally placed.

Gojo spoke to him across the space.

“You call it when you want it called. If I call, you stop too.” ᴳ

“Understood.” ᴹ

Bellion looked up to hear the same arrangement.

“No change to the order until then?” ᴮˡ

Mau met the marshal's gaze.

“No holding back.” ᴹ

For the first time that afternoon, Bellion smiled.

His sword came apart in his hands.

Kamish drew a breath which made the pools below him ripple.

Beru disappeared.

Mau's first block arrived a fraction late.

The impact drove him twenty paces across the stone before he could turn his fall into a landing, and the place where he put his foot was already moving beneath Tank's weight.
