*When: Tank reaches the place where Mau would have landed.*

Mau pulled his knee up.

The bear's shoulder passed beneath it. A dark ridge of armour scraped his boot and flung him sideways, close enough that the first archer did not bother aiming at the centre of his body.

The arrow struck the place his hand needed to go.

He turned his wrist, caught the shaft on the outside of a dagger and landed on the wrong foot. Beru was there before he could correct it.

Four blows. The first turned his weapon. The second drove his elbow against his ribs. Infinity caught the third, but the fourth never came; Beru had already dropped out of its line.

A segmented blade wrapped around the ledge behind Mau.

Bellion pulled.

The stone came away beneath Mau's heel.

He fell through it, used the grip to catch the largest piece and brought it under him hard enough to give his next step a direction.

The piece burst beneath Fang's first strike.

White light filled the gap.

* * *

“They're moving him,” Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Yuka was staring at the far terrace. She could see Mau there, coat torn at one shoulder, turning fast enough that the loose fabric seemed to follow a different body.

“Where?” ʸᵏ

Maki pointed before Yuta could answer.

Three lines of soldiers were becoming two. The space between them looked clear.

“He likes a clean lane.” ᴹᵃ

Below, Mau took it.

Igris came out of the apparent opening.

His sword struck both daggers with one downward cut. Mau gave ground to keep his wrists from taking the whole weight. The knight's next step landed inside the retreat, and the next blow came from the side Mau's blurred vision made him turn his head to check.

He blocked it.

Beru hit him from the other side.

Yuka's fingers tightened on the marker.

“He told them to,” Maki said quietly. ᴹᵃ

“I know.” ʸᵏ

Yuka kept watching.

* * *

Mau went through a rank of infantry with his shoulder first.

Armour broke against armour. He caught one spear below its head, turned its owner into the line of the next thrust and drove a dagger through the soldier closing on his hip.

The form came apart around the blade.

He felt the restoration begin behind him.

*One company trying to keep you in contact,* Ciel said. *Bellion isn't asking them to win the exchange.*

*He's asking them to keep me here.*

*Yes.*

Mau released Infinity and set Blue beyond the far end of the rank.

The pull took loose weapons first. Three soldiers lost their footing. A shield turned edge-on, and he moved through the opening it made with his right dagger leading.

Igris stepped away before the force reached him.

The knight had seen the placement begin.

Mau let Blue go, changed the route and cut across the ground with Shrine.

Stone split in a low, fast line. The nearest company fell apart as the ledge beneath it divided. For a breath he could see Bellion beyond the broken bodies, sword extended, watching him change.

Then Kaisel crossed overhead.

Mau looked up.

Beru drove him through the side of the terrace.

* * *

He came out in dust.

His back struck lower ground, and he rolled before Tank's paw closed over the space his head had occupied. The strike cracked stone. Mau got a knee under himself, drove upward and hit the bear beneath the jaw with the heel of his hand.

The permanent strength in his body did not wait for a copied technique to answer.

Tank's head lifted. Mau followed it, put both daggers across the broad neck and tore the form apart in a sweep of dark light.

He landed beside the scattering shape.

The rebuilding cost arrived almost with the satisfaction.

Beru had made him look up. Igris had made him turn. Bellion had made him move where the army wanted him.

He had been calling that protection when they did it for him.

Now he understood how much work had been hidden inside the word.

“Again,” he said, though nobody had stopped. ᴹ

Bellion lifted one hand.

The infantry separated.

Mau saw the strip of bare ground open in front of him and did not take it this time.

He went up instead.

* * *

The grip lifted a shelf of broken stone beneath his feet. He released the copied cutting pattern as he rose and gathered Red in his right hand.

Beru came after him.

Mau waited until the ant committed, then threw the force down past his shoulder rather than directly into him.

The blast struck the terrace below. Stone went out in a widening crown, carrying archers and infantry into the air. Beru twisted through the shock, used a falling slab to change his angle and met Mau before the echoes had returned.

Their weapons struck.

One dagger skidded along a claw. Mau turned it, caught the forearm and kicked Beru hard enough to send him into the ascending debris.

He let Red finish and reached for the sky's surface.

Fang's next attack folded over his left shoulder.

It tore a bright path through empty air instead of taking the place where his head had been. Mau felt the edge he had caught resist his hand. He changed the angle by less than he wanted and let go before he lost the sense of where the ground waited beneath it.

Kamish filled that ground's entire horizon.

The dragon's jaws opened.

Mau stopped trying to find a narrow gap.

There was none.

* * *

On the ridge, Shoko stood.

Gojo had his blindfold down around his neck. His face was still, but the fingers of his right hand had unfolded beside his leg.

Mau faced the dragon.

Yuka heard the first sound of the breath leave Kamish's throat and lost the little physical certainty that came from watching a familiar person practise in a place she knew.

The air above the basin became white.

For a moment she could not see Mau at all.

Then a blue point appeared beneath the torrent, small against the dragon and growing bright enough to draw the eye through the glare.

The light bent toward it.

Gojo's hand stayed open.

“Come on,” Yuka whispered. ʸᵏ
