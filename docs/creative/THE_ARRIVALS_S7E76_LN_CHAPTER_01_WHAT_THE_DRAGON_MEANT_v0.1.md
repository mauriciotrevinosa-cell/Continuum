The first line of the breath curved.

Mau had put Blue below it, close enough to the dragon's jaw that the pressure took the torrent before it could spread. A bright stream bent down into the ledge. Stone flashed and broke apart.

Then Kamish moved.

The rest came across Mau's position like a wall being dragged sideways.

He let Blue go.

The grip tore his footing upward. He left the slab as the torrent struck it and reached for a rough seam in the air. The fold caught only part of the attack. Heat ran past the edge and burned the outside of his arm before he could let the surface snap away from his fingers.

He fell into colder air with his sleeve smoking.

*Arm,* Ciel said.

*I know.*

*I need you to tell me whether you can close your hand.*

He closed it. The fingers answered. Two of them answered in the old, reluctant way; the others hurt for a new reason.

*I can.*

*Then use the grip. You haven't got a landing.*

Lantern passed beneath him.

Mau recognised the eyes before the jaws turned.

He caught the air against his body, checked the fall and moved sideways under his own flight. It bought him the width of the smaller dragon's head.

Kaisel took the width away.

* * *

He had ridden that turn.

He knew where the wing would lift, how the narrow body would seem to fall inward before catching its speed again. His own hand moved automatically toward a harness which was nowhere near him.

Kaisel's tail struck his hip.

The impact knocked him out of the small steady hold. He tumbled, found ground too far away and got Infinity into place as Lantern's teeth closed over the point he had almost reached.

The pressure stopped short of his chest.

It did nothing about the ground arriving from below.

Mau hit one knee first, rolled across the stone and came up with a dagger still in his right hand. The left had opened on impact. Its weapon lay beyond Lantern's lowered head.

He called the blade back into storage instead of throwing himself under the jaw to fetch it.

Two seconds later it was in his hand again.

Bellion's sword met it.

Below, Mau had finally driven Bellion back.

The marshal's weapon contracted along its joints. Mau stepped inside the shortening reach, struck a guard aside and put his shoulder into the heavy armour.

Bellion moved a single foot.

Three companies came out of the channels behind him.

* * *

The first soldier took the dagger in its shield.

The second struck the elbow which had driven it there.

Mau let the weapon go for an instant, caught the shield with the grip and turned it across the second soldier's throat. The two forms broke against each other. His empty hand closed around the returning dagger.

He cut through the third.

Above them, Fang drove a column into the channel mouth.

Mau was already moving when it hit. The pressure struck his back and sent him through the rest of the line faster than he had meant to go. He got Infinity between himself and a spear point, but Igris was waiting beyond it with a blade already aimed toward his next step.

Mau changed the step.

The knight changed with him.

For three exchanges they moved so close that the sword's full reach should have been a disadvantage. Igris used the pommel, then the guard, then his empty gauntlet. Mau caught the last strike on his forearm and answered with his knee.

The armour gave.

Igris went back one pace.

Beru came through the space behind him, claws level with Mau's chest.

Mau threw himself between the two attacks and felt the left dagger's edge catch something hard. One of Beru's claws broke. The ant caught his wrist with the other hand and smiled into his face.

“There you are, my king.” ᴮʳ

He hit Mau with his forehead.

* * *

The blood reached Mau's mouth before he felt his nose.

He dropped a dagger, seized Beru's forearm and used the grip against the stone under them both.

The slab rose.

For a heartbeat the ant had nothing to push from.

Mau brought his elbow down through the arm holding him. The form burst. He turned under the scattering dark, caught Igris's sword between the remaining dagger and the rising rock, and drove the knight through it.

Two restorations began behind him.

He could feel the cost without looking at a page.

The army did not need to be stronger than he was at every point. It needed to make him spend the same advantage twice, then three times, until he could no longer reach it cleanly when it mattered.

Kamish landed.

Stone climbed around the dragon's feet. One wing swept across the field, clearing a wide lane through his own soldiers; the companies stepped into shadow before it touched them and emerged beyond the end of the sweep.

Mau watched it happen.

“You practised that,” he said. ᴹ

Kamish lowered his head.

“We have been working while you have been living, little monarch.” ᴷᵐ

There was no accusation in it.

Mau heard one anyway, for a moment, before he could stop himself.

The dragon saw his face change.

Then Bellion's order carried across the basin, and the next attack gave neither of them time to speak.
