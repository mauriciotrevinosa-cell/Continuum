# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 42
## Chapter 6 — The Other End of the Order

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-28)
**Volume:** 42
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S7E76
**Continuity state:** B+7 spar continuous. Army tactical command delegated to Bellion; Mau retains recall/stop but does not inspect commanders' planned thoughts. Existing reconstruction and finite current access stressed. Katana/Rika-derived construct used for physical interception, not Copy kit. Mau switches equipped daggers to sword and loses only equipment boosts. No autonomous guest involvement, new unit, XP or new permanent buff.

*When: Bellion gives the next order, Mau hears only the last word aloud.*

“Close.” ᴮˡ

Kamish lifted.

The wind from his wings drove Mau's coat against his legs. Beneath the dragon, the soldiers who had been waiting in the dry channels began to climb. Shield ranks came first. Behind them, archers found higher ground while heavier shapes took the spaces between the shields.

Mau could feel where they were.

He had given Bellion their movements. The command to return remained his; the knowledge of what the marshal intended to do next did not arrive with every footfall. He would have had to reach into the work of the exercise to take that back.

He kept his attention on the field.

The shields stopped.

Fang's staff struck stone.

Three separate pressures reached Mau at once: the light building at the staff's head, the downward line of Kamish's flight and Beru's approach low enough to hide behind the first rank.

Mau looked at the spaces they had left.

*I need the reconstruction costs as they happen,* he told Ciel.

*I have them.*

*And my own work separately.*

*Yes. You're beginning to make the difference smaller than you think.*

He had no time to ask her to explain.

* * *

He went toward the shields.

The first soldier braced. Mau planted a foot on the lower edge, drove the dagger above it and used the body as a step. The shield did not fall when its owner broke apart; the next soldier had already caught it.

Mau came over the top and found Igris.

The knight's sword struck his leading dagger outward. Mau caught the returning cut on the second blade, heard the edge bite and felt the grip begin to turn in his hand.

He stored both weapons.

His next movement lost their added speed. He had expected the loss; Igris had expected it too.

The knight's gauntlet closed on his sleeve.

Mau drew the katana inside the clinch.

The scabbard fell away. A pale hand formed over his shoulder and caught the sword coming down past his left ear.

Igris looked up.

Mau used the instant to break the hold and get his own blade into the knight's side.

Dark armour opened.

The hand above him tightened around Igris's sword and stayed for a second after its opponent's body had scattered.

It was still searching for the next thing which needed to be kept away from him.

*Give it the task,* Ciel said.

Mau brought his own shoulder round.

The pale hand struck the shield rank behind him.

* * *

On the ridge, Yuta let out a breath he had been holding since the scabbard fell.

“He can keep it shaped,” he said. ʸᵗ

Gojo watched the arm which had reached past Mau's shoulder. More of the form was gathering behind it, a suggestion of ribs and a head turning toward the incoming light.

“For now.” ᴳ

Fang's attack came down into the space opened by the broken shields.

The shape caught the first edge, and Mau cut sideways under its arm. The technique struck the ground behind him. Its light shone through the unfinished part of the construct's shoulder.

Yuka pressed her lips together.

She had felt something like that hand around Tsurugi. Seeing it reach from Mau's sword made the distances between the lives he remembered feel physical for a moment: someone could stand here, in the same afternoon as her, carrying a thing from a battle none of them had been able to attend.

Yuta was watching with an expression she could not immediately name.

She touched his wrist.

He put his hand over hers without looking away.

* * *

Beru came through the falling fragments of the shields.

The pale hand reached to catch him. He went under it, struck the elbow and drove his other claws toward the place the construct joined Mau's attention.

Mau felt the shape tear.

He cut its supply himself.

The body dissolved before the ant's next blow could drag more work through it. His own sword met the claws with a sound sharp enough to hurt his teeth.

“Better,” Beru said. ᴮʳ

Mau shoved him back.

“You sound like Gojo.” ᴹ

“I have been obliged to listen to him.” ᴮʳ

The ant's next strike split the stone where Mau had been standing.

Mau laughed as he moved. He could not have said whether the laugh came from the answer, the near miss or the fact that this was finally difficult enough to make the voice in his memory stop being the loudest thing he heard.

He wanted the feeling to last.

* * *

Bellion met him at the edge of the channel.

The segmented blade came across low. Mau jumped it, struck down and found that the upper half had already reversed through its own arc.

The point opened his coat beneath the ribs.

He twisted. Pain followed the turn, hot and clear.

For a moment he was on the dark grass of another field, listening to someone say he was not yet what they needed.

Then stone struck his boot and the present returned with Igris rebuilding three paces away.

Mau put the katana away.

He needed his hands free.

Infinity met the first of the marshal's returning segments. He released it as he moved beyond the line and used the grip to wrench a slab upright between them.

The blade went through the slab.

Mau had already drawn the daggers.

He went under the falling stone, through the gap Bellion's own weapon had made and inside the reach of the marshal's arm.

His right dagger struck the breastplate.

For the first time since they began, Bellion had to give him more than a step.
