*When: Bellion gives ground, the army moves with him.*

The space behind the marshal filled before Mau could put his foot in it.

He drove the first defender back into the next. Their armour broke against each other, but the line folded rather than opened. Something heavy struck his thigh. He turned the point of a spear aside, kicked its owner down and reached for Bellion again.

The marshal was already beyond the next shield.

Mau stopped chasing him.

He could spend the afternoon destroying the things nearest his hands. They would return. Every returning body would cost him, while Bellion kept choosing the shape of the next exchange.

*I have an anchor past their back line,* he told Ciel.

*One crossing available. They know the operation.*

*They don't know when.*

He ran toward the wrong company.

The soldiers braced.

Mau changed places with one at the edge of Fang's position.

* * *

For an instant the whole basin was facing away from him.

He felt the crossing close behind the movement. It would not give him the same answer again in a moment just because this one had worked.

Fang turned his staff.

Mau caught it below the head, put the left dagger through the shadow caster's guard and drove him backward into the stone shelf. The form burst around the weapon. Mau let it scatter rather than gripping the empty staff.

Beru's claws came past his face.

He had expected a fast answer.

He had not expected Igris to be inside it too.

The two commanders crossed their attacks without crossing each other's paths. The knight took away the turn Mau preferred; the ant struck the space it left him. He used the grip to lift himself over the first line, caught a forearm with a dagger and felt the second strike glance across his back.

He landed with blood running warm beneath his coat.

Bellion lifted a hand.

Kamish answered.

* * *

This time the dragon did not breathe.

He struck the ridge behind Mau with both forefeet.

The shelf came down whole. Mau tried to lift its leading edge, felt the mass resist him and stopped trying to hold all of it. He turned one block, let another fall and ran through the lane they made.

Lantern waited at its end.

Kaisel's shadow passed across the opening above.

Beru stood behind him.

Mau looked up through the narrowing gap in the rock and saw Kamish watching him.

The dragon knew the shape of his rescue routes. He had carried him through too many of them not to.

Mau's face tightened.

Then he placed Blue outside the lane.

* * *

The pull took the fallen shelf sideways.

Not the entire mountain. The loose pieces nearest the opening, already broken under Kamish's weight. They struck each other and began to circle. Mau moved against the stream, using Infinity only long enough to keep one edge from taking his shoulder.

He let it go before beginning the next operation.

Red formed low in the hand he had kept behind his body.

Bellion saw it.

The marshal's sword extended toward his wrist.

Mau threw the push into the gathered rock before the blade could reach him.

The basin filled with fragments.

He was inside them, moving with a steadiness which had finally caught up to his speed. A touch of the grip changed one slab's direction. A dagger broke the soldier waiting behind it. A released pull took his next step farther than Igris expected, and Mau struck the knight in the centre of his armour.

Beru caught his shoulder.

Mau turned into the hold and put the second dagger up beneath the ant's guard.

The form broke around his arm.

Bellion arrived through it.

* * *

For three blows Mau stopped trying to make room.

Their weapons met close. He could feel the separate joints of the marshal's blade changing the force against his own, a new angle arriving before the previous one had finished.

He gave the right-hand dagger a short, ugly movement which Yuta would have corrected if they had been using wood.

The guard caught one joint.

Mau stepped on the next.

Bellion's sword pulled taut between them.

Mau let the dagger go, hit the marshal with his empty hand and drove him through the upright remains of the shelf.

Armour shattered against rock.

Behind the spreading dark, Kamish opened his jaws.

Mau looked toward the outer wall of the basin. The axis beyond it was empty. Gojo and the others were far to the east; the dragons had drawn him toward the uninhabited western cut.

He lifted his hands.

*Outward line clear,* Ciel said.

“I know.” ᴹ

* * *

He had made Purple in fear, in pain and in anger.

This one began with an interval he chose.

The remaining pull held the broken material where he needed it for a fraction longer. The reversal met the structure at the point he had watched, corrected and watched again. His hands did not have to become strangers to him for the two operations to find their relation.

Violet filled the space between them.

Kamish's breath met it above the broken shelf.

The collision tore the last standing edge out of the terrace. Mau felt the pressure come back through the route he was sustaining and changed his footing instead of feeding it more because it resisted him.

He sent the attack through the clear line.

The dragon's form broke in the light.

Beyond it, stone opened all the way to the sky.

The sound reached the ridge after Yuka had already seen the far wall go.

She gripped Yuta's sleeve.

Down below, Mau stood alone in the falling dust.

Then the first soldier rose again.
