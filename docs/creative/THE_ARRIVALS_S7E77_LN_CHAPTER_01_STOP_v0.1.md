*When: Mai reaches the ridge, the army is getting up.*

Marin had brought back the good paper.

That was the whole reason Mai had gone looking for him: two useful drawings, a question about the band and the small irritation of having made time for someone who had apparently decided the afternoon belonged somewhere else.

She found the ordinary practice ground empty.

Then she heard the basin.

By the time she reached the others, the biggest noise had ended. White dust hung above a gap in the far wall. Below it, shadows were assembling themselves from scattered dark.

Mau stood in the middle.

One sleeve was burned through. There was blood down the side of his face, and more along the coat which no longer closed properly beneath his ribs.

Beru was kneeling, a hand pressed to his breastplate as the last broken part of it settled back into shape. Bellion's sword was forming one joint at a time beside him.

Mai stopped beside Shoko.

“How long?” she asked. ᴹᵃⁱ

Shoko told her.

Mai looked down again.

“And he wanted to know what?” ᴹᵃⁱ

Nobody answered quickly enough.

* * *

Mau turned his right hand and watched the damaged skin pull across his knuckles.

The army had cost him more than the attack had.

Ciel separated the returning demands as clearly as she could. The deeper reserve answered, but the passage through him had begun to feel narrow. His own cursed-energy work had acquired a delay which he noticed before anyone told him about it.

*We have enough to examine,* she said.

He looked at the opening he had put through the far wall.

It had been a good line. He had chosen it, made it and held it. He remembered what his hands had done. Tomorrow he could look at the account with Gojo and understand which part had been useful.

Beyond that thought, another one was already moving.

What if he had been in the temple without any of it? What if the next room took something else? What if the body he was making stronger was the exact thing somebody wanted him to finish?

Bellion stood.

Mau raised the dagger.

“Again.” ᴹ

* * *

Mai put the drawings into Shoko's hands.

Maki turned toward her.

“Mai?” ᴹᵃ

“I need to ask him something.” ᴹᵃⁱ

She went down the first part of the path.

Gojo looked from her to Mau. His hand moved once at his side, ready; he did not call across the basin.

Below, Bellion was putting a company into a new line. Beru rose. The dragons were gathering above the outer terraces, still forming the last edges of their wings.

Mai stepped from the path onto the broad ledge which led toward the centre.

Mau saw her.

“Stop.” ᴹ

The word reached the whole army together.

Bellion's blade stopped opening.

Beru's foot stayed planted where he had been about to push off. Igris lowered his sword. The front ranks became still with their shields half-raised, and the wings above them held for a breath before the dragons settled onto the stone.

No new attack left a hand or a throat.

The silence was so immediate that Mai could hear a little piece of the broken terrace falling into a pool.

She kept walking.

* * *

Mau put the daggers away before she reached him.

He was suddenly conscious of how he must look. Of the burned cuff. Of the drawing she had carried at breakfast. Of the fact that she had come all the way here to find him standing inside another ruined place with his clothes stuck to him.

“Are you hurt?” he asked. ᴹ

Mai stopped in front of him.

“No.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked past her at the path. The distance she had crossed seemed longer now that nobody in it was moving.

“You could have called.” ᴹ

“You saw me.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And you stopped.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked at her.

Mai waited until he was looking at her properly.

“I knew you would.” ᴹᵃⁱ

The words did something the blows had not. His face tightened, and for a moment he had to find somewhere to put his hands.

She pointed to a flat piece of stone.

“Sit down before I have to make this a longer conversation.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He lowered himself onto it.

* * *

The army stayed still.

A thousand presences surrounded the little place where Mai brushed dust from the space beside him and sat. Kamish rested his head along the upper terrace. Igris turned away to give them what privacy a field full of witnesses could offer.

Mai looked at the fresh cut under Mau's coat.

“Shoko's going to enjoy that.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“She knew what we were doing.” ᴹ

“Did you?” ᴹᵃⁱ

He drew a breath.

“Training.” ᴹ

“I saw that part.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked at the stopped soldiers. At the weapons turned down because he had said one word.

“I wanted to know whether I could handle them when they weren't helping me.” ᴹ

“And?” ᴹᵃⁱ

“They're very good.” ᴹ

Mai almost smiled.

“You sound pleased.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“I am.” ᴹ

She let him have the answer. Let him look at what he had actually learned before asking the next question.

“Then what was the next round for?” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked down at his empty hands.

For a long time, that was where his answer stayed.
