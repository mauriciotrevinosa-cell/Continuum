# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 42
## Chapter 11 — Something to Talk About

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S7 v0.1 (2026-10-28)
**Volume:** 42
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S7E78
**Continuity state:** B+7 night, directly after supper/design. Shared bedroom and existing Our Year map/Two Beginnings retained. Mau/Yuka discuss ordinary wishes and vessel fear without turning her into therapist or changing guest consent. No sex, proposal, new family promise, colour cure or power use. Rika-derived construct inactive; fresh spar injuries cared for.

*When: Yuka decides their ordinary conversation requires moving the chair.*

“It's in the wrong place.” ʸᵏ

Mau looked at it.

The chair had stood beside the little table since he moved into the room. His scarf hung over its back. One of Yuka's sleeves had fallen through the space below the seat and was hanging there as if the chair had been trying it on.

“It's been there for months.” ᴹ

“I've been patient.” ʸᵏ

She picked up the sleeve and put it on the bed, then took his scarf off the back and folded it properly.

Mau rose to help.

“Sit,” she said. ʸᵏ

He stopped halfway up.

She pointed to the bed.

“That was very commanding.” ᴹ

“You spent the afternoon practising. I watched.” ʸᵏ

He sat.

Yuka moved the chair six inches to the left. Stood back. Moved it another three. The small difference made room to open the cupboard without turning sideways, but placed the table inconveniently far from the person in the chair.

She looked at it for a while.

“The table is wrong too.” ʸᵏ

Mau laughed and caught the edge when she began to drag it. He used his uninjured hand, and she allowed the assistance with the gravity of someone deciding whether a defeated army could be trusted to carry furniture.

When they finished, the room looked almost exactly the same.

Yuka sat in the chair and put her feet on his knees.

“Better.” ʸᵏ

* * *

She had washed the McLaren hoodie and hung it out before dinner. Now she wore a plain loose shirt and the bracelet he had given her. The flower rested against the inside of her wrist as she pushed her hair behind one ear.

He watched the colour move.

She caught him looking and smiled.

“Was this what you wanted to talk about?” he asked. ᴹ

“It's an excellent subject.” ʸᵏ

“Furniture?” ᴹ

“Where we live.” ʸᵏ

He looked around their room again.

His shoes beneath the shelf. Her clothes taking up more of the drawer they had agreed to divide. Two books on the table, one of them hers, one left there by someone who had come in to ask a question and ended up staying to explain a completely different thing.

“I like it,” he said. ᴹ

“Me too.” ʸᵏ

She pulled one foot back so he would stop rubbing the wrong part of her ankle and put it down a little to the side.

“Here.” ʸᵏ

He corrected his hand.

“Very precise.” ᴹ

“You said you wanted to improve.” ʸᵏ

* * *

There was a small picture at the edge of Our Year which he had never asked her about.

He reached for the map when she let him lean forward. She took it from him before the rolled edge could catch the bandage on his arm.

“That one,” he said, pointing. ᴹ

A mountain. A path going up one side and a crooked little shape at the top which might have been a tent, a tree or a very determined bird.

“That's us,” she said. ʸᵏ

“Both of us?” ᴹ

“Your criticism has been received.” ʸᵏ

He bent closer.

There were two marks beneath the crooked shape. Close together, one slightly taller, their feet going in different directions because the paper had been folded while she was drawing them.

“I wasn't criticising.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸᵏ

He touched the edge of the mountain, careful not to put a fingertip across the figures.

“You want to go?” ᴹ

“Yes. When the weather's good. Somewhere we can sit for long enough that going up isn't the whole trip.” ʸᵏ

“What would you bring?” ᴹ

“Food.” ʸᵏ

“An extensive plan.” ᴹ

“You're invited. There has to be food.” ʸᵏ

He looked up at her.

“What else?” ᴹ

She thought about it.

“Something warm for later. A blanket that doesn't slide down the mountain. And I'd like to be able to see the house. I like being far enough away to miss it a little while still knowing where it is.” ʸᵏ

He looked back at the two marks beneath the crooked shape.

“I can manage that,” he said. ᴹ

“After your arm stops looking like that.” ʸᵏ

He nodded.

* * *

She left the map open on her lap.

For a while they argued about whether the bird-shaped object was a reasonable representation of a tent. He drew a small improvement in the air with a finger, and she told him that a man whose ring drawings had required an intervention should choose another subject.

Then she asked about the fight.

He told her about Bellion leaving the lane open on purpose. About the moment he had tried to reach for Kaisel's harness while Kaisel was trying to hit him with his tail.

Yuka laughed so hard that she had to put the map on the table.

“You really did that?” ʸᵏ

“For a second.” ᴹ

“Did he notice?” ʸᵏ

“He hit me.” ᴹ

“That's a yes.” ʸᵏ

Mau laughed with her until his side objected. She put a hand against his shoulder and waited for him to take a more careful breath.

Her expression settled afterward.

“Were you angry with him?” ʸᵏ

“Kaisel?” ᴹ

“Ashborn.” ʸᵏ

Mau looked down at the map lying open beside them.

“At first. In there.” ᴹ

“And now?” ʸᵏ

He thought of the quiet room, the guest who had asked for nothing but the chance to remain in it.

“No. I know he didn't ask for any of it.” ᴹ

“Knowing didn't make the feeling leave.” ʸᵏ

He shook his head.

Yuka came out of the chair and sat beside him.

“You can tell me when it comes back,” she said. ʸᵏ

He took her hand.

“Even if I already know what you think?” ᴹ

“Especially if you start arguing with me in your head before I've said anything.” ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She raised an eyebrow.

“You do.” ʸᵏ

“Sometimes.” ᴹ

“And am I winning?” ʸᵏ

“Usually.” ᴹ

She leaned into him, pleased.

Later, when he lay down, she moved the new position of the chair into reach of the bed so his water would be nearer.

He watched her do it.

“We moved the table for that.” ᴹ

“Then we improved two things.” ʸᵏ

She got in beside him and put out the light.
