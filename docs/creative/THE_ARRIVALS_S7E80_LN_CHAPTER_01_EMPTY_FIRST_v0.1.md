*When: Gojo asks what Mau is going to put inside the boundary, Mau holds up an empty hand.*

“Nothing.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴳ

They had chosen level ground away from the basin's broken shelves. Shoko had checked the fresh repairs that morning and made him demonstrate a slow turn without disguising a pull at his side. The arm was healing cleanly. It still needed care. He had agreed to one short piece of work, with the next attempt decided from what the first actually did.

Yuka stood beside Yuta with the rolled drawings beneath her arm.

Maki had brought a marker which looked very much like a weapon until she used it to scratch the limit of the empty test space into the ground.

“If this moves,” she said, “I want to know which of you moved it.” ᴹᵃ

Gojo put a hand to his chest.

“The suspicion.” ᴳ

“Experience.” ᴹᵃ

Mau smiled and took his place.

* * *

He knew the beginning.

The old attempt against Tsurugi had been brief enough that remembering it as an achievement was easier than remembering the work inside it. The rematch had taught him the opposite lesson. He could make the opening. Keeping the conditions precise against another person's refinement was another matter.

Today nobody was putting a world against his.

He began with the boundary.

The space inside the marked circle deepened. The far side seemed to retreat, though the scratched line stayed beneath his feet. He could see a dark breadth where there should have been only a few paces of ground.

He kept the information effect out of the work.

There was no target waiting to receive it. No reason to make that part simply because the shape reminded him of the territory from which he had learned it.

*Boundary stable,* Ciel said. *No attached exposure.*

Mau breathed once.

The far edge trembled.

He recognised the temptation to add more and held the same operation instead.

* * *

“The right side,” Gojo said. ᴳ

Mau followed his attention.

He had expected a fault in the wall. What he found was a mismatch between the distance he meant and the shape he was making it carry. He corrected it too quickly. The opposite side tightened.

“Slower,” Yuta said. ʸᵗ

He eased the correction.

For a moment the whole little territory agreed with itself.

It was quiet in a way which did not come from the observers being quiet. Every piece of the opening seemed to be waiting for one chosen instruction instead of carrying several competing ones.

Mau could feel how much of his attention had gone into that simple result.

He let it close.

The ground returned to its ordinary scale.

* * *

The copied technique did not immediately answer when he checked for its familiar edge.

He stopped reaching for it.

Gojo saw the movement in his face.

“There it is,” he said. ᴳ

“Burnout.” ᴹ

“Yes. We haven't invented our way out of that by deciding the room was for practice.” ᴳ

Mau sat on the flat stone Shoko had selected before they began. She checked his eyes and asked about pressure, speech and the difference between tiredness and pain.

He was tired. There was no headache yet.

Yuka crouched beside him and unrolled the paper.

“When you corrected that side,” she said, pointing, “this one came in. Did it feel like two mistakes?” ʸᵏ

He considered it.

“It felt like one thing I hadn't understood yet.” ᴹ

She looked up at Gojo.

He smiled.

“Keep that answer.” ᴳ

* * *

Yuka's own work came afterward.

She used the Garden she knew, over a defined patch of ground, and showed him the point where the surface could receive a movement without becoming an instruction for everything beyond it. Mau watched from outside. Nobody tried to make their territories coexist around the same person.

She missed the first placement and corrected it herself.

Yuta asked her why the second had worked.

Her answer began with the shape she had imagined and ended with something she had felt under her feet. He made her distinguish the two. She scowled at him, thought again and gave him a better account.

Mau listened.

He had helped her find parts of the route. Watching her explain a choice he had not made for her felt better than showing somebody a finished mechanism.

She closed the Garden before she was too tired to tell what had changed.

When she came back, he held out her water.

“That one was yours,” he said. ᴹ

She drank, then looked at him over the rim.

“They all are.” ʸᵏ

He grinned.

“Yes, they are.” ᴹ

* * *

They spent the afternoon with the papers.

Mau did not attempt a second opening. The difficult part was leaving a useful question unanswered while it was still interesting. Gojo helped by being interested enough to keep drawing alternatives without letting the conversation become a challenge.

They marked three kinds of connection.

One belonged to the boundary. One to the medium inside it. One would eventually belong to whatever effect he chose to attach.

For the combination, they would begin with the first two alone.

That sentence was written clearly at the top of the next sheet.

Mau read it again before they put everything away.

Outside, Rem was calling people to move the table before dinner. Yuka took the paper out of his hand, rolled it loosely with her own and nudged his shoulder toward the door.

“You can keep thinking while you carry a chair.” ʸᵏ

He picked up the chair with his good arm.

The smallness of the job felt restful.
