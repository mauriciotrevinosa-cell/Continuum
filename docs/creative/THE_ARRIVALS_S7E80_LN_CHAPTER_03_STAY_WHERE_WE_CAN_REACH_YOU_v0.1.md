*When: Rimuru reaches the clinic door, Mai is already holding it open.*

She had heard Yuta on the stair outside and gone to see what he was carrying.

There had been no time to ask a complete question. Mau's head rested against Yuta's shoulder, his arm hanging loose, and Shoko was telling them which bed she wanted.

Mai moved the chair out of the way.

Now she kept her hand on the door while Rimuru came through, and watched him look at the blood on the cloth beside the pillow.

“What happened?” he asked. ᴿ

“An experiment,” Gojo said. ᴳ

He was standing at the foot of the bed. His blindfold was still down around his neck. The word sounded inadequate even before he finished saying it.

Shoko did not look up.

“Ciel. I need what she saw from inside.” ˢʰ

Rimuru became still for a moment.

Then he pulled the spare stool closer.

* * *

Yuka sat beside the head of the bed.

They had asked her to give them room during the first part, and she had. She had stood at the wall with her hands held together, listening to words which ought to have helped because they had meanings and instead became more frightening whenever someone said one too carefully.

Now Shoko let her stay close.

Mau's hand was warm. It did not close when she put hers around it.

“There was a third connection,” Rimuru said. “He wasn't trying to open a third world. Ciel says he reached for a condition shared by more than the two arrangements they were watching.” ᴿ

Gojo nodded.

“That's where it changed outside too.” ᴳ

Shoko asked another question. Rimuru listened, then answered in Ciel's words as closely as he could.

Yuka watched Mau's face.

He had looked annoyed with the diagram that morning. Had made an enormous stick beside the little drawing of Beru and asked if she thought the ant would object to the likeness. She had told him Beru would demand a caption explaining the size of his devotion.

The joke had been less than an hour ago.

She rubbed her thumb once against his knuckle.

* * *

Shoko's attention changed before anyone announced a result.

The work in her hand steadied. Her shoulders came down slightly, and the next breath she took did not seem held away from the room.

She stayed with him longer.

When she finally moved her hand, Gojo stepped forward half a pace.

“Shoko?” ᴳ

“I've stopped the acute damage from continuing,” she said. “He's sleeping now. I want to see him wake, speak and move before anybody asks me for a better sentence.” ˢʰ

Yuka shut her eyes.

Her hand tightened around Mau's, then eased when she realised how hard she was holding him.

“Can I stay?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Yes.” ˢʰ

The simplicity of the answer nearly undid her.

* * *

Outside, Yuta stood in the little space beside the clinic window.

Maki came to him without asking whether he needed someone there. He looked at his hands, at the marks left by carrying a body which had been entirely unresponsive to the movement.

“He was talking,” Yuta said. ʸᵗ

Maki leaned beside him.

“I know.” ᴹᵃ

“Then he wasn't.” ʸᵗ

She put her hand against the back of his wrist.

For a while neither of them tried to make a useful explanation out of it.

Mai stood farther down the wall, looking at the papers Yuka had dropped. Someone had gathered them before leaving the field. One corner was dirty. The instruction at the top remained perfectly clear.

*The first two alone.*

Gojo came out and stopped beside her.

“He wasn't sneaking another one past us,” he said. ᴳ

She looked up.

“I didn't say he was.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Gojo looked at the line on the page.

“No.” ᴳ

He sounded as if he needed to hear it himself.

* * *

Rimuru brought Ciel's next message when Shoko had finished writing the first account.

“The ordinary connections are quiet. She's keeping the experimental route closed. Mai and Yuka don't need to do anything through their channels tonight.” ᴿ

Mai nodded from the doorway.

“They aren't cancelled?” she asked. ᴹᵃⁱ

“No. She says it's a pause in the work, not the end of the agreements.” ᴿ

Yuka looked down at the hand she was holding.

She had not wanted to use anything from him. Hearing that she could choose not to, that the absence of use did not mean another part of him had disappeared, helped in a way she could not have explained to someone outside the room.

Rimuru listened again.

“The guests are all right,” he said. “No change to those boundaries. Ashborn asked whether Mau was being looked after. Ciel told him yes. DIO knows where he is.” ᴿ

No one asked either guest to become the answer to the bed in front of them.

Shoko put her pen down.

“Good.” ˢʰ

* * *

Rem brought food when the room had become quiet enough for people to notice they had missed it.

Yuka refused the first offer without looking away from Mau.

Mai took the bowl and sat beside her.

“Two choices,” she said. “Eat it yourself, or I'll get very embarrassing about helping.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka looked at her.

Mai raised the spoon.

“You wouldn't.” ʸᵏ

“I have been an aunt for a very short time. There are traditions to establish.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka made a small, broken sound which became a laugh only at the end.

She took the bowl.

Mai stayed while she ate, talking about the ordinary indignity of finding that someone had used the good drawing paper to calculate cupboard dimensions. The ring drawings remained away from the clinic bed. There would be time to decide what the surprise needed when Mau could take part in it again.

* * *

After dark, he moved his fingers.

Yuka stopped breathing for a second.

His eyes did not open. The movement was small and uncoordinated, a hand finding pressure against the palm it had been resting in. She called Shoko softly.

Shoko watched it happen again, checked him and let Yuka keep the hand.

“Sleep too,” she said. “I can call you from that chair.” ˢʰ

Yuka put her forehead against the edge of the mattress. She had meant only to rest her neck.

Mau's fingers shifted against hers once more.

She lifted her head long enough to whisper that she was there, then settled back into the chair with the blanket Rem had brought.

Across the room, Shoko turned the lamp down.

Gojo sat beside the door, awake, listening to a friend write the things they would need to understand in the morning.
