*When: Mau wakes, the first thing he sees is Yuka's sleeve.*

It lay across the mattress beside his hand. Her wrist had slipped partly out of it during the night. The bracelet was turned inward, the little flower hidden between her arm and the blanket.

He tried to lift his head.

The room tilted with an immediate, concentrated pain behind his forehead.

He put it down again.

Yuka moved.

For a moment she stared at him as if her own sleep had become unreliable. Then she sat forward so quickly that the chair scraped.

“Mau?” ʸᵏ

“Hi.” ᴹ

The word sounded rough.

She put a hand over her mouth.

He looked at the clinic wall, the lamp turned low though morning had reached the window, the folded cloth on the table. He remembered standing on the marked ground. A correction. The second condition giving way beneath the first.

Then he was here.

“What happened?” he asked. ᴹ

Yuka pressed his hand between both of hers.

“I'm getting Shoko.” ʸᵏ

* * *

He had to answer some very ordinary questions before anybody explained the extraordinary thing.

His name. Where he thought he was. What he remembered doing. He followed Shoko's hand, told her about the headache and showed her the old stiffness in his fingers. The fingers had not found a new way to be wrong. His vision was still blurred at the familiar left edge, and Yuka still held the colour in the room.

Shoko listened to the difference between each complaint.

When she was satisfied enough to sit, Mau became aware of how hard he had been waiting for her expression to change.

“You overloaded the work you were trying to perform,” she said. “It injured you. We stopped the damage from continuing. Today I want you in this bed while we see how you recover.” ˢʰ

“I remember trying to put them together.” ᴹ

“Tell us that part.” ˢʰ

Gojo came in with Yuta.

They took the chairs which Shoko indicated instead of standing over the pillow. Yuka stayed on the other side, close enough that Mau could turn toward her without lifting his head.

He told them about holding the boundary. About changing the distance and finding that the surface moved with it. The shape had seemed close enough to understand if he could only put his attention on the right point.

“Then I tried to correct which part belonged to which operation,” he said. “I felt something I recognised, but it was the cutting route. I hadn't meant to reach that.” ᴹ

Gojo nodded.

“We saw it begin.” ᴳ

“Did I open—” ᴹ

“No. It never became a third complete domain. Nothing cut the people standing beside you.” ᴳ

Yuka's thumb moved against his hand.

* * *

“So I can't combine them.” ᴹ

“That's not what I said.” ᴳ

He leaned forward, elbows on his knees.

“One territory already asks you to keep a great many things precise at once. The boundary, what it means to contain, what you're attaching to it. Call it nine tenths of your attention if that helps you picture it. We aren't measuring your reserves with that number.” ᴳ

Mau listened.

“You changed the conditions while you were still learning which parts shared the same support,” Gojo continued. “You corrected one thing by moving another. Then the correction reached a third pattern because you've got more available than the two you were watching.” ᴳ

Yuta put a hand on the folded account in his lap.

“Seeing where the connections went wasn't the same as being able to perform that correction cleanly,” he said. ʸᵗ

Mau's face tightened with recognition.

He had understood the information. He had mistaken that relief for having finished learning how to use it.

“My body didn't have the control to give all three what the change asked for.” ᴹ

“Exactly,” Gojo said. “A lot of fuel doesn't teach the machine how to turn three different ways at once.” ᴳ

Ciel had been quiet while he listened.

He reached for her carefully, as if the headache might have made the ordinary contact another thing to hurt.

*I'm here,* she said before he could ask.

*I heard you begin to tell me.*

*The new transition was faster than the earlier model. I had enough time to recognise that it was wrong. I didn't have enough to prevent all the feedback.*

*You stayed.*

*Yes.*

Her answer was very firm.

He told the others what she had said. Shoko added it to the account without making either of them responsible for being able to predict an interaction they had never observed.

“Next time—” Mau began. ᴹ

“You are allowed to finish the sentence tomorrow,” she said. ˢʰ
