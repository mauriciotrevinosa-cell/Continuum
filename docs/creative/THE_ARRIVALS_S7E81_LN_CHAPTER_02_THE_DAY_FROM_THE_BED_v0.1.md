*When: Mau asks what time it is, Yuka tells him he has already asked twice.*

“Because nobody's given me a useful answer.” ᴹ

“After lunch.” ʸᵏ

“That's the same answer.” ᴹ

“It's the same afternoon.” ʸᵏ

He turned his face toward her and smiled. The movement hurt less than it had in the morning. He took the small improvement very seriously and tried not to turn it into permission to sit all the way up.

Yuka put his cup where he could reach it.

“Are you bored?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“A little.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸᵏ

He looked offended for her benefit.

“I'd like you to spend enough time being bored that you begin to appreciate it,” she said. ʸᵏ

* * *

The room acquired people in small numbers.

Rem brought food which could be eaten without turning the bed into a complicated piece of furniture. Ram came behind her with a napkin, took one look at Mau trying to reach past the bowl and moved the water herself.

“A difficult opponent,” she observed. ᴿᵃ

“The cup?” ᴹ

“The concept of remaining still.” ᴿᵃ

He ate while they told Yuka what they had done to the evening meal. Momo had wanted the spice kept separate for anyone who disagreed with her idea of mild. Ram considered this an unnecessary concession to weakness. Rem had made two bowls anyway.

For a while the conversation had nothing to do with his head.

When Rem asked which he wanted later, he had an answer other than whatever was easiest to carry to him.

“The one with the spice,” he said. ᴹ

Yuka turned toward him.

He thought about the pepper at breakfast two days before.

“Some of the spice.” ᴹ

Ram inclined her head, accepting this limited retreat.

* * *

Rimuru arrived when Yuka went upstairs for clean clothes.

He took her chair and leaned back far enough that Mau had to look at him to decide whether the rear legs were still touching the floor.

“Don't,” Mau said. ᴹ

“The excitement might help.” ᴿ

“Shoko will put you in the next bed.” ᴹ

Rimuru set the chair down.

“An excellent point.” ᴿ

For a moment they smiled at each other without speaking.

Then Rimuru's expression softened.

“You gave her a fright.” ᴿ

Mau looked at the empty doorway.

“I know.” ᴹ

“I meant Ciel too.” ᴿ

Mau listened inward.

*I can say it myself,* she told him.

He shut his eyes briefly.

“She's objecting to the spokesman,” he said. ᴹ

Rimuru nodded as if this was a familiar professional difficulty.

“She objects to a lot of the management.” ᴿ

* * *

Ciel waited until he was ready to listen rather than merely ready to apologise.

*I can help you distinguish what the information says. I can't promise that knowing the difference will make every new operation safe before we've seen it work.*

*I know.*

*You knew afterward. Next time I need you to let that uncertainty remain part of the decision before you finish the movement.*

He looked at the ceiling.

The difficult part was not that she was asking him to learn more. He liked learning more. The difficult part was leaving a movement unfinished while knowing there was something on the other side which he might be able to understand.

*I thought I was already stopping the wrong part,* he said.

*I know. That's why we'll examine the transition, rather than write down that you intended to do something you didn't intend.*

His throat tightened.

*Thank you.*

*You can help by sleeping when Shoko tells you.*

Rimuru saw the change in his face.

“Good conversation?” he asked. ᴿ

“Persistent management.” ᴹ

Rimuru grinned.

* * *

Mai came near sunset.

She brought no drawing paper.

Mau noticed at once. She saw him notice and lifted the book in her hand.

“I have interests,” she said. ᴹᵃⁱ

“I'm aware.” ᴹ

“Occasionally they aren't your fingers.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He laughed, then regretted the quick movement of his head.

Mai sat while the pain settled.

“Marin has improved the line,” she said. “We're leaving the making until you aren't a terrible place to put the supply.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“You could do a little without—” ᴹ

“I could also read.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked at her.

She opened the book.

“Nobody's waiting for me to hurt my hand over a deadline we haven't chosen.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He lay back against the pillow.

For a while she read and he listened to the house beyond the clinic door. The sound had layers: plates, a chair moved, someone laughing too loudly at a thing he could not hear. He began to drift while trying to decide whether he recognised the laugh.

When Yuka came back, Mai put a finger to her lips.

The two women spoke quietly beside the door. He caught only the soft movement of their voices, neither asking him to solve anything, and went back to sleep before he could make himself wake enough to join them.
