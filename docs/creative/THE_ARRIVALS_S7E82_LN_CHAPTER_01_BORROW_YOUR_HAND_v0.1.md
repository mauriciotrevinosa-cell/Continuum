*When: Marin asks to borrow her hand, Yuka looks at her for a very long time.*

They stood outside the clinic. Mau had fallen asleep after breakfast, and Shoko had told her to go do something in a voice which made staying to argue sound like a task nobody needed.

Marin held a narrow strip of paper.

“I can say something terribly unconvincing about sleeves,” she offered. ᴹᵃʳ

Yuka looked down at the strip.

Then up again.

Marin's smile became helpless.

“I'm trying to be respectful of the surprise.” ᴹᵃʳ

Yuka held out her left hand.

Her face had gone warm. She looked away while Marin wrapped the paper around her finger, adjusted the overlap and made a careful mark.

“He knows I don't want something that catches on everything?” Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

“He started with that.” ᴹᵃʳ

She looked back.

“He did?” ʸᵏ

Marin nodded.

Yuka watched the little strip come away, carrying an embarrassingly ordinary measurement for something which had suddenly become difficult to hold still in her chest.

“Don't tell him I asked.” ʸᵏ

“About not catching?” ᴹᵃʳ

“About how he started.” ʸᵏ

Marin folded the paper and put it safely away.

* * *

Inside, Mau was awake again.

Ori sat on the end of the bed with a stack of cards. Frieren had taken the chair beside her and was watching the cards with more attention than she gave most explanations before breakfast.

“You have to pick one,” Ori told him. ᴼ

“I picked one.” ᴹ

“You picked the same one.” ᴼ

“It's a very good card.” ᴹ

Frieren looked at his hand.

“It isn't.” ᶠ

Yuka leaned against the door frame.

Mau saw her and tried to appeal silently for help.

She folded her arms.

“You heard the expert.” ʸᵏ

He put the card down.

Ori took it back into the stack, considered a moment, then gave him two different ones.

“There. Now you can't do it again.” ᴼ

Frieren's mouth moved at one corner.

* * *

They played until Ori had won enough times to begin explaining that winning was not the important part.

“How fortunate,” Mau said. ᴹ

Yuka laughed.

Frieren asked how his head felt. He told her the light no longer hurt as much and that Shoko had permitted a short time sitting up. He did not describe this as having recovered completely.

“Then sit,” Frieren said. “You don't need to demonstrate the rest.” ᶠ

Ori gathered the cards. When she reached across the mattress, the compass on its cord slipped out of her collar.

Mau moved it away from the edge before it could strike the bed frame.

“Thanks,” she said, putting it back. ᴼ

She went with Frieren to find Milim, who had apparently agreed to a game and then fallen asleep during its preparation.

Mau watched them leave.

Yuka took the space at the end of the bed.

“You were very generous about losing,” she told him. ʸᵏ

“Some of it was talent.” ᴹ

* * *

Beru came later.

Mau had asked Igris to let him know he could visit, and still the ant stopped at the clinic door as if someone had put an instruction there he could not bear to interpret incorrectly.

Shoko looked up from the table.

“If you break my chair, you're bringing another one.” ˢʰ

Beru inspected the chair.

Then lowered himself with extraordinary care.

Igris stood behind him.

“My king,” Beru said. ᴮʳ

“I'm awake.” ᴹ

“I can see.” ᴮʳ

“You're staring as if you intend to keep checking.” ᴹ

Beru did not deny it.

His gaze moved over Mau's face and the hand resting on the blanket. There was nothing theatrical in the first part of the silence. He was looking for damage he could recognise, a task he could do with the strength which had been very useful in the basin and was no answer at all to a headache in a quiet room.

Mau reached out.

Beru offered his clawed hand with almost painful gentleness.

“The fight was good,” Mau told him. “This wasn't from that.” ᴹ

“I was informed.” ᴮʳ

“I want to make sure you heard me say it.” ᴹ

The ant bowed his head.

Igris turned slightly away.

Yuka put a hand over her mouth when Beru's first very large tear landed on the blanket.

“Oh, don't start,” she said softly. ʸᵏ

“Little Rope, I have not started.” ᴮʳ

Another tear fell.

Mau looked at Igris.

The knight inclined his helm with the weary dignity of someone who had been made responsible for a weather event.

Shoko brought a cloth before the blanket required changing.

* * *

After they left, Yuka helped Mau settle back against the pillow.

He looked at the damp mark near his knee.

“I should ask him to bring his own.” ᴹ

“He'd bring a ceremonial one.” ʸᵏ

“With embroidery.” ᴹ

“And a speech.” ʸᵏ

They were both laughing when Shoko came to tell them that the experiment of remaining upright had reached its very ordinary end.
