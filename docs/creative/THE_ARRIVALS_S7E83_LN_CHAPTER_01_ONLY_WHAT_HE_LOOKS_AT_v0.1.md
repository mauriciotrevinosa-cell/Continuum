He looked at the people around the table.

Yuta. Mai. Marin and Wakana. Rimuru had arrived with a cup and stayed when he saw Mau trying to decide how to begin. Gojo stood by the door beside Shoko, who had asked whether this was a lesson and received a very firm no.

“The asking part,” Mau said. ᴹ

Yuta's smile came before the answer.

Maki held up a finger.

“Yesterday you wanted a mountain with no crowd waiting for her to answer.” ᴹᵃ

“I still do.” ᴹ

“Then explain.” ᴹᵃ

Mau sat down.

* * *

He had asked Ciel about the possibility the evening before.

Not about making the whole house stand on the mountain. Not about putting his own attention into other people's heads, or showing anybody what happened behind his eyes when he looked at Yuka.

One outside view.

A soldier who knew where to stand. A temporary connection offered to people who wanted it. The image could use the same chosen-display principle they already knew, with the source kept apart from the information nobody had asked to share.

Noise had worked on that ordinary route with Ciel while leaving the failed domain arrangement closed.

They had a small test ready, not a promise that every distance and every person would work simply because he liked the idea.

“I want them to be part of it,” he said. “If she wants that too. We'd tell her the connection exists before I ask, and close it if she'd rather have the mountain to ourselves.” ᴹ

Mai folded her arms.

“And if we all make terrible noises?” ᴹᵃⁱ

“They stay here.” ᴹ

“A useful feature,” Shoko said. ˢʰ

* * *

Igris stood at the workshop door for the first trial.

Mau asked Mai and Rimuru whether they were willing to receive one view for a few moments. He explained the source, the direction and how to leave it. Both agreed.

The connection settled with much less sensation than Mai had expected.

At the edge of her attention appeared the workbench, seen from the door. A cup stood beside Wakana's elbow. She could hear Marin ask whether the cup was supposed to be part of the picture.

Mai turned toward the actual bench.

The little view did not turn with her.

“That's strange,” she said. ᴹᵃⁱ

“It's his eyes,” Mau answered. “You haven't borrowed his neck.” ᴹ

Igris looked down.

The picture became the floor.

Marin laughed.

“Very artistic.” ᴹᵃʳ

Igris looked up again with patient dignity.

* * *

Rimuru checked the same boundary with Ciel from his side.

What arrived was the image and the sound the source was allowed to supply. No private conversation between the hosts. No sensation from Mau's body, no original memories and no taste of the tea Rimuru was using to test whether having two views would make him spill it.

He did spill a little.

Shoko took the cup away.

“An important contribution,” she said to Gojo. ˢʰ

Gojo put a hand over his heart.

Mau asked Mai to withdraw.

She did. Her view closed; Rimuru's remained until he chose to end it too. Then Mau shut the temporary route himself, and Igris's eyes belonged only to the knight and the ordinary connection through which Mau knew his army.

There was no headache.

Shoko asked him anyway.

He answered her without trying to make the success larger than the short, local test they had actually done.

* * *

They named the next checks.

A few more willing viewers together. A source farther away. The actual mountain position before the day, with the house as the receiving point. If it failed there, they would use the people at home as people to tell afterward, not turn the proposal into a demonstration he had to force through.

The route would end with the occasion. No hidden recording. No right to look through somebody because they had once accepted a view of a cup.

“What about her?” Yuta asked. ʸᵗ

Mau looked at him.

“She gets the choice on the mountain. Before there's a question to answer. I won't make her decide whether to disappoint an audience she didn't know existed.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴹᵃ

By noon, the test had acquired more volunteers than Mau had expected.

They came in ordinary groups. People asked sensible questions, made embarrassing comments about how long he intended to kneel and practised leaving the small picture when he asked them to. Nobody needed to surrender their afternoon to keep a connection open indefinitely.

Umaru demanded a rehearsal of the moment so she could assess the angle.

“No,” Mau said. ᴹ

“Production value matters.” ᵁ

“You can assess the chair.” ᴹ

Igris turned obligingly toward it.

Behind Umaru, Bocchi covered a smile. Ori asked whether she could watch beside Frieren rather than on a separate seat. Fern wanted to know when they should have dinner ready, and then remembered that the whole point was for two people to eat somewhere else first.

The house continued moving around the little trials.

Mau stood beside the table with the closed box against his chest and began to understand how many people were quietly helping him ask one person a question.
