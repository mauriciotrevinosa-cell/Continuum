## Chapter 23 — A Blanket That Stays

*When: Beru sees the blanket, he asks how many defenders it requires.*

“Four corners,” Mau said. ᴹ

The ant looked at the fabric.

Then at the ledge.

Then at the narrow spaces between the stones where the wind was already trying to get underneath it.

“I can arrange a perimeter.” ᴮʳ

“You can arrange four stones.” ᴹ

Igris bent to pick up the first.

Beru watched him for a moment, reconsidered the scale of the operation and found three more.

* * *

The mountain was on the other side of the familiar ridge, away from the white scar the spar had put through the old basin.

From the ledge, the house sat below the trees with its roof lines following the courtyard rather than closing over it. Mau could find the workshop by the light on one window. The garden was a different patch of grey from the ground beyond the fence. A narrow path curved through it, disappearing under leaves before it returned to the door.

He stood looking at that door for a while.

*This is the view she meant,* he told Ciel.

*You should leave room to sit and see it,* she answered. *The table is a little too far forward.*

He moved the table back.

* * *

They had brought what already existed.

A small table Wakana had checked for a loose joint. Two ordinary chairs. Cloth from the cupboard, with Rem's instruction that he bring it back before somebody decided romantic occasions required leaving good linen on a mountain.

Two workers carried the folded pieces up the last turn while Mau walked the route with Igris. Kaisel had brought the heavier load to a landing place below the ledge. Nobody asked the dragon to balance furniture where a guest would later need to stand.

Mau put stones along the edge of the blanket, then tried sitting on it himself.

The view stayed open.

He put his hand where Yuka would probably reach to brace herself and found a sharp edge beneath the cloth. He shifted the blanket, then decided that was too small an answer and had the workers bring a flatter piece from the prepared pile.

Beru watched every adjustment with a seriousness he usually reserved for something attempting to kill his king.

“Comfortable?” the ant asked when Mau sat again. ᴮʳ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Little Rope must find it so.” ᴮʳ

Mau looked up at him.

“I agree.” ᴹ

* * *

The food required more care than the furniture.

Rem had helped him choose things which would remain good during the journey. He would finish the warm part before bringing Yuka up, with one ordinary helper tending it until they arrived. Momo had corrected his first idea about the sauce, and Ram had explained that pouring twice as much into a container would not make it travel twice as well.

There were bread, vegetables, a savoury portion he had practised rather than improvised and a small dessert Yuka liked. A drink she would enjoy; water too. He had stopped adding things when Rem asked how much time he intended to spend eating instead of looking at the person he had brought.

They arranged the covered containers in shade.

Mau checked the chairs again.

Igris watched him.

“They have not moved, my liege.” ᴵᵍ

“I know.” ᴹ

He checked anyway.

* * *

The longer viewing test used the position they would actually need.

Igris stood on the path above the ledge, where the image could include the table and the view without hovering over either person. Mau offered the source for a few minutes to the volunteers waiting with Rimuru in the house. The quiet route held over the measured distance they had now tested; it did not become a promise to send every view to every place in the world.

Rimuru confirmed the image through the ordinary return arrangement after the test. The viewers withdrew. Mau closed it.

No pressure behind his eyes. No strange connection beginning beside the chosen one.

He sat on the blanket and allowed himself one slow breath of relief.

* * *

Before going down, he called Beru and Igris close.

“When we're up here tomorrow, you stay on the other side of the path,” he said. “Until I ask. If somebody wants help, I'll call.” ᴹ

Igris bowed.

Beru looked toward the table, then at Mau's pocket.

“Of course, my king.” ᴮʳ

Mau waited.

“Beru.” ᴹ

“I have heard the order.” ᴮʳ

“Good.” ᴹ

The ant's hands folded with great deliberation.

* * *

Yuka was downstairs when Mau returned.

She looked at the dust on his shoes and the leaf caught in his cuff.

“Ordinary work?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Very ordinary.” ᴹ

“You look pleased with it.” ʸᵏ

He leaned against the back of her chair.

“Would you come out with me tomorrow?” ᴹ

Her expression softened before she answered.

“Where?” ʸᵏ

“Somewhere you put on the map. Bring something warm for later.” ᴹ

She looked up at him.

“Does the blanket stay?” ʸᵏ

He smiled.

“I've had it inspected by professionals.” ᴹ

Yuka reached back for his hand.

“Then yes.” ʸᵏ

Later, he told the two guests the next day's plan. DIO preferred to remain with the people in the house; Ashborn chose his quiet and wished him well. Nothing about the mountain required someone resting to become part of the performance.

Upstairs, Yuka had laid out the clothes she wanted before he reached their room.

The little box remained in his pocket until she went to wash. He put it safely away, then helped her find the second glove which she was certain had not decided to leave on its own.
