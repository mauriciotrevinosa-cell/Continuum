Mau looked down.

He had wrapped the bread. He had checked the lid on the food. He had found the second glove twice, first beside the washbasin and then in Yuka's pocket, where she had put it after asking him why he was carrying it around.

The butter was in his hand.

“Possibly.” ᴹ

“Then use a dish.” ᴹᵃ

Across the table, Yuka began to laugh.

He looked at her in an appeal she made no effort to answer. She had her chin on her hand, her sleeve drawn back enough for the bracelet to catch against her wrist. The little flower shifted when she reached for the dish and slid it toward him.

“I'll take responsibility for this part,” she said. ʸᵏ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“Very demanding work.” ʸᵏ

Maki sat down beside Yuta and let them make a little more trouble for themselves over breakfast.

* * *

The morning still had things in it which were not his.

Rem wanted the containers brought back rinsed. Mai found him beside the workshop and pulled a thread from the edge of his coat without asking whether she was allowed to improve it. Ori wanted to know whether the mountain had snow, and when he said not where they were going she reconsidered the suitability of the journey for a moment.

“You can bring me a stone,” she decided. ᴼ

“Any stone?” ᴹ

“A good one.” ᴼ

He accepted the specification seriously.

Frieren, sitting behind her, looked up from the page.

“Don't make it too large.” ᶠ

Ori turned.

“Mum.” ᴼ

“We already have a doorstop.” ᶠ

Mau left them discussing the difference between a stone and a stone with a purpose. On the stairs he met Yuka coming down with a warm coat over her arm.

She stopped a step above him.

“Do I look all right?” ʸᵏ

He looked at her properly, the question catching him more effectively than every careful thing he had been trying to remember.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Yuka waited.

He realised he had not moved out of the way.

“Very all right.” ᴹ

She smiled and bent to kiss him from the step. When she drew back, she brushed the place beside his collar which Mai had missed.

“You too.” ʸᵏ

* * *

He spent the last part of the morning in the kitchen.

The warm dish behaved better than it had during his first attempt. He had learned when to stop stirring it, and Momo watched him reach that point without having to take the spoon away.

Momo reminded him about the lid.

He obeyed. Rem fitted the covered dish into the basket and put the folded cloth around it. Yuka carried the bread; she had insisted on being responsible for something other than standing attractively by a door while he worried.

At the back step, Shoko asked the ordinary questions she had asked yesterday.

He gave her ordinary answers.

No headache. No new blur. The walk still felt like a walk.

“Good,” she said. “Enjoy yourselves.” ˢʰ

Gojo leaned around the frame behind her.

“And if you need—” ᴳ

Shoko caught his sleeve.

“They don't.” ˢʰ

Yuka grinned at both of them and took Mau outside.

* * *

Kaisel waited beyond the nearest garden beds, folded down until the saddle was easy to reach.

Yuka put a hand against the dragon before climbing on. Mau checked the basket's fastening and settled behind her. She found his hand at her waist and drew it a little closer, as matter-of-factly as he had pulled the blanket over her feet that morning.

“Ready?” he asked. ᴹ

“Are you?” ʸᵏ

He laughed into her hair.

Kaisel lifted.

The house drew back without becoming small all at once. There was the turn beside the workshop, the roof over the familiar corridor, a gap in the leaves where somebody had hung cloth to dry. Mau watched the garden path disappear beneath them and felt Yuka settle against his arm as the dragon rose into open air.

For a while they said nothing.

Then she pointed.

“There. That's the bend I drew badly.” ʸᵏ

He followed her hand toward the mountain path.

“I recognised it.” ᴹ

“You recognised the mountain. That doesn't mean you recognised my bend.” ʸᵏ

“I was being generous.” ᴹ

She turned far enough to look offended at him. Kaisel banked gently while Mau laughed, and Yuka held his arm until they were facing the same way again.

* * *

The landing place was below the ledge, sheltered from the strongest wind.

Mau got down first. He held up a hand, and Yuka used it without pretending that the short descent was beyond her. They untied the basket together. Kaisel lowered his head when she thanked him, then withdrew after Mau checked that they would not need him until later.

The path turned twice.

At the second turn, Yuka slowed.

A length of plain cloth moved gently above a small table. Two chairs stood on the level stone, angled toward each other and the view. Beyond them, a blanket waited with four remarkably unromantic stones holding its corners down.

She looked at the stones first.

Mau watched her face.

“Professionals,” she said. ʸᵏ

“Very thorough.” ᴹ

Yuka walked ahead of him. She touched the back of one chair, looked at the covered places on the table and then at the open ground where the mountain fell away toward the trees.

The house was there.

Not hidden behind the hill, not lost so far below that she had to imagine its shape. The courtyard had a little clear space around the tree. The windows caught the afternoon. A person moved along a path too distant to recognise.

Yuka stood with her hand on the chair.

Mau put the basket down rather than ask her too quickly whether she liked it.

“You remembered which way I wanted to sit,” she said. ʸᵏ

He came to stand beside her.

“You put an arrow.” ᴹ

She turned toward him, smiling with her eyes a little wet.

“It was a very bad arrow.” ʸᵏ

“I recognised it.” ᴹ

This time she let him have that.

She reached for his coat, pulled him down far enough to kiss him and kept him there until the worker tending the last covered dish very discreetly found a reason to face the mountain.

When she let go, Mau had forgotten which container needed opening first.

Yuka looked at the table.

“Sit with me,” she said. ʸᵏ

He did.
