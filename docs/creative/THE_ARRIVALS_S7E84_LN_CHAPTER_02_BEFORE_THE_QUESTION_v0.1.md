She took a second bite before answering the question he had not asked.

“It's good.” ʸᵏ

He let out a breath.

“You don't have to look quite that surprised,” she added. ʸᵏ

“I was aiming for relieved.” ᴹ

Yuka reached across the table and moved his plate a little nearer him.

“Now you try.” ʸᵏ

He did. The dish had travelled well. Nothing had leaked into the bread. The drink was still cool enough, and when the wind shifted he could hear a small dry sound in the grass above the ledge rather than the anxious rattle he had imagined from every piece of crockery.

He began to eat.

After that, she told him the part of her morning he had missed.

Tsurugi had asked where they were going. She had told him the mountain, and he had asked which mountain with such studied indifference that she had offered him the map. He had immediately lost interest in geographical accuracy.

“He said he hoped you brought decent food.” ʸᵏ

Mau looked down at the dish.

“That's almost friendly.” ᴹ

“I thought so.” ʸᵏ

“What did you tell him?” ᴹ

“That if you didn't, I knew the way home.” ʸᵏ

Mau began laughing. Yuka smiled into her cup and let him.

* * *

When they had eaten, he carried the dishes to the basket. The soldier who had tended the food withdrew at his thanks, leaving the table and the path quiet.

Yuka had moved to the blanket.

She patted the space beside her. He sat down, careful of the little box in the inner pocket, and she immediately stole the warmer side of his coat by leaning against him.

“I brought my own,” she said when he looked down. ʸᵏ

“I noticed.” ᴹ

“This one's occupied.” ʸᵏ

He put his arm around her.

Below them, someone had taken the drying cloth in. The place it had hung was only a line among the branches now. Yuka found the workshop window and pointed it out, then the route they had taken from the back step.

“It looks peaceful from here,” she said. ʸᵏ

“It was loud enough when we left.” ᴹ

“That too.” ʸᵏ

She settled lower against his shoulder.

For a while he could feel her thinking without having to ask what every silence meant.

“Do you remember when we were talking about a room?” she asked. ʸᵏ

He nodded.

“I was so certain somebody would find a reason it had to be more difficult.” ʸᵏ

“There were several suggestions.” ᴹ

“Rimuru was very committed.” ʸᵏ

He laughed, and she caught the movement against her back.

“I like waking up and having you there,” she said. “Even when you're attached to the wrong part of the room.” ʸᵏ

“The ceiling has good qualities.” ᴹ

“I've learned.” ʸᵏ

Her fingers found the seam at his wrist. She rubbed it between finger and thumb, looking at the roof which covered the room they now shared.

“I like knowing which drawer you put things in. And knowing you'll ask where they are anyway.” ʸᵏ

“I don't always.” ᴹ

She turned her face up.

He reconsidered.

“Some things move.” ᴹ

Yuka laughed and kissed the place beneath his jaw before turning back toward the view.

* * *

He waited until she had finished the last little account of her morning.

“There's something I wanted to ask you first,” he said. ᴹ

She moved enough to see his face.

“First?” ʸᵏ

“The people at home asked if they could watch part of the afternoon. Only if you want them to.” ᴹ

Yuka looked toward the house, then back at him.

“Watch from where?” ʸᵏ

He pointed to the higher turn of the path. Igris stood there, far enough away to leave their conversation its space, his gaze directed politely beyond the ledge until needed.

“His view. Picture and sound, one way. They've tried the connection with me. Nobody's been watching us eat.” ᴹ

Yuka looked at the knight.

Mau waited, letting the offer remain an offer instead of explaining it until it became impossible to refuse.

“They wouldn't see what you see?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“No. Just what Igris looks at.” ᴹ

“And they can't shout advice?” ʸᵏ

He shook his head.

“We tested that feature very thoroughly.” ᴹ

She began to smile.

“Who's waiting?” ʸᵏ

He named the people who had said they wanted to be there. Her grandparents. Mai. Rimuru. Their friends moving between the room and whatever still needed doing at home. Ori beside Frieren. Umaru with opinions about the angle.

Yuka looked down at his hand, then at the table someone had helped him bring up the mountain.

“They helped you with all this.” ʸᵏ

“Quite a lot.” ᴹ

She sat with that for a moment.

“Then I'd like them here,” she said. “For this part.” ʸᵏ

He touched her cheek.

“If you change your mind, tell me. We can close it.” ᴹ

“I will.” ʸᵏ

* * *

Mau opened the route they had practised.

At home, the willing viewers accepted the offered image. Igris looked toward the ledge. Two people sat on a blanket held down by stones, with the table behind them and the house far below.

In the room where Maki sat, nobody needed to ask whether it had begun.

Yuta put down his cup.

Mai drew one foot back beneath her chair and fixed her attention on the small band of light at the edge of the offered picture. She could not see the ring yet. Her fingers remembered making it.

Ori leaned against Frieren's arm.

On the mountain, Yuka lifted a hand toward Igris.

“Hello,” she said, and laughed at herself. ʸᵏ

There was no answer from the house.

The quiet belonged to them.

Mau stood, offered her both hands and helped her up from the blanket. She came close enough to straighten the edge of his collar again.

He covered her hand with his.

For the first time that afternoon, she saw how much it was trembling.
