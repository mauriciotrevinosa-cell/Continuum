*When: Yuka feels his hand trembling, she stops fixing his collar.*

“Mau.” ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

The wind had lifted one strand of her hair against his coat. He moved it away from the fastening before he answered, a small familiar thing his hands could still do without asking him to remember the speech.

“I had quite a lot to say.” ᴹ

“You can say it.” ʸᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

She waited.

Below the mountain, the house lay in the late light. He knew the route from the back step to their room, the place in the corridor where someone usually left a basket, the cupboard he had helped move because reaching for a cup should not require Yuka to lean over his shoulder every time.

He had been happy when she did it anyway.

For weeks he had tried to put that into something better than a list of furniture.

Now she was standing in front of him, warm fingers beneath his, waiting for the thing he wanted to ask.

“I used to think about the future as somewhere I needed to get everybody to,” he said. “If we could get through the next thing, there would be a day when I could stop and work out what I wanted.” ᴹ

Yuka's expression changed a little. She knew the habit. She had sat beside it and sometimes argued with it; she had also been tired enough to understand why it kept returning.

“And then we'd eat,” he said, “or you'd be waiting upstairs, annoyed because I was looking for something I'd put in the drawer myself. And I'd realise the day had already happened.” ᴹ

Her mouth moved at the corner.

“You still look in the other drawer first.” ʸᵏ

“I have a system.” ᴹ

“A terrible one.” ʸᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

The laugh eased something in his chest. He took her hand properly instead of holding it where it had been adjusting his coat.

“I like our room,” he said. “I like that you have plans for it which don't stop when we go to sleep. I like hearing what you thought about something I didn't even notice. I like that you want to go places with me, and that sometimes you'd rather stay in.” ᴹ

Yuka blinked.

“I like being the person you tell.” ᴹ

She pressed her lips together. He could see she was trying to let him finish.

“And I want to keep doing it. The ordinary part. Asking where you want to sit. Finding out which thing you're excited about this time. Having an opinion about a chair and losing.” ᴹ

She laughed, and a tear escaped before she could catch it.

He brushed it away with his thumb.

“I want to make a family with you,” he said. “We've talked about it, and I still want us to get to decide what that looks like. When we're ready. Together.” ᴹ

She nodded once, eyes fixed on his.

“I want to be there when you change your mind about something. I want you to be able to tell me if I'm getting it wrong. I want there to be years of things we haven't thought of yet.” ᴹ

Yuka squeezed his hand so hard that he nearly lost the next sentence.

“You make me happy,” he said. “I don't think I tell you that simply enough.” ᴹ

* * *

At home, Mai looked down at her own hands.

On the picture, Mau reached inside his coat.

Yuta's fingers stopped against the side of his cup. Beside him, Maki sat very still, looking at Yuka in the patient face of someone trying to remember every part before the afternoon moved on.

Ori had one hand on Frieren's sleeve.

Nobody spoke over the little voice coming from the mountain.

* * *

The box caught for an instant against the lining.

Mau exhaled a small, disbelieving laugh. All the practice with the chairs, all the careful carrying, and he had never tried taking the thing out with Yuka holding his other hand.

She loosened her grip without letting go.

“Here,” she whispered. ʸᵏ

He freed it.

She looked at the box, then at his face, and he saw the exact moment when knowing became happening.

He lowered himself onto one knee.

The stone beneath him was solid. He could feel its roughness through the cloth. Her hand remained in his, and the whole prepared mountain seemed to narrow to the little space between the lid and her expression as he opened it.

The silver band lay against the dark lining.

It was the size of something a person could wear to breakfast.

He had never wanted anything to be more beautiful.

“Yuka,” he said. “Will you marry me?” ᴹ

Her free hand went over her mouth.

For a heartbeat he could hear only the wind against the tablecloth.

Then she nodded so quickly that the tears fell, and she bent toward him with a small broken laugh.

“Yes.” ʸᵏ

He stared at her.

“Yes, Mau.” ʸᵏ

The second answer reached somewhere the first had left him unable to move.

He began smiling. He could feel it happen, helpless and enormous, and she laughed again because now he was the one who looked as though he might need a moment before doing anything useful.

“You have to put it on,” she whispered. ʸᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

His fingers disagreed with him for one small second.

Yuka kept her hand steady. He took the ring from the box, found the finger Marin had measured and slid it into place. The band passed the knuckle and settled where it belonged.

He looked at it on her hand.

She looked at it too.

Then Yuka pulled him up so suddenly that he had just enough time to close the empty box before she kissed him.

* * *

He put an arm around her waist.

She was crying into the kiss and smiling at the same time. He could feel the strange uneven breath against his cheek when she drew back, only far enough to see him.

“You asked everyone except me first,” she said. ʸᵏ

“Not everyone.” ᴹ

“How many?” ʸᵏ

He looked helplessly toward the table.

She began laughing again and tucked her face beneath his jaw.

“I wanted it to be right.” ᴹ

“It is.” ʸᵏ

Her hand moved against his back. The new band touched the cloth between his shoulder blades, and the slight unfamiliar pressure made him close his eyes.

She stayed there until both of them were breathing more evenly.

When she lifted her head, she held her hand between them and turned it in the light.

“Mai?” ʸᵏ

He nodded.

“With Marin and Wakana. They made sense of my drawing.” ᴹ

“I saw your drawing.” ʸᵏ

“Then you know how much help I needed.” ᴹ

She touched the shallow line with her other thumb. Followed the little turn, found where it came back around and did it again, as if she were learning a path she intended to use often.

“It doesn't catch,” she said softly. ʸᵏ

“You use your hands.” ᴹ

She looked at him for a long time.

Then she took his face in those hands and kissed him again.

* * *

At home, the silence broke in several places at once.

Marin had both hands over her mouth. Wakana was smiling at the floor, having spent the last minute discovering there was no dignified way to watch someone use the box he had made without feeling responsible for every second of it.

Yuta looked at Maki.

She wiped beneath one eye with the base of her thumb.

“Don't,” she said. ᴹᵃ

He shook his head, smiling too much to offer a convincing denial.

Ori looked up at Frieren.

“She said yes.” ᴼ

“I heard.” ᶠ

Frieren rested her hand over the small fingers gathered in her sleeve and watched the two people hold each other on the mountain.

* * *

Yuka stepped back just far enough to see his face.

“Say it again,” she said. ʸᵏ

He blinked.

“The question?” ᴹ

She nodded, suddenly shy.

He laughed softly.

“Will you marry me?” ᴹ

Her answer came without a moment's hesitation.

“Yes.” ʸᵏ

Mau bent his forehead to hers.

This time, he heard it without forgetting to breathe.
