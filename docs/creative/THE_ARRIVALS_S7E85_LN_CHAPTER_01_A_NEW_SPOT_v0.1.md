*When: the first sound beyond the path is a very carefully suppressed sob.*

Mau lifted his head.

Yuka turned with him, one hand still caught in the front of his coat.

Beru stood where the path widened.

He had a handkerchief.

It was already open. One corner was pressed beneath an eye; the rest hung from his hand in the exhausted state of a thing which had been brought for a purpose and discovered that the purpose was larger than expected.

“Little Rope,” the ant said. ᴮʳ

Mau looked at him.

Beru took another step.

“I cannot call you Little Rope anymore.” ᴮʳ

Yuka made a small sound against Mau's shoulder.

Mau kept looking at the ant.

Beru saw the look. He pressed the handkerchief more firmly to his face.

“I know, my king. I know.” ᴮʳ

The words shook with feeling.

“But I'm saying goodbye to my side next to yours.” ᴮʳ

* * *

At home, Rimuru bent forward so abruptly that Gojo caught his cup.

The view from the higher path held the whole dreadful dignity of Beru walking toward the newly engaged couple. Yuka had covered her mouth. Mau stood with the expression of a man finding out that one of the most powerful soldiers in his army had come to mourn a seating arrangement.

Mai made a helpless noise.

“He brought a handkerchief,” Marin whispered. ᴹᵃʳ

Beside Frieren, Ori watched very seriously.

“Is he sad?” ᴼ

Frieren considered the image.

“He's having a difficult afternoon.” ᶠ

* * *

“Beru,” Mau said. ᴹ

The ant straightened at once.

It did nothing for the tears.

Yuka stepped far enough out of Mau's arm to reach for him.

“If we're together, you can stand on my side,” she said. ʸᵏ

Beru stopped breathing.

Or performed the idea of stopping so completely that Mau had to look away for a second.

“Here,” Yuka added, patting the space beside her. ʸᵏ

The ant crossed to it as carefully as if she had shown him the last bridge over a burning country.

He stood beside her.

He looked at Mau on her other side.

Then he began crying harder.

Yuka turned toward Mau, her own tears interrupted by laughter which she was making a brave and unsuccessful effort to contain.

“You see?” she said. “Enough room.” ʸᵏ

Mau touched the back of her hand.

“I see.” ᴹ

* * *

Igris spoke from the upper path.

“Sorry, my liege.” ᴵᵍ

Mau looked up at him, then at Yuka.

She nodded, still laughing.

He closed the viewing route.

At home, the mountain disappeared. The last thing the willing viewers received was Beru trying to fold the handkerchief into a shape equal to his new responsibilities.

Then the room had only its own noise again.

Igris came down the path. He put one hand against Beru's arm, waited until the ant looked at him and gently turned him back toward the way he had come.

“It's okay,” he said. “It's okay. You've got a new spot protecting Little Rope.” ᴵᵍ

Beru looked at him in devastation.

“She is not Little Rope anymore.” ᴮʳ

Yuka lost the last of her composure.

The ant pressed the cloth to his eyes.

“I need time to think.” ᴮʳ

“Of course.” ᴵᵍ

Igris led him up the path with the patience of someone prepared to provide as much time as the terrain could reasonably contain.

Beru looked back once.

Yuka lifted her new ring hand and waved.

The sound which followed them around the turn made Mau sit down on the blanket before his knees chose for him.

* * *

Yuka sat beside him.

For a while neither managed to say anything useful.

He laughed until his face hurt. She leaned against him with one hand pressed over her mouth, gave up and laughed openly into his coat. Whenever one of them began to settle, the other would remember the handkerchief.

Finally Yuka held out her hand.

“Look what I have.” ʸᵏ

He took it.

The silver caught the light along its shallow curve. He ran a thumb beneath the band, checking without intending to that it had not become too tight in the minutes since he had put it there.

“Is it comfortable?” he asked. ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸᵏ

He nodded.

She turned her hand and threaded their fingers together.

“You can stop checking.” ʸᵏ

“I probably won't.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸᵏ

Her shoulder rested against his.

The light changed slowly over the trees. Mau could see the difference in brightness on the roofs, the way the shadows filled a path before reaching the next one. Beside him, Yuka's hair caught a warmer colour as she leaned forward to reach the dessert they had forgotten.

She offered him the first bite.

He took it, and she complained that he had left too little on the spoon.

They argued about that until she kissed him to win.

The tablecloth moved in the wind behind them. All four stones held.
