The attempt was impressive.

Rem was dealing with the returned containers. Umaru had discovered an urgent interest in the arrangement of the cups. Gojo stood beside the table with both hands in his pockets and a smile which made the idea of innocence seem actively inconvenient.

Yuta looked up.

“Good afternoon?” he asked. ʸᵗ

Yuka stopped in the doorway.

Mau, carrying the basket behind her, nearly walked into her back.

She looked at her grandfather. At Maki sitting beside him. At Mai, who had taken such care to appear occupied with something on the table that the blank sheet in front of her had become rather obvious.

Yuka began smiling.

“I have something to show you,” she said. ʸᵏ

She held out her left hand.

The room gave up.

* * *

Maki reached her first.

She took Yuka's hand, looked properly at the ring and then pulled her into an embrace without finding a sensible introductory sentence.

Yuka closed her eyes against her shoulder.

“You saw?” she asked quietly. ʸᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹᵃ

Maki drew back enough to see her face.

“I still wanted you to tell me.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka nodded, smiling so hard that she looked close to crying again.

Yuta put an arm around them both. When he looked over Yuka's head at Mau, there was nothing to answer except the feeling in his expression.

Mau put down the basket.

Mai caught his sleeve as he passed.

“Well?” she asked. ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked at her in disbelief.

She held the look for two seconds before smiling.

“It fitted.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“It fitted.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He hugged her before she could find another practical thing to say.

For a moment she stood with her hands suspended beside his back. Then she patted his shoulder twice, and he could feel her trying not to make too much of it.

“You're wrinkling the sleeve I fixed,” she said. ᴹᵃⁱ

He laughed and let her go.

* * *

The ring travelled no farther than Yuka's hand, but the hand went most of the way around the room.

Marin examined the line with the reverence of someone who had argued about it at a much larger scale. Wakana asked whether the inside sat comfortably, and Yuka made a fist to show him before thanking him for the box too.

Tsurugi looked at the band, then at Mau.

For a moment, neither said anything.

“About time,” Tsurugi said. ᵀˢ

Yuka elbowed him.

Mau smiled.

Ori waited until there was room to reach them. She took Yuka's hand in both of hers, turned it carefully and looked at the small line.

“Mum, look.” ᴼ

Frieren bent enough to see.

“It's pretty,” she said. ᶠ

Yuka beamed at her.

“Thank you.” ʸᵏ

Mau remembered the promise he had made before leaving. He found the little mountain stone in the basket's side pocket and put it into Ori's waiting hand.

She inspected its pale edge and the smooth place his thumb had found.

“This is a good one,” she said. ᴼ

He felt absurdly proud.

* * *

Later, sitting with a cup while the room carried on around them, Yuka held her hand above the table again.

Her old ring rested on its cord beneath her collar. The bracelet shifted at her wrist, its flower turned outward now. Each thing had kept the place it already held in her life.

Mau touched the new band.

“We could do more with it someday,” he said. “If you wanted. Something like the support in your grandfather's ring.” ᴹ

Yuka looked from him to Yuta.

Then back to the silver.

“No.” ʸᵏ

Mau withdrew his hand at once.

She caught it, amused.

“A new one. For my wedding present.” ʸᵏ

Yuta put down his cup very carefully.

Mau stared at her.

“He had about sixty years to get that good,” he said. “You've given me until a wedding we haven't even planned.” ᴹ

“An excellent reason to start learning,” Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Yuta began laughing.

“I wasn't working on it every day for sixty years.” ʸᵗ

“Please don't help her.” ᴹ

“You offered.” ʸᵗ

Mai leaned into the conversation from the other side of the table.

“If you're changing the drawing again, you can wait until I've finished my tea.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“Nobody's changing this one,” Yuka said. ʸᵏ

She put her hand beneath Mau's and turned his palm over it.

“This one is my engagement ring.” ʸᵏ

He looked down at their hands.

After a moment, he nodded.

“All right.” ᴹ

* * *

Dinner had expanded without anyone declaring it an occasion.

They had eaten on the mountain, so Yuka said she only wanted a little. Rem served her the amount she asked for. Ten minutes later she reached toward Mau's plate, and he moved it within range without breaking his conversation with Rimuru.

Beru had not returned to the room.

Igris appeared briefly at the open door. Mau met his gaze, and the knight inclined his head with a restraint which somehow conveyed a considerable report.

“Is he all right?” Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

“He is considering the future, my lady.” ᴵᵍ

Yuka pressed her lips together.

“He can still come and sit with us.” ʸᵏ

“I have explained that.” ᴵᵍ

Mau lowered his head to the table.

Beside him, Rimuru began laughing again.
