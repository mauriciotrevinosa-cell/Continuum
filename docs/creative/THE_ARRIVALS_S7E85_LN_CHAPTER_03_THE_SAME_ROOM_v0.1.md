*When: they finally get upstairs, Yuka shuts the door and laughs.*

Mau turned from putting down the empty basket.

“What?” ᴹ

She leaned against the door, still wearing the coat she had forgotten to take off downstairs.

“I'm engaged.” ʸᵏ

He began smiling.

“I heard.” ᴹ

“To you.” ʸᵏ

“That part I remember.” ᴹ

She came across the room so quickly that he only just straightened before she put her arms around his neck.

This kiss had no wind in it. No relative choosing very carefully not to stand too close. No ant trying to work out whether he had lost a position in the army.

Yuka drew back, looked at him and kissed him again because she could.

His hand found the fastening on her coat.

“Are you keeping this on?” ᴹ

She looked down as if somebody had added it while she was occupied.

“Possibly for the whole marriage.” ʸᵏ

“I'll put that in the drawing.” ᴹ

She let him help her out of it, laughing against his shoulder.

* * *

They put their things away badly.

The basket made it beside the door. His coat reached the hook, but her scarf ended up across the back of the chair. The empty box sat on the table until Yuka picked it up again.

She opened it.

The small hollow in the lining looked suddenly different without the ring.

“Can I keep this?” she asked. ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

“Of course.” ᴹ

She closed it carefully and put it beside Two Beginnings. After a moment she moved the book so neither object hid the other.

Mau watched her choose the place.

“Happy?” she asked without turning. ʸᵏ

He nodded, then remembered she was looking at the shelf.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She turned then.

It was a very small room in which to have so much room left in the future.

* * *

Later, washed and changed, she sat cross-legged on the bed with Our Year open between them.

The mountain was still badly drawn.

She followed the arrow with a fingertip, paused at the little open place and looked up at him.

“We need to add something.” ʸᵏ

“The stones?” ᴹ

“The very important stones.” ʸᵏ

He found a pencil. Yuka took it from him before he could improve the mountain into something neither of them would recognise, and added four little marks around the blanket.

Then she wrote one word beside it.

*Yes.*

He read it.

She waited until he looked at her before closing the map.

“That wasn't difficult to decide,” she said. ʸᵏ

His smile changed. She could see the relief in it, more clearly now that the evening no longer had an audience.

“I didn't want to assume.” ᴹ

“Good. You asked very nicely.” ʸᵏ

He laughed, and she moved the map to the table before settling beside him.

* * *

Her hand lay on his chest.

He could feel the band when she shifted it. An unfamiliar edge among familiar things: her bracelet against his shirt, the warmth beneath her sleeve, her hair caught under his chin until she pulled it free with a complaint.

For a moment, an old picture tried to attach itself to the word he had asked her to say.

He recognised it. Let it remain a memory long enough to know where he was.

Yuka lifted her head.

“Here?” she asked. ʸᵏ

He looked at her, took a breath and nodded.

“Here.” ᴹ

She touched his cheek.

“We don't have to plan everything tonight.” ʸᵏ

“I wasn't planning.” ᴹ

“You were getting the face.” ʸᵏ

“Which face?” ᴹ

She demonstrated.

He stared at her in offence so immediate that she started laughing before she could hold the expression.

“I don't look like that.” ᴹ

“You do when you think a chair might have defeated you.” ʸᵏ

He rolled her carefully toward the pillow and kissed the argument out of reach.

* * *

When they lay down properly, she put the lamp out.

The room settled around them in the same shapes it had held the night before. The chair was still close enough for him to reach his water. Someone went past in the corridor, paused outside another door and continued when a voice answered.

Yuka found his hand beneath the blanket.

“My fiancé,” she said very quietly. ʸᵏ

He turned toward her.

“You like that?” ᴹ

“I'm practising.” ʸᵏ

“How am I doing?” ᴹ

She brought his hand closer and kissed his knuckles.

“I'll let you know.” ʸᵏ

He could hear the smile in it.

After a while, her breathing changed. He remained awake just long enough to realise he had stopped looking for another useful thing to do with the day.

Then he slept beside her.
