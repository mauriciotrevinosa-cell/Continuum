“I thought we brought everything,” Mau said. ᴹ

Rem looked at him.

He remembered the table. The folded cloth. The blanket still held down by its four diligent stones.

Beside him, Yuka took another bite of breakfast.

“We brought the important part,” she said, lifting her left hand. ʸᵏ

Rem smiled despite herself, then pointed toward the cupboard where the returned crockery belonged.

Mau put the cups away.

* * *

The mountain looked less ceremonial in the morning.

He went up after breakfast with Igris and the workers who had helped carry the furniture. Yuka stayed to finish the task she had arranged with Mai, having told him that being engaged had unfortunately failed to complete it for her.

The blanket had not moved.

Mau lifted the first stone and found the grass beneath it pressed flat. He brushed the cloth, folded it properly and looked for a moment at the place where she had stood.

Igris waited beside the table.

“Was the position all right?” Mau asked. ᴹ

“She seemed pleased, my liege.” ᴵᵍ

Mau nodded, smiling down at the last corner of the blanket.

“Thank you for helping.” ᴹ

The knight inclined his head.

“It was a good use of the afternoon.” ᴵᵍ

They carried the pieces down to the landing place, leaving the ledge clear. Mau put the four stones back near the path rather than arranging them into a monument nobody had asked the mountain to keep.

* * *

By lunch, the chairs had returned to ordinary service.

Umaru occupied one while explaining something to Ori. Neither considered its recent history a reason to be careful of the crumbs. Frieren had borrowed the other, turned it toward better light and was making a note beside a page while Fern told her something Mau could not hear from the door.

He stopped for a moment.

Fern looked up first.

“You're in the way,” she told him. ᶠᵉ

He moved aside, laughing, and the person behind him carried a basket through.

Frieren raised her eyes.

“Congratulations,” she said. ᶠ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

She looked at him for a second longer, then at the chair beneath her hand.

“This is comfortable.” ᶠ

He smiled.

“We checked.” ᴹ

Fern resumed her account. Frieren turned back to listen, and Mau went to find Yuka.

* * *

She was in the workshop, holding a narrow piece of wood while Mai checked the spacing between two marks.

The ring was still on her hand.

He managed not to point that out.

“I'm making the hook now,” Mai said when she saw him. ᴹᵃⁱ

“You don't have to tell me every—” ᴹ

She gave him the look.

He sat down.

“The small hook. Understood.” ᴹ

Yuka hid her smile behind the wood.

The work took a few quiet minutes. Mai kept the shape she wanted in front of her; Mau felt the familiar small demand through the supported connection and answered when she asked whether it was all right.

Afterward, she flexed her hand, picked up an ordinary tool and carried on with the next part.

Yuka fitted the new hook into place.

For a while the three of them argued about whether it was straight.

Mau found the disagreement deeply satisfying.

* * *

In the afternoon, he carried a cup to the quiet corridor.

Ashborn opened his door at the knock.

Mau held the cup out, then realised he had not asked whether he wanted anything and began to withdraw it.

The man took it.

“I heard there was something to congratulate you for,” he said. ᴬˢ

“Yes.” ᴹ

It remained a remarkably pleasing answer.

Ashborn looked toward the sound of voices at the far end of the corridor.

“Will you be having a celebration?” ᴬˢ

“We seem to be having several very small ones.” ᴹ

The man smiled slightly.

Mau told him he was welcome downstairs whenever he felt like it. Ashborn thanked him and kept the cup, leaving the choice where it had been offered.

At the stairs, DIO was trying to decide which side of a household argument she had entered.

“Do you know what they're doing?” she asked Mau. ᴰⁱᵒ

He listened.

Below them, Umaru was explaining a rule which had apparently existed since the beginning of civilisation and had only just been invented.

“Winning,” he said. ᴹ

DIO looked toward the noise again.

“I thought so.” ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

That evening, Beru appeared beside Yuka's chair.

He had washed the handkerchief.

He did not mention this, but the clean fold in his hand made the fact difficult to ignore.

Yuka put down her cup.

“Have you finished thinking?” she asked. ʸᵏ

Beru considered her with immense seriousness.

“For today, my lady.” ᴮʳ

“Then sit with us.” ʸᵏ

He looked at Mau.

Mau moved the empty chair a little farther out.

Beru sat on Yuka's side. For several minutes he remained extraordinarily upright while she asked him about helping on the mountain and he explained, in considerable detail, how difficult it had been to choose four stones of equal tactical value.

Across the table, Igris lowered his head.

Yuka leaned toward Mau.

“The professionals were thorough,” she whispered. ʸᵏ

He took her hand beneath the table and listened to the rest of the report.
