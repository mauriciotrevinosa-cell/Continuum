# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 43
## Chapter 1 — An Afternoon to Spend

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S8 v0.1 (2026-10-30)
**Volume:** 43
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S8E01
**Continuity state:** B+18, consecutive day after the S7 close. Mau returns Kaneki's borrowed tools, then keeps the afternoon he and Yuka agreed to share. Existing shared bedroom and ordinary engagement band preserved. Grey world except Yuka, old blur/fingers/scars unchanged. No new training clearance, powers, loot, purchases, wedding date or guests. Couple scene and ordinary family life, no arrival yet.

*When: Yuka asks him to put the basket down, Mau looks at the only basket he is holding.*

“This one?” ᴹ

“Unless you're hiding another.” ʸᵏ

He set it on the workbench.

She took his empty hand and turned it over. A strip of cloth had been wound around the handle where it had rubbed his palm. She unwrapped that, too, folded it and tucked it into the basket.

“There. Finished.” ʸᵏ

“I said I'd return the tools.” ᴹ

“You returned the tools. Then you offered to sort the nails.” ʸᵏ

Across the bench, Kaneki looked down at the little heaps they had made.

“I can finish.” ᴷ

Mau opened his mouth.

Yuka lifted their joined hands.

“I made a reservation.” ʸᵏ

Kaneki's expression barely changed, which made his agreement worse.

“You should honour it.” ᴷ

She thanked him with excessive politeness and led Mau out before he could discover another useful object.

Outside, he began laughing.

“I was coming.” ᴹ

“I know. Eventually there would have been no nails left in the world.” ʸᵏ

She had brought the bag herself. Two cups knocked softly together inside it as they walked. Mau offered to carry it; she passed it over without making him negotiate for that, then caught his sleeve when he almost turned toward the kitchen.

“We already have food.” ʸᵏ

“I was going to say we were leaving.” ᴹ

“I did.” ʸᵏ

At the courtyard door, Rem saw them and pointed at the bag.

“Bring back both cups.” ᴿᵉ

Mau looked at Yuka.

She pulled him through the doorway.

“She thinks you might offer one to a tree.” ʸᵏ

* * *

They stopped where the stream bent around a flat shelf of stone, well inside the familiar ground.

Yuka tested the surface with her palm before sitting. There was no table to arrange, no cloth waiting beneath four carefully selected stones. She moved a small twig, decided that was sufficient preparation and patted the place beside her.

Mau put the bag down.

“You planned this.” ᴹ

“I planned bread.” ʸᵏ

She began taking things out. Bread, a little cheese, sliced fruit in a covered bowl. The bottle had been wrapped in a cloth so it would not strike the cups.

He reached for it.

She let him pour, watching with the interest of somebody prepared to enjoy a very small failure.

He filled both cups without spilling.

“Disappointed?” ᴹ

“A little.” ʸᵏ

The stream was shallow enough for light to move along the stones underneath. He could follow its ripples until they passed into the uncertain edge of his left vision; then he turned his head and found them again.

Yuka drew her knees up beside him. Her sleeve had caught under the bag. When she tugged it free, the ring made a quiet tap against a cup.

She looked down at her hand.

He watched her do it.

“Still there,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I was going to ask.” ᴹ

She held it out for inspection, then curled her fingers around his before he could turn the gesture into anything solemn.

For a while they ate. Somewhere beyond the water a bird kept trying the same short call. Yuka answered it once, badly, and glanced at Mau to see whether he would defend her performance.

He took another bite of bread.

“Coward.” ʸᵏ

“I don't want to interfere in a conversation I don't understand.” ᴹ

The bird called again.

“Apparently he agrees.” ᴹ

She pushed his shoulder. He leaned farther than the push required, caught himself on the stone and made her laugh hard enough that she had to put her cup down.

* * *

When the food was mostly gone, she stretched out with her head on his thigh.

Mau moved the bag so she would not kick it into the stream.

“Very practical,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I know what happens to the cups.” ᴹ

She shut her eyes.

He settled his hand against her hair. The two fingers that did not quite move as he wanted rested where they were comfortable; she reached up and adjusted his wrist without looking.

He began again, more gently.

“Better?” ᴹ

“Keep checking.” ʸᵏ

He could hear the house if he listened for it. A distant voice, something being moved, the faint irregular knocks of work continuing without him. He found himself counting the tasks he might have finished before dinner.

Yuka opened one eye.

“What?” ʸᵏ

“Nothing important.” ᴹ

“Tell me.” ʸᵏ

He looked toward the sound of the hammer.

“I think that's the cupboard.” ᴹ

She considered this.

“Are you the cupboard?” ʸᵏ

He looked down.

“Not today.” ᴹ

“Then I think they'll manage.” ʸᵏ

Her hand found his where it rested by her shoulder.

“I didn't drag you out because you were doing something wrong,” she added. ʸᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I wanted the afternoon.” ʸᵏ

He stopped listening for the hammer.

She waited, her face tilted toward him, the sunlight making her narrow her eyes. Against the water and stone and all the familiar shades he could name without seeing their colour, she was so plainly herself that he forgot to look anywhere else.

“You have it,” he said. ᴹ

“All of it?” ʸᵏ

“Until somebody wants the cups.” ᴹ

She smiled, satisfied, and closed her eyes again.

* * *

Later she asked him what he wanted their next ordinary day to look like.

He thought she meant tomorrow and began explaining about a short practice Yuta had offered to watch. She shook her head against his leg.

“I mean later. When people stop looking at this every time I reach for bread.” ʸᵏ

She lifted her left hand.

“I might not stop.” ᴹ

“You get an exception.” ʸᵏ

He watched the ring move as she opened and closed her fingers.

“Breakfast,” he said after a moment. ᴹ

She turned her head.

“Ambitious.” ʸᵏ

“I wasn't finished.” ᴹ

“Go on.” ʸᵏ

“You tell me what you're doing. I tell you what I'm doing. We argue about which of us stole the other one's blanket.” ᴹ

“You.” ʸᵏ

“We haven't reached the argument yet.” ᴹ

“I'm preparing.” ʸᵏ

He brushed a strand of hair away from her mouth.

“Sometimes we go somewhere. Sometimes we come down and everyone is already talking and we have to work out what happened before we got there. I want you to have things to tell me.” ᴹ

Her expression changed a little.

“Even when you aren't in them?” ʸᵏ

“Especially if you want to complain about your grandfather. I'd like to be able to say I wasn't there.” ᴹ

She laughed, then caught his hand and held it against her cheek.

“I want that too.” ʸᵏ

“Complaining about Yuta?” ᴹ

“A day where I can decide something small, and know I'll see you afterward.” ʸᵏ

He bent to kiss her.

The angle was terrible. She complained against his mouth, sat up and took the task away from him.

This time, when she drew back, she stayed close enough that he could feel her breath.

“There,” she said. ʸᵏ

He rested his forehead against hers.

“Better?” ᴹ

“You can keep checking that too.” ʸᵏ

* * *

They went home when Yuka decided she wanted something warm to drink.

Mau checked the bank for anything they had left behind. She let him finish, then counted the cups aloud before putting them in the bag.

Near the courtyard, Milim met them carrying a wooden piece from a game neither recognised.

“You're late.” ᴹⁱ

Yuka looked at the sky.

“For what?” ʸᵏ

“We're deciding.” ᴹⁱ

From behind her, Ori waved them over with both hands.

Yuka passed Mau the bag.

“You do the cups. I'll find out what we've agreed to.” ʸᵏ

He caught her hand for a moment before she went.

“Thank you for the afternoon.” ᴹ

She looked back at him, pleased.

“You can reserve another.” ʸᵏ

Then she went to join the argument, and Mau carried both cups into the kitchen.
