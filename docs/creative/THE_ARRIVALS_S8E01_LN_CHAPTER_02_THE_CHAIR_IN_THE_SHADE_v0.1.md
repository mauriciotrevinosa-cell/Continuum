# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 43
## Chapter 2 — The Chair in the Shade

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S8 v0.1 (2026-10-30)
**Volume:** 43
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S8E01
**Continuity state:** B+18 late afternoon, concurrent household viewpoint after the stream outing. Ori/Frieren/DIO/Milim ordinary game; DIO physically present, weak/recovering and free to choose company or rest. Compass still points Frieren/Mum. No new promise to Ori, public Ciel, powers, border change or guest arrival. Short independent family chapter.

*When: Ori explains that the chair is important, DIO asks whether she is allowed to sit in it.*

Ori looked at the piece of wood in her hands.

“Yes.” ᴼ

Milim leaned over the board.

“But you can't move.” ᴹⁱ

“Ever?” ᴰⁱᵒ

Ori frowned at her.

“You can move. She means the chair.” ᴼ

DIO settled into it cautiously. The shade reached across her knees, and the cushion someone had brought out was thick enough that the edge of the seat did not trouble her side.

Frieren turned a page beside them.

“Ask about winning,” she suggested. ᶠ

“Am I allowed to win?” ᴰⁱᵒ

Milim looked offended.

“You have to try.” ᴹⁱ

“I see.” ᴰⁱᵒ

She did not see, but Ori was already placing a piece beside her hand and explaining where it wanted to go.

The game had acquired a bridge since the last explanation. Its ownership was disputed. DIO listened to both accounts, chose the answer which appeared least likely to break the bridge and immediately lost it to Milim.

Ori put her face in her hands.

“You didn't tell me that could happen.” ᴰⁱᵒ

“We didn't know it could,” Ori admitted. ᴼ

Frieren's book lowered slightly.

“An educational game.” ᶠ

* * *

They played until the small shadows around the pieces grew long enough to make the narrow paths difficult to distinguish.

DIO had lost twice. She thought she might have won once, but Milim had called it an unfinished round and removed the evidence before anybody could agree.

When she shifted in the chair, her hand went to her side.

Ori noticed.

“Does it hurt?” ᴼ

“A little. I've been sitting badly.” ᴰⁱᵒ

Frieren looked up properly then.

DIO let go of the chair's arm.

“I would like to stay for a little longer.” ᴰⁱᵒ

Frieren nodded and moved the cushion behind her without fuss. Ori pulled the board closer, shortening the reach to the pieces.

Milim began explaining the next rule.

“That wasn't there before,” DIO said. ᴰⁱᵒ

“It's new.” ᴹⁱ

“Then I would like a new bridge.” ᴰⁱᵒ

Ori considered the fairness of this while Frieren hid her mouth behind the book.

* * *

Yuka arrived while they were deciding.

She listened to the argument, asked who had written the rules and discovered the difficulty immediately.

“Oh. All of you.” ʸᵏ

“Not me,” DIO said. ᴰⁱᵒ

Yuka looked at her lost pieces.

“That explains a lot.” ʸᵏ

She sat on the low step to help Ori draw the disputed crossing. For a few minutes the game became a different activity, everyone leaning toward the same small space to see what would fit.

DIO rested her hands in her lap.

She could have gone inside. There would be quiet there, and a place to put her feet up, and nobody would have objected.

Instead she watched Ori borrow Yuka's pencil, rub out a line and try again.

Frieren touched the compass at Ori's side to keep its cord from catching beneath the board.

“Here,” she said. ᶠ

Ori turned toward her without checking which way the needle pointed.

DIO watched the little hand reach back.

“One more round,” she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

Milim began setting up the pieces before anybody could change her mind.
