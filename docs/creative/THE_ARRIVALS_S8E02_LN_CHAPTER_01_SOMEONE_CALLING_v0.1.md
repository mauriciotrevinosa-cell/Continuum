* * *

Rimuru had brought a sheet of reports downstairs with him.

He moved it aside when Mau sat at the table, leaving room for the bowl Rem was carrying.

“Anything?” Mau asked. ᴹ

“One washed-out stretch of path. A cart that turned back. Igris is checking the other approach.” ᴿ

Mau read the first line, then reached for his spoon.

The reports were more useful when nothing dramatic happened in them. They meant somebody had looked, somebody knew where the road could still be used and Hollis would not have to discover the problem with a loaded cart.

“Tell him to mark where it gives way,” he said. “I don't want the next person testing it with their leg.” ᴹ

“Already did.” ᴿ

Mau ate.

Across the room, Mai was explaining to Wakana why the thing he wanted could be made but should probably be cut from the material already sitting beside his elbow. He listened, looked at the material and accepted the correction without injury to either party.

Rimuru followed Mau's glance.

“She told me the same thing yesterday.” ᴿ

“Did you listen?” ᴹ

“Eventually.” ᴿ

DIO found them when the bowls had been taken away.

She paused behind the empty chair until Mau noticed and moved it out for her.

“Could I use the quiet room today?” ᴰⁱᵒ

“The one inside?” ᴹ

“If it isn't inconvenient.” ᴰⁱᵒ

“Are you feeling worse?” ᴿ

“I spent too long outside yesterday.” ᴰⁱᵒ

“An exhausting game.” ᴰⁱᵒ

She had. Rest was the recommendation she was trying to follow.

Mau checked with Ciel rather than assuming that a room already prepared was a room which needed no attention.

*The existing space is available. The route back remains under her control. No change to your other channels is required.*

He explained that aloud. DIO nodded.

“Ashborn?” he asked. ᴹ

“He said he would be reading. In his own room.” ᴰⁱᵒ

“Then it's yours.” ᴹ

She took his offered hand. He opened the familiar route with the care they had already learned, waited until she had stepped through and asked whether she was comfortable.

Yes. She would rest.

Rimuru watched Mau sit back down.

“Did you tell Ori?” ᴿ

“I'll tell her when I see her.” ᴹ

“Tell her before Milim decides there's been a kidnapping.” ᴿ

* * *

The report arrived while Mau was washing his hands.

He felt the pull of attention first. Then Igris's voice, precise enough to cut through the ordinary noises of the kitchen.

*A woman on the lower approach, my liege. Injured. Two attackers.*

Mau dried his hands on the cloth beside the basin.

*Can you reach her?*

*I am between them and the path. She has fallen beyond the broken cart.*

The picture he took through the knight's view lasted only long enough to establish the positions. Road, overturned wood, something too large moving across it. A human shape on the ground trying to get behind a wheel.

He did not recognise the woman.

He turned toward Rimuru at the doorway.

“Emergency on the lower approach. Wounded woman, two devils. I'm exchanging with Igris.” ᴹ

Rimuru was already moving the reports aside.

“I'll let Shoko know. Send the position.” ᴿ

Mau sent it through the existing connection, clear enough to distinguish the damaged road from the turn above it.

Then the floor was gone.

* * *

He arrived with one heel on loose gravel.

The first creature was already striking where Igris had stood. Mau shifted under its arm, read the woman's position past the cart and placed the cut across the creature's exposed neck, away from her.

The head separated before the hand reached the road.

He moved again.

The second devil had gone around the other side, its body low enough that the ruined cart no longer hid the woman from it. It turned toward Mau with its mouth opening.

He did not wait for whatever came next.

The second Dismantle crossed its body. He followed the collapsing pieces with his eyes until he knew neither would fall on her.

Silence came unevenly: a last scrape against the stones, wood settling, the woman's breathing.

Mau let the technique fall quiet.

*No other movement in the immediate approach,* Ciel told him.

That was as far as she could certify. He checked the trees himself before going down on one knee beside the wheel.

The woman's sleeve was dark with blood. More had dried along her cheek, pulling the skin when she tried to turn toward him. She held one arm across her middle.

“Can you hear me?” he asked. ᴹ

Her eyes found his face.

She stared.

Mau kept his hands where she could see them.

“You're safe from those two. I need to know where you're hurt.” ᴹ

Her mouth moved before any sound came out.

He leaned closer, trying to hear.

“Mau?” ᴹᵏ
