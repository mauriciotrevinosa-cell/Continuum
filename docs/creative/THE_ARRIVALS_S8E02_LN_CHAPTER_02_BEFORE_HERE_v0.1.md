*When: she says his name again, Mau looks behind him.*

There was nobody on the road.

The woman gave a small, incredulous laugh which caught against whatever hurt beneath her arm.

“It is you.” ᴹᵏ

Mau turned back.

“Do I know you?” ᴹ

Her expression held still for a moment.

Then she looked at the ruined cart and the bodies beyond it, as though trying to find the point at which the morning had stopped making sense.

“Makima.” ᴹᵏ

He waited for the name to do something.

It remained a name.

“I'm sorry,” he said. “Where do you know me from?” ᴹ

She moved her injured arm. Blood slipped between her fingers.

The question could wait.

Mau took the clean cloth he had tucked into his belt after washing, folded it and showed it to her before reaching for the wound.

“I need you to hold this here. Can you do that?” ᴹ

She obeyed, drawing a breath through her teeth when he pressed the cloth into place.

“I can get you to someone who can help.” ᴹ

“You really don't remember me?” ᴹᵏ

He looked at her face.

“I don't remember anything before I woke up in this world.” ᴹ

She stopped trying to move.

* * *

He gave Igris the road again once the knight had been recalled and could take form clear of the broken wood.

“Check the other side. Don't touch anything you don't need to move.” ᴹ

The knight looked from the woman to the fallen creatures.

“Yes, my liege.” ᴵᵍ

Mau stood where he could see both the approach and the woman. She watched Igris go with her hand held very carefully over the cloth.

“You have people.” ᴹᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“I heard that. I wasn't sure how much to believe.” ᴹᵏ

“What did you hear?” ᴹ

“That someone called Mau lived near here. That I shouldn't come looking.” ᴹᵏ

He looked down the road.

“And you came anyway.” ᴹ

“I was looking for you.” ᴹᵏ

He had spent too long wanting an answer to pretend those words did not reach him.

For a few seconds he could see the question taking shape in everything she did: the way she searched his face, the familiar use of a name he had not given her, the pause in which she seemed to be waiting for the person she knew to come back into the conversation.

He crouched again, leaving a little distance between them.

“Tell me something small.” ᴹ

She looked at him.

“You and I worked together. You used to complain that I asked whether a thing was finished when I could see you were still doing it.” ᴹᵏ

It was the kind of detail which should have brought a room with it. A desk, a voice from a doorway. Something he could compare with the way she said it now.

Nothing came.

He let out the breath he had been holding.

“I'm sorry.” ᴹ

“Don't force it.” ᴹᵏ

*Ciel?*

Her answer was immediate and private.

*I have the statement. I cannot verify a life you do not remember from the statement itself.*

He was grateful for the plainness of it.

*Let Rimuru know what she's saying. That she claims to know me. Not that we've found someone who does.*

*Understood.*

The woman watched him while he listened.

“Are you all right?” ᴹᵏ

“I'm telling the people at home you're coming.” ᴹ

* * *

Igris returned with no further attackers in the section he had checked.

The cart's owner was not on the road. There was no answer when Mau called, and no sign among the wreckage of somebody waiting to be pulled out. He asked Igris to continue looking and report rather than declare the whole area empty.

The woman tried to stand while he was speaking.

Her knee gave under her.

Mau caught her beneath the uninjured arm before she struck the wheel.

“Don't.” ᴹ

“I thought I could walk.” ᴹᵏ

He waited until she had found her breath, then looked at the distance back toward the house.

The path was uneven. Walking her along it would mean asking the injured side to take her weight every few steps.

“I'll carry you. Keep pressing on that.” ᴹ

She nodded.

He told her where he was going to put his hands and lifted only after she had adjusted her arm. Her breath caught once. He stopped moving until she said he could continue.

Behind them, Igris took position beside the cart.

“I'll send someone if I find the owner,” he said. ᴵᵍ

Mau nodded and began the walk home.

* * *

They did not speak for the first part of the climb.

He watched the ground. The cloth stayed under her hand. Once, at a rougher turn, she gripped his shoulder hard enough that he stopped and asked whether the wound had worsened.

“No. I'm sorry.” ᴹᵏ

“You don't have to apologise.” ᴹ

She studied his profile.

“You still say that.” ᴹᵏ

He did not know what to do with the little kindness hidden in the claim.

“What happened?” he asked at last. “Before you got here.” ᴹ

“You were going away. Spain. Races, a match, a concert—your friends had managed to make a small trip sound like three different holidays.” ᴹᵏ

His next step came a fraction late.

She noticed.

“Do you remember?” ᴹᵏ

“No.” ᴹ

He wished the answer had been different. He would not change it to give either of them something easier to hold.

“You didn't reach the meeting place,” she continued. “At first everyone thought there had been a delay. Then your phone stopped being useful. People called people who had already called everyone else.” ᴹᵏ

He could imagine that too readily. A person checking a screen. Somebody deciding not to worry yet. The moment a small inconvenience became something nobody wanted to name.

He had no screen to remember.

“You looked for me?” ᴹ

“For a week. Then I was here.” ᴹᵏ

She gave the last word a bewildered weight, letting the road and the trees and the strange sky finish it for her.

Mau adjusted his grip before the path narrowed.

“Do you have anyone with you?” ᴹ

“Not now.” ᴹᵏ

“When did you hear my name?” ᴹ

“People at a market. I asked where to go. Most of them wouldn't tell me.” ᴹᵏ

“We'll talk when you're warm.” ᴹ

She rested her head against his shoulder for a moment, eyes closed.

He kept watching the path.

* * *

At the approach to the protected ground he slowed and gave the position again.

Rimuru answered through the channel they were already using. Shoko was preparing a place. Yuta had been told there was an injured visitor. They would meet him at the house.

Mau looked down at the bloodied cloth.

“We're nearly there.” ᴹ

The woman opened her eyes.

She looked past him at the ground ahead.

“This is where you live?” ᴹᵏ

“A little farther.” ᴹ

He crossed with her held against his chest, intent on the footing and the next breath she took.

The familiar pressure of the boundary did not stop them.

On the other side, she lifted her face enough to see the house.

Mau saw Yuka waiting near the door and felt the small, ordinary relief of finding her exactly where the message said she would be.

“Shoko's ready,” she called. ʸᵏ

He brought the injured woman inside.
