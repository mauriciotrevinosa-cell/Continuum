Maki arriving with the expression of somebody who had heard two accounts and intended to learn which parts belonged to the same event.

“She says she knew me before,” Mau told them. ᴹ

He wished he had found a way to say it which did not make everybody look at his face first.

Shoko worked before letting the conversation grow.

She asked permission before moving the injured arm, warned before cleaning the wound and made the woman answer questions which had nothing to do with Mau's missing life. Where it hurt. Whether she could feel the pressure. Whether a breath changed it.

Mau stood where he could help if asked.

When Shoko finally put the used cloth aside, the bandage was secure and the woman's shoulders had begun to ease against the pillow.

“No getting up to prove a point,” Shoko said. ˢʰ

“I wouldn't dream of it.” ᴹᵏ

“People usually do.” ˢʰ
