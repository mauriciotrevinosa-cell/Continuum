*When: Mau turns back, the woman is no longer looking at him.*

Her gaze had gone to the doorway.

To Ori first, then Frieren. After that it moved across the room, taking account of people she had already been introduced to as if the introductions now meant something different.

“The Goddess,” she said. ᴹᵏ

Ori's hand found Frieren's sleeve.

Mau straightened.

“She's a guest.” ᴹ

Makima set the cup down.

She did it carefully. Shoko watched her uninjured hand leave the rim and did not move away from the bed.

“I thought I had a little longer,” Makima said. ᴹᵏ

Mau looked at Rimuru.

The slime had heard it too.

“Longer for what?” Mau asked. ᴹ

Makima looked at him again.

The pain had not vanished from her face. Something else had: the effort to make it a face he might remember.

“To find out how much of you there was to speak to before she joined the conversation.” ᴹᵏ

Maki moved first.

Not toward the woman. Toward the passage, putting her body between the bed and the open doorway.

Frieren drew Ori back without being asked.

Mau stayed where Makima could see him.

“You said you knew me.” ᴹ

“I know quite a lot about you.” ᴹᵏ

“Before this world.” ᴹ

She considered him for a moment.

“That was the part you wanted.” ᴹᵏ

* * *

The anger arrived slowly enough that Mau could still choose what to do with his hands.

He kept them empty.

At his side, Yuka had gone still. He could hear somebody in the passage being told to keep moving; Mai's voice, low and quite firm. The door was clear now. Ori was no longer in it.

Rimuru's attention touched his through the connection.

*DIO,* Mau told Ciel. *Ask her to leave the room. Now. Out to the house. Tell her something is wrong.*

*I am informing her.*

He did not look inward to watch. There was somebody on the bed who had just stopped pretending to be helpless, and he did not know what she could do.

“What do you want?” he asked. ᴹ

Instead of answering, Makima spoke in a different cadence.

“Our town refuses his goods.” ᴹᵏ

The words entered the room with a familiarity which belonged to another road.

Mau saw the market. The fruit he had bought. A man with a speech waiting to be delivered, selling the crowd an explanation it already feared.

“Stop,” he said. ᴹ

“So their leader comes in person. To make sure we understand what happens to people who say no.” ᴹᵏ

The pause was in the right place. The same small space in which Pell had looked up from the paper and let the crowd decide whether to be afraid.

He had heard the voice which read it.

This voice knew the page.

“How do you know that?” ᴹ

“I wrote it.” ᴹᵏ

Yuta had moved closer to the doorway, keeping the line toward the hall covered. Maki was on the other side. Nobody had rushed the bed.

Shoko took one step back, leaving the instrument tray beyond Makima's easy reach.

Mau moved between her and Yuka.

“Pell,” he said. ᴹ

“He was very committed to his delivery.” ᴹᵏ

He wanted to ask who had brought the pages. How much of the market had been hers. Whether the man on the road and the story of the brave visitor had come from the same mouth.

He heard Yuka shift behind him and remembered that this was not the place to get every answer.

“Rimuru. Clear the hall.” ᴹ

“They are,” Rimuru said. ᴿ

It was a small relief, knowing somebody else had begun before he had thought to ask.

* * *

Makima drew the blanket away from her knees.

Shoko's bandage remained around her arm. There was blood along the edge where she had pressed against it, but she no longer protected the wound when she moved.

Mau saw that now.

He had seen the same arm tremble on the road.

“Was any of it true?” ᴹ

“I came looking for you.” ᴹᵏ

“The people who looked for me before?” ᴹ

“I thought you might want there to have been people.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's hand touched the back of his shirt. Brief pressure, then gone so he could move.

The contact kept his next breath from becoming the answer Makima seemed to be waiting for.

“Stay seated,” he said. ᴹ

“Will you make me?” ᴹᵏ

He did not answer the invitation.

She put her feet on the floor.

Maki's hand moved. Yuta changed his stance. Across the room, Rimuru was no longer shaped entirely for the conversation they had been having.

Makima watched them prepare.

“So many people,” she said. “And all of them look to see what you will do.” ᴹᵏ

“They can decide for themselves.” ᴹ

“Of course.” ᴹᵏ

Her gaze returned to his face with something like genuine interest.

“You could be very useful, Mau.” ᴹᵏ

He said nothing.

“Would you like to be my pet?” ᴹᵏ

The silence which followed was not the one she had offered him on the road. There was no kindness he might mistake for an answer to his missing past.

He heard Ciel.

*DIO is at the exit. Rimuru is receiving her.*

Mau kept his eyes on Makima.

“You should leave,” he said. ᴹ

She looked past his shoulder.

At Yuka.

“Ah,” she said. “You already have someone.” ᴹᵏ

He stepped enough to take Yuka out of her direct line of sight.

Makima smiled at the movement.

“Perhaps if I remove her, you'll consider it.” ᴹᵏ
