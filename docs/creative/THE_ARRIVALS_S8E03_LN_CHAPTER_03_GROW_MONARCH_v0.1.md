*When: his hand opens, the body falls forward against him.*

He caught it.

It was the same motion he had used on the road, stopping an injured person from hitting the ground.

That was what made him let go.

The body struck the floor. Blood spread from the place where his hand had been. He stepped back, looked toward the head and could not make the two things become a fight again.

“No,” he said. ᴹ

There had been a threat. He had been holding her. He had wanted her away from Yuka.

He had wanted—

His next breath did not go in properly.

*Ciel.*

He tried again, harder, as if the silence meant he had forgotten how to ask.

*Ciel. Please.*

The room gave him the sound of his own breathing.

* * *

He put a hand against the pillar and found blood on the stone afterward.

The sight made him rub his palm against his clothes. Once. Again. It did nothing useful, and he knew it did nothing useful, but his hand kept moving until something black rose beside him and took shape.

Beru did not touch him.

“My king.” ᴮʳ

Mau looked at him without managing an answer.

The ant turned toward the fallen woman.

His body lowered. The change was immediate, protective, familiar enough that Mau understood there was something happening beyond his own inability to breathe.

“Behind me,” Beru said. ᴮʳ

Mau stared at the floor.

A finger moved.

* * *

He would remember the sound before he remembered the sight.

A small intake of air. Cloth drawing over stone. Then a laugh from a throat he had just severed.

Mau stopped trying to wipe his hand.

The figure on the ground gathered itself with an ease that did not belong to what he had done to it. He saw enough to know she was moving before he could understand how the blood and the broken shape had ceased to prevent her.

When she lifted her face, she was looking straight at him.

“Great,” Makima said. “A step closer.” ᴹᵏ

Beru moved.

His first strike cut the air where her shoulder had been. Something beneath her heel flashed against the floor: the same flat darkness Mau had seen under the bandage, thinner now, breaking at its edges.

Mau reached for her.

The distance became a narrow black seam.

He drove his hand into it and felt stone on the far side, an ordinary pillar with no person against it. Beru's next blow took a strip from the edge. The strip dissolved before it hit the floor.

Makima was no longer there.

He could still hear her.

“I look forward to seeing what comes back.” ᴹᵏ

The seam closed.

Mau struck the stone hard enough to break its surface.

There was nothing behind it except more stone.

* * *

For a while he stood with his hand against the broken place.

He had not returned home by killing her. He had not kept hold of her by choosing not to let go. The two facts sat beside each other, ugly and unresolved.

Beru came back to him.

“My king. Let me see your hand.” ᴮʳ

He looked down.

His knuckles had split against the pillar. He closed them, opened them and gave his own repair the small attention the cut required. The familiar process answered, then dragged with a resistance he had not expected.

He waited until the skin had begun to close.

“I'm here,” he said, though Beru had not asked. ᴹ

The ant bowed his head a little.

“Yes.” ᴮʳ

Mau lowered himself onto the base of the pillar.

He breathed until he could tell which breaths he was choosing.

The room remained grey. Without Yuka there was not even the small piece of it which always made him turn his head.

He thought of the water in the stream. Her head on his leg. The ordinary afternoon she had taken away from the nails and given back to him.

He would go back to her.

That was a thing he could decide without knowing how yet.

* * *

The message appeared above the empty floor.

**GROW, MONARCH.**

Mau stood.

The letters did not belong to anything Ciel had said. He could still feel the little local ordering of information that helped keep the world from arriving all at once; it had not become her, and it did not answer when he wanted her voice.

Deeper, Noise stirred against the unfamiliar pressure of the room.

He reached for the quiet guest he had left at home before the rescue. Ashborn was not a soldier waiting to be commanded through the army. There was no order which could put him here in Ciel's place.

Beru watched the letters with growing offence.

“My king needs no—” ᴮʳ

“Wait.” ᴹ

The ant stopped.

A second line formed beneath the first.

**EXTERNAL COMBAT CONDITIONS ACTIVE.**

Below it, smaller text arranged itself into three separate entries.

**Effective damage to constructed opponents: 10%.**

**Damage received: ×5.**

**Restoration efficiency: suppressed.**

He read them twice.

The slow closing of his hand was suddenly less mysterious.

“Those are yours,” he said to the empty room. ᴹ

His own voice returned from the columns.

“Not mine.” ᴹ

The new text flickered.

For a moment he thought it might answer.

Instead, the floor beyond the pillar began to move.

* * *

He called Igris before the first shape had finished rising.

The knight appeared, took in the room and the absence of the clinic, then looked at Mau's hand.

“We are separated?” ᴵᵍ

“From home. Ciel isn't reaching me.” ᴹ

Igris accepted the information without offering an explanation he did not possess.

Mau looked past him.

A line of light had opened across the far end of the hall. It widened into a long, shallow arch, and through it came the sound of something heavy being drawn across the ground.

He knew his body. Knew the weight in his arms, the strength he had already bought and could not simply set aside. Nothing in the message had taken those years of learning from him.

Ten percent of a good answer could still be an answer.

He summoned the daggers into his hands.

“We find a way out,” he told them. “Together.” ᴹ

Beru moved to his right. Igris took the left.

Beyond the arch, something stopped dragging its weapon.

Then it lifted it.
