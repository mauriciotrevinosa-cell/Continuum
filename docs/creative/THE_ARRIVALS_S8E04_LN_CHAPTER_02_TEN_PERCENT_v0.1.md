*When: the creature comes through the arch, its weapon touches both sides.*

It turned the haft sideways to bring the blade clear.

Mau saw four hands. Two held the long weapon; the others rested against plates which covered the creature from the back of its neck to the floor. It walked on two legs, but its shoulders were broad enough that a turn seemed to move the whole room around it.

Beru tilted his head.

“An ugly thing.” ᴮʳ

“Let it come clear of the doorway.” ᴹ

Igris shifted left.

Mau let the creature take another step and sent a cut across the unarmoured strip beneath one arm.

The skin opened.

Not far enough.

He knew the depth that strike should have reached. The knowledge was useless for a fraction of a second while his body waited for a creature which should have been falling to fall.

It brought the weapon down instead.

Infinity stopped the edge short of his forehead.

The floor beneath him broke.

* * *

Mau stepped off the cracking slab and kept the blade in sight.

The creature dragged it sideways. Broken stone came with it, striking the column behind him hard enough to strip the first layer from the surface.

He moved out of the debris, released the copied route and set Shrine across the exposed arm again.

This time he was watching for what happened instead of what should have happened.

The cut opened. The creature did not lose the limb.

Beru struck from above, driving a claw into the same line.

The wound widened another little distance.

“The room is protecting it,” Mau said. ᴹ

Igris's sword met the wrist of the lower hand as it tried to seize Beru's leg. The knight cut and withdrew before the grip closed, leaving a mark which ought to have taken the joint apart.

Mau called Bellion.

The marshal arrived, saw the two incomplete wounds and did not waste a question on whether the first strikes had been serious.

“How much?” ᴮˡ

“A tenth. It takes a tenth.” ᴹ

Bellion looked toward the supporting leg.

“Then we stop spending it in separate places.” ᴮˡ

* * *

Six infantry formed where Mau called them, behind the reach of the weapon rather than in its path.

Bellion put them to work immediately.

Two drove the creature to turn. Two caught its trailing feet with spear shafts. The last pair waited until the smaller hands moved away from its back to deal with the obstruction.

Mau took the opening under the raised arm.

His daggers found the first cut.

The equipped weight and speed were familiar. He had not lost what his body had learned. He could feel each choice reaching the right place, could see the edge travelling along the wound—

And the creature kept far too much of itself intact.

Beru caught the upper wrist. Igris went beneath the second hand. Mau used the grip to pull the damaged leg across the other, changing the balance without asking another cut to do the whole task.

It fell onto one knee.

The sound shook dust out of the arch.

Mau came around its shoulder and saw the plates separate at the back.

A thin seam ran down toward the hip. Something moved inside it, holding the heavy armour in place.

He put a dagger into the gap.

The first supporting bar broke.

* * *

The plate dropped far enough to reveal a joint beneath it.

Mau smiled despite himself.

There. A structure that had to move in order to work. Enough force applied where it could not spread across the whole body.

“Bellion. Keep it facing you.” ᴹ

The marshal's blade changed length as he stepped into the creature's view. He was not trying to meet the descending weapon with strength alone; the segmented steel took it at an angle, dragging it across the line where Igris could make it catch the floor.

Beru struck the damaged arm again.

Mau drove his second dagger into the exposed joint and turned.

The thing inside the seam snapped.

He felt the whole back of the creature give way.

For one clean instant it was exactly the answer he had expected to find.

Then the released plate moved toward him.

* * *

It was not falling.

A narrow limb unfolded from behind it, too low and too close to belong to any of the hands he had counted. The broken support had been holding it back. Mau saw the joint extend and understood the mistake in the same moment.

He let the left dagger go, set the grip against the moving plate and pushed himself away.

It bought part of a step.

The hidden edge struck him across the lower back before he had space to turn Infinity into place.

He knew what a blow like that felt like.

This one became five of them inside his body.

He hit the pillar sideways. The breath left him before he could make a sound.

His feet touched the floor.

Then he was on it.

* * *

Someone was saying his name.

He could tell that because it was the short sound Beru used when he forgot the title.

Mau tried to get his leg beneath him.

Nothing happened.

He looked at it, certain for a second that the armour or a piece of the broken floor had trapped the knee.

His other leg did not answer either.

Above him, the creature tried to rise.

Bellion drove his sword through the damaged joint Mau had opened. Igris struck the supporting wrist at the same time. The infantry went under the falling weapon instead of letting it find the place where Mau lay.

One form broke.

The cost of restoring it reached him through the shock, familiar and terribly ordinary.

“Hold the room,” he managed. ᴹ

Bellion looked at him once.

“We will.” ᴮˡ

Beru was beside him now, keeping his own hands away from the part Mau could not bear to move.

“Tell me what you need.” ᴮʳ

He tried to find Ciel's voice and remembered the silence before he could finish reaching.

There was another shape he could call.

His shadow opened beside his hand.

The deer's foreleg appeared on the stone, and Mau shut his eyes until he could take the next breath.
