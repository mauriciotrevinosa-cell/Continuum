*When: the deer lowers its head, Mau feels the first thread of relief and mistakes it for the end of the pain.*

Then he tried to move.

Beru caught his shoulder before the small twist could become a turn.

“Stay still.” ᴮʳ

It came out without the title. The ant seemed to notice, but he did not take the order back.

Mau stopped trying.

Shrine was already quiet. He let the old route close completely and held his attention on the deer, on the repair beginning through the places the blow had torn apart.

It was answering.

Too slowly.

Behind Beru, steel struck the floor hard enough to make Mau's teeth meet. He tried to lift his head to see and received another warning from the hand at his shoulder.

“Tell me,” he managed. ᴹ

Beru looked over him.

“The marshal has it down.” ᴮʳ

“Not dead?” ᴹ

“Not yet.” ᴮʳ

Mau shut his eyes.

He had opened the weakness. He could picture the gap under the plate, the supporting joint Bellion had driven his sword into. He could still remember the small satisfaction before the hidden limb had moved.

“The bar across the joint,” he said. “It has to pull against something.” ᴹ

“I heard,” Bellion answered from farther away. ᴮˡ

* * *

Mau listened to the next part of the fight from the floor.

The creature struck three times. Each impact arrived through the stone before he heard the weapon land. On the fourth, another sound interrupted it: something long drawing tight, then a hard crack which did not belong to a blade.

Igris said one word.

“Now.” ᴵᵍ

The infantry pulled.

Bellion had turned the creature's own haft across the exposed assembly. The soldiers did not have to cut through the full weight of its protected body; they had to keep pulling while it tried to stand, until its effort drove the trapped pieces against one another.

A scream began and changed into the sound of air escaping somewhere it should not have been.

Then came a succession of smaller strikes.

Mau counted until he lost the number.

He remembered the message. A tenth. Enough damage applied to the same ruined place would still become enough.

The final impact was followed by the shifting weight of a body which had stopped trying to rise.

Bellion called the halt.

For several breaths, nobody approached Mau.

He was grateful. They were checking that the thing was actually finished, and he did not want another clever answer to turn into a weapon behind someone's back.

Igris came into view first.

“No movement. The assembly has broken apart.” ᴵᵍ

Mau nodded once.

The knight went down on one knee beside the deer.

* * *

There was a small beginning of sensation in his left foot.

Mau tried to hold on to it without ordering the foot to prove anything. The repair was following more than one injury, meeting the place where the impact had become five times what his body had braced to take.

He knew how to reverse damage. He had learned that through failures he did not want to revisit here.

Knowing the operation did not make the room let it happen at its ordinary speed.

He sent more into the attempt.

The answer grew hotter, harsher, then began spreading past the small course he had meant it to follow. Pain climbed along his side.

Mau pulled back before the effort became another injury.

He lay breathing through it, furious.

The deer remained close. Its quieter work did not reach him unrestrained either, but it did not stop because he had lost patience.

“My king?” Beru asked. ᴮʳ

“It works. It's just…” ᴹ

He ran out of the breath he had intended to use for the explanation.

Igris waited until he had found another.

“Not enough for you to stand yet.” ᴵᵍ

Mau looked at him.

Then he nodded.

“Not yet.” ᴹ

* * *

The room changed after the body had ceased moving.

Beru rose immediately, placing himself between Mau and the far wall. Bellion called the infantry back from the arch. Igris stayed beside him.

This time the change was a narrow separation in the stone, opening onto a lower corridor.

No weapon came through it.

Mau felt the pressure of another message waiting at the edge of his attention. He dismissed it long enough to watch the opening.

“Check it,” he told Bellion. “Don't send everyone.” ᴹ

The marshal sent two soldiers, then followed at a distance which left the others between the corridor and Mau.

They returned with a report that was almost offensively ordinary.

A room. A raised place which might be used as a bed. Water coming from a spout into a trough. A shelf holding covered bowls and wrapped food.

No visible route beyond it.

“A place to repair what he's using,” Mau said. ᴹ

The bitterness in it surprised him.

Beru looked toward the corridor.

“Then we will use it.” ᴮʳ

Mau turned his eyes back to him.

For a moment the ant looked ready to explain that food and a bed did not belong to the enemy simply because the enemy had put them there.

Mau almost laughed.

It hurt too much to finish.

“Give me a minute.” ᴹ

* * *

They did not move him until he said he could endure being moved.

The deer stayed close while he brought the first chaotic work under better control. He could feel the difference between what was beginning to hold and what he had not repaired. He used that difference instead of letting the urge to get off the floor make the decision.

Bellion found a broad panel among the fragments, inspected both sides and had the infantry carry it over.

Beru looked at Mau before putting a hand beneath him.

“Tell me when.” ᴮʳ

He did.

They lifted together. Mau held his breath, then let it out in a sound he could not stop. Igris kept the panel steady while the soldiers took its weight.

When it was done, Beru remained beside his head.

“All right?” ᴮʳ

“No.” ᴹ

The ant's face changed.

Mau caught the edge of his wrist before the answer could become an apology.

“You did it right. I just hurt.” ᴹ

Beru looked at the hand on him, then nodded.

“Tell me that too.” ᴮʳ

* * *

They took him through the corridor with the deer following.

There was a dull light above the shelf. He could see the grain in the raised platform, the folds in the rough covering laid over it, the steam escaping one of the bowls.

No attempt to make it resemble his room.

He was glad of that before he knew why.

Bellion put guards where they could see the only opening. Igris checked the other walls again. Beru helped transfer Mau onto the platform with the same warning and the same wait for permission.

When they had finished, Mau stared at the low ceiling.

The thing in the first room was dead.

He was not.

Neither fact made his feet answer when he asked them to move.

He turned his head toward Beru.

“Stay for a while.” ᴹ

The ant lowered himself beside the bed.

“Of course, my king.” ᴮʳ
