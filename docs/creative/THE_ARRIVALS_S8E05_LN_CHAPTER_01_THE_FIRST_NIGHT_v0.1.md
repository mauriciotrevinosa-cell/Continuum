*When: Beru offers him the bowl, Mau discovers that holding a spoon can require too many decisions.*

It was broth. Thin, warm and without much taste.

He managed three mouthfuls before his back began insisting on the angle at which he had raised himself. The spoon touched the bowl with a small clatter.

Beru held out a hand.

Mau looked at it.

“I can eat.” ᴹ

“Yes, my king.” ᴮʳ

The hand remained there.

After a moment Mau gave him the bowl, keeping the spoon.

Beru moved it closer. The next mouthful required less reach.

Mau ate.

* * *

Igris came back from the hall while the broth was still warm.

The room had not produced another opponent. The remains lay where they had fallen, without offering the knight anything he wished to disturb. The guards had found no route leading out of the place through which they had arrived.

“And the other wall?” Mau asked. ᴹ

“Stone beyond the first broken layer. The same marks continue through it.” ᴵᵍ

He had not spent an army trying to excavate an opening no one had located.

Mau nodded.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

Igris looked at the bed.

“The leg?” ᴵᵍ

“I can feel more than before.” ᴹ

He tried not to make it sound like a larger answer than it was.

The knight accepted exactly what he had said.

“I will tell the marshal.” ᴵᵍ

He went back to the corridor, leaving Mau with the broth and the slow, infuriating labour of something that should have been easier to fix.

* * *

He called the information closer when the bowl was empty.

No new voice came with it.

The small local filter sorted the sensations he already had: where the work was meeting resistance, where he was asking too much of a damaged route, which part he had repeated without making progress. Noise followed the attempt with a pressure he could distinguish from the room's restrictions.

*Can you keep track while I sleep?*

The answer was a brief assent, without the explanation Ciel would have given about how long he had already been awake.

He missed the explanation so sharply that he shut his eyes.

Then he opened them again and put his attention back where it could help.

*Don't build something new to force it. Help me finish this correctly.*

The pressure settled along the work already under way.

The deer lowered itself beside the platform. Its presence gave the repair a steadier rhythm, though the suppression remained around every useful part of it.

Mau lay still until the effort stopped spreading into places he had not meant it to go.

* * *

At some point Bellion replaced the two guards in the passage.

Mau heard the change and tried to turn his head toward it.

“Same number,” Beru told him. ᴮʳ

He looked back.

“What?” ᴹ

“You counted.” ᴮʳ

He had.

Beru rested his hands on his knees.

“The marshal knows who is where. Igris knows the corridor. I am here.” ᴮʳ

Mau stared at him for a moment.

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then sleep.” ᴮʳ

He could hear what the ant had kept out of the last two words. The title, perhaps. The effort to make an order sound like a request Mau might accept from somebody he was supposed to command.

Mau looked at the low ceiling.

“I thought I had it.” ᴹ

Beru waited.

“I saw where the plate was attached. I thought if I broke it, we'd be through.” ᴹ

“You opened what we used.” ᴮʳ

“And missed what was behind it.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴮʳ

He had not expected the agreement to be comforting.

Beru looked toward the passage, where Bellion was speaking quietly enough that only the shape of the words reached them.

“We will look with you next time.” ᴮʳ

* * *

The room did not turn out its light.

Igris found a spare covering on the lower shelf and hung it so the dull brightness no longer fell straight across Mau's face. He left the passage visible from the bed.

It helped more than Mau wanted to admit.

“We need to know how long,” he said. ᴹ

“We have begun keeping it,” Igris answered. ᴵᵍ

“Not what it says. What we can actually count.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴵᵍ

The knight showed him the first mark on a piece of broken wood. A beginning, not a claim about the hour at home. They would count rest, meals and the intervals they could measure until the room gave them something more reliable.

Mau touched the mark with his thumb.

“Don't let me forget what we're doing.” ᴹ

Both shadows looked at him.

“I want to get out,” he said. “I'm not staying to prove I can beat everything he puts in front of me.” ᴹ

Igris inclined his head.

“We will remind you.” ᴵᵍ

Beru shifted closer to the bed.

“And we will get you there.” ᴮʳ

* * *

When they left him quiet, he thought of the room he shared with Yuka.

The sock under his knee. Her complaint about the blanket. Her hand stopping him from getting out of bed before she had found what she was looking for.

He tried to remember whether he had told her he would see her at lunch.

He could remember her saying breakfast. He could remember promising the afternoon before that she had all of it.

He did not turn uncertainty into a promise he could punish himself for breaking.

She knew he had meant to come back.

He could hold that much without hearing her say it again.

Near his foot, something changed.

Mau looked down.

His smallest toe had moved against the covering.

Only a little. When he tried again, it did not repeat the movement.

He let his head rest on the bed before the attempt became an argument with his own body.

“Beru.” ᴹ

The ant looked up immediately.

“You can tell Igris that part too.” ᴹ

Beru followed his gaze. For a moment his face became so openly hopeful that Mau almost warned him not to make too much of it.

Then the ant nodded, carefully.

“I will.” ᴮʳ

Mau shut his eyes.

The hand which came to rest near his was large, clawed and entirely familiar.

He slept before he could decide whether to hold it.
