*When: he wakes, Mau hears someone moving a bowl and thinks, for one unguarded second, that he knows which kitchen it came from.*

He opened his eyes.

Beru had put the bowl on the shelf. Steam moved around the ant's fingers, rising into the same dull light.

Mau looked at the cloth Igris had hung beside the bed.

“Morning?” ᴹ

“You have slept,” Beru said. ᴮʳ

It was the answer they could prove.

Mau nodded and tried to swallow. His mouth was dry enough that the first word did not come out properly.

Beru brought the water before he could try again.

* * *

The pain had changed while he slept.

Not less everywhere. There were places he could locate more clearly now, which felt unfair until he remembered what the absence had meant the night before.

He asked for the bowl to be left within reach and spent some time finding a position which let him drink without pulling against the repair.

Beru watched him do it.

“If you say you can manage, I will still be here,” the ant said. ᴮʳ

Mau looked up.

He had been preparing those exact words.

“All right.” ᴹ

“Would you like the bowl moved?” ᴮʳ

“Closer. A little.” ᴹ

Beru moved it a little.

For once neither of them pretended the small distance did not matter.

* * *

He opened the waiting display after eating.

The record named the constructed guardian without giving it a life before the room. One completed encounter. The reward it had held while he concentrated on staying conscious now settled into the branch he recognised.

**LEVEL: 127.**

**UNALLOCATED POINTS: 62.**

Mau read the numbers.

One level. Five more points.

He looked down at his legs.

Nothing had happened to them when the level changed.

He was not surprised. He still found himself angry at the part of him which had hoped the room might take a shortcut he had spent so long refusing to rely on.

The old fifty-seven were there. Umaru's carefully retained margin, joined by a small new gain.

He imagined explaining that he had nearly broken himself for five points and immediately knew she would object to the way he had phrased it.

She would ask what he had learned. What had actually happened. Whether he had eaten before trying to compare numbers.

Mau closed the display without spending anything.

“My king?” Beru asked. ᴮʳ

“I need to look at the body first.” ᴹ

The ant glanced at the bed as if the body had not been making itself the subject of every decision since they arrived.

Mau smiled faintly.

“Differently.” ᴹ

* * *

He asked the small local filter for the work it had followed overnight.

It gave him no spoken account. The information came in the pattern he already used for sorting too much input: a route held here, an unfinished part there, the places where his attention had repeatedly gone in circles before he slept.

Noise had kept to the instruction.

No new route forced through him. No attempt to answer the silence by becoming someone else. The existing repair had continued, slowly, in the body it was supposed to repair.

*Thank you.*

A brief pressure answered.

Mau followed the work until he found the place he had tried to rush. The damaged support had begun to hold; that did not mean the other things passing through it had recovered their ordinary paths.

He could push harder at the whole injury.

Or he could stop treating it as one thing.

He rested his head, found the slower rhythm of the deer beside him and chose a smaller part to finish.

* * *

Igris brought the first practical problem of the day.

“We need to change what is beneath you.” ᴵᵍ

Mau looked at the covering.

He had been trying not to notice the blood drying against his clothes.

“Can it wait?” ᴹ

“It could. It would not improve by waiting.” ᴵᵍ

Beru stood.

Mau shut his eyes, opened them again and asked how they intended to do it.

They told him. Slowly enough that he could interrupt when one part sounded worse than the others.

The next few minutes were not heroic.

He held the edge of the platform while Igris steadied him and Beru drew the soiled fabric clear. The first turn hurt. He told them, and they stopped. When he was ready to continue he had to say so aloud, which he disliked almost as much as needing to say stop.

They did not hurry him through either word.

Afterward he lay with clean cloth under him and sweat cooling at his neck.

Beru brought water without asking whether he had earned it.

* * *

Later, Bellion showed him the positions of the soldiers in the hall.

He had drawn them with the tip of a broken shaft against dust on the floor. Mau could see from the bed without having to sit up. The six infantry had their own sequence of tasks; the destroyed form was back, accounted for, and had not been assigned the same exposed place as if nothing had happened.

“The front arch?” Mau asked. ᴹ

“Still closed beyond the cleared chamber. No change since the last report.” ᴮˡ

“And if something comes through?” ᴹ

Bellion pointed to a second line of marks.

Mau studied it.

“That's a retreat.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴮˡ

“You'd let it have the hall?” ᴹ

“If holding the hall meant letting it reach you.” ᴮˡ

He traced the route with his eyes. A delay at the first column. A second angle by the corridor. Enough room for the soldiers to fall back without asking the ones behind to move through them.

He wanted to correct something.

He found nothing which required it.

“Use it if you need it.” ᴹ

Bellion inclined his head.

Mau watched him return to the passage, then looked at the small mark Igris had added to their record of rest.

He still wanted to know whether Yuka had eaten lunch.

He could not ask her.

For this part, he would have to let the people at home know how to look after each other too.
