The ant had already begun moving it closer.

He stopped.

Mau looked from the cup to the edge of the platform. Yesterday he had needed Igris behind his shoulders to remain upright long enough to finish a meal. Today the same position felt possible before anybody had offered to hold him there.

He put his hand against the bed.

The first attempt ended before he reached the cup.

Beru caught him at the shoulder and waited until his breathing settled.

Mau stared at the distance he had left.

“Again,” he said. ᴹ

The ant did not move.

Mau looked up at him, heard himself and took another breath.

“Not right now.” ᴹ

Beru eased the support rather than removing it all at once.

“The water can wait,” he said. ᴮʳ

“I don't want to make a ceremony out of a cup.” ᴹ

“Then we won't.” ᴮʳ

* * *

There were six rest marks on Igris's piece of wood.

Not six days at home. Mau repeated that distinction to himself whenever the number threatened to become an account of what he was missing. The display's elapsed intervals were at least agreeing with the meals and sleeps they could count here. That was enough to organise this room.

It was not enough to tell him what Yuka was doing.

He had tried the connection at the start and end of every long rest. Once each time, then he stopped, because asking the silence a hundred times did not make it another kind of answer.

Today he asked about his own body instead.

The new routes were not entirely new. He had looked at them before the mountain, before the clinic, while Umaru made him explain why one number could affect more than the branch it had first belonged to.

Now the little local filter let him separate what he was increasing from the injury he still needed to repair.

He could make more available to the work.

He could not purchase the fact of having done it.

*Show me the two entries again.*

Noise followed his attention without finding a spoken answer necessary.

Vitality. Perception.

He chose five points for each and kept the confirmation open long enough to check that he had not pressed twice.

Ten points from sixty-two.

Fifty-two left.

The change arrived in his body with the quiet firmness he recognised. A little more room in the effort he could sustain. More separation between the things he had been trying to follow through pain and fatigue.

The wound remained a wound.

His feet remained on the bed.

Mau let the window close.

* * *

Bellion returned with the piece they had taken from the first creature's back.

A broken support, no longer carrying any active light. Igris had checked it, kept it apart from the sleeping space and brought it only after Mau asked to look at the thing he had missed.

Bellion held it where Mau could see both sides.

“Here.” ᴮˡ

The fracture ran straight across the narrowest part.

Mau followed the shape beyond it.

The brace had not just held the plate upright. It had kept something folded behind the plate. He had used a true observation and stopped one question too soon.

“It wasn't hiding a weak point,” he said. ᴹ

“It had one,” Bellion answered. ᴮˡ

“And I assumed that was all it had.” ᴹ

The marshal turned the piece.

“That is what we can change.” ᴮˡ

Mau thought of the way they had ended the fight. Multiple eyes, one opened assembly, someone watching the rest of the body while the others used the opportunity.

He looked at the passage, where one of the infantry stood with its spear lowered far enough not to catch the ceiling.

“Next time somebody sees what moves when I break it.” ᴹ

Bellion inclined his head.

“Already added.” ᴮˡ

* * *

That afternoon—or what they had agreed to call afternoon—Mau reached the cup.

Igris was beside him. Beru had made sure the water was no hotter than before. The deer remained near the platform, its steady presence part of an effort which had become work rather than a succession of emergencies.

Mau sat, waited for the pain to stop climbing and reached.

His hand closed around the cup.

He took a drink, put it back and looked at Beru.

The ant had clasped his own hands so tightly that the small edges pressed against one another.

“Don't,” Mau said. ᴹ

“I have said nothing, my king.” ᴮʳ

“You're preparing.” ᴹ

Beru lowered his head with immense dignity.

Igris looked at the wall.

Mau began laughing and had to stop before the laughter hurt.

They helped him lie down again.

This time, when he asked for the cup afterward, he did not mind that Beru brought it to his hand.
