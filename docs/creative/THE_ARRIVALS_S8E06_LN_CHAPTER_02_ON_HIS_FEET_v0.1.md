## Chapter 16 — On His Feet

Level127/52retained/Int460, own Deer/repair demand ongoing, no other stat allocation, enemy/loot. Next chamber not entered. Ciel still private remote disconnected.

*When: Igris asks whether he wants the support moved, Mau says no.*

The knight left it within reach.

Mau sat on the edge of the platform with both feet against the floor.

He had spent the last several days finding out which part of an apparent improvement would still be there after sleeping. The first twitch had not been a promise. The first minute sitting had not entitled him to a second. Progress came in small, repeatable things, each one made less dramatic by having to be done again.

Today he could feel where his heels were without looking at them.

He put his weight forward.

Beru rose from the other side of the room. Mau saw him stop himself from reaching before he had been asked.

The deer remained close enough for the steady work of repair to continue.

Mau stood.

* * *

For a few seconds he did nothing else.

There was a great deal to do in doing nothing. Hold the new weight without letting the old pain make him shift too far. Keep breathing. Let his body find its balance before deciding whether balance meant he could walk.

Igris stayed at his left.

“All right?” ᴵᵍ

“So far.” ᴹ

He looked at the floor between himself and the shelf.

Four steps if he took the ones he remembered. More if he took the ones his body was prepared to give him now.

He chose the second kind.

The first was ugly. His knee turned in, and he caught Igris's forearm with more force than he intended.

The knight did not shift his feet.

Mau waited, corrected and took the next.

When he reached the shelf he kept hold of it long enough to decide whether getting back should be part of the same attempt.

Beru had moved the chair into the space behind him.

Mau looked at it.

Then he sat.

* * *

“You were supposed to make a terrible speech,” he said after a while. ᴹ

Beru looked at Igris.

Igris gave him no assistance.

“I was waiting to learn whether you wished to sit before or after it, my king.” ᴮʳ

Mau laughed once, carefully.

The ant's shoulders lowered.

“Before,” Mau said. “Definitely before.” ᴹ

Beru placed one hand against his chest.

“Then allow me—” ᴮʳ

“Tomorrow.” ᴹ

Igris turned his head toward the passage.

Mau could not see his expression. He did not need to.

* * *

The light was still the same light when they began the next check.

Mau had washed, eaten and slept after the short walk. He did not turn one success into permission to keep adding things until he failed.

Bellion came to the recovery room while Igris took position by the first pillar in the cleared hall.

“Only the inside route,” the marshal said. ᴮˡ

Mau nodded.

They had been testing the difference whenever he could spare enough attention to make the result useful. His soldiers were still his. He could call a registered form inward, as he had called Igris from the road. The call did not leave behind a usable hole in the room.

Trying to send a message back through that same route met the closure from its other side.

An outside position did not answer as an Exchange destination. A soldier brought inward could not simply be ordered to step out of the trial and deliver a note. The pressure stopped the outward route without taking away the existence of the army which had made him worth testing.

He had wanted one of those attempts to make the whole problem ordinary.

None had.

Today they were checking something smaller: whether the room had also taken away the move between two places they could actually see inside it.

Mau stood, waited until his feet were properly beneath him and reached for Igris's position.

The shelf disappeared.

He was beside the pillar, with the recovery doorway in front of him and Beru waiting several paces away.

The move had worked.

His back still hurt.

* * *

He went no farther.

Beru stood between him and the unopened arch. The creature's remains had been moved clear of the safe path, its broken pieces kept where they could be inspected rather than left to trip someone returning in a hurry.

Mau looked at the pillar which had stopped him.

There was a dark mark along its lower surface.

He remembered the impact first. Then his own attempt to get up without understanding why his legs were not beneath him.

His hands wanted the daggers.

He let them remain empty.

“Not today,” he said. ᴹ

Beru looked toward the next arch.

“No, my king.” ᴮʳ

Mau touched the pillar with his uninjured palm.

He had nearly died in the first room. He could call it a lesson and still hate the person who had decided to teach it that way.

Learning from it did not require gratitude.

“When we do go,” he said, “we go with a retreat already chosen.” ᴹ

“The marshal has been looking.” ᴮʳ

“I know.” ᴹ

This time he meant the words without having to prove he had checked every arrangement himself.

* * *

He took the local Exchange back to the recovery room.

Igris appeared at the pillar in his place. Bellion waited until Mau was seated before going to hear the knight's account of the move from the other end.

The deer settled beside him again.

Mau looked at their count of days. Fourteen by the schedule they had been able to keep. Long enough to have learned the room's small noises, the order in which Igris checked the corridor and the exact offended silence Beru used when told he did not need to hold a cup.

He wished he knew how long it had been for Yuka.

He tried the connection once.

No answer.

He did not try to turn the silence into her face.

Instead he took the broken wood on which Igris had begun their record and set it beside the bed where he could reach it.

Below the marks, he wrote the first words he had managed since arriving.

*I can stand.*

He looked at them for a moment, then added another line.

*We are leaving this place.*
