*When: Bellion asks where Mau wants the reserve, Mau points behind himself.*

The marshal looked at the place, then at the doorway leading back to the recovery room.

“For the retreat.” ᴮˡ

“Before we need it.” ᴹ

Bellion nodded.

Four more intervals of rest had changed what Mau could ask his body to repeat. He could cross the room, turn and return without discovering a new reason to stop halfway. He could dress without making Beru pretend he had entered only to inspect the shelf. The fresh repair held when he moved; it still made him conscious of every time he tried to hurry.

He was not taking that awareness as an insult.

Today it came with him.

* * *

They checked the plan in the cleared hall.

Bellion, Igris, Beru. Six infantry, not a thousand bodies crowding a doorway they had yet to see from the other side. Fang took shape far enough from the ceiling that he could straighten without striking it, inspected the arch and made a disapproving sound.

Mau almost smiled.

“I didn't choose it.” ᴹ

The sorcerer inclined his head as though forgiving him the architecture.

The deer remained in the recovery space. Its task was not to follow him into the first attack and be lost beneath whatever came through. If he needed it, he would come back along the route the infantry were keeping clear.

Mau looked at the arch again.

“If I say back, we don't argue about whether I'm nearly finished.” ᴹ

Beru watched him.

“Including you, my king?” ᴮʳ

“Especially me.” ᴹ

Igris turned toward the knight marking the safe line.

The movement was small, but Mau recognised the agreement in it.

* * *

The next space did not reveal itself by opening a normal door.

The view under the arch shifted. Stone slid past stone without the sound the weight required. For a moment Mau could see two edges where there should have been one, as if somebody had begun turning a page before moving their finger out of the way.

He stepped closer.

The interval was gone.

Beyond it lay a broad floor divided by low ridges, with thin pillars rising toward a ceiling he could not quite judge from here.

“Did you see that?” he asked. ᴹ

Igris was watching the same edge.

“The change?” ᴵᵍ

“Something between.” ᴹ

The knight looked again.

Nothing remained to point at.

Mau stored the observation with the others instead of trying to force a doorway out of a glimpse he did not understand. It might be useful. That did not make it an escape yet.

Bellion sent the first pair through.

* * *

They made it to the nearest ridge before the ceiling began to unfold.

Mau saw the first shape detach above the left soldier. Long wings, thin enough that he had mistaken their edges for seams in the stone. A narrow head followed, then legs which drew up against the body before the creature fell.

It struck the ridge where the soldier had been a moment earlier.

Bellion had already called him back.

The point of the creature's head drove through the surface like a thrown blade.

Around it, more seams opened.

Mau took the daggers from storage and looked at the line behind him.

Clear.

Then he went through with Igris.
