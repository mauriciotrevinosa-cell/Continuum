## Chapter 19 — Make Them Turn

*When: the first creature pulls its head free of the ridge, Mau is already watching what moves with it.*

The wings opened.

A plate beneath the throat lifted at the same time, exposing a narrow fold which closed again as soon as the creature left the ground.

“Under the head,” he said. ᴹ

Igris moved toward the ridge.

The next creature dropped between them.

Mau got Infinity in place before the point struck his chest. The impact stopped short, but the rush of air carried a second creature through the space above his shoulder.

It was going for the arch.

For the way back.

“Bellion!” ᴹ

“Seen.” ᴮˡ

The marshal's blade caught the flying shape across the neck and dragged it down into the ridge. The protection which kept it from losing its head did not keep its flight straight. Stone broke beneath the impact.

Two infantry drove their spears through the opened fold.

The creature struck until one of them broke.

The other held on.

* * *

Mau let Infinity go and pulled with Blue above the centre of the chamber.

Loose fragments lifted first. Then the closest winged shapes began fighting the direction of their own descent. He was not crushing them; the room kept too much of their bodies whole for that to be a cheap answer.

He was making them turn.

Beru rose through the confused line and struck one across the lifted plate. Igris waited below, using the ridge to make the next creature raise its head before it could climb again.

Fang took the far side of the formation with a broad pressure which denied the flock a clean route around the pull.

For a moment Mau could see the whole arrangement.

The creatures were spending their movement trying to escape a force that did not need to kill them to be useful.

“Now,” he said. ᴹ

He released Blue.

Shrine followed along the place where three exposed folds crossed.

The cuts did not finish all three.

They did finish one, already opened by Beru's claw. Its body folded around the wound instead of recovering its wings, and it struck the floor beneath the next attacker.

Igris took that one's head between two plates and turned it into Bellion's reach.

Mau went after the third.

* * *

It twisted toward the side he did not see clearly.

He turned his head rather than trusting the blur to improve because he wanted it to. The creature's wing scraped his arm. He used the grip against its body to hold it off his shoulder while his dagger found the fold it had opened by trying to bite him.

The first strike went in.

The second followed the same line.

He wanted a third.

Above him, another shape changed its angle.

He let the creature go.

The new dive hit it instead of his back.

Mau landed against the ridge, knees taking more weight than he liked. A hard warning travelled through the recently repaired place.

He stopped.

One breath. Enough time to find whether the pain was continuing to climb or only telling him where the impact had gone.

“My king?” Beru called. ᴮʳ

“Holding. Keep them off the left.” ᴹ

The ant obeyed without abandoning the position Mau had asked him to keep.

* * *

The next group did not dive separately.

They gathered above the low ridges and came in a tight line, the first taking the position that would force Mau to block while the others passed on either side.

He could stop the first with Infinity.

He would have to give up Shrine to do it, and the others would still be coming for the unprotected route behind him.

He called the warning, reached for the surface they were crossing and folded it away from its ordinary angle.

The first two creatures followed the apparent direction of their own dive into a place the air no longer offered them. They struck the far ridge at the wrong height.

The third saw the change early enough to climb.

Beru met it above Mau's head.

He caught one wing, lost part of a claw to the creature's throat edge and tore it sideways rather than letting it follow the fold toward its owner.

Mau let the sky settle.

He looked toward the arch and found Bellion already moving the last reserve across it.

“Back,” he said. ᴹ

* * *

He wanted to stay for the two creatures struggling against the far stone.

He had made the opening. They were damaged. It would take a few seconds to finish what he had already begun.

Igris arrived beside him.

The knight did not argue. He simply held the place from which the next attack would come while Mau made the decision he had asked everyone to obey.

Mau stepped back.

Then another step.

Bellion took the open lane and turned it against the flock. The creatures following Mau found the marshal and the last two spears waiting beside a ridge they could no longer see around.

Mau reached the arch.

He did not have to reach the bed to be useful.

From the steadier ground he put Blue above the far ridge again, drawing the struggling shapes upward enough that Fang could strike the same place Bellion's blade had opened.

A second infantry form broke at the nearer line. The cost reached Mau; he restored it and sent it behind the reserve instead of demanding the same body take the same blow again.

The flock thinned.

The last creature tried the ceiling.

Beru followed, caught it before its wings could settle between the seams and brought it down with both hands.

He held the lifted head still for Igris's sword.

* * *

The room stayed quiet after that.

Bellion counted the bodies before calling the line clear. Nobody moved toward the marks appearing on the far wall until Fang had checked the ceiling which had produced them.

Mau put a hand against the arch and looked at the blood on his sleeve.

Only the scrape on his arm.

He could still feel his feet.

The repair in his back had held.

Beru landed close enough to see where he was looking.

“You came back,” the ant said. ᴮʳ

“I said I would.” ᴹ

It was such a small version of the promise he wanted to keep that it almost hurt.

Then Igris stepped beside them and Mau let the two of them walk with him to the room they already knew.
