*When: Beru tries to hide his damaged claw, Mau puts down the bowl.*

“Show me.” ᴹ

The ant looked offended by the suggestion that there was anything worth showing.

Then he held out the hand.

One edge had broken where the creature caught it. The shape was beginning to restore, drawing on the connection Mau knew well enough to distinguish it from the healing of his own arm.

He gave the work what it needed.

Beru watched the missing edge return.

“You could have said,” Mau told him. ᴹ

“You were moving.” ᴮʳ

“And now I'm sitting. Tell me when you're hurt.” ᴹ

The ant looked at him for long enough that Mau remembered the first night.

He sighed.

“Yes. Me too.” ᴹ

* * *

The room's provisions had been replaced while they were gone.

Mau inspected the shelf before choosing a bowl. He did not thank the room. He ate what he needed from it and let the deer finish the quieter part of the repair along his arm.

It was easier to keep that work small now that he was not asking it to do everything at once.

The reward waited until he chose to read it.

**LEVEL: 128.**

**UNALLOCATED POINTS: 57.**

He sat looking at the second number for a moment.

Back where he had started, but not by undoing anything. Ten points spent, ten gained across two encounters. The body kept what he had put into it. The ledger needed to keep the distinction too.

Igris brought a clean strip from the broken panel. Mau recorded the two purchases and the two rewards on it before dismissing the display.

The little act made him feel closer to Umaru than imagining what she might have said.

He had something he could actually show her when he got home.

* * *

Bellion came with Igris when the food was finished.

They did not need a meeting about who had been brave.

They needed to know why the last group had changed its route.

“They found the retreat,” Igris said. ᴵᵍ

Mau nodded.

“Or they were made to look for it.” ᴹ

“Either way, it became a target.” ᴮˡ

Bellion put the ridge positions on the floor. He marked the first dive and the line which had followed it. Mau added where he had stood when the third creature climbed instead of going through the fold.

There was no proof yet that the whole dungeon could learn. There was enough proof that opponents did not have to repeat the first bad approach until they died.

“We move the reserve sooner,” Mau said. “And I tell you before I change the space.” ᴹ

Igris pointed at the gap the fold had briefly left above Bellion's line.

“That part.” ᴵᵍ

Mau studied it.

His answer to one attack had changed what the knight could see from below. It had helped, but it had also asked Igris to understand the new angle without being warned.

“I'll call the side first.” ᴹ

Bellion rubbed out the old reserve mark and placed another.

The conversation continued until they had two things to try next time instead of twelve things to remember perfectly.

* * *

Later Mau asked Igris to show him the arch again.

He walked to it. Slowly, because he had already done enough fast things for one interval.

The next part of the dungeon remained beyond their cleared room. He could see that it was different now. The far wall had become a deeper line of shadow, and somewhere past it the ground seemed to fall away.

At the edge of the change, for less than a breath, the two outlines separated again.

Mau lifted his hand.

The surfaces joined before his fingers reached them.

“There,” he said. ᴹ

Igris had seen it this time.

“Yes.” ᴵᵍ

Mau kept his hand where the gap had been.

It was not a door. Not yet something he could use.

But the room was making an effort to become another room. It had to do that work somewhere.

He lowered his hand before the thought could turn into a reckless attempt to reach the next change faster than his body was ready to move.

“We watch for it,” he said. ᴹ

The knight nodded.

They went back for the broken wood on which they were keeping the days.
