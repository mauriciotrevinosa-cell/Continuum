*When: the bridge moves, Mau is already in the air.*

The first section dropped without warning. Stone struck water far below, and the cavalry coming toward him did not slow. Their mounts cleared the widening gap while the next section turned under the feet of his infantry.

“Second line left,” Bellion called. ᴮˡ

Mau caught one falling soldier with the grip. Another struck the edge and broke apart before he could reach it. He paid the restoration and formed it beside the reserve instead of back over the water.

The nearest rider lowered a hooked lance.

Infinity held the point away from his chest. The hook opened behind the rider's hand and threw three smaller blades past the protected line, toward the infantry preparing to jump.

Mau turned his head in time to see Igris catch the first.

The second took a soldier across the neck.

The third drove into the stone beside Bellion's foot.

“They aren't all coming for you,” the marshal said. ᴮˡ

Mau dropped below the next lance, let Infinity go and put Blue beneath the leading mount.

It lost the bridge.

The room kept its body intact when it struck the lower stone. That did not give it somewhere to stand.

* * *

They had twenty-four more soldiers than in the first two encounters.

Mau had called them before entering, with room for each company and a task which did not depend on him watching every spear. It had felt like admitting the first small group could not do everything.

Now Bellion sent a company across the turning section while Igris held the rider trying to cut them off. The second company waited, refusing an opening which would have put them on a bridge already beginning to fall.

Mau saw the pattern of their movement and understood how much work they had done while he was counting the days from a bed.

He went above them, keeping the sky clear.

One rider looked up.

Mau released Blue, set Shrine through the gap beneath its raised arm and followed the incomplete cut with the dagger. His body still knew how to turn through that narrow space. The repaired place in his back pulled, held, let him finish.

The rider fell.

The mount turned under him, giving the next lance a clear line across his ribs.

He caught the haft with the grip and used it to swing outside the point. The force dragged the mount off balance. Igris came beneath it, took the joint already opened by Mau's strike and made its fall permanent.

“Above,” the knight said. ᴵᵍ

Mau went up without asking whether he meant danger or direction.

A blade passed through the place where his head had been.

* * *

The far end of the bridge was not an exit.

Three riders waited there with the armour of their mounts joined into a single moving wall. The first two ranks had been driving Bellion's companies toward it.

Behind Mau, the bridge began dropping in sections.

He could kill one rider at a time. The damage reduction made every clean strike take too long. He could bring out Kamish, but the larger shape would fill the only route his infantry still had and offer the room a dragon-sized target before he knew what lay above it.

For a moment he felt the familiar weight of the wheel as a possibility.

Mahoraga could learn an attack. It could not make this room stop deciding how damage crossed its boundary simply because Mau was tired of the decision.

There was a simpler thing to try first.

“Bellion. Stop coming forward.” ᴹ

The marshal raised his hand.

His companies halted at the last stable section.

Mau climbed until he could see the supports beneath the armoured wall.

The riders could keep their bodies.

They still needed the bridge.

* * *

He released Shrine and placed Blue off the right side of the structure.

The pull dragged loose stone and water into a rising spiral. It took the front of the nearest mount just far enough that its rider had to correct against the two joined beside it.

Mau watched the correction, not the armour.

“Fang. Beneath the middle.” ᴹ

The sorcerer's strike met the support where the weight had shifted.

It did not destroy the whole bridge. Mau had not asked it to.

The middle support dropped a hand's breadth. The mounts at either side tried to compensate in opposite directions.

For the first time, the joined armour opened.

Mau let Blue go.

“Igris.” ᴹ

The knight was already moving.

He crossed the opening with a company behind him. Spear points found the exposed inner sides of the mounts while Beru descended on the rider trying to close the gap. Mau followed the ant, changed to Shrine and cut where Igris's blade had already held the plate apart.

One joined section broke.

The other two fell against each other.

The bridge took them down.

* * *

It was not finished until Bellion said the last rider had stopped moving.

Mau landed beside him with his daggers still in his hands. Water ran from the bridge in a hundred thin streams. Some of it was on his face; he had no idea which impact had put it there.

Ten ordinary forms had needed reconstruction during the encounter. He felt each completed return as the companies assembled, thirty soldiers again instead of a row of absences.

He counted them with Bellion this time.

Then he counted the people behind the commander: Igris, Beru, Fang, each doing something necessary which Mau had not ordered in the last minute.

“Thank you,” he said. ᴹ

Bellion followed his gaze.

“They know why they are here.” ᴮˡ

The words stayed with Mau as the next safe corridor opened.

He read the reward before putting it away.

Level one hundred and thirty. Sixty-seven points waiting.

He did not feel taller when the numbers settled. He felt wet, tired and able to walk back under his own strength.

For today that was enough.
