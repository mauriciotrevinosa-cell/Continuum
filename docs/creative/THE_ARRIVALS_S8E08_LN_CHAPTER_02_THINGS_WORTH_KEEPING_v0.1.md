*When: Mau cannot remember the joke Yuka made about the bird, he puts the bowl down.*

He could remember her laughing.

The stone beneath them. The cup making a small sound when her ring touched it. He could remember deciding not to interfere in a conversation he did not understand.

Then the next line came back, so plainly that he laughed before he could stop himself.

Beru looked up from the end of the bed.

“My king?” ᴮʳ

“She called me a coward.” ᴹ

The ant went very still.

“Over a bird,” Mau added quickly. ᴹ

Beru considered the explanation with the gravity it apparently required.

“A formidable opponent?” ᴮʳ

“I didn't want to find out.” ᴹ

The laugh hurt less than it had a few weeks before.

He finished the broth, then asked Igris for another clean piece of the wood they were keeping.

* * *

By their count, it was the forty-fifth day.

Four more sorties stood between the broken bridge and this room. One had taken them through a gully full of low creatures which tried to pull soldiers beneath the ground. Another had ended with Mau and Fang working from opposite sides of a sheet of falling stone. Two had been long enough that returning to the water and food felt like reaching a place they had made rather than one somebody else owned.

They had gained six levels across those four clears.

Mau wrote the rewards beside the earlier ones before he spent anything.

Ninety-seven points. He took sixty.

Fifteen Strength. Fifteen Dexterity. Ten each in Vitality, Perception and Intelligence.

He confirmed the distribution once, then waited through the change instead of standing too quickly to test it. Thirty-seven remained. Intelligence had reached four hundred and seventy.

The old wound did not disappear. Neither did the reluctant fingers or the blur which made him turn his head when an attack came from the left.

What changed was the amount he could ask of the body without making every request an emergency.

He wanted to tell Umaru that he had written it down properly.

Instead he put the piece beside the other records and started a different one.

* * *

He did not begin with the things he was afraid of forgetting.

He began with the things he wanted to bring back.

The bridge worked because Bellion had stopped when Mau asked, although the enemy had left something that looked like an invitation. Igris had found a way to turn a long weapon inside its owner's reach. Beru had learned to come back for his own repairs before the damaged claw became a problem in the next fight.

Mau wrote the bird joke underneath those discoveries.

Then the cupboard he had not repaired because Yuka had taken the afternoon.

He stopped there for a while.

The records would not be a substitute for seeing her. He did not need to make them one. They were something he could carry until he did.

* * *

There were ordinary tasks even here.

Someone had to wash the cloth. Somebody needed to check whether the next room's water could be reached without standing beneath its only light. The soldiers marked safe footing; Bellion changed their positions so the same bodies were not always first into a trap.

Mau helped when it was useful and sat down when it was not.

The first shirt he had worn through the gate had finally become more repair than fabric. He washed it anyway. The plain replacement from the provision shelf fitted badly across one shoulder and carried no blessing, armour or improvement the window could find.

He kept the old one folded with the records.

“It is ruined,” Beru observed. ᴮʳ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You could have a better one.” ᴮʳ

“I have a worse one that isn't torn.” ᴹ

The ant looked at the replacement and could not dispute the distinction.

Mau smoothed the old cloth once more.

Yuka had held the back of that shirt in the clinic. A brief touch, then room for him to move.

He folded it without trying to turn it into a relic of someone who was still alive.

He intended to complain about the replacement when he got home.

* * *

Before sleeping he tried the connection.

One attempt, long enough to recognise the same closure. He did not tear at it until pain became the proof that he had tried hard enough.

Noise followed the attempt and settled when Mau stopped.

*We'll try again tomorrow.*

The assent was brief.

Mau looked at the records beside the bed.

“Igris.” ᴹ

The knight turned from the doorway.

“If I start writing the same thing twice, tell me.” ᴹ

“Which thing?” ᴵᵍ

Mau looked at the bird joke and smiled.

“Not that one.” ᴹ

Igris inclined his head with an understanding which did not require him to read it.

The light behind the cloth stayed on. Mau lay down anyway, with tomorrow's work no longer arranged as a punishment for surviving today.
