*When: Beru sees what is waiting at the centre of the next room, he stops beside Mau.*

The creature had his shape.

Not precisely. The shoulders were broader, the plates sitting differently around a body which had never learned to lower itself beside a bed. Its head turned at their approach, and the small movements were close enough that Mau felt recognition before revulsion.

Beru took one step forward.

“That,” he said, “is not my king's soldier.” ᴮʳ

Across the floor, the other ant opened its mouth.

The sound which came out was almost a laugh.

* * *

Mau caught Beru's arm before he could take another step.

The ant looked back immediately.

“Tell me first.” ᴹ

Beru's hands closed.

“Let me face it.” ᴮʳ

“With us.” ᴹ

“With you,” Beru agreed. “But let me be the one it sees.” ᴮʳ

Mau looked at the creature again. It had no shadow waiting to answer his call. The pattern belonged to the room, made from the same kind of borrowed knowledge which had given the first guardian a weapon behind its apparent weakness.

Noise was already following the differences.

Mau felt the beginning of that attention and gave it a narrow task.

*Watch what it does. Help him understand it. Not the whole room at once.*

Then he released Beru's arm.

“If Bellion calls you back, you come.” ᴹ

The ant bowed his head.

“Yes, my king.” ᴮʳ

* * *

The first exchange sounded like somebody breaking tools.

Beru struck high, changed his angle before contact and drove his other hand beneath the raised guard. The false king took the blow in its side. Its answer crossed Beru's chest before he had room to withdraw.

A strip of shadow tore away.

Mau paid the rebuilding and stepped to the left when the room opened behind the two fighters.

There were smaller shapes coming out.

Of course there were.

“Bellion. The sides are ours.” ᴹ

The marshal raised the reserve.

* * *

Mau cut the first small ant before it reached Beru's back.

The second leapt into his face. Infinity stopped its jaws; he let it fall, released the route and finished the wound with Shrine before it found a soldier to climb.

At the centre, Beru had opened the other creature's shoulder.

The shoulder closed.

Mau saw the process catch on a pattern he recognised from outside this room: damage repaired through a remembered structure, something in the creature able to make that structure useful for more than its own flesh.

He had seen it without being able to give it to Beru.

Here the room had put it in front of him wearing Beru's face.

The ant struck again and again, each contact teaching him less about how to kill his opponent than about how it refused to remain hurt.

“Under the plate,” Mau called. “It starts there.” ᴹ

Beru turned his next strike.

The false king caught his wrist.

It pulled him into the bite.

Mau moved before deciding which technique he wanted. The grip caught Beru's upper body and changed the line just enough that the jaws took a shoulder edge instead of his neck.

Bellion's voice cut across the chamber.

“Back.” ᴮˡ

Beru obeyed.

The false king followed.

* * *

That was the first useful mistake it made.

It expected retreat to belong to prey. Beru's withdrawal carried it into a place the marshal had already given Mau, with no infantry behind the line and nothing below it they needed to protect.

Mau put Blue beside the false king's heel.

The pull stole half its turn.

He let Blue go before Beru crossed the altered space. The ant landed on the exposed side and drove both hands into the plate which kept beginning the repair.

The first effort was not enough.

The second broke one claw.

On the third, Beru changed the motion. He stopped trying to punch through the protected whole and held the opening apart while his jaws found the structure beneath it.

Mau felt Noise recognise what had become visible.

The false king screamed.

Beru did not let go.

* * *

The room's smaller ants came harder when the central body began to fail.

Mau and Igris took the nearest line together, the knight turning a creature's own leap into the opened wound of the next. Bellion held the place behind Beru. Fang struck the side passages until the rush had to come through two narrow lanes instead of six.

Mau changed from Shrine to the sky, bent one incoming line across the other and let the creatures break their own spacing. He released the fold before cutting the ones which landed.

At the centre, the false king's shoulder tried to close over Beru's face.

“Now,” Mau said. ᴹ

Noise followed the exposed pattern through the connection Beru already belonged to. Mau accepted the cost of keeping that route open while the ant pulled the false king apart.

This was no borrowed human soul. No second Beru waiting to be called into the army. It was a constructed enemy coming undone, with something useful inside it which had to be held before the room took it back.

Beru took it.

The creature stopped screaming.

* * *

Mau finished the last small attacker with his dagger and stayed still until Bellion had checked the chamber.

Beru stood over what remained of the false king.

There was blood which was not blood along the edges of his jaws. His shoulder had already begun restoring through the familiar demand on Mau's reserve, but another pattern was settling beneath it, different enough that Mau could feel where Noise had not finished making it safe to use.

“Don't try it yet,” he said. ᴹ

Beru turned toward him.

For a moment Mau thought the ant would protest.

Instead Beru looked at his master's arm, at the shallow new cut beneath the sleeve.

“When?” ᴮʳ

“When we know what it does.” ᴹ

The ant came to him, very carefully, as if the distance itself had become something he wanted to get right.

“Then let us find out,” he said. ᴮʳ
