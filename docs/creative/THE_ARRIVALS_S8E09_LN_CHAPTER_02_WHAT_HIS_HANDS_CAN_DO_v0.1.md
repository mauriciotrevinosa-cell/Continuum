*When: Beru reaches toward the cut, Mau catches his wrist.*

The ant stopped instantly.

“Here,” Mau said, turning his arm. “Only this.” ᴹ

It was a small wound along the outside of the forearm. Shallow enough that Mau could close it himself, new enough that neither of them had to pretend the old marks were part of the test.

Beru looked at the place, then at Mau's face.

“Tell me if it hurts.” ᴮʳ

“I will.” ᴹ

He let go.

* * *

Noise had worked through the long rest, holding the useful part of the pattern apart from the creature which had carried it.

There was no other voice in Beru now. Nothing asking to be recognised as the person it had belonged to. The false king had been a body the room made; the thing Mau had allowed into the link was a way of using power which needed to become Beru's before he trusted it against a living person.

They had begun with a frayed edge of the ant's own form.

That part was familiar. The next was not.

Beru's hand hovered above the forearm. Mau felt the warmth gather, too broad at first, trying to make the whole patch of skin its task instead of the thin opening he had chosen.

“Smaller.” ᴹ

The ant narrowed it.

The warmth became a line.

The cut began to close.

* * *

They stopped before it was finished.

Mau had asked for that too. A small beginning they could end deliberately was more useful than another success they could not interrupt.

He looked at the half-closed line.

Beru looked at it as though it were the most important wound in the room.

“Again?” he asked. ᴮʳ

Mau checked the demand through their connection. The room was still resisting the useful part. This did not give them a secret way around the suppression; it gave them another pair of hands which could work inside it.

He nodded.

“Finish this one.” ᴹ

Beru did.

When he drew his hand back, the skin had closed without the old scar beneath it changing at all.

Mau flexed his fingers.

The two reluctant ones remained reluctant.

“Good,” he said. ᴹ

Beru did not answer.

* * *

After a moment Mau looked up.

The ant was staring at his own hand.

“What?” ᴹ

“The first room.” ᴮʳ

Mau understood before he said anything else.

The bed. The bowl. The careful turn which had hurt even when everybody did it correctly.

“You didn't have this in the first room.” ᴹ

“If I had—” ᴮʳ

“I would have asked you to use it.” ᴹ

Beru looked at him.

“And since you didn't, I asked you to hold the bowl.” ᴹ

The ant lowered his head.

Mau waited until he looked up again.

“I needed that too.” ᴹ

For a little while the only sound was the water reaching the trough.

Then Beru placed the healed arm gently back on Mau's knee, as though returning something he had been trusted to keep.

“Next time,” he said, “I can do both.” ᴮʳ

* * *

The clear had brought three levels.

Mau wrote one hundred and thirty-nine on the record. Fifty-two points waiting. He spent forty, ten each into Strength, Dexterity, Vitality and Intelligence, checking the last confirmation before it settled.

Twelve remained. Intelligence reached four hundred and eighty.

The increase did not remove the room's restrictions. It made the work they could sustain inside them a little less narrow. When Mau called the same force through the familiar route again, there was more room before the strain began climbing into the rest of his body.

He sat still and let the change finish.

Beru was still examining his hands.

“You can stop looking at them,” Mau said. ᴹ

“No, my king.” ᴮʳ

Mau laughed.

“All right.” ᴹ

* * *

That evening Igris came back from the next arch with a report about the ground beyond it.

Bellion began changing the order of the companies. Fang complained about a ceiling which had apparently been built by someone who disliked sorcerers. Beru listened to all of it with a new kind of attention, following the places in which the plan would put someone beyond his immediate reach.

Mau watched him do it.

The room had meant to put a king in front of another king.

What it had given him was a soldier thinking about how to bring the rest home.
