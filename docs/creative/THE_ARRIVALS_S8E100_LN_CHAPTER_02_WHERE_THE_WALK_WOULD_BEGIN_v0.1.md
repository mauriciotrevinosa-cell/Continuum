*When: Ori asks where the wedding begins, Yuka points toward their own door.*

“There.” ʸ

Ori looks at it.

Then at the tree.

“That's a walk.” ᴼ

“Yes.” ʸ

Mau, beside the table in the sitting room, smiles at the simplicity of the conclusion.

Ori has brought the thing she said she wanted to show them. Frieren knows where she is. The visit has begun with the important question and will apparently continue until the two adults have explained why a person needs quite so much cloth to walk a short way through her own home.

Yuka takes the page and sits where Ori can see.

* * *

The path will use the doors people have chosen.

Not to make every private home into a stage. Those who want to join them can step into the shared route from their own place. Some will bring music. Some a small thing they have made. Some will simply be there, which Yuka has become very firm about counting as enough.

They will walk together toward the tree, with the people they love close enough to see their faces rather than arranged in a distant block behind a platform.

Ori puts one finger on the curve drawn beneath the branches.

“Mum here?” ᴼ

“If she wants that place,” Yuka says. ʸ

“And Fern?” ᴼ

“We ask.” ʸ

Ori appears satisfied by the consistency.

Mau watches her begin deciding which question she wants to take back to Frieren first.

* * *

The little test after sunset uses two lights and a length of trial cloth.

It is enough to show why Wakana wanted the folds softer and why Marin insisted the door should remain recognisable behind them. The light falls through the fabric, then leaves a space where the dark makes the next part visible. Nothing heavy hangs from the tree. Nobody has drawn a line which pretends the roots will politely move aside for a wedding.

Mau stands with Yuka at the beginning.

“You can change something,” she tells him. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You're looking like you're afraid to touch the plan.” ʸ

He looks at the people waiting near the turn.

Mai is holding one corner of the cloth. Maki has the page. Umaru is arguing with Milim about how much of the route can be declared essential Fort territory before the ceremony is technically an occupation.

“I want time to see them,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks toward the same group.

“We can go slowly.” ʸ

“Not because anything stops us.” ᴹ

She turns back to him.

The answer changes the smile on her face.

“Because we want to.” ʸ

* * *

They try the first part together.

Only the first part. There are no vows waiting at the end, no hidden ceremony assembled because somebody decided the two of them could be surprised into getting married early.

Mau sees the contrast of light and cloth, the shape of the people beside the path and Yuka in colour at his side. The world does not have to pretend to cure him in order to be beautiful here.

Bocchi offers a few notes from where she has chosen to sit.

They are not yet a finished piece.

Yuka stops to listen.

Mau stops with her.

For a moment, the future has a sound instead of another demand.

* * *

Afterwards, Ori takes the answer back to Frieren.

Mau and Yuka walk with her as far as the door. Himmel is there with a book Frieren asked him to find among the ones they had not finished shelving. She accepts it, listens to Ori's explanation and lets the child point at the place on the drawing she has been considering.

“We can ask Fern together,” Frieren says. ᶠ

Ori looks pleased and goes inside to put away the thing she brought.

Himmel asks Frieren whether she wants to see how the lights look from the other side of the court.

She glances at the book.

Then leaves it on the shelf.

“For a little while.” ᶠ

They go at their own pace.

* * *

Back home, Yuka puts the wedding page in a place they can both reach.

“No date yet,” Mau says, looking at the empty space at the top. ᴹ

“No date yet.” ʸ

He takes her hand.

“But that's where we begin.” ᴹ

She looks toward their door, then at him.

“Yes.” ʸ

He kisses her, and the first ordinary evening after the small rehearsal belongs to them rather than the page.
