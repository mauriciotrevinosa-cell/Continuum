*When: Beru discovers the small door will open, he calls Igris over with the urgency of somebody who has found an enemy.*

Igris looks at the piece.

Then at the hinge.

Then back at Beru.

“It is meant to do that.” ᴵ

“Yes.” ᴮ

Beru opens it again.

Bellion, who has spent considerably longer deciding how the surrounding pieces should fit, waits until the third demonstration before asking whether they might now return to the rest of the work.

“It will be seen,” Beru says. ᴮ

“Then we should finish what is beside it,” Bellion answers. ᴮᵉˡ

* * *

Wakana has given them tools which make the smallest work possible without strength becoming its enemy.

Mai has helped them understand the join which needs to remain neat when opened. Neither was ordered to provide a wedding gift on behalf of the army. They were asked for help by people who wanted to make something with their own time, and have become interested enough to stay.

Kamish has an opinion about the unfinished shape of the tree.

It is a good opinion, which Beru finds irritating because the dragon has been enjoying the right to offer several from a comfortable distance.

Igris alters one line.

The piece looks better.

Kamish accepts this with the dignity of someone who expected nothing less.

* * *

They cover the work when Mau comes past.

He looks at the cloth, then at the four faces pretending the subject is unremarkable.

“Anything dangerous?” he asks. ᴹ

“No, my king.” ᴮ

“Anything Geld will have to move because you put it in the wrong place?” ᴹ

“No.” ᴮᵉˡ

Mau looks toward Wakana.

The craftsman shakes his head, smiling.

“Then enjoy yourselves,” Mau says. ᴹ

He goes on before curiosity becomes an argument for taking the choice away from them.

Under the cloth, the little door remains open.

* * *

In the afternoon, Yuka finds him outside their home with a cup which has gone cold.

“Thinking?” she asks. ʸ

“A little.” ᴹ

She sits beside him and follows his gaze toward the people going back and forth through the courts.

Inside, Noise has offered a few quiet observations about the new place and left the much larger work where they agreed to keep it. The guests have their own choices. Ciel has reminded him to drink the cup before it becomes a useful example of something else he has forgotten to do.

Mau tastes it.

Too late.

Yuka sees his expression and begins laughing.

“Come inside,” she says. “I'll make another.” ʸ

“I can.” ᴹ

“Then make two.” ʸ

He gets up with her.

* * *

The visitors are on the road today.

Their rooms are ready. The message is on the page where Rimuru keeps the arrivals they actually expect. Nobody in the house has to imagine a knock before remembering why it might come.

Mau passes the page on his way to the kettle.

He looks at the names, then returns to the two cups Yuka has put on their table.

There is room to finish the small thing first.
