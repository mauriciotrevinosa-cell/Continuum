*When: Geld says they can stop calling it the site, Mau looks toward the people still carrying things.*

“There will always be something to carry,” Geld says. ᴳᵉ

Mau looks back at him.

“You're telling me it's ready.” ᴹ

“I'm telling you people can live here without needing a builder to decide whether they may open the next door.” ᴳᵉ

They walk the last checks together.

Doors. Water. The routes used when someone is carrying something too awkward to turn easily. The place where the rain now goes. Lights which can be maintained by people who have chosen to learn them, with the crystal supply still serving the work it serves best.

The root circle remains open.

The protection has not been asked to follow the outermost idea somebody drew.

At the common room, Umaru calls through to the Fort herself.

Ori answers.

Geld nods.

* * *

The workers discuss their next journeys before anyone thanks them as though gratitude ought to decide where they sleep forever.

The twelve who came in the second road party plan to return with Kurobe in two mornings. Geld and the two who first came with him will remain two mornings beyond that to finish handing over the upkeep and hear the questions people only discover after using a home.

They can choose the camp they have made comfortable for the remaining nights or ask about the rooms available at the inn. No one has to announce a permanent life in order to be offered a dry place to rest.

Kurobe tells Mau which tools will go back.

Which finished fittings belong to the house.

Which questions he would like brought to him before someone decides their clever improvement is too small to need discussing.

Senku listens with the expression of a man who feels unfairly anticipated.

Byakuya enjoys it openly.

* * *

Mau lets the eighty ordinary hauling soldiers return to the quiet of his shadow once their assigned loads are finished.

No one vanishes with a beam still needing them to hold it. Geld releases the jobs first. The named shadows who have asked for their own time remain by choice and under the same simple rules they accepted before beginning.

The home watch keeps its separate work. Muster and the forty ordinary soldiers have learnt the routes the fixed boundary still requires. Building a town has not added thousands of beds for an army or made an uncounted new army appear to guard it.

Mau looks at the cleared stack where the last load waited.

For once, putting something away feels as useful as calling it out.

* * *

The private conversation with Souei and Testarossa is brief enough to remain a beginning.

Ram gives the account of what she actually saw. Mau names the foreign activity and the point where it ceased after the creature was caught. Rimuru keeps the distinction between an observation, a suspicion and a person they have not identified.

They discuss ways of investigating without turning every visitor into an accused spy. Souei asks for the route, not a name somebody wishes the route would prove. Testarossa asks what information has been spoken aloud near the public parts of the house.

There is useful work to do.

There is no revelation which demands that Mau abandon the evening to go hunting alone.

They agree the next questions and leave the sensitive details out of the common room.

* * *

Yuka finds him near the tree after the visitors have gone to rest.

“Finished?” she asks. ʸ

He looks toward the homes.

“The part we can call finished.” ᴹ

She studies his face.

“Does that make you want to find the next impossible thing?” ʸ

Mau considers the question honestly.

Inside him there are drawings he has chosen not to build yet. There are guests whose peace has become something more precise than a kind intention. There is a whole world which has not decided what it will ask him to become.

He looks back at Yuka.

“Right now, I'd like dinner.” ᴹ

She smiles slowly.

“Very promising.” ʸ

* * *

They put the wedding pages away before the meal.

Not because they have changed their minds. Because the day deserves to be about the thing the whole house has finished choosing, making and learning to use.

Mau leaves the page where he and Yuka can find it tomorrow. The date is still empty. The place is no longer something they need to imagine as a home before it can be a wedding.

Downstairs, somebody begins setting out the first dishes.

He hears the sound and goes to wash his hands.
