He stared at the thin crack.

Then he loosened his grip on the point he was using as a pencil.

“Not the board's fault,” he muttered. ᴹ

Igris took the broken piece, checked that the writing was still legible and laid it beside the others.

One hundred and twenty-one days by the record they had been able to keep.

Mau had stopped asking the room to make the number feel reasonable.

* * *

Eight sorties had taken them from the false king to this place.

One was an army on a slope which would not let them rest their feet on the same ground twice. Bellion won it by moving the reserve sideways until the enemy had to choose between turning and keeping its line together. Mau remembered it because he had finally listened before spending half the battle correcting the move himself.

Two later chambers wore the light and the shapes of powers he associated with Rulers. They were built things, not people who had arrived to explain what they wanted. One spoke with a voice whose sentences ended in the same mechanical pause. The other never spoke at all.

Mau had needed Red against the second. He had needed the grip and a long, ugly struggle to keep its weapon from turning toward his chest while the copied route changed. Beru had closed a wound at his side afterward, working slowly enough that Mau could tell him where the pressure had spread too far.

Nothing about those encounters made the originals small.

It told him how much the room wanted from a person it still thought it could finish building.

* * *

Level one hundred and fifty-three.

He had eighty-two points before the new allocation. Seventy-five went into the five familiar entries: twenty each to Strength, Dexterity and Vitality, ten to Perception, five to Intelligence.

Seven left. Intelligence four hundred and eighty-five.

He kept the full ledger on several pieces now. It would have been easy to remember the last number and lose what he had done to reach it. He did not want to get home and make Umaru work out which purchases had been wishes and which were already part of the body she was trying to help him understand.

The body was changing.

He could feel it in the space between a thought and a step, in the amount of work he could keep separate without all of it arriving as the same headache. It did not make him immune to bad decisions.

It made good ones go farther.

* * *

Beru brought the washed shirt while Mau was finishing the record.

The original one. Folded badly, but clean.

“You don't have to keep doing that,” Mau said. ᴹ

“It needed washing.” ᴮʳ

“It isn't dirty.” ᴹ

“It was before I washed it.” ᴮʳ

Mau looked up.

The ant's expression remained entirely serious.

He began laughing and had to put the writing down.

Beru waited until he could take the shirt without dropping it.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

The ant inclined his head, pleased by the usefulness of the task and unwilling to admit the argument had been a joke.

* * *

Later, while Fang and Bellion were disagreeing about how much room the next spell needed, Mau took out the records which were not numbers.

He read the small things first.

Yuka correcting his account of who stole the blanket. Ori explaining a game with the certainty that every rule had always existed. Mai warning him about making a hook after telling him several times that she would continue warning him.

He added the sound Kaneki's repaired door no longer made.

There were things he had lived which he did not want to turn into lessons. They could remain ordinary. He could miss them without asking them to become a reason he deserved to suffer more here.

Igris stood in the passage, looking toward the next room.

“Can I ask something?” Mau said. ᴹ

The knight turned.

“If you know we've been somewhere before, tell me even if the room looks different.” ᴹ

“You think it is repeating?” ᴵᵍ

“Parts of it. The ground changes. The way the change starts doesn't.” ᴹ

Igris considered that.

“Then I will watch the beginning.” ᴵᵍ

Mau nodded and put the records away.

He had not found an exit.

He had found something the room kept doing whether it wanted him to notice or not.
