## Chapter 26 — Two Skies of Fire

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S8 v0.1 (2026-10-30)

*When: Kamish sees the two shapes on the far ridges, he opens his wings without being asked.*

The basin had a sky.

Mau distrusted that first. After so many ceilings, the distance above him seemed like an invitation to discover something too late. He sent Kaisel high enough to observe the edges while Bellion moved the ground companies behind the nearest rise.

Two dragons watched them.

One crouched low, its jaw almost resting on the stone. The other stood farther back with its head raised, a slow brightness moving between the plates of its throat.

Kamish looked from one to the other.

“They have borrowed poorly,” he said. ᴷᵃ

“Tell them afterward,” Mau answered. ᴹ

He called Lantern to the left, Agito to the sheltered ground and the wheel-bearer into the space where the first direct breath would have to pass.

Mahoraga arrived whole.

There were attacks it could learn. There was no reason to spend its existence asking it to learn ownership of a room which belonged to somebody else.

Mau chose the first dragon's fire.

Then the second dragon moved before the first had breathed.

* * *

The raised head released a narrow line which struck the ridge beside Bellion.

Stone burst outward in hard white fragments. The companies split on the marshal's signal, already moving toward the two shelters they had marked. Agito took the falling mass above the slower line and drove it aside.

Mau was in the air before the next line came.

Infinity held it off his body. He saw the first dragon's jaw open below and understood the arrangement: one kept him spending his answer; the other waited for him to change it.

Kamish struck from the side.

His breath met the low dragon's first torrent above the wheel-bearer. The collision filled the basin with a light Mau could see only as brightness, violent enough to make the old blur spread until he turned his head and found the edges again.

The wheel turned.

Mahoraga stayed where Mau had put it.

“Left ridge,” he called. ᴹ

Kaisel answered by diving along the place he meant. Lantern followed lower, drawing the raised dragon's attention away from the ground for half a breath.

Mau released Infinity and put Blue against its jaw.

The head turned.

The next line went into the empty ridge instead of the company behind it.

* * *

He went in behind the altered angle with Shrine ready.

The first cut opened a line under the eye. The second deepened it. The room swallowed most of what both should have done, leaving a wound which would have been decisive somewhere else and was merely an opportunity here.

Mau used the opportunity.

He landed against the plates of its neck, daggers finding the same place while the grip held him through the creature's turn. He could feel the structure beneath him trying to throw his weight clear before he reached the vulnerable joint.

Below, Beru and Igris were taking smaller attackers away from the refuge. The room had begun producing them along the far ground, bodies with enough of the dragons' protection to make every little fight expensive.

Bellion moved the reserve without asking Mau to see it happen.

Mau cut once more.

The dragon's neck bent farther than it intended.

He almost had the eye.

Then Kamish warned him.

* * *

The low dragon had stopped trying to win the contest of breath.

It drove through its own fire, crossed beneath Kamish's wing and struck the side of the raised creature with its whole body.

Mau was between the moving masses.

He let Shrine go and caught the surface of the collision with the sky. The first pressure folded away from him. Enough of the second followed an angle he had not been able to give it that his hold tore loose before he had room for Infinity.

He hit the ground chest-first.

Something beneath his ribs moved in a direction it should not move.

The pain did not stay at the point of contact. The room made it larger, drove it through the body he had strengthened until the next breath arrived with blood in it.

He tried to rise.

The basin rolled out from under his hand.

* * *

Above him, Mahoraga met the returning fire.

This time the shape of its answer was different. It held the line long enough for Agito and the infantry to pass behind it, moving the exposed company into shelter. The learned response belonged to that fire. When the other dragon's broken ridge came down beside it, the impact still drove the wheel-bearer onto one knee.

Mau saw all of that from the ground.

He wanted to tell somebody where to stand.

What came out was another thin breath.

Beru landed beside him, put a hand against his chest and went absolutely still.

“My king.” ᴮʳ

Mau tried to answer.

The ant's other hand found the blood at his side.

“Stay with me,” he said. ᴮʳ

Across the basin, Kamish drove the wounded dragon into the ridge hard enough to make the whole sky shake.
