*When: he closes his eyes, Mau sees the stream instead of the basin.*

Not a vision sent by the room.

A memory. Her head on his leg, the impossible smallness of the argument about a bird, the pressure of her fingers when she told him she had wanted the afternoon.

He had given it to her.

He wanted another.

Beru said his name again.

Mau opened his eyes.

* * *

The ant was trying to do too much.

Mau could feel it through the same connection which carried the repair. Chest, side, the places the impact had torn through on its way to becoming five times its own weight. The new healing could reach them. The room slowed all of it until every unfinished piece asked for attention at once.

Mau caught Beru's wrist.

“One,” he managed. ᴹ

The ant bent closer.

“Tell me.” ᴮʳ

He moved the hand a little, toward the place he could not afford to leave open.

“Here. Hold this.” ᴹ

Beru narrowed the effort.

The change gave Mau enough room to find his own repair without asking it to climb over every other demand.

He had done this before. He had done it badly before. Neither fact meant he had to do it at the same speed as the last time he was frightened.

He found the smallest part he could finish.

* * *

The familiar display opened at his request.

Seven points.

He put all seven into Vitality, checked the entry and confirmed before the next wave of pain could turn the decision into an attempt to buy everything at once.

Zero remained.

The gain was real. So was the hole he still had to close.

He let the window go and asked Noise for the structure they had already used, not a new one, not an escape route built through tissue which had not finished becoming itself.

The local filter held the arriving information apart. What was broken. What was being held by Beru. What Mau could touch without undoing the bit he had just made stable.

He chose.

For the next several breaths, the world was that small.

* * *

The memory of Yuka did not heal him.

It gave him something to return to when pain made the next piece of work seem longer than he could bear to do.

His own power moved through a route he had learned to keep narrow. The first tiny reconstruction held. He did not throw it away by asking the whole injury to follow before it was ready.

Another piece.

Another breath.

Beru kept his hand where Mau had put it, making no attempt to turn the task into something grander. When the pressure began wandering, Mau tightened his fingers and the ant corrected.

The next breath went in farther.

Mau coughed, tasted blood and found that he could do it again.

* * *

Bellion appeared at the edge of the broken ground.

“We can hold the lower ridge. Not this place.” ᴮˡ

Mau looked toward it.

He could see the route. Agito standing beneath a falling edge. Igris keeping the smaller attackers from reaching the company which was opening a lane. The wheel above the line of fire, turning through a problem which still had a shape.

He had an answer to a few breaths.

He did not have an answer to another whole impact.

“We move,” he said. ᴹ

Beru looked at his face.

“With you.” ᴹ

The ant nodded.

* * *

They did not carry him as far as he feared they would have to.

At the first sheltered rise, he found his feet. Beru stayed beneath his arm while the repair continued. Mau used the grip for balance rather than making the ant prove he could hold all his weight.

From there he could see the wounded dragon.

Kamish had torn away the plate above its eye. The other creature was trying to force the larger shadow into the path of its next breath.

Mau watched the timing.

“Kamish,” he said. ᴹ

The dragon's head turned just enough to show he had heard.

Mau drew the daggers into his hands and felt the equipped speed settle against the body which had almost failed to bring him back to this ridge.

He did not use it yet.

“On my word, let him turn.” ᴹ

Beru looked toward the gap.

Then he moved his supporting hand from Mau's shoulder, leaving it close enough that it was still there if he needed it.
