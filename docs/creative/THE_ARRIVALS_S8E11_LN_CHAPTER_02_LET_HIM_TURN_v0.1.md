*When: Kamish lets go, the wounded dragon believes it has won something.*

Its head came around.

The exposed eye turned toward the lower ridge, where Mau stood with blood drying beneath his borrowed shirt.

He let it see him.

“Now.” ᴹ

Kamish dropped beneath the line of the open jaw.

Mau released the grip which had been keeping his balance, gathered Red and put the small, concentrated answer straight into the wound his dragon had opened.

The eye vanished.

The creature's head struck the ridge behind it, not dead, not allowed to be dead from the damage it should have taken. The room kept the body moving for a few more terrible seconds.

Mau went with it.

* * *

He used his own flight for the distance and the daggers for the last narrow part.

The head tried to lift. Kamish caught it again, lower this time, his teeth locked around the part Mau needed held away from the exposed joint.

Mau took the first cut down the opened line.

Then another.

The repaired place beneath his ribs burned. He let Beru keep the one part they had agreed on and refused to turn every warning into a reason to stop seeing what he was doing.

Below, Bellion moved the ground line out of the shadow of the struggling head.

The next strike reached something which no longer had enough protection left around it.

Mau felt the joint part.

Kamish finished the motion.

The first dragon fell.

* * *

There was no room to celebrate it.

The second dragon's breath crossed the place where Kamish had been standing.

Mahoraga met the learned fire on the line Mau had given it, keeping the ground company from being caught beneath it. The new movement of the creature's body still forced the wheel-bearer back.

Mau got Infinity between himself and the returning edge, waited until the torrent passed and let the route go.

Blue went under the far leg.

He did not ask it to destroy a protected dragon.

He asked it to change where the weight arrived.

Fang struck the ground before the foot came down. The ridge gave way. The dragon's forequarters dropped into the split while Lantern and Kaisel pulled its attention in opposite directions above.

Its wings opened too late to catch all of its weight.

Kamish hit from behind.

* * *

Mau could not keep that arrangement forever.

He told Bellion before he ran out of the ability to hold it.

The marshal answered by sending the reserve into the opening, not by asking him for more time. Igris took one exposed hinge. Beru took another. The companies pinned smaller attackers away from the hands which were doing the precise work.

Mau released Blue and went to Shrine.

He cut only where somebody had already opened a way in.

The temptation to make a larger answer was almost unbearable. A full room-filling thing which might end the fight and the thought of another fight after it.

He could feel how badly his body would receive the request.

He chose the smaller wound.

Then the next.

* * *

The second dragon died with its head half-raised toward the sky it had never left.

Mau stayed airborne until Bellion gave the clear. Then he found the sheltered ridge and let his knees bend before the landing became another impact.

Beru was there.

“My king.” ᴮʳ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You don't know what I was going to say.” ᴮʳ

Mau looked at the blood on the ant's hand.

“Was it about sitting down?” ᴹ

“Immediately.” ᴮʳ

He sat.

* * *

The reward carried him to one hundred and sixty.

Seven levels for the two calamities and the field which had tried to keep them apart. Thirty-five points after spending the last seven on the ground.

He put thirty into the body: ten Strength, ten Dexterity, ten Vitality. Five remained. Intelligence stayed where it had been.

He wrote it after they had reached the recovery space, with Beru still holding the part of the repair Mau had asked him not to abandon.

Kamish rested his enormous head beyond the open arch. Mau could see one eye watching the room and no part of the satisfaction with which the dragon would eventually describe the fight.

“You can say it,” Mau told him. ᴹ

Kamish's eye narrowed.

“They borrowed poorly.” ᴷᵃ

Mau began laughing and stopped when Beru looked at him.

“Later,” the ant said. ᴮʳ

He nodded, still smiling, and let them help him lie down.

* * *

When he could sit long enough to look at the field again, Mau asked Bellion what they had left on it.

The marshal named the two great bodies first, then the lesser creatures which had kept coming around the dragons while the companies held the refuge.

“The complete ones?” Mau asked. ᴹ

“Separated. Some will not hold an outline.” ᴮˡ

“Then we take the ones that do.” ᴹ

He did not get up to prove he could supervise a collection while Beru was still closing the wound in his side. Igris organised the work at the chamber entrance. The old soldiers brought the eligible bodies within reach of Mau's attention, kept the next group out of the passage and moved the remains away after he had finished with them.

Mau gave the command.

The first group rose in dark, uneven ranks. He sent them into storage, rested and began again only when his own repair no longer had to compete for the same moment of concentration.

It took more than one interval of work.

Four hundred ordinary shapes accepted the binding. Fallen attempts stayed attempts, never additions. A soldier already his which had broken during the fight belonged in the repair column, not among these new recruits.

Igris checked both columns before showing him the result.

**NEW ORDINARY SOLDIERS: 400.**

**REGISTERED TOTAL: 1,614.**

Beru looked from the record to the field.

“The large ones?” ᴮʳ

Mau watched Kamish resting beyond the doorway.

“Not another pair of heavy bodies while I'm trying to finish repairing this one. We have dragons. I need more hands and more eyes.” ᴹ

He pointed out the intact scale sections which the companies had already marked for removal. Twelve pieces, small enough to pack and strong enough to be worth showing the workshop. No promises about what they would become before someone had tested them.

Bellion had them wrapped apart from the troop records. Mau put the bundle into ordinary inventory storage, not the army register.

He could leave the two great carcasses and still refuse to leave everything useful on the ground.
