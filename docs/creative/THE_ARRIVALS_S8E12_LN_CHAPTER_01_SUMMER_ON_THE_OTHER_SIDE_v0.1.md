*When: the room gives them rain, Mau stands in it longer than the inspection requires.*

The water was cold.

It ran through his hair, down the back of his neck, into the plain shirt he had learned to dislike less than the one before it. Beyond the entrance, a forest waited with shapes moving between the trunks.

He did not mistake it for home.

He wanted to hear rain on something other than stone.

Igris came beside him and remained quiet until Mau stepped back beneath the arch.

“Slippery ground,” the knight said. ᴵᵍ

“I noticed.” ᴹ

“I meant for the soldiers.” ᴵᵍ

Mau looked at his wet sleeves and began laughing.

* * *

It was the two hundred and seventy-sixth day of the count.

Ten clears since the two dragons. Ten separate records, each with a date they could support, a reward and the names of the companies which had taken the first approach. Several encounters had lasted long enough to need their own rest and food carried from the provision rooms. Some had ended in an afternoon.

All of them had left something to practise.

He had stopped using more force as the first answer to resistance. Sometimes a better angle did more than another purchase. Sometimes a purchase gave his body enough room that he could keep the better angle while something else tried to kill him.

At one hundred and eighty-three, he had one hundred and twenty points available.

He spent one hundred and fifteen: thirty Strength, thirty Dexterity, twenty-five Vitality, twenty Perception, ten Intelligence.

Five remained. Intelligence four hundred and ninety-five.

He wrote the distribution before the next sleep and woke to find Beru had put a flat stone on the records so the draft from the doorway would not scatter them.

There were enough pieces now that someone had made a box from the provision-room panels.

Mau had not asked who.

He thanked all three commanders anyway.

* * *

The rain-forest encounter ended with mud on everyone who had a body capable of holding it.

Mau had taken a cut above the hip. Beru repaired the part Mau could not reach while he cleaned the blade he had used. It was not the first time they had worked through that arrangement; it was the first time the ant complained about the angle.

“Turn.” ᴮʳ

“I'm turning.” ᴹ

“You are moving the knife. That is different.” ᴮʳ

Mau looked at him, then put the weapon away.

“Better?” ᴹ

“Considerably.” ᴮʳ

Across the room, Igris shook water from the edge of his cloak. Bellion was explaining to a company leader why reaching the last creature first did not mean leaving the person behind you with no bridge to use.

They were still learning.

It should have been a comfort that he was not the only one.

Today it was.

* * *

The voice came while Mau was putting the records away.

“Good. My monarch is getting back to his former level.”

He stopped with one hand on the lid.

Beru rose.

Mau held up a hand.

“I'm not him,” he said to the empty room. ᴹ

Nothing answered.

“And he doesn't want this.” ᴹ

The light above the shelf brightened by a small, meaningless amount.

Mau looked at it until he was certain he was not about to spend a whole evening arguing with a ceiling.

Then he closed the box.

The room could be wrong about him. He did not have to make its mistake the only thing he thought about.

* * *

Before sleeping, he took out the page which was really a thin piece of washed wood and read Yuka's words again.

*I wanted the afternoon.*

There would be a summer somewhere outside this place. Or autumn, or a morning much closer to the one he had left than his count suggested. He did not know.

He knew which part of the day he wanted back.

He put the words away, tried the connection once and lay down when it did not answer.

At the arch, Igris had placed another small mark where the room had begun to change.

They had seen the interval from both sides now.
