*When: the next arch opens, Mau does not move toward the enemy.*

He watches the edge.

For most of a year he had been too slow to do anything useful with what he saw there. A second outline, a pressure which did not yet know which direction it was meant to hold. The room had to release one place before it could finish pretending the next had always been there.

Today his hand reached the unfinished edge.

It hurt.

He drew back before the closing surfaces could turn the lesson into another missing part of his body.

Igris looked at the blood along one knuckle.

“Through?” ᴵᵍ

“Not far enough.” ᴹ

For once that was an answer he could work with.

* * *

Three hundred and sixty-seven days.

The record no longer looked like something a person should have been able to keep while supposedly attending one emergency before lunch.

Eleven more clears had brought him from one hundred and eighty-three to one hundred and ninety-seven. Seventy-five points before the latest allocation. Sixty spent: ten each to Strength, Dexterity and Vitality, fifteen each to Perception and Intelligence.

Fifteen left. Intelligence five hundred and ten.

There was a ceiling in the window now.

Not a prophecy. A limit someone had put into the allocation structure, two hundred, with a prepared place for the last three levels.

Mau looked at it without feeling the gratitude the room seemed to expect.

“That's your ending,” he said. ᴹ

He did not know whether Noise could find anything beyond it. He did not have to know today in order to want the world which existed outside its idea of a finished body.

* * *

They had tested the gap whenever a clear gave them the opportunity.

Exchange went to an inside position because it had a position to use. The unfinished interval had no stable destination. Trying to give it a name only made the route close on itself.

He had to get there with his body.

Fast enough to reach the edge before it joined. Strong enough to hold the movement for the next part of a breath. Precise enough not to cut the limb he needed against two spaces which had stopped agreeing about where it belonged.

He had watched every variation he could survive watching.

Now Igris knew the starting movement. Bellion knew how long he could keep the force in position. Beru knew exactly which bit of Mau would be left open if he made the attempt while still injured.

They did not call it a safe plan.

It was a plan which led somewhere other than the next room.

* * *

The last recruitment entry was longer than the entry for his own purchases.

Eleven more clears since the rain-forest record. Two thousand eight hundred additional ordinary shadows, bound from selected bodies before each field changed. Some groups had been small. Others had taken repeated calls, repairs to the collection route and a proper rest before he could finish holding the new shapes securely.

He had kept the failures out. He had kept the repairs out. He had kept the occasional tempting body which would have cost more attention than it would have returned to the work.

The arithmetic now held without any of those pretending to be recruits.

Two hundred from the ant chamber.

Four hundred from the lesser bodies around the two calamities.

Two thousand two hundred across the next ten fields.

Two thousand eight hundred across the final eleven.

Five thousand six hundred ordinary additions to the one thousand and fourteen he had brought in.

**REGISTERED TOTAL: 6,614.**

The greater part waited in storage. Bringing them all out at once would not give him six thousand places to stand; in a narrow chamber it would give Bellion six thousand bodies to keep out of one another's way. Deploying a company and restoring it when it broke still asked something of the channels inside Mau, whatever the register could hold.

He had more soldiers, not an excuse to stop choosing how to use them.

Mau gave Bellion the count. The marshal checked the stored companies, the small group still on duty and the older units whose names would have fitted on a much shorter page.

Then they packed the material bundles beside the records, keeping the twelve scale sections, eighteen chitin plates and twenty-four untested core fragments in their own labels. They were cargo, not more soldiers and not a payment he had already spent.

He put the records into storage himself.

The broken pieces of wood, the little box, the original shirt. None of them improved his speed or gave the daggers another edge. He checked that they were there anyway.

Beru watched him.

“All of it?” ᴮʳ

“All the things we're taking.” ᴹ

The ant looked toward the beds, the trough, the shelf which kept replacing its plain food.

“I will not miss this room.” ᴮʳ

“Me neither.” ᴹ

Igris stood at the arch.

“Then let us leave it properly.” ᴵᵍ

* * *

The next place was bright enough to hurt: an enormous field of ice catching light from a sky he did not trust.

At its centre waited a figure with a crown-shaped growth above its face.

The message appeared before it spoke.

**MONARCH PATTERN: FROST.**

**CONSTRUCTED OPPONENT.**

Mau read both lines.

The thing across the ice raised a hand, and an army began climbing out of the ground around it.

He took the daggers from storage.

“One more,” Beru said beside him. ᴮʳ

Mau looked at the place where the arch would change afterward.

“Then we run.” ᴹ
