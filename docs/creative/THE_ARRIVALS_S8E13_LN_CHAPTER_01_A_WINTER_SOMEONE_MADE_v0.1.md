*When: the first rank reaches the arch, Bellion lets it pass the place it wants.*

The soldiers on Mau's left gave ground.

The ice creatures followed, fast enough that their second rank began crossing the place the first had just occupied. Fang struck the floor under the overlap. The broken surface took their footing away while Igris cut the lifted joints.

Mau went above the line.

The crowned figure looked up.

Cold gathered around the place he was going to land.

He stopped his own flight short, used the grip to change the angle and watched a forest of points rise through the empty air beneath his boots.

“It follows the finish,” he called. ᴹ

Bellion changed the advance.

Nobody finished where the first step had promised.

* * *

The figure did not care whether its army survived.

It shattered the nearest ranks to send their bodies across the distance between itself and Mau. Infinity held the first spray away. Behind it, larger shapes began forming at the edges of the field, moving toward the refuge he had refused to leave unguarded.

He could stand there protecting himself while the room took the people doing his work.

He released Infinity and put Blue across the flank instead.

The ice army bent sideways. Beru went through the gap with a company at his back, ending one larger shape before it completed the turn toward the arch.

Mau saw the crowned figure's hand close.

The ice beneath his next step broke upward.

He folded the first line away with the sky. The second found his forearm during the change.

The wound was shallow until the room made it five times more.

Blood struck the ice.

* * *

He kept the arm close and moved.

The small, ugly discipline of the first room held. Find one part to finish. Stop letting the whole injury ask the same question at once.

Beru reached him, set the healing where Mau directed and withdrew before the next strike came through both their positions.

There would be time to do it properly if they made time exist.

Mau changed to Shrine, cut a line in the ice army and did not follow it.

The crowned figure watched the false advance.

Kamish struck the other side.

* * *

The shadow dragon's breath tore a trench across the field.

The room protected the bodies in it. It did not give the floor beneath every body the same resistance. Ice collapsed, separating the crowned figure from part of its army and forcing it to make a new route before it could reach him.

Mau sent Mahoraga to the trench's edge, giving it the attacks already seen rather than asking the wheel to become an answer to winter itself. Agito guarded the returning company while the adaptation began.

Mau took Kaisel's rising line for a few seconds, used the dragon's speed to make the figure follow a longer approach and left the turn before it was complete.

His own flight carried the last distance.

Red struck the shoulder beneath the crown.

The figure slid backward, still intact where the attack should have broken it.

Igris was waiting in the direction of the slide.

His sword kept the arm from closing. Mau landed inside its reach with the dagger already going toward the same damaged place.

* * *

It was not a clean duel.

Bellion kept changing the ground fight so Mau could remain close without leaving the whole army behind him exposed. Fang broke the formations trying to return across the trench. Beru moved between the wounded places in Mau's body and the wounded places in the enemy line, never pretending he could do both from the same position.

Mau used the dagger, released it for the grip, called it back and cut again.

The false monarch learned the first rhythm.

He gave it another.

It caught the third with a blade of ice which opened his side beneath the ribs.

He almost went down.

Igris put his body into the return strike.

The knight's form broke.

Mau felt the cost of rebuilding him and paid it before anger could turn into an order he had no time to make useful.

Then he looked at the figure's hand instead of the wound it had given him.

It had opened to make the blade.

For that instant, the crown's protection was reaching away from the place which supplied it.

* * *

“Bellion. One step.” ᴹ

The marshal understood.

His segmented blade crossed the figure's retreat, not trying to kill the whole protected shape. It denied the single step which would have put the hand beyond Mau's reach.

Mau took the wrist with the grip.

He let Shrine go.

The copied route changed to Limitless with a precision that would have failed him a year before. Blue gathered close, taking the open hand across the figure's own damaged shoulder. He released the pull, put Red through the meeting point and let the two pressures break the structure from opposite sides.

The crown split.

Mau went through the opening with the dagger in his good hand.

This time the wound reached what the room had been making them work so hard to touch.

The figure fell.

* * *

For a moment he heard only his own breathing.

Then Bellion's orders. Beru coming toward him. The sound of the ice army losing the shape which had kept it together.

Mau put one knee down before the field could take the decision from him.

The reward began forming at the edge of his sight.

Beyond the body, the arch was changing.

He had seen the first movement too many times to mistake it now.

“Not yet,” he told Beru. “The opening.” ᴹ

The ant looked.

Then he put his hand against the worst of the bleeding and helped Mau stand.
