*When: the reward opens, Mau reads it while the room is still deciding where to put the next floor.*

Two hundred.

Thirty points. The last fifteen he had kept, fifteen more from the clear.

He put them where the body still had room to receive them: five Strength, five Dexterity, three Vitality, seven Perception, ten Intelligence.

Zero remaining.

The new capacity arrived beneath the pain. It did not turn the pain off.

Mau closed the display and looked at the fallen figure.

“Arise.” ᴹ

* * *

The shadow did not come freely.

He felt the room trying to take its pattern back, the cost of holding a defeated construct long enough that Noise could keep the usable shape apart from the machinery which had made it.

Mau gave the command again with a steadiness he had once needed Ciel to help him find.

Not that first enemy. Not the original being whose name the room had borrowed.

This one, here, defeated and beginning to answer.

The shadow rose.

The crown had become a dark ridge above a face which lowered before him. Cold gathered close to the form instead of reaching across the whole field.

“Rime,” Mau said. “We are leaving.” ᴹ

He stored it before it could offer a reply.

Six thousand six hundred and fifteen registered. One new shape beyond the five thousand six hundred ordinary soldiers he had gathered along the way.

He closed the count. They still had to get out.

* * *

Beru's hand moved toward Mau's side.

“I can finish—” ᴮʳ

The arch shifted.

Igris called the beginning.

“Now.” ᴵᵍ

Mau caught Beru's wrist.

“When we're out. If we stop here, we lose it.” ᴹ

The ant looked once at the separating outlines and understood.

Bellion had already begun recalling the companies. The dragons folded into shadow in sequence, taking their great bodies out of the path before the gap could make them an obstruction. Mau dismissed Agito and the wheel-bearer through their own routes, felt the deer return and took the last of the infantry in.

Igris stayed until he knew the timing had reached the point they had counted.

Then Mau recalled him too.

The stored companies answered the final check. The labelled material bundles and the little box were still in inventory. Nothing they had chosen to take was waiting on the field.

Beru was the last.

“I'm with you,” Mau told him. ᴹ

The ant bowed once and vanished into the shadow beneath his feet.

* * *

Mau ran.

There was no position beyond the gap he could borrow. No soldier waiting at an address the room would agree to recognise. He had to reach the unfinished place before it stopped being unfinished.

His feet found the ice, left it, found a strip of stone which was already becoming something else.

He caught the edge with the grip and drove his body through the part it bought him.

The space tried to finish around his shoulder.

He turned with the movement instead of fighting the whole room. The new strength held the narrow line. His injured arm failed halfway through and the other took its work before he could decide whether he had enough left to give.

There was a place beyond it.

Not another battlefield. The old return branch the changing chamber had not yet sealed, a direction he had carried in memory for longer than the room wanted him to remember it.

Mau put everything into the last part of the step.

* * *

The air changed.

Warmth. Cloth. The familiar smell of soap beneath something Shoko kept near the bed.

His foot struck a floor without ice on it.

He stumbled, caught himself against a solid edge and broke a piece from it before he knew how much force he had used.

Voices stopped.

Mau lifted his head.

Someone was standing in front of him with colour in her face.
