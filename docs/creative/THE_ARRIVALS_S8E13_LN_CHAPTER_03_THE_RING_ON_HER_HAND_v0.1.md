*When: Yuka says his name, Mau puts the daggers between himself and the room.*

He did not remember calling them.

He saw the blades. Saw Yuta stop with both hands visible, Maki turn without coming closer, Rimuru halfway out of a chair beside the bed.

Yuka was still in front of him.

He looked at the door behind her. The scrape on the floor where the bed had moved. The clean cloth Shoko held, stopped halfway between the table and her hand.

“Are you real?” he asked. ᴹ

His voice sounded wrong.

“Did I get back?” ᴹ

* * *

Beru rose at his side.

The ant saw the room, took one quick account of the people in it and put his hand back against the place he had been holding before the run.

Mau felt the repair answer differently.

The room's pressure was gone.

He did not know what to do with that fact before Yuka lifted her left hand.

The ring was where he had put it.

No great light. No voice promising the metal could not lie to him. Just her hand, shaking a little, the shallow turning line catching the ordinary brightness from the window.

“You came back,” she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at her face.

Colour. The same small crease between her brows when she was trying to decide whether she could touch him without hurting him.

The blades went back into storage.

“Yuka.” ᴹ

She crossed the last step.

* * *

He did not manage to hold her properly.

His hand found her sleeve, then stopped because there was blood on it and a strength in the grip he had not yet translated out of the place he had used it.

She put her arms around him instead.

“You're here,” she said against his shoulder. ʸᵏ

Then he heard her.

*Mau.*

He stopped breathing for the wrong reason this time.

The voice was so familiar that for a moment his mind tried to put it into one of the old attempts. The bed. The closed arch. One try before sleeping, then stop, because silence did not become an answer if you hurt yourself hard enough asking.

*I am here.*

He closed his eyes.

“You didn't leave.” ᴹ

*The route was closed. I could not reach you.*

“You didn't leave. You didn't—” ᴹ

They sat him down with Yuka beside him.

Mau gave Ciel permission to read the present damage, the repair he was maintaining and the part Beru was holding. He did not send a year of memories through the connection because he had finally found someone who could receive them.

The familiar attention settled against his own.

He cried harder for a little while.

Shoko waited until he could answer where it hurt. Then she asked which injuries were new, which movements he could make and whether the ant could stop briefly while she checked the place beneath his hand.
