*When: the water reaches his knees, Mau stops trying to explain anything.*

The bathroom door was closed. Yuka was on the other side of it, near enough that he could hear her ask Shoko where the clean clothes should go.

His arm had been wrapped where the new skin was still tender. The worst of the side wound was closed. Shoko had made him sit through the last inspection before agreeing to the bath, and she had been quite clear that being able to stand was not permission to disappear upstairs with a promise to report later.

He had agreed to all of it.

Beru had finished the last narrow repair. Mau thanked him before letting him return to shadow, and the ant went only after asking twice whether he was certain.

Now there was water.

Ordinary water, arriving from something the people he loved had built and maintained while he was learning to hate a trough in a room that had no morning.

He put his hand beneath it.

The first red cloud moved away from his fingers.

* * *

It took longer than he expected.

There was dried blood beneath the cuff, along the seam at his side, in a small fold of skin near the wrist which he could not remember hurting. The last few rooms had offered no time to deal with any of it. He cleaned what he could reach, stopped when the movement pulled and accepted the clean cloth passed through the partly opened door without pretending the interruption was an embarrassment.

“All right?” Yuka asked from outside. ʸᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“A real yes?” ʸᵏ

He looked at the water.

“A tired yes.” ᴹ

“I'll take that one.” ʸᵏ

The door closed again.

Mau leaned back carefully.

For a while he listened to the people beyond the wall. Someone bringing another basin. Shoko asking Rimuru to stay nearby. The sound of Mai finding out which latch he had damaged on the way in and announcing that it could wait.

Nothing in the ceiling offered him another room.

He shut his eyes.

“I'm home.” ᴹ

*Yes,* Ciel answered.

He let the breath out slowly enough that it did not become another sob.

* * *

When he stood, he did it as if the floor were made of something much more delicate than it was.

He knew how to walk. He had walked through more of that place than he cared to count. What he had not done was spend the last allocation, tear through its unfinished edge and find an ordinary handle waiting for the same hands which had been driving everything into protected stone.

The body did not shrink when he closed the window.

He had to decide how much of it each small movement needed.

So he went slowly.

Ridiculously slowly, he thought while putting on the clean shirt. Then one finger caught and he remembered there were still old things his new strength had not made simple.

He stopped pulling and worked the sleeve free.

Yuka knocked once before opening.

She found him standing beside the folded towel, looking as though he had been asked to perform an unfamiliar ceremony with both feet.

“You don't have to look so offended,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I used to be very good at sleeves.” ᴹ

She smiled.

He had missed the small change in her face more than any story he could tell would make clear.

* * *

He took a step toward her.

Then stopped.

Yuka waited.

His hands were already lifting, the beginning of the movement he had made beside the stream, in their room, in a hundred ordinary moments which had not required him to calculate whether he could hold the person he wanted to hold.

He looked down at them.

“I want to,” he said. ᴹ

She came a little closer.

“What?” ʸᵏ

“Hug you.” ᴹ

Her expression softened, then sharpened with the effort to understand why he had stopped.

He did not make her guess.

“I'm afraid I'll get it wrong. I know you're strong. I know that. I just—give me a little time to learn this without making you the thing I practise on.” ᴹ

Yuka looked at his open hands.

Then she stepped between them and put her arms around his waist, above the place Shoko had told her to leave alone.

“Then I'll hold you.” ʸᵏ

* * *

He bent toward her without tightening his arms.

She did the work of the embrace, drawing him close enough that he could rest his forehead against her shoulder. There was no test in it. Nothing he had to perform correctly before she would agree he had come home.

After a while one of his hands settled against the back of her shirt.

Very lightly.

She turned her face toward his cheek.

“There.” ʸᵏ

He nodded without lifting his head.

“What do you want to do?” she asked. ʸᵏ

It took him a moment to find an answer which did not begin with a report.

“Rest. With you. All day, if I can. I don't want to think about the next thing yet.” ᴹ

“I can arrange that.” ʸᵏ

He breathed against her shoulder.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

She leaned back enough to look at him.

“You concentrate on learning how to hug me again.” ʸᵏ

“I will.” ᴹ

“Quickly. Your fiancée isn't very patient.” ʸᵏ

He laughed, softly enough that the new wounds did not object.

She kissed him before he could decide whether there was a proper answer.

* * *

They went back to the room Shoko had prepared for him.

Not upstairs yet. Not an argument about which bed had been damaged or whether he could prove the stairs were within his new capacity.

Yuka told him where to sit, sat beside him and took his hand after he had placed it palm-up between them.

He watched the ring while she settled their fingers together.

The room was grey beyond her sleeve.

He had never been so grateful for the familiar limits of what he could see.
