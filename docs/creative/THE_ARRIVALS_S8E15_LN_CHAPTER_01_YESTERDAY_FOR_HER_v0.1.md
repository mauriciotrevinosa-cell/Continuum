“The bird?” ʸᵏ

He nodded.

She held the thin piece of wood carefully. The writing was cramped in places, easier in others, as if the hand which made it had needed to learn how much of itself to use between one line and the next.

“That was yesterday,” she said. ʸᵏ

He looked at the little record.

For her it was.

Yuka set it down, shifted closer and put her head against his shoulder without making him answer for the difference.

“Tell me the version you kept.” ʸᵏ

* * *

He told her about remembering the wrong part first.

Her laugh before the words. The cup before the ring. How he had been annoyed with himself for not finding the line immediately, then realised he was trying to make an ordinary afternoon pass an examination it had never agreed to take.

Yuka listened until he reached the joke.

“I stand by it,” she said. ʸᵏ

“The accusation?” ᴹ

“You abandoned me in a diplomatic incident with a bird.” ʸᵏ

He began laughing.

This time he did not have to stop because something newly repaired objected.

She noticed. Her smile changed, just a little.

“Can I keep this one near the map?” ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“The rest can stay shut until you want them.” ʸᵏ

He put his hand over hers. Carefully, then with a little more confidence when Ciel's quiet check agreed with what he could already feel.

The body did not have a small setting. He was learning what a small movement meant inside it.

* * *

Shoko made him demonstrate a cup before she listened to his argument about the stairs.

Fill it. Put it down. Lift it again without the handle becoming a piece separate from the cup.

He did all three and tried not to look pleased enough to lose the next part of the negotiation.

“Again after you've eaten,” she said. ˢʰ

He looked at her.

“You manage a thing once and immediately assume you can do it tired,” she explained. ˢʰ

Gojo, at the door, became very interested in the corridor.

Shoko did not look away from Mau.

“I know more than one person who does it.” ˢʰ

Mau accepted the bowl Yuka handed him.

“She's talking about you too,” he told Gojo. ᴹ

“A very fragile patient,” Gojo said. ᴳ

Shoko laughed before she could stop herself.

The sound made the room easier to sit in.

* * *

The next day he went into the courtyard.

Not to test a strike. Not to look for the next opening. Ori wanted her mountain stone back, and he had agreed that feeling well enough to carry it outside counted as feeling better.

She took it from his palm, examined it and put it in its old place on the low wall.

“It worked,” she announced. ᴼ

Mau looked at Frieren.

The elf was choosing with great care not to improve the explanation.

“It helped,” he said. ᴹ

Ori nodded, satisfied with the correction because she did not seem to hear one.

Milim arrived a moment later to ask whether he could play.

“Something sitting down,” Frieren said. ᶠ

Milim looked at the board.

“I can make it more dangerous.” ᴹⁱ

“Something sitting down,” Frieren repeated. ᶠ

Mau sat before the game acquired another army.

* * *

Bocchi came with a small question in the afternoon.

Not a concert arranged around his return. She wanted to know whether he remembered which part he had asked them to play twice.

He did.

He hummed it badly enough that she looked relieved to be able to correct him.

Yuka came back from helping Maki and found them working through the same few notes, Mau stopping whenever he lost the shape and Bocchi beginning again without making it a test.

She sat with them for the last attempt.

Afterward Bocchi stayed to drink something instead of vanishing as soon as her useful question had been answered.

Mau asked what else they had been trying.

She began telling him.

* * *

By the third evening, Shoko allowed their own room.

Mau took the stairs slowly because he had agreed to, not because someone had made him prove obedience before he could see his bed.

Yuka carried the little box of records. He carried the original shirt, folded carefully enough that she asked whether it was a priceless garment.

“No,” he said. “A very irritating one.” ᴹ

She put the bird memory beside Our Year.

Then she looked at him standing in the doorway.

“Are you coming in?” ʸᵏ

He smiled.

“Yes.” ᴹ

That night he put his arm around her without stopping halfway.
