“From there,” she said, pointing. ʸᵏ

He looked at the place beside Maki.

“You could make that sound less threatening.” ᴹ

“You could stop looking as if I'm asking permission to leave the house.” ʸᵏ

He thought about it.

Then he nodded and went where she had asked.

* * *

They had chosen a cleared stretch inside the normal dungeon.

Room to move, no civilians to protect, the ordinary route back kept open. Shoko had cleared Mau for controlled work and observation, with an instruction about not inventing anything new inside his own head that made Gojo look away again.

This work was Yuka's.

Nine waited ahead of her. Eight familiar families belonged to the shadow at her feet; the next would have to be earned by the person calling it.

She checked the agreed boundary and looked toward Yuta.

He nodded once.

The tiger came out low enough that its first movement was almost entirely claws.

* * *

Yuka moved back before it landed.

The dogs took opposite sides. One met the turn, the other denied the easy line toward her ankle. The tiger did not chase either; it struck through the space they left between them and forced Yuka to change the retreat she had planned.

Mau leaned forward.

Maki's hand touched his sleeve.

He stayed where he was.

Yuka saw the second pounce in time. Rabbits filled its landing, breaking the creature's view of where her body had gone. Nue rose through the confusion above it while a toad's tongue caught a strip of loose cloth at her wrist and pulled her the last step clear.

The tiger turned toward the new sound.

She put water beneath its feet.

* * *

It did not fall as completely as she wanted.

One claw found stone. The body twisted around it, and the next strike came from a lower angle than the first. Yuka's sleeve tore. She moved behind the elephant's bulk, dismissed it before the tiger could make its whole body an obstruction and called the ox along the lane she had been clearing since the first retreat.

Mau saw the line then.

Not an escape she kept losing. A direction she had been making the tiger accept every time it thought it was pushing her back.

The ox came through.

The impact caught the tiger broadside. Nue's strike followed before it could turn the recovery into another leap.

Yuka did not relax.

She called the dogs in, held the space with the serpent and made the last exchange from somewhere the wounded creature had not been allowed to reach.

When it finally broke apart, her hands were shaking.

She looked at the place it had occupied until the ritual's answer settled.

Then she called it again.

* * *

This time the tiger turned its head toward her and waited.

Yuka stood breathing hard.

Mau did not say anything until she looked at him.

“Now you can come over,” she said. ʸᵏ

He did, smiling so openly that she began smiling before he reached her.

“You moved it,” he said. ᴹ

“I tried.” ʸᵏ

“You kept making the place it wanted to put you become the place you wanted it to stand.” ᴹ

She looked at him for a moment.

“That sounds better when you say it.” ʸᵏ

“It looked better when you did it.” ᴹ

Maki examined the torn sleeve and the shallow mark beneath it. Yuta waited until she had decided it needed only ordinary care before congratulating his granddaughter.

Yuka leaned into Mau's side and looked at the new shape beside her feet.

“One more,” she said. ʸᵏ

He knew which one she meant.

For now he let her have the one she had just won.
