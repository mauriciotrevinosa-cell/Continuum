“Another one?” ʸᵏ

“Not another family.” ᴹ

She waited.

He told her about Agito.

Not the version which required her to destroy the shapes she had spent so long learning to use. The independent construction Noise had made in the other line, the one Mau still carried beside the native technique rather than in place of its pieces.

“You have it,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I do.” ᴹ

“And I can?” ʸᵏ

“We can see whether the same route will hold for you. Then you still have to make it yours.” ᴹ

Yuka looked down at her shadow.

“Show me the route.” ʸᵏ

* * *

They worked with Ciel in the part of the connection Yuka had already agreed to share.

No whole archive dropped into her head. Mau showed the useful old pattern and the way it sat apart from his native summons. Noise followed it, holding the construction steady while they checked that none of her existing families was being spent to make room.

Yuka called the tiger afterward.

Then Nue. The deer. The serpent.

Each answered separately.

Only then did they move to the cleared ground.

* * *

Agito made the first exchange expensive.

Yuka opened a wound and watched it begin to close before she had finished repositioning. She changed the attack, tried the next angle and learned that a body which repaired could still be moved into a place where repairing did not solve its whole problem.

Water took the footing away.

The toad denied the first recovery. Nue forced the head up. The tiger struck where she had made it turn, and Yuka brought the next force down before the shape could gather the work into one completed repair.

It took more than one attempt.

Mau watched her lose an opening, keep the piece of the plan which remained useful and refuse to spend the rest of the fight chasing the exact success she had imagined before starting.

When the constructed form finally yielded, she sat down on the ground instead of trying to look impressive for the people watching.

He brought her water.

She drank, rested and made the new call only when she had enough left to know what she was checking.

Agito stood beside her.

Her tiger remained available.

That was the part she checked twice.

* * *

At home, she waited until everyone who would object was close enough to hear.

“I want to try Mahoraga.” ʸᵏ

Maki put her cup down.

“Not alone.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looked at Mau.

He did not give her the easy agreement she had hoped to find there.

“I want to begin it myself,” she said. ʸᵏ

The silence which followed made the request feel smaller than she had meant it to be.

Mau sat beside her rather than answering from the other side of the table.

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then—” ʸᵏ

“If I think you need to go into the shadow, you go. I don't care whether you've only just called it or you've been fighting for twenty minutes. If I have to take the front, I take it.” ᴹ

She watched his face.

“You won't do it because you're nervous?” ʸᵏ

“I'll be nervous. I'll do it because I see something you haven't had time to answer.” ᴹ

Yuta leaned forward.

“We check the shared protocol first. No one wins anything by pretending the help doesn't count until after she needs it.” ʸᵗ

Yuka nodded slowly.

* * *

Later she asked Mau how the other version had ended.

He told her plainly.

Kamish finishing hers. Then his. Two wheels, two different invocations, the dragon's answer before the adaptation could make it useless.

“So you didn't do the last part?” she asked. ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

“I was involved.” ᴹ

“I'll ask Kamish.” ʸᵏ

He began laughing.

She let herself smile, then took his hand.

“If you tell me to move, I will.” ʸᵏ

He held her hand a little more firmly.

“Then we'll make a plan.” ᴹ
