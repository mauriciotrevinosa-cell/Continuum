*When: the wheel appears, Mau sees Yuka's hand tighten before the blade moves.*

She had chosen her position.

She had checked the route behind her, the reach of the first cover, the place from which she could bring her own shadows into the opening.

The cut did not use the opening she expected.

Mau saw the shoulder change and the line become a place she would still occupy when the blade arrived.

“Shadow. Now.” ᴹ

She went.

He took the space she had left before the edge finished crossing it.

* * *

He did not meet the first blow head-on.

The blade passed beside his face as he turned inside the reach. His hand struck the wrist, his other drove against the chest, and the thing which had forced Yuka to abandon her first answer went back a step under his.

The wheel turned.

Mau felt the small, savage pleasure of a fight whose boundaries they had chosen before entering it.

There was no one behind the ceiling deciding how much damage he was allowed to keep. No voice calling him somebody else's king.

There was an opponent which would learn what he gave it.

He gave it one thing.

* * *

Force through his body, placed and reinforced.

No Infinity to become a second problem. No sequence of different techniques presented for the wheel to learn all at once. He kept the blows in the same family, changing the angle, changing the timing, making the body he had spent a year building answer a creature which refused to remain behind the first correction.

The next punch moved it less.

The next return reached him sooner.

He ducked, stepped across the line of the blade and caught a cut along the outside of his arm because he had enjoyed the exchange long enough to stay where the next answer could find him.

He repaired the small injury and moved.

Yuka was safe in the route they had checked. That fact was a task he had completed, not a reason to stop remembering why he was fighting.

“Ready?” he asked without turning. ᴹ

Kamish's answer arrived through the connection.

Ready.

* * *

At the boundary, Yuta watched the space where Yuka had withdrawn.

Maki watched Mau's feet.

“He's enjoying it,” she said. ᴹᵃ

Gojo's smile had become entirely unhelpful.

“Yes.” ᴳ

“That's not a compliment.” ᴹᵃ

“I know.” ᴳ

The wheel turned again.

Below them, Mau's next strike did not buy the same retreat.

He saw it, grinned once and let the creature follow the opening it thought he had left by mistake.

* * *

The last exchange belonged to the plan.

Mau drove it toward the line Kamish had kept clear. The blade came in, faster now, the body's answer to the force he had shown it becoming more confident with each repetition.

He gave it one final reason to keep following that answer.

Then he left the line.

Kamish breathed.

The new phenomenon filled the space the wheel had not been given time to solve. Mau kept moving until he was clear of the whole reach, not trusting the first disappearance to mean every part had been caught.

When the light fell away, nothing of the hostile form remained standing.

The wheel was gone.

Mau waited until the ritual had finished answering the person who had begun it.

Then Yuka came out of her shadow.

* * *

She stood looking at the empty ground.

Mau did not tell her it was done until she had made the next call herself.

The wheel appeared above her own shape.

Mahoraga answered her.

For a few seconds she simply stood there, feeling the difference between having wanted a thing and having it recognise her.

Then she dismissed it, turned and walked directly past Mau.

He looked after her.

She stopped in front of Kamish.

“Thank you.” ʸᵏ

The dragon lowered his head with magnificent satisfaction.

“An excellent choice of conclusion.” ᴷᵃ

Yuka turned toward Mau.

“Three,” she said. ʸᵏ

He began to smile.

“I was involved.” ᴹ

“Kamish has finished him three times. I see a pattern.” ʸᵏ

She stuck her tongue out at him before he could answer.

Gojo laughed loudly enough that Maki did not need to turn to know whose fault the sound was.
