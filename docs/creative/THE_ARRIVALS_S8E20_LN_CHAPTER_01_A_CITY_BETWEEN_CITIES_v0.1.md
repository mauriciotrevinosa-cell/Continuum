They had spent enough time here that morning to know he was not talking about another night.

Veldora's smile became smaller. Hinata sat back, watching him with the attention she gave a decision she intended to take seriously. Chloe's hands rested around her cup.

Mau sat beside Yuka, close enough to be company and far enough not to make the answer look like one he had helped prepare.

“I love our people,” Rimuru said. “That hasn't stopped.” ᴿ

Shion looked down at the reports.

“Then come home.” ˢʰⁱ

The quiet after it belonged to both of them.

“This is home too,” he answered. ᴿ

He did not say it quickly. He gave the words enough time to hurt where they were going to hurt, instead of wrapping them in a joke about who had stolen his room.

“I'm happy here. I have a life I didn't know I could have. I don't want to turn that into something I apologise for every time I remember the people I still love somewhere else.” ᴿ

Shion looked up.

“You don't want to lead us?” ˢʰⁱ

“Not in the old way. Not by going back and pretending every decision still has to begin at my door.” ᴿ

Veldora moved his cup a little.

“They have been making decisions,” he said. “Some rather good ones.” ⱽ

Rimuru nodded.

“I want to help them keep doing that. I want to see the city. I want to know what this world is doing to it. But I don't want to promise I'm moving back because it is the easiest answer for this morning.” ᴿ

Nobody told him the answer was easy to hear.

Mau was glad of that.

The next message arrived with Ramiris.

Beretta carried the practical part of the luggage. She carried the explanation, which was considerable.

The first party's position had reached the people still making sense of the displaced city. Roads had been checked, a route confirmed, and the people who had been told to wait had decided waiting was no longer the most useful activity available to them.

Rimuru looked at the small figure hovering in front of him.

“You could have sent a note.” ᴿ

“I did. Beretta has it.” ᴿᵃᵐ

Mau turned away before Rimuru could catch him laughing.

* * *

They introduced her after she had finished objecting to the things she had not been invited to object to sooner.

Ramiris examined the household with immediate interest. The tree. The Fort. The number of people who appeared to have an opinion about where chairs belonged. She asked a question about space and received three different answers before Rimuru intervened.

“We aren't rebuilding it this afternoon.” ᴿ

“I hadn't said this afternoon.” ᴿᵃᵐ

He looked back with the expression of someone who had begun to understand how a place could become much bigger without moving any walls yet.

* * *

The invitation came over food.

Not a demand for a king to inspect his subjects. A chance for the people who had kept missing each other to see what the other half of Rimuru's life actually looked like.

“A festival,” someone suggested.

The word acquired listeners.

Rimuru looked toward Mau and Yuka.

“Come with me. Stay close until we've got everyone through the introductions. I'd like them to know you when no one is trying to stop anyone else.” ᴿ

Mau nodded.

“We'd like that.” ᴹ

Yuka took his hand under the table.

Rimuru looked at the route on the map, then at the people already beginning to make the visit sound like a celebration.

For the first time, Tempest seemed capable of becoming a city between other cities instead of the single place he would have to choose at the expense of everything else.
