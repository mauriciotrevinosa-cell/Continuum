The question had begun over breakfast. Rimuru wanted to know who would come, what they would need when they arrived and which of the things he remembered offering visitors could still be offered without somebody having to invent them again. He had brought one sheet of paper.

Fern had given him three more.

Now the bread was disappearing and the sheets were full of matters he would not have thought to ask about when a festival had still been a word he could say over a map.

Maomao wanted somewhere to leave things she did not intend children to drink. Marin asked whether they were expected at a formal reception. Shoko wanted to know who had been looking after the people in Tempest who still needed a doctor.

Rimuru answered what he could.

When he reached the last question, he turned to Shion.

“Do we have that report?” ᴿ

She already had a page halfway out of the folder. Then she stopped, looked at it and set it between them rather than standing to deliver it.

“This is what we knew when we left. It will need updating.” ˢʰⁱ

“Thank you.” ᴿ

She nodded and sat back down.

Mau watched Rimuru make a small mark beside the answer. There were people working there. Names on Shion's page, people who had kept waking up and deciding what to do while the friends who had come looking for him were here.

He thought he understood why Rimuru kept touching the edge of that particular sheet.

* * *

They carried the discussion into the courtyard after breakfast because Rem needed the table.

For a few minutes it became a procession of cups, maps and people remembering that they had left a question inside. Mau took the bench with Yuka. Rimuru stood in the shade, looking from the old house to the tree as though he wanted to bring both with him for the introductions.

“There will be places to eat,” he said. “Shops. Baths. Proper beds. You won't have to camp outside because you came with me.” ᴿ

“I rather hoped that was included,” Mai said. ᴹᵃⁱ

“It is.” ᴿ

“And we can go and see things without somebody deciding the visit needs an escort of twenty people?” Anko asked. ᴬⁿ

Rimuru opened his mouth, considered the people most likely to arrange that escort and wrote something down.

“I'll speak to them.” ᴿ

Yuka leaned forward.

“What do you want to show us first?” ʸᵏ

The question changed his expression.

He began with a street. Not a room in a palace, or a place a ruler was meant to stand while everyone else admired it. A stretch with shops where visitors could get lost for a morning and still emerge fed. He described the food with more certainty than the map, corrected himself about a turn and started explaining what lay beyond it.

Mau watched his hand moving above the page.

“You miss it,” he said. ᴹ

Rimuru looked at him.

“Parts of it. People. Quite a lot of the food.” ᴿ

“Then show us.” ʸᵏ

He set his pencil down.

“There is something you should know before we go.” ᴿ
