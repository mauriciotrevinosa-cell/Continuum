*When: DIO asks whether the festival has chairs, Mau thinks she is asking about Tempest.*

“I assume so.” ᴹ

She gave him a look.

He was standing in the little room they used when she wanted to come out without finding an audience in the passage. Yuka had gone to return a borrowed bowl. Rimuru leaned against the wall beside the open way, listening with the expression of someone who knew he was about to enjoy the mistake.

“Not those chairs,” DIO said. ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau looked through the entrance to the furnished space beyond it.

“Oh.” ᴹ

“I was hoping to be included in some of it.” ᴰⁱᵒ

“You are.” ᴹ

“Then I would like to know where I will sit.” ᴰⁱᵒ

Rimuru made a small sound against his hand.

Mau turned toward him.

“You could help.” ᴹ

“I'm waiting for you to offer her a bench in the main square.” ᴿ

DIO smiled, then sat down before the effort of standing became something she had to explain.

* * *

They went through the journey first.

Mau would be outside the protected grounds. The refuge would still be a space with its own boundary and the way out they had tested, but it would not make the city into an extension of the house's protection. Returning from her room meant returning beside him. She could not simply open the door, decide she disliked the view and choose a safer address.

“I remember,” she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

“I know. We're going somewhere new.” ᴹ

She considered him, then nodded.

“I want the room. If I want to come out, we speak first.” ᴰⁱᵒ

“And if the route changes, I tell you.” ᴹ

Rimuru asked about the ordinary signal, the one which said someone was awake and well enough not to need a knock. Ciel checked that the return and call still meant the same thing while Mau's attention was elsewhere.

Nobody asked DIO for an answer about why the world was moving in pieces before accepting her own answer about the trip.

Ashborn listened from the chair beyond the adjoining space.

When Mau asked him, he looked toward the door.

“The quiet is sufficient.” ᴬˢ

“We might be louder than usual.” ᴹ

“Then I can close it.” ᴬˢ

Rimuru looked at Mau.

Mau nodded, trying not to smile.

“You can.” ᴹ

* * *

The question of company came after the question of leaving. Mau and Rimuru stayed on the outside of the opening, close enough to speak across the threshold without entering the space it held.

DIO looked at the table they had moved between the rooms for the visit. Two people could use it comfortably. More could use it if one of them stopped arranging a cup as though every guest would need a whole corner to themselves.

Mau moved the cup.

“Better?” ᴹ

“Much.” ᴰⁱᵒ

She looked past him without looking into the work beyond the rooms.

“Would he like to join us?” ᴰⁱᵒ

Mau felt Noise notice the question.

He did not answer for it.

For a little while the presence remained at the edge of the invitation, attentive in a way which had nothing to do with the walls or the unfamiliar structure of a guest.

Then the dark outline which Mau remembered from another doorway took a place within the space they had agreed to share.

Its edges did not settle all at once.

DIO moved a chair back.

Noise looked at it.

“If you would rather stand, you can,” she said. ᴰⁱᵒ

The outline moved closer to the table.

Mau watched a hand find the chair, stop, then try the ordinary motion of pulling it out.

The sound was very small.

He had to look down for a moment before he could speak normally again.

* * *

“We are missing someone,” DIO said. ᴰⁱᵒ

Ciel answered in the channel Mau and Rimuru already heard.

*I can observe the proceedings.*

“That isn't quite the question,” Rimuru said. ᴿ

The silence which followed was unusual enough that Mau glanced toward him.

Rimuru looked back, then spoke more gently.

“Would you like to sit with them?” ᴿ

Mau felt the answer begin with all the practical matters which would usually have come first. Keeping their hosts informed. Dividing attention. What could happen while she was watching something she had chosen instead of something she had been asked to watch.

He waited.

Ciel stopped before making any of those the answer.

*Yes.*

DIO smiled.

“Then let us see what you would find comfortable.” ᴰⁱᵒ

* * *

They did not make another body waiting to be taken outside.

Ciel used the means she already had to present something within a host's awareness. DIO helped hold that presentation to the small, shared guest space so the people sitting there could perceive it too. They tried the empty place beside the table before giving it any shape worth sitting in.

Mau remained where he was. Rimuru kept the outside door in sight. Nobody opened the closed work to find more room.

The first outline was too bright.

Ciel changed it before anyone else could decide how to tell her.

The second looked toward the chair instead of arriving already seated.

She had chosen the movement.

It made the whole thing feel different.

*There,* she said privately.

Then, within the small room, the same voice tried the ordinary distance between two people.

“There.” ᶜⁱ

Noise turned toward her.

* * *

For a while they tested very little beyond staying.

Ciel could withdraw the presentation herself. She did, returned to the familiar channel, then chose to take the chair again. DIO could stop supporting the local appearance without ending Ciel or severing either host's connection. The figure was a way to share the room, not a new person placed where she had been.

Mau asked whether it was tiring.

“Not in the way you mean,” Ciel said. ᶜⁱ

“You can still stop when you've had enough.” ᴹ

“I am aware.” ᶜⁱ

Rimuru laughed.

“You could try telling her once and believing she heard it.” ᴿ

“I am practising.” ᴹ

Ashborn moved his chair far enough from the door that no one needed to keep walking around it.

It was the only contribution he made to the test.

Nobody asked him for another.

* * *

Noise had continued watching Ciel.

Mau could recognise the attention now, different from the rapid work which went looking for a thing to reproduce.

The dark head tilted.

“Friend.” ᴺ

Ciel looked toward it.

Before she could choose an answer, DIO rested her hand beside the place Noise had found for its own.

“Yes. We are all friends.” ᴰⁱᵒ

The outline went still.

Then Mau felt something so openly delighted that he could not keep the answering smile from his face.

*You heard?*

“I heard.” ᴹ

*Multiple friends.*

Rimuru looked at him.

Mau repeated the words aloud, and DIO's smile became broader.

Noise looked around the table again, taking in each place separately.

Ciel sat very still in her chair.

Then she moved it a little closer.

* * *

When they began making actual plans, Ciel wanted to see the city introductions and some of the matches. DIO wanted food which had not been chosen because it was what happened to be within reach of the house. Noise wanted nearly everything. Ashborn declined to provide an itinerary.

“You may change your minds,” Rimuru said. “Just tell us which thing you don't want to miss.” ᴿ

“And if the plan changes, we ask,” Mau added. ᴹ

Noise looked pleased with the word *plan*.

“There is another thing,” Mau said, looking around the table. “Being friends doesn't mean somebody has to say yes because somebody else wants them to.” ᴹ

DIO inclined her head.

“Particularly if the request begins with that argument.” ᴰⁱᵒ

Ciel looked satisfied.

Noise considered it for a while.

“Ask,” it said. ᴺ

“Ask,” Mau agreed. ᴹ

Mau and Rimuru stepped back from the threshold after arranging the next time, leaving the others to choose how long they wished to stay together.

At the outside door, Rimuru caught Mau looking back.

“What?” ᴿ

Mau shook his head.

Inside, DIO was asking Ciel which chair she preferred to keep for the next visit. Noise had an opinion, apparently based on the view.

Ashborn had not closed his door.

“Nothing,” Mau said. “I'm glad we asked.” ᴹ
