## Chapter 17 — A Question for the Road

**Volume:** 44
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S8E23

Anko/Hinata share route-review task and first voluntary walk to check marker, small personal ease without instant romance label. *When: Hollis sees the bags beside the stairs, he asks whether the house has finally decided to go somewhere without finding another person on the way.*

“That isn't something we can promise,” Anko said. ᴬⁿ

He considered her.

“Fair.” ᴴᵒ

The cart stood where deliveries had learned to stop. Hollis had come with a question for the workshop and a bundle which belonged to an earlier arrangement, neither of them improved by leaving the horse wondering whether the whole household intended to put its luggage in the road.

Mau helped move the bags back to the steps.

Hollis watched him lift two, stop, set one down more carefully and adjust his grip.

“Still finding out what things weigh?” ᴴᵒ

“What they expect me to think they weigh.” ᴹ

The merchant nodded as though that was an ordinary difficulty somebody could have with a bag.

“Tempest?” ᴴᵒ

Rimuru looked up from the receiving table.

“Have you been?” ᴿ

“No. Heard enough people being certain about it to know I'd rather ask someone who has.” ᴴᵒ

Rimuru moved a cup away from the papers and offered him a place.

* * *

They began with the road.

Hollis knew the stretch which served his customers. Hinata knew the route the first party had taken after finding the city. Neither had a map which made the other's information unnecessary.

Anko brought her own notes and sat down without asking somebody to summarise all of them into a conclusion first.

Mau stood beside Yuka and watched them compare a bend, a marker and the place a cart could no longer take the direct line.

“We went around it,” Hinata said. ᴴⁱ

“On foot?” Hollis asked. ᴴᵒ

“That section.” ᴴⁱ

He made a mark.

“Then the cart needs the other approach.” ᴴᵒ

Anko looked at his page.

“The one beside the old post?” ᴬⁿ

“If the ground hasn't changed again.” ᴴᵒ

“It hadn't yesterday.” ᴬⁿ

Hinata glanced toward her.

“You checked?” ᴴⁱ

“I like knowing what I'm advising people to stand on.” ᴬⁿ

Something at the corner of Hinata's mouth changed.

“Useful habit.” ᴴⁱ

“Frequently unpopular.” ᴬⁿ

They bent over the page together.

* * *

Rimuru explained the invitation while Hollis rested his hands around the cup.

There would be food and somewhere to stay. People from the house were going to meet the people who still lived in the city. Others might come if the messages reached them and they could make the journey. Nobody knew yet how many would be able to do that.

“So you're inviting a city to introduce itself,” Hollis said. ᴴᵒ

Rimuru smiled.

“I'd like that.” ᴿ

“And the rest?” ᴴᵒ

He looked toward the people discussing a place to fight with far too much interest for the word *visit* to cover it.

“That part is still being prepared.” ᴿ

Hollis looked at Mau.

Mau held up both hands.

“Nothing has broken.” ᴹ

“Yet,” Yuka said beside him. ʸᵏ

The merchant drank his tea.

* * *

The invitation acquired another question before he left.

There were makers who would want to see tools rather than a contest. People who had been asking Hollis whether the city had things for sale, and whether the stories about its inhabitants meant they would be welcome if they went looking.

He did not promise Rimuru those people would come.

“Some will say yes to me and decide otherwise after talking at home. Some will want to know where to leave a cart before they ask what the place is called.” ᴴᵒ

“Then ask them,” Rimuru said. “I'll send what I can confirm about the arrangements.” ᴿ

Fern looked toward Jinshi.

He had already separated a small space on his page for questions which needed an answer before departure.

“We can keep the inquiries together,” he said. “That doesn't mean everyone has agreed to trade.” ᴶⁱ

“Good,” Hollis said. “I'd hate to miss the point at which I supposedly sold somebody else's stock.” ᴴᵒ

Maomao, passing with an empty bowl, stopped just long enough to look at the drawing on Rimuru's map.

“Do they sell plants?” ᴹᵃ

He considered the question with appropriate care.

“Yes.” ᴿ

“Interesting ones?” ᴹᵃ

Jinshi's pencil paused.

“I think that depends rather heavily on what you consider interesting,” Rimuru answered. ᴿ

“Then we should find out,” she said. ᴹᵃ

She continued toward the kitchen.

Jinshi looked at the page as though he had forgotten the sentence he meant to finish.

Mau kindly refrained from helping.

* * *

The question of the house remained after the merchant had gone.

They were not shutting its doors for everybody who had ever used the road. Neither did a celebration somewhere else oblige them to leave the sleeping rooms open to whoever might arrive with an interesting story.

Mau offered ordinary soldiers for the familiar routes and Muster for the work they already understood at home. Forty would remain with the named worker if the placements passed the check he intended to make before leaving.

Anko asked which routes.

He showed her.

Fern asked what they would do if somebody came asking for help.

“Keep them at the receiving place, let us know through the arrangements we already use. If it's immediate danger, protect them and bring the problem to somebody who can answer. No new guest goes into the house because they know a name.” ᴹ

Rimuru nodded.

“I'll help check it.” ᴿ

No one suggested that the protection had moved just because its guests were going on holiday.

The rooms they were leaving remained inside it. The road did not.

* * *

Anko found Hinata still examining the corner of the route map when the others began carrying the table's contents indoors.

“The marker isn't far,” she said. ᴬⁿ

Hinata looked up.

“I was wondering whether you had drawn that turn too tightly.” ᴴⁱ

“Come and complain about the real one.” ᴬⁿ

She waited while Hinata put the map away.

They passed Mau beside the steps. He had Yuka's bag in one hand and his own in the other, which did not appear to be helping his argument that one of them was carrying more than necessary.

“Checking the marker,” Anko told him. “Back before dinner.” ᴬⁿ

“All right.” ᴹ

Hinata looked at the bag Yuka was pointing to.

“Which one is yours?” ᴴⁱ

“The smaller one,” Mau said. ᴹ

“The one he hasn't finished packing,” Yuka added. ʸᵏ

Anko laughed.

Hinata waited until they were beyond the steps to ask whether that was normal.

“Which part?” Anko replied. ᴬⁿ

“I suspect that answers the question.” ᴴⁱ

They took the path to the post, talking about the road at first.

On the way back, Hinata asked where the small case Anko carried had come from. Anko answered, then asked about the sword she had been careful not to touch at the table.

Neither question was necessary to correct a map.

They still reached the house before dinner.

* * *

Taihei found Mau trying to make the bags occupy less of the passage.

“I can take the rear on the first stretch,” he said. ᵀ

Mau looked up.

“With the luggage?” ᴹ

“And the people who decide they forgot something after we start. I've had practice.” ᵀ

From inside, Umaru objected to the implication without coming far enough out to establish whether she had heard it correctly.

Taihei smiled.

“Do you want that job?” Mau asked. ᴹ

“For the first stretch. After that I intend to be a visitor.” ᵀ

“Then we'll change over.” ᴹ

Taihei took the bag Mau offered him and set it beside the others.

Across the yard, Veldora was explaining to Rimuru how much time a message would save if it were allowed to travel properly. Rimuru listened, pointed at the papers and made him repeat which people were meant to receive them.

Mau watched the dragon look offended at being asked to remember names instead of simply arriving magnificently.

Yuka appeared at his shoulder.

“Are we ready?” ʸᵏ

“We're becoming ready.” ᴹ

She inspected the stack, took his hand and drew him toward dinner.

“Then that can wait until after we eat.” ʸᵏ
