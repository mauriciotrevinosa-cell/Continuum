“You gave them the pages?” ᴿ

“Naturally.” ⱽ

“The ones I gave you.” ᴿ

Veldora looked offended.

“I did not replace your correspondence with an epic.” ⱽ

Mau, standing beside the receiving table, made a small mistake with his expression.

The dragon turned toward him.

“There was sufficient material for one.” ⱽ

“I'm sure.” ᴹ

“I showed restraint.” ⱽ

Rimuru took the folded reply.

“Thank you.” ᴿ

Veldora accepted the words with the dignity of someone whose greatest achievement had gone largely unrecognised.

* * *

He had left after dinner the night before with the shorter copy of the arrangements. The road party still carried the fuller questions and Rimuru's personal letter; a faster messenger did not make all the people using the road disappear from it.

What Veldora brought back was enough to decide when to leave.

Rooms were being held in two neighbouring guest courtyards. The residents arranging them had asked which people needed to stay close and which would prefer somewhere quieter. They had not moved anyone out of a bed merely because the person returning had once been their ruler.

Rimuru read that part twice.

Mau waited.

“They thought of this,” the slime said at last. ᴿ

“Is it good?” Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

“Very.” ᴿ

He showed them the small plan attached to the reply.

A common place to eat. Rooms off the adjoining spaces, some facing the busier way and some turned from it. There would be enough room to talk without making every visit a reception. If the household had different needs, they could say so before the next group of guests arrived.

Yuka leaned closer to see.

“This looks like somewhere we can actually stay.” ʸᵏ

Rimuru nodded.

He had been preparing to solve the problem when he reached the city. Someone else had already begun.

* * *

The first general welcome was proposed for the morning after their expected arrival.

If the road stayed as they had checked it, leaving tomorrow would bring them in by the following evening. They could sleep, find what they had put in the wrong bag and meet people after breakfast rather than arriving in somebody else's discussion with mud on their boots.

Hinata studied the dates.

“That gives the first messengers time to return.” ᴴⁱ

“Some of them,” Veldora said. “Others are still finding out which roads lead where.” ⱽ

Rimuru set the letter down.

“Then we welcome the people who arrive. We don't pretend we can call the whole world to a table because we chose a morning.” ᴿ

Fern looked at the plan.

“And they know how many of us there are?” ᶠᵉ

“The people arranging rooms have the first count. Shion has the corrections and the questions.” ᴿ

“Good.” ᶠᵉ

She took a clean page and began making the changes they could settle here.

Mau watched Rimuru lean over to help, then stop when Jinshi answered a question before he did.

There were people making decisions at both ends of the road now.

* * *

The smaller paragraph at the bottom of the letter caused more difficulty.

Rimuru reached it, stopped and looked at Veldora.

“What did you tell them about Mau?” ᴿ

“That he was coming.” ⱽ

“And?” ᴿ

“That you wished him to meet the others.” ⱽ

Mau's stomach gave a small, unnecessary turn.

Rimuru read the paragraph aloud. It asked whether the reception required a separate place of honour for his companion, or whether he would prefer to be seated among the household.

Yuka looked at Mau.

He looked at the little drawing of tables.

“Can I have a chair beside her?” ᴹ

Rimuru nodded immediately.

“That is going in the reply.” ᴿ

“I don't need a raised place.” ᴹ

“Neither do I, but apparently this is a difficult subject.” ᴿ

Veldora folded his arms.

“It is customary to make an impression.” ⱽ

“A chair will make a wonderful impression on the backs of my legs,” Mau said. ᴹ

Yuka put her face against his shoulder before the laughter became something she had to explain to everyone in the courtyard.

Rimuru wrote the answer himself.

* * *

They checked the house arrangements before packing the last food.

Muster stood at the receiving place with the ordinary soldiers Mau had selected. The two guides had returned from the difficult turn that morning; one took its familiar place along the route while the other waited for the next instruction.

Mau walked the placements with Anko and Rimuru. He did not spread the soldiers across a map and call it a tested plan. They took the turns, repeated the signals and found one place where a watcher could not see the part of the path it had been given.

They moved that post.

At the door, Muster waited while Mau repeated what counted as a request for help and what did not count as permission to admit a new guest.

The worker acknowledged each instruction.

Forty ordinary soldiers remained assigned to the house and its known approaches. The others would be available for the journey and the work Mau already understood how to ask of them. No new commander appeared because a holiday had become complicated.

Rimuru looked at the familiar doorway when they finished.

“It feels strange leaving it.” ᴿ

Mau nodded.

“We'll be glad to come back.” ᴹ

“I intend to be insufferable about the baths first.” ᴿ

“We have been warned.” ᴹ

* * *

Rem caught him trying to help pack the food after the useful part of that job had been done.

“You can carry it tomorrow,” she said. ᴿᵉ

“That sounds suspiciously like a way to stop me helping.” ᴹ

“It is.” ᴿᵉ

He looked at the parcels.

She looked at him.

“Go and do something which still needs doing.” ᴿᵉ

He obeyed far enough to reach the doorway before turning back.

“What are you looking forward to?” ᴹ

Rem paused with a folded cloth in her hands.

For a moment she seemed ready to list what she wanted to see in a kitchen. Then she looked past him, toward the people whose voices were arriving from the yard.

“I don't know all of it yet,” she said. ᴿᵉ

“You can choose some of it when we get there.” ᴹ

She smiled.

“I think I will.” ᴿᵉ

He left her with the parcels and took none of them away before morning.

* * *

Yuka found a little time after supper to make sure he had heard the answer to his request.

She held the room plan beside the lamp.

“We can be next to the quieter courtyard.” ʸᵏ

Mau sat beside her on the bed.

“Are we?” ᴹ

“If we want. They haven't assigned us a place to be grateful for.” ʸᵏ

He looked at the mark she indicated.

“What do you think?” ᴹ

“Close enough to hear people arrive. Far enough that we can close the door.” ʸᵏ

“Then that.” ᴹ

She folded the copy and put it with their travel things.

For a while neither of them added another useful thing to the evening.

Mau touched the ordinary band on her hand, careful not to turn it while she was still looking at him.

“What?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Nothing. I like that we can decide where we're staying and the answer is both of us.” ᴹ

She turned her hand and held his.

“You have been staying with me for quite a while.” ʸᵏ

“I've noticed.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸᵏ

She leaned into him, put her head against his shoulder and stayed there until he stopped looking as though he needed to finish deciding how to enjoy it.

Downstairs, somebody remembered a bag.

Somebody else said it could wait until morning.
