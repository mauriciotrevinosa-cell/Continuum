*When: they leave, Mau has to stop looking back before the house disappears.*

It was still there between the trees. The roof, the part of the upper wall he could recognise by the repaired edge, the place where somebody had left a cloth hanging because there had been enough to remember without bringing in every piece of washing before breakfast.

Muster stood at the receiving place.

The soldier did not salute a departing king. It acknowledged the last familiar instruction and turned toward the route it had been given.

Mau lifted a hand.

Then Yuka touched his elbow.

“Coming?” ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

She had her bag in its place and the expression of someone who intended to remind him, if necessary, that they had packed for a visit. Beyond her, Rimuru was arguing with Veldora about whether a dramatic entrance could sensibly begin two days before reaching the destination.

Mau smiled.

“Yes.” ᴹ

He went with her.

* * *

The first part of the road was familiar enough that people spent it remembering small things.

A fastening. Something put in another bag. A question about who had the food which became urgent only after the person asking had decided it would be embarrassing to be hungry already.

Taihei brought the rear through the narrow part, then handed the responsibility over where they had agreed. Kaneki took the next stretch after checking with Touka, who was walking with Ichika and appeared more concerned about the child's intention to collect every unusually shaped leaf than about whether they would reach a city.

Mau took one of the ordinary loads when its owner asked.

He left the others where their owners wanted them.

That turned out to be a useful way to begin a holiday.

* * *

At the turn Hollis had marked, Hinata walked ahead with Anko.

They reached the post, compared it with the line on the map and guided the first luggage through before moving on. It took less time than the conversation about it had taken in the courtyard.

Rimuru looked pleased.

“Don't say it,” Mau told him. ᴹ

“I hadn't said anything.” ᴿ

“You have the same face I do when something works.” ᴹ

Yuka looked between them.

“That's unfortunate for both of you.” ʸᵏ

Rimuru laughed and put the map away.

Past the turn, the road opened enough that the group could stop making a single long argument out of the order in which they walked.

People found the person they had meant to speak to before breakfast. Some preferred the quiet. Frieren let Ori count the next markers until the girl decided counting had become less interesting than asking Chloe what the city smelled like.

Chloe considered the question seriously.

“Food, near the shops.” ᶜʰ

“All the time?” Ori asked. ᴼ

“Sometimes you can smell it before you know what it is.” ᶜʰ

Umaru moved closer.

Mau saw Frieren notice and look away with a small smile.

* * *

They ate beside a place the earlier travellers had used, far enough from the road to let another party pass without stepping into somebody's lunch.

There was no contest over who had brought the most practical meal. People opened the parcels they had been given, traded a little, discovered where a cup had gone and found it again after accusing precisely the wrong person.

Mau sat with his back against a pack.

Bocchi was turning a small wrapped piece of food in her hands as though choosing where to begin had become an unexpected part of eating it.

“What are you going to look for?” he asked her. ᴹ

She looked up too quickly.

“In Tempest?” ᴮᵒ

He nodded.

She glanced at the people beside them, then down.

“Maybe music.” ᴮᵒ

Kita smiled without interrupting.

“I mean, other music. Not just ours.” ᴮᵒ

“Would you show me if you find some?” ᴹ

Bocchi blinked.

He held up the hand he had been resting on his knee.

“I can still listen without these deciding whether they're interested.” ᴹ

She looked at his fingers, then back at him.

“You don't have to—” ᴮᵒ

“I want to. I'm asking whether you want company.” ᴹ

The answer took a moment.

Then she nodded.

Yuka leaned forward from his other side.

“Can I come too?” ʸᵏ

“Yes,” Bocchi said, more quickly. ᴮᵒ

Kita held out her cup for the water.

“Then one of the things we do is already decided.” ᴷⁱ

Bocchi finally unwrapped the food.

* * *

The afternoon asked more of their feet than of anyone's courage.

They changed the people carrying the awkward loads. A short climb took longer than it would have taken the strongest half of the group alone, which did not make it a failure of planning. Someone needed to stop. Someone else was glad they had said it first.

Mau stood beside the track while Ichika showed Touka the leaf she had selected after an extensive process of rejecting all the others.

Kaneki watched them with his hand resting on the strap over his shoulder.

“You're smiling,” Mau said. ᴹ

Kaneki looked toward him.

“She's been explaining that leaf for most of the last hill.” ᴷ

“A thorough selection.” ᴹ

“Apparently.” ᴷ

For a while they watched without adding anything to the conversation.

Then Kaneki shifted the strap.

“I like that this is what we have to get there for.” ᴷ

Mau understood.

“Me too.” ᴹ

* * *

By the time they reached the place selected for the night, the people who had walked it before had begun recognising individual trees.

They checked the ground before unpacking. The route guards took their positions. Nobody confused a familiar camp with the house's protection, but there was room to eat without keeping every person awake to prove they were taking the road seriously.

Mau helped carry water, then stopped when the person arranging the cups told him there was no useful place to put another full one.

He found Yuka sitting beside their things.

“Nothing left to do?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Nothing they want me to do.” ᴹ

“Excellent.” ʸᵏ

She moved enough of the folded blanket for him to sit.

He did.

At the other side of the camp, Maki was objecting to something Gojo had offered as a perfectly reasonable way to decide where to put a light. Shoko contributed one sentence which made Yuta turn away laughing.

Yuka looked pleased to have missed none of it.

Mau leaned closer.

“You could go and ask what he said.” ᴹ

“I'd rather hear what he claims he said tomorrow.” ʸᵏ

He laughed and let his shoulder rest against hers.

* * *

Rimuru found them after the food had been put away.

He came in his small form, settled in the space Yuka offered and spent a while saying nothing which required anyone to decide something.

The dark around the camp remained ordinary dark. Ciel checked in when Mau asked, then returned to the company she had chosen. The work he had promised to leave alone remained a record waiting for later.

“Are you enjoying this?” Mau asked at last. ᴹ

Rimuru turned toward him.

“I've had three questions about accommodation, one about whether a dragon counts as luggage and none about moving back permanently.” ᴿ

“A good day?” ʸᵏ

“An excellent day.” ᴿ

He leaned into the fold of blanket without pretending he had not wanted the place between them.

Mau looked toward the bags and the people beginning to settle for the night.

Tomorrow there would be a city. Introductions, questions, things he could not yet imagine correctly.

Tonight he had walked far enough to be tired, eaten with people he loved and found a place beside Yuka without needing the evening to earn another purpose.

He put his hand on the small slime beside him.

Rimuru made a contented sound.

Yuka reached over both of them and caught Mau's fingers.

“Stay a little,” she told the slime. ʸᵏ

He did.
