“You know we believe you,” Mau said. ᴹ

“I can recognise this part.” ᴿ

“You recognised the last part.” ʸᵏ

“This one is more recognisable.” ᴿ

Veldora laughed behind them.

The road had changed over the course of the morning. More people used it. A pair coming the other way stopped to speak to Hinata, then noticed who stood a little farther back and forgot the question they had begun with.

Rimuru answered the questions they found instead.

He did not give them a speech from the middle of the road. He asked whether the route ahead was clear, listened when they named a place which had become difficult and told them where the household had found good ground for the night.

They left smiling.

Mau watched his brother look after them.

“You knew them?” ᴹ

“One. The other was introduced too quickly for me to catch the name properly.” ᴿ

“I thought you always remembered.” ᴹ

“I used to have a great many people helping me look as if I did.” ᴿ

Yuka laughed.

They walked on.

* * *

The first clear view came from a rise which did not need to be dramatic to stop the conversation.

There were roofs beyond it. Streets. Signs of work where people had decided a damaged place would have to become a useful one again. The city did not fit every line on the map Rimuru had carried out of his memory.

He was quiet for longer than Mau expected.

Then he pointed.

“That street should have gone farther.” ᴿ

Mau followed the direction of his hand.

The visible line ended at a change in the ground, with traffic taking another way around it. Somebody had put markers where the old route would otherwise have invited a cart into trouble.

Yuka moved closer to Rimuru.

“They found a way through.” ʸᵏ

He nodded.

“Yes.” ᴿ

After a moment he put the map away.

“Come on. I'd rather show you what is here.” ᴿ

* * *

The people waiting for them had intended to make the arrival straightforward.

That intention lasted until the first person recognised Rimuru from farther down the road than anyone had expected.

The name travelled faster than the party.

By the time they reached the place where visitors were being directed, there were people looking up from work, coming out of doorways, stopping with something still in their hands. Some bowed. Others smiled first and appeared to remember the bow afterward.

Mau stepped a little to the side to give them room.

Rimuru caught his sleeve.

“You can stay.” ᴿ

“I'm not leaving.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴿ

He turned back as Benimaru reached them.

For a second the man appeared to be choosing between the words he had prepared and the ones which belonged to seeing him.

Rimuru saved him the trouble.

“I'm glad you're here.” ᴿ

Benimaru's expression changed.

“Welcome back.” ᴮᵉ

* * *

Introductions acquired a manageable order only after Shuna intervened.

She came through the small crowd, greeted Rimuru and looked past him at the travellers who had been walking since morning.

“The rooms are ready to be shown. If we begin here, none of you will get to sit down.” ˢʰᵘ

Rimuru looked grateful.

“Yes. Please.” ᴿ

She turned to Yuka first, offering her name rather than assuming every visitor had received a history of the city before coming.

Yuka introduced herself, then Mau.

Shuna's gaze moved briefly to their joined hands and the ordinary band on Yuka's finger. When she smiled again, the warmth belonged to both of them.

“I'm glad you came together.” ˢʰᵘ

“So are we,” Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Shion appeared beside the luggage before Mau could offer to move it all himself.

She had been back long enough to change the order of the instructions and not long enough to stop looking toward Rimuru whenever someone else began speaking to him.

Yuka saw her and raised a hand.

Shion smiled before remembering whatever she had intended to explain.

“We found you a window.” ˢʰⁱ

* * *

They reached the first street with their arrival still following them in pieces.

Rigurd knew which groups needed which courtyard. He checked the corrections Shion had brought and asked about the people whose preferences had not fitted neatly onto a page. Rimuru began to answer, stopped and let Fern speak for the arrangement she had helped make.

Mau took his own bag and the one Yuka handed him.

Ichika had stopped beside a figure near the entrance to a shop.

It was round, unmistakable and engaged in being made into something much more dignified than the living subject appeared to appreciate.

Kaneki looked at it.

Then at Rimuru.

Rimuru shut his eyes.

Maki caught up at exactly the wrong moment.

“You did warn us.” ᴹᵏ

“It was supposed to help.” ᴿ

Mai examined the little display, leaned closer and looked pleased to discover a detail which required no comment to make the situation worse.

Mau kept walking for two steps before the laughter made that impossible.

Yuka held his sleeve and laughed with him.

Rimuru looked toward Shuna.

She had an excellent view of the next street and appeared determined to keep it.

* * *

There were other things to see.

Mau noticed them as the first amusement settled. A shopkeeper moving a tray to make room for someone shorter. A visitor asking the way with enough uncertainty to be relieved when the answer came with a hand pointing down a street. Workers resting beside a part of the road they had not finished repairing.

The unfamiliar faces did not all look like the people from the old towns. They also did not all look alike.

Nobody stopped the woman beside the shop from offering a child a better view of what she was selling.

Yuka slowed.

Mau looked toward her.

She was watching the same thing.

Rimuru followed their glance and said nothing for a little while.

Then he touched Mau's arm.

“This is one of the things I wanted you to see.” ᴿ

Mau nodded.

He was glad his brother had waited until they could see it themselves.

* * *

The workshop street announced itself by sound before anyone offered to explain it.

Wakana looked up. Marin noticed, smiled and put a hand against his elbow while Shuna explained which parts visitors could walk through without getting in somebody's way.

A man who had come to the doorway to see what the arrival was doing to his street recognised Rimuru and called to him.

Rimuru answered, then introduced Kurobe to the people who had already become interested in the things behind him.

There was no promise to make everything the house had ever wanted before dinner.

There was an invitation to come back when they had put their bags down.

Mai accepted before Mau could ask whether she wanted to.

He caught her glance and smiled.

She looked pleased to have beaten him to it.

* * *

At the turn toward the guest courtyards, Benimaru found enough room beside Mau to offer his hand.

“We didn't manage this at the road.” ᴮᵉ

Mau shifted his bag and took it.

The grip was brief, direct and did not ask him to prove anything.

“Thank you for helping arrange all this.” ᴹ

Benimaru looked toward the people following Shuna.

“You have been taking care of him.” ᴮᵉ

“He takes care of us too.” ᴹ

“I heard.” ᴮᵉ

Mau did not know which parts he had heard. For once he did not try to answer a whole imagined account before the other person had asked a question.

Ahead, Rimuru had stopped to let a smaller group of children pass before continuing. One recognised him, hesitated and received a smile which made the approach easier.

Benimaru watched the exchange.

“I'm glad he wanted to show you.” ᴮᵉ

Mau looked toward Yuka waiting at the next corner.

“So am I.” ᴹ

He went to join her.
