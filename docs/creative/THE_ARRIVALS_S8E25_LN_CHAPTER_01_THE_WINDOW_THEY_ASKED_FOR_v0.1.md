*When: Shion opens the door, Yuka goes to the window first.*

Mau set their bags down and waited until he knew where she wanted them.

The room held an ordinary bed, a table large enough for two people to argue over a map and a place to hang clothes which had not been designed by someone determined to prove that visitors could live out of a bag indefinitely. Beyond the window lay the quieter courtyard they had asked about.

Yuka opened it a little.

Voices came through from another room. A question about a towel, followed by an answer which suggested the towel had never been missing at all.

She turned back, smiling.

“This is good.” ʸᵏ

Shion looked pleased.

“They kept this side for people who wanted to stay near the others without opening straight onto the street.” ˢʰⁱ

Mau put the bags where Yuka indicated.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

Shion nodded, then looked down at the smaller fold of paper in her hand.

Yuka noticed.

“Did you ask?” ʸᵏ

The woman looked up.

* * *

She had asked Shuna what she would do if a whole afternoon of the festival did not need to be used getting somebody else to the next thing.

The answer had been longer than she expected.

“There are places she wants to go,” Shion said. “People she wants to see. Apparently some of them have been inviting her for a while.” ˢʰⁱ

Yuka sat on the edge of the bed.

“Are you going with her?” ʸᵏ

“I asked whether I could.” ˢʰⁱ

“And?” ʸᵏ

“She said I should have asked sooner.” ˢʰⁱ

The surprise was still there beneath the words.

Mau took a folded shirt out of his bag to give her somewhere else to look if she wanted it.

Shion watched him put it away, then looked back at Yuka.

“You could come for part of it. If you want.” ˢʰⁱ

Yuka smiled before she answered.

“I'd like to.” ʸᵏ

“I'll tell her.” ˢʰⁱ

She folded the little paper more carefully.

At the door she remembered to explain where the evening food would be served and which entrance would stay open after the first courtyard had settled for the night.

Then she left before those useful answers could become the whole visit.

* * *

Mau found the basin and the place for their boots. Yuka checked the room's second fastening, then took out the things she did not want crushed beneath the travel gear.

They moved around one another with the small pauses which meant they were beginning to understand a new room together.

He put his notes on the wrong end of the table.

She moved them.

He looked at the cleared space, then at her.

“I was going to leave you half.” ᴹ

“You had left me the part behind the lamp.” ʸᵏ

“An intimate working environment.” ᴹ

“Try the other end.” ʸᵏ

He did.

She put a hand on the back of his neck as she passed and kissed his cheek without making him earn the correction back.

Mau stayed where he was for a moment.

“What?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Nothing. I like your management style.” ᴹ

She laughed and went to find the clothes she had chosen because there would be a place to wear them.

* * *

The meal was in a room which had already learned to accommodate visitors with different ideas about how close a chair should be to the next one.

There were people to ask. Food came with names for the dishes rather than a challenge to recognise everything before being allowed to enjoy it. Momo stopped looking toward the kitchen only after someone made it clear she was welcome to sit and eat.

Rem took the place beside Ram. Umaru arrived with Chloe and a description of the route between the two courtyards which appeared to include an important discovery about a corner no adult had thought to name.

Rimuru listened to the discovery in his small form on a chair someone had made comfortable for him.

He seemed extremely happy with his work.

Mau tried the dish Yuka pushed toward him, then offered her the one he had begun with. They discussed what each thought it tasted like. Neither described the food as a useful source of anything.

At the far end of the table, Shoko asked Gojo whether he intended to spend the entire visit standing where he could look interesting in other people's backgrounds.

“I can sit interestingly,” he said. ᴳ

She moved the chair beside her with her foot.

He sat.

Mau caught Yuta noticing and both of them looked down before their amusement became a public contribution.

* * *

Echidna found Mau in the courtyard after dinner.

He had stopped beside the low table while Yuka went upstairs for the wrap she had decided she wanted. The air was cooling without making the evening unpleasant, and he had been looking toward the window which now belonged to them for a few nights.

“Does everyone have a seat?” Echidna asked. ᴱ

He looked at her.

“Rimuru asked me something very similar.” ᴹ

“Then he has been paying attention.” ᴱ

She took a chair. He took the other, waiting to see whether she intended to make him guess the whole conversation.

For once she offered him the beginning.

“You told me there was a second guest. Now I hear you have company at a shared table.” ᴱ

Rimuru had mentioned over supper that the rooms had acquired an occasion for more chairs. He had left the identities of their company to the people who might choose to introduce themselves.

“Sometimes.” ᴹ

“And would that table ever receive visitors?” ᴱ

He glanced toward the quiet room of his awareness without opening it.

“That's a question for the people using it.” ᴹ

Echidna smiled.

“Then will you ask them?” ᴱ

* * *

He did not answer at once.

The thought was unexpectedly pleasant. The seven at a table where Noise had learned to pull out a chair. Daphne asking which food had been worth bringing. Ciel deciding whether she wished to explain something. Ashborn hearing a room full of people whose afternoon did not depend on him being ready to rule anything.

Echidna watched his face.

“A tea party,” she said. “If they want one. He needn't speak because I am curious.” ᴱ

“You know which part of that I would ask.” ᴹ

“I do listen.” ᴱ

He smiled.

“I know.” ᴹ

There would be ordinary matters to settle. How many people the existing space could comfortably hold, which door would remain available, whether somebody wanted to remain in a room of their own instead. He had promised not to turn the festival into the construction of something nobody had agreed to use.

“I'll ask,” he said. “And we check what is already possible before anyone starts making arrangements.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴱ

She looked toward the building where the others were staying.

“I think the next pot might be more interesting there.” ᴱ

Yuka came down the steps with her wrap over one arm. Echidna greeted her, asked whether they had found what they wanted at dinner and left them the little table without treating the invitation as a bargain already accepted.

Mau waited until Yuka sat.

“Would you like to walk?” he asked. ᴹ

She looked at the courtyard, the open passage and the people beginning to find their way back to their rooms.

“A little. Then I want to go upstairs with you and stop arriving.” ʸᵏ

He stood and offered his hand.

She took it.

* * *

Their walk reached only the next courtyard and the quiet way back.

It was enough to learn which sound came from the kitchen and which window caught the last light. Enough to find the small place beside the turn where they could sit another evening if they wanted the air without another conversation.

When they returned, Yuka closed the door behind them and set her wrap where she could reach it in the morning.

Mau left the notes alone.

She saw him notice them, then choose the bed instead.

“Tomorrow?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Tomorrow will have quite enough people telling us what they'd like.” ᴹ

“Then what would you like now?” ʸᵏ

He looked at her, at the familiar colour in a room whose shapes he had only just learned.

“Come here.” ᴹ

She did.

The window stayed open a little. He could hear the voices settle below it while she fitted herself beside him, her hand finding the place on his chest she knew without needing the room to be familiar first.

For a while the city continued introducing itself outside.

They let it wait.
