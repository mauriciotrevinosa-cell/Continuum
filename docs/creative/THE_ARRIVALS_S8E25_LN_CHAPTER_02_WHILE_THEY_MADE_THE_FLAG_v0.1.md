“Come in.” ʸᵏ

Mau was trying to persuade a fold of paper to become something with two sides which agreed about their size.

He had found the blank scrap among the ordinary packing pieces and begun without deciding what he meant to make. By the time Ori reached the table, it had acquired a shape she considered worth investigating.

“What is it?” she asked. ᴼ

He looked down.

“That is becoming an excellent question.” ᴹ

Yuka leaned over his shoulder.

“It was a boat.” ʸᵏ

“It remains a possibility.” ᴹ

Ori put her hands on the edge of the table, careful not to touch the paper before he offered it.

He did.

She examined the fold with the concentration he had once watched her give a much more important stone.

“This side is wrong.” ᴼ

“I suspected that.” ᴹ

“Both of you agree,” Yuka said. “That's progress.” ʸᵏ

* * *

The Fort was making its place downstairs.

Not a building which had appeared overnight. A corner of the courtyard the hosts had said they could use, a table which could survive paper and a question about where to put the little flag so people would notice it before walking into somebody's game.

Umaru had opinions about the flag.

Ori had been helping until the argument about its size became less interesting than finding Mau.

“Mum is with Fern,” she explained. “She said she'd come here when they finished.” ᴼ

“Then we know where to be,” Mau said. ᴹ

Yuka put the empty cup farther from the paper.

“Do you want us to help with the flag?” ʸᵏ

Ori thought about it.

“They said it was nearly ready.” ᴼ

“Then we can wait.” ʸᵏ

The child looked pleased by the answer in a way which made Mau stop trying to repair the whole boat before she had shown him what she wanted to do.

* * *

Ori's correction required unfolding a part he had become rather attached to.

He let her do it.

She flattened the paper with both hands, turned it toward Yuka and explained what she thought ought to happen next. Yuka tried the fold, found the same stubborn edge Mau had found and held it while Ori pressed it into place.

Mau watched them make his possible boat into a considerably more convincing one.

“You've done this before?” he asked. ᴹ

“I watched,” Ori said. ᴼ

“An excellent technique.” ᴹ

Yuka looked at him over the paper.

He raised his uninjured eyebrow.

“I used my eyes. Nothing else.” ᴹ

“I wasn't going to ask.” ʸᵏ

“You had a face.” ᴹ

Ori laughed at the explanation without needing to know why Yuka had begun laughing too.

The boat opened into its little upright shape.

They all looked at it for a moment.

Then Ori asked whether it would float.

* * *

They found a shallow basin which was empty and clean, and enough water to make the answer worth testing.

Yuka put a cloth beneath it before Mau could set it on the table. Ori carried the boat with both hands and stopped above the surface, looking toward him.

“What if it sinks?” ᴼ

“We take it out.” ᴹ

She considered the simplicity of that.

Then she let go.

The boat rocked once and remained upright.

Mau watched Ori's face change.

Yuka touched the edge of the basin with one finger, giving it a very small motion. The boat moved. Ori put a hand beside it in the water, then remembered not to push it down and drew her fingers back.

“Where is it going?” Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

Ori looked toward the window.

“To the city.” ᴼ

“A sensible destination.” ᴹ

“We are in the city.” ᴼ

He nodded gravely.

“An excellent journey. Very efficient.” ᴹ

She laughed again and pushed the water gently toward him.

* * *

For a while they gave the little boat places to go.

A folded corner became a harbour which would not stay where Yuka put it. Mau made a small obstruction with his finger and accepted Ori's ruling that he had become a rock rather than a person. When the boat touched him, she asked him to move because it had important business.

He moved.

Yuka began to explain how a traveller might be expected to ask more politely.

Ori turned toward her at once.

“Please.” ᴼ

“Much better.” ʸᵏ

The boat reached the edge of the basin.

“What was the important business?” Mau asked. ᴹ

Ori looked at him, then at Yuka.

“Breakfast.” ᴼ

“We have just had breakfast,” he said. ᴹ

She smiled.

“The boat hasn't.” ᴼ

Yuka had to put her hand over her mouth before answering that.

* * *

Frieren found them when the bottom of the boat had begun taking the journey too seriously.

She stood at the open door for a moment, watching Ori explain that Mau was a rock and therefore should not be allowed to provide opinions about the route.

“That sounds reasonable,” the elf said. ᶠ

Mau looked up.

“I was hoping for an independent judgement.” ᴹ

“You have one.” ᶠ

Ori turned toward her.

“Mum, it floats.” ᴼ

Frieren came to the table and looked where she pointed.

The small vessel leaned a little, but had not yet abandoned the effort.

“So it does.” ᶠ

Ori explained the journey. Frieren listened, asked where it had been before reaching this harbour and accepted the answer with the attention the child had wanted to bring to her.

Yuka handed over the cloth when Ori showed her wet fingers.

The girl dried them, then moved beside Frieren to look at the boat from another direction.

Mau let the conversation continue without needing his part in it to become larger.

* * *

Chloe appeared at the door with the news that the flag had found a place everyone was prepared to accept for at least the next ten minutes.

Ori looked toward her.

Then back at the basin.

“Can we leave it?” ᴼ

“We can put it somewhere to dry,” Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Ori lifted it out herself. The paper had softened enough to require care. Yuka found a dry place beside the window, and together they set it down without promising that it would look the same after recovering from its voyage.

The child looked at Mau.

“Thank you for waiting with me.” ᴼ

“I liked it.” ᴹ

She smiled as though that had answered something she had not asked aloud.

Frieren touched her shoulder.

“Go and see the flag. We can look at the shops later.” ᶠ

Ori went with Chloe, carrying the explanation of the boat into the passage before either had reached the stairs.

Frieren watched them leave.

Then she looked at the water on the cloth, the damp paper and the two people who appeared unexpectedly pleased with the morning's work.

“A very accomplished rock,” she told Mau. ᶠ

Yuka began laughing again.

* * *

Rimuru found them after the basin had been emptied and returned.

He looked at the boat by the window.

“Should I ask?” ᴿ

“It has already arrived,” Yuka said. ʸᵏ

“Good. We have somewhere else to be.” ᴿ

Mau turned toward him.

Rimuru's smile held the beginning of the morning's less ordinary work.

“Come on. The two idiots should probably meet the people who have been asking where I found the second one.” ᴿ

Yuka straightened the collar Mau had managed to disturb while becoming part of the harbour.

“Go. I'll find you after.” ʸᵏ

He took her hand for a moment and kissed the fingers beside the ring.

Then he went with his brother, leaving the little boat where Ori could come back and see it.
