They crossed the courtyard and took the passage which led away from the guest rooms. The city had begun its morning around them. People carried things with somewhere particular to put them; visitors stopped at signs and tried to decide whether they had taken the correct turn.

Rimuru paused to give directions twice.

The second person thanked him, went three steps, recognised him and turned back so sharply that Mau had to move out of the way.

Rimuru waved before the new conversation could require everyone to begin again.

“Later. Enjoy the morning.” ᴿ

They continued.

Mau waited until they were past the next corner.

“I can see why you wanted company.” ᴹ

“I also thought you might enjoy meeting them.” ᴿ

“Both things can be true.” ᴹ

Rimuru laughed.

* * *

Near the meeting rooms, the unfamiliar attention became easier to feel.

Mau did not reach for a weapon. He slowed enough to notice what his body wanted to do, then let the old lesson settle into the work which held his presence close. Frieren had made him practise that part when it was boring. He was grateful for the boring part now.

*I would like to observe this,* Ciel said.

*You wanted the introductions.*

*Yes.*

He felt the familiar company of her attention without needing her to tell him what to say.

Farther in, Noise stirred.

*Many.*

*I know. We keep the plans.*

The answer came with amusement.

*Home. Then talk.*

Mau smiled before he could help it.

Rimuru saw.

“Good news?” ᴿ

“I am being reminded of something I said.” ᴹ

“A promising development.” ᴿ

“You might be less pleased if they start doing it to both of us.” ᴹ

His brother considered that and opened the door.

* * *

Veldora's voice reached them first.

“—and the field was already changing when he turned toward us. A remarkable way to announce oneself.” ⱽ

Rimuru stopped on the threshold.

“That is not what happened.” ᴿ

The room looked toward him.

Veldora appeared delighted to discover the opportunity for a second narrator.

“You were there.” ⱽ

“Which is how I know.” ᴿ

Hinata, seated near the side of the table, looked as though she had been waiting for that interruption for some time.

“I did suggest beginning with the misunderstanding,” she said. ᴴⁱ

“A good suggestion,” Rimuru answered. ᴿ

Mau came far enough into view that the people who had been looking at the slime could see whom he had brought.

The conversation stopped.

* * *

The woman near Hinata turned before most of the others finished the motion.

She had been listening with an expression which made Mau think the dragon's account had entertained her less than Veldora believed. Now her attention settled on him, precise enough that he could feel the difference between being seen and being examined.

“So this is the one.” ᴸᵘ

Gazel's hand stopped beside his cup.

Across the table, a man whose ease had survived the interruption sat a fraction more upright. Another simply watched, his face giving Mau very little help in deciding which question would come first.

Mau kept his hands where they could be seen.

The pressure he was holding did not have to spill into the room to make itself difficult to mistake. He saw a glance toward Rimuru, the small adjustment of a chair, fingers which had been resting openly come together.

Nobody attacked.

For a breath, it seemed possible that nobody would begin the ordinary part of the introduction either.

Rimuru did.

“This is Mau. My brother.” ᴿ

* * *

The title changed something less obvious than their attention.

The woman by Hinata looked toward Rimuru, then back at Mau.

“Your brother.” ᴸᵘ

“Yes.” ᴿ

“That was absent from the dragon's account.” ᴸᵘ

“Several useful details were.” ᴿ

Veldora made an objection which Hinata appeared prepared to let pass unanswered.

Rimuru put a hand briefly against Mau's shoulder.

“He's here because I asked him to come. I'd like all of us to have a conversation before we decide how many alarming things the doorway needs to mean.” ᴿ

Mau let out a breath.

“I would appreciate that.” ᴹ

The man across the table smiled.

It was not a reassuring smile. It did, however, appear interested in the actual person who had spoken.

“Then let him sit down,” he said. ᴳᵘ

Rimuru looked grateful enough that Mau decided to count it as a useful beginning.

* * *

He introduced them properly.

Luminous first, the woman whose attention had caught the layered part of Mau's presence before he had reached the room. She inclined her head as though accepting a name did not mean surrendering any of the questions attached to it.

Gazel, who ruled Dwargon and looked at Mau's grip when he offered his hand, then at his face instead of making the old stiffness into a question in front of everybody.

Elmesia, whose welcome had a lightness which did not make Mau believe she had missed anything happening around the table.

“He rarely introduces someone without first explaining why the introduction will be perfectly straightforward,” she said, looking at Rimuru. ᴱˡ

“I was trying a new approach.” ᴿ

“How refreshing.” ᴱˡ

Guy, the man who had suggested the chair, looked pleased with that answer.

Leon spoke less. He returned Mau's greeting with a directness Mau found easier to meet than a long, polished expression of welcome would have been.

There were other people in attendance, some taking notes and some listening on behalf of people who had not yet arrived. Rimuru named the people making decisions, then made sure Mau could place the others without requiring him to remember every responsibility on the first hearing.

Mau asked when he had not caught a name.

Nobody treated that as a failure worth stopping the meeting for.

* * *

He turned toward Hinata before taking the chair.

“I know I've said this, but meeting everyone here seems like a good time to say it properly. I'm sorry for what happened when you came looking for him.” ᴹ

She watched him for a moment.

“You stopped. You explained. I accepted the apology.” ᴴⁱ

He nodded.

Veldora leaned closer.

“And there will be a considerably better place for the next encounter.” ⱽ

Rimuru looked toward him immediately.

“If the people preparing it say so.” ᴿ

“Naturally.” ⱽ

Luminous looked between them with renewed interest.

“I imagine that will be an entertaining report.” ᴸᵘ

Mau took the chair beside Rimuru.

It was a very ordinary chair.

He was glad he had asked for one.

* * *

The first direct question came from Guy.

“What are you?” ᴳᵘ

Mau looked at him.

He could have mistaken the ease of the delivery for carelessness if everyone else had not become slightly quieter.

“I don't have a reliable answer to that.” ᴹ

“How inconvenient.” ᴳᵘ

“Occasionally.” ᴹ

Elmesia smiled into her cup.

Mau kept his hands on the table.

“There are things I know how to do, things I'm learning and a great deal I don't remember from before I arrived. I can answer questions about the first two without pretending the last part is settled.” ᴹ

Guy considered him.

“And that collection of impressions?” ᴳᵘ

Mau felt Ciel notice the question. Noise did not move beyond the work they had agreed to keep.

“It isn't one thing,” Mau said. ᴹ

“Clearly.” ᴸᵘ

She had been listening closely enough that the single word did not feel like an interruption.

“It does not all settle where one expects it.” ᴸᵘ

Rimuru reached for the papers between them.

“Neither does the city any longer. That is rather more urgent for the people who live in it.” ᴿ

Gazel looked at him, then set his cup down.

“Then begin there.” ᴳᵃ

Mau let his hand relax beside the edge of the table.

The conversation found a place to start.
