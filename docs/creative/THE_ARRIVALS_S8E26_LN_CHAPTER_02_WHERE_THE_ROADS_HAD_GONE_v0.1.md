*When: they put the two maps beside one another, nobody tries to force the edges to meet.*

The first had been drawn from the world the visitors remembered. The second had marks added by people who had walked, flown or followed another traveller far enough to come back with something more useful than certainty.

There were places which belonged to both.

Others existed only on the older page.

Rimuru touched the route he had pointed out on the road yesterday.

“We saw where this stops. Traffic is already going around it.” ᴿ

Gazel leaned forward.

“And the far side?” ᴳᵃ

Benimaru indicated a note.

“A party reached the next crossing. Beyond that, the old direction no longer brought them to the place they expected.” ᴮᵉ

“Did they continue?” ᴳᵃ

“Far enough to need to come back before losing the route they could still identify.” ᴮᵉ

The king nodded.

Mau liked that nobody told the people who had come back that they ought to have kept walking until the map became simpler.

* * *

Elmesia had questions about people before she asked about borders.

Which reports included occupied homes. Which groups had arrived together. Whether the person who had named a place had seen it or merely heard the name from somebody pleased to find a familiar word in an unfamiliar country.

The answers were not all comfortable.

Some had enough detail to follow. Others amounted to a direction and a story which had grown each time it was passed on.

“Then keep those separate,” she said. “We can send someone toward a possibility without printing it as a border.” ᴱˡ

Mau thought of the notes Hollis carried, the distinction between what the merchant had watched happen and what somebody had told him while trying to sell something.

Rimuru glanced toward him.

“You're thinking of home.” ᴿ

“Our maps aren't much better. Our people have become quite good at writing down which part they actually know.” ᴹ

Gazel looked at him.

“Useful people.” ᴳᵃ

“Very.” ᴹ

* * *

Leon asked the question which had been waiting beneath the map.

“What brings the pieces?” ᴸᵉ

Rimuru rested his hands beside the paper rather than putting a finger on an answer he could not show them.

“We know the earlier arrivals weren't happening without direction. We have reason to believe that control has been interrupted.” ᴿ

“By whom?” ᴸᵉ

“That is not something I can give you as a complete answer.” ᴿ

Luminous watched him.

“But you have more than a theory.” ᴸᵘ

“We have an account from someone involved, and injuries which make that account rather difficult to dismiss. We do not have every participant, every method or a reliable explanation of what those people can still control.” ᴿ

Mau kept his eyes on the table.

No one had been promised the identity of the person resting behind a door they could choose to close. Rimuru had given them the part which mattered to the roads without taking that choice away from her.

Guy tapped one finger against his chair.

“So the person moving the pieces may no longer be the person who began the game.” ᴳᵘ

“Or there may be several people pulling at something none of them fully understands,” Rimuru answered. “That part is a possibility. What we can all see is that the arrival pattern has changed.” ᴿ

Gazel looked back at the maps.

“Then we act on the change we can see.” ᴳᵃ

* * *

The room did not become peaceful simply because nobody drew a weapon.

There were questions about approaches to the city, who would report another change in the road and what people meant when they asked whether a new community was safe to visit. A messenger brought a small addition to the morning's arrivals and waited until Benimaru had read the name before returning to the corridor.

Nothing about the world had stopped moving while they sat down to describe it.

“If we wait until every answer is complete,” Rimuru said, “people will spend the whole time meeting one another through rumours.” ᴿ

Luminous looked toward Mau.

“There have been a considerable number of those.” ᴸᵘ

“I've noticed,” he said. ᴹ

“The sun was a memorable detail.” ᴱˡ

He looked at the woman speaking, then at Rimuru.

His brother appeared prepared to let him answer that one himself.

“I didn't make a sun.” ᴹ

Guy smiled.

“A disappointing beginning.” ᴳᵘ

“There was a fight. Some of the people telling the story have left out rather a lot of what we were trying to protect.” ᴹ

Gazel watched him for a moment.

“That happens to useful stories.” ᴳᵃ

“It becomes less useful when it reaches somebody holding a stone.” ᴹ

The king's expression changed, very slightly.

He did not ask Mau to turn that sentence into a performance for the room.

* * *

The talk reached equipment by way of a road party which had found its familiar tools less reliable against things it had never needed to cut before.

Mau let the people with the report finish before describing what the house had tested.

He gave them the difference between a useful sample and a thing the makers were prepared to supply. The materials they still had questions about. The fact that Hollis could tell them more about the customers and the roads than he could.

“And these makers answer to you?” Gazel asked. ᴳᵃ

Mau shook his head.

“They live with me. Some have come for the visit. They should be allowed to enjoy it before somebody offers a workshop an order it hasn't agreed to make.” ᴹ

Rimuru nodded.

“I'll introduce the people who want the conversation.” ᴿ

Elmesia smiled.

“How unusually considerate of the people whose hands will do the work.” ᴱˡ

“I've carried enough things to know it is worth asking first.” ᴹ

Gazel looked at his hands again, then back at the map.

“Bring the questions to the makers, then. We can start with a small one.” ᴳᵃ

There was no new currency agreed over the next cup of tea. No promise to equip every visitor who knew which name to put at the top of a request.

There were names written beside questions which now had somebody willing to receive them.

Mau thought that was quite a lot for one morning.

* * *

During the pause, Hinata moved a page toward Luminous.

Mau recognised the subject before she spoke.

“The edge reached his arm. Not the whole body. He was already moving, and he repaired what remained injured afterward.” ᴴⁱ

Luminous read the small note, then looked toward him.

“You can distinguish what was resisted from what was restored?” ᴸᵘ

“Some of it. I wouldn't claim every answer from that one exchange.” ᴹ

“Sensible.” ᴸᵘ

He could feel the interest without having to mistake it for a threat.

“I'd like to understand what I was looking at too,” he added. ᴹ

Luminous held his gaze for a moment.

“Then perhaps we will find an appropriate occasion to ask one another.” ᴸᵘ

She returned the page to Hinata.

Inside, Noise remained very still around the things it was keeping for later.

Ciel said nothing in a voice the room could hear.

Mau took a drink of the tea before it cooled any further.
