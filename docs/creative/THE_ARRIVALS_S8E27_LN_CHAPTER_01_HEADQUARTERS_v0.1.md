* * *

The conversation which followed was less dramatic than the one some of the room appeared to have expected.

Benimaru described what was already being handled in the city. Rigurd had the practical lists and the names of people who would object if an account of their work became a description of two men doing everything themselves.

They had questions for Rimuru. Things which belonged to him, decisions he could help with, others which had been waiting only because nobody knew where to send the next page.

Rimuru listened.

“Then keep those responsibilities clear. Tell me which things need me, and which things you want me to hear before the decision is made.” ᴿ

“I would like to. We will have to make the communication work reliably. That isn't the same as pretending I can be in every room.” ᴿ

“I know.” ᴿ

“Good. Make sure everyone writing the questions knows it too.” ᴳᵃ

The discussion reached the festival after the papers which needed signatures had been separated from the things people simply wished they could settle before lunch.

There would be time to hear more reports. Time to make arrangements which did not depend on every guest leaving the city with a promise to supply something they had not measured.

There would also be food, music and people who had not crossed an unfamiliar world merely to sit beside a map of it.

“And the contest,” Veldora added. ⱽ

Guy was interested. So were others, for reasons which became less similar the longer they discussed them.

One wanted to see what this world's limits had done to a familiar skill. Another wanted an occasion to ask a question with an opponent allowed to answer properly. Some plainly liked the prospect of a fight in a place which did not end the day with an argument about who owed someone else a road.

Mau listened without putting his own name in the first empty space.

The first empty space did not yet belong to an agreement.

“Ramiris is working on the place,” Rimuru said. “We have people checking what it can safely do here. When that is ready, we explain the conditions and let people choose.” ᴿ

When they finally stood, the room had not agreed how the whole new world should be arranged.

It had agreed who would receive the first questions. Which routes needed checking. What could be brought to the people making tools, rather than promised in their absence. That the city could celebrate while still being unfinished.

Mau considered that a respectable amount of work for people who had also managed to discuss turning his house into an acronym.
