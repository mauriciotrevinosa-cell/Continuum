* * *

One errand concerned a thing which needed to be collected. The other concerned a place Shuna wanted to see now that the people working there had opened the part facing the street again.

The second won without anyone needing to pretend it had become urgent.

They walked together. Shuna introduced the people she knew and asked after someone who had been expected but had not yet returned. Shion stopped before following them into a shop, looked at the display outside and asked what one of the pieces was for.

The answer made her look at it differently.

Yuka waited while she asked another question.

For a while the conversation had nothing to do with who had chosen where to live.

That appeared to make the next turn easier.

* * *

They found the Fort on the way back.

Its temporary corner had acquired a flag, a set of things on a table and several people who had arrived intending to make an ordinary visit and were now being asked whether they understood the rules.

Chloe stood with four children she had begun introducing before anyone else found an opportunity to help.

Kenya had a question almost immediately.

“This is a fort?” ᴷᵉ

Umaru looked at the table, then at him.

“The current field location.” ᵁ

Ori nodded with the seriousness the explanation deserved.

Ryota looked toward Chloe.

She was trying not to laugh.

Alice had already become interested in one of the small game pieces. Gale waited until Umaru had finished explaining which ones could move before he put his hand near the table.

Rem sat beside the open space, helping keep the pieces from being mistaken for things someone should clear away before lunch.

“Are they joining?” Yuka asked. ʸᵏ

“We're finding out,” Rem said. ᴿᵉ

* * *

Chloe introduced Yuka next, then Ori insisted on explaining the boat before the rest of the introductions could continue.

The story had grown a little in the telling. Mau remained a rock. Yuka's harbour had become more complicated. The boat had eaten an imaginary breakfast which Ori described with enough conviction that one of the children asked what it had been made of.

“Paper,” she said. ᴼ

Chloe put a hand over her mouth.

Rem began laughing so softly that Umaru looked toward her before she looked back at the visitors.

For a moment the table became easier for everyone to approach.

Yuka watched Chloe lean into the conversation, no longer standing between one group and another as though she had to choose which people should know her first.

The people who returned from the morning's meeting did not all come back together.

They settled the changeover in a few sentences.

There would be time for the children. Time for the people who wanted to enter a contest to learn whether they could. Nobody had to be in both places simply because they were good at being needed.

Yuka found Mai on the way to the other courtyard.

She and Marin had returned from looking at a workshop notice and were arguing about whether looking at a thing long enough to understand its shape counted as beginning work during a festival.
