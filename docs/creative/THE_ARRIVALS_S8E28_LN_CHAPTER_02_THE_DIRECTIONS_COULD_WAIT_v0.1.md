*When: Anko finds the place, Hinata is already there and has chosen a table from which neither of them needs to watch the whole street.*

Anko noticed that before she noticed the cup.

She stopped beside the empty chair.

“You found the correct directions.” ᴬⁿ

“I had the advantage of knowing where I meant.” ᴴⁱ

“An unfair competition.” ᴬⁿ

Hinata moved the small folded page away from the second place.

“Would you like to sit?” ᴴⁱ

Anko did.

The place was busy enough to belong to the city and quiet enough that a conversation did not have to be announced to the next table. A woman came to ask what she wanted. Anko chose after looking at what Hinata had ordered, then set her own notes beside the cup instead of between them.

Hinata noticed.

Neither mentioned it.

* * *

They did compare the directions first.

One route had been shorter than expected. Another had become useless because the entrance a person remembered was no longer the entrance visitors were using. The third belonged to somebody who had assumed Anko wanted to find a quite different place and had not allowed the uncertainty of that assumption to interrupt the answer.

Hinata listened to the account with increasing amusement.

“You asked twice?” ᴴⁱ

“I was being thorough.” ᴬⁿ

“And received a fourth route.” ᴴⁱ

Anko looked at her.

Hinata lifted her cup.

“I have spent a morning here too.” ᴴⁱ

Anko laughed, surprised by how little help the sound needed once it had begun.

When the drink arrived, she took a moment to try it before deciding the conversation required her to have an opinion about that as well.

It was good.

She said so.

Hinata looked pleased with the choice.

* * *

Anko had expected someone to arrive with a question before they finished the first cup.

Nobody did.

The absence of it became noticeable only after she had looked toward the door twice and found that both times she was looking because she had become used to it.

Hinata followed the second glance.

“Were you meeting someone else?” ᴴⁱ

“No.” ᴬⁿ

“Good.” ᴴⁱ

Anko looked back at her.

The answer had been simple enough to be easily missed if she had been busy with the person who might have arrived.

She put her hand around the cup.

“So nobody has assigned you a problem for the next hour?” ᴬⁿ

“Several people would like to. I told them when I would be available.” ᴴⁱ

“Does that work?” ᴬⁿ

“Sometimes one must explain the first answer was the answer.” ᴴⁱ

Anko smiled.

“That I recognise.” ᴬⁿ

* * *

The small case lay beside Anko's notes.

Hinata glanced at it, then at her.

“You said you had carried it longer than some of the things inside.” ᴴⁱ

“I did.” ᴬⁿ

“What do you keep when something stops being useful?” ᴴⁱ

Anko looked down at the case.

For a moment she considered giving the practical answer. A useful case could hold something new. A thing could be repaired. There were probably three people at the house who would already have begun asking what precisely had stopped working.

Hinata waited without helping her escape the question.

“Depends what it reminds me of,” Anko said. ᴬⁿ

“And if you dislike the reminder?” ᴴⁱ

“I might keep it anyway. At least until I know whether I dislike the thing or just the day I last needed it.” ᴬⁿ

Hinata considered that.

“A distinction worth making.” ᴴⁱ

Anko looked toward the sword she had been careful not to crowd with her chair.

“You asked because of something particular?” ᴬⁿ

Hinata followed her glance.

“Partly.” ᴴⁱ

She did not add the part she had not chosen to give.

Anko let the answer stand.

* * *

They found easier things to disagree about afterward.

Whether a person who said a place was nearby should be required to explain how quickly they normally walked. Whether the visitor who had asked to see everything had meant the promise literally. The remarkable confidence of people who considered the answer *you cannot miss it* a substitute for naming a building.

Hinata had a story about the last of those which she began with enough restraint that Anko had time to be surprised when the end was funny.

She laughed into the edge of her cup.

Hinata watched her for a moment before looking down at her own.

It was small enough that Anko might have let it go if she had not been paying attention.

She was paying attention.

* * *

The next drink became a choice rather than something they ordered because the first had ended.

Anko checked the time she had promised to return the borrowed things. Hinata checked the one commitment which still belonged to the afternoon. There was room between them.

“We could walk part of the way,” Hinata said. ᴴⁱ

“In which direction?” ᴬⁿ

“I thought we might choose one.” ᴴⁱ

Anko smiled before she answered.

“A dangerous approach. We might end up enjoying ourselves.” ᴬⁿ

“We shall have to accept the possibility.” ᴴⁱ

They finished their drinks without ordering the next part of the day on behalf of anybody else.

* * *

Outside, the street had become busier.

Anko moved beside Hinata rather than half a step behind her. The conversation found its own pace. They stopped when something interested one of them and discovered the other was quite capable of waiting without turning it into a favour.

At a narrow place, Hinata stepped aside to let someone carrying a load through. Anko waited with her. By the time the way was clear, they had begun a discussion which carried them past the turn they had initially meant to take.

Hinata noticed first.

“We missed it.” ᴴⁱ

Anko looked back.

“Did we need it?” ᴬⁿ

Hinata looked at her.

Then she smiled properly.

“Not yet.” ᴴⁱ

They continued a little farther.

* * *

The end of the walk came where the practical part of the afternoon could no longer be made to wait.

Anko shifted the small case in her hand.

“Thank you for the directions.” ᴬⁿ

“You hardly used them.” ᴴⁱ

“I may need another attempt.” ᴬⁿ

Hinata did not answer immediately.

For the first time since they sat down, Anko felt the small possibility that she had misjudged a quiet answer as an invitation which had not been meant.

Then Hinata asked when she would be free.

Anko gave her a time she could actually keep.

Hinata gave one back.

It required almost no arranging at all.

When they parted, Anko put the folded directions away beside the things she intended to keep.

She did not need to decide what to call the afternoon before being glad she had come.
