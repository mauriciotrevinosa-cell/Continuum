## Chapter 3 — Her Name on the Door

**Volume:** 45

*When: Ramiris discovers that somebody has written “possible arena” on the drawing, she crosses out the first word.*

“It is an arena. Whether I let you use it is the question.” ᴿᵃ

The pencil was almost as tall as she was.

She put it down with both hands, folded her arms and looked across the table at the people who had come to inspect her work.

Gojo leaned closer to the drawing.

“Excellent. I prefer a confident host.” ᴳ

“I am an extremely confident host.” ᴿᵃ

“Then we should get along.” ᴳ

“That remains to be seen.” ᴿᵃ

Rimuru covered his mouth. Beretta waited beside the door with the patience of somebody who had watched Ramiris practise this part of the afternoon and knew she had been looking forward to it.

Mau stood back while Shoko finished asking about the return point.

It was the first question she had asked on entering. She had let Ramiris show them the seating, the floor and the passages people would use to reach them. Then she had brought the conversation back to the place where an injured participant would arrive.

“Here,” Ramiris said, pointing. ᴿᵃ

“Where you can watch them?” ˢ

“Where I can watch them.” ᴿᵃ

Shoko put a finger beside the mark.

“And where I can get to them without crossing the fight.” ˢ

Ramiris looked at the drawing again.

Then she took the pencil back.

* * *

The room beyond the passage was larger than the entrance had allowed Mau to expect.

There was no ceiling high enough to mistake for open sky. He could see where the light came from. The floor lay level beneath their feet, uninterrupted except for a low boundary which made the intended fighting space plain.

At one side, a second doorway opened into the room they had just discussed.

Ramiris flew ahead of them.

“The audience comes through the other way. Beretta will show you before you leave. Nobody needs to pass the fighters to reach a seat.” ᴿᵃ

Mau looked toward the entrance behind him.

Rimuru noticed.

“Ordinary exit first?” ᴿ

Mau nodded.

They walked it together.

It was a short walk. Mau went all the way through, waited on the other side and came back when his brother did. Nothing shut behind them. Ramiris watched without making a joke about the time it took.

When Mau returned to the floor, she showed him where the same passage appeared on her drawing.

“There. You can leave without waiting for somebody to defeat you.” ᴿᵃ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“Obviously.” ᴿᵃ

She sounded almost offended that anyone might have built otherwise.

Mau liked her considerably more for it.

* * *

The bracelets came next.

Ramiris showed them how she registered one to the labyrinth, then handed it to Rimuru. He put it on and let her check the connection before walking across the boundary.

Gojo stayed where he could see both the floor and the return room.

Yuta asked what happened if a person removed the bracelet.

“They stop being a registered participant,” Ramiris said. ᴿᵃ

“During a fight?” ʸᵗ

She looked at him.

Then at the drawing.

“That should mean the fight stops.” ᴿᵃ

“Yes,” Shoko said. ˢ

Beretta made the note before the discussion could turn it into three different rules.

Rimuru raised his hand from inside the boundary.

“May I come out now, or have I become the first attraction?” ᴿ

Ramiris waved him through.

He walked into the return room, came back and offered the bracelet to her.

“No missing slime,” he said. ᴿ

“That was not the difficult test.” ᴿᵃ

“I thought I performed it beautifully.” ᴿ

* * *

Ramiris had prepared a simple target for the next part.

It moved when she asked it to, crossing from one floor mark to another. It carried no face, no borrowed voice and no suggestion that she had fetched an unwilling creature to demonstrate what happened when something died.

She explained what she had registered before she let anybody strike it.

Gojo crouched beside it, interested in the connection rather than the shape. Yuta watched from the other side. Mau kept his hands at his sides.

“A cut first,” Ramiris said. ᴿᵃ

Beretta made one.

The target lost an arm. The separated part hit the floor. Shoko watched the break while Ramiris let the damaged figure take another step, then ended the test.

The target disappeared.

In the adjoining room, something landed with a small, distinct sound.

They went to look.

The figure stood on the marked place with both arms attached.

Ramiris pointed at it as though presenting an argument she had already won.

Shoko crouched and inspected the place where the cut had been.

“Do it again,” she said. ˢ

Ramiris's wings stopped for a moment.

“You saw it.” ᴿᵃ

“I did. Now I want to know what happens the second time.” ˢ

Gojo grinned.

“I like her.” ᴳ

“You came here together,” Ramiris said. ᴿᵃ

“It keeps becoming relevant.” ᴳ

* * *

They repeated it.

The next return was complete too. Ramiris changed the point of damage. Beretta changed the angle. Yuta asked her to end a test before the target had finished moving, and they found that its return did not require it to reach the final floor mark first.

That mattered enough for Shoko to stand up and ask to see the adjoining room again.

Mau stayed on the other side of the threshold while the others went through.

*You are not volunteering for the next part,* Ciel said.

He looked down at the bracelet Ramiris had left on the table.

*I wasn't going to.*

There was a pause.

*You were considering how useful it would be.*

Mau looked toward his brother. Rimuru had stopped beside Shoko, listening to her question about a person who arrived unable to answer.

*That is unfortunately a very broad category,* Mau replied.

Ciel did not laugh.

He set the bracelet down.

*I know. Not me.*

Her answer came more quietly.

*Thank you.*

* * *

Gojo asked about effects which did not leave a visible cut.

Ramiris began answering, then stopped when he explained what he meant.

The room became quieter.

Yuta added a question of his own. Shoko asked whether the return restored the state from registration or repaired the state at the instant of the injury. Ramiris had an answer for the ordinary operation. It did not answer everything Gojo had just described.

For the first time that afternoon, she came down to stand on the table instead of hovering above it.

“I am not saying it fails,” she said. ᴿᵃ

“Neither are we,” Rimuru replied. ᴿ

“You are all looking very serious.” ᴿᵃ

Mau leaned against the wall where he would not crowd her.

“We want to be able to enjoy it when we're in there.” ᴹ

Ramiris looked at him.

“I know.” ᴿᵃ

She looked back at the notes. Then she told Beretta to make another column.

They would test those effects separately. Nobody would volunteer an unprotected mind to prove a guess. If a particular attack could not be checked, it would not become an acceptable surprise halfway through the festival.

Gojo took that answer without argument.

“We can work with that.” ᴳ

“I know you can. It is my excellent arena.” ᴿᵃ

* * *

The last test was almost disappointingly ordinary.

Rimuru put the bracelet on again. Ramiris registered him. He crossed the boundary, surrendered aloud and stood still while she returned him to the adjoining room.

This time everybody waited where they had been told to wait.

Shoko reached him first.

“Name?” ˢ

“I feel we should know each other by now.” ᴿ

“Good. Still irritating.” ˢ

She checked him anyway.

Mau watched his brother lift a hand toward the entrance and give them a small, cheerful wave.

There was room beside Shoko for another person to kneel. Room for somebody frightened to be kept away from the noise. No fighting floor to cross, no locked door to convince into opening.

He looked at Ramiris.

“That part is very good.” ᴹ

She followed his glance.

For once she did not make the praise easier to hear by turning it into a boast.

“I want people to come back,” she said. ᴿᵃ

* * *

When they left, the drawing had acquired enough additions that Beretta offered to make a clean copy.

Ramiris let him, after checking that her name would remain above the entrance.

Gojo stayed behind with Yuta to discuss the next small demonstration. Shoko went with them as far as the table, already objecting to an assumption one of them had made about what counted as *small*.

Rimuru nudged Mau toward the passage.

“You promised to find Yuka.” ᴿ

Mau looked back once.

Ramiris had reclaimed the pencil. She was telling Gojo where he could put his notes and where he absolutely could not.

Mau smiled.

For the first time since somebody had proposed the contest, he could imagine arriving to watch a fight without also counting all the ways to leave it.

He still counted the door on his way out.

Then he went to find her.
