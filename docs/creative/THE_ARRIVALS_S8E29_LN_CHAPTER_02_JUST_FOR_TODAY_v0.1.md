# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 45
## Chapter 4 — Just for Today

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S8 v0.1 (2026-10-30)
**Volume:** 45
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S8E29
**Continuity state:** B+38 afternoon. Maomao/Jinshi chosen outing: one brownie, voluntary hand-holding, plants then shared meal; no drug compulsion, invented medical effect or resolved canon romance. Jinshi agency/future Gaoshun coin not spent. Mau rejoins Yuka after venue visit and her Mai time; distant observation respects privacy. No new purchases specified or items/currency ledger changes.

*When: Jinshi has prepared three possible ways of asking her to spend the afternoon with him, Maomao arrives carrying a small piece of cake and ruins all three.*

“Are you busy?” ᴹᵐ

He looked at her.

Behind him, the paper on the table contained nothing which could honestly be described as urgent. He considered moving it anyway, then decided against allowing a seating chart to become the rival he lost to.

“Not presently.” ᴶ

“Good.” ᴹᵐ

She unwrapped the brownie with a concentration which might have discouraged a less practised observer. Jinshi had learned that she could apply the same expression to a substance which interested her and a substance which had committed some offence against her expectations.

He hoped the cake belonged to the first category.

“Where did you find that?” ᴶ

“There was some with the food offered to the visitors.” ᴹᵐ

“And you chose it?” ᴶ

She looked at him.

“It did not climb into my hand.” ᴹᵐ

An encouraging beginning.

* * *

She ate a small piece first.

Jinshi waited. He had no intention of making the mistake of asking whether she liked it before she had finished deciding what she thought of it.

Maomao examined what remained.

“You are staring.” ᴹᵐ

“Am I?” ᴶ

“Yes.” ᴹᵐ

He looked toward the window.

She sighed, broke off another piece and held it out.

Jinshi turned back so quickly that her expression changed by a fraction.

“Take it before I reconsider.” ᴹᵐ

He did.

It was richer than he expected. The small amount left sweetness against his tongue, and he was deciding how to say something pleasant about it when Maomao began folding the wrapper.

“There are plants I want to see.” ᴹᵐ

“I remember.” ᴶ

“And afterward I would like to eat somewhere.” ᴹᵐ

He looked at her properly.

She folded the wrapper a second time.

“With you,” she added, with the restrained irritation of somebody obliged to supply the part which should already have been understood. ᴹᵐ

* * *

Jinshi stood up.

It was too immediate. He knew it was too immediate from the way she looked at the abandoned table, but sitting down again would have made it worse.

“We could ask someone to show us—” ᴶ

“I know where the first place is.” ᴹᵐ

“Of course.” ᴶ

“I asked this morning.” ᴹᵐ

He was smiling. He tried to moderate it and saw from her face that the attempt had failed.

“Do you wish to bring anything?” she asked. ᴹᵐ

His eyes went toward the paper.

There were questions he could usefully ask on the way. A person he might find. Something about the arrangements which would take only a moment if he happened to pass the right door.

He had been here before.

“No,” he said. ᴶ

Maomao waited a moment, as though checking he meant it.

Then she held out her hand.

* * *

He looked down at it.

She looked at his face.

“Do you intend to make me stand here all afternoon?” ᴹᵐ

Jinshi took her hand.

Her fingers settled against his without ceremony. The movement was so practical, so entirely hers, that he found himself considerably less prepared for it than he would have been for something accompanied by a warning.

“Just for today,” she said. ᴹᵐ

He could not have kept the smile from her now if it had become a matter of state.

“Then I shall try to make good use of today.” ᴶ

“Start by walking.” ᴹᵐ

He did.

* * *

The first place was not a grand garden.

It was a collection of growing beds beside a working building, where somebody had put the things they could still identify after the change in the land. There were labels. There were also several places where labels had been left blank rather than made confident.

Maomao liked those considerably.

The woman who met them had been expecting one visitor. She looked at Jinshi, looked at their hands and asked whether he too wanted to see the plants.

“Very much,” he said. ᴶ

Maomao gave him a sideways glance.

“He can listen.” ᴹᵐ

The woman accepted that division of labour without difficulty.

They followed her along the beds.

Jinshi discovered that Maomao did not release his hand when something interested her. She drew him closer instead, then forgot she had done it while asking a sequence of questions specific enough to earn a thoughtful answer.

He listened.

It was easier than she had made it sound.

* * *

One plant refused to look as remarkable as Maomao apparently found it.

Jinshi studied the modest leaves, then her face.

“That one?” ᴶ

She looked at him with the faint incredulity his question deserved.

“What were you expecting?” ᴹᵐ

“Something more ostentatious.” ᴶ

“You would be a very easy person to sell the wrong plant.” ᴹᵐ

“I rely upon excellent advice.” ᴶ

She looked back at the bed before he could enjoy the answer too openly.

The woman tending it explained what they had observed since arriving. Maomao asked what they had tried, what had failed and whether anybody had written down the difference.

There was a notebook.

Jinshi watched her expression when it appeared.

He had been trying to give her an afternoon she would want to keep. It occurred to him, with a small and useful embarrassment, that she had already found one without waiting for him to provide it.

He could be there with her.

He did not need to arrange every reason she smiled.

* * *

They stayed longer than they had intended.

Maomao did not ask to take anything from the beds. She asked when she might return and whether the woman would mind another conversation once the visitors had stopped arriving quite so quickly.

The invitation was given willingly.

Outside, Jinshi waited while she finished making a note.

“You enjoyed that,” he said. ᴶ

“Yes.” ᴹᵐ

She closed the folded page and looked at him.

“Did you?” ᴹᵐ

He could have made the answer easier by turning it into a compliment she would dismiss.

“I liked hearing you ask something you actually wanted answered.” ᴶ

Maomao considered him for a moment.

Then she took his hand again.

“There is another place.” ᴹᵐ

* * *

Mau saw them at the far side of the street when Yuka was describing something Mai had wanted him to come and see later.

He stopped listening halfway through a question.

Yuka followed his glance.

Jinshi was smiling at something Maomao had said. Maomao had his hand and was pointing toward a turn, looking up at him with the impatience of somebody who had already decided they were going together.

Yuka's expression softened.

“We can go the other way.” ʸᵏ

Mau nodded.

They did not call out.

* * *

By the time Maomao decided they should eat, Jinshi had learned three things he could have asked on their behalf and had resisted asking all of them.

They found a place with a table which faced neither the entrance nor a waiting delegation.

He let her choose where to sit.

When the food came, she moved one dish between them and told him to try it before deciding whether he wanted to ask what was in it.

“Should I be concerned?” ᴶ

“About lunch?” ᴹᵐ

“About the way you phrased that.” ᴶ

She took a mouthful from the same dish and looked at him.

He tried it.

It was good enough that he forgot to ask the question.

* * *

They were nearly finished when Maomao asked whether he intended to make an announcement about the afternoon to the first person who saw them returning.

Jinshi put down his cup.

“Would you prefer that I did?” ᴶ

Her expression supplied an immediate answer.

“Then no.” ᴶ

“Good.” ᴹᵐ

He watched her reach for the last small part of the food.

“Though I may be pleased about it privately.” ᴶ

“You have not been particularly private.” ᴹᵐ

He laughed.

She did not tell him to stop.

When they stood to leave, he waited beside the table without holding out his hand and making her answer the question again.

Maomao looked at him, adjusted the folded notes she was carrying and took it anyway.
