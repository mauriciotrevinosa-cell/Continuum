*When: Umaru catches her hand, Rem is still trying to explain why they cannot win a prize by challenging the same person indefinitely.*

“That is a terrible rule.” ᵁ

“It lets other people have a turn.” ᴿᵉ

“Other people can challenge the second-best person.” ᵁ

Rem looked at her.

Umaru looked back with an expression of such untroubled sincerity that Rem had to turn away before she laughed.

They had come to see what was being prepared for the next day's visitors. Most of the stalls were still incomplete. That had not discouraged the Fort, which had discovered that asking the person building a game what it did often resulted in being allowed to try it.

Kenya had immediately decided that trying a game was much the same as winning one. Alice had disagreed at a volume sufficient to settle the matter for everybody nearby.

Now Ori and Chloe were waiting beside the next table, and Umaru had found something which apparently required Rem's immediate attention.

“Come on. You have to see this.” ᵁ

Her fingers closed around Rem's.

Rem followed.

* * *

It should have been an entirely ordinary thing.

People took her hand. She took people's hands. There were doorways to guide someone through, people to help up, children who wished to show her something and had never thought to begin by scheduling a conversation.

Umaru was talking over her shoulder, delighted by the discovery ahead of them.

Rem heard the words.

She also noticed the place where Umaru's thumb rested against her fingers, and the small pressure which told her where they were going before she needed to look.

When they reached the table, Umaru did not immediately release her.

There were rings to throw over pegs. The man arranging them had made the nearer ones simple enough for someone learning to enjoy the attempt, and the farther ones infuriating enough that people who considered themselves very capable would probably spend the afternoon proving otherwise.

“We can do this together,” Umaru said. ᵁ

“I can see that.” ᴿᵉ

“You sound doubtful.” ᵁ

“I was thinking you might permit other people a turn.” ᴿᵉ

Umaru squeezed her hand once before letting go to take a ring.

Rem almost missed the explanation of the rules.

* * *

They were not particularly good at it.

Umaru argued that the first attempt should count as practice. The man running the table agreed, smiling, and then informed her the second would count as her first attempt.

Chloe covered her mouth.

Ori did not bother.

Rem tried next. The ring touched the peg, turned and fell neatly into the space beside it.

“We have identified a structural problem,” Umaru announced. ᵁ

“With your aim,” Ram said behind them. ᴿᵐ

Umaru turned.

Seika stood beside Rem's sister, carrying a folded programme which she seemed to have acquired without volunteering to organise the event printed on it.

Nazuna had paused beneath an awning a little farther down the row. She called back to ask whether Seika wanted to see the next place before they returned.

Seika looked at the game, then at Ram.

“I might stay to watch this disaster.” ˢᵉⁱ

“A sensible use of an afternoon,” Ram replied. ᴿᵐ

Nazuna grinned and went on along the shaded stalls.

Rem watched Seika choose a place beside her sister. Ram moved enough to give her a better view without being asked.

Then Umaru put another ring into Rem's hand.

“We need a strategy.” ᵁ

* * *

By the time they left, they had won nothing, established that the farther peg was entirely possible and persuaded the man to let the next group try without offering them unsolicited advice.

Umaru walked beside Rem.

Her hand swung freely now.

Rem found herself noticing it before she could decide why she was looking.

“Tomorrow,” Umaru said. ᵁ

Rem looked up.

“We come back tomorrow and win.” ᵁ

“We?” ᴿᵉ

Umaru seemed puzzled by the question.

“Unless you don't want to.” ᵁ

“I would like to.” ᴿᵉ

The answer came more quickly than Rem had expected.

Umaru smiled and began telling Ori what they would need to practise. Chloe listened with the serious attention of somebody who intended to improve the plan before Kenya claimed he had invented it.

Rem walked with them until the courtyard came into view.

She was still thinking about the moment before Umaru had let go.
