*When: Echidna asks where she should put the tea, Noise moves the chair she was about to use.*

He had moved it twice already.

The first position had put the back too near the edge of the table. The second had left a narrow place through which Sekhmet would have had to make more effort than anybody wished to require of her.

Now he stood beside it and looked toward Mau.

“Good?” ᴺ

Mau remained at the open threshold.

“Ask her.” ᴹ

Noise looked at Echidna.

She set the covered tea down on the table and examined the chair with far greater seriousness than she had given many things which considered themselves more important.

“Very good.” ᴱᶜ

Noise let go.

* * *

The visit had begun with considerably fewer people.

Rimuru had carried the first borrowed chair into the existing common room while DIO watched, then returned to the doorway. Mau had stood outside his own threshold through all of it, able to see the chair and his brother without needing to imagine that a second Mau should be seated in the room as well.

They had checked the way back. DIO had asked for a pause before the next arrival, and the pause had happened without anyone trying to talk her out of needing it.

By the time Echidna came with the tea, Ashborn had chosen a chair near his own door.

He had not promised to answer questions.

He had simply come out.

Mau had told Echidna that part before she crossed the threshold. She had listened, looked toward the quiet man beside the far wall and asked whether there was a place where he would prefer the cups to be set.

Ashborn had pointed.

That had been enough.

* * *

Minerva looked at DIO for a moment before deciding not to make their first conversation a demand to inspect the place she was recovering from.

“Would you like me nearer or farther away?” ᴹⁱ

DIO looked at the empty seat beside her.

“Nearer. Unless you intend to shout.” ᴰ

“I can avoid shouting.” ᴹⁱ

Echidna raised her eyes from the tea.

Minerva pointed at her without turning.

“I can.” ᴹⁱ

Carmilla came in quietly after Satella. Daphne was already asking what had been brought with the tea. Pride selected a place from which she could hear without being placed on display, and Sekhmet accepted the chair Noise had left easy to reach.

There were more bodies in the room than at any time since the refuge had been offered.

There were still fewer than the room could comfortably hold.

Rimuru checked the ordinary way out one last time, caught Mau's eye and nodded.

The door stayed open.

* * *

Ciel arrived after the first cups had been put down.

Her temporary presentation did not announce itself. She was simply at the place they had agreed, looking at a gathering which had begun without requiring her to make it possible.

DIO moved the plate slightly toward her.

“We kept you a place.” ᴰ

“I can see that.” ᶜ

Noise looked from Ciel to the chair.

“Friend.” ᴺ

“Yes,” Ciel said. ᶜ

He looked toward the rest of the table, evidently considering whether he ought to improve the introduction by giving it a greater audience.

Mau began to smile.

“She heard you.” ᴹ

Noise turned toward him.

Then back toward Ciel.

“Good.” ᴺ

She sat down.

* * *

Echidna asked what everybody wanted to see outside.

It was a wonderfully simple question to make a complicated gathering consider.

DIO wanted food whose name she had not yet heard. Daphne immediately approved the principle and began asking which names DIO had heard so far, so they would not waste an opportunity through inadequate preparation.

Minerva wanted to see a competition people could leave smiling. Carmilla spoke so softly that Noise leaned closer, then turned the cup beside him a little to make room for her hands.

Satella wanted to know whether they could see the lights when evening came.

“We can ask Mau to take the longer way back,” Rimuru said. ᴿ

Mau looked at him from the threshold.

“Will Yuka be there?” ᴹ

“I had assumed you would find a way.” ᴿ

Echidna hid a smile behind her cup.

Pride asked what happened if two people wanted different things at the same time.

Noise looked at the table with the sudden alertness of someone who had discovered a problem worth solving.

“We decide together,” Ciel said before he could begin improving the room. ᶜ

He looked at her.

“Or we do different things,” she added. “I can remain here if I wish. You can watch something else. Nobody is required to enjoy every minute in the same way.” ᶜ

Noise considered that for a long moment.

“Still friends?” ᴺ

The question quieted the table.

DIO answered first.

“Yes.” ᴰ

Ciel put her hand on the table near his.

“Still friends.” ᶜ

* * *

Mau needed to look away for a moment.

Rimuru saw and did him the kindness of finding something unimportant to adjust beside the doorway.

When Mau looked back, Noise was listening to Daphne describe the advantages of a route planned around several meals rather than one.

He looked happy.

Not triumphant. There was no new power waiting to be named, no barrier he had finally convinced into opening, no injury which had required the world to surrender an answer.

He had made space beside a table.

People had come.

“You may sit down too,” Echidna told Mau. ᴱᶜ

He looked toward the chair outside the threshold.

Rimuru had put it there earlier.

Mau sat.

* * *

The tea lasted long enough for Sekhmet to object that someone had placed the good plate too far away.

Noise moved it immediately.

She examined the new position, accepted a small piece and told him he was a considerate host.

He stood very still.

“Host?” ᴺ

“Yes.” ˢᵏ

Noise looked toward Mau.

Mau nodded.

The dark figure looked back at the room as though seeing the same chairs arranged in an entirely different way.

Ashborn had been quiet through most of the conversation. When the plate came within reach, he took something from it without being watched into making a decision.

Echidna noticed. She poured another cup and moved it to the place he had indicated at the beginning.

She did not ask him what he had been before he came to sit with them.

* * *

Later, she did ask Noise a question.

“What would you like to be called?” ᴱᶜ

Mau looked up.

DIO leaned back with the satisfaction of somebody whose earlier idea had found an ally.

“We have been thinking about that.” ᴰ

Noise glanced toward his host, then toward the table.

“Not now,” he said. ᴺ

Echidna inclined her head.

“Then not now.” ᴱᶜ

He relaxed a little.

“Thinking.” ᴺ

“So are we,” DIO assured him. ᴰ

Mau watched him accept the promise without hearing a deadline inside it.

Outside the room, the city was beginning to call people toward the next part of the morning. They could hear none of it unless Mau chose to let them listen, and for a little while he left the sounds alone.

There was already enough to hear.

* * *

They left by the same way they had come.

Minerva asked DIO whether she would like another visit before they went home. DIO said she would like to be asked again when she knew how tired she was. Minerva accepted the answer and thanked her for the tea.

Carmilla thanked Noise.

Then Satella did. Then Pride, quietly enough that the thanks belonged to him rather than the room.

When Echidna collected what remained of the cups, Noise reached for the nearest one.

“I can do that,” she said. ᴱᶜ

“Host,” he replied. ᴺ

She let him carry it.

Mau waited until the last borrowed chair had come back through before standing up. Inside, Ashborn remained beside his own door with the cup he had not yet finished. Ciel had chosen to stay with DIO for a little longer.

Noise looked from them to Mau.

“Good?” ᴺ

Mau could still remember a table where he had sat opposite that same darkness and tried to explain that being protected was not the same as being allowed to live.

This time he did not need a speech.

“Very good.” ᴹ
