*When: Gojo arrives to collect her, he is carrying a folded sheet of paper which Shoko sincerely hopes is not a plan.*

“What is that?” ˢ

“A list.” ᴳ

“Of patients?” ˢ

“Of things we might do.” ᴳ

She looked at him.

He unfolded it just enough to establish that the writing existed, then folded it again before she could read the whole page.

“There are options.” ᴳ

“How reassuring.” ˢ

Maomao came through the inner doorway with the small things Shoko had asked her to check. Nothing needed her to stay. Nobody was waiting to be seen, and the next person coming back from the venue knew where Maomao would be.

Shoko thanked her.

Gojo looked at the clean table, the packed bag and the woman standing in front of him.

“We can actually leave?” ᴳ

“If you keep asking, I might reconsider.” ˢ

He opened the door.

* * *

The list was not bad.

Shoko gave him that after making him show it to her on the way. There was somewhere to eat, somewhere people had begun putting up things to look at and one place he had described so poorly that she suspected he had written it down because the description entertained him.

None of it required her to supervise him fighting somebody.

She pointed that out.

“I am very considerate.” ᴳ

“You have been taking notes.” ˢ

“From the finest available examples.” ᴳ

“Mau?” ˢ

Gojo put a hand against his chest.

“You might have allowed me a moment to imply someone grander.” ᴳ

“He's a better teacher than you.” ˢ

He looked at her.

She smiled.

“Don't tell him.” ˢ

Gojo laughed, and the sound surprised her enough to remind her that she had been waiting to hear it without somebody almost dying first.

* * *

They chose the place to eat before either of them became hungry enough to make choosing a nuisance.

Gojo tried to order something for her, caught her expression and redirected the sentence into asking what she wanted.

Shoko rested her chin on her hand.

“There is hope.” ˢ

“You could be less delighted by my progress.” ᴳ

“I could.” ˢ

She chose something she had not tried before. He chose something sweet enough that she asked whether it was intended to be the whole meal.

“We are celebrating.” ᴳ

“What?” ˢ

He looked at her across the table.

For a moment she expected him to give the answer to somebody else's festival. The city, Rimuru, their extraordinary luck in having arrived at a place where people were preparing games instead of graves.

“You came,” he said. ᴳ

Shoko looked down at the menu she had already finished using.

“You did ask.” ˢ

“I intend to remember that it worked.” ᴳ

* * *

The conversation found a way through things they could both recognise.

How much effort certain people put into pretending not to be pleased by attention. Which of the new visitors would prove impossible to seat quietly at a tournament. Whether Yuta had acquired the patience of an old man or had always been destined to look as though he was apologising for the people around him.

Shoko told Gojo about the expression Yuka had worn when Mau tried Shion's dinner.

He complained that nobody had fetched him.

“You were discussing something with Yuta.” ˢ

“You could have said there was an emergency.” ᴳ

“It tasted good.” ˢ

“A missed emergency.” ᴳ

She laughed before she could improve the answer.

Gojo watched her.

It was not the first time he had done it lately. This time he did not turn away quickly enough to make it possible for both of them to pretend he had been looking past her.

“What?” she asked. ˢ

He shook his head.

“Nothing bad.” ᴳ

* * *

They walked after eating.

There were people moving displays into places which had apparently been chosen by somebody who had not considered the width of the street. Gojo offered advice from a distance until Shoko told him he could either help carry the thing or allow the people carrying it to continue without his commentary.

He helped.

She waited at the side, watching two strangers discover that the cheerful man giving opinions could lift the problem they had been arguing about.

When he returned, she handed him the small packet he had left with her.

“Very heroic.” ˢ

“You may tell people.” ᴳ

“They saw.” ˢ

“A modest audience.” ᴳ

She took a small piece from his packet before giving it back.

He looked delighted by the theft.

* * *

At the next quiet place, she asked whether he had meant to say something earlier.

Gojo looked toward the street.

“I was thinking you sound more like yourself.” ᴳ

Shoko followed his glance without needing to find what he was looking at.

“Which version?” ˢ

The question stayed between them for a few steps.

“One I liked hearing,” he said. ᴳ

She let out a small breath.

There were versions of both of them she missed. Days she could remember in the shape of somebody else's empty place, jokes which had become difficult because the person who should have objected to them was not there to object.

She did not want to spend this afternoon proving that she remembered the loss.

He already knew.

“I'm still here,” she said. ˢ

Gojo looked at her.

“I know.” ᴳ

“Then stop sounding surprised when you find me.” ˢ

He was quiet for a moment.

Then he nodded.

* * *

They reached a stretch where the stalls had not yet acquired their crowd.

Shoko slowed to look at something hung beneath a shaded awning. Gojo waited beside her. Neither of them needed the thing enough to ask the person arranging it to stop what they were doing.

“You were right about the list,” he said. ᴳ

“That it was tolerable?” ˢ

“That I had help.” ᴳ

She turned.

He held the folded page loosely in one hand.

“I asked Mau where he would take someone if he wanted them to have a good afternoon.” ᴳ

Shoko looked at him for long enough that he began smiling in defence.

“He asked whether I knew anything she liked,” Gojo added. ᴳ

“An excellent teacher.” ˢ

“I am coming around to your position.” ᴳ

“Did you?” ˢ

He looked at her.

“Know?” she asked. ˢ

His smile changed.

“Some things. I'd like to find out the rest.” ᴳ

* * *

Shoko looked back toward the awning.

She could have made a joke. He had left her more than enough room to put the afternoon away among the things people said when they were happy and did not intend to be held to afterward.

Instead, she held out her hand.

Gojo looked down.

“You are becoming slow,” she said. ˢ

He took it.

His hand was warm. There was no technique between their palms, no careful joke about how close a person could get. He folded the list with his free hand and put it away.

“Where next?” he asked. ᴳ

Shoko considered the afternoon still waiting in front of them.

“I thought you had options.” ˢ

“I do.” ᴳ

“Then show me another.” ˢ

They went together.
