## Chapter 9 — What the Road Brought

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S8 v0.1 (2026-10-30)

*When: Hollis arrives, the first person he asks to see is somebody who knows where the carts can go.*

He had brought Tamsin, two contradictory reports about the road and a visitor who had spent the last part of the journey discovering how little use an excellent old map could be to a person standing in a new place.

Mjollmile introduced himself to the man directing arrivals, explained which cart required attention first and only then asked whether Rimuru had a moment.

Hollis liked him more after that.

“He may have a moment,” he said. “Keeping it will be the difficult part.” ᴴᵒ

“That also sounds familiar.” ᴹʲ

Tamsin looked at the two of them.

“I am going to find the place we're meant to sleep before either of you decides the carts need a committee.” ᵀᵃ

Hollis gave her the paper.

She took it, looked at the name written beside the guest entrance and asked the man actually standing there whether it was still correct.

It was.

They had made a promising start.

* * *

Rimuru found them while Mau was helping move the last small bag out of the way of a wheel.

The reunion interrupted what Mjollmile had intended to say so completely that the first few words became quite different ones.

Rimuru listened to those instead.

Mau stepped away from the cart, waited until there was room and accepted the introduction when it came. He could tell the man knew considerably more about his brother than a name on an invitation. He could also tell from the questions he asked that he had not decided this made him an authority on everything Rimuru had done since leaving.

Hollis held up one of the papers.

“Before you ask, this route is wrong.” ᴴᵒ

Rimuru looked at it.

“All of it?” ᴿ

“Enough.” ᴴᵒ

Mjollmile produced the second.

“The destination is right on mine.” ᴹʲ

“That is generous of it,” Mau said. ᴹ

Hollis laughed.

* * *

They found a table where a person could put down papers without displacing somebody's lunch.

The reports did not agree on the direction from which the broken stretch had been approached. They agreed that it was broken. A cart had turned back. People travelling on foot had found another way, but nobody at the table had personally followed it far enough to promise that a loaded wagon would manage the same journey.

Rimuru marked the difference.

Mjollmile asked about the people who had turned back.

Hollis answered what he knew and stopped where he did not.

Mau watched the conversation begin to build something more useful than the confident map.

No one asked him to fly over the horizon and finish the uncertainty before supper.

He was grateful enough for that to volunteer the smaller thing he could actually offer: the route their own group had walked and the markers they had checked on the way.

Tamsin returned while he was describing the second day's approach.

“Rooms found,” she said. “And somebody has already asked what we're selling.” ᵀᵃ

Hollis looked at the bags.

“I was hoping to unpack before becoming disappointing.” ᴴᵒ

* * *

More people arrived before the papers had been put away.

Youm came with Mjurran, looking toward the city with the expression of somebody who had expected a familiar place to make more sense of the unfamiliar journey. Rimuru went to meet them himself.

Fuze arrived later, tired enough that the chair offered to him was more welcome than the first question.

Mau learned the names as people gave them. There were rulers and people whose work would continue whether a ruler found time to smile at them or not. A person could ask about a road because it carried trade, or because somebody had last been seen walking along it.

Those were not always different questions.

When one of the arrivals recognised the rumour attached to Mau's name, Rimuru began to answer.

Mau touched his arm.

“You can ask me,” he told the visitor. ᴹ

The man looked embarrassed.

“I was told there had been a sun.”

Mau sighed.

From the other side of the table, Hollis said, “He does that when people mention the sun.” ᴴᵒ

Rimuru began laughing before Mau could object.

* * *

The questions about goods took longer.

Mjollmile wanted to see a sample before discussing how it might be sold. Hollis wanted to know who would be expected to stand behind a promise if somebody carried an unsuitable weapon into a dangerous place. Tamsin asked which currencies the visitors actually possessed, rather than which ones their old books considered respectable.

Those questions interested Gazel's people when one of them came to find the new arrivals.

Mau listened until somebody asked how quickly the Inn could produce a larger order.

“We haven't accepted one,” he said. ᴹ

The questioner paused.

“And the people making things need to decide what they want to make.” ᴹ

Hollis nodded without turning the answer into an apology.

“We can begin by showing you what exists.” ᴴᵒ

Mjollmile accepted that readily. He had brought questions of his own about what the new roads would permit, what could be replaced and what should not be promised until somebody had tried it.

Rimuru sat down again.

The discussion became more useful as soon as it stopped pretending anybody at the table had a whole world ready to deliver.

* * *

Yuka found Mau after the visitors had begun finding their rooms.

She had Shion's invitation for the next workshop visit, a story about something Mai intended to tell him herself and the patient expression she used when he had been about to agree to one more small task.

“You promised me an afternoon.” ʸᵏ

“I have some of one left.” ᴹ

“I noticed.” ʸᵏ

He put the paper in his hand down on the table.

Hollis looked at Mjollmile.

“That is the other arrangement you should learn.” ᴴᵒ

Yuka smiled sweetly at him.

“I heard that.” ʸᵏ

“I was being helpful.” ᴴᵒ

“Of course.” ʸᵏ

She took Mau's hand and drew him away before anybody discovered a matter which required his remaining fingers.

* * *

Outside, the space between the courtyards had acquired more people than it held that morning.

Some had come to compete. Some had come because they had heard that Rimuru was alive and wanted to see him before deciding what they should ask next. A few had come with things to show, hopeful that somebody else's working idea might help with the thing they had lost.

Mau looked toward his brother through the open doorway.

Rimuru had found another chair for Fuze. He was listening with both hands around his cup.

Yuka squeezed Mau's hand.

“He looks happy.” ʸᵏ

Mau nodded.

Then he let her lead him into the afternoon they had left.
