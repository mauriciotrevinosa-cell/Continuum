“Nothing,” he said. ᴹ

She looked at him.

“For one evening.” ᴹ

Yuka considered the offer with more care than he had expected.

“You can manage that?” ʸᵏ

“I am willing to attempt something difficult.” ᴹ

“How reassuring.” ʸᵏ

She moved closer until their shoulders touched.

Mau put his hand around hers and looked toward the turn he had chosen earlier, before being useful had briefly become the way he intended to spend the whole day.

“This way.” ᴹ

* * *

The place he had found did not have a particularly good view of anything important.

That was part of why he liked it.

The tables stood a little away from the busiest street. People passing could see where they were going without seeing what was on his plate. Somewhere behind the building, somebody was carrying out a task which occasionally required a short argument and then resumed in the reassuring absence of applause.

Yuka looked around when they sat down.

“You found somewhere we won't be discovered being impressive.” ʸᵏ

“I have high hopes.” ᴹ

“Rimuru could still find us.” ʸᵏ

“He would need a convincing reason.” ᴹ

“He can become very small.” ʸᵏ

Mau considered that problem.

“We could deny the table belongs to us.” ᴹ

Yuka laughed and reached for the things they had been given to choose from.

* * *

They ordered without asking what would be most sensible to reproduce at home.

Mau almost asked a question about one of the ingredients. Yuka saw him begin, raised an eyebrow and let him discover that wanting to know did not require him to stop enjoying the evening until he had found out.

He ordered it anyway.

“Good,” she said. ʸᵏ

“You sound proud of me.” ᴹ

“I am.” ʸᵏ

She said it simply enough that he looked at her for a moment before finding something clever to say and deciding he preferred not to use it.

The light reached her face from one side. He could see the colour in her cheek, in the small familiar things on her wrist. Beyond her, the room settled into the shades he had learned to stop expecting to change.

Yuka noticed where his eyes went.

She put her hand on the table between them.

He took it.

Neither of them had to explain the gesture before continuing their conversation.

* * *

She told him about the workshop.

Not everything she and Mai had discussed would interest the whole house. Some of it had interested Yuka precisely because it belonged to something she might choose for herself.

Mau asked what she had liked best.

She described a small space arranged so that the people working there could leave things half-finished without moving the room out of the way of dinner.

“You want somewhere like that?” he asked. ᴹ

“A little. It doesn't have to become a whole workshop.” ʸᵏ

“It could be a table nobody clears without asking.” ᴹ

Yuka looked pleased by how quickly he had understood.

“With a place beside it where you can sit and be distracting.” ʸᵏ

“A dedicated position.” ᴹ

“You would take it very seriously.” ʸᵏ

“I already have experience.” ᴹ

* * *

Their food arrived before the table became the first item in a room they had not yet decided to build.

Yuka tried his choice. He tried hers. They disagreed about which was better and continued eating from both until the disagreement became difficult to defend.

For a while the conversation was only that.

He liked hearing her say she wanted more of something while there was still some left to offer. He liked the way she looked at him when he attempted to move the better part of his plate toward hers without being noticed.

“I can ask for it,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then stop pretending the food is relocating itself.” ʸᵏ

Mau looked down at the plate.

“A troubling phenomenon.” ᴹ

“Very.” ʸᵏ

She took the piece he had moved and gave him something from her own.

* * *

When they had finished the first part of the meal, Yuka asked what he wanted to keep if the house changed.

Mau looked toward her.

“People?” ᴹ

“I am assuming we keep the people.” ʸᵏ

“A sound beginning.” ᴹ

“Mau.” ʸᵏ

He smiled.

Then he took the question properly.

The tree came first. Not because it required him to explain a memory to her, but because he could imagine the house having grown without the centre of it becoming a place people only pointed toward from farther away.

The common room came next. Close enough that choosing to be alone did not mean arranging to be absent from everybody's life.

“And a door,” he said. ᴹ

Yuka waited.

“One we can close because we want a quiet evening.” ᴹ

Her thumb moved against his hand.

“We have that now.” ʸᵏ

“I know. I want to keep it.” ᴹ

* * *

She wanted her family close.

She said it without apologising for what it might mean for the distance between their room and everybody else's. Mau had never asked her to become easier to love by wanting fewer people in her life.

He asked how close.

“Close enough that I can visit without deciding I ought to have brought a bag.” ʸᵏ

“Mai might find that alarming.” ᴹ

“Mai would put a chair in the doorway and demand to know why I hadn't come sooner.” ʸᵏ

Mau laughed.

Yuka looked down at their hands.

“But I want our space too.” ʸᵏ

He waited, letting her choose the next part.

“Something we decide together. Not where there happens to be room after everybody else has finished choosing.” ʸᵏ

“Then we will.” ᴹ

She looked up.

“You don't have to solve it tonight.” ʸᵏ

“I wasn't solving it. I was agreeing with you.” ᴹ

Yuka smiled.

“You may continue.” ʸᵏ

* * *

They took the longer way back.

Inside, DIO had asked whether they could see the evening lights. Mau let the shared view open by the small amount he had promised and told Yuka why he had slowed near the end of the street.

She moved her hand from his palm to the bend of his arm.

“Then they should get a good view.” ʸᵏ

They stood together until the people inside had had enough time to choose whether they wanted to keep watching.

Nobody asked Noise to build anything.

Nobody asked Ciel to explain how it worked.

When they returned to the guest courtyard, Mau was carrying nothing he had not brought with him.

Yuka looked at his empty hands.

“You did it.” ʸᵏ

“Something difficult,” he reminded her. ᴹ

She pulled him closer and kissed him beside their door.

For a moment, there was nothing he could think to improve.
