*When: Bocchi finds him, she has prepared the first sentence so carefully that Mau knows not to interrupt it.*

“They're playing again this afternoon.” ᴮ

He put down the page he had just finished reading.

“The people you heard yesterday?” ᴹ

Bocchi nodded.

“If you still wanted to come. I mean, if you haven't already decided to watch somebody else do something more—” ᴮ

“I wanted to come.” ᴹ

She stopped.

Mau looked toward Yuka, who was sitting on the low wall beside the guest entrance and asking Mai a question which had already made Mai begin drawing in the air.

“Should I ask her?” ᴹ

Bocchi nodded again, with enough immediate relief to make him glad he had asked rather than assumed.

* * *

They found the place before the music began.

Kita was already there with Nijika and Ryo. Nijika had discovered someone whose instrument interested her enough to ask permission before touching it. Ryo was listening to an explanation with the remarkably attentive expression she sometimes used when something might be obtained more cheaply through patience.

Seika stood back from the group.

“Nobody is volunteering for a show,” she told Mau as he approached. ˢᵉⁱ

“I came to listen.” ᴹ

“Good.” ˢᵉⁱ

Bocchi looked grateful to both of them.

Yuka found room beside her where they would not block the path. Mau stood on Bocchi's other side and waited while the people setting up finished disagreeing about which part should begin first.

He had heard much more ominous arguments resolved with considerably less care.

This one ended with someone laughing and moving a chair.

* * *

The first piece was not the one Bocchi had brought them to hear.

Mau knew from the way she listened. She was interested, but she kept glancing toward the person waiting with a different instrument, checking whether they had begun preparing the next part.

He let her listen without asking her to explain each thing he did not recognise.

Yuka leaned slightly toward him when the rhythm changed.

Mau looked at her.

She smiled and moved her fingers once against her own knee, following the part she had noticed.

On his other side, Bocchi had gone very still.

The second piece had begun.

* * *

It was quieter than he had expected.

The first notes left space before the answer. A second sound came beneath them, easy to miss until the pattern returned and he found himself waiting for it. Nobody at the front seemed anxious about whether the audience could count the difficulty correctly.

They simply played it.

Bocchi's hand moved against the hem of her sleeve.

Mau saw the small motion without making her aware he had seen it. She was following something he could not have named, finding the shape of another person's idea with the part of herself she usually needed a guitar to show him.

Halfway through, she looked up at him.

He smiled.

She looked back at the music before her own smile had quite finished arriving.

* * *

Afterward, Kita asked the first question.

Nijika asked the next. By the time the conversation came back to the beginning of the piece, Bocchi had moved closer without apparently noticing she had done it.

One of the musicians showed her the instrument's neck and explained where the sound changed.

She looked at their hand.

Then she asked whether the earlier movement had belonged to the same phrase.

Mau watched the musician look properly at her for the first time.

“You heard that?”

Bocchi nodded.

They began explaining again, this time without leaving the difficult part out.

Mau stepped a little farther back with Yuka.

* * *

“You look pleased,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I am.” ᴹ

Yuka followed his glance.

Bocchi was pointing now, then immediately withdrawing the hand as though she had interrupted something by asking. The musician shook their head, repeated the movement and waited while she listened.

Yuka smiled.

“She wanted to show you.” ʸᵏ

“I'm glad she asked.” ᴹ

“You should tell her.” ʸᵏ

He looked at her.

“Without making it a speech,” she added. ʸᵏ

Mau laughed softly.

“You know me alarmingly well.” ᴹ

* * *

When Bocchi came back, she had the expression of somebody returning from much farther away than the other side of a small street.

“They said I could listen again tomorrow.” ᴮ

“Did you want to?” Mau asked. ᴹ

She nodded before remembering tomorrow contained other things.

“I can come after the matches. Or before. I should check when—” ᴮ

“We can find out.” ᴹ

He waited until she had stopped preparing an apology for wanting two parts of the same day.

“Thank you for bringing us.” ᴹ

Bocchi looked at him.

“It was good,” he said. ᴹ

She smiled so plainly that he was very glad Yuka had told him to keep the speech short.

* * *

They stayed while Nijika finished her questions.

Ryo had acquired no instrument, which Seika appeared to consider a satisfactory outcome. Kita asked whether anybody wanted to walk with the musicians to see where the next small performance would be, and the answer became a group of people moving down the street rather than a decision which needed a leader.

Marin and Wakana appeared coming the other way.

Marin began describing a piece of clothing she had seen before finishing her greeting. Wakana listened while Nijika asked where it was, then quietly corrected the turn so that they would actually find it.

Yuka asked whether Mai had been told.

“Not yet,” Marin said, with the pleasure of someone discovering another person who would enjoy being found. ᴹᵃʳ

* * *

Mau walked beside Bocchi until they reached the place where the group would split.

She had begun describing a part she wanted to try when they went home. Halfway through, she stopped and looked at him as though remembering he might not know enough to understand why it mattered.

“You can show me,” he said. ᴹ

“It might take a while.” ᴮ

“Then we'll sit down.” ᴹ

Yuka slipped her hand into his on the other side.

Bocchi looked at the two of them, then back toward the people waiting for her.

“Okay.” ᴮ

She went to join them.

Mau watched her catch up to Kita, already beginning the explanation again, and found that he was looking forward to an ordinary afternoon at home with a song he did not yet know.
