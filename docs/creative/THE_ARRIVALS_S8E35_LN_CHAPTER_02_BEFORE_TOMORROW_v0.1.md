*When: Ori knocks, Yuka is trying to persuade Mau that tomorrow's rules will remain the same if he stops reading them for the evening.*

He looked up from the page.

Yuka opened the door.

Ori stood with Frieren in the passage, holding something wrapped in a small piece of cloth.

“I wanted to come before tomorrow.” ᴼ

Mau put the rules down.

“Come in.” ᴹ

* * *

Frieren stayed by the door long enough to ask when Ori intended to return.

Ori considered the question, looked at the room and chose an answer which allowed her to see the paper boat without promising the whole evening to somebody else's idea of bedtime.

Frieren accepted it.

“I'll be in the courtyard.” ᶠʳ

Ori nodded.

Then she went to the window.

The boat had dried into a shape which suggested the voyage had been much longer than the basin. One edge had lifted higher than the other, and the fold at the front no longer quite agreed with the fold behind it.

Ori looked at it for a while.

“It still looks like a boat.” ᴼ

“I thought so,” Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Mau came to stand beside them.

“A very experienced one.” ᴹ

Ori glanced up at him, decided she approved of the description and asked whether she could take it home.

* * *

They found a stiff piece of the wrapping they had used for their own travel things.

Yuka held it flat while Ori decided which part would support the boat without folding the damaged edge. Mau offered a suggestion which Ori tested by looking rather than touching, then corrected before he could become too pleased with himself.

“This side,” she said. ᴼ

Mau moved it.

“Better?” ᴹ

She nodded.

They put the boat down together.

It took much less time than making it had taken. Ori seemed satisfied by that, as though a thing which had already survived its adventure deserved to be allowed an easier trip afterward.

She sat on the edge of the chair and unwrapped what she had brought.

It was a small folded drawing.

* * *

Mau recognised himself after Ori pointed out which person was him.

The figure beside him was Yuka. He had been spared the need to identify the rest by a set of marks which Ori explained in an order neither adult had predicted.

“That one is the place you have to stay inside,” she said. ᴼ

“The boundary?” ᴹ

Ori nodded.

“They said if you go out you lose.” ᴼ

“That's right.” ᴹ

She looked at him to establish that this important piece of information had reached the person most likely to need it.

Yuka put a hand over her smile.

“I think he understands.” ʸᵏ

“He flies,” Ori said. ᴼ

Mau looked at the drawing again.

He could see why she had wanted to make the reminder clear.

* * *

Ori had questions about tomorrow.

Where they would sit. Whether she could clap for two people if both were from home. Whether being pleased that someone had won meant the person who lost would feel bad if they heard her.

Mau answered the first from the arrangement they had actually seen. Yuka answered the second before he could make it complicated.

“You can clap for whoever you want.” ʸᵏ

Ori looked toward her.

“Both?” ᴼ

“Both.” ʸᵏ

Mau turned the drawing so it lay where all three of them could see it.

“I think I'd like hearing you happy,” he said. ᴹ

Ori considered that.

“Even if you lose?” ᴼ

“Even then.” ᴹ

She leaned against the back of the chair, apparently deciding he had understood the important part of that question too.

* * *

They stayed together until the sound of somebody arriving in the courtyard made Ori sit up.

It was not a warning.

It was the first of two large baskets being carried through the entrance.

Umaru appeared before the second reached the table. Milim came beside her. Chloe followed with Kenya and Alice, who had apparently been given responsibilities concerning the safe arrival of the contents and intended to take them very seriously.

Rimuru stood at the far end of the courtyard looking deeply pleased with how much trouble a piece of paper had caused him.

“Your settlement,” Yuka said. ʸᵏ

Ori went to the door to look.

Mau followed her with the carefully supported boat.

* * *

Frieren was where she had said she would be.

Himmel stood beside her, listening to something she was telling him about the next day's contest. His expression suggested that he had no intention of improving the evening by pointing out how long she had been talking about a fight she claimed not to be especially concerned about.

Ori went to show her Mum the boat.

Frieren took the support from Mau and let Ori explain what they had done to make it ready for home. She listened to the whole explanation. When Ori finished, she asked whether the place they put it tonight would keep it safe from the baskets.

Ori looked toward Umaru.

“Maybe upstairs.” ᴼ

“I think so too.” ᶠʳ

Himmel offered to walk the little distance with them.

Ori took Frieren's hand and told him which fold had nearly come apart.

* * *

Before they went, she came back to Mau.

For a moment she seemed uncertain whether the visit had already counted as what she had come to say.

“Good luck tomorrow.” ᴼ

Mau crouched enough to meet her eyes without making her look up.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“And Yuka too.” ᴼ

Yuka smiled.

“I'll remember.” ʸᵏ

Ori looked at them both once more, satisfied, and went to her Mum.

Mau watched the three of them cross the courtyard before Yuka touched his arm.

* * *

They did not take the rules upstairs.

Yuka noticed and said nothing until Mau had closed their door.

“A remarkable recovery,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I received very good advice.” ᴹ

“From me?” ʸᵏ

“Among others.” ᴹ

She drew him toward the bed by the front of his shirt.

“Then you can listen to the rest of mine.” ʸᵏ

He sat beside her.

Outside, the Fort was dividing its compensation with an energy which suggested tomorrow's cheering would not lack preparation. Somewhere below, Rimuru protested that an agreement to provide sweets was not an agreement to taste-test every piece first.

Yuka leaned against Mau's shoulder.

“You sound happy,” she said. ʸᵏ

“I haven't said anything.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸᵏ

He turned enough to kiss her hair.

Tomorrow, there would be people waiting to see what he could do.

Tonight, she moved closer, and he put his arm around her without needing to do anything else at all.
