*When: Masayuki's name is announced, the applause begins before anyone has explained what he can do.*

Mau looked across the floor.

The man waiting on the other side did not look especially pleased by this advantage. He had listened to Beretta repeat the surrender rule twice. Now he stood just inside his starting mark, holding his weapon with the careful attention of somebody who knew the crowd had made assumptions about what came next.

Mau knew that expression.

He had worn a version of it while people argued over whether he was a sun.

Behind the barrier, somebody called Masayuki's name again.

Rimuru put his face in one hand.

* * *

The morning had begun with the draw.

There had been enough names on the board for people to find their own first, then the one beside it, then somebody else's which promised a fight they had not known to hope for.

Yuka had found Souei and gone quiet in a way Mau recognised as interest. Fern had looked at Frieren, who had returned an entirely innocent expression. Gojo had complained about the handwriting used for his name despite having supplied it himself.

The Fort had discovered that choosing seats together required a more complicated negotiation than the war reparations.

Now they were there.

The lights did not imitate a sun closely enough to create the conditions Nazuna had asked to avoid. The audience sat behind the host's separate protection. People who wished to leave could leave without stepping through the fighting floor. Shoko had her way into the return room, and Maomao knew when her own turn began.

Mau had walked the exit before putting on the bracelet.

Then he had found Yuka in the seats.

She was smiling at him.

* * *

Beretta asked both men whether they were ready.

Masayuki took a breath.

Mau nodded.

The signal sounded.

He moved first, without drawing the daggers.

It should have been a simple approach. A step to the outside of the weapon, a change in angle before the hand holding it could become a useful threat. He had enough room to make the first attack a question rather than an injury.

Halfway in, he hesitated.

Masayuki's sword came up.

Mau turned his wrist out of its path and found himself farther from the position he had meant to take.

The audience cheered.

Across the field, Masayuki looked almost as surprised as Mau felt.

* * *

Mau tried the other side.

His foot caught the minute change in the surface where the first turn had disturbed the ground. It was hardly enough to deserve the word *stumble*. Against someone waiting for the opportunity, it would have been enough to matter.

Masayuki stepped back rather than taking the opening.

Mau recovered and stopped advancing.

He could hear the explanation forming in the seats. A masterful retreat. An opponent whose confidence had begun to fail. The hero was allowing the stranger to discover the difference between them.

For a moment, Mau almost agreed.

It felt reasonable.

That was what made him look up.

*That pause did not belong to the movement you chose,* Ciel said.

Her voice stayed where it belonged, private and familiar.

Mau watched the man across from him shift his weight.

*I know.*

He did now.

* * *

He took another step and felt the same reluctance arrive with it.

There were people one did not wish to hurt. That was ordinary. He had entered a contest with more than a dozen of them.

This was different.

His attention kept supplying Masayuki with the explanation most favourable to him. A mistake became restraint. A retreat became an invitation. Mau's own hesitation began to feel like the sensible recognition of something he had not yet seen.

He closed the small active copied operation he had kept ready at his hand.

The wheel appeared above him.

The crowd made a new sound.

Masayuki looked up.

“That looks concerning.” ᴹᵃ

“I'm trying to understand something.” ᴹ

“So am I.” ᴹᵃ

Mau almost laughed.

It helped.

* * *

The next approach was slower on purpose.

He let the feeling arrive. Let himself recognise its direction without asking Ciel to turn another person's entire existence into a weakness he could use before the man had moved.

Masayuki lifted the sword again.

Mau stopped at its reach.

The wheel turned.

Nothing disappeared from the room. The cheers did not stop. Masayuki did not become smaller, less fortunate or suddenly unworthy of the people who were pleased to see him.

Mau simply felt a little more space between the man's raised hand and the conclusion his own mind had been trying to give it.

He could choose the next movement again.

* * *

From the seats, Yuka saw his shoulders loosen.

She had been watching the pauses rather than the sword. They looked wrong in a way she could not have explained to someone who had only seen him fight once.

Now he moved, and she recognised him.

Beside her, Maki leaned forward a fraction.

“There.” ᴹᵏ

Yuta nodded.

Ori looked from one to the other.

Yuka put her hand lightly against the girl's sleeve.

“He found something.” ʸᵏ

On the other side of the room, a man who had spent most of the first minute praising Masayuki began explaining that this was precisely the sort of opponent a hero ought to face.

Rimuru made a sound which might have been agreement if he had not been trying so hard not to laugh.

* * *

Mau reached for the sword wrist.

Masayuki's retreat took him across the place where Mau's earlier turn had scuffed the floor. His foot slid, his balance broke and the sword changed direction through an opening Mau had not intended to leave.

The edge caught his forearm.

Pain arrived before the blood.

Mau let go, turned with it and came away with a narrow cut instead of a hand he would have needed to repair.

The audience rose halfway out of its seats.

Masayuki looked horrified.

“Sorry.” ᴹᵃ

“It's a fight.” ᴹ

“I know. I just—” ᴹᵃ

Mau looked at the cut, closed it with the familiar effort and nodded toward the weapon.

“Good angle.” ᴹ

Masayuki gave him an expression of such genuine uncertainty that Mau decided not to make him accept credit for it twice.

The wheel had helped him keep his own judgement.

It had not taken the luck out of the man's feet.

* * *

He stopped trying to make a perfect approach.

There was no need.

He could give the next exchange two safe answers instead of one spectacular one. If the sword arrived early, he would turn it. If it arrived late, he would already be inside. He left enough room for a bad step to become another route rather than the end of the route he had chosen.

Masayuki moved back.

Mau followed.

The sword rose, touched his guarding hand and slid along the angle he gave it. His other hand reached the wrist before the accidental second turn could begin.

He stopped the weapon without striking the person holding it.

For a moment, both men stood inside the same small quiet space.

Mau lifted two fingers and tapped Masayuki lightly on the cheek.

“Would you like to stop?” ᴹ

Masayuki looked at the hand around his wrist.

Then toward Beretta.

“Yes,” he said, with considerable feeling. ᴹᵃ

* * *

Mau released him before the result was announced.

The wheel disappeared.

The applause was confused for approximately as long as it took somebody to decide the generosity of the surrender had itself been an excellent demonstration of character.

Masayuki closed his eyes.

Mau put a hand against his shoulder, carefully enough not to turn the congratulations into another accident.

“Are you all right?” ᴹ

“Mostly.” ᴹᵃ

“You can sit down now.” ᴹ

Masayuki looked toward the seats with a relief Mau could not have mistaken for a performance.

“That is the best part of losing I've heard yet.” ᴹᵃ

* * *

Beretta checked them before letting them leave the return area.

Shoko looked at Mau's repaired arm, asked whether the movement was ordinary and accepted the answer after seeing him make it. No level had changed. There was no reward waiting behind the applause. He had spent a little, learned a little and finished with an opponent who could share a joke.

He could get used to this.

“You noticed?” ᴿ

“I suspect his afternoon is more tiring than mine.” ᴹ
