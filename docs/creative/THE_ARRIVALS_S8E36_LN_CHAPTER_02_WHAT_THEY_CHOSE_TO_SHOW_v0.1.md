*When: Luminous asks whether he intends to begin with the limbs behind him, Kaneki looks toward the space she has chosen to leave open.*

“Would you prefer a warning?” ᴷ

“I prefer an interesting answer.” ᴸᵘ

The signal sounded.

Kaneki gave her one.

The first tendril struck where she had stood. The second crossed the retreat before she had completed it, forcing her to turn toward the empty part of the floor he had left beside his own advance.

Luminous moved into it.

Kaneki changed direction before the opening could become her invitation instead.

From the seats, Mau leaned forward.

* * *

He had seen Kaneki fight when the consequences of staying still were much less forgiving.

Here the movement looked different. He could spend an exchange finding out what happened. A failed reach did not leave Touka somewhere behind a danger he had promised to take away from her.

Luminous let the tendril cross her first guard.

The light at her hand changed. A narrow cut opened along the reaching flesh, precise enough that Kaneki did not feel its full extent until he had already begun withdrawing it.

He regenerated the damaged part.

She watched.

Kaneki saw her watching and sent the next two reaches along different heights, refusing to become a specimen simply because his recovery interested her.

One struck her forearm.

The other forced her back a full step.

Touka's hands loosened in her lap.

* * *

Luminous stopped giving him the same retreat.

She let him drive her toward the boundary, then turned toward the body at the centre of his reach rather than continuing to fight each extension as though it were a separate opponent.

Kaneki caught the first movement. The next came behind a brief flare which took his eyes in the wrong direction without pretending the world had changed its rules for him.

Her hand closed against his wrist.

He pulled away.

For one breath, his recovery became harder to sustain.

Not impossible. He could still feel the damaged flesh answering him. The demand had changed beneath it, something drawing through the point where she held him while the rest of his body was already paying for the repeated repairs.

He struck toward her shoulder.

She let go before the hit arrived.

Kaneki stepped clear, breathing harder.

Luminous looked at the place where his last attack had touched her arm.

“Again?” she asked. ᴸᵘ

Kaneki smiled faintly.

He went in.

* * *

The next exchange lasted longer than the first two combined.

He used less of the reach she had begun cutting and more of the floor she needed to cross to reach him. When she tried to repeat the grip, he turned his wrist and made her choose between keeping it and taking the tendril already returning behind her.

She gave up the hold.

A cheer went up from the house seats.

Luminous looked toward the sound for the smallest instant.

Kaneki did not mistake it for distraction.

He made her turn back to him anyway.

This time she caught the arm close to his elbow, where a retreat would require his whole body to follow. Light gathered at her other hand. He could attack through it and accept the cost of repairing what happened, or he could spend that same breath breaking the hold.

For a moment he tried to do both.

His knee touched the floor.

Nothing sounded. It was not the rule which ended the match.

Kaneki felt what the next decision would require, looked at her and said, “I yield.” ᴷ

Luminous released him at once.

* * *

She offered her hand after he had stood up by himself.

Kaneki took it.

“You learned quickly,” she said. ᴸᵘ

“I had a good reason.” ᴷ

Her glance moved toward the seats, then returned to him without deciding he needed a lecture about the people waiting there.

They walked out together.

Mau watched the way she let the last light disappear from her hand.

*There are several different operations in what looked like one exchange,* Ciel observed.

*Did you get them?*

*Some of what she showed. Not what she chose not to show.*

Noise made a small, interested sound behind the words.

No new structure began building.

* * *

Touka met Kaneki after the check.

“You enjoyed that.” ᵀᵒ

He looked almost surprised to hear it said aloud.

Then he nodded.

She handed him the drink she had kept.

Ichika asked whether the reaching limbs hurt when they were cut. Kaneki answered honestly enough for the question she had asked, then let her decide which part of the match she wished to explain back to him.

He sat down beside them before the next names were called.

* * *

Gabil entered as though the arena had been an excellent idea he had intended to propose himself.

Gojo waited until the introduction ended.

“Do I get one of those?” ᴳ

“You may introduce yourself,” Beretta said. ᴮʳ

“A dangerous invitation.” ᴳ

Shoko looked up from the side of the return room.

Gojo decided his name would suffice.

Gabil set the trident in both hands, opened his wings and gave his opponent a perfectly serious assessment before smiling again.

The signal sounded.

He took the air.

* * *

The first rush came from above.

Gojo waited until the weapon reached the place where it should have touched him. The trident slowed into an interval it could not finish. Gabil felt the refusal through both hands and changed his angle without trying to solve it by pushing harder.

He rose, turned and sent the next attack across the space Gojo would need to use if he left his first position.

Water followed the turn in a concentrated sweep.

It did not reach Gojo either.

But it changed the floor around him, cutting a wet route across the place where an ordinary retreat would have put his feet.

Gojo looked down.

Then up at the dragonnewt.

“Much better.” ᴳ

Gabil grinned.

He came down again.

* * *

Infinity would keep the direct approach from becoming contact.

It would not win the match for Gojo while he stood there admiring it.

He moved with Blue, taking a short step across a much less ordinary distance. Gabil altered his flight before the hand reaching for his shoulder could arrive. The wingbeat threw spray through the light, momentarily filling the place where Gojo's next movement should have been easy to read.

Gojo did not chase the first shape.

The pull gathered beyond it.

Gabil felt it take the edge of his turn and corrected with a stronger beat. He escaped the first draw, came round the far side and found Gojo already waiting nearer the line he had intended to use.

The trident changed hands.

Gojo let the shaft stop short of his chest and reached beneath it.

Gabil folded his wing, dropping out of the grip before it closed.

The audience cheered the escape.

Gojo laughed.

* * *

The next Blue did not pull toward the same point.

Gabil noticed the difference quickly. Too quickly to be caught by the simple version twice. He turned across the weaker part of the field and used his own momentum to reach a height from which the boundary would be easy to avoid.

Gojo shifted the gradient.

The useful gap moved.

Gabil followed it for half a breath before realising that the thing he had learned to recognise was being used to lead him somewhere.

He tried to turn back.

Gojo shortened the route between them and put one hand against the guarded haft of the trident.

The push was not large.

It did not need to be.

Gabil's body crossed the fixed boundary in the air above the floor. He beat his wings hard, still trying to return, and heard the count end before his foot reached the proper side again.

* * *

He landed in the return space looking personally offended by geometry.

Gojo came through a moment later.

“You did nearly have it.” ᴳ

“Nearly?” ᴳᵃ

“The first escape was very good.” ᴳ

“Naturally.” ᴳᵃ
