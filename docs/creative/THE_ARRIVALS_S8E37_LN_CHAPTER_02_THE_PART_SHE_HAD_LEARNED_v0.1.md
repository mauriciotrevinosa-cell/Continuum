* * *

Fern fired first.

The spell crossed the opening so quickly that Frieren had only the defence she had already prepared time to put in its way.

A second struck the edge of it.

The third was lower.

Frieren rose instead of correcting the first shield to answer all three. Fern followed the movement with a new line, refusing the ordinary pause in which her teacher might have discovered what she meant to do next.

The first exchange lasted long enough for the people watching to stop believing it had been only the beginning of one attack.

Fern moved left.

Frieren turned the smallest part of her guard to meet the change and found another shot arriving from the angle she had just given less attention.

Her sleeve tore.

Behind the staff, Fern's expression did not change.

* * *

Stark had gone quiet.

Mau noticed because he had been hearing small, involuntary sounds from him since the first names went on the board. Now he watched with both hands together, following Fern's movement as though the distance across the room had become something he could feel in his own body.

Beside him, Himmel leaned forward.

“She's fast.” ᴴ

Stark nodded.

He could have said many things about that.

For once, saying nothing allowed him to watch more of her.

* * *

Frieren let the next beam strike a guard she could afford to lose.

The broken light gave her a small interval in which to change height. She took it, returned a measured shot and watched Fern stop the counter without allowing it to become the centre of her attention.

That was different.

There had been a time when a return attack from Frieren would have become the question Fern needed to finish answering before asking another of her own.

Now she answered it and continued.

Frieren smiled.

Fern saw the smile and attacked the place beneath it.

The shield came up late enough for the impact to drive Frieren back through the air.

“That was not an invitation,” she said. ᶠʳ

“I know.” ᶠᵉ

* * *

They crossed the first minute without either reaching the place the other had chosen for them.

Fern had stopped using the whole width of the floor. She was leaving one path apparently easier than the others, not as an obvious trap but as an accumulation of small decisions Frieren could continue making without being wrong until the last one.

Frieren took two of them.

The third she refused.

Instead of retreating upward again, she came down toward the floor and sent her next spell into the empty stone beside Fern's intended landing.

Fragments burst through the air.

Fern widened her defence to take the physical debris as well as the spell. The choice cost her the next shot.

Frieren used the interval to move sideways.

By the time Fern could fire, the easy path had stopped being easy for either of them.

* * *

Fern landed lightly.

Dust hung close enough to the floor to make the next step less clear from the seats. It did not stop either woman reading the magic where it mattered.

Frieren waited for the familiar pressure of the next barrage.

It did not come.

Fern lowered the visible part of her presence instead, becoming less convenient to find while the fragments still moved. She had not disappeared. A person who knew she was there could continue looking.

But looking took the fraction Frieren would otherwise have spent choosing the next attack.

Fern fired through it.

Frieren stopped the first shot.

The second clipped her shoulder.

The impact turned her before she corrected the movement.

In the seats, Stark made a sound he could not quite call back.

* * *

Fern saw the hit.

For a moment the part of her which had watched Frieren come home tired, fall asleep over a book or look at her as though a perfectly ordinary request required more effort than an ancient spell tried to find its way into the fight.

She kept it out.

Frieren had agreed.

She did not need Fern to apologise for having believed her.

The next spell came toward Fern before she had finished making that decision.

She blocked it, turned the edge of the guard and fired back.

This time Frieren had already moved.

* * *

The change was small enough that Fern understood it late.

Frieren was no longer trying to finish every defence in the best possible position for the next shot. She was accepting a slightly worse first angle to leave herself a better second one.

The pattern Fern had been reading became less useful.

She adjusted.

Her first correction brought the spell nearer. The next forced Frieren to answer properly. Fern stepped into that answer, choosing the short distance instead of allowing the older mage to carry the exchange back into the shape she preferred.

Frieren's eyes sharpened.

The shot struck the smaller shield between them hard enough to make Fern feel the force through both hands.

She rose with it.

Frieren followed.

* * *

There was very little speaking now.

The audience could see the light and the shapes it left against the host's barrier. People who could read the exchanges saw much smaller decisions inside them: the moment a guard became too narrow, the instant a person trusted a route which had ceased to exist.

Luminous watched without commenting.

Gojo's smile had become the interested one he wore when somebody else's precision made him want a turn.

Yuka rested her uninjured hand on Mau's arm.

“She wanted this,” she said softly. ʸᵏ

* * *

The last part happened close to the boundary.

Fern knew where it was. She had counted the distance as soon as Frieren's retreat made that half of the floor useful.

What she had not seen was how carefully the next counter had been placed to make her choose the same correction she had used twice already.

Frieren fired.

Fern put up the guard, took the impact and moved across its weaker edge rather than giving the whole force a surface to strike.

The second spell was already waiting for the place that movement would require.

Fern changed height.

Frieren rose with her, close enough now that the next guard had to be fast rather than generous.

Fern caught the shot.

It carried her backward.

She saw the line below her and drove the flight forward again, almost angry at how small the remaining distance ought to have been.

The count finished before her body crossed back inside.

The signal cut through the next breath she had been about to turn into an attack.
