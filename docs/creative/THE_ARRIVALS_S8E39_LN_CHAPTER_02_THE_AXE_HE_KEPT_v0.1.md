Stark set his feet.

He could see Fern in the seats. She had found the place she wanted and was looking at him without appearing remotely uncertain that he belonged on the floor.

That helped.

The signal sounded.

Hakuro moved.

* * *

Stark felt the first touch against his guard rather than his body.

The sword had not come from where he expected it to begin. The old man's shoulders had offered one story. His feet had told another, and the small movement which joined them had made the difference difficult to see until the blade was already there.

Stark answered with the axe's haft.

The force behind the block would have made a less careful opponent stay where the next attack could find him.

Hakuro was already gone.

He struck the outside of Stark's arm with the flat, stepped away and waited for the younger man to turn.

Stark followed too quickly.

The next touch came against his side.

He took a breath.

Then another.

* * *

The axe was familiar in his hands.

He had earned the right to trust what it could do. That did not mean every person standing in front of it was obliged to give it the same place to land.

Stark let the next broad attack end early.

Hakuro had already begun answering the larger version. The change made him shorten his approach instead of taking the convenient line he had used before.

Stark followed with the haft rather than trying to bring the blade back from the wrong side of his body.

The strike reached Hakuro's shoulder.

Not cleanly enough to finish anything.

Cleanly enough that the old man had to move with it.

In the seats, Fern sat forward.

* * *

Hakuro reset without pretending the contact had been a gift.

“Better.” ᴴᵃ

Stark did not answer.

He was watching the feet now. The place the weight went before the sword arrived, the small choice which told him which direction would be difficult to change once the exchange began.

He still missed some of it.

He could feel the difference between missing it and never having looked.

The next two attacks drove him backward. He caught the third at a better angle and sent the force across the ground beside them instead of letting it remain inside his own wrists.

Stone cracked.

Hakuro stepped over the first broken piece.

Stark used the second as the place he would not be standing when the reply came.

* * *

The exchange became louder.

That did not mean Stark had begun winning it. Hakuro had stopped giving him the small comfortable pauses in which he could recover the shape of his own stance.

The axe came around.

The sword slid along the wrong side of the shaft for the block Stark had intended. Hakuro's foot entered the space he needed to close it, and suddenly the strength in Stark's arms had nowhere useful to go.

He could let go.

He could turn with the weapon and accept that the next position would be worse.

He chose to turn.

For one brief moment, the axe's head passed through a line Hakuro had not fully denied.

The old man moved his body instead of trying to make the sword answer it.

Stark's strike missed by less than it had any right to.

Then the tip was at his throat.

* * *

He stopped.

The axe remained in his hand.

Hakuro had one foot placed where Stark would need to move to turn away, and the sword close enough that even a very stubborn person could understand what the next exchange would cost.

Beretta ended it.

For a moment Stark simply stood there, breathing hard.

Then he looked up.

“I nearly got that one.” ˢᵗ

“Yes.” ᴴᵃ

The answer pleased him considerably more than the polite consolation he had prepared to receive.

Ultima's smile sharpened.

* * *

He did not move like a person planning to win by accepting more damage than the opponent expected.

The first near miss told him where the second should not happen. He shortened his attacks, let her spend on the wider answer and used the intervals to keep the distance from becoming entirely hers.

Once, he reached her.

The sword took a small part of her sleeve. She looked down at it with an expression which made several people in the audience reconsider the wisdom of smiling.

Then she came back faster.

Himmel turned the first approach.

The second left him near the boundary.

He moved sideways before the third could finish the pattern.

* * *

Frieren watched his feet.

It was an old habit.

She could remember the shape of a road from the way he chose where to stand in it. The part of an unfamiliar room he would leave open so somebody behind him could get past. Here there was nobody behind him who needed protecting.

He still found the space.

Ultima took more of it away.

By the last half-minute, he had to answer each attack with less room than the one before. He found another clean approach, forced her to break a spell she had already started and came away with a fresh cut along his arm.

The clock sounded before he could make the next exchange his.

* * *

The decision went to Ultima.

She had held the stronger positions, landed more of what she had committed and denied more of the floor. Himmel accepted it before anybody tried to make the audience decide by enthusiasm.

“You made that less simple than I intended.” ᵁˡ
