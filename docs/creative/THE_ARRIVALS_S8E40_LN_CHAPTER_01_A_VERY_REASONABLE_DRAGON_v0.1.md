“That was not my concern.” ᴿᵃⁿ

The signal sounded.

He was no longer where she had been looking.

Milim's first rush met the edge of a turning wind. It did not stop her. It changed the approach enough for Ranga to cross behind her instead of being caught in front of her hands.

She turned, delighted.

“Good!” ᴹⁱˡ

In the seats, Umaru stood to see better.

“We have that one,” she announced. ᵁ

Several visitors looked toward the Fort with renewed interest.

* * *

Ranga used the floor before Milim could make him abandon it.

The wind pressed low, taking the loose fragments from the earlier matches into a moving screen. He crossed behind it, changed direction and let her find the first wrong answer with a punch which opened a clear space through the dust.

She laughed.

The second approach came from above her shoulder.

Milim ducked beneath it instead of trying to catch everything by force. Her hand found the movement at its lower edge, and for a moment Ranga had to give up his own intended landing to keep her from taking hold of him.

He landed, spun and sent the next rush of air into the space between them.

Milim came through it with both arms braced.

The force drove a line beneath her feet.

It did not keep her away.

* * *

She caught him on the next crossing.

Not by waiting for him to become slow. She let the first turn take her arm out of position, used the same movement to put her body nearer the place he needed next and closed both hands around a secure hold before he could make the new direction useful.

Ranga tried to turn through it.

Milim remained exactly as inconvenient as he had feared.

For a moment the audience watched her consider the shortest route to the boundary.

Ranga looked toward Beretta.

“I yield.” ᴿᵃⁿ

Milim stopped lifting.

“Already?” ᴹⁱˡ

“I have evaluated the available landing places.” ᴿᵃⁿ

She looked down at her hands.

Then grinned and let him go.

* * *

Frey's match with Carrion began much nearer the floor than most people expected.

She let him close first, making the decision to take the air an answer to his attack rather than an announcement of what she intended to do for the whole match.

Carrion followed with a speed which made the first retreat considerably less comfortable than she had planned.

He caught the edge of her guard.

Frey turned with the hit, rose beyond his immediate reach and made him move to keep the pressure on her.

There was nowhere in the rules which required her to return to the place his best attack preferred.

She made use of that.

* * *

Carrion's answer shook the contained air above the floor.

Frey had to change height instead of finishing the approach she had prepared. The next rush brought him close enough to catch her wing at the guard before she could make the same escape twice.

She struck down through the opening beside his weapon.

He gave ground.

By the last minute, both had found something they could repeat if the other became careless. Neither became careless enough to finish it before the clock.

The decision went to Frey for the cleaner attacks and the positions she had kept while denying his.

Carrion looked toward the empty air she had used so effectively.

“A very large amount of running away.” ᶜʳ

“You should have caught me.” ᶠʳᵉ

He laughed.

She offered him her hand as though she had not just spent three minutes making certain he could not get hold of it.

* * *

Anko had watched both matches before checking the pouch at her side.

Two small coils of ordinary line. The dull baton the organisers had offered and she had chosen after trying the balance. A little powder which would show her where the moving air went if the body in front of her became too difficult to read.

Nothing which pretended she had acquired a new life between breakfast and the draw.

Hinata stood beside the entrance.

“You don't have to make this impressive.” ᴴⁱ

Anko looked at her.

“That sounds suspiciously like a low expectation.” ᴬⁿ

“It was intended as an excellent opportunity to surprise me.” ᴴⁱ

Anko smiled.

“I'll see what I can do.” ᴬⁿ

* * *

Veldora looked genuinely pleased to see her.

He listened while Beretta confirmed that he understood the particular care required in the match. Anko had read the same conditions. Neither needed the audience to mistake the difference between them for a secret.

“Ready?” Veldora asked. ⱽ

“Against my better judgement.” ᴬⁿ

He laughed.

She moved as the signal sounded, throwing the first line across the place where a very confident person might choose to put his next foot.

Veldora noticed it.

Anko had expected him to.

The powder opened on the other side of his turn.

* * *

For the next few seconds she could see the answer more clearly than she could act on it.

The air changed. The line which should have become a small obstacle lifted away from the floor before it could matter. Veldora turned toward the approach she had hidden behind the first two movements and found her already trying to change it.

The baton touched his guarding arm.

It made a perfectly respectable sound.

It did nothing else of consequence.

Anko looked at the weapon.

Veldora looked at her.

“A useful experiment,” she said. ᴬⁿ

“Indeed.” ⱽ

He took the end of the baton between his fingers.

* * *

She let go immediately.

It saved her wrist, which she considered the most sensible decision she had made since entering. She moved toward the open side, discovered it was only open because Veldora had already reached the next one and felt a careful pressure against her shoulder.

It was almost absurdly gentle.

It was also entirely sufficient.

Her shoes crossed the boundary before she found an argument which might persuade the distance to become smaller.

Veldora stopped inside it and held out the baton.

She took it.

“Well,” she said. ᴬⁿ

He waited with admirable patience.

“I think that part was quite conclusive.” ᴬⁿ

* * *

Hinata met her after the check.

Anko had expected to find the look people sometimes gave a person who had attempted something wildly beyond them. The kind part of it was often harder to endure than the unkind one.

Hinata simply looked at the powder on her sleeve.

“You made him change his first step.” ᴴⁱ

“And then he changed the rest of mine.” ᴬⁿ

Hinata laughed.

Anko decided she liked that considerably better than consolation.

“Did you enjoy watching?” she asked. ᴬⁿ

“Yes.” ᴴⁱ

The answer was sufficiently direct that Anko found she had asked a slightly different question from the one she had prepared.

Hinata glanced toward the entrance where her own name would soon be called.

“Would you stay for mine?” ᴴⁱ

Anko put the baton back where it belonged with the other loaned equipment.

“I was planning to.” ᴬⁿ
