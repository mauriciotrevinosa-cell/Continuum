*When: Ranga asks whether he has a plan, Gobta nods so quickly that the wolf immediately asks whether he has a second one.*

“That is the second one.” ᴳᵒ

Ranga waited.

“The first involved you,” Gobta admitted. ᴳᵒ

The wolf looked toward the condition which prevented one competitor from joining another's individual match.

Gobta sighed.

“Yes. I read it.” ᴳᵒ

Ranga lowered his head enough for the reply to belong to the two of them.

“Then I will watch.” ᴿᵃⁿ

Gobta looked up.

For a moment he stopped appearing to calculate how many people would notice if he failed to find the entrance.

“Right,” he said. ᴳᵒ

* * *

Testarossa greeted him with such perfect courtesy that it nearly made his first plan return.

He bowed, remembered the sword in his hand and moved it before the attempt to be polite became the first injury of the match.

She smiled.

Beretta asked whether both were ready.

Gobta looked toward Ranga once.

Then he nodded.

* * *

The first attack did not arrive where Testarossa expected it.

Gobta's movement into shadow was his own, practised often enough that he did not have to think about what a much grander person might call it. He came out low, changed the height halfway through the approach and found her hand already turning to put the useful part of the attack somewhere else.

He ducked.

The spell crossed above his head.

He continued running instead of waiting to discover whether somebody wished to congratulate him on surviving the first exchange.

From the seats, Rimuru sat up.

* * *

Gobta had brought a little more than the weapon.

Nothing rare. Nothing which required him to explain where an unprecedented skill had appeared between lunch and the last opening match.

He used the loose outer layer of his clothing to give her the beginning of an outline she might read as the next approach, let it drop at the wrong height and moved beneath the place her first answer covered.

For one brief moment, the real blade reached nearer than she intended.

It took the edge of a sleeve.

Testarossa looked down.

Gobta did not wait for her expression to finish changing.

He was already moving away.

* * *

She began making the places he wanted to use less convenient.

The light did not remove every shadow from the floor. That would have been too simple an account of the movement he had actually shown.

She watched where he chose to emerge and placed the next demand near the exit instead. A small force he had to turn away from. A narrowing angle which made the useful landing less useful before the second step could follow it.

Gobta changed the route.

She changed with him.

By the fourth approach, he had almost convinced himself she would need to be in two places at once to answer the next one.

Then she let him discover that making the first place look unattended was something she knew how to do as well.

* * *

The binding caught his sword arm without taking the hand from it.

He tried to turn through the lower angle.

Testarossa stepped into it, stopped the movement at the shoulder and held him in a position from which continuing would require an injury neither of them needed to prove was possible.

Beretta ended the match.

Gobta stopped straining against the hold.

For a moment he looked disappointed enough that Testarossa let the last of the restraint go before speaking.

“The sleeve was very well judged.” ᵀᵉ

He looked at the small tear.

“I nearly had more.” ᴳᵒ

“You did.” ᵀᵉ

He straightened a little.

* * *

Ranga met him at the exit after the check.

Gobta had found the part of his clothing he had dropped and put it back on with the dignity available to a person who had just used it to mislead a demon.

“I lost.” ᴳᵒ

“I watched.” ᴿᵃⁿ

Gobta looked at him.

The wolf's tail moved once.

“The second plan was good.” ᴿᵃⁿ

Gobta smiled before remembering to make the expression less obvious.

* * *

The board changed while people began leaving their seats.

Thirty-two names became sixteen continuing lines. Nobody needed to remain in the arena to learn whether they were being asked to fight again before eating or sleeping. Rimuru had listened closely enough to the people doing the checks to leave the next rounds for the following morning.

Milim objected to the delay for approximately as long as it took Umaru to tell her there were still places outside they had not visited.

Mau stood slowly, letting Yuka decide which side she wanted her bandaged wrist on before they moved into the passage.

“Tomorrow?” she asked. ʸᵏ

“Tomorrow.” ᴹ

Shion had stopped there too.

For a moment the two women looked at the same pair of advancing lines.

Then Yuka smiled.

Shion smiled back.
