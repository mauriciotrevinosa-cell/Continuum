* * *

Yuka was already in her seat.

The wrist had been checked that morning. She had accepted a lighter support for the part which still wanted reminding not to be overused, eaten what she needed and refused the offer to spend the first half of the day being treated like a parcel which must not be jostled.

Mau had kissed her before going down.

Now she watched him look up once, find her and turn his attention fully toward the woman in front of him.

Ciel had chosen to watch this one.

Inside, Noise had become very quiet.

* * *

The first light struck Infinity and stopped short of his shoulder.

It was such a clear answer that several people in the audience made the mistake of believing the exchange had already explained the whole match.

Luminous did not.

She let the first spell remain long enough to see the shape of the refusal, then changed the second attack's route while Mau approached the space she had left beside it.

His hand reached for her guard.

The guard was farther away than it had appeared.

A small barrier took the touch instead.

He felt its resistance, saw the answering light gather near his face and withdrew before finding out which part she intended him to notice next.

Luminous watched the movement.

Then she changed where she stood.

* * *

Mau knew the difference between an opponent who kept moving because she did not want to be caught and one who was choosing where the catching should happen.

Knowing did not tell him every place she had chosen.

The next step brought a line of light up from the floor beside him. It did not pass magically through his defence because someone had called it holy. It made the ordinary escape less ordinary, requiring him to consider the next position while Luminous was already deciding what to put there.

He answered with a small Red.

The repulsion crossed the open space.

She turned the first part with a prepared guard and moved beyond the rest, conserving more than he would have conserved if he had tried to stop it all in place.

Mau smiled.

“You're making that look very inexpensive.” ᴹ

“You are welcome to continue helping me practise.” ᴸᵘ

* * *

He changed the scale of the next approach without changing its apparent beginning.

Blue shortened the distance. His body arrived sooner than the last exchange had taught her to expect. The blade he had drawn stopped against a new layer of protection rather than the hand shaping it, but the force behind the hit carried her a full step.

The second dagger was already moving.

Luminous let the guard break at the point where keeping it would have committed too much to the wrong answer.

He reached the edge of her sleeve.

She caught his wrist with the space between two narrow surfaces of light, turned it just enough to spoil the cut and struck the ground beneath the place where his next foot would have settled.

Mau gave up the position.

The dagger remained in his hand.

That was a better result than the one she had offered.

* * *

In the seats, Hinata watched Luminous stop smiling quite so automatically.

Anko followed her glance.

“That was different?” ᴬⁿ

“He reached her,” Hinata said. ᴴⁱ

“Not very much.” ᴬⁿ

“Enough that she had to choose what to lose.” ᴴⁱ

Anko looked back at the floor.

The match had become easier to understand once she stopped trying to decide which person was stronger before either had finished asking the question.

* * *

Mau sent the next Red wide.

Luminous did not follow it with her eyes.

She watched his hands.

He released the projectile's direction with the launch, closed the copied operation and took the surface ahead of its path with the familiar turn he had practised long before this city arrived.

The shot came around the side of her first guard.

Luminous moved before the fold had finished presenting its new angle.

Her counter reached the place his body occupied now that the neutral defence was no longer active.

The cut opened across his upper arm.

Mau pulled away.

The blood reached his wrist before the repair began catching it.

* * *

*She read the change before the attack arrived,* Ciel told him.

*I noticed.*

*You did not need to pay for the information with quite that much arm.*

Mau laughed under his breath.

Luminous heard the sound without hearing the conversation which had earned it.

“Amusing?” ᴸᵘ

“Useful advice.” ᴹ

“From a person with good timing?” ᴸᵘ

“Usually.” ᴹ

He closed the sky operation completely.

The next part would need a different answer.

* * *

He used the floor she had begun marking.

Shrine's cuts did not become a promise to ignore every defence because a different world had given them a frightening name. They struck the visible seams of the material beneath one of her prepared positions.

Stone fractured.

The physical pieces rose under the separate force he could already command.

Luminous had to move the next barrier or let the fragments alter where she could safely stand.

Mau came behind them.

The first impact took the edge of the guard.

The second was his body rather than another spell.

He had given up the copied cut before trying to carry the next operation through the changing space. His own strength remained, with the familiar advantage of the daggers still in his hands.

This time the guard moved.

Luminous stepped inside it.

* * *

Her hand closed against his forearm.

He had expected a strike.

For one dangerous fraction, the lack of one felt like an opening he could afford to take.

Then the effort in the repairing arm changed.

The body which had been supplying the next movement found the cost arriving from somewhere else as well. Not a wound. Not the simple absence of a reserve he could replace by assuming a larger source existed behind it.

Something was drawing through the contact she had actually made.

Mau saw her expression sharpen as she encountered the difference between what his body was using and what lay farther behind it.

Luminous did not mistake the difference for an invitation to reach indiscriminately deeper.

She held the accessible line she had found.

It was enough to make the next breath feel shorter.

* * *

Mau sent the pressure of his separate grip across the small space beside them.

It broke the physical hold before his hand could make the faster, uglier decision it remembered from a different room.

He stepped away.

Luminous let him go and looked at the fresh repair on his arm.

“The same problem, from another direction,” she said. ᴸᵘ

Kaneki watched from the seats with immediate recognition.

Mau flexed his hand.

The daggers were still there. The floor was still open. There was time left on the clock, and the woman across from him had not required him to become a different creature to continue.

*Old tools,* Ciel said.

He looked up.

*Yes.*

Mau began to smile again.
