Gojo crossed the first distance with Blue.

Diablo's hand was there to meet him.

The strike stopped at the interval around Gojo's body, a refusal so complete that it should have made the defender look comfortable. Diablo did not waste a second testing whether more force would make the same approach become a different one.

He turned the hand into a mark against the space beside them.

Gojo struck.

The barrier took the first hit. It gave under the next, and Diablo withdrew through the part of the floor he had already made useful for leaving.

Gojo followed.

The pressure began where he arrived.

* * *

It did not cross the distance to him.

The small prepared region had been waiting for a body to occupy it. Its force closed around the place where his legs now stood, not as a physical loop travelling toward him but as an operation begun in the space itself.

Gojo felt the distinction immediately.

He moved with Blue before the region completed its hold.

Diablo's next attack met him at the edge of the escape.

He blocked with his arm, close enough to feel the impact through the reinforcement, and landed a return strike against the demon's guard.

The sound reached the audience a fraction after the floor cracked beneath them.

Diablo laughed softly.

Gojo's grin became considerably less decorative.

* * *

The next Blue gathered above rather than behind him.

Diablo moved before the pull could take the position he preferred. Gojo shortened his own approach, struck across the route and made the first defensive surface become something the demon needed to abandon.

The second surface held.

Diablo came around it with a hand already shaping the next confined pressure.

Gojo's vision bent.

Not his eyes. The route of the light arriving at them.

The place Diablo could see his body occupy shifted away from the place the next punch actually began.

Diablo's guard answered the wrong part of the information for a single instant.

The blow reached his ribs.

He slid backward across the broken stone, looked up and began smiling as though somebody had finally stopped being polite.

* * *

It worked once.

Diablo did not confuse knowing the image was wrong with being certain of the correct reach behind it. The next exchange cost him another guard before he could make the underlying route legible enough to use.

Then he stopped aiming at the image at all.

A wider prepared region took the floor through which Gojo would need to move. It required more of Diablo's attention. It also meant the distinction between two apparently nearby positions stopped being enough to escape it.

Gojo took the air.

Diablo was already there.

Their hands met above the broken first mark, fists and guards changing too quickly for most of the audience to decide which one had begun the attack.

Gojo's shoulder struck a barrier he had not seen Diablo finish preparing.

He turned off it, came down hard and rolled clear before the next pressure could make the landing final.

* * *

Shoko had stopped pretending she would remain seated through the whole match.

Maomao stood where she could see the return entrance without blocking it.

“He knows,” Shoko said, mostly to herself. ˢ

That did not make watching it as simple as she would have liked.

* * *

Gojo made two small Blues.

The space between them did not become safe simply because it contained less of the immediate pull. The difference changed when he shifted the relationship, turning a useful route into a poor one and then back before Diablo's next attack had completed.

Diablo watched the control.

He gave Gojo a spell large enough to occupy both stronger regions at once and followed through the lower-gradient strip while it was apparently the best way in.

Gojo changed the strip.

Diablo changed with it.

For several breaths the two of them made the same small part of the arena extraordinarily difficult to enter for entirely different reasons.

Neither possessed unlimited attention.

The first to pretend otherwise would lose the part he had stopped watching.

* * *

Diablo found the cost first.

He let one barrier fail deliberately, kept the next near Gojo's right shoulder and struck the floor beneath the place where the second Blue required a clean line through the moving debris.

Gojo could hold the pattern or keep the best landing.

He held it for one breath too long.

The pressure caught him at the edge of a region Diablo had prepared during the earlier exchange.

It did not stop Infinity from answering direct contact. It made direct contact the wrong problem.

Gojo's heel crossed near the boundary.

Diablo moved to close the return route.

Mau stood up.

Red struck the anchor.

The prepared force broke at the point where it depended on its relation to the floor. Gojo used the interruption to take the other side of the line before the count could begin against his whole body.

Diablo did not stop moving.

He had already prepared the answer for the position Gojo would choose if he wanted the easiest escape.

Gojo saw it.

He chose the harder one.

* * *

Purple formed beyond his hands.

The room changed its sound.

Diablo's expression sharpened, not with surprise at a name he had failed to hear at registration, but at the direction from which the first completed route promised to approach him.

He placed the strongest part of his guard across it and moved away from the centre of the impact.

Gojo's prepared spatial turn changed the route.

It was one turn. Something he had arranged and paid attention to, not a projectile granted permission to follow an opponent forever.

One was enough.

Diablo abandoned the guard before keeping it would have forced him to take the full attack with his body. The edge of the output crossed his retreat, tore the outer layer of protection from his shoulder and struck the empty floor beyond.

The host's containment held.

The audience did not immediately remember to breathe.

Gojo had released the attack.

Blue drew across the space beside the demon's next footfall.
