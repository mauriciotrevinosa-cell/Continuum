She noticed.

The absence of a mistake could be useful information too.

“You've met people who do that,” she said. ᶠʳ

“I have met people who wished me to think they had done less than they had.” ᴸᵉ

Frieren nodded thoughtfully.

“That does make things less convenient.” ᶠʳ

The signal sounded.

Her first spell was considerably less modest than the preparation had suggested.

* * *

Leon caught it with a guard set at an angle rather than a wall spread across the whole approach.

Frieren watched where the force went.

The next shot came beneath the place which had received the first, followed by another at the height from which he would most easily answer the lower one.

He gave ground.

She advanced into it without allowing the movement to make the next spell late.

For a few seconds the audience saw much the same basic attack Fern had used that morning become an entirely different kind of conversation.

There was less hurry in it.

Very little of the apparent lack of hurry meant there was time to waste.

* * *

Leon's return struck near the edge of her guard.

The light was fast. Frieren did not try to become faster than a completed beam. She watched the part which had to happen before it became one and moved while the choice still belonged to both of them.

The first passed beside her.

The second forced a wider defence.

She put the answer to the third into the floor.

Broken stone rose between them under a small spell, physical matter sharing the route with the next magical shot. The guard which would have been sufficient for one needed a different shape for the other.

Leon changed it.

Frieren found the interval she had wanted.

Her spell touched his shoulder before the new defence had finished closing.

* * *

Fern was smiling now.

Stark saw and did not interrupt the reason for it.

Himmel watched the man on the other side of the arena realise that the unhurried opponent had made him spend on three decisions before showing which one she actually wanted answered.

Leon looked at the torn edge of his sleeve.

Then he changed his rhythm.

* * *

The next attack began in the interval Frieren had been using to lower one guard and raise the better one.

She caught it.

The second asked the same question from another direction.

She rose, maintaining enough distance for the first correction to remain useful. Leon let her take that height and prepared the next line where the flight would need to end if she wanted to keep attacking him.

Frieren did not land there.

She sent another spell instead.

The exchange became more expensive for both.

* * *

Neither needed to explain the difference between having a large reserve and choosing well enough not to waste the part one could presently spend.

Frieren accepted a worse angle to conserve the next answer.

Leon used the space it offered to bring the following attack nearer.

She changed the height of the debris again. He separated the physical pieces from the spell with two different parts of the defence, leaving the next attack ready sooner than it had been the first time.

Frieren had made him learn the arrangement.

Now he was using the lesson.

She smiled despite the force driving her guard backward.

* * *

The last half-minute belonged more to him.

She could still make every step cost something. She could still see where a longer fight might permit another answer to grow from the information she had gathered.

The clock was not a longer fight.

Leon's next three clean approaches made her give up positions she had meant to keep. She landed the best of her final counters against a properly raised guard instead of a body.

The signal sounded while she was preparing the next one.

She stopped.

The technical decision went to Leon.

* * *

Frieren lowered her staff and looked at the line of his last defence.

“That small part,” she said. ᶠʳ

Leon looked toward the place she meant.

“The separation.” ᴸᵉ

She nodded.

“I'd like to ask about it sometime.” ᶠʳ

He considered her for a moment.

“A better opening than trying to learn it while somebody is still attacking you.” ᴸᵉ

Frieren looked almost disappointed by how reasonable that sounded.

“I did think about it.” ᶠʳ

* * *

Fern met her after the ordinary check.

“The stall is still open,” she said. ᶠᵉ

Frieren looked toward the room where more matches were being prepared, then back at the person waiting beside her.

“We said we'd go.” ᶠʳ

“Yes.” ᶠᵉ

“Then let's go.” ᶠʳ

Fern's smile arrived before she could make it look like a practical acknowledgement.

They left together.

* * *

Hakuro's second match began with much less conversation than his first.

Ultima had watched him teach Stark the difference between the first visible attack and the one which arrived when the answer became predictable.

She did not offer him the same shape of opponent.

The first movement came short. The next came late. The third left an opening so obvious that he ignored it and struck the smaller one she had been less certain he would find.

His blade touched her guard.

She looked up, properly interested.

* * *

He made her turn three times before she stopped treating the shorter reach as something she could answer by making the distance inconvenient.

Each turn left him somewhere from which a small correction became another attack. She could spend enough force to make the whole arrangement costly. Doing so at every approach would leave her acting after him rather than choosing for herself.

Ultima changed the question.

The next pressure came low enough to require his feet to answer before his sword could. She followed with her body, not another spell, taking the contact he had prepared for a more distant attack at a much less comfortable angle.

Hakuro turned the first reach.

She came through the second.

* * *

There was a moment in which he might have withdrawn the blade sooner.

He recognised it as it passed.

Ultima caught the weapon arm close to the wrist, denied the step which would have brought his balance back beneath him and stopped the next touch where it would have needed very little more movement to become a severe injury.

He looked at the position.

Then he smiled.

“Yours.” ᴴᵃ

She let go.

* * *

Stark met him after the check with a question he had clearly been keeping since the earlier match.

Hakuro listened to the whole thing.

Then he asked Stark which part he thought had happened first.

The younger man looked back toward the floor, thought about it and gave a much more specific answer than he would have given that morning.

Hakuro nodded.

“Good. Remember that version.” ᴴᵃ

Stark walked beside him to the seats, still talking about a fight he had lost and a thing he had learned which losing had not taken away.
