* * *

Testarossa's first spell took away the comfortable approach.

The second was prepared for the one which remained.

Veldora saw both. More importantly, he saw the difference between a route which looked possible and the one whose next event was likely to remain useful when he reached it.

He chose the smaller gap.

The pressure changed before his foot settled.

He had to answer with his own guard rather than merely admire how well he had read the invitation.

Testarossa smiled.

“There you are.” ᵀᵉ

* * *

The force of his next approach made the room feel much smaller.

He had kept the output within what the host had agreed to receive. That still left enough to make Testarossa spend properly on the defence rather than turn it into another lesson about how little a direct attack could accomplish against her preparation.

She moved.

He moved with her.

The first crossing cost the outer part of her barrier. The second cost the position she had meant to recover behind it.

Her third answer touched his arm.

He felt the injury and changed the next line instead of deciding being a dragon made it unimportant.

The available path narrowed.

He took it before it ceased to be available.

* * *

There was no moment in which Testarossa ran out of all possible answers.

There was a moment in which the answer she had prepared required the body in front of her to remain where its last movement suggested it would.

Veldora did not.

He went through the costly route, taking a shallow hit across the guard instead of following the cheaper one she had arranged to close. His shoulder reached the defended space beside her before she could turn the whole preparation.

The impact moved her.

She corrected in the air.

He crossed the useful return route with the next bounded force.

The count ended outside the line.

* * *

Testarossa landed, looked at the small damage along his arm and accepted the result with considerably more composure than the floor had accepted the exchange.

“You chose the unpleasant possibility.” ᵀᵉ

“It was still possible,” Veldora said. ⱽ

“A distinction many people find expensive.” ᵀᵉ

He laughed.

From the seats, Mau watched the dragon go to have the arm checked rather than use the result as proof he no longer needed anyone to look.

He suspected tomorrow would be interesting.

* * *

Frey waited for Milim to finish speaking to the Fort.

The conversation involved a promise about where she would sit afterward and a disagreement about whether winning meant she had to remain on the floor until somebody else asked to fight her.

Umaru settled the second by pointing at the schedule.

Milim accepted the evidence reluctantly and came down.

Frey looked at her.

“You have people expecting you.” ᶠʳᵉ

Milim smiled.

“Lots.” ᴹⁱˡ

Frey's expression softened for a moment before she put her hands where the first attack would require them.

“Then let's not make them wait forever.” ᶠʳᵉ

* * *

She took the air first this time.

Milim followed with far more enthusiasm than caution, which was precisely the beginning Frey had expected. The first turn carried the smaller girl past the place where a simple straight approach would have become contact.

Frey's wing changed the pressure.

Milim had to turn again.

The third approach came from the side she had just left open.

She caught the guarded attack on both forearms, felt the impact and laughed.

Then she stopped chasing the end of Frey's last movement.

* * *

She chose a place Frey would need if she wanted to keep the next turn as comfortable as the first.

The older woman saw the choice and changed it.

Milim changed with her.

The exchange became a succession of short, violent corrections made more difficult by the fact that neither had ordinary ground beneath her feet.

Frey landed a clean hit against the raised guard.

Milim let it turn her rather than spending the next breath stopping the whole motion.

She came out of the turn nearer than Frey intended.

Her hand closed on the defended wrist.

* * *

Frey tried to recover the distance.

Milim's other hand was already there, holding a second secure point and denying the rotation which would have made the first grip useless.

They dropped toward the floor together.

Milim turned the landing so neither would strike with the part of the body which could make a sporting hold into something much worse.

Frey found herself with the next movement denied and enough strength behind the denial that continuing would prove something neither needed to prove.

Beretta ended it.

Milim let go immediately.

* * *

For a moment she looked almost anxious.

“Are you all right?” ᴹⁱˡ

“Yes.” ᶠʳᵉ

“I won.” ᴹⁱˡ

“I was there.” ᶠʳᵉ

“Go back to the people expecting you,” she said. ᶠʳᵉ
