*When: Yuta asks whether she is ready, Yuka looks at the ring on his hand before looking back at his face.*

“I was going to ask you that.” ʸᵏ

He smiled.

“A worrying start.” ʸᵗ

Her wrist had passed the morning check. She had moved it without the tenderness which had made the previous day's choice of guard more important than she wished. The support was off. The warning to report anything different had remained.

Now she stood opposite her grandfather with nobody between them who needed to agree that she ought to be allowed to try.

The signal sounded.

She called the Ox.

* * *

Yuta moved before the straight charge could become the whole problem.

Yuka had expected him to.

Nue came above the route he chose, cutting the useful height away from the retreat. The Toad reached from below, not to hold him by strength but to require him to spend the next movement avoiding it.

He shortened his step.

The Ox passed where the longer one would have taken him.

Yuka came behind the opening with her weapon already moving.

Yuta caught the first strike.

Her second made him turn properly.

In the seats, Maki smiled without saying anything at all.

* * *

Rika's arrival changed the size of the answer.

The larger hand denied the route Yuka had made for herself. She recalled the Ox before the next collision could turn a familiar living technique into a permanent loss for a game.

Nue rose out of reach.

Yuka ducked beneath the first sweep and let the Toad draw her toward the shadow she had already chosen.

Yuta followed the body rather than the impressive thing overhead.

That was irritatingly sensible of him.

She met the next attack with a guard which saved the shoulder and cost the position.

He took the space she had given up.

* * *

For several exchanges, she could make him answer more than he wanted at once.

She could not make him forget what the answers belonged to.

The summons required her choices. The weapon required her hands. Every clever route still needed her body to arrive before the person watching it understood why she wanted that particular place.

Yuta began denying the returns rather than chasing the departures.

Yuka saw it happen once.

She did not give him the second for free.

The native forms withdrew completely before she reached for the small borrowed sky operation.

The first turn bent his approaching blade away from the guard he expected her to need.

She stepped through the changed angle.

Her weapon touched his side.

* * *

The audience cheered.

Yuta moved with the hit, feeling the difference between a lucky opening and one she had waited long enough to choose.

He did not make the next answer smaller because he was pleased.

His own surface turn took the approach from a different angle.

Yuka recognised enough to know she could not hold her version against his by wishing she had practised longer.

She closed it, gave up the position and landed nearer the part of the floor where her own shadow would be most useful.

Yuta let her reach it.

The permission worried her more than the pursuit had.

* * *

She opened the Garden.

Small. The part she could actually hold.

The floor darkened beneath the distance between them, and the next figure rising from it was not the body Yuta's first return attack found.

Rika turned toward the duplicate.

Yuka came from the lower angle with the Toad already reaching across the retreat.

For one breath, the whole arrangement worked.

Yuta had a sword between his own body and the real weapon, Rika had a false target under one hand and the next safe step belonged to the field Yuka had made.

He looked at her through the small space she had left him.

Then he changed the room.

* * *

His domain did not arrive as a louder copy of hers.

It was his own, with the refinement of somebody who had learned where the boundary must hold because he had needed it to hold before a friendly audience existed to admire it.

The change pressed against the smaller Garden.

Yuka tried to keep the edge she could feel beginning to fail.

For a moment she nearly had it.

Then the medium beneath her feet broke apart into the larger arrangement.

Swords stood where the open floor had been. The part of the sure-hit Yuta had chosen was the brief, bounded interruption already declared to the host, aimed at the one opponent he had actually brought into the exchange.

“Let go.” ʸᵗ

Her hand opened.

* * *

The command did not last long.

It lasted long enough.

Yuka reached for the next operation and found the raw absence where the ended domain had left the channel. Borrowing a different pattern would not repair the route by making its name different.

She moved with her body instead.

Rika's hand was already denying the approach.

Yuta came through the smaller gap, caught the weapon as it fell and stopped his own blade short of the place her next movement would have to cross.

He did not touch the old wound.

He did not need to.

Yuka stood very still.

Then she let out the breath she had been trying to make into another answer.

“I yield.” ʸᵏ

* * *

The room returned to ordinary floor.

Yuta closed the domain, accepted the familiar silence of his own technique afterward and gave her weapon back with the handle toward her.

Yuka took it.

For a moment neither spoke.

Then she looked at the place where the Garden had broken.

“I wanted that part to work.” ʸᵏ

“It did.” ʸᵗ

She looked up.

“It made me use the part I wasn't planning to use first.” ʸᵗ

There was no need to tell her that meant she had almost won. She knew the difference. The truth was more useful than making the disappointment easier by lying about the size of it.

She looked down at the weapon.

“I need a better way to hold it.” ʸᵏ

Yuta nodded.

“We can practise.” ʸᵗ

* * *

Maki met them beyond the check.

Yuta tried to begin explaining the best part of Yuka's first arrangement.

Maki put a hand against his arm.

“I saw.” ᴹᵏ

Then she looked at their granddaughter.

“You made him stop being comfortable.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka smiled despite herself.

“Not for long enough.” ʸᵏ

“Longer than last time.” ᴹᵏ

Mai came beside them and told Yuta that he would be hearing about the match every time he attempted to win an argument about training from now on.

He looked resigned enough to make Yuka laugh.

* * *

Mau waited with the drink she had asked him to keep.

Yuka took it, sat down beside him and let the last of the adrenaline become ordinary shaking in her fingers.

He did not try to tell her that winning did not matter before she had been allowed to dislike losing.

She appreciated that enough to rest against him while she drank.

“Next time,” she said after a while. ʸᵏ

“Next time.” ᴹ

He kissed her temple.

“The first plan was yours.” ᴹ

She looked up at him.

He smiled.

“I liked watching him discover it.” ᴹ

Yuka leaned back against his shoulder, tired and disappointed and pleased in a combination she had not expected the morning to make room for.

On the other side of the barrier, Yuta sat down to rest his own closed channel.

The larger reserve beside him could supply energy.

It could not buy him an immediate second domain.
