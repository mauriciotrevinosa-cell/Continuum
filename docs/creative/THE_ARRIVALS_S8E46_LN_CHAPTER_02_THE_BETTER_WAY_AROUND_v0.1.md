*When: Ultima lets her first attack arrive a little wide, Milim goes after the place she has left open.*

The second spell is already waiting there.

Milim twists in the air. The edge of the force catches her guard and carries her sideways before she can make the landing belong entirely to her.

Ultima smiles.

Milim grins back.

“That was good.” ᴹⁱˡ

Then she comes through the next opening much faster.

* * *

Seeing the false part of the invitation did not tell her how to finish the movement she had already chosen.

Her eyes gave her more of the real flows than most of the audience could see. Ultima made the information costly to use, putting a change in pressure at the end of one route while beginning another nearer the part of Milim's body which would have to turn.

Milim stopped trying to catch every completed answer.

She struck before the next one had become useful.

The impact broke the outer guard.

Ultima moved with it, keeping the force from becoming a direct hit, and returned a narrow spell which scored the girl's forearm.

Milim looked at the scratch.

Then at her.

The smile remained.

The next approach became far more difficult to answer.

* * *

Ultima used the whole height she had been permitted.

Milim followed without allowing the turn at the top to become the comfortable pause the older fighter wanted. Her first punch took the defence. Her next drove it closer to the body it protected.

Ultima changed the angle.

Milim changed with it.

The third impact went through enough of the remaining guard to take the next coherent reply away.

Ultima struck the floor, began rising and found her balance still arriving a fraction after the movement she meant to make.

Beretta stopped the bout before another punch followed.

Milim stopped too.

Her hands lowered immediately.

* * *

Ultima sat back, blinked once and looked up at the small girl waiting to know whether she was all right.

“That was unpleasant.” ᵁˡ

“You made the first one unpleasant.” ᴹⁱˡ

Ultima stared at her.

Then laughed.

The check took longer than Milim would have liked and exactly as long as the people doing it required. When Shoko confirmed that the brief disorientation had ended properly, Milim finally allowed herself to turn toward the Fort.

Umaru was already cheering.

* * *

Leon's first question to Gojo concerned the damaged shoulder of the man he had fought yesterday.

“He said you were very inconvenient.” ᴸᵉ

“He was extraordinarily complimentary.” ᴳ

“I noticed.” ᴸᵉ

Gojo looked toward the small marks where their feet would begin.

“Did you?” ᴳ

Leon set his weapon in the guard he intended to use.

The answer became unnecessary.

* * *

The first beam stopped short of Gojo's body.

The next did not attempt the same arrival. It struck the prepared region beside his foot, forcing him to decide whether maintaining his position was worth continuing to stand where the second part would begin.

He moved.

Leon moved sooner.

The sword met a reinforced guard instead of the effortless interval the first shot had apparently promised to keep between them.

Gojo let the impact turn his arm, caught the next angle with Blue and came out close enough to require Leon to answer with his body.

Light flashed between them.

The cloth at Gojo's upper arm burned.

He drew back with the first breath of a laugh caught behind his teeth.

* * *

He had learned something from Diablo yesterday.

That did not make a different man's preparation the same problem with a less entertaining smile.

Leon needed less room to make the near position costly. His attacks were compact, the useful part already prepared while the visible gesture gave an observer something convenient to watch.

Gojo stopped following the gesture.

The next Blue gathered at the edge of the place where the light would complete, altering the physical route before the attack had finished choosing what it would strike.

Leon changed it.

Gojo had to change too.

For several exchanges the audience saw bright lines bend through apparently empty space and found that the simplest part of the spectacle—the two men attempting to hit each other—was the best clue to what any of it meant.

* * *

Leon had watched Purple.

He did not wait for its name to make the preparation interesting.

When Gojo began placing the smaller fields which could make a curved route useful, Leon struck the relation between two of them rather than wasting the next shot on the body whose defence had already answered that question.

One field broke.

The other remained.

Gojo abandoned the arrangement before recovering it would cost more than making a different one.

He had not completed Purple.

The smaller Red came through the place Leon's answer had left less protected.

Leon turned it with his guard.

The movement gave Gojo the approach he actually wanted.

* * *

His first punch struck protection.

The second hit did not try to overpower the same point. It changed the position of the hand maintaining it.

Leon moved back, put a fresh light beneath the next step and caught the angle of Gojo's retreat as though he had been waiting for the attempt to become personal.

Gojo felt the heat catch his sleeve again.

He could repair the damage he had taken.

He still had to choose when to spend the attention.

For one breath he gave up the best attack and made the body whole enough to stop the next guard becoming unreliable.

Leon pressed the interruption.

Gojo let him.

* * *

The boundary came nearer.

Leon knew it. He had already made the field behind Gojo's next convenient movement less useful than it appeared.

Gojo put two Blues nearer the ground.

The gap between the stronger pulls offered a route Leon had seen him move before. Leon prepared the light along it rather than following the body into a position Gojo had chosen.

The gap shifted.

Gojo did not take it.

He shortened the other approach, entered nearer Leon's guard than the prepared shot allowed and drove Red against the defended edge which had to turn before the blade could come back between them.

The force moved the body behind it.

Leon corrected.

The remaining Blue made that correction longer than the one he had chosen.

* * *

It was a small difference.

Enough space for Gojo to reach the defended arm instead of the sword. Enough delay that the next light had to be started from a position its caster had not meant to occupy.

Leon tried to complete it.

Gojo made him spend the fraction on staying upright instead.

The whole body crossed the boundary. The pull denied the first return, and the count finished before the second could be made useful.

Gojo released both fields.

For a moment he stood with one hand against his burned sleeve and watched Leon straighten outside the line.

Then he smiled.

* * *

“You did not make the larger attack,” Leon said. ᴸᵉ

“You were very interested in stopping it.” ᴳ

Leon looked toward the broken arrangement on the floor.

Then back at him.

“A useful interest.” ᴸᵉ

“I thought so.” ᴳ

Neither needed to pretend the small error was the whole of what the other could ever do.

They went to the return room together.
