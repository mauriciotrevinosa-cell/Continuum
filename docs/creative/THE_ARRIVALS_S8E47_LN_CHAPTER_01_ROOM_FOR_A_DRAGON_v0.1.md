*When: Ramiris opens the cleared extension, Veldora looks pleased enough to make Rimuru point at him before anybody else speaks.*

“The conditions are the same.” ᴿ

“I read them.” ⱽ

“Read them as a dragon.” ᴿ

Veldora grinned.

Mau looked toward the much wider floor which the host had reserved for the larger declared forms. The boundary was marked before either fighter entered. The return room belonged to the same tested part of the labyrinth, and the people moving to the next bank of seats did so by an entirely ordinary passage.

Nobody had moved a line beneath an opponent's feet.

They were simply allowing the body to fit before asking what it could do.

Mau turned toward Ramiris.

“This is very generous.” ᴹ

“Remember that when you start breaking it.” ᴿᵃ

* * *

Mai caught him before he crossed the last passage.

“Nothing from me during this one.” ᴹᵃⁱ

He looked toward her hands.

She gave him the familiar expression which meant she knew he would tell her she did not need to announce every small use of the agreement and intended to keep announcing it anyway.

Mau smiled.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

Yuka waited beside her.

He kissed her before going down. She put one hand against his chest for the brief moment they had to themselves and told him to enjoy the match.

He looked at her.

“I intend to.” ᴹ

This time she believed how easily he said it.

* * *

The shape changed before the signal.

What had been a man grinning at the edge of the floor became a body large enough that the whole audience had to rearrange its understanding of the word *nearby*.

Wings unfolded beneath the high, contained space. Light travelled across scales and stopped in the ridges between them. The head lowered toward Mau with an expression the smaller body had somehow prepared him to recognise.

“Better?” Veldora asked. ⱽ

Mau looked up.

Then farther up.

“An excellent introduction.” ᴹ

The dragon laughed.

The sound moved through the floor.

* * *

The first sweep came with more air than the previous arena had contained.

Infinity refused the direct approach of the pieces driven toward him. It did not turn the whole moving floor into a promise that he could remain comfortably attached to it.

Mau used Blue to shorten the path to a higher piece of ground before the force reached it.

Veldora's head turned.

The next attack was already following the place he would need when the first apparently convenient landing stopped being one.

Mau went up instead.

The separate grip took enough of his own motion to keep the fall from completing too soon. He released the copied operation, took the narrow surface ahead of the first breath and bent only the edge he needed to escape through.

The rest struck the unoccupied floor beyond him.

Stone came apart in a line large enough to stop the cheering for a moment.

* * *

He could not fold the whole answer.

He did not try.

Sky closed before Shrine's cuts crossed the exposed seam of the ridge beside the dragon's foreleg. The lines were precise. The effect was considerably less dramatic against the body than it would have been against a wall.

Veldora felt them.

He moved the leg before the second attack could repeat the same reach.

Mau used the separate force on the broken fragments, made them cross the retreat and followed with his own body while the dragon chose which part to turn away from first.

The dagger struck a guarded joint.

It cut enough to be noticed.

Not enough to make remaining there sensible.

Veldora's turn caught him before he left.

* * *

The impact went through Mau's reinforced side.

He felt something in the ribs begin to give and turned with the force before it could finish doing much worse. The separate grip caught a broken slab beneath him, not as a perfect flying platform but as a thing which could take the first collision instead of his spine.

The slab shattered.

Mau hit the floor beyond it, rolled and came up with both daggers still in his hands.

Repair gathered along the injured side.

The next breath hurt less.

It still cost.

Above him, the dragon's eyes narrowed with delighted concentration.

“You kept the weapons.” ⱽ

“I've become attached to them.” ᴹ

Veldora's answering laugh came with the next rush of air.

* * *

In the seats, Yuka had stood without deciding to.

Maki put a hand lightly against the back of the empty chair rather than trying to make her sit down.

Mau was moving again.

Not scrambling toward a door somebody had hidden. Not using the only breath he had left to ask someone who could not hear him for help.

He looked up at the real dragon and grinned.

Yuka let out the breath she had been holding.

Beside Rimuru, Milim's eyes shone.

“I want a turn.” ᴹⁱˡ

“You have a schedule,” Umaru informed her. ᵁ

Milim looked offended by the quality of the evidence again.

* * *

Mau sent a Red toward the higher guard.

Veldora moved through the less costly path before the shot had finished making the obvious route unpleasant.

Mau changed the next attack.

The dragon changed with it.

The third choice looked almost lucky from the seats. Ciel could see enough of the timing to know the apparent luck belonged to an examination of the alternatives rather than a wish made after the result.

*He keeps choosing the route you make least costly,* she said.

Mau landed, breathing hard.

*Then I should stop offering one.*

*You may find that expensive.*

He looked up at the enormous body beginning its next turn.

*I have been noticing a theme.*

* * *

He did not attempt to learn a new power from the problem.

The next Blue gathered low, a concentrated demand on the space beneath the dragon's intended landing. Veldora read it and lifted instead of placing the foot where the pull would become most useful.

Mau let the field remain just long enough to change the loose ground.

Then he gave it up.

Shrine cut the ridge the lifting body had left less defended. The fragments rose under the separate force. Veldora turned away from the first group and found a second crossing the more comfortable route.

Neither would have stopped him by itself.

Together, they made him choose what to take.

Mau came through the choice with the stronger part of his physical reach.

This time the dagger found the edge beneath a scale.

* * *

Veldora moved before the wound could become the beginning of a larger one.

The wingbeat took Mau sideways.

He closed the last copied operation and reached for the sky surface along which the following breath would arrive. The fold shook under the demand. It gave him a narrow way through, no more.

He took it.

The heat passed close enough to score the outside of his shoulder.

Then there was air beneath him, the floor farther away than his body wished it to be and a dragon already turning toward the place where falling would put him.

For a moment every easy route belonged to Veldora.

Mau looked down.

The broken ridge lay beyond the dragon's right side.

The boundary waited farther beyond it.

He closed the fold.

Then he chose the route which would require both of them to work.
