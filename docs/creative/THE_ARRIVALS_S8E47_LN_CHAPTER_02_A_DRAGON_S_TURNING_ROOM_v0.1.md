*When: Mau lands on the broken ridge, Veldora sees the line he has chosen and takes away the easiest place to leave it.*

Mau had expected the dragon to.

That did not make the force of the next attack less convincing.

He used the separate grip to take the first turn of a falling block, changed his own motion with the short reach of Blue and came down nearer the body than Veldora intended.

The wing turned.

Mau closed the copied operation before cutting across the exposed stone with Shrine.

The ground broke at the place the wing's movement would make useful for the next answer.

Veldora changed it.

Mau changed with him.

* * *

It was not the sort of movement one could win by becoming very good at being thrown.

He had to choose before the body struck.

The first pressure caught his guarded shoulder. The next would have put him beneath the part of the landing which needed no special name to be fatal in a place without a host waiting to stop it.

Mau used Red low.

The repulsion did not launch the whole dragon away as though the body had forgotten what it weighed. It changed the contact between the foreleg and the broken ridge, enough that the landing had to become a turn.

He went through the turn instead of retreating in front of it.

The second dagger reached the guarded joint.

Veldora pulled the limb clear.

Mau used the withdrawal to leave with his own body still intact.

* * *

His shoulder hurt.

The ribs were answering properly again, at a cost he could feel in the operations beginning to require more deliberate attention.

Nothing in the body had become a weaker version of itself. He had simply spent on several things and could no longer pretend the next one began with an untouched morning behind it.

*You have time for one good arrangement,* Ciel said.

*I was hoping for two.*

*Then make the first good enough that the second is unnecessary.*

He laughed once.

Veldora heard it.

The dragon's answering expression made Mau very glad the room was real.

* * *

He stopped attacking the centre of the enormous body.

The next cut struck a supporting ridge. The next movement did not send the whole broken mass into Veldora's face. It lifted just enough of it to make the route beside the boundary look less useful for a body which wanted to turn there.

Veldora read the obstruction.

He took the higher path.

Mau had left it open.

Blue gathered beneath the point where the dragon would need to put his weight if he wished to complete the same turn with the least effort.

Veldora did not put it there.

He came toward Mau instead.

* * *

It was the right answer to the first half of the plan.

Mau could see why the dragon chose it. The pull would become less useful if its caster had to abandon the position from which he maintained it. The physical approach would require a smaller body to spend on escaping before it could spend on anything clever.

He escaped upward.

Not far enough to pretend the dragon could no longer reach him.

Far enough to release the first Blue, take the surface ahead of the approaching force with Sky and make the next route bend toward the guard Veldora was already turning to use.

The breath struck the changed approach.

Mau held only the narrow portion he could manage.

The rest went past into empty space.

* * *

His left hand shook.

The old stiffness had not been cured by excitement. He took the next hold with the hand he could trust, let the fold close completely and used the separate grip on the moving fragment nearest his feet.

The fragment carried the first part of his next approach.

His own body carried the rest.

Veldora turned to catch him with the guarded side.

Mau did not strike the scale.

He drove Red against the position which guard and body had just committed to sharing.

The force made the next part of the turn wider.

That was the part he needed.

* * *

The boundary had not moved.

The dragon had.

His wing crossed near the edge while his body still had room to recover. Veldora saw it, began drawing the turn back and found the familiar pull waiting where the shorter correction should have been easiest.

Mau had released the repulsion before reaching for Blue.

The second operation cost more than the first version of the plan had promised.

He held it anyway.

The separate force kept the loose fragment from giving Veldora an effortless place to complete the counter. It could not stop the dragon. It could make stopping in the wrong direction cost another fraction.

Veldora paid for the first correction.

Then the second.

The body crossed fully beyond the fixed line before the return could become the useful part of either.

* * *

Mau heard the count over his own breathing.

He could feel the dragon fighting the pull now, not because he had run out of strength but because the route had become inconvenient at the worst possible time.

The next answer would break the arrangement.

Mau knew it.

He held through the remaining fraction instead of trying to make the arrangement immortal.

The signal sounded.

He released every force he had been maintaining.

Veldora landed outside the line hard enough to send a tremor through the host's cleared floor.

For a moment nobody spoke.

* * *

Then the dragon laughed.

It began low, rolled through the room and became loud enough that the applause had to find its own way through it.

Mau sat down where he stood.

He had not planned that part.

His legs had made a convincing proposal.

Veldora changed back before approaching, bringing the conversation to a height at which Mau could answer it without leaning the rest of the way onto his back.

“You made me turn where I didn't have room.” ⱽ

Mau took a breath.

“You gave me very few other hobbies.” ᴹ

* * *

He held out a hand.

Mau took it after deciding standing up was a useful thing to begin doing again.

The dragon in the smaller shape had marks where the daggers had reached him and an expression of genuine pleasure at being given an afternoon worth remembering.

“Again,” he said. ⱽ

“In an approved place!” ᴿ

“We are in one!” ⱽ

“Tomorrow is also a day!” ᴿ

Shoko checked the fresh side injury, watched the last of the immediate repair finish and made Mau move the arm before accepting his opinion of it. Veldora submitted his own marks to the ordinary check without explaining that being a dragon made other people foolish for looking.

Ramiris came through with a drawing of the damaged ridge.

“I'm sorry.” ᴹ
