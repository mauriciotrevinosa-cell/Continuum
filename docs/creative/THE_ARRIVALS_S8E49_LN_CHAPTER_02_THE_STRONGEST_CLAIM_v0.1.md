Rimuru is there when she looks farther.

So are the people she has spent the last few days coming back to after being told she must wait for the next interesting thing.

There is no continuing attack. No hidden second stage. The host has ended the exchange and used the return they had checked, and Shoko is still making certain the girl in front of her is the same coherent, irritated person who agreed to the small test yesterday.

Milim looks at the three men.

“Did I lose?” ᴹⁱˡ

Then all three answer.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸᵗ

It is a much smaller change than the punch which had made half the floor become airborne.

Her eyes shine. Her mouth tightens around the next question as though asking it matters more than whether everyone in the audience has seen her stop moving.

“Because you were stronger?” ᴹⁱˡ

Mau looks at Yuta.

Yuta looks at Gojo.

Gojo, who has never enjoyed admitting a disadvantage without improving the wording, looks back at the girl sitting in front of him.

“No.” ᴳ

Mau shakes his head.

“There were three of us.” ᴹ

Yuta's smile is gentle, tired and entirely honest.

“We cheated.” ʸᵗ

For a moment Milim considers the explanation.

The tears stop being the next thing her face intends to do.

“You did.” ᴹⁱˡ

“Terribly,” Gojo agrees. ᴳ

She looks at all three again.

Then she brightens so completely that Shoko has to remind her to remain seated until the last question has been answered.

“Then I won.” ᴹⁱˡ

Mau begins laughing.

Yuta puts a hand over his mouth.

Gojo looks toward the host as though inviting her to find a better ending to the afternoon.

Ramiris folds her arms.

“You are all extremely troublesome.” ᴿᵃ

Milim answers the remaining questions.

She moves what Shoko asks her to move. Remembers what she is asked to remember. Complains at a familiar volume about the delay between being all right and being allowed to act like it.

When Shoko is satisfied, she lets the girl stand.

Milim looks toward the floor she has left.

Then at the other three participants, who appear quite prepared to accept that the five minutes have become enough excitement for one afternoon.

“I won,” she repeats. ᴹⁱˡ

“If you're asking me to go back in there and argue,” Mau says, “I'm willing to concede.” ᴹ

Gojo sighs with such elaborate regret that she laughs at him.

The applause is different from the sound which met the first attack that morning.

More relieved that the person they came to find is still willing to be found, even if the room in which he intends to live is not the one they first imagined.
