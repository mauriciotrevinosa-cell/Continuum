* * *

They had kept the appointment.

It was late enough for the busiest part of the festival to have become music, conversation and people deciding whether they actually wanted one more thing to eat. Mau had walked with Yuka through the streets she had meant to see, stopping when she asked rather than treating the journey as the distance between the arena and the next bed he might reasonably be told to use.

His fresh wounds had been checked.

The exhaustion was ordinary, and his to keep company with rather than a hidden danger he was pretending not to understand.

Yuka had found something he liked enough to stop making jokes while eating it.

Then his brother had arrived.

Mau decided to hear the proposal before deciding whether this counted as being found unfairly.

* * *

Rimuru wanted to show people the labyrinth.

Ramiris wanted people to see it as a place they might return to, not a drawing attached to a room in which four very unreasonable people had spent the afternoon breaking her floor.

There were visitors considering a longer stay near the Inn. People who had only just stopped believing every version of the rumours attached to Mau's name. They had watched him fight as one person.

Rimuru thought it would help them understand the other part of what he could choose to offer.

“You want them to see the army,” Mau said. ᴹ

“If you want to show it.” ᴿ

Yuka looked between them.

“And if he wants to spend tomorrow eating with me?” ʸᵏ

Rimuru smiled.

“Then Ramiris will have to terrify visitors with her own excellent work.” ᴿ

From behind him came the small, outraged voice of the host herself.

“I can do that!” ᴿᵃ

* * *

They found a table instead of deciding the details across a busy street.

Mau asked the first question before Rimuru could make the invitation sound more impressive.

“The things living there return?” ᴹ

“Under my control,” Ramiris said. ᴿᵃ

“Then I won't use Arise. Not once.” ᴹ

She looked at him for a moment.

Rimuru nodded.

“Thank you.” ᴿ

It mattered that the agreement was made before somebody found an interesting body and began deciding the original promise probably meant something smaller.

Mau had enough soldiers to show.

He did not need to take the host's inhabitants away to make the visit worthwhile.

* * *

The viewing belonged to the labyrinth's own arrangements, checked through the panels Rimuru's people were already using there. Mau would not reopen the temporary proposal contract and place a permanent observer in the back of everybody's head.

He would show no private system page, no stock, no source-room door.

The audience could watch the route Ramiris chose to make visible.

He could leave by the ordinary agreed return if something stopped being enjoyable or safe. His own soldiers would remain his responsibility; withdrawing or rebuilding a form would not become a permanent new recruitment just because a different dungeon could return its own residents.

Ramiris listened to the last part, then showed him the entrance she intended him to use.

“Conquer it if you can,” she said. ᴿᵃ

Rimuru smiled.

Mau looked at the route.

“I'll try.” ᴹ

* * *

He checked the home posts before they went back.

Muster and the forty ordinary guards would remain exactly where the household had agreed to leave them. Nothing about a performance in another city made the people who had stayed home less entitled to the protection they had been promised.

The remaining companies were available.

Six thousand five hundred and seventy-four, with the named officers and the quiet gifted sentinel counted in their proper places. The native shikigami belonged to a different call and a different set of promises.

He would not need them for this.

Yuka watched him close the private record without offering it to the public street.

“You know you aren't obliged to make all of them fit through a door at once.” ʸᵏ

Mau looked at her.

“I was hoping someone would mention that.” ᴹ

* * *

Ramiris left with a much happier drawing.

Rimuru stayed long enough to finish the small amount Yuka offered him and ask whether the place they had chosen was good.

“It was very private,” she said. ʸᵏ

He looked around.

Then back at her.

“A terrible fault of mine.” ᴿ

Mau laughed.

His brother stood, wished them both a good evening and left without finding another reason they ought to remain useful.

* * *

Yuka took Mau's hand on the way back.

“You want to do it.” ʸᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Because of the people thinking about coming home?” ʸᵏ

“Partly.” ᴹ

She waited.

He looked toward the place Ramiris had flown ahead of her own plans, excited enough to forget that being followed was not the same as being listened to.

“I think I might enjoy taking them somewhere the end isn't a person trying to keep me.” ᴹ

Yuka squeezed his hand.

“Then don't forget to leave when you're finished.” ʸᵏ

He leaned closer.

“I have an important appointment afterward.” ᴹ

“Several,” she corrected. ʸᵏ

Mau smiled.

They walked back slowly enough to keep the rest of the evening for themselves.
