*When: Mau walks into the cleared starting space alone, the first laughter comes from a visitor who has mistaken the absence of a party for a joke he understands.*

It is not unkind laughter.

The man has watched the tournament. He knows the person standing beside the entrance can make a very dangerous few minutes for somebody else.

He simply expected a demonstration of a household's safety to involve more of the household.

Mau looks up toward the panel showing the audience.

Rimuru lifts a hand.

Ramiris announces that the return route is open and the rules have not become less important because people are now watching from somewhere comfortable.

Mau nods.

Then he looks at the shadow beneath his feet.

* * *

Igris comes first.

Not with a flourish. He steps into his place beside Mau and turns his head toward the unoccupied ground.

Bellion follows, already looking at the route he will need to divide between the companies.

Beru rises on Mau's other side with an expression of such extravagant pride that Yuka begins smiling before the rest of the room has understood why.

“My king,” he says. ᴮᵉʳ

Mau looks at him.

“We discussed the volume.” ᴹ

Beru makes a visible effort.

“My king,” he repeats, only slightly quieter. ᴮᵉʳ

Bellion looks away with exceptional professionalism.

* * *

The ordinary companies begin rising behind them.

At first the audience can count the rows.

Then the rows become groups, and the groups continue appearing after the space in which a person might casually have called it a large party has been used up.

Knights settle beside soldiers whose bodies have never needed to resemble human ones. The ant companies form with the precision Beru has been delighted to teach them. Heavier shapes take their positions farther back, leaving the routes the smaller bodies will use clear before anyone has to be ordered out of danger.

The visitor who laughed stops.

Nobody has put a new spell in the air for him to admire instead.

The ground simply continues acquiring people who are waiting for one man's next instruction.

* * *

Tank settles near a flank with a weight the panel cannot entirely make small.

Fang takes a place from which the route ahead will be visible without making the ordinary soldiers stand inside his working line. Rampart's several hands remain lowered while a column passes between the positions he has been asked to hold.

Lantern unfolds one wing and waits.

Kaisel rises behind the lower shapes. Kamish follows, larger, quiet enough that the audience hears the small shift in Rimuru's chair before anybody begins explaining what they are seeing.

The remaining officers take their existing places.

Rime stands among them without being named to the audience as a stolen original Monarch. The quiet sentinel remains a separate presence, no announcement of the private person whose gift it was required to make it useful.

Nothing about the call opens the rooms Mau has promised to keep private.

* * *

In the seats, Umaru counts the division she can recognise.

“Muster stayed home,” she says. ᵁ

Rimuru nods.

“And the guards.” ᴿ

She looks relieved by the ordinary answer, then returns to the extraordinary one spreading across the panel.

Yuka has seen the record.

That does not make the actual breadth of the formation the same thing as reading a number while sitting beside a bed.

Maki looks toward Mau's small figure at the front.

Yuta remains quiet for a long moment.

At the other side of the room, Gazel watches how the heavy bodies have left the smaller routes clear.

That interests him almost as much as the dragons.

* * *

Rimuru speaks before the silence can turn the demonstration into a rumour which will be difficult to correct later.

“These are the companies he's chosen to bring. The household still has its home posts.” ᴿ

Somebody asks how many.

Mau answers through the agreed communication, without showing the private page which would make the answer convenient.

“Six thousand five hundred and seventy-four here.” ᴹ

The number takes its time reaching every part of the room.

Rimuru looks toward the people considering a future near the Inn.

“One of the people choosing to help protect that home,” he says. “Not its owner.” ᴿ

Mau hears it.

He is grateful enough not to improve the introduction with a joke.

* * *

The army does not enter as a crowd.

Bellion divides the first companies. Igris checks the approach. The heavier forms return to storage until a room actually requires them, leaving the entry available to the group which will use it rather than proving how many bodies can be made inconvenient at once.

The ordinary scouts move ahead.

Mau walks behind their confirmed route with Beru at his side.

The remainder waits in the shadow where waiting does not require a wall to be removed.

There is no chant over the first resident they encounter.

No fresh registration.

The agreement has begun.

* * *

Ramiris's first room is a much better invitation to the public than a cleared fighting floor.

There are routes to examine, places where an eager group can overextend and a guardian which will not remain quietly in front of the person trying to strike it.

Igris moves the first two soldiers away from the apparently obvious approach.

Bellion puts another company where the retreat will need to remain open.

Beru looks at the guardian.

Then at Mau.

“May I?” ᴮᵉʳ

“Without taking the ceiling.” ᴹ

The insect's smile becomes radiant.

* * *

The guardian lasts much less time than Ramiris has expected it to.

It still makes the first mistake costly enough to prove the room is doing what she intended. The mistake simply belongs to a shape whose captain has already arranged how to recover it.

The route is clear.

The army moves.

On the next panel, a second group has already reached the point where the first will need its answer.

Ramiris leans forward.

Mau looks toward the next doorway with the expression of somebody beginning to understand that he has brought a very unusual family on an outing.

Beru walks beside him, pleased beyond all reason.

“A wonderful morning, my king.” ᴮᵉʳ
