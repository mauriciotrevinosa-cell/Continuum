*When: the third company stops before the fork, the people watching realise the army is not simply running faster than the dungeon can hurt it.*

Igris waits for the scout to come back.

The returned shape points toward the passage whose floor has remained suspiciously clear. Bellion sends a small group to the other side, keeps the central route open and lets the room reveal what it expects an impatient visitor to do.

The trap closes over empty stone.

Beru looks almost disappointed.

Mau walks past him.

“There will be another one.” ᴹ

“I am certain, my king.” ᴮᵉʳ

The confidence does not make Ramiris feel better.

* * *

The early floors pass in groups of different problems.

Not every group requires a captain. Not every room deserves a dragon. The soldiers who find a turn report it before somebody important arrives to become surprised on their behalf.

When the corridor narrows, the larger bodies withdraw instead of breaking the walls to demonstrate how little patience a powerful person needs.

When a guardian forces a crossing, the ordinary company gives ground toward the place Igris has already arranged for the second to meet it.

Two forms go down in the exchange.

Mau pays the familiar cost to rebuild the existing links after the route is clear. Nobody new appears in the register. The guardian is left to the host's return, its body neither gathered nor called into somebody else's service.

On the panels, Gazel watches Bellion move the rear company forward before the front has become exhausted.

He asks Rimuru whether they have practised together for long.

Rimuru takes a moment before answering.

“Longer than the outside world saw.” ᴿ

He does not offer the part Mau has not chosen to show.

* * *

The flooded room makes the first genuinely poor-looking crossing.

There is a route above the water. There is also something waiting for the person who decides above must therefore mean safe.

Mau lets the scouts find enough of it before calling the company back.

Rime takes a place near the lower edge. The constructed cold leaves a bounded footing across the first useful stretch, not a command over every winter that has ever existed.

Fang uses the next opening to take the guardian's attention while the ordinary company changes banks.

Rampart holds the near support long enough for the smaller bodies to finish crossing.

Then they stop maintaining what no longer needs holding.

The room remains a room Ramiris has made.

It has simply encountered people who know how to leave one.

* * *

The first flying chamber earns Kaisel a place outside storage.

The wyvern does not fit through the next doorway. He does not attempt to make that the doorway's problem.

Mau recalls him after the crossing, lets the ordinary scouts take the corridor and continues on foot while Beru gives an unnecessarily stirring account of the manoeuvre to nobody who needs convincing it happened.

In the audience, Yuka has begun laughing at the things she can tell Mau is hearing beside him.

Shion looks at her.

“Is he always like that?” ˢʰⁱ

Yuka looks toward Beru's extravagant gesture on the panel.

“Sometimes he's quieter because he's preparing something worse.” ʸᵏ

Shion considers that.

Then she laughs too.

* * *

By the fortieth floor, Ramiris has stopped announcing quite so confidently how long the next arrangement will require.

She still watches every room.

She sees the parts which work. The guard which makes a captain turn. The environmental change which forces Mau to recall a large body rather than keep enjoying how easily it answers the first problem.

She also sees Bellion put an entire reserve where a smaller party would have needed to rest.

The next cup is very small.

Rimuru notices it anyway.

* * *

The fiftieth guardian gets Kamish.

Mau calls the dragon only after the first companies have withdrawn from the line the host has agreed can receive his attack. Igris checks the last crossing. Bellion raises a hand. The ordinary soldiers stop.

The guardian rises to meet the larger shape.

For a moment the room becomes the sort of spectacle the audience has expected since the first wings unfolded.

Kamish's force strikes the defended part, breaks enough of it to expose the next reach and leaves Beru an opening the insect accepts with almost indecent joy.

The fight ends much sooner than the introduction to it.

Ramiris stares at the panel.

* * *

Her guardian returns where she tells it to return.

The dungeon is doing its work.

That ought to be comforting.

She looks at the room the army has just cleared, then down at the small cup.

Rimuru moves it a little farther from the next pour and asks Ciel to pass his brother a message, if she is willing.

She is.

*Ramiris is beginning to take this personally,* Ciel tells Mau.

He looks toward the route ahead.

*She asked me to conquer it.*

There is a pause before Rimuru's reply arrives.

*Yes. I helped.*

Mau starts laughing.

Beru looks up at once.

* * *

The request takes a little explanation.

People who come in ordinary parties will need to see the working parts of the rooms, not only what happens when an army spots the entrance before the front line is tired.

Ramiris has built things which deserve to be encountered properly. The demonstration can give them that as well as showing that Mau has enough hands to avoid them.

*Let a few traps actually catch the people you've assigned to test them,* Rimuru asks. *Don't bring every answer back before anyone can see the question.*

Mau looks at the captains.

*And the end?*

*The guest inn on ninety. No new argument with Veldora on a hundred. He has already begun making suggestions.*

Mau looks up toward the watching panel.

Rimuru has acquired the expression of a man regretting an invitation he does not entirely regret.

Mau nods.

*Ninety. Show the traps. Leave the residents with their host.*

*Thank you.*

* * *

He explains the change to the officers before proceeding.

Igris looks toward the next doorway.

Bellion adjusts the groups, assigning the trial approaches to forms which can withdraw safely instead of making the whole army pretend it has forgotten its training.

Beru listens with an intensity which grows more alarming as the explanation becomes more theatrical.

“My king,” he says. ᴮᵉʳ

Mau looks at him.

“May I be defeated?” ᴮᵉʳ

For a moment, all the ordinary soldiers nearby remain wonderfully still.

Mau closes his eyes.

“Briefly.” ᴹ
