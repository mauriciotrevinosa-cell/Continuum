*When: the first trap after fifty actually closes around the advance group, Ramiris sits forward so quickly that the small cup nearly goes over.*

“There,” she says. ᴿᵃ

Rimuru looks at the panel.

“There.” ᴿ

It is a good trap.

The obvious route closes behind a visitor before the less obvious one becomes the necessary way out. The room does not kill somebody for choosing quickly. It makes the quick choice become a problem the rest of the party must help answer.

Bellion has left exactly that sort of party in place to answer it.

Two soldiers hold the near approach. Igris tests the mechanism from the proper side. Mau waits while the assigned group works instead of pulling the entire obstacle apart with a larger force.

The audience sees what the room does.

Ramiris begins explaining it again.

* * *

Beru's opportunity arrives three floors later.

He approaches the next guardian with the solemnity of a person who has been entrusted with an important role and intends to make everyone aware of the trust.

Mau watches him take two entirely reasonable defensive positions before selecting the third, which has the considerable disadvantage of being exactly where the guardian's next attack will go.

Igris turns his head.

Bellion looks steadily down at the route notes.

Beru raises one hand toward his king.

“At last,” he says, “a worthy end.” ᴮᵉʳ

Mau puts a hand against his face.

* * *

The guardian strikes the prepared shape.

Beru lets the body break apart around the safe withdrawal he has already been told to use. The link remains his own. Nothing has been lost from the register except the dignity of the people who know precisely how much he is enjoying this.

His voice carries through the last part of the performance.

“Tell my future queen—” ᴮᵉʳ

“She can hear you,” Mau says. ᴹ

Beru's remaining outline turns toward the panel.

In the audience, Yuka begins laughing hard enough to lean against Mai.

“Then let her know I was magnificent.” ᴮᵉʳ

The form disappears.

There is a beat of silence.

Then the household loses all composure.

* * *

Ramiris looks toward Rimuru.

He has both hands over his mouth.

“That one talks a lot.” ᴿᵃ

“He does.” ᴿ

“Did the attack work?” ᴿᵃ

Rimuru looks back at the guardian, whose successful timing has indeed reached the form the insect placed in its way.

“The attack worked.” ᴿ

That is entirely true.

He decides not to improve it until she has finished explaining why the next room will be more difficult.

* * *

The rooms do become more difficult.

Not in the way the architect made a year out of an hour. No hidden command insists Mau must continue after the choice has ceased to be one he can make.

There is simply more to notice.

A path which changes while people are crossing it. A guardian whose first fall does not mean the room is finished. A turn which makes the rear of a party matter as much as its front.

Mau lets the assigned groups find the problems.

The captains let them solve what they can before supplying a larger answer.

Some forms withdraw. Others are rebuilt with the cost which already belongs to them. The army does not gain a single new link from the host's inhabitants, and no reward page opens to announce that an afternoon of showing off has improved Mau's body.

* * *

Beru returns after enough rooms have passed for his absence to become, in his opinion, emotionally significant.

He steps beside Mau with the grave face of a survivor.

Igris looks at him.

Beru looks back.

“My sacrifice was not in vain.” ᴮᵉʳ

Bellion makes a small sound which he recovers into a perfectly professional report about the next landing.

Mau accepts the report.

Then he looks at Beru.

“You are not doing a second one.” ᴹ

The insect's expression becomes unbearably wounded.

“There will be another outing,” Mau adds. ᴹ

Beru brightens immediately.

* * *

The deepest part they visit requires more than enthusiasm.

The army can still supply force. Mau still has more available bodies than any ordinary party will bring through the entrance. What changes is how much attention the route itself asks him to give, even with officers who can make decisions without waiting for his eyes to reach them.

He recalls the larger groups where the corridor does not need them.

Kaisel is not asked to become smaller because somebody has put an inconvenient door on the map. Kamish remains stored once the next room is plainly better answered by a careful approach than a dragon's immediate opinion.

At the last defended crossing, Igris puts the ordinary soldiers where the return will remain possible.

Bellion keeps the next group back until the landing is confirmed.

Mau follows the cleared route on his own feet.

* * *

The inn on ninety is almost offensively comfortable after so much dungeon.

He stands in the entrance for a moment, looks at the places where a tired person could sit down and begins laughing.

Beru looks around with instant concern for whether anything in the room has failed to meet the dignity of his monarch.

“It's good,” Mau tells him. ᴹ

The insect relaxes.

Mau sits.

At the other end of the communication, Rimuru lets out the breath he has been holding since Veldora began describing how much more interesting a hundred would be.

“Ninety,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Ninety,” Rimuru agrees. ᴿ

* * *

They take the agreed return.

The companies are counted and recalled in their existing places before the route closes. The home posts have never moved. The register remains six thousand six hundred and fifteen, with the native calls separate and the quiet rooms still belonging to their guests.

Mau has taken no chest, no resident, no new currency.

He has acquired a considerable amount of Beru's performance and suspects the household will not let either of them forget it.

* * *

Ramiris reaches him before he has finished having the ordinary check.

“I am going to make something harder for you.” ᴿᵃ

Mau looks toward her.

She is no longer drooping over the cup. Her hands have begun describing the shape of an idea she has not yet finished deciding how to build.

“For an army,” she adds. “Properly. So you can't simply bring more people than I expected.” ᴿᵃ

“That sounds interesting.” ᴹ

She narrows her eyes.

“Did the traps really work?” ᴿᵃ

Mau looks toward the place Beru has begun accepting praise from an audience which appears to understand the difference between magnificent and useful.

“Yes,” he says. “I cheated a little before I let them.” ᴹ

Ramiris stares at him.

Then she laughs.

“I knew it.” ᴿᵃ

She tells him about the next idea until Shoko asks whether he can hear it sitting down.

Ramiris follows him to the chair and keeps talking.

Yuka comes to sit on the arm beside him, careful not to put the fresh weight on the shoulder which has done enough for one day.

“Did you enjoy the outing?” she asks. ʸᵏ

“Very much.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸᵏ
