*When: the musician offers her the instrument, Bocchi looks at the part of the neck she has been asking about rather than the people standing nearby.*

“Only if you want,” the woman says.

Bocchi nods.

She sits before the answer can become a promise to entertain everybody on the street. Nijika moves one of the chairs so her elbow has room. Kita waits beside her without counting down to the first note.

Mau stands with Yuka at the edge of the small audience.

He does not wave to make certain she knows he is there.

She already saw him arrive.

* * *

The first attempt is wrong.

Bocchi stops, looks at her hand and asks whether she has misunderstood the place where the pressure changes.

The musician shows her again.

She tries it more slowly.

The sound she wanted arrives between two others which are not yet quite what she meant.

Bocchi looks up.

The woman smiles and asks whether she wants to put the earlier phrase before it.

She does.

By the time she gives the instrument back, there has been no grand performance, no announcement which requires her to find a new way of surviving the next round of applause.

She has simply played something she wanted to understand with people who wanted to hear her understand it.

Mau tells her which small part he liked.

She knows exactly the one he means.

* * *

Seika watches from a seat she has not needed to arrange for anyone else.

Ram comes beside her with the drink she went to find.

“You can hear it from here,” Ram says. ᴿᵐ

“That was the ambitious part of the plan.” ˢᵉⁱ

Seika smiles into the cup.

For a while they listen without being obliged to turn the quiet into a better conversation.

When the group begins putting the borrowed things away, Seika asks whether Ram would like to have tea sometime after they return home.

“Am I assisting with something?” ᴿᵐ

“Sitting down. If that proves difficult, I can demonstrate.” ˢᵉⁱ

Ram's expression changes by the smallest amount.

“A useful offer.” ᴿᵐ

“Is that a yes?” ˢᵉⁱ

“Yes.” ᴿᵐ

* * *

Farther down the street, Umaru has discovered something Rem has not seen.

Rem knows from the way she turns that the next explanation will involve going back through the crowd and asking somebody to demonstrate the game again.

This time Rem reaches for her hand first.

Umaru looks down, surprised.

“So we don't lose each other,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

It is true.

It is not the whole reason she chose it.

Umaru closes her fingers around Rem's and begins explaining where they ought to stand to see best. Rem follows with a smile she no longer needs to hide from herself in order to understand it later.

Neither has promised the afternoon a name.

They have chosen to keep walking through it together.

* * *

Hinata finds Anko where they agreed to meet.

There is no question of whether the invitation has survived somebody losing a match. They have already answered that part.

Anko looks toward the small folded paper Hinata is carrying.

“Work?” ᴬⁿ

“A reply I finished before coming.” ᴴⁱ

“A remarkable technique. I should learn it.” ᴬⁿ

Hinata smiles and puts the paper away.

They walk toward the quieter turn without needing a pair of maps to make the choice look sensible.

* * *

The next question takes longer to ask.

Anko has been thinking about the return journey. About a city whose distance from the house will not become smaller simply because she has enjoyed someone more than she expected.

Hinata listens to the practical part before answering the part underneath it.

“I would like to see you again,” she says. ᴴⁱ

Anko looks at her.

“Not only if a meeting takes me past the right road.” ᴴⁱ

“Good.” ᴬⁿ

The word comes out almost too quietly.

Hinata steps nearer.

“May I?” ᴴⁱ

Anko's smile is small enough to belong to just the two of them.

“Yes.” ᴬⁿ

* * *

The kiss is brief.

Neither turns it into something which must decide where the rest of her life will be lived before she is allowed to enjoy the evening.

When they part, Anko looks at the woman in front of her with the pleased uncertainty of somebody who has reached the thing she intended and discovered she would like to remain there a little longer.

Hinata offers her hand.

She takes it.

The next part of the walk requires no new explanation.

* * *

Mau and Yuka find a place to sit before the light changes.

He has done enough that the invitation to remain still is unusually easy to accept. She rests beside him, watching people choose which part of the festival they want to keep a little longer.

Bocchi comes past with the others, talking about the borrowed instrument. Rem and Umaru stop long enough to show Ori what they have found. Nazuna calls to Seika while Ko waits beside the place she has chosen to stand in the shade.

Yuka leans against Mau's shoulder.

“Nobody wants to go home yet,” she says. ʸᵏ
