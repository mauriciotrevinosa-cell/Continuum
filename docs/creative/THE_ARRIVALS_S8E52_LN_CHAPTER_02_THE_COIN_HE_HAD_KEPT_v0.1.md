*When: Rimuru asks whether they have found a roof, Mau knows which conversation has finally come back to them.*

He looks toward the people finishing supper.

Yuta is listening to Maki's account of something which happened while the rest of them were occupied with the dungeon. Gojo has found a chair close enough to Shoko's that he can ask a question without making the whole courtyard part of the answer.

“We can ask,” Mau says. ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

That is the whole arrangement at first.

* * *

The terrace belongs to the people putting them up.

They ask before taking cups there and are shown the ordinary way up, the part of the edge to keep clear and the place where putting four chairs will not prevent somebody from opening the door.

Gojo complains that good manners have made the expedition less dramatic.

Yuta hands him the chair he can carry himself.

By the time they sit down, the city has become the kind of sound a person can listen to without feeling he must answer every piece of it.

Mau waits.

Rimuru takes the coin out.

* * *

He has carried it long enough for the small weight to have become familiar in a place where almost everything else has changed at least once.

It does not contain an answer to what he feels.

He has stopped asking it to.

“Shizue,” he says. ᴿ

Yuta looks toward him.

Gojo's expression becomes quieter without anybody needing to ask him to make it so.

Rimuru tells them enough to understand the name. The woman he met, the life which had already asked more of her than he knew how to put right, the wish she left him and the body in which he learned how many people might look at a familiar face and expect somebody else.

Mau listens to the parts his brother chooses to add to the earlier conversation.

He does not finish the story on his behalf.

* * *

“I thought having everyone here would make it simple,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

He looks down at the coin.

“Then I thought wanting it for myself might be a bad reason.” ᴿ

Yuta rests his cup against his knee.

“It depends what you're asking her to do with it.” ʸᵗ

Rimuru looks up.

“If you're asking her to make every difficult thing you feel become easy, she can't agree before she's here.” ʸᵗ

“I know.” ᴿ

“If you're asking whether she'd like another day,” Gojo says, “that seems like something she should get to answer.” ᴳ

The wind moves the edge of the paper Rimuru has kept beneath the cup.

For a moment all four watch it.

* * *

“I don't know what I want it to become,” Rimuru says at last. ᴿ

Mau nods.

“You don't need to promise the whole future before you say hello.” ᴹ

His brother looks toward him.

“You seem to have made a considerable future out of it.” ᴿ

Mau smiles.

“Eventually.” ᴹ

Gojo lifts his cup.

“A notably unrestrained example.” ᴳ

Yuta laughs softly.

Mau lets them have the joke because it makes his brother smile without taking the serious part away.

* * *

The coin catches the terrace light.

Rimuru turns it once between his fingers.

“I want to know whether she would like this place,” he says. “Not the city I made for everybody. The one where I learned how to be happy without being responsible for the answer before anyone asked a question.” ᴿ

He looks toward the courtyard below.

“I want to hear what she thinks. I want her to be able to tell me I have misunderstood something.” ᴿ

Gojo smiles.

“You have us for that.” ᴳ

“An excellent argument against acquiring a larger audience.” ᴿ

They laugh together.

The coin remains in Rimuru's hand afterward.

It feels lighter without having changed at all.

* * *

Mau asks the practical question only when his brother has finished the part which deserves not to be hurried.

“Would you like company when you speak to DIO?” ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

“Not to decide for me.” ᴿ

“I know.” ᴹ

Yuta leans back.

“And after she arrives?” ʸᵗ

Rimuru thinks about it.

There are children who will have questions. People who remember her. People who know only that the name matters enough for him to have carried the coin while refusing to pretend the choice was simple.

“I'd like a little time first,” he says. ᴿ

Nobody objects.

* * *

They remain on the terrace after the decision has been made.

Gojo asks whether Mau intends to allow Beru another performance when they go home. Yuta asks whether the performance has ever depended entirely on Mau's permission. Rimuru offers an opinion which makes the other three look at him before laughing.

The conversation becomes ordinary.

That is part of why Rimuru is glad he asked them to come.

When they go down, the coin is back where he keeps it.

It has not been spent because somebody won a tournament or because the city expects its old ruler to become a simpler person now they have found him.

He knows why he will use it.

Tomorrow, he intends to ask her what she wants to do with the life it brings.
