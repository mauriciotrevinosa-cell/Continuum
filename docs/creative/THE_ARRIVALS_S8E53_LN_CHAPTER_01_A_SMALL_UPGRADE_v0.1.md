*When: DIO looks at the coin, Rimuru finds he has become more careful with the small weight than he has ever been with a crown.*

He stands beside Mau at the threshold of the existing room.

Inside, the table still has the cup Ashborn has chosen to leave there. Ciel has come to sit with the people who want company. Noise looks from the coin to the man holding it and waits instead of deciding an interesting object must become his next project.

Rimuru tells DIO who he wants to call.

Then he tells her the part he does not want to lose.

“The person I said goodbye to. Her memories. Even the difficult ones.” ᴿ

DIO looks at him.

“You aren't asking me to make her remember a better answer.” ᴰ

“No.” ᴿ

Mau watches the coin between his brother's hands.

* * *

They speak about the body before speaking about the light.

Shizue's last living moment does not have to be discarded to give her a body which can live beyond it. Yuta's arrival has already shown them that the age of the memory and the state of the body which carries it are not required to be the same exhausted ending.

“And the fire spirit?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“Not inside her.” ᴰ

“She won't need him to keep that body alive?” ᴿ

DIO shakes her head.

Rimuru lets out a breath he had not meant to hold quite so long.

Mau asks the question he cannot leave unasked.

“Only her arrival.” ᴹ

DIO meets his eyes.

“Only the body she arrives in. Nobody else's history. Nothing made convenient inside her head.” ᴰ

* * *

She touches the coin briefly.

The movement is small. When she takes her hand away, she rests it against the table instead of pretending the weakness she has brought into the room is a disguise she may remove whenever she wants applause.

Rimuru waits until she looks up.

“Thank you.” ᴿ

DIO studies the concern on his face, then gives him the smallest, driest smile.

“A small upgrade. So she lasts.” ᴰ

Mau looks down for a moment.

When he looks back, his brother is smiling too.

* * *

The room outside has been prepared with permission from the people lending it.

A chair. Water. A bed which belongs to the current visit rather than a future room somebody has already decided Shizue ought to want. The way out is ordinary, and the people waiting farther away know they have been asked to give the first conversation a little time.

Shoko has agreed where she can be found.

Rimuru keeps his human shape when he stands beside the chair. Mau waits near the door, there because his brother asked, not because this is a story he intends to tell first.

The coin is still the one Rimuru has kept.

He closes his hand around it.

This time he does not put it away.

* * *

The change in the air is quiet.

Quiet enough that the person arriving does not seem to belong to an audience waiting for an effect.

Shizue draws a breath.

Rimuru does not move until she has finished it.

She sits where the arrival has left her, one hand against the place in her chest she had expected to become still. The younger body answers with a strength which makes the next breath startling for an entirely different reason.

There is no fire living behind it.

There is no spirit buying another moment by holding an old life together.

The moment continues anyway.

* * *

She looks up.

At first the face in front of her is unfamiliar in the way something deeply familiar can become when it has changed how it stands in the world.

Then she sees the eyes watching her and the effort the person behind them is making not to turn hope into a demand.

“Rimuru?” ˢᶻ

He nods.

The name takes the whole room away from the possibility that he has called somebody who will need him to explain why she ought to know it matters.

“Yes.” ᴿ

Shizue looks at him for a long moment.

“You grew.” ˢᶻ

He laughs once, helplessly.

* * *

She looks down at her own hand.

Turns it.

The body is younger than the last one she remembers asking to stop. The memories remain exactly where she left them. She remembers being tired. Remembers the person willing to listen when there was so little she could offer him besides something else to carry.

“I thought…” ˢᶻ

The sentence does not finish.

Rimuru comes nearer only when she looks toward him again.

“It's another world,” he says. “Another chance to choose what you want to do.” ᴿ

She listens while he gives her the small part she needs first. Not the history of every crisis. Not a catalogue of what the body can now be asked to survive.

She is alive.

She does not owe the room an answer about the rest of her life before drinking the water beside her.

* * *

Mau introduces himself when his brother has found the breath to remember there is another person at the door.

Shizue looks toward him.

“His brother,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Mau smiles.

Shizue looks from one to the other as though a small, useful piece of the new world has become easier to understand.

Mau tells them where he will be and leaves the door in the position she chooses.

No hidden voice follows him into the passage to announce the Goddess's gift to the people waiting there.

The small private line has belonged to the person who needed to hear it.

* * *

Inside, Shizue holds the cup in both hands.

Rimuru sits in the chair beside her instead of making the bed feel like a place from which she must begin reassuring him.

For a while they say very little.

Then she looks toward him with the question he has known would come as soon as she found room for it.

“The children.” ˢᶻ

His smile arrives before the rest of the answer.

“They're here.” ᴿ

She goes very still.

“All five.” ᴿ

* * *

Shizue puts the cup down carefully.

Rimuru watches her hand remain against the table after the cup is safe.

“You found them.” ˢᶻ

He nods.

For the first time since arriving, her face begins to lose its effort to make the impossible manageable through being composed.

“Could I see them?” ˢᶻ

“Whenever you want.” ᴿ

She laughs through the first tears, surprised by both.

Rimuru reaches out only far enough that she can choose to take his hand.

She does.

The coin he has kept is gone.

The person he kept wishing he could speak to is still there when the next breath comes.
