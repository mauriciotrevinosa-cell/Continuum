# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 47
## Chapter 18 — An East-Facing Window

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S8 v0.1 (2026-10-30)
**Volume:** 47
**Reading order:** 18
**Source episode:** S8E54
**Continuity state:** B+45 later afternoon. MY/R future accommodation wishes only, current MY former Yuka room/Rimuru loft/Diablo bed loan remain. No new approved plan or build. Yuka grants Rimuru proposed nearby room for one nonrefusable favor, prospective Shizue guestroom/own consent future. Mau east window. Tree root circle preserved/main Inn not demolished; families near chosen people, privacy/food/materials not solved by cash. Ori Mum stays Frieren. Source band/ring/grey unchanged.

*When: Mau discovers that the room beside the room he is drawing already belongs to someone else, Yuka takes the pencil out of his hand.*

“You made the stairs disappear.” ʸ

He looks down.

She is right.

He has drawn their table carefully, two chairs, a place where she could leave something unfinished and a window broad enough to sit beside. In making space for all of it, he has erased the only way a person could get upstairs.

“We could fly.” ᴹ

“My grandfather is going to visit.” ʸ

“He could—” ᴹ

“Mau.” ʸ

He moves the table.

* * *

They have borrowed a corner of the guest courtyard where the afternoon light reaches the page. Yuka is sitting beside him with one knee tucked under her, the silver band catching against the pencil whenever she turns it.

He can see that small change in light.

He can see her.

Beyond her, the courtyard has the familiar greys of things he knows are coloured. He has learned which pot is blue from other people's arguments about it. It is beside a wall which could be several colours without changing anything he sees.

Yuka leans across his arm to extend a line.

The difference is still startling sometimes.

“What?” she asks. ʸ

“Nothing.” ᴹ

She gives him the look that answer deserves.

“You,” he admits. ᴹ

Her mouth changes before she manages to make it a disapproving expression.

“Help me find the stairs.” ʸ

* * *

They are not drawing a palace.

That becomes important when Rimuru arrives, sees the space Mau has left blank around the house and asks whether he is aware other people might also like homes.

“That's the tree.” ᴹ

“It's a very large tree.” ᴿ

“You live there.” ʸ

Rimuru considers the page again.

“It's a very reasonable tree.” ᴿ

Yuka laughs and moves her hand so he can see the circle they have kept clear. The old house goes around the roots. The proposals they bring home can grow from that arrangement; they do not require anyone to wake up and discover their bedroom has been demolished to make a more impressive entrance.

Mau taps the edge nearest the common room.

“Near here, if it works.” ᴹ

“You want to hear every argument?” ᴿ

“I want to be able to come downstairs.” ᴹ

It is such an ordinary answer that Rimuru looks at him a little longer.

Then takes the empty chair.

* * *

Yuka wants a door they can close.

She says it while drawing the little table back into a place which leaves the stairs intact.

“Everyone near us is fine,” she says. “Everyone in our room isn't.” ʸ

Mau nods.

She wants room for her grandparents to come over without having to stand in the doorway. She wants Mai close enough to complain about an idea while it is still a sketch. A shelf which does not require putting every other thing they own beneath the bed.

And the table.

“You keep drawing the table.” ᴹ

“Because you keep moving it.” ʸ

“I like the table.” ᴹ

“Then stop putting the bathroom through it.” ʸ

Rimuru begins laughing into his cup.

Mau has not noticed that line either.

* * *

The blank space beside their proposed room eventually attracts the attention of the slime.

“How attached are you to that?” ᴿ

Yuka follows his gaze.

“Why?” ʸ

He sits up a little straighter.

“I have been a very considerate neighbour.” ᴿ

Mau looks at him.

“I've been an excellent neighbour.” ᴿ

“You used to throw things at me when I wouldn't wake up.” ᴹ

“You woke up.” ᴿ

Yuka takes the page away from both of them before this becomes evidence in a case neither one should be allowed to judge.

She studies the space.

Then looks toward Rimuru with an expression Mau recognises too late to help him.

“One favour.” ʸ

Rimuru relaxes.

“Of course.” ᴿ

“You don't get to refuse.” ʸ

His smile pauses.

She waits.

“What kind of favour?” ᴿ

“One.” ʸ

Mau sets his cup down very carefully.

Rimuru looks from her to the appealing little space beside their room and makes the decision of a person who knows better.

“All right.” ᴿ

Yuka draws a small mark on the page.

He watches it become much more permanent than the room.

“Please don't be cruel.” ᴿ

“We'll see.” ʸ

* * *

The laughter fades into the comfortable sound of a pencil moving.

Rimuru looks toward the other courtyard, where the children have taken Shizue to show her something which has required several different explanations.

Yuka notices before he speaks.

“There could be a guest room.” ʸ

Mau looks down at the paper.

“What guest room?” ᴹ

“The one I'm drawing.” ʸ

“I thought we were still finding our bathroom.” ᴹ

She nudges his leg with hers.

“No vote.” ʸ

Rimuru's expression is doing something unusually difficult to tease.

Yuka sets the pencil down.

“If she wants to be nearby,” she adds. “Not because we put her name in a box.” ʸ

He nods after a moment.

“I'd like her to have somewhere she can choose.” ᴿ

“Then we ask her what she wants in it.” ʸ

Mau turns the paper slightly so the new lines will not cover the root circle.

* * *

The drawing becomes worse once everyone tries to improve it.

The table acquires four different positions. A covered path joins two buildings which may not be in the right places. Somebody remembers laundry exists. Rimuru asks what happens when the people upstairs need water, and both of them look at him as though this is an unreasonable complication to introduce into their beautiful afternoon.

“Geld is going to hate this,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Geld will know how to make it possible.” ᴿ

“That's a very polite way of saying he'll cross out most of it.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴿ

They leave the crossed-out things visible.

It is easier to laugh at a bad staircase than a home someone has already promised to build.

* * *

Ori comes through the courtyard with Frieren and stops beside their table.

“Is that us?” ᴼ

Yuka turns the paper toward her.

“It's some ideas.” ʸ

Ori studies the window seat.

“I could sit there.” ᴼ

“You could.” ᴹ

She points toward another edge of the page.

“Mum likes the light.” ᴼ

Frieren, who has been looking at the terrible staircase, raises her eyes.

“Mum also likes stairs.” ᶠ

Mau puts his face in one hand.

Yuka has to move the cup before her laughter spills it.

* * *

Later, when Rimuru has gone to fetch the people who might rescue their plan, Yuka rests her head against Mau's shoulder.

The page lies between their cups. He has started another small room at its edge, then stopped before deciding what to put inside it.

She touches the unfinished line.

“For later?” ʸ

“If we need it.” ᴹ

Neither of them puts a name there.

They have spoken about that future. Wanting it does not mean they have to fill every blank space before they have learned how to live in the rooms they already share.

Yuka draws the second chair closer to the first.

“I like this part.” ʸ

He does too.

For a while they sit without adding anything, watching the people who will want opinions on all of it pass through the courtyard.

Then Mau takes the pencil.

“One thing.” ᴹ

She tips her head to look.

He writes beside the bedroom window.

“East,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka reads the word.

“That's all you're asking for?” ʸ

He kisses the top of her head.

“I'm already asking for a lot.” ᴹ
