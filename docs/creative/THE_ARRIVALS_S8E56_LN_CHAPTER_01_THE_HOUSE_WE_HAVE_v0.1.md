*When: Ori sees the tree, she stops explaining the house and starts pulling Frieren toward it.*

The last stretch of road has contained several versions of the same explanation.

Kenya has asked whether the Fort can be seen from outside. Alice has wanted to know where the kitchen is. Chloe has listened to all of it, then asked Ori which place she likes best when she does not want to do anything.

That answer has taken longest.

Now the branches are above the roof, and Ori remembers she can show them.

Frieren changes the boat to her other hand and lets herself be hurried.

* * *

Muster is where Mau expects him.

The ordinary posts around the house are still occupied. No one has abandoned a familiar watch to be part of a spectacular afternoon elsewhere.

Mau stops long enough to listen.

The forty ordinary soldiers who stayed with Muster are accounted for. The travelling companies and officers remain his own, stored or walking with the returning household as they have been throughout the journey. There are no new names to add to the roll and no missing place hidden by a large number.

Muster finishes.

Mau thanks him.

Behind him, Yuka shifts the strap of her bag and waits without looking as though she is waiting for a king to finish a duty.

When he turns, she holds out her hand.

* * *

Rimuru slows at the door.

Shizue, beside him, looks at the house before looking at him. It is much less imposing than several places she has seen people build around somebody important.

Someone is laughing inside.

Someone else calls that if the luggage is left there, nobody will be able to bring the rest in.

“This is it?” she asks. ˢᶻ

“This is it.” ᴿ

Rimuru goes in beside her.

* * *

The Fort is both larger and smaller than the children expect.

Larger, because the explanation has somehow failed to include how many things can become part of a place people keep improving. Smaller, because Umaru's description of its political importance could have applied to a country.

Chloe steps inside with Ori.

Gale looks toward the passage and carefully keeps his bag out of it.

Umaru notices.

“Excellent.” ᵁ

Rem carries in the bedding before anyone can award him a rank.

The cushions are moved for the night instead of stacked across the route to the kitchen. Five places, ordinary blankets, the screen they have agreed to borrow where someone wants a little privacy. Frieren walks the edge while the children put down their things. She can reach each place without climbing over a sleeping person.

The door opens all the way.

Shoko checks that nobody has turned the cold hearth into a plan for keeping warm.

Umaru looks at her as though she has interrupted a promising thought.

“No,” Shoko says. ˢᵒ

Rem will be there for the first settling-in. Frieren and the other willing adults know who to call if one of the children wakes. Shizue can sit with them because she wants to, then leave when she wants to rest herself.
