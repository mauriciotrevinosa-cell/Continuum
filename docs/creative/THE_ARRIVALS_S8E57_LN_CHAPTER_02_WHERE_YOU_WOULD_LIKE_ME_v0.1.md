## Chapter 2 — Where You Would Like Me

*When: Rem asks Mau where he would like her to live, he is holding a bucket and answers the easier question first.*

“We haven't worked out where anything goes yet.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

He looks at the bucket.

“That wasn't what you asked.” ᴹ

“No.” ᴿᵉ

They have brought the breakfast cloths out together. Rem has found the line she wants to use; Mau is trying to discover whether the knot on the nearest post is meant to be untied or has become part of the building.

She lets him work at it for a few seconds before taking the cloth from his shoulder.

“You don't have to answer immediately.” ᴿᵉ

He gives up on the knot.

“I'd like you close.” ᴹ

Rem keeps the cloth folded over her hands.

It is a very ordinary answer to have taken so long.

“All right,” she says. ᴿᵉ

Then, because she knows him, she waits.

* * *

Mau puts the bucket down before trying again.

“I don't mean because somebody needs to be near the kitchen. Or because you're helping with the children this week.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴿᵉ

“I'd miss having you come past.” ᴹ

Rem looks toward the open door.

From here she can hear Umaru telling somebody that a small flag is still a flag, but that this particular one has been made offensively small.

Mau follows her glance.

“There might be other people you'd miss more.” ᴹ

“I could miss more than one person.” ᴿᵉ

He smiles.

“That's inconvenient for the drawing.” ᴹ

“Yes. I thought so.” ᴿᵉ

She gives him one edge of the cloth. They stretch it between them, shake the worst of the water from it and hang it over the line she has chosen.

The remaining corner catches at his wrist.

Rem frees it without making him stop.

“I would like to be near you,” she says. “And near Umaru. And I don't want Ram somewhere else because I've asked for those things.” ᴿᵉ

“Then we put all three on the page.” ᴹ

“That simple?” ᴿᵉ

“Writing it down? Yes. Making the building stand up is someone else's terrible afternoon.” ᴹ

She laughs, and passes him the next cloth.

* * *

Ram finds them while looking for the missing end of the conversation.

She has heard enough through the door to know that somebody is deciding where her sister might live. She is carrying a cup and appears prepared to remain until the matter has improved.

“Do I get a say?” ᴿᵐ

“We were going to leave you the bucket,” Mau says. ᴹ

She looks down at it.

“Generous.” ᴿᵐ

Rem takes the cup from her while she steps over the wet edge of the path. Ram accepts the exchange, then leans against the post Mau failed to untie.

“If you want to be beside her, put that down too,” he says. ᴹ

“I do.” ᴿᵐ

The answer arrives before he has finished making room for it.

Rem looks toward her.

Ram lifts one shoulder.

“I've become accustomed to you.” ᴿᵐ

“How unfortunate.” ᴿᵉ

“Extremely.” ᴿᵐ

Mau reaches for the last cloth and allows them the time it takes to unfold it.

Then Ram looks at him again.

“I'd also prefer to be near people who are pleased when she sits down.” ᴿᵐ

He stills a little.

Inside, a chair scrapes. Somebody is asking Rem a question before checking whether she is in the room.

She does not hurry toward it.

“We can probably manage that,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ram studies him, then takes her cup back from her sister.

“That answer was better.” ᴿᵐ

* * *

Umaru arrives with a page which has already suffered a correction.

Suika walks beside her, carrying the pencil with both hands because Umaru has been using her own hands to explain a staircase.

“You were supposed to be helping.” ᵁ

Mau looks at the washing.

“I thought I was.” ᴹ

“With the house.” ᵁ

“A considerable distinction,” Ram says. ᴿᵐ

Umaru lays the paper on a dry patch of wall and begins telling them what the problem is. She has put herself beside the Fort, which is sensible. She has put Suika beside her, which is necessary. Then Taihei has asked where the rest of the people go, and somebody has moved Mau much too far away.

Mau studies the page.

“Is that the tree or a very large cup?” ᴹ

“It's not finished.” ᵁ

“I can see that.” ᴹ

“I want both of you near.” ᵁ

She says it with enough irritation to make it clear that the difficulty is architectural and ought to have been solved before adults began wasting her morning.

Taihei comes through the doorway behind her with the empty bread basket.

“She's told me twice.” ᵀ

“Because nobody is fixing it.” ᵁ

Suika points with the pencil.

“What if we turned this?” ˢᵘ

She waits until Umaru has stopped moving the page, then draws the smallest possible change to the shape beside it.

Taihei sets the basket down.

Mau bends closer.

The altered line does something useful. It gives the little group more than one side to stand on. There is still far too much missing from the drawing to tell whether a carpenter would forgive it, but Umaru can put her two brothers where she wants without moving Suika into the tree.

“There,” Suika says. ˢᵘ

Umaru takes the pencil carefully.

“Yes.” ᵁ

* * *

Yuka finds them arguing over a corner.

She stops beside Mau, looks at the paper and rests her chin briefly against his shoulder.

“Did you leave the stairs this time?” ʸ

“Suika's in charge.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸ

Umaru points at the places she has marked.

“He's here. Taihei's there. We can still go downstairs.” ᵁ

Yuka studies the drawing.

Then she turns toward Rem.

“What do you want near you?” ʸ

The question makes Rem smile before she answers.

“Apparently everyone.” ᴿᵉ

“That's going to happen a lot,” Yuka says. ʸ

She takes the spare page Taihei has brought out and sets it beside the first.

Her grandparents have already made their wishes difficult to misunderstand. Mai will want room to leave something unfinished. Her brother will claim indifference, then ask exactly which door everyone intends to use.

There has to be room to be together without walking through somebody's bedroom to do it.

Yuka draws a little space between the proposed entrances.

“We could sit here.” ʸ

Mau looks at her.

“You and the chairs.” ᴹ

“You keep finding people you want to talk to.” ʸ

“That's fair.” ᴹ

Rem touches the empty place beside Umaru's mark.

“Could Ram and I be here?” ᴿᵉ

Yuka moves her hand so Rem can draw it herself.

* * *

Seika appears looking for Ram's cup.

She finds Ram attached to it and pauses long enough to realise that the conversation has acquired enough paper to become dangerous.

“Am I late?” ˢᵏ

“To a terrible drawing,” Ram says. ᴿᵐ

Seika comes to look.

Umaru explains the improvements. Suika quietly points out the part that was hers. Rem moves a dry cloth before the edge of the page can touch the washing.

Seika listens to all of it, then asks where Nijika and the others are supposed to be.

“We haven't asked them yet,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Bocchi won't want to be far.” ˢᵏ

“Neither will her music,” Mau says. ᴹ

Seika considers that, her eyes moving between the marks.

“And neither will I.” ˢᵏ

Ram looks at her.

Seika takes a drink from the cup she has just been handed.

“From my sister,” she adds, with excellent timing. ˢᵏ

Ram's mouth curves.

“Naturally.” ᴿᵐ

* * *

They do not solve the house beside the washing line.

The drawing still has no sensible place for a bathroom. The entrance Yuka has made wider now appears to end in a cupboard. Taihei says someone will have to ask how many of the proposed walls can actually carry the roof, and Umaru announces that this is why she has left the roof out.

Rem keeps her hand on the edge of the page while they laugh.

There are two places marked near hers. A short turn to another. Somewhere to sit without needing a reason to be useful first.

Mau catches her looking at it.

“We'll ask someone who knows what they're doing,” he says. ᴹ

“I hope so.” ᴿᵉ

“But those stay on the list.” ᴹ

She nods.

Yuka collects the pages before the next cloth is shaken out. Ram takes the bucket. Taihei objects that it was Mau's task, and Ram gives it back to him with such immediate satisfaction that Rem laughs again.

Mau carries it to the water point.

When he returns, there is still room beside them.
