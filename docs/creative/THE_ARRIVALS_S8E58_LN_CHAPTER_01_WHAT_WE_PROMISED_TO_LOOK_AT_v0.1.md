*When: Rimuru arrives for the review, Mau has given the only chair with a cushion to the person who cannot sit in it.*

“Was that for me?” ᴿ

Mau looks at the chair.

“It was for Ciel.” ᴹ

Rimuru stands beside it for a moment.

*I appreciate the intention,* she tells them.

There is enough warmth in her voice to make Mau smile before she adds that neither of them has brought the papers she asked for.

He reaches for the shelf.

Rimuru sits down.

* * *

They have chosen a quiet downstairs corner because the house is already using the table where everything was supposed to happen.

Mau can hear Yuka speaking to Mai through the open passage. The words are indistinct from here. A chair moves. Somebody asks whether the page beneath a cup is important.

“Yes,” three people answer from different directions.

Rimuru sets his drink down on bare wood.

“Good to be back.” ᴿ

Mau hands him the paper they kept before the festival.

It contains fewer spectacular possibilities than either would have managed to put on it without help. Most of the marks concern things already being used: what causes strain, what holds steady, what should stop when the person using it stops feeling steady.

At the bottom, a line has been underlined twice.

Rimuru taps it.

“All right. We came home. Now we actually look.” ᴿ

*Together,* the Noise says. ᵀᴺ

“Yes.” ᴹ

* * *

The first difficulty is choosing what to show them.

The Noise offers so much that Mau has to close his eyes before the room begins asking his attention to be in two places at once.

“Slower.” ᴹ

The information stops moving.

He rests one hand on the edge of the table, waits for the ordinary corner to become the ordinary corner again and opens his eyes.

Rimuru is watching him.

“That was an ambitious beginning.” ᴿ

*Many.* ᵀᴺ

“We noticed,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ciel asks whether they can separate the observations from the things prepared from them. It takes another attempt. What Mau receives next is easier to follow: a small part of a much larger arrangement, kept apart from the rest long enough for him to recognise it.

Nothing is being built.

He can feel the distinction now that they have taken the time to hold it still.

*There,* Ciel says. *Can you keep that amount?*

The answer is a pleased adjustment.

Mau lets out a breath.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

* * *

Rimuru cannot see every private image Mau is being shown simply because he is sitting opposite him.

They make the review slower by remembering this.

Mau describes the part he has understood. Ciel corrects a word before it becomes an incorrect conclusion. Rimuru asks what happens when the thing works for ten minutes instead of the length of a demonstration.

“That's usually where the argument begins,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know. I'm helping it begin earlier.” ᴿ

The Noise has a proposal concerning the filter Mau already uses. Ciel listens carefully. She asks about the part beneath it, then another which Mau had not known mattered to the first.

The answer becomes complicated enough that both men wait.

Rimuru takes a drink.

Mau looks toward the passage again. Mai has begun laughing. Yuka tells her something he cannot make out, and the laugh becomes louder.

He wants to find out what they are doing.

The wanting feels like a useful thing to bring into the meeting.

* * *

“I need to be able to go out there,” Mau says. ᴹ

Rimuru follows his glance.

“We haven't locked the door.” ᴿ

“I mean when something looks interesting. When there's a way it might work if we give it another hour.” ᴹ

He looks back at the page.

“I don't want every day to end with me finding out I missed the part I was doing it for.” ᴹ

Ciel remains quiet for a moment.

Then she asks him how he would like to know when that is happening.

Mau considers it.

“Ask me what I said I was going to do that day.” ᴹ

Rimuru smiles.

“That's going to be unpleasantly effective.” ᴿ

“You can have it too.” ᴹ

“I was afraid of that.” ᴿ

*Both,* the Noise says. ᵀᴺ

Ciel agrees with immediate satisfaction.

* * *

They write down the work already keeping him comfortable and capable of using what he has. Maintenance continues. Holding the filter where it is does not require pretending that every interesting thing found at the festival has been understood or fitted to his body.

The new proposals remain proposals.

“Choose one when we know what it costs,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“And when,” Mau adds. ᴹ

*Yes,* Ciel says.

The Noise takes longer.

*Keep?* ᵀᴺ

“The plans? Of course.” ᴹ

*All?* ᵀᴺ

Mau feels himself begin to grin.

“You can keep your drawings. We are currently keeping some very bad drawings downstairs.” ᴹ

Rimuru looks at the page beside his cup.

“Several of them have stairs.” ᴿ

“Now.” ᴹ

The quiet that answers is unmistakably pleased.

* * *

The question of a name arrives because Rimuru reads the word Noise aloud while making a note.

Mau feels the attention turn toward him.

“We said we'd think.” ᴹ

*Thinking.* ᵀᴺ

“Me too.” ᴹ

Rimuru looks between the paper and the man in front of him.

“You do understand you live in a house where a child named a country before anyone had agreed it was one.” ᴿ

“That's why we're being careful.” ᴹ

The Noise offers a fragment of the room from their tea party: a place set beside another, someone choosing where to sit, the particular pleasure of enough chairs.

Mau receives only a little of it.

He knows what it is.

“We'll talk about what you like,” he says. “We don't have to finish that today.” ᴹ

*No finish?* ᵀᴺ

“The name. Today.” ᴹ

He waits until the distinction settles.

“We're still talking.” ᴹ

The answer is warm and small.

*Okay.* ᵀᴺ

* * *

Rimuru checks the page once more before folding it.

“So the interesting things can wait until we've chosen something specific. And we keep the things helping already.” ᴿ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And you eat lunch.” ᴿ

Mau looks toward the door.

“Is it that late?” ᴹ

“No. I wanted one condition I could enforce.” ᴿ

He gets up with the cushion beneath one arm.

*That was my chair,* Ciel says.

Rimuru lowers the cushion back onto it.

Mau has to put his face in his hand until the laughter passes.

* * *

Yuka meets them in the passage with the page Mai has been improving.

“How did it go?” ʸ

“We agreed I should keep my appointments.” ᴹ

“A promising beginning.” ʸ

“And Rimuru's.” ᴹ

She looks toward the slime.

He seems much less pleased with this part of the result.

Yuka folds the page beneath her arm and takes Mau's hand. He walks with her to see where Mai has put the shelf. The house has continued having opinions in his absence, which is a considerable improvement over the alternatives he has known.

Inside, Ciel waits until he has finished listening to the explanation.

*Mau?*

He lets her know he is there.

*There is something I would like to ask you. For myself.*

He stops beside the window.

Yuka feels his hand change in hers and turns.

“Ciel?” she asks. ʸ

He nods.

Then answers the person waiting inside.

*All right. Ask me.*
