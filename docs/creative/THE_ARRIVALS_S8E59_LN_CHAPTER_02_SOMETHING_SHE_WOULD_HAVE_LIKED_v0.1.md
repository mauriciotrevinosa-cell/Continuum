*When: Mau tries to decide where to begin, he remembers a flower before he remembers what he meant to explain about it.*

Yuka is beside him on the bed.

Rimuru has put his chair near enough to speak without making Mau turn toward the far corner. The cup on the table is within reach. The door is closed, and Shoko knows they are trying something which may need her without turning the room into an appointment she must supervise from the pillow.

Mau looks toward the window.

“I don't remember anything before waking there.” ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

He knows the boundary. It has not moved because somebody is finally willing to ask about what comes after it.

“That's where I can start.” ᴹ

*When you're ready,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

Mau rests his hand on the blanket.

Yuka turns hers over beside it.

He takes it.

* * *

The first memory takes longer to hold than he expects.

There is the ground beneath him. The strangeness of his own clothes. Air which smells of leaves he does not know, and the woman whose expression he has spent years learning in more than one life.

He feels himself trying to explain too much before he has let the memory become clear.

*You don't have to tell me what to think of her,* Ciel says gently. ᶜⁱ

He almost laughs.

*I know.* ᴹ

*Tell me what you remember.* ᶜⁱ

He lets the explanation go.

Frieren is there.

For a moment he remembers the colour of the world so clearly that coming back to the ordinary room makes him blink.

Yuka feels his hand close.

“Here?” she asks. ʸ

He looks at her.

“Here.” ᴹ

The room has its familiar greys around her. Her hair is still itself. He knows which colours ought to be in the blanket, and the knowing remains different from seeing them.

He draws a breath and returns to the memory without trying to make it repair anything.

* * *

He chooses the small magic.

The care with which he watched Frieren show him something that existed because somebody had wanted it enough to learn how. The awkwardness of trying to place his own attention where she had placed hers. The pleasure when a thing finally did what he had hoped.

Ciel becomes very quiet.

Rimuru watches Mau's face, hearing her take in something he cannot see.

“All right?” he asks. ᴿ

*Yes,* she tells him. *He is showing me the beginning.* ᶜⁱ

Mau opens his eyes for a moment.

“The small beginning.” ᴹ

Rimuru smiles.

“Those tend to be where people keep the important things.” ᴿ

Mau rubs a finger over Yuka's knuckle.

There is no good reply to that which improves it.

He lets her see the flower.

* * *

The next part does not arrive in order.

A common table. Someone taking the wrong cup. Rimuru close enough to wake him when he would have preferred to remain asleep. Yuta teaching him with the patience of a person who would eventually enjoy reminding him how bad he had been at the beginning.

The details come with unexpected sharpness: an elbow on a table, a familiar way of making space on a bench, the effort of pretending not to care whether someone has come back.

Ciel recognises people.

She also recognises the small differences in how Mau expects them to move around him.

He has told the house that he remembers another history. The telling has never contained the experience of looking for a person in the place they would once have stood.

*You were happy here,* she says. ᶜⁱ

He keeps his eyes closed.

*Yes.* ᴹ

Yuka moves her thumb against his hand.

He tells them what Ciel has said.

Rimuru leans back a little, relief and sadness crossing his face without needing to cancel each other.

“I'm glad,” he says. ᴿ

Mau looks at him.

“You were a considerable problem.” ᴹ

“That sounds familiar.” ᴿ

Yuka laughs softly, and the sound follows him into the next memory.

* * *

He does not hurry past Frieren.

Yuka knows who she was to him. He has already told her. Still, saying that there was love has always been easier than sitting beside someone he loves now while remembering the shape of an earlier happiness.

“Do you want a little space?” he asks. ᴹ

Yuka looks down at their joined hands.

Then back at him.

“Do you?” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“Then I'm comfortable here.” ʸ

He has to look away for a moment.

Rimuru finds something to adjust on his cup and waits.

*We can stop,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

Mau shakes his head.

*This is one I want you to have.* ᴹ

* * *

The festival comes in pieces he has kept without meaning to.

There is too much sound to understand all at once. An argument over where to stand. Frieren turning toward him with an expression which has none of the distance he once thought belonged to her face.

He remembers wanting the day to last.

Then her wanting to do it again the next year.

Ciel hears what that did to him before he has found words for it.

The future was suddenly small enough to imagine. A place to return to, a person who intended to be there, a thing worth remembering the way it had happened.

His mouth moves.

Yuka sees the smile first.

“What is it?” she asks. ʸ

“She wanted to do it again.” ᴹ

He swallows.

“The festival. The next year.” ᴹ

Yuka listens.

“I think that was the part I kept replaying.” ᴹ

He can feel Ciel with him in the memory. Attentive. Following the turn of his own attention rather than trying to turn the remembered woman into a question she can solve.

Then her voice changes.

*I understand why.* ᶜⁱ

Rimuru looks up from his cup.

* * *

Mau returns to the room before he asks whether she is all right.

The shift takes effort. He wants to remain in the light of the memory, and he wants Yuka's hand where it already is. Both feelings are strong enough to make him sit quietly while they settle into places which can exist beside each other.

*Ciel?* ᴹ

*I'm here.* ᶜⁱ

*Do you want to continue?* ᴹ

Her answer does not come immediately.

Rimuru's fingers tighten around the cup.

*I would like a moment,* she says. ᶜⁱ

Mau nods.

He does not reach for the next thing.

* * *

Yuka pours him some water.

He accepts it with the hand she has freed, then sets it down again before he has drunk. She touches the bottom of the cup until he notices and takes a mouthful.

Rimuru laughs under his breath.

“That part hasn't changed.” ᴿ

Mau looks at him, then down at the cup.

“I'm concentrating.” ᴹ

“Yes. That's the dangerous part.” ᴿ

Yuka gives Rimuru a look of complete agreement.

Mau drinks again in self-defence.

Inside, Ciel makes a small sound.

He looks up before remembering there is no place in the room where he can turn to find her face.

*Was that—* ᴹ

*Yes,* she says. ᶜⁱ

Her voice is unsteady with something he has been afraid she might never find in the things he remembers.

*It was lovely.* ᶜⁱ

* * *

There is more he could show her tonight.

He knows how to go from the festival to another room, another day, the familiar gathering of people around something which has gone wrong. The paths exist close enough together that he has often found himself on one while trying to remember the other.

For once, he does not follow them simply because they are there.

“Do we have to keep going?” he asks aloud. ᴹ

Rimuru answers before the question can become an apology.

“No.” ᴿ

Yuka takes his empty cup.

*No,* Ciel says too. *I would like to keep this one for a little while.* ᶜⁱ

Mau looks toward the drawings on their table.

The future they have been making badly with a pencil is still there, beside the page Ori gave them.

“All right.” ᴹ

He leans back against the wall. Yuka settles against his side. Rimuru remains in the chair and begins telling them what Alice insisted the Fort needed before anyone was allowed to declare breakfast successful tomorrow.

Mau listens.

Ciel stays with them.

Later, when Yuka laughs at something Rimuru has left out of his first explanation, Mau feels the memory of the old festival move a little farther from the place where he has been keeping it alone.
