*When: Ciel reaches the part before she existed beside him, she asks Mau how long he had already been there.*

He tries to answer with a number.

The memory does not give him one that fits comfortably inside the room.

“This is the Trial,” he tells Yuka and Rimuru. ᴹ

Rimuru leans forward.

Yuka puts her cup down before asking whether he still wants to continue.

Mau looks at the table, at the place where the cup has left a wet mark. He follows its edge with his eyes until the shape stops competing with something much larger.

“For a little while,” he says. ᴹ

*A little while,* Ciel agrees. ᶜⁱ

* * *

The beginning is almost cruel in how ordinary it feels.

A day being lived. Someone still there to speak to. The ridiculous confidence of thinking that because a person has answered a question this morning, there will be another chance to ask them something tomorrow.

He remembers wanting things which fit inside a home.

More time in the morning. Something to eat. The chance to hear a story again because he enjoys the way she tells it. A future he can imagine continuing until it becomes hard to remember what it was like to want anything else.

Ciel follows what he can bear to show her.

*This was a life,* she says. ᶜⁱ

He closes his fingers around Yuka's hand.

*Yes.* ᴹ

*Not something you watched.* ᶜⁱ

The distinction makes his throat hurt.

He tells Rimuru what she has said.

For a little while the slime does not answer.

Then he says, quietly, “I know that's what you meant. I don't think I knew how small I was making it when I heard you.” ᴿ

Mau looks at him.

He has never needed Rimuru to find the perfect sentence before being allowed to remain in the chair.

* * *

There was a wedding.

He has already spoken about it. He has stood in another room with the memory crowding against the walls and let his friends stay. He has chosen a mountain and a ring and the woman now sitting beside him.

All of that is still true when the older room returns.

His body does not politely put the past in its proper place because he has done the work of deciding what he wants next.

The familiar expectation rises before the memory has reached the moment he dreads.

His hand becomes very still.

Yuka looks at it, then at his face.

“Mau.” ʸ

He hears her.

For one terrible second the voice is somewhere he cannot reach quickly enough.

*Stop,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

He stops trying to hold the image open.

* * *

The room returns by pieces.

His heel is against the bed frame. The wood beside the window has a small uneven place he has touched before. Yuka is looking directly at him, saying something he has to ask her to repeat.

“Do you want my hand here?” ʸ

She has lifted it toward his cheek without touching him yet.

He nods.

Her palm settles against his skin.

Rimuru has put the cup within reach, then left it there when he saw Mau could not use it immediately.

“I'm here,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know,” Yuka answers. ʸ

He looks at her until the words become easier to believe than the expectation still moving through him.

* * *

Ciel is very quiet.

Mau asks whether she has stopped.

*Yes.* ᶜⁱ

He nods, then realises she does not need the movement in order to know he has heard her.

*Did you see—* ᴹ

*Enough to understand why we needed to stop.* ᶜⁱ

Rimuru hears the answer too.

His face has lost the look he wore when he arrived with the chair.

He knows the outline Mau has given them. He has heard years put into sentences and sentences put into a conversation which eventually ended so everyone could eat.

Now Ciel cannot finish an answer about a memory which lasted only a moment.

Rimuru rests his hands on his knees.

“You made it easier for us,” he says. ᴿ

Mau's eyes move toward him.

“When you told us.” ᴿ

Mau takes time before replying.

“I was trying to get through telling it.” ᴹ

Rimuru swallows.

“Yes.” ᴿ

* * *

Yuka reaches for the water when Mau asks for it.

This time he drinks without being reminded.

His hand trembles enough that she supports the bottom of the cup and lets him keep holding it. He looks at her hand beneath his, then at the ring she is wearing.

“I still want to,” he says. ᴹ

She knows what he means before he finds the rest.

“Marry you.” ᴹ

Yuka takes the cup when he has finished.

“I know.” ʸ

He looks down.

“I don't want you thinking this means I don't.” ᴹ

She waits until he is looking at her again.

“You asked me on a mountain,” she says. “You brought an audience with terrible manners.” ʸ

A breath catches in him.

“Beru.” ᴹ

“Extremely terrible manners.” ʸ

The laugh hurts on the way out, but it is a laugh.

Yuka puts her forehead against his for a moment.

“I remember what you asked. I'm still wearing my answer.” ʸ

His eyes close.

Rimuru looks toward the window and gives them the time he can.

* * *

Mau does not try to return to the wedding.

He explains what came after it in the amount of language he can manage: how the end of one life did not remain an end, how the next could begin before his body had learned how to stop expecting the previous loss.

Ciel asks questions about the parts he can answer without reopening an image.

Sometimes he can answer.

Sometimes he has to say he does not know where the memory begins, only where he has learned to turn away from it.

They leave those places alone tonight.

Nobody asks him to count every death aloud. Nobody tells him that living here has made those lives stop belonging to him.

When he says something would take too long to explain, Rimuru asks whether he means tonight or ever.

Mau looks at him for a long time.

“Tonight.” ᴹ

“Then we can leave it tonight.” ᴿ

* * *

Ciel speaks only to Mau when she next answers.

*I wasn't there.* ᶜⁱ

He understands which absence she means.

*You couldn't be.* ᴹ

*I know.* ᶜⁱ

Her voice changes on the next words.

*You were already carrying this when I began knowing you.* ᶜⁱ

Mau looks at the door.

Beyond it, something small has fallen in the passage. A child apologises, and Rem says she can help pick it up. There are no hurried footsteps coming toward their room.

*Yes,* he tells Ciel. ᴹ

*Thank you for letting me know it now.* ᶜⁱ

He presses the heel of his free hand against his eye.

Yuka's arm goes around him when he leans toward her.

* * *

Rimuru stays until Mau has stopped looking surprised whenever an ordinary noise reaches him.

Then he asks whether they would like the chair left where it is.

Yuka says yes.

Mau reaches for his sleeve before he gets to the door.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

Rimuru looks at the hand, then at him.

“I said I had time.” ᴿ

“I know.” ᴹ

He lets go.

After the door closes, Yuka waits while Mau decides whether he wants to lie down. He asks if the lamp can stay a little longer. She moves it where the light will not be in his eyes and gets into bed beside him.

“Is this all right?” she asks. ʸ

He shifts until he can rest against her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Her hand moves slowly through his hair.

Ciel remains present without asking for the next memory.

Mau listens to Yuka breathe until the rhythm stops needing his attention to believe it will continue.
