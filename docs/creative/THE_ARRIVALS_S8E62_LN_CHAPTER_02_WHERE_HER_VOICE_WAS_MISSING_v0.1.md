*When: Ciel reaches the silence where her voice should have been, she asks Mau whether this is where she lost him.*

He keeps the memory closed long enough to answer.

*Yes.* ᴹ

Rimuru is sitting with them again. Yuka has moved the cup before Mau can knock it with the heel of his hand, and he knows from the way she is watching that his face has already told her more than he intended.

“The gate,” he says aloud. “The last one.” ᴹ

She nods.

They have both been living with what the word means.

Now he has to work out how to return to the inside of it while she remains beside him.

* * *

He begins before the first room.

There is a woman close enough for him to touch. Anger. The certainty that something must be stopped immediately. The sudden absence of any question he can ask her afterward.

Mau comes back to the room with his mouth dry.

Yuka reaches for the water.

He lifts a hand, waits until he can use it properly and takes the cup.

“I didn't know,” he says. ᴹ

Rimuru leans forward.

“About her coming back?” ᴿ

Mau nods.

“At that moment.” ᴹ

He can remember her voice afterward with terrible clarity. What he cannot do is make the second part undo what he had believed he had done during the first.

Ciel remains very quiet with him.

*You can tell me this part in words,* she says. ᶜⁱ

He does.

* * *

The room which followed had rules.

He remembers how easy it was to mistake understanding the numbers for understanding what those numbers could do to a body.

His own body, in particular.

The first movement goes wrong. He knows where the memory will reach and still feels the shock of finding his legs somewhere beyond an instruction they are no longer answering.

Yuka feels him pull away from the place where he is sitting.

“Mau.” ʸ

He closes the memory before she says his name twice.

The bed is beneath him.

His feet are on the floor.

Rimuru has moved closer without putting a hand on him before he can see it coming.

“Here?” he asks. ᴿ

Mau nods.

He presses his heel down until the floor answers with an ordinary resistance.

“Here.” ᴹ

* * *

He had described being injured.

He knows that much. He remembers being brought home, the people around him, the explanations which seemed important enough to offer before he was allowed to close his eyes.

Some of the rest had become a useful summary while he was still trying to stop it being a place.

“My back,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka puts the cup down.

“In the first fight?” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

He looks at her, then away.

“I couldn't stand up.” ᴹ

She waits for him to finish the sentence in a way which will fit inside what she already knew.

He cannot.

* * *

The deer is there in the next part he can hold.

So are the voices which stayed when the human ones did not come back. Someone deciding how to lift him. Someone putting a cup where he could reach it. A command he has to give carefully because somebody else will carry out exactly what they understand him to have asked.

Ciel follows only as far as he can remain with her.

She sees the time inside the injury.

The interval before a small movement means something. The ordinary indignity of needing help with a thing he has done alone for most of the life he remembers. The way the camp has learned where he can bear to be touched.

*Stop here,* she says. ᶜⁱ

He does.

* * *

Rimuru hears her try to speak.

The first effort produces no complete sentence.

He puts his hand beside Mau's on the blanket and waits until Ciel has decided she wants him included.

*He was hurt for days,* she says. ᶜⁱ

Mau looks at the floor.

*They were learning how to move him without making it worse.* ᶜⁱ

Yuka watches Rimuru's face change. She cannot hear Ciel, but she sees what the words have done by the time he turns toward her.

Mau speaks before either needs to find a way of asking.

“The repair took time.” ᴹ

His voice sounds wrong to him.

“There wasn't somebody I could ask to make it stop hurting for a night. We were working it out.” ᴹ

* * *

Ciel asks about the people with him.

It is the question he is most grateful to answer.

He shows her the remembered edge of Beru's attention, the care which had to fit itself into hands made for other work. Igris present when he gave an instruction and still present after there was nothing further to be done. The others taking the difficult parts seriously enough that he could sometimes let them.

“They looked after me,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka moves nearer.

“I believe you.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“I don't want that part to disappear because the other part was horrible.” ᴹ

She puts her hand over his.

“Then keep telling us that part too.” ʸ

* * *

They take a long break before the next memory.

Mau gets up. He walks to the window, returns, stands beside the table and has to laugh a little when Yuka asks whether he would like to do the entire route once more before sitting down.

“Possibly.” ᴹ

“I'll move the bag.” ʸ

She does.

Ciel stays quiet while he makes the ordinary circuit again.

Then he asks whether she wants to continue.

*Only if you can,* she says. ᶜⁱ

He listens to the room for a moment.

*A little more.* ᴹ

* * *

He does not show every fight.

He shows what a day could feel like when the way out had become the thing he checked before letting himself sleep. An attempt to clean something which no longer became clean. A cup he had grown used to seeing in the same place because moving it would make the next waking feel less manageable.

There are memories of jokes.

That surprises Ciel enough to hurt in a different way.

He had still been himself there. Irritated. Grateful. Capable of saying something ridiculous because someone was waiting for him to stop treating every word as a command.

Then the remembered space changes again, and the effort of surviving returns with the first movement.

Ciel asks to pause.

He waits with her.

* * *

Yuka knows how long he was gone inside.

He told them when he came back. She has tried to put a year beside the hour she lived outside the closed route, and it has never stopped being an impossible comparison.

What she has not known is where those days were spent.

When he tells her a little more, her hand becomes very still.

“You said you had to recover,” she says. ʸ

“I did.” ᴹ

“I know. I thought—” ʸ

She stops.

Mau waits, looking at her.

“I didn't know it meant that.” ʸ

He has no answer which makes the difference smaller.

* * *

They finish at his return.

The ring. Her face. The voice he had been waiting to hear for so long that hearing it had made the last part of his effort fail all at once.

Ciel hears him remembering that she had not abandoned him.

He feels her reaction before she finds a sentence.

Mau opens his eyes.

Yuka is in front of him. Rimuru is still beside the chair. His own hands are on a blanket which somebody washed in a place with water and a door and people who know where he sleeps.

*You were here,* he tells Ciel. ᴹ

*I was trying to reach you.* ᶜⁱ

*I know.* ᴹ

This time he can say it before he begins crying.

* * *

It takes time for the room to become easy to look at again.

Ciel thanks him. Her voice will not quite hold steady through the words, and Mau lets her say them without explaining why she does not owe him gratitude.

He is beginning to understand how much of the conversation disappears when he decides which answer other people are allowed to give him.

Rimuru sits with his hands open on his knees.

Yuka stays close enough for Mau to rest against her when he needs to.

After a while, he looks toward the door.

“I need to tell them more,” he says. ᴹ

Rimuru nods slowly.

“You can choose when.” ᴿ

“I don't want to wait until another thing makes it difficult to explain.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him.

There is something she wants to ask. He can see it in the way she has been holding her mouth, and he is tired enough to be frightened of how much of the answer he has still not found for himself.

He turns his hand over beneath hers.

“Stay?” he asks. ᴹ

Her fingers close around his.

“I'm here.” ʸ
