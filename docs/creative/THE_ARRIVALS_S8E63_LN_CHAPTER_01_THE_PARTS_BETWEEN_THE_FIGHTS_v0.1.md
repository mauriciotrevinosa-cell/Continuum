*When: Mau asks whether they can stay after dinner, Yuta puts his empty bowl down instead of carrying it to the kitchen.*

“Of course.” ʸᵗ

Mau looks around the table.

There are people who have already heard pieces of what he wants to say. People who were in the clinic when he came back. People who have asked him how he is and received an answer he could manage without having to visit the place the question was touching.

He has asked Rimuru to stay beside him.

Yuka remains on his other side.

“I want to explain something better,” he says. ᴹ

The chairs stop moving one at a time.

* * *

Momo takes the game someone has left beside the bowls into the Fort. Rem asks whether she wants help with it, then stays when Momo says she has enough willing experts already.

The door remains where it has always been.

They can hear a small argument begin inside and settle into the ordinary noise of people doing something together.

Mau listens until he has found where to begin.

“Ciel asked to see more of what I remember.” ᴹ

Shoko's eyes move toward him.

He nods once. She knows they have been trying.

“We've been doing it slowly. She sees what I can show her. Rimuru hears her when she wants him to. Yuka's been with us.” ᴹ

Mai leans her forearms on the table.

“You don't have to show us.” ᴹᵃ

“I know.” ᴹ

It takes him a moment to be sure he does.

“I want to tell you. In words.” ᴹ

* * *

He begins with the part they know.

Makima. The closed route. The difference between the time they spent trying to reach him and the time he had to live inside it.

Nobody looks surprised at the year.

He is grateful for that. He could not bear to discover he has somehow failed to tell them even the largest thing.

“I told you about the fights,” he says. “How it was changing what reached them. What reached me.” ᴹ

Gojo nods.

“I don't think I explained the part between them.” ᴹ

Mau puts both hands on the table.

Then takes one back because it is easier to speak with Yuka holding it.

“The first time I thought I understood the room, I got it wrong.” ᴹ

* * *

The words are too tidy.

He can hear it even as he finds them.

An injury. A period of recovery. Help from something he could call. Each sounds like the beginning of an account someone can put beside the others and understand.

“My legs wouldn't do what I asked,” he says. ᴹ

Shoko sits forward.

“From the injury to your back?” ˢᵒ

He nods.

“Did you know it would recover?” ˢᵒ

Mau looks at her.

The answer takes longer than the question.

“No.” ᴹ

Yuka's fingers close around his.

“We knew what we could try. The deer. What I could repair. What we could keep from getting worse while we worked out the next part.” ᴹ

His eyes go to the table again.

“I didn't know how long it would take. I didn't know if the next thing would find us before I could get up.” ᴹ

* * *

Kaneki has stopped turning his cup.

Mau finds it easier to look at the familiar hand beside it than at the expression above it.

“They had to move me. Clean things. Work out how I could eat without making it worse.” ᴹ

He feels himself beginning to smile at the wrong point in the sentence and lets the smile go.

“Beru was very serious about it.” ᴹ

“I'd expect him to be,” Yuta says quietly. ʸᵗ

Mau looks toward him.

“They all were. That's important.” ᴹ

Yuta nods.

For a little while Mau tells them about that part: the instructions carried out carefully, the cup put within reach, the officers learning which decisions he could manage and which ones required somebody to wait for him to finish being tired.

They listen to that too.

* * *

Rimuru waits until Mau has finished the sentence before asking whether he can say something Ciel has asked him to pass on.

Mau nods.

“She says she understands the difference now. Between knowing he survived and being there while he didn't know whether the next part would work.” ᴿ

He has to stop before repeating the rest.

Mau looks at him.

“She doesn't want us to make him prove it by telling every part.” ᴿ

Shoko reaches for the cup near her, finds it empty and sets it down again.

“We don't need that.” ˢᵒ

“I know,” Mau says. ᴹ

His voice sounds less certain than the words.

She looks at him directly.

“You don't have to persuade me it hurt.” ˢᵒ

He lowers his eyes.

Yuka keeps his hand.

* * *

The long stretches are difficult in another way.

He can describe a fight by what moved and what had to stop moving. It is harder to explain why he remembers an ordinary object with such uncomfortable clarity, or what it meant to wake up and have the same way out remain closed.

“I kept thinking of things I'd tell you when I got home,” he says. ᴹ

Somebody shifts a chair.

“Then I had to stop thinking about home for a while because I couldn't do the next thing if I was still there in my head.” ᴹ

He draws a breath.

“Sometimes I was funny. Sometimes I was horrible to be around. Sometimes a good day was just being able to sleep without making everyone move the camp again.” ᴹ

Mai looks down at her hands.

Frieren has been still beside her cup since he began describing the recovery.

“When you came back,” she asks, “did you think you had finished it by getting out?” ᶠ

He looks at her.

The question finds the part he has been struggling to name.

“I wanted to.” ᴹ

She nods, and lets the answer stand.

* * *

He needs water before he can speak about Makima.

Yuka passes the cup. Rimuru waits until he has put it down again.

“I didn't know she would come back,” Mau says. ᴹ

Maki's gaze sharpens.

“When you were inside.” ᴹᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

He can remember the space after it with greater clarity than the movement itself. His hand. The sudden lack of an answer from the person he has been demanding one from.

“I thought I'd killed her. A person.” ᴹ

Rem watches his face change before the next sentence.

“Then she was talking again. Laughing. And I couldn't make the first part stop being what I thought I'd done just because the second part happened.” ᴹ

Yuka looks down at their hands.

Maki's expression changes slowly.

“We remember what you were trying to stop,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“I know.” ᴹ

“That doesn't mean you couldn't be frightened afterward.” ᴹᵏ

He nods without looking up.

* * *

There are questions.

Some help him find words. Others he cannot answer yet, and saying so is harder than agreeing to tell them more tomorrow.

Gojo asks whether Mau knew what the room would permit him to carry from one part to another. Mau explains the things he could test and the ones he had to risk before he had enough information to name them.

Senku starts to ask about the restriction itself, catches sight of Shoko and sets that question aside in favour of whether there is still something happening in Mau's body which they have mistaken for recovery already finished.

Mau answers as honestly as he can.

Some things have settled. Some still need practice. Remembering does not mean his back has just been injured again, even when his body expects the next movement to hurt.

Shoko asks him to tell her when he cannot tell the difference.

He agrees.

Nobody uses the agreement to take away the chair where he is sitting with them.

* * *

By the time he stops, the food left on the table has gone cold.

It seems a ridiculous thing to feel guilty about after everything he has just said, but his eyes keep returning to the serving bowl.

Yuta notices.

“We'll warm it.” ʸᵗ

“I wasn't—” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸᵗ

Yuta picks up the bowl.

Seiko asks who wants tea. Rem gets up to find the cups, and Ram catches her wrist lightly, passes the stack already beside her and goes to help with the pot herself.

The familiar movements do more to unsettle Mau than a speech would have.

He has told them something terrible.

They are making room for a cup.

* * *

Yuka stays beside him while the first conversations begin moving again.

He can tell she is thinking about what to ask. It is there in the pauses between answering someone else, in the way she keeps returning her attention to him after a voice reaches her from across the table.

He waits until Maki has gone to help Mai collect the pages they left out this morning.

“Do you want to talk?” he asks. ᴹ

Yuka looks at him.

“Yes.” ʸ

He nods.

She does not get up immediately.

For a little while they sit with the others, accepting the tea and the food which has been warmed, and letting the house continue around what he has told them.

Then Yuka takes his hand, and they go upstairs together.
