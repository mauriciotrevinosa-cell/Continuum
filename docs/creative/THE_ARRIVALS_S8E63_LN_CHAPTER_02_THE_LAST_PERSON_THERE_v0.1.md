*When: Yuka closes the door, she leaves her hand on it for a moment before turning toward him.*

Mau sits on the edge of the bed.

He has been waiting for the question since before they came upstairs. Knowing that does not help him decide where to put his hands.

“Why didn't you tell me?” she asks. ʸ

He looks at her.

“All of it?” ᴹ

“That it was like that.” ʸ

Her voice is quieter than he expected. It makes it harder to find an answer which can survive being said aloud.

* * *

She comes to sit beside him, leaving enough space to look at his face.

“I knew the time. You told us.” ʸ

He nods.

“I knew you came back hurt. I knew you had to get used to things again.” ʸ

She looks toward the place where she has hung the hoodie.

“I remember you being afraid to hold me.” ʸ

Mau closes his eyes.

“I know.” ᴹ

“I thought I was listening.” ʸ

He opens them again.

Yuka's mouth has tightened, and he finally understands that part of her anger has been going somewhere he cannot bear to leave it.

“You were.” ᴹ

“Then why did I have to find out the rest like that?” ʸ

* * *

There are several answers.

He can feel each trying to become the one which will hurt her least. The habit is old enough that he almost obeys it before noticing what he is doing.

Yuka watches him take a breath and stop.

“I didn't want it here,” he says. ᴹ

She looks at him.

“In the room. At breakfast. Every time you looked at me.” ᴹ

He presses his fingers together, then lets them separate when the two which move less easily begin making the effort uncomfortable.

“I could tell you something had happened. I could put a beginning and an end on it. If I went into the part in the middle, I had to go there too.” ᴹ

Yuka says nothing.

“And I didn't want you to have it.” ᴹ

The words come faster now, with less of the care he has been using to keep them tidy.

“I know how that sounds. I know it wasn't only mine to decide what you could hear. I just—” ᴹ

His breath catches.

“I didn't want anybody else to have that place in their head.” ᴹ

* * *

Yuka looks down.

“You let me sit beside you without knowing how much you were trying to carry.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

He says it before he can turn the answer into an explanation.

She wipes at one eye with the heel of her hand, irritated by the need to do it while she is still trying to speak clearly.

“I can't ask the right question if I don't know there's something to ask.” ʸ

Mau nods.

“I know.” ᴹ

“And if you can't tell me yet, I want you to be able to say that.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“I don't need you to make a whole explanation out of it so I won't worry.” ʸ

Her voice breaks on the last word.

Mau reaches for her hand and waits until she gives it to him.

* * *

“I don't know how much of it I can hold,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka turns toward him fully.

“Some of it I remember as something I know. Some of it comes back and it feels like I'm still doing it. Some of it—” ᴹ

He stops.

The next part has no comfortable edge.

“There are things I think I've been keeping from myself.” ᴹ

Yuka's expression changes.

“From yourself?” ʸ

He nods.

“I know where I stop looking. I don't always know what's after it.” ᴹ

Ciel remains quiet inside him.

Mau tries to find the difference between choosing not to tell a story and discovering that the place where it ought to continue has become somewhere he cannot enter without losing the room around him.

“I thought if I could just keep enough of it in its place—” ᴹ

He looks toward the window.

* * *

There is a quiet he knows.

It has no house in it.

No one has left a cup somewhere he can hear another person moving it. No floorboard answers a footstep farther down a passage. There is no voice which might reach him if he waits long enough to stop hearing his own breath.

He remembers the names people had given him.

Monster.

Demon Lord.

God of Destruction.

He remembers the pressure which had met his, the effort that had gone on without anything yielding. He still cannot find a face to put against it.

Then there is the part after.

The part his memory has been leaving empty.

* * *

Yuka feels his fingers change in hers.

“Mau?” ʸ

He hears her from much too far away.

There had been a world.

People in it. Places. Ordinary lives which had continued while he was learning how to become something no one could make stop.

He had destroyed it.

He has remembered that before.

What returns now is the last person left there afterward.

Himself.

He had ended his own life.

* * *

The recognition does not arrive as a story he can finish telling her.

It is the certainty of remembering a decision from inside the person who made it. The terrible absence after everything he had once been able to imagine wanting.

There is no relief in finding it.

There is no answer to put beside the destruction and make either part bearable.

Mau tries to breathe.

The room will not remain the size it was.

“No,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka moves in front of him.

“Look at me.” ʸ

He cannot find her quickly enough.

* * *

*Mau. I have stopped,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

He hears the words and cannot work out which part she has been able to stop. The memory has already become something he knows again.

His hands are shaking.

He pulls them back from Yuka before he understands why he is doing it, horrified by the possibility that the hands in the memory and the hands she is holding have never been different enough.

She does not chase them.

She puts hers on the blanket where he can see them.

“I'm here,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks at the place between them.

He has been trying so hard to return to her. He has spent days remembering things with people beside him. There was a flower. There was a festival. There was a table he wanted to bring downstairs without falling through a badly drawn staircase.

The ordinary things are all still there, and he cannot make them reach the place which has opened inside him.

* * *

Yuka hears him try to say something.

She leans closer without touching him.

“What is it?” ʸ

“I did—” ᴹ

He cannot finish.

“All right. You don't have to say it yet.” ʸ

He shakes his head so sharply she has to stop herself from reaching toward his face.

He is trying to tell her something. That matters to him enough to keep hurting himself against the words.

“Rimuru,” he manages. ᴹ

*I'm asking him to come,* Ciel tells him. ᶜⁱ

Yuka stays on the bed.

She says his name again, waits until his eyes move toward her, then tells him where they are in the plainest words she can find.

Their room.

Their bed.

He is sitting with her.

There is a knock at the door.

Mau flinches.

Yuka looks toward it and answers without leaving him.

“Come in.” ʸ
