*When: Hollis asks whether Qifrey can read the name on a folded letter, Qifrey stops reaching for the bundle of cloth.*

“Where did you get that?” ᵠ

The merchant looks at the letter again.

“From a man who hoped I would know where to send it.” ᴴᵒ

Mau, holding the other end of the cloth, watches Qifrey put it down carefully.

Hollis has arrived with enough notice that somebody has cleared the usual place for his goods. Nobody has had to move a guest out of a bedroom to receive him. There is flour to discuss, something Momo wants opened before buying and a length of material Marin has been examining from several angles while Wakana pretends not to have begun the same calculation.

The letter sits apart from all of it.

“He gave his name?” Qifrey asks. ᵠ

Hollis says it.

Mau knows the name before the merchant has finished.

Olruggio.

* * *

Qifrey does not open the paper immediately.

He looks at the outside, then asks Hollis to describe the person who handed it to him. The answer is practical, assembled from the things a merchant notices when deciding whether someone is likely to pay, steal or leave him responsible for a problem he cannot afford.

Qifrey listens through it without helping the description become more recognisable.

Mau puts the cloth where Marin can reach it and remains beside him.

“Do you think it's his?” he asks. ᴹ

“It looks like his writing.” ᵠ

“That wasn't quite the same question.” ᴹ

Qifrey glances toward him.

Then nods.

“No.” ᵠ

Hollis waits while he examines the fold.

There is no elaborate device worked into it. No demand that the person opening it complete a shape. Qifrey checks it anyway before unfolding the plain letter.

* * *

The first lines make his face change.

Mau sees it happen before he sees the reason: something familiar enough to interrupt the caution for a moment, then the return of a question he has been holding behind it.

Qifrey reads the page twice.

Hollis looks toward the goods, judges that they will survive a few minutes without his attention and draws a chair nearer.

“There have been people asking after a teacher,” he says. “And some girls.” ᴴᵒ

Mau's eyes return to him.

“Here?” ᴹ

“On the road. Farther along than my usual turn. I didn't tell them which door the girls sleep behind.” ᴴᵒ

“Thank you.” ᵠ

Qifrey folds the paper once, then opens it again because his thumb has covered a line he wants to look at.

* * *

They move a little farther from the table where people are choosing cloth.

Qifrey asks for the places Hollis can name. The merchant gives them in the order he travelled, adding where somebody else told him the thing rather than letting a report become something he has seen with his own eyes.

There is talk of unfamiliar buildings near the coast. People arriving with their own ways of doing things and the confidence of having come from somewhere those ways were understood.

Some have been trying to organise what they found.

Some have been looking for people who did not arrive with them.

“How much of it did you see?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“The man. The letter. The people who sent him to ask me.” ᴴᵒ

“Not the buildings.” ᴹ

“Not yet.” ᴴᵒ

Qifrey nods.

He writes the places down separately.

* * *

The girls notice that he is still holding the page when they come to see what Hollis has brought.

Coco reaches the edge of the table first. Tetia is beside her, looking at a small wrapped thing which may or may not belong to the goods for sale. Agott glances at Qifrey's face before looking at the letter.

Richeh stops on his other side.

“Someone you know?” Coco asks. ᶜᵒ

Qifrey lowers the page enough for them to see the name.

The change in their attention is immediate.

Questions begin before he has answered the first.

He listens, tells them what he can honestly tell them and asks them to let him find out the rest before promising an arrival the paper has not given him.

Richeh is quiet.

Mau sees Qifrey notice.

“We will ask about the others too,” he tells her. ᵠ

She looks up.

He does not put a name on the page as though that has already brought her brother home.

She stays beside him while he reads the last lines aloud to the girls.

* * *

Olruggio wants to speak to him.

That much is plain.

There is also enough caution in the wording to tell Qifrey that the conversation should happen before somebody else decides how it ought to happen for them.

He explains that part when the girls have gone to tell Coco's mother what has arrived.

Mau remains beside the chair.

“What do you think he means?” ᴹ

Qifrey looks toward the house.

He has watched people use things here which would require a very different explanation in the places he remembers. He has taught. Answered questions. Chosen what to share and what to protect, with children he cares about living among people who do not fit any arrangement his old world prepared him for.

“That people are going to have questions,” he says. ᵠ

“About what you taught us.” ᴹ

“Among other things.” ᵠ

Mau looks toward the ordinary front door.

The space beyond it has been becoming less empty for a long time.

* * *

Hollis cannot take them to a person he has already left behind simply by telling them where they met.

They discuss what he can carry back. Where an answer could be left. How to ask for another meeting without handing the exact route through the family's private space to anyone who likes the sound of an invitation.

Mau offers help observing a route if they choose to use one.

Qifrey asks to think before deciding who should go.

Hollis accepts the delay, then reminds them that the flour is still waiting for someone who can judge it.

Momo calls from the table that she has been judging it while the men were becoming mysterious.

Mau turns.

“Badly?” ᴹ

“The flour or the men?” ᴹᵒ

Hollis goes to defend his goods.

* * *

Qifrey folds the letter when the space around them is quiet again.

“I'd like your help,” he says. ᵠ

Mau looks at him.

“With finding out what's actually there?” ᴹ

“Yes. And with going, if that's what we need to do.” ᵠ

Mau considers the request.

The instinct to answer before the next breath is still easy to find. He has spent enough of the last few days discovering what happens when he mistakes an instinct for the whole of a decision.

“Let's talk to the others,” he says. ᴹ

Qifrey nods.

“I was hoping you would say that.” ᵠ

Mau smiles a little.

“I still want to help.” ᴹ

“I heard you.” ᵠ

They go inside with the letter between them.
