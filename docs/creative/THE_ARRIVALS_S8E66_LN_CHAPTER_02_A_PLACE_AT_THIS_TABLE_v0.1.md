*When: Yuka makes room for Qifrey's letter, she moves the wedding page rather than putting one over the other.*

“Tell us.” ʸ

Mau sits beside her.

Rimuru brings his cup. Maki waits until Yuta has found the spare chair before looking at the name on the paper. Anko asks Hollis's version first, and Qifrey tells her exactly which part the merchant saw.

There is enough uncertainty to prevent anyone from enjoying the easy answer.

“You recognise it,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

“His writing. How he says certain things. Yes.” ᵠ

“And you want to speak to him before anyone comes here looking for the girls.” ᴹᵏ

Qifrey nods.

Yuka turns toward Mau.

“You want to go with him.” ʸ

“If we need to go. I want to find out what we're agreeing to first.” ᴹ

* * *

They leave the letter where everyone can read the words which are actually on it.

The gaps remain gaps.

Olruggio may be somewhere they can reach. The people near him may be willing to speak. The unfamiliar buildings may belong to the same arrival, or to another report somebody has already joined to it because the road has become easier to explain that way.

Anko takes another page.

“Separate those.” ᴬⁿ

She puts the places beside the things Hollis knows directly, then leaves room beneath the rest.

Rimuru watches the list grow shorter and more useful.

“We can answer a request to meet without offering the entire house,” he says. ᴿ

“I would prefer that,” Qifrey answers. ᵠ

Maki looks toward him.

“And the girls?” ᴹᵏ

“We tell them what we're considering. We listen to what they want. We don't make them responsible for satisfying anybody's questions.” ᵠ

Yuta nods.

* * *

Mau keeps his hands beside the paper while they discuss the journey.

There are ways he could make part of it faster. That does not make a route known, or a person trustworthy, or the house's boundaries something he can move to a meeting by arriving there first.

He says what he can offer.

Then what he would need to know before offering it.

Qifrey asks a question about the difference. Mau answers without becoming defensive when the answer is less impressive than the first possibility sounded.

Rimuru looks at him once across the table.

Mau catches it.

The smile is very small.

He keeps talking.

* * *

Yuka waits until the others have begun working out how to carry an answer before asking the question she wants him to hear without a roomful of faces around it.

“Are you going because you want to help him?” ʸ

Mau turns toward her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She waits.

“I'm not trying to get away from here.” ᴹ

He looks down at the papers.

“I think that's what you're asking.” ᴹ

Yuka takes time before answering.

“Part of it.” ʸ

He nods.

“I don't know how every day is going to feel yet. I can tell you if it's a bad one before I make the whole day somebody else's problem.” ᴹ

“You can tell me if it's a bad one,” she says. “That can be the whole sentence.” ʸ

He looks at her.

Then smiles, tired and genuine.

“I'm learning.” ᴹ

* * *

They agree to prepare a response rather than leave tonight.

Qifrey will speak to the girls and Coco's mother. They will establish where a meeting could actually happen. Hollis can carry a reply through a route he knows; nobody will turn him into a courier for a promise he has not agreed to make.

The house continues its own work in the meantime.

There are people who have been waiting to be asked about rooms. A builder who still has not seen the ground. Ordinary meals and more questions about where the washing should go than any drawing seems capable of surviving.

Rimuru collects the copy of his earlier letter so it will be ready when a reply comes.

Mau puts the map beside it.

For once, deciding to do something has included leaving time for everyone else to decide what happens around it.

* * *

Yuka gets the wedding page back after Qifrey has gone to find the girls.

Mau watches her smooth its corner.

“How much have you decided?” ᴹ

“Enough to know Umaru needs smaller writing.” ʸ

“A difficult negotiation.” ᴹ

She laughs and shifts her chair toward him.

“I want it to feel like here,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks toward the common room, the people beginning to move between the chairs, the open Fort door and the tree beyond the window.

“I like here.” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

She is quiet for a moment, turning something over which she has not yet found a way to draw.

“But I want to look at it that day and remember how we made it. Everyone's little part. Something we couldn't have had anywhere else.” ʸ

Mau studies her face.

“That sounds like a lot of opinions.” ᴹ

“There will be complaints.” ʸ

“I might survive.” ᴹ

She reaches over and closes her hand around his.

“You have to be here for them.” ʸ

The words settle between them, ordinary and more important than the joke she has placed them inside.

Mau lifts her hand and kisses the fingers beside the ring.

“That's what I want.” ᴹ

* * *

Mai comes back for the page.

She has already heard enough of Yuka's explanation to begin asking what they could make themselves, and who else would have ideas worth stealing politely.

“Borrowing,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Depending on the idea.” ᴹᵃ

Mau offers the pencil he has been holding.

Mai takes it, looks at him and waits.

“What?” ᴹ

“You can stay.” ᴹᵃ

He looks toward Yuka.

She moves the next chair nearer.

For a little while, the letter and the road remain on the other page while he helps them think about something they want to make.
