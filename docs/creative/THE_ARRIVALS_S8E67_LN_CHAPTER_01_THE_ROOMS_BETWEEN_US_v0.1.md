*When: the reply arrives, Rimuru reads the part about the children before the part addressed to him.*

All five have reached Tempest with Hinata.

There is a short account of the road, a question someone has asked her to pass back and enough of the familiar irritation in the wording to make Anko smile when Rimuru hands her the smaller folded page which came with it.

She takes that one elsewhere to read.

Shizue sits down while he tells her the rest.

The courier has been on the road long enough to be interested in breakfast. Rem points him toward the place cleared for visitors, and Jinshi goes to ask what he needs before anybody begins treating him like a moving shelf for their next letters.

Rimuru reads the other pages more slowly.

* * *

Geld would like to see the ground.

Kurobe would like to see the measurements after Geld has stopped being polite about which parts are missing. There are people willing to help, provided they know what they are coming to do and where they will sleep while doing it.

Shuna has added questions which make several of the house's beautiful proposals look unfinished in ways nobody can deny.

Shion has asked whether coming to see them needs to be part of the work.

Rimuru rests his hand on that line.

Mau looks toward him.

“What?” ᴹ

“She's asking again.” ᴿ

“Then answer again.” ᴹ

The slime looks up.

Mau smiles.

“You said she could.” ᴹ

* * *

They prepare a sensible answer before preparing a spectacular welcome.

How many people might come first. What can be borrowed with consent. What meals and supplies the house can actually offer. Where tools can be kept without making everyone learn to step over them on the way to breakfast.

The visitors need a place to stop and look. They are not arriving to discover a town someone has already promised their labour will produce.

There is a journey between wanting to visit and reaching the house.

The courier tells them when an answer could reasonably be carried back. Rimuru listens, writes the dates where Fern can see them and asks that nobody leave before the house has confirmed the sleeping arrangements.

Shuna's questions stay beside the plan.

Mau has begun enjoying how many people are willing to make the drawing worse before making the building possible.

* * *

Qifrey has spoken with the girls.

They have asked questions he cannot answer yet. Coco's mother has asked some more, with less patience for the parts adults sometimes hope will remain understood without being said.

He has told them what he is considering.

When he sits beside Mau with the reply to Olruggio, it is still only a reply. A request to establish where they can meet, carried by a route Hollis has agreed to use. Nothing in it invites unknown people through the children's bedroom doors.

“I haven't promised we are coming immediately,” Qifrey says. ᵠ

“Good.” ᴹ

“You sound pleased.” ᵠ

Mau looks toward the table where Yuka has been helping Mai hold two edges of paper together.

“I'm getting used to having something between the question and the running.” ᴹ

Qifrey follows his glance.

“We can keep that.” ᵠ

* * *

The day contains a little work which can be done without waiting for a visitor.

Lists of what already belongs in the house. Which shelves can be rearranged. What would help someone find a quiet place now, while the future quiet places are still marks on paper.

Wakana takes a measurement and writes down where he took it. Byakuya asks who would need to be able to get through a proposed doorway with someone helping them. Coco's mother adds somewhere to sit beside the place where the cleaning things might go, because her daughter has seen the first version of that part and reminded her.

Umaru tests whether Ori can hear her from the Fort.

Everybody can hear Umaru.

Maki asks her to try at the volume she uses when declaring something other than a war.

They try again.

This time the result becomes useful enough for Fern to write it down.

* * *

Mau remembers his appointment with Yuka before Ciel has to ask what he said he would do.

He is almost absurdly pleased by this.

*I noticed,* she says. ᶜⁱ

*You sound surprised.* ᴹ

*Pleased,* she corrects. ᶜⁱ

He goes to find Yuka before the argument can require a witness.

She has finished helping Mai and is trying to rescue the pencil from Milim, who believes the next idea is easier to explain by drawing the entire thing herself.

“I can wait,” Mau says. ᴹ

Yuka looks toward him.

“I don't want you to.” ʸ

Mai takes the page.

Milim begins explaining the idea to her instead.

* * *

They go out beneath the tree with something to drink and no drawing to hold flat between them.

Yuka chooses the place where they can hear the house without being in the path everyone uses to reach it. Mau sits beside her, then shifts when she decides his shoulder would be a more comfortable place for her head.

She closes her eyes.

For a while he watches the small movements of her hand against the cup.

“How was your day?” he asks. ᴹ

She opens one eye.

“Do you want the version with all the arguments?” ʸ

“Particularly those.” ᴹ

She tells him about them.

* * *

There is no finished wedding hidden inside the account.

There are things she likes. Things she would like to try. Something Mai believes could work if two other people stop insisting it needs to be larger. An idea the Fort has claimed ownership of before deciding whether it can be described without shouting.

Mau listens until he has enough of the shape to know where his opinion would be useful.

Then offers it.

Yuka disagrees with part of it and keeps another part, and the pleasure of being allowed to want something imperfectly is still new enough that he sits quietly afterward to enjoy it.

“What?” she asks. ʸ

“Nothing.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

He smiles.

“I like doing this.” ᴹ

“Disagreeing with me?” ʸ

“Surviving it.” ᴹ

She laughs against his shoulder.

* * *

Ciel speaks while Yuka is looking toward the house.

*How are you?* ᶜⁱ

He thinks before answering.

There are places he still does not want to go in the things he remembers. Last night did not remove them. This afternoon has not made him forget how frightening it felt to need someone and have to ask before he knew whether the words would work.

He can also feel Yuka against him.

Rimuru is inside arguing about a question Shuna has put in a letter. Rem has chosen a chair beside Umaru. Bocchi has left the music-room door open enough for a phrase to reach the passage.

*Here,* he answers. ᴹ

*Yes,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

He hears the smile in her voice.

*I'd like to stay here for a while,* she adds. ᶜⁱ

*Me too.* ᴹ

* * *

Yuka notices the change in his face.

“Ciel?” ʸ

He nods.

“Is she all right?” ʸ

He asks rather than deciding which answer she would prefer to have repeated.

Ciel takes a moment.

*I'm glad we're here. I will need more time with the rest.* ᶜⁱ

Mau tells Yuka exactly that.

She puts her cup down and takes his hand.

“We have this evening,” she says. ʸ

He turns toward her.

She kisses him before the doorway acquires someone with another page they need to look at. His free hand settles against her back, and she comes closer, unhurried.

Behind them, the familiar house is making room for things it has not learned how to contain yet.

Yuka rests against him again.

When someone calls that the food is ready, they stay long enough to finish the quiet first.

Then they go in together.
