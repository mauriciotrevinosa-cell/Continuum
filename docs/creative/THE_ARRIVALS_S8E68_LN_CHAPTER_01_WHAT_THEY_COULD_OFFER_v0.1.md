“I thought we'd start with the beds.” ᴿ

“You can eat while disagreeing about beds.” ᴹᵒ

Ram nods from the other side of the table.

Mau brings the cups before the conversation can become an experiment in whether either of them is wrong.

The reply from Tempest is open between them. Geld is willing to look at the ground. Kurobe has questions about what they intend to make and what they already have to make it with. Other people have said they would like to come without agreeing that wanting to visit requires them to become part of the work.

Rimuru has a great deal he would like to answer yes to.

The house has a sensible number of places to put an actual person tonight.

* * *

They decide which question the visit needs to answer.

It is easier after that.

Geld does not need the entire town to exist before he can say where they ought to begin. A small group can come, look at the ground, help establish what needs measuring and go home with a better idea of the work.

If people want a longer stay, they will discuss it after there is something real to agree to.

“They can bring their own camp,” Mau says. ᴹ

He looks toward the ordinary patch of ground where the house has kept supplies clear of the path.

“We can ask if that's comfortable for them. We know where it's safe to put a few tents. We don't have to claim it proves the whole new plan fits.” ᴹ

Byakuya comes to the window beside him.

He looks at the ground, then at the route people use to carry water.

“We should walk it before putting the offer in the letter.” ᴮʸ

Mau picks up his cup.

“All right.” ᴹ

* * *

The walk changes the offer.

Somewhere dry enough to put a tent is not necessarily somewhere a person can reach after dark without discovering the washing line with their face. A path which looks clear from the window has a low place that will remember the next rain.

Mau moves a basket. Stark helps shift the stack beside it. Rem asks which door the visitors would use if somebody needed to find them at night.

Jinshi writes down the answer because he would like to tell a visitor where to go without using the phrase past the thing which may have been moved.

They return with a smaller offer and fewer lies in it.

Rimuru reads the page.

“Tents if they want them. Their own private sleeping spaces. Meals with us. A place for tools which doesn't become the route to the Fort.” ᴿ

“And ask what they need us to change,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

He adds it.

* * *

Sending the answer becomes a different problem from choosing it.

The road will carry another letter eventually. Rimuru would like to answer the practical questions while he can still ask the next one without waiting for two journeys between every sentence.

Mau finds him looking at the route on the page.

“You could go.” ᴹ

Rimuru looks up.

“I'm aware.” ᴿ

“I meant you can borrow a ride.” ᴹ

He glances toward the doorway.

Rimuru follows the glance, then looks back at him.

“And you?” ᴿ

“I'll be here.” ᴹ

* * *

Kaisel emerges where there is room for him.

The first beat of his wings moves the loose edge of the paper before Rimuru has tucked it securely away. Mau waits until the path is clear, then checks that the simple travel arrangement is actually comfortable for the person using it.

“You remember the route?” Rimuru asks him. ᴿ

“Enough to point you along the one we've travelled. You know the city better than he does.” ᴹ

“If something has changed between us?” ᴿ

The journey is through the same world, along a route they know, with time allowed to change their minds if the land has become less familiar.

No new door opens beneath anybody's feet.
