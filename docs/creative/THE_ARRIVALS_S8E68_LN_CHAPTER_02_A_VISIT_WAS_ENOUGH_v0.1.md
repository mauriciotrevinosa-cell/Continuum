*When: Rimuru arrives carrying questions instead of instructions, Geld asks to see all of them.*

“Even the difficult ones?” ᴿ

“Especially those.” ᴳᵉ

Rimuru hands over Yuka's folded page.

Geld reads the first part, looks toward the shape of the tents on the next sheet and asks whether the person who drew the water route has walked it.

“Yes. This morning.” ᴿ

He nods, visibly pleased.

Rimuru begins to understand how little of a builder's gratitude requires an impressive building.

* * *

Kurobe is less gentle with the missing measurements.

He does not seem offended by them. He simply asks how anyone expects to know what tools to bring without knowing which problem has been mistaken for a straight line.

Rimuru admits that several people have begun asking the same question.

“Good,” Kurobe says. ᴷᵘ

Geld puts the page down beside him.

“A first look. Ground, materials, routes. We can bring what we need to sleep. We should not bring a promise to finish something we haven't seen.” ᴳᵉ

“That's what we wanted to ask for.” ᴿ

They discuss the size of the first group. The work which can wait until they have spoken to the people who live there. What the house can supply and what is easier to take from here.

The answers become a visit with a beginning and an end, after which everyone can decide what the next part ought to be.

* * *

Shuna finds them while the paper is still spread out.

She looks at the marked sleeping area, asks two questions neither man has yet thought to answer and then asks Rimuru whether she may see the parts people have drawn for themselves.

He shows her the copies they have allowed him to bring.

She studies the little gathering of doors with unexpected care.

“They want to remain close.” ˢʰᵘ

“Very much.” ᴿ

Her finger stops beside one of the passages.

“Then this space matters as much as those rooms.” ˢʰᵘ

“They've been discovering that.” ᴿ

Shuna smiles.

“I'd like to hear what Yuka and Mai want to do. And see how the others are settling after the journey.” ˢʰᵘ

Rimuru puts down his cup.

“You can come to see them.” ᴿ

“I know.” ˢʰᵘ

The ease of the answer makes him happy.

* * *

Shion comes later.

She has heard he is here. She has also heard enough about the proposed visit to arrive trying to work out where she could be needed without asking the question in quite those words.

Rimuru knows the effort when he sees it.

He has asked her to find something different from a place beside his work. He cannot expect that wanting to give her an answer has made it simple for her to ask the question.

“I'd be glad to see you there,” he says before she has finished considering the papers. ᴿ

Shion looks toward him.

“If I came with them?” ˢʰⁱ

“If you want to.” ᴿ

She nods, then seems to find that the answer has left her without the work she prepared in order to remain in the conversation.

Shuna shifts the chair beside her.

“We could talk on the way.” ˢʰᵘ

Shion looks at the chair.

Then sits down.

* * *

There are things she has thought about since the festival.

Some are uncomfortable enough that she does not put all of them into words. Rimuru listens to the ones she can say without turning each into proof she has understood his whole new life correctly.

“I don't know what I'd do there,” she admits. ˢʰⁱ

“On a visit?” ᴿ

She nods.

He thinks about the afternoon in which he has already asked a dozen people how a house can make room for someone before deciding what they must contribute.

“You could begin with who you'd like to see.” ᴿ

Shion looks down at her hands.

“You.” ˢʰⁱ

“I'd like that.” ᴿ

He waits.

After a while, she asks whether Diablo will be there.

Rimuru smiles.

“He has become very particular about when the music room opens.” ᴿ

The explanation requires enough ordinary detail that Shion begins asking questions before remembering to be uncertain about wanting the answers.

* * *

The children have questions of their own.

Rimuru gives Shizue's reply to them where Hinata can sit long enough to hear what has come back. They read it together, interrupting at points which seem much less important to the adults than to the people explaining why they matter.

He is asked whether Ori has tried the boat again.

Whether the Fort has finished the game they were making.

Whether Shizue really found somewhere she wanted to put her own things.

“She has,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Chloe smiles at the page.

He does not use the moment to decide where the rest of their childhood ought to happen. There will be another conversation if they want one, with the people who need to be part of it.

For now, a letter is enough to make the distance feel less empty.

* * *

The answer to the house is ready before he leaves.

Geld will confirm the small group and departure once the equipment is packed and the house has the notice it needs. The journey will take the ordinary road time. Shuna and Shion have chosen to ask for the same visit, not to arrive with permanent jobs someone assigned them from a chair in Tempest.

Rimuru carries the decisions home on Kaisel.

By the time he reaches the familiar roofs, the evening meal has mostly been cleared.

Mau is outside with Yuka when they see him descending.

“You missed dinner,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I was fed.” ᴿ

“We thought you might be.” ʸ

Rimuru gets down carefully and hands over the papers before anyone can ask whether he has brought every person he has spoken to hidden inside his bag.

Mau thanks Kaisel and lets him return to the place he knows, the journey finished without becoming a new name on the roll.

* * *

Shizue is still awake.

Rimuru finds her with a cup and more questions than the children managed to put inside the answer he has brought.

He sits down.

“They read it together,” he says. ᴿ

She smiles before he has told her the rest.

Across the room, Yuka begins reading Shuna's comments aloud. Mau listens to the first, looks toward Rimuru and raises his eyebrows.

The slime smiles over his cup.

There are people who want to come to his home.

For once, he has been able to tell them what they are being invited to before asking what they might do when they arrive.
