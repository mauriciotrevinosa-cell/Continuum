“Tomorrow. If everything is packed.” ᴿ

Armin turns the page so Fern can see.

“Six?” ᴬʳ

“Geld. Kurobe. Two people Geld asked who want to help with the first survey. Shuna and Shion.” ᴿ

“Then we prepare for six and leave room to change it if the message changes,” Fern says. ᶠᵉ

She puts the arrival estimate after it. The following evening, if the road remains the one they know. There is enough time for the house to arrange what it has actually offered, and a way for the visitors to stop rather than discover the offer has become a command to keep coming.

Mau watches Armin move the paper away from a cup.

Across the room, Jiji is helping Umaru with a game which appears to have become more difficult because he keeps suggesting rules Okarun remembers hearing somewhere else.

Rimuru asks what the house would like him to carry if anything needs answering again.

Fern gives him the folded list.

Then goes to find lunch before it becomes another appointment.

* * *

Qifrey has chosen a table where all four girls can see the page.

Coco's mother sits beside her daughter. Mau arrives with another chair, looks at the number already there and puts it where nobody will have to ask a person to move in order to leave.

“Is that where you're going?” Tetia asks, pointing at the route. ᵀᵉ

“It's where we can ask to meet,” Qifrey answers. ᵠ

“Olruggio.” ᴬᵍ

He nods.

Agott looks at the letter again, then at the places Hollis has named.

“And if someone else comes?” ᴬᵍ

Mau rests his arms on the table.

“We find out who they are before inviting them back.” ᴹ

She looks at him long enough to decide whether he is only saying the pleasant part of the answer.

“We can leave,” he adds. ᴹ

Qifrey nods beside him.

* * *

Coco has been quiet since the beginning.

Her mother notices the way her hands have stopped moving before she asks the question.

“Would they want us to go back?” ᶜᵒ

Qifrey takes a moment.

“Some may want to ask where we've been. What has happened here.” ᵠ

“What you taught.” ᴬᵍ

“Yes.” ᵠ

Coco's mother looks toward him.

“And if they dislike the answers?” ᶜᵐ

He does not tell her there will be no difficulty. The girls have heard enough of the rules of his world to recognise the shape of one.

“Then we will have to speak about what authority they believe they have here,” he says. ᵠ

Mau looks at him, then toward Coco.

“Nobody is taking you away because somebody else has arrived with a familiar rule.” ᴹ

Her mother puts a hand over Coco's.

“They can speak to me too,” she says. ᶜᵐ

* * *

The memory question changes the silence at the table.

Nobody has asked it yet. It is in the way Agott watches the letter, and the way Qifrey has stopped trying to make the unknown parts sound smaller.

Mau knows enough of what they have explained before to recognise why the question matters.

“No one gets to decide that the way to make us easier to deal with is to remove what we know,” he says. ᴹ

Qifrey meets his eyes.

“I agree.” ᵠ

Coco's mother looks between them.

“I would like that said before anyone begins deciding whose memories belong to a rule.” ᶜᵐ

Mau nods.

He is very careful to keep the next breath inside the room where she has asked him to help, rather than the place her words have briefly touched.

* * *

They discuss what the house knows.

Who has been taught. Who understands enough to use an object carefully. Who has watched a demonstration without asking to become an apprentice. The difference between giving a friend something useful and telling a passing merchant how to make the material which lets it work.

Qifrey has drawn those distinctions before.

Now he has to consider what they look like to people who have not lived here long enough to know why he made them.

Tetia asks whether learning another kind of magic makes someone part of the same argument.

Mau looks down at his hands.

“They're going to have to learn that there are other kinds,” he says. ᴹ

“And people behind them,” Coco's mother adds. ᶜᵐ

He looks up.

She is watching him with a steadiness he has needed more than once in the last few days.

“Yes.” ᴹ

* * *

Richeh puts her page on the table near the end.

She has kept the message she wanted to send. There are places where she has changed a word and left the old one visible, as though erasing it completely would make the amount of thought she gave it harder to remember.

“Can you ask about Rili?” she says. ᴿⁱ

Qifrey looks at the name.

Then at her.

“Yes.” ᵠ

“Even if he isn't with them.” ᴿⁱ

“We can ask what they know. Where a message could go next.” ᵠ

She nods, pressing one edge of the paper flat.

Mau waits while she chooses how much of it she wants to give Qifrey to carry.

The message remains hers.

* * *

When the girls have gone to think about their own questions, Coco's mother remains a little longer.

“Thank you for asking them,” she tells Qifrey. ᶜᵐ

He folds the reply beside the page Richeh chose to send.

“They're the people most likely to notice when we've mistaken speaking for them for keeping them safe.” ᵠ

Mau smiles faintly.

“I'm beginning to receive quite a lot of advice on that subject.” ᴹ

“Good,” she says. ᶜᵐ

He laughs.

She gets up to join Coco, leaving both men with the papers and considerably fewer excuses for pretending the next part belongs only to them.

* * *

Mau finds Yuka where she said she would be.

There is fabric across the table and a question about light which he can answer only by explaining which parts of it he cannot see the way they do.

She listens, holds two pieces nearer one another and asks what difference he can make out.

“That one's softer,” he says. ᴹ

“You haven't touched it.” ʸ

“The way it falls.” ᴹ

Wakana looks up, interested.

Yuka smiles and gives Mau a place beside her.
