Qifrey stops halfway across the last stretch of grass.

For a moment Mau thinks the voice has hurt him.

Then Qifrey laughs.

“He was very quiet.” ᵠ

“He's blotting out half the road.” ᴼˡ

Olruggio has folded his arms. There is dust on the lower edge of his cloak and a damp patch where one boot has gone farther into the ford than he intended. He gives Kaisel another long look before returning his attention to Qifrey.

“You look tired.” ᴼˡ

“So do you.” ᵠ

“I've been looking for someone.” ᴼˡ

The answer takes the humour out of Qifrey's face.

He crosses the remaining distance.

Olruggio starts to say something, stops, and catches him by the upper arm instead. The grip lasts longer than a greeting requires. Qifrey puts his hand over it.

Mau stays where he is.

There are questions in his bag and a person in front of him who has spent a long time being the answer to one of somebody else's.

He lets them have it.

* * *

“The girls?” Olruggio asks. ᴼˡ

“Safe. All four.” ᵠ

“All four.” ᴼˡ

Qifrey nods.

Olruggio looks down, rubbing his thumb once over the seam at Qifrey's sleeve. When he looks up again, his gaze moves to Mau.

“And you are?” ᴼˡ

“Mau.” ᴹ

“The one from the stories.” ᴼˡ

“That depends very heavily on which stories.” ᴹ

Qifrey's mouth moves.

Olruggio notices.

“You find that funny?” ᴼˡ

“Sometimes.” ᵠ

“Then tell me the true parts before I have to choose them myself.” ᴼˡ

Mau looks toward the shelter.

“Can we sit down first? Someone will ask whether I ate.” ᴹ

Olruggio glances at Qifrey, who is already reaching for the food.

“Good,” he says. “At least someone has started.” ᴼˡ

* * *

They share lunch on the low stone beside the shelter.

Mau gives Qifrey the first wrapped portion, then offers Olruggio another. Olruggio accepts it after looking at the amount left in the bag.

He begins with the coast.

There are roads which end where they did not end before. People who know the name of one town and have never heard of the next. A fisherman who has been using the same stretch of shore for years came home to find buildings beyond it that his father had never seen.

“The Hall is there,” Olruggio says. ᴼˡ

Qifrey looks up sharply.

“You've been?” ᵠ

“Yes.” ᴼˡ

“And the people?” ᵠ

“Enough of them to have opinions about everyone missing.” ᴼˡ

The bread in Qifrey's hand remains untouched.

Olruggio notices that too.

“Eat. I'll still be talking when you're finished.” ᴼˡ

Mau quietly takes another bite of his own lunch.

“They know we're here?” Qifrey asks. ᵠ

“They've heard that children are living with someone who teaches magic to people who shouldn't know it exists.” ᴼˡ

The words arrive without accusation. Olruggio is watching his friend's face rather than enjoying the effect of them.

“That isn't the whole of it,” Qifrey says. ᵠ

“I assumed.” ᴼˡ

Mau brushes a crumb off his hand.

“A lot of people in our house brought magic with them. It existed before anyone showed them a pen.” ᴹ

Olruggio's eyes move toward him.

“And now they know what the pen does.” ᴼˡ

“Some do,” Qifrey answers. “I can tell you who I've taught and why.” ᵠ

“Tell me.” ᴼˡ

So he does.

Mau listens to his friend explain decisions which have become ordinary enough at home that he has almost forgotten they were decisions. Lamps. Lessons. Children asking what a line means. Adults choosing to learn how to use an object safely and others happy to leave the drawing to someone who enjoys it.

Olruggio interrupts twice. Both times with a question Qifrey needs a moment to answer.

Neither man looks to Mau to settle it.

* * *

The change comes when Qifrey mentions Coco's mother.

Olruggio stops turning the wrapping in his hand.

“Her mother?” ᴼˡ

“She's with us.” ᵠ

“With you.” ᴼˡ

“She helped Coco decide what she wanted us to ask today.” ᵠ

Olruggio looks at Mau.

There is something careful about the look now.

Mau feels it land and waits for a question about what he can do.

“Is she well?” Olruggio asks instead. ᴼˡ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And Coco?” ᴼˡ

Mau remembers the girl leaning against her mother's shoulder at their table.

“She's got quite a lot to tell you.” ᴹ

Olruggio lowers his eyes to the food again.

This time he is the one who needs reminding to finish it.

* * *

Richeh's page comes out last.

Qifrey unfolds only the part she asked him to share. Olruggio reads it without reaching for the rest.

“I haven't found him,” he says. ᴼˡ

Qifrey nods slowly.

“Can a message go farther?” ᵠ

“I can ask. I won't tell her I've already put it in his hand.” ᴼˡ

“She'd rather have that answer.” ᵠ

“I remember.” ᴼˡ

For the first time since he arrived, Olruggio smiles without seeming to catch himself doing it.

Mau wonders how many small things about the girls he has had nowhere to put while looking for them.

There is room on the stone beside Qifrey. Olruggio has sat down close enough that their cloaks overlap.

Neither has moved them apart.

* * *

“I should speak to them,” Qifrey says eventually. ᵠ

Mau looks up.

“At the Hall?” ᴹ

“Before the next report becomes a reason for someone to decide how this ends.” ᵠ

Olruggio folds Richeh's message along its existing crease.

“It won't be a pleasant conversation.” ᴼˡ

“I know.” ᵠ

“I want to see the girls first.” ᴼˡ

Qifrey turns toward him.

Olruggio looks irritated by the surprise.

“Did you think I came all this way for your report?” ᴼˡ

Qifrey laughs under his breath, then looks at Mau.

Mau thinks of four girls trying not to look as though they are waiting near the door.

“We can ask the house,” he says. ᴹ

Olruggio's eyes narrow slightly.

“Ask?” ᴼˡ

“They'll want to put a kettle on.” ᴹ

“Then ask before they put it on too early.” ᴼˡ

*Rimuru? We found him.* ᴹ
