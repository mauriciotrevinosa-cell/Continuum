* * *

The tree is still in the centre of the page.

Wakana has drawn it lightly enough that the branches do not look like walls. The space around the roots remains clear, with the old house marked beside it and the new paths still in pencil.

Yuka has spent part of the evening looking at the empty space where people are supposed to stand.

“I want to see them,” she says. ʸ

Marin looks up.

“You will.” ᴹᵃ

“I mean when I'm there. Not a big crowd behind him that I stop noticing because I'm trying not to forget what I'm saying.” ʸ

Seiko takes the page and turns it a little.

“Then put them where you'll look.” ˢᵉ

She marks two places facing one another beneath the reach of the branches. Around them she draws a loose curve, close enough to be people rather than rows in a hall.

Yuka leans in.

“And the children?” ʸ

“Where they can see,” Maki says. “Before they decide to climb something.” ᴹᵏ

Ori looks up at once.

“I can see from the ground.” ᴼ

“Excellent,” Mai says. “We've settled another thing.” ᴹᵃⁱ

* * *

Wakana asks about the dress.

Yuka looks down at herself, at the sleeves of Mau's hoodie pulled over her hands. Marin catches the glance and smiles.

“We can make it comfortable.” ᴹᵃ

“I want it to look like something I chose.” ʸ

“Then you have to tell us when it doesn't.” ᴹᵃ

She puts two sketches beside the page. Neither has become a dress yet. Yuka likes the movement of one and the neckline of the other, and spends several minutes explaining why the sleeves bother her.

Wakana changes the line.

Mai watches him work, then looks at her own drawing of the ceremony.

“I'd like to make something for it,” she says. ᴹᵃⁱ

Maki turns toward her.

“Something you want to make.” ᴹᵏ

“Yes, Maki.” ᴹᵃⁱ

“I'm allowed to say it.” ᴹᵏ

Mai rolls her eyes, but there is no anger in it.

“I thought something that can stay. After the day.” ᴹᵃⁱ

Yuka touches the ring on her left hand.

“I'd like that.” ʸ

* * *

Ori brings the conversation back to the tree.

“Can it be different at night?” ᴼ

“Different how?” Wakana asks. ᵂᵃ

“You can see the house through it now. When the lights are on.” ᴼ

Yuka looks toward the window.

The view has become familiar: dark branches, the softer shapes of the garden and the places where a lit window makes a room feel close enough to visit.

“Not just the tree,” she says slowly. ʸ

Marin moves the other sketches aside.

Yuka takes the pencil.

She draws the doors of the places they have been talking about building. The place where the band can open up to the courtyard when they choose. The short route out of the Fort. The gallery people will use when the weather is bad. All the ordinary ways somebody will leave their room and find supper.

“Could we make it feel like everyone is coming home?” ʸ

Maki watches the pencil move.

“For the wedding?” ᴹᵏ

“Not a stage we go away to. The place we get to stay.” ʸ

For once, Umaru does not interrupt.

* * *

The page changes after that.

It acquires a route instead of an entrance. Places for people to join without having to stand in a line. Little pools of light along the way, with room between them for the dark to make the next one worth seeing.

Marin suggests letting the people who want to help choose something recognisably theirs to add. Music. Cloth. A drawing. Something small that belongs to a friendship and will mean more to the two people walking past it than it would to a stranger watching from the road.

“We need to ask before promising any of that,” Wakana says. ᵂᵃ

Yuka nods.

“I want to ask.” ʸ

Seiko looks at the tree again.

“And the two of you end up here.” ˢᵉ

Yuka puts two small marks beneath the branches.

She smiles before realising everyone can see it.

Mai nudges Maki with her elbow.

Maki nudges her back.

* * *

Mau is sitting on the edge of their bed when Yuka comes upstairs.

He has a page in his hand and the expression of someone who has read the same part twice without improving it. She takes it from him and finds a note about a place for wet boots.

“Important?” she asks. ʸ

“Apparently very.” ᴹ

She puts it on the table.

He watches her sit beside him.

“Good evening?” ᴹ

“We removed your exile from the wedding plan.” ʸ

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“You were very close to waiting outside.” ʸ

He smiles, then reaches for her hand.

Yuka turns hers over in his before he can ask.

“I want you there for the parts before it too,” she says. ʸ

“Even the boot conversation?” ᴹ

“Especially that. I'm not marrying someone who leaves them in the doorway.” ʸ

He leans against her shoulder, laughing.

She rests her cheek against his hair and looks at the room they have already learnt how to share.

Tomorrow there will be more questions about the place around it.

For now she has the person she wanted to bring home.
