*When: Qifrey says they are nearly there, Mau is still looking for a roof above the sea.*

Olruggio watches him discover the mistake.

“You were told.” ᴼˡ

“I believed him.” ᴹ

“You keep looking up.” ᴼˡ

Mau turns toward Qifrey, who is smiling with considerably more satisfaction than the situation deserves.

“It's a habit.” ᴹ

“We can give you a different view,” Qifrey says. ᵠ

Kaisel has settled far enough from the water that the spray cannot reach the bag. Mau waits while Qifrey checks the papers one last time, then lets the mount return to his shadow.

The place where the dragon stood becomes ordinary ground again.

Olruggio looks at it.

“Still a longer conversation?” ᴼˡ

“Definitely.” ᴹ

* * *

The entrance requires Qifrey to explain something Mau would otherwise have mistaken for a reason to stop breathing.

He listens.

Olruggio checks the access magic before they follow it below. Qifrey makes Mau wait at the first point of shelter while he explains what is providing air and where it will continue to work. There is a way out. They look at it together before going farther.

“Your protection won't do that part for you,” Qifrey says. ᵠ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I would like you to keep knowing it while looking at everything else.” ᵠ

Mau looks toward the shape beyond them.

For once, the warning is entirely justified.

The sea has turned the distance into something he cannot measure with his eyes alone. Pale moving light crosses the structure below. Things which should have belonged above him are suspended beyond walls with a whole other world moving on the far side.

He cannot see the colours Qifrey begins to name.

He can still see the wonder in the man's face when he stops trying to describe them and points out the passage instead.

“This way.” ᵠ

Mau follows.

* * *

*Anything I need to know before we go in?* he asks Ciel. ᴹ

*The breathing effect is continuing as they described. Keep your position relative to Qifrey if you intend to cover him.* ᶜⁱ

Mau moves close enough for the arrangement they tested at the shore.

He can hold a small shared margin around the two of them. It requires him to pay attention to where Qifrey is. It will stop things which have to cross that margin to reach them; it will not tell him what every mark on every surface means.

Qifrey touches his arm briefly.

“Tell me if I need to move.” ᵠ

“I will.” ᴹ

Olruggio looks between them.

“And tell me before you decide to turn the corridor into an example.” ᴼˡ

Mau glances toward him.

“I'm hoping to avoid examples.” ᴹ

“Good.” ᴼˡ

* * *

People notice Qifrey first.

Mau sees the small recognitions moving ahead of them. A face turning, a step slowing, someone looking over a shoulder toward the person beside him. By the time they reach the place where they have been asked to wait, a guard is already looking at Mau as though the answer to a question has arrived before the question itself.

Qifrey gives his name.

The guard looks at the invitation, then at Olruggio.

They are shown into a room with space between the chairs and the door.

Mau chooses the one beside Qifrey.

Olruggio remains standing until the other door opens.

* * *

The man who enters looks at Qifrey with the expression of someone whose knowledge of him has never made his work easier.

“Easthies,” Qifrey says. ᵠ

Mau watches the greeting land.

Easthies looks toward him next.

“You brought him.” ᴱ

“I asked him to come.” ᵠ

The distinction makes Olruggio shift slightly beside the wall.

Easthies sits, setting the invitation on the table.

There are others with him. Mau lets Qifrey hear the names and roles first, following the shape of a conversation whose history belongs to his companion.

Then Easthies asks where the apprentices are.

“At home,” Qifrey answers. ᵠ

Mau rests his hands on the table.

* * *

The first questions are ordinary enough to make the later ones more difficult to dismiss.

Have the girls continued their studies? Who is responsible for them when Qifrey is away? What does he know about the lands around the place where they live?

Qifrey answers. He names the people who actually care for them and the things he can personally speak about. When the conversation reaches Coco's mother, he repeats the sentence she chose yesterday.

Easthies' eyes move toward Mau.

“And what authority does he claim?” ᴱ

Qifrey does not turn to ask permission.

“He lives there.” ᵠ

Mau looks at his friend.

“And he came because I wanted someone beside me when we discussed what you intend to do about that.” ᵠ

For a moment, the only movement in the room is the light from the water beyond it.

Then Mau leans forward.

“We can start with what you think you need to take away.” ᴹ
