*When: the woman beside Easthies asks how many outsiders Qifrey has taught, he asks her what she means by an outsider.*

She looks at him for a moment.

“You know the answer.” ᴴ

“I knew it where we came from.” ᵠ

Mau watches the woman's hand settle on the paper in front of her. She has introduced herself as one of the people authorised to answer for this Hall's arrangements in the new territory. The man beside her has said considerably less. Easthies is still watching Qifrey.

“The practice has not become safe merely because the land has changed,” the woman says. ᴴ

“No,” Qifrey answers. ᵠ

She waits for the argument which would let her put him on the other side of that sentence.

He leaves it where it is.

“That's why I've been teaching them how to use it.” ᵠ

* * *

They begin with the ink.

Qifrey tells them what he has not put into the hands of merchants. He explains the difference between a finished useful object and the means of making anything a person can imagine if they are willing to pay the consequences. Olruggio speaks when one of the questions turns the apprentices into a rumour rather than children he has seen.

“I was with them,” he says. ᴼˡ

“For an afternoon,” Easthies replies. ᴱ

“Long enough to hear what they were frightened you'd do.” ᴼˡ

The words make Qifrey glance toward him.

Olruggio keeps his attention on the table.

“They've continued learning. They knew which work was theirs. They knew what they'd been told not to attempt. Ask what happened before deciding the report has answered it for you.” ᴼˡ

The woman picks up her pen.

“Then we will need an account of what has happened.” ᴴ

“You can have mine,” Qifrey says. ᵠ

* * *

Mau speaks when they reach the question of entering the house.

“Invited,” he says. ᴹ

Easthies turns toward him.

“You don't have the authority to exempt a place from every law.” ᴱ

“I'm telling you how you're going to enter our home.” ᴹ

Qifrey rests a hand near the edge of the table.

Mau sees it. Takes the time it gives him.

“People live there. Some of them have spent a long time learning they can close a door and expect it to stay closed. You can ask to speak to someone. They can answer. You can bring a concern to us and we'll hear it.” ᴹ

“And if the concern requires more?” ᴱ

“Then say what you want to do.” ᴹ

Easthies looks toward Qifrey.

Qifrey holds his gaze.

“There will be no taking the children,” he says. “And no changing the people who know the secret until they no longer remember why they would object.” ᵠ

The room seems to narrow around the last words.

Mau keeps his hands still.

* * *

“You have a dangerous amount of confidence,” the man who has spoken least says. ᴴ

Mau looks at him.

“No. I'm trying to make this very clear.” ᴹ

He thinks of Coco's mother at the table. Of a child asking whether the adults who knew the rules could take her home away. Of Yuka making him say what he was promising before anyone else had to live inside it.

“If you make this Hall a place from which people are sent to hurt our family, take them, or erase them, I will tear it apart. I won't leave you the walls to keep doing it from.” ᴹ

The man's chair moves against the floor.

Qifrey speaks before he can stand.

“Which is why we came here to speak before anyone is harmed.” ᵠ

Mau lets his breath out slowly.

The woman looks between them.

“And what prevents your family from giving the means to do those things to everyone who comes to the door?” ᴴ

Qifrey turns the page toward her.

“A rule we are willing to keep, rather than a rule you will have to enforce by taking people away.” ᵠ

* * *

It takes longer to make that sentence useful.

They separate the people who live in the house from customers who buy something from it. They discuss future family members without promising to name children who have not been invited into the world yet. Qifrey accepts responsibility for the instruction he gives. Olruggio asks how changes will be reported without turning every private arrival into a request for permission to exist.

The woman crosses out a line.

Mau watches her do it.

The ink stays on the page. A decision can still be changed when the person holding the pen is willing to listen.

“Teachers, not a census of beds,” he says. ᴹ

She looks up.

“Names of the people responsible for keeping the agreement,” Qifrey explains. “An account of what we teach, and who has chosen to learn enough to use it. With their knowledge. No room assignments or private family histories.” ᵠ

“And no public instruction in manufacturing the ink,” she says. ᴴ

“Yes.” ᵠ

Mau looks to his friend before agreeing.

This is the part Qifrey will have to live with after the chairs are put away.

* * *

They break before finishing the last page.

Olruggio puts food in front of Qifrey with an irritation so familiar that the woman almost smiles.

Mau hears Rimuru through the connection long enough to say they are still talking, that nobody has been taken or hurt, and that the house should expect them later than the first part of the afternoon. Rimuru tells him Geld has found another reason their first sketch needs changing.

*Good reason?* ᴹ

*The stairs would land in the washing place.* ᴿ

Mau closes his eyes briefly.

*Very good reason.* ᴹ

When he opens them, Easthies is looking at the doorway.

There are people waiting beyond it who do not seem pleased that the meeting has reached this point without an arrest.

* * *

The challenge comes from one of them.

He has been listening to the last part of the discussion with increasing impatience. When the woman asks whether his concern changes the wording, he says that promises are only useful when the people making them can keep the dangerous thing contained.

He is looking at Mau when he says it.

Olruggio's face hardens.

“Say what you mean.” ᴼˡ

The man asks for a demonstration. One opponent, in a place prepared for it. Someone the Knights trust to stop the stranger if the confidence at the table has been misplaced.

Mau studies him, then looks at Easthies.

“Will losing make you less willing to keep the agreement?” ᴹ

“You haven't won,” Easthies says. ᴱ

“That wasn't my question.” ᴹ

The silence lasts long enough for Qifrey to finish putting the papers in order.

Then Easthies says, “The agreement is about the house and the teaching. It remains that.” ᴱ

Mau nods.

“Then I'll show you enough to answer the other question.” ᴹ

* * *

Qifrey catches his arm before they leave the room.

“You don't owe them everything they ask to see.” ᵠ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I wanted you beside me. Not to spend the whole of yourself making them believe I'm allowed to come home.” ᵠ

Mau looks at him properly.

Then at Olruggio, who has remained near enough to hear without pretending he has not.

“A short answer,” Mau says. ᴹ

Qifrey lets his arm go.

“Please.” ᵠ
