*When: Fern puts the new sheet on the table, Jinshi says, “Mine is still reserved.”*

She looks up.

“I haven't asked anyone to hand one over.” ᶠᵉ

“I know. I'd like it written before the useful plan becomes an expectation.” ᴶ

Mau pulls out a chair.

Fern writes Gaoshun's name in the place Jinshi indicates. Beside it she puts the condition he gives her: if news comes of a place which might be his world, they investigate before deciding whether the coin should be used.

“Not donated,” she says. ᶠᵉ

“Thank you.” ᴶ

Maomao sits beside him without commenting on the relief which makes him look younger.

* * *

The question is not whether they want families.

The drawing has already answered that with spaces waiting beside people who have spent too long imagining them alone. The question is what happens when someone has a coin and no longer needs to use it for the person they first had in mind.

“They might still want to keep it,” Mai says. ᴹᵃⁱ

“Yes,” Fern answers. ᶠᵉ

“Then that's an answer too.” ᴹᵃⁱ

She puts her cup down and waits until somebody nods before reaching for it again.

Kaneki asks what happens if a person offers one, then learns something which changes whom they need to bring.

Fern turns the page.

“Before it has been given and used, we talk. An offer isn't an arrival.” ᶠᵉ

Mau thinks about all the ways the two things can get confused when everyone is happy enough to begin counting people who are not there yet.

“And nobody owes us a room's worth of labour because we made room for them,” he says. ᴹ

Rem looks toward him.

He gives her a small smile.

* * *

Bocchi asks her question quietly enough that Nijika leans toward her before the rest of the table hears.

“If there are several people?” ᴮ

Fern waits.

“In a family.” ᴮ

The empty part of the drawing beside Bocchi's home seems to become visible to everyone who has been trying not to stare at it.

“Then we write down who you want to find,” Fern says. “We don't pretend one name makes the others less important.” ᶠᵉ

Mau looks at Bocchi.

“You don't have to choose tonight.” ᴹ

She touches the edge of the page.

“I know who.” ᴮ

“Then we can start there.” ᴹ

Nijika stays beside her while she asks how much information ought to be put on the shared list and how much can remain with Fern.

The answer takes longer than simply writing three names.

It is better for it.

* * *

They keep three different things separate on the paper.

People already here. People someone is trying to find. Offers which have not become decisions to give anything away.

Rimuru points out Shizue's name when it appears on an old copied request. Fern moves it to the first list and marks the old request as fulfilled rather than making it available for someone else to count again.

Across the table, Richeh puts her brother's name where a message has already begun travelling toward him.

It remains on the search list.

No one puts a coin beside it on her behalf.

* * *

“What about meals?” someone asks when the drawing comes back out.

Umaru looks up as though a border has been threatened.

“Dinner stays.” ᵁ

“We also have kitchens in some of the homes,” Touka says. ᵀᵒ

“Dinner can still stay.” ᵁ

Momo rests her chin on her hand.

“Then dinner needs more than one person deciding what everyone is eating.” ᴹᵒ

Shuna looks toward her.

So does Rem.

The conversation turns into suggestions about shared nights, smaller tables which can be joined, meals people can make with their own families and a common place which remains welcoming even when someone has already eaten.

Mau notices Fern stop writing for a moment.

She listens while the house finds several answers without waiting for her to turn them into a duty first.

* * *

They put the coins away where their owners have chosen to keep them.

No invocation follows the meeting. No one has become less generous by leaving with the same object they brought. Fern has a record of questions to return to when the town and the wedding are no longer trying to occupy every free surface.

Mau helps move the empty cups.

Bocchi catches his sleeve before he reaches the kitchen.

“It really can be next to us?” she asks. ᴮ

He looks toward the page she is holding.

“We left the space.” ᴹ

“Even if it takes time.” ᴮ

“Even then.” ᴹ

She lets his sleeve go, smiling at the drawing instead of trying to hide it.
