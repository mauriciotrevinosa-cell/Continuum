* * *

By the time he reaches Tempest, Rimuru has stopped worrying that every person he speaks to will hear departure as abandonment.

It still happens with enough of them that he has to explain the difference more than once.

Benimaru listens to the first part and asks about the communication arrangement. Rigurd asks who has answered for the houses and who is still deciding. Kurobe takes his own list to the people who can tell him whether the materials are ready to travel.

Nobody tries to pack the city behind Rimuru's back.

Twelve additional workers volunteer for the first phase after hearing what Geld actually needs. They know it is a project stay with food, private rest and a return to discuss. Kurobe will travel with them tomorrow. He wants to see the loading himself.

Rimuru looks at the size of the carts he is proposing.

“The road will take longer than my flight.” ᴿ

“I noticed,” Kurobe says. ᴷᵘ

* * *

Ramiris has several opinions about a room she has not seen.

Beretta waits until she finishes before asking whether she intends to say the part about the labyrinth.

She stops flying in a small, aggrieved circle.

“I'm getting to it.” ᴿᵃᵐ

“Please do.” ᴮᵉ

The labyrinth stays in Tempest. The people attending it must know who is responsible when she is at the new house, how to reach her and which decisions will wait for her. Veldora has opinions of his own. They have discussed enough to make the next visit possible, not solved every future absence by deciding the word delegation sounds useful.

“And I promised his shadows a better challenge,” she says. ᴿᵃᵐ

Rimuru thinks of Beru selling his death in her dungeon.

“I remember.” ᴿ

“Do you know how much work that is?” ᴿᵃᵐ

“I'm beginning to.” ᴿ

Beretta chooses to come with her after making clear which part of the work he wants to keep and which he expects her to ask about before offering it to somebody else.

She tells him he is becoming very demanding.

He sounds pleased.

* * *

Ranga's answer is shorter.

He has enjoyed being here. He would like his next home to be where Rimuru is making his.

“You can visit,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

“I know.” ᴿᵍ

The ease of the answer makes Rimuru put the reassurance away instead of repeating it until it sounds like a reason Ranga ought to reconsider.

They talk about where he wants to rest and whether the Fort is likely to ask him to become a border dispute.

Ranga seems less troubled by that possibility than Rimuru.

* * *

The children have Shizue's latest letter between them when he finds them.

All five speak.

Kenya wants to know whether the new rooms are ready. Ryota asks what happens to their lessons. Alice asks whether they can keep the places they like to visit here. Gale has thought about the people who would miss them. Chloe asks whether Shizue has actually said she wants to stay, rather than being placed there because everyone thinks she ought to.

Rimuru answers that one first.

“She chose it. I asked.” ᴿ

Chloe smiles down at the letter.

They want to try making their home there too.

Hinata sits with them through the rest of the conversation. They discuss who teaches, who can be asked for help and how care will be shared with the people who have agreed at the house. Shizue will not become five people's only answer to every bad dream and difficult afternoon merely because she has returned.

The rooms are not ready yet. The visit and the move have to be arranged in stages.

“Then we'll bring what we need now,” Gale says. ᴳᵃ

Rimuru looks at the others.

They nod.

* * *

Hinata agrees to escort the road party and stay while the first arrangements settle.

Rimuru asks whether she wants a message taken ahead.

She gives him a small folded page with Anko's name on it.

He has the good sense to accept it without smiling too broadly.

Testarossa and Souei will each come for a visit after the first party is settled. Their work is to remain between places, not be quietly moved into a list of permanent neighbours. They will send times once they know them.

Rimuru keeps those answers on a separate part of the page.

* * *

He returns on Kaisel as the house is preparing the evening meal.

Alone, with more paper than when he left.

Kurobe is still in Tempest with the materials. The people choosing to come will begin tomorrow and take the ordinary road; no one will walk out of a bedroom tonight and find twenty strangers already living beyond it.

Mau meets him outside with Yuka.

“How many questions?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Enough that I'd like dinner first.” ᴿ

“We can arrange that.” ʸ
