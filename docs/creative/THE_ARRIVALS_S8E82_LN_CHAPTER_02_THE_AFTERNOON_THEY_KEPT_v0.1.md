*When: Yuka asks him to leave the drawings for an afternoon, Mau looks at the page in his hand and discovers it is upside down.*

She waits while he turns it.

“Important work?” ʸ

“Very.” ᴹ

“Then it should survive until tomorrow.” ʸ

He puts the page on the table.

She has brought a small bag and the expression she wears when the decision has already survived several arguments he has not been invited to make.

“Where are we going?” ᴹ

“Far enough that you can't point at something somebody is building.” ʸ

Mau looks toward the window.

“That's becoming quite a difficult distance.” ᴹ

She takes his left hand.

“Try.” ʸ

* * *

They tell the people who would otherwise come looking and walk to the old garden inside the known protected ground.

The way is short enough that Yuka cannot call it a journey, though she insists on carrying the bag as if she has brought provisions for one. Mau offers to take it. She refuses, then lets him carry the folded blanket because she says a passenger ought to contribute something.

He looks at the trees.

“Passenger?” ᴹ

“I organised this.” ʸ

“I'm honoured.” ᴹ

“You should be.” ʸ

She chooses a place where the shade moves over the grass without hiding the path. The house can still be heard when a voice rises above its ordinary sound, but they cannot see the working table from here.

Mau looks once to make sure.

Yuka catches him doing it.

“You'll live.” ʸ

He sits down beside her.

“I think so.” ᴹ

* * *

The bag contains food, two cups and a game Umaru has assured her is fair.

Mau becomes suspicious at once.

“Those were her words?” ᴹ

“Exactly.” ʸ

“Did she laugh?” ᴹ

Yuka puts the cards between them.

“Only afterwards.” ʸ

He studies the first hand with the seriousness of someone expecting to be robbed by a hamster at any moment.

Yuka beats him before he finishes explaining why the combination ought to work.

He looks at her cards.

Then at her.

“Again.” ᴹ

She grins.

* * *

They discover the unfair part in the third round.

One card has acquired a second rule in handwriting neither of them recognises until Mau turns it sideways. Yuka compares it with the small note at the bottom and begins laughing so hard he has to take the cup out of her reach.

“She sold us two versions of the rules,” he says. ᴹ

“You read the wrong one.” ʸ

“You gave me the wrong one.” ᴹ

“I organised this.” ʸ

He looks at her expression and puts the cards down.

“You're very pleased with yourself.” ᴹ

“A little.” ʸ

Mau leans toward her.

She catches the front of his shirt before he has quite reached her, and kisses him with the confidence of someone who has won at least two games honestly enough to be satisfied.

When he draws back, she is still smiling.

“Rematch later,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

“With one set of rules.” ᴹ

“We'll negotiate.” ʸ

* * *

The food is simple.

They eat it sitting shoulder to shoulder while the breeze tries to turn the loose cards into a fourth round. Yuka traps one beneath the bag and asks him whether the hand hurts.

He turns his right wrist carefully.

“Not now. The edge still feels odd.” ᴹ

She watches the movement, then returns to her food.

He is grateful for the ease of it: a question asked, an answer heard, and the afternoon allowed to remain an afternoon afterwards.

“What?” she asks when she catches him looking. ʸ

“I like this.” ᴹ

“Being defeated?” ʸ

“Being somewhere with you where you don't need me to win anything.” ᴹ

Her smile changes.

“I don't usually need that.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

He says it before the older habit can turn it into a thing he only hopes is true.

Yuka leans her head against his shoulder.

* * *

For a while they try to imagine the room they will share in the new place without turning it into another planning meeting.

It works until she describes the chair she wants near the window and Mau suggests where a small shelf could go.

“No drawings,” she reminds him. ʸ

“It was a very small shelf.” ᴹ

“Tomorrow.” ʸ

He puts an invisible pencil away with enough ceremony to make her laugh.

She rests against him more fully, taking care of the right arm without making a project of it. He adjusts the blanket at her side with his left hand and discovers she has fallen quiet in the way which means she is listening to the place around them.

“Will it feel different?” she asks. ʸ

“The town?” ᴹ

She nods.

He thinks about the distance between a door and a person who knows why you have come through it.

“Some things.” ᴹ

“But dinner stays.” ʸ

Mau smiles.

“I think Umaru has negotiated that at a very high level.” ᴹ

* * *

They do not fall asleep for long.

Long enough for Yuka to wake with an imprint of the fold in his shirt against her cheek. Long enough for Mau to hear Ciel tell him, quietly, that the hold remained steady while he was doing something else.

He thanks her without moving.

Yuka opens her eyes.

“We should go back.” ʸ

“Before the rules change again?” ᴹ

“Before Umaru charges us for borrowing them.” ʸ

He looks at the cards scattered over the blanket.

“We were supposed to return these?” ᴹ

She begins laughing before he can finish looking horrified.

They pack slowly and walk home together, reaching the courtyard in time to help move the cups before Rimuru's mount appears above the roof.
