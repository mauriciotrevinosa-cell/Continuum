*When: Beru asks to be useful, Mau looks at the eighty soldiers already carrying things and says, “You'll have to be more specific.”*

The ant straightens.

“I can carry more.” ᴮ

“You can also get excited.” ᴹ

“I will carry with restraint.” ᴮ

Igris stands beside him in a silence which has acquired the weight of a second application.

Bellion looks toward the marked ground, then at Geld.

“The order of the loads needs someone who can see beyond the next one,” he says. ᴮᵉˡ

Mau turns toward him.

“You're asking him, then.” ᴹ

Bellion inclines his head and goes to do exactly that.

Beru watches him leave with profound interest.

* * *

The work finds different uses for them.

Bellion keeps the lifting and the movement from becoming the same narrow lane. Igris waits where a load needs to remain steady while hands finish something beneath it. Beru discovers that holding a beam motionless for several minutes is a different sort of challenge from being able to tear one apart.

He performs it with such devotion that Jiji asks whether he is trying to intimidate the timber.

“It will not move,” Beru answers. ᴮ

“I believe you,” Jiji says. ᴶⁱ

Armin moves the next mark on the plan when Geld asks. The ordinary soldiers follow the changed route, and the stack which looked as though it would block dinner traffic becomes a wall only on the side where a wall is actually wanted.

Mau watches from the shade.

For once, having an army means he can sit down.

* * *

Kamish has to be given a much more carefully chosen job.

He is interested in the scale of the undertaking and somewhat offended by the idea that a dragon might wait until someone knows where to put him.

“There,” Mau says, indicating the open ground away from the frames. “No wings over the work. No landing among the loads. They tell you what they need moved.” ᴹ

Kamish studies the places marked out before answering.

“These people are very particular about their little walls.” ᴷ

“You used to be particular about a treasure.” ᴹ

The dragon looks toward the Fort.

“An inadequate defence. I remember it bitterly.” ᴷ

Mau starts laughing before he can help it.

* * *

It is Igris who asks about the hours after the work.

Mau looks up from the cup Yuka has left beside him.

“You want to stay out?” ᴹ

“For something we would like to do, my liege.” ᴵ

“All of you?” ᴹ

Beru is suddenly paying extremely close attention to a knot in the wood.

Mau looks between them.

“As long as you're not frightening people or building anything Geld hasn't agreed to put here.” ᴹ

Igris bows.

Beru bows considerably farther.

“And you can ask someone for help,” Mau adds. “You don't have to turn every unfamiliar thing into a test of loyalty.” ᴹ

For a moment, the three of them are quiet.

Then Bellion says, “We will remember.” ᴮᵉˡ

* * *

Mau is called inside before he sees what happens next.

Igris waits until the door has closed. Beru turns toward Kurobe's temporary work surface, remembers that its owner is still on the road, and looks faintly betrayed by geography.

Wakana has remained behind to put away a sample.

Beru approaches him with a gravity usually reserved for important battles.

“How,” he asks, “does one make a small hole in wood without destroying the surrounding wood?” ᴮ

Wakana looks at his hands.

Then the wood.

“We should probably begin with a tool.” ᵂᵃ

Igris steps nearer to listen.

None of them tells Mau what the lesson is for.
