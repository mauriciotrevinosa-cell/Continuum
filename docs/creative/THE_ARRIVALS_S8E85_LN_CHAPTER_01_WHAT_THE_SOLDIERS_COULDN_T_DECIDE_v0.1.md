*When: the first wall is nearly in place, Geld asks the people who will use the door to walk through it before he lets anyone close the frame.*

It looks unnecessary until Maomao stops with the tray in her hands.

“Turn,” she says. ᴹᵐ

The worker holding the other side looks up.

She turns her body as far as the temporary opening permits, showing him where the tray will catch if somebody has to pass another person in the corridor.

He moves the mark.

The two ordinary soldiers supporting the frame do not complain about holding it longer.

Mau, watching from beside Yuka, discovers another thing an army cannot decide merely by being strong enough to carry it.

* * *

The carts have brought timber, prepared stone, tools and people who know which one they need first.

Work spreads without becoming a race through every part of the site. The covered route beside the old house receives its roof. Foundations are laid where Geld has marked the first family homes. Kurobe sets up the part of the temporary workshop which needs to remain dry and tells Bellion why one stack must stop being impressive enough to block the light.

Bellion apologises with such formality that Kurobe pauses to see whether he is being mocked.

He is not.

Beru is holding a beam nearby and watching the exchange as though it contains a secret technique.

“You can ask him,” Mau says when he comes past. ᴹ

“I am studying.” ᴮ

“He'll still know after you stop staring.” ᴹ

The ant inclines his head and goes back to preventing the beam from moving.

* * *

The first new light is made in the closed teaching room.

Qifrey has invited the people already learning with him to watch the practical lesson. The workers outside will use the finished fitting without being given a demonstration of how the ink is produced. Olruggio has checked the way it can be opened for maintenance and closed safely before anyone carries it near an unfinished wall.

Mau watches Coco's mother explain the part she was taught yesterday.

Coco waits until she finishes before pointing out one place the fitting can be held more comfortably.

“Like this?” her mother asks. ᶜᵐ

The girl nods, pleased.

The light is modest. It will not replace the crystal supply or turn every house into something which needs no attention.

It does mean one safe corridor can stay lit without using a crystal for that particular job.

Mau thinks that is worth seeing.

* * *

The meals change with the work too.

Momo asks for names for the next three shared dinners. She gets enough offers that they can choose who wants to cook with whom instead of choosing who looks least busy and hoping that means willing.

Shuna asks for one evening with Rem and Ram. Taihei offers another with Mau helping in the part Shoko has allowed. Kaneki and Touka want to make something their family likes, with help from people who want to learn it.

“And you?” Rem asks Momo. ᴿᵉ

Momo looks toward Okarun, who has been waiting with a question about the route outside.

“Tomorrow I want someone else to know what we're eating before I do.” ᴹᵒ

Rem writes it down.

Momo takes Okarun's hand and leaves them to find out.

* * *

Shizue has kept Gojo's old room after the review they held two mornings ago.

He and Shoko chose another week of the separate-bed arrangement; she chose another week of the quiet door. The next date is on the little page she keeps, and neither decision has been allowed to turn into a permanent answer simply because everyone has become used to it.

Rimuru finds her by the window after the afternoon lesson.

“Tired?” he asks. ᴿ

“A little.” ˢᶻ

“Do you want company?” ᴿ

She looks toward him.

“Yes. If we don't have to decide anything.” ˢᶻ

He settles in the place she clears beside her.

For a while they watch people carry the same length of wood past the window in opposite directions, hear Kurobe explain why, and laugh together when the third journey finally takes it where it belongs.

* * *

Mau is outside when the first rain reaches the new roof.

It is not enough to send everyone running. It is enough to make Geld stand very still and look at where the water goes.

Several people join him.

The run-off misses the place they mean to keep dry. At one corner it does not. Geld points, the worker beside him nods, and the next conversation begins with a real drop falling in the wrong place instead of a beautiful drawing which cannot get wet.

Yuka looks at Mau.

“Still pleased?” ʸ

He nods.

“Now we know which bit to change.” ᴹ
