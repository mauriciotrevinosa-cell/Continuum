*When: Olruggio gives her the envelope, Richeh knows the writing before she sees the name.*

She does not open it at once.

Her thumb rests at the edge where the paper has been folded. The shape of one letter is wrong in exactly the familiar way. He has always made that part too quickly, as if the word was already somewhere ahead of his hand and the pen had to catch it.

“Rili?” she asks. ᴿⁱ

“The answer came through the contact who found where to send yours,” Olruggio says. ᴼˡ

She looks at him.

“You haven't met him.” ᴿⁱ

“Not yet. I won't tell you I have.” ᴼˡ

She nods, keeping the distinction.

Then turns the envelope over.

Qifrey moves a chair out of the way so she can sit where she likes.

* * *

The first line makes her put a hand over her mouth.

He has answered the part of her message she almost removed.

Not the sensible questions about where he is or how an answer might find her. The little thing she had written beneath them because she could not make a page addressed to him sound like a request passed between strangers.

He remembers it.

She reads the line twice.

Then the rest.

He is in the coastal region. The changes have frightened people there too. He has heard enough reports to know why finding one ordinary route for a letter mattered, and he has asked questions of the person who brought hers before believing the easy parts of the answer.

There are questions for her. About the people she named. About the place she said she could work. About whether she still keeps the things she likes instead of only the things someone else thinks are useful.

Richeh bends closer to the page.

She has so much to answer that, for a moment, she cannot choose a word.

* * *

Tetia finds her still reading.

She stops herself from leaning over the paper.

“Is it good?” ᵀᵉ

Richeh nods.

Tetia sits beside her and waits with the effort of somebody who has decided being a friend is more important than immediately knowing the interesting thing.

After a while, Richeh turns one part toward her.

“He wants to meet.” ᴿⁱ

“Here?” ᵀᵉ

“At the shore first.” ᴿⁱ

The arrangement is written carefully: a morning three days from now, near the place Olruggio used to meet Qifrey. He has agreed to come there with people who can identify the reply and tell her where he is choosing to go. He has not promised his whole life to a house he has never seen.

Richeh likes that more than she knows how to explain.

It sounds like a person who expects to have time to decide.

* * *

Qifrey comes back when she asks for him.

She shows him the practical part of the letter and keeps the rest folded beneath her hand. He reads only what she offers.

“I'd like to go,” she says. ᴿⁱ

“Then we'll arrange it.” ᵠ

“Not send another answer and wait.” ᴿⁱ

He looks at her.

“No. We can tell him you'll be there.” ᵠ

Her fingers loosen slightly against the paper.

Olruggio agrees to go with them. They ask Mau about Kaisel rather than assuming a shadow has become a public carriage. Mau says yes to the journey they describe, asks who should have the place and expected return written down, and lets Richeh answer him herself.

“Rimuru. And the house,” she says. ᴿⁱ

“Okay.” ᴹ

He does not ask to read what her brother wrote.

* * *

That afternoon she takes the page to the place she has been using to work.

Agott is there, correcting a line which has become more annoying because it is almost right. Coco has brought a question she expects to ask once Agott stops glaring at the paper. Tetia follows with the object Richeh said she wanted left where she could find it.

They look at the letter only when she holds it up.

“He's coming to meet us,” she says. ᴿⁱ

“You,” Agott answers. ᴬᵍ

Richeh looks at the three of them.

“I'd like him to know you.” ᴿⁱ

Agott's expression changes, almost too slightly to see.

“Then bring him back if he wants.” ᴬᵍ

“After I've spoken to him.” ᴿⁱ

“Obviously.” ᴬᵍ

Tetia leans against Richeh's shoulder for a moment, then goes to find a safe place for the object she is still holding.

Richeh puts the letter beside her own work.

For the first time since she began the search, the space between wanting to tell him something and doing it has an ordinary morning written at the far end.
