*When: the Ruler says the world is accommodating what has been brought into it, Mau asks how much longer it can keep doing that.*

The answer does not come quickly.

He notices the delay. He has spent enough time with people trying to sound as though they understand the machine which is already hurting someone to recognise the value of a pause before a confident explanation.

“The new territory is stabilising,” the Ruler says.

“That wasn't what I asked.” ᴹ

The third figure turns slightly toward him.

The first does not stop looking at his face.

“It is the answer we can presently give.” ᴸ¹

Mau lets that remain a fact rather than turning it into an insult.

“Then we should talk about what you can't.” ᴹ

* * *

They know Tempest has arrived.

They know a coastal region containing the witches' Hall has been joined to the land. They speak of other additions whose boundaries are not yet clear enough to give Mau a complete map. He refuses to fill the missing parts with the names he has heard at the dinner table merely because a familiar person would make them easier to imagine.

“People are finding roads that don't belong together,” he says. “They still need time to learn where those roads go.” ᴹ

“The territories will settle.” ᴸ¹

“With their people alive in them?” ᴹ

The quieter Ruler answers this time.

“Not every displacement has been clean.” ᴸ²

There is no pleasure in the admission.

Mau feels the anger arrive anyway. He thinks of the fishermen Olruggio described and the children who had to wait for an ordinary letter to find out where a loved person had gone. He thinks of how easily distance becomes an inconvenience to someone who can fold it.

“Then you need to slow it down.” ᴹ

* * *

The coins enter the conversation because one of them asks how many more people the house means to bring.

Mau does not give them the private search list.

He asks whether they understand the difference between a coin's invitation and taking a whole inhabited region from somewhere that still needs its rivers and ground to fit together.

They know the objects. They have not described the distinction the way DIO once built it.

Inside, she has been silent long enough that Mau asks whether she wants to speak to him.

*Yes,* she says. ᴰⁱ

He gives her the part of his attention she asks for without moving his body out of the clearing.

* * *

*They were anticipated,* she tells him. *An acceptance. A refusal. Either result already had a place in the balance. It was never a promise that everyone named would come.* ᴰⁱ

Mau listens.

*A person and the relationship which makes the invitation possible are not the same kind of demand as land, a city and everyone carried inside it. Those have to be accounted for in more than one world.* ᴰⁱ

*And they haven't been.* ᴹ

Her answer is quiet.

*Not carefully enough.* ᴰⁱ

He looks toward the figures waiting in front of him.

*Can you help without going back?* ᴹ

For the first time since the encounter began, he feels something in her reaction that is fear rather than the old taste of authority.

*I don't want to go back.* ᴰⁱ

*I'm asking because I don't want to promise it for you.* ᴹ

She takes a moment.

*I can explain how to stop making it worse. I can't mend every place by naming what went wrong.* ᴰⁱ

*That's an honest answer.* ᴹ

*You'll need to be sure they accept the part where I remain alive to give it.* ᴰⁱ

* * *

Mau returns the distinction to the people waiting in the light.

He keeps it practical. Slow the additions. Identify what is being taken before deciding where to put it. Allow the receiving land time to settle. Do not count a familiar city as a complete object merely because its walls make a convenient edge; water, weather, people and the place left behind do not end at those walls.

The quieter Ruler begins asking sharper questions.

Mau answers what DIO is willing to explain and says when she would need information she does not have. He does not turn her into an excuse for knowing every world on the map.

Eventually the first Ruler says what all three have understood.

“She is with you.” ᴸ¹

Mau looks at the visible way home.

Then back at them.

“She isn't trying to take your place.” ᴹ

“That is not a sufficient guarantee.” ᴸ¹

“Then we'll make an actual one. She wants refuge. You need the world to survive the way you're handling it. We can discuss those two things without anybody surrendering her to be killed.” ᴹ

* * *

The third Ruler asks why he would protect her.

Mau remembers more than one answer, some of them unpleasant enough to make easy loyalty impossible. He does not owe her a story in which she has never harmed him. He also does not have to decide that a person who has caused harm can only remain useful as a corpse.

“Because I offered her somewhere to stay,” he says. “And because she has to live with what she's done too. I don't need her throne to make either of those things true.” ᴹ

Ashborn remains silent inside him.

DIO does not thank him in the middle of the negotiation.

He is grateful for both.

Across from him, the first Ruler lowers its gaze to the place where the light meets the earth.

“Then tell us the limits of the refuge you propose.” ᴸ¹

Mau takes another breath.

This part has to be precise enough that someone can live inside it.
