*When: Mau says the protection stays where it is, the first Ruler asks whether the woman inside him intends to hide there forever.*

“That's a question for her,” he answers. ᴹ

“Then let her answer.” ᴸ¹

Mau listens inward before moving a word into the clearing.

DIO has heard enough of what he has chosen to share to know the shape of the demand. She has not been brought out of the refuge to make the demand easier for someone else to enforce.

*I intend to live,* she says. *I can decide what comes after that when living isn't the part being negotiated.* ᴰⁱ

He gives them the words exactly.

The quieter Ruler looks toward the ground between them.

For the first time, Mau thinks it may understand the answer as something other than an evasion.

* * *

They begin with what she will not do.

She will not attempt to reclaim control of the arrivals. She will not use the guidance she gives them as a way to return herself to that place through someone else's hands. She will explain what she can of the precautions which allowed individual invitations to coexist with the balance, and where the uncontrolled movement of territory has departed from them.

“You will need to give her information if you want more than general advice,” Mau says. “She can't account for a place you won't let her know you're moving.” ᴹ

“She does not receive the authority to move it herself.” ᴸ¹

*I don't want it,* DIO tells him. ᴰⁱ

He repeats that too.

The third Ruler watches his mouth as though expecting the words to change when they pass through it.

Mau keeps them precise enough not to.

* * *

The refuge is the more difficult part.

The protected ground is fixed. It has not moved with every journey Mau has taken and will not expand because the house has drawn a larger plan. While DIO lives, the protection remains what it has been. The people before him have waited outside it; they do not get to turn the negotiation into permission to reach through it.

“No taking her out by force,” he says. ᴹ

“Within that boundary, our intervention will not be directed against her.” ᴸ¹

“Or a visitor you send to do the same thing for you.” ᴹ

The Ruler studies him.

“Nor by our order through another.” ᴸ¹

The quieter one inclines its head.

Mau waits until the third gives its own answer.

It comes reluctantly.

It comes.

* * *

Outside the boundary, she can remain in the private refuge Mau has willingly offered.

He refuses to make the inside of his body a place they can search in order to extract a guest. They refuse to recognise that as a moving version of the protection around the house.

For once, both refusals describe what he meant.

“I'm not asking you to say nothing can ever touch me,” he says. “I'm asking you not to use me as the route to her.” ᴹ

The third Ruler's gaze shifts toward his shadow.

“And the other sovereign you shelter?” ᴸ³

“Has not asked to command a war.” ᴹ

He does not promise Ashborn's soul or service in exchange for DIO's safety. The room inside him remains a place people can decline to leave.

“If she comes out in her own body beyond the protected ground,” the first Ruler says, “that refuge does not accompany her.” ᴸ¹

Mau hears DIO's silence.

Then her answer.

*I understand.* ᴰⁱ

He makes sure she has heard the difference between remaining inside him on a journey and stepping out where the agreement will no longer cover her.

She understands that too.

Only then does he continue.

* * *

They define whose promise this is.

The three present answer for the orders they issue and the interventions they command in the current control of the convergences. They will communicate the conditions to those acting under that authority. Mau does not accept the phrase as a revelation that every being called a Ruler shares a single thought, nor do they pretend they can bind a stranger who has not taken the same undertaking.

The house still has its protection.

That is the part which keeps a change in somebody else's politics from becoming an invitation to walk through its door.

DIO gives the final assent through the channel she has chosen. Ciel holds the words for Mau to check rather than inventing an extra promise because the speakers are powerful enough to make silence dangerous.

The light before him takes a shape all three recognise.

No new piece enters his chest.

No reward appears where the agreement has ended.

He is glad of both.

* * *

The last questions concern the worlds.

They agree to reduce the scale and rate of the forced additions while the existing territory stabilises, and to examine what is being taken with more care before taking it. DIO offers a first account of the distinctions they have been failing to preserve. It is a beginning they must carry back to their own work, not a sentence which rebuilds every road already broken.

The coins remain a different process.

An invitation can be accepted or refused within the provision already made for it. Neither result gives the house a right to demand that a loved person arrive. Neither requires them to treat every family search as another continent torn out of its place.

Mau asks what can be done for the places already unsettled.

The quieter Ruler tells him what they will check first.

He makes it repeat the part which describes people, not territory.

It does.

* * *

When the way back opens fully, Mau stays long enough to say one last thing.

“If you want another conversation, ask us before moving the ground under me.” ᴹ

The first Ruler looks toward the path.

“You remained.” ᴸ¹

“After you let me choose.” ᴹ

The answer seems to matter more to the quieter one than to the person who required it.

Mau turns toward the ordinary trees.

Inside, Noise is holding the impression of a structure so large that the missing places in its old work have begun to look like places rather than absences.

*Not yet,* Mau reminds it. ᴹ

*Kept,* it answers. ᴺ

He tells Rimuru he is coming back.

Then walks home before lunch has time to become a second negotiation.
