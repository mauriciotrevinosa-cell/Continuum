*When: Richeh sees her brother at the shore, the things she meant to say become impossible to arrange.*

He is standing where the letter said he would stand.

That is the first thing her mind gives her: an ordinary promise kept in an ordinary place. Then he turns at the sound of the landing, sees her beside Qifrey and stops holding the bag as though he has forgotten what it was for.

“Richeh?” ᴿˡ

She gets one foot onto the ground before Olruggio can remind her to wait.

Rili reaches her after that.

For a moment they only look at one another. He says her name again, more quietly, and she recognises the way he makes it a question when he is trying to find out whether something hurts.

“I'm all right,” she says. ᴿⁱ

Then her face changes, and the answer stops being large enough.

He puts down the bag.

She goes into his arms.

* * *

Qifrey turns away long enough to settle the things they brought.

Olruggio stands beside him, looking toward the water with considerably more interest than it requires. Neither needs Richeh to prove she knows the man before letting her finish holding him. The personal questions can be answered by the people to whom they belong.

When she finally draws back, Rili looks at her face, then at the work case she has refused to put down.

“You brought something.” ᴿˡ

She nods.

“I didn't know which one.” ᴿⁱ

“So you brought more?” ᴿˡ

The small smile in the question is familiar enough to make her throat hurt again.

“Yes.” ᴿⁱ

He sits on the low wall beside the path and makes room for the case between them.

“Show me.” ᴿˡ

* * *

She begins with the thing she almost left behind.

It is not the most complicated piece. It is the one she wanted to make because the idea would not leave her alone, even after a simpler useful version would have answered the original question.

Rili holds it carefully.

He asks where she began. She tells him. He follows the explanation without taking it away from her to make the next line himself.

Qifrey watches her start speaking faster.

It happens so rarely when she feels she has to defend the reason for the work. Here, her brother asks one good question and the next part arrives before she has time to worry it will be unwelcome.

Olruggio sits a little farther along the wall.

He has brought lunch.

By the time Richeh has finished her second explanation, Rili has noticed that too.

* * *

They eat together before discussing what comes next.

Rili tells them what he knows about the changed coast and what he does not. He explains where the message reached him and whom he has told he will be away today. Qifrey gives him the account of the house without turning its people into an impressive collection.

The girls. Coco's mother. The places to work. The new homes still being built. A rule about being allowed to ask for help which Richeh has found irritating enough to remember.

Her brother looks toward her at that.

“You asked?” ᴿˡ

She looks down at the case.

“Sometimes.” ᴿⁱ

“Good.” ᴿˡ

“I still do things myself.” ᴿⁱ

“I hoped so.” ᴿˡ

The answer lets her look up again.

* * *

She wants him to see it.

That is the difficult thing to say after the easier ones have finally come. She does not want to make a demand because she has spent a long time wanting him close. She does not want to tell the story so beautifully that refusing a visit sounds like refusing her.

Rili waits while she chooses the words.

“Would you come back with us? To visit,” she says. ᴿⁱ

He looks toward Qifrey.

“Would that be all right with the people there?” ᴿˡ

“We can ask before we leave,” Qifrey answers. ᵠ

Richeh's grip tightens on the edge of the case.

Her brother notices.

“I'd like to,” he says. ᴿˡ

She lets the breath out very carefully.

* * *

The message home is practical.

Rimuru knows the meeting has taken place. Richeh has spoken to the person who wrote the reply. He would like to visit, and they have time in the return window for the four of them to come together if the house is comfortable offering him the place they discuss.

There is an unused travelling mat and a screen in the teachers' temporary arrangement. He has his bag. He will send word back before changing a longer stay into something other people have to guess at.

The answer comes while Richeh is packing the case.

She closes it, then opens it again to make sure the small piece her brother liked is safe.

He watches without teasing her.

“I'd like to meet them,” he says. ᴿˡ

“Tetia will tell you a lot.” ᴿⁱ

“Will you tell me which parts to believe?” ᴿˡ

Richeh smiles.

“Most.” ᴿⁱ

* * *

Kaisel lowers at the place they used to land.

Qifrey gives the mount the return direction through the simple arrangement Mau authorised before they left. No new ownership passes with the journey. Olruggio makes them settle the bags before anyone settles their feelings by sitting on something breakable.

Rili looks at the shape beneath his feet.

“You mentioned a dragon,” he says to his sister. ᴿˡ

“I did.” ᴿⁱ

“I thought that might be the part I should ask about.” ᴿˡ

She looks toward him with an expression so pleased that he has to begin smiling before she answers.

“You can ask at home.” ᴿⁱ

She says the last word naturally.

Neither of them needs to correct it before they leave.
