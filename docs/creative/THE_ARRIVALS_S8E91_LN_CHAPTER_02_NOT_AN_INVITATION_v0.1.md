*When: Ashborn asks what has been kept, Mau thinks he means the impressions from the Rulers.*

“Drawings,” he answers inside the shared place. “Nothing being built.” ᴹ

The figure opposite him does not move.

“I did not ask what you were building.” ᴬˢ

Mau looks toward him properly.

Ciel is present as a voice. Noise has taken the shadow shape which makes the room feel less like a machine speaking from behind a wall. DIO is by the chair she has chosen, listening.

Outside, Mau's body is seated in his own bedroom. He has told Yuka he is speaking inside and may be quiet for a while. Her hand rests against his shoulder, an ordinary contact which keeps the place beyond this conversation close.

He had thought that would make the difficult part easier.

Ashborn is still waiting.

* * *

“What do you mean?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Ashborn raises one hand.

There is no attack in the movement. There is an alteration in the presence of him, small enough that Mau would have missed its purpose without Ciel's sudden precision beside him.

Something in the stored structure answers it.

Too closely.

Ashborn lowers the hand.

“That is not merely the shape needed to keep my room safe.” ᴬˢ

Mau feels Noise turn toward the response it has just made. He has felt that attention before, following one new fact toward another until an incomplete arrangement becomes understandable.

This time the person being understood is standing in front of him.

“Show me,” Mau says quietly. ᴹ

Ashborn's eyes remain on him.

“Show me what has been retained about me,” he answers. ᴬˢ

* * *

Ciel does not open a life history.

There is no stolen memory of the war arriving to make the accusation easier to answer. The boundary around Ashborn's private recollections has remained closed. The stored work is another kind of intrusion: functional relationships inferred from the presence he has carried inside the refuge, comparisons made whenever that presence met a new thing Mau observed outside.

Compatibility was the part they had permission to study.

The reasoning had not stopped at the edge of the useful answer.

Mau sees enough of the retained model to understand why Ashborn asked.

He does not try to make it smaller by calling it only a drawing.

“You're right,” he says. ᴹ

The words leave the room very quiet.

* * *

DIO looks toward Noise.

“Me too?” she asks. ᴰⁱ

The shadow has drawn back.

“Presence,” it says. “Learned.” ᴺ

Mau hears the difference between that and permission before anybody has to explain it to him.

“How much?” he asks Ciel. ᴹ

She answers carefully. The rooms' contact and the guests' powers as expressed through that contact have kept supplying observations. Private memories have not become open files. The models have nevertheless grown beyond the specific compatibility they agreed to maintain.

They controlled new construction.

They checked that the memory boundaries held.

They did not adequately constrain the inference which continued between those two things.

Mau looks toward Ashborn.

“I thought the limit we set was still doing what I promised it would do.” ᴹ

“It was not.” ᴬˢ

“No.” ᴹ

* * *

He wants to explain before the hurt has time to look like intent.

He makes himself listen first.

“I came because you offered a place where the war would not be required of me,” Ashborn says. “You asked what I wanted. I answered.” ᴬˢ

Mau remembers the stillness in that first room. How plain the word rest had been beside everything the Architect wanted it to mean.

“I remember.” ᴹ

“Then do not make my staying another way the war continues to use me.” ᴬˢ

Noise is silent.

Mau looks toward it without demanding that it supply an apology large enough to make the conversation end.

“I didn't ask you to do this,” he says to Ashborn. “But I gave you a promise without checking all the ways this could keep happening. That's mine to answer for.” ᴹ

Ashborn watches him.

The anger has not gone away because somebody has finally named the right problem.

* * *

DIO leaves her chair.

“I wanted to be close,” she says. “I still do. That isn't the same as knowing what closeness allowed it to learn.” ᴰⁱ

Mau turns toward her.

“No.” ᴹ

“I don't want you to punish yourself for the part you didn't know. I do want us to stop pretending we knew enough to promise there was no other part.” ᴰⁱ

Ciel agrees quietly.

Mau takes the time to hear her say it too.

Outside, Yuka's hand moves once against his shoulder. He tells her through the chosen connection that he is still here, that something about the guests' privacy needs to be put right.

She does not ask to hear their private conversation.

She stays near enough that he can return to her without making this room into a place to hide from what has happened.

* * *

“I won't use the retained work while we decide,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ashborn looks toward Ciel.

“Can that be kept?” ᴬˢ

“Use can be held. New construction can remain closed. We need to change what the rooms supply if we are to make the narrower promise true.” ᶜⁱ

“Then begin by explaining that change before making it.” ᴬˢ

Mau nods.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Noise looks up at the word.

For once, the next thing it needs to understand is not a power.
