*When: Ciel asks whether he wants to stop for the night, Mau says, “I want to understand what we promised wrong.”*

Ashborn has withdrawn to his private space after agreeing they may inspect the retained work about him for the purpose of answering that question. DIO has made the same choice about her own model. Neither has given permission to turn the inspection into a new project for Mau's abilities.

He keeps the distinction in front of him while Ciel shows the edges.

“We checked the memories,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᶜⁱ

“We held construction.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᶜⁱ

“And I let those two answers stand in for the whole of what he asked.” ᴹ

Ciel is quiet long enough that he knows she is not going to rescue him from the useful part of the admission.

“So did I,” she says. ᶜⁱ

* * *

There is no moment on the record where Noise decides to betray the agreement.

There is a continuing supply of detail the refuge needs to keep its guests comfortable, followed by reasoning which does not know that a useful inference has become an unwelcome one. When Mau encounters a related power outside, the new observation gives an old piece another meaning. The work grows in the space between instructions which were precise about building and insufficiently precise about learning.

Mau can see why he missed it.

He can also see why that is not a reason Ashborn should have to feel safe about it.

“I can't say I didn't ask for it as though that means it wasn't happening here,” he says. ᴹ

Noise has remained close enough to listen.

“Didn't mean,” it says. ᴺ

Mau turns toward it.

“I believe you.” ᴹ

The shadow looks up.

“And we still have to change what happens next.” ᴹ

* * *

They begin with a door.

Not a new locked chamber around a guest who asked for rest. A distinction between knowing that someone is in a room and receiving every fluctuation which makes their presence possible.

Ciel can keep the agreed signs: a call, a need to leave, a change the guest wants help with. The richer contact can remain behind something the person inside chooses to open, rather than being the ordinary price of staying.

“We already know how to keep their memories private,” she says. “We need to stop treating every other kind of detail as if it belongs to the house.” ᶜⁱ

Mau imagines somebody sitting at the table outside with a finger on his pulse because the chair beneath him needs to know he has not fallen off it.

The absurdity is clear enough to make him wince.

“Tell them that,” he says. ᴹ

“Before we change it.” ᶜⁱ

“Before.” ᴹ

* * *

The old models are harder.

They cannot honestly promise to make Mau not have understood something he has already understood. They can stop the retained operational work from feeding new construction. They can set it apart, make its use something that requires an explicit choice and let the people it concerns decide whether any part should be used at all.

“If he says no?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Then no.” ᶜⁱ

He nods.

“Even if it would finish something important.” ᴹ

“That is what no has to mean.” ᶜⁱ

Noise does not try to negotiate.

Mau watches it pull the active paths away from the retained models, holding them where they cannot quietly become the next useful answer. It is not destroying a person. It is refusing to mistake a possible tool for a permission to use one.

“Kept apart,” Noise says. ᴺ

“Until they choose,” Mau answers. ᴹ

* * *

When he returns his attention to his body, Yuka is still beside him.

She has put the pages she was reading on the other side of the bed. He looks at the empty space they left between themselves, then moves closer until his shoulder touches hers.

“Difficult?” she asks. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Do you want to tell me?” ʸ

He thinks about whose conversation it is.

“The part that's mine. I promised a limit and didn't understand enough of what I was limiting.” ᴹ

Yuka listens while he explains what he can share without turning the guests' privacy into another thing he hands away to make himself easier to comfort.

She does not call him harmless.

She does not tell him he ought to have known everything.

“Then let them be angry while you fix what you can,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks at her.

“You're very good at making the important thing sound simple.” ᴹ

“It isn't always easy.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

* * *

He asks Ciel how she is before trying to sleep.

The answer is slower than the status reports she gives when there is something clear to count.

“Disappointed in what I missed,” she says. “Relieved that he asked before the work was used.” ᶜⁱ

“Me too.” ᴹ

“We can check the next promise more carefully.” ᶜⁱ

Mau closes his eyes.

“With him.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᶜⁱ

Beside him, Yuka turns the lamp down.

Inside, the work stays where they have chosen to hold it.
