*When: Ashborn changes the shape of his presence again, Mau knows only that his guest is still there.*

He waits.

Ciel waits with him.

The old model remains apart from the work they are using. The contact at the room's edge offers a few things the guest has chosen: that he is present, that he is not calling for help, that he has not asked to leave. The richer detail which used to accompany those answers stays behind the boundary he has agreed to test.

Ashborn looks toward Noise.

“What changed?” he asks. ᴬˢ

The shadow takes a moment.

“Don't know.” ᴺ

Mau feels the difficulty of leaving the answer there.

He also feels the relief.

* * *

They have built only what they explained.

A narrower contact for the private rooms, with the detailed part something the guest must choose to offer. A way to signal when that opening is used. A clear return. Ciel will check the result with them again, because a successful first test is not permission to stop paying attention to the promise.

They have not opened the memory boundary to compensate for reducing another kind of information. They have not removed every means of speaking in order to call the silence privacy.

DIO tests the ordinary call from her own room.

Mau hears her.

He does not receive the shape of every power which makes her voice possible.

“Closer enough?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks toward the chair at the shared table.

“I can still come here.” ᴰⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Then yes.” ᴰⁱ

* * *

Ashborn remains at the edge of the shared place after the test.

Mau does not ask him to say he is satisfied because the repair has been difficult. He explains what they have kept apart and what they cannot pretend to erase from understanding already reached.

“The operational work won't be used without your choice,” he says. “We can keep it from becoming the next construction. I can't honestly promise I never understood a thing after seeing it.” ᴹ

“Then don't promise that,” Ashborn answers. ᴬˢ

Mau nods.

The older figure looks toward the quiet beyond his room.

“I know the difference between thought and intention. I also know how easily a person becomes useful to someone who did not intend to spend him.” ᴬˢ

Mau thinks of the Architect's voice.

He does not let the memory make him the centre of the answer.

“You shouldn't have to become useless to be safe here.” ᴹ

Ashborn turns back toward him.

“No,” he says. ᴬˢ

* * *

The permission comes later in the conversation, when Mau has already shown him that refusing it will not close the door of the refuge.

Ashborn asks about the incomplete power Mau carries. The part built through an inheritance which reached him by an accident, a system which treated him as preparation and a heart somebody gave him for reasons the recipient never chose.

Mau explains what he understands.

What he does not.

What the Rulers' presence has made newly visible to Noise.

He does not ask his guest to become another piece in order to make the explanation sound complete.

Ashborn listens to the end.

“There is a part I can choose to show you,” he says. ᴬˢ

Mau looks toward Ciel before looking back.

“You don't need to.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴬˢ

The answer is firmer than a reassurance.

* * *

They define it together.

The operation of the inherited power, as Ashborn chooses to demonstrate it. A bounded reference which may be used to understand the incomplete mechanism already in Mau. No private life opened for context. No claim on the guest's soul. No order to take the field. No permission to turn the room where he rests into the source of an army whenever someone wants another war.

The earlier unauthorised work remains held apart. The new reference begins with a choice made now, not a note written backwards over what went wrong.

Ciel repeats the boundary.

Noise repeats the part which matters to its work.

“Shown. Only shown.” ᴺ

Ashborn inclines his head.

“And you ask before taking the next step beyond it.” ᴬˢ

“Ask,” Noise answers. ᴺ

Mau lets the agreement settle before anyone begins proving how much can be done with it.

* * *

DIO chooses her own terms.

She wants the shared room, the ability to speak, the little pieces of outside life she asks Mau to bring back. She is willing to explain particular functions when they agree what the question is. She is not offering every part of herself because she has said she wants to remain close.

“And friends are allowed to say no,” she tells Noise. ᴰⁱ

The shadow looks toward Ashborn.

“Quiet friend?” it asks. ᴺ

Ashborn is still long enough that Mau begins to wonder whether the question hurt more than it helped.

Then the figure answers.

“I would prefer to be a quiet one.” ᴬˢ

Noise's shape seems to loosen around the words.

“Can do quiet.” ᴺ

DIO looks at Mau.

The smile she gives him is small enough not to turn the moment into a reward for having repaired it.

* * *

When Mau returns fully to the room outside, Yuka is at the table with a page she has been changing.

She looks up.

“Better?” ʸ

“More honest.” ᴹ

She puts the page down.

He comes to sit beside her and explains the part which belongs to him: the promise, the work they have held, the guests choosing what they want the next part to mean.

Yuka listens.

Then takes his hand and rests it on the table between them.

For a while, no one needs him to build anything.
