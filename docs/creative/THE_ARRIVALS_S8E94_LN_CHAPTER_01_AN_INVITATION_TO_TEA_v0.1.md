*When: Echidna asks whether the quiet king takes tea, Mau tells her he has not made a list of his guests' drinks.*

She looks disappointed in him.

“A poor beginning to hospitality.” ᴱᶜ

“I offered a room before I knew whether he wanted a menu.” ᴹ

“One needn't exclude the other.” ᴱᶜ

Coco's mother sets a cup beside her daughter and sits down. She has brought the question Coco asked her about the little light fitting, and Echidna has managed to turn it into a discussion of curiosity before Mau can decide whether he is still being criticised.

He takes the remaining chair.

“What are you proposing?” ᴹ

Echidna smiles.

* * *

The next tea need not belong only to people who can walk into the ordinary room.

She has heard enough about the shared place inside Mau to be interested in it. More importantly, she has heard enough about the people using it to want to invite them, rather than treating them as an explanation of what Mau can do.

“Ashborn needn't answer anything,” she says. “He needn't attend. It seems discourteous not to ask merely because I might have questions.” ᴱᶜ

“You will have questions.” ᴹ

“I have several now.” ᴱᶜ

“That isn't helping your argument.” ᴹ

Coco laughs into her cup.

Her mother tells her to put it down before she spills.

* * *

They leave it as an invitation to discuss.

Mau will ask his guests. Ciel will choose whether she wants to take part. They will decide what a visit means before someone treats an interesting room as permission to fill it with people. A conversation about tea does not quietly become a new doorway through every private space in him.

Echidna accepts the limits with enough grace that Mau is briefly suspicious.

“I can want to know something and still be invited back after being told no,” she says. ᴱᶜ

He looks at her.

“Useful skill.” ᴹ

“You should practise it.” ᴱᶜ

“I've had a demanding week.” ᴹ

“So I hear.” ᴱᶜ

* * *

Daphne arrives with a question about the path to the growing ground.

Sekhmet objects to moving her chair for a second time. Minerva asks which part of the new route needs somebody to stop leaving tools where children will run. Carmilla has an opinion about the quiet corner which is so softly offered that Coco's mother has to ask her to repeat it.

She does.

Satella listens beside Pride while the page turns between them.

Mau stays long enough to hear the court become a place they want for reasons which have nothing to do with what the rest of the house needs from them.

Then Coco asks Echidna a question about the thing they were discussing before he arrived, and he is allowed to stop being the subject.

He finishes his tea with considerable pleasure.
