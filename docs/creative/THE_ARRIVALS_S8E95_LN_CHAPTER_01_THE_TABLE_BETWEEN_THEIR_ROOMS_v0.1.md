*When: Gojo puts the table down, Shoko tells him it was facing the other way yesterday.*

He looks at it.

Then at the two doors beyond it.

“This way we both reach it.” ᴳ

She comes closer, tries the route from her own room and moves the chair a little.

“This way we both get past it.” ˢᵒ

He smiles and leaves it there.

* * *

Their things arrive in an order neither of them intended.

The blankets first. A box Gojo thought he had packed more sensibly. Shoko's books, which she catches somebody trying to put in the wrong room. Two cups which have become a joke only because the joke has had enough ordinary mornings to become something else.

Mau carries a small box with Yuka and looks toward the doorway when they have finished.

“You can tell us where,” he says. ᴹ

Shoko points.

He puts it there without reading what is on the folded page inside.

Gojo watches the respect of that small movement and finds himself suddenly unwilling to fill the quiet with the cleverest thing he can think of.

“Thank you,” he says instead. ᴳ

Mau looks up.

Then smiles.

* * *

The folding bed goes back to Diablo's travelling equipment.

It does not return to the music room. That loan ended long ago, and the room has enough uses of its own. Shoko's own bed has come from her old room. Gojo has the bed they prepared for this place, leaving the one Shizue is using where it belongs until she chooses her move.

By afternoon, they have no reason to go back to the old arrangement except to check they have not forgotten something small.

Shoko goes once.

Gojo goes with her.

The space seems larger without the screen and the second bed. There is a mark where one leg rested against the floor. Shoko looks at it for a moment before turning toward the door.

“All right?” he asks. ᴳ

“Yes.” ˢᵒ

He lets her close it.

* * *

They choose where the remaining things go together.

Not every choice is graceful. Gojo has brought something Shoko insists he cannot possibly like enough to display. He insists that she misunderstands its significance. She asks him to explain the significance and watches him discover he has committed too early to the argument.

It goes on his shelf.

She keeps her own shelf clear of it with a satisfaction which makes him laugh.

There are two bedrooms. There is also a room neither of them has to leave in order to let the other be alone. The difference becomes real when Shoko goes to put something away and returns without feeling she has ended the visit.

Gojo has filled the cups.

One is waiting where she can reach it.

* * *

At supper, Mau asks how the first afternoon went.

“He has dreadful taste,” Shoko says. ˢᵒ

“She kept moving the chair.” ᴳ

Yuka looks between them.

“So it went well.” ʸ

Neither finds an argument worth making.

The walk back is short. They can still hear the common room when they leave it and the sound softens by degrees instead of disappearing at a border. Shoko notices the lights along the gallery. Gojo notices she has taken his hand while looking at them.

He keeps his own around hers.

* * *

That night, he stands at his bedroom door after she has turned down the lamp in the shared room.

“Good night,” he says. ᴳ

She looks toward him.

There is enough privacy between the two doors to make the ordinary words feel chosen.

“Good night.” ˢᵒ

He goes inside.

Later, she comes out for water and finds his cup on the table.

Precisely where it will not catch her sleeve.

She smiles in the quiet, drinks, and goes back to her own bed.
