*When: Bocchi reaches the top of the new stair, she looks back to see whether she has taken the wrong one.*

Nijika is still behind her.

So is Kita, carrying something she said would be lighter if they stopped worrying about it. Ryo has the expression of a person who has found a very useful reason to arrive after the large things have been moved.

The door in front of Bocchi opens where the drawing said it would.

She goes through it anyway with the feeling that the page may have been exaggerating.

There is a room.

A window. A place to put her instrument without keeping it under the bed. The short passage to the others. The stair down to the music space which belongs beneath them rather than beneath Mau and Yuka's bedroom.

For a moment, she cannot decide which part to look at first.

* * *

They test the noise before unpacking everything.

Nijika stays in the music room with Ryo while Bocchi and Kita walk the gallery. The first notes travel through the open door. When it closes, the small entrance space does the work Kurobe said it should do, taking the sharpness from the sound without making the room feel as though it has been buried somewhere the house will forget.

Mau is waiting at the common end of the route.

“Can you hear it?” Bocchi asks. ᴮ

“Some.” ᴹ

Her face changes.

“I can hear people too,” he adds. “It sounds like you're in the house. Not in somebody's pillow.” ᴹ

Kita laughs.

Bocchi looks toward the closed door again.

The next note is softer out here.

It is still theirs.

* * *

The future family space is beside her door.

Bocchi has seen the mark so many times that its actual emptiness is almost a disappointment. There is no miraculous person waiting behind it to reward her for having made room. The parents and sister she wants to find are still people she wants to find.

Mau stands beside her while she looks.

“It's really empty,” she says. ᴮ

He nods.

“For now.” ᴹ

She touches the edge of the doorframe.

“It doesn't mean they'll come.” ᴮ

“No.” ᴹ

She looks at him.

“But if they do, nobody has to tell me there isn't room because I chose to stay near my friends.” ᴮ

Mau smiles slowly.

“That's what we kept it for.” ᴹ

* * *

She asks him to help choose where the little shelf should go.

The question leads to another one about the light, then one about whether she can still find him without looking as though she needs something urgent. He points out the route to the place he and Yuka will move into once it passes its last checks.

“You can come,” he says. ᴹ

“Even if you're busy?” ᴮ

“You can ask. Sometimes I'll be busy. It won't mean you were wrong to come.” ᴹ

Bocchi looks down at the shelf between them.

She has known him long enough to hear the difference between that and a promise nobody could keep.

“Okay.” ᴮ

They put the shelf where she can reach it.

* * *

The others return with something to eat before Bocchi realises how long they have been arranging one room.

Kita sits down on the place which is meant to become a chair once somebody finds where it has gone. Nijika asks whether Bocchi likes the stair now that she has used it. Ryo offers a confident opinion about the best view, then declines to admit she chose it before helping carry anything.

Mau listens to the argument until it no longer needs him.

At the door, he pauses when Bocchi calls his name.

“The music,” she says. “For the wedding. I have a beginning.” ᴮ

“Would you like me to hear it?” ᴹ

She thinks about the answer.

“Not yet.” ᴮ

He smiles.

“Then tell me when.” ᴹ

* * *

Later, he hears the first notes from the common passage.

He does not stop outside the door to turn a private attempt into a performance she has not offered. He simply recognises the sound, lets himself be pleased by its place in the evening and goes to find Yuka.

Upstairs, Bocchi hears somebody laugh along the gallery.

The room is hers.

The house is still there.
