*When: Yuka asks where he has put the clean shirt, Mau points to the wrong bag.*

She opens it.

Looks inside.

Looks at him.

“We've lived together long enough for you to know what a shirt is.” ʸ

“I know the category.” ᴹ

“Then find one.” ʸ

He searches the next bag and finds the shirt beneath the thing he was certain he put on the shelf. Yuka takes it with an expression of satisfaction so familiar that the new room immediately becomes easier to live in.

He had expected the first evening to feel grander.

He is delighted that she has begun it by proving he is still the same person at unpacking.

* * *

They join the common meal before trying to finish the last boxes.

The walk is as short as Yuka wanted. They pass Mai, who asks whether Mau has found where his things go, and Tsurugi, who offers no assistance to an answer he clearly expects to enjoy.

Yuta and Maki are already at the table.

Yuka sits beside them without having spent the journey deciding whether the distance deserves a visit. Mau takes the place near her and lets dinner happen around them.

Across the room, Bocchi is talking to Kita. Rem has brought something she wanted to share rather than an entire meal she was obliged to make. Shuna is eating food somebody else planned. The children appear from the Fort when they are called.

Mau notices all of it more clearly because, tonight, he knows which door he will go through afterwards.

* * *

Rimuru is waiting in the sitting room when they return.

“Well?” he asks. ᴿ

Yuka looks down at him.

“You still have a room.” ʸ

“Good to establish.” ᴿ

“You still owe me.” ʸ

He makes a small, unhappy sound.

Mau puts the empty cups down before he starts laughing.

Shizue opens her door at the noise and finds all three looking at her with varying degrees of innocence.

“Tea?” she asks. ˢᶻ

Rimuru accepts so quickly that Yuka lets him have the change of subject.

* * *

They sit together while the new place begins making ordinary evening sounds.

Somebody passes along the gallery. A door closes farther away. The music room offers a brief phrase, then stops while someone talks. Shizue has put one object from the children on the shelf she chose. Rimuru looks at it, asks which child made it and listens to the whole answer.

Mau catches Yuka watching them.

She gives him a tiny shake of the head before he can decide she is proposing an explanation for someone else's feelings.

He smiles and stays with his own cup.

There is room for people to become clear to themselves without the house naming them first.

* * *

In their bedroom, Yuka takes off the borrowed hoodie and folds it where she wants to find it in the morning.

Mau looks toward the chair.

“Still yours?” he asks. ᴹ

She puts the hoodie on it.

“Still mine.” ʸ

He sits on the bed, laughing softly, and lets her come between his knees to inspect the way his shirt has twisted while unpacking. She straightens it with a tenderness which makes the joke leave his face before she leans down to kiss him.

He holds her waist carefully, then comfortably when the arm remains exactly where he expects it to remain.

Yuka rests her forehead against his.

“Here,” she says. ʸ

He looks at her.

“Here.” ᴹ

* * *

They talk about the small things before sleeping.

Which shelf needs moving. What they would like to eat on the first morning they do not have to leave the room to find a cup. Whether the curtain lets in enough light for Mau and too much for Yuka. She reminds him that wanting the east window does not give him permission to pretend the sun has made the decision about when she wakes.

“I would never.” ᴹ

“You were thinking it.” ʸ

“A little.” ᴹ

She takes his hand and pulls him down beside her.

No vow has to be completed tonight. They have chosen to wait for the wedding, and the closeness they already know does not become less real because it has an edge they keep together.

Mau kisses her hair.

She settles against him with the ease of someone who knows where his breathing will be.

* * *

He wakes briefly with the mattress farther below his back than it ought to be.

Only a little.

The old, familiar lift has caught him in a good dream without asking the room to become a demonstration. Yuka has one hand resting against him and the other reaching for the blanket which he has almost taken with him.

He lets himself settle slowly.

She opens one eye.

“Very happy?” ʸ

He looks at her face in the low light.

“Very.” ᴹ

“Stay down and prove it.” ʸ

He laughs quietly, gathers the blanket around them and does as he is told.

Outside their door, the home grows quiet by degrees.

Inside it, he goes back to sleep beside the person he wanted to wake with.
