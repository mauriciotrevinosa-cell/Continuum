# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 53
## Chapter 1 — The Last Plate

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.1 (2026-11-02)
**Volume:** 53
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S9E01
**Continuity state:** B+84 after final S8 dinner, then B+85 morning. Existing inhabited town; MY engaged/abstinent, three-bedroom home with Rimuru and Shizue. Visitors/crew retain agreed plans. New Ruler request delivered outside fixed ward; previous pact not retrospectively changed. No trial yet.

*When: the last plate reaches the kitchen, Yuka is still telling Mau that he has carried enough of them.*

“It's one plate.” ᴹ

“It was one plate the last four times.” ʸ

He considers the evidence. Behind her, Maki lifts the stack out of his hands and passes it to Shuna without interrupting the conversation she is having with Rem. His defence leaves the room before he can improve it.

Yuka holds out her hand.

“Walk me home.” ʸ

He takes it.

They make it as far as the open route beside the tree before Ori catches them. She has something in her hand, folded badly enough that Mau can see one corner of the drawing and none of its intended subject.

“Tomorrow,” she says. “If you're not doing anything.” ᴼ

He crouches a little to look at what she offers. A small table, or perhaps a house. There is an object beside it which could be a dragon until he notices the enormous ears.

“Ear?” ᴹ

Ori nods, delighted that the important part has survived.

“He has to sit there. Mum said I should ask you before deciding.” ᴼ

Yuka leans over his shoulder.

“What have you decided for us?” ʸ

“Nothing.” ᴼ

The answer arrives with suspicious speed.

Mau looks at the second little shape near the table.

“Is that me carrying something?” ᴹ

“Nothing heavy.” ᴼ

He starts laughing. Ori looks toward Frieren, who is waiting within easy hearing distance, and receives the smallest possible shrug from her mother.

“After breakfast,” Mau says. “You can show us.” ᴹ

Ori folds the page again and runs back. Frieren catches the corner before it can fall, smooths it once and gives it back to her. They disappear into their own route together.

Yuka watches them go.

“You've accepted a contract without reading it.” ʸ

“I recognised the client.” ᴹ

“That's how they get you.” ʸ

She pulls him toward their door.

* * *

Rimuru is on the sitting-room cushion when they come in. Shizue is putting the kettle away, her sleeve caught above one wrist. She asks whether they want anything before she finishes.

“We're fine,” Yuka says. “Unless you've hidden something good.” ʸ

Shizue looks at Rimuru.

Mau looks at Rimuru.

The slime sinks fractionally into the cushion.

“There was a perfectly reasonable distribution.” ᴿ

“Was there?” Shizue asks. ˢᶻ

“There was a distribution.” ᴿ

Yuka laughs, lets go of Mau's hand and goes to inspect the small plate Rimuru has been protecting. There are two pieces left. She gives one to Shizue, breaks the other and hands half to Mau.

Rimuru watches his portion disappear into the household with the expression of a man witnessing a legal failure.

“You could have asked.” ᴿ

“You could have shared,” Yuka answers. ʸ

Shizue sits down to eat hers.

Mau stays standing for another second, looking at the three of them. The place has already begun acquiring the small signs of occupation: a cup in the wrong place, Yuka's book turned over beside the seat, something from the children propped against Shizue's door until she finds a better home for it.

There is nothing grand about the sight.

He wants to keep it so fiercely that he almost forgets to eat.

Yuka nudges his wrist.

“Before I change my mind.” ʸ

He puts the piece in his mouth. She steals the last crumb from his fingers and looks entirely pleased with herself.

* * *

In their room she finds him trying to pull the blanket straight while still standing on one corner of it.

“How did you survive before me?” ʸ

“A lot of supervision.” ᴹ

“Clearly.” ʸ

She frees the corner with her foot, waits while he succeeds at the second attempt and sits beside him. For a while they talk about tomorrow. Ori's mysterious project. The shelf Yuka would like Mai to look at. A walk which could remain a walk if Mau did not notice something halfway through it and decide to help.

“I can walk past things.” ᴹ

“You carried a chair to somebody who was moving it six steps.” ʸ

“It was going my way.” ᴹ

Yuka leans against him, laughing into his shoulder. He rests his cheek against her hair.

“We could go farther,” he says. “When the last little things are done.” ᴹ

“You said that before the last little things became a town.” ʸ

“I have improved my definition.” ᴹ

She turns her face up. He kisses her, slowly enough that her answer becomes a hand behind his neck and a second kiss she takes for herself.

When she settles beside him again, he stays awake a little longer than she does. Her breathing quietens against his chest. The two familiar rhythms inside his own body continue without asking anything of him.

*A good evening,* Ciel says again, more softly than she had at dinner. ᶜⁱ

*Still going,* he answers. ᴹ

He closes his eyes.

* * *

Ori's plan turns out to require a low table outside the Fort, two stools, Ear and a bowl of fruit which she has already persuaded Rem to cut.

Umaru objects to the name of the event before anybody has been told what it is.

“You can't call it a meeting. Those require agendas.” ᵁ

“It has food,” Ori says. ᴼ

“Accepted.” ᵁ

Yuka takes the lower stool. Mau folds himself beside her, lets Ear put an unreasonable amount of his weight against his thigh and waits while Ori explains the rule about everybody getting a turn to choose what they do next.

It is a very small plan.

He gives it his full attention.

When she chooses a game, he plays. When Yuka wins, he accepts that the rules may have acquired an amendment he failed to hear. Umaru tells him that a competent participant would have read them before arriving.

He looks toward Yuka.

She gives him exactly the expression he deserves.

They have almost finished the fruit when Rimuru comes along the covered route. He stops outside their little circle instead of bringing the interruption into it.

Mau sees the change in his face first.

“Something happened?” ᴹ

“A message at the outer post,” Rimuru answers. “The three from your conversation. They asked for an answer, not for you to come immediately.” ᴿ

Yuka's fingers close around her cup.

Mau keeps his hand against Ear's back.

“About DIO?” ᴹ

“They say the arrangement requires another discussion.” ᴿ

The fruit bowl sits between them, almost empty.

Mau looks down at Ori, who is waiting to learn whether their small meeting has already ended.

“Tell them I'll answer after this,” he says. ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

Yuka places her cup down. She stays beside Mau while Ori explains the next turn, and he listens until she has finished.
