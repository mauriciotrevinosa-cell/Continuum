# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 53
## Chapter 3 — What DIO Had Learned

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.1 (2026-11-02)
**Volume:** 53
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S9E02
**Continuity state:** B+85 afternoon/evening. DIO objects to renewed Trial proposal from learned responsibility, knows old harm without private memory broadcast. Inner meeting voluntary with Ashborn/Noise/Ciel/M; emergency owner-return reference authorised within bounded inheritance mechanism, private histories closed. No construction/fragment/fullawakening. Family meeting next; M has not accepted.

*When: DIO asks to speak where he can look at her, Mau sits down before asking whether she wants anyone else there.*

“Ciel,” she says. “If she is willing.” ᴰⁱ

*I am,* Ciel answers. ᶜⁱ

Yuka is in the sitting room. Mau tells her where his attention is going and leaves the bedroom door open. He has never found a better way of explaining the ordinary strangeness of being present in more than one place than telling somebody before his face goes quiet.

Inside, DIO is waiting at the table.

She has asked for the space which can be shared. Her private door is behind her, closed. Ashborn has remained on the other side of his own. The distinction matters enough that Mau notices it even now.

He sits opposite her.

“You heard them.” ᴹ

“I heard you asking the questions I didn't know to ask.” ᴰⁱ

The words take the easy reply out of his mouth.

DIO puts her hands on the table. There is nothing theatrical in the gesture. Her fingers look small, which makes him remember with an uncomfortable suddenness how strange it is to be afraid of somebody who can also look tired.

“When I made your trial,” she says, “I understood the restoration.” ᴰⁱ

Mau stays still.

“I knew the body could return. I treated that as an answer to what I was doing to you.” ᴰⁱ

He can hear Ciel with him, quiet enough to let him keep listening.

“It wasn't,” he says. ᴹ

“I know that now.” ᴰⁱ

* * *

She does not tell him that learning it makes the old thing smaller.

For a while she speaks about what came after. The way he moved through a room after coming back. The questions he stopped asking because he believed he already understood the price of the answer. The times she had thought the next decision was generous simply because it was less cruel than the previous one.

Mau looks down at his hands.

He remembers things this woman did. He remembers things the people outside never lived. Neither set can be made easier by choosing the most convenient version of her.

“You can't fix that by offering to leave,” he says. ᴹ

DIO's mouth tightens.

He had guessed correctly.

“I'm not asking you to forgive it,” she answers. ᴰⁱ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then hear me when I tell you I don't want you to go.” ᴰⁱ

He looks up.

She is angry now. He finds the anger easier to listen to than another apology.

“They spoke about returning you to your entry state,” she says. “As if that tells anyone what returns with you. I used to think the important number was whether you were alive at the end. I have watched you carry the rest.” ᴰⁱ

Mau swallows.

“I carry good things too.” ᴹ

“I know. I would like you to be allowed to keep having them without another examination.” ᴰⁱ

* * *

He asks whether the three can withdraw their existing promise if he refuses.

She considers it carefully.

“They gave it for their own actions and those they command. That part was completed.” ᴰⁱ

“So this is new.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴰⁱ

“Then I need to know what they think they're buying.” ᴹ

DIO looks away for a moment.

“They said next stage.” ᴰⁱ

“I heard.” ᴹ

“There are more hands on the controls than the three you met. Some will want the displaced authority surrendered before they agree to anything she suggests. Others will object to taking directions through something they can't classify.” ᴰⁱ

He lets the sentence settle.

“Me.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴰⁱ

Ciel speaks then, close to him rather than through a borrowed body at the table.

*That may explain the demand. It does not establish that the assessment is necessary.* ᶜⁱ

Mau nods.

“We ask again. Together. They can explain the part they left out.” ᴹ

DIO looks back at him.

“And if the answer is bad?” ᴰⁱ

“Then it stays bad. I'm not going to call it good because you need somewhere safe.” ᴹ

Her shoulders lower a little.

“Thank you.” ᴰⁱ

He sits with her until she can ask for something ordinary. She wants the window in their shared space left open to the impression of the evening outside. He asks Ciel to help him show it without opening anything else.

They do that much.

* * *

Ashborn joins them after Mau has told him why they are talking.

He does not emerge with a lesson about courage. He listens to the terms, asks one question about the restoration and goes quiet long enough that Noise begins shifting closer to Mau's side of the table.

“What do you recognise?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“A place which thinks it knows who owns what it restores.” ᴬˢ

Ciel's attention sharpens.

Ashborn watches Mau rather than the empty place her voice occupies.

“You have the reference I offered,” he says. “The mechanism by which inherited power remains attached to its bearer. Have you used it?” ᴬˢ

“Only to understand the permitted part,” Mau answers. ᴹ

Noise makes a small movement.

“Kept.” ᴺ

Ashborn inclines his head.

“If a place attempts to separate you from the ordinary relation between your body and what belongs to you, that reference may help it recognise the error.” ᴬˢ

“Will it open a door?” ᴹ

“I haven't seen the place.” ᴬˢ

The honesty is almost a relief.

* * *

They make the permission narrower instead of grander.

Noise may use the offered reference to follow the continuing connection to Mau's own body and possessions if an external enclosure interferes with it. It may work on a way back. Ashborn's private memory remains closed. His refuge is his. Nobody has been asked to become an army or another heart to power an escape.

Mau repeats the terms because he wants to hear whether they sound like the thing they intend.

Ashborn says yes.

DIO listens without volunteering her body to the solution.

Noise looks toward Mau.

“Bring back?” ᴺ

He feels the question in more places than one. The old urge to protect. The care which has been learning to ask before becoming construction.

“If something cuts us off,” Mau says, “you can work on bringing me back. You don't have to wait for me to notice what you found.” ᴹ

“No hurting friends.” ᴺ

“No hurting friends.” ᴹ

Noise settles beside him.

They have not decided that he is going.

For the first time that afternoon, they have decided something which makes him feel less alone if he does.

* * *

When he opens his eyes, Yuka is sitting in the bedroom doorway with one knee pulled up.

“Bad?” she asks. ʸ

“Honest.” ᴹ

She gets up, crosses the room and sits on the bed beside him.

“Did she tell you not to go?” ʸ

“Several times.” ᴹ

Yuka takes his hand and rests it between both of hers.

“I like that answer from her.” ʸ

He gives a small laugh which becomes a breath instead.

“Me too.” ᴹ

She stays until he is ready to stand. Then they go together to the people waiting to hear the rest.
