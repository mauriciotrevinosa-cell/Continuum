*When: Gojo offers him the first move, Mau almost answers the way he would in the training ground.*

The habit reaches his mouth before the room reaches his thoughts.

Almost.

He looks past the blindfold toward the pale line over the door.

“Let me talk first.” ᴹ

Gojo's likeness smiles.

“You already know the rules.” ᴳ

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then what is there to talk about?” ᴳ

Mau thinks of the real man stopping a fight to ask whether they are still enjoying it. He thinks of the second before both of them agreed to take the next part seriously. The delight of being allowed to try something difficult with someone who wanted to see him succeed at it.

He can remember that without giving this room the right to use it.

“Anything,” he says. “Pick something.” ᴹ

Gojo lifts two fingers.

* * *

The first exchange is almost familiar enough to bear.

Mau draws the space beside the likeness inward, changes position and lets the resulting pull carry an attack past his shoulder. He drops Limitless to use the angle he knows from Sky Manipulation, turns the next line away from himself and reaches the back of the room while the pressure passes through the place he occupied.

He does not try to hold both techniques in the same body at once. Ciel has taught him enough about waste that even without her voice he can hear the objection before committing it.

The thought hurts.

He uses the hurt to move.

Gojo's likeness comes after him, quicker than the last one, fed the answer to his route by the place which is watching every turn. Mau strips the distance between them into a thin line, presents one direction and escapes along the other.

For a few seconds he has the fight.

Then the room gives Gojo a second arrival point.

The strike meets Mau before his body has finished leaving the first.

* * *

He wakes with the full breath he did not get to finish.

Gojo is waiting.

Mau rolls before looking up. A shallow cut passes through the ground where his head was. The likeness has changed its opening.

He counts three moves and steals a fourth.

This time he gets close enough to put his hand beside the familiar throat. Amplification closes around his arm. He can feel the little place where defence will no longer keep them apart.

Gojo's smile disappears.

There is a tiny moment in which the likeness looks afraid.

Mau pulls his hand back.

The room uses the hesitation.

His arm breaks before he can turn. He hears the sound, feels the pressure arrive through his shoulder and meets the floor hard enough to lose the next breath.

Gojo crouches beside him.

“You could have finished.” ᴳ

“I know.” ᴹ

The likeness takes his injured hand.

“Then why are you still here?” ᴳ

He tries to answer. The sound which comes out is too small to contain the explanation.

Gojo begins to straighten the broken arm.

Mau asks him to stop.

* * *

After a while the room changes to the yard outside the house.

Maki is standing beneath the covered route. A cup is on the low wall beside her, exactly where he has watched her leave one between conversations. The ordinary detail makes him angrier than the weapon in her hands.

“Don't use this place.” ᴹ

She walks toward him.

He gives ground. The path widens until there is more distance than the real courtyard contains. Beyond the likeness he can see people at the table, turned away from the approaching fight.

He knows what the chamber will do before it does it.

Maki changes direction toward those people.

Mau moves between them.

The weapon enters his shoulder because he has put the shoulder there. He catches the shaft and prevents the second motion, his feet cutting tracks through the false ground.

“They're not real,” Maki tells him. ᴹᵏ

“Then leave them out of it.” ᴹ

“You put yourself here.” ᴹᵏ

He looks into her face.

For a moment the room loses its hold on that particular sentence. The real Maki has said hard things to him. She has also walked into danger to make him stop thinking that danger belonged to him alone.

He keeps that memory where the chamber can fail to reach it.

Then she drives the weapon farther.

* * *

The fights keep changing their numbers.

One pursuer becomes two. Two become four. The space in which he can turn becomes narrower while the force of each arrival increases. When the chamber cannot make the shape of a loved person carry the next demand cleanly, it changes the ground, removes a landing or lets an attack begin from a direction which had not existed a moment before.

His body remains the body which entered.

He has enough strength to kill the face approaching him.

The thought stays true through every arrangement. The chamber makes sure he knows it. It leaves an opening beside the eye, presents the throat, allows his grip to close before making him choose what to do with it.

Sometimes he breaks the weapon.

Sometimes he breaks a limb and spends the seconds he gained trying to repair it.

Sometimes he simply runs until the room has taken running away.

The door never opens.

* * *

He starts counting by the things he can ask before the first attack.

Can I sit down?

Can you give me a minute?

Can we talk about something else?

He knows the answers vary. That becomes enough to make him ask again.

Once, a likeness of Rimuru lets him sit on the ground beside it. Mau puts his head back against the wall and closes his eyes. He does not believe the quiet is mercy. He takes it anyway.

“I miss you,” he says. ᴹ

“Then go home.” ᴿ

His eyes open.

The likeness is watching his hand.

Mau puts the hand in his lap.

“Can I stay like this a little longer?” ᴹ

Rimuru smiles.

For almost a minute, nothing hurts.

* * *

He loses the number he had been keeping after a room in which he has no hands with which to mark it.

The next restoration gives them back.

He stares at the fingers until he remembers which two should feel stiff. Their familiar resistance becomes proof that something from before still exists.

He tries to remember the shirt he packed.

Yuka had laughed at him.

The real Yuka.

He says the distinction silently while another familiar face approaches.

The chamber lets him see the door behind it.

He turns away from the handle and starts looking for the place where he might be allowed to sit.
