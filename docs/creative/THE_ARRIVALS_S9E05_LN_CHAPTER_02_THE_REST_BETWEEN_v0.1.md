*When: the next thing comes through the doorway, it has no face he recognises.*

Mau feels relief before it reaches him.

The creature is broad enough to touch both sides of the opening. There is bone over its shoulders and a second set of arms folded against its ribs. Its first movement breaks the table between them.

He goes through the gap the movement leaves.

His dagger finds the place beneath its jaw. He drives the blade in, turns his body and takes the weight of the fall along the floor instead of into his own spine.

For once, he does not have to stop halfway through the useful thing.

The creature dies.

The door remains closed.

* * *

There are two in the next room.

Mau uses Red to take the first across the second's path. He drops the technique before the recoil has finished, changes the angle of the air around the returning limb and cuts through the exposed joint.

The cost moves through him without the old comfort of Ciel naming it.

He knows enough to keep moving.

The second creature catches his leg. Bone gives beneath its grip. He is halfway through a counter before he realises the door has opened behind it.

Umaru is standing there.

His hand stops.

The creature throws him.

He meets the wall on his injured side. Something inside his chest tears when he tries to breathe. He forces healing toward the damage and finds the chamber taking more for the same repair than it took in the previous room.

The creatures approach.

Umaru sits down beside the door as if she has come to watch a game.

“You know what would make them stop,” she says. ᵁ

Mau tries to get his unbroken foot beneath him.

“Go away.” ᴹ

The likeness waits.

“Please.” ᴹ

* * *

He fights the creatures because they are coming.

There are moments in which the room becomes a sequence he can still understand: a shoulder moving, a narrowing space, the choice between losing an arm and taking the wound somewhere that will not end the next breath.

He learns to stop trying to repair everything at once.

He makes a hand useful before making it whole. Keeps one eye clear. Takes a slower breath through a chest which cannot yet bear a full one. These are decisions made from what he already knows, with a body the chamber refuses to let improve beyond its retained state.

The next creature is faster.

He lets it break his landing rather than carry him into the likeness by the door. The impact empties his lungs. He reaches for a point above it and pulls himself away before its foot comes down.

At the top of the movement he sees every line he would need to finish the fight.

The light over Umaru makes its own instruction visible again.

He turns his attack back into the creature.

The room shakes.

The likeness is still there when the dust clears.

* * *

The creatures grow unfamiliar enough that he stops trying to name what he is fighting.

The faces remain familiar.

Sometimes the chamber places a loved person where the next attack must go. Sometimes it lets one approach him while he is trying to heal. Once it gives him a creature whose broken covering falls away halfway through the strike, revealing a face he has been trying not to see.

He takes the blow which follows his hesitation.

Afterwards he cannot remember whether the face was Yuta's or Rimuru's. That frightens him more than the wound.

He repeats their names quietly while the repair works.

Tries to give each one a place which belongs outside.

Rimuru on the cushion, hiding a plate.

Yuta smiling over a practice sword.

Yuka taking the shirt out of the wrong bag.

He gets through that list.

The room begins again.

* * *

Shoko's likeness asks him where it hurts.

He answers before he remembers.

She lets him lie down on the examination table. The surface is almost comfortable. He closes his eyes, feels the familiar placement of a hand beside an injury and waits for the small instruction which usually follows.

“You keep making this longer,” she says. ˢᵒ

His eyes open.

He tries to sit up.

The table holds him.

He says her name once, then stops giving the room that much of the real person. He looks past her shoulder while she begins hurting him with the same care the real Shoko uses to avoid it.

The familiar voice asks whether he will finish the condition now.

He shakes his head.

When the voice asks again, the movement is smaller.

* * *

He discovers that asking a question can delay an attack.

Some of the figures answer. Some wait until he has finished speaking. The pause is uneven and sometimes the chamber takes it away as soon as it recognises what he is doing.

He uses it while it lasts.

With Frieren's likeness, he asks what the spell does.

With Mai's, he asks what she was making.

With Gojo's, he asks whether they can start farther apart.

He no longer believes the answer will become a way out. He wants the time between asking and receiving it. Wants his breathing to settle before the next thing happens.

Once he gets long enough to sleep.

He wakes to the attack, but the moment of sleep remains a thing he had.

He begins trying to get it again.

* * *

The return state does not clear what he remembers.

Noise finds that distinction after following the same damaged paths too many times to mistake it for an accident. The chamber restores the held body. It carries forward the experience which makes the next presentation useful. Somewhere, the new hurt has to remain attached to the same person.

That attachment is smaller than the great reference Ashborn offered.

It is also the part which matches.

Noise follows it past the surface where the room has been pretending that every sound comes from someone Mau knows.

On the far side is another obstruction.

It keeps working.

* * *

Mau wakes sitting on the floor.

Yuka stands several steps away. The little distance is enough that he can think before she reaches him.

“Can we talk?” he asks. ᴹ

She stops.

He knows it will not last. His shoulders lower anyway.

“What about?” ʸ

He tries to find something the room cannot turn into the instruction.

For a moment all he can remember is her asleep beside him, breathing against his chest while the evening continued outside their door.

“Anything,” he says. “Please. Just for a little while.” ᴹ
