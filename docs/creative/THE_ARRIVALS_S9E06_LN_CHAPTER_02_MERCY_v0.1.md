*When: Yuka asks what he wants for breakfast, Mau begins to cry before he can answer.*

It is a small sound. He tries to swallow it and fails. He is sitting at their little table, the one in the apartment, with a clean cup beneath his hands. Light comes through the window he asked to face east.

He knows what the room is.

For a few seconds he lets himself look anyway.

The likeness waits by the kitchen corner.

“Anything?” she asks. ʸ

He looks down at the cup.

“I don't remember what we had.” ᴹ

“When?” ʸ

“Before I left.” ᴹ

The face is gentle. That has become one of the things he is most afraid of.

He tries to remember Shizue's hands, the thing she set down, Rimuru telling him he had picked up the wrong bag. The words remain. The taste has gone somewhere he cannot reach.

“You can have something else,” the likeness says. ʸ

Mau wipes his face with the heel of his hand.

“Okay.” ᴹ

* * *

She puts a bowl down.

He stares at it for so long that she sits opposite him. There is no weapon in either of her hands. He has learnt that this can still mean the pain will begin somewhere else.

He takes a mouthful before it does.

Warmth reaches his throat.

He nearly cries again.

“Slowly,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

For a little while the chamber allows him to eat.

He keeps his attention on the spoon because looking directly at her makes it too difficult to continue. She talks about a shelf. The position of a chair. The ordinary problems he had wanted to keep having with her.

He answers carefully, trying to make each answer long enough that she might ask another question.

The likeness smiles.

“You could be doing this with me.” ʸ

His spoon stops.

The light appears beside the window.

He puts the spoon down before his hand can begin shaking around it.

* * *

“Do you understand?” she asks. ʸ

He looks at her face.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“Then tell me.” ʸ

He has been made to say versions of the answer before. He gives it because sometimes speaking buys him time.

“I destroy the people holding the exit. They aren't the people outside. You say nothing happens to them. I finish the set and you let me go.” ᴹ

“And you want to go.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

She reaches across the table, turning his hand so the palm faces up.

“Then why won't you?” ʸ

He looks at the place where she has put the knife.

There are answers he once would have tried to make her understand. What it means to ask him to practise that movement through every person waiting at home. How a face can be false and the experience of putting a blade through it still belong to him afterwards.

He cannot gather the whole thought now.

“I can't,” he says. ᴹ

The likeness's fingers close over his.

“You can.” ʸ

He lifts his eyes to hers.

“Then I won't.” ᴹ

* * *

He remembers something the real Yuka did not let the room have.

The laugh after he promised he had packed a shirt. Her palm against his cheek when he said he was afraid. She had not answered by explaining what he was capable of. She had told him to come home so they could think about what the others said together.

He tries to keep that conversation in its own place.

The likeness hurts him while he does.

He asks it to wait once. When it doesn't, he keeps the real conversation running through his thoughts without speaking it aloud.

He wants to see her.

That remains true when he cannot keep the rest of the sentence together.

* * *

Gojo is quicker in one of the rooms.

Mau wakes, sees the familiar shape and braces for the first approach. The likeness turns its hand and ends the scenario before he has to spend long trying to protect a broken part of himself.

Darkness.

Then the next waking.

He lies still for a moment, surprised by how fiercely grateful he is for the difference.

It becomes something else he remembers.

When Gojo comes again, he tries asking for the same thing.

The likeness studies him.

“You could avoid it altogether,” it says. ᴳ

Mau closes his eyes.

The room does not give him the mercy twice in succession.

* * *

After that he stops trying to guess which face will answer kindly.

He asks anyway.

Sometimes the pause is long enough to get air into his lungs. Sometimes a familiar hand rests against his hair before becoming the source of the next injury. Once, he laughs at something said in a gentle voice and feels the pain begin while the laugh is still leaving him.

He does not lose the knowledge of where he wants to go.

He loses the distance between wanting and imagining he can reach it.

Home becomes the place he will ask about later, when he has enough rest to think.

For now he asks for the chair.

For water.

For another minute before they begin.

* * *

At the next handover, something fails to settle around him in the old way.

He feels the beginning of a restoration and the familiar pull toward the retained body. He sets himself to wake before the first strike. There is usually a small advantage in being ready for the face.

The pull changes direction.

His shoulder catches against a surface which is not part of the room. Pain goes through his side without finishing the passage into darkness.

He tries to curl around it.

There is no floor beneath his knee.

For a moment he thinks the chamber has discovered a new way to begin.

He draws the next breath as carefully as he can and waits for somebody he knows to arrive.
