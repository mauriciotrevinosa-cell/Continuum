*When: Yuka comes back for breakfast, Mau has put two cups on the little table.*

She notices before he says anything.

“Did you sleep?” ʸ

“Some.” ᴹ

“Better?” ʸ

He considers the answer instead of giving her the one which would end the question fastest.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She sits down.

They eat slowly. He asks whether Maki made her feel as though sleeping in the sitting room had become a formal inspection of his character. Yuka says that Mai supplied most of the commentary and that he should be grateful she did not bring a written account.

He smiles into his cup.

Before she leaves to speak to the others, he catches her hand.

“You can tell them the part about the copies. Why I might need a moment. I don't want everybody guessing.” ᴹ

“How much?” ʸ

“Enough to understand. I don't want to show it.” ᴹ

She nods.

“Then that's what I'll tell them.” ʸ

* * *

There is no empty chair placed at the middle of the discussion.

Mau is sleeping in his own room. The people at the common table have come because Yuka asked them to hear what he gave permission to explain. Others will receive the practical part later, without the account becoming a story told to every person who wants to know why he missed breakfast.

Yuka tells them the condition.

The room stays quiet when she finishes.

Gojo looks down at his hands. Shoko notices and sets her cup nearer his without turning toward him.

“He understood they weren't us,” Yuka says. “He still couldn't do it.” ʸ

Rem presses her fingers together in her lap.

“So he stayed?” ᴿᵉ

Yuka nods.

Maki's jaw tightens.

“They made staying the price of saying no.” ᴹᵏ

Nobody mistakes the distinction for an attempt to make what happened smaller.

* * *

“He asked for space,” Yuka says after a while. ʸ

Mai looks at her.

“From you?” ᴹᵃ

“To sleep. He told me before he tried pretending everything was fine.” ʸ

She hears the ache in her own voice and allows it to remain part of the answer.

“It hurt,” she says. “And I was glad he asked. Both.” ʸ

Shoko nods.

Gojo looks up.

“He knew me after Ciel reached him,” he says. “Before that, he was trying to make another version of me give him a rest.” ᴳ

He stops there.

The particular words Mau used remain with the people who heard them. He has no wish to turn them into evidence which must be repeated until everyone is frightened enough to care.

Frieren rests a hand against her cup.

“Then let him have a rest which doesn't require an explanation every time.” ᶠʳ

* * *

They discuss the ordinary things which could become difficult by accident.

Opening the bedroom door while he sleeps. A training invitation made too soon. A whole table going silent because he has entered the room. Everybody assuring him that he is welcome until the welcome begins sounding like something rehearsed for his benefit.

Bocchi glances at Yuka.

“I was going to ask him to listen to something.” ᴮᵒ

“Ask him,” Yuka says. “Let him say when.” ʸ

“And if he says not today?” ᴮᵒ

“Then tomorrow is still allowed to exist,” Rem answers. ᴿᵉ

Bocchi looks toward her.

Rem smiles a little, as if she recognises the question from somewhere other than this table.

* * *

Umaru arrives while they are deciding what the children need to hear.

She has already been told that Mau came back hurt, that he is being cared for and that visiting needs to wait until he chooses it. She looks at the adults' faces and pulls out a chair instead of demanding the part they have kept private.

“Can he still be invited to things?” ᵁ

Yuka lets out a small breath.

“Yes.” ʸ

“Without everybody staring if he doesn't come?” ᵁ

“That would help,” Shoko says. ˢᵒ

Umaru nods with the satisfaction of someone given a useful rule.

“Then I have something.” ᵁ

She keeps the idea to herself when Mai asks what it is. For once, the refusal is gentle enough that nobody tries to argue her out of it.

* * *

Rimuru comes late, after Mau has settled.

He tells them the outside record is being kept: the assurances, the first recall, the false display. They will seek answers through a channel which does not require putting Mau back in the room with the people who did this.

The existing arrangements for DIO and the protected ground are not being surrendered because the assessment became an abuse of them.

“What does he have to do?” Jinshi asks. ᴶ

“Recover,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Maki nods.

For now, that is enough work to give him.

* * *

When Yuka returns, the bedroom door is still a little open.

Mau is awake. He looks toward her, sees the familiar shape in the doorway and takes a moment before his shoulders settle.

She waits outside until he lifts his hand.

Then goes in.

“How bad?” he asks. ᴹ

“They love you. They have opinions. Umaru is planning something.” ʸ

He closes his eyes briefly.

“So fairly normal.” ᴹ

Yuka sits beside the bed.

“Fairly normal.” ʸ
