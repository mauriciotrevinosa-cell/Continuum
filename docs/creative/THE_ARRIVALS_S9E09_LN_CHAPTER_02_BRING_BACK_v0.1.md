*When: Noise comes to the shared room, it stops just short of the chair beside Mau.*

He has asked to see it.

DIO is already at the table. Ashborn arrives after saying he is willing to hear the account. Ciel stays close to Mau's attention without borrowing a shape to make her presence easier to name.

For a moment nobody speaks.

Then Mau holds out his hand.

Noise looks at it.

“Here?” ᴺ

“Here.” ᴹ

The dark shape approaches slowly enough that Mau understands it has noticed him flinch at arrivals.

He wants to tell it that it does not have to learn another way to be careful around him. The thought catches before he says it. Being careful is something a friend can choose.

He leaves his hand where it is.

* * *

“You brought me back,” he says. ᴹ

Noise settles beside the offered hand.

“Asked.” ᴺ

He remembers the conversation before leaving. How easily he gave permission for a future in which he would need to be brought home. How little he understood the value of someone being allowed to act when he could no longer organise the need himself.

“I didn't help you,” he says. ᴹ

“Stayed.” ᴺ

He looks toward it.

The shape makes a small motion, searching for the rest.

“Still you.” ᴺ

Ciel is quiet for a moment before helping.

*The chamber kept returning the body. It carried your experience forward because it wanted your choices to change. Noise followed the part which continued to belong to you.* ᶜⁱ

Mau lowers his gaze to his hand.

The old fingers are there. The body which has been used as the beginning of so many new injuries is, for this moment, sitting safely in his own room outside.

He lets himself feel it.

“Thank you,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Ashborn asks what part of the reference was used.

Mau lets Noise show the bounded route they agreed it could examine: the continuing relation between the bearer and the power which recognises him. The attempt to address that bearer outside the chamber's next presentation. The brief handover in which Noise redirected the return.

No private history opens with it.

Ashborn considers the work for a long time.

“You did not enter what I kept closed,” he says at last. ᴬˢ

Noise waits.

“No,” Mau answers. “It used what you offered.” ᴹ

Ashborn looks at him, then at the dark shape beside him.

“Then I am glad I offered it.” ᴬˢ

Mau has to look away for a moment.

There are ways to help someone which do not ask that someone to become the thing being used. He would like to remember this one as carefully as he remembers the others.

* * *

DIO has not interrupted.

When he looks toward her, she is watching his hand rather than the rescue work. Her own are folded together tightly enough to make the knuckles pale.

“You told me not to go,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴰⁱ

“You don't have to say you should have said it better.” ᴹ

She lifts her eyes.

“I wasn't going to.” ᴰⁱ

He is surprised enough that a little breath almost becomes a laugh.

DIO's expression does not soften away from what she means.

“I was going to say I am sorry I once gave anyone a reason to describe returning you alive as sufficient.” ᴰⁱ

The room is quiet.

“I can't undo my part by recognising theirs,” she says. “I can recognise it.” ᴰⁱ

He nods slowly.

“They said it was for you.” ᴹ

“They said many things.” ᴰⁱ

He looks at her.

For a moment she sounds like someone who has become very tired of hearing her name used to decide what should happen to a body which is not hers.

He understands that feeling.

* * *

“I said yes,” Mau says. ᴹ

Ciel answers before he can make the sentence carry the entire event.

*You asked to leave when the condition you set failed. So did the people outside.* ᶜⁱ

He knows.

He also remembers saying yes. Both facts remain stubbornly real.

DIO leans forward.

“You can reconsider why you accepted. You can be angry with yourself. You can also hold them to what they promised. They don't get your permission for one thing and call it permission for everything they do afterwards.” ᴰⁱ

He listens until she has finished.

Then lets the words stay without arguing that his own involvement must make them less true.

* * *

They decide what to do with the work which brought him out.

The rescue route is not left running through his ordinary senses. It was made to cross a particular obstruction which no longer holds him. Ciel and Noise keep a bounded account, return the guest filters to their chosen state and leave the unfinished larger projects where they were.

Ashborn checks the part which concerns his own space. DIO checks hers.

Noise waits until both have answered.

Mau sees it waiting and feels something warm enough to hurt behind his ribs.

“Friends,” Noise says, looking between them. ᴺ

“Yes,” DIO answers. “Friends who can say what they need.” ᴰⁱ

Noise turns toward Mau.

“Need?” ᴺ

He thinks about it.

For once there is no useful construction he wants to put in place of the answer.

“Rest,” he says. “And you close enough that I can ask for company.” ᴹ

Noise settles again.

“Here.” ᴺ

* * *

Outside, Yuka is reading beside the window when his attention returns.

She looks up before putting the page aside.

“How did it go?” ʸ

He moves his hand toward hers.

“They stayed.” ᴹ

She takes the hand.

“Good.” ʸ

He nods, leaning back against the pillow.

After a while she goes back to the book. He listens to the page turn, to an ordinary movement in the next room, to Ciel nearby without asking anything of him.

When he sleeps, he has not finished being grateful.
