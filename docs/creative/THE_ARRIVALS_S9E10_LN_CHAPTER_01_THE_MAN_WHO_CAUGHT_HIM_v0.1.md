*When: Mau says Makima's name, Rimuru puts his cup down without drinking.*

Yuka is beside Mau on the sofa. Gojo has taken a chair where Mau can look toward him without having to turn his injured side. Maki and Yuta sit opposite. Shoko has stayed after the morning check because Mau asked whether she would listen too.

He has chosen this much of the account.

“She appeared later,” he says. “After it had already been happening for a while. I don't know how long.” ᴹ

Rimuru waits.

“She said she'd found their assessment. That it was useful. The gate changed to the same pattern she used when she took me the first time.” ᴹ

Yuka's hand rests beside his, close without holding it still.

He looks down at the small gap, then takes her hand himself.

“She did the speech from the market. Exactly.” ᴹ

* * *

Maki asks whether he saw her enter.

He shakes his head.

“I woke up and she was there.” ᴹ

He tries to place that waking among the others. His hand tightens around Yuka's before he notices.

“I was counting in hundreds at first,” he says. “I'd lost which group I was in before she appeared. After that I stopped trusting the number.” ᴹ

Gojo looks toward Shoko. She keeps her attention on Mau, letting the sentence reach her without making him carry her reaction as well.

“There were more after her?” Maki asks. ᴹᵏ

Mau nods.

“I don't know how many.” ᴹ

Yuka shifts her hand until he no longer has to hold it so tightly to keep the contact. She does not ask him to count again.

“Did anything change outside when she appeared?” Yuta asks Rimuru. ʸᵘ

“We didn't have a live picture. Ciel was holding the underlying connection, not watching the rooms.” ᴿ

Mau feels the first little pressure of being asked to prove something he knows with his whole exhausted body.

Rimuru sees it reach his face.

“I believe that's what you experienced,” he says. “I want the record to say exactly what we know and what they made you see. They were already using appearances.” ᴿ

“It was her.” ᴹ

Rimuru holds his gaze.

“Then we find evidence which doesn't come from a place built to make you believe things.” ᴿ

Mau looks away.

He is angry for a moment. Not because the sentence is cruel. Because it is reasonable, and reasonable sentences cannot take the woman at the table out of his memory.

Yuka presses his hand once.

“You don't have to settle it today,” she says. ʸ

He takes a slower breath.

“Okay.” ᴹ

He still knows whose voice he heard.

* * *

The outside record remains beside his account.

The three arranged the place. They gave assurances about the recall. They left a false display and did not answer when the observers tried to bring him out. Whatever happened after that, none of those facts becomes somebody else's action merely because Mau later saw Makima.

He listens while Rimuru says it.

“I don't want to defend them,” Mau answers. ᴹ

“I know.” ᴿ

“I don't want to go anywhere today.” ᴹ

Shoko leans back a little.

“That is an excellent plan.” ˢᵒ

For the first time in the conversation, he almost smiles.

They leave the report incomplete where it is incomplete. Nobody asks Ciel to turn Mau's memories into a screening. The private content of his earlier lives stays where he chose to keep it.

* * *

Gojo stays after the others move to the sitting-room table.

Mau watches him decide whether to ask something.

“You can,” he says. ᴹ

Gojo looks up.

“You said you wanted it quick.” ᴳ

Mau's hand closes against the sofa.

He remembers.

For a second he can feel himself looking up from the ground, choosing the least dangerous version of a plea.

“I thought it was another room,” he says. ᴹ

Gojo nods slowly.

“I worked that part out.” ᴳ

Mau looks at him properly.

“I wanted to go home. I kept wanting that. I just thought you were going to hurt me first.” ᴹ

Gojo's face does something he tries to hide by looking down.

Mau waits until he looks back.

“One of them let it end quickly once. I was asking for that. I thought maybe if I asked properly—” ᴹ

He stops.

Gojo moves his chair closer, slowly enough to leave the decision visible.

“You asked properly,” he says. ᴳ

The answer catches Mau somewhere he had not protected.

* * *

“I know you wouldn't do it,” Mau says. ᴹ

“You don't have to take care of me about that.” ᴳ

“I'm not. I'm telling you.” ᴹ

Gojo lets the distinction stay.

The room goes quiet around them, with ordinary conversation continuing a little farther away. Shizue asks someone where a cup belongs. Yuka answers from the kitchen corner. Nobody has made the apartment stop being a home because two people are having a difficult conversation in it.

Mau rubs his face.

“I missed you.” ᴹ

Gojo's mouth changes before the rest of his face catches up.

“Naturally.” ᴳ

Mau lets out a small, incredulous laugh.

Gojo's smile becomes real when he hears it.

“There he is.” ᴳ

Mau looks at him.

“I was here before I laughed.” ᴹ

Gojo goes quiet for half a beat.

Then nods.

“Yes. You were.” ᴳ

* * *

Hinata comes to say goodbye in the afternoon.

Testarossa and Souei are returning with her; their own people and work have had the messages promised at the visit's review. The inquiries they agreed with Rimuru remain arranged through contact, not permanent residence in Mau's hallway.

Hinata asks whether he wants her to tell him to behave.

“Would it help?” he asks. ᴹ

“Probably not.” ᴴⁱ

She sits for a few minutes anyway. Anko has chosen to walk the first part of the departure with her. The two speak briefly near the door with the ease of a conversation which will continue somewhere else.

Mau watches them without turning it into a reason to feel guilty that the world has kept moving.

Before leaving, Hinata touches the back of the empty chair.

“Next visit,” she says. ᴴⁱ

He nods.

He likes being offered one.
