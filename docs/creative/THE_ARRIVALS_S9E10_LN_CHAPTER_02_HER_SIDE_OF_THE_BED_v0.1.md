*When: Mau asks whether she wants to stay, Yuka is looking for the book she left in their room.*

She finds it on her side of the bed.

He has put the ribbon between the pages instead of leaving it open against the blanket. The small, careful act makes the question harder to answer lightly.

“Tonight?” she asks. ʸ

He nods.

She sits beside the book.

“Do you want me here, or are you worried I've been away too long?” ʸ

He looks at her.

For a moment she can see him trying to decide which answer will ask less of her.

“Both,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka exhales.

“Thank you for saying both.” ʸ

* * *

She has been in their home every day.

They have eaten together, shared the sofa, talked while he grew tired halfway through a sentence. On the second night she chose Maki's sitting room again after they discussed the sleep he had managed. On the third she stayed late enough that Rimuru asked whether she was planning to make the short route into a ceremonial journey before leaving.

She had thrown a cushion at him.

Mau had laughed after the cushion landed.

Those parts have been theirs too.

Still, she misses putting her head beside his without first deciding whether the movement will make him brace.

She wants him to know that much.

“I missed you in here,” she says. ʸ

His face tightens.

“I know.” ᴹ

“You can hear that without deciding asking was wrong.” ʸ

He looks down at the book.

“I'm trying.” ᴹ

“Me too.” ʸ

* * *

Yuka gets her things from Maki's herself.

It is a small bag. There has never been enough in it to pretend she moved out. Mai sees it in her hand, glances once toward Yuka's face and asks whether she is taking the spare cup back too.

“You said it was mine.” ʸ

“I said you could use it.” ᴹᵃ

“Then I'm still using it.” ʸ

Mai gives her a look which would have been more effective if she were not smiling.

Maki comes to the door.

“If either of you needs another night, say so,” she tells her. ᴹᵏ

Yuka nods.

“I know.” ʸ

She goes home along the route which has remained short enough for this to be an ordinary visit.

* * *

Mau has made room on the little table by the bed.

Yuka looks at the two cups of water.

“Very romantic.” ʸ

“Shoko has high expectations.” ᴹ

“So do I.” ʸ

She puts the borrowed cup beside his and leaves the bag open on her chair. The hoodie is back where she likes it. Her book remains on the bed until she picks it up and lies down on her own side.

Mau stays sitting for a moment.

“You can get in,” she tells him. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You're doing the thing where knowing takes a while.” ʸ

He looks at her, startled.

Then laughs softly.

“A little.” ᴹ

She lifts the edge of the blanket.

He gets in.

* * *

They begin with a little space between them.

Yuka reads aloud because he asks what is happening in the book. She stops twice to explain something which makes him object that the person in the story could have avoided the problem by asking a single obvious question.

She lowers the page.

Looks at him.

He closes his eyes.

“Don't.” ᴹ

“I haven't said anything.” ʸ

“You're saying it very loudly.” ᴹ

Yuka laughs. The sound reaches him before the old expectation of something following it.

Nothing follows except the next page.

He finds that he has moved closer while listening.

She lets him notice without celebrating it on his behalf.

* * *

He tells her about the false breakfast.

Only that much. The room, the food, the terrible relief of being allowed to sit opposite her before the condition appeared again.

Yuka closes the book on her finger.

“You don't have to give me that memory to explain why this is difficult.” ʸ

“I want you to know why the nice parts can catch me too.” ᴹ

She listens.

He tells her that he knew. That he kept knowing. Sometimes the first second of seeing her still reached his body before the knowledge did.

“I'm not waiting for you to turn into her,” he says. “I'm trying to stop arriving here from somewhere else.” ᴹ

Yuka puts the book aside.

“Then arrive,” she says. “I'll be annoyed with you about the cups when you get here.” ʸ

His face changes.

The small laugh which escapes him is wet enough that she moves closer.

* * *

He puts his hand against her cheek.

For a moment she thinks he is checking her face. Then his thumb moves, the way it did before, and she knows he is simply touching her.

“I love you,” he says. ᴹ

“I love you too.” ʸ

She leans into the hand.

“And I'm keeping my side of the bed.” ʸ

“It was never in danger.” ᴹ

“Good. I have several things here.” ʸ

He looks past her toward the hoodie, the book, the cup she borrowed from Mai as if ownership were a subject best settled by continuing to hold the object.

“I've noticed.” ᴹ

Yuka kisses him.

He answers slowly, his hand settling behind her neck. The closeness makes his breathing catch once. She draws back just far enough to see him, and he shakes his head before she can mistake it for a request to leave.

“Stay,” he says. ᴹ

She does.

* * *

They keep the promise they made about waiting until the wedding. Tonight asks for nothing beyond the closeness they already want to find again.

Yuka settles with her head near his shoulder. Mau lies awake for a while, listening to her breathing and the small sounds of the home around them.

When sleep takes him, it does not keep him for the whole night.

He wakes once with his hand already closing. Stops it against the blanket, hears Ciel say his name and looks toward Yuka until the room settles into its actual shape.

She is awake too.

“Here?” she whispers. ʸ

He takes a breath.

“Here.” ᴹ

“Water or company?” ʸ

He considers the cup.

Then her hand.

“Company.” ᴹ

She gives it to him without making him earn the return to sleep.

* * *

In the morning she finds him awake, watching the light along the edge of her face.

“What?” she asks. ʸ

“You stole the blanket.” ᴹ

Yuka looks down.

She has.

For one second she considers apologising. Then sees the small, expectant change in his mouth.

“Get better at keeping it,” she says. ʸ

He laughs and reaches for his share.
