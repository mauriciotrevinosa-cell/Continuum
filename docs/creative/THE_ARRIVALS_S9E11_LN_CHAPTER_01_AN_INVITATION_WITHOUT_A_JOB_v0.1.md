*When: Bocchi asks whether he wants to hear something, Mau asks what she needs him to carry.*

She blinks.

“Nothing.” ᴮᵒ

He looks past her toward the music-room route as if the unmentioned object might be waiting there.

“Really,” she says. “I wanted you to listen.” ᴮᵒ

Mau closes his mouth.

“Yes,” he answers, a little more quietly. “I'd like that.” ᴹ

Yuka is finishing a conversation with Shuna. She sees Bocchi waiting, hears Mau ask whether they can walk slowly and tells him where she will be afterwards.

He goes with his sister.

* * *

Bocchi has left a chair where he can see the instrument without sitting in the middle of the room.

Kita is there for the beginning. She says hello, moves one thing which would catch Mau's foot and returns to what she was doing. The ordinary lack of ceremony makes it easier to sit down.

Bocchi plays the first part twice.

The second time she stops at the same place and makes a face.

“That bit.” ᴮᵒ

“I liked that bit.” ᴹ

She looks at him with immediate suspicion.

“You don't have to.” ᴮᵒ

“I know. I liked the bit after it more.” ᴹ

That gives her a useful objection.

They spend several minutes discussing what he means, with Mau providing a description so technically inadequate that Kita starts laughing from the far side of the room.

Bocchi plays it again.

This time he listens without looking toward the door.

* * *

He grows tired before the visit is over.

For a moment he tries to hide it. Bocchi notices the effort, perhaps because she knows something about staying in a room longer than the body wants to make another person comfortable.

“We can stop.” ᴮᵒ

He looks toward her hands.

“Could you do the first part once more? Then I should go.” ᴹ

She nods.

The last attempt has a different ending. He asks whether she changed it. She says she has not decided whether to keep the change.

“Then tell me when you do.” ᴹ

The answer matters to her enough that she looks down before putting the instrument aside.

She walks him back to the covered route, where the common room is close enough to hear someone disputing a rule.

“Your next appointment,” she says. ᴮᵒ

Mau recognises Umaru's voice.

“I should have brought legal advice.” ᴹ

* * *

Umaru has acquired a game, several people and an authority over the arrangement of the table which no one remembers granting.

Rem sits beside her with a cup. Ori is explaining her previous move to Milim, who considers the rule an obstacle which ought to yield to enthusiasm. Yuka looks up when Mau appears and lifts the empty chair beside her.

He sits.

“What's my job?” he asks. ᴹ

Umaru looks offended.

“Playing.” ᵁ

“That's all?” ᴹ

“You can lose if you like. It saves time.” ᵁ

Rem hides a smile behind her cup.

Mau looks at the board.

For a moment he has to fight the strange guilt of being welcomed into an afternoon to which he has brought nothing useful. Then Yuka hands him the pieces and Umaru complains that he is taking too long to choose a side.

He chooses.

* * *

He loses the first game.

Umaru is delighted with the efficiency. Milim insists the victory should count for less because Mau has just arrived. Ori says arriving is not a rule, which leads to another discussion of whether something can become a rule simply because Milim needs it.

Mau laughs.

The sound comes easily enough that he notices only afterwards.

At the edge of the room, Beru is watching the board with an intensity usually reserved for approaching threats. When Umaru asks whether he wants to help, he looks toward Mau.

Mau nods.

Beru moves behind Yuka's chair.

“I will defend this position,” he says. ᴮ

Yuka looks up at him.

“It's a board game.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴮ

He sounds thoroughly reassured that they understand one another.

* * *

Ori leaves before the next round because Frieren has promised to read something with her.

She gathers her things, stops beside Mau and asks whether he wants the drawing she made for the little table.

“If you want me to have it.” ᴹ

She gives it to him.

Ear's ears are still much too large.

Mau smooths one fold with his thumb.

“I'll put it in our room.” ᴹ

Ori smiles and goes to find her mother. Yuka watches him keep looking at the page for a moment before placing it safely away from the cups.

She touches his knee beneath the table.

“Another round?” ʸ

He considers his tiredness.

“One. Then I need to lie down.” ᴹ

“Good,” Umaru says. “We have a schedule.” ᵁ

Rem reaches for her hand when she goes to move the wrong piece. Umaru looks down at the contact, then back at the board, suddenly less certain of the argument she was about to make.

Mau sees Yuka notice.

They keep the little smile between themselves.

* * *

When they walk home, someone asks whether Mau can help carry a crate to the workshop.

He stops automatically.

Yuka waits beside him without answering for him.

“Not today,” he says at last. ᴹ

The person nods, asks someone else and moves the crate.

Nothing falls apart in his absence.

Mau watches it reach its destination.

Then goes home with Yuka, carrying only the drawing Ori wanted him to have.
