*When: Shoko asks how he has been sleeping, Mau gives her an answer with several parts.*

Some stretches are better. He still wakes expecting an approach. He can usually recognise the room before the expectation turns into movement now. Yuka is back beside him. Once he asked her to move away for a minute, and she did, and afterwards he asked her to come close again.

Shoko listens to all of it.

“And the body?” she asks. ˢᵒ

He tells her where the last tenderness remains. Which movement tires sooner than he expects. How easy it is to mistake being able to stand for being ready to do everything he could do before entering.

She examines the things which need examining and leaves the old injuries out of any promise to make him new.

“Keep the walks short for now. Stop before you have to prove you could continue.” ˢᵒ

He nods.

“No training?” ᴹ

“You knew the answer before asking.” ˢᵒ

“I like to hear the rules.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him.

He realises what he said.

The small silence which follows is different from the ones in the chamber.

“Then ask,” she says. “I'll tell you the ones I actually mean.” ˢᵒ

He looks up at her and smiles a little.

* * *

The afternoon walk reaches the tree.

It is not far. Yuka has chosen a route with somewhere comfortable at the end and refuses to call it a medical expedition when he objects to the planning.

“I wanted to sit here,” she says. ʸ

“With the blanket.” ᴹ

“It's a very good blanket.” ʸ

They settle where the roots still have their ground. Through the open common route he can hear the Fort, somebody moving cups, the irregular beginning of a piece of music which stops when its players begin arguing about it.

Yuka leans against him.

He puts his arm around her without first arranging a safe version of the movement in his head.

When he notices, he keeps the arm there.

* * *

“Are you thinking about it?” she asks after a while. ʸ

He knows which it she means.

“A little.” ᴹ

She waits.

“I keep expecting to owe somebody an answer for getting out,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks toward the house.

“You gave us one. You wanted to come home.” ʸ

He watches her face.

“That isn't the one they asked for.” ᴹ

“I wasn't asking for theirs.” ʸ

He leans his head back against the support behind them. For a while the wind moves through the branches without making either of them speak.

At last he tells her that he is glad Noise did not wait for him to become useful to his own rescue.

Yuka's hand closes around his.

“Me too.” ʸ

* * *

Rimuru joins them carrying a cup which he claims he was bringing to himself.

Yuka accepts it.

He watches her drink, then looks at Mau for support.

Mau raises the hand she is not holding.

“I have been advised against strenuous activity.” ᴹ

“Defending me is strenuous now?” ᴿ

Yuka glances toward Rimuru.

He considers the evidence and settles beside them without taking the cup back.

For a little while the three sit together. When Mau asks whether the outside record needs anything from him, Rimuru says the words he already chose to give are enough for the next inquiries. Nobody is taking him to another clearing to make the account count.

Mau nods.

The work remains real. It also remains somewhere he does not have to sit inside it all afternoon.

* * *

He helps set out a few things for the evening meal.

Only the things near his seat. When he starts to reach for another stack, Yuka points toward the food and tells him she is not interested in watching him attempt a rematch against the dishes.

He remembers the last plate after the first dinner.

The memory belongs to a place he can reach without entering another room made to hurt him.

He sits down.

Yuta asks about the game. Mau tells him Beru has discovered that standing behind Yuka is a defensible interpretation of participation. Mai wants to know whether he won anything. He says he won the drawing, which she refuses to accept as a result of the game until Ori explains that it was a gift.

Gojo laughs at the argument.

Mau turns toward the sound.

For an instant the old expectation reaches him.

Then Gojo leans toward Shoko to ask for the dish he cannot reach, and the moment becomes this evening again.

Mau stays at the table.

* * *

Back home, Shizue is reading something from the children when they enter. Rimuru asks whether she wants tea. She says yes, then tells him she will make it if he finds the cups.

Yuka walks into their bedroom to put the drawing where Mau can see it from the bed.

He follows her.

“Not too high,” he says. ᴹ

She looks over her shoulder.

“You could help.” ʸ

“I thought I wasn't allowed to carry anything.” ᴹ

She hands him the paper.

“Try surviving this.” ʸ

He laughs and holds it while she decides where it belongs.

* * *

They sleep together.

Mau wakes once, with no face waiting above him and Yuka breathing beside his shoulder. He lies still, feels the two familiar rhythms inside himself and listens until the sounds around them become ordinary.

*Here,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

*I know.* ᴹ

After a moment he feels Noise settle, quiet and near.

He does not need a new construction to make the room safe enough to rest in. He needs the people who stayed and the chance to keep returning to them when sleep takes him somewhere else.

He closes his eyes.

* * *

Morning reaches the east window.

Yuka opens her eyes to find him awake. He is holding the edge of the blanket beneath her hand, preventing its gradual migration entirely onto her side.

“Learning?” she asks. ʸ

“Adapting.” ᴹ

She smiles.

He moves closer and kisses her before she can make the obvious joke. She answers with a hand at the back of his neck, drawing the moment out until he forgets the sentence he was preparing.

When they part, he stays near.

“Breakfast?” she asks. ʸ

He thinks about getting up. About the small table, the cups, the people beyond their door who have their own mornings to begin.

“In a minute.” ᴹ

Yuka settles against him again.

This time the minute belongs to them.
