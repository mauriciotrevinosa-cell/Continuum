*When: Yuka finally gets her share of the blanket back, she asks him a question he has been waiting for someone else to ask.*

“When do you want to marry me?” ʸ

Mau looks at her.

She is still beside him, hair caught beneath one shoulder, watching him with the expression she wears when she already knows there is an answer and intends to wait until he gives it.

“Today would be difficult,” he says. ᴹ

“For you. I know where my clothes are.” ʸ

“So do I.” ᴹ

“You knew where a shirt was yesterday. We shouldn't get ambitious.” ʸ

He starts laughing. She waits until it settles, then touches the hand he has left on the blanket.

“Really.” ʸ

The word makes the room quieter without making it frightening.

He has been thinking about the unfinished page. About a date which seemed easier to leave blank while his body still needed instructions about how far to walk. There was always another sensible thing to wait for. Some of them were genuinely sensible. Others had begun to resemble an argument in which he would never have to admit he wanted something before knowing he could protect it.

“Soon,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka smiles.

“That's a direction. I'm asking for a day.” ʸ

* * *

They bring breakfast back to their little table.

Rimuru is already awake, trying to persuade Shizue that choosing which food to bring from the common room is a contribution comparable to carrying it. Shizue asks him to choose the cups too. He goes to find them before the comparison can be examined further.

Mau sits beside Yuka and opens the wedding page.

The route is there. The place beneath the branches. A note in somebody's handwriting which appears to question the Fort's proposed jurisdiction over the whole ceremony.

He reads that note twice.

Yuka watches his mouth change.

“We settled it.” ʸ

“Did we?” ᴹ

“We can marry without paying a toll in sweets.” ʸ

“An important protection.” ᴹ

She draws the page closer.

“Eleven days.” ʸ

He looks up.

“You have been thinking about a day.” ᴹ

“Several. I wanted to know whether you wanted one too.” ʸ

He turns his hand beneath hers.

“I do.” ᴹ

* * *

She tells him what still needs doing.

Some of it is practical. People to ask. Clothes which need a final fitting with the person actually wearing them. A piece of music Bocchi has been allowed to finish without everybody coming to hear whether it is finished yet.

Some of it belongs to the two of them.

“What do you want to say?” she asks. ʸ

Mau looks down at the page.

“I haven't found the words.” ᴹ

“Good. Neither have I.” ʸ

“That's reassuring.” ᴹ

She smiles and puts her head against his shoulder for a moment.

“I don't want a speech about everything you survived to get here,” she says. “I know some of it. You can tell me more when you want to. That day, I want to hear about us.” ʸ

He stays still beneath the small weight of her.

The request gives him something he can picture. Waking with her. Losing an argument about a cup. Following her through a market while she decides something he will be happy to carry.

“I can talk about us.” ᴹ

She lifts her head.

“You do quite a lot of it.” ʸ

“Mostly because you keep doing things.” ᴹ

She kisses him before he can improve the explanation.

* * *

Shoko hears the proposed date at the morning check.

Mau gives her the idea in the careful voice of someone submitting a complicated piece of work for inspection. Yuka sits beside him, listening until he has managed to make a short walk and a meal sound like an expedition.

Shoko looks between them.

“Are either of you planning to fight at this wedding?” ˢᵒ

“No,” Yuka answers immediately. ʸ

Mau opens his mouth.

Yuka turns toward him.

“No.” ʸ

“I was going to agree.” ᴹ

“Excellent.” ʸ

Shoko waits for their argument to become quiet enough to finish.

“Then keep doing what is helping. Sleep. Eat. Tell me when something changes. We check you again before the day. If you need to sit, you sit.” ˢᵒ

Mau looks at her.

“That's all?” ᴹ

“For getting married? I was hoping you two had discussed the rest.” ˢᵒ

Yuka laughs first.

Mau follows, the relief arriving somewhere inside it.

* * *

Outside, Yuka takes the page back from him.

“Do you want eleven days?” she asks. ʸ

He looks toward the tree. Someone is walking beneath the gallery with a basket, talking to a person Mau cannot see from here. Beyond them, the ordinary noises of the house continue without waiting to learn whether he has become certain of everything.

He thinks of the first morning after the trial when holding a cup was enough work for his hands.

Today he is standing beside the woman he wants to marry.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Yuka writes the date.

He watches until her hand stops moving.

For a second neither speaks.

Then she gives him the page so he can read the part which has changed.

* * *

Rimuru is halfway through explaining something to Jinshi when the two of them find him.

Mau waits for the explanation to end. Yuka waits for Mau to begin. Rimuru notices both kinds of waiting and looks immediately suspicious.

“What did you agree to?” ᴿ

“A wedding,” Mau says. ᴹ

“That part I remember.” ᴿ

Yuka shows him the page.

His expression changes.

“Oh.” ᴿ

She smiles.

“We wanted to ask you something.” ʸ

Rimuru looks from her to Mau.

“The ceremony,” Mau says. “The bit where somebody stands with us and helps everybody stop talking long enough for us to do it.” ᴹ

“That's a demanding role.” ᴿ

“We know your experience with difficult people,” Yuka says. ʸ

Rimuru looks at Mau for assistance and finds him smiling in exactly the wrong way.

“You want me to marry you?” ᴿ

“Help us get married,” Mau corrects. “I already picked her.” ᴹ

Jinshi makes a sound which he disguises as a cough.

Rimuru ignores him with considerable dignity.

“You are both terrible.” ᴿ

Yuka takes Mau's hand.

“Will you?” ʸ

Rimuru looks down at the date again.

When he answers, the teasing has gone quiet without disappearing.

“Yes,” he says. “I'd like to.” ᴿ

Mau's smile changes.

Rimuru reaches for the page and begins asking what the two of them actually want him to say.
