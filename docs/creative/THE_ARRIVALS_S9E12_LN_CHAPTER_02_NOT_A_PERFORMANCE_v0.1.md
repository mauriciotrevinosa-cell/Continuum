*When: Bocchi hears the date, the first thing she says is, “I can finish it.”*

Mau looks at the pages in her hands.

“I came to ask whether you want to.” ᴹ

She blinks at him.

The answer she prepared has nowhere useful to go.

Kita, leaning against the door beside her, looks down at the pages too.

“She wants to,” she says. “She also wants it to stop changing its mind in the middle.” ᴷⁱ

Bocchi turns toward her in betrayal.

Kita smiles.

Mau asks whether he can come in.

* * *

He listens to the whole piece once.

Bocchi has played parts for him before. This time she carries the first idea into the next without stopping to apologise for the place where her hands have not yet decided what her thoughts mean.

The music has room in it.

He can imagine walking slowly with Yuka while it continues. Can imagine catching someone's eye along the route and being allowed to smile before reaching the next part.

When the last note ends, Bocchi looks at him and then immediately down.

“There should be another part,” she says. ᴮᵒ

Mau has to return to words.

“Why?” ᴹ

She touches the edge of the page.

“Because it's a wedding.” ᴮᵒ

He considers that.

Then asks her to play the ending again.

* * *

The second ending is quieter.

When she stops, Mau looks toward Kita, who has been watching Bocchi rather than waiting for the correct response to the music.

He looks back.

“I like where it leaves us,” he says. ᴹ

Bocchi presses one finger against the written line.

“There?” ᴮᵒ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“But it doesn't really announce anything.” ᴮᵒ

“We'll be standing there. I think people will work it out.” ᴹ

Kita laughs softly.

Bocchi looks at him for long enough that he can see the question behind the other questions.

“You don't have to make it bigger because it's for me,” he says. ᴹ

Her shoulders move with a breath she has been holding.

* * *

Nijika comes in while they are talking about where the next person can join the sound.

Ryo follows with a suggestion which is useful enough that nobody can make the joke they had prepared about her arriving after the work began. She appears pleased with the advantage and immediately offers another.

Mau listens until the discussion belongs entirely to the four of them.

Then stands, slowly enough to spare himself the little pull Shoko asked him to report if it worsens.

Bocchi notices.

“You can stay sitting.” ᴮᵒ

“I know. You don't need an audience to argue about it.” ᴹ

“We could charge you,” Ryo says. ᴿʸ

“Family rate?” ᴹ

She considers him seriously.

“More complicated emotionally. More expensive.” ᴿʸ

Nijika tells her to stop negotiating with the groom.

Mau leaves them laughing.

* * *

Bocchi catches him in the passage before he reaches the common turn.

She has left the pages inside.

For a moment he thinks she has another musical question. Then she looks toward the door of the empty family space beside her own.

“I wanted them to see it,” she says. ᴮᵒ

He follows her gaze.

The parents and sister she hopes to find remain absent. Neither of them has acquired a reason to pretend otherwise because a wedding has a date.

“I know.” ᴹ

“I'm happy,” she says quickly. “I really am.” ᴮᵒ

“I know that too.” ᴹ

She breathes out.

He stays beside her until she stops trying to decide which feeling she is allowed to keep.

* * *

“You can tell me when you miss them,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Even this week?” ᴮᵒ

He looks at her.

“Especially this week, if that's when you miss them.” ᴹ

She nods, a little unsteadily.

He opens his arm without making the next part an instruction.

Bocchi steps close and holds him carefully. He rests his hand against her back, the familiar relation between them returning without asking whether either has earned it by being cheerful enough.

“I'm glad you're here,” he says. ᴹ

Her answer is small against his shirt.

“Me too.” ᴮᵒ

* * *

Kita is waiting when Bocchi comes back.

She does not ask for the conversation. She takes Bocchi's hand and gives her the little space beside her which has become familiar through a hundred ordinary choices.

“The ending?” she asks. ᴷⁱ

Bocchi looks at the page.

Then at her.

“I think I like it.” ᴮᵒ

Kita smiles.

“Then let me hear it again.” ᴷⁱ

* * *

At home, Yuka is waiting with the two cups Mau has been learning to finish while they are still warm.

“Did you hear it?” she asks. ʸ

He sits beside her.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She studies his face.

“That good?” ʸ

He looks toward the door as a few notes reach the passage beyond it, soft enough to belong to someone practising in another part of the house.

“I could see us walking.” ᴹ

Yuka puts her cup down.

She takes his hand and pulls him up for two slow steps across their own room, ending close enough that he has to look down to see her smile.

“Like that?” ʸ

He holds her waist.

“A little more elegantly.” ᴹ

She pushes lightly against his chest.

He laughs and kisses her before she can improve her objection.
