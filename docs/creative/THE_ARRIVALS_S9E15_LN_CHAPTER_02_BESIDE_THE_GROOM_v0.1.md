*When: Mau asks Gojo to walk with him, Gojo looks toward the door as if the request must have left something important outside.*

“Now?” ᴳ

“At the wedding.” ᴹ

Gojo looks back.

Mau has rehearsed enough complicated versions of the question to discover that saying it simply is harder than any of them.

“The part before we join everyone. I asked Rimuru to do the ceremony. I wanted you beside me when we get there.” ᴹ

Gojo sits back a little.

For once, he does not answer at the first available opening.

“You realise how good I'll look.” ᴳ

Mau lets the breath out in a laugh.

“I've accepted the risk.” ᴹ

“Brave.” ᴳ

The smile stays on Gojo's face. Something behind it has become quieter.

“Yes,” he says. “Of course.” ᴳ

* * *

Shoko comes in while Gojo is explaining that the wedding morning needs competent supervision.

She looks at Mau.

“Find some.” ˢᵒ

Mau laughs so quickly that Gojo turns toward him in offence.

“You asked me.” ᴳ

“I'm still happy with the choice.” ᴹ

Shoko puts the small thing she came for in her pocket and looks at Gojo's face again. She sees enough there to stop making the next joke.

“You should,” she says. ˢᵒ

Gojo looks up at her.

She lets the answer remain ordinary and goes back toward their shared sitting room.

Mau suddenly feels he has been allowed to see something without being made responsible for naming it.

He is grateful enough to leave that part alone.

* * *

They talk about the morning itself.

Mau does not want to spend it hidden away while people decide what a groom ought to be feeling. He would like company, help finding the things he has managed to put in the wrong bag and somebody capable of telling him when his attempt to be calm has become annoying.

Gojo appears delighted with the final responsibility.

“Yuta can help,” he says. ᴳ

“He's walking with Yuka and Maki.” ᴹ

“He can enjoy your problems first.” ᴳ

Mau smiles.

“I was going to ask him.” ᴹ

“Then ask.” ᴳ

The little sentence sounds different from the way Mau used to hear instructions about being ready. He takes it home with him.

* * *

Rimuru is waiting with Yuka at the table when he returns.

There is a page between them which neither looks guilty enough to be hiding properly.

“Have I done something?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Several things,” Yuka says. “This is about afterwards.” ʸ

Rimuru shows him the offer.

The island. Five nights. Meals, room, the ordinary trips available from the harbour. Enough detail that the gift has become something they can picture using instead of a promise somebody made because it sounded generous in a conversation.

Mau reads it twice.

Then looks up.

“Five nights?” ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

“From Elmesia. She wanted to know whether you'd like it before confirming the stay.” ᴿ

Yuka is watching Mau rather than the page.

“Would you?” she asks. ʸ

* * *

He pictures the water first.

Then the unfamiliarity of a place where he and Yuka can wake up without somebody outside already knowing what they might be useful for that morning.

He looks toward their own bedroom door.

This is the home he wanted. Wanting to come back to it does not mean he has to refuse every chance to leave it happily.

“Yes,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka's smile answers before the word reaches Rimuru.

“Me too.” ʸ

Rimuru turns the page and explains the rest of the idea. The house has put something aside for ordinary travel. They can take days before the island and days after it, choose stops along the checked route and let Kaisel make the distance something they can enjoy.

“Two weeks?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“About that. If you want.” ᴿ

She takes the page.

Mau watches her begin imagining the places as something she will get to do rather than something she will hear happened elsewhere.

“We want,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

They thank people in person when the ordinary day brings them past.

The gift has enough contributors that trying to make one large speech would require inventing another gathering before the wedding. Mau catches Shuna near the common route. Yuka finds Shion and asks about one of the stops she suggested. Shizue accepts their thanks and tells them that doing nothing for an afternoon will also count as using the gift correctly.

Mau promises to consider the difficult assignment.

That evening, Yuka spreads the route beside the smaller wedding page and points to the first place she wants to stop.

He asks why.

She starts telling him.

He listens until the idea has become something he wants too.
