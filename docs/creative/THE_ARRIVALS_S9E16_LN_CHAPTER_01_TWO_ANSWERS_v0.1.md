She waits.

He closes his mouth.

Marin turns away, smiling. Wakana pretends to be entirely occupied with the little measurement in his hand.

“Two bands,” Mai says. “One at a time. The shape we agreed. If something feels wrong, say so.” ᴹᵃ

“I will.” ᴹ

“Before telling me it isn't important.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks at Mau.

He puts both hands where everyone can see his surrender.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Mai begins.

* * *

The cost reaches him in the place he knows to expect it.

It is small beside what he could spend on a fight. That does not make it nothing. He follows the demand while Mai works, lets Ciel keep the comparison she asked to keep and answers honestly when Yuka asks whether he feels it.

“Yes.” ᴹ

Mai glances up.

“Pain?” ᴹᵃ

“No. A little weight. Mostly the energy moving.” ᴹ

She nods and finishes the first band before beginning the second.

The work in her hands is steadier than it used to be when every small object required spending herself against the limit of a body which could not afford it. She still makes the object. Mau does not lend her the judgement which tells her where its edge should sit.

When she puts the second band down, nobody touches it until she says they can.

* * *

Yuka tries hers first.

She moves her fingers, takes it off, puts it on again and looks toward Wakana when one position catches more than the other. He checks the fit and asks Mai whether she wants the adjustment marked before she makes it.

They work on that part together.

Mau has never thought so carefully about the ordinary experience of wearing something all day. Watching them do it makes him understand why Yuka asked for this ring to begin with.

He tries his own.

The band is plain enough that he can imagine forgetting it is there until the moment he wants to touch it.

Yuka watches his face.

“Good?” ʸ

He nods.

“Very.” ᴹ

* * *

The little function is tested after the fit, separately from it.

Mau and Yuka have already let Ciel and Noise examine the existing connection for this one purpose. The work does not open a guest's memory or borrow another person's body. It gives an ordinary, chosen request a physical place at which to begin.

The ring is not expected to answer for the person wearing it.

That is the part Ciel makes them test first.

Yuka touches hers with a little of her own intent.

Mau feels the familiar request arrive through the route they already share, quieter and simpler than a spoken message. He waits, then chooses to answer.

A small warmth touches Yuka's finger.

She looks down.

For a moment she simply smiles.

“Again,” she says. ʸ

* * *

The second time, he does not answer.

Yuka feels nothing beyond the ordinary ring.

They let the silence stay until it has become clear it will not turn into a warning, a demand or another system supplying the response Mau chose not to give.

“It isn't a way to know whether I'm safe,” he says. ᴹ

Yuka looks up.

“I remember.” ʸ

“It can be unanswered because I'm busy. Or because the connection can't carry it.” ᴹ

“Or because you want a minute.” ʸ

He nods.

She tests that possibility herself, choosing not to answer his request and grinning when he realises what she has done.

“A powerful tool,” he says. ᴹ

Mai leans back.

“You've invented waiting for someone to reply.” ᴹᵃ

Wakana laughs first.

* * *

They try the small signal along the nearby passage, then stop before a pleasant result becomes an excuse to discover every distance at which it might fail.

Ciel keeps the limits they observed. Noise leaves the work at the scope it accepted. There is no new private voice inside either band, no unseen person listening to every request to decide whether the wearer deserves a reply.

The answer belongs to the wearer.

Yuka returns both rings to Mai so she can check the finished edges one last time. Then they go into the small box prepared for the ceremony.

Mau looks at the empty place on his hand.

Five more days.

* * *

On the way home, Yuka takes his hand without using any signal at all.

“You can still do that,” he says. ᴹ

“I intend to.” ʸ

They walk slowly enough that somebody with an ordinary basket passes them on the route. Mau watches the person go and resists the old urge to make the walk useful by carrying something else.

Yuka sees the thought arrive.

“You're carrying me emotionally,” she says. “Very demanding.” ʸ

He turns toward her.

She keeps her face serious for almost a second.

Mau laughs and draws her close enough to kiss her hair.

“I'll report the strain at my check.” ᴹ

“Please do. I expect proper care of my husband.” ʸ

The word stays with him for the rest of the walk.
