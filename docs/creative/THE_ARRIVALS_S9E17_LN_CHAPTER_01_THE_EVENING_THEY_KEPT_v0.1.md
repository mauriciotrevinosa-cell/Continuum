*When: Mau asks whether they would like to be there, DIO asks which there he means.*

He is glad she asks.

The room inside him has become familiar enough that the first easy answer can hide several different invitations. A place at the ceremony. A view from the shared room. The chance to hear without being watched by people who do not know whom they are looking at.

He tells them which parts he can offer.

Ashborn listens from the place beside his own door. Noise has come to the table without deciding everyone else ought to sit because it has. Ciel is with Mau, waiting for the answer before making the arrangement simpler than the people inside it.

“The shared room,” DIO says. “If we can stop watching when we want.” ᴰⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Ashborn considers it.

“I will listen,” he says. ᴬˢ

Noise looks toward Mau.

“Happy day?” ᴺ

He feels himself smile.

“I hope so.” ᴹ

“Here.” ᴺ

* * *

They use the arrangement they have already tested for their ordinary shared company.

Nothing opens into a private memory. The view will be of the ceremony the people outside have chosen to witness, with the guests inside able to turn from it or close it. DIO offers Ciel the small local shape she used during the festival if she wants to sit with them rather than remain only a voice in the conversation.

Ciel takes a moment before answering.

*I would like that.* ᶜⁱ

Mau can hear the pleasure she has not tried to hide inside usefulness.

“Then you have to let me get through the words,” he says. ᴹ

*I have been practising restraint for a considerable time.* ᶜⁱ

DIO laughs.

Noise looks between them, pleased without needing to understand the whole joke.

Ashborn moves far enough from his own door to take the place he has chosen at the table.

* * *

When Mau's attention returns, Yuka is waiting with a plate.

“Did they want to?” she asks. ʸ

He nods.

“Their own way.” ᴹ

“Good.” ʸ

She gives him the plate and sits beside him. Someone outside calls for the thing which has apparently been put in the wrong place again. Another voice answers with the confidence of a person who has moved it there deliberately.

Yuka listens until the argument becomes funny enough to recognise.

“I'm not going out,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks toward the door.

“They may need—” ᴹ

She takes a piece from his plate.

“They have an entire village. I have one evening before my wedding.” ʸ

He looks back at her.

After a moment, he puts the plate between them.

* * *

They eat slowly, talking about the small things they expect tomorrow to make them forget.

Where the rings are. Who has the finished pages. The fact that Gojo has promised to find Mau's shoes before making any observation about the man meant to be wearing them.

“He won't keep that order,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“You asked him anyway.” ʸ

Mau smiles.

“I know that too.” ᴹ

She leans against him for a while.

The afternoon has already contained several attempts to persuade each of them to be somewhere useful to the preparations. They have done the parts they wanted to do, answered the questions which belonged to them and left other capable people to finish the rest.

There is a satisfaction in that which he is still learning to recognise.

Yuka seems to have recognised it earlier.

He is happy to follow her example tonight.

* * *

“Are you nervous?” she asks later. ʸ

He looks at the page beside the bed.

“About forgetting something.” ᴹ

“You can look at it.” ʸ

“In the middle?” ᴹ

“I intend to be standing there quite a while.” ʸ

He laughs softly.

She turns toward him.

“You don't have to make it look easy for me.” ʸ

The old habit of agreeing too quickly rises, meets the way she is looking at him and settles.

“I want it to be good,” he says. ᴹ

“So do I.” ʸ

She touches the folded page, leaves it closed and brings his hand to her lips.

“We get to help each other if one of us forgets where we're supposed to stand.” ʸ

“You won't forget.” ᴹ

“Mau.” ʸ

He smiles at the warning in her voice.

“I will help.” ᴹ

* * *

They do not spend the night apart.

Yuka has already told the people who will help her dress when to come. Mau will leave in the morning to find his own company, then return to the door where they chose the walk to begin. The arrangement contains no danger which can be avoided by inventing a rule about the last night before marriage.

She settles beside him as she always does.

For a while they whisper about the trip. The water. A place Yuka wants to see on the road before reaching the island. Whether Mau can be persuaded to leave an interesting problem in a shop without becoming responsible for fixing it.

“I can look at things,” he says. ᴹ

“Then look at me.” ʸ

He does.

She smiles, satisfied with the redirection, and kisses him until the travel conversation can wait for another evening.

* * *

He wakes once before dawn.

Yuka is asleep with one hand resting against his chest. He listens to the ordinary breathing, the familiar rhythms beneath it and the quiet around their door.

For a moment he thinks of what tomorrow means.

Then remembers that it is already today.

He kisses her hair without waking her and lies beside her until the light begins to reach the window.
