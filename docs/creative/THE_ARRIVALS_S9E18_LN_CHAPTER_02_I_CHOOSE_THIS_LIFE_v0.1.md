*When: Rimuru looks at the two of them, he forgets the opening line he practised.*

Yuka sees it happen.

So does Mau.

For a moment the three look at one another with the private understanding of people who have spent enough mornings together to know exactly how this could become an argument about preparation.

Rimuru takes a breath.

“I had something very dignified to say.” ᴿ

Gojo makes a small, helpful sound from nearby.

Rimuru ignores it.

Yuka starts smiling before the rest of the family joins her.

“But you asked us to be here because you wanted the people who know you,” he continues. “So I suppose you expected this.” ᴿ

Mau laughs softly.

“A little.” ᴹ

* * *

Rimuru speaks about the life they have already begun making.

He does not list the dangers. The people gathered have lived close enough to many of them. He speaks about the ordinary things they have seen the pair choose: time, company, room for the other person's answer. The pleasure of finding somewhere to return together.

“You asked me to stand with you,” he says. “I can do that as someone who loves you both. The choice belongs to you.” ᴿ

Yuka's hand moves a little inside Mau's.

Rimuru lets them keep the moment.

Then asks whether they have words they want to give one another.

Mau looks toward the page in his pocket.

Yuka's smile becomes tender enough to make him stop looking for it.

“You can,” she whispers. ʸ

He takes the page out.

* * *

“I like our mornings,” he says. ᴹ

The sentence is simple enough that he can hear himself saying it.

Yuka looks at him.

“I like waking up and finding out you've already decided something about my day. Sometimes it's breakfast. Sometimes it's a place you want to go. Sometimes it's that I should find a shirt before offering an opinion.” ᴹ

Her laugh is small and unsteady.

He lets it reach him before continuing.

“I like the way you find things you love. How you look at them before telling me why. I like being the person you turn toward when something makes you happy.” ᴹ

The page trembles a little.

He lowers it.

“I want to keep being there for the parts which aren't easy to explain too. I want you to be able to tell me what you need without first deciding how much of it I can bear to hear.” ᴹ

Yuka draws a breath.

He watches her take it.

“And I promise to keep learning how to do that with you. To ask. To listen. To tell you when I need you, even when I would rather look as if I've already solved it.” ᴹ

* * *

He has reached the end of the words he wrote.

There is one more thing which arrives while he looks at her.

“I don't know how to make a whole lifetime fit into this afternoon,” he says. “I want the chance to spend it with you. The little days. The ridiculous days. The days we haven't thought of yet.” ᴹ

Yuka's eyes are wet now.

He moves his thumb gently against her hand.

“I choose you. I choose the life we get to make after everybody goes home tonight.” ᴹ

For a moment he cannot speak.

Then finds the last sentence where it has always been waiting.

“I love you.” ᴹ

Yuka nods once before she trusts her voice.

“I love you too.” ʸ

* * *

She takes out her own page.

Looks at it.

Then looks at him with an expression which makes Mau realise she is about to do exactly what she told him he was allowed to do.

“I wrote something,” she says. “And now you're here.” ʸ

Someone behind them laughs softly through a breath which does not quite sound steady.

Yuka folds the page once.

“I was going to tell you that you're brave. And kind. And terrible at noticing when you've left your drink somewhere.” ʸ

Mau smiles.

“But you know most of that. The last part has witnesses.” ʸ

Rimuru makes no effort to look innocent.

She takes Mau's hand properly again.

“What I want you to hear is that I love being with you. I like the man who argues with me about blankets. The one who gets excited and forgets what he was doing. The one who listens when I tell him about something he never would have looked for himself.” ʸ

Mau is crying before he notices the first tear.

* * *

“I want you to have things you want for yourself,” Yuka says. “I want to hear about them. I want to go places because we're curious, and come home because we miss everybody, and sometimes stay in our room because I don't feel like sharing you with an entire village.” ʸ

He laughs through the tears.

She laughs with him.

“I promise to tell you when I'm angry. And when I'm afraid. And when I need you to stop deciding what I can handle before I've had a chance to answer.” ʸ

Her thumb brushes the edge of his hand.

“I'll listen when you ask for something too.” ʸ

The quiet around them becomes very close.

“There are days you carry which I didn't live,” she says. “I'm choosing the life we're making here. I want to keep meeting the person you become in it. I want you beside me while I find out who I'm becoming too.” ʸ

He looks at her as though the rest of the day has been waiting for that sentence.

“I choose you,” she says. “My husband. With all the cups I'm going to keep finding.” ʸ

His smile breaks completely.

She squeezes his hands.

“I love you.” ʸ

* * *

Mai brings the rings.

She has kept her expression under admirable control through most of the words. It becomes less reliable when Yuka looks at her and mouths a thank-you before taking Mau's band.

Mau puts the new ring on Yuka's hand beside the one she has chosen to keep.

It takes a little longer than he expects. Yuka holds still, smiling at his concentration, then helps with the last small movement instead of letting the moment become another thing he must do perfectly in front of everyone.

When she puts his on, her own fingers shake.

He holds her hand until they settle.

Rimuru asks the simple question they agreed he would ask.

Mau says yes.

Yuka says yes.

Neither word needs help being heard.

* * *

Rimuru smiles at them.

“Then we'll remember what you chose here,” he says. “And help make room for the life which follows.” ᴿ

Mau looks toward Yuka.

Her eyes are bright, her smile already reaching for him.

“You can kiss her,” Rimuru adds, a little late. ᴿ

Yuka has moved first.

Mau catches her carefully at the waist and kisses his wife.

The sound around them begins as a few people unable to keep quiet. It grows into laughter, applause, somebody crying without bothering to hide it and Milim's delighted voice rising above all the sensible limits of a ceremony.

Mau hears it while Yuka's hand rests at the back of his neck.

For once, he does not try to attend to everything.

He stays with her until they both need to laugh.

* * *

When they turn toward the family, Beru is holding his handkerchief in both claws.

Igris stands beside him, prepared for a difficulty which has become familiar enough to deserve advance planning.

“My queen,” Beru manages. ᴮ

Yuka looks toward Mau.

He looks back at her.

The two of them begin laughing at the same time, and the first moment after their wedding belongs to the people who know them well enough to join in.
