*When: Yuka calls him her husband for the first time after the ceremony, Mau turns toward her as if she has used a spell.*

She sees the effect.

Considers it.

“Husband,” she says again. ʸ

Mau starts smiling before he can answer.

“Yes?” ᴹ

She holds out the plate she has been trying to balance while accepting congratulations.

“Carry this.” ʸ

He takes it, laughing.

Mai, passing beside them, looks at the exchange and shakes her head with the satisfaction of someone whose predictions have become reliable.

“A promising beginning.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks entirely unashamed.

* * *

They eat before the celebration can turn them into two people watched so constantly that nobody remembers they might be hungry.

Rem brings the dish she chose to make. Shuna has brought one of her own and is already sitting down beside Shion instead of waiting for an occasion to become a permanent assignment in the kitchen. Momo explains to Okarun which part she wants him to try before somebody else discovers it.

Yuta takes the place Yuka keeps for him long enough to ask whether she is happy.

She gives him the look he deserves.

He smiles anyway.

Maki sits beside him. Mai has acquired food without permitting the question of whether she has eaten to become another gathering.

Mau looks around their table.

For a moment he cannot decide where to put the feeling.

Yuka finds his hand beneath the edge.

He leaves it with her.

* * *

The first attempt to stand becomes several conversations.

People want to touch Yuka's hand, look at the rings, tell Mau something they meant to say before the ceremony made the words unexpectedly difficult. Some do it clumsily. He is glad enough they came that he cannot imagine wanting the careful version instead.

Bocchi approaches with Kita beside her.

Mau stands before remembering he has promised to let himself remain seated when he needs to. This time he wants to.

“The music,” he says. ᴹ

Bocchi looks immediately worried.

“There was one part—” ᴮᵒ

“I know. I was walking through it with her.” ᴹ

Yuka reaches for Bocchi's hand.

“It was ours while we walked,” she says. “Thank you.” ʸ

Bocchi looks between them.

Then down.

Kita squeezes the hand she is already holding.

The thank-you has reached the right place.

* * *

When there is music again, Yuka asks Mau to dance.

He looks at the place they have left for people to move, then at her feet.

“You've had a very long time to reconsider this,” he says. ᴹ

“I married you anyway.” ʸ

“A binding commitment to unfortunate decisions.” ᴹ

She pulls him up.

He goes willingly.

They begin with the two slow steps from their own room. He remembers them with absurd gratitude. There is a hand he knows how to hold, a place beside her waist which does not ask him to perform something grand and the little movement she makes when she is about to correct his direction without announcing it.

He follows.

Yuka laughs softly.

“There.” ʸ

“Improving?” ᴹ

“Manageable.” ʸ

He leans close enough that she can hear him without the rest of the circle having to share it.

“My wife has high expectations.” ᴹ

She stops smiling for one small, lovely second.

Then rests her forehead briefly against his.

* * *

Gojo asks Shoko after watching long enough that she notices him preparing the invitation.

She looks toward the moving people.

Then back at him.

“You could simply ask.” ˢᵒ

He holds out his hand.

She takes it before he can turn the question into a performance.

Across the space, Jinshi has achieved something similar by asking Maomao whether she would like to see where the cloth catches the evening light. She tells him she can see it perfectly well from here, then gets up with him anyway.

He makes no attempt to conceal how pleased he is.

Seika is listening to something Ram says when the music changes. Ram looks at the movement, at Seika's hand near her own and then offers a question quietly enough that it belongs to the two of them.

Seika answers by standing.

Nobody needs to announce what the answer has made possible.

* * *

Milim comes to claim a turn before Mau has decided how to ask whether he can sit.

Yuka sees both questions arrive on his face and answers the one she is entitled to answer.

“A little one,” she tells Milim. ʸ

Milim takes the instruction seriously enough to make the first few steps almost suspiciously careful.

Mau looks down at her.

“You can breathe.” ᴹ

“I'm being good.” ᴹⁱ

“I noticed.” ᴹ

She looks up, delighted with the acknowledgement. The next movement is less careful and much more like her.

He laughs and follows as well as he can.

At the edge, Veldora starts to offer an expert opinion until Rimuru looks toward him.

The opinion becomes a compliment about the music.

Rimuru accepts this improvement in diplomacy.

* * *

Later, Ori sits beside Mau with a small portion of dessert and the serious question of whether a wedding changes what she should call Yuka.

Yuka, on his other side, looks over.

“You can still call me Yuka.” ʸ

Ori considers the answer.

“And Mau?” ᴼ

Mau looks at her.

“Still works.” ᴹ

She nods, satisfied, then goes back to the thing she had been telling Frieren before deciding the question needed to be answered now.

Frieren listens from the next place, smiling faintly when Ori turns toward her to check one detail. The relation which brought the girl into this home has not been rearranged by another person's happy day.

Mau watches them for a moment.

Then Yuka steals a little of his dessert.

He turns back to the life beside him.

* * *

As evening settles, there are people still eating and people who have moved into conversations they did not expect to have when they arrived.

Nobody has called the meal finished because the bride and groom need a grand exit. Mau sits with his wife, hears somebody laughing near the common door and lets his body be tired from something which was worth doing.

Yuka leans against his shoulder.

“Good day?” she asks. ʸ

He looks down at her.

He thinks of the first sight of her at their door. The words she gave him. The small silver weight on his hand.

“Very good.” ᴹ

She smiles.

Beyond her, Beru has begun making a serious effort to attract their attention without looking as though he is doing it.

Mau notices.

Yuka follows his gaze.

“I think there's something else,” she says. ʸ
