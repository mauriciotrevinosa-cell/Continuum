*When: Jinshi gives them the travel purse, Mau asks whether he should sign for it.*

Jinshi looks at him.

Then at Yuka.

“You may say thank you.” ᴶ

Yuka starts laughing before Mau can find a less administrative answer.

“Thank you,” she says, taking the purse. ʸ

Mau follows her example.

* * *

The gifts do not arrive as a second ceremony.

People find them through the ordinary day, bring what they wanted to give and stay for the part of the conversation they have time to enjoy. Some gifts are things. Others are offers, small choices which will make the trip easier without asking the couple to repay every kindness by coming back with a story worthy of the expense.

Marin gives Yuka the little repair kit she asked to have for travelling and then adds something Yuka had not asked for, because there are pleasures a honeymoon ought to permit.

Yuka looks inside, laughs and closes it before Mau can become curious enough to ask.

He accepts the boundary with dignity which lasts until she smiles at him.

“Later,” she says. ʸ

He smiles back.

“I'm learning that word.” ᴹ

* * *

Mai finds them after the larger things have been put away.

She has a small finished piece for the house which Mau recognises from the description she gave before beginning it. The familiar standing connection has carried a modest cost, and he tells her how it felt before she has to ask.

She listens.

Nods.

Looks toward Yuka.

“An unexpected improvement.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka examines her husband.

“Early days.” ʸ

Mau folds his arms.

“I am present.” ᴹ

“We noticed,” Mai answers. ᴹᵃ

She stays for tea without turning the gift into a reason to ask what they owe her.

* * *

At his check, Shoko asks what the journey actually contains.

Mau gives her the route, the ordinary stops and the part where Kaisel will be carrying them rather than testing how long he can keep his own body aloft while pretending not to be tired.

She looks pleased by that distinction.

Yuka supplies the meals and the five days in which they intend to make very few decisions more urgent than whether to go down to the water.

Shoko listens to Ciel's current account through Mau, examines the things which still need checking and lets him say how the last few days have felt before interpreting them for him.

The ordinary movement has held. The old injuries are old injuries. The fresh damage from the assessment is no longer deciding how he sits or reaches for something.

“Rest when you need it,” she tells him. “Tell someone if something changes. A holiday doesn't become a test because there's water at the end.” ˢᵒ

Mau nods.

Yuka takes the travel page back.

“We intend to be very disappointing adventurers.” ʸ

Shoko looks toward Mau.

“Excellent.” ˢᵒ

* * *

Outside, Veldora has an opinion about which part of a journey ought to contain the most interesting encounter.

Rimuru has already heard enough of it to be waiting beside him when the couple arrives.

“They're going on a honeymoon,” he says. ᴿ

“An adventure of great significance.” ᵛ

“A quiet one.” ᴿ

Veldora considers this limitation with the seriousness of a scholar presented with an unfamiliar school of thought.

Milim asks whether the island has sweets.

Yuka tells her they will find out.

That answer immediately becomes a request for evidence.

Mau promises to look, and Umaru, who has arrived in time to hear the promise, informs him that she is keeping a list.

He looks at Yuka.

She looks delighted by the burden of office he has accepted.

* * *

By evening, they have a collection of things to take, a larger collection of wishes to bring back and two bags Yuka has begun examining as if the bags have personally overpromised their usefulness.

Mau watches her move one item out and another in.

“You can take both.” ᴹ

“I'm deciding what we can fit.” ʸ

He looks at the bags.

Then at the small box beside them.

There is a conversation he has assumed they already had.

Yuka looks up when he starts smiling.

“What?” ʸ

“Tomorrow,” he says. “I want to show you something before you finish arguing with the luggage.” ᴹ
