She looks up from the bed.

“Because we're travelling.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“For two weeks.” ʸ

“I helped choose it.” ᴹ

She gives him a look which suggests he has become a less useful witness to that fact than she expected.

“Then help me choose what stays.” ʸ

Mau picks up the folded shirt beside her.

“This can come.” ᴹ

It disappears from his hand.

Yuka stops moving.

* * *

He brings the shirt back.

Puts it on the bed.

Yuka looks at it.

Then at his hand.

“Like the daggers?” ʸ

He nods.

“The item inventory. It doesn't have to be a weapon.” ᴹ

For a moment she says nothing.

Mau sees the argument she has been having with two innocent bags begin to rearrange itself behind her eyes.

“Show me again.” ʸ

He takes the shirt, stores it and retrieves it. Then lets her choose the shoes so she can see that the thing returned is the thing they put away, not a newly made copy which has somehow spared him explaining where the original went.

She inspects one sole.

Looks at him.

“And the bag?” ʸ

* * *

He takes the packed bag after they have checked what is inside.

Ordinary clothes. Small things for washing. The little kit Marin gave her. Nothing alive, nothing borrowed which ought to remain with somebody else, nothing they are quietly transferring into his army because the word storage has become convenient.

The whole bag disappears.

Mau calls it back onto the bed.

Yuka opens it.

Everything is where she packed it.

She sits down slowly.

“Why haven't we been doing that?” ʸ

He looks toward the ceiling for a moment.

Then back at her.

“I have offered.” ᴹ

“When?” ʸ

He gives her the general shape of several conversations in which offering something helpful had ended in being told to bring less, let someone else carry it or try doing an ordinary thing in an ordinary way.

Yuka listens.

The corners of her mouth begin moving despite her best attempt to keep the inspection serious.

“You were all very persuasive,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

She looks at the little collection of things she has already decided she cannot take.

At the space on the bed where the bag no longer has to be.

Then at her husband.

“That's changing.” ʸ

Mau starts laughing.

“I thought it might.” ᴹ

Yuka reaches for the shoes first.

He lets her put them in the larger bag without offering an opinion about whether two pairs are enough to enjoy a honeymoon properly.

Then she looks at the travel purse.

“We still have to choose what we buy.” ʸ

“Yes. This doesn't make money.” ᴹ

“It makes room.” ʸ

He smiles.

“It makes room.” ᴹ

She looks so pleased with the distinction that he is immediately happy they had the conversation before reaching the first shop.

* * *

They keep one small ordinary pack outside the inventory.

Water. Something to eat during a stop. The route page Yuka wants to hold while discussing it. Enough that a journey can remain manageable without asking Mau to retrieve an object every time she wants to look at it.

The rest goes away in a few deliberate turns.

Yuka chooses one more thing from the pile she had abandoned.

He takes it without comment.

She catches him smiling anyway.

“You like this.” ʸ

“You're very happy about shoes.” ᴹ

“They're excellent shoes.” ʸ

He stores the second pair and holds out his empty hands as proof of agreement.

* * *

Rimuru looks into the room before dinner and discovers a suspicious absence of luggage.

Yuka points at Mau.

“We've improved the plan.” ʸ

Rimuru looks from her to his brother.

Mau says nothing.

The silence is apparently enough to explain a great deal.

“Remember that you still have to bring yourselves home,” Rimuru says. ᴿ

Yuka leans against Mau's side.

“We'll make room.” ʸ

Rimuru leaves them laughing.

* * *

That night the route waits beside the bed, with the first day's ordinary choices marked where they can both see them.

The bags are ready. The farewells which matter can happen in the morning. DIO has her chosen room in the protected house; Ashborn has the quiet place he chose to keep. Ciel is nearby without filling the evening with a list of things to remember.

Yuka settles beside Mau and tells him the first thing she wants to do when they reach the road.

He asks why.

She begins explaining.

He listens until the excitement in her voice becomes his own, and goes to sleep beside his wife with somewhere happy to go when morning comes.
