*When: B+109, after breakfast. Yuka has promised Maki they will eat properly. Mau is beginning to suspect that everyone intends to stand beside Kaisel until they can personally verify the first meal.*

Umaru gives him the list again.

“I have it.” ᴹ

“This is the final version.” ᵁ

He unfolds it. There are additions down the side, an arrow leading to the back and a small drawing of something round which has acquired three increasingly important outlines.

“Is this a cake?” ᴹ

“That depends on what you find.” ᵁ

Beside her, Ori has a request of her own. She wants something from the water, if they see something which can come home without needing to be fed. Mau promises to look. Milim begins suggesting exceptions to the feeding condition until Frieren tells her that a creature needing several cows does count.

Yuka kisses Maki's cheek, then Mai's, enduring the latter's complaint that this is an excessive public display with the cheerful indifference of a woman about to leave for two weeks. Yuta holds her a moment longer. When he lets go, she straightens the part of his collar she has disturbed.

“We'll tell you when we get there.” ʸ

“Whichever there you choose first.” ʸᵗ

She smiles at the concession.

Mau reaches for his hand and finds himself pulled into a hug instead. Yuta pats his back, says something about enjoying himself and releases him before the advice can become a list.

Rimuru is waiting by Kaisel.

“If you bring back a sea monster, you explain it to Fern.” ᴿ

“I wasn't planning to.” ᴹ

“I have learned to cover the obvious things.” ᴿ

Yuka puts her foot on the place Mau shows her and climbs up. He follows, settles behind her and checks the ordinary strap she asked to have for the longer flights. Her hand comes back to find his. For a moment, with the house gathered below and the morning opening beyond the trees, neither of them speaks.

Then Umaru calls that the list includes enough for everyone.

Mau looks down.

“I'll need a larger dragon.” ᴹ

Kaisel turns his head.

Yuka laughs against Mau's arm.

* * *

They are not far from home before she asks to go lower.

He looks at the route she holds against her knees. The line Rimuru helped them choose follows a road for the first part, giving them places to stop whenever they want something besides sky. Kaisel banks when Mau asks. Yuka leans with the movement, trusting him to keep the part of the map she has nearly released from escaping altogether.

“There.” ʸ

A row of cloth roofs stands beside the road. People have gathered around a cart which has apparently broken an axle. Beyond it, somebody is cooking something flat over a hot plate.

“Our first destination?” ᴹ

“I'm hungry.” ʸ

“We had breakfast.” ᴹ

“I remember.” ʸ

He asks Kaisel for the clearing beyond the market, far enough away that a descending dragon will not become the principal event in somebody else's morning. They walk the last part with the little pack between them. Yuka folds the route and puts it away.

The woman at the hot plate has savoury cakes on one side and sweet ones on the other. Yuka asks which she recommends. She recommends both, with the honest confidence of somebody selling them. Mau buys one of each from their travel purse.

Yuka takes the first bite, shuts her eyes and immediately exchanges cakes with him.

He tries the one she has already bitten.

“You could have told me it was good.” ᴹ

“I needed a second opinion.” ʸ

They sit on a low wall to finish them. A child further down is attempting to make a little wooden bird climb a string. The bird slides back every time he lets go, which annoys him so visibly that Yuka begins watching despite herself.

Mau follows her gaze.

“You want to try it.” ᴹ

“I want him to win.” ʸ

The child finally reaches the knot at the top. Yuka makes a small triumphant noise. He turns, startled to discover he has an audience, then displays the bird with renewed importance.

Mau applauds with the hand which is not holding breakfast.

The boy bows.

“He's going to be terrible now,” the woman at the cart tells them. ᵛᵉ

“He was excellent.” ʸ

“That's what he'll say.” ᵛᵉ

They leave with two little string birds, one for Ori and one which Yuka claims is necessary to understand the mechanism. Mau stores the wrapped pair after they have stepped out of the crowd. She pats his empty hands with satisfaction.

“Excellent husband.” ʸ

“I thought you liked me before the luggage improvement.” ᴹ

“I did. The improvement is considerable.” ʸ

* * *

At the fork after the next bridge, Yuka chooses the left-hand road.

The route says right. Mau points this out without attempting to turn her.

“I can hear water.” ʸ

“We have quite a lot of that planned.” ᴹ

“This is different water.” ʸ

He concedes the point. They walk until the sound becomes a millstream and the millstream becomes a little fall beyond a cluster of houses. Yuka steps carefully down to the stones, holding his hand more firmly when one moves beneath her shoe.

There is nothing to buy here. No sign to tell them how long to stay. A woman on the opposite bank lifts a basket and continues home, leaving the afternoon to the two strangers who have arrived to admire something she walks past every day.

Yuka sits, takes off her shoes and puts her feet in the water.

Her expression changes.

“Cold?” ᴹ

“Lovely.” ʸ

He sits beside her and tries it.

“Liar.” ᴹ

She splashes him before he can move the shoes.

The response is immediate and badly aimed. She laughs, pulls her feet back and catches his wrist, apparently hoping that stopping one hand will prevent the other from making a second attempt. He leans toward her. She abandons the wrist in favour of his collar and kisses him while the water drips down his sleeve.

He forgets his planned retaliation.

Yuka notices.

“Useful.” ʸ

“You don't need more advantages.” ᴹ

She kisses him once more anyway.

* * *

By evening they have reached a roadside inn further along the route, with time to wash before supper and a room the owner gives them after asking one ordinary question about the length of their stay.

Mau sends Rimuru their location through the familiar connection. They arrived, they ate, they are staying the night. Rimuru's answer contains enough amusement to suggest the house has been asking, but no instruction to return.

Yuka is at the table when Mau turns back. She has taken the route out again. There is a little mark beside the place where they bought the birds and another beside the millstream.

“Neither of those was on the plan,” he says. ᴹ

“They are now.” ʸ

He sits next to her instead of opposite. She moves the page so they can both reach it and asks whether he remembers the name the woman gave them for the cakes.

He does. She writes it down.

When the food arrives, Yuka puts a dot beside the inn too.

Mau waits until she has taken a bite before asking whether it deserves one.

She pushes the plate toward him.

“Second opinion.” ʸ
