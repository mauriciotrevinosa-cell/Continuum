*When: B+110, at the second inn of their journey. Yuka discovers that having no one to meet downstairs has made Mau astonishingly easy to distract.*

He has been trying to put the route away for some time.

Every time he turns back to the table, she asks him something else. Whether he liked the village they passed. Whether they should have bought the little painted bowl. Whether he intends to keep standing over there when she has already made an excellent place beside her.

Mau puts the page down unfinished.

“You could have started with that.” ᴹ

Yuka holds out a hand.

“I wanted the answer about the bowl.” ʸ

He takes the hand, sits and discovers that the space beside her was an inaccurate description of where she intended him to end up. She leans across him, catches the fold of his shirt and kisses him before he can make the observation aloud.

He forgets the bowl.

For a while they forget the journey too. There is the familiar warmth of her against him, the small laugh which interrupts a kiss when his hand catches the end of her hair and the way she shifts closer after he apologises. He has held her through ordinary sleep, through days neither of them wants to repeat and through mornings when getting up seemed a particularly unreasonable request. Wanting her here is easy. Knowing she wants him is still capable of taking his breath.

Yuka touches his cheek.

“You're looking at me like that again.” ʸ

“I like looking at you.” ᴹ

“I noticed.” ʸ

She kisses the corner of his mouth. When she comes back, slower, he follows her.

Then her fingers stop at his collar.

He feels the pause before he understands it. He draws back enough to see her face, keeping his hand where it has been resting against her back.

“Hey.” ᴹ

“I'm here.” ʸ

He nods. Waits.

Yuka looks down at the little space between them and takes a breath which seems to annoy her for needing to be taken.

“I want to,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“No. I want you to know that part properly.” ʸ

He brushes a strand of hair away from her mouth.

“I do.” ᴹ

She looks at him again. There is relief in it, but she is still thinking. He waits until she has found the words instead of filling the quiet with an explanation of what he thinks she ought to feel.

“We've talked about a family,” she says at last. “A lot.” ʸ

He feels something in his chest answer before he speaks.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“And I do want that. I just don't know whether wanting it means I'm ready for it to happen now.” ʸ

Mau rests his forehead against hers. For a moment she lets him stay there, breathing with him, before he moves back enough to answer.

“I've been trying to find a way to say the same thing.” ᴹ

Her hand loosens on his shirt.

“You have?” ʸ

“I want a life with you. Including that part, if we both still want it when we get there.” ᴹ

“We will.” ʸ

He smiles at the certainty, then looks down at his own hand beside hers. The old fingers do not lie straight quite as easily as he would like. They are familiar enough now that he sometimes forgets what another person might read into the gesture.

“I don't understand everything this body does yet,” he says. “And there are days when my head gets somewhere before I can tell it we're home.” ᴹ

Yuka's thumb moves against his wrist.

“I know.” ʸ

“I want to understand more before there's somebody else depending on the answer.” ᴹ

She is quiet for a little while. He can hear people on the road outside, a cart being taken around the back of the inn and somebody calling that a bag has been left behind. The ordinary sounds make it easier to stay in the room with the thing he wants rather than rushing ahead to everything which might go wrong.

“I don't need you to become somebody nothing ever happens to,” she says. ʸ

He looks up.

“I know.” ᴹ

“Because we'd be waiting forever.” ʸ

He laughs before he expects to.

Yuka smiles a little, relieved by the sound.

“I want us to have time being married,” she says. “I wanted this trip. I want to go places with you and make stupid plans about curtains and find out whether you leave cups in more places when nobody's watching.” ʸ

“Those are strategic cups.” ᴹ

“They're abandoned cups.” ʸ

“You always know where to find one.” ᴹ

She presses her forehead into his shoulder to hide the laugh. He holds her there, glad enough she is laughing that the joke stops mattering almost immediately.

When she lifts her head, he kisses her gently.

“We have time,” he says. ᴹ

“Then I want some of it for this.” ʸ

She touches his wedding band with one finger.

“I want that too.” ᴹ

* * *

They move only enough to become comfortable.

Yuka stays against him, her cheek resting where she can hear one of his hearts. The other is there too, differently placed, familiar in a way she had to learn. Neither sound gives them an answer about what a child might inherit. Mau lets the question remain a question instead of constructing a frightened certainty out of what he does not know.

“We can ask about the body part when we go home,” he says. ᴹ

“Without making it a meeting?” ʸ

“I was thinking fewer than the entire house.” ᴹ

“Good. Grandpa would bring paper.” ʸ

“I think Mai would take it away.” ᴹ

She laughs softly against him.

For a moment she is quiet enough that he thinks she may be falling asleep. Then her hand moves up his arm and she looks at him with sudden seriousness.

“You're not disappointed in me?” ʸ

The question hurts in a small place he cannot reach by telling her not to have asked it. He holds her face with his free hand and waits until she is looking directly at him.

“No.” ᴹ

She searches his face.

“You can want me and still say we should stop,” he says. “I can want you and agree.” ᴹ

Yuka nods once, slowly.

“You too,” she says. ʸ

He nods back.

She gives him a small, almost embarrassed smile and leans in to kiss him again. This one is unhurried. When she settles afterward, it is with the comfortable authority of somebody who has made a decision and intends to enjoy the rest of the evening anyway.

Mau closes his eyes.

“The bowl,” she says. ʸ

He opens them.

“What about it?” ᴹ

“We should have bought it.” ʸ

“We can go back tomorrow.” ᴹ

She considers the offer, then puts her head back on his shoulder.

“After breakfast.” ʸ
