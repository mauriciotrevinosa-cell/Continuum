*When: B+111, late morning. The woman selling the painted bowl recognises Yuka immediately and asks whether the argument has been resolved.*

“There wasn't an argument,” Mau says. ᴹ

The woman looks at the bowl, which Yuka has already picked up.

“I see.” ᵛᵉ

“She agrees with me,” Yuka explains. ʸ

He pays without attempting to discover what he has lost. The bowl is shallow enough for the little things Yuka leaves beside their bed, painted with a design she has now described to him twice. He wraps it in the cloth the woman gives them. Yuka watches him store it, takes his hand and begins leading him toward the next row of stalls with the confidence of somebody whose principal regret has been corrected.

There is a clothes shop near the end.

Mau sees it at almost exactly the moment she does.

Her hand tightens.

“No,” he says, already smiling. ᴹ

“You don't know what I was going to ask.” ʸ

“You looked at the shirt and then at me.” ᴹ

“It's a very good shirt.” ʸ

“So you should buy it.” ᴹ

“For you.” ʸ

The shopkeeper saves him by asking whether they would like to come inside, a rescue Mau recognises as deeply flawed only after Yuka has answered for both of them.

* * *

The first shirt fits across his shoulders and hangs too loosely everywhere else.

Yuka looks at it from the doorway of the changing space. Mau holds his arms out.

“Comfortable.” ᴹ

“You look like you borrowed it.” ʸ

“From a comfortable man.” ᴹ

She takes it from him and goes back to the rail.

He does not entirely understand how she chooses the next one. The difference looks small until he has put it on. Then the shopkeeper asks him to turn, Yuka stops halfway through an explanation and her face changes in a way which makes him look down to check whether he has fastened something incorrectly.

“What?” ᴹ

“Nothing.” ʸ

“You stopped talking.” ᴹ

“Enjoy it.” ʸ

He tries to see himself properly in the mirror. The body looking back is his, the old marks appearing above the collar where they always appear, the pale interruption in his hair a little harder to mistake under the clear light. The shirt is only cloth. It has no reason to make the man in the mirror look as though he has come somewhere because he wanted to be seen.

Yuka comes up beside him and adjusts the fold at his wrist.

“This one.” ʸ

He looks from their reflection to her.

“All right.” ᴹ

She glances up, surprised by the easy surrender.

“You're sure?” ʸ

“You look very pleased.” ᴹ

Her fingers stop on the cloth.

Then she straightens it again with unnecessary concentration.

* * *

Choosing something for her proves considerably more dangerous.

Mau makes the mistake of saying he likes the first dress before Yuka has properly turned around. She gives him a look, goes back inside and emerges in another. He likes that one too. By the fourth, the shopkeeper has begun pretending not to listen.

“You have to choose,” Yuka says. ʸ

“I can like four things.” ᴹ

“We can't buy four things every time I ask.” ʸ

“We can buy the ones you want.” ᴹ

“That's worse.” ʸ

He sits forward, considering her with the seriousness the occasion apparently requires. The dress she is wearing now shifts when she moves, catching against her knee and falling back. The colour belongs to her in his sight, while the folded alternatives on the counter have already gone back to the quiet greys of the room.

“That one,” he says. ᴹ

“Why?” ʸ

“You've touched the skirt every time you stopped thinking about my opinion.” ᴹ

She looks down. Her hand is doing exactly that.

He smiles.

Yuka turns once, deliberately, and comes to stand between his knees.

“You do pay attention.” ʸ

“Occasionally.” ᴹ

“An encouraging habit.” ʸ

She kisses his forehead and goes to change before he can point out that she is undermining his ability to give an impartial answer.

* * *

They sit with the prices before deciding.

Yuka wants the dress, a lighter set for the water and something she can wear on the journey home. Mau keeps the shirt she chose, trousers which fit properly and a second shirt after she points out that he is going to get the first one wet at some point. They leave two things they like on the counter. Having room to carry them has not made the purse larger.

The shopkeeper asks whether they would like the bundles tied for travel.

Yuka looks at Mau.

“We have a system.” ʸ

“I carry things,” he explains. ᴹ

“A very good system.” ʸ

Outside, she waits until they have reached a quiet turning before handing him the purchases. He stores each wrapped bundle while she checks that nothing they intend to wear today has disappeared with it.

When he reaches for his old clothes, she keeps the new shirt in place with one hand.

“Stay like that.” ʸ

“I was only going to put this away.” ᴹ

“Excellent.” ʸ

He looks at the old hoodie folded over his arm. She follows his glance, then touches the orange part along its edge.

“That still comes home,” she says. ʸ

He nods. Stores it carefully.

She takes his empty hand.

* * *

At lunch, Yuka asks whether he feels dressed up.

He considers the question while trying a piece of grilled fish she has put on his plate. Nobody here has commented on the change. The people at the next table are arguing about whether the afternoon boat will leave before the wind changes; a man near the door has fallen asleep over an empty cup.

“A little,” he says. ᴹ

“Bad?” ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

She smiles, relieved enough that he realises she had been asking more than the shirt question.

He reaches across the small table and touches her hand.

“I like being somebody you want to take places.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at him for a long moment.

Then picks up her cup with her other hand.

“You're already somebody I want to take places.” ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

“And now you'll photograph better.” ʸ

He laughs, she laughs with him, and the man sleeping by the door wakes just enough to look offended before going back to his cup.
