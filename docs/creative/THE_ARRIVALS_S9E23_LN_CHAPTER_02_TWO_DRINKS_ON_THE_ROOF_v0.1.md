*When: B+112, after supper at the coast. Yuka finds the stair to the roof terrace before Mau has finished asking the owner whether there is somewhere to sit outside.*

“There,” she says, pointing upward. ʸ

The owner laughs and tells them the steps are steep.

Mau follows Yuka with two borrowed cups. She has the water. By the time he reaches the top, she has found a place where they can sit with their feet against the lower wall and watch the last boats come in. He sets the cups between them and lowers himself beside her.

The sea is somewhere beyond the harbour lights, mostly sound from here. The boats become dark shapes as the sun goes. Yuka tells him which sail is still catching colour. He follows the direction of her hand and finds the one she means.

“Tomorrow we'll be on the island,” she says. ʸ

“If you don't find another bowl.” ᴹ

“You were right about going back.” ʸ

“I'll need that in writing.” ᴹ

She nudges his leg with her foot.

For a while they watch without talking. There is a man on the quay who keeps calling to somebody who apparently cannot hear him. Whenever the reply finally comes, it arrives from somewhere completely different. Yuka begins predicting where the next one will be. Mau is so consistently wrong that she accuses him of doing it on purpose.

He declines to explain his method.

* * *

The request comes back to him when she opens the water.

Umaru's list. A round outline which has become more important with every revision. Mau takes the folded page from the small pack and smooths it over his knee.

Yuka leans closer.

“An official matter?” ʸ

“Very.” ᴹ

He opens the familiar personal interface where she can see it with him. The inventory is the part she knows now. Beside it, the shop tab has a little unread mark he has been passing whenever he wants something he already owns.

Yuka points.

“You have money in there?” ʸ

“Apparently.” ᴹ

“Mau.” ʸ

“I'm checking before I make promises to a government.” ᴹ

He asks Ciel for help with the record rather than selecting the first attractive thing on the page.

Her answer comes easily, from the old entries already attached to his own account. The dagger purchase is there, dated before this body returned to this branch. He does not enjoy remembering how it happened in his sleep, but the transaction itself is ordinary enough to read. Later come the credits booked for rooms he actually cleared, their clearance records surviving the Architect's attempt to make everything else in those rooms unbearable.

The little total has been sitting here while he looked past it.

Eighteen thousand six hundred and forty.

“Is that a lot?” Yuka asks. ʸ

“Let's compare it to something I understand.” ᴹ

He tries the food category.

Then sits very still.

Yuka looks at him instead of the page.

“What?” ʸ

“Umaru.” ᴹ

* * *

Her choices are still here.

Not the girl herself issuing fresh instructions from a shelf, although Mau suspects she would consider that a sensible improvement. The drinks she argued for, the ordinary food selected for journeys, the little consumables which had once made everyone reconsider what the shop could be used for. The old catalogue has survived in his personal pages alongside the things he first learned to buy alone.

Mau asks before calling it an improvement somebody has just made.

*The listings were already preserved,* Ciel tells him. *I am reading what is there. We aren't constructing anything new.* ᶜⁱ

*Can I pay with this?* ᴹ

*The page offers your settled credits. It will not take the money in Yuka's purse.* ᶜⁱ

He tells Yuka.

She peers at the two figures, then at the very ordinary drink beneath them.

“We could have had one yesterday.” ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“You have an impressive collection of things you forget to mention.” ʸ

“I didn't know this part was still here.” ᴹ

She looks at his face, decides he means it and leans against him again.

“Then we should test it.” ʸ

He chooses two cans. The page asks for confirmation, with the price made clear: two credits each, four in all. Mau reads it before accepting. The total becomes eighteen thousand six hundred and thirty-six, and two small, familiar shapes arrive in his open hands.

Yuka laughs at his expression.

“You look happier than when you found the shirts.” ʸ

“The shirts didn't make this noise.” ᴹ

He opens a can.

The little hiss makes her sit up. He gives it to her and watches while she takes a cautious first drink, then a much less cautious second.

“Cold,” she says, surprised. ʸ

He opens his own.

The taste is sharp and sweet and immediately explains several of Umaru's diplomatic positions. Mau drinks too fast, coughs and has to endure Yuka laughing at him while attempting to offer the water.

From the quay below, the distant man calls again.

Yuka guesses left.

Mau guesses right.

The answer comes from inside the boat directly beneath them.

They nearly spill both drinks.

* * *

Mau reads the rest of the purchase entry before closing the page. Two ordinary drinks, consumed here. No improvement to his body, no recovered energy concealed inside a promise of refreshment. The soldiers remain soldiers. The things waiting to be made remain waiting. For once a number has changed because he and his wife wanted something nice on a roof.

Yuka rests the empty can beside her cup.

“You'll have to bring some home.” ʸ

“I think we're legally obliged.” ᴹ

“Enough for everyone.” ʸ

“That's how it started.” ᴹ

She takes the list from his knee and adds one line beneath the increasingly important circle. When he looks over, she turns it just far enough away to make him ask.

“What did you write?” ᴹ

“Avoid an international incident.” ʸ

Mau laughs into the last of his drink. Down below, somebody begins calling for the owner of a missing rope, and Yuka immediately points to the wrong boat.
