*When: B+113, just before noon. The hotel attendant asks Yuka whether they would like lunch brought to their room, and she looks at Mau with the expression of someone discovering a dangerous new possibility.*

“You don't have to decide now,” the attendant adds. ᴬᵗ

That makes it worse.

Mau takes the letter out again, checks that this is the place they were sent to and briefly wonders whether Rimuru has underestimated the effect of giving Yuka five days with no reason to carry her own plate.

She is already asking about breakfast.

* * *

They saw the island from Kaisel's back while the morning mist was still lifting from the water.

Yuka had gone quiet against him. At first he thought she was trying to read the buildings, which appeared a little at a time between the slopes and the beach. Then she put the route away without asking whether they had chosen the right landing and leaned back until her head touched his shoulder.

“What?” he asked. ᴹ

“Nothing. I just wanted to look.” ʸ

He asked Kaisel to take the longer turn.

They came down at the agreed approach beyond the hotel grounds. The staff had been told to expect a dragon carrying two guests; the young man sent to meet them had apparently accepted the instruction in writing before considering what a dragon would look like when it actually arrived. Mau thanked him for waiting while Yuka climbed down. By the time Kaisel returned to his shadow, the young man had remembered how to introduce himself.

He carried their small pack with the determination of somebody offered one ordinary task in an extraordinary morning.

Yuka let him.

* * *

The room is ready.

There is a little plate of fruit near the letter wishing them a happy stay. Yuka reads the letter aloud, stops at the line explaining that no formal appointments have been arranged and looks so relieved that Mau begins laughing.

“You were expecting a meeting?” ᴹ

“I live with you and Rimuru.” ʸ

“A reasonable fear.” ᴹ

She sets the letter down and goes to the balcony. He follows with the fruit, losing one piece to her before he has found anywhere to put the plate.

Below them, a few people are already in the water. Someone has left a hat on an empty chair and is trying to retrieve it without admitting the breeze has made the task difficult. Yuka watches the struggle until the hat finally lands against a parasol.

Then she looks toward the sea.

“We get all of this?” ʸ

“For five nights.” ᴹ

“And then we have to leave?” ʸ

He knows she is joking. Mostly.

“I think they have other guests.” ᴹ

“An unfortunate policy.” ʸ

She takes another piece of fruit and turns back into the room to see what else she has been given permission to enjoy.

* * *

Mau sends the arrival notice while she changes for lunch.

Rimuru answers from somewhere noisy enough that a second conversation nearly reaches the edge of the connection before he turns his attention properly toward Mau. They arrived safely. The hotel has the letter. Yuka has already attempted to calculate how many meals can be included in five days.

*Good,* Rimuru says. *She should make the most of it.* ᴿ

*They offered to bring breakfast to the room.* ᴹ

There is a small silence.

Then unmistakable amusement.

*I may never see either of you again.* ᴿ

Mau laughs.

He asks how things are at home, receives enough ordinary news to recognise the place without being given a job and says he will speak to them again. Ciel stays near after Rimuru turns back to his day.

*You can enjoy it too,* Mau tells her. ᴹ

*I intend to.* ᶜⁱ

Ashborn has asked to see the view later, if they have time to share it. Mau agrees to an interval before supper, with nothing from the room or Yuka's private time included. For now, he leaves the connection quiet and looks up as his wife emerges in one of the things they bought together.

She stops at his expression.

“Good choice?” ʸ

He puts his hand out.

“Very.” ᴹ

* * *

Lunch is beneath an awning near the water.

Yuka keeps discovering that the menu has another side. Mau reads the first half while she reads the second, then they exchange information with enough seriousness that the attendant waits politely for the conference to conclude. They choose two things to share. When the first plate arrives, Yuka immediately decides that choosing had been unnecessary and they ought to have asked for more.

Halfway through the second, she changes her mind.

Mau accepts the remaining portion with the composure of a husband who has begun learning the duties of office.

Afterward they walk down to the beach. Yuka removes her shoes as soon as the sand becomes deep enough to make keeping them on an argument. Mau carries them until she points at his hand, reminding him that they have recently resolved this problem. He stores both pairs.

“Much better.” ʸ

She steps toward the edge of the water, waits for the next little wave and misjudges how far it will reach.

The expression on her face is worth the damp hem.

Mau keeps his laughter quiet enough to make it worse.

She turns.

He has time to say her name before she takes his wrist and pulls him in beside her.

* * *

They do not leave the beach until the shadow of the awning has moved across the place where they left the ordinary pack.

Yuka has collected three shells, rejected two and kept the smallest because Ori asked for something which did not need to eat. Mau wraps it carefully. She watches him put it away, then holds out her hand for him to help her stand.

Back in the room, he expects her to begin planning tomorrow.

Instead she sits beside him, rests her head against his shoulder and closes her eyes.

“Wake me before dinner.” ʸ

He kisses the top of her head.

“How much before?” ᴹ

“Enough to get there.” ʸ

Her hand settles over his. A little later, when he tries to move it without waking her, she catches his fingers more firmly.

Mau gives up.

Dinner can wait five minutes.
