*When: B+114, morning. Yuka has discovered a breakfast table standing in the shallow end of the pool. Mau is trying to understand why that makes the chairs more desirable.*

She gives him the expression he has begun recognising as the end of a discussion he did not realise had started.

“Your feet are in the water.” ʸ

“I understand the mechanism.” ᴹ

“Then come.” ʸ

She takes his hand and leads him down the broad steps. The water reaches their calves before she stops at the little table, chooses the chair nearer the deeper part and waits until he has sat before looking pleased with the arrangement.

Mau rests his feet against the smooth floor below.

Considers it.

“All right.” ᴹ

“See?” ʸ

“I haven't been fed yet. My final opinion is pending.” ᴹ

She kicks a little water against his ankle.

The attendant arrives before he can retaliate, carrying enough plates to persuade him that the table has been designed for a more serious purpose than he first appreciated. Yuka asks about the little filled pastries. The explanation is barely finished before she takes one.

Mau accepts the cup put beside him and settles into the strange pleasure of being exactly where she wanted him.

* * *

The bird comes halfway through breakfast.

It lands on the edge of the pool, watches them with the undisturbed confidence of a creature who has seen this arrangement before and begins approaching Yuka's plate one small step at a time.

Mau notices it first.

“Negotiator.” ᴹ

She follows his gaze.

The bird stops, considering the two of them.

Yuka covers the remaining pastry with her hand.

“No.” ʸ

It takes another step.

Mau looks at her.

“A difficult opening.” ᴹ

“You can have something else.” ʸ

The bird appears unmoved by the distinction. It looks at the pastry again, then at Mau's plate, where a piece of fruit lies dangerously close to the edge.

Yuka sees the opportunity.

“That one's available.” ʸ

“Excuse me.” ᴹ

The bird steals it while he is speaking.

For a moment neither moves. Then Yuka folds over the table laughing while the thief retreats with its prize, hopping sideways to avoid the place where the water would have made the escape less dignified.

Mau watches it go.

“I expected support from my wife.” ᴹ

She tries to answer and cannot.

He takes half her pastry.

She recovers immediately.

“Mau.” ʸ

“Trade reparations.” ᴹ

“That wasn't your fruit.” ʸ

“It was on my plate.” ᴹ

“I put it there because I didn't want it.” ʸ

He looks at the pastry in his hand. Then back at her.

“A complex diplomatic situation.” ᴹ

Yuka takes the larger half before the settlement can become more complicated.

* * *

They move away from the table after the plates have been cleared.

Yuka goes first, lowering herself into the water with an expression so completely different from the one she wears when planning a fight that Mau stays beside the edge for a moment simply to watch. Her hair has been tied up badly. A little piece keeps escaping near her ear; she pushes it away, realises it is wet anyway and gives up.

She looks back at him.

“You're allowed in.” ʸ

“I was enjoying the view.” ᴹ

Her face warms. She splashes him to conceal the effect.

He steps down into the water before she can improve on the attack. She is waiting a little further out, close enough to the shallow ledge that she can stand whenever she wants. When he reaches her, she puts her hands on his shoulders and leans back just far enough to make him decide whether she is about to fall.

He catches her waist.

“You did that deliberately.” ᴹ

“I knew you'd catch me.” ʸ

He draws her nearer. She comes willingly, her knees brushing his side in the water, one hand finding the back of his neck. Her kiss tastes faintly of breakfast. When she pulls away, she is smiling without attempting to make a joke of why.

He rests his cheek against hers for a little while.

Then she whispers that the bird is back.

He turns so quickly she laughs into his shoulder.

* * *

Later, stretched out beneath the shade with a towel beneath her head, Yuka begins counting the things they have already done today.

“Breakfast.” ʸ

“A considerable achievement.” ᴹ

“Swimming.” ʸ

“An international incident involving fruit.” ᴹ

She laughs and puts her hand over his mouth before he can add anything else.

He kisses her palm.

She withdraws the hand with a little look of betrayal, as though he has found a rule she had not considered when designing the interruption.

Beyond the edge of the shade, someone is learning to float while a patient companion offers increasingly unhelpful advice about relaxing. Yuka watches for a moment, then looks at Mau beside her.

“You could do that in your sleep.” ʸ

“I have.” ᴹ

“I know. Rimuru explained.” ʸ

He smiles at the memory of her catching him above the bed and deciding, after the first alarm, that this was an unreasonable thing for a husband to leave out of ordinary conversation.

“You looked happy,” she says. ʸ

“I was asleep.” ᴹ

“You looked happy asleep.” ʸ

He turns toward her. The sunlight reaches one side of her face, bright enough that she has begun narrowing that eye while pretending it does not bother her. He lifts the edge of the towel until the shade covers both.

“I usually wake up with you,” he says. ᴹ

She goes quiet.

Then reaches for his hand and puts it on her stomach, safely over the towel, where she can keep his fingers between hers without either of them having to sit up.

“You can do that again tomorrow.” ʸ

He closes his eyes beside her.

After a while, the floating lesson in the next part of the pool ends with a very loud splash and a complaint about whose fault it was.

Yuka starts laughing first.
