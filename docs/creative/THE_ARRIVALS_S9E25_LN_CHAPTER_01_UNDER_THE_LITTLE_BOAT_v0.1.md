*When: B+114, after lunch. Mau can put the mask on correctly. Yuka considers this proof that his repeated failure to understand what she means by “move your hair” is intentional.*

“I'm helping.” ᴹ

“You're laughing.” ʸ

“I can do two things.” ᴹ

She hands him the mask before he can demonstrate. He stands behind her, moves the strap carefully over the part of her hair she has finally persuaded to stay together and settles it where the guide showed them. When she turns, the glass makes her eyes look slightly larger.

He smiles.

She points one finger at him.

“Nothing.” ʸ

“Beautiful.” ᴹ

The guide looks toward the water with professional discretion.

Yuka makes Mau put his own mask on before accepting the compliment.

* * *

They took the small boat around the quieter side of the island, where the guide knew a shallow place beyond the rocks. There are other guests aboard, a basket of gear and a man at the front who has already told the same story twice because the second listener arrived after the first had stopped being impressed.

Yuka listened through the second telling with the expression she uses when she is trying not to encourage someone into a third.

Mau looked away before she could catch him enjoying it.

Now the boat rests inside the sheltered water. The guide checks that everyone knows the way back to the ladder. Yuka has chosen the floating support at first; the sea moves differently from the pool, and she wants a minute to decide how much of that movement she likes.

Mau goes down first. Turns to make room for her feet.

She enters slowly, takes one startled breath at the cold and catches his forearm even though her other hand still has the rail.

He does not comment.

“You can laugh later,” she tells him. ʸ

“I was planning to charge for silence.” ᴹ

She squeezes his arm.

He decides to waive the fee.

* * *

The first fish appears beneath the boat.

Yuka points so urgently that Mau thinks she has dropped something. Then he follows the direction of her hand and sees the little shape moving through a gap in the rock. Another follows. Then several more, each correcting direction so sharply that the whole group seems to share a thought.

He looks back toward Yuka.

She is already looking at him.

They cannot talk properly with the mouthpieces in. She tries to express the entire discovery with one hand while keeping the other near the float. Mau nods with such exaggerated seriousness that she laughs underwater and immediately has to lift her face to recover from the consequences.

He comes up beside her.

“I understood.” ᴹ

“You made me laugh.” ʸ

“I agreed.” ᴹ

“Aggressively.” ʸ

He puts a hand beneath the float to steady it while she adjusts the mouthpiece. This time she takes a moment, looks down without trying to tell him anything and lets the water carry the movement she does not need to fight.

He stays close enough that her fingers can find his.

Below them, the fish returns.

* * *

They drift along the permitted stretch until Yuka forgets to keep checking the boat.

There are little hollows where something has moved and left a clean space behind. A crab which is certain none of them can see it. A pale wavering thing the guide points out from a safe distance, telling them to look without touching. Yuka studies the small places more closely than the large ones. Mau watches her realise how much can live beneath water that looked empty from the beach.

When she squeezes his fingers twice, he comes up with her.

“Did you see the little one?” ʸ

“Which little one?” ᴹ

She begins describing it with both hands. A wave makes her blink and abandon one hand to the float again.

“It was hiding under the shell.” ʸ

“The one which looked offended?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸ

“I thought that was you when I touched your breakfast.” ᴹ

She splashes him with the hand she can spare.

The guide calls them closer to the boat for the next turn. They go together, taking the longer movement rather than trying to beat the little current between them and the ladder.

* * *

Mau climbs aboard after Yuka.

She is wrapped in a towel by the time he reaches the seat. There is another beside her, which she opens without asking whether he intends to become cold before using it. He sits. She pulls one edge around his shoulders and begins explaining that they ought to have brought something to write down all the creatures they have seen.

“You can draw them later.” ᴹ

“You'll help.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“A dangerous request.” ᴹ

“You've seen them too.” ʸ

“That hasn't improved my hands.” ᴹ

“I'll recognise them.” ʸ

He is not sure whether she means she will recognise the creatures or the effort. Either way, she sounds confident enough to make him agree.

The boat turns toward the hotel. Yuka leans against him, sharing the warm edge of her towel while the wind begins drying the water from his hair.

For a little while he asks whether she would mind sharing the sea itself with Ashborn, who wanted to see it. She looks out, then nods. Mau offers the view from where he sits, keeping their earlier afternoon and the conversation between them their own.

Ashborn watches quietly.

After a time he says the water does not look as though it has been asked to prove anything.

Mau looks toward the low waves.

*No,* he answers. ᴹ

The view closes when the guest has had enough. Yuka is tracing the shape of the crab on Mau's knee with one finger, apparently unwilling to risk forgetting the offended little face before they reach land.

He asks whether it had that many legs.

She tells him to be quiet because she is concentrating.
