*When: B+115. Mau has been told that the boat is leaving after breakfast. He finds Yuka beside the hotel shop wearing a hat which makes it impossible for him to kiss her without first negotiating the brim.*

She watches him discover the difficulty.

“You like it?” ʸ

“It has formidable defences.” ᴹ

“I'll take that as yes.” ʸ

He pays the ordinary price from their purse after she has checked it, then waits while she puts the hat on again with more care than she has shown any piece of their travel plan.

“Now we're ready,” she says. ʸ

He picks up the small pack.

“I was worried the sea wouldn't recognise us.” ᴹ

She tips the brim down over his eyes.

* * *

The cruise boat is larger than yesterday's.

Yuka chooses a place near the side where she can see both the island and the people arranging lunch. Mau suspects the second view is more important to the decision than she admits. There is a shaded part further in, a little open deck and a captain who welcomes them before beginning the same practical explanation he has probably given every morning all season.

Mau listens until he understands where they will stop and how long they can stay in the water. Yuka listens while loosening the strap she has tied beneath her chin.

He looks at it.

She looks at him.

“It's uncomfortable.” ʸ

“The wind might want it.” ᴹ

“The wind can buy its own.” ʸ

He accepts the answer with the caution of somebody who may later be asked whether he could have prevented a tragedy.

* * *

They leave the harbour slowly.

From the water, the hotel becomes a cluster of windows half-hidden by the slope. Yuka finds their balcony, or a balcony sufficiently similar that she refuses to reconsider when Mau points out the angle. She waves anyway. Somebody on the wrong balcony waves back.

“See?” ʸ

“Conclusive.” ᴹ

The boat turns along the coast, gathering enough movement to change the sound beneath them. Yuka's hair escapes from one side of the hat. Mau catches the little tie before it can strike her face, holds it until she notices and lets her decide whether she wants it fastened.

She does not.

For some time the decision works perfectly.

They see a low arch in the rocks, a place where the water becomes clear enough to show the pale bottom and a collection of birds which appear to have chosen one disagreeable outcrop as a matter of principle. Yuka asks Mau which bird is in charge.

He chooses the largest.

“That's how you'd do it?” ʸ

“I'm told a hamster runs our foreign relations.” ᴹ

She laughs so suddenly that the hat lifts.

He reaches for it. She reaches too. Their hands collide in the exact place where the brim was, and the hat sails over the rail with the independence of something which has finally seen its chance.

Yuka stares.

Mau looks down.

The hat lands upright on the water.

For one absurd second it looks perfectly content.

* * *

He is halfway to making a very unnecessary decision when she catches his wrist.

“Don't.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“It's a hat.” ʸ

“A very important one.” ᴹ

“You are not jumping off the boat for a hat.” ʸ

He considers the water, the increasing distance and the captain who has already noticed their little disturbance. Then lets the idea go and raises one hand to explain what has happened.

The captain looks back at the small shape on the water.

Sighs with the weary familiarity of a man who apparently has this position in several marriages.

“We'll go round,” he says. ᶜᵃ

Yuka thanks him so earnestly that he begins smiling before turning back to the helm.

Mau rests both forearms on the rail.

“I'm glad we didn't arrange a funeral yet.” ᴹ

She puts her forehead against his shoulder.

The laughter which shakes her is so quiet at first that he mistakes it for dismay. Then she looks up, sees his face and gives up trying to preserve any dignity at all.

* * *

The hat returns at the end of a long-handled hook.

It is wetter than its original price suggests it ought to be. Yuka takes it with both hands and thanks the man who recovered it. Mau offers a bow to the hat itself, receiving a sharp look from his wife and an appreciative laugh from the couple at the next rail.

For the rest of the morning it sits between them, drying.

At lunch, Yuka gives Mau one of the best things on her plate without saying why. He eats it without making her explain. When she reaches for the hat again, he fastens the troublesome strap loosely enough that she can still breathe and carefully enough that she cannot accuse him of creating another form of imprisonment.

She tips her face up.

He lifts the brim, kisses her and puts it back.

“Improved design,” he says. ᴹ

“Slow learner.” ʸ

* * *

They stop in the cove after lunch.

Yuka leaves the hat in the shaded place before going down to the water. Mau looks back at it once, apparently so visibly that she takes his hand and tells him the staff have agreed to protect it from further political upheaval.

They swim only as far as they feel like swimming. Later she sits on the lower step with her feet in the water while he rests beside her, and they begin inventing the report Umaru will demand when they return.

“A naval incident,” Yuka suggests. ʸ

“An attempted escape.” ᴹ

“A successful rescue.” ʸ

“My contribution was prevented.” ᴹ

She rests her wet head against his arm.

“Your contribution is sitting here.” ʸ

He shifts until the edge of the step is more comfortable beneath them. She stays where she is.

Behind them, the hat falls off its chair.

Both turn at once.
