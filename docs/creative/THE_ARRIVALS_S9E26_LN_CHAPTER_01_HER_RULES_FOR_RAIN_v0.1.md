## Chapter 9 — Her Rules for Rain

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.1 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 56
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S9E26

Neither intimacy/pregnancy decision undone. No new construction/powers/credit spend/source memories invented. Old grey sight and injuries persist.

*When: B+116. Yuka wakes to rain against the balcony and immediately revises everything she said last night about getting up early.*

Mau is already awake. She discovers this after taking his arm and attempting to put it back around her without disturbing the rest of him.

“Morning,” he says. ᴹ

She closes her eyes.

“No.” ʸ

He feels her settle against his side, one leg finding its familiar place across his. The rain makes a steady noise beyond the glass. Somewhere down the corridor a door opens and closes, then the room becomes quiet enough that he can hear her reconsidering whether she is actually sleepy.

“Breakfast?” he asks. ᴹ

She lifts her head.

He smiles.

“Cruel,” she says. ʸ

“They bring it here.” ᴹ

Her head goes back down.

“A solution.” ʸ

* * *

They eat near the balcony with the door open only a little.

Yuka has wrapped the light blanket around her shoulders despite having insisted, while Mau was asking whether she wanted it, that she was perfectly comfortable. He leaves the successful argument unmentioned and takes the toast she offers him.

Across the courtyard, another couple has made a different decision about the weather. They begin crossing beneath one small umbrella, stop when the wind turns and attempt to exchange places without admitting that one of them is simply going to get wet. Yuka watches until they reach the covered path.

“We chose well,” she says. ʸ

Mau looks at the empty plates.

“A historic victory.” ᴹ

She holds out her cup for more tea.

He pours.

* * *

The cards were in the little pack all along.

Yuka finds them while looking for the paper she bought yesterday. She holds the packet up as if Mau has concealed an important amenity from her, then clears enough room on the table to deal.

“Which game?” he asks. ᴹ

“The one you keep losing at home.” ʸ

“You'll have to be more specific.” ᴹ

She laughs and begins explaining a revised version which takes account of there being only two of them. Mau listens. Asks one question. Receives an answer which appears entirely reasonable until the fourth turn, when Yuka explains a condition she has apparently forgotten to mention.

He looks at the cards she has just taken.

Then at her.

“A late amendment.” ᴹ

“You knew it worked like that.” ʸ

“With four people.” ᴹ

“And now you know it works with two.” ʸ

He leans back, considering the depth of the problem. Yuka smiles over her hand with enough innocence to make the situation unmistakable.

“I married into a criminal enterprise.” ᴹ

“Play your card.” ʸ

He does.

She takes that one too.

* * *

By the third game they have invented enough rules to make going home dangerous.

Yuka wants to teach Umaru. Mau explains that the two of them working together would make him an economically vulnerable citizen. She asks whether he is requesting a protection agreement and offers one on terms which somehow require him to bring her tea.

He goes to get the tea because he wants some too.

When he returns, she has found the paper and is writing a little account of yesterday's lost hat. She reads it to him. In her version he threatened to leap from a moving boat with such determination that the hat surrendered and came home by itself.

Mau sits down.

“I thought about retrieving it.” ᴹ

“With your entire body.” ʸ

“I hadn't selected a method.” ᴹ

She crosses out one phrase and replaces it with something longer.

He decides not to ask.

They make a page for the Fort together, with his drawings of the sea creatures from the afternoon before. Yuka identifies the crab immediately. The fish takes longer. She mistakes the bird for a spoon, which he considers unfair given the level of detail in the beak.

Her own bird is excellent.

He draws an arrow toward it and writes that it steals fruit.

She adds that the fruit had already been surrendered by its original owner.

* * *

The hotel will send ordinary letters with its next mainland dispatch. Yuka chooses to keep this page and take it home herself. She wants to see Ori's face when the story reaches the hat.

Mau folds the unfinished letter to Rimuru instead. They have not written anything urgent. There is no reason the news must arrive before they do, but he likes the idea of his brother opening something which has not come to ask for help.

He reads it once, removes the part about whether anything needs doing and leaves the part about the breakfast table in the pool.

Yuka notices the correction without looking at him.

“Tell him about the bird.” ʸ

“He'll take its side.” ᴹ

“He should have all the facts.” ʸ

He writes the bird in.

* * *

The rain eases after lunch.

For a while they stand together at the balcony, watching the first people venture toward the beach. Yuka has one hand on his chest and the other around her cup. Mau rests his chin lightly against her hair.

“We could go out,” she says. ʸ

“We could.” ᴹ

Neither moves.

After a little time she puts the cup down, turns in his arms and begins kissing him with an attention which makes the half-finished sentence unnecessary.

He holds her close. When she smiles against his mouth, he asks what has become funny.

“You still want to go outside?” ʸ

“I hadn't selected a method.” ᴹ

She laughs, takes his hand and leads him back to the place on the bed she claimed this morning. They lie down together without changing the decision they made two nights before, the blanket catching between them until Yuka pulls it free with an offended little sound.

By the time the next shower crosses the balcony, Mau has fallen asleep with her fingers tangled in his.

Yuka stays awake just long enough to decide that he owes her a rematch at cards.
