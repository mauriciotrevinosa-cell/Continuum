*When: B+117, before dinner. Mau has fastened his new shirt incorrectly and is waiting to find out whether Yuka will consider that a flaw in her choice or his.*

She comes out, looks at him and stops.

He looks down.

“I had a difficult button.” ᴹ

“You skipped one.” ʸ

“It was very difficult.” ᴹ

She crosses the room, unfastens the offending part and begins again from the bottom. He holds still while she works. When she reaches the place beneath his collar, she smooths it twice, the second time apparently for no practical reason at all.

“There.” ʸ

He looks at her dress. The one she kept touching at the shop, the skirt which moves a little after she does. She has put the flower bracelet on beside the wedding band he has begun noticing every time she reaches for him.

“You look beautiful.” ᴹ

Her hands stop.

“You said that before I came out.” ʸ

“I was making an informed prediction.” ᴹ

She smiles, stands on her toes and kisses him.

Then makes him wait while she checks the last button again.

* * *

The restaurant has a place for them where they can hear the water without having to compete with it.

Yuka sits facing the view until she realises Mau has sat opposite. Then she moves her chair around the small table and declares the arrangement improved. The attendant watches this happen with enough familiarity to suggest they are not the first couple to find the original design inconvenient.

Mau waits until she is comfortable before opening the menu.

“A difficult decision?” he asks. ᴹ

“I'm not wasting our last night.” ʸ

“We could ask what you liked best.” ᴹ

“I liked several things.” ʸ

“An argument I've heard somewhere.” ᴹ

She presses her knee against his beneath the table and orders the thing she has been pretending not to choose since they arrived.

He orders something else so they can share.

She looks pleased with him.

* * *

They talk about the trip while the first course disappears.

Yuka liked the little market better than she expected. Mau liked the millstream. She asks whether he means the water or the part where she stopped him retaliating for the splash. He admits the second part may have improved his opinion.

She steals something from his plate and calls it an exercise in honesty.

Beyond the open side of the restaurant, two children are trying to persuade an adult to let them walk further down the beach before the light goes. The adult agrees on the condition that they keep their shoes. They immediately begin negotiating which hand has to hold them.

Yuka watches for a moment.

Then looks toward Mau.

He waits instead of assuming she wants the conversation they already had.

“Ori will like her shell,” she says. ʸ

“It's a very good shell.” ᴹ

“You looked at six.” ʸ

“She had specific requirements.” ᴹ

Yuka rests her hand beside his on the table.

“You miss her.” ʸ

“A little.” ᴹ

“You can miss people while we're here.” ʸ

He looks up, uncertain whether she is teasing. She is smiling, but her fingers curl around his with a gentleness which answers the question before she does.

“I miss them too,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

The smaller child on the beach drops one shoe. The larger declares that this has somehow proven their entire argument.

Yuka laughs softly.

“We don't have to go home tonight,” she adds. ʸ

Mau lifts her hand and kisses it.

“I was hoping you'd say that.” ᴹ

* * *

The next plate gives them something else to argue about.

Mau has chosen more successfully. Yuka is willing to admit this provided he agrees not to make it a personality. He takes one bite of her choice and says he likes that one too, earning a look she has now given him in a clothes shop, a market and beside several breakfasts.

“You make decisions impossible.” ʸ

“You make very good decisions.” ᴹ

She folds her arms briefly, apparently refusing to accept the obvious attempt to escape a complaint.

Then uncrosses them because dinner is still here.

Halfway through, she asks what he would like to do when they get home.

Mau's first thought is a familiar little pile of things waiting for his attention. He can almost see where he has left each one. Then Yuka rests her chin on her hand and he understands the kind of answer she actually wants.

“I'd like to take you somewhere for dinner.” ᴹ

She looks down at the table.

Then at him.

“We can do other things.” ʸ

“A dangerous precedent.” ᴹ

“Mau.” ʸ

“I'd like to hear how the band sounds in the room now they've had time to complain about it properly. I'd like you to show me where you want the thing you bought today. And I'd like to have an ordinary morning without deciding we've used up all our permission to enjoy ourselves.” ᴹ

Her expression softens before she can hide it by reaching for her drink.

“I can arrange the morning,” she says. ʸ

“I thought you might.” ᴹ

* * *

They stay for dessert.

The plate is larger than Yuka expects, which leads to a negotiation in which Mau appears to receive half and actually receives all the pieces she wishes were slightly less sweet. He eats them with good grace until she notices what she has been doing and begins laughing.

“You should have said.” ʸ

“I like these.” ᴹ

She leans against his shoulder, defeated by the consistency of the answer.

Afterward they walk down to the water in the clothes she chose. Mau takes his shoes off before she can tell him to. The edge of her skirt brushes his leg when the little waves make her move closer, and he holds her hand while she tries to find their room among the lights above.

She chooses the wrong balcony again.

He agrees with her this time.

When she turns to ask whether he really thinks so, he kisses her instead. She lets the question go, puts her free hand against his chest and stays with him until the next wave reaches both their ankles.
