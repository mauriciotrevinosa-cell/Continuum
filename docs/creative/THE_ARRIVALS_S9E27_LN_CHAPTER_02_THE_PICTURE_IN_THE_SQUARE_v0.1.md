*When: B+119. The man drawing portraits in the square tells Yuka that her husband will have to stop looking at her for a little while if she wants both faces in the picture.*

Mau turns toward him.

“Like this?” ᴹ

“For a start.” ᴬʳ

Yuka begins laughing, which the artist accepts as an improvement until she has moved enough to make the angle useless. He waits with his pencil raised. The patience in his expression suggests this is a regular occupational difficulty.

Mau takes Yuka's hand beneath the little table.

She squeezes it, takes a breath and attempts to behave like a woman who has not just been told exactly what she hoped her husband had been doing.

The artist gets three useful strokes before she looks at Mau again.

* * *

They found him after buying something from a stall whose owner insisted the filling had to be tried while it was hot.

Yuka accepted this instruction with an enthusiasm Mau respected. They took their food to the edge of the square, where there was shade and a little space away from the carts. He saw the finished portraits first. She saw him looking, followed his attention and began asking the price before he had decided whether it was something she would want.

Now he sits beside her on a bench too narrow for them to pretend their shoulders are not touching. She has left one hand in his. The other keeps trying to smooth the same part of her skirt even after the artist asks whether she minds leaving it alone.

“I'm usually better at sitting,” she says. ʸ

“I can confirm that.” ᴹ

She turns her face toward him.

The artist lowers the pencil.

Mau apologises.

Yuka laughs again.

* * *

When the man finally lets them move, she is up before Mau has quite released her hand.

He follows more slowly, grateful that the artist has apparently found a way to include them despite their best efforts. The sheet is still pinned to the board. There is Yuka, the turn of her face which he knows and the little lift at one side of her mouth which usually means something unfair is about to happen to him.

Beside her is somebody he takes a moment to recognise.

The white part of the hair. The old mark near the eye. His shoulders turned a little toward her despite the artist's instructions.

He looks younger than Mau sometimes feels.

Yuka leans close to the paper.

“That's us.” ʸ

“I tried to make it so,” the artist says. ᴬʳ

She points at Mau's expression.

“That part especially.” ʸ

Mau looks again. The man on the page has forgotten to protect his happiness from being noticed.

He pays the agreed price and thanks the artist before the feeling can become too large to say simply.

The artist wraps the picture between two firm sheets for the journey.

Yuka makes Mau keep it out until they have found somewhere to sit and look at it once more.

* * *

At the cafe she spreads the little parcel on the table, opens one side and examines it while they wait for their drinks.

“Where does this go?” she asks. ʸ

“We haven't placed the bowl yet.” ᴹ

“Different question.” ʸ

“I wanted to understand the priorities.” ᴹ

She points at the space beside their window in a sketch which exists only in the air between her hands. He follows the explanation, supplies the shelf she has forgotten and discovers that she has already been thinking about how the afternoon light will reach it.

“There,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

She waits.

“It's a good place,” he adds. ᴹ

“You're allowed to have an opinion.” ʸ

He looks down at their joined hands in the drawing, mostly hidden because the bench and the table have done exactly what they intended.

“I'd like to see it when I wake up.” ᴹ

Yuka's face changes.

She moves the wrapped sheet clear of the drinks before answering.

“Then that's where it goes.” ʸ

* * *

Across the square, a young man turns a page he has stopped reading.

Yuuki has heard several accounts of the people living with Rimuru. Some contradict one another before they have reached the second sentence. A man who makes a sun. A man who commands dead soldiers. A dangerous stranger whom a city allowed into its festival and apparently failed to dispose of afterward.

The man leaving the cafe has just taken a wrapped picture from his wife because she wants both hands to adjust his collar.

Yuuki watches him bend his head to make it easier.

Beside him, Laplace shifts the parcel he has been carrying and turns his masked face toward the same part of the square.

“That one?” ᴸᵃ

“Possibly.” ʸᵘ

“Doesn't look busy conquering anything.” ᴸᵃ

Yuuki smiles faintly.

“People take days off.” ʸᵘ

He has no private account of the couple's journey, no invitation to their home and no reason to mistake a quiet afternoon for a full measure of the man. There are enough questions waiting in the public reports to make it worth remembering his face.

Yuuki marks his place and puts the paper away.

Across the square, Yuka has finished with the collar. She takes Mau's hand and draws him toward the next row of shops, apparently unsatisfied with the number of things they have already bought.

Laplace looks back toward their own unfinished errand.

“Shall we?” ᴸᵃ

Yuuki picks up his bag.

They leave by a different street.
