*When: B+120. Yuka catches Mau counting gifts and asks where he has put the things he chose for himself.*

He looks at the little collection laid out on the guesthouse bed.

Then at the shirt he is wearing.

“I'm using one.” ᴹ

“I chose that.” ʸ

“A very good choice.” ᴹ

She considers letting him escape through the compliment. He can see the possibility come and go. Then she folds the little cloth bag they have just emptied, gives it back to him and announces that they are going out again.

Mau looks toward the window.

It is barely past lunch.

He has time to become worried.

* * *

The market is busier now than it was this morning.

A cart has arrived from further inland, carrying things whose owners appear to be arguing about which place beside the square belongs to whom. Yuka leads Mau around the disagreement with the efficiency of someone who has already identified the stalls worth revisiting. He recognises one, then another, then begins to suspect she has been planning this for longer than the conversation on the bed suggests.

She stops beside a table of little wooden objects.

“Here.” ʸ

There are birds with moving wings, little boxes and a dragon which has apparently been made by someone who has heard several opinions about what dragons ought to look like without ever having seen one. Mau picks it up carefully.

Yuka watches his face.

“Kamish?” ʸ

“He'd demand an apology.” ᴹ

“Beru could explain.” ʸ

“Beru would demand two.” ᴹ

The woman behind the table laughs and asks whether they have very particular children at home.

Mau looks at Yuka.

She keeps her face perfectly straight.

“Some of them,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Yuka chooses the bird after he has put the dragon down.

It is a small thing which fits in her palm. One rounded body, a narrow head and wings that move when the little wooden lever beneath it is pressed. There is no string to climb and no complicated trick to learn. She tries it twice, watches the wings settle and holds it out to him.

“For you.” ʸ

He looks at it, then back at her.

“Are you sure it isn't for Ori?” ᴹ

“Ori has a bird.” ʸ

“So do you.” ᴹ

“And now you.” ʸ

He takes it. The lever catches against the wrong finger when he tries the first movement. Yuka adjusts his grip, letting the broad part of the little body rest where his hand can close comfortably. He presses again. Both wings rise.

She smiles at the disproportionate pleasure on his face.

“There.” ʸ

He gives her the bird back while she pays the ordinary price. Afterward she insists on presenting it properly, which requires him to turn away for three seconds and pretend he has no idea what she is holding.

Mau does his best.

It is apparently unconvincing enough to make the woman laugh again.

* * *

They take their time getting back.

Yuka finds a small packet of the spice Rem might enjoy trying, with the seller's ordinary instructions written clearly enough that someone can disagree with them later. Mau buys a length of good cord for the Fort after deciding that Umaru will always find a purpose for cord, most of them inconvenient to a dragon. They choose a little tin for Mai's small finished pieces and a notebook whose paper Qifrey can use without Mau having to explain anything about what might eventually be drawn on it.

The purse grows lighter. They stop buying before it has become a reason to pretend the things they already chose were not enough.

At a stall near the square, there is a game involving three rings and a row of sticks which look close enough to be easy.

Mau studies it.

Yuka notices immediately.

“You want to try.” ʸ

“It looks simple.” ᴹ

She gives the stallholder the small ordinary fee before Mau can improve his analysis. He takes the rings, feels their weight and lands the first around nothing at all.

Yuka puts a hand over her mouth.

The second hits the right stick and bounces away.

He looks at the last ring.

“A difficult tactical situation.” ᴹ

“You can do it.” ʸ

He cannot.

Yuka laughs so hard that the stallholder gives her one ring to try, perhaps hoping the effort will restore her ability to breathe.

She misses more impressively than Mau did.

He has to lean against the edge of the stall.

* * *

Back in the room, they pack the purchases together.

Yuka wraps the spice separately from the cloth things. Mau puts the tin inside the notebook parcel so it will not knock against the painted bowl. The little bird stays on the table until she asks whether he wants it put away too.

He presses the lever once more.

“In a minute.” ᴹ

She sits beside him and rests her cheek against his shoulder.

For a while they watch the wings rise and settle. Then she shows him a slightly different way to hold it, keeping the old stiff fingers out of the movement without making the thing feel awkward in his hand.

“Better?” ʸ

He nods.

She smiles and kisses the side of his face.

He puts the bird away last, in its own little space, where he will know exactly where to find it when they get home.
