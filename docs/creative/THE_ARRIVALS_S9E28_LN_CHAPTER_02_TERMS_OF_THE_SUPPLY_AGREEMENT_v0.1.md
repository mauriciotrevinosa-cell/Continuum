Mau looks up.

“You're the witness?” ᴹ

“I'm also receiving half the complaints if you get it wrong.” ʸ

“A significant public responsibility.” ᴹ

She moves closer until she can see the page without leaning over his arm. The little table in their room is barely large enough for the list, a cup and the drawing they made for the Fort. He stores the drawing before it can become a coaster.

Yuka takes the cup.

They are ready for government.

* * *

The old catalogue has not acquired anything new since the roof.

Mau is grateful for the familiarity. He can find the drinks now without stumbling past a weapon and wondering whether a quiet evening has somehow become a duty. The personal credit total is where he left it: eighteen thousand six hundred and thirty-six after the two cans they drank together.

Yuka points at Umaru's increasingly important circle.

“That's definitely the drink.” ʸ

“I wasn't going to buy a cake-shaped weapon.” ᴹ

“I'm checking.” ʸ

They begin with enough cans to let people at home try one without turning a treat into a competition. Yuka counts the groups she expects at the common table, gives up before making everyone a problem to be solved by arithmetic and agrees with Mau that a hundred and twenty is a sensible first quantity.

He puts it into the basket.

Then removes the extra zero he has nearly accepted.

Yuka watches him very carefully.

“A good witness,” he says. ᴹ

She takes the cup further away from his hand.

* * *

The ordinary exploration supplies take longer.

They choose the drinks people liked carrying, the bars which were actually eaten instead of left at the bottom of a pack and the little energy gels whose chief advantage is being small enough to remember. Mau pauses over the shakes because he cannot remember which flavour somebody objected to.

Yuka remembers.

He looks at her with renewed respect.

“You were there,” she says. ʸ

“I remember an argument.” ᴹ

“You were in the argument.” ʸ

“That explains the missing information.” ᴹ

She laughs, chooses a modest mixed selection and lets the biscuits become a less serious question. They can take several ordinary packets home. Nobody will need a new magical constitution to eat one after dinner.

At the end, Mau opens the basket before confirming it.

Yuka reads the quantities back to him. A hundred and twenty cans. Twenty-four of the other drinks. Forty-eight bars, twenty-four gels, twelve shakes. Twenty biscuit packets.

The page totals the existing prices. Five hundred and sixty-four credits.

He makes sure it is the personal account, checks the quantity once more and accepts.

* * *

The first box is enough to make Yuka sit back.

“Oh.” ʸ

Mau looks from the solid box beside the bed to the remaining delivery entries.

Then begins moving things into his item inventory before the room becomes an embarrassment they will have to explain to the owner.

She helps with the labels. Drinks together, food together, the things somebody may actually want in a dungeon marked clearly enough to avoid discovering halfway through a climb that they packed biscuits instead of the ration they intended.

When the final packet is stored, Mau checks the purchase record against what they received. The balance is eighteen thousand and seventy-two. The ordinary purse has not moved. Neither has the household's crystal stock, which would have made this a very different kind of gift to explain to Fern.

Yuka sits on the bed and begins laughing at the cleared floor.

“What?” ᴹ

“We spent half the evening supplying the house from a room they think two travellers are using.” ʸ

“Two very hungry travellers.” ᴹ

“We're going to arrive carrying one little bag.” ʸ

He looks at it.

She looks at him.

For a while neither can speak.

* * *

They leave the records where they can read them later and put the last ordinary purchases away.

Yuka has packed the new little bird separately from the Fort things because she has decided somebody will otherwise confiscate it under the claim of communal entertainment. Mau says this is a harsh assessment of their family.

She asks whether he intends to lend it to Milim.

He considers the question.

“A protective measure,” he agrees. ᴹ

Before bed, he sends Rimuru the little update they promised. They are on the final part of the route. They expect to arrive tomorrow before dinner, and neither of them has brought back a sea monster.

Rimuru's answer contains such obvious relief that Yuka asks Mau to tell him the important part too.

*We've addressed the supply problem,* Mau says. ᴹ

*Which supply problem?* ᴿ

Yuka begins laughing again.

Mau decides some diplomatic announcements deserve to be made in person.
