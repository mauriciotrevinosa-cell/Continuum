*When: B+122, late afternoon. Umaru sees the single small pack Yuka is carrying and looks at Mau with the expression of a nation which has discovered a serious failure in its import arrangements.*

“That's it?” ᵁ

Mau looks over his shoulder.

Kaisel has already returned to his shadow. There is no caravan concealed behind him, no ship waiting beyond the next turn and no obvious way to explain the lack of luggage without ruining the moment Yuka has apparently been looking forward to since last night.

He holds his hands out.

“We packed carefully.” ᴹ

Umaru looks at Yuka.

Yuka looks at the pack.

Then makes a very unsuccessful attempt to appear solemn.

* * *

The last part of the journey brought them home slowly enough to see the familiar roof before reaching it.

Yuka had leaned back against Mau when the tree rose above the other shapes. He had already sent the notice that they were close. Rimuru's answer was brief, ordinary and followed by the distant suggestion that someone had heard him laugh and wanted to know why.

Now the common door is open. People who are near it come out first; others will hear they have arrived when somebody remembers to tell them. Yuka is caught by Maki before she has finished putting the pack down. Mai takes her turn immediately afterward, complaining that the trip has apparently improved Yuka's ability to appear without warning.

“We sent a message.” ʸ

“I didn't receive a message.” ᴹᵃ

“You weren't the government.” ʸ

Mai looks toward the official of the Fort currently examining Mau for signs of inadequate snacks.

“A grave oversight.” ᴹᵃ

Mau has time to greet Yuta properly before Umaru resumes the inquiry.

This time Yuka tells her they should go inside.

* * *

The first box changes the atmosphere considerably.

Mau sets it on the table after asking for the space. Umaru watches him retrieve a second, then a third. Milim appears beside her halfway through the movement, apparently drawn by an administrative instinct which has nothing to do with the labels.

Rimuru arrives in time to see the cans.

Stops.

Looks at Mau.

“You found the drinks.” ᴿ

“They were on the list.” ᴹ

“Everything is on her list.” ᴿ

“An excellent system,” Yuka says. ʸ

Umaru has already read enough of the labels to begin calculating how the delivery ought to be received. Mau gives her the quantities, points out which things are for ordinary shared treats and which may be useful when people choose to go exploring. There are biscuits for the common table. Enough drinks for people to try without requiring the Fort to invent a border inspection.

Milim takes one can very carefully.

“Cold?” ᴹⁱ

“Better if we chill it,” Mau says. ᴹ

She looks toward the kitchen.

Umaru has a better idea and begins arranging the first little batch to be taken where the house already keeps things cool. Rem appears beside them, asks which end needs carrying and receives a remarkably detailed answer for a job involving a box.

Mau leans against the table beside Yuka.

“I think we passed.” ᴹ

“You,” Umaru announces, turning back toward him, “are the Fort's official vending citizen.” ᵁ

He considers the title.

“Do I have duties?” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᵁ

“An unfortunate appointment.” ᴹ

Yuka starts laughing before he can negotiate a salary.

* * *

Ori waits until the first excitement has moved toward the kitchen.

She comes to Mau with her hands behind her back and asks whether he saw the sea properly. He tells her they went beneath the surface a little, with somebody showing them where to look. Her eyes widen at that, and Yuka retrieves the drawing before Mau has to describe the crab without evidence.

Ori studies it.

Points at the bird.

“What's that?” ᴼ

Yuka puts a hand over her mouth.

Mau looks at the paper.

“A difficult subject.” ᴹ

He gives Ori the little shell and the string bird. She holds the shell to her ear after Yuka suggests it, frowns when she hears something which may or may not be the room and asks if Frieren has tried one before.

Frieren, coming through the turn with a book, says yes.

Ori takes the shell to her anyway, apparently unwilling to let experience remove the pleasure of showing her mother something new.

Mau watches them examine it together.

Yuka rests her hand against his back.

* * *

The gifts travel outward from the table.

Rem opens the little spice packet after reading the instructions. She asks Yuka how it tasted, and the answer becomes a conversation which draws Momo closer before anyone has decided whether something will be cooked tonight or another day. Mai examines the tin, tests the lid and calls it sensible, which Yuka receives as the compliment it is. Qifrey thanks them for the notebook and then asks about the trip instead of inspecting what they might expect him to do with the paper.

Mau is relieved to tell him about the boat.

By the time Beru hears that part, the lost hat has acquired sufficient importance to require his personal opinion. Yuka supplies hers first. Mau supplies an objection. Igris appears at the edge of the disagreement, takes one look at their faces and remains to hear the rest.

There is no formal report to give the whole room.

They tell the stories as people arrive to hear them, losing details, correcting one another and finding that the best parts are still funny when someone who was not there begins laughing.

* * *

Before dinner, Yuka finally takes Mau by the hand and tells everyone she is going to put her things away.

Umaru looks at his empty hands.

Then at the small pack.

“That shouldn't take long.” ᵁ

“It depends how much we've bought,” Yuka says. ʸ

Mau follows her toward their own door. Behind them the first cans begin arriving from the cool store, and Milim announces that someone should save one for Rimuru before the diplomatic situation becomes complicated again.
