*When: B+122, before the family meal. Yuka opens their bedroom door and stops long enough that Mau nearly walks into her.*

He puts a hand at her waist.

“What?” ᴹ

“Nothing.” ʸ

He looks past her. Their room. Their window. The bed she left slightly less neatly than she claimed on the morning they went away. Somebody has aired the room, then left the ordinary arrangement alone. There is no surprise reconstruction, no gift which requires them to find somewhere else to sleep while admiring it.

Yuka walks in and puts the little pack down.

“It's nice,” she says. ʸ

Mau closes the door behind them.

“The room?” ᴹ

“Coming back.” ʸ

He follows her to the bed and begins taking their things out of storage.

* * *

The portrait goes where they agreed.

Yuka holds it at the place beside the window while Mau looks from the pillow. He asks her to move it a little lower. She moves it too far and accuses him of giving imprecise instructions when he begins smiling.

They settle the matter by making him get up and hold it himself.

She looks from the bed.

“There.” ʸ

He waits for confirmation.

She gives none until he asks, because she has apparently decided the balance of power needs correcting after he laughed at her directions.

The bowl goes beside the bed. A little thing they have been moving from place to place since before the wedding finally has somewhere to belong. Mau puts it in the bowl. Yuka watches, says nothing and looks so triumphant that he takes the little thing out again for three seconds just to annoy her.

She takes it from him and puts it back.

“Improvement,” she says. ʸ

“A demanding marriage.” ᴹ

“You knew.” ʸ

He catches her hand as she tries to move away and draws her toward him.

“I had some indication.” ᴹ

She kisses him before he can add evidence.

* * *

Rimuru is in the shared sitting space when they come out.

He has put two cups beside the tea. Shizue is sitting near the little wooden home the shadows gave them, studying the way its door opens without disturbing the tree beside it. She looks up when Yuka begins telling her which part Beru considered essential.

Mau sits beside Rimuru.

For a little while they listen to the women discussing the model, the portrait and a bowl which has apparently solved an argument Rimuru did not know was happening.

Then Rimuru nudges Mau's arm.

“Good trip?” ᴿ

Mau looks toward his wife. She has one hand on the back of Shizue's chair and is explaining the bird incident with gestures which make his part look considerably less dignified than he remembers.

“Very.” ᴹ

Rimuru smiles.

“I liked the updates.” ᴿ

“There's a letter coming too.” ᴹ

“An emergency?” ᴿ

“The bird required a fuller account.” ᴹ

Rimuru begins laughing before Mau has explained which bird.

Shizue turns toward them, asks what she has missed and receives enough of the story that she has to set her cup down before hearing the end. Yuka crosses the little space to sit beside Mau, apparently unwilling to let him present a version in which she abandoned an innocent piece of fruit to its fate.

For a while the four of them accomplish nothing else.

* * *

They are nearly late for dinner.

The common room has made its own arrangements while they sat talking. There is a place for them, two glasses someone has found for the drink and enough voices to make the story Yuka begins at one end reach the other in a considerably altered form.

Umaru wants the official account of the hat.

Milim wants to know whether the boat could have gone faster.

Ori has put the little shell beside her plate and is consulting Frieren about whether it needs washing before she takes it to the Fort.

Mau eats while Yuka answers the question which can safely be answered first. Then Rem asks about something in the recipe, and the table becomes interested in a different problem, leaving him free to sit beside his wife and listen to the room they came home to hear.

Across the way, Shoko catches his eye and lifts one eyebrow toward the little glass he is holding.

He lifts it back.

She shakes her head, smiling, and turns toward whatever Gojo has just said with suspicious confidence.

Mau makes a private note to tell her tomorrow that the travel went well. Tonight she appears busy correcting a man who deserves it.

* * *

Later, their own door closes on the end of a conversation they can continue in the morning.

Yuka sits on the bed while Mau retrieves the little wooden bird from the place he kept apart from the shared gifts. He puts it near the portrait, presses the lever once to make sure the wings still move and hears her laugh softly behind him.

“You like it.” ʸ

“You chose it.” ᴹ

“That's not an answer.” ʸ

He turns the bird once between his hands.

“Yes.” ᴹ

She holds out her hand for him.

He puts the bird down and goes to her.

They turn the light off after she has told him one more thing she forgot to say on the journey. When the room is dark, Mau can still place the portrait beside the window. The painted bowl waits near enough to find. Outside, someone crosses the shared path laughing at a joke which reaches them only as sound.

Yuka settles against his side.

“Tomorrow,” she says sleepily, “we can put the rest away.” ʸ

He kisses her hair.

“There isn't much.” ᴹ

She makes a small, unconvinced noise.

Mau closes his eyes with his wife beside him and their unopened things still waiting for morning.
