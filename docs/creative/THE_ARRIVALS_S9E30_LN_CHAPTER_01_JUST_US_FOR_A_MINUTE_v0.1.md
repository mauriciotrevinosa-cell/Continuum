*When: B+123, late morning. Mai opens the tin Yuka brought her, discovers a small wrapped sweet inside and looks up with immediate suspicion.*

“What do you want?” ᴹᵃ

Yuka stops smiling.

“I brought you something.” ʸ

“You brought me a tin. The sweet is additional.” ᴹᵃ

“I can take it back.” ʸ

Mai closes the lid with her hand firmly on top.

“You can explain.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka sits beside her. For a little while she describes the market, the woman who was selling the tin and the little bird she chose for Mau. Mai listens while unwrapping the sweet, gives her an opinion about the bird and then waits through the silence which follows the easy part of the story.

Yuka looks toward the door.

“Can we talk about something?” ʸ

Mai puts the sweet down.

* * *

The words come out badly at first.

Yuka knows what happened. She was there. She knows Mau listened, that she listened too and that afterward he held her while they argued about a bowl with exactly the same tenderness he had brought into the part which made the conversation necessary. None of that makes telling Mai less embarrassing.

“We were going to,” she says, looking at her own hands. ʸ

Mai waits.

Yuka makes an impatient little gesture toward the empty room, annoyed that the sentence has somehow become more difficult because Mai has kindly refused to finish it for her.

Mai understands. Her expression changes only enough to let Yuka know she has been heard.

“And?” ᴹᵃ

“We stopped.” ʸ

“Did you want to stop?” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks up quickly.

“Yes. No. I wanted him. I just started thinking about everything afterward, and whether we were actually ready for that part.” ʸ

Mai sits back. Gives her space to complete the explanation.

Yuka tells her about the room on the second night, how easy it had been to forget everything except being there with him. How saying she was unsure had frightened her for one small moment, even though he had never given her a reason to expect the wrong answer.

“He agreed,” she says. “He wanted to talk about it too.” ʸ

“That sounds like him.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka nods. Her eyes have begun feeling warm in a way she finds deeply unreasonable for a story about a husband behaving as he ordinarily behaves.

Mai passes her the cup she has not touched yet.

* * *

“You don't have to tell me every detail,” Mai says. ᴹᵃ

Yuka immediately looks relieved.

“Good.” ʸ

“And if you're hoping for a great deal of personal experience, you've chosen poorly.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks at her.

Mai lifts one shoulder.

“I haven't either.” ᴹᵃ

“I wasn't—” ʸ

“I know. I'm saving us both the part where you think I have some secret handbook.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka laughs, embarrassed and grateful at once.

For a little time they drink. Mai finally tries the sweet, makes an expression which Yuka cannot interpret and breaks it in half so that she can apparently become responsible for her own opinion.

“Do you think we made it too big?” Yuka asks. “The decision.” ʸ

“Do you think it was small?” ᴹᵃ

“No.” ʸ

“Then probably not.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks down at the piece of sweet.

“I want a family with him.” ʸ

Mai's expression softens. She has heard enough of what Yuka wants to know that this part is not a honeymoon invention.

“You can want that and want time first.” ᴹᵃ

“He said he wants to understand his body more. And have his head feel steadier.” ʸ

“Sensible.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka nods slowly.

“I just don't want him to decide he has to become perfect before he's allowed anything good.” ʸ

Mai looks toward the door, as though the man in question might appear simply because someone has described his worst habit accurately.

“Then tell him that part when he starts doing it.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka begins smiling despite herself.

* * *

Mai does not tell her what the correct date ought to be.

She asks whether Mau made Yuka feel wanted after they stopped. Yuka answers so quickly that Mai holds up a hand, accepting the point before it becomes a much longer story than either of them requires.

“Then you talked. You both chose. That's allowed.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka presses her thumb against the edge of the cup.

“Even if we choose differently later.” ʸ

“Later will also require both of you.” ᴹᵃ

She considers the remainder of the sweet before setting it down.

“The part about his body matters,” she adds. “You aren't only deciding what happens to the two of you. If there's a third person afterward, they'll have to live with whatever the answer turns out to be.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka goes quiet.

Mai touches her wrist before the quiet can turn into a verdict.

“I didn't say that to frighten you.” ᴹᵃ

“I know.” ʸ

“Ask what can be understood. Enjoy being married while you do.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka looks at her. Something has eased in her face without quite becoming a smile yet.

“We were good at that part.” ʸ

“I saw the shopping.” ᴹᵃ

She laughs properly this time.

* * *

They leave the conversation in the room.

Yuka finishes her tea. Mai opens the tin once more and begins deciding which of her little finished pieces ought to go inside, apparently so the gift can become useful before either of them starts finding reasons to be embarrassed about the things they have said.

At the door, Yuka turns back.

“You won't tell Granny?” ʸ

Mai looks up.

“About the sweet?” ᴹᵃ

Yuka gives her a look.

Mai smiles faintly.

“It's your conversation.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka nods. Leaves with her empty cup and a little more room to think.

Mai waits until the footsteps have gone before taking the other half of the sweet back out of the tin.
