*When: B+123. Shoko asks whether Mau followed the part of her advice which involved resting, and Yuka's account of the rainy day answers the question before he can.*

“We accomplished very little,” she says. ʸ

“He'll have found something.” ˢᵒ

“I lost at cards.” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him.

“Repeatedly.” ᴹ

She considers this evidence of a promising holiday and goes back to the examination.

Mau has been telling her the ordinary truth. The journey tired him sometimes. They stopped. He slept well most nights, woke awkwardly once, knew where he was and let the room become familiar before trying to go back to sleep. There is no new wound hidden beneath his sleeve, no private fight he intends to reveal only after someone discovers the damage.

Shoko listens to the whole answer.

Yuka sits beside the place where he left his shirt and looks pleased enough that he begins worrying whether there is an argument he has forgotten agreeing to.

* * *

The check does not turn the old things new.

His left eye is still less clear at the edge. Two fingers still resist the movements which would be simplest if they straightened when he asked. The scars have travelled with him, along with the pale part of his hair and the quiet difference between the two hearts Shoko has learned to listen to separately.

But the journey did not take away what the weeks before it gave back. He can sit without planning around fresh damage. He can reach, turn, walk and admit that an afternoon has been long without treating the admission like proof that the day failed.

Shoko checks Ciel's present account through him and asks where the difficult night fell in the route. He tells her. She accepts the difference between a difficult night and a return to being unable to recognise the room.

Then asks what he wants to do next.

Mau looks toward Yuka.

“Ordinary training again,” he says. “The things I already know. With someone there.” ᴹ

“How ordinary?” ˢᵒ

He begins smiling.

Shoko waits until the smile becomes a useful answer.

“Short sessions first. No new domain combinations. No asking Gojo whether we can stop having limits.” ᴹ

“A difficult sacrifice.” ˢᵒ

Yuka makes a soft, approving sound beside him.

Shoko agrees they can begin with that much and review what actually happens instead of negotiating next month's dramatic injuries in advance.

* * *

He has almost put his shirt on when she notices he is still deciding whether to ask something.

“Say it.” ˢᵒ

Mau looks up.

Yuka takes his hand.

“We talked about children while we were away,” he says. ᴹ

Shoko's expression changes before he can add the important part.

“Not now,” Yuka says quickly. ʸ

Shoko looks between them.

Mau laughs once, embarrassed by how fast the room became interested.

“We decided we want time first. Being married. Understanding this.” He touches his own chest lightly. “I don't even know what part of me counts as something a child could inherit.” ᴹ

Shoko does not pretend the question is easier because they have asked it gently.

She asks which part worries him most.

Mau looks at his hand before answering.

“Something being made while I don't know it's being made. Someone else having to carry the pieces I can't put away.” ᴹ

Yuka's grip firms around his.

Shoko lets him finish before she speaks.

“We can keep learning about your body,” she says. “That won't let me promise an answer about everything it might pass on.” ˢᵒ

He nods. He had not expected a promise; hearing the limit honestly still makes it easier to breathe.

“DIO may understand more of the constructed routes,” he says. ᴹ

“Then ask her what she can actually tell you.” ˢᵒ

Yuka looks at him, then back at Shoko.

“Without turning it into something we have to decide immediately.” ʸ

“You just told me you chose time,” Shoko answers. “Use some.” ˢᵒ

* * *

Outside, Yuka stays close enough that their hands remain joined even when the narrow turn makes them walk one behind the other.

Mau looks back when they reach the open part of the path.

“Too much?” ᴹ

She knows which question he means. Shoko's room has not been given the details of the night at the inn. They asked about a future possibility together, leaving the rest where it belongs.

Yuka shakes her head.

“I wanted to ask too.” ʸ

He nods.

She stops walking, which makes him stop with her.

“And I want you to remember something.” ʸ

“What's that?” ᴹ

“We chose to wait. We didn't choose to spend the waiting miserable.” ʸ

He looks at her for a moment.

Then starts smiling.

“I had a very good two weeks.” ᴹ

“So did I.” ʸ

She lifts their joined hands and presses a kiss against his fingers before pulling him toward the common room. Someone has begun making lunch. Yuka has recognised the smell and apparently decided that keeping the waiting pleasant should begin as soon as possible.
