*When: Umaru examines the basket and asks why there is so much food, Mau looks toward Taihei without saying anything at all.*

Taihei folds his arms.

Yuka takes the basket from Mau and gives it to Rem before the silent argument can acquire witnesses.

“There's enough for two,” she says. ʸ

“Two expeditions,” Taihei answers. ᵀᵃ

Rem looks inside.

Then up at Umaru.

“We could stay until dinner.” ᴿᵉ

Mau lifts one hand toward her, apparently presenting conclusive evidence.

Yuka pushes it down.

* * *

They leave while the afternoon is still warm.

There is no line of people at the door to see them off. Suika knows Umaru will be back later. Ram has heard the part Rem wanted to tell her and asked only whether she intends to take the longer path. The Fort has work of its own, involving a little wooden bird whose climbing mechanism has already become an engineering dispute.

Umaru tells Rem about that while they walk.

Rem listens, asks one question which makes the dispute sound even less dignified and laughs when Umaru attempts to restore the reputation of the institution.

By the time they reach the garden, the first awkwardness has become something they can carry without examining every step.

Umaru finds the place Mau meant. A low patch beneath the shade, clear of the path, near enough to the common buildings that neither has to invent a reason to be frightened of being alone there. Rem puts the basket down. They spread the cloth between them.

For a moment both look at the food.

Then at each other.

“He did pack a lot,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

Umaru gives up defending him.

* * *

Rem pours the drinks while Umaru begins explaining the game she brought.

It is simple. Rem can tell because the first explanation takes less than a minute and none of the rules appears to have been written over an older rule in a different hand. Umaru deals with the unusual seriousness of somebody who has become aware that this is not quite the same afternoon they would have had if she had merely called Rem over to play.

Rem looks at her first hand.

Then sets it down.

Umaru looks up immediately.

“Something wrong?” ᵁ

“No.” ᴿᵉ

She smiles a little.

“I just wanted to say I'm glad you asked.” ᴿᵉ

Umaru looks at her.

“You asked.” ᵁ

“You were going to.” ᴿᵉ

Umaru's face becomes warm enough that she looks down at the cards as if the answer to this particular problem may have been printed there.

Rem lets her have a moment.

Then takes a bite of the food instead of filling it with an apology.

* * *

They play two rounds.

Umaru wins the first. Rem wins the second after doing something Umaru has explained badly enough to believe it could not possibly become a threat. The resulting argument is so familiar that both relax into it before noticing they have.

Rem begins gathering the cards.

Umaru reaches to help. Their hands meet over the little pile.

Neither moves away straight away.

Rem looks up.

“I keep finding reasons,” she says. ᴿᵉ

Umaru blinks.

“To take your hand. Show you something. Go somewhere.” ᴿᵉ

The admission comes slowly, but she does not look down this time. Umaru can see that she is nervous. That makes it easier to answer without pretending she has somehow arrived more certain than the woman in front of her.

“You can just take it,” Umaru says. ᵁ

Rem's fingers settle around hers.

Umaru looks at the joined hands for a moment, then back at her face.

“I think that's the answer I have so far.” ᵁ

Rem smiles, small and unmistakably happy.

“I like that answer.” ᴿᵉ

* * *

They leave the cards alone for a while.

There is food to share, a little breeze which keeps moving the edge of the cloth and a bird which Umaru informs Rem may be part of a wider pattern of attacks against household travellers. Rem asks for the evidence. Umaru gives her Yuka's account of the hotel thief, then begins constructing the argument that the Fort requires somebody to investigate.

“Do you want a holiday?” Rem asks. ᴿᵉ

Umaru looks wounded.

“I want justice.” ᵁ

Rem laughs and passes her the last thing she has been pretending not to want.

After eating, they walk the longer part of the garden together. Rem keeps Umaru's hand without needing a turn in the path as a reason. Umaru talks about something she would like to try with the games at home, asks what Rem would like to do next time and waits through the surprise the question puts on her face.

“Next time?” ᴿᵉ

Umaru's hand tightens a little.

“If you want one.” ᵁ

Rem nods.

“I want one.” ᴿᵉ

They go slowly enough that the basket has become much lighter by the time they return for it.

* * *

Mau is at the common table when they bring it back.

He looks at the empty space, then toward Taihei, who has apparently chosen the least advantageous moment to come through the door.

“Enough food?” Mau asks. ᴹ

“Just enough,” Umaru says with perfect loyalty. ᵁ

Rem tries to keep a straight face.

Fails.

Yuka reaches past her husband for the cups, thanks them for returning the good ones and asks whether they would like to sit before the evening meal. Rem says yes. Umaru takes the place beside her without apparently considering any other arrangement.

Nobody asks them to announce what the afternoon has made them.

Mau gives Taihei a deeply irritating smile instead, and Yuka has to remove the empty basket from between them before the brothers can turn lunch into a precedent.
