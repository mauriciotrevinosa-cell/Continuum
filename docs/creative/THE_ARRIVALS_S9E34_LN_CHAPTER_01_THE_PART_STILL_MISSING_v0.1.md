*When: Noise brings the smaller drawing to the table, Mau recognises the old shop window in one corner and his own body in the middle.*

He is not certain which resemblance makes him less comfortable.

Rimuru sits beside him. Ciel has chosen to attend with her attention here instead of trying to keep half the ordinary house in order at the same time. The place is the shared inner room they know, not somebody else's private space opened because a difficult question has arrived.

Noise waits until Mau is looking.

Then points at the line which stops the drawing growing.

“Two hundred,” it says. ᴺ

Mau nods.

“I remember.” ᴹ

* * *

They begin with the holiday agreement.

The larger constructions stayed still. Noise kept its observations and drawings; the ordinary work which let Mau keep moving, using known abilities and living in his own body continued. The food page did not require somebody to invent a new shop in the middle of their trip.

Mau thanks it for keeping that distinction.

Noise moves the drawing closer.

“Now?” ᴺ

“Now we talk about which part.” ᴹ

The dark shape looks toward Ciel. She settles beside the image, pleased enough with its impatience that Mau can feel her amusement without hearing a private second conversation.

*The limit belongs to the allocation structure,* she says. *Your body has not stopped being capable of changing because the window stopped offering another number.* ᶜⁱ

Mau looks at the old line.

He remembers reaching it with every point already spent, already needing more than the room had decided to allow him.

“And this removes it?” ᴹ

Noise puts one hand on the unfinished section.

“Not yet.” ᴺ

* * *

Ashborn joins because they ask him.

Mau tells him which question is on the table before the guest decides whether to sit. It concerns the inherited power route he has already offered as a bounded reference, not the life before it or the private memories behind his own door.

Ashborn studies the small image.

Then points at a connection Noise has left unjoined.

“That was not meant to be a second prisoner,” he says. ᴬˢ

Mau looks up.

“It won't be.” ᴹ

The guest nods and lets Ciel show the difference between a reservoir, the path through which it can supply a body and the body which has to survive receiving it. The scale of the source does not make every passage into it equally wide. It does not turn cursed energy into a second name for mana. It does not finish Mau's eyes merely because something behind them may one day carry a greater load.

Noise has been working on the missing relation, using the offered inheritance reference, the observed light patterns and the parts of Mau it is allowed to know.

It has not been using a guest's private room as a supply closet.

Mau asks the question explicitly anyway. Ashborn answers without offence. The separated material which caused their earlier argument remains separated. This drawing has a source they can name and permission whose edges the guest can still change.

Rimuru listens through the answer before asking about the piece in the middle.

* * *

The new component is intended to let the existing power become Mau's without accepting the Architect's idea of where Mau is required to end.

Noise can make an isolated part first. Let Ciel compare the response against the body they actually have. Stop before it becomes a permanent attachment, before a drawing turns into a change they can no longer undo by closing the page.

Mau looks at the unfinished outline.

“If you finish it too early?” ᴹ

Noise closes one hand sharply.

Ciel supplies the part it has not found words for yet.

*The route could demand more than the receiving body can hold. More power does not fix the damage caused by admitting it badly.* ᶜⁱ

Rimuru looks toward Mau.

Mau keeps his hands on the table.

“Then we don't start by finishing it.” ᴹ

* * *

They agree to a smaller beginning.

One isolated interface component. Its own test space and an actual record of what it draws. Tomorrow's measurement will use the known body and short, familiar operations, with the unfinished route held apart. Nobody will stop his heart to discover whether a diagram can argue with death. Nobody will treat the first good result as permission to attach every missing thing at once.

Mau asks Shoko to be included in the ordinary physical part, through the account he chooses to give her. She will need to know what they are trying and when; she does not need access to Ashborn's history to watch Mau move his own hand.

Ciel accepts the scope. Noise touches the smaller part of the drawing.

“This.” ᴺ

“This first,” Mau agrees. ᴹ

The old cap is still there when the image settles. Two hundred. No new points waiting beneath it. No message declaring that the work has been completed because everybody at the table finally understands what it might mean.

Noise begins arranging the little pieces it is now allowed to make.

* * *

After Ashborn goes back to his own quiet, Rimuru stays a little longer.

He watches Mau look at the unfinished connection.

“You wanted to ask if we could do it faster.” ᴿ

Mau looks toward him.

Then laughs, caught.

“A little.” ᴹ

“I know.” ᴿ

“I didn't ask.” ᴹ

Rimuru puts a hand against his shoulder.

“I noticed that too.” ᴿ

Outside the room, Yuka has apparently finished the thing she could not describe earlier. Her question reaches Mau through their familiar connection, offering him the choice to come and see rather than announcing she has been waiting for permission to begin her own evening.

He answers that he is coming.

Noise looks up from the little piece.

Mau smiles at it.

“Tell us when you're ready.” ᴹ

The dark shape nods and goes back to the work they chose.
