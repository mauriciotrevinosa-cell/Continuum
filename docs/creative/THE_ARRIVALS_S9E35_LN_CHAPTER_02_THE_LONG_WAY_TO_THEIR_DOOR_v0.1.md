*When: Shoko tells Gojo he has chosen the longer way home, he looks down the path as if it has arranged itself without consulting him.*

“Has it?” ᴳ

“We can see our door from here.” ˢᵒ

“Excellent visibility.” ᴳ

She stops walking.

He stops with her, smiling now because the excuse has apparently done all the work he expected of it.

Shoko folds her arms.

“You could have asked for a walk.” ˢᵒ

“Would you have said yes?” ᴳ

She takes his hand and begins going the longer way.

* * *

They pass the common window, where somebody has begun clearing the meal without allowing it to become one person's permanent occupation. A voice calls that the last cloth has been found. Another answers that it was exactly where the first person put it.

Gojo looks delighted by the evidence that incompetence can flourish without his assistance.

Shoko tells him not to take notes.

He asks whether she enjoyed the wedding. She says yes. He asks whether she enjoyed the part where he danced with her. She gives him a look which ought to be sufficient, then answers yes again because apparently he needs the practical encouragement.

“The boy's a bad influence,” she adds. ˢᵒ

“I taught him everything.” ᴳ

“I watched you ask which hand went where.” ˢᵒ

“A precise teacher.” ᴳ

She begins laughing. He does not spoil it by improving the explanation.

* * *

At the quiet turn beyond the garden, she asks whether they are walking anywhere in particular.

Gojo looks at the path ahead.

Then at their joined hands.

“Not particularly.” ᴳ

She studies his face for a moment. There are days he has reasons for being quieter than usual, days she knows which question will make the smile work harder. Tonight he seems to have no secret errand beneath the walk. He wanted her to keep holding his hand after dinner and selected a method almost ridiculous enough to count as honest.

She can work with that.

“You should get better at asking,” she says. ˢᵒ

“I've had encouraging results.” ᴳ

“Despite the approach.” ˢᵒ

He turns toward her, still holding her hand, and asks whether she would like to have tea with him when they get home.

Shoko considers the improvement.

“That was almost normal.” ˢᵒ

“I can try again.” ᴳ

“Don't risk it.” ˢᵒ

* * *

They make the tea together.

His part requires slightly more supervision than she considers appropriate for a man who has been claiming mastery over several difficult things all evening. When she tells him so, he offers to describe those difficult things in more detail.

She declines.

They sit until the cups have gone cool. The separate doors remain where they chose them, with no need to turn the evening into a decision about what somebody else's marriage says they ought to want next.

Shoko stands first.

Gojo begins saying goodnight.

She catches his collar before the sentence is finished and kisses him, unhurried enough that he forgets the rest. When she lets go, the surprised quiet on his face is considerably more pleasing than the several explanations he could have offered instead.

“Ask me again tomorrow,” she says. ˢᵒ

He looks at her.

“For tea?” ᴳ

She smiles and goes toward her own door.

He is still smiling when he remembers the cups.
