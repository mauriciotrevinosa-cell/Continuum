“The travellers are alive,” Igris says. ᴵᵍ

Mau nods once. Beside him, Yuka stops folding the cloth she has been trying to put away since yesterday.

“What happened?” she asks. ʸ

The knight looks toward the ordinary driver waiting beyond the common door.

“Someone wants an answer.” ᴵᵍ

* * *

The twelve soldiers left yesterday after the road observation had been agreed.

They did not enter houses or decide the merchant's paperwork for him. They watched the turn Hollis named, found the place the paper had been shown and stayed far enough apart that a difficulty on one part of the road did not place every eye inside the same trap.

The armed group had gone.

Somebody else had been waiting.

Two forms broke before the company completed its withdrawal. The links remained. Mau can feel the place where the ordinary soldiers are still his, waiting for the familiar work which will give them a shape again. Ten returned by the route Igris had prepared.

No new soldier has taken the place of either lost form. No named officer has been quietly removed from a large total to make the report easier.

Mau listens to Igris give the account, asks which part the soldiers actually saw and lets Ciel compare the brief impressions they brought back with the signature she knows from his own memories.

He has already begun to recognise it.

That does not make hearing the name easier.

* * *

Rimuru comes before the driver begins.

The man has been offered a chair and something to drink. Jinshi has the route page, but nobody makes him begin with dates while his hands are still trying to become steady around the cup.

He saw a man standing beside the empty wagon.

One movement. A seam appearing through wood which had not yet understood it had been cut. The body of the wagon coming apart without a blade being drawn.

“I thought he'd cut me too,” the driver says. ᴰʳ

Mau keeps his hands flat on his own knees.

“Did he touch you?” ᴹ

The man shakes his head.

He was told to take a letter to the settlement. If he refused, the next message would travel another way. He does not know whether that meant another driver or the people waiting beyond the next bend.

Rimuru tells him he is not being blamed for bringing it.

The driver nods so quickly that the relief is painful to watch.

* * *

There is no complicated trap in opening the paper.

They check before doing so. A material page with writing and the remaining trace of the person who chose to leave it in the driver's hands. No contract becomes binding because Mau reads his own name. No person in the house has been volunteered by a sentence an enemy wrote.

Mau recognises the arrogance before reaching the signature.

Sukuna wants to fight him.

Not the old child who ran at a confrontation he could barely understand. Not a rumour about a sun told in a market by somebody who had not been there to see it. The man who came back from a place built to make him grow and then returned to a house full of people he still insists on protecting.

The invitation is written as though there is only one reasonable answer.

If Mau will not provide an interesting fight, there are roads he cannot stand on all at once. Cities beyond his little protected circle. A slime's city Sukuna has heard enough about to recognise.

The final line asks whether Mau intends to make someone else answer for his refusal.

Yuka reads it once.

Then puts the paper down very carefully.

* * *

Mau's first movement is toward the door.

It stops before becoming a step.

Rimuru has not reached for him. Yuka has not ordered him to sit. He looks at the page, at the frightened driver and at the ordinary room which has suddenly acquired a conversation nobody wanted this morning.

“We talk,” he says. ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

“Together.” ᴿ

Mau looks toward Yuka.

She is still looking at the last line.

“He knows exactly what he's doing,” she says. ʸ

Mau does not try to make the observation less true.

* * *

They let the driver finish his drink before asking whether he wants to remain for the afternoon.

Jinshi will help with the broken wagon account. Hollis can be contacted through a route which does not require the same man to go back and discover whether delivering the first letter has made him responsible for a second. Igris withdraws the remaining road company from the exposed turn until the actual people using it can be warned.

Mau tells him to restore the two broken forms when there is room to do it properly. The familiar mana cost belongs to his own army. He acknowledges it before beginning, instead of letting the large source behind it become a reason not to notice what he is spending.

Yuka stays beside him while the ordinary work is done.

Only afterward does he take the letter to the people who need to read it.
