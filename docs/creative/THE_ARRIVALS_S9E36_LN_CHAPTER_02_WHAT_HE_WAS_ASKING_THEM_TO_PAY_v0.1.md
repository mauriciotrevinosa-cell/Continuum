*When: Gojo finishes reading the letter, he turns it over as though the other side might contain a less irritating explanation of the handwriting.*

There is nothing on the back.

Shoko watches him put it down.

Mau sees the way his thumb remains against the fold for a moment after the movement should have ended. Then Gojo removes the hand, leans back and looks at the people gathered around the table.

“He's become more formal,” he says. ᴳ

The joke lands in a room which knows why it has been offered.

Nobody laughs very much.

* * *

They begin with the people outside.

Rimuru wants the road warning sent before the next delivery leaves. Testarossa needs the actual marker, the driver's account he has agreed can be shared and enough of the challenge to understand why the threat names Tempest. Jinshi has already begun finding which consignments can wait without leaving another household short of something it needs.

Ram asks whether the missing road group was connected to the letter.

Mau says they do not know. The obstruction vanished before the soldiers saw Sukuna. That is all the observation gives them.

Anko wants to know whether he can be made to stay in one place.

“Not because I send a letter back,” Mau says. ᴹ

Diablo suggests that people who make threats can sometimes be persuaded to regret the time they spent delivering them. Rimuru accepts the point, then asks whether anybody here has a reliable location to which that persuasion can be sent.

Nobody invents one.

* * *

Milim offers herself before the problem has finished becoming a plan.

She does not make a speech. She puts both hands on the table, looks at the signature and says she can go with them when they find him.

Mau looks at her with an affection which does not answer the practical question.

Gojo answers that part first.

“Finding him together and choosing how to fight him are different things.” ᴳ

She turns toward him.

“He's one person.” ᴹⁱ

“Who makes very good use of what the second person in a fight wants to protect.” ᴳ

His tone has changed just enough that Shoko looks up properly.

Yuta asks which of Sukuna's present tools they actually know. Mau gives him the answer in two parts: the man he remembers from each life and what they have evidence of today. He does not smuggle the old route's cost into this version of the enemy because the memory is clearer than the facts outside the house.

Gojo sits very still through that distinction.

* * *

“We don't know what he's added,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

“No.” ᴹ

“Or what he means by an interesting fight.” ᴹᵃ

Mai is looking at the line beneath the threat, where the invitation has apparently been written by a man who expects the rest of the world to understand why his amusement matters.

“He means one he might lose,” Gojo says. ᴳ

For a moment his smile becomes almost familiar.

Then his gaze shifts to the fold in the paper again.

Maki asks about the army. Mau can place people on known routes, warn the cities they can reach and give an escort to those who want one. He cannot make every unprotected village a room inside DIO's ward. He cannot place a destination at a place nobody has found merely because a dangerous man may choose to stand there tomorrow.

Rimuru has people who can help. So does Tempest. That will matter whichever answer Mau gives.

It does not make the threat imaginary.

* * *

Yuka has been quiet long enough that Mau turns toward her before speaking again.

She looks at the page.

“He wrote it like the people on those roads belong to a game,” she says. ʸ

Nobody interrupts.

“He knows Mau won't want to leave them there. So he makes protecting them the price of giving him what he wants.” ʸ

Her voice is steady. The hand against Mau's knee is not.

“I hate that he knows.” ʸ

Mau puts his hand over hers beneath the table.

She turns it and holds his fingers, accepting the contact without pretending it has resolved the situation.

* * *

They do not finish by signing the first thing the enemy offered.

Rimuru asks for a way to hold the actual conversation somewhere chosen by both sides, with conditions made clear before anyone takes a step toward a fight. If Sukuna wants a measured opponent, he can answer questions about what he intends to bring. If he wants Mau in one place, the people he has threatened will need more than the hope that he briefly becomes distracted.

Mau agrees.

He will not send the frightened driver back. Igris can carry an ordinary reply as a declared messenger, with instructions to withdraw rather than force a meeting if the route cannot be used safely. A reply proposing terms is not the same as promising his life on whatever terms arrive next.

Gojo looks at him when he says it.

For a moment Mau thinks he may object.

Instead he nods once, slowly, and reaches for the letter again.

* * *

Outside, the house has kept the meal moving.

There are people who know a road has become dangerous without being handed the whole adult discussion at once. The Fort has not been assembled to hear how an enemy wrote about civilians. Children can ask the adults caring for them what changes affect their own day and receive an answer which does not require them to become spectators before a fight even exists.

Mau goes through the common door with Yuka beside him.

She stops where the path becomes quiet.

He turns toward her.

“Walk with me,” she says. ʸ

He takes the hand she offers.
