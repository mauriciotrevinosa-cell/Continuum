*When: they reach the quiet part of the garden, Yuka lets go of Mau's hand only to put both arms around him.*

He holds her before asking anything.

For a little while there is the familiar pressure of her cheek against his chest and the sound of people somewhere beyond the path, going on with the afternoon because nobody at the table has asked the whole house to stop living until an enemy makes his next decision.

Yuka's fingers tighten in his shirt.

Mau rests his face against her hair.

“I'm here,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

He keeps holding her.

* * *

She lifts her head when she is ready.

There are tears in her eyes, which makes his first instinct so immediate that he nearly begins apologising before understanding what he would be asking her to forgive.

She sees the movement.

“Don't.” ʸ

He stops.

Yuka takes a breath, presses her forehead against his for a moment and moves back enough to see his face properly.

“I love that you care,” she says. “I love that you asked whether the driver was hurt before you looked at the paper. I don't want to marry you and then spend the rest of our lives asking you to stop being the person I married.” ʸ

Mau listens. The words are harder to receive than a simple order would have been, because there is nothing in them he can solve by promising to obey.

“I hate that he knows how to use it,” she says. ʸ

He nods slowly.

“So do I.” ᴹ

* * *

They sit beneath the shade after a while.

Yuka stays close enough that her knee rests against his. Mau puts their joined hands between them, where neither has to decide whether holding on will interrupt a difficult sentence.

“Are you going to fight him?” she asks. ʸ

He looks toward the path before answering.

“I want to stop him getting to choose the next person who pays for this. I think he knows I'd rather put myself in front of him than keep finding letters beside broken carts.” ᴹ

“That isn't the answer.” ʸ

He looks back at her.

“I haven't agreed to his terms. I want to hear what he actually proposes. And I want us to know which choices we're being asked to give up before I say yes.” ᴹ

She watches his face, checking the difference between a decision shared before it is made and a decision made privately which he has brought her to endure.

He lets her look.

“If there's a better way,” he adds, “I want it too.” ᴹ

Yuka nods, once.

* * *

“I don't want you to go back to thinking the only useful part of you is the part which comes home broken,” she says. ʸ

He closes his eyes briefly.

The portrait beside their window. The little bird she chose because she wanted him to have something of his own. Their argument about a bowl which has apparently become a remarkably effective household reform.

None of those things requires him to be broken first.

“I had a very good holiday,” he says softly. ᴹ

She laughs once through the tears, startled by the answer.

“You did.” ʸ

“I want another one. I want you getting annoyed because I left a cup somewhere you already explained to me. I want to hear whether Rem's next date needs that much food. I want ordinary things with you, Yuka.” ᴹ

Her grip tightens.

“Then keep wanting them while we work out the difficult part,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

She puts her hand against his cheek.

He turns into it before he has thought about the movement.

* * *

They stay until the first sharpness has become something she can breathe around.

Mau does not offer a promise that he cannot guarantee. He has learned what it costs her to hear a grand answer from someone who believes he is being kind by leaving the fear for later. She has learned that asking for the honest version sometimes means sitting beside a question instead of being given something reassuring to carry home.

“Tell me the terms when they come,” she says. ʸ

“All of them.” ᴹ

“Even the part you think would ruin dinner.” ʸ

“Especially that part.” ᴹ

She studies him for a moment.

Then leans in and kisses him, slow enough that he feels the decision in the contact instead of mistaking it for a way to stop talking.

When she draws back, he rests his forehead against hers.

“We still get dinner,” she says. ʸ

He laughs quietly.

“I was hoping.” ᴹ

* * *

On the way back, she asks whether he is going to tell her which part of the training she got right yesterday or simply keep explaining how dangerous his wife is.

He looks at her.

“I thought that was a technical assessment.” ᴹ

“It was a very broad one.” ʸ

He begins describing the way she moved Blue behind her own heel, the ordinary hand movement she used after and the point at which he understood that she had made him look in the wrong place.

Yuka listens closely.

By the time they reach the common door, she has one correction to his account, two ideas for trying it differently and a hand still firmly linked with his.
