*When: Igris returns with an answer, Mau is in the common room with Yuka. He leaves the folded page closed until Rimuru has joined them.*

The knight has followed the instructions they agreed. He carried a reply, declared the purpose of his approach and kept the route back. Nobody has counted bringing paper as consent to remain for a fight. The soldiers assigned to ordinary road work have not become an honour guard for somebody else's amusement.

Mau thanks him before asking about the answer.

“He wants the conversation,” Igris says. ᴵᵍ

Rimuru pulls a chair closer.

Yuka stays beside Mau while he opens the fold.

* * *

Yesterday was spent on the things the threat had not made less necessary.

Warnings carried by the routes they already knew. A message to Tempest explaining what the driver had seen, what Mau had been asked and which parts still belonged to speculation. Jinshi helping with the wagon account. The house continuing its meals while the people who could help took actual work instead of waiting for one man to become the whole answer.

Mau's reply did not accept a deathmatch.

It offered a place to discuss one, with no civilians present, no right to attack the people who came to speak and no permission to follow them when the conversation ended. Any binding version of those conditions would need the actual participants to accept it. The letter named the position outside the protection where a first discussion could be seen from a safe distance without carrying the fight through their door.

It also said something which had made Yuka look at him for a long time before she let the ink dry.

If he beat Sukuna, he intended to try raising him as a shadow.

Mau had left the sentence there.

* * *

Sukuna's answer has less patience for ordinary paragraphs.

He accepts a conversation tomorrow. He objects to the suggestion that Mau will decide how the fight works alone. If Mau intends to bring an army, Sukuna has companions of his own whose presence may improve the occasion. The word companions looks almost insulting in the space he has given it.

Rimuru reads that line twice.

“We get a list,” he says. ᴿ

Mau nods.

There is more beneath it.

The threat of being made a shadow has apparently interested Sukuna enough to receive a proper answer. If Mau wants to discuss the ownership of what remains after death, then they can discuss both possible results.

The second sheet is written in a different hand.

Mau knows the name beneath it before his eyes reach the end.

* * *

Makima proposes that defeat should leave something she can keep.

Not an object without a mind. Not an empty body some considerate contract has spared the person who once lived in it. Mau's own continuing awareness, bound into a shadow which cannot refuse her direction.

The page does not offer death as an escape from the condition.

It offers to preserve him through it.

Mau feels Yuka's hand become still beside his.

For a moment, the ordinary room is too clear. The line of the table. A cup which has been set down with a little tea remaining. The edge of the portrait story somebody left open near the window, still covered in arguments about a bird.

Rimuru reaches the end.

Then puts the sheet down with deliberate care.

“That,” he says, “is a proposal. Nothing here is signed.” ᴿ

Mau nods before attempting to answer with words.

* * *

Yuka reads every line herself.

Mau does not turn the page away when he sees where her eyes have reached. He promised her the part which might ruin dinner. This is the part, and letting her see it is the only honest thing he can do before he has decided what to say about it.

She stops at the sentence describing awareness.

Looks up at him.

“She wants you to know what she's making you do.” ʸ

He swallows.

“Yes.” ᴹ

The answer stays between them for a moment.

Then Yuka takes his hand properly, turning it until she can hold the fingers he has begun pressing against the table.

Rimuru asks Ciel to examine the mechanism described without accepting it. She comes to the edge of the conversation through the hosts who have chosen to include her.

*Reading is not agreement,* she says. ᶜⁱ

Mau lets that fact become solid before asking the next question.

“Could she actually do it?” ᴹ

* * *

Ciel does not give the answer they would all prefer.

The route described uses the continuing relation between Mau and his own shadow, not a claim that Makima can simply decide every person belongs to her. It would require his informed consent to bind the result. It would require the terms to define what a valid result actually is. It would need boundaries around who and what could be claimed, because the draft's convenient silence about his guests is not a protection for them.

Possible is not the same as already happening.

Possible is bad enough to make the room quiet.

Mau looks at the two sheets together: Sukuna amused by the prospect of a worthy fight, Makima carefully finding a way to make even its ending continue.

“Then we don't leave anything to her wording,” he says. ᴹ

Rimuru nods.

“Or decide this alone.” ᴿ

“No.” ᴹ

* * *

Yuka keeps the second sheet while Mau takes the first to the others.

They will read both. They will ask about the people outside, the people watching, the difference between somebody falling and somebody truly dying. They will ask which promises survive the end, and what a promise can bind when the person offering it does not own every life beside his.

No answer is made safer by leaving a difficult word unnamed.

At the door, Yuka stops him for a moment. Her fingers touch his cheek, then the little silver band on his hand.

He waits.

“All of it,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

“All of it.” ᴹ

They carry the pages into the same room together.
