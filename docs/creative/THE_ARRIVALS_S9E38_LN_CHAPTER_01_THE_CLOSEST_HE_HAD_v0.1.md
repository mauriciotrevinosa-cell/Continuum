*When: Mau reaches their door, Gojo is saying something which would sound reassuring if his hand were not still resting on the same fold in Sukuna's letter.*

Shoko sees Mau first. She lifts one hand, asking for a moment.

He stops outside the open threshold.

“I can think of ways to do it,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“I know.” ˢᵒ

“I'm not telling you I've forgotten how to fight.” ᴳ

“I didn't ask that.” ˢᵒ

Gojo looks down at his own hand. Moves it from the paper.

For a moment the little room is quiet enough that Mau could choose to go away and return later. He stays where Shoko can see him, waiting to find out whether they want the next part private.

Gojo looks up.

“Come in,” he says. ᴳ

* * *

Mau sits after Shoko points toward the empty place.

The two sheets are here because everybody who needs to read them has begun doing so. The words have not improved through repetition. Sukuna still wants an opponent. Makima still wants the person who survives a particular kind of ending.

Gojo looks toward Mau.

“You know I want to stand opposite him.” ᴳ

Mau nods.

“I know.” ᴹ

“I can imagine the first exchange. The second. Places where he's going to expect me to be pleased with myself.” ᴳ

He smiles briefly.

It does not quite reach the rest of his face.

“That's the bit I don't trust.” ᴳ

* * *

Shoko lets him find his own words.

Mau has seen him delighted by a difficult answer. Has stood opposite that delight and felt something in himself answer it before either of them agreed to stop having limits. It is one of the things he likes most about the man. He can also see what it costs Gojo to look at the same part of himself and admit it may not be the thing everybody should depend on this time.

“If it starts working,” Gojo says, “I know how certain I'll want to become. If something looks too much like the part we already know about, I don't know whether I'll answer it fast enough.” ᴳ

Mau looks at the letter.

“You don't have to know that tonight.” ᴹ

“If we're choosing who stands there, I do have to tell you.” ᴳ

The answer makes Mau sit back.

Gojo looks directly at him now.

“I can help you prepare. I can make you stop pretending the obvious answer is the only one you've got. That part I trust.” ᴳ

Mau nods slowly.

He is being offered something more useful than another person calling him the strongest and stepping away.

“I want that,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

Gojo stands after a while to fetch the other page Yuta asked them to compare.

Shoko catches his hand before he goes through the turn.

He stops, looks down at her and begins the smile which usually means he intends to make the next thing easier for her to bear.

She stands before he can.

“Don't do that part,” she says. ˢᵒ

He becomes quiet.

She puts a hand against his face and kisses him. Mau looks toward the open door, giving them the little privacy the ordinary room can offer without making an event of leaving it.

When she draws back, her voice is less steady than it was a moment before.

“I can't lose you again.” ˢᵒ

Gojo's hand closes around hers.

“I know.” ᴳ

“No. You know the words. I had days where you weren't anywhere I could ask.” ˢᵒ

He keeps holding her hand. Lets her finish without promising that fear can never have a reason again.

After a little while, she releases him enough to let him find the page.

He goes only as far as the next room.

* * *

Mau has been trying to make his own face easier for her to look at when she turns back.

The joke comes too quickly.

“So I'm the disposable one?” ᴹ

Shoko looks at him.

He knows immediately that it has landed in the wrong place.

Her face changes before he can pull it back. Not anger large enough to make a scene of itself. Something sharper, hurt precisely because she has apparently believed he knows her better than the joke suggests.

“No.” ˢᵒ

Mau puts his hands down.

“Shoko—” ᴹ

“Do you think I don't feel it when they bring you home?” ˢᵒ

He stops speaking.

She sits because the alternative seems to be moving around with a feeling too large for the room.

“Every time somebody says your name and then stops. Every time you're trying to explain why the blood looks worse than it is. I know exactly what it looks like.” ˢᵒ

There is water in her eyes now. She looks annoyed with it, which makes the feeling in Mau's chest worse.

“I love you,” she says. “That doesn't become less true because I love him.” ˢᵒ

Mau swallows.

“I know.” ᴹ

“Then don't hand me that as a joke.” ˢᵒ

* * *

He waits until he can answer without making her comfort him for having hurt her.

“I'm sorry,” he says. “That was a bad joke.” ᴹ

She nods once, looking toward the cup she has not touched.

“I know you care,” he adds. “I know what you do when I'm not good at being easy to care for.” ᴹ

Her eyes come back to him.

“You keep coming back,” she says, quieter. “Some part of me starts depending on it. You have people inside you who can help. You have a goddess who answers when things go wrong. Then I look at him, and I remember being somewhere he was dead and nothing was going to answer me.” ˢᵒ

Mau nods, letting her say the difference as something she has lived instead of turning it into a rule about whose life is more precious.

Shoko rubs one hand over her face.

“Depending on you returning isn't the same as knowing you will,” she says. ˢᵒ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I have to remember that too.” ˢᵒ

He moves his hand toward hers, slowly enough that she can decide whether she wants it.

She takes it.

* * *

“I love you too,” Mau says. ᴹ

Shoko looks up.

He is less embarrassed than he expected. The hard part was apparently believing the room would become impossible if he ever said the thing without first hiding it in a joke.

“I've admired you for a long time. Even when you were calling something I thought was normal a very stupid word.” ᴹ

Her mouth moves at one corner.

“It was a stupid normal.” ˢᵒ

He laughs softly.

“You look after people without making them prove they deserved you first. And then you go and have a life, so they have to learn you aren't only here to fix them.” ᴹ

She looks down at the hand she is holding.

“That sounds more impressive than most of my afternoons.” ˢᵒ

“I've been in some of them.” ᴹ

The smile comes properly this time, small and tired and hers.

She studies him for a moment.

“Like a mother?” she asks. ˢᵒ

He nods.

“The closest I've had.” ᴹ

The answer leaves him quieter than the long explanation did.

Shoko's grip changes. She looks at him for a little while without trying to make the confession either smaller or more useful than it is.

Then breathes out, almost laughing.

“That could make him your father.” ˢᵒ

“I've been waiting for the recognition,” Gojo says from the turn. ᴳ

* * *

Mau looks up so quickly that Gojo begins smiling before he has fully entered the room.

He has the missing page in one hand and the extraordinary confidence of a man who has recovered an advantage without having to land a single punch.

Shoko closes her eyes.

“An immediate consequence,” she says. ˢᵒ

Gojo puts the page down and rests a hand on Mau's shoulder.

For a moment the touch is simply there, warm and heavier than the joke which follows it.

“I expect regular visits. Excellent grades. Possibly repayment for all the wisdom.” ᴳ

“You don't charge,” Mau says. ᴹ

“A generous father.” ᴳ

Shoko begins laughing despite the tears still caught at the edge of it.

Mau looks between them, shakes his head and laughs too.

The letters remain on the table. They will still need an answer. For a little while he sits beside the people who love him and lets them be ridiculous enough to breathe with.
