*When: Ashborn asks whether his name appears anywhere in the draft, Mau says it does not and immediately understands why that is not the reassuring answer he hoped it would be.*

“It doesn't exclude you either,” he adds. ᴹ

The guest nods.

They are at the shared table inside. Rimuru has come because the conversation affects the refuge they helped establish and the person who connects two hosts. DIO has chosen to join them for this interval, with her ordinary room outside still hers when she leaves. No door has opened into a private history merely because an enemy's paper has made the morning difficult.

Noise watches Mau put the proposed terms down.

“Bad,” it says. ᴺ

Mau looks at it.

“Yes.” ᴹ

* * *

He tells them what Makima offered in words which do not depend on everybody having read the same page.

If he accepts that mechanism and loses according to the eventual agreement, his own continuing awareness could be kept in a shadow she directs. Death would not spare him it. That is the intended horror of the proposal, and making it sound less clear would not make the decision kinder to anyone sitting here.

Mau has not accepted it.

He says that part too.

DIO looks at the paper with an expression he has seen when she recognises a kind of harm she once failed to consider.

“You don't get to put a whole room of people inside that answer,” she says. ᴰⁱ

“I know.” ᴹ

She studies him until she is satisfied he is listening rather than politely waiting to be allowed to continue.

* * *

Ashborn chooses the simple part first.

If a fight is agreed, he would like an ordinary room in the protected guest inn before Mau leaves. He is not offering to become another soldier because somebody has misunderstood what being inside Mau means. He wants quiet, his own door and a way to hear that the young man has returned without having to spend the interval as an uncertain clause in another person's wager.

Mau nods.

“We'll ask Jinshi which room is free. You can look before choosing.” ᴹ

Ashborn accepts that.

DIO will keep the room and the plant she already chose. She can speak with them, help understand the draft and remain somebody whose life has not been put on a page she never signed.

The refuge remains a place they may return to by choice afterward. It does not become payment, property or a hostage because its entrance belongs to Mau's body.

* * *

Ciel wants to stay with him if he fights.

She says it after Ashborn has made his choice, without asking the guest to treat a different answer as betrayal. Her relation to Rimuru remains hers. Her thoughts are not a possession Mau can offer; her presence with him is not permission for an enemy to command her through the person she helps.

Mau looks toward the place she has chosen beside the table.

“That has to be explicit,” he says. ᴹ

*Yes.* ᶜⁱ

“And if the terms don't let it be?” ᴹ

*Then we have not found terms I agree to.* ᶜⁱ

He nods.

Rimuru rests one hand on the table, near enough to the little image that Ciel can see the invitation without mistaking it for an instruction to leave the other host.

* * *

Noise does not have a private door to walk through in quite the same way.

It belongs to the beginning of Mau's defences, the work which grew a voice and then wanted somebody to call it a friend. Mau looks at it for a long time before finding a sentence simple enough that the difficulty does not hide inside the wording.

“You aren't something she wins.” ᴹ

The dark shape turns toward Ciel.

She helps them separate the actual question. Work intrinsic to Mau's body is one thing they will need to describe. Another person's will, memories and choices are something else. A promise about his own defeat cannot honestly claim he has become the owner of every mind which helps him think.

Noise comes closer to him.

“Help,” it says. ᴺ

Mau's chest tightens.

“I want your help. I don't want to give you away to get it.” ᴹ

It considers that, then puts one dark hand beside his on the table.

Rimuru waits until both have stopped moving before taking the draft again.

“Then those are the questions we bring,” he says. ᴿ

* * *

They leave the room with choices to respect, not a collection of private promises everybody has been required to make so that Mau can pretend the difficult part belongs only to him.

Yuka is waiting outside with the page she has been reviewing. He tells her the answers the guests have agreed may be shared, keeps the parts they have not offered private and asks what she wants added before the afternoon's conversation.

She moves closer so they can read together.

The first thing she points at is the word *defeat*.

Mau takes the pencil.
