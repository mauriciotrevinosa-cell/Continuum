*When: Sukuna sees the table outside the protection, he looks at Rimuru as though somebody has mistaken the occasion for a trade negotiation.*

Rimuru puts the final copy down.

“Read it.” ᴿ

The short truce has already been spoken and accepted by the people actually coming to this conversation. No attack during it, directly or through an order somebody sends elsewhere. No use of the meeting to plant a follower on the people leaving. Separate departures, with the interval made plain. The ordinary watchers near the house know what has been agreed; nobody has been signed into ownership by a man calling himself their king.

Sukuna has accepted his own restraint.

So has Makima.

That is why Mau steps beyond the protection with Yuka beside him and Gojo on the other side of Rimuru. The familiar line stays behind them. It does not travel beneath his feet.

Makima watches him notice the distance.

Then smiles.

* * *

Mau knows the first thing she is waiting for.

The room. Her raised hand. The instant between seeing a gesture toward Yuka and deciding the whole world had narrowed to stopping it. The gate closing. Her voice continuing after something he thought could never be taken back.

He feels Noise stir toward the memory before he has finished recognising it.

*She's here under the terms,* he says inwardly. *We're here to talk.* ᴹ

Ciel remains close. She has not vanished because somebody dangerous has come within sight.

Mau takes the chair Yuka leaves for him.

Makima looks at the ring on his hand.

“Congratulations,” she says. ᴹᵏ

“Start with the roads,” Yuka answers. ʸ

* * *

Rimuru names the places they can actually identify.

The obstruction on Hollis's route. The marker beside the cut wagon. The other roads the letter threatened and the city it chose to mention as though naming civilians were an amusing way to select an opponent.

Sukuna listens with increasing boredom.

Mau lets him finish looking bored.

“If you want me in one place,” he says, “you don't get to keep choosing other people while we work out how.” ᴹ

Sukuna looks toward him properly.

“Then don't make it tedious.” ˢᵘ

“Then answer.” ᴹ

The stillness lasts long enough that Gojo's hand rests more firmly on the table.

Sukuna smiles.

They settle seventy-two hours for the next stage of negotiation. No attacks by either hostile signatory or forces acting on their orders against the identified roads and people during that interval. It does not pretend to command every creature in the woods. It does make their own use of those people a breach instead of a private interpretation.

Rimuru repeats the boundary before all six accept it.

The little relief beside Mau is Yuka breathing out.

* * *

Sukuna wants witnesses.

Mau looks up at that.

“For your dignity?” ᴹ

“For an ending worth attending.” ˢᵘ

Makima's smile changes only slightly. Mau sees it because he has been making himself watch her instead of treating the man beside her as the only danger in the clearing.

Rimuru asks what the witnesses will be allowed to do.

“Watch,” Gojo says. ᴳ

Sukuna looks at him.

Gojo's expression stays easy. The hand beneath the edge of the table has stopped moving again.

Mau answers before the quiet can become a private contest between them.

“Separate rules. No attacks. No help from the people watching. No placing something on a person during the event and calling it old preparation when you follow them afterward. The way home is part of the agreement.” ᴹ

Makima studies him with renewed interest.

“You have been thinking about it.” ᴹᵏ

“You gave us a detailed page.” ᴹ

* * *

The argument over extra bodies is shorter and less polite.

Sukuna points out that Mau's shadow can contain an army. Mau asks whether that means he intends to accept a list of opponents chosen only after the first attack begins.

Sukuna laughs.

“You want names.” ˢᵘ

“I want the same boundary for both of us. What comes into the field. What stays out. No bringing a city into it and pretending every body is yours to wager.” ᴹ

The final sentence makes Makima look at him for a little longer.

They agree to exchange rosters before any combat vow is accepted. Mau's native summons and existing legion must be named as categories with an actual registered limit. Sukuna's companions will not become an endless permission to add anything he finds amusing after seeing the opening exchange.

No roster is approved here.

No one begins a fight by calling an unfinished list a technicality.

* * *

Makima brings the conversation back to the claim.

She explains it as calmly as she might discuss whether a room includes breakfast. Mau will remain aware. If the agreed result places him in her possession, death cannot be chosen afterward as a way to take back the loss.

Mau keeps looking at her.

“My guests aren't mine to offer.” ᴹ

“Then write them out.” ᴹᵏ

“And the minds helping me aren't property because they're close enough to hear me.” ᴹ

Her eyes stay on him.

“Your body will still have things it knows how to do.” ᴹᵏ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“That would be enough to begin.” ᴹᵏ

Yuka's fingers close against her own palm beneath the table.

Mau understands the distinction. Excluding Ciel's freedom does not make being forced to act with his own body harmless. Keeping Noise's will from being sold does not spare Mau from witnessing an order carried through tools which are already his.

He does not pretend the clarification has made the proposal kind.

It has made it clearer.

* * *

“And the chamber?” Gojo asks. ᴳ

Mau turns toward him before he can stop himself.

Gojo has not named the private things Mau never gave him. He means the more recent room, the familiar faces, the voice Mau came home believing he had heard.

Makima looks genuinely uninterested in the accusation before deciding it may be worth enjoying.

“Another trial?” she asks. ᴹᵏ

Nobody answers for her.

“I already put him in a gate. You know how it ended. Why would I repeat the same failed idea and expect him to call it new?” ᴹᵏ

Mau watches her face.

“You expect us to believe that?” ᴹ

“I expect you to remember what I actually did.” ᴹᵏ

It is an answer, not proof. He feels Ciel keep the distinction steady beside his anger. Somebody used that face in the chamber. Somebody kept returning it to him when he would not obey. Makima denying it has not given them a record of whose hands made it.

Mau lets the question remain open rather than giving her a reaction he cannot take back.

* * *

Near the end, he looks toward Sukuna again.

“One thing I've been meaning to ask.” ᴹ

Sukuna waits, almost pleasantly.

“How did you explain it?” ᴹ

The expression does not change.

“A cut through the space between things,” Mau says. “Or a cut which makes the space itself what you're cutting?” ᴹ

For the first time, Sukuna is still for a reason which looks nothing like boredom.

It lasts less than a breath.

Then he smiles with all of his attention in it.

“You have been thinking.” ˢᵘ

Mau does not tell him which life taught him the difference. Does not take the smile as confirmation of every condition the man may have found in this one.

“I intend to keep doing it.” ᴹ

Sukuna leans back.

“Good.” ˢᵘ

* * *

They leave with a negotiating interval, a framework and work which still has to be done.

No death wager has been signed. No true death has been redefined as a convenient fall. The public event will need a field, the people who can keep a boundary honest and actual consent from those who wish to watch under its rules.

Mau remains seated until the agreed separate departure has put the other two beyond the meeting route. Only then does he stand with Yuka and the others.

When they cross the familiar line back into the protected ground, Yuka puts her hand in his.

He holds it with fingers which have been trying not to shake since the word *aware* was spoken across the table.

She notices.

Says nothing until they are home.
