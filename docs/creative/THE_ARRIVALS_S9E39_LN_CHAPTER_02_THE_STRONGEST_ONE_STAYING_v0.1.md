*When: Milim finds Mau beside the garden, she does not begin by asking whether he is busy.*

“I can fight him,” she says. ᴹⁱ

Mau puts the page down.

For a moment she looks relieved that he has not immediately answered like someone trying to persuade a child to wait outside. She has watched the visitors leave. Has seen the way the adults returned to the house and the new quiet around the papers they are carrying.

She comes to stand in front of him.

“I can,” she repeats. ᴹⁱ

“I know you can fight.” ᴹ

“Then why is everybody talking like I can't?” ᴹⁱ

He moves the page far enough away to give her the whole place beside him.

She sits, still looking angry.

* * *

“Do you remember the tournament?” he asks. ᴹ

She folds her arms.

“They cheated.” ᴹⁱ

“They did.” ᴹ

The agreement makes her turn toward him properly.

“I remember,” he adds. “You announced the result very clearly.” ᴹ

She almost smiles. Refuses the temptation.

Mau rests his hands on his knees.

“For a little while you couldn't move. They stopped and made sure you were all right. That's what made it a place we could enjoy fighting.” ᴹ

Milim's expression becomes more serious.

“He won't stop.” ᴹⁱ

Mau nods.

“He'll use the moment. Or make you choose between the person beside you and the thing coming toward you.” ᴹ

“Then I hit him first.” ᴹⁱ

“If you can make that the fight.” ᴹ

She looks down at his hands.

* * *

Mau turns one forearm so she can see the old, fine lines across it.

He chooses those deliberately. Not the irregular mark through his torso which came from Sukuna's hand in a life she did not live. Not the places another dragon ruined. These are the thin remains of the cuts which fell inside the small, unfinished shrine he once closed around himself and Beru.

Milim touches the air just above one line without putting her finger down.

“His?” ᴹⁱ

“Mine.” ᴹ

She looks up sharply.

“I used the cutting pattern before I knew how to keep myself out of it. I was trying to hold together while it kept cutting.” ᴹ

She has heard enough about the other life to understand which kind of memory he means. He does not make her see the room or the thing which was eating him in order to let the small fact be true.

“I was healing and still came out like that,” he says. “He understands those cuts better than I do.” ᴹ

Milim looks at the lines again.

Then very carefully puts her hand over his wrist instead.

* * *

“You're not saying he's stronger than everybody,” she says after a while. ᴹⁱ

“I'm saying I don't want us to confuse being stronger with being impossible to hurt.” ᴹ

She sits with that answer long enough for him to hear the ordinary sounds beyond the garden again. Somebody calling for a missing bowl. The little burst of laughter which means the Fort has apparently found an answer it intends to make inconvenient for somebody.

Milim looks toward the voices.

Mau watches her.

“If something happens to me, they still need you here,” he says quietly. “You're the strongest one I can ask to stay with them.” ᴹ

Her head turns back at once.

“Don't.” ᴹⁱ

He stops.

Her eyes have become bright with an anger too frightened to remain only anger.

“Don't say it like you've already decided that part.” ᴹⁱ

Mau puts his other hand over hers.

“I haven't.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long time.

“You're going to win.” ᴹⁱ

He cannot make the certainty hers by pretending it is a fact he has checked. He can let her give him the faith without turning away from it.

“I'm going to try everything I can.” ᴹ

She moves into him suddenly, both arms around his middle with a care which tells him she has remembered every line he showed her.

He holds her back.

* * *

When they stand, she asks which part would need protecting if people come to watch.

Mau tells her the actual work they still have to choose: where people can stand, who can help them leave, what must happen if a danger comes from somewhere outside the agreed fight. Being asked to protect the family does not mean being told to close her eyes until someone else announces how it ended.

She listens, adds one blunt opinion about anyone foolish enough to threaten the Fort and asks whether Rimuru knows she is volunteering for that part.

“We can tell him,” Mau says. ᴹ

She keeps his hand as they go toward the house.

At the common door, she looks up once more.

“You still have to come back and lose at cards.” ᴹⁱ

Mau begins smiling.

“A dreadful incentive.” ᴹ

She squeezes his hand and pulls him inside.
