*When: Frieren tells Mau that reading a memory and understanding it are different jobs, he looks at Rimuru as though hoping somebody has brought a more convenient answer.*

Rimuru folds his arms.

“You asked the person who knows.” ᴿ

Frieren puts a cup between herself and Yuka. The lesson is happening in the ordinary sitting room, with the door shut because the people practising have chosen a little privacy. Shoko knows where they are. Nobody has been told a quiet morning requires the rest of the house to become quiet with it.

“A man once gave me a century of a demon's memories,” Frieren says. “Receiving them didn't tell me what every part meant.” ᶠ

Mau looks at her properly.

“You needed to study them.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᶠ

“While he fought you?” ᴹ

She gives him a small, level look.

Rimuru begins smiling.

* * *

Mau explains what he wants without pretending it is the safer question.

If he cannot keep Sukuna, he does not want everything the man has learned to vanish with the body. The refinement. The decisions which make two apparently identical cuts become entirely different problems. What he knows of his own techniques, rather than what somebody watching from outside thinks he ought to know.

Frieren listens through the explanation.

Then asks whether he means to keep him.

Mau looks down at his hands.

“I threatened to.” ᴹ

“That wasn't the question.” ᶠ

He is quiet for a moment.

“I haven't decided what I'll want when I can actually hear his answer.” ᴹ

Frieren nods, accepting the honesty without making the decision for him.

“Then learn this for its own purpose.” ᶠ

* * *

The first attempt uses a memory Yuka chooses.

Nothing from the honeymoon night they have kept between them. Nothing which becomes available merely because they are married. She selects ten seconds from yesterday's training: his shoulder in front of her, the little movement she used to make him look at the wrong hand.

Frieren shows him the mana structure first. Noise follows it through the part of Mau's own remembered interface they have already learned to open and close. Ciel works on keeping the received account separate from the person receiving it. Mini Ciel keeps doing its familiar, silent work with the eyes; it is not asked to become a second mind capable of living somebody else's life for him.

Mau rests two fingers against Yuka's palm.

She nods.

He begins.

For a moment he is watching himself come toward her.

His own reach, seen from the wrong side. The exact little hesitation which made her change the pull. He knows which hand he used. He can also feel the hand she used, and the two certainties arrive in the same place.

His balance goes.

Yuka catches his sleeve before discovering hers has gone too.

Rimuru catches the cup.

Frieren closes the working with one clean movement.

* * *

Mau comes back first.

Not because he understood more. Because Ciel asks him to name the hand touching the table and makes him keep naming present things instead of following the movement he has just received.

His hand. The cup. Yuka breathing in front of him.

Yuka opens her eyes a moment later and looks directly at him.

“That's horrible.” ʸ

He nods.

Frieren waits until both can sit without correction.

“Now imagine he knows you are doing it.” ᶠ

Nobody laughs.

* * *

They keep practising the smaller problem.

The spell opens a path through actual contact. The receiving structure can preserve a whole account without forcing Mau to consciously experience every second as it arrives. That part belongs to the construction they are making together, not a new claim that Frieren always had a convenient way to digest a lifetime in battle.

The danger does not disappear with the improvement. A resisting mind makes the contact harder. A body still fighting for its life makes the return harder. If he confuses his opponent's intention with his own movement, having received more information may simply make him slower at the worst possible instant.

“You need to come back before he does,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Or not give him the opening at all,” Rimuru adds. ᴿ

Mau nods to both.

They try again with the same ten seconds. This time he ends the contact himself and finds Yuka's face before his feet can follow her remembered step.

It is a small success. He has to sit down afterward anyway.

* * *

When Yuka goes to ask Shoko whether the headache needs anything, Frieren remains beside the table.

Mau rubs the bridge of his nose.

“Thank you.” ᴹ

“Don't thank me by deciding the dangerous version has become easy.” ᶠ

He smiles tiredly.

She studies him for a little while.

“You can learn why someone fights without accepting what they did to get there.” ᶠ

Mau looks up.

“I know.” ᴹ

“You sometimes need to hear things you know.” ᶠ

He laughs softly. She takes the cup Rimuru rescued, checks that it survived and leaves him the useful notes instead of another promise he has to live up to.
