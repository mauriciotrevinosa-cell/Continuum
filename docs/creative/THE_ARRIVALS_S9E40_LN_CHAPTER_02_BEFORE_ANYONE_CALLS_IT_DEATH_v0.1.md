Mau looks between them.

Rimuru pushes the page back toward the middle.

“Then write what does.” ᴿ

* * *

They work on the draft before taking it outside.

Shoko will not accept a crowd deciding that somebody looks dead. Ciel will not accept the word *defeat* being left for Makima to explain after it has happened. Yuka will not accept an apparently kind sentence which lets another person surrender Mau's life because watching has become unbearable.

That last one is harder to say than the others.

She says it anyway.

Mau watches her pencil stop against the paper, then reaches beneath the table for her free hand.

They choose two endings a principal can actually give: a conscious surrender, or the true end of the living organisation and every intrinsic restoration still actively keeping it continuous. Falling, silence, a missing visible breath and a stopped ordinary pulse are observations. None alone awards the body to somebody else.

“If you can still repair yourself, you're still fighting,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“If the repair has actually ended and you've died, somebody restarting you afterward doesn't take the result back,” Shoko answers. ˢᵒ

The distinction stays on the page.

* * *

They will not make a god the referee.

Two matching records will be bound to the existing vows, each carrying the same accepted conditions and the principal's own registered continuity. The record can show *active*, *yielded* or *ended*. It cannot read a private archive, invent a medical certainty from a spectator's fear or change its text during the fight. Both opponents must test the harmless state changes and compare the copies before the final acceptance.

Rimuru wants neutral custodians holding those copies, not relatives interpreting an invisible notice beside the combatants.

Gazel has already answered the request for a neutral meeting through the Tempest line. He will send a clerk with the authority he has actually given. Testarossa can hold the second copy, provided Makima accepts her as a custodian bound by the same noninterference.

Nobody asks either to promise a resurrection.

* * *

At the second meeting outside the protection, Sukuna reads the definition without the irritation Mau expected.

“No counting to ten?” ˢᵘ

“If you want a man who has finished falling, find a stage,” Mau says. ᴹ

Sukuna laughs.

Makima reads further down.

Her finger stops at the excluded minds.

“Your own continuing awareness remains offered.” ᴹᵏ

“Mine,” Mau says. “Not a copy you build afterward. Not someone else you decide sounds enough like me.” ᴹ

She nods.

The proposed anchor will preserve that continuity if the agreed loss ends his body. It uses the shadow relation he can voluntarily bind, with the claim handed to Makima only on the defined result. His death cannot destroy the pledge as a method of escape. The same voluntary permission makes Sukuna's remaining shadow accessible if Sukuna loses; it does not grant Mau dominion over every otherworlder by analogy.

Sukuna looks at the reciprocal clause.

“You intend to make me kneel.” ˢᵘ

Mau holds his gaze.

“You intend to make other people pay if I don't stand here. You can live with a risk too.” ᴹ

* * *

The other concessions take longer.

Sukuna withdraws the threatened attacks on the named routes and Tempest from the challenge's possible rewards. No order prepared under that threat may be transferred to another person to finish once he has lost. Makima binds her own orders in that undertaking; she does not promise the behaviour of every enemy in the world, and nobody pretends she has.

Auxiliaries may yield at a marked exit and leave the combat. Doing so closes their permission to re-enter. Their defeat does not award a living body to either principal. A principal leaving the field to abandon the fight yields; moving through permitted positions inside remains a combat choice.

The audience has its own document. No aid, attack, supernatural coercion, private signal or new tracker directed into or out of the fight. No using the event to follow departing people, including through an animal or an agent. Rescue against an unrelated danger remains possible outside; it does not become permission to hit a combatant from the seats.

Makima asks whether conversation is forbidden too.

“You can talk,” Rimuru says. “People can choose not to listen.” ᴿ

She smiles.

* * *

The day they choose is the sixth from the first letter: noon on B+136.

Enough time for the nearby city to receive and return people by the roads it actually has. Enough time to inspect the proposed old quarry basin, clear the spectator routes and prove the equipment does what they claim. No field in the protected town. No labyrinth which returns the dead and makes the wager a performance.

The present moratorium will be extended through noon on137 when the field and rosters are ratified tomorrow. If either inspection fails, they have to negotiate another date before letting the old restraint lapse. Rimuru makes both hostile signatories say that part aloud.

They sign the conditional articles.

Nothing begins fighting because ink has dried. Nothing claims a soul before the condition exists.

On the way home, Yuka asks Mau whether he is all right.

He looks down at the hand she is holding.

“Ask me again after dinner.” ᴹ

She nods and stays beside him until there is somewhere private to hear the longer answer.
