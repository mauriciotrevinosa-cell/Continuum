* * *

Mau has spent the morning at the quarry.

He went with Rimuru, Yuta and the people who know what stone does when a large part of it stops being where it belongs. The abandoned basin lies well beyond the protected town, empty of houses, with two old approaches which can be closed without trapping a village behind them. Hollis knew where the road reached it. Gazel's clerk knew which signatures were needed before someone called an empty piece of land public property.

They did not choose it because there was nothing worth caring about there. They chose it because the people whose consent mattered could actually give it.

The fighting ground occupies the basin. The audience will remain behind the upper ridge, far enough back that the direct view is poor. Ramiris's already tested panels will carry the useful view; ordinary sight and the matching continuity records will remain separate. There will be no return bracelets in the field.

Diablo and the people volunteering to contain stray force work only outside the registered combat volume. Their barriers cannot push an attack back at its owner, reach inside to catch a falling principal or become a concealed source of energy for Mau. If a barrier must be moved, the fight stops under an agreed suspension before anybody moves it.

Mau hears the last condition twice. He wants to know there is an answer for something going wrong besides hoping his family will watch politely while a stone kills someone behind them.

Rimuru makes him walk the evacuation route too.

“Even if you're not using it,” the slime says. ᴿ

* * *

Now Mau is in a considerably smaller place, watching Ashborn decide where to put a cup.

The transition feels strange enough that he nearly starts helping before the guest has asked. Ashborn looks at the hand moving toward the bag.

Mau stops.

“Sorry.” ᴹ

“You may put that on the table.” ᴬˢ

He does, grateful for a task with an uncomplicated beginning and end.

Ashborn has left the inner refuge by choice. The few possessions which came with him are here. His private space inside remains closed and available by their agreement, but nothing of his body or awareness will enter the field merely because Mau carries its doorway.

Jinshi leaves him the key.

When the door closes behind the administrator, Ashborn looks toward Mau.

“You expected a different conversation.” ᴬˢ

Mau considers lying. It seems an especially foolish ambition in front of this particular person.

“A little.” ᴹ

“About whether you are ready.” ᴬˢ

He nods.

Ashborn looks through the open window again.

“I won't make you carry my opinion of the life I was tired of.” ᴬˢ

Mau becomes quiet.

“I would like to hear that you came home,” the guest adds. ᴬˢ

It is somehow much harder to answer than a prophecy would have been.

“I'd like to tell you,” Mau says. ᴹ

DIO is waiting at the next door when he comes out.

She has apparently been trying to decide whether visiting immediately would count as disturbing Ashborn's rest. Mau tells her she can knock and ask. She gives him a look suggesting that he has offered a particularly obvious solution to a problem she was enjoying considering.

He begins smiling.

“The plant?” he asks. ᴹ

“Alive.” ᴰⁱ

“Promising.” ᴹ

She looks at his face for a moment, her own humour fading gently.

“You can come back without having an interesting story for us.” ᴰⁱ

He nods.

She touches his sleeve and lets him leave without turning the wish into another condition.

* * *

At home Yuka is trying to make the new little bird face the correct direction beside their portrait.

Mau finds her standing on the bed, dissatisfied with a problem she could solve much more easily if she stopped insisting she could reach.

He catches her waist.

She looks down.

“I had it.” ʸ

“An important part of my reason for helping.” ᴹ

She laughs, puts the bird where she wants it and lets him lift her down. When her feet touch the floor, she keeps her hands against his shoulders.

“Did he like the room?” ʸ

“The window opens.” ᴹ

She nods as though this explains everything.

Then kisses him before he can begin making the afternoon entirely about what comes next.
