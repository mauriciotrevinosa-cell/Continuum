*When: Ori asks whether she can stay with Mau and Yuka for a while, Mau looks at the little bundle beneath her arm and asks whether she has already chosen the activity.*

“Yes,” she says. ᴼ

Yuka moves the things on the table before asking what it is.

Mau sees this tactical error just too late to intervene.

* * *

The morning has contained enough people arriving to make the guest inn feel like a guest inn again.

Hinata came with Testarossa and the clerk Gazel chose, by the road their notice named. Anko went to meet her before anybody discovered an official purpose for every minute of the visit. The two of them walked back together with the bag between them and a conversation neither was trying to share with the courtyard.

Shoko spent part of her own morning checking the actual supplies the field team has prepared. Gojo brought the things she asked him for, complained about the quality of the gratitude and kissed her when she told him he had begun asking for compliments at an inconvenient time.

Mau saw only the last part.

He left them the courtesy of pretending he had been interested in the window.

Now he is sitting opposite a game Ori has apparently improved while he was occupied.

“Where is the bridge?” he asks. ᴹ

“There.” ᴼ

“That's two bridges.” ᴹ

“We improved it.” ᴼ

Yuka looks delighted.

Mau begins to understand how the afternoon is going to end.

* * *

He loses the first round because he misunderstands which bridge can be crossed without surrendering a piece. Yuka loses the second because Ori has apparently understood something about patience which neither adult was prepared to respect. By the third, Mau has begun quietly advising his wife and discovered that the child considers this an act requiring immediate political consequences.

“We're married,” Yuka says. ʸ

“That's not a rule.” ᴼ

Mau puts one hand over his face.

Yuka starts laughing.

Ear appears at the edge of the room after Mau calls him, lays his head across the place beside Ori and immediately becomes involved in a dispute over whether his tail has moved a border.

There is food on the table. The shell from the sea is safely in Ori's pocket because she intends to show them where she keeps it now. Frieren knows where she is, and comes later to collect her without making her choose which part of the afternoon meant more.

At the door, Ori turns back.

“I'll watch some,” she tells Mau. “Mum said we can stop.” ᴼ

He nods.

“You can.” ᴹ

She looks serious for a moment.

“You still owe me another round.” ᴼ

“I noticed the account.” ᴹ

She accepts the answer and takes her mother's hand.

* * *

Umaru finds him before dinner with a request which turns out not to be about drinks.

She wants to know whether he has put his own little bird somewhere safe. He tells her where. She nods, apparently satisfied, then stays beside him without discovering another task.

Mau waits.

“Rem's coming with me tomorrow,” she says. ᵁ

“I know.” ᴹ

“I asked her.” ᵁ

“Good.” ᴹ

She touches the edge of his sleeve.

He puts an arm around her when she leans against him. She lets him, which is unusual enough to make him keep quiet through the first minute.

“Don't let that woman tell you anything about us,” she says eventually. ᵁ

He looks down.

Umaru's face is turned away, but her voice has become fierce in the little space between them.

“She doesn't live here.” ᵁ

Mau holds her a little closer.

“I know who does.” ᴹ

* * *

They eat with the house.

Mau does not spend the meal making the table memorise him. He asks Kaneki about the thing Ichika has been trying to show people. Listens while Touka corrects the part of the account her husband apparently considers less important. Hears the band disagreeing about something which has nothing to do with tomorrow and feels absurdly grateful when the argument becomes more ridiculous instead of quieter because he has noticed.

Yuka stays beside him.

Afterward they go home before somebody can invent a reason to keep them awake longer.

* * *

She helps with the difficult collar because the old fingers are tired.

He tells her so before she has to ask. She smiles at the improvement, finishes the fastening and lets her hands stay against his chest for a moment afterward.

The equipment is ready. The daggers will be his to choose. The katana is where he can retrieve it. The matched documents wait with the people who will actually hold them. Nothing more useful can be achieved tonight by standing over a page.

Mau turns toward their portrait.

Yuka follows his gaze.

“That's us,” she says. ʸ

He nods.

“I like that man.” ʸ

He turns back toward her, smiling a little despite himself.

She takes his face between her hands.

“You can bring him home. He doesn't have to have a better answer than this.” ʸ

He kisses her. Holds her until the part of him trying to make tomorrow arrive early finally becomes quiet enough to let tonight finish.

They fall asleep in their own bed, with her hand beneath his and the little bird safely beside the window.
