* * *

They travelled with the group by the route which had been checked. No surprise exchange into a crowd. No shadow left on an unwilling traveller to make coming home easier. The home watch stayed on duty. DIO and Ashborn had a room behind the protection and a place to look from on the ridge: one shadow, sitting where nobody would mistake it for a soldier waiting to be called.

Elmesia stopped at the entrance to make sure a child could leave without crossing the seats of an entire delegation. By the time she took her place, the household had come through behind her. Jinshi was still helping people find each other. Maomao had already found the way to the medical screen.

Qifrey and Olruggio came with the girls and Coco's mother. Tetia waved when she spotted Ori beside Frieren; Richeh took one look at the quarry and asked Olruggio if they could sit near the aisle. He moved with her. Farther up, Shizue showed the students where to find her if they wanted to step away. The seats they had been given still belonged to them if they chose to come back.

On the Tempest side, Benimaru put a hand on Veldora's shoulder before he could call out to the field. Veldora took the hint. Even from the ridge it was possible to see Rimuru turn at the sudden silence and look for him.

At the quarry, the admissions took time.

Makima accepted the observer rules in front of both custodians. Yuuki did too, with a pleasant question about whether the record would distinguish looking from giving assistance. Testarossa answered the actual question without offering him a history of what they had built.

Luminous arrived with Hinata, examined the outer protection and made one useful correction to the shielding where a broken fragment might pass beneath an upper panel. She did not reach into the fighting ground. Gazel's clerk recorded the correction before the closure.

Umaru stayed beside Rem while Taihei finished asking where the second exit led. Nobody left her alone to prove she could be brave. Milim went to the outer boundary with Diablo, then turned once to find Mau through the gap between the screens.

She lifted a fist.

He lifted his back.

* * *

Now Yuka rests her hand against his chest.

There is more to say than either has room to carry to the start.

“I'll be there,” she says. ʸ

“I know.” ᴹ

She studies him for a moment, then takes his hand and kisses the little silver band instead of asking it to promise something it cannot know.

When she steps away, he lets her.

* * *

Gojo waits at the approach.

He has no new brilliant secret for Mau to remember. Only a look toward the ground and a small gesture which makes Mau check where he intends to put his first foot.

“There you are,” Gojo says. ᴳ

Mau smiles despite the way his stomach has tightened.

Yuta stands beside him. Maki has one hand around Yuka's, Mai on her other side. Shoko is already near the clinical route, watching the last practical work rather than allowing the waiting to make her count imagined wounds.

Mau looks at all of them once.

Then walks into the basin.

* * *

Sukuna is waiting with his hands loose at his sides.

The others stand apart. The frozen quiet around Uraume. Jogo's restless heat. Hanami with one hand against the barren stone, as though listening for something the quarry has long since lost.

Mau stops at the opposite mark.

“You brought them,” Sukuna says. ˢᵘ

“You don't get to make that embarrassing.” ᴹ

The matching records show both principals active. The auxiliaries have accepted their exits. The closure takes the field routes away from every spectator, including the people who would give anything to keep a hand near Mau's shoulder.

Ciel speaks only to him.

*I'm here.* ᶜⁱ

*I know.* ᴹ

Noise is still with the unfinished join. Mini Ciel keeps the ocular flood at the opening he chose. The repair margin remains untouched. Nothing new has been granted because he reached the start alive.

* * *

Mau draws the daggers.

Sukuna does not lift a finger.

He looks at Mau, and the basin floor between them comes apart in long clean rows, as if somebody has dragged a rake through stone. The rows reach the place where Mau's Infinity begins and stop being a speed. Forty cuts hang in the air around him, marked in dust, each one still arriving and never going to finish.

It is the politest greeting anyone has ever offered him.

Mau walks through it.

The second step is a sprint. Left dagger high, right low, the two blades carrying the extra reach and hunger he paid for in a shop a long way from here. Sukuna turns inside the high hand with amplification running across his skin. He finds the thin edge where the slowing starts and comes through it the way a man comes through a bead curtain, one elbow already on its way to Mau's jaw.

Mau gives up the slash and keeps the step. His low blade draws a shallow line under Sukuna's ribs. Sukuna's knee lands in his side and moves all the air in him somewhere else.

Neither of them goes back.

They trade four blows in the time it takes the dust to notice. A forearm. A forehead. Mau puts Red in the empty space beside Sukuna's heel, a push the size of a plum. It never touches the man. It takes away the piece of the world he meant to stand on, and a shelf of the quarry floor as wide as a barn jumps into the air.

Sukuna falls off the broken step on purpose, turns the fall into an angle and hits Mau in the shoulder before his own feet have come down.

* * *

On the ridge, the panels are a little late. Ramiris's light carries the picture and leaves the sound behind, so that a person watching sees the shelf rise, sees two small figures already somewhere else, and only then feels the thump arrive through the bench.

Hinata has fought enough people to know what she is looking at. She is less sure she could have told you where to look.

“How much of that is real?” ᴴⁱ

Gojo has his chin on his hand. He has been told, this morning, by the person whose opinion he most fears, that he may not narrate. He holds out for almost a second.

“That was hello,” he says. ᴳ

Blood gets into Mau's mouth.

He spits it to one side and looks up and Sukuna is smiling at him, loose and pleased, the way a man smiles at the first bite when the rest of the plate is still to come.

“Better,” Sukuna says. ˢᵘ
