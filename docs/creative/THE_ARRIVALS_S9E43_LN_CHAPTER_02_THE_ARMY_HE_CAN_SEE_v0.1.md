# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 2 — The Army He Can See

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S9E43
**Continuity state:** B136 duel continuous. Legion deployed in companies within the declared 6574. Sukuna's relay marks: cuts applied from charged contacts seeded in terrain and allied energy, a refinement of Shrine rather than a second technique. Second of three appraisals spoken. Fragment unjoined, margin held.

*When: Sukuna takes three steps back, Mau does not follow the third.*

A black opening spreads beneath his heel.

It keeps spreading.

Beru rises first, already turning toward the heat building on the right. Igris comes out facing left. Bellion looks past both of them at what their king has stopped to look at: Uraume has not spent the opening exchange waiting to be invited, and the stone at the far end of the basin has begun to go white.

“Companies,” Bellion says. ᴮᵉˡ

The floor of the quarry stands up.

* * *

From the ridge, it arrives differently than it did at the festival.

There are people up here who saw the panels then, heard a number and laughed at the ant's theatrical death. There are people who came today specifically because they did not believe the number. They watch a dark stain cross a mile of stone, and the stain has shoulders. It has lanes. Six thousand five hundred and seventy-four bodies come up out of one man's shadow without a word of command anyone can hear, and each one is walking toward a job.

A merchant three rows down from Elmesia stops chewing.

Yuuki sits forward.

Makima does not.

* * *

The first company goes at the ice and the ice goes at them.

It takes the leading rank to the knees, then rises in spears through the second. Legs stay behind while bodies keep moving, until there is no useful movement left, and then the bodies break into smoke and something else steps through the smoke. Uraume stands in the middle of it with one hand raised, looking at nobody. They do not repeat the attack. Frost runs out along the gaps between the soldiers and gives the quarry a skin, and every step on that skin belongs partly to somebody else.

Mau lets Bellion change the route.

He could look through every pair of eyes down there, and be six thousand people slow. He takes the one report Igris gives him and puts Blue under an overhanging shelf of the north wall.

Three hundred tons of quarry comes away with a noise like a door being kicked off a house.

It lands across the ice in one long ugly break, and now there is a road.

Uraume looks up.

Beru is already above them.

* * *

Jogo's fire meets Beru halfway down.

The ant turns in the air, leaves an arm in the blast and lands with his other claws buried in the rock. The missing limb goes on breaking into dark. Mau feels the request arrive, a small tug at the back of his ribs, and pays for that one, and only that one, and the arm grows back on the way to its next job.

“Discourteous,” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

Jogo opens a bright mouth in the stone beneath him. The ant is gone before it finds him.

Igris takes the space Jogo has left uncovered. His sword crosses the first line of roots Hanami sends up to protect it, and the roots splinter, and more come behind them, thick as a horse's body and in no particular hurry. Hanami is not trying to outnumber an army with wood. Each root gives Jogo somewhere to stand that Uraume's ice does not own. Each one pulls the three of them a little closer to being one thing.

They have practised, Mau thinks. A volcano, a glacier and a garden have been in a room somewhere, working out whose turn it is.

He is admiring this when Sukuna hits him.

* * *

The blow comes through amplification, close enough that Infinity is a problem to be solved rather than a promise. Mau turns his head away from the worst of it and a knuckle opens his cheek. His right dagger goes for the inner elbow. Sukuna lets the shallow wound happen and walks him backward with short hard punches, one, two, one, toward the root line, like a man moving a chest of drawers.

Mau recognises where he is being moved to.

He leaves a little Red behind his own back and steps to the side before he lets it go.

The roots burst. A slab of stone hits Sukuna between the shoulders. Mau closes on the forced turn and lands one clean reinforced blow under the ribs, and something in there gives, and for the first time today he feels the actual weight of the man move.

Then a cut opens along his own upper arm.

He stares at it.

Sukuna's hands were busy being hit. Infinity is up. Nothing crossed the space. The cut came from *behind* the slowing, from a place inches off his skin, out of a splinter of root still spinning in the air.

* * *

*Not the root,* Ciel says. *What's on it.* ᶜⁱ

Mau opens the filter a little further and looks at the next fraction of a second.

There. Hanami's growth carries traces that do not belong to Hanami. Little marks on the shelf he just dropped. A thin seam in the cooling firestone where Jogo stood. Three of the rows from that polite first greeting, which had not been polite, which had each left a bead of Sukuna's energy in the floor like a man walking through a house and touching every door handle.

Everything Mau knows about Shrine says a cut starts at the hand that makes it.

Sukuna has spent the whole opening laying pipe.

He can send a cut along any of it and have it begin at the far end. Every contact is a hand he does not need to raise. It is not another technique he found because the fight needed a surprise. It is a craftsman, with a thousand years and nothing to do, changing where his first technique starts from.

Mau gives ground rather than cross the next mark.

Sukuna watches him do it. Watches him look at the floor, and watches him choose. The smile changes shape.

“There,” he says, softly, with real pleasure, the way you say it to a child who has found the hidden picture. ˢᵘ

Mau stores one dagger, draws the katana and cuts the charged root in half, cleanly, on the side away from the mark. He steps over the piece which no longer conducts anything.

“I see it,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

The fight changes shape.

Bellion starts spending ordinary soldiers like a man tapping along a wall with a stick. Their routes will cost bodies, and each body that bursts shows him another hidden line. Igris comes behind the advance and destroys the contacts. Kaisel crosses high with Lantern on his wing and draws fire that was meant for the ground. Kamish climbs above all of it, huge, slow, and keeps his mouth shut. The dragon has noticed where the heat is gathering. He has been killed by enough clever people to wait.

Mau moves where his soldiers have made him an honest piece of ground.

Sukuna starts taking it apart again.

Neither army is decoration now. Every decision one of them makes about the ground gives the other a new way to reach him. Up on the ridge a schoolgirl asks her teacher which side is winning, and her teacher, who has a very good eye, opens his mouth and then closes it.

At the edge of the ice, Beru drives Uraume back two full steps.

Jogo sees it. He has been fighting for almost four minutes against things which will not stay dead and do not seem to think he is important. He looks at the soldiers, thousands of them, all the small dark busy shapes. A little flame sits on the crown of his head.

He looks up at the sky.

“Fine,” Jogo says. ᴶᵒ
