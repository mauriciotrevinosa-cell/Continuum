# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 3 — Maximum

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S9E43
**Continuity state:** B136 duel continuous. Jogo's Maximum inside the registered volume; outer shields contain spill only. Kamish's built breath and legion receive it; many forms broken and left stored, links retained. Mau and Sukuna exchange Shrine on a falling fragment; sequential CT change, Infinity down during it.

*When: the sky over the quarry gets brighter, the first person to understand it is a clerk, who looks at the page to check whether it is allowed.*

It is allowed.

Page two, under *declared auxiliaries, own abilities*. The registered volume runs upward a good deal farther than anyone had thought funny at the time. Gazel's clerk finds the line, reads it twice and then looks up with everybody else.

There is a piece of a mountain in the air over the basin.

It has no business being there. It is the colour of the inside of a forge and it is still arriving. It comes out of a hole in the heat above Jogo's raised hand and goes on coming, a shoulder of burning rock as broad as a market town, turning slowly, dropping sparks the size of carts. The light from it reaches the ridge before the sound does. Every shadow in the stands swings around and points the wrong way.

Somebody starts praying in the language of a country which no longer has a king.

At the outer boundary, Milim looks up with her mouth a little open.

“Ooh,” she says. ᴹⁱ

Diablo adjusts one cuff and steps a pace to the left, to where the barrier will need him. Luminous, who corrected that panel herself this morning, does not turn her head.

“It will hold,” she says. “If it lands on the people it is meant for.” ᴸᵘ

* * *

Mau knows about it because the light changes.

He doesn't see it. Sukuna has both hands on him. He has the wrist of Mau's sword arm and a grip on the collar his wife spent an unreasonable amount of time on, and he is using them to keep Mau's face exactly where he wants it, a hand's width from his own. His breath smells of nothing at all. His eyes flick upward, once, then back.

He is delighted.

“Well?” he asks. ˢᵘ

It is a fair question, and it has only two answers. Mau can break this grip and go up there with something large. Purple would do it, if he had the half second, if he didn't mind what the man holding him would do with that half second. Or he can stay here and let it land on his army.

Sukuna has no opinion about which. He would simply like to watch him choose.

Mau headbutts him in the mouth.

*Bellion,* he says, and doesn't look up. *Kamish. It's yours.* ᴹ

* * *

The marshal has been expecting the order for several seconds and has already begun carrying it out.

The companies on the north side stop advancing. They turn inward, shoulder against shoulder, rank climbing onto rank, Tank's big dark shape at the centre with his arms out and his head down. Two thousand soldiers lift their shields above them in a single sloping roof and the roof goes on building upward, bodies on bodies, because nobody ever told them they had to be anything but useful.

They will not stop it. They all know that. They are there to make it hit something before it hits the floor.

Kamish has been waiting since the beginning.

He has been climbing all this time in slow circles, keeping his mouth closed, letting the small fast things have the sky. Now he folds his wings and hangs for one long moment over the burning rock with his neck drawn back, and every person on the ridge who has ever been near a large animal knows what that looks like.

Yuka has seen it four times, from closer. She takes her hand back from Maki's and covers her ears.

The breath is not orange. It isn't anything. On the panels it comes out as a white line with no edges, and the white line goes into the top of the meteor and comes out of the bottom.

*Then* the sound.

* * *

It takes the ridge a while to get up.

Benches have moved. A good many people are lying down without having decided to. The outer shields stand in a long curve of shuddering light, with Diablo's hand flat on one and Milim's fist against another, and against them, slowly, sliding down, is a great deal of fire.

Inside the boundary the mountain has become several hundred smaller mountains.

They fall through the air on the north side of the basin, each one the size of a house, each trailing smoke. They land on the shield roof and the shield roof goes out, a thousand soldiers at a blow, black smoke bursting up in a line as the rock walks through them. Mau feels it as a long tearing along the back of his mind. All those small requests, *mend me, mend me.* He leaves them unanswered. Every one of those links is still his, and every one of those forms goes down into the dark and stays there.

Two thousand soldiers, gone in the time it takes to sneeze. Jogo has spent more than he would like to admit. He stands on his root, breathing hard, watching the wreckage smoke.

He begins to smile.

A hand comes up out of the smoke. It is a soldier's. It belongs to one of the ones the rock missed. The soldier climbs out, looks around, finds the nearest officer and goes to stand where she is pointing.

So does the next one.

Jogo's smile stays where it is, with nothing behind it.

* * *

In the middle of the rain, two men are still hitting each other.

A burning slab comes down on them. There is no time to find out whose fault it is. It is the size of a chapel. Sukuna looks at it, the same way he looked at the floor. Mau lets Limitless go and takes up the other thing, the technique he stole from this man when he was too young to know what stealing it would cost, and he looks at it too.

Two cuts leave them in the same instant.

They cross. Sukuna's goes left to right, a long lazy diagonal. Mau's goes right to left, shorter and a little too careful. The chapel falls apart into four neat slices that go past them on either side, trailing heat, and hit the floor one after another.

Neither of them has looked away from the other.

For a moment nobody is punching anybody.

Sukuna tilts his head. He is looking at the slices, Mau realises. At the angle of them. He has seen that cut in a great many hands, all of them his.

“Your wrist is still late,” he says. ˢᵘ

Mau wipes his mouth. His Infinity is down. There is a cheerful bright pain in his cheek and a burning piece of mountain settling behind him. He has been dreading this man, one way or another, for the whole of two lives.

To his considerable horror, he hears himself start to laugh.

“I'll work on it,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

“Is he *enjoying* that?” Hinata asks. ᴴⁱ

Nobody around her has the breath to answer.

Gojo does, a little late. He has gotten up off the bench and is looking at the panel with Jogo on it. It shows a small furious volcano with its arms hanging at its sides. There's something on Gojo's face that has been there all morning, and it is having some trouble staying fond.

“He used to be so much worse at that,” he says. “The rock. I'd like that noted. He's come along.” ᴳ

“You've fought him?” ᴴⁱ

“I took a student to see him once. We had a lovely time.” ᴳ

Maki, without taking her eyes from the field, reaches over and pushes him back down onto the bench by his belt.

On the panel, Jogo has stopped looking at the sky.

He is looking at Igris, at Beru, at the nearest companies crossing the smoking ground toward him. He brings his two hands together in front of his chest, and his fingers lock.

The fire stops being weather and starts having walls.
