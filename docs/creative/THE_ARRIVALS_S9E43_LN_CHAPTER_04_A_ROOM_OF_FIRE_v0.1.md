# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 4 — A Room of Fire

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 4
**Source episode:** S9E43
**Continuity state:** B136 continuous. Jogo's Domain traps local company and two officers. Mau's native Mahoraga enters through existing shadow, Infinity off; positive sword kills Jogo, whose record closes. No Arise. Fresh Cleave wound to Mau's side. No positive-energy auto-kill on incarnated Sukuna.

*When: the wall of fire closes, Mau feels Igris disappear from the place his report should have come from.*

The link is fine. The knight is still there, still his, still working.

It is the *where* that's gone.

Half a mile of quarry has been folded into something else. On the panels a black shape has swollen up out of the north side of the basin, smooth and sealed, about the size of a good hill, and inside it are a hundred and twelve soldiers, Igris, and Beru. Mau gets one flash through the ant's eyes before he makes himself stop looking. A floor of black rock. A sky the colour of a stove. The nearest soldier's armour has begun to run.

A domain does not need to be better than Mau. It only needs to be somewhere he isn't.

Every shadow in there is on fire and every one of them will burn until there is nothing left to remake. Jogo understands exactly one thing about armies, and it's that they have officers. He has chosen his moment.

Sukuna moves in the same instant, because of course he does.

Mau keeps Infinity for the first blow. Lets it go before the second.

His hands change.

* * *

It is a very simple sign. Two palms and a little patience. Yuka once told him it looked like he was trying to remember where he'd left something.

Mau has shadow in that room. His shadow's in every room his soldiers are standing in. It is about the only rule of this whole absurd technique that has ever been on his side.

On a floor of black rock, beneath a stove-coloured sky, something pale gets to its feet.

It has to bow its head to fit. It has wings on its skull which are not wings, and over the wings a wheel of eight spokes. Its right arm stops at the wrist and goes on as a blade, and that blade does not take the heat of the room the way everything else in there is taking it. The little burning creature which rushes it first hits the edge and goes out. It simply stops being a thing, with a sound like water thrown on a pan.

Jogo, who has been enjoying himself for the first time all day, turns around.

Positive energy. A whole sword of it, in the one place in the world he has made entirely of himself, and he knows exactly what it does to a body made of cursed energy. Everybody here is made of something. Jogo is made of the thing people are afraid of, and this blade is the opposite.

He moves away from it very fast.

* * *

Up here, the price arrives.

Mau has put the answer in that room and taken it out of his own skin. Ten Shadows is the technique in his body now. Nothing is keeping the world a polite distance from him but reinforcement and speed, and Sukuna has known that for as long as it took Mau's hands to move.

His fingers touch Mau's side.

They only touch. Cleave has no need to be dramatic. It reads what it finds, it does a little arithmetic about how much energy this particular person is made of, and it opens him along the ribs, deep enough that he feels the next breath start to whistle.

Mau drives the katana between them and steps *toward* the wound. It is the worst thing to do with a wound, and it's the only way to deny the second hand the distance it wants. They end up chest to chest. Sukuna's chin is on his shoulder like a friend's.

“You spent yourself on your pets,” Sukuna says, conversationally, into his ear. ˢᵘ

“They're on salary.” ᴹ

*Igris,* he sends, through the one command that does not care how much of him is left. *Beru. His feet.*

* * *

In the fire, the knight changes direction.

Igris has lost most of his left side and hasn't brought it up. Beru has lost the top of one wing. They have fought beside each other long enough to stop discussing it. The ant goes high, screaming with what Mau suspects is mostly enthusiasm, and all four claws come down so that Jogo must either defend his head or lose it. Igris goes in low.

Neither blow would kill him. Jogo is a disaster, an entire natural catastrophe, and he slaps Beru out of the air with a backhand that would have flattened a keep.

But the sword has taken his ankle. He tries to step, and the step isn't there.

Behind him, the floor shakes once. Something heavy has stopped walking.

Jogo turns his head.

He has perhaps a second. He spends it, and this is the thing Mau will find himself thinking about at strange hours, looking past the blade. There's a man on a panel he can't see, sitting on a bench far away, with a blindfold on his forehead and his chin on his hand. And there's a sneer on that small round face that is very nearly a question.

*People always have a kinder name for their side.*

The sword crosses his chest.

* * *

The room opens like an egg.

Hot air comes out of it in a single shout. It flattens the nearest companies and carries a storm of broken black glass across the basin floor. Beru and Igris roll out of the middle of it, smoking, along with eleven soldiers out of a hundred and twelve. Then the great pale shape straightens to its full height, with nothing around it now but ordinary sky.

There is nothing at the foot of it you could call a body. A scatter of something like ash, still warm. The small hard knob of a volcano.

At the custodians' table the slate with Jogo's name goes dark. Nobody up there cheers. It's a very odd thing to find you have no wish to cheer for.

On the far side of the basin, Hanami has taken a hand off the stone.

Uraume does not look round.

Sukuna doesn't either. He has not stopped looking at Mau. He doesn't look like a man who has just lost a third of his friends. He looks like a man whose second course has come out, and who has eaten the first without complaint.

Mau feels the loss of his enemy as a practical change first. There's a whole quarter of the basin that isn't trying to burn him.

Then the cost in his own side catches up with him, and he has to look down and find out what's in there.

He begins closing it, quickly, the way Gojo showed him, the way he has closed it a hundred times. It goes well. The flesh comes together smooth and clean over the place where those fingers touched him, and he hardly has to think about it at all.

*He has seen the sword now,* Ciel says. *Look up.* ᶜⁱ

Mau looks up.

There is another wheel.
