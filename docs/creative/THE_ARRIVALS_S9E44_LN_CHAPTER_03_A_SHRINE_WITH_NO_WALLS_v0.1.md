# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 6 — A Shrine With No Walls

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S9E44
**Continuity state:** B136 continuous. Makima/Umaru verbal encounter under observer vows; Rem and Taihei near; no supernatural coercion or field aid. Beru heals with his own reserve; form remade once at cost. Sukuna's first open Shrine; Mau's practised small Void breaks in under two seconds; Mau CT burnout. Companies cut down, links retained. Both principals alive.

*When: Umaru sits down again, Makima asks whether she has always trusted him this much.*

Rem is beside her. Taihei is at the end of the short row. The question has not found a child lost in the crowd; it has found a person who has chosen to be here and is discovering how much choosing can hurt.

Umaru looks toward the panel. They have just watched his arm hit the ground.

“He's my brother.” ᵁ

“That wasn't what I asked.” ᴹᵏ

“It was the answer.” ᵁ

Makima watches the little hand tightening around Rem's.

“If he saw that, he would probably try harder. Perhaps next time he would tell you less. It must be difficult, making someone kind feel safe enough to stop.” ᴹᵏ

Umaru's face changes. For a moment the words find the exact place they were looking for.

Then she turns toward the woman properly.

“He came home and taught me a card trick he was terrible at,” she says. “You weren't there for any of the parts you keep explaining.” ᵁ

Her voice shakes at the end.

Rem moves closer. Taihei asks Makima to leave their row. The observer terms allow the request; they do not require a conversation to continue because somebody powerful enjoys it.

Makima rises without an argument.

Umaru keeps looking at the panel until her eyes stop focusing.

“I hated that,” she whispers. ᵁ

“I know,” Rem answers. ᴿᵉ

* * *

Beru's hand presses against Mau's ruined side.

His healing is his own. It comes out of the reserve that belongs to the ant and to nobody else, and it does nothing about the arm. It finds the place that would kill Mau first and it shuts that, roughly, the way you slam a door on a fire. His next breath has somewhere to go.

Mau moves on that breath.

Sukuna cuts Beru between shoulder and chest. It's not much of a gesture, a sort of flick, like getting a fly off a plate. The ant's form opens all the way down. Mau pays for its return while he is rolling under the second attack, and it costs a great deal more than it did an hour ago, and the new claws come back quickly enough to catch the arm which would have finished the stroke.

“My King,” Beru says through his teeth, “I was *working*.” ᴮᵉ

Mau laughs once. It hurts in several new places.

He takes the instant his knight has made him, and he goes up. He has flight, and the grip, and a badly balanced body that keeps trying to fall to the left. He comes down on a shelf forty feet above the floor with the katana in the only hand he owns. He turns around.

Sukuna has stopped walking.

He stands by himself in the middle of the basin. All around him are the companies, a little more than three thousand still on their feet, closing in from every side. Over them, Kaisel. Over Kaisel, Kamish. It's the kind of picture they'd paint on a wall: one man alone against the whole dark tide.

He looks around at all of it. He seems to find it satisfactory.

He lifts his hands and makes a small sign with them. He doesn't hurry. He has made it more times than this quarry has been standing.

* * *

Behind him, there's a building.

Nobody saw it come. It is simply the case now, as it wasn't a moment ago, that a shrine stands in the middle of the basin, a great dark sagging house of ribs and old jaws with a flight of bone steps. It isn't large. Most of the ridge could not tell you how big it is, because they find they don't want to look directly at it for long.

There are no walls.

Yuta has gotten to his feet. So has everyone who knows what they're seeing. Hinata has seen a domain or two, and she looks for the edge of this one, as you do. A domain is a room; that's the entire idea. You build a room and everything inside is yours.

“Where's the barrier?” she says. ᴴⁱ

“There isn't one.” Gojo's voice is quite calm. He has Shoko's hand, or she has his. “He doesn't use one. He gave it up. Every domain you've ever heard of, somebody built a little house to put you in. He just decided the outside was his too. It's the rudest thing anyone has ever done.” ᴳ

“Then how far—” ᴴⁱ

Bellion's whole northern line falls down.

* * *

It starts with no noise.

Soldiers walking in rows, and then they're soldiers in slices. All of them at once, in every direction, from the middle of the field to the wall. It isn't one great cut. It's two cuts, very simple, the two he has always had. One for things that have no energy in them, and one that does a little sum about the things that do, over and over, more of them each moment than there are grains of dust in the air. Stone goes to gravel. Gravel goes to sand. Shields, spears, the armour of a thousand men comes down as a soft black rain, and through the rain the next rank walks forward, and it comes apart as well.

Kaisel's wing goes. He hangs there a moment in the air as if thinking it over.

Mau feels every one. A thousand. Fifteen hundred. They are not dying, they can't, they are all still his, and the sound of their asking is a noise he's going to hear in his teeth for the rest of his life.

*Down!* he tells them. *Back! Everybody who can, in the dark, now—*

The floor swallows what's left of his army. It is the most sensible order he has given all day. It leaves him alone on a shelf, with nothing between him and the king of curses but three hundred yards of air, every inch of it trying to kill him.

The shelf under his feet drops an inch.

His hand has already come up. One hand; there's only the one. Gojo made him learn the sign with either, on the grounds that he would not always be issued both.

*Three seconds,* Ciel says. *That's your best. Don't look for four.* ᶜⁱ

He closes the room.

* * *

Dark. Depth. Quiet.

For one moment it's the most beautiful thing he has ever done. The little territory he has practised for six weeks stands up around him, a small round black refusal the size of a farmhouse. The far side of it is very far away, and the cuts stop. Everything stops. He's inside a thing that belongs to him. He has opened it like a door into a place where no one can get at him, and his breath goes out, and the blood slows on his chin.

From the ridge, it looks like an ink drop hanging in the air.

Gojo has stood up without noticing. He knows that room. He knows that join, and that small mistake in the right side which Mau never quite got out of it.

“One,” he says. ᴳ

Then something starts to happen to the outside.

Mau can't see it. He can only feel it, the way you can feel someone leaning on the other side of a door. A barrier protects against the inside of a domain, and it's a very good idea. It keeps what's in it from getting out. And it doesn't do much of anything about somebody standing outside with a knife, peeling.

There's no room for Sukuna's shrine to fight his. There's no clash. It just goes round and round the outside of his beautiful black egg, unhurried, taking off long thin curls of it.

“Two,” Gojo says, and then, very quietly, like a man who has a lot of respect for how hard this is, “Oh, kid. Let it go.” ᴳ

Light comes in through the right side.

It comes in everywhere.

The barrier breaks and takes the technique with it. Mau feels the place behind his eyes where Limitless lives go white and hot and then empty, and he's standing on nothing, and the air is full.

* * *

Every technique he has just burned out.

All of them. It doesn't matter which one he reaches for. Reaching for it is like putting his foot on a stair that isn't there.

So the cuts arrive at a body with nothing around it. They arrive at skin. He has more skin than he'd like. The body he has spent so long building is strong enough to make Cleave do its sums twice, and Dismantle comes off him in places as if it had hit a very good coat. He drops. He gets the grip under him, and that one he still has, because it's the other system and doesn't care, and he slams himself sideways out of the worst of it. Something takes the top off his left ear. Something goes through the muscle of his leg and he's making a noise now. There's no help for that.

Repair. He repairs. He closes the ones that look like they'd stop him moving and lets the rest run.

*The thigh first,* Ciel tells him. *Leave the face. Mau. Leave the face, nobody needs it.* ᶜⁱ

He hits the floor of the basin and goes on running with a hand clamped over nothing, because he's forgotten he doesn't have that arm.

Then he stops.

He stops because he has seen the dust.

It's the eyes that see it. They have been feeding him everything this whole time, every line, every sum, with Mini Ciel holding the flood down to a level where he could stay a person. Now he looks at the air, at all that fine grey stone-dust the shrine has spent the last half minute making out of his army and the quarry floor. It hangs from the ground to the height of the walls, thick as a fog.

He's seen a lot of dust in his time. Every grain of this has been touched.

Every grain of it has a door handle.

Sukuna is walking toward him through the fog. He has been waiting for Mau to notice. He looks like a man who has gone to some trouble over a surprise. One hand comes up, the fingers loose. Between the fingers something small and very bright starts to draw out long and thin.

“Open,” Sukuna says. ˢᵘ
