*When: the second pair of hands opens, Mau does not have time to admire the advantage.*

One catches his katana.

One drives a fist into his face.

The other two are already meeting in front of Sukuna's stomach, below a second mouth that has just finished opening in the skin there, wet and new. It takes its first breath with a small contented sound.

The man he has been fighting all day was a stolen body in borrowed clothes. He understands that now. He'd thought that was the man. This one is half a head taller and thick through the chest. There is a second set of eyes coming up under the first, and they blink out of step, and he stands inside that body the way you stand in a house you built yourself.

It is the first time anyone in this world has seen what he looks like.

Sukuna opens the upper mouth to speak, and a thread of blood runs out of his nose.

He wipes it with a lower hand. He has just reached into his own skull, into the burned place, and destroyed it and rebuilt it, all while nobody could see for the change. It's about the most dangerous thing a person can do. He'd once have told you he could do it as often as he liked. He'll do it once today, and he knows that, and his hands come up again already fizzing with what he has bought.

“Better,” he says. ˢᵘ

The lower mouth has started saying something too.

It's very quiet. It doesn't stop. It has the sound of someone reading to themselves in another room.

* * *

Three points. Mau sees them this time.

He has an anchor behind Bellion, one single crossing he has been saving since noon, and he takes it before the hands have finished. There's a lurch like a missed stair. The stone he was standing on slides in two. He's a hundred yards away with a marshal's armour against his back, and all he's lost is the sole of the remaining boot.

Sukuna turns his head and finds him.

That was the crossing. The Monarch's route gives you one per fight. It has never once given him a second. He has used it, and it's spent.

*Mau,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

She sounds odd.

*It's coming back.*

It's coming back. He can feel it, down in the wide new place. The route is filling up like a footprint on a wet beach. Something that had always been rationed has noticed the ration's gone. He starts to count. Six breaths, seven. He has a very nasty, very happy feeling. By nine, there it is, ready, every soldier on the field a door.

Four hands are coming across the field at him. *Hello,* he thinks, and takes the next one.

For about a minute, it's the best thing that's ever happened to him.

He's behind Sukuna. He's over him. He comes out of the shadow of a soldier on the far wall with Red already gone and steps through into a company on the south side, and it's on him, and it hits nothing, and he comes up out of Igris's own shadow at the man's left knee with a dagger going in. He's got an entire army of doors. The ridge can't keep up. Every panel's showing a different place, and each of them has him in it. It's *wonderful*. It's the first time all day he has seen Sukuna actually having to look for him.

Third crossing. Fourth. He takes the skin off a lower forearm. He puts Blue beneath a heel. Fifth. He's laughing again, he can't help it, and Ciel has given up telling him not to.

Sukuna has stopped chasing.

He's standing still, in the middle of the field. All four hands are loose. Blood is drying under his nose, and there's a faint distracted look on the upper face. The lower mouth goes on, quiet and even, reading to itself.

His lips are moving.

Six, Mau thinks, coming out of the wall. Seven. He looks for his next door. Eight—

It's a fourth mark. There's a fourth mark, on the shoulder of the soldier he's about to step to. Three will belong to the cut.

Sukuna hasn't been looking for him. He has been counting.

“Nine,” says the upper mouth, gently, and turns round, and he is exactly where Mau is going to be. ˢᵘ

Mau chooses the wrong third point.

* * *

The line opens through his left side, just under the ribs.

He gets the middle of his chest out of it. He's that fast, even now. He doesn't get his lung out. He finds that out when he goes to breathe. He goes to take the next one, and it doesn't come, and it doesn't come, and there's a bubbling sound he's interested in for a moment before he realises where it's from.

Then there are hands on him.

Two have his weapon arm. A third comes round the back of his head and holds it quite tenderly, like a barber. The fourth is open and it's drawing back. There's a little time left. He can see all of it. The fingers are coming toward his sternum with a charged mark on the middle joint, and the cut will start from *there*, a hand's breadth inside him, and no Infinity on earth is any good for that. He has been told by a very reliable source that it's a bad way to go.

He stores the katana.

It just stops being in his hand. It takes Sukuna's two hands by surprise. They have been holding on to a sword, and now they're holding a wrist.

He stops Limitless too. He goes quiet behind the eyes, altogether, and it's like shutting a door on a loud room. He lets the thin hard nothing come up across his skin, the one thing he ever beat Gojo with. There's no technique to it. It's just his own energy laid over his own hide, with no intentions at all, and anything somebody else has written on the air, when it touches that, goes blank.

His palm meets the finger.

The mark goes out like a pinched wick.

The cut never starts. What he gets is an ordinary fist with a great deal of muscle in it, through his shoulder, and that's fine, it's wonderful. He hasn't been hit by anything so honest all day. It breaks his collarbone and gives him just enough shock to get his knee up between their bodies.

* * *

The first punch lands and it's only a punch.

Sukuna takes it. He has taken a million. He starts his answer, and the second half of the first punch arrives, late, a little after the fist, all the energy Mau held back. It goes off inside the man like a slammed door somewhere in a house. He'd learned it from watching someone who'd learned it from being young. It's only the difference between two arrivals, practised until you can make somebody flinch for the wrong one.

Sukuna's grip shifts.

Mau's other hand comes up under it.

He doesn't plan the next part. Nobody ever does. They've all told him that, Yuta and Gojo and the rest. You can't reach for it. You can get every number right, you can have the best eyes in the world and a small silent machine for reading them. It's still got nothing to do with you. For one fraction of the smallest thing there is, what he means and what his hand's doing and the energy going through it are all simply, for no reason, the same event.

The air goes black.

It tears open around his knuckles in long crooked fractures, and the sound isn't a sound. Up on the ridge, people will swear they felt that in their teeth. Sukuna leaves the ground. He goes up, all that great new weight of him, folded around Mau's fist.

Mau follows. Delight would be a terrible place to stop.

He gets there as the body's still rising, with a dagger drawn. A lower hand catches it across the fingers, and the blade goes in between the bones and stops. An upper hand takes his wrist and folds it back until the new arm stops feeling like an improvement. And the whole body turns in the air, with all the patience of a very large door. The last hand, the one nobody is watching, finds his stomach.

Black fractures answer black fractures.

He doesn't feel that at all.

He's going through something. It's a shelf of stone, then another. There's a good deal of quarry, and he goes through it, and the world turns over a number of times. It stops. He's lying on his back in a long trench of his own making, looking at the sky. He'd like very much to get his left leg to tell him where it is.

On the ridge, nobody has to explain to Yuka why he has stopped moving.

He hasn't stopped being active. The record gives her that much to hold, on a slate on a table a long way off.

* * *

Sukuna lands. He comes on, walking, down the trench. He spits something. It's a bit of his own stomach lining, he examines it, and his RCT is already closing the place. There's the lower mouth, breathing.

It occurs to Mau that it hasn't stopped talking this whole time.

He gets his foot under him, badly. The door's wide open and the mana goes down that leg like a river down a drain. It can't make repairing and getting hit next the same job. Noise is holding the shape of the bone. Ciel has him by the scruff. *Up. Up. Don't you touch that margin, I can see you looking at it.* His chest is leaking on every breath.

Three points. There. And there. There's the one that's going to be a lie.

Nine breaths, and he's on five.

Okay. So, no doors.

He has a thing for this. It's very embarrassing. He learned it from his roommate.

Mau lets go of being a person. It takes no time at all. What's left in the trench when the line goes through it is a large, soft, blue-grey *blob*, with a very confused expression, and it gets cut clean in half. The halves sag. Neither one has the important part in it. Something the size of a fist goes squirting off along the bottom of the trench at a remarkable speed, and it comes back together forty feet farther down, and stands up, and it has two arms and a broken collarbone and is holding its own stomach in.

He isn't healed. It doesn't work like that. He comes back with all the same damage he went in with, and his old stiff fingers, and no cursed technique for a good long moment. He feels slightly seasick.

Sukuna has stopped walking.

All four eyes have gone quite round. It's the same way he looked at the chapel.

“The slime's influence,” he says. ˢᵘ

Mau wipes the blood out of his mouth with what he hopes is the back of his hand.

“Among other bad habits.” ᴹ

High up behind the shields, Rimuru puts a hand over his face. He has been told, by a number of people, on several occasions, that he is a terrible example. A long way along the Tempest benches Veldora is on his feet with both fists in the air, and Benimaru is hanging off one of them.

“THAT'S MY—” ᵛᵉ

“He can't hear you, Lord Veldora.” ᴮⁿ

* * *

Mau has worked something out.

It's a plain enough thing. He has been running from that cut all this time, and he's been lucky twice, and fast once. It's going to go on being there. Nothing he has will stop it. It isn't an attack, there's nothing to block. It's a decision about where the world's edges are. If it gets him it gets him. And there's only one thing he has seen in his whole life, either of them, that ever learned its way around something like that.

He told somebody once. He drew it for her, in the dust, with his finger.

*Ciel. I want to do something stupid.*

*Yes,* she says. *I've been watching you get ready to.* ᶜⁱ

Three points are forming in the air in front of him. The fourth's already there. He can see where it's going to come down. He steps to one side, out of it, using Sky to turn the wall he's leaning on. And then, in the same breath, with the technique closing and his hand already changing, he does what no one in their right mind has ever done. He puts something in the way.

It comes up pale and smeared with its wheel flickering, and half its right arm is still gone from the last time. It's right in the plane. It raises the stump to its head, as if against the sun.

The space divides.

Its arm goes. Its shoulder. Most of its chest, very neatly, along a line that doesn't care.

“*Back,*” Mau says. ᴹ

It drops. There's just enough left to fall.

Up over nothing, up in the empty air where it stood, a wheel he can't see turns over.

*Click.*

It's so quiet. He feels it more than hears it, right down in the bottom of his shadow. Far off on the ridge a woman who has been frowning for several minutes lifts her head.

“One,” Yuka says. ʸ

Maki looks at her.

Sukuna looks at him. He looks for a good long time. He hasn't quite seen what he's looking at, and he knows it, and there's nothing he has ever enjoyed so much.

Mau uses the time. The katana comes back to his hand. He goes forward through the settling dust on a leg that will not forgive this, and the lower hands catch the sword, and he lets them have it. There's a second dagger. It comes out underneath, far too close to be part of the same idea.

It goes in below the second mouth.

The mouth stops.

It's such a small thing, a breath catching, a low voice breaking off. Then Sukuna's elbow hits him and he's sent stumbling back, with the blood of two different men running off his hands. They stand there, six yards apart, chests going like bellows. He's done it. He has got inside. He has seen a thing no one has ever seen, a moment of real surprise on that terrible happy face, and he knows he'll never get the same one again.

The upper mouth is smiling.

“I was wondering,” Sukuna says, “when you'd want to know what you're made of.” ˢᵘ

He lifts one finger, just one, on one of his four hands. It's a small gesture. Mau has seen it before.

“Open.” ˢᵘ
