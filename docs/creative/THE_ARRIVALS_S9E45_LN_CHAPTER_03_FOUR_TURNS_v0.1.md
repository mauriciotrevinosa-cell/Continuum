# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 9 — Four Turns

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S9E45
**Continuity state:** B136 continuous. Sukuna's Mahoraga sent against Mau's Infinity as an example, wheel counted publicly. Gojo's freeze; agency and power intact. Agito destroyed by officers. Red, Sky, Blue, Purple sequential, one CT per body, released techniques persisting; destroys Sukuna's Mahoraga at third turn. Mau's own Mahoraga recalled alive. Sukuna's shrine collapses with burnout. Uraume ordered out, yields alive, no re-entry. No new army.

*When: the blade comes down on Infinity, it stops, the way everything stops.*

It hangs there a yard above Mau's head.

Mahoraga is about as tall as a chapel and it has brought all of that weight to bear on a single sword. The sword isn't moving. It's still arriving. Mau stands under it with his new arm hanging and the air between them full of nothing, and he has time to think, quite distinctly, that he has had this done to him by experts and this does not seem so bad.

Above the blind head, the wheel turns.

*Clunk.*

It's not a loud sound. On the ridge it comes through the panels small and clear, about the noise of a heavy bolt being slid.

There are maybe forty people up there who know what they're hearing. Most of them only know it as a story.

One of them knows it from underneath.

* * *

“Gojo,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

He has said it once already.

Gojo is sitting up very straight. He has taken his hand back from Shoko and has put it on the rail. It looks entirely relaxed. He has a pleasant expression on his face and he's looking at the panel, with his eyes wide open, and Yuta has known this man since he was a frightened boy in a box. He has seen him amused by the end of the world. He's never seen this.

It isn't fear. He'd know what to do about fear.

Nothing is coming out of him at all.

“Sensei. How many turns did it—” ʸᵗ

“He's not trying to hit him.” The voice is the voice. It's just arriving from a long way off. “Did you see where he told it to go? There's a whole boy standing there with a wound in him. A big bleeding easy boy. He sent it to the Infinity.” ᴳ

Nobody speaks.

“It doesn't need to get through. That's the bit that took me so long. It only has to show him how.” ᴳ

*Clunk.*

* * *

The second turn changes everything by about an inch.

Mau feels it. He's got the eyes on it, and the sword is lower than it was. It's still arriving, it's still never going to arrive, but it's arriving at a slightly different never. And the thing about having spent a whole afternoon in a basin with Gojo Satoru is that you learn how this feels. The sum has changed. Something is standing outside the great wall of his defence, feeling along the stones of it with one big patient hand. It has found out what kind of stones they are.

Fine. Move, then. Don't stand there.

He goes left. Agito is on his left. He's had his back to it, and the claws take a long line through healing skin from shoulder to hip. He refuses the turn it wants. He runs *forward*, into its arms, and drags the whole fused howling mass of it along behind him on his back through the place Bellion has spent two exchanges getting ready.

Igris hits the forward joint. Bellion's flexible blade winds around the other, and it cinches. Tank takes the collision neither of them could hold. He gets both big arms around the thing, puts his head down and just stands there, an enormous stupid loyal bear of a shadow, and Agito opens him from belly to throat and he keeps standing.

Beru arrives from above with both claws together.

The first strike doesn't kill. It only makes the body rush to repair itself in exactly one place. The second strike costs the ant his arm again. The third isn't his. Tusk has walked all the way across the field to deliver it, muttering, and he puts the end of his staff against the mended place and lets it go.

Agito's recovery loses the contest.

It goes to pieces all at once, the arms still reaching, the mask spinning off across the stone. It rings when it lands. It was a real thing, one of the ten, and nothing in the world will ever call it up again.

Sukuna glances over at the sound. He gives it about as much thought as you'd give to a dropped spoon.

*Clunk.*

Three.

* * *

Mau has stopped moving.

He's in the open, with the sword coming round again. He has a good solid guess at what happens on the fourth. So does everyone. Up on the ridge the people who know it have begun, very quietly, to tell the people beside them, and it's going along the benches like a draught.

Sukuna is not watching Mau.

Mau sees that. It's the thing that makes him go cold. There's a whole dark army coming at the man's back and Sukuna's got his head tipped to one side, and he is looking at the wheel. He has an expression that Mau knows. He has seen it on Senku, on Gojo, on a very old woman with a book. It's how you look at the last page of the proof.

He doesn't need it to finish. He only needs to see the example.

There's a whole lot of good advice Mau has been given over the years, in this life and the other. Most of it's about not doing what he's about to do.

He lets Infinity go.

It drops from him all at once, every bit of it, on purpose. The blade that was arriving arrives. It comes through the place where his head was, with the entire weight of a chapel behind it, and it's about a thumb's width behind him when his body finds out it can still go a fifth too far.

Nothing to adapt to. Go on. Learn that.

The wheel, halfway through its fourth turn, hesitates.

* * *

The hand he isn't using is already making a sign.

His own comes up through the broken ground right behind the other's knee, smeared at the edges, dimmer by far, wheel flickering. It swings. Two positive blades meet a yard off the floor with a shock that blows the cinders flat for a hundred paces. Sukuna's looks down. Mau's is losing. It's going to lose badly. It has about four seconds of this in it, and they both know it, and neither of them is what matters now, because that sword is one more thing for the white one to look at. It's a second thing, and Kamish's heat is still on its skin, and it has only got the one wheel.

*Get him out of there in three,* Ciel says. *Mau. If you lose him, you lose him for good.* ᶜⁱ

He's got to have Ten Shadows in his body to hold it there. He's got to have Limitless to do what comes next. He can't do both. Nobody can. A man's got one set of hands.

But the hand doesn't have to stay.

One. He drops his own out of the world, half its arm gone, straight down into the dark. *Thanks. Good boy. Go.*

Two. Ten Shadows goes quiet behind his eyes and the other thing comes up, and he throws Red. It's a good one. He doesn't throw it at anything. It goes sailing out past the turning wheel, wide by a dozen yards, a little bright angry ball that isn't anybody's problem. He lets go of it. It's on its own now. It'll just keep going until it hits something.

Three. Limitless off. He reaches up with both hands, the old one and the new one, and takes hold of the sky.

It's the thing he stole from a woman he never properly met, and he only needs it for a moment. He folds it. He's terrible at this, Yuta has told him so. He gets one corner of the air and turns it over like the page of a book, and out there, in the middle distance, the little ball of Red comes round a corner that wasn't there.

He lets go. Limitless again. His nose has started to bleed. Blue.

He puts it on the far side of the wheel, the side nobody has been hitting.

Sukuna sees it.

His head comes round. All afternoon, all through that terrible day, it's the first time anyone has seen him look as though he'd like to be somewhere else, and he is across the field in less time than it takes to blink, one hand out to stop Mau's wrist.

Beru takes the hand.

He has only one arm. It's the one he grew back a minute ago. He comes up off the floor between them and takes the fingers of the king of curses in his teeth. Sukuna doesn't even look down, and the top half of the ant is gone. Mau pays for him. He pays right there, all at once, through that new wide door, and he nearly drops everything, because it's like swallowing a burning coal. Ciel slams something narrow.

Red meets Blue just behind the turning wheel.

It's short and it's ugly. It's the size of a cartwheel. It has none of the cold, clean loveliness of the thing Gojo drew for him on a table with two bowls. It looks like a mistake someone's made with a very expensive material.

It's a colour he has never been able to see. Everyone else on that ridge will tell him later it was purple.

It goes through Mahoraga from behind, and there's no adapting to that. It's all new. It has never had a chance to learn that this could be a thing. For one instant the great white body stands there with a clean round hole where most of its chest and the whole of its wheel used to be. The sword's still raised. There's sky on the other side of it.

Then the rest of it remembers.

* * *

“His elbow's too high,” somebody says on the ridge. ᴳ

Shoko turns her head.

He has let go of the rail. There are four white marks on the palm of his hand, and the whole of his arm has started to shake. He looks at it, and he looks quite interested. He breathes in. It sounds as if it has to come a long way.

“I keep telling him about that,” Gojo says. “It's a very expensive elbow.” ᴳ

Maki, who has not looked away from the field in some time, drops one hand and punches him in the leg, hard, right on the muscle.

“Breathe, you idiot,” she says. ᴹᵃ

He breathes.

Yuka isn't looking at any of them. She's still on her feet, with her eyes on the place in the air where a second, smaller wheel went down into the dark with half an arm gone.

She has one of those. She knows what it's for.

She has started to frown.

* * *

Sukuna looks at the hole in the sky.

He lets what's left fall. It takes a while, there's a good deal of it. He stands in the rain of pale flakes, with the wind of the thing still pulling at his clothes, and he looks at his right hand. Beru's teeth have gone in deep.

Far away across the floor of the quarry, something has begun to come apart. The shrine. It leans, ever so slightly, like a tired man. The damage has gone all the way into its owner and it can't hold up its own weight anymore, and then the ribs and the jaws and the old bone steps fold in on themselves and there's only the broken ground. The air, for the first time in a long time, is merely air.

Sukuna works his hand into a fist. Nothing comes out of it. He's burned out now too.

He doesn't seem to mind. He has the look of someone who's come a long way to buy a thing, paid more than he expected and gotten exactly what he wanted.

He has seen how Mau's wall is built.

Across the ice, which is all that's left of the east side, Uraume is coming. They're coming fast, with a long pale lance in one hand and a wound at their temple. Beru's remains have re-formed around a stump to meet them, and Rime has arrived beside the ant, the frost-body taking the first part of the cold without trying to pretend he's fire. They drive Uraume back a step. They come on. They've served this man for a thousand years and they are looking at nothing else.

“Enough,” Sukuna says. ˢᵘ

Uraume stops.

“Out.” ˢᵘ

He doesn't raise his voice. He doesn't even turn all the way. There's the marked exit, a little red flag in the rubble.

Whatever passes across Uraume's face in that moment, it's never going to be described by anyone there. It takes about as long as a breath. They bow, from the neck, very exact. Then they walk to the flag with blood running into one eye, and the clerk closes that line in the record. No return is allowed.

That's all of them, then. Two friends dead, one sent away. All those careful weeks. The whole enormous quarry has gone very quiet, and it's just a man in it.

Mau stands with the katana low.

The new arm trembles. Around him are thousands of links, a good many forms gone dark. He could call them back, it's a wide door now, and he knows what it'd do to him. He leaves them lying. His side is closed badly enough that every breath pulls at it, and his whole body is a list of things he'll have to deal with later.

He's beaten the army. He killed the wheel. He has taken every single thing the man brought.

Sukuna rolls one shoulder, with blood running under his collar.

“Now,” he says. ˢᵘ

The body begins changing.
