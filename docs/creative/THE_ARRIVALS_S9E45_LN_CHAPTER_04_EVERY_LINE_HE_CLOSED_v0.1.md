# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 11 — Every Line He Closed

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 11
**Source episode:** S9E45
**Continuity state:** B136 continuous. Sukuna's book: Cleave residue stays under repaired flesh and can be opened by word, a pre-duel development. New right arm, built by the Black Heart route, carries none. Mau sees eleven unspent lines near heart, throat, femoral and cuts them out with his own Shrine, new flesh behind; pays V58#4. Forearm scars are his own, as told to Milim. Decides on the memory contact. No record change.

*When: Mau looks down, he is looking for whatever Sukuna has just thrown.*

Nothing has been thrown.

The cut on his cheek is open again.

He knows that one. A knuckle gave it to him an hour ago, and he closed it himself. He raises his hand to it, and while the hand's on the way, the long line on his upper arm opens too, the very first one, from the root. And then his side. That's the one he has closed twice, the one Sukuna only touched. It unzips from the hip to the armpit, with a soft wet sound, and he looks at it with his mouth a little open.

Then all of them.

They go down him like buttons. His neck where the flower was. His thigh. The foot. The back, the whole of it, shoulder to hip, where Agito's claws went in. Every place he has mended since noon, every clever fast clean repair Gojo ever taught him, all of them come undone in the time it takes to draw a breath. There's nothing in the air. Nobody's touched him. He's standing alone in the middle of an empty field, six yards from the nearest person, with his hands at his sides, and he is suddenly, entirely, red.

He sits down.

He didn't mean to. His legs have had a look at the situation and come to a decision.

* * *

There's no shouting on the ridge at first. It takes people a moment to understand that nothing's hit him.

Shoko understands it right away. She has come off the bench to the very edge of the clinical route, as far as the rope, and she's holding the rope. She's looking at the panel and her lips are moving. Bocchi, on the row behind her, thinks at first she's praying. She isn't. She's calling them off, one by one, in a flat clear ward voice, exactly as she would for somebody taking notes.

“Left cheek. Left deltoid. Right flank, that's the whole flank, that's the one he closed twice. Neck. Quadriceps. Dorsal, full length. He closed those. He closed all of those. Those were *closed*.” ˢʰ

“What did it?” Yuta says. ʸᵗ He's come up beside her. “Shoko. What did that?”

“Nothing did that. Look at them. They're not new. They're the same ones. It's every one he ever healed.” ˢʰ

She stops. Her voice has run into something.

“It's every one he ever *healed*,” she says. ˢʰ

* * *

Sukuna hasn't moved. He's holding a lower hand against the dagger in his belly, and he has no great interest in it. He lets Mau look.

He's in a very good mood.

“When I cut a man,” he says, “I do a small sum about him. That's all Cleave ever was. How strong, how thick. A thing like that leaves a little of itself behind. There's no reason it shouldn't. And you people, you clever people, you've learned to heal. You're so quick. You close the flesh right over it. I've never had to carry anything.” ˢᵘ

He tips his head.

“I keep a sort of book.” ˢᵘ

Mau is having some trouble with his eyes. His hands have started shaking. He'd like to tell them to stop. There's so much blood, it's quite remarkable. It's running into his lap. All the reserve he has left is standing around in his body like a crowd at a fire, asking him which way to go. He can feel the door, the huge new door, the ocean behind it. He's got all the mana in the world, and it doesn't stop you bleeding if you can't tell it where.

*Ciel.*

*I'm here. Close the neck first. No. Wait.* ᶜⁱ

*Wait? I'm—*

*If you close it, he'll just keep it. Mau, look at the arm.* ᶜⁱ

He looks at the arm.

He has to think about which one. It's the right one. The one that came off, the new one, that grew out of the stump in the fire and that he has been hitting people with, and it's taken a beating. The whole forearm is black with bruises. There's a long graze on it, from the shoulder of a dead man, and a shallow cut he got on a falling rock. They're not doing anything. It's sitting in his lap, with the fingers curled, the only clean thing on him.

It was never cut. There's nothing in it. It isn't healed flesh, it's flesh that wasn't there.

He opens the filter.

All the way, the whole throttle, the thing he promised Shoko he wouldn't do. Mini Ciel takes the flood without a word and passes him what he's looking for.

It's a good thing he's sitting down.

* * *

Sukuna opened the ones you can see.

He didn't open everything. Why would he? You don't read a whole book out loud to someone at the first sitting. The surface lines are spent now, the marks have gone out of them, they're just wounds. But down below them, there are others. There are little threads of somebody else's energy lying along the inside of his body, each no longer than a finger. He has them in the wall of the big vessel in his groin. There's one along his spine. Two in his throat, on the deep side, where the flower's roots went. One's lying on his heart, as neat as a pressed flower in a book.

Eleven.

Each one has been sitting there, closed over, with a smooth clean scar, since he did such a nice job of mending it. And each is about one word from opening.

So. It's the end, then. He could stop the bleeding, and that doesn't help. He could heal the deep ones, and that's just tucking them in. They'll be there for the rest of the fight, however long that is. He's been carrying the man's knife around in his own chest for an hour, he has been sharpening it for him, he's been so *good* about his repairs.

He thinks about the arm.

He thinks about a small girl with pink hair, in a garden, five days ago. She wanted to know whose those were, the fine pale lines across his forearm, and she was holding her finger just above them so as not to touch.

*Mine,* he said.

“Oh,” Mau says, aloud. “That's horrible.” ᴹ

*I know,* Ciel says. *I'll count. Hold still.* ᶜⁱ

* * *

Limitless goes down. Shrine comes up.

He has never in his life made anything this small.

It's a Dismantle about as long as a grain of rice. Everyone's been telling him all day his wrist is late; well, he hasn't got a wrist in this, he hasn't got anything. He doesn't throw it. He only puts it, from the inside, with his eyes wide open, along the wall of the big vessel at the top of his leg, on the outer edge of that little thread. It goes in, it goes round, and it takes the whole small piece of himself right out.

It comes out through the skin and falls on the ground. It's about the size of a slug.

The door's already open. Behind the cut, and he's got to be very quick here, comes the new stuff, hot and wet and stupid. Noise has the pattern of it. It's not healing, it doesn't close anything over anything. It's just more him, where there wasn't any.

“One,” Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

The vomit surprises him. He hasn't got time for that.

On the panels, in a light that's very clear, a man sits on the ground in a pool of his own blood, in the middle of the largest quarry in the west. There's a great four-armed shape standing over him. He's got his chin on his chest, and he's looking at himself with enormous concentration. Small pieces of him start to come out and fall on the stone.

There's no flash to it. You wouldn't call it a technique, to look at. It's just that the place on his leg opens, then shuts. Then there's a place on his ribs.

A long way up, a merchant three rows down from Elmesia gets up, very quietly, and goes away to be sick.

“Two. Three. That's the spine, go slowly, Mau, I mean it, slower than that, there. Four.” ᶜⁱ

* * *

Milim Nava has been standing at the outer boundary for some time with her fists against the barrier.

She has been pushing on it for no real reason. It doesn't need her anymore. She has the best view of anyone, nearly as good as the panel, and she's stopped blinking.

Diablo, beside her, has seen a great many things and has enjoyed almost all of them. He has one eyebrow up.

“What is he doing?” he asks, with real interest. ᴰ

She doesn't answer. She has hold of one of her own arms, and she doesn't seem to know it, her thumb rubbing back and forth on the skin just above the wrist.

*His?* she'd asked him.

“He told me,” she says. “He *told* me, he said—” ᴹⁱ

She stops. Her jaw's working. She is one of the most powerful living beings in this world, and she has decided, on no authority at all, that she's not going to cry at a boundary.

“He's so *stupid*,” Milim says, with her whole heart. ᴹⁱ

* * *

Sukuna gives him until seven.

That's the courtesy of it. It's not a small one. He has stood there all this while with his head on one side, like a man watching someone else's child do something remarkable with a pair of scissors. And on seven, Mau has to go in for the throat, the deep side, one of the two. He has to tip his head back to get the angle, and he's looking at the sky. That's when the shadow falls across him.

“No, go on,” Sukuna says. ˢᵘ

The foot comes down.

Mau isn't there. He's on four, with the last of a nine-breath door. He's come out of a soldier's shadow forty yards off with a hand at his own throat, and he's still working. Eight. He's running, somehow; he's running on the bad leg, spraying everything he owns, and the cut goes past on his left and takes down a section of wall. Nine's right on top of his collarbone, Ciel's going to have to hold that. Ten, under a swinging fist. He's got one left. It's the one on his heart. He can feel it there, very small and neat, right on the wall of it, going up and down, up and down.

He's got a hand on his chest. He's going a good bit too fast to be doing this.

He looks up, and across the ruin of the quarry, Sukuna has looked up too. All four eyes on him. The upper lip has started to move.

*Now, Mau, now, now—*

He goes in.

It's just a little cut. It's only about as big as a grain of rice.

The world goes a long way off. It's very peaceful. He's interested to find that he's lying down, with his cheek on warm stone. Somebody, somewhere, is telling somebody the same thing over and over in a calm voice, and he can't hear what it is, but it sounds like *eleven, eleven, you did it, eleven, get up, it's beating, eleven, get up.*

A great deal of hot new meat gets very busy in his chest.

* * *

He gets up.

He has about as much blood as you'd get in a good-sized cat. The surface wounds are a problem, a real one, but they're only wounds, and they can close now. Nothing's written under them. He lets them close. He's got a hole in himself in eleven places, packed with stuff he doesn't recognise, and it all belongs to him.

Sukuna has stopped.

He is standing with his head back. His whole body's shaking a little, all that great height of it, and Mau thinks for one lunatic moment that the man's in pain. Then it comes out of him, from both mouths, the one above and the wet ruined one below with a dagger under it. He's laughing so hard he can barely stand. He's got tears on his face. It goes on and on. It rolls around the empty quarry, and it's the most joyful sound anyone there has ever heard, and none of them will ever get it out of their heads.

“*Who taught you that?*” he howls. ˢᵘ

Mau hasn't got a lot of spit. He uses what there is.

“Me,” he says. “I did. I was fighting a bug.” ᴹ

It sets him off again.

Mau stands and lets him laugh. He's swaying. He has been very clever. He has been cleverer in the last two minutes than ever in his life, and it took him an hour and a whole skin to work out one single thing this man has been doing since noon. And what else? What else has he got? What's that second mouth been saying all this time? Why did he want to see the Infinity? How many more books are there, and which of the things Mau thinks he knows are still true?

He can win this exchange. He can win the next one, maybe. It won't matter. He'll just keep finding out. He's going to keep finding out, one thing at a time, for as long as he's got things to lose. And this man has been alive for a very long time, and he has never once in all of it explained anything kindly.

So there's no point in waiting for him to.

Mau looks down at his hand, the old one. He looks at the two fingers that don't close.

There's a person on the ridge, sitting at the end of a bench, who once told him over a cup that receiving a thing and understanding it were different jobs. *Now imagine he knows you are doing it,* she said, and nobody laughed.

*Ciel.* ᴹ

*I know,* she answers. ᶜⁱ

He begins making the opening.
