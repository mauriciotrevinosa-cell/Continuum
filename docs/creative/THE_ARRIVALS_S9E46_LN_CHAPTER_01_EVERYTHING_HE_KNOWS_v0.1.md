## Chapter 12 — Everything He Knows

*When: Mau puts the katana away, Sukuna assumes he is about to try another body.*

The assumption lasts one hand.

The next slash comes for his throat. Mau takes it on a reinforced forearm instead of stepping where the marks expect him to step, and it opens the arm to the bone. He closes only as much as will keep the fingers moving. Then he walks into the man, past the first pair of hands, under the second, close enough to smell him. He smells of hot stone and old blood, and under those, faintly, of something cooking.

His palm finds Sukuna's wrist.

Mana opens the path Frieren taught him, the small plain working she made him do at a sitting room table until he could do it without looking.

Sukuna understands before it has finished. Every one of those eyes comes round.

“You—” ˢᵘ

* * *

The first thing Mau receives is the intention to kill him.

It has no words in it. It's a living movement, precise and immediate, travelling through a body with another hand available where Mau's remembered body has none. It feels like the most natural thing in the world. He has a throat in front of him. It's right there. He begins to reach for it, and it's his own.

Ciel puts his name into the confusion.

*Mau. Your left foot. The ground under it.* ᶜⁱ

He holds the path and doesn't finish the movement.

Down in the dark, Noise opens the receiving structure, the thing it has spent six days building out of Frieren's old hard lesson about what happens to a person who is handed a century all at once. It doesn't try to understand any of it. It only opens, the way a very large empty warehouse opens, and everything starts coming in.

* * *

It isn't a life, politely telling itself in order.

It's hands. Ten thousand pairs of hands, all of them his, learning exactly where cursed energy leaves a cut wasteful. A barrier made smaller by someone who was bored of it. A cold winter, and a river, and a town by the river that he went to on purpose. The patience to notice an opponent has begun enjoying one answer too much. Bodies that promised strength, and promised, and failed to have anything interesting left after the first certainty broke. So many of them. So many brave people. Men who walked out to meet him with their whole villages behind them, and he had been so *hopeful*, every time, right up to the second blow.

A kitchen. Things Mau is not going to think about, not now, and possibly not later.

A long, long time of nothing to do.

Then a shrine with its walls taken off, because why should there be walls. A vow that says this cut has to be told the truth first. He finds that one almost at once, lying on top, because it's so recent, and for a moment he doesn't understand what he's looking at. A clever new lock for an old cut. Three corners, a seal, and three true things said to the target's face. *True.* The man who built it has never had to mean a thing he said to anyone. He built it that way because it was the only price he could think of that he'd never paid.

It would hardly close, at first. He tried it on a warlord in the eastern hills, and on a dragon, and on a very famous swordsman. He stood in front of each of them, thinking it over, and he couldn't come up with three.

*Better.*

*There.*

*Good.*

Mau hears himself make a sound.

Oh, you son of a bitch. You absolute son of a bitch. Since *noon*. He has been so pleased. There's a part of Mau that has been so pleased, every single time.

And the lower mouth, reading to itself. What it has been saying all this while, on and on, very softly, in the warm dark under the ribs. It's been saying what it thinks of him.

It thinks a great deal of him.

* * *

*Keep going,* Ciel says. *Don't stay there. There's more. Find your foot.* ᶜⁱ

Under that is the book, and every page of it. Under that, all the small practical cruelties of the trade. A hand held up empty, for a man to look at, while the real one waits; he has done that eleven hundred times, and it works every time, because nobody can ever believe it of someone so proud.

Further down there is Gojo.

Mau sees him through eyes that watched him fall.

He's so bright. Nobody told him that, not from this side. He's the brightest thing that has ever happened, a whole white sky of a man. He's *laughing*, in the middle of a ruined city, with his eyes wide open, and this awful old heart that has been so tired for so long is going like a drum. Finally. It's taken a thousand years. Finally, finally, someone's come. Look at him. Look at him. And then the sword comes out of nowhere with the world on its edge, and the two halves of that bright laughing thing go over in the air, in the sun, quite slowly.

And then it's quiet.

He wasn't sad. He hasn't got that. It's just that he stood there on the ruins, with blood drying on him, and looked around for what came next.

For one instant Mau's own memories try to answer with a hundred other streets, a hundred other deaths in the same white light, a man with something over his eyes, and a smile like a door left open.

*The ground,* Ciel says again. *You are not that man. Mau, you are not either of those men.* ᶜⁱ

He finds his left foot.

He keeps the path open.

There's one more thing. It's right at the bottom. He has been feeling it for some time, the way you feel a stair in the dark, and it isn't a memory or a technique. It's older than the kitchen, older than any name. There's a warm place, and there's somebody else in it, somebody exactly his size. It's very dark. He's so hungry.

And then there's only one of them.

That's all. That's the whole story. Everything else has just been the rest of the meal.

* * *

The whole account comes in.

What he knows. Every technique he owns, every refinement he has actually built, the decisions under the movements Mau has spent all afternoon barely surviving. Nothing he might have invented and never did. Nothing he merely saw. Noise takes all of it and shuts the warehouse door, and Mau won't have to live those thousand years between one heartbeat and the next. He has just been handed them, in a box, with no idea what most of it means.

Ciel has started sorting. The route of the cut. The moment of assignment. What the fourth mark conceals, and how to tell.

There is one peg, at the very back, with nothing on it. A last lock on a last cut, one with no corners at all. Sukuna built it during the years he was gone, and he has never turned it. It takes one word. Ciel looks at it for some time and doesn't say anything, and goes on to the next thing.

The transfer doesn't spare either mind. Sukuna has stopped being surprised.

It comes back up the path like a fist coming up a sleeve. He's got his own will behind it, a thousand years of being the only thing in any room, and he drives that hostile intention through the contact and tries to make Mau accept a movement which ends only one way. Mau's fingers have begun to make a sign. He hadn't asked them to. It's a very good sign, very clean. It's meant for his own chest.

He ends the spell.

* * *

The return is worse than the lesson.

For a moment he has too many hands.

He has too many hands, and too many positions from which the next cut should be possible, and he's so tall. He's standing in a quarry looking down at a small bleeding person who is somehow also him. He's so hungry. There's a boy here. What a nice boy. What's he made of? He'd like to know.

He names the present things without speaking. He has been shown how. *That's horrible,* she'd said, opening her eyes.

The ground. The pain in his leg. His two hands.

Two.

One of them new and one of them with two bad fingers. Far off, up the long slope, past the shields and the panels and every other grey thing in this whole grey world, one small point of colour, standing. She's about the size of a grain of rice from here. She isn't a lever. A remembered enemy would very much like to use her as one, he's just seen how, and that isn't what she is.

*That's mine,* he thinks. *That one's me. That's how you can tell.*

Mau comes back.

* * *

Sukuna's eyes are still finding the quarry.

All four of them, blinking out of time. He's got one hand held out in front of him and he's looking at it as if he has just been told about hands. There's a thin line of spit running from the lower mouth. For that moment he's not anyone at all. He's a big old tired thing that has just had somebody go through its pockets.

Up on the ridge, Frieren has stood up. She has never been known to do this.

“He's first,” she says. ᶠ

Ori looks up at her.

“You have to come back before they do,” Frieren says. She seems to be telling the bench. “He came back first.” ᶠ

Mau is already moving.

He strikes the nearest conducting point before the man can make the field belong to it. His Dismantle is smaller than the last one Sukuna threw, and it goes to a much better place, because now he knows where the wrist is supposed to be. It's such a little correction. Everyone has been telling him. It breaks a charged surface he had never seen. The second one takes the line Sukuna would have used to fix the first. By the third, the man has enough of himself back to move, and Mau has enough of *his* to know where that move has to go.

Their fists meet.

He doesn't suddenly do everything in the warehouse. He has no idea what most of it is for. He uses what's already in his hand, the same two cuts he's had since he was young and stupid, and for the first time in either life they arrive at the proper moment. It's enough to make Sukuna's next breath come without the cut he'd meant to put in it.

A thin red line appears along the man's upper arm. It's very shallow. It's hardly a scratch.

Sukuna looks at it.

Then, slowly, he looks at Mau. He's all there now. Whatever he's seen on Mau's face, it has brought every bit of him back.

“You took the whole thing,” he says. ˢᵘ

Mau is breathing hard enough that the answer nearly fails him.

“You weren't going to explain it kindly.” ᴹ

Sukuna laughs, sharp and delighted, right through the blood. The lower mouth takes a wet breath and starts to murmur again.

“That's a good wrist,” the upper one remarks. “I'd know. Where did you—” ˢᵘ

“One,” Mau says. ᴹ

It stops him. It actually stops him. The big hands, the one coming round and the other, all pause for about the length of a blink.

Mau wipes his nose on his shoulder. There's so much he doesn't know. His head hurts in a way that's new. He has seen things today that he'd give anything not to have seen, he's got the top of an ear gone and eleven holes in him, and he's looking up at the worst thing that has ever lived. He can feel something on his own face.

“Stop complimenting me,” he says. “It's creepy.” ᴹ

For a moment there isn't a sound in the quarry.

Then the king of curses throws back his head and *howls*.

*Mau,* Ciel says, very quietly, underneath it. ᶜⁱ

*What?*

*You're smiling.*

*So?*

There's a short silence.

*Whose is that?* she asks. ᶜⁱ
