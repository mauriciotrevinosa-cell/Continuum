# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 14 — A Place of His Own

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S9E46
**Continuity state:** B136 continuous. Mau's territory holds: Void-derived barrier, shadow floor by Monarch route. Nested open Shrine succeeds on one strip for one step, then real neural injury and unreliable innate casting. Sukuna answers with Hollow Wicker Basket and a shrine worn at arm's length, burning his CT down. Sukuna loses left upper eye and cheek. No verdict. Both still fighting.

*When: Mau opens the territory, the first shadow which enters does not attack.*

Igris takes the position they practised. Bellion takes the second. Beru arrives at the third with one arm still unfinished.

The barrier closes.

There's no ink drop this time, no little frightened room the size of a farmhouse. It stands up off the floor of the quarry smooth as an egg, and the panels show it from the outside for one moment, black, seamless, about the size of a good-sized barn. Then there's nothing to see, because Ramiris's light doesn't go in there, and every person on the ridge turns, without being asked, to look at the only person who can tell them anything.

Gojo has stopped gripping the rail.

“Oh,” he says. “Oh, that's not bad. You got the right side.” ᴳ

He doesn't seem to know he's said it out loud. He has put his face a little forward, like a man listening to music.

* * *

Mau can feel the part they got right.

The floor keeps its depth. The far wall is a long way off, as Gojo promised it would be. It's a good deal farther than he can see, and it's quiet. He puts his heel down on stone and his heel goes down on shadow too, both, the one laid over the other like a hand on a hand, and he can call an officer up out of the very same place. Nothing in the barrier needs to know how the shadow got there. He built that. He spent a hundred and thirty mornings on that, and a bowl, and a lot of bad drawings with a stick in them for Beru.

It holds.

He'd like to stop for a moment. He'd like someone to come and look.

Sukuna's standing in it with his head tipped back. He's lost an arm and he's breathing through his mouth, and he's looking around at the depth with every sign of enjoying the workmanship.

“Three breaths,” Ciel tells him. “Count the first one.” ᶜⁱ

He does. In that breath Sukuna crosses half the floor.

There's the other difference, between this and what he tries next.

He hasn't got a sure-hit. That was always the point. He has a room. A very expensive room, *congratulations on your room*, with nothing in it but where you'd rather not be standing. A domain has to do something to you. He's spent all these months working out what his should do, and Gojo drew him diagrams, and the two of them always stopped at the same place. It takes a thing to put in there, and whatever you put in it needs a part of the brain to run on, and you only have the one.

But there's a thing he's learned today that nobody ever told him. It's in the warehouse, on top. A domain doesn't need walls.

So, you could have walls. And inside them you could leave a space for a shrine that doesn't have any.

He has seen how it's done. He has seen it ten thousand times. He's done it, with these hands.

They're not these hands.

Sukuna closes two fingers into the sign for Hollow Wicker Basket, and something like a cage of pale reeds goes up around his skin. A third hand's free. It checks Bellion's sword as it comes down. It catches Mau's wrist just as the dagger gets to his ribs.

Mau leaves Limitless in the wall.

He reaches for Shrine with his own body.

* * *

Gojo sees the edge of the barrier bite into the ground.

It should be smooth. Every time Mau practised this, the seam was smooth.

There's a line coming out of it now. It's a second curvature, trying to close inside the first, and it hasn't got a wall of its own, and it can't be seen by anyone at all but him. It's beautiful. He'd like that known. He has lived for a long time, in two worlds, and he's seen the best anyone ever did. He knows exactly what Mau's asking it to do. He can see where it starts to answer.

Nobody in the history of this art has ever done that.

“Let that one go,” he says. ᴳ

Yuta looks at him.

He's speaking to himself. His hands have closed around the railing again, and he's already pulling them away before anyone has to tell him the man down there can't hear him. His eyes are very wide and very blue, and running.

“It's so good,” Gojo says. “You idiot. You can't, it's so *good*. Let it go.” ᴳ

* * *

A strip of stone opens under Sukuna's heel.

It's the width of a road and it runs from Mau's feet to the far side of the man. It goes to pieces, all of it, all in one instant, in fine parallel cuts like the pages of a book. There's no finding it, or aiming. It's just *so*, everywhere on that strip, because it's his, and because he says so. Sukuna lifts his foot barely in time to keep it. The reeds of the Basket go on the same stroke, and Igris's blade goes through the hand that had been waiting for him there.

Sukuna looks up. There's a look of wild surmise on him.

He throws his whole will outward. It doesn't take an instant. He can't open the great house again, he's burned too far down for that. So he opens a small one. He pulls the shrine in close, right against his skin, arm's length, no more, and there he is, wearing it. He's going through his own technique like a man burning his furniture. Everything that comes within reach of him simply comes apart.

The two sure-hits meet on one strip of floor.

It's like nothing at all. It's two people, both of whom own a thing. Stone can't take it. Air can't. Something's happening to the light down there, and they would never see it on the ridge; it's only the two of them who ever will. It's the only place it has ever happened or ever will. All the world's sum of cutting has come to one narrow road, and it's going both ways, and Mau is on that road. He has about one step's worth of room.

He takes it.

It works.

It works for just that long. He gets his foot down and gets his weight over it, and for one stupid, exhilarating instant he can feel how a person could build a whole life around that single step. The point of his dagger goes up under the second jaw. On the way it passes something that's cutting him to ribbons and something he's cutting back. The whole left side of that big happy face comes away, from the brow to the corner of the mouth, cheek, teeth, the upper eye, in a neat wet sheet. And Sukuna is still looking at him out of the other three.

Then both edges ask him to decide what a target is.

He tells the second one to stop.

The first one tries to stop with it.

Something goes white behind his left eye. Not light. Light would have given him something to see.

* * *

Ciel cuts the connection.

She doesn't ask. The nested line dies first. Then the barrier falls outward all at once in dry pieces, like a pot breaking. The sky comes back. All the people on the ridge let out a breath they don't know about. The quarry comes back into his right eye and nowhere else.

Sukuna hits him in the throat.

Mau puts a hand on the stone, because if he doesn't he will fall. The hand lands. He doesn't feel the landing until he is trying to lift it.

“Did it close?” ᴹ

“No.” ᶜⁱ

He hears her say something else. He can tell from the shape of her voice that she has said it twice.

He lets go of Shrine. There's a place where he would go to let go of things. He goes there, and it's a bit farther to the left than it was.

* * *

Sukuna tries to open Mau's hand with a cut. Mau sees the fingers move and drops the dagger himself. It turns once. Bellion catches the handle, drives it across the ground with the flat of his sword and puts it where Mau's other hand can reach.

The cut never arrives.

Sukuna looks down at his own palm. What's left of the upper mouth smiles. The lower one spits blood.

He has used the same hand for that cut a hundred times. This time all it has given him is enough heat to feel the skin part where Igris struck. The small shrine he was wearing has gone out. He's burned through it, all of it, every stick. He stands there holding up that big, red, ordinary empty hand, and he looks at it for a long moment, so that anyone who wants to can see.

He closes it into a fist.

* * *

For a while Elmesia can only make sense of the fight by following the discarded things. A broken sword dragged away from a foot. A fallen piece of a shadow's armour. Two bodies moving around each other where there had been a thing large enough to hide them both.

Coco hears Qifrey set his cane down. She expects him to say something about how the barrier broke. Instead he opens his hand and leaves it on the bench beside hers. She can take it or leave it there.

She takes it.

In the protected room DIO reaches for the back of a chair she has not been using. Ashborn brings the chair the rest of the way. The shadow in the stands does not move. Neither of them speaks down the connection.

* * *

Mau attempts Blue once more.

He knows the shape. That is the terrible part. He can stand in the ruined quarry and explain the shape to himself, down to the turn of his wrist. He has a warehouse full of shapes. He has every shape in the world. When he tries to make it, the pain drops his wrist before he has gathered enough force to pull a pebble.

Infinity isn't there either. He looks for it. Ten Shadows. It's as if someone has been through the house and taken all the doorknobs.

Sukuna sees it.

He takes Mau by the front of what's left of his shirt and runs him backward until the stone stops them. Mau can't pull space out from behind his heels. He has one good leg and the knife in his left hand. He bends his knee, drags Sukuna down to it and feels the knife go in.

Sukuna leans farther into it instead of pulling away.

Mau has to let go of the handle to keep the other man's thumbs out of his eyes.

His fingers close around Sukuna's wrist. The new hand obeys. The old one obeys a little later. He makes them hold anyway.
