# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 15 — Close Enough to Hold

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 15
**Source episode:** S9E47
**Continuity state:** B136 continuous. Both active. Mau's innate casting gone. Sukuna shows an empty hand; Mau knows from the archive what that means and cannot act on it. Wounds and finite reserves accumulate. Bellion's form lost, not permanent. Igris braces the knee. No aid from stands or guests.

*When: Sukuna twists his wrist free, Mau finds that he has kept hold of two fingers.*

He uses them to pull.

Sukuna comes with the pull. His elbow catches Mau above the eye. Mau's knee gives on the next step, and for a moment neither of them is standing; they are only heavier than the piece of stone the other man wants to get to first.

Sukuna gets there. He drives Mau's damaged leg against the edge.

The leg folds. Mau does not scream until he tries to put weight back on it. He can feel the Black Heart send more than enough mana toward the break. All the mana in the world, a whole ocean of it, and nothing that can make the bone hold still long enough to set. Every time he gets the ends to touch, Sukuna makes him move again.

“Leave the knee,” Ciel says. “Brace it.” ᶜⁱ

He gets a shadow under it. The brace is Igris's forearm.

The knight has been looking for something to do. He takes the weight without a word, on one knee in the rubble, with his sword gone and most of his armour burned off. Mau pushes off him and hits Sukuna in the ruin of his face with the top of his head.

Then Sukuna takes hold of the dagger that is still in his own side and drives it deeper. Mau has barely understood why when the handle disappears beyond his reach, and Sukuna's freed hand catches him by the throat.

* * *

Rem feels Umaru turn before she hears her say anything.

She pulls her close. Umaru puts both hands against Rem's ribs as if there is something she should push away, then leaves them there.

“I know he's still up,” Umaru says. ᵁ

“He is.” ᴿᵉ

She holds the back of Umaru's head and covers the gap between their faces and the field with her shoulder. Taihei sits down on the other side. The three of them stay that way through a sound none of them can pretend came from the stone.

A few seats over, Ori asks Frieren if she can go inside without making Mau think she left.

Frieren takes her hand.

“He knows you came.” ᶠ

Ori thinks about it. Then she asks if she can bring somebody. There is a girl in the aisle who has been trying to get past her own knees for several seconds. Ori knows that look. She gets up, goes to Agott and asks her if she wants to come find Shizue.

Agott says yes on the second try.

* * *

Mau tries to draw air through a grip that only tightens when he fights it.

Bellion comes in from Sukuna's blind side, and there is a good deal more blind side than there was. Sukuna lets his own back take the point. It goes through enough flesh to stop before it can find Mau. He catches the long blade under one elbow and brings his other fist down on the marshal's head. The armour loses shape. Mau feels the link sink into the floor. The next time he tries to bring Bellion back, he gets the movement of a hand and nothing attached to it.

He stops asking.

Sukuna hasn't let go.

Mau presses his free palm into the wound where the dagger went. His fingers find the handle.

He can't draw it out. Sukuna has already made sure of that. He can turn it.

The grip loosens for less than a second. Mau gets enough air for one small, animal noise and turns the knife again.

They hit the ground apart.

* * *

Shoko sees Mau roll onto the injured leg to get the other one under him. She taught him how to get off the floor with a broken rib. She never taught him what to do when the list of things he could safely put weight on had come down to one foot.

“I helped send him down there,” she says. ˢʰ

Gojo doesn't tell her the city would have been at risk either way. She knows what was at risk. She counted it before she said he should go.

He puts his hand on the rail beside hers. She takes two of his fingers and grips them hard enough to hurt.

“Don't tell me you can see how this ends.” ˢʰ

“I can't.” ᴳ

She believes him. It is the first thing he has said in a long time that she wishes he had lied about.

* * *

Across the ridge a man who came to see whether the settlement could defend his town asks Elmesia what happens if the one who gets up is Mau.

“He goes home to his family,” she says. ᴱˡ

The man keeps looking at the quarry. He meant to ask something else. He doesn't have the nerve to say it now that she has answered him.

Makima hears him thank her anyway.

* * *

Sukuna gets his feet under him first.

Mau sees him lift a hand. Nothing gathers. Sukuna looks at the hand and then at Mau, and he makes a fist, slowly enough that Mau can see the smallest finger fail to close. He has the look of a tired old man at the end of a long meal. All over the ridge, people who have spent an hour learning what that hand can do watch it fail. They begin to breathe. Word goes along the benches: he's out, they're both out, he has nothing left.

Mau looks at the hand.

He knows that hand. He has been inside it.

There is a warehouse in his head with a thousand years of this man in it, and he can barely find his way to the door. His thoughts come in pieces now, and some of the pieces come twice. But one thing lies right on top. A river town. A man in a good coat, stepping forward at last, relieved. It has worked eleven hundred times. It works every time. No one can believe it of someone so proud.

He has never once, in all that time, shown anybody an empty hand for free.

*Ciel,* he says. It takes some finding. *How much has he got?*

*I can't see it. He's closed up. I can't—Mau, your eyes aren't giving me what they were.* ᶜⁱ

*He's got one.*

*One what?*

He doesn't answer. He's looking at the peg at the back of the warehouse, the one with nothing on it, and there is no possible thing he can do about it. He has no technique, and no doors. He hasn't got the wit left for a new idea. He has got one good leg.

So he does the one thing he can think of. He gets up, before the man can choose which hand to use.

Sukuna sees him do it.

Sukuna sees him look at the hand, and sees the look. And the half of his mouth that's left curls very slightly at one corner. He looks like somebody across a card table, who has just found out that you have read his cards, and doesn't mind a bit.
