*When: Sukuna sets his feet, the people who were beginning to breathe stop.*

Nobody has told them anything. There's nothing to tell. A man with half a face is standing on a flat rock twenty paces from a man who can barely stand at all. His two hands have come up in front of his chest and they aren't shaking.

But Diablo has stopped looking at his cuff.

He's taken three quick steps along the shield and set his palm on it, up high, where it curves toward the sky. Luminous has come down from her seat. Nobody has seen her walk before. Milim stands staring, and she has gone very quiet, and the hair has lifted along her arms.

The whole floor of the basin has started to hum. It's a thin high note, more in the teeth than the ear. The dust is doing something strange. Every grain of it for a mile has begun, ever so slightly, to lean.

“That hand was empty,” Hinata says. ᴴⁱ

“No,” says Gojo. ᴳ

He's on his feet at the rail, and his hands have gone down to his sides. He has the blindfold pushed up. He's looking straight down that long slope with the only eyes in the world that can see it. Nothing's coming out of him at all. Yuta takes one look and doesn't try to say his name.

Shoko has hold of his sleeve. She can feel him shaking through it, the very fine steady shake of a wire.

“How much did he keep?” she says. “Satoru. How much?” ˢʰ

“All of it.” ᴳ

* * *

Mau watches him do it.

He knows what it is. It's the worst part of the whole arrangement, that he's the only other person alive who knows. He has every detail. He can see how the man has been putting this by, a little at a time, from the first minute of the first exchange, through the shrine and the fire, and through losing a friend, an arm, an arm. He let them hit him. He let his technique go out in his own hand, in front of the entire western world, and he smiled about it, and all that time this was sitting under his heart, like the last coin in a very old purse.

It's got no corners. It doesn't need them. It has no plane. He had the shape of Mau's wall from the wheel, he paid for that, and it doesn't need to get through the wall. It's going to take the place the wall is standing in. All of it, with the man inside, from here to the end of the sky.

So. What has he got?

He goes through it. It takes a while. His thoughts are coming to him in pieces, like people arriving late at a funeral.

Infinity: not there. Doors: no, the route has gone away with whatever went white. Sky: no. Blue: no.

*The scale?* he says.

*It targets the space. There's no pressure in the world that helps. And it would kill you.* ᶜⁱ

*The slime.*

*The space, Mau.*

*Run?*

*On what?*

He looks down. There's the leg with Igris's forearm gone from under it, and a brace of shadow he doesn't remember making. He could yield. It's right there, written on page one, in very clear handwriting; somebody's wife insisted. All he has to do is say so and the man will put down his hands. He'll be alive. He'll stay alive a good long time, awake, on a chain, doing what he's told by someone with a nice voice. They'd have to watch that, too.

He stops looking at that one.

There's only the other thing. It isn't an idea. He hadn't got as far as an idea. He only has three notches in the dark, a small dull ache at the bottom of his shadow. He has a thing he said once, in a white place, to someone who was listening very hard. He said it the way you'd tell someone how to carry a bucket. *Think about it, every time. What do you want him to learn? What can he take without going down?*

*Even one piece of him left is enough.*

He hasn't got a him to call. He looked. There's a smear in the dark with no more shape to it than a bruise.

Mau breathes in, as far as that goes. He takes his weight off the brace. He shuffles round a little, on the good foot, until he's standing square.

*Mau,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

He doesn't say anything.

*Mau. Tell me what you're doing.*

*You know.*

There's quite a long pause, for someone who thinks as fast as she does.

*Yes,* Ciel says. *I hate it. Left foot.* ᶜⁱ

He puts his left foot where she wants it.

* * *

“Three,” Yuka says. ʸ

It isn't to anybody. She has come all the way down to the rail. Mai is holding her by the elbow, and Maki's holding her other hand. She doesn't feel either of them.

She's looking at how he's standing.

She has that creature in her own shadow. She has stood where it stood, and she knows what it looks like when it stops moving and raises its head. She has seen it on a white plain and in a dungeon and in a hundred bad drawings on the kitchen table. There's a particular way it plants itself. It isn't a fighting stance at all. It's how you stand when you've been told to hold still for something.

“He needs a fourth,” she says. ʸ

“Yuka,” Maki says. ᴹᵃ

“He hasn't got him. He can't call him, there's nothing left, he's used him all up. There's nobody left to take it.” ʸ

Her voice is entirely level. She has a very clear, reasonable voice. She got it from her grandmother.

“There's nobody left to take it,” she says again. And then, in the same voice, and no one who hears it is ever going to forget it, “Oh, you *stupid*—” ʸ

Below them, Gojo's mouth has come open.

He has got something to say. He knows what it is. It would only take one word, in the voice he uses for this, and it would carry the whole length of that quarry, panels or no panels. There are about nine different people along this rail who would like very much to tell a boy to get down, and only he has a chance of being heard. It's forbidden on page four. It would void everything. It would hand that boy over on a chain.

Shoko puts her hand across his mouth.

He lets her. He stands there with her palm on his teeth and his eyes wide open, and all the colour has gone out of him. He looks the way he must have looked to someone, once, in the last clear moment, on a very good day.

* * *

The hum has stopped.

Sukuna stands with the sign held in front of him. Every bit of a thousand years is in that sign, and it has got to be about the most beautiful thing Mau has ever seen. He wishes it were somebody else's.

“I've been called a great many things,” Sukuna says. ˢᵘ

He doesn't raise his voice. There isn't a person on the ridge who can't hear it. His mouth is ruined and it comes out a bit thick, like a man talking around a hot potato.

“And I've called people. I never saw the use in learning what they called themselves. I had them for an afternoon. Some of them shouted it at me, as if I'd want it. They went to a lot of trouble.” ˢᵘ

He looks at Mau, with three eyes.

“This one needs a name,” he says. “That's all. It's the only thing it needs, and it won't turn for a lie. I had to stop and think who I could mean it about. I thought about that for some time. I built it, and I hung it up, and I went looking. I'd about decided it would stay on the peg.” ˢᵘ

In six days of letters and tables, in all of the long afternoon, he has called Mau a good many things. *Boy. Brat. Little monarch.* Up on the ridge there are perhaps four people, the sort who listen, who have noticed what he's never called him.

It has gone so quiet that you can hear a pebble settle, a long way off, on the far side of the field.

Mau looks back at him.

He'd like to say something. He had something, a good one, it was about the wrist. It's gone. He thinks that his wife is going to be very angry. He can see her from here, she's about the size of a grain of rice. Everything else in the world is grey.

Sukuna smiles, as well as he's able. It is the first entirely kind thing that has ever happened on that face.

“Mau,” he says. ˢᵘ

* * *

Nothing happens.

For the length of one breath, there is simply no change of any sort. A man is standing in a quarry. Behind him stands the great north wall that the fire left. Over that is the sky, with some high thin cloud in it, going gold at the edges. It's getting on for evening.

Then the wall isn't quite right.

There's a line on it. It's perfectly straight and very fine. It runs from one end of the wall to the other, a mile, and it goes on past the end of the wall. It rises, as it goes, at a shallow angle, and there's a place along the outer shield, high up over Diablo's hand, where the light has parted in a long clean seam. Both halves stay exactly where they are. It goes up over the heads of everyone on the ridge, and they don't feel a thing. It goes on rising, and it reaches that high gold cloud, and the cloud has an edge now. It has two edges. Far off, in another country, a shepherd looks up and wonders about the sky.

It takes that long for the sound to start. It isn't really a sound. It's more as though everything has let go of the thing it was holding, for a moment, and then taken hold again.

The line comes back through the middle of the field at about the height of a man's waist.

Gojo's hand has gone there. It's gone there on its own.

On every panel on the ridge, the same picture, very clear, in that kind low evening light. He's standing just as he was, feet square, one arm hanging. He hasn't moved at all. He's looking straight ahead of him. There's a thin bright thread drawn around the middle of him, as if someone has tied a string there.

The upper part of him begins to slide.
