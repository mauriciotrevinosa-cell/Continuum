# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 19 — The Weight of the Other Man

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S9E48
**Continuity state:** B+136 continuous. Physical clinch, no offensive casting on either side. Dagger drawn from Sukuna and into Mau low. Sukuna's head against the bottom step. Both down, both records active. Margin still held.

*When: Mau catches Sukuna's first punch on his shoulder, it is by accident.*

He meant to go left. The knee that used to take him left goes straight down. The blow lands higher than Sukuna intended and takes them both off balance.

There has been so much, today. The sky has been cut in half. It comes down in the end to two people who can't really lift their arms, in the long evening light, on a stone floor. One has half a face. The other's held together at the waist by a great many strangers. Neither of them could tell you what time it is. Sukuna's next punch misses altogether, and he stands looking at his fist as if it has spoken out of turn. Mau hits him with his forehead, because that's the part of him that still comes when it's called.

It makes a small dull sound.

It doesn't go through any guard or crack across any distance. It's about the noise of a dropped loaf.

On the ridge people have started to look away, and it isn't for the reason you would think. They've spent all day watching something too large to look at. This is so much worse. Anyone can see what this is.

Mau goes first. He gets his head under Sukuna's chin and pushes until he feels the edge of the quarry step against the other man's heels.

He has just enough strength to push him over it.

Sukuna has just enough to take him along.

* * *

They fall less than the height of a man. It looks like nothing from the ridge.

Mau lands with the weight of him across his ribs. For a moment the world becomes exactly as wide as the place he cannot draw breath into. The seam around his middle shifts and groans, and a thousand hands take a fresh grip. He's aware of them from a long way off, like hearing someone moving furniture on another floor.

His right arm still won't come up. He can move the elbow a little if he turns his whole body. So he turns. Sukuna gets one hand underneath him. The other goes searching for his throat in the small dark space between them.

Mau finds the handle of the dagger against his stomach.

It's still in the man. It has been in him for an hour, that long dark blade, with a hilt he has lost and found so many times that it's come to feel like the handle of his own front door. He doesn't try to pull it free. He takes the hand that is looking for his throat, and closes it around the handle with his own. Sukuna could let it go. Mau feels him make the decision to use the grip and wrench.

The blade comes out of Sukuna.

Mau rolls toward it. The point goes into him low, under the seam, in a place nobody was holding. He spends everything the good leg has left driving himself farther along the blade, until the guard stops him and the hand between them can't come up.

It's the only way he can think of to take that hand away.

Their foreheads touch.

Sukuna's one good upper eye is about an inch away. It's very dark, and very clear. It's amused. They're breathing each other's breath. There's nothing in the world but this, and for a moment Mau has the absurd idea that he's about to be told a secret.

He slips his free hand behind the man's head, with its two bad fingers, and drives it against the bottom step.

Once.

Sukuna claws under his jaw.

Twice.

Mau's hand opens. He has to look at it to find out why. Then the two of them go down together, the whole loose heavy weight of the other man coming down on top of him, and neither one of them is moving at all.

* * *

Yuka hears the ridge begin to move. One person standing, then another, each waiting to see who will be the first to go down.

It is Milim who puts her hands out across the entrance. She puts them high, where even the people behind her can see what she is doing.

“Wait for them to tell us.” ᴹⁱ

Her voice shakes. The nearest guard stops anyway.

At the tables, both clerks look down. Gazel's clerk raises the slate. Testarossa does the same a breath later. Still active. The same for both.

Yuka looks back. From up here there's so little difference between two living men and two heaps of clothing that she has to find the back of Mau's head to recognize him.

It's easy enough. He's the only grey thing in the world that she's looking at.

* * *

In the protected room Ashborn stands up.

He was standing before he decided to. He goes as far as the door, puts a hand on it and remembers that he would still be on this side if he opened it. The shadow they are watching through sits where Mau asked it to sit.

DIO draws the empty chair closer to hers.

“We can wait for him here.” ᴰⁱ

Ashborn returns to the chair. He sits on the edge of it. Neither of them asks the shadow for a closer view.

* * *

The first thing Ciel can get Mau to do is swallow.

It takes three tries. She waits through all three.

There is a route at the base of his skull that she has been keeping warm with her own attention for most of an hour, because if he loses that, nothing he has ever learned about putting himself back together will have anywhere to begin. Next to it, sealed, exactly where they put it two days ago, is the margin. It's a very small amount of cursed energy. It's the last there is. *I'll think I need it,* he said, at home, at a table. She has told him *not the margin* so many times today that it has begun to sound like his name.

She doesn't open it. She looks at it. She goes on keeping the route warm with what she has.

Mau tries to move his tongue. She tells him to save it.

He tries the knee instead. This time nothing is driving it sideways. The brace takes. He can feel mana move through the bone and the rough line of the repair hold for one breath.

He gives it a second breath before he trusts it with anything.

There's a weight on him. It's very heavy. He'd like to know who it is.

It moves.

A hand comes down on the stone by his face. It's a big hand and it's shaking, and it slides away on the first attempt, leaving a long red smear. On the second, the fingers find dry rock. The weight lifts about the width of a book.

Mau's own hand is under him. He can't remember putting it there. He bears down on it until the rest of the arm comes into view.

Two men on the ground begin to get up.
