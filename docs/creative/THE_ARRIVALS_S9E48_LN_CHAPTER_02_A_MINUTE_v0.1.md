# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 59
## Chapter 23 — A Minute

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 59
**Reading order:** 23
**Source episode:** S9E48
**Continuity state:** B+136 continuous. Verdict stands. Voluntary brief shadow under the reciprocal permission; Sukuna keeps identity, accepts to talk, affirms a worthy death, and returns to the abyss by himself. No permanent roster addition. No knowledge exchanged. Mau alive with major fresh injuries.

*When: Yuka has come down the last of the steps, Mau says something she has to stop to hear.*

“A minute.” ᴹ

He isn't saying it to her. He's looking at the ground beside him. It takes about all he has.

She stops. She puts one hand out toward Shoko, without turning, and Shoko stops as well, three paces short, with her kit in her hand and her whole body leaning. It's the hardest thing either of them will do this year. They stay where he can see them.

He has got his left palm flat on the stone, on the stain that's spreading from the step. There's a thing written on a page, a thing he asked for at a table, on a bad afternoon, to make a man angry. *When I beat you I'm going to make you a shadow.* It's his to do. He's won it. Nobody could say otherwise.

He doesn't want it. He knows what this man fought for, the whole thousand years of it; he has it in a box. It was never something you could keep. You couldn't put it in a line with the others and tell it where to stand. And a man who has lost like that is owed the asking.

“If you want it,” he says, to the stone. ᴹ

He says the word.

* * *

For a little while nothing comes.

The stain lies there. Shoko has stopped breathing. Then something happens to the dark, close beside his knee. It thickens. It takes its time, the way someone does who's been asked to a thing, and who is deciding whether it's worth getting up for.

Sukuna rises far enough to sit beside him.

He comes up black from head to foot and quite whole. He has got his four arms, and his face, all of it. He looks at his hands. He turns one palm up as if he expects to find the marks of the stone in it. Mau watches him flex all five fingers. He flexes the other hand. He seems pleased with the workmanship.

He looks at the thing on the step. He looks for some time, with his head on one side.

“Well,” he says. “That's thorough.” ˢᵘ

His voice is exactly the same. Every mouth he's got is smiling.

He sets his hands on his knees and looks around at the quarry, at the far wall with its long straight line. He looks up at the sky, and there it is. He's a person who has made a good many things. He looks at that one a little longer than the rest.

“You could have asked for longer,” he says. ˢᵘ

Mau shifts his heel out of the blood. It takes a while.

“Would you have said yes?” ᴹ

Sukuna thinks about it.

“I said yes to this.” ˢᵘ

For the length of a breath neither of them says anything. There are people going away, up on the ridge, in long dark lines. There are two women standing very close by, one of them made of the only colour in the world. He knows they're there, the shadow does. He gives them one glance, the same as you would give a clock.

“You bit me,” he remarks. ˢᵘ

Mau considers this. It seems to be true.

“Yes.” ᴹ

“I've been wondering. I've had a good many people try. Nobody's ever got that far. What was it like?” ˢᵘ

He actually wants to know. He has turned all four eyes on him, with deep and courteous interest. He has held a great many opinions on the subject, and has never had anyone to compare them with. Mau runs his tongue around his mouth, over a loose tooth, and spits. He gives the question his full attention.

“Terrible,” he says. ᴹ

It's a good deal more than the man was expecting. It starts in the shoulders. He throws his head back, and for a moment there's no sound at all, because he's got nothing to make it with. Then it comes out, that great delighted roar, from a throat that isn't there, and all the way up at the top of the ridge people stop dead on the path without knowing why.

“I could have told you,” he says, when he can. “A thousand years. It's all gristle.” ˢᵘ

He wipes his eye. He looks at Mau, for a time, without saying anything.

“You, now,” he says. “You were the best thing I ever ate.” ˢᵘ

Mau looks at him.

“One,” he says. ᴹ

That nearly sets him off again.

* * *

The minute's going.

They both know it. It's nothing that's being done to him. There's no timer on it; there's only that he said a minute, and he's the sort of person who'd know. Mau has the feeling that there's something he came here to ask. He'd like to get it right. It's on the page, or it isn't. It's the one thing he wanted out of all this that wasn't for anyone else. He goes down and gets it. It's heavy, and he brings it up in both hands.

“Was it,” he says. “That. Was that a worthy death?” ᴹ

Sukuna stops smiling.

He does it all at once, with all his mouths, and it's not a thing Mau has seen more than once or twice. He turns and looks at him straight. There's a very long road behind him, and at the near end of it is a little thing on a step. He looks at the road. He looks at the boy who walked up it, and who is sitting here, in a pool of blood, with a loose tooth, waiting to hear.

“Yes,” he says. “It was a worthy death.” ˢᵘ

He says it with the same attention he gave Mau's first step into the quarry. He wants to get that right. He seems to feel it matters, even now.

Mau nods. He lets out a breath. It's good. He's glad to have it. It's a thing he can tell someone, maybe. He looks at the stone for a bit. It's got a very interesting grain.

There was something he wanted. He had it just now. He came over here for a reason, and it's very important. It was about the man. There's this man here, sitting beside him, and he ought to ask him. He looks up. It takes some doing. He can't think why everyone is standing so still.

“Was it,” Mau says. “Was that a worthy death?” ᴹ

Three paces away, Shoko shuts her eyes.

The shadow doesn't move. He's looking at Mau's eye, the right one, at how it slips, and comes back, and doesn't quite know where it's been. He knows exactly what he's looking at. He has done that to a lot of people. It has never in a thousand years held the least interest for him, what happens to them after.

He doesn't laugh.

“Yes,” Sukuna says. “It was.” ˢᵘ

He says it precisely the same way, with no less care than the first time. He gives it all his attention. He waits until he's quite sure it has arrived.

“Good,” Mau says. ᴹ

And then, looking down, because it's been bothering him, and he's sure someone ought to mention it:

“Your wrist,” he says. ᴹ

“Mine is excellent. Yours is late.” ˢᵘ

He puts his hands on his knees. All four of them. It's what anyone would do, at the end of a long meal. He pushes himself upright, and the shadow opens beneath him with the motion, wide and very quiet. Mau can feel the choice go through it. There's nothing holding him, no place kept for him; there's not one thing in the whole of that dark that would stop him if he wanted to stay. There are six thousand men down there who'd have to shuffle over.

He stands looking down into it, with his back to all of them. He's so tall. He has been hungry since before he had a name. He's been looking for this for longer than there's been anyone to tell it to.

He breathes in. He doesn't need to. You can see his shoulders go down.

“There,” says the king of curses, and it's the smallest voice Mau has ever heard from him. It sounds surprised. “I've eaten.” ˢᵘ

He steps off.

Mau keeps his hand flat on the ground until he has gone as far as he wishes to go.

The place beside him empties.

* * *

Yuka comes the rest of the way down. There is a place where she can kneel that will keep the weight off his leg. Shoko shows it to her with one hand while the other reaches for his neck.

Mau searches for something to say that will tell them how much of him is still there. The more he looks for it, the farther away it gets.

Yuka sets her palm down in front of him.

“You can just touch it.” ʸ

He moves his smallest finger across the stone.

She meets him where it stops.
