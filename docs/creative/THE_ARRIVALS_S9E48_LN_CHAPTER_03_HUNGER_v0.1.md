*When: Sukuna gets as far as his elbows, he looks at his hands and appears to come to a decision about them.*

They're no use. Anyone can see that. One has been bitten through, by a knight, or an ant, nobody will ever know. The other has three fingers that have stopped taking messages. He holds it up in front of what's left of his face and turns it over. He tells it something; the small muscles in the wrist jump. He lets it drop.

He's lying across Mau's chest. He's very heavy. Mau has got his left arm trapped under the two of them and his right is a thing that's on the ground nearby.

So here they are.

Sukuna looks at him from about a hand's breadth away.

He has stopped smiling at last. He looks rather peaceful. It's the look of someone who's come in out of the weather and sat down, and taken off his boots. He has been at the end of a long road for a very long time, and it has turned out that there's a place there. He has been so hungry, all his life. It has been such a long day.

He lowers his head.

It's almost gentle. It's about the way you'd lean down to tell someone a thing in confidence. His mouth, the half that still closes, comes down in the angle between Mau's neck and his shoulder. It rests there for a moment, warm.

Then it closes.

* * *

The panels carry the sound. No one has thought to tell Ramiris's light that it shouldn't.

It goes along the ridge and it does something the fire couldn't do. People who sat through the arm and the shrine, and the man cutting pieces out of himself, get up from their places all together and they do not look at each other. A mother has got her child's whole head inside her coat. In the third row Bocchi Hitori has put her hands over her ears, and it hasn't helped. Rem doesn't have to do anything; she's been holding Umaru's face against her for a quarter of an hour. She only holds on harder, and starts, very quietly, to hum.

Sukuna's jaw works. He tears, and swallows.

Everyone knows what he is. It's in all the stories. It's another thing altogether to watch it.

“There,” Makima says, to no one, softly, the way you might at the end of a difficult piece of knitting. ᴹᵏ

For the first time since noon, she has sat forward.

* * *

It doesn't hurt very much. That's interesting.

It's only that there's more of it coming than he'd have thought. And there's the noise, which is very close, and wet, and steady. It's a noise he knows. He has heard it from the inside. He has only just been shown a kitchen.

He's got no hands.

He looks for them. He goes all through himself, room by room, the way you'd look for your keys. Nothing. No Blue, no doors, no Red. There's the ocean behind the door, as big as it ever was, and it's so busy now mending what's being eaten that it can't do one single other thing. There are all those soldiers standing in him, every one with both hands full. There's nobody left. It's just him. Him and this, on the floor.

There's a throat right in front of his face.

He can see the pulse going. Sukuna's head is down, and it has stretched out all that long corded neck about an inch from his mouth. It has a little shallow red line across it, the width of a finger. It's going up and down. He's looking at it. It's so close. He can smell it, hot stone and old blood. Under that, faintly, something cooking.

He has no hands. He has one thing left with an edge on it, and the man has been kind enough to lean the softest part of himself right up against it.

Nobody has ever told him about this. There wasn't anything in any of the lessons.

His mouth opens.

* * *

It's like biting a saddle.

It's nothing at all like you'd think. It's tough and it's hot, and it fills up his whole head, and he's got no strength in his neck to speak of, so he does it the only way there is. He gets his teeth in and he hangs on, with all his weight, like a dog. He shakes. Something comes away. He spits it out and goes back for the next place.

Sukuna makes a noise against his shoulder, deep in his chest. It's quite a contented noise.

He doesn't let go either.

That is what's on the panels. There's no way to put it that makes it any better. People will try, for years. They will tell it in towns he's never heard of, and they will get the details wrong and it won't matter. In the long gold light, on the floor of a ruined quarry, there are two shapes lying on each other in a spreading pool, one with a wheel going faintly round above it. They're not moving much. They've both got their faces buried in each other's necks. One of them is eating. The other is trying to kill him with the only thing he has left. From the ridge, nobody can tell the difference, and most of them will never ask.

The man from the ridge, the one with a brother on the road, has stopped looking at the field. He's looking at Elmesia. He wants her to say it's all right. She isn't saying anything. Her face is very calm and there are tears on it. A very long reign has taught her how to go on sitting there.

*Mau,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

He can't answer. His mouth's full.

*Mau, I need you to wait.* ᶜⁱ

She's very close, and she's frightened. She has been frightened since the first moment he opened the path. There's a big warehouse down there, with a thousand years in it, and an appetite that has never in all that time been told no. There's a thing she has asked him once already, today. She hasn't had an answer. She can't go on not knowing it.

She goes down. She's the fastest thing there is, she takes no time at all. She goes all the way to the warehouse door and she looks.

It's shut. Noise is sitting against it with its back to the wood. It's been there all afternoon, not saying anything. The seal hasn't moved. Nothing's got out, not a thread. There's nobody in Mau's head right now who is called anything else.

She comes back up. She looks at him for what is, for her, a long time. She has been with him through every bad night there's been, in the only life she has. She's seen most of what's in there.

*It isn't him,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

He has gone very still, with his teeth in the man.

*I checked. Mau, it isn't the archive. That isn't Sukuna. There's nothing of his in this at all. You aren't hungry. You're killing him with what you've got.* ᶜⁱ

*That's just you.*

He doesn't say anything to that.

He thinks that he'd probably like to feel worse about it. He thinks maybe he will, later on. He has a world he took apart once, and what he did after that, in a place only four people know of. He's always known what was in there. He's known it longer than he's known anyone.

*Okay,* he says.

He shakes his head again, and something tears.

* * *

The bleeding is the trouble.

Ciel's been watching it. He's got a whole ocean behind the door, and every drop has gone to the one job, and it's losing. It isn't going to win. That mouth is coming down again, a little lower. It's got to the big vessel. Sukuna's been at this kind of thing a good deal longer than he has, and he has done a far neater job. The throat Mau's got hold of is so thick, it's like trying to bite through a tree.

There are about two minutes in this. She runs it again. There are about ninety seconds.

And she can see something else.

They're still there. She's been keeping the count, it's the sort of thing she does. She's been counting since he threw the first of them, those silly little dainty cuts of his, each no longer than a finger. The man didn't even bother to close them. Oh, he closed them, in the end, in passing, with what he had to spare. He's very quick. They all are. There's one along the upper arm that he looked at. There's one on the ribs, one on the side of the neck. One on the throat, just here, right under Mau's teeth. Nine. She knows each one. They've each got a small thread lying in the bottom of them. They're only about one word from opening.

They were done by someone who's read the book.

*Mau,* she says. *You planted nine.* ᶜⁱ

*I know.*

*They'd need feeding. Only a little. They're already written, they only want telling. But there has to be something behind the word, some real cursed energy, and you haven't got any. You have spent all of it. You have nothing at all, except*

She stops.

*I know,* he says. ᴹ

* * *

It's such a small thing. It's about as much as you could hold in one hand. It has been sitting at the base of his skull since two days ago, sealed, and she has told him no about it eleven separate times.

They talked about what it was for. It was the one thing they decided in advance, at home, with dinner coming, so that he couldn't argue about it when he was dying. It's there to start the repair of the control structures. It's the part that lets everything else begin. If he spends this, there's no one to catch him. There's nothing to keep that one small warm route alive when the rest of it goes. It's the difference between someone they can bring back up the hill and someone they can't.

She has a will of her own. They were very careful about that. Everyone wrote it down, it was in the articles. Nobody can order her, and he can't either. He can't reach it unless she opens it. He'd be the first to say so.

He doesn't ask.

He only lies there, with his mouth full of the man's neck, and he doesn't say anything. It's a very loud sort of nothing. She's known him for a long time.

*I will not be able to help you,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

*I know.*

*After. I want you to understand this. I'll have nothing. I won't be able to hold your head up. I can talk to you, that's all. I'll be saying your name to a thing that might not be there. You'll have to get up by yourself.*

*I know.*

*You don't get up, Mau. Nobody gets up from what this is going to be. I have looked. I have run it. I can't find you getting up.*

*Ciel.*

*What?*

He takes some time to find it. It's got to come a long way.

*Left foot,* he says.

There's a silence in there about the length of a very long life.

Rimuru, on a bench up on the ridge, doesn't know why he has suddenly started to cry.

*You idiot,* says Ciel. *You stupid, stupid boy.*

She breaks the seal.

* * *

To say a word you have to open your mouth.

Mau thinks about that. It's the last thing he has to think about, and he gives it his full attention. He has this. He's got his teeth in the throat of the worst thing that ever lived. It's the only hold he has on anything, it's the one thing all day that's been entirely his. He'd have to let go.

He'd have to be lying here with nothing, and the man still eating.

He lets go.

It takes an effort. His jaw has locked. His head falls back on the stone and hits it, with a mouth full of something. He spits. He takes what breath there is, a little whistling sip of it, around whatever's left of his neck.

Sukuna feels the grip go. He feels it, and he stops chewing. He lifts his head, slowly, dripping, to see why. All three of his eyes come round. They find Mau's, and there it is, the old bright interest. *Well? What have you got?* He has got such a lot of blood on his chin.

Mau looks at him. He isn't angry. He doesn't think he's ever been less angry at anyone. He knows this man better than anyone has ever known anyone.

He's got one word. He has it on excellent authority it's the only one you need.

“Open,” Mau says. ᴹ

It's hardly a whisper. The panels carry it.

* * *

It starts on the upper arm.

A little line, hardly a scratch. It's come undone from end to end, and it's gone on going, the way they do. The one on the ribs goes. Then the side of the neck. They've got no great hurry about them. They go down him like buttons.

Sukuna's face changes.

He looks down at himself. He's got time. He sees the long clean mouth come open along his side, and he knows it. He has seen it ten thousand times, on ten thousand other people. He wrote this. Nobody has ever in all the world had the nerve to read it back to him. It's a bit late on the entry. It's a very good wrist.

The last one is on his throat, right where the teeth were.

He makes a sound. It's nearly a word. It might be a laugh; it's got that shape. It comes out as a soft wet cough, all down Mau's chest, and his head sinks. It goes on sinking. It comes to rest on Mau's collarbone, quite heavily, like a tired child's.

He doesn't move.

Mau doesn't either.

Inside him, in the warm small room at the bottom of his skull, the last of the light has gone out. Ciel is standing there in the dark with her empty hands. It's very quiet. Something he's had for as long as he has been anyone is slowing down in his chest, going a little slower, and a little slower, like someone going up a very long stair.

It stops.

*Mau,* she says.

Nothing.

*Your left foot,* Ciel says. *The ground under it.*
