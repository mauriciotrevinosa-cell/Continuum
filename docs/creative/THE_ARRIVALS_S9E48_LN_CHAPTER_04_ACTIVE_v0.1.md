“Active,” says Gazel's clerk. “Both.”

Testarossa holds hers at the same height. She has a very beautiful, very still face, and she doesn't add anything.

So nobody can go down.

It takes the ridge some time to understand that. There's a rope at the top of the clinical route and two people from Ram's team holding it, and it's held with a good deal more care than strength, and they've got orders. Those are Shoko's own orders. She wrote them. The closure lifts when a principal has yielded or ended. It's on the first page. Nothing can enter the field while both principals are active: no doctor, no wife, no one. If somebody did, the pledge would break, and all the roads would open, and a boy's whole consciousness would go to a woman with a nice voice.

There are two bodies on the floor of the basin, one lying across the other. Neither has moved. The light is very good, and very kind. It lies along them like a blanket. They've stopped being any trouble to anyone.

“How long?” says someone. Yuta, it might be.

Shoko doesn't answer. She has her eyes on the panel, on the small part of a chest she can see. She has a very good clock in her head. It's an awful thing to own. It was a long time ago that she first found out she couldn't turn it off.

It says a minute forty.

* * *

Two minutes.

Nobody's making any noise now. The merchants, the delegations, the clerks. It's so quiet that you can hear the wind come up the long slope, and a loose piece of the shield ticking as it cools. They have seen something today that they're going to have to tell someone about. They've started to look at one another with a sort of embarrassment. It can't be over. Somebody would have to say. Is that how it ends, with nothing? With the two of them just lying there?

Three minutes.

It says three minutes fifty.

A body can do four. She has told students so. After that you start losing things, one at a time, in an order she could recite. She has brought people back from six. It was with her own hands right there on them, with everything she has. She has one person on the whole of this earth who's ever got up from longer than that, and she's looked at him a great deal, and she has told him, across a table, with tears in her eyes, *depending on you returning isn't the same as knowing you will.*

All she needs is for that other thing to die.

She has never in her whole life wished that on anyone with so much attention. She looks at it, the big heap lying across him. It's got its head on his collarbone. There's still a small, stupid chance they would let her go down in time, if it would only stop being *active*. If it would please, please just stop.

The slate doesn't change.

Whatever he's got left in there, he's using it. He's a very old man. He has never yet been in any hurry about a thing like this.

Makima has her hands folded on her knee.

She's sitting forward, with her head a little on one side. She looks like someone waiting at a window for a person who is very nearly due. There's a line on page three, in very clear handwriting. It doesn't require him to have lost. It only needs him to have ended while the other one was still going. It's the most natural thing in the world. He only has to go first, by a minute, by a second. It doesn't matter at all what he has done to anybody's arm.

She can feel it. There is a little place she has been keeping free for him. It's all ready.

Testarossa turns her head and looks at her, across a fair stretch of bench. Makima turns and smiles back, quite warmly, as one does with a colleague.

Neither of them looks away for rather a long time.

In a room half a day's road off, with the window open, Ashborn is looking at a shadow.

It's not much to look at. It's only an ordinary soldier, sitting down. They've been seeing the quarry through it all day, in a sort of a dim grey shimmer on the wall. Now it has got two heaps of something in it and it's very still. But they aren't really watching that anymore, either of them. Ashborn has turned his chair. He's looking at the actual shadow, the patch of dark that it casts on the floorboards, there beside the door.

They agreed on the terms, back when it was only words on paper. If Mau truly dies, the shadow opens. Either guest still attached to his refuge may step through, and it will take them straight home. The route doesn't care about anyone's opinion. It isn't looking at his pulse, or at what the crowd thinks. It knows one thing, and only one thing. It's fastened to him at the very root, in a way that no slate will ever be.

* * *

It's very grey here.

He has been here before.

*Your left foot,* someone is saying, a long way above him. *The ground under it. Your left foot. The ground under it. That's three hundred and six. I know you can't hear this. Your left foot.*

He knows this place. He didn't think he would. There's no one here to tell him, he's only got the feel of it, the way you know your own stairs in the dark. It's where it goes quiet. There's a bit of pain, and then the pain turns out to have been happening to someone else. Then you're just lying here and it's not so bad. It's actually very nice. You've done all you can. Nobody could say you hadn't.

Four hundred and thirty-seven times.

He's the only one who ever counted them. Well, he told the others, in another life, on a night at a table. They'd looked at him. A person can be told a number. It doesn't tell you anything about the thing he knows, which is the part that comes after this. Because there's a moment, right about here. It doesn't matter how you went. It doesn't matter if you were burned or cut, or if you'd had enough, if you did it to yourself, in the end, on purpose, in the wreck of the whole world, because it was all too much and you couldn't stand one more morning.

There's a moment when you wake up.

It's got nothing to do with you. Nobody asks. Your eyes come open and your whole body's already braced, it's already on one knee, it's already looking for the first thing that's going to kill you. It has done that for four hundred and thirty-seven deaths. You could stop wanting to. He has. It doesn't care. It's the stupidest thing about him. It's about the only thing he's ever been really good at.

There isn't any Trial here. He knows that. He does know that. Nobody's going to put him back at the start of anything. There's no goddess, no anchor, no one's counting him in. It's only a floor. If he doesn't wake up, then he's dead, and that's all, and he'll have been dead since a while ago.

But his body doesn't know that.

It's never been told. It has only ever learned the one thing. In the middle of his chest, where something's stopped, it's got an idea. It's a very old, very stupid idea. It's waiting for the hour.

And under that, there's something else. He has had two of them, for a long time now. He always forgets. They had a very long talk about it once, on a table, Senku and Shoko. *If that counted,* she said, *half the argument would be nonsense.* That big black thing somebody left in him as a joke, that doesn't fit, that was going to be somebody else's. It has got a name in it now.

It hasn't stopped. It's not even tired.

It beats, and he feels it. It isn't for anyone.

His foot.

It's Milim who sees.

She has not left her place at the boundary in six hours. She's got both palms on the barrier, and her forehead too. She hasn't said anything in a long while. She's been saying it to herself, over and over, *wait for them to tell us, wait for them to tell us*. She isn't looking at the panels anymore. They're no good. She's got her own eyes, and they are very good, and they've been fixed for seven minutes on a hand. It's a small grey dirty hand, with two bad fingers, lying open on a stone.

The smallest finger moves.

She doesn't make a sound. She has stopped breathing, which she doesn't really need to do anyway.

It moves again, and it scratches at the rock.

Then, in a silence so complete that everyone from the clerks' tables to the top of the ridge hears it at once, something on the floor of the quarry drags in a breath. It's the worst noise any of them has ever heard. It goes on for a very long time. It sounds like a rusty gate being forced by somebody who hates it. It stops. There's a pause, long enough to give up in.

There's another.

The big heap shifts.

It isn't moving by itself. Something's under it, heaving, an inch at a time. An arm comes out, a thin one. Then a shoulder, with a large dark mess where the neck joins it. The great weight of the other man slides, and rolls, and lies with its face to the sky. And from under it, with no hurry at all, with his one good leg folded under him and his right arm dragging, a wheel of faint pale light turning slowly in the air above his head and all the hands in the world holding him shut, comes a person.

He gets one knee under him.

It's already braced. Anyone who has ever seen him wake up could tell you that.

He stays there for about the time it takes to say a short prayer, and there are a good many being said. His head hangs. His whole body shakes. He looks like something that has been dug up. Then the good foot comes forward and finds the stone, and he puts his left hand on his own knee, and leans.

Nobody helps him. Nobody can.

He stands up.

There isn't any cheering. There are several thousand people on that ridge. Not one of them makes a sound. Many of them, afterward, will find that they have backed away.

His head comes up, slowly. It swings a little from side to side, like a blind thing's, over the wrecked grey field, the grey walls and the grey sky with its long straight scar. He's looking for something. He looks right along the benches, and it's as though there's no one there at all. Then it stops.

It has found the rail. It has found one small place on all that long grey ridge.

Yuka looks back at him.

She doesn't wave, or shout. She has got no voice anyway. She just stands where he can see her, and she stays exactly as she is.

He looks for a moment longer.

Then he turns, and he looks down at his feet, and at the man lying on the stones eleven paces off. That man has his eyes open.

On a table, on a slate, in very clear handwriting, there's a word. It hasn't changed.

*Active.*
