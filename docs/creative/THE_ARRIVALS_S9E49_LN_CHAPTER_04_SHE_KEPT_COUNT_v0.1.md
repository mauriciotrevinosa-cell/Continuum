# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 3 — She Kept Count

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S9E49
**Continuity state:** B+136 late night. Rimuru's night shift. Ciel and Rimuru talk over their existing private thread, which stayed quiet all day by agreement. Not her first tears (DH-132). Her account stays private.

*When: the wall says Rimuru, nights, he comes in without a coat and asks Yuta which side he should sit on.*

Yuta points with his chin. He has about ten minutes left on his time, and he's using all his words for something else.

Rimuru doesn't sit. There's a perfectly good stool. He looks at it, looks at the table, and then there's a small blue shape on the blanket at the foot of the bed, settling itself with a soft wet noise against Mau's good ankle.

“I can feel if anything changes,” it says. “It's the best view in the house.” ᴿ

Yuta nearly smiles. It's more than he has done all day.

When he has gone, the room is quiet. There's the lamp. There's Shoko, asleep sitting up in the corner with her mouth open, on Maomao's instructions. There's a long slow breath going in and out of the person on the table, a little wet at the top, and Rimuru listens to about forty of them.

There's a thread in him. It's very thin. It has been there for a long time. He told her at the quarry that he wouldn't pull on it, not once, not whatever happened, because the contract said no signals and because he knew what he'd do if he heard her. He has spent eleven hours not pulling on it. His lip's still sore.

The field is closed now. The record's sealed. It's a thing he's allowed to do.

He finds that he's frightened.

*Ciel,* he says. ᴿ

Nothing, for a moment.

*You can pull now,* she says. ᶜⁱ

Her voice is so small. He has known that voice for longer than he's known anyone. It has run a nation with him. It has told him, with enormous satisfaction, about the correct storage of forty thousand items. It has been insufferable, and it's never in all their time sounded as though it had been left out in the rain.

*I know I can,* Rimuru says. *How are you?*

There's quite a long silence.

*I killed him,* Ciel says.

* * *

He doesn't move. A slime is very good at that.

*Tell me.*

*There was a margin. We set it aside two days ago. A very small reserve for the control route. It was the one thing we decided in advance. I told him eleven times today that he couldn't have it. He didn't ask me the twelfth. He just lay there, and I had looked, Rimuru. I ran it. I ran it every way there is. If I opened that seal, I had no way of getting him back. I knew it. I said so to him. I said, I can't find you getting up.*

*And you opened it.*

*Yes.*

*Because he told you to?*

*He can't tell me to. That's the whole point of me. He said* left foot. *That's all he said. I'd been telling him that all day, and he gave it back to me, and I opened it. His heart stopped about ninety seconds after that. The ordinary one. I felt it stop.*

The lamp ticks.

*I talked to him,* she says. *I didn't have anything else. I had no route left and no hands. I never have hands. I stood in the place where he used to be and said the same six words to it over and over like a broken thing. I got to three hundred and eleven.*

Rimuru has been told that he is a person who always knows what to say. It's quite an unfair thing to have people believe. He looks up along the length of the blanket at the shape of that chest going up and down.

*I was on a bench,* he says. *Up on the ridge. Benimaru was pretending not to look at me. And all of a sudden I started crying. I didn't know why. I thought I'd got something in my eye. I don't have eyes, I mean, not the kind you get things in. I sat there like an idiot with my nose running, and nobody had told me anything, and I didn't pull.*

She doesn't answer.

*That was when, wasn't it?* he says. *When you opened it.*

*You didn't know that.*

*No. Some part of me did, though. It's the part you live in.*

It isn't very much to say. He has said a great many better things to whole armies. He oozes a little farther up the blanket and settles against the shin, and it's warm there, and he stays. After some time there's a small sound along the thread, and it isn't words. She's done this before, on bad days, and he has heard it. He knows you don't do anything about it. You just stay where it can find you.

* * *

It's getting on for the hour when the lamp wants oil.

*He got up,* Rimuru says. *You know that. You were there. I saw him get up, with my own whatever I've got.*

*I know.* ᶜⁱ

*So you were wrong.*

*I was wrong,* she says. She has got a little of her voice back. It's only a very little, and there's something in it that he recognises. *I have reviewed the calculation. I omitted several factors. He is the most irresponsible host I've ever had. I intend to tell him so at length, as soon as he can follow a sentence.*

*Good.*

*In writing. With an index.*

*He'd love that.* ᴿ

The chest goes up and down. In the corner, Shoko snores, once, and stops herself, and doesn't wake.

Rimuru keeps his place. It says on the wall that he's here until morning.
