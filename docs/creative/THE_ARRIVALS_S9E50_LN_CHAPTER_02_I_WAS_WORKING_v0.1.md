# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 5 — I Was Working

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 5
**Source episode:** S9E50
**Continuity state:** B+138 morning. Mau lucid in patches. First remake of a broken named shadow, at normal cost, with Ciel steering and Shoko's permission. Beru heals with his own reserve and joins the rota. Standing and storage free; remaking paid. Links all retained.

*When: Shoko says he may try one, Mau asks one what, and she has to tell him again.*

“One shadow,” she says. “One. You've got a door in your chest the size of a barn and I don't want to find out what happens if you open it all the way. Ciel's going to hold the handle.” ˢʰ

*I will,* says Ciel. *I have a number of things to say to you, also. They can wait until you can read.*

He doesn't know what that means. He turns his head, the whole of it, toward the floor beside the bed. There's a shadow there. It's his. It's very quiet down in it, and it smells of smoke. He can feel them lying about in there, thousands of them, all the way down, sprawled on top of one another like men after a long march. Every one of them is still his. A good many are waiting on something.

He can't make the hand do it. So he thinks it, as clearly as he can, which isn't very. It takes three tries to think of who.

It costs about what you'd expect. It goes through him like swallowing a hot stone. Shoko watches his colour and doesn't say anything, and her finger stays on his pulse.

The floor heaves.

An insect the size of a horse comes up out of the boards with its head low, all claws, every part of it already moving. It's going for a throat. It's got its wings half open, and it's halfway through a sentence, and it has clearly been halfway through that sentence for two days.

“—*WORKING*—” ᴮᵉ

It stops.

It looks at the lamp. It looks at the curtain with the little flowers on it. It looks at Shoko Ieiri, who has not moved at all, and who has raised one eyebrow by about the width of a hair.

Very slowly, the ant folds its wings.

“My apologies, Doctor,” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

Then it turns around, and it sees the bed.

* * *

Mau has known this creature for a long time, in one way and another. He has heard it boast, he has heard it scream. He has heard it inform a dragon that the dragon's breath was adequate. He has never till now heard it say nothing.

It comes to the edge of the mattress on all those legs, very carefully, the way you'd cross a floor in a house where somebody's sleeping. It looks at the neck. It looks at the long pink new line around his waist, where the blanket has slipped. It looks at the arm lying on top of the covers with the fingers curled. Its big mandibles work once or twice.

“Who,” Beru says, “has been looking after you?” ᴮᵉ

“Hello,” Mau says. It comes out as mostly air. ᴹ

“My King has been left in this condition. For two days. I was not *informed*.” ᴮᵉ

“You were in pieces.” ˢʰ

“That is no excuse.” ᴮᵉ

He puts out one clawed hand. It's shaking a little. He holds it a finger's width above that long new seam, as if he's checking a stove. And down the arm, out of the reserve that belongs to him and nobody else, comes the odd cold green-black seep of it, the thing he has been so vain about for so long.

Mau's whole side lets go. He hadn't known it was clenched.

Shoko has been watching this very closely. She leans over and looks, and she presses with her thumb. She looks at the ant for rather a long time.

“That's quite good,” she says. “Your margins are sloppy. What's your reserve, in hours?” ˢʰ

Beru draws himself up. It's the first entirely happy moment anyone has had in that room since it was a room.

“Madam,” he says, “I can continue until I am *told to stop*.” ᴮᵉ

“Fine.” She gets up, picks up the pencil and goes over to the wall. “Six hours on, two off. You don't touch the neck. You do what I tell you, you report to Maomao and if you call me Madam again I will put you in a jar.” ˢʰ

She writes it down. It goes between *Yuta* and *Rem*, in her big bad doctor's hand.

*The ant.*

* * *

He's asleep by then, or something like it.

Beru settles at the bedside, as near as he has been allowed, with his hand out. Some time later a very low voice comes from the floor, from the shadow, from somewhere well below the boards. Nobody has called him. He has got his chin about an inch above the surface, as they do. All the speaking ones can look out, if they want to.

They've all wanted to, it seems. There's a good many dim shapes down there. A marshal's helm is still badly dented. A long dark horned head is having some trouble fitting.

Nobody says anything for a while.

“Well?” says Kamish, finally, deep enough that the water shivers in the cup. ᴷᵃ

Beru doesn't look round. He's working.

“He told us to stand,” he says. “And we stood.” ᴮᵉ

There's a pause, about as long as a very old creature thinks it should be.

“Adequate,” says the dragon, and it withdraws.

It's the nicest thing Beru has ever heard him say. He'll tell people about it for years.
