# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 6 — Keep Telling It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 6
**Source episode:** S9E50
**Continuity state:** B+138 afternoon. Rem's shift. Creator's scene. Rem/Umaru still without forced label; second outing wanted, she may report ordinary progress at his request. He wants the talk; no private confidence of Umaru's disclosed.

*When: Rem has turned the pillow for the third time, Mau asks whether it has done something wrong.*

“It was flat,” she says. ᴿᵉ

It wasn't flat. She knows that. It was perfectly good when she came in, and she has turned it anyway, and then smoothed the blanket, and moved the water closer, and moved the water back because the first place was better. She has cut his bread into squares. She's cut the squares in half. There is a small neat pile of it on the plate that a mouse could manage, and she's now looking around the room for the next thing.

There's a mark on the windowsill. She takes out her cloth.

“Rem,” he says. ᴹ

It takes him a while, these days. The word goes in, and you can watch him go and get the next one. She stops with her hand on the sill and waits. She has got very good at waiting for that, in one day.

“Sit,” he says. ᴹ

“I'll just—” ᴿᵉ

“Rem.” ᴹ

She sits. She sits very straight, on the edge of the stool, with the cloth in her lap and her hands on the cloth. She has looked after a mansion. She has looked after a sister. She has held the back of a person's head for a quarter of an hour so they wouldn't have to see this man being eaten. And he's lying there now with that thing on his neck, and that eye going off to the side, and his hand on the blanket like something that was left behind. And there has to be something she can do. There has to be. It can't be that there's nothing more to wipe.

He's looking at her. It's the right eye. It takes a moment to settle.

“Tell me,” Mau says. “How's it going. With Umaru.” ᴹ

For a moment she doesn't understand the question. It's in the wrong language.

“With—” ᴿᵉ

“You and. Umaru.” He breathes for a bit. “I packed. A lot of food. I have. An interest.” ᴹ

She opens her mouth to tell him that this isn't the time.

He just goes on looking at her. There's something at one corner of his mouth, under all that. It's very small. She's seen it across a kitchen a thousand times, usually just before he does something unforgivable to Taihei.

“It's,” Rem says. She looks down at the cloth. “It's going. I don't know. She has made me a Deputy.” ᴿᵉ

The eyebrow goes up. It's only the one.

“Of the birds,” she says. ᴿᵉ

* * *

So she tells him.

She doesn't know how to begin, and it comes out in the wrong order. The Fort has reopened the matter of the bird who stole Yuka's fruit, on the grounds that it may be part of a wider conspiracy against household travellers. Umaru has written to the hotel. She has done it on official paper. It has a stamp on it, and the stamp has a small cola bottle. The bird has been charged. And somebody has to look into it, Umaru says, someone of unimpeachable character, who can reach high places, so she has given Rem a badge. It's made out of a jar lid.

“I said I had no training in birds,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

His chest has started to move.

“She said that was what they'd expect.” ᴿᵉ

There's a noise. It's a wheeze, really. It starts somewhere down near that long new line. His face has begun to crease up, and it's going to be a laugh, she can see it coming from a long way off. It's going to be a real one. It gets about halfway up, and it catches on everything he has got, and his whole body goes rigid. All the blood goes out of his lips.

Rem is on her feet.

“Don't! Stop, don't, I'm sorry. Where? Is it the neck? Is it the stitches? I'll get her, I'll get Shoko, Beru—” ᴿᵉ

The ant has already turned his big head. Mau's good hand has come up an inch off the blanket. It's shaking, and it flaps at her, *no, no*, and his eyes are streaming, and he's still doing it. He can't stop. There's no air in it at all, it's only this silent awful helpless shaking, tears running into his ear, and he looks as if someone's sawing him in half.

He looks happier than she has seen him in a week.

“Jar,” he manages. ᴹ

“It's not funny! You're bleeding, you've got a bit of—oh, you stupid man, you stupid, *stupid*—” ᴿᵉ

“*Lid*,” says Mau, and goes off again.

She gets a cloth on it. It's something to do. Beru leans over and seeps at it with the air of someone who has seen a great many royal indulgences. It stops. It takes a while. He lies there getting his breath, white as the pillow, with his eyes shut and his mouth still a little bit crooked.

She's still holding the cloth. Her hand isn't steady, and she can't seem to do anything about that.

“Don't do that,” she says. ᴿᵉ

“Mm.” ᴹ

“I mean it. I won't tell you anything else.” ᴿᵉ

His good eye opens about halfway. It's going, she can see. It goes so fast now, he'll be right there and then the tide just takes him. He's looking at something above her shoulder and trying to come back from it. His hand moves on the blanket, feeling along. It's got no strength. It bumps against her wrist, and it goes around her fingers, very loosely, the two bad ones not closing, and it stays.

“What did,” Mau says. His lips hardly move. “The bird. Say.” ᴹ

She can't speak for a moment.

“It hasn't made a statement,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

There's a long breath, in and out.

“Keep,” he says. “Telling it.” ᴹ

So she sits down. She doesn't let go, and she doesn't move the water. She tells him about the jar lid, and about how Umaru wanted her to swear an oath on a bag of crisps, and how she was so serious about it that Rem had to go and stand behind a door. She tells him they went back to the low patch in the garden. She tells him what Umaru said there. It isn't a secret, it was only something about the light. She says it all very quietly, in the same voice, to a man with his mouth a little open whose breath has gone long and slow. She goes on for some time after he can't possibly be hearing her.

It's a good story. She'd like to get to the end.
