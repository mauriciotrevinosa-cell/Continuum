# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 7 — Why Him

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 7
**Source episode:** S9E50
**Continuity state:** B+138 late afternoon, immediately after. Creator's scene. Corridor outside the clinic. Rem and Yuka.

*When: Rem has got her hand back without waking him, she folds the cloth, puts it on the sill and walks out of the room quite normally.*

She closes the door without letting the latch make a noise. She goes four steps along the corridor. There's a place there where the wall turns and a window looks out on the laundry, and she stops in front of it. She looks at the sheets on the line. Somebody's hung them badly.

She's going to go down and fix that. In just a moment.

Her breath goes in, and it doesn't come out right.

She puts her hand over her mouth. It's the hand he was holding. She bends forward a little from the waist, as if she's looking for something she dropped, and it comes up out of her anyway, with no sound in it at first, and then with some.

She doesn't hear the boots on the stairs.

Yuka has a towel around her neck, and her hair's wet, and there's a long scrape on her arm that wasn't there this morning. She has been out in the yard for three hours with Yuta's practice blade, because it's on the wall. She's got a heel of bread in one hand. She has come up here for her two hours, and she stops at the top of the stairs. She looks at the shut door, and all the colour goes out of her face.

“Is he—” ʸ

Rem shakes her head. She can't do any more than that. *No, no, he's asleep, he's fine.* She flaps one hand at the door, and it's the same way he did it.

Yuka looks at her a moment longer.

Then she puts the bread down on the windowsill, crosses the corridor and takes hold of her.

She's not as tall as Rem. It doesn't matter. She gets both arms around her and she holds on, hard, the way you'd hold a person on a boat, and Rem's whole body gives way against her. She has never done this. Not in front of anybody. She has been so useful for so long. She has got her forehead on Yuka's wet shoulder and she's making a terrible noise.

It goes on for some time. Downstairs, someone starts to come along the passage with a bucket, gets as far as the corner and quietly goes away again.

* * *

“I cut up his bread,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

“I know.” ʸ

“Into such little pieces. He can't even lift—he made a joke. He's lying there with his neck like that, and he asked me about the *birds*.” ᴿᵉ

“I know he did.” ʸ

Rem lifts her head. Her face is a ruin. She has never looked less like a person who could run a house. She looks at the door at the end of the corridor, then at Yuka, from very close. When she speaks it's in a small, plain voice, like a child asking why it's raining.

“Why him?” she says. ᴿᵉ

Yuka doesn't say anything.

“There's so many people. There's Milim. There's Gojo. They're all so strong, they can all—why is it always him? Why does it have to be him, every time? Why does he have to go and suffer for everyone else?” ᴿᵉ

There's a lot that you could say to that. There's a very good answer about roads, and a city with people in it. Yuka was at the table for that. She's read the articles, every one, she asked the worst of the questions herself.

She has started crying too. She doesn't seem to have noticed. It's just running down her face, and she doesn't wipe it, and her voice when it comes is quite steady.

“Because he won't let anyone be like this,” she says. ʸ

Rem looks at her.

“Like him. Like that, in there. Not if he knows he could have stopped it. He won't. He can't do it, Rem, there's something in him that just doesn't work that way. If there's somebody who's going to get hit, and he could be standing there instead, then that's where he's standing. He can take anything. You know that? He can honestly take anything at all, for as long as it lasts, as long as he knows that it's him and somebody else didn't have to. It's the only thing that makes it all right for him.” ʸ

Her mouth twists.

“I'm so *angry* at him,” Yuka says. ʸ

The sheets move on the line outside. They really have been hung very badly.

Rem breathes in. It shakes on the way, and she lets it out again, and her shoulders come down. She looks at that shut door for a little while longer. She has made that man a very great deal of breakfast.

“I know,” Rem says. ᴿᵉ

She does. She's known that for a long time. She only wanted to ask someone.

Yuka nods. She doesn't let go just yet.

After a while Rem takes the towel from around Yuka's neck, because it's there, and wipes Yuka's face with it, and then her own. She looks at the scrape on Yuka's arm, and clicks her tongue, and she picks up the heel of bread from the sill and puts it back in Yuka's hand.

“He's sleeping,” she says. Her voice is almost her voice. “He'll want water when he wakes. It's on the left, I moved it. Don't move it.” ᴿᵉ

“I won't.” ʸ

“And eat that.” ᴿᵉ

She goes down the stairs. A moment later Yuka hears her out in the yard, telling someone in a clear and pleasant tone that if they were going to hang sheets like that they might as well have left them in the river.
