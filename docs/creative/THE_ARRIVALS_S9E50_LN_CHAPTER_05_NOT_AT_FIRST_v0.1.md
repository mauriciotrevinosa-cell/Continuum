# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 8 — Not at First

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 8
**Source episode:** S9E50
**Continuity state:** B+139 morning. Gojo's first real visit. Mau's repetition improving but present. Only two people alive know this particular wound. Father joke continues from V58#1. Gojo's agency and power intact.

*When: Gojo comes in carrying a chair, Mau asks him whether he got the right side.*

“You got the right side.” ᴳ

Gojo puts the chair down backward and sits on it the wrong way round, with his arms along the top. He has shaved. He looks like a person who has slept nine hours for the first time in a very long while, and who was told to by someone holding a rib spreader.

“Smooth all the way around,” he says. “Very nice seam. I'd have given it eight out of ten. I'd take off one for what you did next, and one for the shirt.” ᴳ

Mau considers this. It seems to him to be good news. He turns his head on the pillow, and he looks for a while at the little flowers on the curtain.

“Did I get,” he says. “The right side?” ᴹ

Gojo doesn't so much as blink.

“I'm so glad you asked. You got the right side. I wept. You should have seen me, I was a disgrace. There's a woman who's going to bring it up at my funeral.” ᴳ

“Good.” ᴹ

“Excellent, even.” ᴳ

He sits there for a while with his chin on his arms. Beru, at the far side of the bed, is giving him the long, cold, jealous look of one specialist at another. He wiggles his fingers at the ant. Outside somebody's moving a bench. It's a very nice morning, the sort you get in that part of the world, with the window open and a smell of cut grass from wherever they've been cutting it.

“I went down and looked,” Gojo says. ᴳ

His voice hasn't changed. He's looking at the blanket, at about the place where it goes over Mau's hips.

“Shoko showed me. Before Beru got started on your back. I had her turn you over and I took the cloth off. It was a little strip about this wide.” He holds up his thumb and finger, then shakes his head and brings them closer. “I've had a lot of time to look at that from the other direction. I'd worked out most of it. I had drawings. I had a whole theory about the angle, and what I should have been doing with my feet.” ᴳ

Mau has turned his head back. It takes some time. The right eye settles on him and stays there.

It's strange, about that eye. It wanders so much now. It doesn't seem to be wandering just at the moment.

“Anyway,” says Gojo. ᴳ

He's got the smile going. It's a good one, it has seen him through a lot. He looks at the window. Nobody in this whole enormous world has ever asked him this, because there isn't anyone who could; there was one other person who knew, and he sat on a rock and laughed about it, and he's gone now.

The smile stays where it is, and it doesn't reach anything.

“Did it hurt?” he says. ᴳ

It's so quiet he might not have said it.

Mau thinks about it. He goes down and looks, and it's a long way, past a lot of other things, and he gets a bit lost on the way there. He remembers some evening light. He remembers looking at the ground. He comes back up with it, slowly, and it's the one thing all week he doesn't have to look for twice.

“Not at first,” Mau says. ᴹ

Gojo doesn't move.

The bench outside has stopped. There's only that smell of grass. He's still got his chin on his arms and his eyes are on the window, those terrible blue eyes that can see everything there is. Something goes out of the set of his shoulders, very gradually. It's like watching someone put down a heavy bag they had forgotten they were carrying, after a long walk, in a place they didn't know was home.

“No,” he says. “Not at first.” ᴳ

They stay like that.

It might be a good while. Beru has stopped looking jealous, and he has found something very interesting to examine in the wall.

* * *

“Your elbow, though,” Gojo says eventually. ᴳ

“Mm?” ᴹ

“On the Purple. I don't want to spoil the moment. I've been meaning to bring this up. It was up around your ear. I've seen better form from a man falling off a roof.” ᴳ

“I'd been. Cut in half.” ᴹ

“Later. That was later. Don't try to change the subject.” ᴳ

He gets up. He turns the chair the right way round and sets it against the wall, with a good deal of care, where nobody will trip on it. He stands over the bed for a moment, a very tall and ridiculous person. Then he reaches down and lays his hand on top of Mau's head, on the side that isn't stitched. He leaves it there about as long as it would take to say something. He doesn't.

“Excellent grades,” he says. “I've told everyone. I expect you to be insufferable about it as soon as you can do long division.” ᴳ

He's almost at the door when the voice comes from the bed, thin and puzzled and very polite.

“Did I get the right side?” ᴹ

Gojo stops.

He turns around. He looks at that long, wrecked, patient, hopeful face. He comes all the way back into the room and he leans his hands on the end of the bed.

“Kid,” he says, “you have no idea.” ᴳ

And he tells him. It takes about ten minutes. It's got a great many gestures in it, and it's a good deal better this time, and by the end of it Beru has moved quite a bit closer in order to correct him on the details.
