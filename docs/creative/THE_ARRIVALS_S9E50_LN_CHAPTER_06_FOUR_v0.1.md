# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 9 — Four

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 9
**Source episode:** S9E50
**Continuity state:** B+139 afternoon. Yuka's two hours. She is angry, not his nurse, and has her own day to go back to. Mau clearer. Care and family only.

*When: Mau asks for the water, Yuka tells him it's on the left and doesn't get up.*

He looks at it.

It's on the left. It's about a forearm away. He has a left arm. It's the one with the old fingers and the bite, and Shoko said this morning he might try small things with it. He looks at her. She's sitting in the good chair with one boot up on her knee, oiling a practice blade that she has no need to bring in here, and she's looking at the blade.

He gets the arm moving. It takes about as long as a short sermon. He knocks the cup, and catches it, and spills some down his chest, and he gets it to his mouth with his whole hand shaking and most of his dignity gone. It's the best water he has ever had.

“Thanks,” he says. ᴹ

“You did it.” ʸ

“I know. I meant. In general.” ᴹ

She goes on oiling.

He's better today. He can tell, because he has noticed that she hasn't looked at him. Until now he has mostly had other things to notice. He's still slow, but the words are coming when he sends for them, nearly all of them. He has been awake for an hour and asked only one thing twice.

He has known her for a long time. He has been in a lot of rooms with that particular silence.

“Say it,” he says. ᴹ

The rag stops.

“Say what?” ʸ

“Yuka.” ᴹ

She puts down the blade. She does it very gently. She lays it across her knees and she folds the rag, and she looks at him for the first time since she came in, straight on, with those eyes. She's the only thing in the room that has any colour. It has been a very long three days to look at nothing else.

“You stood still,” she says. ʸ

He doesn't say anything.

“I watched you. I was at the rail. You got your feet square and you turned round to face him, and you stood there. Like *he* does. You've got a whole shadow full of people. You've got Ciel. I've got one of those things myself, I know what it's for, I've seen what's left when it gets back. I've spent the last three days cleaning the floor around what's left.” ʸ

Her voice is very reasonable. She got that from her grandmother. He'd honestly rather she threw the sword.

“And I knew. Before anyone. Gojo was standing there with his mouth open, and I *knew* what you were going to do, and I'm the only one up there who could count it. You had me stand at a rail in front of the whole world and count you. Do you know what I said? When it came through you? I said *four*. That's what I said. I said it out loud like I was reading a *receipt*.” ʸ

“I couldn't—” ᴹ

“I know you couldn't. I know. There was nothing else, you'd have been on a chain, I read the page, I *wrote* half that page. I'm not stupid. It was the right thing to do, it was a brilliant thing to do. Gojo's been crying in the pantry about how brilliant it was.” ʸ

She stops. Her jaw's tight. She takes one breath, through the nose, and it goes all the way down.

“You told me that story,” Yuka says. “With your finger, in the dust. You told it to me like a funny thing you knew. *Even one piece left is enough.*” ʸ

He has got nothing at all to say to that.

“Don't you ever make me count you again.” ʸ

The curtains move a little. Beru has been sent off for his two hours, and it's just them. Mau looks at her for some time. He's got a great many answers. He has all the good ones, about roads, and about how there wasn't any choice. He's got the smile. It's right there, it would only take a little effort, and it has got him out of this before. *It's fine. It doesn't hurt that much.*

He knows what she'd do with that one, and she'd be right.

“I can't promise,” he says. ᴹ

“I know you can't.” ʸ

“I'd do it. Again. If it was. The same.” ᴹ

“I know that too.” ʸ

“I was scared,” Mau says. ᴹ

He didn't know he was going to say it. He looks at the ceiling. It's a nice ceiling. Someone has patched it.

“I thought you'd be. Very angry,” he says. “I thought that. When he said my name. It was the last thing. I could see you. You were about this big.” ᴹ

He tries to show her how big. The fingers won't do it.

Yuka looks at them.

Her face does something complicated. It starts out as one thing, and it's trying hard to go on being that. She looks up at the patch on the ceiling, blinking, then she wipes her nose hard on the back of her wrist, with oil on it, and it leaves a mark.

“You were right,” she says. ʸ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“I'm still very angry.” ʸ

“Good.” ᴹ

* * *

She stays until it says on the wall that she can go.

She doesn't do anything much. She finishes the blade. She tells him who has come by. She mentions that Maki has had her in the yard since breakfast, working on what to do when the Garden loses, and that she got Yuta in the ribs, once, by accident, and he's being very brave about it. She tells him he has egg on his shirt, and doesn't get it off for him. When Rem arrives, with a tray, and looks at the spilled water, and looks at the cup in the wrong place, and draws breath, Yuka catches her eye and shakes her head by about a quarter of an inch.

She stands and sticks the blade through her belt. She looks at him from the door.

“I'm eating with the girls tonight,” she says. “Mai's got some idea about a door. I'll be back in the morning. If you want anything, there's a whole list of people on the wall, and they've all been waiting for you to ask.” ʸ

“Yuka.” ᴹ

She waits.

“What did you do. Today. That was good?” ᴹ

It stops her. It takes a moment. She looks down at the thing on her arm, the long scrape, as if she has just remembered that it's hers.

“I got him in the ribs,” she says slowly, and for the first time in three days something starts to happen at the corner of her mouth. “It was really good. I'll show you. You'll hate it.” ʸ

She goes.

He can hear her boots all the way down the stairs. She's taking them two at a time.
