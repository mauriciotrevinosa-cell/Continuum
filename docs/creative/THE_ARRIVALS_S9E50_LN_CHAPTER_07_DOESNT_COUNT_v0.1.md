# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 10 — Doesn't Count

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 10
**Source episode:** S9E50
**Continuity state:** B+139 evening. Milim pays V58#4 forward. Cards held for him, so the debt stands. New flesh where the eleven lines were.

*When: the door hits the wall, Maomao looks up from the list and says that Milim Nava is down for tomorrow.*

“I can read,” Milim says. “It says tomorrow. It's nearly tomorrow.” ᴹⁱ

It's about seven in the evening. She stands in the doorway with both fists on her hips and a deck of cards stuck in her belt like a sidearm. She has obviously spent some time deciding what her face should be doing, and she has settled on furious.

Maomao looks at her for a moment, then at the patient, and writes something down.

“Quietly,” she says. ᴹᵐ

Milim crosses the room on tiptoe. She's the strongest thing for a very long way in any direction, and a floorboard cracks.

She climbs up on the stool. She looks at him. She has not seen him since the outer boundary, since all that distance. She's had three days of being told that she's next, and that he's better, and that nobody thinks she ought to worry. It's quite different up close. He has got a big bandage on his neck and something going on with his eye. His right arm's on a pillow of its own. He's looking at her with a slow, amiable sort of attention, and he looks like he's just got back from a long way off.

“You look terrible,” she says. ᴹⁱ

“Hello, Milim.” ᴹ

“You look really, really terrible.” ᴹⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹ

Her lower lip has started to do something. She glares at it. She pulls the deck out of her belt and bangs it down on the blanket.

“You have to lose at cards,” she says. “You said.” ᴹⁱ

“You said. I said it was. A dreadful incentive.” ᴹ

“*Deal.*” ᴹⁱ

* * *

He can't.

He gets the left hand onto the deck, and he gets it open. Two of the fingers don't close and one's in a splint now on top of that. The cards go off the side of the bed in a long soft slither. They both look at them. Mau's face is very calm. He tries to pick one up off the blanket, and it takes him quite a while, and he has it nearly to the edge when it slides away from under his thumb.

He tries that again.

Milim Nava watches him do it three times. Nobody has ever been able to make her sit still for anything, but she doesn't move at all. Then she gets down on the floor, on her hands and knees, and she picks up every one. She deals the two hands herself. She looks around the room with a scowl, and takes the tray Rem left, and props it on his knees, and she stands his cards up along the rim of it, one by one, facing him. She has to reach around to do it. She can see every card he's got.

“There,” she says. ᴹⁱ

“You can see them.” ᴹ

“I'm not looking.” ᴹⁱ

“You're looking right at them.” ᴹ

“That one,” she says, pointing. “Play that one, it's your best one.” ᴹⁱ

He nods. She plays it for him. She looks at the table with enormous satisfaction, and she plays something on top of it and takes the trick.

It goes on like that. He loses the first hand in about a minute. He loses the second more slowly. She has him throw away a king, on the grounds that it looks suspicious. He wants to know what game this is, and she tells him it's the one they always play, and she explains a rule she has clearly just thought of. Somewhere in there he starts to smile a little, and it pulls the bandage. She sees it, and she stops, with a card held up in the air.

She puts the card down.

“Show me,” she says. ᴹⁱ

He doesn't ask what. He doesn't have to, she has been looking there all this time. It's easy to see now that the blanket has slipped: the big thigh, the ribs, and that soft shiny pink place in the hollow of his throat. They're small. They've got no lines on them. They look like nothing so much as thumbprints in new bread.

She doesn't touch them. She holds her finger just above each one, and she goes from one to the next, with great care. Her lips are moving.

“Eleven,” she says. ᴹⁱ

“Yes.” ᴹ

“I counted on the panel. I counted. Diablo said I got it wrong, but I didn't.” ᴹⁱ

“No.” ᴹ

“Whose?” she says. Her voice has gone very small and very fierce. ᴹⁱ

He looks at her. It takes him a moment to get there, and then he's there. It comes up in his face, slowly, like the sun on a hill. He remembers a garden. He remembers exactly.

“Mine,” Mau says. ᴹ

Her chin's going now and there's nothing she can do about it. She has fought gods. She has flattened cities on a bad afternoon, and nobody has ever seen her lose an argument with her own face. She moves, very suddenly, up onto the bed. She's so careful. She has got hold of that one small part of his side that nobody has written on the wall as off limits. She has her forehead against his arm, and she stays there, with her shoulders jerking, not making any sound whatsoever, because she was told to be quiet.

Mau gets his bad hand up. It lands on her hair. It's about the weight of a leaf.

“You won,” he says. ᴹ

“*That one doesn't count!*” It's muffled, and it's very wet. “You didn't hold them! It doesn't count if you don't hold them, I'm not beating a person who can't even hold them. That's not fair. You still have to—when you can—you have to hold them *yourself*, and then you have to lose. You still owe me. You said.” ᴹⁱ

“I said.” ᴹ

“Say it again.” ᴹⁱ

Over by the wall, Maomao has been studying her list for some time. She turns a page. She's a very busy woman, and she doesn't have any idea what is going on behind her.

“When I can hold them,” Mau says. ᴹ
