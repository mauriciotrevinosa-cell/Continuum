# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 12 — Bread

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 12
**Source episode:** S9E51
**Continuity state:** B+141 midday. Mau's own reverse technique flickers back, small. Meat turns his stomach, for now. Yuta knows from his own history about hands that did things; own-body rule (DH-083) respected. "Don't laugh" from V35 Everybody Answer.

*When: Yuta takes the lid off the bowl, the smell gets to the bed before he does.*

It's a good stew. Rem has been at it since the morning. It's got barley, and those small sweet onions from the south field, and a piece of shoulder that has been cooking so long it has forgotten it was ever anything else.

Mau has a very clear thought, which is that he's going to be sick.

He is. It isn't much; there's not much in there. Yuta gets the basin under his chin in about the time it takes to say so. Yuta has one big hand flat between his shoulder blades, on the part that doesn't have anything wrong with it. It goes on for a while, in that dry, tearing, pointless way. It's very bad for the neck.

When it stops he lies back. There's a cold sweat all over him. Yuta wipes his face, without any fuss, and he picks up the bowl and takes it outside. There's a low voice in the corridor. He comes back with a heel of plain bread and sits down, and he breaks it, and he eats some himself.

He doesn't say anything. It's something he's always been good at. Mau has been trying to get it from him for years.

“It's not the stew,” Mau says. ᴹ

“No.” ʸᵗ

“It's a good stew.” ᴹ

“I'll tell her.” ʸᵗ

Yuta chews. He looks out the window. He has lost a good deal of weight, this past week, and there are smudges under his eyes that you could lose a coin in. They've got him down to six hours on, and Maomao's taken to weighing him.

“There's a kitchen,” Mau says. ᴹ

It comes out by itself. He hasn't said it to anyone. It's not that he's supposed to keep it from them; it's only that it's big, and it's in a warehouse, and there's something sitting against the door. It doesn't have anything to do with what he did. Or it might. That's the trouble. He has been lying here for a good many hours going round and round, with his tongue feeling along his teeth. There's something in the back one that Beru had to get out with a claw.

Yuta has stopped chewing.

“I bit him,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸᵗ

“I mean I. I wanted to.” ᴹ

He looks at the ceiling, at the patch. He'd like to be clear about this. He has been wanting someone to be clear about it.

“I thought it was him. In me. From the. When I read him. It'd be so easy if it was. So Ciel went and checked. She went all the way down and she looked. And it wasn't.” ᴹ

The window's open. Someone's hitting a piece of metal, a long way off, in no particular rhythm.

“Everyone saw,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ʸᵗ

Yuta sits there with the bread in his hand. He has got a long plain tired face, and it hasn't changed at all. He's looking at his own fingers. He has big hands; they're older than the rest of him. He once borrowed a different pair, for a while, to do something he had decided had to be done, and he went back to his own afterward and lived a long life with them. He held his grandchildren with them. He's never told anyone, not once, exactly what the other pair felt like at the time.

“I killed a lot of things,” he says, “that I didn't have to be that thorough about.” ʸᵗ

Mau turns his head.

“There's a way you go, sometimes. When you've decided. It's very quiet.” He turns the bread over. “Maki's seen it. She doesn't talk about it either. She used to wait a bit before she'd come in the room.” ʸᵗ

He takes another bite and chews it for a long time.

“It's yours,” Yuta says. “I'm not going to tell you it isn't. It's in there with the rest of it. It's right next to the bit that started across a field at him, with nothing, the first time, because he was walking toward the house.” ʸᵗ

That doesn't seem to Mau to be a very comforting thing to say. He lies there and looks at it. He thinks it's possibly the first thing anyone has told him in five days that he actually believes.

“Does it,” he says. “Go away?” ᴹ

“No.” ʸᵗ

“Oh.” ᴹ

“You get so you can eat stew.” ʸᵗ

He tears the rest of the heel in two, and he holds out the smaller piece. Mau looks at it, and he looks at his own left hand. He gets it up off the blanket, with its splint and its two bad fingers. It wobbles across the space between them like a drunk crossing a bridge, and it closes on the bread, more or less. It's the plainest food in the world. It tastes of flour.

He keeps it down.

* * *

It's while he's chewing that he notices the thing on his thumb.

There's a little cut there, a nick from the edge of the tray, hardly worth the name. He has had it since the morning. He looks at it because it's in front of him. There's a place behind his eyes that has been quite empty for five days, a raw silent space like a tooth socket. He has been told not to touch it. He has been very good about that. He's not doing anything now, only looking. And something in there stirs, about the size of a sneeze.

The nick closes.

He stares at it.

*Stop that,* Ciel says instantly. *Stop it this second. Do you know what your cursed energy reserve is? I do. It's about what you'd find in a large dog. I've got it written down. Shoko said a week.*

*I didn't do anything.*

*Tell her.*

He looks up. Yuta has seen. Of course he has. He's got his eyes on that thumb with an expression of grave medical concern. His mouth is shut very tight, and something's happening to the muscle in his cheek. It's been a long week. They've neither of them slept. They're two very tired men, in a clinic, one of whom has just been enormously sick, who are both looking at a thumb.

“Don't laugh,” Yuta says. ʸᵗ

“I wasn't going to.” ᴹ

“I know. I'm telling myself.” ʸᵗ

It's a terrible idea. It's the worst idea anyone has had all week. Mau can already feel it starting, low down, right along the line of the seam, where he has been told it's going to hurt for about another month.

Out in the corridor Maki stops with a bucket and listens to the noises coming through the door. There's a sort of wheezing. Somebody's whimpering, “*Ow, ow, stop, ow*.” She stands there for a moment with no expression at all.

She doesn't go in. She waits a bit.
