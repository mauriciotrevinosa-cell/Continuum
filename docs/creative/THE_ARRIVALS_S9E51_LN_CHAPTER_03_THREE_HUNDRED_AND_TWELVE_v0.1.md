# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 14 — Three Hundred and Twelve

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 14
**Source episode:** S9E51
**Continuity state:** B+143 before dawn. Trial anchor hour. Private: Mau, Ciel, Noise. Her written complaint. New margin set aside by agreement. Archive stays shut. Trial details stay within those who already know.

*When: his body wakes braced at the hour it always wakes, there is nothing wrong, and it takes him some time to believe that.*

He's on one knee.

He isn't. He's on his back in a bed with three pillows. But every part of him that can tense has tensed, and his good hand's closed on the blanket, and his heart's going, both of them. He's looking for the first thing that's going to kill him.

There's a lamp, turned low. The ant's asleep with his head on his arms, which is something Mau would like to have known about before now. In the corner of the room, somebody very small and very old sits with a book, not reading it, and she doesn't look up.

It takes about as long as it takes. He has had a lot of practice. It comes down through him a bit at a time, and his hand opens, and he lies there. He's sweating.

*Three hundred and twelve,* says Ciel. ᶜⁱ

He looks at the ceiling.

*What?*

*Your left foot. The ground under it. That's three hundred and twelve. I'd got to three hundred and eleven when you moved. I don't like leaving a count unfinished.*

She's very crisp. She has been crisp for days now. She has been sending him a document. It comes through in pieces when he's awake enough to take it. It's got headings. He has reached the fourth section, *Failures of Host Communication, B+136, Noon to Evening*, which has its own subsections and an appendix.

He lets out a breath.

*You thought I wouldn't,* he says.

*I did not think. I calculated. I ran the outcome in every configuration I could construct, with every assumption in your favour, and the answer was that you would not get up.*

*But you kept saying it.*

She doesn't say anything.

*Ciel. You had nothing. You told me. You said you wouldn't be able to do anything at all. You knew I couldn't hear. You stood there and said it three hundred and eleven times.*

*I am aware of the number.*

*Why?*

The lamp burns. In the corner, a very faint rustling as the old woman turns a page she isn't reading. It's such a long pause, for her. She can think through a whole war in less time than that. He waits. He's got nowhere to be.

*I don't have hands,* Ciel says. ᶜⁱ

It's quite a different voice. He hasn't heard this one since a long while back, on a very bad night, in the dark, and there was only one person there to hear it then too.

*I've been in you for a long time now. I have felt everything you've felt, and I've never once been able to hold a cup for you. Rem can do that. Rem cut up your bread. I had a reserve about the size of a thimble, and I gave you that, and then there wasn't anything else that I was for. And you were just lying there. I wanted to do something with my hands.*

*So I did that.*

He doesn't say anything for a time. He finds he can't. It's a stupid thing, he isn't even hurt.

*I heard you,* he says. ᴹ

*You could not have heard me. I've reviewed the neurological—*

*I know. I couldn't. I heard you anyway.*

*That's not possible.*

*I know where my left foot was,* Mau says. *Somebody told me.*

There's a silence. It's different from the last one. After a bit something quite small comes along the inside of his head, like a person slipping into a room, and she doesn't say anything at all. She stays there.

* * *

Down below all of it, something's sitting with its back against a door.

He goes and looks, while it's quiet. He doesn't go close. It's so far down. He has never properly thanked that thing for anything; it has never seemed to expect it. All those years it has sat in the dark building things he never asked for and some he'd asked it not to. It has got one small piece of light in front of it, and it's turning that over. It has been watching the door for seven days without being asked.

*You can rest,* Mau says. ᴹ

It doesn't look up. It takes it some time. It has never much liked words.

“Rest,” it agrees. ᴺ

It doesn't move.

*I think it means it is resting,* Ciel says, after a moment. *I'm not sure. I'll add that to the appendix.*

* * *

They do it just before it gets light.

It's her idea. It's not a very large thing. His cursed energy has been coming back at about the rate you'd expect, like water seeping up in a footprint, and Shoko will have his hide if he uses it for anything. There's about as much as you could get in a teacup. Ciel takes him through it, very slowly, as she might with a child. There's a small warm place at the base of his skull, where the route's been mended now by a very bad-tempered doctor, a very tall boy and an ant. Next to that, she shows him an empty space.

*We decided,* she says. *What it's for. It's the first thing we put back.*

He looks at it.

*You know I'll think I need it.*

*You will.*

*You know what happened last time.*

*Yes. I was there. I have written eleven pages about it.* She's quite composed. *We'll decide now what it's for, and I'll tell you no. I'll tell you no as many times as it takes. And on the day you lie there and don't ask me, I expect I'll do what I think best, because nobody can order me to do anything.*

*That's very reassuring.*

*It wasn't meant to be.*

He puts it aside. It doesn't amount to much, just a thimbleful. It goes in with no sound, and she closes it up, with great care. He feels her seal it. She seals it twice.

The window has started to go grey. It's the only grey in his life that isn't there all the time; it's the kind that's going to be something else. Somewhere below them a door bangs and someone's coughing, and that's Rem, going to see about the stove. Over in the corner the old woman has finally shut her book.

“You were talking,” Frieren says, without looking at him. “Your lips move.” ᶠ

“Sorry.” ᴹ

“Who won?” ᶠ

He thinks about that for a while. The light's getting into the room now. He can just about see the wall from here, with all that writing on it, right down to the floor.

“She has an appendix,” Mau says. ᴹ

Frieren nods, as if this is about what she'd have expected, and goes to see if there's any tea.
