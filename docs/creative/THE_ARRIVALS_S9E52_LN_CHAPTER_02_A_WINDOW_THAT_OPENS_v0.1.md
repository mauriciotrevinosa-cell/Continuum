# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 16 — A Window That Opens

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 16
**Source episode:** S9E52
**Continuity state:** B+146 afternoon. Mau walks to the guest rooms with cane and company. Pays Ashborn's V58#7 wish. Guests remain free; no power exchange.

*When: Mau knocks on the guest room door with the top of his cane, Kaneki asks whether he would like to sit down first.*

“No,” Mau says. ᴹ

He would like to very much. It's sixty-one paces from the clinic to the guest wing, by way of the long gallery, and Kaneki has walked every one of them half a step behind his left shoulder, reading a book. The book's upside down. He has been on the wall for this for three days.

Kaneki glances up from it. He looks, for a moment, at the top of Mau's head, where about a finger's depth of new hair has come in, faster than it has any right to, with Beru seeping at everything in reach. He has the look of a man reading something in a language he happens to know.

“It'll keep changing for a while,” he says. “Mine did. You'll want to stop checking.” ᴷᵃⁿ

Mau hasn't got a good answer to that. He puts up a hand. It feels like a brush.

“I'll wait out here,” Kaneki says. “I'm at a good part.” ᴷᵃⁿ

There's a voice from inside.

The latch is a little stiff, and he has to do it with the wrong hand. Then he's standing in an ordinary room, with a bed, a table and one chair. There's a window. It's open. A little ordinary air is moving the curtain, and beyond it someone's explaining to a child that the common door can't be used as a goalpost.

Ashborn is sitting by the window with a cup.

He looks at Mau for quite a while. It isn't the sort of look you can do much about. He has seen everything that has ever walked and he has buried most of it. He looks at the cane and the bandage. He looks at the right hand, tucked into a sling Mai made out of something very strong and rather ugly. The eye has nearly stopped wandering; it only goes off when he's tired. He's tired.

He'd thought about what to say. He has had a lot of time for that. He went over it on the way, sixty-one paces, and he's got a number of quite good things.

“I came home,” Mau says. ᴹ

Nothing moves in that old face.

“So I have been told,” says Ashborn. ᴬˢ

He nods toward the bed, there being only one chair. It's a long way down to it. Mau gets there, with a bad moment in the middle, and he sits, and for a time they just stay as they are. The curtain moves. The argument about the goalpost has been referred to a higher authority.

“You said,” Mau says. “You'd like to hear it.” ᴹ

“I did.” ᴬˢ

“So.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴬˢ

The old monarch takes a sip of his tea. He sets the cup down in its saucer, very exactly. It doesn't rattle. He seems to take some satisfaction from that.

“There is a place on the floor of this room,” he remarks, “by the door. You are looking at it, in fact.” ᴬˢ

Mau looks. It's a floorboard. It's got a knot in it.

“It's a very dull place. I had not noticed until recently how dull. I sat and looked at it for seven minutes, with some attention, and it did nothing whatever.” ᴬˢ

He turns his head and looks out of the window. There's nothing in his voice at all.

“I have lived a long time,” Ashborn says. “I have seen stars put out. I don't believe I've ever seen anything so fine.” ᴬˢ

Mau doesn't know what that means. He looks at the floorboard again, then back at the old man. It's plain that nobody is going to tell him. It's plain, too, that something has just been said to him, in that dry far-off voice, of a size he isn't equipped for today. He'll work it out, maybe. He gets the feeling that he has got a long time to try.

“It's a nice window,” he says. ᴹ

“It opens,” says Ashborn. ᴬˢ
