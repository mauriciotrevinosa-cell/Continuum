# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 17 — Twelve Days Late

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S9E52
**Continuity state:** B+148 evening. Band practice room. Bocchi, chosen sister and his family in her own way; Kita beside her. She pays off what she rehearsed for V59. Right hand starting to answer.

*When: Mau gets to the practice room with the cane, the band stops, and Ryo goes on for two more bars because nobody has told her.*

“Don't stop,” he says. ᴹ

They've all looked up. Nijika has got her sticks in the air. Kita has come halfway off her stool, and there's a whole lot of expression on her face, and she's about to use it.

Bocchi has gone behind her amplifier.

She doesn't seem to have made any decision about it. There was a person standing there, in a very pink tracksuit, with a guitar, and now there isn't. It's a good amplifier, it has got a lot of room behind it. You can just see about an inch of hair.

Mau lowers himself onto the crate by the door. It's not as far down as it was a week ago. He has been coming a little farther every day: the stairs, the gallery, the yard. Today it's the music. He's got the right arm out of the sling, because Shoko told him to, and it's lying on his knee like something that belongs to a neighbour.

“I came to listen,” he says. “That's all. I'm very. Boring now. Ask anyone.” ᴹ

Nijika starts to smile. She counts them in, four small clicks. They start again from somewhere in the middle.

It's new. It's got something odd going on in the bridge. He doesn't know a great deal about this and he never has. He can hear when the guitar comes in, though. It comes from behind the amplifier, a bit muffled, and it's the most ferocious thing he has heard in two weeks.

He shuts his good eye and listens.

The fingers of his right hand have started to move. It's only the first two. He notices that after a while. They're going a little behind the beat, about as much as you'd expect from a neighbour's arm, and they don't know he's watching.

* * *

When it stops, nobody says anything for a bit. Ryo has found something to eat.

An inch of hair has become two. Then there's an eye. It looks at him. It goes down to his neck, and to the long line that you can see the top of, above his shirt. It goes to the cane. Very slowly the whole person comes out, one shuffling foot at a time, holding the guitar in front of her with both arms, like someone who has been told there's going to be a flood.

She stops about a yard away and looks at the floor.

Kita has got up. She doesn't come over, she only goes a bit nearer than she was, and stands there.

“I,” Bocchi says. ᴮᵒ

Mau waits. He's very good at this now. He's had it done to him for a fortnight, by experts. It's a thing he never knew he could give anyone: all the time in the world, and no one in it looking at their watch.

“I had a,” she says. “I was going to. On the day. I practised it. In the morning, I went behind the, by the water tanks, and I practised, so that I'd be able to, when you. So you'd hear. I mean you wouldn't have. It's so far. But I was going to. I had it ready. It was quite loud.” ᴮᵒ

Her lips have gone white. She's shaking, all the way down. The strings are humming with it.

“And then I didn't,” Bocchi says. “When your arm. I made a noise. It wasn't even. I didn't do it. I had one thing, and I didn't do it, the whole day.” ᴮᵒ

He'd like to say that it's all right. It doesn't seem to be the thing that's wanted.

“What was it?” he asks. ᴹ

She looks up.

It's the first time she has met his eye, and he thinks it may be the first time she has met anyone's since about the spring. She has got enormous, terrified, swimming eyes, and she looks at him as if he has asked her to walk off a roof. Kita's hand has moved, about an inch, and stopped. She breathes in. It goes on for a long time. She seems to be taking in about all the air in the room.

“*GO!*” screams Bocchi Hitori. ᴮᵒ

It isn't a very long word. It comes out at a pitch that Ryo's sandwich will never recover from. It's cracked right down the middle. A pigeon leaves the windowsill.

“*GO ON! YOU CAN DO IT! GET HIM! YOU'RE THE—YOU'RE MY—YOU CAN—GO!*” ᴮᵒ

It rings around the room, off the walls, in the strings of all those guitars. It goes on ringing. Down in the yard somebody has dropped something.

Nobody moves.

She's standing there with her mouth still open, bright red to the roots of her hair. She has got two tracks of water running down her face. She's twelve days late. She has just shouted at a man on a crate, in a small room, to go and get someone who's dead. She looks as if she'd very much like to be taken apart and put in several different drawers.

Mau has got his hand over his eyes. It's the left one. His shoulders are going, and it hurts, it hurts like anything, all the way around, and he doesn't care.

“I heard that,” he says. ᴹ

It takes him a moment. He has to wait for his voice. He takes the hand away, and his face is wet, and he's grinning like a fool, with his whole wrecked mouth.

“That was. Really loud,” Mau says. “That was what did it.” ᴹ

She makes a very small sound. Kita's there now. She has got an arm around her, and she's laughing and crying, and telling her she was amazing, she was so good, and she's going to tell everyone. Bocchi has begun slowly to slide down toward the floor. It's a thing she does. They've all learned to give her a minute.

Nijika clicks her sticks together, gently, four times.

Mau leans back against the wall. He looks down.

All on its own, on top of his knee, his right hand has closed about halfway.
