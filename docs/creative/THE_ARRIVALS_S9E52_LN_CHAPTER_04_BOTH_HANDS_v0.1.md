# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 19 — Both Hands

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S9E52
**Continuity state:** B+152. Season Nine ends. Mau back to his prior state of health apart from fatigue and follow-up; nothing he built was lost. Old sequelae as before: grey except Yuka, blurred left edge, two stiff fingers, scars. New permanent: brown-and-white hair. Training not yet cleared. Mahoraga and broken forms mending at normal cost. Archive shut. World's fear continues. No S10 begun.

*When: Shoko tells him to close his right hand, he asks which one that is, and she throws a roll of bandage at him.*

It bounces off his forehead. He catches it on the way down, with the right.

Neither of them says anything. He looks at it. It's only a roll of bandage. All five fingers have closed around it, without any fuss, at about the moment he told them to.

“Again,” Shoko says. ˢʰ

He does it again. She has him touch each finger to the thumb, then in the other order, then with his eyes shut. She has him follow her pencil, and the right eye goes with it and doesn't wander off. The left does what it has done for years. It's got that same soft smear along the outer edge that he has been complaining about since before he can remember, and he has never been so glad to see anything.

She makes him count back from a hundred by sevens. He's bad at it. She says that he was always bad at it. She looks in the old notes, to be sure.

She walks all the way around him, pressing. There's the long thin line around his waist, gone from pink to white, and she runs her thumb along it. The place on his neck is going to scar. It's not pretty. Beru has views on that. There's the top of one ear, grown back slightly the wrong shape, which Gojo has offered to have framed. There's the hair, which she pushes out of the way with a finger and doesn't bother to mention. She has given up on that. On his left hand, there are the same two stiff fingers there have always been. She bends each of them, and she lets them go.

She sits down on the edge of the table. It's the first time he has seen her sit in sixteen days without being told to. She gets out a cigarette and looks at it. She puts it back.

“Well,” Shoko says. “You're you.” ˢʰ

He waits.

“You'll be tired. You'll be tired for a month, and you'll hate it, and you'll tell me you aren't. No training until I say. If you so much as lift a pebble with that Blue, I'll know. I want you in here every morning till the leaves turn. But it's all there. The strength, the eyes, all that ridiculous nonsense you've got stuffed in you. Nothing's gone. I went through you like a customs officer. It only needed time.” ˢʰ

Her voice is quite flat. She's looking at the wall.

It's still there. Nobody has cleaned it. It goes from the door to the window now, floor to ceiling in six or seven kinds of handwriting, with a big bad drawing of a jar lid in one corner. *Rem. Taihei. The ant. Kaneki, stairs. Frieren*, in tiny neat letters, every night for sixteen nights. There are names on there he wouldn't have thought could spell. Toward the bottom, in a small round hand, it says *Ori (checking)*.

“That's a lot of people,” Mau says. ᴹ

“You've got a lot of people.” ˢʰ

He doesn't know what to say. He has the roll of bandage in his hand. He opens his fingers and shuts them.

“I made you cry,” he says. “Again.” ᴹ

“Yes.” ˢʰ

“I'm sorry.” ᴹ

“No, you aren't.” She rubs her eyes with the heel of her hand. “Don't be. Get off my table. They've been holding dinner for half an hour, and if that child comes up here to fetch you once more I'm going to adopt her.” ˢʰ

He gets down. He does it by himself. It's not fast. At the door he stops and looks back at her, sitting there, a tired woman on a table in the evening light. She has a great many other people to look after, and a life, and someone downstairs who's going to complain about the quality of her gratitude.

“The closest I've had,” he says. ᴹ

She doesn't look up. She picks up the pencil and she throws that too.

* * *

The table's loud.

He can hear it from the top of the stairs. It has got to the point where someone's standing on something. By the time he has reached the bottom they have all noticed, and they all very carefully go on with what they were doing, and nobody gets up. There's a place left for him on the side that faces the door. Rimuru's in the one beside it, arguing with Veldora's letter. Taihei comes through with the bread.

There's a stew. It has got barley in it, and a piece of shoulder.

Rem sets it down in front of him, then goes straight back to the kitchen without looking to see what he does about that.

He looks at it for a while. Yuta, across the table, has got very interested in a spoon.

He eats some. It's a good stew.

Yuka's at the far end with Mai and Maki, with a burn on her wrist and a piece of paper covered in drawings of a door. She's in the middle of telling them something, and she's got a fork in the air to show the angle. She looks up, about then, through all of it, all that noise and steam. She's the only colour in the room. She looks at him and at the spoon, and at his right hand holding it. She doesn't come over. She lifts her eyebrows at him, about a quarter of an inch, and goes back to what she was saying.

It's the best thing anyone has done for him all month.

Then something lands on the table in front of him with a slap. It's a deck of cards.

The noise goes down quite a lot. It's interesting how quickly that happens. There's someone standing on the bench beside him, with her fists on her hips and her pink hair sticking up. She hasn't said anything at all. She has got her chin stuck out and her eyes are very bright. Over her shoulder Umaru has appeared, with her official hat on, and Rem behind her with a jar lid pinned to her apron. Ori's got up on someone's knee to see. Beru is looking out of the floor.

Mau puts down the spoon.

He looks at the deck, and he reaches out. It's his right hand, the one that grew in a fire. It goes across the table without any hurry and takes up the whole pack, and he brings the left one up beside it, with its two bad fingers that don't close. He's never been any good at this. It takes some time. He gets his thumb under, he gets the cards to bend. They come together with a long soft ripple, and only two of them fall out.

Nobody's breathing.

He deals. Seven to her and seven to him, with a whole table watching. He picks his up, and he fans them out, in both his hands, and holds them up in front of his chest where nobody else can see.

He looks at them for a long moment.

They're terrible. It's the worst hand he has ever seen.

Milim Nava slowly starts to smile.

Outside it's getting dark. There's a long way to go; there are towns out there that have started drawing wheels on their walls, and a woman somewhere with a nice voice who has got exactly what she wanted. There's a door in the bottom of him with something sitting against it. All of that's going to be there tomorrow.

*Your move,* says Ciel. *I would not play the king.*

He plays the king.

It takes her four minutes to destroy him. He loses everything he has. It's the most complete defeat in the history of the settlement, and Umaru has it entered in the record. Someone has started banging on the table. He's laughing so hard it hurts, all the way around, and he can feel it in every place he has ever been put back together.

He deals again.
