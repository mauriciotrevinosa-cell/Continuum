# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 19 — What Colour Is It

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-03)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 19
**Source episode:** S9E52
**Continuity state:** B+150 afternoon. New permanent mark, creator's request: Mau's hair grows back brown and white mixed all through, no longer only the old white at the left temple. Mau sees grey except Yuka, so he has to be told the colours. Family and comedy. Mau and Yuka talk privately about fear, overtraining and distance during the clinic days. They make plans together.

*When: Maki sits him on an upturned bucket in the yard, she asks whether he would like to keep the other ear.*

“I'd like that,” Mau says. ᴹ

“Then stop looking at the scissors.” ᴹᵃ

They are the big kitchen ones. She tried the small pair first and they gave up. It has been fourteen days, and between the doctor and the ant and whatever that thing in his chest is doing, his hair has been coming in like weeds after rain. It's over his ears. It's in his good eye. This morning Shoko looked at it, said that she had never in her life seen anything so stupid and sent him outside.

Maki takes a handful at the crown and cuts. She has cut a lot of hair. Most of it was on people who wouldn't sit still either.

It falls on his knees. He looks at it.

He picks some up with his right hand, because he's been told to use that one for everything. It's a small damp clump between his finger and thumb. There's some that's dark grey. There's some that's pale grey. They're all mixed up together, strand by strand, like two kinds of thread off the same floor.

He has had a white place at his left temple for a long time now. He knows about that one. People have told him, and he has learned where it is by the way they look at it. He's used to it. It's a patch; it stays where it's put.

This doesn't seem to be staying anywhere.

He turns it over. It's a strange thing to hold a piece of yourself and have no idea what you're looking at.

“What colour is it?” he asks. ᴹ

Maki's scissors don't pause.

“Ask her.” ᴹᵃ

Yuka is sitting on the step with one boot up, eating a pear and looking at nothing. She has a practice blade across her knees and a new bruise. She's got about ten minutes before Mai wants her for something to do with a hinge. She looks over and she chews. She has been telling him what colour things are for quite a long time, and she takes it seriously. She gets up, comes across the yard and takes the clump out of his fingers. She holds it up against the light.

“Brown,” she says. ʸ

“Still?” ᴹ

“Brown and white.” She tilts it. “It's not stripes. It's more like someone dropped a bag of flour on a dog.” ʸ

Maki makes a small sound through her nose.

“How much white?” ᴹ

Yuka walks around him. She pushes his head forward with two fingers, then to the side. She takes her time about it. She pulls a piece up off the top and looks at the root, and she's frowning a little, the same way she looks at a drawing of a door.

“All of it,” she says. “I mean all over. Your temple's not even a thing anymore, I can't find where it starts. It's lighter at the roots than it was last week. I think it's still going.” ʸ

He thinks about that.

He'd like to know what it is about this. He has had a sword through him. He has seen the inside of his own waist. There's no reason in the world why a bit of hair should be what makes his throat go tight, in the sun, on a bucket. It doesn't hurt. It's only that it's his, and it's never going to go back. And he'll never see it, not really, not as anything but one more kind of grey. He'll have to ask.

“Shoko says it's permanent,” Yuka says. She takes another bite of the pear. “She says she doesn't know why. She says your body had a look at everything that happened and this is what it decided to say about it.” ʸ

“Is it bad?” ᴹ

She looks at him. She doesn't hurry. She's never once told him that a thing looked fine when it didn't.

“You look like someone who's been somewhere,” she says. ʸ

“I have.” ᴹ

“Yeah.” ʸ

She puts the clump back in his lap. He thinks she's going to go back to her step, because she's looking at the blade she left there. Instead she comes around the bucket to stand in front of him.

“Can we have the yard for a minute?” she asks Maki. ʸ

Maki takes the scissors away from Mau's ear. She tells him to stay where he is. She takes her cup as far as the other side of the door and closes it behind her.

Yuka picks up the blade. Mau looks at the bruise now that he can see it properly. It's yellow at the edge and dark in the middle. She sees him looking and sits down with the blade across her knees.

“I should have stopped yesterday,” she says. “Maki called it. I went back out when she'd gone.” ʸ

“What were you trying to get right?” ᴹ

She looks down at her arm. She has held it straight since she picked up the blade. Now she rests it against her leg.

“There's a point after the Garden shuts when I keep reaching for the thing I just lost. I've got a way to make myself move instead. I like figuring it out.” ʸ

He nods. He can hear that part in her voice. It sounds the way it does when she has a drawing she wants to show him.

“The extra rounds were something else,” she says. “I was in your room and you shut your eyes in the middle of a sentence. I knew you'd fallen asleep. I'd checked. Then I looked at you again and I was back at the rail waiting for you to move. In the yard there was always one more thing I could do.” ʸ

She takes her hand off the blade.

“Some days I could come up and tell you about it. Some days I got as far as your chair and couldn't look at you. I kept thinking I'd frighten you if I said why. So I sat there with that stupid rag.” ʸ

He remembers the sound of it going along the blade. He was so glad somebody had brought a thing from outside that he had managed to be glad about the sound too. And when he'd wanted to ask whether she was coming back, he'd waited until she was already at the door.

“I missed you even when you were there,” he says. ᴹ

Her chin moves. She puts the blade on the step beside her.

“I missed you too. I'm sorry I left you guessing.” ʸ

“I was trying to guess very quietly.” ᴹ

She laughs once. Her eyes fill up anyway. He opens his left hand on his knee and she comes close enough to put hers in it.

“Tomorrow I'm going to tell Maki about the extra rounds. Today I'd like to go see Mai, then come home and have dinner with you if you're up to it.” ʸ

“I'd like that.” ᴹ

She has to blink a few times before she can look at him again. When she does, she slides her hand up to the side of his head and smooths the bit of hair Maki hasn't cut yet.

“And I like it,” she says. “The hair. You asked if it was bad. I didn't tell you the part I was thinking.” ʸ

He leans his cheek into her palm.

“Will you tell me the part next time?” ᴹ

“If you ask me before I've got a mouthful of pear.” ʸ

He has a good answer to that. Before he can say it she bends and kisses him, with one hand still in his hair.

When Maki knocks from the other side of the door, Yuka says she can come back in. She stays close enough for Mau to touch her knee when the scissors start up again.

* * *

“I'd like it noted,” says a voice from the gallery, “that I said this would happen.” ᴳ

Gojo is leaning on the rail above them, with a cup, and that hair of his standing up in the afternoon light like a dandelion. Nobody has ever been able to do anything about it. He's looking down at the top of Mau's head with an expression of deep and growing satisfaction.

“You didn't,” Maki says. ᴹᵃ

“I said it to Shoko. I told her it runs in the family.” ᴳ

“He's not your—” ᴹᵃ

“I've already had a word with the clerk.” He lifts his cup in salute. “Give it a year, kid. You'll be able to see it from space. People will stop you in the road. It's a terrible burden. You have my deepest sympathy.” ᴳ

Mau starts to laugh, and it gets him in the usual place. He'd bend over, but somebody has a very large pair of scissors next to his ear.

“*Sit still,*” Maki says. ᴹᵃ

He sits still. She finishes the back. She doesn't make it look good; she makes it even. Somewhere in there a small official person in a hat arrives with an envelope and asks whether the Fort may have a sample for the record, and she's given one. Ear turns up and eats some before anybody can stop him, and he's sick behind the woodpile.

When it's done Maki stands back with the scissors over her shoulder and she looks at it, the way you'd look at a fence.

“That'll do,” she says. ᴹᵃ

He puts up his hand. It's short now. It feels like anybody's.

There's a good deal of it on the ground around the bucket, in the sun. He looks at that for a little while. Two kinds of grey. The wind has started to move it around, and it isn't making any difference to him which is which.

Yuka has finished her pear. She throws the core to the dog, gets up and sticks the blade through her belt.

“Brown and white,” she says on her way past, in case he has forgotten. ʸ

“Brown and white,” Mau says. ᴹ

He'll need to remember that. People are going to ask.
