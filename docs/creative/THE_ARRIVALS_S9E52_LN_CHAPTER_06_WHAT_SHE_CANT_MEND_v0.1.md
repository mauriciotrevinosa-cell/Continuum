# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 60
## Chapter 17 — What She Can't Mend

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S9 v0.2 (2026-11-04)
**Volume:** 60
**Reading order:** 17
**Source episode:** S9E52
**Continuity state:** B+146 afternoon, next door to Ashborn. Creator's scene. DIO stayed away from the clinic; cannot repair him; explains, as near as she can tell, that the Black Heart returned the world to an earlier point, so the old line is the past of this one. Same people, different decisions. She did not alter Frieren's feelings this time and changed the Trial. First awakening before the flowers remains unknown. Ori not yet calling him father. Fort rematch is not the card debt.

*When: Mau gets to the next door along, it is already open a hand's width, and DIO is standing behind it with a watering can she has not been using.*

She is not on the wall.

He worked that out a few days ago. Everyone else is there, in six kinds of handwriting. Maomao asked her, he has heard, and she said that she would only be in the way. At the dinner she stood where he could see her, said one sentence and left before the plates did.

Now she looks at the cane. She looks at the neck, at the sling, at the place where the shirt doesn't quite cover the line. She puts the watering can down on the floor, not on anything.

“You walked here,” she says. ᴰⁱ

“Sixty-one paces. And then. About nine.” ᴹ

“Sit down.” ᴰⁱ

There's a stool. He sits. There's a plant on the sill, in a bowl with a chip in it, with four leaves. It had three last time.

She comes across the room. She is very small, for what she used to be. She stops in front of him and lifts her hand, and she holds it over the bandage on his neck without touching it. Her face goes still. He has seen that look on her before, a long time ago, from a long way down. She used to reach into things.

Nothing happens.

He can feel nothing happen. It goes on for a while. Her hand has started to shake. She moves it to the line at his waist. She moves it to the sling.

“It's only a little thing,” she says. “It's only tissue. I made oceans. I could have closed that without looking up.” ᴰⁱ

“I know.” ᴹ

“This one. Just this one. I only want—” ᴰⁱ

She takes the hand back and looks at it, and then she closes it. She turns around and starts pulling the blanket off her bed.

“Lie down.” ᴰⁱ

“I'm all right.” ᴹ

“You are grey. I can see you are grey, and I am told that's not your eyes. Lie down, put that arm on something, you shouldn't be holding it like that. Who let you walk this far? Have you eaten? Did you eat this morning, or did you tell someone you did?” ᴰⁱ

She has got a pillow. He doesn't know where she got it. She has him by the good elbow, with surprisingly little strength, and he finds that he's lying down on a goddess's bed with his boots on, and she's arranging his arm with great concentration and getting it wrong.

He looks up at her. Something has started at the corner of his mouth.

“You sound like Shoko,” he says. ᴹ

Her hands stop.

She looks at him. She has soil on her jaw. Something goes across her face that he has never seen there, in either life, a sort of startled hope she has no idea what to do with.

“So you see me like that as well?” DIO says. “As a mother?” ᴰⁱ

It gets him right along the seam. He tries not to. He gets one hand on his side, and it comes out of him anyway, small and wheezing and very painful.

“That,” Mau says, when he can. “That's a talk. You should have. With her. I'm not. Getting in the middle of that.” ᴹ

She starts to laugh.

It only lasts a moment. It goes wrong halfway. She's standing there beside the bed with his sleeve in her hand and her mouth open, and no sound is coming out at all, and then her face just breaks.

* * *

She sits down on the floor.

He has seen her angry. He has seen her afraid, and he has heard her say *sorry* to a room full of people. He has never seen this. She doesn't seem to know how it's done. She cries like someone who has read about it, with her hands open on her knees and her shoulders very straight.

“I can't fix it,” she says. ᴰⁱ

“No.” ᴹ

“I watched that. I sat in a room, through a soldier. And I couldn't come to the clinic, because I would stand there and I would put out my hand, and I knew. And you'd look at me just like that. Being kind.” ᴰⁱ

He doesn't say anything. There's nothing to say to that, so he stays where she put him.

“I did this,” she says. “Not the quarry. All of it. I put you in a room, in the other life, and I let you die in it for as long as it took. I reached into people. I thought I knew what I was doing, I was so sure. Every day I look at that plant and I hate myself a little more, and it doesn't get any smaller, and it doesn't do you any good at all.” ᴰⁱ

She wipes her face with the back of her wrist. It leaves soil.

“That's why it was different here. The Trial. I moved it. I made it smaller, so you wouldn't have to go down so far. And Ori.” Her voice catches on that. “I brought her younger. A child, so she could have the part you never got to give her. So somebody could carry her home.” ᴰⁱ

The curtain moves. There is a bird somewhere.

“And she doesn't call you that. I know she doesn't. I did that as well. I thought I'd be giving you back your daughter, and I gave her to Frieren.” ᴰⁱ

“You gave her. To herself,” Mau says. ᴹ

She looks up.

It takes him some time. He has it, though. It's one of the things he has had a lot of time to think about, lying in a bed with a small person explaining his position in the accounts.

“She'll decide. It's hers.” He breathes. “She comes to find me. More than she used to. She doesn't say why. She looks at me. Like she's. Reading a page. I think she's seeing. If she wants one.” ᴹ

“And if she doesn't?” ᴰⁱ

“Then I lost at a game. A lot. With someone I like.” ᴹ

His mouth has gone crooked again. He can't do anything about it.

“I can wait,” he says. “I'm very good. At waiting. Now.” ᴹ

She looks at him for a long while. She has stopped crying, or she has forgotten about it.

“Do you know how she was born?” DIO says. ᴰⁱ

“This one? No.” He looks at the ceiling. “The other one. Yes.” ᴹ

* * *

She gets up then. She takes the stool, since he's in her bed. She sits with her knees together, and she speaks to the plant.

“I have looked at it for a long time,” she says. “I still can't swear to all of it. As near as I can tell, nothing was thrown away. That life happened, Mau. It isn't a dream you had, and it isn't somewhere else. It's behind you. It's behind all of this. The heart was in you, I had reached into it, and when it went wrong it took the world back to an earlier picture of itself. Everything went. The whole world went back to that one point, and it started walking again.” ᴰⁱ

He lies quite still.

He has wanted to ask someone this for a very long time. He's been afraid of it for about as long. It's so short, when he finally gets hold of it.

“So they're the same people,” he says. ᴹ

“Yes.” ᴰⁱ

She turns the bowl a little, so that the new leaf can have the light.

“And no. They started from the same place, and they're made of the same things. Yuta is Yuta. But they haven't made the same decisions. They can't have. I changed the Trial, and that moved one stone, and it moved the next. And this time—” She stops. She makes herself go on. “This time I didn't touch Frieren. I didn't reach in and turn anything up, so that she'd feel more than she was going to. I left it alone. So you were her student, and she went on with her road, and that's what she would have done. It's what she chose. All of them. Everything that's happened to you here, somebody chose.” ᴰⁱ

For a time neither of them says anything.

He thinks about that. It's a big thing, and his head isn't what it was, and he has to go around it slowly. He thinks about someone at a rail, who is the only colour in the world. He thinks about nineteen people on a wall. He thinks about a very old woman with a book, who wrote herself down for nights in small neat letters, because she wanted to, and for no other reason.

“Before that,” he says. “The first time. Before the flowers. Where I came from.” ᴹ

“I don't know,” DIO says. “I never did.” ᴰⁱ

He nods. He hadn't really thought so.

He turns his head on her pillow and looks at her. Small. Dirty. Sitting up very straight, waiting to be told what she's owed.

“Thank you,” Mau says. ᴹ

She flinches. It's the only word for it.

“For *what*?” ᴰⁱ

“This time,” he says. “Nobody moved. Anybody's heart.” ᴹ

She puts her hand over her mouth.

She's still sitting like that when somebody knocks on the door frame, three times, at about the height of a person's belt.

* * *

Ori looks at the blanket on the floor. She looks at the goddess, then at the man lying on the goddess's bed with his boots on. She has a map under one arm and a very serious expression, and she doesn't appear to think that any of this is the most urgent matter before her.

“The Fort is holding an emergency meeting,” she says. “They need the vending citizen.” ᴼ

Mau gets up on his elbow.

“What's the emergency?” ᴹ

“Milim heard you can walk.” ᴼ

There's a short silence.

“Who told her?” ᴹ

“She's already preparing the battlefield. She says it's not the cards, the cards are separate, you still owe the cards. This is the rematch.” Ori consults the map. “There's a moat now.” ᴼ

He starts to swing his legs off the bed. It takes a while. Ori comes over without being asked and stands where his hand can find her shoulder, and she doesn't look at it. She just stands there, and frowns at her map, and bears a certain amount of weight.

He looks at the top of her head. He doesn't say anything about that.

He gets the cane. He looks back.

DIO has not moved from the stool. She has soil on her face, and her eyes are red, and she has taken her hand away from her mouth, and she's looking at the two of them with no expression at all. She isn't on any wall. It's very quiet in that room, and very clean, and there's a plant.

“Can she come?” Mau asks. ᴹ

Ori turns around and examines her. It takes some time.

“Has she got clearance?” ᴼ

“I was a goddess,” DIO says. Her voice is hoarse. ᴰⁱ

“That's not clearance.” ᴼ

“No,” says DIO. “I don't suppose it is.” ᴰⁱ

Ori thinks it over. She unrolls the map a little and looks at something in the corner.

“You can be Supply,” she says. “Nobody wants Supply.” ᴼ

* * *

It takes them twenty minutes to do the length of the long gallery.

They go three abreast, at the speed of the cane. Ori has the map held out in front of her and is explaining the position. Milim has the high ground, the moat and a fleet. The government is officially neutral. Mau asks what the government likes. Ori gives him a look of deep contempt, and she tells him that this is what she came for. DIO walks on the other side with a watering can she has forgotten to put down. She asks, after some time, in a small stiff voice, whether anyone has considered attacking from under the moat.

Ori stops walking.

She looks at DIO for quite a while, then she takes out a pencil.

He wins one.

It's the only one he'll ever win, and it's the most disgraceful victory in the history of the settlement. There are two cans left in his storage from the good batch, the cold ones. He puts the first on the table in front of the President of the Fort, in full session, and doesn't say anything at all. There's a brief recess. The government's position on the moat undergoes a review. Then he limps across a floor covered in very small soldiers, and he holds out the second can, at arm's length, in his left hand, to the strongest living thing he has ever met.

Milim looks at the can.

She looks at him. Her whole face is working.

“That doesn't count either,” she says, and she takes it. ᴹⁱ

They have to help him up off the floor afterward. Nobody's quite sure who started that. Supply is found to have been holding the eastern bridge for some time, by herself, with a watering can.

* * *

It's getting dark when he gets home.

He has done about four times what Shoko said. He's going to hear about it. His whole side is on fire and he has someone's small wooden soldier in his pocket, and he can't remember how it got there. He takes the stairs one at a time.

Their door's open. Yuka's sitting on the end of the bed with one boot off, working at the knot in the other, with a new bruise and her hair still wet. She looks up. She sees his face, and she stops with the lace in her hand.

“What?” ʸ

He gets himself across the room and lets himself down beside her. It takes a while. She waits. He sits there breathing, and looking at the portrait by the window, and at the little bird. There's so much of it. It's going to take him a long time, and it's going to come out in the wrong order, and he'll probably ask her twice.

“I had. A really good day,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I love you,” he says. “I want you a little closer, if you don't mind.” ᴹ

Yuka lets go of the lace and moves until her knee rests against his. She goes back to the knot without moving away.

“Start at the beginning,” she says. ʸ
