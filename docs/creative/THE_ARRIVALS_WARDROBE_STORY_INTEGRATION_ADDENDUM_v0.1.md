## Purpose

This addendum audits Season 1 after the creator locked Mau Wardrobe v1 to concrete creator-grounded garments. It converts the wardrobe from a visual-design list into recurring story continuity without turning clothing into exposition or inflating page count unnecessarily.

The goal is that the same small set of clothes acquires history across the season:

`arrival evidence -> ordinary use -> shared-world clue -> domestic familiarity -> absence -> return -> lowered-inhibition callback -> home`

Wardrobe remains separate from character identity.

This document does **not** redesign Frieren or Fern. Their default + cold-weather variants remain as defined in Wardrobe v1.

# 2. Season integration audit — 13 approved wardrobe/story integrations

## 1 — E1: arrival silhouette is now a continuity anchor

Mau's preferred arrival look is concretely read as:

- McLaren hoodie;
- light-blue jeans;
- light sneakers;
- metal watch;
- backpack.

The exact headwear can remain off at awakening so the face is visually unobstructed.

The hoodie should look comparatively clean at first and accumulate believable travel dirt/wear through the end of E1 and into E2.

No extra page is required for this lock; it changes visual continuity on existing pages.

The separately approved E1 name-discovery addendum remains in force and adds its already-approved two pages: backpack inspection -> personalized `MAU T / 5` jersey -> `Maybe your name is Mau.` / `I like it.`

## 2 — E2: first clothing change has a practical cause

Add one quiet page during the inn-cleanup / first-home material.

Mau's arrival clothes are visibly dirty from the first days. He checks the small clothing pool that arrived in the backpack. The McLaren hoodie is left hanging / laid out to dry or air rather than disappearing between scenes.

Mau changes into **M2**:

- plain white tee;
- red Ferrari team button shirt worn casually over it;
- same jeans and sneakers if still serviceable.

No fashion dialogue is needed. The page exists to establish that:

- the tiny wardrobe is physically real;
- clothes repeat;
- changes happen for practical reasons;
- the McLaren hoodie remains in the household and can later become a shared-space object.

**Page delta: +1.**

## 3 — E4: Yuta recognizes a world that Mau cannot remember

Add a compact two-page beat after Yuta has enough calm to notice his surroundings but before it would interrupt the episode's central emotional material.

Yuta notices one or more of the modern sports/motorsport markers among Mau's possessions — ideally the McLaren hoodie/cap and/or the personalized football jersey.

The reaction should begin visually rather than as exposition. A logo / construction / jersey language is familiar to him in a place where it should not be.

Suggested dialogue direction, not mandatory final wording:

> **Yuta:** `Where did you get that?`  
> **Mau:** `It was in my bag.`

Mau cannot explain the team, sport, sponsor language or why he owned it.

Yuta may then give a restrained observation in the spirit of:

> `That's from my world. Or something close to it.`

Do **not** resolve Mau's origin from this. The clue proves only that at least part of Mau's material culture overlaps with Yuta's modern world closely enough to be recognizable.

The emotional asymmetry matters: Yuta can recognize a piece of Mau's past before Mau can.

**Page delta: +2.**

## 4 — E5: ordinary-days clothing rotation proves this is how Mau actually dresses

Use existing ordinary-days / household montage space to show natural recurrence rather than adding pages.

At least one sequence should put Mau in the personalized jersey or another sports top, and another may use the beige bucket hat during outdoor work.

The intent is to make motorsport / sportswear a normal part of Mau's visual language rather than a plot-only clue that disappears after Yuta notices it.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 5 — E7: the broken watch becomes a silent past-without-memory object

Add one quiet page to the approved outside-the-dinner emotional beat.

After Mau hears the others' laughter / stories and feels the absence of his own past, his attention falls to the chronograph watch.

It is damaged or stopped. He turns it over / tries the crown or buttons / studies signs of wear as if physical detail might unlock a memory. Nothing comes.

He still puts it back on or keeps it rather than discarding it.

No exact stopped time is established yet. No supernatural meaning is assigned.

This converts the watch into a recurring symbol: evidence that a life existed even when the life cannot be accessed.

**Page delta: +1.**

## 6 — E8: the trust object is specifically the watch

The existing E8 beat in which Mau leaves a personally meaningful object unattended in common space is now grounded as **M-A3, the damaged chronograph watch**.

Rimuru and Yuta notice it and deliberately leave it untouched. Mau later retrieves it without concern.

This quietly advances the E7 object from private loss to household trust.

No new page is required; use the already-approved E8 breathing room.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 7 — E9: the pre-separation borrowing baseline uses the actual McLaren pieces

The already-approved E9 clothing baseline is no longer a generic cap / generic hooded layer.

Primary recurring borrowed pieces are now:

- **M-H1 McLaren hoodie**;
- **M-A2 white McLaren cap**.

Frieren can casually take one when leaving the room / house or when she is cold. Mau barely questions it. Other household members may notice.

The visual language should remain domestic rather than performatively romantic.

No extra pages beyond E9's already-approved integrated count are required.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 8 — E11: mixed belongings become visually specific

When Mau starts separating belongings after the Fern reunion misunderstanding, the existing visual of mixed possessions should clearly include recognizable Wardrobe v1 pieces.

The McLaren hoodie / cap should have an established place in the shared room rather than appearing only when a later callback needs them.

This makes moving them emotionally legible without dialogue.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 9 — E12: the missing-cap beat is the white McLaren cap

The approved empty-space callback is now specifically **M-A2, the white McLaren cap**.

Frieren reaches to the familiar place by habit, finds it empty, pauses, and continues.

Because the viewer has seen her use this exact cap before, absence carries more weight than a generic prop would.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 10 — E14: `Nice hoodie.` is the orange/black McLaren hoodie

The post-reconciliation morning callback is now locked to **M-H1, the orange/black McLaren hoodie**.

Frieren comes downstairs wearing it. Its visual identity should be obvious enough that the household immediately knows whose it is.

Mau sincerely says:

> `Nice hoodie.`

He still does not immediately realize it is his. Frieren does not correct him.

This is the same hoodie established on Mau from the opening arc, not a newly invented garment.

No new page is required beyond the already-approved E14 callback space.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 11 — E17: workwear rotation carries the wardrobe into the wider world

During repeated guild-job / workday exteriors, use existing montage space to rotate Mau through the small wardrobe rather than defaulting every workday to one costume.

Good practical choices include:

- beige bucket hat for outdoor jobs;
- jersey / Ferrari shirt on warmer workdays;
- McLaren hoodie when colder;
- the same jeans / sneakers with accumulating wear where continuity supports it.

Do not make every day a new outfit. Repetition is desirable.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 12 — E18: the chocolate outfit is now the exact McLaren callback

The already-approved E18 lowered-inhibition wardrobe beat is now specific:

- **M-H1 orange/black McLaren hoodie**;
- **M-A2 white McLaren cap**;
- Frieren's own comfortable short bottoms.

This is the **same hoodie** Frieren wore after the E14 reconciliation and the same wardrobe family seeded in E9.

The progression therefore reads:

`Mau wears it -> shared room normalizes it -> Frieren borrows it -> separation removes it -> reconciliation returns it -> chocolate lowers inhibition and she chooses it even more openly.`

The chocolate does not create the attachment. It only lowers inhibition around an already-established habit.

No extra page is required beyond E18's already-approved integrated wardrobe sequence.

**Page delta: +0.**

## 13 — E19: final home montage closes the clothing motif

In the existing safe-night home-motif pages, make the shared clothing presence concrete rather than generic.

A readable McLaren hoodie / cap can be present in the lived-in shared-space composition without anyone calling attention to it. The object that began as evidence from Mau's lost life is now simply part of the household visual grammar.

This does not resolve romance. It resolves **belonging**.

No extra page is required; use the existing E19 home-motif pages.

**Page delta: +0.**

# 4. Story effects / guardrails

The wardrobe audit intentionally adds only three page-bearing insertions. The remaining ten integrations reuse already-approved montage, domestic, separation, reconciliation and chocolate space.

This protects pacing while giving the wardrobe cumulative meaning.

Hard rules:

- clothing is not identity grounding;
- modern logos / team language may be a clue, not proof of cosmology;
- Yuta's recognition does not solve Mau's origin;
- the watch is not magical by default;
- the chocolate lowers inhibition but creates no feeling;
- Frieren wearing Mau's hoodie is not by itself a romance confirmation;
- the E14 and E18 hoodie is specifically the creator-approved orange/black McLaren hoodie;
- the E12 missing cap is specifically the creator-approved white McLaren cap;
- exact commercial visual reference/provenance remains handled through Continuum's Vault/corpus workflow rather than committing source pixels to Git.
