## Purpose

Lock the narrow opening-arc wardrobe direction now that the creator has supplied the first concrete garment references. This is intentionally a small, believable closet for visual production, not a complete Season 1 wardrobe catalogue.

The opening takes place in autumn moving toward early winter. Clothing should support travel, layering, repeated wear, dirt/damage continuity and later cold-weather variants.

Wardrobe remains separate from character identity.

---

# 1. Frieren — Wardrobe v1

Keep Frieren deliberately narrow for the opening arc.

## F1 — Default / classic travel look

- source-faithful recognizable default outfit;
- primary early-production wardrobe;
- do not redesign merely to create variety.

## F2 — Cold-weather / winter travel variant

- same core identity and recognizable silhouette;
- added cold-weather layering appropriate to late autumn / early winter;
- should feel like a practical extension of F1, not a separate fashion redesign.

## State continuity

Clean, lightly dirty, wet, travel-worn or mildly damaged states are states of the same outfit, not new outfits.

# 3. Mau — creator-grounded Wardrobe v1

Mau's wardrobe should feel contemporary, personal, practical and lived-in. It is grounded in the creator's real clothing habits: motorsport clothing, football jerseys, polos, hoodies, simple jeans, sneakers and casual headwear.

The wardrobe should make clear that motorsport / sportswear is a recurring part of how Mau dresses, not a one-off Easter egg.

The garments below are enough to begin production. Do not expand the opening closet without a story reason.

Creator reference supplied:

- two-tone orange / black McLaren hoodie;
- orange upper/chest/hood area;
- black lower body and sleeves;
- small McLaren chest branding;
- kangaroo pocket;
- casual, comfortable, visually recognizable outer layer.

Use as a strong candidate for Mau's principal casual / travel upper layer.

### M-S1 — Ferrari team button shirt

- team / sponsor visual language;

- may be worn open or partially open over a plain white T-shirt when the scene calls for a more relaxed look.

This is Mau's smart-casual / motorsport alternate rather than formalwear.

# 4. Opening outfit structure

Do not wear every branded piece simultaneously.

## M1 — Arrival / principal casual-travel look

Current preferred composition:

- McLaren hoodie;
- light-blue jeans;
- light sneakers with green accents;
- metal watch;
- backpack as carried story object;
- optional headwear only if composition/story benefits from it.

This should become the most immediately recognizable early Mau silhouette unless rough visual review exposes a stronger combination.

## M2 — Second change / smart-casual motorsport look

Current preferred composition:

- plain white tee base;
- red Ferrari team button shirt worn over it;
- same or similar jeans;
- same sneakers;
- watch if continuity permits;
- bucket hat or McLaren cap may be used selectively.

This gives Mau a believable second change without implying an implausibly large arrival closet.

## M3 — Jersey state / identity-object use

The personalized white `MAU T / 5` jersey is initially a backpack item and identity clue rather than necessarily the arrival outfit.

It can later be worn naturally as part of Mau's normal sportswear rotation.

Its importance is both personal and narrative:

- it helps establish the name `Mau` in E1;
- it visually communicates that Mau came from a contemporary sports culture;
- it can later become one of the objects another arrival recognizes as evidence of a shared or related world.

# 5. E1 → E2 garment-state continuity

A clothes change should be motivated by lived events rather than arbitrary costume rotation.

Preferred early progression:

- M1 begins comparatively clean;
- travel / survival / events make the clothes visibly dirtier or worn through E1 into E2;
- Mau can then switch to M2 or another item from the small carried pool for a practical reason.

Dirty, wet, torn and repaired states remain states of an existing garment, not separate identity designs.

---

# 6. Bomber jacket decision

The previously proposed bomber-jacket arc is **removed from Wardrobe v1**.

Do not currently implement:

- bomber intact;
- bomber torn by monster;
- Rimuru bomber repair / patch progression.

The concept may be revisited later, but it is no longer required for opening production and should not block W6 or character model work.

If a later story beat benefits from a repaired-garment memory object, it must be re-approved deliberately rather than assumed from v0.1.

---

# 7. Motorsport / sportswear as future world clue

Mau frequently wears motorsport and sports clothing. This is authentic wardrobe behavior first and potential plot evidence second.

Future seed:

- an arrival such as Yuta may recognize a team, sporting culture, logo language or contemporary garment as something connected to his own world;
- this can become circumstantial evidence that Mau originates from the same world or a closely related version;
- recognition should emerge naturally from an already-established wardrobe rather than from a conspicuous clue inserted solely for exposition.

Do not resolve the cosmology from clothing alone.

# 10. Production resolution principle

Rendering should resolve:

`CHARACTER IDENTITY`

+

`CURRENT / NARRATIVE-STAGE OUTFIT`

+

`GARMENT STATE`

+

`APPROVED CONTINUITY`

Clothing must never be baked into Mau's permanent identity representation merely because one outfit becomes visually iconic.
