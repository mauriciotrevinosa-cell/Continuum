<!-- scene-id: s3e7-ln02-cost -->

By noon, the search had consumed more than people.

Two wagons.

Four route maps.

Most of Rimuru's spare attention.

Yuta.

Frieren.

Okarun.

Mikasa.

Himmel.

Ori.

Momo.

Anko.

Qifrey for part of the morning.

Agott for longer than she expected.

A normal scouting team would have used a fraction of that.

The future site lost its main survey group.

A merchant carrying stone waited three hours because nobody authorized to change the unloading zone wanted to guess.

A northern verification rumor went unchecked.

A damaged bridge stayed damaged.

The temporary shelter leaked again.

Then a native family arrived asking whether the Otherworlders knew a route around a registration checkpoint.

Fern looked at the people she still had.

The search did not stop.

Neither did anything else.

<!-- scene-id: s3e7-ln02-fern -->

Fern handled the family.

Not Mau.

Not Rimuru.

Fern.

She listened.

The checkpoint was not arresting everyone.

It was recording names.

Asking origin.

Inspecting unusual equipment.

One traveler had been held.

Nobody knew why.

The family wanted another road.

Fern used the bridge report Mikasa had brought back days earlier.

She gave them the safer route.

Food.

Water.

No promise the route remained clear.

The family thanked her.

Then the mother asked:

"Is it true your leader is missing?"

Fern's expression cooled.

"We don't have one leader."

The woman looked embarrassed.

"Sorry."

Fern softened.

"Someone from our family is missing."

That answer traveled with the family when they left.

Later, somebody else would repeat it differently.

<!-- scene-id: s3e7-ln02-rimuru -->

Rimuru returned briefly to the inn because Raphael needed access to the records Mau had been keeping.

He found six new problems waiting.

Nano gave him the list.

Rimuru stared.

"I was gone four hours."

"Correct."

"This is too many."

"Also correct."

Rimuru looked north.

Then at the list.

For the first time he understood the future settlement problem in a new way.

A community could not function only when all its strongest people were home.

That was not resilience.

That was dependency with better walls.

He delegated.

Then went back out to find Mau.
