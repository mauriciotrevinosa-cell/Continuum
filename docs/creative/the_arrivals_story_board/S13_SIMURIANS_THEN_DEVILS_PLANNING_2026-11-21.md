## Direction so far

- **Order (Claude's proposal; Mauri seems to agree):** Simurians first, then the Devils as the closing arc. Yoru's ninety-day contract runs until B+487 and simmers underneath.
- **No breather:** the Devils hit right on top of the Simurians, so Mau has no time to sort anything out with Yuka. The world shakes him while his personal life feels like it's collapsing.
- **Dabura:** the 1v1 at the quarry; Mau wins and doesn't kill him (existing creator plan). Claude would like to write most of the fight.
- **Cross and Mau's jealousy:** depends on Cross and Yuka's dynamic in Modulo. They weren't a couple (Mauri). Fan summaries say:
  - Yuka confided her tumor and her six months in Cross and asked him to go to the doctor with her.
  - Thanks to her, Cross's heart began to waver.

  Verify before writing.
- **Mau's insecurity** tied back to something he did in S1: **pending, Mauri says which scene.**

## Claude's notes (proposals)

- **Where the jealousy comes from:** not "she likes someone else" but "he was there when she was dying, and I wasn't". In our story Yuka arrived already operated on (CANON_TRAPS), so Mau never saw that part of her life.
- **The collapse is internal, never a separation.** Mau and Yuka stay together and Yuka stays clingy (standing rules). Since Mau no longer hides things from her, what crumbles is his ability to name the insecurity while the world won't give him time, not the marriage. The danger is a relapse into "it's fine", and Yuka catches him.
- **Avoid misery fatigue:** small anchors of normality inside the pressure (the plate, Ori, the sailboat *Tomorrow* waiting) so the weight lands.

**Order**
- **"Mau's week" is shelved** (a possibility for later). Two volumes of calm is plenty.
- There's no real breather: Mau is never 100% calm because he's thinking about Yoru. Then the Simurians hit.
- **Yoru watches the Dabura fight.**
- After Mau gets his breath back, Yoru arrives announcing her plan to force him into a war alone.

**The breaking point** (replaces Claude's earlier version of "what Yuka says before the war")
- Yuka finds out Mau went to a dungeon without telling her, and with Makima. She's furious: she'd told him she'd go with him to every future dungeon (the quarry promise, V78 #33).
- Before Mau can say he didn't want to bother her because he saw her with Cross, she gets defensive and starts saying things, and brings up Makima.
- Makima puts on a scolded-puppy face and hides like Power, but this time **behind Mau**. That breaks Yuka. She says her comments and **leaves, furious, with Cross**.
- Mau's heart breaks even more, because now what he believed (the S1 instinct that stepping away is best for her) feels true. He goes to the war with Makima.
- **Makima takes Mau's hand like a handshake between friends,** not with fingers interlaced, so they don't lose each other. Keep this.

**Note:** this is a separation Mauri planned on purpose, as the low point of the arc. It's an explicit exception to the standing rule of never writing them apart. It isn't a breakup; the resolution brings them back together.

## Mau and Makima like Denji and Power (Mauri, 22 November 2026)

> haciendo makima y mau como algo similar de power y denji acorde afujimoto como besties o muy buenos amigos aunqeu fujimoto si los hizo parecer como mas que amigos pero si

- **The model for Mau and Makima is Denji and Power,** the way Fujimoto describes them: best friends, very close friends. Fujimoto made Denji and Power look like more than friends at times, and that ambiguity is part of the charm. For Mau and Makima it stays clearly friendship (Mau is a monogamous husband), even though it can look like more from the outside, which is what Yuka misreads.

> haciedno referencia a lo de power

- **The nod to Power, in scene:** when Power appears in Yoru's war, she sees Makima hiding behind Mau the way Power used to hide behind Denji, and takes offence: Makima is copying her move. A Makima who learned it watching Power, without ever having been on the other side, is the joke and the tenderness at once. (Claude's proposal for how to make the reference.)
