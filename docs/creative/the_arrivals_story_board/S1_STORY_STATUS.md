## Episode-by-episode

### S1E1 — Not This Time — 81 pages
Frieren loses Fern, searches, reaches Continuum, meets amnesiac Mau, and survives the first day with him. Flower-field magic calms Mau; the shared-blanket ending and Frieren's internal “Not this time” establish the emotional foundation. This is the gold-standard reference for Season 1 panelization philosophy.

### S1E2 — First Home — 52 pages
Frieren and Mau discover the tiny abandoned settlement and central inn, inspect the space, survive first snow/cold, begin making it usable, and establish the practical shared-room pattern. Final beat: Bocchi's scream. The current Spatial Bible governs village and inn geography.

### S1E3 — title pending — 60 pages
Bocchi arrives frightened and vulnerable. Mau tells her he will help. She learns that the people helping her are also hurting, especially through Frieren's grief. The household routine begins; Bocchi's “Who's helping you?” becomes a delayed emotional setup.

### S1E4 — title pending — 56 pages
Yuta arrives through a brief lake misunderstanding rather than a major fight. His young body plus later-life memories prove that source chronology/body state do not map cleanly onto Arrival state. Frieren's suppressed hope that Fern may still be alive begins here.

### S1E5 — Ordinary Days — 51 pages
Deliberate breathing episode. Hunting, repairs, cultivation, guitar, meals and practical teaching establish what the four-person household looks like when nothing extraordinary happens.

### S1E6 — Rimuru / Raphael / Mau Overload — 69 pages
Rimuru arrives. Raphael can analyze strange systems but fails to classify Mau; this is ontology failure, not a power-level reading. Mau quietly says “Raphael,” then experiences an internal overload and collapses. Recovery takes roughly two days in this episode's local event. Raphael cannot detect The Noise.

### S1E7 — Stories / The Lake — 68 pages
The household shares past-world stories. Mau realizes everyone else has a past and he does not. Yuta handles the lake conversation; Bocchi pays back Mau's earlier support with “We're here to help you too.” Frieren begins learning that caring for Mau does not mean she must carry everything alone.

### S1E8 — Trust / Building a Home — 75 pages
Rimuru/Frieren trust develops; the household begins materially transforming the inn. The approved overlay adds stronger construction choreography and the well-reclamation beat. The place begins to feel like a home through collective work.

### S1E9 — Making It Ours — 54 pages
Mau returns to the flower site and fails to reconstruct the magic. The story deliberately delays his power payoff. A real flower becomes a memory/home symbol. Frieren's casual borrowing of Mau's clothing begins as domestic attachment language, not formal romance.

### S1E10 — Four Strangers / G2 Arrival — 70 pages
Group 2 — Fern, Maomao, Momo and Anko — travels together after seeing evidence of people. Their route and days together matter. Fern detects Frieren near the end. Closing line: “Mistress Frieren. There you are.”

### S1E11 — There You Are — 78 pages
Frieren/Fern reunion. Frieren breaks composure because she had already mourned Fern. The merged household forms, but Mau gives Frieren/Fern space and begins moving his things. The separation arc starts through mutual attempts to respect each other that are misread as emotional distance.

### S1E12 — Rooms for Everyone — 71 pages
G2 physically integrates into the inn. Rooms and working spaces expand. Mau's “give them space” reasoning mutates into fear that he was temporary; Frieren interprets his independence as reduced need for her.

### S1E13 — Space — 62 pages
The separation hurts both sides. Mau's flower reconstruction still fails. A minor injury treated by Maomao becomes another misread by Frieren: not romantic jealousy so much as evidence, in her mind, that Mau may be fine without her. Hallway/door geography becomes part of the emotional storytelling.

### S1E14 — The Lake — 69 pages
Mau and Frieren finally realize they were solving different imagined problems. Maomao misunderstanding is cleared. Mau tells Frieren she is the most important person to him. No romance label is assigned. Frieren rests her head on him; Fern recognizes the intimacy. “Our room” / “…Yes” restores the shared domestic bond.

### S1E15 — A Birthday for Someone Who Never Had One — 63 pages
The household realizes Mau may never have had a remembered birthday and chooses one for him. Frieren keeps him away while the others prepare. The emotional hinge is not the party itself but the group's declaration that they are glad he exists.

### S1E16 — The World Opens — 77 pages
The cast reaches a functioning town/guild/economy and sees the larger world. “Otherworlder” is coined as a new, initially neutral descriptor rather than an established historical category. Mau gives Bocchi his own share of money so she can obtain a music-related item.

### S1E17 — Guild Jobs / Working Days — 72 pages
Several days of work establish the rhythm leave → work → return → eat. Pairings can rotate. Frieren gradually allows Fern more independence. The inn increasingly reads as a place people leave and return to.

### S1E18 — Day in the Life / Maomao Chocolate — 70 pages
Maomao's chocolate lowers inhibition; it does not create desire and is not a truth serum. Frieren becomes more openly affectionate toward Mau and approaches a kiss, but Fern stops the situation because Mau does not understand the romantic context. No kiss contact occurs. Final Level-4 close: Mau sincerely asks, “What's a kiss?”

### S1E19 — G3 / Road Days / Season Finale — 106 pages
One long intercut finale, not 19A/19B and not a twentieth episode. G3 appears in fixed travel clusters:
- Okarun + Umaru + Kita;
- Coco + Qifrey + Wakana + Marin;
- Mikasa initially alone.

Okarun knows an important person is missing from memory but cannot identify her. G3 does not reach G1+G2 during S1.

After the household's safe night, the story hard-cuts to midday Sukuna. He senses Yuta, says “So that brat is here,” uses Malevolent Shrine against a major native city, then realizes the world/context is wrong and suppresses cursed energy. No Yuta reaction occurs until S2E1.
