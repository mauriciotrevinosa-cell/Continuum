# 2. Durable episodeization rules

## 2.1 Story determines episode count

No fixed S2 count.

No S1 symmetry requirement.

No one-macro-node = one-episode rule.

Episode length may vary substantially.

LN chapters/sections do not need to map 1:1 to master-story episode units.

## 2.2 Quiet story is real story

A full episode may exist because of:
- ordinary life;
- relationship accumulation;
- routine;
- household culture;
- work;
- cooking;
- games;
- dates;
- study;
- craft;
- exploration;
- recovery;
- weather;
- simply spending time together.

A quiet episode does not need a measurable plot payoff.

Its value may only become visible several episodes later.

## 2.3 Episode endings do not close every thread

An episode may:
- open a thread;
- carry it forward;
- end while it remains unresolved;
- return to it later.

Future arrivals may appear in interludes several episodes before convergence.

## 2.4 Ensemble != attendance sheet

Not every resident appears every episode.

No equal screen-time quota.

Creator brainstorms with named characters are **not exhaustive cast lists**.

When someone appears forgotten, backtrace first:
- did they naturally belong earlier?
- was absence organic?
- did the scene accidentally narrow the social field?

If they belonged earlier, reinsert them there instead of compensating with a forced later spotlight.

The assistant/writer may:
- add a missing established character to a proposed setup;
- suggest a fresher pairing;
- split an idea across days;
- add parallel activity elsewhere;
- flag a neglected character;
- challenge a scene that collapses the ensemble.

Do this transparently, not by silently changing creator intent.

## 2.5 Network, not Mau + spokes

Mau has relational gravity, not narrative monopoly.

Relationships should overlap in household/group scenes.

People must have:
- goals;
- work;
- friendships;
- frictions;
- routines;
- relationships

that remain alive when Mau is off-page.

## 2.6 Time density follows material

A quiet episode may cover two or three ordinary days.

A mission may occupy an entire episode.

A week may be shown through fragments.

One evening may deserve a full unit.

Do not stretch or compress for numerical uniformity.
