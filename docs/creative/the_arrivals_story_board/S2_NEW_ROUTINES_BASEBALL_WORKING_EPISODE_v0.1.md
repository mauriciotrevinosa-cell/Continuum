The episode exists to create lived-space after Maki/Stark and before G4 convergence accelerates again.

## Core purpose

This is **not** "the baseball episode" for its own sake.

The episode should make the audience feel, without saying it aloud:

- Maki now lives at the inn;
- Stark now lives at the inn;
- G3 has become the resident group helping newer arrivals;
- Frieren and Maki have started building low-pressure trust;
- Stark is being absorbed into household culture;
- the inn already has its own running jokes, habits and history.

Baseball/improvised baseball is the current favored activity that carries those functions.

## Opening / new routines

A few ordinary days have passed since Maki/Stark arrived.

No explicit time-card is required.

Show changes through behavior.

Possible beats:
- Maki wakes early and assumes Yuta is going to train/patrol, only to discover he is helping with breakfast/household work;
- Yuta/Maki's completed-lifetime familiarity appears through tiny anticipatory exchanges rather than exposition;
- Stark already knows where supplies/tools live and is being called from room to room for ordinary help;
- G3 residents help Maki/Stark navigate the household naturally;
- Frieren/Maki can coexist in the same mundane activity without needing a feelings conversation.

## Baseball origin

Do not begin with a writerly declaration of:
> "Let's play baseball."

Let it grow from ordinary life:
- somebody finds/makes a ball;
- Momo/Marin/Umaru/Kita discuss modern sports;
- someone tries to explain baseball;
- others become curious;
- explanation turns into an actual attempt to play.

The first comedy/problem:
> making rules for a household where several people can physically or supernaturally ruin baseball.

Examples:
- no magic;
- no cursed-energy reinforcement;
- no transformation;
- no Turbo Granny powers;
- no obvious ability abuse.

Maki creates an immediate rules problem because her physicality is innate rather than CE-based.

Stark creates another because he is simply absurdly strong.

## Ball running gag

First ball:
- Stark hits/throws far too hard and destroys/loses it.

Then:
- Wakana reinforces/sews another;
- Qifrey/Coco and others become involved;
- each replacement ball becomes increasingly overengineered for a game that was supposed to be relaxing.

Do not overexplain the joke.

Teams should be socially mixed so the visual language says:

Exact teams remain open.

## Character functions

### Maki / Yuta
Use ordinary lifetime familiarity.

Small beats can show:
- Yuta knows when Maki is about to do something too competitive;
- Maki knows what Yuta is thinking before he says it;
- they bicker like people who have shared a life, not people restarting a romance.

### Frieren / Maki
No emotional diagnosis yet.

A small competitive or teasing interaction is more valuable.

Possible:
- Frieren initially refuses to play because she is reading;
- effortlessly catches/interferes with a stray ball;
- everyone calls foul because she used magic / she claims she is not playing;
- Maki tells her in meaning:
  > if you're going to criticize, play.
- Frieren closes the book and joins.

This can be one of the first genuinely casual Frieren/Maki bonds.

### Mikasa
Strong chance for Mikasa to treat the game too seriously at first.

Progression:
- objective/competition mindset;
- makes an error or gets dragged into nonsense;
- realizes losing/winning does not matter;
- genuinely has fun.

This supports her life beyond security duty.

### Bocchi / Kita
Use existing couple texture without turning episode into their romance milestone.

Possible:
- Bocchi unexpectedly connects well with one hit/play when she stops overthinking;
- immediately panics when everyone celebrates;
- Kita celebrates disproportionately.

### Stark
Strength is funny rather than heroic.

He repeatedly has to learn:
> maximum effort is not useful here.

He should also feel increasingly at home outside the game.

### G3
G3 now behaves like residents:
- explains household quirks;
- helps run the activity;
- complains about rules;
- includes Maki/Stark rather than being newcomers themselves.

## Ending

Do not force a winner/punchline.

By the end:
- rules have changed multiple times;
- balls have been replaced;
- nobody is fully sure of the score;
- people are tired/hungry;
- they naturally drift back to the inn.

Dinner/common-room aftermath:
- arguments about plays continue;
- pairings remain mixed;
- Maki has a natural place at the table;
- Stark is treated like anyone else;
- Frieren returns to reading;
- life continues.

### Stark callback

Favored quiet final gag:

Stark looks at his plate suspiciously before blinking, as a callback to the household breakfast gag where he can look away/blink and discover his food has somehow vanished.

Do not explain the callback.

The joke works because Stark has already learned the inn has its own absurd domestic dangers.

## Structural payoff

The next time Maki finds Hakari/Karane/Shizuka/Nano, she is no longer merely thinking:

> I should bring them to where Yuta is.

The audience can feel that she is thinking:

> I should bring them home.

That is the strongest justification for this episode.
