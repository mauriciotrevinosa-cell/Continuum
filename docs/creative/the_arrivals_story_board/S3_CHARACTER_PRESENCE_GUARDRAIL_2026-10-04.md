# The Arrivals — S3 Character Presence Guardrail — 2026-10-04

**Status:** ACTIVE CREATOR GUARDRAIL  
**Purpose:** prevent a large cast from disappearing whenever they are not carrying the macro plot.

## 0. Presence is not equal screen time

No character needs a dedicated chapter every volume.

A character can remain alive in the reader's mind through:
- breakfast;
- dinner;
- chores;
- arguments;
- entering/leaving a room;
- a recurring gag;
- helping another person's scene;
- background work;
- one line that reflects an existing relationship;
- being mentioned because someone is actually with them.

The failure mode is not unequal page count.

The failure mode is:
> a relationship mattered, then the story behaves as though it stopped existing because the macro plot moved on.

## 2. Core relationship locks

### Mau / Frieren
Do not let citybuilding convert them into administrators who happen to date.

They still need:
- touch;
- teasing;
- stolen clothes;
- ordinary dates;
- room arguments;
- shared silence;
- disagreements;
- separate friendships.

### Mau / Fern
Must exist independently of Frieren.

Fern can:
- correct Mau;
- ask him for help;
- care for him practically;
- refuse to mother him when he is using helplessness as an excuse.

### Mau / Bocchi
Older-brother-like protection + room for growth.

Do not write her progress as no longer needing him.

### Mau / Umaru / Coco
Younger-sister relationships can remain clingy / chaotic while still expanding outward.

### Mau / Yuta / Kaneki / Rimuru
Three different male-family lanes:
- Yuta = older-brother anchor;
- Kaneki = older-brother-like body/identity empathy and quiet reliability;
- Rimuru = brother/peer.

Do not collapse them into the same function.

## 3. G5 / Ring Two check

Every major G5 character does **not** need to love Mau.

But their S3 integration should answer some version of:

> What did they first think when everyone was searching for Mau?
> What did they learn by watching the person they already loved?
> What direct relationship, if any, did they then build with Mau / Frieren / the family?

They must not remain:
- "guest #5";
- "temporary resident #2";
- one source-reunion scene and then disappearance.

## 4. Ring Three check

Ring Three may disappear from the narrative.

That is allowed.

Some people:
- recover;
- travel;
- join another community;
- return to source life later;
- remain background residents.

Their temporary nature is part of the settlement's success.

Do not give every refugee a family arc.

## 5. Crisis check

During large crises, not everyone needs to be physically present.

But established family should register somewhere:
- field team;
- home waiting;
- supply prep;
- medical;
- route monitoring;
- care afterward.

"No crowd" should not accidentally read as "nobody cared."

## 6. Ordinary-life check

Before another major plot acceleration, ask:

> When was the last time the reader saw people live here without advancing the main mystery?

If the answer is too long:
add life.

Candidate reservoirs:
- movie night;
- lake day;
- meals in waves;
- laundry;
- curtains;
- room disputes;
- music;
- game night;
- shopping;
- old-inn maintenance;
- atelier day;
- workshop day;
- someone sleeping through something important;
- Mau getting dragged into somebody else's hobby;
- Frieren existing with people other than Mau;
- characters choosing not to participate.

## 7. Antagonist mirror

As family ties become denser, hostile observers may misread them more aggressively.

This is useful.

Examples:
- closeness -> command hierarchy;
- Searchers -> recruitment;
- Arrival House -> retention;
- shared meals -> indoctrination;
- Mau's influence -> rulership;
- Frieren protecting Mau -> enforcement;
- cross-source relationships -> organized network.

The story should show why the interpretation is wrong **without pretending the settlement has no power**.

## 8. Final rule

> Independent relationships make the family bigger.
>
> They do not require the roots to disappear.
