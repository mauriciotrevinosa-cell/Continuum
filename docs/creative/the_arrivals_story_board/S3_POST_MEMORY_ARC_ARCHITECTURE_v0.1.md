# The Arrivals — S3 Post-Memory Arc Architecture v0.1

**Status:** CREATIVE DEVELOPMENT / STRONG PROPOSALS — NOT SEASON LOCK  
**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Branch:** `m3/critical-path`  
**Scope:** story architecture beginning after the established S3 Memory Arc; exact episode count and season endpoint intentionally open.

## 0. Creative mandate

Season 3 should not be designed as:

> Memory Arc → another villain → another villain → finale.

It should feel like the first season where the household is large enough, experienced enough and rooted enough that the **world starts moving toward them as much as they move through it**.

The central evolution is:

```text
we found each other
→ we survived together
→ we chose where to stay
→ now other people can choose whether to come to us
```

S3 should widen The Arrivals without losing the domestic density learned from S1/S2.

The city is not the story.

The people making a city because they need somewhere to live are the story.

# 1. Existing anchor — Memory Arc

Do not redesign this arc from zero.

Current known spine:

- a hostile actor exploits damage already present in Mau's mind;
- the antagonist does **not** create The Noise;
- Mau receives/manipulates false memories;
- a false Frieren/Himmel memory becomes an important destabilizer;
- Mau increasingly cannot distinguish:
  - actual memory;
  - dream;
  - constructed memory;
  - present reality;
- this catalyzes a **second disappearance**;
- Rem, Kaneki and Aira become materially involved in finding/rescuing him;
- Raphael can help stabilize Mau but cannot cure The Noise;
- resolution must not make all mental damage vanish;
- Frieren does not prove herself by arguing that Himmel meant nothing;
- Frieren/Himmel's real history remains meaningful;
- Frieren proves present romantic choice through lived action;
- a date / intentionally ordinary time together is an important emotional recovery beat.

Memory Arc should leave consequences:

- Mau is more cautious about certainty inside his own head;
- household protocols around memory/dream anomalies become more mature;
- Frieren gains a new fear:
  > not only losing Mau physically, but losing access to him while he is still alive;
- Rem/Kaneki/Aira should not disappear after functioning as rescue tools;
- The Noise remains an unresolved long-form identity/origin problem.

# 2. Post-Memory Arc A — **The Move**

### Core purpose

The chosen S2 site finally becomes inhabited.

Not a construction montage.

A real move.

The old inn has become emotionally sacred enough that leaving it should feel like leaving a childhood home even though everyone knows the move is good.

### Story shape

```text
Memory Arc recovery
→ construction has continued around the crisis
→ first truly habitable cluster at chosen site
→ debate over who moves first / what must remain at inn
→ packing becomes emotional archaeology
→ staged move over multiple days
→ first night at new settlement
→ repeated trips back to old inn
→ old inn transitions from current home to origin-place
```

### High-value lived beats

- Mau packs the backpack he arrived with and realizes it barely contains anything from his current life anymore.
- Frieren steals / wears one of Mau's pieces during the move as if nothing about the location change alters that routine.
- Bocchi has to decide where the music room goes.
- Wakana's workshop becomes one of the first spaces designed around a resident rather than adapted from ruins.
- Maomao cares more about drainage/herbs/storage than room aesthetics.
- Rimuru discovers that seventeen-plus people all have different ideas of what "finished" means.
- Mikasa initially prioritizes defensive sightlines; someone else prioritizes kitchens, baths, light or privacy.
- Yuta/Maki carry the ease of people who have moved homes before.
- Frieren initially treats choosing rooms as unimportant, then becomes unexpectedly particular about where Mau's things go.

### Old inn rule

Do **not** demolish, abandon or emotionally discard it.

Strong direction:

> the inn becomes the first protected historical/home site of the growing Otherworlder settlement.

People still return there.

Later residents who never lived there can know it as:
> where the first family started.

This gives future callbacks physical geography.

# 3. Post-Memory Arc B — **People Who Heard About Us**

### Core purpose

For the first time, Arrival convergence is no longer mostly accidental.

Rumors of a safe Otherworlder settlement begin producing **intentional migration**.

This is a major world-phase shift.

### Important distinction

Not everyone who hears about the settlement comes.

Some:
- think it is a trap;
- distrust powerful Otherworlders;
- have built lives with natives elsewhere;
- are wanted criminals;
- are protecting someone;
- cannot travel;
- reject the idea of becoming part of a larger Otherworlder identity.

That makes migration a choice, not a magnet mechanic.

### Story units

1. First person arrives specifically because they heard the rumor.
2. Household realizes they now need an actual arrival/intake process.
3. Someone is turned away from immediate residence without being treated as an enemy.
4. Someone chooses to remain in a nearby native town instead of moving in.
5. The settlement becomes responsible for the consequences of its own reputation.

### Institution seed

This arc naturally creates an early form of:

> **Arrival House**

Not bureaucracy for bureaucracy's sake.

A place for:
- food;
- temporary beds;
- orientation;
- basic medical check;
- local maps;
- privacy/Contract explanation;
- finding relatives/friends;
- deciding whether a newcomer actually wants to stay.

This lets later arrivals enter without every new character sleeping in Mau/Frieren's hallway.

# 3A. Post-Memory Arc — **The World Turns Against Us**

### Status

**STRONG NEW CREATOR DIRECTION / exact mastermind, regions and rescue outcomes open.**

### Core purpose

The main settlement should become a refuge because the outside world creates a real need for one.

A long-game hostile intelligence / mastermind can weaponize the existing post-Sukuna fear of Otherworlders and convince multiple towns, officials or regional powers that Mau's growing settlement is not a refuge but the center of an emerging threat.

The lie works because it mixes:
- real Sukuna destruction;
- real powerful Otherworlders;
- distorted reports about the settlement;
- fear of a population gathering outside normal political control;
- selected true incidents stripped of context;
- deliberate misinformation.

Do not make every native community believe it.

The danger is fragmentation:
- some towns become openly hostile;
- some impose registration / travel limits;
- some remain neutral;
- some quietly help;
- some officials begin detaining Otherworlders "for public safety";
- some Otherworlders disappear into improvised prisons / holding sites before the main settlement even knows they exist.

### Story effect

This changes the role of the Searchers.

They are no longer only:
> explore / map / find arrivals.

They become capable of:
- tracking missing Otherworlders;
- confirming detention rumors;
- extracting people from illegal or abusive confinement;
- negotiating releases where possible;
- exposing false reports;
- escorting vulnerable arrivals to safer territory;
- deciding when rescue is justified and when intervention would create a war.

### Moral guardrail

Do not turn all native governments into villains.

Some detentions may come from genuine fear after catastrophic Arrival events.

Some officials may believe they are preventing another Sukuna.

Others may exploit that fear deliberately.

The settlement must defend people without adopting:
> anyone who opposes us is evil.

### Refuge payoff

The city/settlement's reputation evolves:

```text
rumor: Otherworlders live there
→ rumor: they protect their own
→ rumor: people disappear elsewhere but reach safety there
→ Otherworlders begin traveling toward it deliberately
→ natives who oppose collective punishment may also help people reach it
```

This is when "refuge" becomes a world-level identity rather than a household intention.

### Mastermind guardrail

Do not expose the mastermind too early.

The family should initially encounter:
- inconsistent policies;
- repeated identical phrases in different regions;
- forged/distorted stories;
- officials who cannot explain where their intelligence came from;
- a pattern that only becomes visible after several unrelated incidents.

The audience may understand there is coordination before the cast can prove it.

### Enemy/defector opportunity

This pressure can create future cases where:
- someone introduced as an antagonist later seeks refuge;
- a hostile Otherworlder cooperates for self-preservation;
- a native official breaks with their own government;
- a former enemy helps free detainees;
- someone the household rescues still refuses to join them.

Exact identities/outcomes should remain unrevealed until their arcs are developed.

# 4. Post-Memory Arc C — **The Searchers**

### Core purpose

Formalize exploration/reconnaissance as a social institution rather than "the fighters go scout."

The emotional motive:

> if rumors can bring people to us, then people who cannot reach us may still be out there.

### Functions

- route mapping;
- missing Arrival searches;
- monster-zone cataloging;
- escort;
- first contact;
- resource survey;
- native-settlement diplomacy;
- artifact/anomaly investigation;
- return routes to known Arrival sightings.

### Character opportunities

This is where **Rem / Kaneki / Aira** can become residents with functions beyond Memory Arc rescue.

Possible organic lanes:

- **Rem:** household/medical/logistics competence + deep sensitivity to dependency and devotion; useful in rescue work without becoming "Mau's rescuer forever."
- **Kaneki:** unusually strong first-contact candidate with frightened/isolated people because he understands being categorized as dangerous before being understood.
- **Aira:** social courage / bluntness / energy that can cut through paralysis in field groups; she should develop relationships beyond Mau and beyond the rescue trio.

They do not need to become a permanent three-person squad.

The arc should intentionally separate them after the rescue so each can prove they entered the story as a person.

### Mau guardrail

Mau does not need to lead every expedition.

After S2 and Memory Arc, one visible sign of growth should be:

> he can stay home while someone he loves goes searching.

That is harder for him than going himself.

# 5. Post-Memory Arc D — **Science Has Somewhere to Land**

### Favored new integration: Senku

Senku makes much more sense **now** than in S1/S2.

Why:

- a settlement exists;
- repeated material shortages exist;
- roads/logistics matter;
- sanitation matters;
- water systems matter;
- building materials matter;
- medical supply production matters;
- communication across scouting routes matters;
- there are enough specialists that scientific advances can diffuse instead of becoming "Senku solves civilization."

### Arrival principle

Do not introduce Senku as:
> genius arrives and upgrades everything.

Use the existing direction:

- Senku knows his cognition/capacity is not identical to his source peak;
- he notices the limitation;
- he tests;
- he rebuilds capability through actual experimentation in Continuum;
- local materials do not always behave exactly like expected;
- magic/supernatural systems create both shortcuts and invalid assumptions.

### Arc conflict

Science vs magic is **not** the conflict.

The conflict is:

> how do different knowledge systems become infrastructure without one invalidating the others?

Possible pairings:
- Senku + Maomao;
- Senku + Rimuru;
- Senku + Qifrey;
- Senku + Wakana;
- Senku + native craftspeople;
- Senku + Mau on Construction vs engineering;
- Senku frustrated by Mau because Mau can understand mechanisms without knowing why he knows them.

### Concrete gains

Keep gains grounded:
- cleaner water;
- better kilns/material processing;
- standardized measurements;
- improved storage/preservation;
- route signaling;
- workshop tooling;
- early communication experiments.

Do not jump to modern city technology instantly.

# 6. Post-Memory Arc E — **Two Communities**

### Core purpose

Avoid turning the main settlement into the only valid way to be an Otherworlder.

Recon teams discover / make contact with another cluster that chose differently.

Possible form:

- small hidden Otherworlder community integrated into a native village;
- mixed native/Arrival settlement;
- isolated fortified group;
- group that distrusts the growing "Otherworlder city."

### Conflict

Their objection should have merit:

> If every Otherworlder gathers in one place, you create a target and confirm the idea that we are a separate nation.

Main-settlement response also has merit:

> scattered vulnerable people are easier to exploit, disappear or kill.

No instant winner.

This becomes the first serious political disagreement **between Otherworlders who are not villains**.

### Payoff

The world evolves from:

`Otherworlders vs natives`

into something more mature:

```text
multiple native interests
+
multiple Otherworlder communities
+
mixed communities
+
dangerous independents
+
people who reject all categories
```

This makes later diplomacy/governance far richer.

# 7. Post-Memory Arc F — **A Person Who Does Not Want Home**

### Favored use: Eren, if/when introduced

Eren should not arrive as "Attack on Titan power event."

His best S3 function is thematic.

By the time he appears, the settlement has become obsessed with:
- safety;
- walls/perimeters;
- self-rule;
- protecting family;
- deciding what freedom means for a community.

That makes Eren extremely dangerous and valuable as a **person**, even before combat.

### Entry possibility

Eren arrives away from the settlement and deliberately avoids it after hearing rumors.

Why?

Because:
- a protected city of displaced people;
- walls/defense;
- leaders deciding safety;
- collective identity;
- fear of outsiders

all hit conceptual wounds for him.

Recon/search characters eventually encounter him.

### Arc question

> When does protection become a cage?

Do not let Eren simply deliver the answer.

Different residents should disagree.

This can force the settlement to define:
- right to leave;
- right to refuse Contracts;
- right not to register;
- limits of council authority;
- emergency powers;
- who controls gates/routes;
- whether security can ever override personal movement.

This is a governance arc disguised as a character arc.

### Mau/Eren

Potentially strong because Mau's instinct is also:
> protect everyone even at personal cost.

Eren can become a distorted mirror:
> freedom at catastrophic cost.

Neither should reduce the other to a lesson.

# 8. Post-Memory Arc G — **The First Generation That Arrived Here**

### Long-form social/cultural arc

At some point the settlement should stop feeling like a refugee camp with better buildings.

Culture starts forming.

Not "Japanese culture + fantasy town."

Actual Continuum culture.

Examples:
- mixed food;
- slang;
- borrowed clothing styles;
- celebrations;
- music;
- hybrid magic/craft techniques;
- games;
- marriage/relationship customs colliding;
- memorial practices;
- naming conventions;
- children/younger residents learning several source histories as stories rather than homes.

### High-value question

What does "home world" mean to someone whose strongest memories were formed **here**?

This is especially powerful for:
- Mau;
- younger arrivals;
- people with missing memories;
- people who arrived after death/after a completed lifetime.

### Memory Arc echo

After Mau was nearly destroyed by unreliable memory, this cultural arc can make the opposite statement:

> memory matters, but identity is also what people build together now.

That is one of the cleanest thematic payoffs available for S3.

# 9. Post-Memory Arc H — **The First Real External Partnership**

### Core purpose

The settlement cannot become autonomous by isolation.

Find one native polity/community willing to establish a meaningful formal relationship.

Not because they suddenly stop fearing Otherworlders.

Because cooperation becomes materially useful.

Possible needs:
- monster-route clearing;
- medical exchange;
- trade corridor;
- bridge/road construction;
- missing-person rescue;
- shared defense;
- access to a port.

### Story value

This forces the household to represent itself externally.

Who speaks for them?

Mau?
Rimuru?
A council delegate?
A native ally?
Several people?

The answer should become:
> no single protagonist permanently owns the voice of the settlement.

This is where early governance begins feeling real.

A strong current braid could be:

```text
S2 finale
→ chosen-site construction / first settlement work
→ MEMORY ARC begins inside unfinished construction
→ Memory Arc rescue / recovery / Frieren date
→ THE MOVE
→ several ordinary weeks of learning the new settlement
→ PEOPLE WHO HEARD ABOUT US / Arrival House
→ THE SEARCHERS formalize
→ one or two rescue / first-contact stories
→ SCIENCE HAS SOMEWHERE TO LAND (Senku)
→ infrastructure/life advances gradually
→ TWO COMMUNITIES
→ first serious inter-Otherworlder political disagreement
→ optional EREN / A PERSON WHO DOES NOT WANT HOME
→ governance/freedom pressure
→ FIRST REAL EXTERNAL PARTNERSHIP

```

This is **not** a season endpoint.

Any of these can:
- move;
- split;
- merge;
- become S4 material;
- remain reservoir.

Do not force all of them into S3.

# 11. What should NOT happen immediately after Memory Arc

Avoid:

- another mind-control villain right away;
- another Mau disappearance immediately;
- Frieren becoming permanently hyper-controlling;
- Rem/Kaneki/Aira vanishing after rescue;
- instant completed city;
- Senku creating modernity in three episodes;
- every Arrival choosing the main settlement;
- every new character needing a Mau relationship first;
- a city council existing at full sophistication before lived need creates it;
- another gigantic battle simply because S2 ended with Sukuna;
- curing The Noise because Memory Arc "addressed it."

S3 needs **growth pressure**, not escalation addiction.

---

# 12. Strongest current post-Memory priorities

If only three post-Memory arcs survive, favor:

1. **The Move** — emotional/physical transition from inn to chosen future.
2. **People Who Heard About Us / The Searchers** — changes the Arrival premise from coincidence to civilization.
3. **Science Has Somewhere to Land** — lets S3 visibly accelerate without turning into generic kingdom-building.

The other arcs can grow naturally from those.

---

# 13. Character-integration principle going forward

A new character earns entry when at least one is true:

- an existing resident is emotionally incomplete without them;
- their worldview creates a conflict the settlement is now ready to face;
- their skills can now materially change daily life;
- they expose a blind spot in an institution;
- they open a part of the world that current characters cannot;
- their arrival creates a meaningful relationship network, not only a protagonist connection.

Never add someone solely because:
> their franchise has not appeared enough.

The Arrivals imports people, not quotas.
