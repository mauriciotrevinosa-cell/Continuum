# The Arrivals — S3 Relationship & Household Canon Lock — 2026-10-04

**Status:** ACTIVE CREATOR LOCK  
**Scope:** post-Memory S3 revision, G5 integration, The Move, settlement lived space, S4 handoff  
**Priority:** creator relationship intent > prior post-Memory implementation > chapter-count convenience

## 0. Why this lock exists

The Memory Arc is broadly working and should not be flattened or shortened. The problem begins mainly after it: S3 sometimes treats independence as distance, lets source-franchise circles harden into separate nuclei, turns G5 into an administrative resident list, advances too quickly between macro beats, and allows established people to disappear when they are not carrying the plot.

That is not the intended heart of The Arrivals.

> The story is not about several franchise households that happen to share a city.
>
> It is about a family that began around Mau and Frieren, kept absorbing new relationships without erasing old ones, and eventually needed a settlement because the home had become too large for one ordinary inn.

The relationship web may grow beyond Mau. Growth does not require erasing the roots.

## 1. Mau and Frieren are allowed to be the relational sun

Mau is the central relational gravity of the story. This does not mean everyone must love him immediately, every friendship routes through him forever, every chapter needs him present, or other relationships are less real.

It means many lives cross because Mau helped create the conditions in which this household could exist. Those people then build independent bonds that make the family denser rather than making Mau irrelevant.

Frieren is not merely "Mau's girlfriend." Mau and Frieren form the central domestic pair. Their emotional gravity overlaps:
- people Mau loves gradually enter Frieren's emotional world;
- people Frieren loves gradually enter Mau's;
- both copy / inherit one another's family habits;
- the family can orbit them without losing agency.

Core thematic truth:

> Mau has almost no usable past. Nearly everyone else does.
>
> Their answer is not to abandon that past.
>
> Their actions show him that having a past does not prevent choosing him in their future.

This must feed directly into the Trial / Return-Stay question.

## 2. What the rings mean

### Ring One — family

Ring One means family-level belonging, not "original cast" and not "must be beside Mau in every scene."

People can need privacy, partners, hobbies, source-world relationships, travel and independent routines without leaving the family.

### Ring Two — deeply close / relationally connected

Ring Two is not "guest forever" or a lesser caste.

It includes people who often arrive through someone already in the family, initially know Mau through the people who love him, and need time to learn why he matters. They may become extremely close without family language, or cross into Ring One later.

The intended G5 progression is:

> "Why is everyone tearing the region apart for this Mau person?"

becoming, at different speeds:

> "Oh. I understand now."

### Ring Three — society / refuge / temporary residents

Ring Three proves the settlement is more than a private household:
- refugees;
- travelers;
- temporary residents;
- Searcher rescues;
- people waiting for another route;
- people who deliberately choose another community.

Their right to shelter does not depend on loving Mau.

Do not flatten family, community and temporary refuge into one administrative resident list.

## 3. Locked relationship intent

### Mau
Central relational nucleus. Not a sovereign. His recurring blind spot is failing to believe people with real pasts could freely choose him as part of their future. His growth must include accepting closeness when other people choose it.

### Frieren
Mau's girlfriend and co-center of the household. Quiet, blunt, selective and herself. Her bond with Mau should connect them more deeply to the family, not isolate them from it.

### Fern
Frieren's adopted-daughter bond remains real, while Fern often becomes the practical/maternal one. She can treat Mau similarly when he behaves like a child. Mau/Fern need a family bond independent of Frieren.

### Bocchi / Hitori Gotoh
Older-brother-like trust toward Mau. He protects and helps her without preventing growth. Kita/Kessoku expand Bocchi's life; they do not replace her Continuum family. Privacy and sound control must not be written as emotional distance.

Younger-sister relationship with Mau. He supports her interests, protects her and joins ridiculous adventures without demanding repayment. Her growth expands outward through the family rather than requiring distance from Mau.

## 4. G5's dramatic job

G5 is not random refugees, an administrative resident list, a franchise checklist, or a replacement cast.

They first learn Mau through the hole his absence leaves:
- Frieren deteriorating;
- Bocchi worried;
- Ori trying to function;
- Yuta/Rimuru carrying responsibility;
- people reaching for Mau's help and remembering he is gone;
- chores and habits that reveal what Mau normally does.

After Mau returns, different G5 members build different direct relationships with him. Some cross into family quickly. Some remain close Ring Two. Some need longer.

## 5. The household must not fragment into franchise enclaves

Do not design:
- Mau/Frieren house;
- Witch Hat house;
- Kessoku house;
- Dandadan house;
- JJK house;
- Tensura house.

Preferred physical logic:

> one expanding family-house / mega-inn core  
> + specialized attached or nearby spaces  
> + broader community / Arrival House outside the intimate core

Possible attached/nearby spaces:
- Kessoku/music room;
- Wakana workshop;
- Witch Hat atelier;
- medical/chemical storage;
- Senku engineering area;
- kitchen/dining;
- common room;
- baths/wash;
- storage;
- guest overflow.

The spaces should feel like one organism, not a return to franchise silos.

## 6. S3 construction ceiling

S3 builds the **first habitable essential cluster**, not the final mega-inn or finished city.

S3 may reach:
- site survey;
- water testing / access;
- drainage testing and basic working drainage;
- sanitation / latrines;
- partial roofs;
- first enclosed sleeping spaces;
- common room;
- basic medical/storage;
- some finished floors;
- basic route/warning systems;
- enough utilities for staged occupancy.

S3 does **not** need to finish:
- final family-house shape;
- every permanent bedroom;
- permanent atelier;
- final workshop complex;
- final clinic;
- mature Arrival House;
- final civic district;
- final defense architecture;
- finished city.

The main building is still being defined by use. S4 can build deliberately from S3 occupancy evidence.

## 7. Locked Mau/Frieren room-layout beat

Mau and Frieren initially choose/request a quiet corner.

Then everyone else starts choosing nearby rooms — not because Mau assigns them there, but because they want to be close.

Ori wants nearby.
Umaru wants nearby.
Bocchi wants acoustic privacy, not emotional distance.
Coco wants to be able to appear easily.
Fern wants practical access.
Others make similar choices for their own reasons.

Mau and Frieren look at the plan and realize, in substance:

> "I don't think we're winning this battle."

The solution is not pushing everyone away.

Frieren wants an east-facing window. The family clustering around the chosen corner compromises the original plan, so the wing/building is **rotated / reoriented** instead. Frieren keeps her east-facing window and the people who chose nearby rooms remain nearby.

Core construction rule:

> architecture bends around relationships.

Not:

> relationships are separated to preserve an efficient plan.

## 8. The mega-inn is an evolution

The eventual family core should feel like the old inn's emotional logic scaled beyond anyone's plan.

A sensible residential cluster slowly mutates because people actually live there:
- sound insulation;
- workshop;
- safer chemical storage;
- books;
- larger table;
- covered paths;
- better views;
- more rooms;
- someone building too close to Frieren's window and discovering this was a mistake.

"planned family residence" can eventually become:

> "How did we accidentally build another enormous inn?"

That is good.

## 9. Old inn remains origin-home

The old inn is not discarded. It remains origin-home, emotional/historical site and useful satellite. Moving forward does not erase where the family formed.

## 10. Ordinary life is structural, not filler

Post-Memory S3 requires breathing room:
- movie nights;
- nothing-days;
- breakfast/dinner waves;
- lake time;
- ordinary Mau/Frieren dates;
- common-room lounging;
- chores;
- bathroom arguments;
- clothes/room arguments;
- Marin forcing rest;
- Anko teasing/writing/observing;
- Momo feeding people;
- Bocchi playing;
- Umaru dragging Mau into nonsense;
- Coco dragging Mau into Witch Hat life;
- characters entering scenes that are not "their arc";
- callbacks proving earlier relationships remain alive.

Favored image:

> Mau sits on the couch. Frieren lies with her head on his lap. Umaru falls asleep nearby. Bocchi gradually joins. Mau talks with Anko and Fern. Rimuru ends up on Frieren's stomach. Nothing world-changing happens.

That is evidence for why later danger and Return/Stay matter.

## 11. Antagonistic growth must run in parallel

The protagonists are not the only side allowed to grow.

As the household builds something loving, hostile actors can build the opposite interpretation:

Family-house growth -> "an anomalous concentration around an unknown central figure."  
Arrival House -> "recruitment."  
Searchers -> "teams collecting more Otherworlders."  
Defenses -> "fortification."  
Mau avoiding office -> "a leader pretending not to be leader."

Sukuna also remains active/persistent. S3 should seed that hostile momentum so S4 escalation feels earned rather than newly invented.

## 12. Revision rule

From this lock forward:

1. Preserve the Memory Arc's strongest lived material.
2. Add aftermath/family presence where crisis cast temporarily eclipsed established relationships.
3. Rebuild The Move around chosen closeness and unfinished essentials.
4. Separate family / Ring Two / Ring Three clearly.
5. Reintroduce ordinary-life cadence between macro escalations.
6. Restore recurring presence for Anko, Marin, Momo/Okarun, Maki/Frieren, Seiko, Nazuna, the 100 Girlfriends group and other established relationships.
7. Let G5 learn Mau rather than merely receive status labels.
8. Seed enemy narrative growth and Sukuna persistence in parallel.
9. Never pay for new material by compressing existing lived scenes.
10. Chapter count is allowed to grow.
