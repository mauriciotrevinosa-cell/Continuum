## 0. Core architectural rule

The settlement grows outward from a family home.

It does **not** replace the family home with a collection of franchise-specific houses.

Preferred long-term image:

> one increasingly ridiculous shared family-house / mega-inn  
> + attached or nearby specialist spaces  
> + broader public/refuge/civic settlement around it

Architecture bends around relationships.

Relationships are not reorganized to make an efficient site plan look clean.

## 2. Orientation lock

Cardinal orientation matters.

### East
Mau/Frieren's room keeps an **east-facing window**.

This survives the room-clustering conflict by changing the building plan, not by moving everyone who wants to be near them away.

Locked lived beat:
- Mau/Frieren tentatively choose a quiet corner;
- Ori, Coco, Umaru and others independently choose nearby rooms;
- Bocchi chooses privacy/acoustic buffering but explicitly does **not** want to be far;
- Mau realizes the quiet corner is disappearing;
- Mau/Frieren: in substance, **"I don't think we're winning this battle."**
- rather than separating the family, the unfinished residential wing is rotated / reoriented;
- Frieren keeps her eastern morning light;
- family proximity remains.

### Center
Shared common room + family courtyard are the emotional center.

The center should support:
- people passing through without invitation;
- lounging;
- meals in waves;
- movie nights;
- music spilling in;
- Fern being able to find people;
- Mau/Frieren being accessible without their bedroom becoming public space.

### Quieter residential edge
Bocchi's sleeping room sits on the quieter side of the family cluster.

Her music room / storage / acoustic buffer helps separate sound from sleep.

Rule:
> acoustic privacy is not emotional exile.

### Work / noise edge
Wakana's heavy workshop and Senku's louder work areas sit farther from sleeping rooms.

They remain easily reachable from the family core.

### Medical / chemical edge
Maomao/Kusuri medical and chemical storage stays close enough for emergencies but separated from:
- kitchen food storage;
- children's casual traffic;
- uncontrolled heat / workshop hazards.

### Witch Hat / atelier direction
The future atelier should be attached / near the family-house but maintain:
- safe spell geometry;
- maintenance access;
- enough separation for experiments;
- direct walkable connection to common family life.

### Arrival House direction
Arrival House is **not inside the intimate family bedroom core**.

It should sit toward a main approach / intake route:
- easy for newcomers to find;
- easy to leave;
- private enough for frightened arrivals;
- connected to medical / food / records;
- close enough for family help;
- clearly distinct from "you arrived, therefore you live with Mau."

This physical distinction supports Ring Three autonomy.

## 4. Mega-inn evolution

The long-term family-house should feel accidental even when later additions are planned.

S3:
- first cluster;
- people discover what they actually need.

Early S4:
- permanent family wing begins around real occupancy data;
- east window preserved;
- covered connectors improve;
- common room expands;
- more permanent bedrooms;
- sound / smell / privacy problems corrected.

Later S4+:
- workshop and atelier become mature attached / nearby structures;
- clinic becomes real;
- common room / dining may expand again;
- more courtyards;
- multiple entrances;
- guest rooms;
- family rooms added because people keep staying.

Running joke / emotional truth:

> "How did we build another inn?"

Because the old inn's **social logic** survived even when the building did not scale.

## 5. What not to do

Do not create:
- JJK house;
- Witch Hat house;
- Dandadan house;
- Kessoku house;
- Tensura house;
- 100 Girlfriends house.

Source-world relationships remain roots, not zoning categories.

Do not use architecture to prove character independence.

Do not isolate Ori, Bocchi, Umaru or Coco merely because Mau must respect agency.

Do not treat a private room as emotional distance.

Do not turn the family core into Arrival House.

Do not finish the whole settlement in S3.

## 6. Visual-continuity requirement

Future concept art / diagrams should ideally show four stages:

1. **Bare Ground** — survey stakes, water tests, drainage lines, temporary shelters.
2. **First Habitable Cluster** — S3 Move stage; common room, temporary sleeping blocks, sanitation, provisional specialist rooms.
3. **End S3** — visibly lived but unfinished; crowded family courtyard, east-window wing line, provisional Arrival House outside the core, foundations/stakes for later growth.
4. **S4 Mega-Inn Direction** — larger connected family-house with courtyards and attached specialist spaces, still organic rather than palace-like.

The art must not depict S3 as a finished fantasy city.

## 10. Creator layout pass — 2026-10-05: the Muslin, then the real house

People live in it, it shows where it doesn't fit, and it gets altered. The real house is drawn from that evidence at the end of S3 and built in S4.

- Wakana names it ("you sew it in cheap cloth first, see where it pulls, then cut the real thing"). Marin insists on "toile". "The Muslin" is the name that sticks (V14 "Finished Means What?").

- Wakana marks problems with chalk "hooks" on a board in the common room. The board is the season's running record of what doesn't fit.

### 10.3 The real house (drawn V18, built S4+)
- **Two floors.** Downstairs: common room, kitchen with bell and speaking tube built in, dining, music room, atelier, Wakana's workshop at the noisy end, and Maomao's medical room at the far end from the kitchen. Upstairs: bedrooms in **group sections**. Walls between people who want to be together are designed to come out. Walls for quiet are thick.
- **East section.** Mau and Frieren at the east end: east window, built-in library bay, reading chair, inner-corner closet with Rimuru's loft above. Ori through one wall. Bocchi through the other, with a private stair down to the music room. Milim at the end, with a window toward Rimuru's office. Yuta and Maki across the corridor. **The shelf is not drawn:** "We'll argue about it when there's a wall to put it against."
- **Other rooms.**
  - The big room for the five, with one thick outer wall.
  - The Witch Hat room with a built-in alcove for Agott, which has a door and the star curtain.
  - Qifrey, measured exactly between his students and the east section.
  - Umaru and Suika next to the Witch Hat room, with **Umaru's Door, Mark Two**: a covered landing bridge, eleven steps.
  - Anko and Nazuna on the dark north side, with the high loft.
  - Fern at the top of the main stair, with Stark beside her.
  - Himmel facing the road.
  - Mikasa with two exits.
  - Senku beside his downstairs workroom, with a door Fern can lock from outside.
  - **REM at the turn, in pencil.**
  - **One unnamed room**, added by Mau, for "somebody who arrives and doesn't stay a stranger". It is distinct from Arrival House, which stays for strangers.
- **Cellar = maintenance floor** under the whole house: every pipe, drain, glyph line and beam stays walkable and checkable. Aira: "Nothing goes under this house that we can't walk to." This is a direct response to the suppression device under the central refuge.
- **Two exits under every section** (Mikasa). Each section has a stair down to the cellar, and from the cellar one tunnel runs toward the refuge and one away from it. Eren: "You're building exits before you've built rooms." / "Good."
- **The house goes around the tree's root circle**, not on it.
- **Growth.** The east end has room to extend, and a dotted third storey is marked "Someday." / "Not yet."

- Rooms come in group sections, and walls come out for people who choose each other.
- Nobody knocks on Mau and Frieren's door after dark unless something is on fire or someone is dying (Fern's rule). That is why Rimuru sleeps there.
- Arrival House stays outside the family core. The unnamed family room is not intake.
- The shelf's placement is never resolved.
