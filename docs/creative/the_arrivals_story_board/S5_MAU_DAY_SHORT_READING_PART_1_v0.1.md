Frieren had taken his hand in her sleep and put it under her cheek.

That was the first difficulty.

The second was that she had also taken most of the blanket, although the morning coming through the east window was already warm. Mau lay still and considered what could be done about either problem without waking her.

Nothing, probably.

There was a little crease between her eyebrows. A strand of hair had caught against her mouth. He moved it with his free hand, and the crease disappeared.

"You're awake," she said.

"You're pretending."

"I'm resting."

"With my hand."

She adjusted her cheek on it.

"It's a good hand."

He looked at the ceiling for a moment, trying not to laugh enough to move her.

From the other side of the adjoining door came a soft knock, then a second knock much lower down, as though something had bumped the wood.

"You can come in," Mau said.

Ori appeared in the doorway with a red ribbon tied around her wrist.

The other end was tied loosely around Milim's ankle.

Milim floated behind her, asleep on her back, one arm trailing toward the floor. The ribbon had caught on the door handle. Ori stopped, went back, lifted it off, and came in again. Milim revolved very slowly.

Frieren opened one eye.

"She was going the other way," Ori explained.

"Where?" Mau asked.

"Toward the window."

"Good catch."

Ori looked pleased. She brought her floating passenger to the side of the bed and sat down, leaving enough slack that Milim drifted above the footboard.

"Are we going to breakfast?"

"As soon as she gives my hand back."

"You can have it," Frieren said.

She did not move.

Ori leaned over her and nudged Mau's shoulder. "I need you to decide something."

"That sounds serious."

"Milim says the person who wakes up first gets to choose what we do. But she doesn't wake up."

"That sounds like you've already won."

"She says floating counts as being up."

Frieren shut her eye again. "It doesn't."

"Thank you," said Ori, with the satisfaction of someone who had brought an appeal to the proper authority.

Milim made a small sound in her sleep.

Mau raised his voice a little. "Someone might finish the breakfast before we get there."

Her eyes opened.

For a moment she looked at all three of them upside down. Then she rolled over in the air, put her feet down, and discovered the ribbon.

"Oh. Good morning."

"You were escaping," Ori said.

"I was asleep."

"Both," said Mau.

Frieren finally released his hand. There was a warm mark on his palm from her cheek.

He sat up. Ori began untying the ribbon, and Milim waited, unusually patient, while Frieren found the end that had tightened against itself. The four of them bent over the knot as if it were the morning's first important undertaking.

By the time it was undone, Mau had lost the blanket altogether.

* * *

Rimuru was halfway through asking somebody to pass the bread when Milim caught him.

He had chosen his slime shape that morning. Milim scooped him from the bench and pressed him to her cheek.

"Rimuru!"

"I was using that seat."

"Good morning!"

"Apparently."

Mau took the bread that had been left suspended between two people and put it in reach of Rimuru's new position.

"There. Now you can still participate."

"You're enjoying this."

"A little."

"Brotherhood is dead."

"Morning, Oppa."

Yuka had saved a place with one hand and was eating with the other. She moved her cup as Mau sat down. Frieren took the place on his other side, and Ori went around to sit beside Milim before she could carry Rimuru away from the table altogether.

"You're late," Yuka said.

"There was a knot."

"That's a terrible excuse."

"It was a complicated knot."

Yuka glanced past him. Frieren was tearing a piece of bread in half without the slightest interest in defending the explanation.

"Cards after lunch," Yuka said. "Umaru says you're playing."

"Does Umaru know I'm playing?"

"It was her idea."

"Then why are you asking?"

"I'm telling you."

Across the table, Umaru raised her cup in confirmation.

"And no letting Milim win. She knows when you do it."

"Of course I know," Milim said. "He gets the same face."

"What face?"

She attempted an expression of enormous, gentle stupidity.

Rimuru started laughing. It made her hands wobble.

"All right," Mau said. "I deserve that."

Yuka slid him the jam before he could ask for it. Then she began explaining a disagreement about the rules which, from Umaru's immediate objection, had begun well before breakfast.

Mau listened. When he tried to settle it, both of them told him he had misunderstood.

Frieren put the other half of her bread on his plate.

"Eat first," she said.

He did.

Farther down, Momo was arguing with Okarun about whose turn it had been to fetch something. Someone laughed in the kitchen. A chair scraped, and another person squeezed in without requiring everyone to move. The table made room in small increments.

Mau reached for the jam and discovered Yuka had already taken it back.

"You were slow," she said.

* * *

Shoko's first question was how he had slept.

Mau sat on the edge of the examination bed and thought about the trapped hand, the ribbon, the long interval between hearing Ori and finally getting up.

"Pretty well."

She looked at him, then wrote it down.

"That sounded almost unqualified."

"I can try again."

"Don't."

She took his wrist. Her fingers were cool, her attention exact. The window stood open enough to let the air move through the clinic. On the desk, a page had been turned face down under a cup.

"Any dizziness?"

"No."

"Pain?"

"No."

"Anything you're planning to call insignificant until I ask the same question a different way?"

He considered this.

"Gojo's waiting outside."

"Unfortunately, that's chronic."

Mau laughed. Shoko kept her fingers on his wrist until he had finished.

She checked the things she always checked, asked him to follow her finger, and made him repeat an answer when he gave it too quickly. When she was satisfied, she let go.

"The short practice you agreed on. Not the short practice Satoru will describe afterward."

"I heard that," Gojo called from outside.

"Good. Saves me explaining twice."

Mau pulled his sleeve down. "What are you doing after this?"

"Finishing these. Then going out."

"Where?"

"I haven't decided. That's part of the appeal."

He smiled. "Sounds nice."

"I thought so."

She reached past him for the next sheet on the desk. He took that for the dismissal it was and got off the bed.

At the door he almost collided with Gojo, who had been leaning on the frame.

"How am I?" Gojo asked Shoko.

"In the way."

"Excellent. Stable condition."

Mau followed him out before he could ask for a second opinion.

* * *

Gojo put three small stones on the ground.

"That's the whole lesson?"

"Do you want more stones?"

"I'm trying to understand the budget."

"Walk through. Don't look down."

Mau looked at the stones, then at him.

Gojo smiled.

"You already did. Start again."

They worked in the shade, far enough from the door that nobody needed to duck around them. No techniques. No attempt to force his body into something it was not ready to hold. Gojo changed where he stood, lifted a hand, asked Mau a question at exactly the point where the answer made him forget his feet.

The second stone caught his heel.

"Again," said Gojo.

"You moved it."

"I moved me. You followed."

Mau went back. This time he kept his eyes up and shortened the step without thinking about it.

Gojo tapped his shoulder as he passed.

"Better."

After a while the three stones became an absurdly difficult arrangement for three stones. Mau stopped trying to anticipate what Gojo wanted and began to notice what he was actually doing. The small shift of weight. The shoulder that lied. The foot that didn't.

He got through once.

"There," Gojo said.

Mau turned, pleased, and kicked the last stone on his way back.

Gojo bent over laughing.

"You could've waited," Mau said.

"I really couldn't."

They sat down when the agreed time was up. Gojo sat first, which made it easier for Mau to follow without either of them having to announce anything about stopping.

For a few minutes they watched a procession of people going about things neither of them was responsible for. Coco passed with Tetia and a sheet she was protecting from the wind. Qifrey, coming after them, told them to walk rather than run. They slowed until they were out of sight.

Gojo held out his water.

Mau took a drink and passed it back.

"Doing anything later?" he asked.

"Several extremely impressive things."

"Such as?"

Gojo watched the doorway to the clinic. "Undecided."

Mau looked at him.

"Don't," Gojo said.

"I didn't say anything."

"You have an apprentice face. It's insufferable."

Mau smiled into his own shoulder.

They stayed until Gojo had finished the water.
