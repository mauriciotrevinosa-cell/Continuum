# The Arrivals — Story Constitution

**Status:** DURABLE CREATIVE RULE LAYER  
**Date:** 2026-09-27  
**Scope:** cross-season story architecture, pacing, ensemble use, lived-space and authority hierarchy.

This file collects rules that were previously scattered across Story Principles, Creative Direction, lived-space inventories, ensemble audits and creator refinements.

It is intentionally different from episode/status summaries, continuity inventories, audits, decision history and open questions.

Those files describe **what the story currently contains or how it evolved**.

This file describes **how The Arrivals should be written unless the creator explicitly changes a rule later**.

---

# 1. Authority hierarchy

When documents disagree, use this order:

1. **latest explicit creator decision**;
2. **this Story Constitution / other explicit durable rule documents**;
3. current locked production source for the specific episode/scene, if not superseded;
4. current story-status / continuity inventory / major-arc summary;
5. audits and planning passes;
6. decision history as provenance;
7. floating ideas / open questions / brainstorm reservoirs.

Core rule:

> **History is evidence, not law.**

A stale Story Status, Inventory or Decision History sentence does not defeat a later creator correction.

Branch age is not canon level.

A later correction should normally replace only the semantic node it actually changes; compatible surrounding material remains.

---

# 2. Story determines episode count

Do **not** choose a season/arc episode quota first and then stretch/compress the story to fit it.

Preferred method:

```text
define the story blocks
→ determine what actually needs room
→ give required emotional/plot material enough episodes
→ add development/adventure/ordinary-life episodes where they improve the story
→ derive the final episode count
```

Rules:
- no fixed episode-count target merely for symmetry;
- no requirement that S2 resemble S1 in episode count;
- an arc that naturally needs three episodes gets three;
- an arc that lands in half an episode does not need artificial obstacles;
- episode length/page count may vary when the story benefits.

> **Story quality > page quotas.**

---

# 3. Not every episode must advance the main plot

An episode may matter because it changes:
- a relationship;
- routine;
- trust;
- a household role;
- craft/technology;
- training;
- recovery;
- culture;
- a social problem;
- an institution;
- emotional understanding;
- the audience's sense of home.

Quiet material is not filler when people or the world change.

Full episodes may center on food, building/repair, dates, conversations, games, chores, recovery, exploring, study, work, family or simply processing what already happened.

---

# 4. Not every character appears in every episode

The cast is an ensemble, not a mandatory attendance sheet.

Rules:
- no requirement that every resident appear every episode;
- no requirement for equal screen time;
- a source protagonist may be low-focus for long stretches;
- Mau can be absent while other characters carry a story;
- people may be alive in the household without receiving a foreground beat;
- do not add a cameo solely to prove someone still exists.

The correct audit question is:

> **Across enough story time, does each established character still feel alive and connected to the social mesh?**

If someone is being forgotten structurally, fix the underlying relationship/story reservoir. Do **not** compensate with a forced later cameo.

---

# 5. Do not manufacture pairings for coverage

Not every pair needs chemistry immediately.

Do not create a scene solely because:
> these two names have not interacted yet.

Cross-group relationships should arise from chores, rooms, work, interests, conflict, travel, recovery, shared friends/family or believable circumstance.

Some relationships can remain light or nonexistent.

---

# 6. The ensemble must not collapse into Mau

Mau is important, but Continuum must remain a relationship network.

Rules:
- other people build relationships that do not depend on Mau;
- couples/families/friendships continue when Mau is off-screen;
- other characters solve problems without waiting for Mau;
- discoveries do not have to belong to the protagonist;
- major household systems should increasingly function without Mau's physical presence.

A strong ensemble sometimes requires **deliberately letting Mau be absent**.

---

# 7. Lived-space is required, not decorative

Major arrivals, reunions and crises need ordinary life around them.

The plot can bring people together.

> **Small moments make them decide to stay together.**

Use meals, sleeping arrangements, cleanup, errands, repeated jokes, shared work, recovery, awkward conversations, hobbies, quiet nights, failed plans and casual affection.

A relationship payoff is not earned merely because its plot prerequisites occurred.

The audience needs to have **lived with the relationship**.

---

# 8. Growth must not become instant personality replacement

After a major emotional lesson:
- characters may improve;
- they do not become perfectly healed;
- old habits can persist in weaker/new forms;
- maturity should often be demonstrated later under pressure.

Example principle:
> Ori can genuinely accept chosen family in S2 without becoming permanently insecurity-free in one conversation.

Likewise, Mau can learn not to hide catastrophic secrets while remaining impulsive when someone needs help.

---

# 9. Consequences need breathing room

Do not treat:
```text
event
→ one conversation
→ everyone is fine
```
as emotional closure unless the event genuinely warrants that speed.

After major trauma, rejection, reunion or sacrifice:
- let behavior change across ordinary scenes;
- allow anger and affection to coexist;
- let embarrassment/awkwardness remain;
- allow recovery to take visible time.

Breathing room is not synonymous with many episodes.

Use exactly as much as the story needs.

---

# 10. Do not escalate after the story has already landed

A season does not need another threat or spectacle just because runtime remains.

Once the major emotional/plot work is complete:
- allow the story to exhale;
- end on ordinary life when ordinary life is the payoff;
- do not manufacture a villain, expedition, council or construction obstacle to make the finale "bigger."

Current S2 application:
> **compact after Ori, not compact Ori.**

Ori receives as much lived-space as necessary.

After her foundational arc resolves, do not add a long new tail.

---

# 11. Current S2 endpoint guardrail

The future-home candidate is discovered and verified during the Ori-era background thread.

The household's site choice should be complete or effectively complete before the Mau/Frieren formalization payoff.

Mau/Frieren formalization is the **last major S2 payoff**.

After it:

```text
celebration dinner
→ sleep
→ one quiet final day at the inn
→ ordinary family life / rest / light preparation
→ Ori may still be a little embarrassed or awkward while clearly remaining family
→ chosen-site map / unfinished future-home plan on the table
→ END S2
```

Do not place after the formalization:
- another major site-choice arc;
- a new threat;
- a surprise climax;
- an expedition;
- a dramatic departure montage;
- arrival at the new site;
- first construction.

Those would lengthen S2 without improving its completed arc and would steal runway from S3.

S3 owns the actual move, surveying, terrain preparation, foundations, infrastructure and early settlement construction.

---

# 12. Ordinary endings are allowed to be endings

The finale does not need to announce itself.

A cup covering part of a map, somebody sleeping late, Ori avoiding eye contact for a second, Frieren reading beside Mau, people arguing about breakfast or doodling the wrong-scale building on the new plan can carry more emotional truth than a final speech.

The story may end a season because:
> **the characters have reached a place where the next thing genuinely belongs to the next season.**

---

# 13. Character/source integrity over mechanical franchise coverage

Characters are not collectible power packages.

Rules:
- preserve recognizable personality and emotional grammar;
- growth can change behavior without erasing identity;
- slice-of-life characters do not need combat powers to matter;
- a fighter does not need to fight every time they appear;
- a romance mechanic from one source does not automatically transfer;
- nobody automatically falls in love with Mau;
- source importance does not guarantee Continuum screen-time priority.

Use what serves The Arrivals while respecting the person being adapted.

---

# 14. Planning documents remain editable

A status label such as STRONG DIRECTION, MACRO RESOLVED, APPROVED ROUGH, LEVEL 4 or LOCKED describes confidence/scope at that moment.

It does **not** prevent the creator from changing direction later.

When a later correction happens:
1. preserve the new decision;
2. propagate it to current authoritative summaries;
3. record the supersession when useful;
4. do not erase compatible older work unnecessarily.

---

# 15. The series is open-ended; seasons are chapters of one life

Creator rule, 2026-10-06.

- **No fixed number of seasons and no predetermined ending.** The creator watches how each season ends and lets that decide where the next one goes. The seasons themselves tell us where the story is heading, and whether it is closing.
- **Everything stays connected.** No season should feel like a new show. The same people, places and consequences carry forward.
- **Each season starts exactly where the previous one ended** and first shows its ramifications: consequences, reactions, aftermath. Only then does it move into what the new season builds. S2 opened on S1's ending, and S3 opened on S2's before its memory crisis began. S4 opens on the Trial's aftermath. (Creator clarification, 2026-10-06: this is the "respiro", meaning the aftermath before the new build, not a calm reset.)
- **Every new season does three things:**
  - it keeps continuity with everything before it;
  - it **expands what already exists** (characters, places, systems, unresolved seeds) before inventing replacements;
  - it brings in new things on top of that, not instead of it.
- **Ideas are captured in git the moment they come,** from either the creator or Claude. Chat is not storage.
  - Direction goes into the relevant story-board document.
  - Unplaced ideas go into `FLOATING_IDEAS.md`.
  - Decisions go into `DECISION_HISTORY.md`.
- **An ending is allowed when the story reaches it, and not before.** No season is written as a finale "just in case".

---

# 16. Final test

Before forcing an episode, scene or character into the story, ask:

> **Does the story actually need this, or are we satisfying a document/template/coverage expectation?**

If the answer is only the second:

> **do not force it.**
