# The Arrivals — Light Novel Style Bible v0.1

**Status:** PROVISIONAL STYLE BIBLE — CREATOR REVIEW REQUIRED BEFORE FULL ADAPTATION  
**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Branch:** `m3/critical-path`

## Purpose

Define the prose language for adapting `The Arrivals` from its manga-first story corpus into English light-novel form.

This Bible controls **presentation**, not story authority.

Story precedence remains:
1. latest explicit creator decision;
2. Story Constitution / durable rules;
3. current authoritative episode/story source;
4. current audit/continuity;
5. manga staging as evidence.

---

# 1. Base prose mode

## Language
Canonical drafting language:
> **English**

Dialogue should remain natural English rather than literal anime-subtitle English.

## Tense
Recommended:
> **past tense**

Reason:
- flexible for ensemble narration;
- natural for reflective Frieren material;
- handles time skips and remembered material cleanly;
- avoids forcing manga-present-tense captions into prose.

## Person / POV
Default:
> **close third person, chosen scene by scene according to what the story needs**

This is **not** a Mau/Frieren-locked novel.

The ensemble should gain POV access organically whenever another character gives the scene the strongest:
- emotional angle;
- comedy;
- mystery;
- information limit;
- thematic contrast.

One scene should still have one primary interior anchor.

Allowed:
- Frieren POV;
- Mau POV;
- Bocchi POV;
- Yuta POV;
- Rimuru POV;
- Fern/Momo/Maomao/Anko/G3 POVs when useful;
- brief exterior/cinematic passages;
- scene break → new POV.

Avoid:
> omniscient head-hopping inside the same beat.

The goal is flexible POV, not chaotic POV.

The reader may understand both sides of a misunderstanding because consecutive scenes use different anchors.

The characters should not gain that knowledge.

---

# 2. Narrative distance

Default distance:
> close enough to feel what the POV character notices, restrained enough not to explain them better than they understand themselves.

The narrator should often use free-indirect style.

Example principle:

Bad:
> Frieren was jealous because she feared Mau no longer needed her.

Better:
> Mau's books were gone from the corner. Frieren looked at the empty space longer than the missing books deserved.

The second version gives evidence.

---

# 3. The anti-overexplanation rule

The manga corpus was built around silence and acting.

Do not convert silent panels into therapy-language narration.

Avoid routine phrases such as:
- `she realized that...`;
- `he understood that...`;
- `she felt jealous because...`;
- `this made him realize...`;
- `deep down...`

Use them only when the character genuinely reaches a conscious conclusion.

Prefer:
- body;
- objects;
- room geography;
- sensory detail;
- action;
- interruption;
- failed attempts;
- short thought fragments.

Core rule:

> **Prose evidence before prose explanation.**

---

# 4. Interior thought

Primary method:
> free indirect discourse.

Use italicized direct thoughts sparingly.

Do not italicize every internal reaction.

Direct thought is strongest when:
- wording itself matters;
- the character cannot say it aloud;
- comedy requires a sharp internal contrast;
- a decision crystallizes.

Bocchi may use more direct thought than Frieren.

Frieren should generally use less explicit self-analysis.

---

# 5. Dialogue

Existing manga Voice Checks are the **baseline**, not the final LN voice lock.

Approved lines should be preserved when they still work, but prose adaptation may legitimately:
- expand;
- trim;
- re-time;
- or rephrase

when the manga version depended on visual acting/panel timing.

Do not rewrite simply to sound "more literary."

Do adapt when prose needs additional language to convey what the manga previously carried visually.

Every change must preserve the speaker's established character voice.

Dialogue rules:
- contractions where natural;
- sentence length belongs to the speaker;
- pauses are allowed;
- characters may leave thoughts unfinished;
- overlap/interruption can be described rather than giving everyone a perfect turn;
- exposition should emerge from need, not author convenience.

No universal dialogue polish that makes everyone equally witty.

## Final prose dialogue format

The screenplay/source-draft convention:

```text
**CHARACTER**
Dialogue
```

does **not** survive into finished LN prose.

Use standard prose dialogue integrated with action, attribution and interiority.

The manga drafts remain source evidence for wording, cadence and behavior; they are not a formatting template for the novel.

Authoring Markdown may retain stable scene markers/headings for Continuum parsing and hashing, but those markers are production structure, not necessarily printed chapter subheads.

---

# 6. Speech translation / language barrier

Continuum's translation effect preserves:
- actual voice;
- cadence;
- speech habits;
- cultural terminology where meaningful.

The English LN represents understood meaning.

Do not repeatedly remind the reader that translation is occurring.

Mention the phenomenon only when:
- a term has no clean equivalent;
- an accent/cadence matters;
- wordplay fails;
- someone notices a speech habit;
- cultural terminology matters to the scene.

Do not homogenize every character into the same neutral voice.

---

# 7. Address forms / honorifics

Do not add Japanese honorifics globally as decoration.

Preserve address forms that matter to characterization/source behavior.

Example:
- Fern's `Mistress Frieren` remains meaningful.

Other source-specific terms may remain when:
- the character would actually use them;
- the term has relational value;
- English replacement would erase identity.

Consistency over fan-translation flavor.

---

# 8. Character essence preservation

The LN adaptation must not normalize the cast into one shared prose personality.

For every character, preserve:
- dialogue cadence;
- emotional range;
- humor style;
- social reflexes;
- blind spots;
- what they notice first;
- what they avoid naming;
- how they behave when comfortable;
- how they behave under pressure.

Medium adaptation may expand expression, but it must not replace characterization.

Rule:

> **Expand from the character's essence, not around it.**

A character who is awkward should remain awkward in their own specific way.
A restrained character should not become eloquent because prose allows more words.
A direct character should not become literary.
A comedic character should not become a permanent gag machine.

---

# 9. Character-specific narration rules

## Frieren POV

Narration should notice:
- time;
- repetition;
- magic;
- objects connected to people;
- small behavioral changes;
- absences.

Do not give her modern psychological vocabulary she has not earned.

S1 forbidden shortcut:
> Frieren consciously naming Mau as romantic love.

She can notice:
- wanting him nearby;
- irritation at distance;
- relief;
- fear of replacement;
- habit;
- importance.

Let the category remain unresolved.

## Mau POV

Narration should feel:
- observant;
- curious;
- literal without being simplistic;
- structurally attentive;
- occasionally amused as he learns people.

Missing personal memory appears as holes in context.

Never narrate him as intellectually childlike.

His competence should become more visible over S1.

## Bocchi POV

Allowed more:
- social overprediction;
- absurd imagined consequences;
- compressed panic;
- self-consciousness.

But maintain a human emotional floor.

Do not make every paragraph a gag.

When she feels safe, prose should quiet down.

## Yuta POV

Calm surface, older-lived weight underneath.

Useful contrasts:
- young hands/body;
- old memories;
- protective habits.

Avoid constant tragic introspection.

## Rimuru POV

Fast practical assessment + social adaptability.

Comedy can come from the gap between competence and absurd household reality.

## Raphael

Raphael's language is exact, bounded and evidence-driven.

Never invent certainty for drama.

Hard S1 rule:
> Raphael cannot detect The Noise.

## Fern POV

Practical observation.

Blunt emotional logic.

Care is often expressed by correcting, organizing or staying.

## Maomao POV

Clinical curiosity can be sharper than social concern.

Do not soften every unusual thought to reassure the reader.

## Momo POV

Fast social read, direct reactions, kinetic attention.

Do not equate energy with lack of depth.

## Anko POV

Adult awareness, language/social pattern sensitivity, participation in household rather than commentary-only role.

---

# 10. The Noise

The Noise is not conventional dialogue.

In manuscript Markdown, render brief fragments distinctly, currently favored as inline/display code:

`...unk...`

`...known...`

Rules:
- no speaker label;
- no quotation marks;
- no explanatory voice attribution;
- fragments remain incomplete until story progression earns clarity;
- Mau may experience them as not-quite-sound/not-quite-thought.

Do not narrate hidden meaning the reader has not earned.

Final ebook typography can later replace code styling with a dedicated text style.

---

# 11. Raphael / internal entities

Raphael is an intelligible internal voice, unlike The Noise.

Use normal dialogue semantics with narration establishing that only Rimuru hears it.

Do not use the same typography as The Noise.

If frequent back-and-forth becomes visually confusing, a later manuscript style may use italics or a dedicated internal-voice style, but semantic distinction is mandatory:

> Raphael = coherent speaker.  
> Noise = fragmented unknown intrusion.

---

# 12. Comedy

Comedy should usually end one beat earlier than temptation suggests.

Do not explain the joke after it lands.

Use:
- reaction;
- timing;
- callback;
- interrupted seriousness;
- mundane consequences.

Running gags should reward memory.

Do not force a final punchline into every scene.

Bocchi visual-deformation comedy from manga should become:
- compressed hyperbole;
- POV panic;
- physical behavior;
- sharp narration.

Do not attempt to literally describe every chibi distortion.

---

# 13. Romance / intimacy

The Arrivals relationship writing depends on slow category recognition.

Rules:
- physical comfort is not automatically romance;
- shared room is not automatically sex/romance;
- jealousy-like behavior need not be correctly named by the character;
- narration must not promote later conclusions early.

For Frieren/Mau S1:
> intimacy is real; romantic categorization remains unresolved.

E18:
- chocolate lowers inhibition;
- it does not create or reveal a magical "truth";
- avoid deep Frieren POV that explicitly labels desire;
- no kiss contact.

Later S2:
- Frieren is the first to consciously cross the romantic threshold;
- Mau learns the category later.

---

# 14. Grief

Do not make grief eloquent by default.

Characters may:
- repeat;
- stop;
- misremember;
- sit silently;
- become practical;
- avoid looking at an object;
- cling to routine.

Frieren especially should not deliver polished grief essays.

Mau comforts through presence before he develops sophisticated language for it.

---

# 15. Action

Priority order:
1. geography;
2. intention;
3. consequence;
4. technique detail.

Do not write fights as ability wiki entries.

Describe what the POV can actually perceive.

Named techniques remain when:
- canonically spoken/invoked;
- recognition matters.

Unknown power:
> effect first, explanation later.

E4 Yuta/Frieren:
- brief, readable, not showcase.

E19 Sukuna:
- catastrophic and incomprehensible;
- no indulgent gore;
- architecture/absence/silence carry scale.

---

# 16. Worldbuilding

Worldbuilding should arrive through:
- work;
- shopping;
- maps;
- weather;
- distances;
- money;
- food;
- local assumptions;
- character confusion.

Avoid lore dumps before a character needs the information.

The mercantile town must feel inhabited independently of the protagonists.

`Otherworlder` begins neutral.

Do not narratively foreshadow its later stigma too loudly in E16.

---

# 17. Home / spatial writing

The inn is a recurring character-space.

Track:
- hearth;
- common table;
- stairs;
- rooms;
- corridor;
- windows;
- well;
- field;
- lake path;
- nearby ruined houses;
- hill/tree;
- reclaimed materials.

Objects should migrate over time.

The reader should feel home changing without needing repeated inventory paragraphs.

Use spatial callbacks in E11–E14:
- empty corner;
- wrong doorway;
- extra cup;
- absent cap/hoodie;
- footsteps stopping;
- room habits.

---

# 18. Time passage

Avoid title-card prose like:
> Two weeks passed.

unless simplicity genuinely serves.

Prefer selective anchors:
- snow depth;
- stacked firewood;
- cleaner window;
- changed sleeping habit;
- new repaired corner;
- repeated breakfast;
- body healing;
- route familiarity.

Then use a concise time sentence where needed.

The E2 expansion should feel lived, not montage-only.

---

# 19. Chapter openings and endings

A chapter may open on:
- action;
- ordinary task;
- sensory change;
- dialogue;
- a different POV.

Avoid formula.

Chapter endings can be quiet.

Strong ending types for this series:
- object;
- line;
- unanswered question;
- someone choosing to stay;
- room changing meaning;
- arrival signal;
- hard tonal cut.

Do not manufacture cliffhangers.

---

# 20. Scene breaks

Use a clean scene break when changing:
- POV;
- substantial time;
- location;
- narrative thread.

Working manuscript convention:
> `* * *`

Do not change POV in the middle of a paragraph/beat.

---

# 21. Prose density

Default:
> clean, readable, sensory, emotionally precise.

Avoid:
- purple prose;
- endless adjective stacks;
- screenplay fragments pretending to be prose;
- light-novel cliché narration;
- constant rhetorical questions;
- overuse of em dashes/ellipses as substitute for rhythm.

Short fragments are allowed when the character/scene earns them.

---

# 22. Description hierarchy

Spend detail where it creates:
- orientation;
- atmosphere;
- character;
- callback;
- emotional evidence.

Do not describe every outfit/object every time.

First appearance and changed states deserve more detail.

Repeated home spaces should rely on accumulated reader memory.

---

# 23. Canon vs narration

Narration cannot create canon accidentally.

Before writing a sentence that defines:
- motive;
- romance;
- power mechanics;
- hidden knowledge;
- metaphysics;

ask:
> Does the POV actually know this, and is this conclusion already story-authorized?

If not, describe evidence instead.

---

# 24. LN-specific adaptation rule for visual motifs

Manga motif:
> repeat the same panel/composition.

LN equivalent:
> repeat a sensory/object/spatial phrase with variation.

Examples:
- same corner, now empty;
- same route, now familiar;
- same bowl/chair, now occupied;
- same hoodie, different wearer;
- same flower, different emotional meaning.

Do not repeat full paragraphs verbatim unless deliberately stylized.

---

# 25. Chapter length

No chapter-size equality.

A chapter ends when its dramatic movement is complete.

Short chapters are allowed before/after large ones.

Do not pad:
- quiet material;
- comedy;
- action;
- emotional aftermath

to meet a word target.

---

# 26. Adaptation QC checklist

Before marking an LN chapter ready:

### Story
- same authorized event/order?
- any accidental new canon?

### POV
- one clear anchor per scene?
- knowledge limited correctly?

### Voice
- dialogue preserves approved cadence?
- narrator does not overwrite character voice?

### Pacing
- enough room for reaction?
- any manga silence converted into unnecessary explanation?
- any visual-only beat missing prose evidence?

### Continuity
- rooms/objects/weather/body state correct?
- callback retained?
- later payoff not spoiled?

### Format
- no panel/page/camera language left?
- no manga production instruction inside finished prose?

---

# 27. LN Voice Check / calibration gate

Do not make the creator reread complete chapters simply to verify character voice.

Use short **character calibration pairs**.

For each major character as they enter the LN:
- Sample A: ordinary/comedic/social register;
- Sample B: serious/emotional/interior register.

Use actual canon scenes or directly adapted slices rather than unrelated test fiction.

Each sample should test:
- spoken cadence;
- internality;
- narrator distance;
- what the character notices;
- what the character would never articulate directly;
- how manga-only acting becomes prose.

Initial order:
1. Frieren;
2. Mau;
3. Bocchi;
4. Yuta;
5. Rimuru + Raphael;
6. G2 characters as E10/E11 approaches;
7. remaining ensemble/G3 voices when their POV becomes useful.

A creator GREEN LIGHT means:
> that character's LN voice is trusted as the working baseline.

After green light, continue drafting at speed and reopen voice only when:
- a new emotional register exposes a problem;
- the prose drifts;
- or creator feedback changes the direction.

This Bible remains provisional until the first core voice set is approved.
