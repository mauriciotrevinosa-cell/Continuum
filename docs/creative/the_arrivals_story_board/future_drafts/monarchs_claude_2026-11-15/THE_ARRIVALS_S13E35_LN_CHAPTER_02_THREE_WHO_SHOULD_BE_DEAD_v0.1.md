# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 28 — Three Who Should Be Dead

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 28
**Source episode:** S13E35
**Continuity state:** B+326. The Rulers come to the house in person. Monarchs from Jin-Woo's world are being pulled through the convergence by someone they cannot yet name; at least three are already through. Jin-Woo killed them once. He goes very quiet. Mau's first instinct is to stand in front; Yuka sees it. He promises to tell her when he goes, as in the war exception. Jin-Woo says it's his mess; Mau says Jin-Woo's family lives here now. They agree to train together and keep the anchors as they are.

*When: Four days after the gate with Yuka. Mid-morning. Three figures are standing at the edge of the orchard when Muster's sentries report them, and nobody saw them arrive.*

The Rulers don't knock.

They never have. They're simply there: three tall figures at the edge of the orchard, robed in something that doesn't move in the wind, with faces that are hard to look at directly. The last time they came this close to the house, they were taking the Architect out of a dungeon alive.

Mau goes out to meet them.

He doesn't decide to. He's on the porch with a cup of Shuna's hot chocolate, and then he's crossing the yard, and the cup is on the porch rail behind him, and Yuka's hand is closing on his sleeve too late. Beru is behind him. Igris. He didn't call them. They just came.

He stops ten paces from the three.

“You don't come here,” he says. ᴹ

His voice is very quiet. He didn't plan to say that, either.

“We know,” says the one in the middle. ᴸ¹

* * *

They tell it quickly.

They tell it standing in the orchard, while the house gathers on the porch behind Mau, because he doesn't invite them in and they don't ask. Rimuru comes out. Gojo. Frieren. Jin-Woo, last, coming up the path from the Sungs' house at a walk that isn't quite a walk.

“Something is drawing them,” says the one on the left. “Through the convergence. From his world.” ᴸ²

It looks at Jin-Woo.

Jin-Woo stops walking.

“Drawing who?” he says. ᴶʷ

“The Monarchs,” says the one in the middle. ᴸ¹

* * *

The yard goes very still.

Mau knows the word. He's heard Jin-Woo say it, a handful of times, on the porch steps, in the voice he uses for things he'd rather not remember. *Monarchs.* Kings of other things. Kings of beasts and plague and frost and dragons and giants. Kings that Jin-Woo fought one by one, a long time ago, in a world that was ending, and killed. All of them.

“They're dead,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“They were,” says the one on the right. ᴸ³

“I killed them.” ᴶʷ

“In your world, you did. The convergence doesn't care about your world. It reaches back. It reaches *across*. Something has learned to reach for them, specifically. We don't know what. We don't know who.” ᴸ³

“How many?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“Three, that we know of,” says the one in the middle. “Already through. Somewhere close. We felt them come.” ᴸ¹

“Which three?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶʷ

The Rulers look at each other.

“Fangs,” says the one in the middle. “Transfiguration. Plague.” ᴸ¹

Jin-Woo doesn't move.

Mau has never seen him like this. Jin-Woo is always so still, so calm, so completely in control; he's the one person in the house who's never once looked frightened. He doesn't look frightened now. He looks like something has walked up behind him that he buried a long time ago and thought he'd never see again.

“Why here?” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“Because you're here,” says the one on the left. ᴸ²

And then it looks at Mau.

“And because *he* is,” it says. “The one Ashborn chose a second time. They'll know about him. They'll want to know what he is.” ᴸ²

* * *

Mau feels it happen.

He feels it before he understands it, the way he always does. Something in him going hard and cold and very, very clear. His shoulders settling. His weight shifting forward onto the balls of his feet. Something in the back of him turning to face a direction it can't see yet.

*They're coming here. To this house. Where Ori sleeps.*

*Then I'll be in front.*

It isn't a decision. It's older than decisions. It's the thing that made him stand in front of everyone else for four hundred and thirty-seven deaths, in a place nobody saw. It's the thing that made him stay in a room with a bed for a year rather than let anyone else walk through the door. It's the thing that made him tell Gojo to tell Yuka she didn't have to be at the door.

*Nobody else in front. Not while I'm standing.*

He doesn't say any of it. He doesn't move. He just stands there in the orchard, ten paces from the Rulers, with his weight on the balls of his feet.

And behind him, on the porch, Yuka sees it.

* * *

He feels her before he hears her. Her hand on his back. Flat. Between his shoulder blades.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't pull. She just puts her hand there and leaves it.

He knows what it means. He's known her long enough. It means *I see you*. It means *I know what you just decided*. It means *don't*.

Or not *don't*. Not exactly. She's never once told him not to go. It means *not without telling me*.

He breathes out.

* * *

The Rulers leave the way they came. They're there, and then they aren't. Nobody sees them go.

“They could have stayed for tea,” Gojo says, into the silence. ᴳ

Nobody laughs.

They go inside. All of them. They sit around the big kitchen table, the whole house, and Jin-Woo tells them about the Monarchs, as much as he can, as much as he's willing to. Fangs: Rakan, the king of beasts, fast and savage and proud, who hates anything that walks on two legs. Plague: Querehsha, queen of insects, who fights with swarms and poison and doesn't care what dies. Transfiguration: Yogumunt, the oldest and the cleverest, who doesn't fight if he can scheme, and who wears faces that aren't his.

“Yogumunt will plan,” Jin-Woo says. “He always plans. He'll watch first. He'll want to know where we are and where we aren't. He'll want to know who matters to us.” ᴶʷ

Hae-In is at the table, beside him. Her hand is on his arm.

“And then?” Rimuru asks. ᴿ

“And then he'll try to split us,” Jin-Woo says. “It's what he did before. He'll try to make me go one way and whoever's strongest go another. He'll try to make sure we're never in the same place when they come.” ᴶʷ

He looks at Mau.

“He'll try to split *us*,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

“It's mine,” Jin-Woo says, later.

They're on the porch, just the two of them, in the evening. Everyone else has gone to bed, or pretended to. Jin-Woo is standing at the rail, looking out at the dark in the direction of his house.

“This is my world's mess,” he says. “My war. I killed them once. If they're back, I'll kill them again. You don't have to—” ᴶʷ

“Your family lives here now,” Mau says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo stops.

“Suho's got a soldier in his shadow called Sir Shadow,” Mau says. “Your mother asked me if it hurt. Your sister's going to take Fern's job if Fern isn't careful. They live *here*. In this valley. Down that path.” ᴹ

He looks at Jin-Woo.

“It's not your world's mess,” he says. “It's ours.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo doesn't say anything for a long time.

“You'll want to be in front,” he says finally. “When they come.” ᴶʷ

It isn't a question. Mau doesn't pretend it is.

“Yeah,” he says. ᴹ

“I saw it,” Jin-Woo says. “In the orchard. When they said they'd want to know what you are. I saw you decide.” ᴶʷ

“Yuka saw it too.” ᴹ

“I know. I saw her see it.” ᴶʷ

Jin-Woo turns from the rail. He looks at Mau for a long moment, with those calm dark eyes that have seen the end of a world.

“I know what that is,” he says. “What you just did. Deciding to be in front. I did it for years. I did it until I was the only one left in front of anything.” ᴶʷ

He pauses.

“Don't,” he says. “Or don't *alone*. Whatever this is, we do it together. You don't go off and fight three Monarchs by yourself because you think you're protecting me. I don't go off and fight them by myself because I think it's my mess. Together. Agreed?” ᴶʷ

Mau looks at him.

He wants to say yes. He means to say yes. He knows Jin-Woo is right.

And he knows, the way he knows the third stair creaks, that if one of them comes for Suho, or Hae-In, or Ori, or Yuka, he won't wait for anyone.

“Agreed,” he says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him a moment longer. Mau doesn't know if he believes it. He doesn't know if *he* believes it.

* * *

He tells Yuka that night.

In the dark, in their room, with the wind at the window. She's lying beside him with her head on his shoulder, very still, and he tells her about the porch, about Jin-Woo, about *together*.

“You won't wait,” she says. ʸ

It isn't an accusation. It's just true.

“If something comes for someone,” he says. “If I'm the closest. If there's no time—” ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“I'll tell you,” he says. “Like the war. If I go, I'll tell you. Before, if I can. As soon as I can, if I can't.” ᴹ

She's quiet for a long time.

“That's the rule,” she says. “That's my rule. The one from the war. You can go. You tell me. As soon as you can.” ʸ

“As soon as I can,” he agrees. ᴹ

“And you come back.” ʸ

He doesn't answer that one right away. She waits.

“And I come back,” he says. ᴹ

She puts her arm across him, the way she does, and holds on, and neither of them sleeps for a long time.
