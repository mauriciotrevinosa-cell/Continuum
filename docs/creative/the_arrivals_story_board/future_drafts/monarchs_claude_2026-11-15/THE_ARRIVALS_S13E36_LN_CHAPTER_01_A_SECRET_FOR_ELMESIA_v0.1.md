# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 29 — A Secret for Elmesia

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 29
**Source episode:** S13E36
**Continuity state:** B+330–331. A week of quiet after the Rulers. Mau trains with Jin-Woo every day; nothing comes. Yuka writes to Elmesia and gets an invitation to the capital of Thalion for one day, alone: she wants to talk to Elmesia about something without Mau knowing (a surprise, or a long holiday; Yuka won't say). Mau teases, flies her there on Kaisel, leaves her at the citadel with Elmesia. Both women carry his anchors. He flies home and goes to the yard to train with Jin-Woo.

*When: A week after the Rulers came. Nothing has happened. Jin-Woo says that's exactly what Yogumunt would want.*

Nothing comes.

That's the worst part, for the first few days. Mau wakes up every morning braced for it. He checks the anchors before he opens his eyes: thirty-one in the house, one under the Sungs' porch, four in the Sungs' shadows, Makima's, Hinata's, Elmesia's. All quiet. Every morning, all quiet. He trains with Jin-Woo in the yard until Shoko comes out and makes them stop. He eats. He sleeps. Nothing comes.

“He's waiting,” Jin-Woo says, on the fourth day. ᴶʷ

They're sitting on the training posts in the yard, breathing hard, sweat cooling on their faces. Jin-Woo has a bruise coming up on his jaw where Mau got through. Mau has two on his ribs where Jin-Woo got through twice.

“Yogumunt,” Mau says. ᴹ

“He wants us to stop waiting. He wants us to get bored. He wants us to go back to normal and stop looking over our shoulders.” ᴶʷ

“And then?” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at the orchard.

“And then he'll come,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

By the end of the week, the house has gone back to normal anyway. It can't help it. That's what houses do.

Ori goes back to arguing with Milim about whether dragons sleep. Umaru starts a second tournament, this time with a strict ban on Authority that she has written in large letters at the top of the chart. Mai and Yuka have their mornings in the workshop. Gojo's tally passes sixty.

Mau keeps checking the anchors. Every morning. Every night. All quiet.

* * *

Yuka gets the letter on the seventh day.

He sees her get it. The morning boat isn't a thing here; letters come by Souei's runners, or by one of Elmesia's birds, a big dark one with a band on its leg that lands on the porch rail and waits, very dignified, for someone to take what it's carrying. Yuka takes it. She reads it standing on the porch. Then she folds it up very fast and puts it in her pocket and looks at him.

“What?” he asks. ᴹ

“Nothing,” she says. ʸ

“That's a letter from Elmesia.” ᴹ

“It might be.” ʸ

“The bird is Elmesia's bird.” ᴹ

“It's *a* bird.” ʸ

“It has her seal on its leg.” ᴹ

Yuka looks at the bird. The bird looks back at her, unhelpfully.

“It's a letter from Elmesia,” she admits. ʸ

“What does it say?” ᴹ

“It says,” Yuka says, “that I can come and see her.” ʸ

“We just *saw* her.” ᴹ

“*I* can come and see her,” Yuka says. “Me. Not you.” ʸ

He stares at her.

* * *

She won't tell him why.

He tries all day. He tries at lunch, and in the afternoon, and at dinner. He tries the direct approach (“*What are you going to talk about?*”) and the indirect approach (“*Elmesia must get bored, talking to the same people all the time*”) and the sneaky approach (“*Ciel, did you see what the letter said?*” to which Ciel replies, very primly, *I don't read other people's letters*).

“It's a secret,” Yuka says, every time. ʸ

“What kind of secret?” ᴹ

“The kind you don't get to know.” ʸ

“Is it a good secret?” ᴹ

She thinks about it. “It might be.” ʸ

“Is it about me?” ᴹ

“*Everything's* about you,” Yuka says. “That's why it's a secret.” ʸ

He looks at her across the dinner table. She looks back at him, completely serene, eating her soup.

“Is it a surprise?” he asks. ᴹ

“Might be.” ʸ

“Is it the island? Are we going back?” ᴹ

Yuka's spoon pauses for exactly one second.

“*Might* be,” she says. ʸ

Across the table, Gojo makes a small noise into his cup that might be a laugh.

“You know,” Mau says to him. ᴹ

“I know nothing,” Gojo says. ᴳ

“You *know*.” ᴹ

“I know that if you keep asking her, she'll make it a worse surprise out of spite,” Gojo says, “and then you'll have nobody to blame but yourself.” ᴳ

Mau stops asking.

* * *

He flies her there the next morning.

It's the only part of it she lets him have. She asked him to — standing in the doorway of their room the night before, very casual, too casual, *you could take me on Kaisel, if you wanted* — and he said yes before she'd finished, and she smiled in a way that made him suspect she'd planned exactly that.

Kaisel comes up out of his shadow in the yard, huge and dark and patient, and lowers his head so they can climb on. Yuka sits in front. Mau sits behind her with his arms round her waist. Ori stands on the porch steps and waves until they're too high to see.

It's a long flight. The capital of Thalion is far to the south and west, across the river and the hills and the long green valleys, and the wind is cold up here, and Yuka leans back against him the whole way.

“You're going to tell me eventually,” he says into her hair. ᴹ

“Eventually,” she agrees. ʸ

“How eventually?” ᴹ

“That depends on how good you are.” ʸ

“I'm very good.” ᴹ

“You asked Ciel to read my letter.” ʸ

“She didn't.” ᴹ

“You *asked*.” ʸ

He doesn't have an answer to that. He holds her a little tighter.

* * *

The capital comes up out of the haze in the late morning.

He's seen it before, from a distance. Up close it's enormous: a great walled city on a hill, with streets winding up it in rings, and at the very top, the citadel, a cluster of pale towers and courtyards and gardens, all of it glittering in the sun. Yuka tells him what colours the roofs are. He listens. He can see the light on them, the way it catches and moves, but nothing else.

Elmesia is waiting for them on a terrace at the top of the citadel, with guards in a long row behind her and a table already laid with something to drink.

“You brought her,” she says, as Kaisel settles on the flagstones. ᴱˡ

“She asked me to,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know. She told me she would.” ᴱˡ

He looks at Yuka. Yuka looks innocently at the sky.

* * *

He doesn't stay.

That's the deal. Yuka was very clear. *You drop me off. You fly home. You come back for me tonight. And you don't ask Elmesia anything, and you don't ask Ciel anything, and you don't hang around the courtyard looking sad.*

“I don't look sad,” he said. ᴹ

“You look *so* sad. You look like Lantern when Ori puts him away.” ʸ

He stands on the terrace beside Kaisel while Yuka goes to Elmesia. They embrace, briefly, the way women do who've decided they like each other. Elmesia says something in Yuka's ear that makes her laugh.

He checks the anchors. He can't help it.

Yuka's: the quiet one, the one from the north wall, sitting in her shadow on the warm flagstones.

Elmesia's: another quiet one, in her shadow under the long table.

Both still. Both fine.

“Go,” Yuka says, turning back to him. “Go *home*. I'll be fine. I'm in the safest place in the whole south. Look at all these guards.” ʸ

He looks at the guards. There are a lot of them.

“Tonight,” he says. ᴹ

“Tonight.” ʸ

She comes back and hugs him, quickly, and then pushes him in the chest, the way she did at the gate. Like pushing a boat away from a dock.

“Go and train with Jin-Woo,” she says. “Get tired. Don't think about it.” ʸ

He climbs back on Kaisel. He looks down at her once more, standing on the terrace in the sun beside Elmesia, the only thing in the whole glittering city with colour.

Then Kaisel lifts, and the citadel falls away beneath them.

* * *

He's home by early afternoon.

Jin-Woo is waiting in the yard. He's already warmed up. He's already got the trainers out, the blunted daggers they use for practice. He tosses one to Mau as Kaisel settles.

“She tell you?” Jin-Woo asks. ᴶʷ

“No.” ᴹ

“Did you ask Ciel?” ᴶʷ

“*Everyone* knows I asked Ciel.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo grins.

They train. It's good. It's the best session they've had all week, maybe because Mau needs to not think about the terrace and the citadel and what Yuka might be saying to Elmesia right now, and maybe because Jin-Woo can tell that and doesn't make it easy. They go at each other for an hour, the blunt daggers flashing, and Mau gets through twice and Jin-Woo gets through three times, and Shoko comes out onto the porch and watches with her arms folded and doesn't make them stop yet.

They're sitting on the posts afterward, breathing hard, when Mau checks the anchors again.

Thirty-one in the house. Quiet.

One under the Sungs' porch. Quiet.

Four in the Sungs' shadows. Quiet.

Makima's, inside the house. Quiet. Hinata's, far away. Quiet.

Yuka's. Quiet.

Elmesia's.

Elmesia's is pulling.
