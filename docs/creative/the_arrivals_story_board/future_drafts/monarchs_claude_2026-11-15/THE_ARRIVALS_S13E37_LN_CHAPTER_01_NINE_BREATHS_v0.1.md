# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 31 — Nine Breaths

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 31
**Source episode:** S13E37
**Continuity state:** B+331, afternoon. Mau's point of view, starting a few seconds earlier in the yard. Elmesia's anchor pulls; a breath later Yuka's pulls too. He says "Elmesia. Yuka." to Jin-Woo, asks Noise for the form, and goes. In the citadel: the cut on Rakan's shoulder is Shrine. He sees he's in the middle of a city full of people and cannot fight at full scale. He tells Yuka he's here and to get them down. He buys time with Bang: each shot opens a hole Rakan has to stop and heal. He counts the Exchange recharge (nine breaths, as in V59). Rakan gets a claw across his back once. Elmesia, bleeding, holds a barrier over the stairs. At the eighth breath, Sir Shadow pulls, then the anchor under the Sungs' porch, then Hae-In's: the split Jin-Woo predicted.

*When: The same afternoon. A few seconds before the shadow broke.*

Elmesia's anchor is pulling.

It's not a tug. It's not a hand on his sleeve, the way Yuka's was in the yard when she tested it. It's a *pull*, hard and sudden, a hook set in him somewhere under the breastbone and yanked. It comes with a direction, south and west, very far. It comes with a feeling, and the feeling is *now*.

He's off the training post before he's decided anything.

“Mau?” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

And then the second one.

Quieter. Smaller. The quiet soldier from the north wall, the one that doesn't like to be noticed, the one he put in Yuka's shadow in front of the whole house. It doesn't pull the way Elmesia's does. It *trembles*. It's close to something it's afraid of.

Yuka hasn't called. She'd have called louder. The soldier is just very close to something very bad, and it's telling him.

“Elmesia,” he says. “Yuka.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo's face changes.

“Noise,” Mau says. “The form.” ᴹ

“Shoko said—” ᴺ

“*The form.*” ᴹ

He feels it come over him. Like stepping into cold water, then into fire. Something running through his body from the deep still place where Makima's hand is, outward, along every channel. His hair moves on his scalp. His eyes go hot. The world tips and sharpens and goes very, very clear.

Behind him and above, the wheel.

“Mau, wait—” ᴶʷ

He doesn't wait. He finds Elmesia's soldier, far away, under a table, in the dark.

He goes.

* * *

The terrace.

That's the first thing. Flagstones, broken cups, an awning flapping, the long table flying sideways off him as he comes up out of the shadow. The wheel goes first, tearing up through the dark above his head, and he comes up after it into light and heat and the smell of blood.

And there's a thing.

The eyes read it before he's finished standing. Big. Upright on two legs but not built for them, hunched, furred, clawed. A face that's half a man's and half a wolf's. Heavy, dense, fast, the kind of fast the eyes have to work to follow. And old. Old in a way that makes the long things in the red gate look like children. It's standing over Elmesia with one hand drawn back, its claws long and curved and wet, and it's looking at him.

Its hand starts to come up.

He's already cut it.

He doesn't remember deciding to. Shrine is just there, in his hands, the way the daggers are there when he needs them. Dismantle, one clean line, shoulder to chest, measured for what the eyes read of its hide. It doesn't feel it go in. He sees it look down at the line, and look up at him, and he sees something go across its wolf-face that he recognises, because he's seen it in the mirror.

*Prey.*

* * *

And then he sees where he is.

The citadel. The top of the city. The eyes open all the way and take it in all at once: the terrace, the towers, the courtyards and gardens below, the rings of streets winding down the hill, every one of them packed. People. Thousands of people. Tens of thousands. Running in the streets, crowding in doorways, standing on balconies looking up at the citadel where the bells are ringing. He can feel all of them. Every heartbeat.

He thinks about what he was going to do.

He was going to open the Shrine. All of it. The whole domain. He was going to cut this thing into pieces too small to heal, and it would have worked, and it would have taken the citadel with it, and half the hill, and everyone on it.

He can't do that here.

He can't do anything big here. Not Purple. Not the full Shrine. Not anything that spreads. Every time he lands a blow that misses, every time something gets deflected, there are people underneath it.

*Buy time*, he thinks. *Get it out of here.*

He looks at the shadow under his feet, the one he came through. Elmesia's soldier. Exchanged, now: the soldier is in the yard at home, standing where he stood, and he's here. To go back the same way, he'll need—

“Nine breaths,” Noise says. ᴺ

The recharge. Like the duel. Nine breaths before he can Exchange again.

*Nine breaths, then.*

* * *

He looks at the stairs.

She's there. Halfway down, stopped, turned back. Her Mahoraga behind her, great pale arms spread over a knot of servants and fallen guards. She's staring up at him. At his hair, his eyes, the wheel. He's never let her see him like this. He sees her see it.

He keeps his promise.

“I'm here,” he says. Loud, so she hears it over the bells. “Yuka. I'm here. Get them down.” ᴹ

She doesn't argue. She doesn't wait. He watches her turn and shout at Mahoraga, and Mahoraga sweeps the servants down the stairs ahead of her like a man sweeping leaves.

*One.*

* * *

The wolf comes at him.

It's fast. Faster than anything he's fought since the duel. Its claws come at his face from the left and he isn't there; Movement Learner was screaming the angle before it moved. They come from the right and he isn't there either. It's snarling now, deep and furious, blood running down its chest from the line he cut, and the line is already closing. He can see it closing. The edges of the wound pulling together, the fur growing back over it.

*Two.*

It heals. Of course it heals. It's a king of beasts.

*Then make it heal.*

He brings his hand up. Two fingers. Thumb. Like Ori playing with Milim.

“Bang,” he says. ᴹ

A hole opens in the wolf's thigh. Clean. Round. The size of a coin, front to back.

The wolf stops.

It looks down. It looks at the hole. And he watches its whole body change, watches the fury go inward, watches every bit of its attention swing down to the wound and *pull*. The flesh knits. The fur grows. It takes a breath. Maybe a little more.

*Three.*

That's the trick. That's the whole trick. It can heal anything. But it has to *stop* to heal. Every time it heals, it isn't attacking.

“Bang,” he says. ᴹ

Its shoulder. The wolf jerks. Stops. Heals.

*Four.*

* * *

Elmesia is on her feet.

He sees her from the corner of his eye. She's bleeding from the arm and the shoulder and a long cut on her face, and she's standing at the top of the stairs with her hand raised, and there's a barrier over the stairs behind her, glittering, holding, between the wolf and everyone Yuka is getting down.

“Mau,” she says. ᴱˡ

“I know.” ᴹ

“The city—” ᴱˡ

“I know. I'm not doing anything big. I'm getting it out.” ᴹ

“How?” ᴱˡ

“Five more breaths.” ᴹ

She doesn't ask what that means. She nods once, and holds the barrier.

*Five.*

* * *

The wolf gets through on six.

It learns. That's the worst part. After the third Bang it stops waiting for him to point; it moves the instant his hand starts to rise, so the shots land in places it's already left. After the fourth it starts coming in low and twisting, so there's no clean line. And on the sixth breath, as he brings his hand up, it goes *under* the hand, and around him, and he feels its claws open his back.

Four lines. Shoulder blade to hip. Deep.

He hears himself make a sound.

He doesn't have Infinity up. He can't. The form is running, and the wheel is out, and Bang is ready in his hand, and Noise told him once in the yard, very calmly, that if he tries to run a fourth thing on top of those while Makima is holding the sea, he'll find out exactly where his limit is. So no Infinity. Just his body and the eyes and the learner and the cut.

The wheel turns above his head. *Click.*

He spins. He puts a Bang through the wolf's chest at point-blank range, so close he feels the heat of its breath, and it staggers back and stops and heals.

*Six.*

* * *

His back is wet. He can feel the blood running down under his shirt, hot, soaking into his waistband. RCT, a little, enough to close the deepest parts, not enough to waste. He needs it for later.

*Seven.*

The wolf is circling. Wary now. It's realised what he's doing. It's realised that every time he points, it loses a breath, and it's started to hate him for it with a deep, personal, animal hatred that he can feel coming off it like heat.

“Who are you?” it snarls. ᴿᵏ

He doesn't answer.

“*What* are you?” ᴿᵏ

“Busy,” he says. ᴹ

*Eight.*

* * *

And then the anchors start pulling.

Not Elmesia's. Not Yuka's. Others. Far away, in the other direction. North. Home.

Sir Shadow first.

He feels it before anything else: a sharp, frantic, *tiny* pull, like a child yanking on his sleeve with both hands. Suho's soldier. The one with the terrible name. Pulling as hard as it can.

Then the one under the Sungs' porch. The one by the door. *If something comes for them and it gets through what I've left, I'd like you to know too.*

It's pulling.

Then Hae-In's.

Then Jin-Ah's.

Then their mother's.

All of them. All at once. All pulling, from a house past the orchard path, where the ground slopes down toward the stream.

And he understands. He understands all of it, standing on the terrace with blood running down his back and the wolf circling him and the bells ringing over the city. Yogumunt's plan. Jin-Woo said it on the porch. *He'll try to split us. He'll try to make sure we're never in the same place when they come.*

This one is the bait. It always was. It was sent here to pull him south. And while he's here, three days' march from home—

*Nine.*

He looks at the wolf.

The wolf looks at him, and grins, with all its teeth. It knows. It can see on his face that he knows.

“Too late,” it says. ᴿᵏ

Mau smiles back.

And the wolf's grin falters, because it's never seen a smile like that before.
