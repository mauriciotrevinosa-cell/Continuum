# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 33 — Not Alone

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 33
**Source episode:** S13E38
**Continuity state:** B+331, afternoon, the Sungs' yard. Mau holds the door alone against three Monarchs for about a minute. Querehsha's swarm stings him through his clothes; the poison burns; the wheel turns at it. Rakan comes in low. Yogumunt puts on Yuka's face and walks toward him, and Mau knows at once it's false, because it's grey: the only person he sees in colour is Yuka, and this isn't her. Then Jin-Woo arrives through his own knight's shadow, back from the empty quarry, and sees Mau bleeding alone in front of his door against three Monarchs. "No. No, no, no. He is not going to die alone." He is furious. His army rises.

*When: The same afternoon. One minute, more or less. Mau will not remember most of it in order.*

They come at him all at once.

That's the first thing. They don't take turns. They're not that kind of thing. The wolf comes in low, the way it learned on the terrace, twisting under his hand before he can point. The swarm comes down from above, a roaring black wave. And the thing at the gate doesn't move at all, just watches, its face rippling.

He goes to meet them.

He doesn't think about it. He's past thinking. He's in the place he goes when there isn't time for anything else, the place he lived in for four hundred and thirty-seven deaths and a year in a room with a bed, where the body does what it knows and the head just watches.

The wolf first. Under its claws. A dagger, his real one, into the soft place under its arm, and out, and away before the claws close. It doesn't even slow down. It heals as it turns.

Then the swarm.

* * *

He's never been stung by a thousand things at once.

It's not like a blade. A blade is one line, clean, and then it's done. This is everywhere, all at once, through his shirt and his trousers and his hair, on his face, on his hands, in his ears. Tiny bright points of fire, thousands of them, and each one of them doesn't just hurt. It *burns*. Something going in with each sting and spreading, hot and sick, under his skin.

“Poison,” Ciel says, very fast. “Neurotoxic. A lot of it. Mau, you need—” ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

Infinity. He needs Infinity. One layer, just the neutral, just enough that they can't touch him.

He can't. The form. The wheel. Bang. The sea. *If you run a fourth thing, you'll find out exactly where your limit is.*

So he burns instead.

Zoltraak: not at the queen, at the swarm. A wide flat sheet of it, sweeping, the way Frieren taught him to clear a field. The swarm comes apart in front of it like smoke. Thousands of small bodies falling, burning, out of the air.

There are millions more.

Above and behind him, the wheel turns. *Click.* At the poison. Learning it.

The next stings burn a little less.

* * *

He doesn't remember the next part in order.

He remembers the wolf's claws in his thigh. He remembers putting a Bang through its face, point-blank, and the way it fell back and screamed and *stopped*, healing, and how he used that breath to cut the queen's swarm in half with Dismantle. He remembers the queen herself coming down out of the cloud, wings blurring, and her hand on his chest, and something like a needle going in under his collarbone, and the world going grey at the edges. More grey than usual. He remembers pulling the needle out. He remembers his legs not working right for a second.

He remembers that he didn't move from in front of the door.

Jin-Woo's knight is beside him. He doesn't know when that happened. The knight is cutting the swarm apart on his left, silent, tireless, and the other shadows of Jin-Woo's are in a ring behind them, and they're all holding the door, the empty door, with nobody behind it.

He's bleeding from a lot of places now. He's stopped counting.

* * *

Then the thing at the gate moves.

It walks toward him. Slowly. Through the swarm, which parts around it. Through the yard. Its hands still folded in its sleeves.

And its face changes.

He watches it change. Watches it ripple and shift and settle into a shape.

Yuka.

Her face. Her hair, the way it falls. Her eyes. Her mouth, which is smiling the small smile she uses when she's about to say something she knows will make him laugh. Her body, her height, the way she holds her shoulders. Even her walk. Light and quick and a little uneven.

It walks toward him with her face, and holds out her hands.

“Mau,” it says, in her voice. “Mau, stop. It's me. Come here.” ʸᵒ

The wolf, on the grass, has gone still. The queen has gone still. Even the swarm hangs in the air. They're watching. Waiting to see what he'll do. Waiting for him to hesitate. That's what this is for. That's what it was always for. The thing that wears faces has been watching his valley for days. It saw a girl climb into his lap on a porch. It knows exactly what face to wear.

It takes another step.

And it's grey.

* * *

He almost laughs.

It's *grey*. All of it. Her hair, grey. Her skin, grey. Her eyes, grey. The smile, the walk, the hands held out to him: all of it a thousand shades of grey, exactly like the yard and the house and the swarm and the trees, exactly like everything else in the world.

Because the only thing in the world he's ever seen in colour is her. Not her face. Not her shape. *Her.*

And this isn't her.

“You got everything right,” he says. ᴹ

The thing with her face stops.

“The hair. The walk. The smile. Everything.” He looks at it. “You just got it in the wrong colour.” ᴹ

He watches it not understand. Watches the face ripple, uncertain, for the first time.

“Bang,” he says. ᴹ

The hole goes through her face. Through *its* face. Right between the grey eyes.

It screams, in a voice that isn't hers any more, and the face comes apart, rippling, shifting, a dozen faces at once and none of them, and it reels back across the yard with its hands over the hole.

* * *

That costs him.

He knew it would. He's been feeling it building, the whole minute: the pressure behind his eyes from holding the wolf, the burn of the poison, the drain of the form and the wheel and every Bang. That one, through the thing's face, was the hardest yet. He put everything behind it. He *wanted* it.

The world tips.

He goes down on one knee. He doesn't decide to. His leg just folds.

He hears the wolf. It's laughing. He hears the swarm start to roar again. He hears the queen's wings. He tries to get up, and his leg doesn't listen, and there's something warm running out of his nose and over his lip.

Not now. *Not now.*

He puts his hand on the ground. He pushes. His leg starts to listen.

And somebody lands beside him.

* * *

It's Jin-Woo.

He comes up out of the knight's shadow. Mau feels it happen, the way you feel a door open behind you. One moment the knight is cutting the swarm on his left; the next Jin-Woo is there, standing where the knight stood, and the knight is gone, sent wherever Jin-Woo came from.

He's breathing hard. He's been running. There's dust on him, quarry dust, pale and gritty, all down his clothes.

He looks at the house. The empty doorway. The ring of his shadows around it.

He looks at the yard. The swarm. The wolf. The queen. The thing with no face, reeling, clutching the hole in its head.

And then he looks down at Mau.

Mau, on one knee, in front of the door. Bleeding from his back, his thigh, his arm, his chest. Stings all over his face and hands, swelling and dark. Blood running from his nose. Hair plastered to his skull with sweat, in whatever colour the form has made it; eyes that Mau can feel burning; the wheel turning slowly above his head. Holding the door. Alone.

Jin-Woo's face changes.

Mau has seen it calm, and amused, and gentle with Suho, and very far away on the porch at night. He has never seen it like this.

“No,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

He says it very quietly.

“No. No, no, *no*.” ᴶʷ

He isn't talking to Mau. He isn't talking to the Monarchs. He's talking to something else, something much older, something that stands behind everything he's ever fought.

“He is *not*,” Jin-Woo says, “going to die alone.” ᴶʷ

* * *

And the ground opens.

Mau feels it before he sees it. Jin-Woo's shadow, spreading out from his feet across the whole yard, across the grass and the path and the gate, black and endless and deep. And out of it, rising, his army.

Not a dozen. Not the ones at the house. All hundred of them, every one he chose to keep. Knights and soldiers and mages, great beasts and small ones, things Mau has never seen, things with wings and things with horns and things that are only shapes of darkness with eyes. Rising and rising, row on row, until they fill the yard and the orchard and the slope behind the house.

Jin-Woo's ant is back, on the roof, its wings spread. Jin-Woo's knight is back, at the foot of the steps, its sword raised.

And Jin-Woo himself, standing beside Mau, with his hands at his sides and his face like a closed door.

“Get up,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

His voice is shaking. With anger. With something else.

“Get *up*, you idiot. You said together. You *said*.” ᴶʷ

Mau gets up.

He doesn't know how. His leg holds. Jin-Woo's hand is under his arm, taking his weight, hard and angry and steady.

“I told you,” Mau says. His voice comes out cracked. “Sir Shadow told me.” ᴹ

“I don't *care* what Sir Shadow told you.” ᴶʷ

Jin-Woo lets go of his arm. He steps forward, past Mau, until they're standing side by side in front of the empty door. He looks at the three Monarchs in his yard.

The wolf has stopped laughing. The queen has gone very still. The thing with no face has taken its hands away from the hole in its head, and its face has settled, finally, into one Mau hasn't seen before. Old. Thin. Ancient and tired and very, very afraid.

“You came back,” Jin-Woo says to them. ᴶʷ

Nobody answers him.

“I killed you once,” Jin-Woo says. “All of you. I remember every one.” ᴶʷ

His army moves. One step forward. All of it. The sound of it is like the earth turning over.

“You came into my home,” Jin-Woo says. “You came for my wife. My son. My mother. My sister.” ᴶʷ

He looks at Mau, beside him. Bleeding. Burning. Standing.

“You made him stand in front of my door alone,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

He looks back at the Monarchs.

“I'm going to kill you again,” he says. “Slower.” ᴶʷ
