# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 78
## Chapter 34 — The Empty Hills

**Status:** LN PRODUCTION DRAFT — S13 v0.1
**Volume:** 78
**Reading order:** 34
**Source episode:** S13E38
**Continuity state:** B+331, afternoon. Mau and Jin-Woo take the fight off the Sungs' land and up into the empty hills north of the stream, where there is nobody for miles: Mau throws Rakan with Sky and Authority, Jin-Woo's army drives Querehsha's swarm. Mau calls Beru and Igris; Beru tells the insect queen he already has a king. The two of them fight the way they have trained: dagger and Authority, the learner reading Rakan, Jin-Woo's authority pinning what Mau cuts. Yogumunt stays at a distance throwing faces and illusions at Jin-Woo. Querehsha's poison keeps working in Mau; the wheel keeps turning at it.

*When: A few minutes after Jin-Woo arrived. The sun is lower. Up at the main house, Gojo is standing on the porch with the Sungs behind him and Ori in his arms, watching smoke rise from the hills.*

“Not here,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

He doesn't look at Mau when he says it. He's looking at the house behind them. At the porch where Suho played with the knight. At the window where his mother hung the curtains.

“The houses,” Mau says. ᴹ

“The houses. The orchard. Yours, up the path. Everything.” ᴶʷ

Jin-Woo points, north, past the stream, to where the land rises into long bare hills, empty, grass and rock and nothing else for miles.

“There,” he says. ᴶʷ

“How?” ᴹ

Jin-Woo finally looks at him. Something in his face is almost a smile, and it isn't a nice one.

“You throw,” he says. “I'll herd.” ᴶʷ

* * *

The wolf is the closest.

It's crouched on the grass at the edge of the yard, watching Jin-Woo's army with its lips drawn back, and it hasn't noticed Mau looking at it. It's afraid now. He can see that. It's afraid of the man who killed it once and has promised to do it again, slower, and it isn't watching the one who's been bleeding on the grass.

Mau puts Sky under it.

Not a patch. All of it. The whole surface of the sky beneath the wolf's feet, tilted up and north, sharp as a ramp. And then Authority on top, the grip from the terrace, the one that tore something behind his eyes, taking hold of the whole of it.

He throws.

The wolf goes up. It goes up and *out*, over the stream, over the trees, a dark shape tumbling against the afternoon sky, howling. Mau feels the throw go out of him like a breath he's been holding for a minute. His vision goes white at the edges and comes back.

Behind him, Jin-Woo's army moves.

It doesn't attack the swarm. It *walks into it*. All of it, all hundred, in a great dark wave, and the swarm can't sting what has no skin, and the queen screams, and the army keeps walking. North. Over the stream. Up the slope. Pushing the swarm in front of it like a broom pushing smoke.

The thing with no face is already gone. Mau didn't see it go.

“It'll follow,” Jin-Woo says. “It always follows. It wants to see.” ᴶʷ

He looks at Mau.

“Can you fly?” ᴶʷ

Mau tries to stand up straighter and mostly fails.

“Yeah,” he says. ᴹ

* * *

The hills are empty.

He reads them as he comes down. Grass and rock and a long ridge running east to west, and below it a wide bare valley with a stream at the bottom. Sheep, very far off, on the far side of the ridge. No houses. No people. Nothing for miles in any direction that has a heartbeat, except the things he's brought with him.

He lands hard. His thigh gives and he catches himself on one hand.

The wolf is already up. It came down halfway along the valley and it's on its feet, shaking itself, furious, and it's coming.

“Beru,” Mau says. “Igris.” ᴹ

They come up out of his shadow on either side of him.

Igris first, silent, his sword already drawn, his helm turned toward the wolf. Then Beru, unfolding, huge and armoured, his wings snapping out, his many eyes taking in the valley. He's still wearing the hat.

“My king,” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

“The wolf,” Mau says. “Keep it off Jin-Woo.” ᴹ

“And you, my king?” ᴮᵉ

“I'm the wolf's problem.” ᴹ

* * *

The swarm comes over the ridge.

It comes over like a storm cloud, Jin-Woo's army still walking through it, and at the centre of it, the queen, wings blurring, her chitin catching the low sun. She's angry. He can feel it from here. Her army has been walked over by something that doesn't care about stings, and she's a queen, and queens are not walked over.

She sees Beru.

She stops.

The swarm stops around her, hanging in the air. Every one of those millions of small bodies turned, very slightly, toward the big ant in the cloth hat.

“*You*,” the queen says. Her voice is like a thousand wings. ᵠ

Beru doesn't move.

“I know what you are,” the queen says. “I know your kind. Ant king. Child of my brood. Kneel.” ᵠ

Beru looks at her for a long moment with all his eyes.

“I have a king,” he says. ᴮᵉ

He tilts his head, very slightly, toward Mau.

“He is not you,” Beru says. ᴮᵉ

And he goes up into the swarm with his wings spread and his claws out and the little cloth hat that Ori made with Mai still sitting perfectly between his antennae.

* * *

The wolf reaches Mau.

He's ready for it. He's had time, now, to watch it. On the terrace, in the yard, every time it came at him, the learner was recording. He knows how it moves. Low, and then twisting. Claws from the side it isn't looking. Always going for the place he's already hurt.

It goes for his back.

He isn't there. He's turned, the way Jin-Woo taught him, the shoulder roll from the first lesson, and his dagger is coming up into its ribs from below. It twists away. He follows. Authority, a small grip on its foreleg, just enough to pull it a hand's breadth off the line it wants. The dagger again. Into its side. Out.

It heals. It turns. It comes again.

And Jin-Woo is there.

* * *

He doesn't see Jin-Woo arrive. Jin-Woo is just *there*, on the wolf's other side, his own daggers out, and they're fighting it together.

It's like the yard.

It's exactly like the yard. All those mornings. All those afternoons, on the posts, with the blunt trainers, with Shoko on the porch with her arms folded. Jin-Woo correcting his grip. Jin-Woo showing him where to drop his shoulder. Jin-Woo coming at him again and again until he stopped flinching.

The wolf goes for Mau and Jin-Woo is on its back. It turns on Jin-Woo and Mau's dagger is in its side. It tries to get away from both of them and something invisible closes around its legs: Jin-Woo's authority, his own, nothing like Mau's, a crushing weight that comes down out of the air and pins it to the ground. And Mau is there, and Shrine is there, and the cut goes through it from shoulder to hip.

It screams.

It heals.

“It's going to keep doing that,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know,” Jin-Woo says. “It did that last time too.” ᴶʷ

“How did you kill it last time?” ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him across the wolf's heaving body.

“Faster than it could heal,” he says. ᴶʷ

* * *

The faces start then.

Mau sees them out of the corner of his eye. On the ridge, at the edge of the valley, in the grass. Figures. Standing. Watching. A woman with a sword. A boy. An old woman. A girl with a notebook.

Hae-In. Suho. Jin-Woo's mother. Jin-Ah.

All grey.

“They're not real,” Mau says. ᴹ

“I know they're not real,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

But he looked. Mau saw him look. Saw the half-second his head turned toward Suho on the ridge, and the wolf's claws came up in that half-second, and Mau had to put a Bang through its arm to stop them.

“He's doing it to you,” Mau says. ᴹ

“He's always done it to me.” ᴶʷ

More of them. On the ridge. In the valley. Hae-In, bleeding. Suho, crying. Jin-Woo's mother, holding out her hands. All of them grey, every one, as grey as the grass and the rock and the sky.

“Look at me,” Mau says. ᴹ

Jin-Woo looks at him.

“They're grey,” Mau says. “All of them. I'd know. If any of them was real, I'd know. I'll tell you. Don't look at them. Look at me.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo stares at him for a second.

Then he understands. Mau sees him understand. Sees him remember, maybe, something Mau told him once on the porch, about colour, and one person.

“Grey,” Jin-Woo says. ᴶʷ

“All of them.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo turns back to the wolf, and doesn't look at the ridge again.

* * *

The poison is still in him.

He can feel it. Under his skin, in his blood, spreading slowly from a hundred stings and the place under his collarbone where the queen's needle went in. Hot and sick. His hands are shaking. Every few breaths his vision goes soft at the edges and he has to blink it back.

The wheel keeps turning. *Click.* *Click.* Every time the poison reaches something new, it turns. The burning gets a little less each time. Not enough.

“Mau,” Ciel says. ᶜ

“I know.” ᴹ

“The form, the wheel, the authority, every shot. You're spending faster than you can—” ᶜ

“I *know*.” ᴹ

“And the sea,” Noise says, very quietly. “She's holding. But she can feel what you're doing. It's getting heavy for her.” ᴺ

He thinks about Makima. Somewhere deep inside him, in the still place, holding back everything that nearly killed him a week ago, while he throws wolves across rivers.

*If you die, I go with you.*

“How long?” he asks. ᴹ

Noise doesn't answer right away.

“Not long enough to keep doing this,” it says. ᴺ

Above the valley, Beru comes down out of the swarm in a spiral, trailing smoke, and lands on one knee in the grass, his hat askew, his armour cracked across the back. The queen comes down after him, screaming.

The wolf is getting up again.

And on the ridge, all along it, the faces are multiplying, grey, grey, grey, and somewhere behind them the thing that wears them is watching, and waiting, and learning.

Mau looks at the empty valley. Empty for miles. No houses. No people. Nothing with a heartbeat but the things he brought.

“Jin-Woo,” he says. ᴹ

“What?” ᴶʷ

“Get your army back. All of it. Behind the ridge. And get behind it yourself.” ᴹ

Jin-Woo turns to look at him.

“Why?” ᴶʷ

Mau looks at the wolf, and the queen, and the faces on the ridge.

“Because there's nobody here,” he says. “And I can finally open it.” ᴹ
