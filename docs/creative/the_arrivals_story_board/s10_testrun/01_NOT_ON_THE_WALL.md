## Chapter 1 — Not on the Wall

**Status:** PRUEBA S10 v0.1 (2026-11-04), con marcas para Work. No canon.
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 1
**Source episode:** S10E01
**Continuity state:** B+153 morning. Mau functional, tired, daily Shoko check, no training yet. Permanent new marks: neck scar, waist line, ear, brown and white hair. Grey except Yuka.

*When: B+153, a little after the hour his body still wakes for. It wakes him, finds nothing to do and leaves him lying there.*

There is an arm across his chest.

He looks at it for a while. It has a bruise above the wrist and a long scrape that has nearly finished healing. It is the only thing in the room with any colour, and it weighs about as much as a sack of flour. She has taken all of the blanket.

For sixteen nights there was a lamp, and somebody in the corner with a book, and the sound of an ant being told to keep his hands to himself. Last night there was none of that. Shoko looked at him after the cards and said, “Go home.” So he went home.

He can hear the house starting. A door somewhere. Rem at the stove, two floors off. In the next room, Rimuru is explaining to someone that he is not asleep, and clearly has been.

Nothing hurts more than it should.

He turns his head. Yuka's hair is over her face and her mouth is a little open. He lies there and looks at her, since nobody has written down that he can't.

`[PARA WORK: Primera mañana a solas en su cuarto desde antes de la pelea. Diecisiete días con gente alrededor. EMPIEZA: ella despierta y lo encuentra mirándola. Lo que quieran decirse o hacer, con tu tono y el de Mauri. TERMINA: se levantan porque Shoko lo espera y ya van tarde. Lo siguiente empieza en el pasillo de la clínica.]`

Shoko presses his side with her thumb. She looks in his eye. She has him close both hands, and asks what day it is.

“The one after yesterday.” ᴹ

“You're fine. You're as fine as you ever get. Same time tomorrow.” She is already reaching for someone else's chart. “If you lift anything heavier than a chair, I'll hear about it.” ˢʰ

So they go out into the corridor, and Yuka closes the door behind them, and they stop.

The wall is there. Nobody has washed it. Maomao has forbidden that, and when Fern asked why, she said it was the only accurate record of the household's labour she had ever seen. It goes from the door to the window. Mau finds today's date near the top, in that small mean handwriting.

There is nothing written under it.

He reads it again. He looks farther along, in case it has moved.

“Who's got me today?” he says. ᴹ

Yuka comes and stands beside him. She has one boot not properly laced. She looks up and down the column with her eyes narrowed.

“Nobody,” she says. ʸ

“Somebody must.” ᴹ

“There's nobody down.” ʸ

He has been handed from person to person for more than two weeks, like a pot too hot to hold. He knows who walks him to the stairs and who pretends not to watch him eat. He has not had to decide where to go since before the quarry.

A person goes by with a bucket. She nods to them and keeps walking.

“So what do we do?” he says. ᴹ

Yuka doesn't answer right away. It occurs to him that she is not on the wall either. There's no two hours next to her name. Maki hasn't asked for her until after lunch. Mai said she could have the morning. They are standing in a corridor with a whole day in front of them, and neither one of them can remember how this is done.

She looks at him. It's a strange look, a little shy, and it doesn't suit her at all. He has seen her charge a dragon with less hesitation.

“Have you eaten?” she says. ʸ

“No.” ᴹ

“Are you going to want me to cut it up?” ʸ

“If you touch my bread, I'll file a complaint.” ᴹ

Something in her shoulders comes down about an inch.

She digs in her pocket. What she pulls out is a piece of paper, folded so many times that it has gone soft along the creases. There's pencil on it, on both sides, in her writing. Some of it has been crossed out and written again. She doesn't hand it over. She holds it against her chest, where he can't read it.

“What's that?” ᴹ

“It's a list.” ʸ

“Of what?” ᴹ

“Things I couldn't tell you.” ʸ

He goes still.

She sees it, and she rolls her eyes.

“You couldn't follow a sentence, idiot. You asked Shoko what day it was four times in one morning. I'd come in for my two hours with something, and you'd look at me like you were very pleased somebody had come, and I'd sit there and oil a sword.” She looks down at the paper. “So I wrote them down. For when you could.” ʸ

He doesn't know what to do with his face.

“How many are there?” ᴹ

“Forty-one.” ʸ

She pockets it again, turns around and heads for the stairs. At the top she stops and looks back over her shoulder, with one boot still flapping.

“You'll want to sit down for some of them,” Yuka says. “Bring bread.” ʸ
