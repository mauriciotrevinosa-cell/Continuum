## Chapter 2 — Forty-One Things

**Status:** PRUEBA S10 v0.1 (2026-11-04), con marcas para Work. No canon.
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 2
**Source episode:** S10E01
**Continuity state:** B+153 morning, continuous. Bench under the tree, root circle clear. Yuka's list of things she saved during his recovery. No new event.

*When: B+153, mid-morning. They take the bench by the tree, where the roots leave room for two people and a heel of bread.*

“One,” Yuka says. “Tsurugi walked into the door of the clinic.” ʸ

Mau waits.

“That's it. That's the whole thing. It was closed. He was carrying a basin, and he was looking at you through the little window and walked straight into it. All the water went on Stark. And Stark said, ‘It's fine,’ and Tsurugi said, ‘It's not fine,’ and he was crying, and neither of them could stop.” ʸ

She crosses it off with the stub of a pencil.

He has stopped chewing.

“I wanted to tell you that one so badly,” she says. “I came up the stairs and I had my mouth open.” ʸ

“Why was he crying?” ᴹ

“Because nobody knew yet, Mau. That was day one.” ʸ

She says it the way you would say it rained. She has already looked down for the next one.

“Two. Momo and Rem had a fight about salt.” ʸ

* * *

They go on like that.

Some of them are nothing. Ear got into the laundry. Kaneki read a whole book upside down, and when Maki told him, he said he knew. Somebody has been leaving very small drawings on the wall in the clinic corridor, in among the names, and it took Maomao nine days to catch that it was Ori.

Some of them are not nothing, and she doesn't change her voice for those.

“Eleven. Gojo slept in the pantry. Three nights. He told Shoko he was checking the stores.” ʸ

“Twelve. Your soldiers. When Shoko closed you up and they let go. I'd thought they would just go. They went one row at a time, and every one of them touched you on the way out. Just with a finger, like when you're checking if a stove is hot. Nobody saw it but me and her, and she told me not to tell you.” ʸ

She crosses that one out.

“I'm telling you,” Yuka says. ʸ

He finds that he needs to look at the tree for a little while. It's a good tree. It goes up a long way, all those shades of nothing, with the light coming through.

She eats some of his bread while he is doing that. She doesn't look at him. It is about the most tactful thing he has ever seen her do.

“Nineteen,” she says, when he is ready. “I hit Yuta.” ʸ

“In the ribs. You told me.” ᴹ

“No, that was practice. This was on the second day. In the kitchen. He said you were going to be fine and I hit him in the face.” ʸ

He looks at her.

“He said thank you,” she says, and her voice has gone strange and flat. “He stood there with his nose running and he said thank you. And Maki gave me a cloth for my hand.” ʸ

She looks at the paper for some time. She doesn't cross that one off. She moves her thumb across it, and then goes on to the next.

* * *

It takes until nearly noon.

People come through the courtyard on their own business. Taihei with a sack. Two of the schoolgirls, arguing. Nobody stops. Rimuru comes as far as the arch, sees the bench and the paper, and turns around with a great show of having forgotten something. There is sun on Mau's left knee, and he is more tired than he'd like to admit, and he wouldn't move for anything.

“Forty,” Yuka says. “I don't remember what I meant by this one. It just says ‘the spoon.’” ʸ

“The spoon?” ᴹ

“It was really funny.” ʸ

“I'm sure.” ᴹ

She frowns at it. She has got pencil on her chin. He doesn't mention that. He has decided it is his, for now.

She looks at the last line for a long time, and then she folds the paper up, along all its soft old creases.

“That's forty,” he says. ᴹ

“I know.” ʸ

“You said forty-one.” ᴹ

“I can count.” ʸ

He waits.

She turns the little square over between her fingers. She is looking across the courtyard, at nothing, and the back of her neck has changed colour. It's the only neck in the world that can.

* * *

She stands up. She sticks the paper in her pocket and brushes the crumbs off herself, without any ceremony, onto his boots.

“Come on,” Yuka says. ʸ

“Where?” ᴹ

“You've been promised something, and you're going to hate it.” ʸ

He looks up at her, squinting. It takes him a moment. It was a long time ago, and his head was not what it is.

“The ribs,” he says. ᴹ

And there it is, finally, the whole of it, spreading across her face, the worst and happiest smile she has.

“Bring your cane,” she says. “You'll want something to lean on.” ʸ
