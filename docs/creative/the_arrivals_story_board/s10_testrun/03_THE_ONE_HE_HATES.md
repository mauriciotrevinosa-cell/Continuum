# The Arrivals — Light Novel — Volume 61
## Chapter 3 — The One He Hates

**Status:** PRUEBA S10 v0.1 (2026-11-04), con marcas para Work. No canon.
**Volume:** 61
**Reading order:** 3
**Source episode:** S10E02
**Continuity state:** B+153 afternoon. Pays "I'll show you. You'll hate it" (V60 #9). Yuka's own growth: training with Maki, what to do when the Garden loses. Mau watches; no training for him. Practice blades. No new technique.

*When: B+153, after lunch. Yuta sees who has come out to the practice yard with her, and he starts to take off his shirt so that Mau can look at the bruise.*

“Put that back,” Maki says. ᴹᵃ

Yuta puts it back.

They give Mau the upturned bucket. He is starting to think of it as his. Maki stands next to him with her arms crossed and a practice spear on her shoulder, and she doesn't look at him at all. She has spent many years not looking at people she is glad to see.

“She's been at this for a week,” she says. “It's not pretty.” ᴹᵃ

Out in the middle of the yard, Yuka rolls her neck. She has a wooden blade in each hand. Her shadow lies on the packed dirt in front of her, and it looks like nothing, like anybody's.

“The Garden loses,” she calls across. “That's the setup. Say you broke it. You got me. I've got no floor, I burned it, I'm out. What do I do?” ʸ

Mau considers the question. He has some experience in this field.

“Fall down?” ᴹ

“Wrong.” ʸ

She looks at Yuta and nods.

He comes at her. He isn't going easy. Mau has fought that man for a long time and knows what his feet do when he means it. The first cut comes in high, and she takes it badly, on the wrong blade. She goes back a step. The second one drives her back two. She's losing. It's quite clear. She opens her hands a little, and her shadow gives a kind of shiver and starts up off the ground, and it's slow and it's smeared, and it comes apart.

There's a soft dry sound, like a sheet tearing.

The Garden has failed. Anyone could see it. There's a scatter of dark on the dirt around her boots, going out, like the last of a fire. She staggers.

Yuta steps in to finish it, and his foot comes down in the scatter.

It's only a little. It's about as much as you could hold in one hand. It's what's left. It has no business doing anything, and it closes on his ankle like a dog that has been told it's dead and has decided to bite someone about that.

He looks down.

She drops both blades. She has no use for them; she never meant to. She comes in under his arm with her whole small body, all the way in, closer than anyone with any sense would come. Her elbow goes in below the last rib with everything she weighs.

Yuta sits down.

He doesn't fall. He just stops being upright. He stays where he is on the dirt with his mouth open, trying to work out how breathing goes.

The yard is quiet for a moment. Somewhere a pigeon is having a difficult time.

“It's *in* the losing,” Yuka says. She's panting, and she's lit up from the inside like a lamp. “See? You make them think it's over. It has to really break, or they can tell. You have to actually lose it. And then you've got maybe a second where they're looking at the wrong thing, and it's so close you can't even use a weapon. That's the whole trick.” ʸ

She turns around to look at him.

“Well?” ʸ

Mau has a number of things going through his mind. He is thinking of a quarry. He's thinking that she has found out how to give up everything she's got, on purpose, in front of an enemy, in order to get within arm's reach. She has built an entire technique out of it. She's got it nearly right, and she's so happy. He would like very much to go and lie down in a dark room.

“I hate it,” he says. ᴹ

She flings both arms in the air.

“*I told you!*” ʸ

“Who taught you that?” ᴹ

“Nobody,” says Maki, beside him, not looking at him. Her voice has no expression in it at all. “She came and told me one morning. Day after you got your hand back. She'd been sitting up there with you, watching you do stupid things to stay alive, and she said she'd had an idea.” ᴹᵃ

He turns his head.

Maki shifts the spear on her shoulder.

“It's a family thing, apparently,” she says. ᴹᵃ

* * *

They do it four more times. It works twice.

The third time, Yuta is ready for it, and he steps over the scatter and puts her on her back. She lies there looking at the sky and swearing at him in a clear, carrying voice. She gets up with dirt in her teeth, and goes straight back to her mark.

Mau stays on his bucket. No one has told him he has to. He's got a bad knee and a sore side, and he's not allowed to lift anything heavier than a chair. He watches every single one. By the fourth, without really meaning to, he has started to look at her feet, and at the half step before it breaks, where she gives it away.

“Your left heel,” he says. ᴹ

She stops, breathing hard, and looks over.

“When you're about to let it go. It comes up. Just a bit. I'd see that.” ᴹ

Yuka looks down at her heel. She looks at him. For a moment he thinks that he may have made a mistake, and that he's about to hear a very clear opinion on people who were recently asked to sit quietly and hate things.

She walks over. She takes the cane out of his hand, without asking, and draws a line in the dirt with the end of it, about where a heel ought to go. She looks at that. She hands it back.

“Show me again what you saw,” she says. ʸ

He gets up.

It's a very small thing. He shows her with his own foot, with one hand on the bucket, and it hurts. She watches him do it, with her head on one side, frowning at his boot. Nobody's looking after anybody. There's a good solid hour of afternoon left, and Yuta hasn't got his breath back. Maki has turned away to look at something, and if you didn't know her, you wouldn't see that she's smiling.
