# 1. Settlement spatial base — unchanged / approved

- Small abandoned settlement surrounded by forest.
- Inn is the largest surviving building and household center.
- Roughly five small abandoned houses surround it; roofs are partially or fully collapsed.
- Nearby houses provide finite salvage: cookware, boards, doors, glass, shelves, cloth, jars, tools, hardware and stone.
- Some houses visibly shrink/disappear over time as materials are reclaimed to repair/expand the inn.
- Small cultivation plot beside the inn, large enough to meaningfully supplement food for roughly ten residents.
- Well beside the inn on the cultivation/service side.
- Low hill with a prominent shade tree behind the inn.
- Narrow path to the lake.
- Former path toward the mercantile settlement is almost completely reclaimed by vegetation and does not initially read as a usable road.
- After discovering the mercantile town, the cast recognizes a route from town that leads back toward home.
- Lake lies on the sunset side of the settlement.
- Lake includes small weathered hut + short dock, but no usable boat initially.
- Lake connects to a river/waterway that can later lead toward the island region.

Visual evolution rule:
> The dead village slowly contracts while the inn becomes more alive.

---

# 2. Inn light / orientation — unchanged / approved

- Bedrooms receive stronger morning light.
- Common areas receive warmer late-afternoon/evening light.
- Lake side corresponds broadly to sunset.
- This orientation is part of visual continuity and should be respected in manga staging.

# 3. Ground floor — approved layout

## Entry

The inn uses a double-entry transition:
1. covered exterior porch/entry beneath the second-floor projection;
2. small shoe-removal / muddy-boots transition area inspired by genkan spatial logic;
3. one raised step into the lived common-room floor.

The threshold visually marks `outside → home`.

## Common room

- large shared table;
- hearth/chimney;
- generous circulation for ensemble scenes;
- space for reading, eating, conversation, maps, guild papers and domestic clutter;
- warm late-day light.

## Kitchen

Open or semi-open to the common room so cooking remains social.

Includes salvaged/repaired:
- shelves;
- work surfaces;
- pots/pans;
- utensils;
- jars;
- food-prep area.

## Underground storage / cellar

Primary bulk storage is subterranean, accessed from the kitchen/service side by small stairs or trapdoor + stairs.

Used for:
- grain;
- root vegetables;
- preserves;
- jars;
- spare cookware;
- salvage inventory;
- later purchased supplies.

## Bathroom / shower / wash area

Ground-floor service zone contains:
- toilet/latrine solution appropriate to the setting;
- washing area;
- shower/bathing capability once repaired;
- sensible water/drainage connection.

## Main stairs

Visible/easy to access from common circulation. The stair/landing is important for overhearing, hesitation and nighttime movement.

## Rear/service exit

Connects directly toward:
- well;
- cultivation plot;
- hill/tree;
- salvage houses;
- lake-side path.

# 4. Upper floor — corrected canonical room plan

The upper floor must provide actual room capacity for the complete G1+G2 household.

## G1+G2 roster

**G1:**
- Frieren
- Mau
- Bocchi
- Yuta
- Rimuru

**G2:**
- Fern
- Anko
- Maomao
- Momo

Total residents represented by room logic: **9**.

Because Bocchi + Fern intentionally share one room after G2 integration, the stable upper-floor plan needs **8 primary sleeping/private rooms** for G1+G2, plus at least one extra/damaged/flexible room.

---

## 4.1 Frieren's room — largest room

The largest room upstairs is technically **Frieren's room**.

Features:
- largest bedroom footprint;
- strong morning light;
- small private or semi-private terrace/balcony;
- enough floor/storage space for two people's belongings.

Critical canon:
- Mau has his own room elsewhere;
- nevertheless, Mau habitually sleeps and spends personal time in Frieren's room;
- his books, cup, clothing and ordinary belongings accumulate there;
- behaviorally it becomes `their room` long before anyone names it that way.

Spatial shorthand:
> `Frieren's room` architecturally → `their room` behaviorally.

E14's `Our room? / ...Yes.` recognizes an existing lived reality rather than creating a brand-new sleeping arrangement.

---

## 4.2 Bocchi + Fern room — shared room

The room directly beside / very near Frieren's is the **Bocchi + Fern room**.

It is somewhat larger than a standard single room so two people can comfortably share it.

Occupancy progression:
1. Bocchi initially occupies it alone.
2. Fern arrives with G2 and initially stays with Frieren.
3. As Fern/Bocchi's sister-like bond develops, Fern moves into Bocchi's room.
4. From that point forward the room is intentionally shared; Fern does **not** require a separate permanent bedroom.

This room supports:
- E14 sleeping-arrangement payoff;
- Bocchi/Fern intimacy and sister-like domestic beats;
- Bocchi soundproofing improvements;
- practical reason Fern can tell Frieren she already has somewhere else to sleep.

---

## 4.3 Mau's nominal room

Mau has a real room of his own.

Placement:
- in the same general wing as Yuta;
- close enough to Frieren's room for the separation arc to remain spatially intimate;
- reachable through the main upstairs corridor/landing.

Function:
- early S1: sparse and rarely used for sleeping;
- gradually becomes study/books/overflow;
- E11–E13: Mau moves belongings back into it and sleeps there more consistently;
- post-E14: remains his nominal/study room while his ordinary sleeping/domestic rhythm returns to Frieren's room.

The house therefore distinguishes:
- `Mau has a room` from
- `Mau lives with Frieren`.

---

## 4.4 Yuta's room

Yuta has his own room near Mau's nominal room.

Narrative utility:
- brother-like conversations;
- spontaneous visits;
- separation-arc scenes where Mau spends time with Yuta;
- Frieren can approach Mau's nominal room, hear Mau talking with Yuta, hesitate at the door and leave without interrupting.

Approved spatial beat:
> Frieren wants to speak with Mau, approaches his door, hears Yuta inside with him, raises/reaches a hand toward the door, then quietly walks away. Mau never realizes she came.

---

## 4.5 Rimuru's room

Rimuru has his own nominal/private room despite not requiring human sleeping conditions in the usual way.

Uses:
- storage;
- private planning;
- personal belongings;
- comic contrast when Rimuru sleeps/works somewhere else anyway.

The important point is that the household still gives Rimuru a place of his own.

---

## 4.6 Momo's room

Momo has her own room.

Recommended placement:
- closer to the stair/social side of the upper corridor;
- easy route downstairs toward common room/kitchen;
- outside the most emotionally loaded Frieren/Mau/Yuta cluster.

---

## 4.7 Maomao's room

Maomao has her own room.

Recommended placement:
- slightly quieter than Momo's;
- reasonable access to stairs/service circulation;
- enough shelving for personal supplies.

Her bedroom does not need to become her complete laboratory. Serious medicine/workspace can expand elsewhere in the inn later.

---

## 4.8 Anko's room

Anko has her own room. This room is mandatory in the canonical plan and must not disappear from visual floor plans.

Recommended placement:
- quieter edge/corner of the upper floor;
- enough wall/window space for a small desk;
- suitable for notebook/writing/observation scenes;
- private without feeling isolated from the family.

---

## 4.9 Extra / damaged / flexible room

At least one additional room remains:
- partially damaged;
- under repair;
- storage/guest/future-arrival capable.

Every architectural room should not be perfectly occupied or restored at the start.

# 6. Hallway / landing requirements

The upper corridor is not merely functional. It must be wide/long enough to stage:
- people stopping outside doors;
- voices heard through doors;
- someone deciding not to knock;
- late-night crossings;
- carrying belongings between rooms;
- E11–E14 separation/reconciliation imagery.

This hallway is part of the emotional geography of the inn.

- small linen/utility closet for bedding/towels/cleaning supplies;
- terrace attached or immediately adjacent to Frieren's large room;
- optional secondary/shared balcony if later visual design supports it;

# 8. E11–E14 spatial continuity

1. Mau technically owns a separate room before G2 arrives but usually lives in Frieren's room.
2. Fern returns and initially sleeps/stays with Frieren.
3. Mau begins moving belongings into his nominal room to give Frieren and Fern space.
4. Frieren reads Mau's move as independence / reduced need for her.
5. Mau reads Frieren adapting to his move as confirmation he was temporary.
6. Fern later moves in with Bocchi.
7. Bocchi + Fern's shared room gives Fern a real sleeping arrangement and makes E14's `Fern kicked me out` beat spatially honest.
8. Frieren reclaims Mau's belongings / shared domestic rhythm.
9. Mau's nominal room continues to exist afterward; reconciliation does not erase his independence.

# 9. Well micro-beat — retained

Rimuru inspects the well, discovers the water is not yet potable, goes down to clean/repair the situation, then launches himself comedically out of the well once finished. Yuta or another household member sees the ridiculous exit; Rimuru is briefly embarrassed despite having solved a real domestic problem.

The scene should establish both world function and character comedy.
