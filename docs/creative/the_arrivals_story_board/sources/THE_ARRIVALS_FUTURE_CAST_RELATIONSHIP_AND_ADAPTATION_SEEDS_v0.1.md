## 0. Purpose and precedence

This document preserves creator discussion about long-range cast growth, relationship architecture, franchise adaptation and Mau's emotional role.

Nothing here should silently override:
- approved S1 manga-ready material;
- current Mau/Frieren direction;
- existing character arrival-state decisions;
- source character identity;
- later explicit creator approvals.

Use this document as a **development reservoir**. A later story audit may promote, revise, split, relocate or reject individual ideas.

Important distinction:

> A character being loved by the creator, important to Mau, or emotionally central does **not** automatically make that character a romantic partner.

## 1. Cast scale: large world, not one giant room

The project may eventually pull from roughly **54 source families/franchises** as a planning-scale estimate. Even an average of only two characters per source family would imply 100+ possible characters.

The solution is **not** to make every source protagonist a protagonist of `The Arrivals`.

Working cast-importance model:

- **Core** — structural spine of the overall story; decisions reshape the main work.
- **Main Ensemble** — recurring major characters with POV, arcs and meaningful influence.
- **Arc Main** — can function almost like a protagonist during one arc, then recede.
- **Recurring** — emotionally or functionally important without constant page presence.
- **Reserve / World Cast** — exists in the world and can later rise in importance.
- **Faction / Antagonist Cast** — primarily anchored to another region, organization or conflict.

These levels are **fluid**, not permanent.

Examples of valid movement:

```text
Reserve -> Arc Main -> Main Ensemble
Main Ensemble -> Recurring -> Arc Main
```

The guiding principle is:

> **Mau has relational gravity, not narrative monopoly.**

Mau can remain the central axis of the work without appearing in every episode/chapter.

Future macro-arcs may follow several simultaneous threads:
- Mau/Frieren / settlement;
- Momo/Okarun or another expedition;
- a distant Arrival group trying to reach the settlement;
- hidden Otherworlders;
- native political/faction activity;
- Sukuna / antagonists;
- independent communities.

Importance is not measured by constant screen time.

## 2. Arrival state is decided per character

Do **not** define one universal franchise-level rule for:
- body age/state;
- memory endpoint;
- power compatibility;
- equipment;
- source-world chronology.

Each Arrival can have a distinct snapshot.

Yuta remains the clearest existing example: a younger body can coexist with memories extending much later in life.

The same principle can later apply independently to Gojo, Rem, Subaru, or any other character. Do not infer their states from Yuta or from franchise-wide assumptions.

A future Character/Franchise Adaptation Registry should record per character:
- source identity;
- selected arrival snapshot;
- body state;
- memory endpoint;
- power state;
- retained relationships;
- intentional Continuum divergences;
- open questions;
- approval status.

## 3. Mau as creator-proximate protagonist

Creator-facing design note:

> Mau is intentionally creator-proximate / self-insert-inspired.

This should inform emotional plausibility, but it does **not** remove the need for internal character consistency.

Useful two-step test:

1. Would this emotional reaction feel authentic to the creator-proximate core of Mau?
2. Does it make sense for the Mau who has actually lived through the story so far?

Mau should become a coherent person inside Continuum rather than a consequence-free wish-fulfillment container.

---

## 4. Mau's relational essence

The preferred core idea is **not**:

> everyone falls in love with Mau.

It is:

> Mau repeatedly helps people without expecting payment, loyalty or romance; that makes him deeply important to many different people.

Possible resulting bonds include:
- romantic love;
- family;
- sibling-like attachment;
- friendship;
- admiration;
- loyalty;
- mentorship in either direction;
- protection;
- chosen-community attachment.

The people close to Mau must also have:
- relationships with one another;
- goals independent of Mau;
- conflicts independent of Mau;
- work and responsibilities;
- reasons to live even when Mau is off-page.

This prevents the cast from becoming “people waiting for their turn to talk to Mau.”

---

## 5. Current romantic orientation — working direction

The creator strongly prefers **monogamy** as the emotional default, while still enjoying harem comedy as fiction.

Current active story direction remains:
- **Frieren is Mau's central romantic love / intended partner.**
- broader old multi-romance/harem material remains historical/open rather than active direction;
- do not dismantle Mau/Frieren merely because future beloved characters arrive.

This is not a rule that no other character can ever develop feelings for Mau.

Important distinction:

```text
could fall in love with someone
!=
must build a simultaneous romantic relationship with them
```

A person can remain profoundly important to Mau without becoming his partner.

## 6. Prospect / attachment notes discussed by creator

These are **not a ranking** and do not automatically create romance.

### Frieren

- explicitly loved by the creator;
- current romantic endpoint for Mau;
- relationship has already accumulated major structural weight;
- should not be discarded casually to “make room” for later characters.

### Anko

Creator originally saw genuine romantic potential before Mau/Frieren became so developed.

Favored present possibility:
- intimate adult friendship;
- dry humor / provocation / mutual understanding;
- strong chemistry without needing to activate romance.

A romantic version could have worked in another branch of the story, but current material does not require it.

### Nijika

Strongly loved by the creator.

Working instinct:
- emotional/home stabilizer;
- social glue;
- someone Mau can love deeply as family/core friend;
- does not presently require romance.

### Rem

This is the strongest unresolved emotional complication raised in discussion.

Creator loved Rem strongly from `Re:Zero` S1 and wants to avoid treating her suffering as disposable.

Do **not** decide in advance:
- “Rem must become Mau's partner,” or
- “Rem is forbidden from ever having feelings because Frieren already exists.”

Preferred development rule:

> Let Rem live in Continuum, build independent relationships and a life, and discover what her bond with Mau actually becomes.

Rem should not receive Mau as a “reward for suffering,” and she should not need an emergency replacement romance to prove that her life ended happily.

Possible outcome:
- Core / emotionally profound;
- perhaps romantic feelings emerge;
- perhaps not;
- perhaps feelings transform over time;
- decision deferred until lived story supports it.

### Ori

Ori has unusually high long-range potential because she is Continuum-original and tied to the goddess.

Core origin direction:
- the goddess is fascinated by love, especially how Mau loves Frieren;
- Ori is created partly from that fascination / attempt to understand love;
- Ori develops beyond the creator's initial purpose and becomes an individual.

Favored emotional evolution:

```text
Mau = first stable attachment
-> Ori interprets attachment as romance
-> experiments with romance / wants reciprocation
-> learns Mau does not reciprocate romantically
-> pain is real and not villainized
-> understands that love does not create entitlement
-> chooses family / belonging
-> continues loving Mau in a transformed way
-> comes to admire/love Frieren as family too
```

Desired endpoint flavor is closer to:

> “I know I may not be your number one, but you are still one of the most important people in my world.”

Ori should not be framed as “the loser of a love triangle.”

Her larger thematic value:
- what makes someone real;
- created purpose vs chosen identity;
- romantic love vs friendship vs family vs admiration;
- loving someone without possessing them.

Future seeds may include:
- Ori helping Mau/Frieren rather than competing with Frieren;
- Ori making a controversial choice for Mau's wellbeing;
- Ori's link to the goddess becoming narratively essential.

### Bocchi

Preferred Mau/Bocchi direction remains **chosen sibling / older-brother-younger-sister** rather than romance.

The existing care callback chain is more valuable if kept non-romantic.

Creator also enjoys Bocchi/Kita as a possible relationship space worth allowing to breathe.

## 7. Large emotional nucleus without harem structure

A useful model is not “six girls waiting for Mau.”

It is a **large central family/community** in which different people occupy different emotional roles.

Examples already discussed:
- Frieren -> partner / romantic life;
- Bocchi -> chosen younger sister;
- Yuta -> brother-like / family / combat trust;
- Okarun -> close friendship potential;
- Rimuru -> deep peer relationship around responsibility, systems and caring for many people;
- Ori -> chosen family with complicated love history;
- Rem -> unresolved, potentially profound;
- Anko -> intimate adult friendship potential;
- Nijika -> home/social-core role.

A character may be absent for 100+ pages and still remain emotionally important.

## 8. Qifrey / Coco relational seed

Possible future dynamic:

Qifrey may recognize something others normalize:
- Mau looks like an adult;
- speaks and functions at an adult-like level;
- but has almost no lived personal history.

Mau is not merely amnesiac under the current working premise.

His remembered continuity begins inside Continuum.

Qifrey's experience caring for / teaching Coco gives him a useful lens for noticing that Mau is learning the world without the childhood/background everyone assumes an adult possesses.

Coco can become a meaningful peer/friend to Mau through:
- curiosity;
- learning;
- discovering rules that others treat as obvious;
- shared wonder, despite radically different histories.

Neither needs to become “a Mau character.” Their own relationships and story remain primary.

## 9. Rentarou / 100 Girlfriends adaptation seed

The creator likes many `100 Girlfriends` characters, including Nano, Karane, Shizuka and others, without wanting to redirect them all romantically toward Mau.

Preferred adaptation experiment:

> Continuum may not preserve the original world's supernatural “love-at-first-sight / soulmate or death” compulsion exactly.

Important protection:
- do **not** retroactively declare that Rentarou or his girlfriends never loved each other;
- the supernatural origin of a bond can differ from the reality of what was built afterward.

Potential Continuum question:

> If the metaphysical requirement disappears, what do these people freely choose now?

Possible outcomes:
- some remain romantically with Rentarou;
- some realize the relationship has changed;
- some need time;
- some develop other relationships later;
- no one must be paired off merely for closure.

Rentarou can be treated with less constant gag escalation while preserving his extraordinary capacity to care.

Strong thematic possibility:
- Rentarou admits that fear of the soulmate-death rule influenced his initial choices;
- nevertheless, he genuinely grew to love them;
- in Continuum he is willing to let each person choose freely even if it means losing relationships.

Possible lateral relationships should matter as much as Rentarou-centered ones (examples discussed: Hakari/Karane, Nano/Shizuka, without pre-deciding romance vs friendship).

Shizuka can become deeply important to Mau as a friend without romantic conversion.

Rentarou himself may become a strong male friendship for Mau: two people who care intensely for many others but reach different conclusions about romance and responsibility.

## 10. Franchise adaptation should create new choices, not erase history

General rule:

> When Continuum removes or changes a supernatural rule from a source world, it should not erase the choices and relationships characters already lived through under that rule.

Instead, it creates **new decisions from this point forward**.

A future adaptation file should separate:
- source fact;
- arrival state;
- rules that still function;
- rules Continuum alters/rejects;
- consequences;
- character choices after gaining new freedom.

---

## 11. World-scale rule changes from new franchises

Possible long-range world mechanic:

New Arrival families may introduce or activate phenomena that previously did not exist or were not observable.

Example direction inspired by `Re:Zero`:
- strange spirits begin appearing;
- mana behavior changes;
- existing creatures react differently;
- characters notice the phenomenon **before** finding the new Arrivals.

This can preserve Arrival mystery.

Important guardrail:

> New systems should generally **expand** Continuum's rule space rather than retroactively invalidate everything already established.

Continuum may:
- assimilate some rules;
- reject some;
- transform some.

Do not assume “a franchise arrives -> its entire cosmology instantly replaces local reality.”

## 12. Season / macro-arc model

The labels “S1”, “S2”, etc. are increasingly functioning as **macro-era / macro-arc labels**, not strict anime cour sizes.

S1 is relatively concentrated:
- first arrivals;
- survival;
- first home;
- G1/G2;
- learning to live;
- world opening;
- Sukuna rupture.

Later macro-seasons may contain many simultaneous arcs.

Do not force equal page counts between S1 and later macro-seasons.

Current S1 figure remains roughly **1,304 provisional pages before final pacing audit**.

Long-range page-count discussion is planning only, **not a production target**. The project may become many thousands of pages if the story earns that scale.
