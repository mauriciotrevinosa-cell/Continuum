# The Arrivals — Future Macro-Arcs: Trust, Foundation, Memory Fracture & Reconstruction v0.1

**Status:** PROVISIONAL / PRESERVE FOR FUTURE STORY AUDIT — NOT FULL CANON  
**Date:** 2026-09-25  
**Project:** `The Arrivals`  
**Branch written:** `m3/critical-path`

## 0. Purpose and precedence

This document preserves a long-range development session covering:
- why the post-S1 macro-story may be much larger than S1;
- G3 integration and trust;
- contracts;
- Rimuru's possible `Foundation` mechanism;
- its thematic relationship to Mau's later one-use Hollow Purple construction;
- post-Sukuna autosacrifice consequences;
- a much later Mau memory/perception crisis;
- Ori, `Re:Zero`, the witches and Tensura Primordials inside that future arc;
- the requirement that major arcs permanently change characters rather than reset them.

These are **working seeds**, not episode locks.

Do not promote exact costs, villains, mechanics, page counts, dialogue, timelines or cast placement without later creator approval.

---

## 1. “S2” is a macro-era containing several arcs

The creator originally used seasons because S1 had a concrete shape.

The later “S2” concept now behaves more like a large macro-era that can include multiple concurrent arcs:
- G3 joining G1/G2;
- learning whether/how to trust newcomers;
- social fallout from Sukuna;
- Otherworlder fear;
- debate over stay / fortify / relocate;
- fortification of the current base;
- search for a long-term home;
- scattered Arrival groups elsewhere;
- possible G4/G5/G6 and other batches;
- contracts;
- Foundation;
- migration;
- early new-settlement construction;
- parallel regional stories.

It is acceptable for this macro-era to be substantially larger than S1 if the relationships/world actually deepen.

Do not use page count as a quota.

A rough brainstorming range of **~2,000–3,000 pages** was discussed only as scale intuition, not a target or promise.

## 2. Trust after G3 — transferred trust is not automatic trust

Possible setup:
- G3 is still being integrated;
- some existing characters can strongly vouch for specific newcomers;
- e.g. Momo can vouch for Okarun;
- Bocchi can vouch for Kita.

But:

> “I trust you, and you trust them” does not automatically equal full trust.

This can create an arc about:
- what information to share;
- what powers to reveal;
- how much trust can be transferred socially;
- what must still be earned directly.

This is a natural place to introduce early **Contracts**.

## 3. Contracts — start small, scale with the world

Possible Continuum mechanic:

Contracts begin limited and understandable.

Early examples might cover:
- confidentiality;
- bounded promises;
- a small mana cost;
- temporary inability to disclose;
- another modest consequence.

As Continuum evolves and more systems interact, contracts may become much more powerful:
- ability exchange;
- technique restrictions;
- memory locks;
- territorial bindings;
- cost transfer;
- group oaths;
- extreme conditions enabling otherwise impossible constructions.

Important rule:

> Contracts should not begin as a universal broken power system.

Their growth should be visible over time.

This can eventually become part of the grammar that makes Foundation and Mau's one-use construction feel earned.

## 4. Rimuru — early discovery of an unknown system function

A possible early post-S1 scene:

Raphael tells Rimuru she has detected something unknown inside / adjacent to his system.

At first:
- Raphael cannot classify it;
- Rimuru cannot simply use it;
- the pact group may discuss it before G3 is fully trusted;
- it becomes a long-running background thread.

Working name:

> **Foundation**

The name and exact classification remain provisional.

---

## 5. Foundation — long setup, late payoff

Foundation should **not** be a convenient city-building button.

Preferred progression:

```text
Raphael detects anomaly
-> research
-> discover conditions
-> realize the cost is severe
-> gather requirements gradually
-> other characters help satisfy conditions
-> Rimuru debates whether using it is worth the loss
-> ability is held in reserve
-> eventual one-time / extremely limited use when the long-term settlement requires it
```

Characters can periodically ask Rimuru how progress is going.

Example rhythm:
- “We have three requirements.”
- “The fourth is the problem.”
- later a distant expedition unexpectedly solves one requirement.

This keeps the thread alive without turning the story into resource-farming chapters.

---

## 6. Possible Foundation cost

Exact mechanics remain OPEN.

Candidates discussed:
- permanent level loss;
- temporary or long-term skill lock;
- sacrificing an existing ability;
- consuming a large accumulated resource;
- converting a valuable skill into the construction itself;
- a combination of the above.

The cost must be high enough that Rimuru can reasonably hesitate even after completing the prerequisites.

Do not decide the exact sacrifice until Rimuru's power state and the settlement arc are fully designed.

---

## 7. Foundation's use — build the board, not the city

Possible long-term location remains island / island-adjacent.

Foundation could allow Rimuru to reshape terrain at settlement scale:
- create / extend mountains;
- connect selected islands;
- leave other channels intentionally open;
- create natural fortification;
- improve harbors;
- create defensible corridors;
- reserve future expansion zones;
- shape drainage / water access;
- protect agricultural areas;
- separate dangerous industrial zones;
- prepare future infrastructure routes.

Rimuru should think beyond immediate shelter because he has nation-building experience.

But:

> **Foundation creates the physical board. The people still build the city.**

After Foundation, the cast still needs:
- roads;
- housing;
- sanitation;
- water systems;
- farms;
- workshops;
- hospitals;
- schools;
- trade;
- defense;
- institutions.

This keeps the settlement collective rather than “Rimuru builds Tempest 2.”

## 8. Foundation as precedent for Mau's one-use Hollow Purple

Existing future direction already favors Mau eventually constructing an unstable, one-use Hollow Purple-like phenomenon after gaining partial understanding through Yuta/memory access.

The discussed design connection is:

```text
partial understanding
+ Continuum adaptation
+ strict conditions
+ real cost
= temporary impossible construction
```

Foundation can teach the reader this grammar first in a constructive context.

Later Mau uses a far more desperate/destructive version.

Mau should not simply “copy Limitless.”

His construction has:
- its own conditions;
- incompleteness;
- severe penalties;
- one-use / non-durable nature;
- consequences closer in spirit to a brutal binding-vow logic, but not necessarily identical to JJK binding vows.

## 9. Post-Sukuna promise — preserve the existing layer

Current saved direction already states that after Mau survives Sukuna, Frieren's anger is not simply “you protected me.”

It is that Mau:
- accepted his own death as an acceptable cost;
- made the decision alone;
- hid a secret of that magnitude;
- failed to consider what his death would do to her.

The promise is:

> **no more unilateral sacrifice / secrets of that magnitude**

not:

> never risk yourself again.

Future arcs must respect this.

---

## 10. Mau's autosacrifice wound should heal before it is attacked again

A later dark arc should **not** reveal that Mau “never learned anything.”

Preferred direction:

- the post-Sukuna relationship work genuinely helps;
- Mau learns his life matters;
- he keeps the promise;
- the wound is meaningfully healed.

Only later does an external manipulation reopen it.

This makes the later fall tragic rather than a reset.

---

## 11. Mau's first memory — critical identity fact

Working premise to preserve:

> Mau has **zero memories before opening his eyes in Continuum and seeing Frieren looking at him.**

That is his first remembered moment.

This makes memory/perception attacks uniquely dangerous:
- other Arrivals can anchor themselves in a life before Continuum;
- Mau cannot;
- corrupting his Continuum memories attacks essentially his entire autobiographical continuity.

---

## 12. Later memory/perception crisis — placement

Preferred placement is **well after Sukuna**, not immediately afterward.

By then:
- Mau/Frieren are already in a real relationship;
- Mau has healed substantially;
- a mini-city / functioning settlement exists;
- there is a large network of people who love and know Mau;
- the reader feels secure in Mau's identity.

Only then is it worth attacking his certainty about reality.

Desired total in-world duration from early fracture through escape(s), rescue and return to functional new normal:

> roughly **no more than about one month**

This can still span many chapters/pages, but should not feel like years of paralysis.

---

## 13. Symptom progression — start tiny

Early symptoms should be plausibly dismissible.

Examples:
- Mau puts an object down, later searches for it somewhere else;
- remembers storing something in a different drawer;
- remembers the end of a conversation slightly differently;
- believes a dream was a waking event;
- remembers visiting a place where others say he never went.

Do not begin with immediate obvious insanity.

The horror comes from escalation and uncertainty.

---

## 14. Attack model — not only “fake memories”

Preferred toolbox includes several categories:

- dreams consolidated into autobiographical memory;
- fully implanted experiences;
- real memories with altered dialogue/details;
- emotional valence changed while facts remain;
- hidden/blocked memories later resurfacing;
- memories whose origin cannot be proven;
- potentially information from elsewhere presented as if Mau experienced it.

Avoid committing to time loops / alternate timelines merely because they are available.

If the project ever uses timelines, creator preference is closer to `Dark`:
- serious causality;
- structural consequences;
- no casual reset button.

The memory arc does **not** require timelines.

---

## 15. The reopened wound

Because the old autosacrifice wound was healed, manipulation can weaponize earlier fears in a more devastating way.

Possible false conclusion Mau develops:

> “Maybe Continuum created me without a past because I was never meant to have one. Maybe I only existed to help everyone else become independent.”

The growing city and successful relationships can be misread:
- Frieren has Fern;
- Bocchi has Kita and others;
- Okarun has Momo;
- Rimuru can lead;
- the settlement functions;
- everyone has a widening network.

Objectively, this proves Mau helped build a thriving family.

Under manipulation, Mau can interpret it as:

> “They finished needing me.”

The deeper wound becomes:

> “Maybe that was all I was made for.”

---

## 16. Manipulation should use truth fragments

A fully fabricated villain monologue is less interesting than manipulation built from real fragments.

Possible method:
- true locations;
- real people;
- sentences actually spoken at other times;
- reordered context;
- altered ending;
- dream material inserted into an otherwise real memory.

This makes later correction harder.

Even after proving a memory was manipulated, Mau can still ask:

> “But what if the conclusion was still true?”

---

## 17. Frieren confrontation — first concrete hope

At some point Frieren reaches Mau while he is pulling away.

Mau tells her **exactly what he remembers hearing her say**.

Frieren becomes confused rather than simply angry:
- the setting may be real;
- some details may be real;
- the conversation is not.

This is the moment when Frieren gains hope that:

> Mau is not simply choosing to abandon them. Something else is happening.

It also reframes the conflict for the wider group.

---

## 18. First rescue is not the end

Desired structure:

```text
fracture
-> disappearance
-> apparent enemy
-> first rescue
-> return home
-> relief
-> inconsistencies return
-> realization that the damage persists
-> true reconstruction arc
```

The first rescue should be **real help**, not a fake-out where nothing mattered.

They genuinely stabilize and protect Mau.

They simply discover that removing immediate danger did not reconstruct what was damaged.

---

## 19. Reconstruction scene with Frieren

A favored small scene shortly after the first rescue:

- Frieren recounts an early shared memory from her perspective;
- Mau adds a mundane detail she deliberately did not mention;
- the detail is correct;
- Frieren freezes / smiles;
- Mau notices.

Possible exchange in spirit:

> Mau: “Did that actually happen?”  
> Frieren: “Yes.”

The emotional value is that Mau has recovered something **from inside himself**, not merely accepted somebody else's account.

Keep the scene small.

---

## 20. Memory reconstruction should be collaborative

Do not use a simple magical TRUE/FALSE scanner.

Possible categories during recovery:
- provably false;
- real but altered;
- dream-derived;
- implanted;
- authentic but emotionally manipulated;
- blocked then recovered;
- permanently uncertain.

Different people can corroborate different parts of Mau's history:
- Frieren — earliest continuity;
- Yuta — shared travel/conflict;
- Rimuru — system records/observations;
- Bocchi — domestic/relationship memories;
- Okarun — specific friendship moments;
- Coco — drawings/places;
- Ori — special continuity;
- settlement records and mundane evidence.

The group slowly builds a **corroborated history of Mau**, but the goal is not to make other people the permanent owners of his identity.

---

## 21. Identity endpoint — choice becomes stronger than perfect memory

The recovery should eventually shift Mau from:

> “How do I prove this memory is mine?”

toward:

> “I may not be able to prove every part of yesterday, but I can choose who I am today.”

Core thematic endpoint:

> **Mau does not need a perfectly verified past to choose his future.**

This also answers the old “what was I made for?” wound.

---

## 22. Mau slowly returns through behavior

During reconstruction, people may ask Mau to rest and stop helping.

He initially complies.

Then, without planning it:
- sees someone struggling and helps carry something;
- explains the settlement to a lost newcomer;
- sits with someone frightened;
- fixes a small problem;
- offers help exactly as he used to.

Frieren or another close person may notice before Mau does.

Important realization:

> Mau's kindness is not merely a memory file. It reappears in present choices.

Possible powerful response to Mau's old “I must be useful to belong” wound:

Mau asks:

> “Do you need help?”

Someone answers:

> “No. But you can stay.”

The lesson becomes:

> **Mau does not need to be necessary in order to be loved.**

---

## 23. Permanent aftereffects — no reset to status quo

When the arc ends, Mau can again:
- laugh;
- work;
- help;
- live normally.

But he should be a **new normal**, not a restored save file.

Possible residual changes:
- occasionally asks “Do you remember it that way too?” when something important feels inconsistent;
- greater comfort admitting uncertainty;
- stronger distinction between being useful and being loved;
- less willingness to treat his life as expendable.

Frieren also changes:
- she may verbalize affection/desire more often;
- “I want you here,” “I missed you,” etc.;
- still Frieren, not suddenly hyper-verbal.

The family changes:
- they learn that “Mau looks fine” is not proof he is fine;
- they become better at noticing him rather than only receiving his help.

Principle:

> **Arcs end. Their consequences do not.**

## 24. Ori as the stable anchor during the second collapse

A strong future seed:

After the first rescue, Mau's inconsistencies return.

For reasons possibly tied to Ori's goddess-derived existence, Mau notices something unusual:

> memories involving Ori remain comparatively consistent while other memories are contaminated.

Do not lock the exact metaphysics yet.

Possible emotional logic:
- Mau does not trust Ori merely because he likes her;
- he trusts her because she is the one pattern that still behaves consistently.

This powerfully inverts Ori's own identity question:
- Ori once wondered whether she was “real” or merely a created instrument;
- later she may become the one reality Mau can still recognize.

## 25. Mau confides in Ori and plans to leave

Possible scene:

Mau tells Ori:
- the memories are returning / changing;
- he believes there is a person/place/source connected to it;
- staying in the settlement is making it harder to know what is real;
- he plans to leave and investigate.

Ori first wants to tell Frieren.

Mau refuses, not from lack of love, but because he believes Frieren will stop him.

He may explicitly acknowledge:

> “Because she loves me.”

The tragedy is that even knowing Frieren loves him no longer proves to Mau that his remembered evidence for that love is trustworthy.

Ori concludes:

> “Then I'm coming with you.”

---

## 26. Ori does not tell Frieren

This should **not** be framed as romantic theft or betrayal.

By this stage Ori may love/adore Frieren as family.

Her reasoning can be:

> If I tell Frieren, Mau will run alone. If I go with Mau, at least he is not alone.

This creates a serious conflict when Frieren learns Ori knew.

Frieren may ask:

> “Why didn't you tell me?”

Ori can answer in substance:

> “Because he would have gone without me.”

The painful part is that Frieren may recognize the logic.

This is a major test of trust between Frieren and Ori.

---

## 27. Frieren's arc — loving someone she cannot fix

By this point Frieren and Mau should already be an established couple.

That lets the arc ask a more mature question than “will they get together?”

Frieren is:
- powerful;
- experienced;
- normally competent;
- one of the people who knows Mau best.

But this problem cannot be solved by:
- mana;
- combat;
- simply loving harder;
- telling Mau “trust me.”

Mau may genuinely answer:

> “I'm trying.”

Frieren has to experience:
- helplessness;
- uncertainty;
- fear that she cannot reach someone she loves;
- pain that Ori can reach a part of Mau she currently cannot.

This is **not** ordinary romantic jealousy.

The truth can both hurt and relieve her:
- Mau still loves Frieren;
- his damaged perception prevents that love from functioning as reliable evidence of reality;
- Ori, for a specific metaphysical reason, currently cuts through the noise.

Frieren eventually has to trust Ori.

---

## 28. Ori's payoff — from wanting to be chosen to helping Mau return

This arc can become a major payoff for Ori's earlier love development.

Earlier Ori might have wanted Mau to choose her over Frieren.

Later:
- Ori temporarily becomes the one person Mau trusts most;
- she does **not** use that position to replace Frieren;
- she uses it to help Mau find his way back to himself, Frieren and the family.

That demonstrates Ori's growth from:
- possession / romantic uncertainty;
to:
- chosen family / selfless attachment / independent identity.

Ori remains important afterward. She does not become disposable once Mau trusts Frieren again.

---

## 29. The witches — suspected threat, possible unexpected protectors

The witches may initially look like the obvious cause:
- dreams;
- obsession;
- perception;
- memory disturbance;
- unusual interest in Mau.

But a stronger direction is:

> **They are not necessarily the ones implanting the ideas.**

They may be genuinely interested in Mau and still become furious that something else is manipulating him.

“Treasure” can mean different things to different witches:
- intellectual fascination;
- emotional fixation;
- anomaly value;
- something else entirely.

They do not need uniform motives.

Possible reversal:

```text
everyone suspects witches
-> witches are found
-> they reveal / detect external interference
-> uncomfortable alliance
```

Do not decide the true manipulator yet.

## 31. Subaru — anomaly becomes normal

In `Re:Zero`, Subaru's otherworldly status is extraordinary.

In Continuum:
- being from another world is increasingly common;
- Subaru is no longer uniquely “the impossible outsider.”

Possible major change:

> Return by Death may not function here, or may not function in the form Subaru expects.

This should be treated as a massive psychological shift if used.

Initial consequence:
- terror: death may simply be death.

Later liberation:
- Subaru no longer has to solve everything by sacrificing himself and resetting;
- he can learn to live/help without being the person who privately absorbs every failure.

Subaru may recognize Mau's self-destructive responsibility pattern before others because he has lived a different version of it.

He can be horrified by Mau behaving like someone with infinite retries when Mau has none.

Possible later inclusion of:
- Diablo / Black;

Potential role:

## 34. Second escape / “we found him” vs “we brought him back”

A strong structure is:

First problem:
> Find Mau.

Later realization:
> Finding Mau physically was not the same as bringing him back psychologically.

After the first rescue:
- people genuinely help;
- Mau returns home;
- things look better;
- then a small inconsistency reopens the wound.

Mau may eventually leave again with Ori.

This second departure can be more painful precisely because:
- he is not simply kidnapped;
- he makes the choice under damaged epistemic conditions;
- Frieren cannot solve it by defeating an enemy.

## 35. Desired ending state

Do **not** end with:

> spell removed -> Mau is exactly who he was before.

End with:
- functional life restored;
- relationship with Frieren intact but evolved;
- Ori permanently deepened as family;
- Mau more comfortable with uncertainty;
- community better at caring for Mau;
- some memories perhaps permanently unverifiable;
- Mau no longer requiring perfect autobiographical certainty to justify living.

Possible thematic statement:

> “I may not know whether every memory is true. I know who I love, where I want to be, and who I choose to be tomorrow.”

Exact dialogue is not locked.
