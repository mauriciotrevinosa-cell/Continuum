# The Arrivals — Mau / Frieren Post-Revival Promise Beat v0.1

Status: **PROVISIONAL S2 EMOTIONAL BEAT — CREATOR-DIRECTED, NOT EPISODE-LOCKED**

Purpose: preserve the small but necessary emotional consequence after Mau survives the Sukuna / Second Chance arc. This is not meant to become a second major crisis. It is a quiet repair scene between Mau and Frieren before they finally sleep / settle down after the ordeal.

## Placement

Most likely after the immediate reunion / awakening and before the broader celebratory meal or final emotional cooldown.

The scene can happen late at night or just before sleep, when the public relief has faded and Mau and Frieren are finally alone enough to address what happened.

## Core conflict

Frieren is angry because Mau's plan knowingly accepted his own death as an acceptable cost.

The problem is not that he wanted to save her.

The problem is that he decided alone that his life could be spent for hers, without giving Frieren any agency in that decision and without fully considering what his death would do to her.

This should remain a small intimate disagreement, not a prolonged breakup or melodramatic fight.

## Tone

Frieren can still sound like Frieren: restrained, direct, emotionally simple on the surface, with the weight coming from what the words mean rather than from shouting.

A small comic line can survive inside the emotion, for example:

> `If you die again, I'll kill you.`

The contradiction is intentional and affectionate, but the boundary underneath it is serious.

## Essential exchange

Exact wording is not locked, but the emotional structure is:

Frieren tells Mau that he cannot do something like that again.

She establishes that there should not be secrets of that scale between them.

Mau answers approximately:

> `That was the first and last secret I'll ever keep from you.`

He explains why he made the choice:

> `I couldn't imagine a world where you weren't there.`

Frieren answers with the realization Mau failed to consider:

> `And you think I can live in a world without you?`

That line is the real emotional turn.

Mau understands that his sacrifice was not only an act of love; it also assumed his own absence would be easier for Frieren to bear than hers would be for him.

He apologizes sincerely.

They do not need a long explanation afterward.

They look at each other, close the distance, and embrace.

## Story function

This beat prevents the Sukuna sacrifice from being framed only as noble heroism.

It establishes a relationship rule for Mau and Frieren:

- protecting each other does not mean deciding alone that one life is expendable;
- love does not grant either of them the right to disappear for the other without discussion when another path may exist;
- major secrets are not acceptable between them going forward;
- Mau's instinct to sacrifice himself must now coexist with the knowledge that his life has value to Frieren too.

For Frieren, this is another step in consciously understanding love while the person is still alive in front of her.

For Mau, it is the first direct lesson that loving Frieren does not mean treating himself as disposable.

## Guardrails

- Keep the scene compact.
- Do not undo the emotional truth of Mau's choice during the Sukuna fight.
- Do not make Frieren dismiss or mock what he did.
- Do not turn the scene into a breakup threat.
- Do not make Mau promise he will never risk himself again; the promise is about secrecy and unilateral self-sacrifice, not cowardice.
- Preserve warmth at the end.
- An embrace is enough to close the beat.
- This scene should help them finally rest, not launch another full episode of conflict.
