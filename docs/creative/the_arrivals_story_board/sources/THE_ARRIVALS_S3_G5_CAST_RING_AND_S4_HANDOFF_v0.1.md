**Project:** The Arrivals

## 0. Core correction

G5 is **not**:
- a franchise showcase;
- an excuse to import more protagonists;
- a requirement that every active source family gain a new character.

G5 exists primarily to:
1. close emotionally important gaps around people already in the Mau/core network;
2. give the current ensemble missing relationships from their own worlds;
3. enlarge Mau's **second relational ring** without turning every arrival into Core;
4. materially help an unfinished settlement during the Memory crisis;
5. let strangers learn who Mau is first through the **hole his disappearance leaves**;
6. demonstrate that the world is already changing beyond the settlement.

Guiding rule:

> Continuum imports people, not franchise checklists.

A character not appearing in G5 does **not** mean they cannot exist later in The Arrivals.

# 2. G5 formation mechanism — Diablo's accidental haul

G5 forms because **Diablo is searching for Mau**.

Correct chain:

```text
Mau disappears
-> Frieren deteriorates over ~2 days of searching / almost no sleep
-> Diablo reaches the old inn looking for Rimuru
-> Frieren identifies an unknown absurdly powerful demon near her home
-> exhausted Frieren attacks first
-> Rimuru stops the clash and explains Diablo is from his world
-> Diablo learns who Mau is
-> Diablo accepts the search mission
-> search criteria are necessarily broad because Mau's signature/body/system are unstable
-> Diablo repeatedly finds anomalous Otherworlders and thinks he may finally have Mau
-> he returns proud / increasingly less certain
-> each false positive becomes another Arrival at the inn
-> G5 forms accidentally around Mau's absence
-> later Diablo detects the separate cluster of **eight signatures**
-> that is the witches/Mau-search lead, not the origin of G5
```

Milim is an exception:
- Diablo encounters her while moving/searching;
- Milim recognizes Diablo;
- she asks what is happening / where Rimuru is;
- Diablo brings her to the inn;
- she is part of this arrival window but not necessarily a genuine "Mau false positive."

Running emotional gag:
- the door opens;
- for a fraction of a second the household hopes Diablo brought Mau;
- it is someone else;
- the newcomer asks some version of **"Who is Mau?"**

The gag must become progressively bittersweet rather than only comedy.

# 5. What G5 does while Mau is still missing

New arrivals do **not** instantly love Mau.

Many have never met him.

They learn him indirectly through:
- Frieren's deterioration;
- Bocchi's worry;
- Ori;
- Rimuru;
- Maki/Yuta;
- people automatically starting to ask Mau for help and then remembering he is not there;
- chores / small responsibilities that Mau usually picked up without announcing;
- stories that disagree in tone but overlap in pattern.

Strong perspective:
> "This is the guy everyone is tearing the region apart for?"

Possible reactions:
- disbelief;
- irritation at resource cost;
- curiosity;
- empathy for someone they do not know;
- practical concern that a missing unstable anomaly is dangerous;
- concern for people who care about him.

This preserves:
> Mau has relational gravity, not universal adoration.

Agott remains especially valuable as the person willing to ask why so much capacity is being spent on him.

# 6. G5 and the unfinished settlement

The worksite continues through the Memory crisis.

G5 can help because:
- half the usual labor is searching;
- the old inn is overcrowded;
- drainage/material/shelter problems do not wait for Mau;
- helping gives strangers something concrete to do while surrounded by a family crisis they do not yet share.

Examples:
- Senku spots inconsistent measurements and records them for later;
- Suika helps scout/carry/observe small route issues;
- WHA girls help with safe, maintainable magic under Qifrey/Agott/Coco;
- Jinshi notices organizational/logistical bottlenecks;
- Kessoku members help domestic/cultural life, not construction optimization;
- Seiko helps identify supernatural hazards around routes;
- Milim can move material only under strict direction so she does not solve terrain by destroying it.

Important:
Senku's **major civic audit remains later**.

Early Senku:
```text
arrives
-> self-tests
-> notices Continuum limitation
-> helps with small measurable problems
-> learns local context
-> builds relationships
```

Later Senku:
```text
enough flawed work exists
-> full audit
-> "no"
-> tear-out / redesign arc
```

This preserves the existing V16 payoff.

# 7. Senku's Continuum limitation

Senku should visibly have answers that feel like they are **almost accessible** but sometimes fail to arrive at source-speed.

Not stupidity.
Not random amnesia.

Preferred effect:
- reduced working-memory / cross-domain throughput under unfamiliar Continuum load;
- he can still reason brilliantly;
- complex answers sometimes require paper/testing instead of appearing immediately;
- he notices the difference himself;
- he measures it.

This becomes a constructive limitation:
- documentation;
- peer checking;
- teaching;
- distributed expertise;
- city systems that survive without one genius.

Do not romanticize the loss.
Senku is allowed to hate the reduction.

# 8. Mau's RCT remains real

Do not forget Mau's Reverse Cursed Technique / regenerative capacity after Sukuna.

Guardrail:
- it does not make Mau casually immortal;
- it costs energy/processing;
- it can stabilize local injuries;
- severe systemic crisis still requires Yuta/medical/support;
- memory corruption is not a wound RCT can simply heal;
- cross-template body adaptation is especially dangerous because RCT needs a stable concept of what the body is repairing **toward**.

Strong Memory-Arc use:
Mau's RCT may successfully repair local tissue while being unable to solve — or being deliberately limited during — the larger identity/body-template cascade.

This is why:
> "he has RCT" does not invalidate Rem/Kaneki/Aira/Rimuru/Raphael/Maomao/Yuta.

# 9. Mau body adaptation / Frieren lifespan hope

During second-disappearance stabilization, Raphael discovers that Mau's body has an adaptive mechanism/process **analogous in function, not identity, to Raphael-like analysis/optimization**.

It has observed multiple viable survival models and is attempting too many simultaneously.

Possible active templates/signals include:
- baseline Mau/human-like arrival body;
- elf-like longevity / magical compatibility;
- ghoul-like regeneration / kagune biology;
- slime-like restructuring;
- cursed-energy/RCT-informed repair;
- other partially understood systems Mau has observed.

The crisis is **not**:
> Mau is becoming one clean new species.

It is:
> several incompatible adaptation attempts are firing at once and causing a biological/metaphysical short circuit.

Rimuru/Raphael stabilization:
- filters/cancels the competing active adaptations;
- returns Mau as closely as possible to **the state in which he arrived**;
- does not choose "human" as metaphysical truth;
- does not prove Mau's species/origin;
- does not cure The Noise.

### Required Frieren reaction

When Frieren hears:
> "I restored him to how he arrived."

she asks whether Rimuru left him as an elf / made the longevity adaptation stable.

Answer:
**No.**

Frieren reacts badly.

This is emotionally tied to the fact that if Mau is simply human, his remaining lifespan is tiny relative to hers.

Rimuru must explain:
- forcing a new template **right now** risks sending Mau into shock;
- his systems are still too unstable to safely select a permanent biological form;
- this does **not** mean he can never adapt;
- the fact that an elf-like longevity route appeared at all means future controlled adaptation may be possible;
- nobody knows what Mau's true original/baseline species actually is.

This gives Frieren **hope without a promise**.

Do not resolve the lifespan problem in S3.

# 10. Post-Memory Mau/Frieren date

After Mau reaches enough lucidity and physical stability, they need intentionally ordinary time **alone**.

Purpose:
not "romance cures trauma."

Purpose:
> create one new memory they both know they are making now.

Required emotional beat:

Mau can ask:
> "Is this real?"

Frieren confirms the present without pretending certainty about every memory.

Later:
> "What if I forget again?"

Frieren:
> "Then I'll remind you."

Mau:
> "How many times?"

Frieren:
> "As many as it takes."

Exact final prose can vary, but the meaning should remain.

Frieren is not made owner of Mau's identity.
She is promising continued presence if access to shared memory fails again.

# 11. Mau's absence becomes visible labor

During the first disappearance / recovery, people begin noticing small things Mau normally did because they try to ask him automatically and remember he is gone.

Examples:
- carry something;
- check a route;
- help interpret a strange system;
- fix / prototype a small object;
- mediate an awkward conversation;
- help a newcomer;
- go with someone to inspect something.

Do not imply:
> the community cannot function without Mau.

Instead:
> people had normalized his constant low-level availability so thoroughly that absence reveals the pattern.

This gives G5 a way to know Mau through other people's habits before meeting him.

After recovery, some G5 members can be underwhelmed by Mau visually/personally.

That is healthy.

# 12. S3 third ring: arrivals as society, not cast collection

After G5, S3 should **not** keep escalating by adding more major franchises.

A third ring can form from:
- secondary characters from already active source families;
- travelers;
- refugees;
- temporary residents;
- people who eventually join the second community;
- people who stay only a few days;
- people who later choose Return;
- people whose main narrative function is reporting what is changing elsewhere.

Not every new arrival receives a permanent ensemble slot.

This lets the settlement feel large without the story becoming a roll call.

# 13. Regional world-adaptation reports

S3 should begin showing that Continuum is adapting unevenly across geography.

Example:
an Otherworlder arrives from the north and describes:
- roadside apparitions;
- strange spirits;
- humanoid monsters;
- behavior they do not have terminology for.

They do **not** say "yokai" if they do not know the concept.

Okarun / Momo / Seiko / Turbo Granny can recognize the pattern.

This reveals:
- systems are appearing outside the main cast;
- not every region experiences the same phenomenon;
- not everything attacks simultaneously;
- Continuum is developing localized ecologies / rule interactions.

Guardrail:
do not turn the map into franchise theme parks.

Better:
```text
one region shows higher incidence of phenomena that resemble X
another has mixed manifestations
some remain mostly native
rules overlap and mutate
```

This is S3 seeding.

S4 can investigate it much more directly.

# 14. Parallel villain growth during S3

The antagonistic side must also evolve while the settlement grows.

Do not let S3 feel like:
> heroes build city while villains wait offscreen for finale.

Required long-running thread:
- Sukuna survives / persists according to existing continuity;
- a hostile higher-order entity or intelligence finds him;
- it offers a plan / operating freedom;
- Sukuna is allowed or encouraged to destroy broadly outside whatever the entity considers protected/claimed/useful;
- Sukuna accepts for his own reasons, not as a loyal subordinate;
- the entity gains a catastrophic instrument it does not fully control;
- additional villains / dangerous arrivals may align, cooperate temporarily, or be manipulated.

Do not lock the full villain roster yet.

Important:
Sukuna must still feel like Sukuna.
He should not become a henchman who follows orders because plot needs a team.

This thread should advance in interludes across S3 so the Last Defense / later S4 escalation feels earned.

# 15. S4 Return/Stay + recovery opportunity

The later Goddess option for people who **Stay** may include a limited opportunity to request/recover someone from another world/continuity.

Creator clarification:

> Choosing to bring/recover someone is **not morally bad**.

It may be:
- family;
- a lost friend;
- someone believed dead;
- someone unreachable;
- someone whose absence has defined the Arrival's life.

Preferred emotional bias:
> use this opportunity especially for people characters feel they **lost**, not merely as a shopping list of favorite cast.

But do not moralize against choosing family.

The consequence is agency after reunion.

The recovered/invited person may:
- love the Arrival and still disagree with Stay;
- ask whether the choice was right;
- want to Return;
- want to remain;
- be grateful and angry simultaneously;
- create new family conflict;
- reveal that "getting someone back" does not erase what happened while apart.

This is S4 material.

Do not frame:
> "you chose to recover someone, therefore you were selfish."

The question is what both people choose **after** reunion.

Exact divine mechanics remain open.

# 18. Call of the Night adaptation — Anko/Nazuna medical consequence

Creator seed:

At some point Nazuna and Anko have a serious clash.
Nazuna bites Anko in circumstances that echo their source relationship, but Continuum's adaptation rules differ.

Possible result:
- Anko transforms / begins vampire conversion despite source-world prerequisites not matching exactly.

Important guardrails:
- do not make every casual Nazuna bite automatically create a vampire;
- Anko's specific compatibility/history/Continuum state can make this an exceptional cascade;
- transformation creates a real medical/social problem;
- Maomao has to help establish safe blood access / collection / storage;
- consent and supply matter.

Critical privacy rule:

> Blood consumption does **not** automatically reveal autobiographical memories.

Do not introduce a blood-memory mechanic that destroys the privacy/trust architecture built through Memory Arc.

Blood may carry biological information or other limited signals if later needed, but not automatic access to the donor's memories.

This can become one more example of Continuum:
- changing a source rule;
- creating consequences;
- forcing society to build infrastructure around a person rather than treating them as a monster.
