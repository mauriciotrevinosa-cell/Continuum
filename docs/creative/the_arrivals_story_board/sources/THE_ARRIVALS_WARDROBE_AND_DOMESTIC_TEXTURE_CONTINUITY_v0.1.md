# The Arrivals — Wardrobe + Domestic Texture Continuity v0.1

**Status:** APPROVED CREATOR DIRECTION  
**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Scope:** S1 LN hardening + S2/S3 continuity  
**Authority:** later creator decisions surgically supersede older generic wardrobe treatment

## 1. Core adaptation rule

Wardrobe and ordinary household objects are story-bearing continuity.

The LN should prefer **expansion over compression** when a small visual/domestic beat:
- shows a relationship becoming habitual;
- preserves an object that will matter later;
- makes a room feel lived in;
- demonstrates a character's changing place in the household;
- creates a future callback;
- or simply gives the cast ordinary life between plot beats.

These beats do not need to advance macro plot to earn space.

Do not reduce recurring objects to generic wording when their identity already carries history.

Examples:
- a specific hoodie instead of `an outer layer`;
- a specific shirt disappearing from a shared room instead of `some clothes`;
- the same cup changing rooms;
- a cap no longer sitting where a hand expects it;
- a garment being casually borrowed on an otherwise uneventful morning.

This is the prose equivalent of the lived-in visual acting that manga panels would otherwise carry.

## 2. Mau — arrival wardrobe / belongings lock

Mau arrives in the new world with modern personal belongings that become some of the only physical evidence of a life he cannot remember.

### Worn on arrival
- **2021 McLaren Team Hoodie**, black/orange.
- **TAG Heuer Formula 1 watch**, gray with orange accents.

Jeans / sneakers remain visually non-symbolic and may be resolved by the approved character model/wardrobe reference. Do not elevate them into continuity symbols unless later locked.

### Backpack
Mau arrives with a backpack containing changes of clothing and accessories.

Locked recurring pieces:
- **McLaren Triple Crown Edition cap**;
- **navy Massimo Dutti cotton knitted polo**;
- **red 2025 Ferrari Team long-sleeve/button shirt**;
- **white T-shirt**;
- **white 2026 Real Madrid long-sleeve jersey**, personalized on back:
  - `MAU T`
  - `5`
- **Un Verano Sin Ti Tour bucket hat**.

The previously explored bomber is **removed** and must not reappear by drift.

### Narrative function
These belongings are not fashion trivia.

For an amnesiac character they are evidence:
- Mau can recognize categories/brands/functions without recovering the personal memories attached to owning them;
- the clothes gradually stop feeling like evidence from an unknown previous life and become simply `Mau's things`;
- once his belongings mix into Frieren's room, their movement/absence can carry relationship information without dialogue.

## 3. Mau name discovery — later correction

The older S1E1 version where Mau simply retrieves the name `Mau` from memory is superseded.

Current direction:
1. Mau cannot retrieve his name when Frieren asks.
2. His backpack is inspected among the belongings that arrived with him.
3. The personalized Real Madrid jersey provides the readable identity clue `MAU T / 5`.
4. Frieren proposes:
   - `Maybe your name is Mau.`
5. Mau accepts the offered anchor:
   - `I like it.`

The emotional point is not forensic certainty that the jersey legally proves his identity.

The point is that his first usable name in the new world comes from:
- a physical object from the inaccessible life;
- and Frieren helping him turn that evidence into something he can use now.

## 4. Frieren wardrobe is stateful, not a single default

Frieren does not remain in one default outfit across domestic life.

At minimum her continuity distinguishes:
- normal day / travel clothing;
- winter/cold-weather clothing;
- sleep / pajama state;
- later borrowed Mau garments where approved.

Exact visual designs continue to come from the approved wardrobe/character packs.

The LN should name the state when it matters rather than silently rendering every scene as default travel Frieren.

## 5. Borrowing Mau's clothes = recurring domestic behavior

Frieren casually borrowing Mau's things is an ongoing household behavior, not only a setup/callback device.

Approved existing arc:
- pre-separation: borrowing is already normal;
- separation: missing garments/accessories make the room feel wrong;
- E14 reconciliation: McLaren hoodie returns and Mau obliviously says `Nice hoodie.`;
- E18: lowered inhibition exaggerates an existing behavior into McLaren hoodie + McLaren cap + Frieren's own comfortable short bottoms.

Additional ordinary-life uses are encouraged when they fit the day naturally.

Example already favored by creator direction:
- Frieren can casually wear Mau's red Ferrari team shirt on an ordinary day.

The beat should not stop the plot for symbolism.
The fact that nobody treats it as extraordinary is part of the point.

## 6. The shared-room arc should use identifiable absences

When Mau moves into his nominal room during the E11–E13 misunderstanding, do not compress the change into `some clothes disappeared`.

Use recognizable objects whose previous presence has already been established.

Examples:
- McLaren cap missing from its usual place;
- red Ferrari team shirt no longer hanging where Frieren had casually left/used it;
- Mau's cup gone;
- specific books moved;
- watch/accessories stored elsewhere where appropriate.

The reader should be able to feel the room becoming wrong before the characters explain why.

## 7. Rimuru = first household-wide wardrobe expansion

Mau is not the only character allowed wardrobe variety.

After Rimuru arrives, the household can gradually gain:
- repaired garments;
- resized/adapted clothing;
- warmer/cooler practical variants;
- replacement basics;
- simple new pieces made from available/salvaged/traded materials.

This applies across the cast.

Rules:
- additions must enter gradually;
- source of the garment should be legible when important;
- do not spawn an unlimited wardrobe off-screen;
- recurring pieces should be reused;
- characters should develop individual clothing habits.

Rimuru enables practical expansion. He does not instantly turn the inn into a fashion shop.

## 8. Wakana = later complexity / personalization step

After Wakana reaches the household and has time/resources to work, wardrobe can become more sophisticated.

Wakana can enable:
- better fit;
- deliberate tailoring;
- repair that preserves favorite garments;
- layered combinations;
- personalized pieces;
- more character-specific everyday, work, winter and formal/casual options.

This should happen progressively and through character interaction.

Wakana's presence is a natural story reason for the visual wardrobe language of the ensemble to deepen.

## 9. S2 hoodie callbacks and continuing life

The McLaren hoodie should continue to live as an ordinary household object in S2 rather than appearing only for payoff.

### Post-Sukuna / Second Chance
Strong direction:
- when Mau wakes after his death/revival, Frieren can be wearing his familiar McLaren hoodie;
- Mau notices and makes a simple, character-consistent comment that it looks good / is a nice hoodie;
- exact wording remains open unless separately locked.

This rhymes with E14 without requiring Mau to deliver a speech about it.

### Newcomer observation
Strong direction / wording open:
- at a suitable S2 domestic moment, a newcomer may notice Frieren wearing Mau's clothing and ask some version of:
  - `Isn't that Mau's hoodie?`
- an established resident can answer with the emotional equivalent of:
  - `Don't ask. This is normal.`

This is not mandatory dialogue and should be used only where timing is natural.

## 10. General ensemble rule

Wardrobe progression should happen for **everyone**, not only Mau/Frieren.

A lived-in household naturally develops:
- favorite repeated pieces;
- repaired pieces;
- weather variants;
- sleepwear;
- work clothing;
- borrowed/shared items where relationship-specific;
- new garments after trade/Rimuru/Wakana expansion.

The goal is not fashion catalog detail.

The goal is for the reader to feel that these people wake up, change clothes, wash things, repair them, borrow them, lose them, recognize them and live together between major events.


## 12. S2 loss / S3 recovery — McLaren Triple Crown Edition cap

Continuity direction promoted during S3 development:

```text
Momo places Mau's McLaren Triple Crown Edition cap on Bocchi
-> Bocchi keeps it for the S2 ordinary supply trip
-> the Bocchi incident erupts
-> the cap is lost in the mercantile-town street
-> on the road home Bocchi notices it is gone
-> Mau refuses to care about the object while she is injured
-> the cap remains unrecovered for the rest of S2
-> sometime afterward it is discarded / washed / carried toward a river
-> Rem finds the water-worn cap during the S3 Memory Arc search for Mau
```

Important prop rule:

> Do not silently restore the cap to Mau's wardrobe before this S3 recovery.

The exact route from mercantile street to river is intentionally mundane and may remain only implied.

The recovery works as continuity texture, not as a supernatural object mechanic.
